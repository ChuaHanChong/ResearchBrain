# "PhyWorldBench": A Comprehensive Evaluation of Physical Realism in Text-to-Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of California, Santa Cruz, NVIDIA Research, and Northeastern University introduce PhyWorldBench, a comprehensive benchmark to evaluate text-to-video models' adherence to physical laws. The benchmark evaluates 12 state-of-the-art models across fundamental, composite, and "Anti-Physics" categories and proposes a Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) method using Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for efficient, objective assessment of physical realism.

## Problem

- Text-to-video (T2V) models produce visually compelling content but struggle with accurately simulating and adhering to the laws of physics.
- Existing T2V benchmarks often lack comprehensive coverage of diverse physical phenomena, including scenarios specifically designed to test for physical understanding.
- Evaluating physical realism in generated videos is costly and subjective, requiring a more efficient and objective methodology.

## Method

- Developed **PhyWorldBench**, a multi-dimensional benchmark featuring 10 main physics categories, 50 subcategories, and 1,050 curated prompts, including a novel "Anti-Physics" category to probe models' understanding of physical law violations.
- Introduced the Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) method, which leverages Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with explicit context and Chain-of-Thought reasoning for scalable and objective zero-shot physics evaluation.
- Conducted an extensive evaluation of 12 state-of-the-art text-to-video generation models by generating 12,600 videos, assessing their physical realism through both human annotation and the CAP method.

## Results

- Pika 2.0 achieved the highest overall success rate of 0.262 in physical realism, with other models demonstrating varying strengths but generally low performance, revealing significant room for improvement across the board.
- Models struggled particularly with scenarios involving erratic visual changes (22.1% success rate) and complex physical interactions (11.7% success rate), often simplifying or avoiding abrupt transformations.
- Physics-enhanced prompts generally led to better results, indicating models can utilize explicit physical cues in prompts, but detailed narrative prompts primarily enhanced visual aesthetics rather than physics accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art text-to-video models exhibit consistently low success rates (e.g., Pika 2.0 at 26.2%) in generating physically accurate content, indicating substantial limitations in physics understanding.
> - Models frequently 'rationalize' failures in "Anti-Physics" scenarios by reverting to physically plausible or static scenes, suggesting they primarily reproduce patterns from real-world training data rather than demonstrating a deep causal understanding of physics.
> - The Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) method significantly improves MLLM-based evaluation, achieving ROC-AUC scores of 80.3 for Semantic Adherence and 75.1 for Physical Commonsense, validating MLLMs as effective tools for objective and scalable assessment.
