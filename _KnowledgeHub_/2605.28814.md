# Self-Improving Language Models with Bidirectional Evolutionary Search

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harvard and MIT developed Bidirectional Evolutionary Search (BES), a framework that integrates evolutionary operators with a backward search for goal decomposition, enabling language models and agentic systems to discover higher-quality solutions. The method showed performance gains of up to 3.8% on multi-hop reasoning for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and consistently outperformed baselines on open problem-solving tasks like Circle Packing and Heilbronn problems.

## Problem

- Existing sampling methods for language models (LLMs) and agentic systems, such as best-of-N and tree search, rely on sparse, often binary verification signals, hindering effective guidance for complex tasks.
- These methods primarily use autoregressive expansion, confining candidate solution generation to high-probability regions of the model's distribution, limiting exploration for solutions in low-probability areas.

## Method

- Introducing Bidirectional Evolutionary Search (**BES**), a coupled framework that combines a forward search using evolutionary operators with a backward search for goal decomposition.
- The forward search incorporates expansion (autoregressive generation) and novel evolution operators (combination, deletion, translocation, crossover) to generate diverse candidate trajectories beyond typical autoregressive outputs.
- The backward search recursively decomposes the main problem into a hierarchical tree of fine-grained sub-goals, providing dense, interpretable verification signals for evaluating partial trajectories.

## Results

- For post-training, BES achieved substantial performance gains of +**3.0%** on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and +**3.8%** on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for multi-hop reasoning (MuSiQue), outperforming GRPO and Tree-GRPO baselines.
- For inference, BES consistently surpassed all open-source frameworks on open problem-solving benchmarks (e.g., Circle Packing, Heilbronn), yielding higher average and best objective values with lower variance.
- An ablation study confirmed that both the answer reweighting mechanism (supporting bidirectional search) and the evolution operators independently contributed to BES's overall effectiveness across tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolutionary operators can generate candidate trajectories with log-probabilities strictly beyond the 'entropy shell' of autoregressive expansion, allowing exploration of a wider, less-probable solution space.
> - Backward-guided search offers an exponential advantage in sample complexity over terminal-only search by transforming problem-solving into collecting evidence for sub-goals, providing dense intermediate feedback.
> - Combining bidirectional search with evolutionary operators creates a powerful mechanism for self-improving LLMs and agents, applicable to both post-training sample generation and inference-time problem-solving.
