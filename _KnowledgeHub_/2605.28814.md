# Self-Improving Language Models with Bidirectional Evolutionary Search

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harvard and MIT developed Bidirectional Evolutionary Search (BES), a framework that integrates evolutionary operators with a backward search for goal decomposition, enabling language models and agentic systems to discover higher-quality solutions. The method showed performance gains of up to 3.8% on multi-hop reasoning for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and consistently outperformed baselines on open problem-solving tasks like Circle Packing and Heilbronn problems.

## Problem

- Existing sampling methods for language models (LLMs) and agentic systems, such as best-of-N and tree search, rely on sparse, often binary verification signals, hindering effective guidance for complex tasks.
- These methods primarily use autoregressive expansion, confining candidate solution generation to high-probability regions of the model's distribution, limiting exploration for solutions in low-probability areas.
- Reinforcement learning post-training without dense intermediate feedback is prone to reward hacking, where models learn to guess final answers rather than genuinely performing multi-step search or reasoning.

## Method

- Introducing Bidirectional Evolutionary Search (**BES**), a coupled framework that combines a forward search using evolutionary operators with a backward search for goal decomposition.
- The forward search incorporates expansion (autoregressive generation) and novel evolution operators (combination, deletion, translocation, crossover) to generate diverse candidate trajectories beyond typical autoregressive outputs.
- The backward search recursively decomposes the main problem into a hierarchical tree of fine-grained sub-goals, providing dense, interpretable verification signals for evaluating partial trajectories.

## Results

- For post-training, BES achieved substantial performance gains of +**3.0%** on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and +**3.8%** on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for multi-hop reasoning (MuSiQue), outperforming GRPO and Tree-GRPO baselines.
- For inference, BES consistently surpassed all open-source frameworks on open problem-solving benchmarks (e.g., Circle Packing, Heilbronn), yielding higher average and best objective values with lower variance.
- An ablation study confirmed that both the answer reweighting mechanism (supporting bidirectional search) and the evolution operators independently contributed to BES's overall effectiveness across tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolutionary operators can generate candidate trajectories with log-probabilities strictly beyond the 'entropy shell' of autoregressive expansion, allowing exploration of a wider, less-probable solution space.
> - Backward-guided search offers an exponential advantage in sample complexity over terminal-only search by transforming problem-solving into collecting evidence for sub-goals, providing dense intermediate feedback.
> - Combining bidirectional search with evolutionary operators creates a powerful mechanism for self-improving LLMs and agents, applicable to both post-training sample generation and inference-time problem-solving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) and agentic systems have demonstrated capabilities in complex reasoning and problem-solving, including generating code and solving mathematical and scientific problems. A central challenge in advancing these systems involves efficient and effective sampling strategies, particularly for tasks at the frontier of current model capabilities where naive sampling methods are often insufficient. Improved sampling is crucial both for post-training self-improvement and for test-time scaling at inference.

Current dominant sampling methods, such as best-of-N sampling and tree search (e.g., beam search, Monte Carlo Tree Search), exhibit two primary limitations. First, they rely on sparse verification signals, often providing only binary or coarse-grained feedback, which can hinder effective search guidance. Second, they construct candidate solutions primarily through autoregressive expansion, thus confining exploration to regions of the solution space where the model assigns substantial probability mass. This limits their ability to discover correct solutions that may reside in low-probability regions.

This work introduces Bidirectional Evolutionary Search (BES) as an alternative framework designed to address these limitations. BES integrates concepts from evolutionary biology and classical search methods into the context of LLM and agentic system self-improvement. It builds upon prior research in LLM and agent self-improvement, which includes methods like STaR, Self-Refine, Self-Rewarding Language Models, Reflexion, and Voyager, typically focusing on refining individual trajectories or using models for self-evaluation. BES distinguishes itself by framing self-improvement as a structured search problem aimed at discovering high-quality solutions. The paper also connects to existing LLM and agent search methods such as Tree of Thoughts, Graph of Thoughts, and evolutionary approaches like AlphaEvolve, ShinkaEvolve, and ThetaEvolve, which have explored search-based reasoning but often retain the limitations of autoregressive generation. Furthermore, BES draws inspiration from classical search algorithms like A*, bidirectional search, branch-and-bound methods, and evolutionary computing techniques such as genetic search and differential evolution. By combining bidirectional search with evolutionary operators, BES attempts to expand the search space and leverage denser feedback, thereby positioning itself as an advancement in methods for discovering robust solutions for LLMs and agentic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more effective search framework for self-improving language models and agentic systems, applicable to both post-training sample generation and inference-time problem-solving. This objective is motivated by two fundamental limitations identified in widely used search methods:

1. **Sparse Verification Signals:** Existing methods like best-of-N sampling and tree search typically rely on sparse feedback mechanisms. In many common settings, such as reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) or similar post-training paradigms, verifiers provide only binary (correct/incorrect) or coarse-grained evaluations. This sparsity makes it challenging to guide the search process efficiently, as intermediate steps or partially correct solutions often do not receive informative feedback. Consequently, the search might struggle to converge on complex problems that require multiple steps of reasoning or action. The motivation here is to provide denser, more granular feedback to guide the search more effectively.

2. **Confined Candidate Generation:** Traditional methods primarily construct candidates through autoregressive expansion, where each new step or token is generated sequentially based on preceding ones. This approach confines the generated candidates to the support of the model's own distribution. While effective for problems where optimal solutions align with high-probability paths, it limits exploration in challenging tasks where correct solutions may lie in low-probability regions or require non-obvious combinations of steps that a single autoregressive rollout is unlikely to produce. The motivation is to enable the generation of a wider diversity of candidates that can escape the "entropy shell" of typical model rollouts, facilitating the discovery of novel and effective solutions.

To address these motivations, BES proposes a dual approach:
- **Bidirectional Search:** This component aims to overcome the sparse verification signal by coupling a forward search (generating candidates) with a backward search (decomposing the problem into verifiable sub-goals). The backward search provides dense, intermediate feedback by evaluating how well candidate trajectories address these finer-grained sub-goals, even if a full solution has not yet been found. This continuous feedback is intended to provide better guidance than end-of-trajectory, binary signals.
- **Evolutionary Operators:** This component aims to broaden candidate generation beyond autoregressive expansion. Drawing inspiration from biological evolution, BES introduces operators (combination, translocation, deletion, crossover) that recombine or modify segments of existing partial trajectories. This recombination allows for the creation of candidates that might be difficult to obtain through sequential generation alone, effectively enabling the search to "escape" the narrow entropy shell imposed by standard autoregressive processes and explore a wider, potentially more fruitful, solution space.

The theoretical motivations provided in the paper further bolster these objectives, demonstrating that evolutionary operators can indeed generate candidates with log-probabilities beyond the typical set of expansion-only search, and that backward search can exponentially reduce the sample complexity for finding a solution by transforming a multiplicative success probability problem into a collection problem for sub-goals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Bidirectional Evolutionary Search (BES) operates as a coupled framework integrating forward candidate generation with backward goal decomposition.

#### 3.1. Forward Search: Expanding the Reachable Solution Space

The forward search component is responsible for generating and evolving candidate partial trajectories. Each candidate, or node, is represented as a sequence of steps. The search maintains a `candidate set P` of these nodes and iteratively applies one of two types of operators:

- **Expansion:** This is a standard autoregressive process where a selected parent node is extended by sampling new steps from the policy ($\pi_\theta$). A step count `K` (uniform between 1 and `K_max`) determines the number of new steps appended to the parent trajectory.
- **Evolution Operators:** These operators are designed to restructure and recombine existing trajectories, inspired by biological evolution. They aim to generate candidates that are less likely to be produced by simple autoregressive expansion. Four specific operators are defined:
 - **Combination:** Merges two trajectories by concatenating their suffixes beyond a shared prefix.
 - **Deletion:** Removes a single interior step from a trajectory, producing a shorter candidate.
 - **Translocation:** Replaces a single step in one trajectory with a step from another trajectory.
 - **Crossover:** Splices the prefix of one trajectory onto the tail of another, effectively swapping segments.
 In settings where direct concatenation or token-level edits are not meaningful (e.g., executable programs), these operators can be implemented by prompting the policy model to perform the recombination or editing task.

At each forward search step, an operator is chosen from a fixed probability distribution. For single-parent operators (expansion, deletion), the parent node is selected from the eligible candidate set `C_t` using a Boltzmann distribution based on its backward score `s(n)`. This distribution prioritizes higher-scoring nodes, with a small bonus for unexplored nodes to encourage diversity. For two-parent operators (combination, translocation, crossover), a pair of parent nodes is selected from `C_t` using a Boltzmann distribution based on a pair score `s(n_a, n_b)` that favors complementary parents covering different problem aspects. A temperature parameter `τ_t` is annealed linearly throughout the search, shifting from exploration to exploitation.

#### 3.2. Backward Search: Better Verification through Goal Decomposition

The backward search component addresses the sparsity of verification signals by decomposing the main problem into a hierarchical tree of fine-grained sub-goals.

- **Goal Tree Construction:** Starting from the root goal (solving the entire problem), the policy `π_θ` is prompted to recursively break down each goal into finer sub-goals (`ch(g)`). Each sub-goal `g` is associated with a specific verifier `V_g(x, n) ∈` that measures how well a candidate trajectory `n` addresses that sub-goal for problem `x`. The verifier for the root goal is the original problem verifier `V`.
- **Adaptive Decomposition:** This decomposition process is re-invoked every `K_dec` forward search steps. During re-invocation, a leaf sub-goal that no current candidate fully satisfies is selected, and `π_θ` is prompted to split it into even finer sub-goals. All existing forward nodes are then re-scored against this updated goal tree.
- **Sub-goal Verifiers:** Verifiers are task-dependent and can range from rule-based checkers, test-case code executors, and embedding similarity models to LLM judgers.
- **Scoring Mechanism:** For any candidate node `n` and sub-goal `g`, a sub-goal score `s(n, g)` is computed. If `V_g(x, n) = 1` (sub-goal fully satisfied), the score is `1`. Otherwise, `s(n, g)` is a blend of the local verifier output `V_g(x, n)` and the average score of its children, weighted by `α`. The overall score for a node `n` is `s(n) ≜ s(n, g_root)`. A pair score `s(n_a, n_b, g)` is also defined, favoring complementary parents by taking the maximum of their individual sub-goal verifier scores. These scores provide dense, interpretable feedback, guiding the selection of promising candidates in the forward search.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Motivations

The paper provides theoretical arguments to support the efficacy of BES:

- **Evolution Operators:** It is formally motivated that autoregressive expansion-only search methods are confined to a narrow "entropy shell," meaning the candidates generated have log-probabilities concentrated around the expected trajectory entropy. Evolution operators, by recombining parts of different trajectories, can generate candidates with log-probabilities strictly beyond this shell, enabling exploration of a wider, lower-probability solution space.
- **Bidirectional Search:** It is theoretically shown that backward-guided bidirectional search offers an exponential advantage over terminal-only search. By leveraging sub-goal signals, BES can find evidence for all sub-goals with significantly fewer samples (polynomially dependent on `p_min` and logarithmically on `m`) compared to terminal-only search which requires a number of samples inversely proportional to the product of individual sub-goal success probabilities (exponentially dependent on `m`).

#### 3.4. Using BES for Post-Training and Inference

- **Post-Training:** BES is integrated into the sample-generation stage of post-training algorithms. It produces a set of high-quality trajectories that are then used as training data, replacing samples obtained from i.i.d. rollouts. This is expected to provide a stronger training signal, especially for hard problems.
- **Inference:** BES is run with a fixed compute budget on open problems, returning the highest-scoring terminal trajectory as the final answer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of BES spanned both post-training and inference tasks across LLM and agent settings.

#### 4.1. Bidirectional Evolutionary Search for Post-Training: Logical Reasoning (Knights-and-Knaves)

- **Task:** Deduce identities (knight/knave) in logical puzzles.
- **Baselines:** GRPO and MaxRL (applied on top of MaxRL).
- **Results:** On challenging training sets, GRPO and MaxRL showed minimal or no improvement in validation accuracy. In contrast, BES enabled consistent and steady improvements throughout training, indicating its effectiveness in discovering high-quality training samples where baselines struggled.

#### 4.1.2. Multi-Hop Reasoning (MuSiQue)

- **Task:** Answer complex questions requiring information retrieval and integration across multiple documents.
- **Baselines:** GRPO and Tree-GRPO (state-of-the-art for search agent post-training), with BES applied on top of GRPO.
- **Models:** Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
- **Results:**
 - GRPO consistently degraded performance from the base model, suggesting reward hacking where the model learned to guess answers without proper search.
 - Tree-GRPO provided only marginal improvement on the 8B model (+0.8%) and failed to improve the 3B model.
 - BES achieved substantial gains on both model scales (+3.0% on 3B, +3.8% on 8B), significantly outperforming all baselines.
 - Beyond accuracy, BES-trained agents exhibited a higher number of valid search actions and higher finish ratios, indicating that the agents learned to actively engage in problem-solving rather than making random guesses.

#### 4.2. Bidirectional Evolutionary Search for Inference: Open Problem Solving Benchmarks

- **Tasks:**
 - Circle Packing (Square): Maximize circle radius sum in a unit square.
 - Circle Packing (Rectangle): Analogous problem for a rectangular container.
 - Heilbronn (Convex): Maximize the minimum area of any convex polygon formed by points in a unit square.
- **Baselines:** OpenEvolve, GEPA, and ShinkaEvolve (BES applied on top of ShinkaEvolve). Human experts and AlphaEvolve (closed-source, higher compute) were included as reference.
- **Model:** GPT-5.
- **Results:** BES consistently outperformed all open-source frameworks on every benchmark in terms of both average and best objective values. It also exhibited lower variance across runs, indicating more stable and reliable search performance. The best programs identified by BES were briefly summarized, highlighting hybrid global optimizers and multi-start constructors with two-stage refinement techniques.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the Knights-and-Knaves benchmark demonstrated the contribution of each BES component. Removing either the answer reweighting mechanism (from MaxRL) or the evolution operators individually led to reduced performance compared to the full BES method, although the ablated versions still outperformed the GRPO and MaxRL baselines. This suggests that both bidirectional search (which MaxRL's reweighting supports by using better samples) and evolution operators contribute to the overall effectiveness of BES.

#### 4.4. Cost Analysis

- **Post-Training (Multi-Hop Reasoning):** For Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, BES incurred less than 30% additional wall-clock time compared to Tree-GRPO, while achieving substantially higher accuracy and more valid search actions. GRPO, despite having the lowest wall-clock time, suffered from reward hacking and poor performance.
- **Inference (Open Problem Solving):** BES achieved consistently higher average objective values with only modest additional API costs compared to ShinkaEvolve across all three open problem-solving benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents BES as a general-purpose search framework that addresses identified limitations in existing LLM and agent sampling methodologies. Its significance lies in offering a theoretically motivated and empirically validated approach to improve the quality of outputs from language models and agentic systems, particularly for complex reasoning and problem-solving tasks.

The framework's dual approach of combining evolutionary operators with a bidirectional search mechanism holds several potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By enabling LLMs and agents to explore a wider range of candidate solutions and receive denser feedback, BES can lead to stronger reasoning performance. This could facilitate the discovery of solutions that are difficult for conventional autoregressive methods to reach.
- **Improved Self-Improvement:** The ability of BES to generate high-quality samples for post-training can make self-improvement algorithms more effective, potentially reducing the need for extensive human supervision or very large models to achieve desired performance levels. This could contribute to more efficient model development and deployment.
- **Broader Problem-Solving:** The demonstrated success on challenging open problems suggests that BES could be a valuable tool for scientific discovery and algorithmic innovation, where novel solutions often reside in less-explored areas of the search space.
- **Increased Interpretability:** The backward search component, by explicitly decomposing problems into verifiable sub-goals, offers a more transparent and interpretable view of the search process. This can provide insights into why certain solutions are favored and what sub-problems remain unsolved.
- **Resource Efficiency:** By improving the sample efficiency of self-improvement and problem-solving, BES could potentially reduce the computational and environmental costs associated with training and running large language models, by achieving more with smaller models or fewer samples.

However, the authors also acknowledge potential limitations. BES requires a clearly defined objective reward signal, which may restrict its applicability to subjective tasks like creative writing. The effectiveness of the backward search is also contingent on the policy's ability to meaningfully decompose problems, which might be a challenge for very weak base models. Additionally, the post-training experiments were conducted with relatively smaller models (up to 8B parameters), and scalability to much larger models would require further investigation. The broader impact also includes the consideration that more effective search methods, while beneficial, could potentially enhance capabilities for misuse if applied to harmful tasks.
