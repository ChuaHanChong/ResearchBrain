# MobileH2R: Learning Generalizable Human to Mobile Robot Handover Exclusively from Scalable and Diverse Synthetic Data

> [!summary] Summary
> MobileH2R presents a framework for learning generalizable human-to-mobile-robot handover skills entirely from scalable and diverse synthetic data, eliminating the need for real-world demonstrations. This approach achieves a 26.3% higher success rate and reduces execution time by 5.06s compared to non-end-to-end baselines in simulation, successfully transferring to real robots with an 80% success rate in simple handover tasks.

## Problem

- Acquiring generalizable human-to-mobile-robot (H2MR) handover skills is challenging due to the dynamic workspace and the necessity for coordinated base and arm control.
- Collecting diverse and scalable real-world human-robot interaction (HRI) data for training is costly, time-consuming, and carries significant safety risks.
- Existing H2R handover methods primarily focus on fixed-base robots, neglecting the complexities introduced by mobile platforms and full-body human interaction.

## Method

- Develops **MobileH2R**-Sim, a novel simulation environment, to generate diverse, natural, and reactive full-body human motions and object-aware interactions for handover scenarios.
- Creates an automated method to produce safe and imitation-friendly robot demonstrations using an optimization-based planner, incorporating future obstacle avoidance and a vision-state recovery estimator.
- Employs a 4D imitation learning approach that fuses multi-camera point clouds and temporal flow as input, training a single network to output coordinated base and arm actions simultaneously.

## Results

- The end-to-end MobileH2R framework achieved a **26.3%** higher success rate and reduced execution time by **5.06** seconds compared to non-end-to-end baselines in simulation.
- Leveraging full-body human input and coordinated base-arm actions, MobileH2R improved success rates by **17.0%** to **20.5%** over fixed-base H2R methods adapted for mobile robots.
- Policies trained exclusively on synthetic data successfully transferred to a real mobile robot, demonstrating an **80%** success rate in simple real-world handover scenarios and **63.3%** in complex ones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, scalable, and diverse synthetic data can fully replace real-world data collection for learning complex human-to-mobile-robot interaction skills.
> - Generating "imitation-friendly" robot demonstrations that optimize for clear vision-action correlation and proactively avoid future collisions is crucial for training robust and safe visuomotor policies.
> - A single, end-to-end visuomotor policy that simultaneously decodes both mobile base and robotic arm actions from scene-fused 4D input enables inherently coordinated and fluid H2MR handover.
