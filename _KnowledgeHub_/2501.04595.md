# MobileH2R: Learning Generalizable Human to Mobile Robot Handover Exclusively from Scalable and Diverse Synthetic Data

> [!summary] Summary
> MobileH2R presents a framework for learning generalizable human-to-mobile-robot handover skills entirely from scalable and diverse synthetic data, eliminating the need for real-world demonstrations. This approach achieves a 26.3% higher success rate and reduces execution time by 5.06s compared to non-end-to-end baselines in simulation, successfully transferring to real robots with an 80% success rate in simple handover tasks.

## Problem

- Acquiring generalizable human-to-mobile-robot (H2MR) handover skills is challenging due to the dynamic workspace and the necessity for coordinated base and arm control.
- Collecting diverse and scalable real-world human-robot interaction (HRI) data for training is costly, time-consuming, and carries significant safety risks.
- Existing H2R handover methods primarily focus on fixed-base robots, neglecting the complexities introduced by mobile platforms and full-body human interaction.

## Method

- Develops **MobileH2R**-Sim, a novel simulation environment, to generate diverse, natural, and reactive full-body human motions and object-aware interactions for handover scenarios.
- Creates an automated method to produce safe and imitation-friendly robot demonstrations using an optimization-based planner, incorporating future obstacle avoidance and a vision-state recovery estimator.
- Employs a 4D imitation learning approach that fuses multi-camera point clouds and temporal flow as input, training a single network to output coordinated base and arm actions simultaneously.

## Results

- The end-to-end MobileH2R framework achieved a **26.3%** higher success rate and reduced execution time by **5.06** seconds compared to non-end-to-end baselines in simulation.
- Leveraging full-body human input and coordinated base-arm actions, MobileH2R improved success rates by **17.0%** to **20.5%** over fixed-base H2R methods adapted for mobile robots.
- Policies trained exclusively on synthetic data successfully transferred to a real mobile robot, demonstrating an **80%** success rate in simple real-world handover scenarios and **63.3%** in complex ones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, scalable, and diverse synthetic data can fully replace real-world data collection for learning complex human-to-mobile-robot interaction skills.
> - Generating "imitation-friendly" robot demonstrations that optimize for clear vision-action correlation and proactively avoid future collisions is crucial for training robust and safe visuomotor policies.
> - A single, end-to-end visuomotor policy that simultaneously decodes both mobile base and robotic arm actions from scene-fused 4D input enables inherently coordinated and fluid H2MR handover.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. The Challenge of H2MR Handover

The development of Human-to-Robot (H2R) handover skills is a critical challenge in embodied AI, particularly for mobile robots (H2MR). Unlike fixed-base robotic arms, mobile robots operate in a large, dynamic workspace, necessitating robust perception of full-body human actions and coordinated control of both their base and arm. Traditional approaches often decompose the handover task into separate sub-problems (e.g., grasp estimation, trajectory planning), which limits holistic environmental modeling and generalizability. Learning these skills in the real world is inherently difficult due to safety risks, high costs, and the non-scalability of collecting diverse human-robot interaction (HRI) data.

#### 1.2. Limitations of Prior Work

Previous research in H2R handover, such as HandoverSim and GenH2R, primarily focused on fixed-base robotic arms. While these works leveraged synthetic assets and imitation learning to scale training, they often simplified human motion (e.g., only hand gestures) or lacked full-body human interaction modeling. Crucially, they did not address the complexities introduced by a mobile robot's base movement and the need for coordinated base-arm actions. Moreover, existing large-scale human motion datasets (e.g., AMASS, MotionX) often lack task-specific interactivity for complex HRI scenarios. Methods relying on real-world motion capture data, like DexYCB, provide realism but are limited in scale and diversity, failing to capture the full spectrum of real-world interactions. Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for mobile manipulation exist, but ensuring safety during human interaction, particularly with dynamic human movements, remains a significant hurdle for direct RL training.

#### 1.3. MobileH2R's Position in the Landscape

MobileH2R directly addresses these limitations by focusing on *generalizable human-to-mobile-robot (H2MR) handover* using *exclusively synthetic data*. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating that high-quality synthetic data, when generated scalably and diversely, can eliminate the need for real-world demonstrations. This positions MobileH2R as a pioneering framework that pushes the boundaries of sim-to-real transfer for complex HRI tasks. By proposing a comprehensive solution that scales both human assets and robot demonstrations, and by employing a novel 4D imitation learning approach for coordinated base-arm control, MobileH2R bridges the gap between simulated training and real-world H2MR deployment, offering a scalable and safe alternative to traditional data collection methods. It builds upon previous synthetic data generation paradigms but extends them significantly to full-body, interactive human models and mobile robot platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of MobileH2R is to develop a framework for learning generalizable, vision-based human-to-mobile-robot (H2MR) handover skills. The central motivation is the insight that such complex interaction skills can be effectively acquired in simulators, entirely circumventing the challenges associated with real-world data collection, provided that the synthetic data is of sufficiently high quality, diverse, and scalable.

Specific motivations and objectives include:
- **Overcoming Scalability Challenges:** Real-world H2R training is expensive, time-consuming, and carries significant safety risks. The paper aims to create a fully synthetic pipeline to generate large-scale, diverse, and realistic human motion and robot demonstration data.
- **Enabling Generalizability:** Traditional methods often struggle with generalizing to novel human motions or environments. MobileH2R seeks to achieve robustness by exposing the robot to a vast array of synthetic scenarios, including full-body human movements and dynamic robot-human interactions.
- **Addressing Mobile Robot Specifics:** Unlike fixed-base systems, mobile robots require coordinated base and arm movements. A key objective is to learn a closed-loop visuomotor policy that outputs both base and arm actions simultaneously, ensuring fluid and safe interaction.
- **Ensuring Safety in HRI:** The framework prioritizes safety by generating "imitation-friendly" demonstrations that proactively avoid collisions and uncomfortable human-robot configurations, rather than relying on reactive collision avoidance during policy execution.
- **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A core goal is to enable seamless transfer of learned policies from the simulation environment to real-world mobile robots, validating the efficacy of the synthetic data approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MobileH2R framework comprises three main technical components: MobileH2R-Sim for scalable synthetic data generation, an automated method for producing safe and imitation-friendly demonstrations, and a 4D imitation learning approach for policy training.

#### 3.1. MobileH2R-Sim: Scalable and Diverse Synthetic Human Motion Synthesis

MobileH2R-Sim is a novel simulation environment designed to generate diverse, natural, task-driven, and reactive full-body human motions for H2MR handover.
- **Two-Phase Motion Synthesis:**
 - **Pre-handover Phase:** Generates diverse, task-independent full-body movements. This leverages controllable generative models like Guided Motion Diffusion (GMD) trained on large-scale datasets (e.g., AMASS). To make these motions object-aware, a Large Language Model (LLM) like GPT-4 is used to generate natural language prompts for GMD, aligning human motion with the semantic attributes of the object being held (e.g., holding a phone to the ear).
 - **Handover Phase:** Generates specific arm-only movements for object transfer. This involves identifying a feasible handover region, randomly sampling a final handover position, and using a kinematic optimizer to solve for the arm's joint configuration, considering human joint constraints and movement habits. Smooth trajectories are then interpolated.
- **Interactive Design:** The human agent in the simulation reacts to the robot's proximity. Full-body motion transitions to arm-only handover motion when the robot approaches within a threshold (e.g., 1m) or after a set pre-handover time limit (6 seconds).
- **Asset Integration:** Utilizes large digital asset libraries like ShapeNet for diverse objects and Acronym, DexGraspNet for grasping poses, enriching the variety of interaction scenarios.

#### 3.2. Safe and Imitation-friendly Demonstration Generation

To train a robust closed-loop visuomotor policy, MobileH2R generates high-quality robot demonstrations using an optimization-based planner (CHOMP), emphasizing safety and "imitation-friendliness."
- **Safety Enhancements:**
 - **Future Obstacle Avoidance:** Unlike traditional planners that only consider immediate collisions, MobileH2R utilizes oracle information to foresee future human and robot positions over a time window. This allows the planner to proactively avoid potential collisions and generate smoother, more natural robot movements by calculating obstacle loss at future timestamps.
 - **Final Pose Constraints:** The robot's final position is constrained to be directly in front of the human, ensuring a visible, face-to-face handover and avoiding potentially unsafe or uncomfortable movements (e.g., reaching from behind).
- **Imitation-Friendliness:** To ensure the learned policy can effectively map vision to action, the demonstrations are optimized to ensure visual clarity and informativeness.
 - **Vision Neural Loss:** A key insight is that if object pose can be accurately recovered from vision, the vision-action correlation is strengthened. A pose prediction network (P) predicts object pose from vision, defining a "vision neural loss" (prediction error).
 - **Vision-State Recovery Estimator (E):** Since rendering is non-differentiable, a separate estimator (E) is trained to predict the vision neural loss based on the robot's state. This allows gradients from the estimated neural loss to propagate back to the trajectory optimization, guiding the planner to generate demonstrations where the object is clearly visible and its pose accurately inferable from the robot's cameras.

#### 3.3. Imitation Learning for Coordinated Base-Arm Actions

The large-scale, high-quality demonstrations are distilled into a visuomotor policy that outputs coordinated base and arm actions.
- **Scene-Fused 4D Input:** The policy takes segmented point clouds of the human, hand, and object from both head and wrist cameras. Point clouds are aligned to the robot's end-effector frame. To capture temporal dynamics, 4D flow information is incorporated by using the Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm to approximate previous timestamp point cloud coordinates, enriching current point features.
- **Modified PointNet++ Architecture:** To handle the significant scale differences between the human body and hand-object point clouds, the PointNet++ encoder uses distinct set abstraction layers with varied sampling radii for each component. These features are then concatenated into a unified global representation.
- **Coordinated Action Output:** Unlike previous works that focus solely on arm actions, a single Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) decodes the unified global feature directly into an egocentric 6D action for the robotic arm (3D translation, 3D rotation) and a 3D action for the mobile base (2D translation, 1D rotation). This simultaneous decoding ensures inherent coordination between the base and arm movements.
- **Loss Function:** The policy is supervised using L1 losses for base and arm actions, with an auxiliary task predicting the object's grasping pose (`L_pred`), further enhancing the robot's anticipation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MobileH2R's effectiveness is validated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Performance Against Baselines

- **End-to-End vs. Non-End-to-End:** MobileH2R, as an end-to-end framework, significantly outperforms "Grasp Selection + Trajectory Planning" (a non-end-to-end baseline). MobileH2R achieves a 26.3% higher success rate, reduces execution time by 5.06s, and increases average success (AS) by 28.6% on average across test sets ("m0", "n0", "s0"). This highlights the superior ability of end-to-end policies to capture global context and dynamic interactions, leading to fewer human collisions.
- **Impact of Human Information & Mobility:** Compared to GenH2R (a fixed-base approach), MobileH2R (which incorporates full-body human information and learns base-arm coordination) shows substantial improvements. GenH2R (reprod.), when retrained with collision-avoiding demonstrations for a mobile robot, improved success rates significantly over the original GenH2R. However, MobileH2R achieved further improvements of 17.0%, 20.5%, and 16.7% in success rate across the three test sets, demonstrating the critical importance of full-body human input and learning coordinated base-arm actions for mobile robots.

#### 4.2. Impact of Data Scaling

- **Demonstration Quantity:** Increasing the number of synthetic demonstrations from 1k to 10k significantly improves success rates (13.9% increase), and further to 100k yields additional gains (3.3% increase on average). This validates the paper's core hypothesis that large-scale synthetic data is crucial for generalizability.
- **Asset Diversity:** Training on the diverse "n0" dataset (complex human motions) yields better performance than training on the simpler "m0" dataset (straightforward human motions) or the unscalable real mocap "s0" dataset. The "s0" dataset leads to a 34.6% average drop in success rate and a significant increase in object drops, emphasizing that small-scale real-world data can be insufficient and that synthetic diversity is paramount.

#### 4.3. Efficacy of Demonstration Strategies

- **Safety Features:** Demonstrations generated with "future obstacle avoidance" reduce human contact rate from 16.1% to 10.9%, and those with "final pose constraints" further decrease it to 6.6%. This proves that a well-designed safety policy in demonstration generation dramatically minimizes critical human collisions.
- **Imitation-Friendliness:** Demonstrations generated without the "imitation-friendly loss" (vision neural loss) lead to an 11.6% drop in success rate, indicating that explicitly optimizing for clear vision-action correlation during demonstration generation is vital for policy learning. Conversely, naive heuristic designs (e.g., forcing camera orientation) were found to disrupt natural robot motion and degrade performance.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on Policy Design

- **4D Flow Information:** Removing temporal (flow) information results in a 12.1% decrease in success rate, confirming the importance of incorporating dynamic cues.
- **Human Information:** Excluding full-body human point clouds as input leads to a 12.5% decrease, underscoring the need for rich human context in HRI.
- **Coordinated Action Decoding:** Decoding arm and base actions using independent networks results in a dramatic 17.8% decrease in success rate, highlighting the necessity of a single, unified network for simultaneous, coordinated action generation.
- **Multi-Camera Input:** Using only a single camera (head or wrist) significantly degrades performance due to occlusions (13.5%-36.1% drop), validating the multi-camera setup.

#### 4.5. Sim-to-Real Transfer and Real-World Experiments

- **Successful Transfer:** The policies trained exclusively in simulation successfully transfer to a real mobile robot (Galbot G1).
- **User Study Validation:** In a user study, MobileH2R achieved an 80% success rate in simple real-world handover scenarios and 63.3% in complex scenarios, significantly outperforming GenH2R(reprod.) (40% and 30% respectively). This robust real-world performance confirms the effectiveness of the synthetic data pipeline and the learned policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Key Contributions and Implications

MobileH2R represents a significant advancement in human-robot interaction and robot learning, primarily by demonstrating the viability and superiority of an entirely synthetic data-driven approach for complex mobile robot tasks. Its key contributions are:
- **Scalability for HRI:** It provides a blueprint for generating large-scale, diverse, and high-quality synthetic human-robot interaction data, effectively bypassing the severe limitations and risks of real-world data collection for HRI. This opens doors for training robots on an unprecedented scale.
- **Generalizable H2MR Skills:** By focusing on full-body human motions, reactive human agents, and coordinated base-arm actions, MobileH2R enables the learning of highly generalizable handover skills for mobile robots, crucial for deployment in dynamic, unstructured environments.
- **Safety-Centric Demonstration Generation:** The emphasis on "future obstacle avoidance" and "imitation-friendliness" during demonstration generation is a novel and critical aspect, ensuring that learned policies are inherently safer and more robust, a paramount concern in human-robot co-existence.
- **Holistic Perception and Control:** The 4D imitation learning approach, integrating multi-camera point clouds, temporal flow, and coordinated base-arm output, demonstrates a powerful paradigm for learning complex, full-body visuomotor policies.
- **Validated Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful real-world deployment on Galbot G1 validates the entire framework, proving that sophisticated HRI capabilities can be effectively transferred from simulation, reducing development costs and time.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Directions

Despite its breakthroughs, MobileH2R acknowledges certain limitations, paving the way for future research:
- **Robot Versatility:** The current work focuses on a specific mobile manipulator configuration (omnidirectional base + 7-DOF arm). Future work could explore extending the framework to other mobile robot types, including bipedal humanoids, which might require different network architectures or control strategies. However, the core data generation and demonstration principles are broadly applicable.
- **Baseline Scope:** The comparison mainly focuses on non-end-to-end methods and fixed-base H2R learning. Further comparisons with more advanced RL-based mobile manipulation methods, if safety constraints can be effectively integrated, would be valuable.
- **Real-World Robustness:** While sim-to-real transfer is successful, real-world challenges like segmentation errors (e.g., from SAM2) or depth camera inaccuracies could be further addressed. Incorporating random perturbations during training or exploring hybrid training with limited real-world data could enhance robustness in deployment.
- **Control Type:** The model outputs delta positions, requiring a robust position controller. Future work could investigate learning velocity or force control, which might offer greater flexibility and compliance in real-world interaction.

Overall, MobileH2R makes a substantial contribution by providing a comprehensive, scalable, and safe framework for training mobile robots for human-to-robot handover, primarily through synthetic data. This work underscores the increasing potential of high-fidelity simulation to drive advancements in complex, safety-critical human-robot interaction tasks.
