# TACO: Temporal Consensus Optimization for Continual Neural Mapping

> [!summary] Summary
> TACO (Temporal Consensus Optimization) presents a replay-free framework for continual neural mapping that balances adaptation to dynamic scene changes with the preservation of valid historical knowledge. This method successfully avoids persistent ghost artifacts and fragmented geometry, outperforming replay-based and regularization-based baselines in dynamic environments while reducing CPU memory usage by over 50% compared to full replay.

## Problem

- Existing neural implicit mapping systems struggle with continual adaptation in dynamic environments, often producing "ghost" artifacts or fragmented geometry when scenes change.
- Current methods either assume static environments, leading to ghost artifacts (replay-based), or over-constrain models, hindering adaptation to genuine scene changes (regularization/distillation-based).
- Memory and computational constraints on robotic platforms limit the long-term deployment of memory-intensive replay-based approaches that store and re-process historical data.

## Method

- **TACO** reformulates continual neural mapping as a ==temporal consensus optimization== problem, enforcing agreement between current model parameters and frozen historical snapshots without replaying past data.
- It defines a temporal consensus target as an ==importance-weighted average== of current and historical parameters, allowing selective adaptation by giving higher influence to more important parameters.
- The constrained optimization is solved using the ==method of multipliers==, iteratively updating primal parameters and dual variables, with parameter importance estimated based on the sensitivity of model predictions.

## Results

- TACO accurately adapted to dynamic environments, capturing object movements without generating persistent "ghost" artifacts or geometric fragmentation, unlike replay-based and regularization-based methods.
- The framework achieved reconstruction quality comparable to replay-based methods in static scenes and quantitatively outperformed other continual learning baselines in dynamic scenarios.
- TACO demonstrated significant memory efficiency, reducing CPU memory usage by **over 50%** compared to full replay-based approaches, making it suitable for resource-constrained robotic platforms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating continual learning as a temporal consensus problem offers a principled way to balance stability and plasticity in dynamic mapping without the memory overhead of data replay.
> - Parameter-wise importance estimation, derived from prediction sensitivity, enables selective preservation of reliable historical knowledge while allowing flexible adaptation in changing or uncertain regions.
> - A masking mechanism, which suppresses temporal constraints for parameters below a certain importance threshold, improves robustness by filtering out unreliable early-stage gradients and allowing unconstrained adaptation in less reliable areas.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of neural implicit mapping, a paradigm for dense scene representation and understanding in robotics. Neural implicit representations encode scene geometry and appearance as continuous functions, offering compact, expressive, and high-fidelity models compared to traditional discrete map representations. Recent advancements have enabled real-time online dense mapping using streaming RGB-D observations.

However, a significant challenge for real-world robotic deployment is continual adaptation to dynamic environments under stringent memory and computation constraints. Existing neural mapping systems typically fall into several categories, each with limitations in dynamic settings:
- **Replay-based methods:** These approaches (e.g., Co-SLAM, KR, CNM) store and replay historical observations or keyframes to stabilize training and mitigate catastrophic forgetting. While effective in static scenes, they assume environmental stationarity. In dynamic environments, replaying outdated observations can lead to persistent "ghost" artifacts, where both old and new scene configurations are reconstructed simultaneously. They also incur substantial memory overhead.
- **Regularization-based methods:** These methods (e.g., EWC, MAS) draw inspiration from continual learning literature to prevent forgetting by penalizing changes to parameters deemed important for past knowledge. However, they often over-constrain the model, making it resistant to adaptation when the environment actually changes, resulting in distorted or fragmented geometry.
- **Distillation-based methods:** Approaches like UNIKD leverage knowledge distillation to transfer information across incremental training stages. Similar to regularization methods, their primary focus on preserving past states can hinder adaptation to dynamic scene evolution.

The authors identify a gap where existing methods fail to balance the need to retain valid historical knowledge in unchanged regions with the necessity to adapt to newly observed scene changes. They specifically highlight that the objective for continual neural mapping extends beyond preventing catastrophic forgetting to actively updating historical geometry in response to new observations, without requiring access to past data.

To address this, the paper draws inspiration from **consensus optimization**, a paradigm typically used in multi-robot systems to reconcile conflicting information across distributed agents. By shifting this concept from the spatial domain to the temporal domain, the authors propose to coordinate multiple model states over time. This approach also incorporates concepts from **parameter importance estimation**, where methods like MAS use output sensitivity to identify critical parameters. TACO aims to integrate these ideas to achieve a flexible continual mapping system that allows selective adaptation while remaining replay-free and memory-efficient.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a replay-free framework for continual neural mapping that can robustly adapt to changing environments while operating under strict memory and computational resource constraints typical of real-world robotic deployments.

The specific key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Enabling Continual Adaptation in Dynamic Environments:**
 - **Motivation:** Real-world robotic applications necessitate mapping systems that can operate over extended periods in environments where scene geometry may change due to object movement, additions, or removals. Current neural implicit mapping systems primarily assume static scenes, which limits their practical applicability in dynamic scenarios.
 - **Objective:** To create a mapping system that can continuously update its representation to reflect new scene configurations without accumulating outdated information.

2. **Balancing Stability and Adaptability:**
 - **Motivation:** A core challenge in continual learning is the "stability-plasticity dilemma" – the need to preserve previously learned knowledge (stability) while integrating new information (adaptability). Existing methods often prioritize one over the other. Replay-based methods attempt to enforce stability by re-optimizing on past data, which leads to "ghost" artifacts when scenes change. Regularization-based methods, in contrast, overly constrain updates to prevent forgetting, making them rigid and unable to adapt to genuine scene modifications.
 - **Objective:** To devise a mechanism that can selectively revise outdated or unreliable regions of the map while preserving accurate and consistent geometry in unchanged parts of the environment.

3. **Achieving Memory and Computation Efficiency:**
 - **Motivation:** Robotic platforms often operate with limited onboard memory and processing power. Replay-based methods, which store and re-process large volumes of historical RGB-D frames or keyframes, are memory-intensive and computationally demanding, hindering long-term deployment.
 - **Objective:** To develop a framework that does not rely on storing or replaying previous data, thereby reducing memory footprint and computational overhead, making it suitable for long-term online mapping under realistic resource constraints.

4. **Producing Accurate and Up-to-Date Reconstructions:**
 - **Motivation:** For downstream robotic tasks such as navigation, manipulation, or human-robot interaction, an accurate and current environmental map is crucial. Maps containing ghost artifacts or fragmented geometry can negatively impact the performance and safety of these tasks.
 - **Objective:** To consistently generate high-quality, up-to-date 3D reconstructions that accurately reflect the current state of the environment, free from persistent artifacts or structural degradation.

By addressing these objectives, TACO aims to overcome the limitations of current continual neural mapping approaches, providing a more robust and practical solution for robots operating in complex, evolving real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, TACO (TemporAl Consensus Optimization), reformulates continual neural mapping as a temporal consensus optimization problem. The approach is replay-free and aims to balance the preservation of reliable past knowledge with adaptation to new observations, especially in dynamic scenes.

#### 3.1. Neural Implicit Mapping Backbone (Co-SLAM)

TACO builds upon Co-SLAM, a real-time neural implicit mapping framework. Co-SLAM represents a 3D scene using a neural implicit map comprising:
- A **multi-resolution hash-based feature grid** ($\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}$) parameterized by $\alpha$. This grid encodes scene features at various scales.
- A **geometry decoder** ($\mathbf{F}_{\tau}$) parameterized by $\tau$.
- A **color decoder** ($\mathbf{F}_{\phi}$) parameterized by $\phi$.
The collective learnable parameters are denoted as $\mathbf{\Theta} = \{\alpha, \tau, \phi\}$. Given a 3D world coordinate $\mathbf{x}$, the map predicts its truncated Signed Distance Function (SDF) value ($s$) and RGB color ($c$) by querying the feature grid and passing through the decoders.

Scene rendering is performed via volume rendering along camera rays to produce predicted RGB colors ($\hat{c}$) and depths ($\hat{d}$). The weights ($w_i$) for volume rendering are computed using a sigmoid function applied to the SDF values, which assigns higher weights to samples near the estimated surface and smoothly truncates contributions from points far from the surface.

The neural implicit map is optimized by minimizing a comprehensive loss function, $\mathcal{L}_{obj}(\mathbf{\Theta}, \mathbf{R})$, which includes photometric ($L_{rgb}$), depth ($L_d$), SDF supervision ($L_{sdf}$), free-space ($L_{fs}$), and geometric smoothness ($L_{smooth}$) terms.

#### 3.2. Temporal Consensus Optimization

The core of TACO involves enforcing agreement between the current model parameters ($\mathbf{\Theta}_t$) and its historical snapshots ($\mathbf{\Theta}_{t-k}$), which are frozen past model states. This is formulated as a constrained optimization problem:
$$ \min_{\mathbf{\Theta}_t} \mathcal{L}_{obj}(\mathbf{\Theta}_t, \mathbf{R}_t), \quad \text{s.t. } \mathbf{\Theta}_t = \mathbf{z}_{t,t-1}(\mathbf{\Theta}_t) $$
where $\mathbf{R}_t$ are the current observations, and $\mathbf{z}_{t,t-1}(\mathbf{\Theta}_t)$ is the temporal consensus target.

For simplicity, the paper primarily considers consensus between the current model ($\mathbf{\Theta}_t$) and its immediate predecessor ($\mathbf{\Theta}_{t-1}$). The consensus target is defined as an importance-weighted average:
$$ \mathbf{z}_{t,t-1}(\mathbf{\Theta}_t) = (\mathbf{W}_t(\mathbf{\Theta}_t) + \mathbf{W}_{t-1}(\mathbf{\Theta}_t))^{-1} (\mathbf{W}_t(\mathbf{\Theta}_t)\mathbf{\Theta}_t + \mathbf{W}_{t-1}(\mathbf{\Theta}_t)\mathbf{\Theta}_{t-1}) $$
Here, $\mathbf{W}_t$ and $\mathbf{W}_{t-1}$ are square diagonal weight matrices encoding parameter-wise importance for $\mathbf{\Theta}_t$ and $\mathbf{\Theta}_{t-1}$, respectively. This formulation ensures that parameters with higher importance (weights) exert a stronger influence on the consensus target, thereby preserving reliable historical information while allowing less important parameters to adapt.

#### 3.3. Constrained Optimization with Method of Multipliers

The constrained optimization problem is solved using the method of multipliers. An augmented Lagrangian ($\mathcal{L}_a$) is constructed:
$$ \mathcal{L}_a = \mathcal{L}_{obj}(\mathbf{\Theta}_t, \mathbf{R}_t) + (\mathbf{\Theta}_t - \mathbf{z}_{t,t-1}(\mathbf{\Theta}_t))^{\top} \mathbf{p} + \frac{\rho}{2} \|\mathbf{\Theta}_t - \mathbf{z}_{t,t-1}(\mathbf{\Theta}_t)\|_2^2 $$
where $\mathbf{p}$ is the vector of dual variables and $\rho > 0$ is a penalty parameter controlling the strength of the temporal consensus constraint.

The optimization proceeds iteratively through primal and dual updates:
- **Primal Update:** The current model parameters ($\mathbf{\Theta}_t$) are updated by minimizing the augmented Lagrangian using stochastic gradient descent steps.
- **Dual Update:** The dual variable $\mathbf{p}$ is updated based on the violation of the constraint: $\mathbf{p}^{(k)} = \mathbf{p}^{(k-1)} + \rho(\mathbf{\Theta}_t^{(k)} - \mathbf{z}_{t,t-1}^{(k-1)})$. This accumulated disagreement signal guides subsequent updates to enforce consensus.
The weight matrices $\mathbf{W}_t$ and $\mathbf{W}_{t-1}$ are re-computed at each iteration using the current estimate of $\mathbf{\Theta}_t$.

#### 3.4. Importance Estimation for Temporal Consensus

To determine the importance weights, TACO estimates a parameter-wise importance based on the sensitivity of model predictions to individual parameters.
- **Proxy Objective:** A task-agnostic proxy objective ($\mathcal{L}_{proxy}^s$) is defined on the rendered outputs (color and depth) for a sampled set of rays at iteration $s$. This objective measures the overall magnitude of model predictions.
- **Importance Vector:** The importance of each parameter, $\mathbf{u}_t^{(k)}$, is estimated by accumulating the element-wise absolute value of the gradients of $\mathcal{L}_{proxy}^s$ with respect to the model parameters across past iterations. Parameters with larger $\mathbf{u}_t^{(k)}$ are considered more influential on rendered outputs and thus more important.
- **Weight Matrix Construction:**
 - The importance vectors for the current ($\mathbf{u}_t^{(k)}$) and historical ($\mathbf{u}_{t-1}$) models are combined to compute a joint importance $\mathbf{u}_{sum}^{(k)}$.
 - A global scaling factor $\epsilon^{(k)} = \rho / \text{avg}(\mathbf{u}_{sum}^{(k)})$ is applied to ensure numerical stability and align the average magnitude of weights with the penalty parameter $\rho$, while preserving relative importance.
 - The diagonal weight matrices are then constructed as $\mathbf{W}_t^{(k)} = \text{diag}(\epsilon^{(k)} \cdot \mathbf{u}_t^{(k)})$ and $\mathbf{W}_{t-1}^{(k)} = \text{diag}(\epsilon^{(k)} \cdot \mathbf{u}_{t-1})$.
- **Masking Mechanism:** To enhance robustness, especially in early training stages or poorly observed regions, a threshold $\beta$ is applied to the historical weight matrix $\mathbf{W}_{t-1}$. Parameters with importance values below $\beta$ have their temporal constraints suppressed, allowing unconstrained adaptation in less reliable regions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TACO was conducted across simulated and real-world scenarios, encompassing both static and dynamic scene conditions. The method was compared against various continual learning baselines, including replay-based (Co-SLAM, KR, CNM), regularization-based (EWC, MAS), and distillation-based (UNIKD) approaches. Co-SLAM with and without replay served as upper and lower bounds, respectively.

#### 4.1. Results on Static Simulated Scenes

- **Performance:** On standard RGB-D benchmarks (Replica, ScanNet), TACO achieved performance comparable to Co-SLAM with replay, which optimized over both past and current observations. It matched or exceeded the performance of other continual learning baselines, including the strong MAS method, across various 3D geometric metrics (Artifacts, Holes, Chamfer Distance, Completion Ratio, Precision@5cm, F1@5cm).
- **Mitigation of Forgetting:** These results indicate that TACO effectively preserves global geometry over time, demonstrating its capability to mitigate catastrophic forgetting without the need to revisit past observations.
- **Scalability:** In larger and more complex Gibson scenes, TACO produced complete and smoother geometric reconstructions compared to MAS, with fewer discontinuities and fragmentation, suggesting effective scalability to extensive environments.

#### 4.2. Results on Dynamic Simulated Scenes

- **Adaptation to Changes:** In dynamic simulated environments (Habitat Synthetic Scenes Dataset) with staged object motion (e.g., relocating a sofa, tree, table), TACO accurately captured these modifications. It revised outdated geometry and produced updated maps reflecting the current scene without introducing artifacts in stable regions. This highlighted TACO's ability for selective adaptation.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **MAS (Regularization-based):** Failed to adapt properly to conflicting observations, leading to "mesh tearing" and fragmented surfaces near changed object locations due to its over-constraining nature.
 - **Co-SLAM (Replay-based):** Generated persistent "ghost" artifacts, reconstructing objects at both their old and new positions due to its reliance on replaying past, now inconsistent, observations.
- **Quantitative Advantage:** Quantitatively, TACO outperformed MAS in dynamic scenes, exhibiting fewer reconstruction artifacts, a higher completion ratio, and a higher F1@5cm score.

#### 4.3. Continual Mapping in Dynamic Real-World Environments

- **Single Object Movement:** In a real-world scenario where a yellow stool was moved, TACO successfully reconstructed the stool only at its current location, without generating ghost artifacts or geometric breakage. In contrast, replay-based methods produced ghost artifacts, and regularization-based methods resulted in distorted geometry.
- **Multiple Sequential Scene Changes:** Across multiple stages of complex object reshuffling (cubes and stool), TACO demonstrated consistent adaptation. It revised outdated geometry while preserving stable regions, resulting in coherent reconstructions throughout the sequence. Co-SLAM accumulated severe ghost artifacts with progressive changes, and MAS failed to adapt under repeated dynamics, leading to torn and fragmented geometry.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Memory Usage

- **Memory Efficiency:** TACO exhibited a CPU memory footprint (1864.5 MB Resident Set Size) comparable to other replay-free baselines like KR, UNIKD, and MAS (ranging from 1800.6 MB to 1945.1 MB).
- **Reduction vs. Full Replay:** This represented a reduction of over 50% in CPU memory usage compared to Co-SLAM with full replay (4165.1 MB), underscoring its memory efficiency for real-world robotic deployment.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Mask Mechanism:** Introducing a masking mechanism with a moderate threshold ($\beta=10^{-4}$) consistently improved reconstruction quality (lower Chamfer Distance, higher F1@5cm) compared to not using a mask or using an overly strong mask. This suggests the mask stabilizes importance-aware temporal consensus by filtering out unreliable early-stage gradients.
- **Number of Temporal Snapshots (K):** Using a single historical snapshot (K=1) for temporal consensus yielded the best overall performance. As K increased, reconstruction quality degraded, indicating that including too many older snapshots introduced conflicting constraints that hindered adaptation to recent changes. This suggests prioritizing recency in temporal consensus.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of TACO represents an advancement in continual neural mapping, particularly for robotic applications in dynamic environments. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Enabling Robust Robotic Autonomy in Dynamic Worlds:** By providing a mechanism for neural maps to continually adapt to scene changes without accumulating artifacts or overly constraining updates, TACO addresses a critical limitation for robots operating in real-world, non-static environments. This capability is fundamental for tasks such as long-term navigation, object manipulation, and persistent environmental monitoring where the scene is subject to evolution.

2. **Addressing the Stability-Plasticity Dilemma for Mapping:** The temporal consensus optimization framework offers a principled approach to balance the preservation of existing knowledge (stability) with the integration of new information (plasticity). This is achieved through importance-aware weighting, allowing selective revision of outdated geometry while maintaining consistency in stable regions, a significant improvement over traditional replay-based and regularization-based continual learning methods in dynamic mapping contexts.

3. **Memory and Computation Efficiency for Resource-Constrained Systems:** The replay-free nature of TACO substantially reduces the memory footprint and computational overhead associated with storing and processing historical data. This efficiency makes it more suitable for deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms, extending the viability of real-time, online neural mapping for practical applications.

4. **Improved Map Quality in Dynamic Settings:** TACO's ability to avoid "ghost" artifacts (common in replay-based methods) and mitigate geometric fragmentation (seen in regularization-based methods) leads to more accurate, coherent, and up-to-date scene representations. High-fidelity and current maps are crucial for reliable perception, planning, and control in autonomous systems.

5. **Novel Application of Consensus Optimization:** The reformulation of continual learning as a temporal consensus problem extends the applicability of consensus optimization beyond its traditional use in spatially distributed multi-agent systems. This demonstrates a new conceptual pathway for handling sequential data and model evolution in machine learning.

6. **Guidance for Future Continual Learning Research:** The findings from the ablation studies, particularly regarding the mask mechanism and the optimal number of temporal snapshots, provide insights into the dynamics of importance-aware consensus. This could guide future research in designing robust and efficient continual learning algorithms, emphasizing the importance of recency and targeted constraint application.

In summary, TACO provides a robust, memory-efficient, and adaptable solution for continual neural mapping in dynamic environments, which could significantly impact the capabilities and deployment potential of autonomous robotic systems.
