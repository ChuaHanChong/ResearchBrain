# Track-Leakage-Free Hold-Out Self-Validation for Photogrammetric Reconstruction: Protocol, Sensitivity, and Limits

> [!summary] Summary
> Behnam Asadi empirically characterized a ground-truth-free self-validation protocol for photogrammetric reconstruction, demonstrating that while it can detect model fragmentation, it is systematically blind to coherent global distortions. This work highlights that high internal consistency does not equate to absolute accuracy in 3D reconstructions.

## Problem

- Automated photogrammetric inspections often produce metric measurements from 3D reconstructions whose absolute reliability is unknown.
- Traditional external validation using ground-control points (GCPs) is frequently costly, time-consuming, or physically unfeasible.
- Existing internal consistency checks primarily assess precision or local agreement but fail to provide guarantees about global correctness or detect gross errors.

## Method

- A **"track-leakage-free hold-out protocol"** was developed, withholding a ==deterministic subset== of images from reconstruction and independently ==re-localizing== them against ==trusted 3D points== from the remaining model.
- Disagreement between re-localized and original camera poses is quantified by ==geodesic rotation error== and a ==scale-free translation error== (angle between translation directions).
- A confidence metric, ==Mean Average Accuracy (mAA)==, is computed by averaging the fraction of held-out views whose combined error falls below set ==angular thresholds==.

## Results

- High-quality reconstructions consistently scored a perfect **1.00** confidence, with median errors in the sub-hundredth-degree range, indicating internal consistency.
- In scenarios with injected match corruption, models with severe absolute errors (**55-122 m**) frequently maintained a perfect **1.00** confidence score, demonstrating the protocol's blindness to coherent global distortions.
- The protocol successfully detected fragmentation, where a **20x** increase in RTK RMSE caused a confidence drop from **1.00** to **0.96**, but it failed to provide a reliable continuous signal for absolute accuracy due to unstable correlation across datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internal geometric consistency, as measured by the protocol, is not equivalent to absolute accuracy due to the intrinsic gauge freedom of bundle adjustment, which allows for globally distorted but internally consistent models.
> - The self-validation mechanism is systematically blind to failures manifesting as coherent global distortions (e.g., scale errors, gentle warps) because these errors preserve local geometry.
> - While the protocol can qualitatively detect model fragmentation, its confidence signal saturates for healthy models and fails to reliably track absolute accuracy or detect self-consistent global errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Automated photogrammetric inspection, utilizing Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS), is a prevalent method for assessing infrastructure and industrial sites. These systems generate metric measurements from 3D reconstructions, but the reliability of these measurements often remains unknown without external validation, typically achieved through expensive and sometimes unfeasible ground-control points (GCPs).

The broader research landscape for evaluating 3D reconstruction quality encompasses several areas:
- **SLAM Keyframe and Consistency Heuristics:** Online methods that monitor tracking consistency for real-time applications but do not provide post-hoc accuracy statements.
- **Reconstruction Quality Assessment Benchmarks:** Metrics like those in ETH3D, Tanks and Temples, DTU, and aerial photogrammetric benchmarks compare reconstructions against laser scans or surveyed control points. These methods inherently rely on external ground truth.
- **Uncertainty in SfM/MVS:** Bundle adjustment covariance and learned uncertainty estimations primarily assess precision (repeatability under noise) rather than absolute correctness under gross model errors. Self-supervised photo-consistency also rewards internal agreement, which the current paper demonstrates can be misleading for globally inaccurate models.
- **Visual Re-localisation and Learned Pose/Uncertainty:** This field focuses on estimating the pose of a query image against a trusted 3D map. While the current work uses a PnP resection mechanism similar to re-localisation, it inverts the problem to interrogate the map's reliability itself. Learned uncertainty prediction, a modern competitor, is acknowledged but not directly benchmarked, with the theoretical caveat that it would likely inherit the same "gauge blindness."
- **Classical Reliability Theory and Gauge Freedom:** Photogrammetry distinguishes internal reliability (detecting blunders from network residuals) from external reliability (effect of undetectable errors on estimated quantities). Bundle adjustment possesses intrinsic gauge/datum freedom, meaning global position, orientation, and scale are unobservable without external references. This theoretical concept underpins the paper's negative findings regarding coherent distortions.

This research distinguishes itself by focusing on a "ground-truth-free" approach to self-validation, explicitly asking what such an internal self-estimate can *actually* measure, particularly regarding absolute accuracy and gross failure detection, in the absence of external references. It refines existing concepts of leave-out re-localisation by introducing a critical "track-level leakage barrier" to ensure the self-assessment is not trivially self-consistent. The contribution lies in an empirical characterization of the *limits* of self-validation, rather than algorithmic novelty, providing a clear delineation of what internal consistency signals can and cannot support.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the practical challenge in photogrammetric inspection: automated systems produce metric measurements from 3D reconstructions, but the inherent reliability of these reconstructions is often unknown without costly and sometimes impossible external surveys. This leads to a fundamental "ground-truth-free" question: can a 3D reconstruction estimate its own reliability solely from its internal data?

The paper identifies two key questions that are relevant to practitioners:
1. **Q1: Does the self-estimate track absolute accuracy?** This question probes whether an internal self-assessment can serve as a substitute for traditional, external ground-control point (GCP) checks.
2. **Q2: Does it at least detect gross failure?** This investigates whether the self-assessment can identify catastrophic errors in a reconstruction, thereby gating untrustworthy outputs even if it cannot provide a precise accuracy statement.

The overarching objective is to provide a precise protocol for "leakage-free hold-out" self-validation and, crucially, an honest empirical characterization of its capabilities and limitations using operational data. The author aims to delineate what signals of internal consistency can realistically support, explicitly addressing the "blind spot" inherent in such internal checks due to bundle adjustment's gauge freedom. The work is positioned as an empirical characterization of limits rather than an algorithmic breakthrough.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a "track-leakage-free hold-out protocol" designed to assess the internal consistency of a photogrammetric reconstruction without external ground truth.

**A. Track-Leakage-Free Hold-Out Protocol:**
The protocol involves several steps:
1. **Partition:** A deterministic subset of images (H, default 10% of total) is withheld from the reconstruction. This deterministic selection ensures reproducibility.
2. **Trusted Points:** Only 3D points (P⋆) observed by at least two *retained* images (τ=2) are considered "trusted." This "track-level barrier" is critical to prevent a held-out view from being tested against 3D points it itself helped triangulate, which would lead to trivial self-consistency.
3. **Independent Re-localisation:** For each held-out image in H, its 2D-3D correspondences are restricted to P⋆. A new pose (ˆR, ˆt) is then estimated using Perspective-n-Point (PnP) resection within a RANSAC loop.
4. **Disagreement:** The re-localised pose is compared to the reconstruction's original pose (R, t). Disagreement is quantified by a geodesic rotation error (eR) and a scale-free translation error (et), which is the angle between translation directions. The combined error for a view is e = max(eR, et). The translation error is designed to be scale-free to avoid corruption by an unknown global scale, which is common in monocular reconstructions.
5. **Aggregate:** The confidence metric, Mean Average Accuracy (mAA), is computed by averaging the fraction of held-out views whose combined error e falls below a set of angular thresholds Θ (default {1°, 3°, 5°}). A view passes a threshold if it agrees in both rotation and translation direction within that angular limit.
6. **Gate (Candidate Use-Case):** The protocol defines a potential use-case where metric measurements could be tagged with this confidence, and reconstructions below a certain threshold (γ) flagged as out-of-tolerance. However, the study's results indicate this gate is not yet operational.

**B. What the Metric Measures (and Does Not):**
The protocol measures internal geometric consistency—how well a withheld view's pose is predictable from the rest of the model's geometry. It explicitly states this is *not* equivalent to absolute accuracy due to:
- **Gauge/Scale-Unobservability:** Bundle adjustment has intrinsic gauge freedom (absolute position, orientation, scale are unobservable without external references). A coherent global similarity distortion leaves internal residuals unchanged, making it invisible to purely internal checks. The method is blind to locally near-rigid transformations that can result in significant global distortions.
- **Scale-Blindness of Translation Term:** By design, the translation error is direction-only, making pure scale errors invisible. An ablation study (Section V-D) confirms this is due to unobservable gauge, not just the direction-only projection.
- **Track-level, Not BA-level Leakage:** While track-level leakage is prevented, the held-out views' observations still contributed to the initial joint bundle adjustment that built the trusted structure. A stricter, BA-independent variant (Section V-B) is explored to quantify this residual coupling.

**C. Experimental Setup:**
- **Datasets:** Six operational aerial-survey captures on five physical sites (Helenenschacht, Tuniu River 0916, Tuniu River 0411, Bellus, Sellin, GUT Campus), all with per-image GNSS references (five RTK-fixed, one metre-grade). Public benchmarks (ETH3D, EuRoC MAV, KITTI, IMC 2025) are used for independent confirmation and generality.
- **Metrics:** Confidence (ground-truth-free mAA) and Absolute Error (RMSE between reconstructed camera centers and RTK GPS positions after a seven-parameter similarity (Umeyama) alignment). The Umeyama alignment ensures that reported error is residual shape distortion, not global scale/rotation/translation errors.
- **Degradation Harness:** To systematically vary reconstruction quality, the COLMAP feature-match database is used to generate degraded models. Two modes are applied:
 - **D1: Sparsity:** Retaining every k-th image.
 - **D2: Match Corruption:** Shuffling correspondences of a fraction of verified two-view geometries to inject geometrically wrong but trusted matches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several findings regarding the track-leakage-free hold-out self-validation protocol:

**A. Computational Well-Posedness on Good Reconstructions:**
For high-quality, delivered reconstructions, the protocol consistently scores a confidence of 1.00. Median rotation and translation errors are in the sub-hundredth-degree range (e.g., Helenenschacht: median eR 0.0025°, et 0.0052°), with ample support (around 1,100 trusted correspondences per held-out view). This indicates the metric genuinely reflects internal consistency in well-formed models. Specificity under benign variation is also high, with confidence remaining high across different hold-out seeds, though some benign sub-threshold readings exist, complicating threshold definition for a gate.

**B. Track-Leakage-Free vs. BA-Independent:**
An ablation comparing the primary protocol (v1, track-leakage-free but BA-coupled) with a strictly BA-independent variant (v2, re-mapping on train-only data) shows:
- At standard 3°/5° thresholds, the self-consistency saturation holds even for the BA-independent v2 (mAA ≥ 0.975), confirming the negative result is not merely a leakage artifact.
- V1's millidegree residuals are flattered by the shared gauge. V2's median rotation errors are 37–82x larger (0.14°–0.68°). However, control experiments show that most of this difference is due to re-mapping noise and Sim(3) alignment residual, with the *genuine* leakage contribution being modest (1.7–7.3x above the re-mapping noise floor). At a tighter 1° threshold, v2 reveals more sensitivity (mAA 0.67–0.80 where v1 reads 1.00), exposing real center drift. This justifies the "track-leakage-free" terminology rather than "leakage-free."

**C. Hold-Out Fraction (ρ) Invariance:**
Sweeping the hold-out fraction ρ from 0.05 to 0.50 demonstrates that confidence is largely ρ-invariant, remaining saturated between 0.93 and 1.00. Larger ρ values (e.g., 0.50) can thin the retained structure, leading to slightly reduced localization rates and increased median errors, but the fundamental saturation and blindness to global distortion remain.

**D. Barrier Strength and Metric Translation:**
- **Barrier Strength (τ):** Increasing the barrier strength from τ=2 to τ=5 (requiring more retained views per 3D anchor) increases median rotation error by 5–15x but still keeps it below 0.12°. Confidence remains saturated (≥ 0.89), though localization rates can drop due to insufficient trusted points. This confirms that the saturated readings are a property of the reconstructions, not the loosest barrier.
- **Metric (Scale-Aware) Translation:** A variant using full metric camera center offsets (∥Cstored - Creloc∥) instead of direction-only error also remains saturated. The metric offsets are minuscule (median ≤ 0.04 reconstruction units). This indicates the blindness to coherent distortion is due to the unobservable gauge, where global similarities move both stored centers and anchor points together, rather than being an artifact of discarding the scale axis of translation.

**E. Thresholded Self-Consistency (Coarse mAA) Saturates; No Detectable Tracking of Absolute Accuracy (Sparsity, D1):**
Across 35 degraded reconstructions from four captures (sparsity degradation), confidence remained at 1.00 in 33 cases, while the true RTK RMSE varied significantly (up to 14.1x, e.g., Tuniu 0916 from 1.48 m to 20.87 m). The pooled naive correlation between coarse mAA and absolute error was Pearson r = -0.08. A fifth independent RTK capture (GUT Campus) replicated this saturation, with confidence remaining at 1.00 while RTK RMSE varied 2.4x.

- **Statistical Power:** The study acknowledges that with n=35, the sample size is underpowered to detect small true correlations. The conclusion is that the confidence signal saturates and shows no *meaningful positive* coupling to absolute accuracy at this sample size, rather than proving a zero correlation.
- **Underlying Mechanisms:** Saturation is attributed to mAA thresholds being much coarser than observed errors (views re-localize almost perfectly) and sparsity creating smaller, internally consistent models rather than intrinsically wrong ones.

**F. Detection of Fragmentation, but Blindness to Self-Consistent Global Distortion (Match Corruption, D2):**
Injected match corruption exposed two failure modes:
- **Fragmentation (Caught):** Helenenschacht fragmented into two components, causing RTK RMSE to jump 20x (to 11.5 m) and confidence to drop (1.00 → 0.96). This indicates the metric *can* flag failures that destroy internal consistency.
- **Coherent Global Distortion (Missed):** For Tuniu 0916, Tuniu 0411, and Bellus, severe corruption led to a *single, internally self-consistent but globally distorted model*. RTK RMSE exploded to 55–106 m, yet confidence remained at 1.00. For example, Tuniu 0916 was 63.6 m wrong with a perfect confidence score of 1.00. This is presented as "sharpest possible evidence that internal consistency ≠ absolute accuracy." Denser corruption sweeps confirmed this pattern, with one model being 122m wrong at confidence 1.00.
- **Cross-Domain Validation:** On public KITTI odometry sequences, natural monocular scale drift (19.2 m absolute error) also resulted in perfect self-consistency (confidence 1.00), confirming this blindness arises unprompted and beyond drone-nadir captures.

**G. De-Saturation: Continuous/Sub-Degree Signal Recovers Partial—But Unreliable—Sensitivity:**
Using continuous median per-view rotation error instead of coarse mAA:
- The naive pooled correlation with RTK accuracy rose to r = +0.38. However, a random-effects meta-analysis across five captures showed a pooled r = +0.23 (95% CI [-0.45, +0.75]), still spanning zero, with high heterogeneity (I²=69%).
- Crucially, per-capture coupling ranged from -0.57 (Bellus, inverse correlation) to +0.98 (GUT Campus, strong positive). This *sign instability* across sites renders the continuous signal unreliable as a general accuracy predictor, even if it locally appears stronger.
- While sub-degree mAA thresholds did drop on failures the coarse mAA missed (e.g., from ~0.85 to 0.33-0.64 for 55-106m errors), they still did not cleanly separate accurate from catastrophic models due to overlapping ranges. This indicates that internal consistency, at any threshold, remains a poor estimator of absolute accuracy.

**H. Competing Internal Proxies Fail the Same Way:**
Testing other internal consistency proxies (reprojection RMSE, BA covariance, track length, registered images) on failure committees (coherent distortion, fragmentation, repeated structure) confirmed the predicted gauge-blindness:
- **Coherent Distortion:** Reprojection RMSE showed inverse correlation (AUROC 0.15), meaning it preferred some catastrophically wrong models. BA covariance was at or below chance (AUROC 0.38). Hold-out confidence was weakly better (AUROC 0.66).
- **Fragmentation:** Hold-out confidence was the most effective consistency signal (AUROC 0.91).
- **Track-Count Statistics:** These appeared informative (e.g., mean track length AUROC 0.89-1.00) but were attributed to a "harness confound" where the degradation knob directly reduces tracks *while* creating error.

**I. Scene-Level Evaluation on a Community Benchmark (IMC 2025):**
Evaluating 30 independent scenes from IMC 2025 using a vanilla COLMAP baseline:
- **Failure Separation:** For scenes that failed to reconstruct outright (7/30), treating no-model as zero confidence resulted in a Pearson r = 0.79 / Spearman ρ = 0.68 against true GT mAA. This confirms confidence as an effective failure separator driven by separation of failed vs. successful reconstructions.
- **Quality Ranking:** Among the 22 successfully reconstructed scenes, confidence saturated at 0.95–1.00 for every scene (ρ = 0.01), providing no rank signal, while true GT mAA varied widely (0.11–0.75). The continuous median rotation error also provided no meaningful rank signal.

**J. Natural Pipeline Variation: Confound-Free Ranking Test:**
Reconstructing six IMC datasets under five natural pipeline variants (feature budget, pyramid depth, matching strictness, pairing method) showed:
- Failure separation was perfect (no evaluable hold-out meant zero GT mAA).
- Thresholded confidence again saturated, ranking nothing (pairwise ranking accuracy 0.511 - chance).
- The continuous median eR was modestly better (pairwise 0.660) but unstable and subject to an accuracy-vs-coverage confound.
- Registered-image count performed best for ranking (pairwise 0.757), emphasizing that completeness can dominate quality ranking under natural variation.

**K. Withheld-GCP Point Accuracy:**
Analysis of withheld GCPs on the Helenenschacht dataset showed:
- The nominal model achieved decimeter accuracy at the point level (0.15 m horizontal RMSE).
- Withheld-GCP RMSE tracked camera RTK RMSE almost perfectly (Spearman 0.99), validating camera position error as a proxy for point-level metric error on this site.
- The blindness to coherent distortion carried over to the point axis, with coarse confidence at 1.00 for a corrupted model showing 11.0 m GCP RMSE.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a rigorous empirical characterization of a ground-truth-free self-validation protocol for photogrammetric reconstructions, clearly delineating its capabilities and, more significantly, its limitations. The findings have several implications:

- **Clarification of "Internal Consistency" vs. "Absolute Accuracy":** The paper establishes that high internal consistency, as measured by leakage-free hold-out, is not a substitute for an external accuracy statement. Reconstructions can be internally self-consistent yet globally distorted by tens or hundreds of meters. This is a crucial caution for practitioners who might otherwise over-interpret internal consistency scores as indicators of absolute accuracy.
- **Qualitative Fragmentation Warning:** The protocol *can* serve as a qualitative tripwire for fragmentation-type failures, where the reconstruction breaks apart. A confidence drop warrants investigation, as it may indicate structural issues. However, it is not a calibrated detector, as benign undersampling or changes in barrier strength can also lower confidence to similar levels, leading to potential false alarms if strict thresholds are applied without further context.
- **Blind Spot to Coherent Distortion:** The study demonstrates that the protocol is systematically blind to failures that manifest as self-consistent global distortions (e.g., scale errors, gentle warping, repeated-structure merges) because these types of errors preserve local geometry. This highlights a fundamental limitation rooted in the gauge freedom of bundle adjustment, impacting not only the proposed protocol but also other internal consistency metrics.
- **Impact on Automated Gating:** The findings strongly caution against automated gating of reconstruction outputs based solely on internal consistency metrics. A model can be catastrophically wrong (e.g., 63-122 m absolute error) while scoring perfectly on self-consistency, making a "pass" score unreliable as a safety certificate.
- **Release of Protocol and Harness:** The public release of the protocol and a reproducible degradation harness enables other researchers to replicate and extend these findings, fostering further investigation into robust quality assessment methods.
- **Future Research Direction:** The paper opens avenues for future work, including expanding the dataset for increased statistical power, developing external signals (e.g., from IMU, scale bars, or world priors) to catch coherent distortions, and working towards calibrated detection thresholds for fragmentation. It also highlights the need to characterize which corruption regimes lead to fragmentation versus coherent distortion in real-world scenarios.

In summary, this work provides a valuable negative result, showing clearly what a class of ground-truth-free self-checks can and, importantly, cannot promise. It contributes to a more informed understanding of the limitations of purely internal validation signals in photogrammetry.
