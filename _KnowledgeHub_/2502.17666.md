# Yes, Q-learning Helps Offline In-Context RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Artificial Intelligence Research Institute (AIRI) demonstrate that explicitly incorporating reinforcement learning objectives into offline in-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) models enhances performance. Their methods achieved approximately 30% average performance improvement over Algorithm Distillation (AD) across over 150 diverse datasets, and doubled performance in complex environments like XLand-MiniGrid.

## Problem

- Existing offline In-Context Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) methods, such as Algorithm Distillation (AD), primarily rely on supervised training objectives, which do not directly optimize for cumulative reward.
- The supervised learning paradigm in offline ICRL often leads to suboptimal policies, especially when dealing with low-quality datasets or a lack of optimal problem-solving trajectories.
- Current AD-like methods often assume structured 'learning histories' in the data, which may not always be available or easy to curate in real-world offline datasets.

## Method

- The authors modified the standard ==Algorithm Distillation (AD)== architecture by replacing its supervised next-action prediction head with explicit ==value-function and policy heads==.
- They implemented in-context versions of several reinforcement learning algorithms, including ==IC-DQN, IC-CQL, and IC-IQL== for discrete environments, and IC-TD3, IC-TD3+BC, and IC-IQL for continuous environments.
- The proposed methods were rigorously evaluated across over **150** synthetically generated datasets, varying in coverage, expertise levels, and the structural ordering of learning histories, spanning GridWorld and MuJoCo environments.

## Results

- RL-based approaches achieved an average performance improvement of approximately **30%** (measured by Normalized Area Under the Curve) over Algorithm Distillation (AD) across more than **150** GridWorld and MuJoCo datasets.
- In the challenging XLand-MiniGrid environment, RL-based methods significantly outperformed AD, doubling its performance even with a highly subsampled (**1%** of original size) dataset.
- RL-based methods demonstrated greater robustness to variations in dataset coverage, expertise levels, and the absence of explicit learning history structure, consistently outperforming AD in these challenging conditions across discrete and continuous environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly optimizing reinforcement learning objectives, rather than relying solely on supervised learning, is crucial for effective offline In-Context Reinforcement Learning (ICRL).
> - RL-based ICRL methods are more robust to data imperfections, exhibiting higher performance with limited dataset coverage, varied data expertise levels, and absence of structured learning histories.
> - Offline-specific regularization (e.g., as in Conservative Q-Learning or Implicit Q-Learning) is particularly important for robust ICRL performance in continuous state and action spaces.
