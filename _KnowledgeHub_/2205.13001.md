# Towards Diverse and Natural Scene-aware 3D Human Motion Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical three-stage framework synthesizes long-term, scene-aware 3D human motion from a target action sequence: an action-conditioned pose-placing stage samples diverse human-scene interaction anchors, a Neural-Mapper-guided A* planner produces varied obstacle-free paths, and a Transformer CVAE completes body movements. On PROX it cuts Fréchet Distance to 112.74 and raises motion diversity (APD 2.77) over SAMP and prior scene-aware baselines.

## Problem

- Prior scene-aware synthesis relies on manually predefined target objects or positions, so interaction diversity collapses and scene semantics barely influence where motions actually happen.
- Methods using 2D scene representations model body movement only, failing to capture the valid 3D interaction locations that surrounding geometry makes possible.
- Standard A* planners used by earlier pipelines emit a single deterministic path, and naive noise injection buys diversity only by sacrificing naturalness.

## Method

- Stage I pose placing: a ==conditional VAE== samples scene-agnostic action poses, a ==POSA==-style grid validity score places them, plus pose-relationship sampling and a **Placing Refiner CVAE** for offsets.
- Stage II **Neural Mapper**: a VAE over ==BPS==-encoded local 3D context emits scene-conditioned probabilistic moving-direction guidance that steers an adapted ==A*== search into diverse yet natural obstacle-free paths.
- Stage III motion completion network: a ==Transformer CVAE== with a **Path Refiner** first samples trajectories loosely following planned paths, then generates ==SMPL-X== poses conditioned on anchors, actions, and scene.

## Results

- On PROX motion completion, Fréchet Distance drops to **112.74** versus SAMP **115.34**, Wang et al. **121.22**, SA-CSGN **176.20**, while APD diversity rises to **2.77** against **2.56**.
- Neural Mapper paths score **4.27** user naturalness, near standard A*'s **4.31**, while random-noise and shared-noise variants score only **2.52** and **3.52** despite larger path spread.
- Anchor ablation: POSA baseline entropy **2.62** rises to **2.74** with pose-relationship sampling and **2.77** adding the Placing Refiner; position cluster score climbs **0.68** to **0.83**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing generation by diversity type (where to interact, which route, how to move) lets each level get a purpose-built module instead of one monolithic network.
> - Injecting raw noise into a planner buys diversity but destroys naturalness; a learned scene-conditioned prior gives variation that stays consistent across similar local geometry.
> - Diversity at the anchor level, not the pose level, is what scene semantics actually control; fixing interaction targets by hand silently mutes the scene.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of synthesizing long-term human motion sequences within realistic 3D environments, a capability that holds relevance for applications in virtual reality, animation, and robotics. Prior research in human motion synthesis primarily focused on generating body movements in isolation. More recent efforts have begun to incorporate scene context, acknowledging its influence on human actions.

Existing scene-aware motion synthesis methods typically operate under certain constraints that limit the diversity of generated motions. These limitations include:
1. **Limited 2D Scene Representation**: Some approaches utilize 2D representations of scene context, which may not fully capture the complexities of 3D human-scene interactions.
2. **Reliance on Pre-defined Targets**: Many methods necessitate manually assigned target objects or specific interaction positions within the scene. This deterministic approach restricts the variety of human-scene interactions, as it does not account for the natural stochasticity and diversity of human behavior in choosing interaction points or movement paths. For example, previous works like Wang et al. synthesize motions between given begin-end points, and SAMP uses 3D-oriented objects as specific interaction targets.
3. **Focus on Body Movements**: While these methods model body movements effectively, they often overlook the inherent diversity in how humans choose *where* to interact within a scene, *which path* to take, and the subtle variations in *how* they perform actions.

This paper positions itself to overcome these limitations by proposing a framework that explicitly models and generates diversity across multiple granularities of scene-aware human motion, moving beyond the simple synthesis of body movements to encompass a broader spectrum of human-scene interactions and movement strategies. The authors aim to enable the synthesis of diverse motions guided solely by target action sequences within a given scene, without requiring explicit pre-defined interaction points or movement paths.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize diverse and natural long-term 3D human motion sequences in real-world scenes, guided by a sequence of target action labels (e.g., sitting, lying, standing). The authors specifically aim to address the limitations of previous scene-aware motion synthesis approaches that are constrained by pre-defined target objects or positions, which inherently restrict the diversity of human-scene interactions and movement patterns.

The motivation for this work is rooted in the observation that real-world human behavior exhibits significant diversity across multiple levels when interacting with an environment. This diversity can be decomposed into three distinct aspects:

1. **Interaction Diversity**: Humans can perform the same action (e.g., "sitting") in various valid locations within a scene, using different objects or parts of objects, and assuming different poses (e.g., sitting on different chairs or a bed, with varied orientations). Existing methods often fix these interaction points, limiting variability.
2. **Path Diversity**: When moving between interaction points, humans do not always follow the shortest or a single deterministic path. Instead, they may take multiple valid, obstacle-free paths, influenced by preferences or minor contextual variations. Previous path planning modules, such as the standard A\* algorithm used in some works, typically generate only a single optimal path.
3. **Body Movement Diversity**: Even when performing similar actions along similar paths, individual body movements and styles can vary (e.g., subtle differences in walking gait or transitions between actions). Current approaches often focus on generating a single "natural" motion for a given path and action.

To address these limitations and capture the full spectrum of human motion diversity, the authors are motivated to develop a hierarchical framework that systematically models and generates each of these three aspects of diversity. This factorization aims to enable the synthesis of miscellaneous yet coherent human motions that are inherently more realistic and varied than those produced by existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for diverse and natural scene-aware 3D human motion synthesis is structured hierarchically, decomposing the problem into three sequential stages, each addressing a specific aspect of motion diversity. The input to the framework consists of a 3D scene representation (triangular mesh) and a sequence of target action labels. The output is a sequence of SMPL-X human body models (global translation, global orientation, and body pose parameters).

#### 3.1. Overview of the Framework

The framework comprises three main stages:
1. **Human-Scene Interaction Anchor Synthesis**: Generates diverse starting and ending poses (anchors) for specific actions within the scene.
2. **Diverse Path Planning**: Produces various obstacle-free paths connecting these synthesized anchors.
3. **Motion Completion**: Synthesizes diverse and continuous body movements to fill the intervals between consecutive anchors, following the planned paths.

#### 3.2. Stage I: Human-Scene Interaction Anchor Synthesis

This stage aims to generate diverse 3D human poses and their placements within the scene, corresponding to the target action sequence.

- **Scene-Agnostic Pose Synthesis**: A Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) is employed to generate diverse scene-agnostic body poses (θ). The CVAE takes a sampled noise vector from a prior Gaussian distribution and an encoded one-hot vector representing the action label (a) as input. This allows for generating varied poses for a given action without immediate scene constraints.
- **Scene-Conditioned Anchor Placing**: The synthesized poses are then placed into the 3D scene. This process adapts the approach of POSA with two key modifications to enhance diversity:
 - **Candidate Selection**: The 3D scene is uniformly discretized into non-overlapping grids. For each grid, eight uniform orientations parallel to the ground plane are sampled, creating "placing candidates" (translation *t*, orientation *φ*). Each pose is ranked against these candidates based on a compatibility score that considers affordance and penetration.
 - **Diversity Enhancement**:
 - An additional penalty is introduced for placing anchors with the same action label too close to each other, promoting a wider distribution of interaction points for similar actions.
 - A "Place Refiner" module, implemented as another CVAE, takes the latent code of the pose, scene context (encoded by PointNet), and the initial sampled anchor (¯P) as input. It outputs small offsets (∆t, ∆φ) to the initial translation and orientation, adding micro-diversity to the final anchor positions and orientations.

#### 3.3. Stage II: Diverse Path Planning

This stage generates diverse, obstacle-free paths between the synthesized human-scene interaction anchors.

- **Standard A\* Algorithm Adaptation**: The A\* algorithm is used to find paths across the same discrete 3D grids used for anchor placing. A cost function `f(q) = g(q) + h(q)` is defined, where `g(q)` is the cost from start to grid `q`, and `h(q)` is the heuristic cost from `q` to the target. Obstacle-free paths are ensured by filtering out grids where a cylinder model (approximating human volume) would collide with the scene.
- **Neural Mapper for Diversity**: To overcome the deterministic nature of standard A\* (which typically finds only the shortest path), a novel "Neural Mapper" module is introduced. This is a CVAE model that provides stochastic, scene-conditioned probabilistic guidance to the A\* algorithm.
 - For each grid `p`, the Neural Mapper takes a sampled latent code and local scene context features (obtained via BPS encoding) as input.
 - It outputs a "feasibility score" `m(p, q)` for moving to each neighboring grid `q`.
 - The A\* cost function is modified to `f(p, q) = g(q) + h(q) + (1 - m(p, q))`, where `(1 - m(p, q))` acts as a data-driven penalty/guidance term, enabling the algorithm to explore diverse yet natural paths. The Neural Mapper is trained in a data-driven manner to learn reasonable path variations.

#### 3.4. Stage III: Motion Completion

This final stage synthesizes diverse and continuous body movements that transition smoothly between the interaction anchors, approximately following the planned paths. The motion completion network is a Transformer-based CVAE, comprising two components:

- **Path Refiner**: This module refines the discrete, piecewise-linear paths generated by the Neural Mapper into continuous, smooth human trajectories.
 - It takes scene context (PointNet encoded) as input.
 - It uses positional encodings based on sinusoidal functions of time steps `t` (for continuity) and additional positional encodings derived from the discrete planned path (to ensure adherence to obstacle-free regions).
 - It synthesizes a sequence of refined translation and orientation pairs `{(t_1, φ_1), ..., (t_M, φ_M)}` for `M` frames. During training, it uses straight-line directions between start/end points as "planned paths" for efficiency, while during inference, it uses the paths from the Neural Mapper.
- **Motion Synthesizer**: This module generates the body poses (`{θ_1, ..., θ_M}`) for the `M` frames, making them scene-aware and consistent with the refined path and interaction anchors.
 - Inputs include scene context (PointNet encoded), action labels, and the poses and actions of the human-scene interaction anchors.
 - The refined path from the Path Refiner is used as an additional positional encoding to guide motion synthesis.
 - "Action tokens" (encoded action labels and poses of the start/end anchors) are added to the beginning and end of the positional encoding. This helps the Transformer decoder establish the relationship between the anchors and synthesize reasonable, smooth transitions between them.

#### 3.5. Training and Optimization

All CVAE models are optimized using ADAM. For physical plausibility, an additional optimization post-process (similar to) is applied to refine both human-scene interaction anchors and the completed motion sequences, considering affordance, penetration, floor contact, and motion smoothness. This includes L-BFGS for anchors and ADAM for motion sequences. For longer motions during inference, planned paths are split into equal-length pieces, intermediate anchor states are generated, and motions are synthesized for each piece and then concatenated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed framework was assessed on the PROX dataset and further evaluated on the Matterport3D dataset for generalization, focusing on diversity and naturalness metrics.

#### 4.1. Human-Scene Interaction Anchors

- **Diversity Evaluation**: Measured by K-Means clustering (K=20) on synthesized anchors, considering (θ, t, φ) for "Anchor" diversity and only (t, φ) for "Position" diversity. Metrics included entropy of cluster sizes and average distances between cluster centers and samples.
- **Results (Table 1, Figure 7)**:
 - The baseline (POSA) showed moderate diversity.
 - Introducing the pose-related sampling strategy (S) based on proximity penalties for anchors with the same action label significantly improved both Anchor and Position diversity (e.g., Anchor Entropy increased from 2.62 to 2.74, Position Entropy from 2.63 to 2.69).
 - Further incorporating the "Place Refiner" (R) provided additional micro-diversity, leading to the highest diversity scores (e.g., Anchor Entropy 2.77, Position Entropy 2.72).
 - Qualitative results (Figure 7) demonstrated that the pose-related sampling prevented similar poses from being placed too close, and the Place Refiner, combined with post-optimization, generated more varied optimal solutions.

#### 4.2. Diverse Path Planning

- **Diversity Evaluation**: Measured by the standard deviation (STD) of distances between sampled paths and paths generated by a standard A\* algorithm, computed at 1/6, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, and 5/6 points along the path.
- **Naturalness Evaluation**: Assessed via a user study (scores 1-5, higher is better).
- **Results (Table 2, Figure 5)**:
 - The standard A\* algorithm yielded zero diversity (STD = 0) as expected, producing deterministic paths. It achieved a high naturalness score (4.31).
 - Methods using random noise ("Random Noise" and "Shared Noise") generated diverse paths (higher STDs) but suffered significantly in naturalness (scores 2.52 and 3.52, respectively), often producing unnatural or zig-zag paths.
 - The proposed method using "Neural Mapper" achieved a balance, producing diverse paths (STDs ranging from 0.233 to 0.508) while maintaining high naturalness (user study score 4.27), comparable to the standard A\*. This indicates the Neural Mapper successfully provides learned, scene-aware guidance for diverse yet plausible paths.

#### 4.3. Motion Synthesis

- **Quality and Diversity Metrics**:
 - **Frechét Distance (FD)**: Measures the overall similarity between synthesized motions and ground-truth motions, computed on SMPL-X parameters (lower FD is better).
 - **Average Pairwise Distance (APD)**: Measures the diversity among synthesized motions for the same conditions (higher APD is better).
- **Naturalness Metrics (from Supplementary)**:
 - **Non-Collision Score**: Percentage of frames without human-scene penetration (higher is better).
 - **Contact Score**: Percentage of frames with physically plausible contact points (higher is better).
 - **User Study**: Scores 1-5 for overall naturalness.
- **Results (Table 3, Table 4, Figure 8)**:
 - **FD (Quality)**: The proposed "Ours" method achieved the lowest FD (112.74 without optimization, 111.65 with optimization), outperforming state-of-the-art methods like SAMP (115.34/114.22) and Wang et al. (121.22/120.01). This indicates higher fidelity to natural motion.
 - **APD (Diversity)**: "Ours" demonstrated the highest APD (2.77 without optimization, 2.78 with optimization), surpassing SAMP (2.56/2.57) and SA-CSGN (2.13/2.15). Wang et al. reported 0.00 APD, suggesting no diversity in their output. The "Ours*" variant (without Path Refiner) showed slightly lower diversity (2.46).
 - **Naturalness (Table 4)**: "Ours" achieved the highest non-collision (95.93/99.61) and contact scores (98.33/99.35) without/with optimization, respectively, and the highest user study score (3.68). This indicates that the synthesized motions are physically plausible and perceived as more natural by users. The Path Refiner was shown to be advantageous in synthesizing natural motions.
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 8)**: Showcased the framework's ability to synthesize diverse interaction anchors, planned paths, and completed motions on both PROX and unseen Matterport3D scenes.
 - **Generalization to Matterport3D (Table 5, 6)**: The framework demonstrated similar improvements in anchor diversity (Table 5) and motion quality/diversity (Table 6) on the Matterport3D dataset, despite not being trained on it, suggesting robust generalization capabilities.

#### 4.4. Further Discussion (from Supplementary)

- **Human-Centric Paradigm**: The paper argues that its human-centric paradigm (placing poses matching their physical structure) is more effective than scene-centric paradigms from previous works for generating physically plausible poses with specific actions, as prior methods struggle to explicitly link action to suitable scene structures (Figure 9).
- **Neural Mapper with Constraints**: The Neural Mapper can also integrate manual constraints (e.g., avoiding certain regions) while still producing natural and diverse paths (Figure 11).
- **Effect of Planned Path on Motion Synthesis**: Without the positional encoding from the planned path, synthesized motion can fail to follow the path and penetrate objects (Figure 12a). Both translation and orientation from the planned path are crucial; omitting orientation leads to unnatural body orientations (Figure 12c).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work contributes to the field of 3D human motion synthesis by addressing a fundamental limitation of prior methods: the lack of inherent diversity in synthesized scene-aware motions. Its significance lies in several key aspects:

1. **Comprehensive Diversity Modeling**: By explicitly decomposing human motion diversity into three distinct hierarchical levels—human-scene interaction anchors, motion paths, and body movements—the research provides a structured approach to generating a wider range of realistic motions. This decomposition offers a more nuanced understanding and control over motion synthesis.
2. **Novel Hierarchical Framework**: The proposed three-stage framework, with specialized modules for each level of diversity, offers a systematic and effective solution. This modular design enhances clarity and allows for independent optimization and improvement of each aspect.
3. **Technical Innovations**:
 - **Action-Conditioned Pose Placing Framework**: The modifications to the POSA approach, including pose-relationship based sampling and the Place Refiner, enable the synthesis of diverse and contextually appropriate interaction anchors, moving beyond predefined targets.
 - **Neural Mapper for Diverse Path Planning**: The integration of a data-driven stochastic module (Neural Mapper) into the A\* algorithm addresses the deterministic nature of traditional pathfinding. This allows for generating multiple natural and obstacle-free paths, reflecting real-world human path choices.
 - **Transformer-based Motion Completion Network**: The use of a Transformer-based CVAE, comprising Path Refiner and Motion Synthesizer, effectively generates continuous, smooth, and diverse body movements that adhere to the planned paths and transition naturally between interaction anchors.
4. **Improved Performance**: The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms previous state-of-the-art approaches in terms of both diversity (as measured by APD and other metrics) and naturalness (assessed through FD, physical plausibility scores, and user studies). The framework also shows strong generalization capabilities to unseen scenes (Matterport3D).
5. **Enhanced Efficiency and Generality**: The factorized design and data-driven modules contribute to a more efficient and generalizable system for generating human motions in varied scene contexts.

The potential impact of this research is substantial across various application domains:
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Gaming**: Enables the creation of more immersive and dynamic virtual environments with human characters exhibiting diverse and unpredictable behaviors, enhancing user experience.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction**: Provides more natural and diverse motion planning capabilities for humanoid robots, facilitating better interaction with human users and complex environments.
- **Animation and Content Creation**: Offers animators and content creators powerful tools to generate varied and realistic human motions for films, advertisements, and digital media, reducing manual effort.
- **Simulation for Training and Research**: Can be used to generate diverse human behavior datasets for training AI models or simulating complex scenarios in fields like crowd dynamics or safety research.

In summary, this work advances the state-of-the-art in scene-aware 3D human motion synthesis by providing a comprehensive, hierarchical, and data-driven approach to model and generate the inherent diversity in human interactions, paths, and body movements within complex environments.
