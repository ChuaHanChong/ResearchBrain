# Invertible Neural Network Adapter for One-Step Flow Matching in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an Invertible Neural Network adapter that enables one-step flow matching for robot manipulation. The method generates precise, high-dimensional actions conditioned on multimodal observations, achieving significant reductions in inference latency and improved task success rates across simulation and real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Existing flow-matching policies often suffer from high inference complexity and latency due to iterative steps, making them unsuitable for real-time robotic control.
- Current methods struggle with robustness and generalization in complex dexterous manipulation tasks, particularly in capturing multi-finger dynamics and integrating diverse multimodal observations.
- Prior one-step flow matching approximations often rely on auxiliary losses, leading to learned dynamics that do not accurately represent the true velocity field, impacting robustness and generalization in unseen scenarios.

## Method

- An ==Invertible Neural Network (INN) adapter== is integrated into a one-step flow matching framework, processing an initial denoised action estimate through a forward and inverse INN transformation.
- The INN maps action estimates into a compact, normalized spherical latent space and then inverts this representation to directly output the final predicted action for robot manipulation.
- A combined loss function is employed, incorporating both an adapter loss (enforcing consistency in data and latent spaces) and the original flow matching loss, to ensure stable training and preserve structural consistency.

## Results

- Achieved superior or near state-of-the-art performance across RoboTwin 2D and 3D dexterous manipulation tasks with a single inference step, improving average success rates by **3.2%** (2D) over multi-step baselines.
- Reduced real-world inference latency by approximately **45%** (from 110 ms to 61 ms) and improved the success rate on the precision-sensitive 'block stacking' task from **80%** to **90%**.
- Demonstrated enhanced performance in large-scale Vision-Language-Action tasks, achieving a **97.4%** average success rate with one inference step, and significantly reduced training costs, converging within 300 epochs compared to 2000 epochs for baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The bijective property of INNs ensures information preservation throughout the action transformation, maintaining structural consistency and mitigating approximation errors common in direct single-step inference methods.
> - Mapping complex action trajectories into a compact, task-adaptive latent manifold allows the mean velocity field to be directly inferred, enabling efficient one-step action generation without compromising accuracy.
> - Jointly optimizing for consistency in both the data space and the INN's latent space improves training stability and performance, leading to more robust and generalizable policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning faces the fundamental challenge of generating reliable, precise, and dexterous actions in complex, unstructured environments. Recent advancements have seen the emergence of diffusion-based policy learning, which has improved the modeling of high-dimensional and multimodal action distributions, leading to progress in robotic manipulation. More recently, flow matching has been proposed as an alternative generative modeling paradigm, demonstrating competitive performance and often superior training efficiency compared to diffusion-based methods.

Despite these advancements, existing flow-matching-based policies often exhibit limitations in terms of efficiency, robustness, and generalization when applied to complex real-world dexterous manipulation scenarios. Specific challenges include accurately capturing the fine-grained dynamics of multi-finger interactions, maintaining temporal consistency over long action sequences, and generalizing to novel environments or object configurations. Furthermore, current architectures frequently lack a unified mechanism for integrating heterogeneous multimodal observations, such as visual inputs, linguistic instructions, and proprioceptive feedback, which are critical for robust real-world robotic operations.

Prior work has attempted to address the efficiency of flow matching for one-step generation. For instance, ManiFlow introduced a consistency regularization term to enforce stable mappings for fewer inference steps, while Mean Flow aimed to directly learn the mean velocity field for single-step generation. However, these methods often rely on auxiliary loss terms to approximate one-step generation, which can lead to learned dynamics that do not faithfully correspond to the true underlying mean velocity field during deployment. This can result in degraded robustness and generalization when encountering unseen scenarios.

This research contributes to this landscape by proposing an invertible neural network (INN) adapter designed to mitigate these identified limitations. By constraining the action generation trajectory within an invertible latent space, the work aims to achieve efficient, high-quality, one-step dexterous action synthesis without compromising accuracy or robustness, thus offering an alternative to iterative flow-matching policies and addressing the shortcomings of previous one-step approximation methods. The method seeks to bridge the gap between computational efficiency and reliable performance in complex, multimodal robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an invertible neural network adapter for general robotic manipulation that enables the generation of precise, high-dimensional actions conditioned on multimodal observations through a single-step denoising process.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the current state of robotic manipulation policy learning:

- **Inference Complexity and Latency:** Conventional diffusion-based and existing flow-matching policies often require multiple iterative steps for action generation, leading to high inference complexity and latency. This makes them less suitable for real-time robotic control applications where rapid decision-making is crucial. The motivation is to reduce this computational overhead significantly by enabling one-step generation without sacrificing performance.
- **Robustness and Generalization in Complex Scenarios:** Current flow-matching policies frequently struggle to maintain robustness and generalize effectively in complex real-world dexterous manipulation tasks. This includes difficulties in capturing the intricate dynamics of multi-finger interactions and ensuring temporal consistency over extended action sequences, especially in novel environments or with unfamiliar object configurations. The research aims to enhance these aspects by imposing structural constraints on the learned velocity field.
- **Integration of Multimodal Observations:** Many existing architectures lack a principled and efficient mechanism for integrating diverse multimodal observations, such as visual data, language instructions, and proprioceptive feedback. These inputs are essential for a robot to understand and execute tasks effectively in human-centric environments. A key motivation is to provide a framework that can seamlessly incorporate these heterogeneous data types.
- **Limitations of Prior One-Step Generation Approaches:** Previous attempts at one-step flow matching (e.g., ManiFlow, Mean Flow) have often relied on auxiliary loss terms to approximate the mean velocity field. While these methods can achieve strong fitting performance on training data, empirical evidence suggests that the learned dynamics may not accurately represent the true underlying velocity field during deployment, leading to reduced robustness and generalization in unseen scenarios. This research is motivated to address these approximation errors by employing an invertible latent space, thereby maintaining information preservation and structural consistency.
- **Achieving Faithful Velocity Field Alignment:** The goal is to ensure a more faithful alignment between the learned velocity field and the true underlying action distribution. By constraining the action generation trajectory within an invertible latent space, the proposed method aims to prevent irreversible loss of latent information and maintain the structural consistency of the underlying flow dynamics.

In summary, the core motivation is to enhance the efficiency, robustness, and generalization capabilities of flow-matching policies for robot manipulation, particularly for real-time applications involving multimodal inputs, by introducing an invertible neural network adapter that facilitates accurate and stable one-step action generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces an Invertible Neural Network (INN) adapter integrated into a one-step flow matching framework for robotic manipulation. The approach builds upon the foundations of flow matching and invertible neural networks.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

- **Flow Matching:** The method leverages the continuous normalizing flows paradigm, where a forward process defines a straight-line path between a data sample $x_1$ (from the data distribution $D$) and a noise sample $x_0$ (from $N(0, I)$). An interpolated state $x_t$ at time $t \sim U$ is given by $x_t = (1 - t)x_0 + tx_1$. The corresponding velocity $v_t$ is defined as the displacement $x_1 - x_0$. A flow model, parameterized by $\theta$, is trained to predict this velocity $v_\theta(x_t, t)$ given $x_t$ and $t$, conditioned on observations $o$. The flow matching loss $L_{FM}(\theta)$ minimizes the squared difference between the predicted velocity and the true velocity $x_1 - x_0$.
- **Invertible Neural Network (INN):** The INN design, based on coupling layers, ensures exact invertibility. An input vector $x$ is partitioned into two disjoint subsets, $x_a$ and $x_b$. A coupling transformation updates one subset based on the other, for example, $y_a = x_a$ and $y_b = x_b + f(x_a)$, where $f(\cdot)$ is a non-linear neural network. This structure allows for a closed-form inverse transformation: $x_a = y_a$ and $x_b = y_b - f(y_a)$. A key property of this design is that the determinant of its Jacobian matrix is 1, which facilitates efficient likelihood computation and stable training. Multiple coupling layers with alternating partitions allow for complex, non-linear, yet invertible transformations.

#### 3.2. Invertible Neural Network Adapter

The core of the proposed method is the INN adapter, which addresses the limitations of standard one-step flow matching where the predicted vector field may deviate from the ideal straight-line path due to complex data distributions.

The adapter works as follows:
1. **Velocity Prediction:** The initial flow matching model predicts a velocity field $v(x_t, t | o)$ given a noisy sample $x_t$ at time $t$ and observation $o$.
2. **One-Step Denoised Estimate:** A one-step denoised estimate $\hat{x}$ is computed as $\hat{x} = x_t + (1 - t) v(x_t, t | o)$.
3. **Forward INN Transformation:** This estimate $\hat{x}$ is then fed into the forward process of the invertible neural network, $y = g(\hat{x})$.
4. **Latent Space Normalization:** The output $y$ from the INN is then normalized to a spherical space, $\tilde{y} = \frac{y}{\|y\|_2}$. This step is based on the assumption that a single-step action can be mapped to this spherical latent space.
5. **Inverse INN Transformation:** Finally, the normalized latent representation $\tilde{y}$ is fed back through the inverse process of the invertible neural network, $x_{pre} = g^{-1}(\tilde{y})$. This $x_{pre}$ directly represents the final predicted action for robot manipulation.

This process aims to map entire trajectories into a compact latent representation, effectively collapsing them into a single point from which the mean velocity can be directly inferred. By utilizing the bijective property of INNs, the method preserves information throughout the transformation, maintaining structural consistency and mitigating approximation errors inherent in direct single-step inference methods.

#### 3.3. Loss Function

The training objective incorporates two main components:

- **Adapter Loss ($L_{adapter}$):** This loss enforces consistency in both the data space and the latent space:
 $L_{adapter}(\theta) = E_{x_0 \sim N(0,I), x_1 \sim D, t \sim U} \left[ \|x_{pre}(x_t, t) - x_1\|^2 + \|\tilde{y}(x_t, t) - g(x_1)\|^2 \right]$.
 The first term minimizes the reconstruction error between the final predicted action $x_{pre}$ and the true target action $x_1$. The second term minimizes the discrepancy between the normalized latent representation $\tilde{y}$ and the true latent representation of the target action $g(x_1)$. Joint optimization of these terms aims to improve training stability and performance by enforcing consistency across the forward and inverse INN processes.
- **Final Training Objective:** The total loss $L(\theta)$ is a weighted sum of the adapter loss and the original flow matching loss:
 $L(\theta) = L_{adapter}(\theta) + \alpha L_{FM}(\theta)$, where $\alpha > 0$ is a hyperparameter balancing the two objectives.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Experiments:**
 - **RoboTwin 1.0 Benchmark:** Five dexterous manipulation tasks were selected.
 - **2D Image Observations:** Compared against ManiFlow, Diffusion Policy, and Flow Matching Policy, all using a ResNet-18 encoder. Training was conducted for 300 epochs.
 - **3D Point Cloud Observations:** Compared against ManiFlow, 3D Diffusion Policy, and 3D Flow Matching. Training involved 600 epochs. Modifications for 3D included initializing noise from an all-zero distribution and omitting timestep embeddings, based on empirical findings for 3D stability and performance.
 - **Libero Benchmark:** Four language-conditioned manipulation tasks were used to assess large-scale vision-language-action (VLA) learning.
 - The baseline models were built upon QwenVL-3-4B with either a Pi-style or Groot-style flow matching action head.
 - The proposed adapter was inserted after the flow matching action head. Conditional tokens for the adapter were extracted from the last three transformer layers of QwenVL-3-4B.
 - Training used four NVIDIA A100 GPUs for 8 epochs with a batch size of 32.
- **Real-World Experiments:**
 - Three dexterous manipulation tasks: "fruit picking and placement into a basket," "block stacking," and "dual-arm bottle grasping."
 - Hardware: UR robotic arm and OpenArm platform (single-arm and bimanual scenarios).
 - Baseline: Pi 0.5 model.
 - Both baseline and "Pi 0.5 + Adapter" models were trained using identical demonstration datasets, optimization strategies, training schedules, and hyperparameters to ensure fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic platforms yielded several key findings:

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **RoboTwin 2D Dexterous Manipulation Tasks:**
 - The proposed method, operating with a single inference step, achieved superior or near state-of-the-art performance across nearly all evaluated tasks (Pick, Diverse, Dual, Empty, Shoe).
 - Specifically, the proposed adapter improved the average task success rate by 3.2% compared to ManiFlow operating with 10 inference steps (Proposed: 47.6% ± 1.8 vs. ManiFlow (10 steps): 46.1% ± 2.7).
 - This indicates that the adapter preserved the expressive capacity of flow matching policies while aggressively accelerating inference and enhancing policy optimization through latent space constraints.
- **RoboTwin 3D Dexterous Manipulation Tasks:**
 - Similar to 2D observations, the proposed method achieved state-of-the-art or near state-of-the-art performance across nearly all RoboTwin tasks under 3D point cloud observations, maintaining a single inference step.
 - The average success rate for the proposed method was 64.9% ± 1.4, compared to ManiFlow (10 steps) at 61.9% ± 2.5 and ManiFlow (1 step) at 63.7% ± 2.2.
 - The study validated architectural modifications for 3D inputs, such as initializing noise from an all-zero distribution and omitting timestep embeddings. A simplified structure demonstrated comparable or improved performance (average 64.9% ± 1.4 vs. 60.1% ± 3.1 for the normally structured approach), indicating that the flow generation process can be streamlined for structured geometric observations.
- **Training Cost Reduction (RoboTwin):**
 - The proposed method converged within 300 training epochs, a significant reduction compared to the 2000 epochs required by ManiFlow, while still improving performance.
- **Libero Dexterous Manipulation Tasks (Vision-Language-Action):**
 - Incorporating the proposed adapter into the flow matching policy consistently yielded performance improvements across language-conditioned manipulation tasks (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long-horizon).
 - The "QWen3+Flow matching+adapter" achieved an average success rate of 97.4% with 1 inference step, outperforming "QWen3+PI" (95.7% with 4 steps) and "QWen3+GROOT" (96.5% with 4 steps).
 - These gains were achieved without introducing additional flow matching inference steps, demonstrating enhanced policy effectiveness while preserving inference efficiency in VLA models.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Task Performance:**
 - On the "fruit picking and placement into a basket" and "dual-arm bottle grasping" tasks, both the baseline Pi 0.5 model and "Pi 0.5 + Adapter" achieved perfect success rates (10/10). This indicates that the adapter preserved the strong manipulation capabilities of the original policy.
 - On the more challenging and precision-sensitive "block stacking" task, the proposed method improved the success rate from 80% (8/10) with Pi 0.5 to 90% (9/10) with "Pi 0.5 + Adapter." This demonstrates an enhancement in the policy's action generation capability for tasks requiring higher precision.
- **Inference Efficiency:**
 - The original Pi 0.5 model, which required 10 denoising steps, had an average inference time of approximately 110 ms per action prediction.
 - The proposed "Pi 0.5 + Adapter" achieved action generation with a single inference step, reducing the average inference time to approximately 61 ms. This represents a nearly 45% reduction in inference latency, highlighting the computational efficiency of the adapter for real-time robotic applications.

Overall, the findings indicate that the invertible neural network adapter effectively enables one-step flow matching for robot manipulation, providing a favorable trade-off between computational efficiency and policy quality. It consistently achieved superior or comparable results while reducing both training and inference overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of the invertible neural network adapter for one-step flow matching in robot manipulation presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field:

- **Enhanced Inference Efficiency for Real-Time Control:** The most prominent impact is the substantial reduction in inference complexity and latency. By enabling robust one-step action generation, the proposed method significantly lowers the computational overhead associated with iterative flow matching and diffusion policies. The demonstrated 45% reduction in real-world inference latency (from 110 ms to 61 ms) makes advanced generative policies more practical for real-time robotic control, where low-latency decision-making is critical for safety and responsiveness.
- **Improved Performance and Robustness:** The adapter not only accelerates inference but also maintains or improves action prediction accuracy and task success rates across diverse manipulation tasks and observation modalities. The observed improvements, such as the 3.2% average success rate increase on RoboTwin 2D and the 10% increase on the precision-sensitive real-world block-stacking task, indicate that the latent space constraints and information preservation properties of the INN lead to more accurate action generation and potentially stronger generalization. This addresses a key limitation of prior one-step approximation methods that suffered from degraded robustness.
- **Efficient Training:** Beyond inference, the method also reduces training costs, converging in significantly fewer epochs (300 vs. 2000 for a baseline), which contributes to faster model development and iteration cycles.
- **Principled Multimodal Integration:** The framework provides a mechanism for effectively integrating multimodal observations, including visual, linguistic, and proprioceptive inputs. This is crucial for developing generalist robotic policies that can understand and respond to complex instructions and sensory data in unstructured environments, especially in the context of vision-language-action (VLA) models.
- **Versatility and Compatibility:** The adapter's design allows for seamless integration into existing flow-matching architectures and VLA models (e.g., QwenVL-3-4B, Pi 0.5). This modularity means that current research and development efforts can benefit from the proposed improvements without requiring a complete overhaul of their underlying generative policy frameworks.
- **Advancement in Generative Models for Robotics:** By constructing a task-adaptive latent action manifold through invertible transformations, the method offers a novel approach to learning action distributions. This maintains the structural consistency of flow dynamics and provides a more faithful alignment between the learned velocity field and the underlying action distribution, addressing fundamental challenges in generative policy learning.

While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding highly multimodal/discontinuous action distributions and long-horizon tasks, its success in short-horizon, dexterous manipulation tasks with significant efficiency gains positions it as an important step forward. The potential to scale these efficiency and performance benefits to larger foundation models, once training overhead is optimized, could further amplify its impact, contributing to the development of more capable, responsive, and robust robots for a wider array of real-world applications.
