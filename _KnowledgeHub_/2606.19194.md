# Invertible Neural Network Adapter for One-Step Flow Matching in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an Invertible Neural Network adapter that enables one-step flow matching for robot manipulation. The method generates precise, high-dimensional actions conditioned on multimodal observations, achieving significant reductions in inference latency and improved task success rates across simulation and real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Existing flow-matching policies often suffer from high inference complexity and latency due to iterative steps, making them unsuitable for real-time robotic control.
- Current methods struggle with robustness and generalization in complex dexterous manipulation tasks, particularly in capturing multi-finger dynamics and integrating diverse multimodal observations.
- Prior one-step flow matching approximations often rely on auxiliary losses, leading to learned dynamics that do not accurately represent the true velocity field, impacting robustness and generalization in unseen scenarios.

## Method

- An ==Invertible Neural Network (INN) adapter== is integrated into a one-step flow matching framework, processing an initial denoised action estimate through a forward and inverse INN transformation.
- The INN maps action estimates into a compact, normalized spherical latent space and then inverts this representation to directly output the final predicted action for robot manipulation.
- A combined loss function is employed, incorporating both an adapter loss (enforcing consistency in data and latent spaces) and the original flow matching loss, to ensure stable training and preserve structural consistency.

## Results

- Achieved superior or near state-of-the-art performance across RoboTwin 2D and 3D dexterous manipulation tasks with a single inference step, improving average success rates by **3.2%** (2D) over multi-step baselines.
- Reduced real-world inference latency by approximately **45%** (from 110 ms to 61 ms) and improved the success rate on the precision-sensitive 'block stacking' task from **80%** to **90%**.
- Demonstrated enhanced performance in large-scale Vision-Language-Action tasks, achieving a **97.4%** average success rate with one inference step, and significantly reduced training costs, converging within 300 epochs compared to 2000 epochs for baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The bijective property of INNs ensures information preservation throughout the action transformation, maintaining structural consistency and mitigating approximation errors common in direct single-step inference methods.
> - Mapping complex action trajectories into a compact, task-adaptive latent manifold allows the mean velocity field to be directly inferred, enabling efficient one-step action generation without compromising accuracy.
> - Jointly optimizing for consistency in both the data space and the INN's latent space improves training stability and performance, leading to more robust and generalizable policies.
