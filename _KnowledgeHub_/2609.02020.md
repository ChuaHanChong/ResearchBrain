# Real-Time Dynamics-Based Torque-Sampling MPPI for Compliant and Force Aware Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A torque-sampling Model Predictive Path Integral framework that embeds analytic rigid-body dynamics inside the optimization loop for task-space manipulation. Custom CUDA kernels recompute the inertia matrix and bias forces per rollout per timestep, while cost terms encode end-effector pose, desired force, joint limits, and collisions. On a 7-DoF Franka it runs at 166 Hz with a 0.18 s horizon, giving compliant hybrid motion-force control without force sensors.

## Problem

- QP-based MPC assumes constant contact forces or linearized dynamics over the horizon, yet still costs enough compute to force short horizons and low update rates.
- DDP-based MPC reduces model approximation but stays sensitive to initialization, converges to local minima, and demands strictly differentiable cost functions.
- Existing MPPI manipulation work samples only kinematic joint positions, velocities, or accelerations, so torque-level optimization with full rigid-body dynamics remains unexplored.

## Method

- ==Torque-sampling== rollouts perturb nominal joint torque, solve ==forward dynamics== for acceleration via the ==inertia matrix== and bias force vector, then ==Euler-integrate== velocity and position each step.
- A GPU dynamics solver assigns one CUDA thread per rollout, storing link masses, joint transforms, and CoM offsets in constant memory and rebuilding inertia from link-wise ==Jacobians== every timestep.
- A hybrid cost sums ==SE(3) motion error==, force error using the ==dynamically consistent generalized inverse Jacobian== for end-effector force estimation, plus joint-limit and indicator-based collision penalties.

## Results

- Free-space hybrid control tracked a **10 N** x-axis force step with mean position error **0.0137 m** and orientation error **0.0208 rad** over the converged interval.
- Contact on a curved water bottle held a **-4 N** normal force with **1.80 N** mean absolute error, while position error stayed **0.012 m** and orientation **0.0921 rad**.
- The solver sustained **166 Hz** updates with a **30-step**, **0.006 s** horizon using **128 rollouts**, and verified real-time feasibility up to **1,024 rollouts**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sampling at the torque level rather than acceleration level makes compliance and force objectives emerge from the optimizer itself, with no force sensor or impedance layer.
> - Recomputing analytic dynamics per rollout per step is affordable on a GPU, dispelling the assumption that full-fidelity models force short horizons or slow updates.
> - Force accuracy degrades with unmodeled surface geometry and open-loop force estimation, so curved or irregular contacts still need explicit force feedback.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The expansion of robotic manipulators from controlled factory settings to unpredictable daily environments necessitates enhanced capabilities for safe and flexible physical interaction. This requirement underscores the importance of robust force control and compliance in robotic systems, particularly for managing unexpected collisions and performing contact-rich tasks.

Traditional dynamics-based control methods, such as inverse dynamics controllers, have demonstrated effectiveness in reactive control scenarios. However, these approaches often yield locally optimal solutions that may fail to satisfy physical limits or critical safety constraints.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) has been widely adopted as an alternative, offering the ability to generate optimal trajectories while adhering to complex constraints. A significant challenge in real-time MPC is the computational burden associated with explicitly incorporating full rigid-body dynamics, which are inherently nonlinear and high-dimensional.

Existing MPC methodologies have attempted to address this challenge with varying trade-offs:
- **Quadratic Programming (QP)-based MPC:** These methods are favored for their computational tractability but often rely on simplifying assumptions, such as treating contact forces or system dynamics as constant over the prediction horizon, or linearizing nonlinear dynamics. Despite these approximations, the resulting optimization problems can still be computationally intensive, leading to limitations in prediction horizon length and update frequency.
- **Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP)-based MPC:** These approaches aim to reduce model approximations and enable full dynamic control with improved computational efficiency. However, they are often sensitive to initialization, can converge to local minima, and require strictly differentiable cost functions.

Sampling-based MPC approaches, such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), have emerged as a promising alternative. MPPI's sampling-based formulation can naturally handle nonlinear dynamics and non-smooth or discontinuous cost functions, with GPU-based parallelization enabling efficient computation. Despite these advantages, existing MPPI applications for robotic manipulation have largely focused on kinematic-level tasks, with sampling typically performed over joint-space variables like positions, velocities, or accelerations. Explicitly accounting for full rigid-body dynamics and performing torque-level optimization within MPPI for manipulation has remained largely unexplored.

Some studies have utilized numerical simulators like Isaac Gym, MJPC, or Brax for estimating future system states. However, the authors note that these simulator-based methods may encounter limitations at short control time-steps due to discretization errors and can remain computationally intensive, thereby restricting their applicability to real-time torque-level optimization in manipulation scenarios.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the gap in MPPI-based manipulation, specifically by integrating full rigid-body dynamics to enable real-time torque-level optimization. The proposed framework aims to overcome the computational and modeling limitations of prior MPC and MPPI approaches, offering a novel method for compliant and force-aware manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this study is to propose and validate a novel Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)-based task-space control framework that explicitly integrates rigid-body dynamics into a real-time MPC formulation. This integration is designed to enable accurate motion and force control, resulting in compliant behaviors essential for safe and effective physical interaction of robotic manipulators in unstructured environments.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this research are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Challenges in Unstructured Environments:** As robots move from structured factory environments to complex, unstructured settings, the capability for safe and flexible physical interaction becomes paramount. This requires robust force control and inherent compliance to handle unexpected collisions and perform contact-rich tasks effectively.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Conventional Dynamics-Based Control:** While traditional dynamics-based control methods are effective from a reactive perspective, they often generate locally optimal solutions that may violate physical limits or safety constraints. The study aims to achieve globally optimal (within the prediction horizon) and constraint-satisfying behaviors.
- **Enhancing MPC with Full Rigid-Body Dynamics:** Existing MPC approaches struggle with the computational cost of incorporating full rigid-body dynamics due to their nonlinear and high-dimensional nature, which hinders real-time application. A key objective is to develop an MPC framework that can explicitly solve rigid-body dynamics within the optimization loop without sacrificing real-time performance.
- **Mitigating Approximations in QP-based MPC:** QP-based MPC methods often rely on simplifying assumptions (e.g., constant contact forces, linearized dynamics) to achieve computational tractability. This work aims to reduce such model approximations to ensure higher fidelity control.
- **Improving upon DDP-based MPC Limitations:** DDP-based methods, while reducing model approximations, are sensitive to initialization, prone to converging to local minima, and require strictly differentiable cost functions. The study aims to leverage a sampling-based approach to inherently handle nonlinear dynamics and non-smooth cost functions.
- **Extending MPPI to Dynamics-Based, Torque-Level Control:** Current MPPI applications in robotics are predominantly kinematic-level, sampling joint-space variables. A core motivation is to extend MPPI to explicitly account for full rigid-body dynamics and perform torque-level optimization, which enables inherent compliance and seamless integration of force-related objectives.
- **Achieving High Computational Efficiency for Real-Time Performance:** To enable torque-level optimization for dynamics-based MPPI in real-time, high computational throughput is necessary. The study aims to leverage GPU-based parallelization to achieve efficient computation, allowing for prolonged prediction horizons and high update frequencies.
- **Avoiding Simulator-Based Limitations:** Simulator-based approaches, while valuable, can introduce discretization errors at short control time-steps and remain computationally intensive for real-time torque-level optimization in manipulation. This work aims to provide an alternative that maintains high fidelity without these specific simulator-related drawbacks.

In summary, the motivation is to develop a real-time, dynamics-based MPPI framework that provides compliant and force-aware manipulation capabilities by directly optimizing joint torques, handling nonlinear dynamics, satisfying constraints, and achieving high computational efficiency through GPU parallelization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a torque-sampling Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) framework designed for real-time manipulation, explicitly incorporating an analytic rigid-body model directly into the optimization process. This approach treats joint torques as primary control inputs, enabling integrated motion and force control with inherent compliance.

**A. Analytic Dynamics for Torque-Sampling Rollouts**
The core of the method involves a torque-level sampling strategy, where control sequences ($u_{k,t} = \tau_t + \delta\tau_{k,t}$) are generated by perturbing a nominal torque. To evaluate the cost of each sampled sequence, the framework predicts future states by propagating the robot's configuration over a finite prediction horizon. Since control inputs are sampled at the torque level, the system explicitly computes joint accelerations via forward dynamics before integrating them to update the robot's state.

1. **Rigid-Body Dynamics:** The robot's dynamics are governed by the equation:
 $M(q)\ddot{q} + h(q, \dot{q}) = \tau + \tau_{ext}$
 where $M(q)$ is the inertia matrix, $h(q, \dot{q})$ is the bias force vector (Coriolis, centrifugal, gravitational), $\tau$ is the joint torque, and $\tau_{ext}$ is the projected external joint torque ($J^T(q)F_{ext}$). $\tau_{ext}$ is assumed constant over the prediction horizon.
2. **Joint Acceleration Computation:** For each sampled torque, joint acceleration $\ddot{q}_{k,t}$ is explicitly computed by solving the dynamics equation:
 $\ddot{q}_{k,t} = M^{-1}(q_{k,t-1})(u_{k,t} + \tau_{ext} - h(q_{k,t-1}, \dot{q}_{k,t-1}))$
3. **State Integration:** Joint velocity and position are updated using an explicit Euler integration scheme with a time step $dt$:
 $\dot{q}_{k,t} = \dot{q}_{k,t-1} + \ddot{q}_{k,t} dt$
 $q_{k,t} = q_{k,t-1} + \dot{q}_{k,t} dt$
 To maintain high fidelity, $M(q)$ and $h(q, \dot{q})$ are recomputed at every time step using the analytic rigid-body model.

**B. GPU-Based Dynamics Solver**
To achieve the high computational throughput necessary for high-frequency control, the recursive integration process is implemented directly within custom CUDA kernels.
- **Parallel Rollouts:** Each GPU thread is assigned to an individual rollout. This thread performs forward kinematics, determines link-wise Jacobian matrices and their derivatives, and computes the end-effector pose.
- **Dynamics Term Computation:** The inertia matrix $M$ is constructed by accumulating transformed link inertias and mass properties. Similarly, the bias force vector $h$ is derived from link-wise Jacobian matrices and their time derivatives.
- **Memory Optimization:** Robot parameters (e.g., link masses, kinematic chains) are identical across all rollouts and are stored in the GPU's constant memory. This enables parallel computation without inter-thread communication, supporting simultaneous computation of thousands of rollout trajectories.

**C. Cost Function Design for Hybrid Control**
The cost function $\ell(x_t, v_t)$ is formulated as a weighted sum of task-related objectives and physical constraints: $\ell(x_t, v_t) = C_{task} + C_{constraint}$.

1. **Task Cost ($C_{task}$):** Decomposed into motion and force control components: $C_{task} = C_{motion} + C_{force}$.
 - **Motion Control Cost ($C_{motion}$):** Penalizes deviations from the desired end-effector trajectory in SE(3). It includes terms for position error (L2-norm of desired minus current position) and orientation error (L2-norm of the log map of rotation matrix error). Diagonal weighting matrices ($W_{pos}$, $W_{ori}$) specify axis selection and priority.
 - **Force Cost ($C_{force}$):** Formulated to penalize the deviation from a desired end-effector force ($F_{des} - F_{ee}$) and includes a regularization term for $F_{ee}$. Weighting matrices ($W_{force}$, $W_{reg}$) are used.
 - **End-effector Force Estimation:** The end-effector force $F_{ee}$ is calculated as $F_{ee} = \bar{J}^T \tau$, where $\bar{J}$ is the dynamically consistent generalized inverse: $\bar{J} = M^{-1}J^T(JM^{-1}J^T)^{-1}$. This formulation accounts for system inertia and aims to decouple from null-space torques.

2. **Constraint Cost ($C_{constraint}$):** Ensures safety and kinematic feasibility: $C_{constraint} = C_{joint} + C_{collision}$.
 - **Joint Cost ($C_{joint}$):** Enforces position and velocity limits for kinematic feasibility and regularizes redundant motions to promote balanced postures.
 - **Collision Avoidance Cost ($C_{collision}$):** Uses an indicator function that imposes a penalty if the minimum distance between predefined collision pairs (link-to-link, link-to-object) falls below a safety threshold. A scalar collision penalty weight ($w_{collision}$) is applied.

**D. MPPI Control Loop**
The overall control loop is executed in parallel on the GPU. At each time step, $K$ random control noise samples ($\delta\tau_{k,t}$) are generated and used to propagate the analytic dynamics. Following the parallel rollouts, the costs are evaluated, and the optimal control input is updated using importance sampling rules. The first control input is applied, and the remaining sequence warm-starts the next optimization.

**E. Experimental Setup**
Real-world experiments utilized a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 (FR3). The MPPI controller ran on a desktop PC (i9-10900KF, RTX 2070 Super GPU), while an Intel NUC13 (i7) served as a real-time communication bridge with the robot via the Franka Control Interface (FCI) at 1 kHz. Communication between the NUC and the MPPI controller used ZeroMQ. A zero-order hold (ZOH) was applied to MPPI torque commands to bridge the frequency gap between the MPPI solver (average 166 Hz) and the FCI (1 kHz). MPPI parameters included $K=128$ rollouts, a prediction horizon $T=30$ steps, and a time step $dt=0.006$ s.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents several findings derived from computational performance analysis and real-world experiments on a 7-DoF manipulator.

**A. Computational Performance**
The proposed torque-sampling MPPI framework, leveraging its GPU-parallelized analytic dynamics solver, achieved a solver update rate exceeding 166 Hz. This was accomplished with a prediction horizon of 0.18 seconds (30 steps at 0.006 s per step). The authors state that this performance places the method among the faster dynamics-based MPC approaches capable of both motion and force control reported to date. While the solver was verified to process up to 1,024 rollouts in real-time, $K=128$ was empirically selected as sufficient for the given tasks, balancing convergence and computational load.

**B. Control Performance Verification in Free-Space**
A hybrid motion-force control experiment was conducted in free-space with the robot subjected to external perturbations. The task involved tracking a desired force ($F_{x,d} = 10 \text{ N}$) along the x-axis and maintaining specific target positions for the y and z axes, along with an initial orientation.
- **Nominal Tracking:** Under free-space conditions without disturbances, the controller successfully tracked the desired force and pose.
- **Response to Motion Blocking:** When the end-effector's motion along the force-controlled x-axis was intentionally obstructed (t = 3–4.5 s), the controller demonstrated compliant behavior.
- **Response to External Forces:** Upon application of external forces in different directions (world frame's +z, +y, and -y), the manipulator responded compliantly, yielding in the direction of the applied force, and subsequently converged back to the desired force and pose once the disturbance was removed.
- **Quantitative Performance (nominal interval t = 4.5–6 s):** The mean L2-norm position error in the position-controlled axes (y, z) was 0.0137 m, and the mean orientation error was 0.0208 rad.

**C. Hybrid Motion–Force Control under Maintained Contact**
Experiments were performed to assess the framework's performance in contact-rich scenarios, specifically maintaining contact with a cylindrical water bottle. Force control was applied along the z-axis ($F_{z,d} = -4 \text{ N}$), while the y-axis followed a linear trajectory. Other task-space degrees of freedom were regulated.
- **Contact Establishment and Maintenance:** After activating the force-related cost at t = 7.5 s, stable contact was established at approximately t = 7.67 s. The controller reliably maintained contact with the curved surface throughout the interaction phase.
- **Force Control Performance:** The mean absolute error for force control over the contact interval (t $\geq$ 7.67 s) was 1.80 N. This error was attributed by the authors to the unmodeled curvature of the surface and the absence of explicit force feedback. The study highlighted that force-aware behavior emerged directly from the torque-sampling optimization, even without prior geometric information or dedicated force sensors.
- **Pose Tracking Performance:** While maintaining contact force, stable pose tracking was observed. The mean L2-norm errors over the hybrid control interval (t $\geq$ 7.67 s) were 0.012 m in position (x, y) and 0.0921 rad in orientation. These results indicated the framework's ability to manage trade-offs between force and motion objectives during contact-rich manipulation.

**D. Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance**
The framework's real-time reactivity and safety were evaluated in a dynamic obstacle avoidance scenario. The robot was commanded to exert a constant force ($F_{y,d} = -4 \text{ N}$) while maintaining its posture. An Intel RealSense L515 tracked the obstacle's position at approximately 20 Hz.
- **Reactive Collision Avoidance:** When a dynamic obstacle (a mustard container) intersected the robot's path, the robot autonomously adjusted its motion towards the +y-direction to avoid a collision. This reactive motion momentarily superseded the primary force command directed towards the -y-axis.
- **Priority of Safety:** This behavior was governed by a high priority assigned to the collision cost weight ($w_{collision}$), demonstrating that safety constraints took precedence over task execution.
- **Modeling for Collision Detection:** Obstacles and robot links were modeled as simplified cylinders with a safety threshold of 0.2 m for efficient collision detection. This experiment validated the framework's capability to manage dynamic obstacles while maintaining responsive real-time control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in real-time robotic manipulation by proposing a computationally streamlined, torque-sampling Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) framework that directly integrates rigid-body dynamics into the optimization loop.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Addressing Fundamental Trade-off:** The work tackles the long-standing challenge in real-time Model Predictive Control (MPC) of concurrently maintaining high dynamic fidelity, achieving high update rates, and extending prediction horizons. By evaluating analytic forward dynamics within GPU-parallelized rollouts, the framework achieves a solver update rate of 166 Hz and a predictive window of 0.18 seconds, while using small time-step sizes to ensure physical fidelity.
- **Novelty in MPPI for Manipulation:** To the authors' knowledge, this work represents the first implementation of an MPPI-based manipulation framework that explicitly incorporates full rigid-body dynamics for torque-level optimization. This distinguishes it from previous MPPI applications that were largely limited to kinematic-level tasks or joint-space sampling.
- **Enabling Compliant and Force-Aware Behaviors:** By directly sampling and optimizing joint torques, the proposed method inherently enables compliant behaviors during physical interactions. This facilitates accurate motion and force control, which is critical for safe and effective physical interaction in unstructured and contact-rich environments.
- **Integrated Constraint Handling:** The framework effectively incorporates diverse constraints, including task-space motion and force objectives, joint limits, and dynamic obstacle avoidance. The demonstration of collision avoidance overriding primary task commands underscores the practical importance of its safety mechanisms.
- **High-Frequency, Stable, and Responsive Control:** Real-world experiments validate the framework's capability to provide stable and responsive manipulation across various scenarios, including disturbance rejection in free-space and maintained contact with complex, curved surfaces without explicit geometric models. This reliability is a direct result of its dynamics-based formulation and computational efficiency.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy in Unstructured Environments:** The ability to perform compliant and force-aware manipulation with real-time dynamics-based control could significantly broaden the applicability of robots in human-centric environments, such as service robotics, healthcare, and advanced manufacturing where physical interaction and safety are paramount.
- **Foundation for Advanced Manipulation Tasks:** The framework lays a foundation for more sophisticated manipulation capabilities. The authors specifically mention future work extending the framework to include object dynamics, which is crucial for in-hand manipulation and multi-arm collaborative tasks.
- **Robustness in Dynamic and Uncertain Scenarios:** By explicitly accounting for rigid-body dynamics and efficiently handling non-linearities and non-smooth costs via MPPI, the method offers increased robustness to dynamic uncertainties and physical contact, which are prevalent in real-world applications.

The presented methodology and experimental validations indicate a significant step towards developing more intelligent, adaptable, and safe robotic manipulators for complex interaction tasks. Future work on incorporating object dynamics and addressing dynamic uncertainty and contact effects suggests continued development towards even more sophisticated robotic capabilities.
