# Controlling Multimodal LLMs via Reward-guided Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces Multimodal Reward-Guided Decoding (MRGD), the first method to enable dynamic, inference-time control over Multimodal Large Language Models. It significantly reduces visual hallucinations and allows users to balance object precision, object recall, and computational cost for image captioning tasks.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) offer limited or no dynamic, fine-grained control over their outputs at inference time.
- MLLMs frequently suffer from visual hallucinations, generating descriptions of objects not present in the input image, which compromises reliability.
- Existing alignment methods like SFT and RLHF produce static MLLMs, while adapting reward-guided decoding from text-only LLMs to multimodal scenarios presents unique challenges.

## Method

- The ==Multimodal Reward-Guided Decoding (MRGD)== strategy is introduced, enabling dynamic control during the MLLM's autoregressive generation process.
- Two distinct multimodal reward models are developed: ==r_hal== to penalize object hallucinations and ==r_rec== to reward the mention of present objects, composed from off-the-shelf tools.
- A ==search-based decoding algorithm== combines these reward models using a configurable weight w to balance object precision and recall, with parameters k and T controlling compute-quality trade-offs.

## Results

- The object hallucination reward model (r_hal) achieved **82.05%** validation accuracy in distinguishing hallucinated from factual responses.
- MRGD reduced instance-level visual hallucinations by approximately **70%** on the COCO benchmark (from **15.05%** to **4.53%**) when prioritizing precision, while also enabling state-of-the-art object recall when prioritizing coverage.
- The approach consistently surpassed existing hallucination mitigation methods, including fine-tuning and other guided decoding strategies, across multiple MLLMs like LLaVA-1.5, Llama-3.2-Vision, and SmolVLM-2, demonstrating robustness and generalizability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal reward models, specifically designed for object hallucination and recall, can effectively steer MLLM generation towards desired visiolinguistic properties.
> - Inference-time dynamic control allows for a flexible trade-off between object precision (reducing hallucinations) and object recall (improving comprehensiveness) based on user-defined priorities.
> - The proposed search-based decoding strategy, when combined with intermittent reward evaluation, offers a more computationally efficient means of guiding MLLMs than naive rejection sampling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement and widespread adoption of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) mark a pivotal shift in AI, enabling machines to understand and generate content across visual and linguistic modalities. However, this proliferation has brought to light critical challenges, primarily concerning **controllability** and the mitigation of **hallucinations**.

Prior research in MLLMs has largely focused on increasing their capabilities across a wide range of visiolinguistic tasks. While techniques like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) have been instrumental in aligning MLLMs with desired behaviors and reducing certain forms of undesirable outputs, they possess inherent limitations when it comes to *inference-time control*. SFT and RLHF typically result in a static model that, once trained, offers little to no dynamic adjustment to its output characteristics based on real-time user needs or computational constraints. Prompt engineering provides some coarse control but lacks fine-grained dynamic adjustment.

A significant gap exists when comparing MLLMs to their text-only counterparts. For traditional LLMs, reward-guided decoding strategies have emerged as powerful tools for achieving fine-grained control over output attributes like helpfulness, harmlessness, or summary quality. These methods leverage reward models during the decoding process to actively steer text generation towards desired criteria. However, adapting such techniques to MLLMs presents unique challenges: multimodal reward models must simultaneously process and understand the complex interactions between visual inputs and textual outputs, a task significantly more intricate than processing a single modality. Visual hallucinations, where MLLMs generate descriptions of objects not present in the image, are a particularly prevalent and critical issue that current methods struggle to control dynamically.

This paper addresses precisely this gap by introducing the *first method for reward-guided decoding specifically for MLLMs*. It positions itself as a pioneering effort to bring the fine-grained, on-the-fly controllability demonstrated in text-only LLMs to the multimodal domain. By focusing on visual grounding—specifically object precision (reducing hallucinations) and object recall (ensuring thoroughness)—the work directly tackles one of the most pressing issues for MLLM reliability and trustworthiness. Furthermore, by enabling dynamic control over the trade-off between output quality and computational cost at inference time, it contributes to making MLLMs more adaptable and practical for diverse real-world applications with varying resource constraints and user priorities. This work represents a significant step towards more transparent, controllable, and user-centric MLLM deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the controllability of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) at inference time through reward-guided decoding**. The authors aim to provide users with unprecedented dynamic control over the quality and characteristics of MLLM-generated outputs, specifically focusing on image captioning tasks.

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations and aims:

- **Addressing the Demand for Adaptive MLLM Behavior:** As MLLMs become more prevalent across various applications, there is a growing demand to adapt their behavior to diverse user needs and scenarios. Current MLLMs, primarily aligned via fine-tuning (SFT, RLHF), offer limited or no dynamic control over their outputs once deployed.
- **Tackling Visual Hallucinations:** A major challenge in MLLMs is their propensity for visual hallucinations – generating descriptions of objects or attributes that are not present in the input image. This significantly undermines the trustworthiness and utility of MLLMs, particularly in critical applications. The authors aim to significantly mitigate these hallucinations.
- **Enabling Fine-Grained Control over Output Characteristics:** The paper specifically targets two crucial, often conflicting, output characteristics:
 - **Object Precision:** The accuracy of object mentions, minimizing hallucinations.
 - **Object Recall:** The comprehensiveness of object mentions, ensuring all relevant objects are included.
 The motivation is to allow users to dynamically trade off these aspects based on their immediate requirements.
- **Balancing Compute and Quality:** Users operate under varying computational budgets and quality expectations. For instance, an on-device application might prioritize low latency (less compute) over maximal detail, while an offline data generation task might tolerate higher compute for superior visual grounding. The research aims to provide a mechanism to control this trade-off.
- **Bridging the Gap from Text-Only LLMs:** Reward-guided decoding has proven effective for text-only LLMs but has been absent for MLLMs due to the complexities of multimodal reward modeling. A key motivation is to extend this powerful paradigm to the multimodal domain, acknowledging and addressing the unique challenges posed by processing both visual and textual information.
- **Improving on Existing Mitigation Methods:** Current hallucination mitigation strategies (prompting, fine-tuning, post-hoc rectification) offer either coarse control, no inference-time control, or can be computationally expensive. The authors are motivated to develop a more effective, flexible, and potentially more compute-efficient solution.

In summary, the core motivation is to make MLLMs more **flexible, reliable, and user-adaptive** by empowering users with real-time, fine-grained control over generation parameters that directly impact factual accuracy and comprehensiveness, while also managing computational resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Multimodal Reward-Guided Decoding (MRGD)**, a novel inference-time strategy designed to control the behavior of MLLMs. The methodology is built upon two core components: specialized multimodal reward models and a search-based guided decoding algorithm.

#### 3.1. Building Multimodal Reward Models

The effectiveness of MRGD hinges on accurate reward functions that can evaluate different aspects of visual grounding. The authors construct two distinct reward models (RMs):

- **Object Hallucination Reward Model (`r_hal`):**
 - **Objective:** To incentivize factual precision and penalize the generation of non-existent objects.
 - **Training Data:** Trained on a mixture of publicly available multimodal preference datasets known for distinguishing hallucinated vs. non-hallucinated responses (LLaVA-RLHF, RLHF-V, POVID, Sugar-Crepe).
 - **Architecture:** Uses PaliGemma as the backbone, a powerful multimodal model, with a linear regression head appended to project the last output token embedding to a single scalar score.
 - **Loss Function:** A modified Bradley-Terry model loss, augmented with mean-squared error terms to encourage scores of preferred responses (`y+`) to be close to 1 and rejected responses (`y-`) to be close to 0. This promotes well-calibrated reward scores suitable for combination.
 - **Training Details:** Finetuned with LoRA on the PaliGemma backbone.

- **Object Recall Reward Model (`r_rec`):**
 - **Objective:** To incentivize thoroughness by rewarding the mention of objects actually present in the image.
 - **Architecture (Composed of Off-the-Shelf Modules):**
 - **Object Detector:** OWLv2 is used to extract "reference objects" (`O_ref`) from the input image.
 - **POS Tagger:** Classical NLP tools (NLTK) are used to extract "predicted objects" (`O_pred`) from the MLLM's generated caption.
 - **Word Embedding Model:** Sentence-BERT is employed to embed both reference and predicted objects into a common semantic space.
 - **Similarity Function:** Semantic similarity (dot product) is computed between embedded predicted objects and reference objects.
 - **Recall Calculation:** A predicted object is considered a true positive if its maximum semantic similarity with any reference object exceeds a predefined threshold (τ=0.5). The `r_rec` score is then calculated as the ratio of true positives to the total number of reference objects, effectively measuring object coverage.

#### 3.2. Multimodal Reward-Guided Decoding (MRGD) Strategy

This strategy integrates the two reward models into the MLLM's autoregressive generation process.

- **Combined Score Function:** A user-defined guidance strength hyperparameter `w ∈` is introduced to linearly combine the two reward models:
 `s(x_v, x_q, y) = w * r_hal(x_v, x_q, y) + (1 - w) * r_rec(x_v, x_q, y)`
 This allows dynamic control over the precision-recall trade-off: `w=1` prioritizes hallucination reduction, `w=0` prioritizes object recall, and intermediate values balance them.
- **Search-Based Decoding (Algorithm 1):**
 1. The MLLM generates `k` candidate completions (sentences in this case) for a partial response.
 2. Each candidate completion is evaluated using the combined score `s`.
 3. The candidate with the highest score is selected and appended to the current response.
 4. This process iterates until the ` <EOS>` token is generated.
- **Reward Evaluation Period (`T`):** To address the challenge of evaluating incomplete sentences (which might score poorly due to syntactic incompleteness), the reward models are applied not at every token, but every `T` sentences (using "." as a delimiter). This ensures that the reward models evaluate more semantically complete chunks of text, making the guidance more robust. A larger `T` increases compute but potentially allows for more holistic evaluation. Setting `T = ∞` is equivalent to rejection sampling.
- **Base MLLMs:** The method is evaluated on various base MLLMs, including LLaVA-1.5, Llama-3.2-Vision, and SmolVLM-2, demonstrating its generalizability.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Standard object hallucination benchmarks (CHAIR and AMBER) are used, reporting instance-level and sentence-level hallucination rates, object recall/coverage, and caption length.

This modular approach, combining specialized reward models with a configurable search strategy, allows for flexible, fine-grained control over MLLM outputs at inference time, addressing critical challenges in multimodal AI.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, demonstrating the efficacy and flexibility of the proposed MRGD method:

1. **Effective Reward Model Performance:**
 - The `r_hal` (object hallucination) reward model achieved an average validation accuracy of 82.05% on preference data and 67.68% on a held-out dataset (VLFeedback), indicating its ability to reliably distinguish between hallucinated and non-hallucinated responses.
 - The `r_rec` (object recall) model, built from off-the-shelf components, also showed reasonable performance in detecting objects and POS tagging, empirically proving its utility in guiding recall.

2. **Achieving Fine-Grained Controllability over Precision-Recall Trade-off:**
 - MRGD successfully allows dynamic control over the trade-off between object precision (lowering hallucinations) and object recall (increasing coverage) by adjusting the `w` hyperparameter.
 - For LLaVA-1.5 on the COCO benchmark:
 - `w=1.0` (prioritizing precision): Achieved a significant ~70% reduction in instance-level hallucinations (CHAIR_i from 15.05% to 4.53%) compared to greedy decoding, with a moderate 6.5% decrease in recall.
 - `w=0.0` (prioritizing recall): Achieved state-of-the-art object recall, albeit with a higher hallucination rate, demonstrating the inherent trade-off.
 - Intermediate `w` values effectively balance these two objectives. For example, `w=0.5` significantly increased recall compared to `w=1.0` while maintaining a low hallucination rate.
 - The optimal `w` value was found to vary by benchmark (e.g., COCO benefiting more from higher `r_rec` weight due to more detected objects), highlighting the need for dynamic control.

3. **Superior Performance Compared to Existing Methods:**
 - MRGD consistently outperformed a range of existing hallucination mitigation methods, including both fine-tuning approaches (e.g., LLaVA-RLHF, HA-DPO, EOS, HALVA, mDPO) and other guided decoding strategies (e.g., VCD, CGD).
 - Notably, MRGD with `w=1.0` achieved a ~50% lower hallucination rate on COCO and ~30% on AMBER compared to CGD, which also uses guided decoding but with a simpler CLIP-like model.
 - A surprising finding was that some fine-tuning methods (LLaVA-RLHF, VCD), while effective on discriminative VQA tasks, actually showed *higher* hallucination rates than greedy decoding on generative captioning benchmarks, suggesting a potential disconnect in evaluation paradigms.

4. **Generality and Robustness Across MLLMs:**
 - MRGD was successfully applied to newer and architecturally diverse MLLMs, including Llama-3.2-Vision and SmolVLM-2, demonstrating its broad applicability without retraining the reward models.
 - Even for Llama-3.2-Vision, which is already aligned with DPO and less prone to hallucinations, MRGD provided further improvements, achieving additional ~1-2% reduction in instance-level hallucinations or significant boosts in recall depending on `w`.

5. **Controlling Visual Grounding vs. Compute Trade-off:**
 - The parameters `k` (number of samples) and `T` (reward evaluation period) allow control over the trade-off between visual grounding quality and test-time computational cost.
 - Increasing `k` and decreasing `T` generally improves visual grounding. Crucially, MRGD with `T=1` (evaluating at each sentence) was found to be significantly more compute-efficient than naive rejection sampling (`T=∞`). For example, MRGD with `k=5` achieved better hallucination rates than rejection sampling with `k=30`, making it over 6x more sample-efficient.

6. **Robustness to Reward Model Design Choices:**
 - Ablation studies confirmed MRGD's robustness to variations in reward model components, such as using a different backbone for `r_hal` (PaliGemma-2) or a different object detector for `r_rec` (DETR), and varying the semantic similarity threshold (`τ`). Most ablated variants still significantly outperformed the greedy baseline.

7. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - Finetuning `r_hal` was computationally inexpensive (~9 minutes on 8x NVIDIA H100 GPUs).
 - While increasing `k` increases latency, it is less than linear due to batched generation, making the approach practical for various inference budgets.

In essence, the findings highlight MRGD as a powerful, flexible, and robust method for enhancing MLLM control, particularly in mitigating hallucinations and managing the precision-recall-compute trade-offs, surpassing many existing state-of-the-art approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Multimodal Reward-Guided Decoding (MRGD) represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, particularly for Large Language Models. Its potential impact spans across several dimensions:

1. **Pioneering Inference-Time Controllability for MLLMs:** MRGD is the first method to successfully implement reward-guided decoding for MLLMs. This is a crucial breakthrough, as it addresses a fundamental limitation of current MLLMs, which largely lack dynamic, fine-grained control over their outputs during inference. This capability has been a long-standing desire for LLMs and its extension to multimodal models opens up new avenues for research and application.

2. **Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness:** By providing direct control over object precision (hallucination reduction) and object recall, MRGD significantly improves the factual accuracy and comprehensiveness of MLLM-generated content. Reducing hallucinations is paramount for building trustworthy AI systems, especially in applications where factual correctness is critical (e.g., medical imaging analysis, autonomous driving descriptions, accessibility tools for visually impaired users). The ability to dynamically prioritize precision over recall or vice-versa allows MLLMs to be tailored for safety-critical tasks that cannot tolerate errors, or creative tasks that might benefit from more imaginative, albeit less precise, outputs.

3. **Adaptive and User-Centric MLLM Deployment:** The on-the-fly controllability offered by MRGD allows MLLMs to adapt dynamically to diverse user needs and resource constraints. A user requiring immediate, concise, and highly accurate information (e.g., on a smartphone with limited compute) can prioritize precision and lower compute, while another user generating synthetic data for model training might prioritize comprehensive recall, even with higher compute. This flexibility makes MLLMs much more versatile and practical for real-world integration.

4. **Improved Efficiency and Resource Management:** The method introduces a novel way to manage the trade-off between visual grounding quality and test-time computational cost. By showing that MRGD can achieve better results with fewer samples compared to naive rejection sampling, it offers a path towards more compute-efficient high-quality generation, which is crucial for scaling MLLM deployment.

5. **Generalizability and Modularity:** The reward models can be readily applied to various base MLLMs without retraining, as demonstrated with LLaVA-1.5, Llama-3.2-Vision, and SmolVLM-2. This modularity reduces the overhead of adapting new MLLMs and allows for widespread adoption of the control mechanisms. The robustness of MRGD to variations in reward model design further strengthens its practical utility.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The work opens up several promising directions for future research, including:
 - Extending reward models to mitigate other types of visual hallucinations beyond objects (e.g., attributes, counts, spatial relationships, negation).
 - Developing reward models for semantically incomplete outputs and discriminative hallucination tasks (like POPE).
 - Exploring gradient-based optimization techniques as an alternative to search-based decoding for potentially even greater efficiency and control.
 - Investigating the synergy between reward-guided decoding and other alignment techniques like RLHF.

In conclusion, MRGD provides a powerful new paradigm for steering the behavior of MLLMs. By enabling dynamic, fine-grained control over crucial output characteristics and resource utilization, it promises to make MLLMs more reliable, adaptable, and ultimately, more useful tools in a wide array of applications. This work brings MLLMs a step closer to being truly intelligent and controllable agents that can respond precisely to human intent and context.
