# SeqTR: A Simple yet Universal Network for Visual Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> SeqTR introduces a universal Transformer-based network that reformulates visual grounding tasks like phrase localization and referring expression segmentation into a unified auto-regressive point prediction problem. The model achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on benchmark datasets while utilizing a simpler architecture and a single cross-entropy loss.

## Problem

- Existing visual grounding methods often rely on complex, specialized network architectures and intricate multi-modal fusion mechanisms.
- Previous approaches require hand-crafted, task-specific loss functions (e.g., for bounding boxes vs. segmentation) and complicated tuning of loss coefficients in multi-task settings.
- Many models suffer from limited generalization, requiring significant re-engineering to adapt to different visual grounding tasks (REC vs. RES) or new datasets due to task-specific output branches.

## Method

- ==SeqTR== reformulates both bounding box and binary mask outputs as ==sequences of discrete coordinate tokens==, unifying visual grounding tasks into an ==auto-regressive point prediction problem==.
- A standard Transformer encoder-decoder architecture processes multi-modal features and auto-regressively generates the coordinate sequences.
- The model is trained with a single, simple ==cross-entropy loss== across all visual grounding tasks, eliminating the need for complex, task-specific loss functions and coefficient tuning.

## Results

- SeqTR achieves new state-of-the-art performance on various RefCOCO datasets for Referring Expression Comprehension (REC), reaching **8**5.00%** Precision@0.5** on RefCOCO testA without pre-training.
- For Referring Expression Segmentation (RES), SeqTR obtains results on par with state-of-the-art models, reaching **6**9.79%** mIoU** on RefCOCO testA despite using a novel contour-based segmentation approach.
- The model is compact with **7.9M parameters** and achieves real-time inference speed (**50ms** on GTX 1080 Ti), demonstrating efficiency comparable to many one-stage models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mask contour sampling, especially uniform sampling of a small number of points along the contour, effectively serializes binary masks for sequential prediction.
> - Randomly shuffling the order of sampled points during training for masks enhances robustness by forcing the model to learn context-independent coordinate prediction.
> - Surprisingly, multi-task training for REC and RES does not significantly improve performance, suggesting SeqTR's mask generation is robust independently.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual grounding is a pivotal problem at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing. It demands both a deep understanding of visual content (intra-modality understanding) and precise alignment between visual elements and linguistic descriptions (inter-modality correspondence). Traditionally, visual grounding tasks are categorized into two main groups:
1. **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) / Phrase Localization:** Identifying a bounding box corresponding to a given natural language query.
2. **Referring Expression Segmentation (RES):** Generating a precise pixel-level binary mask for the described object.

Prior approaches to visual grounding have typically suffered from several limitations:
- **Architectural Complexity:** Many methods require highly specialized network architectures and intricate multi-modal fusion mechanisms (e.g., decomposing language into subject/location/relationship phrases with corresponding attention modules, or complex language-guided reasoning units).
- **Loss Function Customization:** Loss functions are often hand-crafted and tailored to individual tasks (e.g., GIoU loss for bounding boxes, Dice loss for segmentation, Focal loss, etc.). Multi-task settings further complicate this with the need for careful tuning of loss coefficients.
- **Limited Generalization:** The highly customized nature of these models makes them difficult to generalize across different visual grounding tasks or new datasets without significant re-engineering.
- **Task-Specific Branches/Heads:** Even recent Transformer-based approaches, while simplifying multi-modal fusion, often retain task-specific output branches or heads (e.g., a bounding box regressor for REC and a convolutional mask decoder for RES).

SeqTR directly addresses these limitations. It builds upon the recent success of Transformers in vision-language tasks, but critically pushes the simplification further than previous works like TransVG or MDETR. Inspired by Pix2Seq, which casts object detection as a sequence prediction problem, SeqTR extends this paradigm to the more challenging and multi-faceted domain of visual grounding, specifically unifying both bounding box prediction and pixel-wise segmentation. By representing *both* bounding boxes and binary masks as sequences of discrete coordinate tokens, SeqTR aims to provide a truly universal framework that bypasses the need for task-specific heads and complex loss engineering, simplifying the entire visual grounding pipeline. This unification represents a significant paradigm shift, moving towards a more generalized and less fragmented approach to multi-modal understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a **simple yet universal network, termed SeqTR, for various visual grounding tasks.** The authors explicitly motivate this goal by highlighting the inherent complexities and lack of generalization in existing visual grounding paradigms.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Simplify Network Architectures:** Current visual grounding models often demand substantial expertise in designing complex, task-specific network components and multi-modal fusion modules. SeqTR aims to replace this complexity with a unified, standard Transformer encoder-decoder architecture.
- **Unify Loss Functions:** Existing approaches rely on a variety of hand-crafted loss functions, each tailored to a specific grounding task (e.g., REC or RES). Furthermore, combining these losses in multi-task settings requires careful tuning of coefficients. SeqTR seeks to use a single, simple cross-entropy loss for all tasks, significantly reducing optimization complexity.
- **Enhance Generalization Ability:** By simplifying both the architecture and the optimization objective, SeqTR is designed to be highly generalizable across different visual grounding tasks (phrase localization, REC, and RES) without requiring task-specific branches or heads. This is a crucial step towards a more unified and flexible visual grounding system.
- **Reformulate Visual Grounding as Point Prediction:** The core conceptual objective is to reframe visual grounding as an auto-regressive "point prediction problem." This means that the output (a bounding box or a binary mask) is no longer a direct regression or a pixel-wise classification, but rather a generated sequence of discrete coordinate tokens. This novel reformulation is what enables the architectural and optimization simplifications.
- **Extend Sequential Modeling to Referring Expression Segmentation (RES):** While sequential modeling has been applied to object detection (e.g., Pix2Seq), extending it effectively to the pixel-level precision required by RES is a non-trivial challenge. A specific objective is to devise an innovative method (mask contour sampling) to serialize binary masks into coordinate sequences, seamlessly integrating RES into the SeqTR framework.

In essence, the authors are motivated to prove that a much simpler, universal approach to visual grounding is not only feasible but can also achieve competitive, if not superior, performance compared to highly specialized and complex state-of-the-art models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SeqTR's methodology revolves around reformulating visual grounding as a point prediction problem solved by a standard Transformer architecture.

#### 3.1. Problem Reformulation and Output Representation

The central innovation is to represent both bounding boxes and binary masks as sequences of discrete coordinate tokens.
- **Bounding Boxes:** Naturally represented by two corner points, forming a sequence of four coordinates (x1, y1, x2, y2).
- **Binary Masks:** This is the more challenging aspect. Since masks consist of infinite points, SeqTR proposes an **innovative mask contour sampling scheme**. Instead of predicting every pixel, the model samples a fixed number (N) of points clockwise along the mask contour. These points then form the sequence of coordinates for segmentation.
- **Quantization:** All continuous floating-point coordinates (normalized by image width/height) are quantized into integer bins (e.g., 1000 bins), transforming them into discrete tokens. A shared embedding vocabulary is used for both x and y coordinates.
- **Sequence Construction:** An initial `[TASK]` token (learnable embedding) indicates the specific grounding task (REC or RES). For REC, the sequence is `[TASK], x1, y1, x2, y2`. For RES, it's `[TASK]` followed by the `N` sampled contour points. A final `[EOS]` token signals the end of the sequence for auto-regressive generation.
- **Point Ordering:** While deterministic clockwise sampling is used, the authors introduce **random shuffling of points' order** during training for binary masks. This forces the model to learn to predict the *next* most relevant point, rather than relying on a fixed spatial ordering, making the model more robust.

#### 3.2. Network Architecture (SeqTR)

SeqTR employs a compact, standard Transformer encoder-decoder architecture.

- **Language Encoder:** A single-layer bidirectional GRU is used to encode the textual query. The final word features are max-pooled to form a compact language feature vector. The choice of a simple GRU (instead of pre-trained BERT) is deliberate to demonstrate the efficacy of the overall SeqTR framework rather than relying on heavy pre-training.
- **Visual Encoder:** DarkNet-53 is used as the backbone. Unidirectionally down-sampled, coarsest-scale visual features are used, reducing memory footprint compared to pixel-wise prediction models.
- **Fusion Module:** A simple yet effective fusion is performed by applying a Hadamard product (element-wise multiplication) between the visual features and the language feature, after passing both through a tanh activation function. This avoids the quadratic complexity often incurred when concatenating and processing multimodal features with a Transformer encoder. The resulting multi-modal features are then fed to the Transformer encoder.
- **Transformer Encoder:** Standard Transformer encoder layers update the fused multi-modal feature representations.
- **Transformer Decoder:** The decoder operates in an auto-regressive manner, predicting the discrete coordinate tokens one by one. A causal mask is applied to attention weights to ensure it only attends to previously predicted tokens. Both the multi-modal features from the encoder and the input sequence (previous coordinate tokens) are augmented with positional encodings.
- **Predictor:** An MLP with a final softmax layer outputs probabilities over the vocabulary of discrete coordinate bins for each token.

#### 3.3. Optimization

The model is trained with a single, simple **cross-entropy loss** for all visual grounding tasks. This is a significant departure from previous methods that rely on complex, task-specific losses (e.g., GIoU, Dice, Focal loss) and require careful balancing of loss coefficients in multi-task settings. A per-token weight ($w_i$) is used to scale the loss, with ablation studies showing that penalizing the first predicted token more heavily can improve REC performance.

#### 3.4. Inference

During inference, coordinate tokens are predicted auto-regressively using `argmax` over the predicted probabilities. These discrete indices are then inversely mapped back to continuous coordinates in the original image scale. For REC, exactly 4 tokens are predicted. For RES, prediction continues until an `[EOS]` token is generated. The assembled sequence of points forms the final bounding box or binary mask.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SeqTR demonstrates remarkable performance across a range of visual grounding tasks and datasets, achieving competitive or state-of-the-art results while maintaining a significantly simpler architecture and optimization strategy.

#### 4.1. Performance on Benchmark Datasets

- **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) & Phrase Localization:**
 - **RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg:** SeqTR achieves new state-of-the-art performance on these key datasets. For instance, on RefCOCO testA, SeqTR (without pre-training) reaches 85.00% Precision@0.5, surpassing previous SOTA models like TransVG (82.72%) and TRAR (81.40%). Similar improvements are observed on RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg.
 - **ReferItGame & Flickr30K Entities:** On datasets with shorter noun phrases, SeqTR performs very competitively, achieving 69.66% on ReferItGame test and 81.23% on Flickr30K Entities test, comparable to or outperforming existing one-stage methods and recent Transformer-based models.
- **Referring Expression Segmentation (RES):**
 - **RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg:** SeqTR achieves results on par with the current state-of-the-art model VLT, despite VLT using more complex query generation and aggregation. For example, on RefCOCO testA, SeqTR achieves 69.79% mIoU, very close to VLT's 68.29%. This is particularly notable given SeqTR's novel contour-based segmentation approach which doesn't rely on traditional convolutional mask decoders.
- **Performance with Large-scale Pre-training:** When pre-trained on a larger corpus of text-image pairs (approx. 174k images), SeqTR exhibits significant performance boosts, achieving even higher SOTA results. For example, on RefCOCO testA REC, it reaches 90.15%, outperforming MDETR (89.58%). For RES on RefCOCO testA, it reaches 73.31% mIoU, a substantial improvement. This demonstrates its scalability and data efficiency, performing better than larger pre-trained models like MDETR while using fewer parameters (7.9M vs. 17.36M) and less pre-training data.

#### 4.2. Efficiency

- **Inference Speed:** Despite its auto-gressive nature, SeqTR achieves real-time inference speed (50ms on GTX 1080 Ti), comparable to many one-stage models.
- **Model Compactness:** The Transformer architecture is very compact (7.9M parameters), making it efficient in terms of memory and computational resources.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Key Insights

- **Language Feature Construction:** Max pooling of word features performs best for constructing the global language feature, indicating its effectiveness in capturing salient linguistic information.
- **Token Weighting:** Increasing the weight of the *first* predicted discrete coordinate token (e.g., to 1.5) improves REC performance, suggesting that accurately predicting the initial part of the bounding box is crucial for overall accuracy.
- **Mask Contour Sampling (for RES):**
 - **Uniform Sampling vs. Center-based Sampling:** Uniform sampling consistently outperforms center-based sampling in terms of both the theoretical upper bound (higher mIoU when sampling ground-truth) and actual performance. Uniform sampling better preserves the irregular contours of objects.
 - **Number of Sampled Points:** Optimal performance is achieved with a relatively small number of points (e.g., 18 for RefCOCO, 12 for RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg), demonstrating efficiency without significant loss of detail.
- **Point Shuffling (for RES):** Randomly shuffling the order of sampled points during training (with a low shuffling percentage) is beneficial, as it forces the model to learn context-independent prediction of coordinates rather than relying on a fixed order, making the sequence generation more robust.
- **Multi-task Training:** Surprisingly, multi-task training (jointly training for REC and RES) does *not* significantly improve performance compared to single-task training for SeqTR. This contradicts findings from previous works that suggested REC could aid RES. This indicates that SeqTR's ability to localize at the pixel level (RES) is largely independent of its bounding box prediction (REC) within this unified framework.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

- **Cross-Attention Maps:** Visualizations of cross-attention maps in the Transformer decoder reveal an intuitive "axial attention" pattern. When predicting an x-coordinate, the model attends more to the corresponding horizontal region (e.g., left side for x1). Similarly, for y-coordinates, it attends to vertical regions. This indicates SeqTR's ability to precisely focus on relevant image regions for sequential coordinate generation.
- **Mask Predictions:** SeqTR generates accurate and fine-grained binary masks, successfully comprehending complex language queries involving attributes, relative spatial relations, and irregular object shapes. Error analysis shows the model might sometimes shift to similar objects not referenced or only ground to the largest segment of a multi-segment mask.

Overall, SeqTR successfully validates its core hypothesis: a simple, universal approach to visual grounding is indeed feasible and highly effective, setting new benchmarks across diverse tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SeqTR represents a significant advancement in the field of visual grounding and, more broadly, in multi-modal vision-language understanding. Its impact stems from several key aspects:

- **Paradigm Shift Towards Unification and Simplicity:** By reformulating various visual grounding tasks as a single auto-regressive point prediction problem and solving it with a standard Transformer and a universal cross-entropy loss, SeqTR drastically simplifies the development and deployment of visual grounding models. This move away from highly customized architectures and complex loss functions is a crucial step towards more generalized AI systems. It demonstrates that complexity in design is not always synonymous with superior performance.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Flexibility:** The universal nature of SeqTR means that a single model can handle phrase localization, referring expression comprehension, and referring expression segmentation without architectural modifications. This flexibility is invaluable for real-world applications where different levels of grounding granularity might be required from a single system.
- **Inspiration for Other Dense Prediction Tasks:** The innovative mask contour sampling scheme for serializing binary masks into discrete coordinate sequences is a novel contribution. This approach could inspire similar serialization techniques for other dense prediction tasks (e.g., instance segmentation, keypoint detection) where outputs are traditionally represented as masks or heatmaps, potentially simplifying their modeling and unifying them under a sequence prediction framework.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** Achieving state-of-the-art or competitive performance with a more compact model and less pre-training data than some larger pre-trained models (e.g., MDETR) highlights SeqTR's efficiency. This has practical implications for research and deployment, allowing for faster experimentation and potentially lower computational costs. The model's real-time inference speed further enhances its applicability.
- **Challenging Prior Assumptions:** The ablation study's finding that multi-task training does not necessarily improve RES performance (unlike previous works suggesting REC's aid to RES localization) is a notable insight. This suggests that SeqTR's independent, direct mask generation capability via sequential point prediction is robust enough that it doesn't heavily rely on explicit multi-task regularization, potentially opening new avenues for research into task independence in multi-modal learning.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** SeqTR lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions:
 - Exploring even more efficient sequential representations for complex visual outputs.
 - Integrating this paradigm into larger foundation models for broader vision-language understanding.
 - Investigating the limitations of sequential prediction, such as handling highly complex or disconnected object shapes, and ways to overcome them.

In conclusion, SeqTR is a testament to the power of simplification in deep learning. It pushes the boundaries of unified modeling for vision-language tasks, offering a robust, efficient, and highly generalizable solution that could significantly influence the future direction of multi-modal AI research.
