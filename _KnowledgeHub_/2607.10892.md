# A Single Diffusion-Policy Controller for Multi-Task Block Pushing with Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harvard University and Georgia Institute of Technology developed a framework for training a single diffusion policy for multi-task block pushing using online reinforcement learning from scratch. Their Energy-Guided Score Matching (ESM) algorithm achieved a 100% success rate in simulation and demonstrated robust zero-shot transferability by completing all 36 real-world tasks under varying environmental conditions.

## Problem

- Existing behavior cloning (BC) based diffusion policies for multi-task manipulation require substantial, diverse expert demonstrations, limiting scalability.
- Integrating online reinforcement learning (RL) with diffusion policies for multi-task scenarios without expert demonstrations, and demonstrating reliable sim-to-real transfer, remains challenging.
- Training complex policies in sparse-reward, multi-task environments often presents significant exploration challenges.

## Method

- Introduces **Energy-Guided Score Matching (ESM)**, a simplified ==diffusion policy== optimization method for online RL, allowing training from ==sparse rewards== without expert demonstrations.
- Utilizes an ==objective-centric state representation== (relative coordinates, shape encoding) and actions defined in the goal frame to enhance generalization and data efficiency for multi-task block pushing.
- Employs a ==reverse curriculum generation== strategy to systematically increase task difficulty and address exploration issues in sparse-reward multi-task settings.

## Results

- The ESM algorithm achieved a **100%** success rate and an average episode length of **21.1 ± 5.6 steps** in multi-shape block pushing simulations, outperforming other RL and BC-based diffusion policy baselines.
- ESM demonstrated strong generalization by achieving **100%** success on trained block shapes and **61%** on an unseen I-shaped block in simulation without additional training.
- The trained diffusion policy successfully completed all **36** real-world zero-shot transfer tasks, adapting to variations in surface friction, block weight, and goal poses without real-world fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion policies can be effectively trained from scratch with online reinforcement learning for complex multi-task manipulation, reducing reliance on extensive expert demonstrations.
> - Objective-centric state representations and goal-frame actions are crucial for improving policy generalization across varying task parameters and enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
> - Curriculum learning is vital for efficient exploration and successful policy training in sparse-reward, multi-task environments when combining online RL with expressive diffusion policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation often seeks to develop policies capable of performing complex tasks in diverse environments. Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs), or diffusion policies, have demonstrated expressiveness in representing and generating intricate data distributions. In robotics, these models have been leveraged for tasks such as generating long-horizon robot trajectories and imitating expert policies, primarily using behavior cloning (BC) or offline reinforcement learning (RL) when expert demonstrations are readily available.

However, existing BC-based approaches face limitations, particularly in multi-task manipulation scenarios, due to the substantial need for diverse expert demonstrations across various tasks. This requirement constrains scalability. Furthermore, when RL is used for fine-tuning BC-trained policies, it often encounters issues like fragility and catastrophic forgetting, which hinder adaptation to new tasks without compromising previously learned skills.

A developing area of interest involves training diffusion policies from scratch using online RL methods. While online RL has shown potential in multi-task learning for domains like arcade games and robotic locomotion, its application to multi-task diffusion policies has largely been confined to BC-based training. Few studies have investigated the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of diffusion policies, especially when trained with online RL.

This paper addresses these gaps by proposing a comprehensive pipeline to train a single diffusion policy for multi-task, contact-rich manipulation tasks using an online RL framework, without relying on expert demonstrations. It specifically aims to evaluate the zero-shot sim-to-real transfer performance of such policies, contributing to the broader goal of developing general-purpose robotic intelligence that can operate reliably in real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a framework for training a single diffusion policy to perform multi-task robotic manipulation from scratch using online reinforcement learning. This goal is pursued without the use of expert demonstrations, which differentiates it from many existing diffusion policy applications.

The specific motivations and objectives that drive this work include:

- **Overcoming Reliance on Expert Demonstrations:** A central motivation is to move beyond behavior cloning (BC) approaches for diffusion policies, which necessitate abundant and diverse expert demonstrations. The paper aims to enable diffusion policies to learn complex behaviors directly from sparse rewards through online RL, thus enhancing scalability for multi-task learning where obtaining comprehensive demonstrations is challenging.
- **Integrating Diffusion Policies into Online RL:** The authors seek to seamlessly incorporate the expressive power of diffusion policies as the policy learning module within standard RL algorithms. Current RL applications with diffusion policies often treat RL as an optional fine-tuning step, which can be unstable. The objective is to design a robust policy loss function that facilitates direct online RL training.
- **Addressing Exploration Challenges in Sparse-Reward Multi-Task Settings:** Training complex policies in environments with sparse rewards and multiple tasks typically presents significant exploration challenges. The research aims to integrate techniques like curriculum learning and objective-centric state representations to mitigate these difficulties and enable efficient learning.
- **Developing Generalizable Policies for Multi-Task Manipulation:** The ultimate goal of autonomous robotics is to learn universal policies capable of solving a variety of tasks. This paper focuses on multi-task block-pushing, involving blocks of varying shapes and weights, as a representative contact-rich manipulation task. The objective is to train a single policy that can adapt to these variations.
- **Investigating Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A critical challenge in deploying RL policies in the real world is the sim-to-real gap. The paper aims to evaluate the ability of the trained diffusion policy to transfer directly, without any real-world fine-tuning (zero-shot), to physical robotic tasks under varying environmental conditions such as goal positions, block shapes, block weights, and surface friction. This investigation seeks to provide evidence regarding the robustness and adaptability of diffusion policies in real-world scenarios.
- **Leveraging Diffusion Policies' Expressiveness:** The inherent flexibility and expressiveness of diffusion models are posited as crucial for capturing the variability present in contact-rich, multi-modal manipulation tasks, where the planning landscape is sensitive to task objectives and disturbances. The research is motivated by exploring how this expressiveness translates to improved learning and generalization in an online RL context.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates diffusion policies with online reinforcement learning for multi-task block-pushing, designed for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Simplified Loss Function for Diffusion Policies (Energy-Guided Score Matching - ESM)**
The core of the RL policy learning module is a novel, simplified diffusion policy optimization method termed Energy-Guided Score Matching (ESM). It is derived from policy mirror descent, which typically uses a KL-divergence regularization term. The optimal solution for policy mirror descent involves an intractable normalization constant, making direct fitting challenging for parameterized policy families like Gaussian policies.

The ESM loss function reweights the evidence lower bound (ELBO), a standard objective in Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs). Specifically, the loss is formulated as:
$L_{ESM} = E_{s \sim D, t \in \{1...,T\}, a \sim \pi_{old}, \epsilon \sim N(0,I)} [ \exp (Q_{\pi_{old}}(s, a) / \lambda) \cdot \| \epsilon_\theta (a_t; s, t) - \epsilon \|^2 ]$
where $a_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} a_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon$ is the noisy action, $s$ is the state, $t$ is the diffusion timestep, $\epsilon_\theta$ is the noise prediction model, and $Q_{\pi_{old}}(s,a)$ is the action-value function of the current policy $\pi_{old}$. The term $\exp (Q_{\pi_{old}}(s, a) / \lambda)$ acts as an importance sampling ratio, guiding the diffusion model towards actions with higher Q-values. Proposition 1 in the paper states that this ESM loss is equivalent to training a diffusion policy to fit the target policy derived from policy mirror descent. This design aims to address the intractability of the normalization constant and enable direct integration into RL algorithms.

Compared to existing diffusion-based RL methods, ESM avoids learning a separate baseline function for weights (unlike IDQL and QIPO), which can be biased and computationally expensive. It also handles negative Q-function values effectively, a limitation noted in QVPO.

**B. Objective-Centric State Representation for Observations and Actions**
To reduce task complexity and improve generalization for multi-task block-pushing, the observation and action spaces are carefully designed:

- **Implicit Goal Conditioning:** Instead of conditioning on absolute goal positions, which can limit generalization to simple spatial transformations, the state vector (`s_state`) is constructed using an "objective-centric" representation. This includes the agent's position, the goal's position, and the goal's orientation, all observed *from the block's current frame of reference*. This relative coordinate system implicitly embeds task information and aims to enhance generalization to arbitrary goal poses and improve data efficiency.
- **Shape Encoding:** To enable multi-task learning across different block shapes, a 4-dimensional shape-encoding vector (`s_shape`) is included in the observation. This simple encoding serves as a proof-of-concept for differentiating tasks based on object geometry.
- **Action Space:** The action is defined as a 2D velocity command (`G_v_A`) observed in the *goal frame*. This is then transformed to the world frame for execution. This framing aims to make the actions more intuitive and generalizable with respect to the task objective.

**C. Curriculum Learning via Reverse Curriculum Generation**
To address exploration challenges in a sparse-reward setting, a reverse curriculum generation strategy is employed. The curriculum gradually expands the difficulty of the task by increasing the distance between the initial block position and the goal position. The learning process is divided into multiple stages, and the curriculum progresses to a more difficult stage once the policy successfully completes the task a predefined number of times.

**D. Real-World Implementation**

- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer Benefits:** The state-based inputs and objective-centric design are intended to facilitate sim-to-real transfer. By using relative coordinates, policies trained with a fixed goal pose in simulation can generalize to varying real-world goal positions. The authors contend that a powerful policy might overcome "non-significant" sim-to-real gaps, such as friction variations in 2D pushing.
- **Block Pose Estimation:** Since the policy requires state-based inputs (block pose, agent pose, goal pose), accurate real-time pose estimation of the blocks is critical.
 - **ArUco Markers:** Used as the primary method, affixed to blocks for robust pose estimation.
 - **Point Cloud Registration:** An alternative vision-based method is developed for marker-less operation. It leverages the regular shapes of the blocks by projecting them onto the table surface before performing 2D point cloud registration.
 - **Additional Filters and Simulators:** A Kalman filter is applied to pose estimation results to handle observation noise. In cases of significant occlusion by the robot arm, a parallel simulator runs as a backup, and simulated pose estimations are used when vision-based methods are unreliable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation comprises extensive simulations and real-world zero-shot transfer tests.

**A. Simulation Results**

1. **Simulation Performance and Baselines:**
 - The proposed ESM algorithm was compared against classic model-free RL algorithms (PPO, TD3, SAC), recent diffusion-based RL algorithms (QVPO, QSM, DACER), and a Behavior Cloning (BC) based Diffusion Policy.
 - **Performance:** ESM achieved a 100% success rate and an average episode length of 21.1 ± 5.6 steps in the multi-shape pushing task. Among the baselines, only SAC showed partial success (66.3 ± 12.2% success rate, 118.9 ± 130.0 steps), while TD3, PPO, QVPO, DACER, and QSM recorded 0% success. The BC-based Diffusion Policy, while not 0%, achieved only 35% success with 179.54 ± 34.12 steps.
 - **Conclusion:** ESM significantly outperformed all baselines in simulation, demonstrating superior learning capability for multi-task block-pushing.

2. **Multi-shape Pushing and Generalization:**
 - ESM and SAC (the best baseline) were evaluated on individual trained shapes (T-block, C-block, L-block) and an *unseen I-shaped block* (zero-shot).
 - **Performance:** ESM achieved 100% success on all three trained shapes (T, C, L) and a 61% success rate on the unseen I-block without any additional training. SAC, in contrast, showed highly variable performance, failing completely on the T-block and the unseen I-block, though achieving 100% and 99% on C-block and L-block, respectively.
 - **Conclusion:** ESM demonstrated robust multi-task capabilities and strong generalization to novel block shapes.

3. **Ablation Studies:**
 - Ablations were conducted by removing curriculum learning and objective-centric state representations individually, comparing ESM against SAC.
 - **Impact of Techniques:** Both curriculum learning and objective-centric state representations were found to be vital for improving data efficiency and performance for ESM. Removing either technique reduced ESM's success rate (e.g., "ESM No curriculum" and "ESM No spatial" still achieved around 0.8-0.9 success rate after 4e6 samples), but ESM still outperformed SAC, which largely failed under these ablated conditions.
 - **Conclusion:** The design choices regarding curriculum learning and objective-centric state representations are crucial for the proposed method's performance.

**B. Real-World Experiments**

- **Setup:** Experiments used a UR5e manipulator, Intel RealSense D435 camera for block localization (via ArUco markers or point cloud registration), and various block shapes.
- **Evaluation Tasks:** The learned diffusion policy was tested across 36 real-world tasks under diverse conditions:
 - **Friction:** High (floor mats) and low (parchment paper) friction surfaces.
 - **Block Weight:** Standard (4 cm thick) and lighter (1 cm thick) T-blocks.
 - **Goal Poses:** Two random goal positions different from training.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer Performance:** The learned diffusion policy (ESM) successfully solved *all* 36 real-world tasks. This indicates robust zero-shot transfer capabilities and adaptability to variations in real-world environmental conditions.
- **Comparison with SAC in Real-World:** For a subset of 9 training tasks, ESM significantly outperformed SAC. For T-block pushing, ESM achieved 3/3 successes, while SAC failed in all 3 trials. For C-block and L-block, where SAC succeeded, ESM demonstrated better efficiency with lower total distance traveled and fewer steps to achieve the goal.
- **Empirical Observations:** While both ESM and SAC learned multiple skills (pushing, rotating) in simulation, real-world deployment revealed that agents tended to fall back to specific movement sequences, possibly due to real-world complexities or sim-to-real gaps. This observation highlights an area for further investigation regarding the multi-modality of diffusion policies in real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that demonstrates the viability of training a single diffusion policy for complex, multi-task, contact-rich robotic manipulation directly through online reinforcement learning, without the need for expert demonstrations. This represents a notable advancement in the field, as many prior applications of diffusion policies in robotics have relied on behavior cloning or offline RL from large demonstration datasets.

The key contributions and their potential impact include:

- **Advancement in Online Reinforcement Learning for Diffusion Policies:** By proposing a simplified, energy-guided score matching (ESM) loss function, the paper addresses challenges associated with integrating diffusion policies into online RL. This enables the policies to learn from scratch with sparse rewards, expanding the applicability of expressive diffusion models to a broader range of robotic tasks where demonstrations are scarce or difficult to obtain.
- **Enhanced Multi-Task Learning Capabilities:** The successful training of a single diffusion policy to handle block-pushing tasks with multiple shapes, weights, and goal positions using techniques like objective-centric state representations and curriculum learning, marks a step towards more general-purpose robotic intelligence. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured environments where they must adapt to variations.
- **Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The empirical validation of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for all 36 real-world tasks, encompassing variations in friction, block weight, and goal poses, is a significant finding. This suggests that the expressive power of diffusion policies, combined with careful observation and action space design, can contribute to bridging the simulation-to-reality gap, reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection and fine-tuning. This has direct implications for rapid deployment of learned skills.
- **Methodological Contributions to RL Efficiency:** The inclusion and empirical validation of techniques like objective-centric state representations and reverse curriculum generation highlight their importance in making online RL tractable for challenging, sparse-reward tasks. These methods can be broadly applicable to other goal-conditioned manipulation problems.
- **Implications for Future Research:** The work opens avenues for further exploration into how the multi-modality of diffusion policies translates from simulation to real-world performance, especially when faced with sim-to-real gaps. It suggests that while policies might exhibit diverse behaviors in simulation, real-world constraints can lead to convergence on specific robust strategies. The paper also points to potential future work in integrating domain randomization or residual learning techniques to further enhance sim-to-real transfer for more significant gaps or broader task domains.

In summary, this research contributes to the development of more autonomous, adaptable, and deployable robotic systems by demonstrating a robust pipeline for learning complex, multi-task manipulation skills via online RL with diffusion policies and validating their practical zero-shot transferability to the real world.
