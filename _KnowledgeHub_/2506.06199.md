# 3DFlowAction: Learning Cross-Embodiment Manipulation from 3D Flow World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from South China University of Technology, Tencent Robotics X, and Hong Kong University of Science and Technology develop 3DFlowAction, a cross-embodiment manipulation framework that uses 3D optical flow as a unified action representation, creating ManiFlow-110k dataset from 110,000 human and robot videos through an automated moving object detection pipeline, training a video diffusion world model to predict 3D motion trajectories conditioned on language instructions, and implementing flow-guided optimization policies that achieve 70% success rates across complex tasks like pouring and insertion while enabling seamless transfer between different robot platforms (Franka and XTrainer) without hardware-specific fine-tuning, outperforming 2D flow baselines (25%) and video world models (20%) while demonstrating competitive performance against imitation learning methods despite requiring no robot action labels during training, with closed-loop planning using GPT-4o verification of rendered predicted states providing 20% performance improvements and validating that object-centric 3D flow representations can overcome the data scarcity and generalization limitations that plague current robotic manipulation systems.

## Problem

- Current robotic manipulation systems suffer from data scarcity requiring expensive robot-specific teleoperation data and struggle with generalization across different robot embodiments due to varying action spaces (joint angles, end-effector poses)
- Existing approaches like 2D optical flow and video world models fail to capture complex 3D object motions (rotations, depth changes) and are often non-object-centric, modeling irrelevant background content that hinders generalization

## Method

- Develop automated moving object detection pipeline to synthesize ManiFlow-110k dataset from 110,000 human and robot manipulation videos, extracting clean 3D optical flow using depth projection and object tracking
- Train video diffusion world model (adapting AnimateDiff) to generate 3D motion trajectories conditioned on language instructions and scene observations, with direct 3D flow input bypassing problematic latent space compression

## Results

- Achieved 70% success rate across complex manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming 2D flow methods (25%) and video world models (20%) while demonstrating seamless cross-embodiment transfer between Franka and XTrainer robots
- Closed-loop planning with GPT-4o verification provided 20% performance improvement, and large-scale pretraining contributed 40% improvement, with competitive performance against imitation learning baselines despite using no robot action labels during training

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D optical flow serves as an effective embodiment-agnostic action representation that captures universal object motion patterns independent of specific robot kinematics or hardware configurations
> - Object-centric 3D flow modeling combined with large-scale pretraining enables strong generalization to novel objects, backgrounds, and tasks while requiring minimal task-specific demonstration data

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation remains a grand challenge in AI and robotics, primarily due to the difficulty in acquiring large, unified datasets and developing action representations that generalize across diverse robots and scenes. This paper addresses these fundamental limitations by proposing a novel, embodiment-agnostic approach grounded in 3D optical flow.

The existing research landscape can be broadly categorized into:

- **Imitation Learning (IL) for Manipulation:** This paradigm trains robots by observing human or expert robot demonstrations. While effective, IL often relies on vast amounts of robot-specific operational data, which is expensive and time-consuming to collect. Furthermore, policies are often 2D image-based, limiting their ability to grasp 3D spatial relationships, and even advanced Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) struggle with broad generalization due to dataset limitations. 3DFlowAction seeks to overcome the data scarcity by leveraging diverse human and robot videos and eliminating the need for robot-specific action labels during data collection.
- **Video World Models for Manipulation:** Recent advancements in video generation have led to models that predict future scene states to guide robot actions. However, these models face several challenges:
 - **Non-Object-Centricity:** They model the entire scene, including irrelevant background and the robot arm itself, leading to poor generalization.
 - **2D Limitations:** Planning is primarily confined to 2D planes, making accurate representation and execution of complex 3D object movements (e.g., rotation, depth changes) difficult.
 - **Computational Demands:** High-resolution video generation is computationally prohibitive, while low-resolution outputs can introduce inconsistencies.
 3DFlowAction directly tackles these by focusing on object-centric 3D flow, making it more efficient and robust.
- **Optical Flow for Manipulation:** Early work recognized optical flow's potential as a universal descriptor for motion. However, most prior research has focused on 2D optical flow, which, similar to 2D video models, fails to fully capture 3D object motion like rotation or movement perpendicular to the camera. Moreover, these methods were often trained on limited datasets, hindering their generalization capabilities. 3DFlowAction elevates this concept by utilizing *3D* optical flow and training on a *large-scale, diverse* dataset, addressing the critical shortcomings of previous flow-based approaches.

In essence, 3DFlowAction positions itself at the intersection of these fields, aiming to leverage the strengths of world models and flow representations while mitigating their weaknesses. By proposing 3D optical flow as a "unified and embodiment-agnostic action representation," it offers a solution that is object-centric, capable of representing full 3D motion, and can learn from diverse sources (both human and robot videos) to generalize across different robot embodiments. This work represents a significant step towards more flexible and generalizable robotic manipulation systems, moving beyond robot-specific data collection and limited 2D representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind 3DFlowAction stems from an observation about human manipulation: humans understand tasks by envisioning how objects should move in 3D space, a perception that is inherently embodiment-agnostic. Current robotic systems, however, lack this unified, robust representation, largely due to the scarcity of large-scale, consistent datasets that capture complex physical and semantic interactions for generalized manipulation learning. Traditional robot datasets suffer from variations in action spaces (joint angles, end-effector poses in different coordinates) and simple scenes, hindering the learning of a universal action representation.

Building on this, the paper defines several key objectives:

1. **To learn a 3D flow world model:** The primary objective is to develop a model that can predict the future movement of interacting objects in 3D space. This model is envisioned as a "motion cue" for action policy, serving as an embodiment-agnostic guide for both humans and diverse robots.
2. **To synthesize a large-scale 3D optical flow dataset:** To train such a world model, the authors recognize the need for extensive data. A specific objective is to create "ManiFlow-110k" by developing a "moving object auto-detect pipeline" capable of extracting clean 3D optical flow from existing human and robot manipulation videos, addressing the challenge of cluttered backgrounds and identical objects in raw video data.
3. **To develop a video diffusion-based world model:** This model will learn manipulation physics from the synthesized 3D flow data, enabling it to generate 3D optical flow trajectories conditioned on natural language instructions. The model aims to be object-centric and robust to irrelevant scene content.
4. **To propose a flow-guided rendering mechanism for closed-loop planning:** To enhance the reliability of flow predictions and enable self-correction, a mechanism is needed to visually assess the predicted final state. This involves rendering the predicted final object state and leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like GPT-4o to verify alignment with task descriptions, thus providing closed-loop planning capabilities.
5. **To formulate an optimization policy guided by 3D optical flow:** The predicted 3D optical flow needs to be translated into executable robot actions. A key objective is to treat this 3D flow as constraints for an optimization policy, which determines a sequence of robot actions without requiring hardware-specific action labels during training. This also includes generating task-relevant and reachable grasp poses.

In summary, the core motivation is to bridge the gap between human-like 3D motion understanding and robotic manipulation capabilities by developing a unified, data-driven framework that leverages 3D optical flow as a versatile action representation, enabling strong generalization and cross-embodiment adaptation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "3DFlowAction" framework is meticulously designed, integrating data synthesis, a 3D flow world model, and a sophisticated flow-guided action planning system.

#### 3.1. Extracting 3D Flow from Raw Video (ManiFlow-110k Dataset)

Recognizing the lack of large-scale, clean 3D flow data, the authors developed a **moving object detection pipeline** to synthesize ManiFlow-110k from existing open-source human and robot videos. This pipeline addresses the challenge of cluttered backgrounds and similar-looking objects.
1. **Gripper Masking:** Uses Grounding-SAM2 to segment the robotic gripper from the first video frame.
2. **Initial Point Generation:** Randomly generates points across the first frame, excluding those within the gripper mask.
3. **Moving Object Identification:** Employs Co-tracker3 (a 2D tracking model) to track these points. Points exhibiting significant movement are identified, and a maximum bounding box around them defines the manipulated object's location.
4. **2D Optical Flow Extraction:** Co-tracker3 is again used to extract 2D optical flow for the identified moving objects. Camera motion is optionally removed using techniques like Magma.
5. **3D Projection:** DepthAnythingV2 performs depth prediction on the video, and the 2D optical flow is then projected into 3D space, yielding the final 3D optical flow.
This process generated 110,000 instances of 3D optical flow data from datasets like BridgeV2, RT1, AgiWorld, and DROID, forming ManiFlow-110k, an object-centric and embodiment-agnostic dataset.

#### 3.2. Learning 3D Flow World Model

The goal is to generate 3D motion trajectories of target objects conditioned on task instructions and scene states.
1. **Flow Generator Architecture:** The model utilizes a video diffusion model, adapting AnimateDiff. The model (denoted as *G*) generates time-varying 3D flow *F* conditioned on the initial RGB observation, a language task prompt, and initial object points (*F₀*).
2. **Input Encoding:** CLIP encoder processes RGB observation and task prompt. Sinusoidal positional encoding is used for initial points.
3. **Direct 3D Flow Input:** Crucially, unlike some prior approaches, the 3D flow is *directly* input into the U-Net without compression into a latent space. This decision was made because image VAEs (e.g., from StableDiffusion) were found to struggle with effectively encoding depth information, even after fine-tuning.
4. **Temporal Dynamics:** AnimateDiff's motion module is incorporated to model temporal dynamics of the 3D flow. The motion module layers are trained from scratch, while only LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) layers are inserted into the pre-trained SD model to preserve its generative capabilities.
5. **Inference:** Initial points *F₀* are obtained via a detector and undergo a corrosion process to prevent interference from points outside the object.

#### 3.3. Flow-Guided Action Planning

This system translates the predicted 3D flow into executable robot actions, incorporating robustness and task-awareness.

#### 3.3.1. Closed-Loop Motion Planning for Manipulated Objects

To ensure accuracy and stability, a self-correcting mechanism is proposed:
1. **Transformation Matrix Estimation:** The predicted 3D optical flow provides 3D positions of an object at each timestep. The transformation matrix *T* between initial (P₁) and final (P₂) object points is estimated using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).
2. **Target State Rendering:** This *T* is applied to the initial point cloud of the manipulated object to obtain its predicted target state. This transformed object point cloud is then integrated into the current 3D scene and reprojected into a 2D image, representing the predicted final scene.
3. **VLM Verification:** The task instructions and the rendered predicted image are fed into GPT-4o. GPT-4o assesses if the predicted flow aligns with the task description. If not, the system re-predicts the 3D optical flow, enabling a closed-loop planning ability.

#### 3.3.2. Task-Aware Grasp Pose Generation

To ensure successful manipulation, a proper and reachable grasp is vital:
1. **Task-Relevant Part Identification:** GPT-4o is prompted to identify the optimal part of the object to grasp based on task instructions.
2. **Candidate Grasp Pose Generation:** AnyGrasp generates a series of candidate grasping poses around the identified object part.
3. **Reachability Check:** The transformation matrix *T* (from 3D flow prediction) is applied to these candidate poses, yielding target gripper poses corresponding to the predicted final object position. Inverse kinematics (IK) of the robot arm is then used to determine if these target poses are reachable. Only task-relevant and reachable grasp poses are selected.

#### 3.3.3. Flow-Based Action Generation

The predicted 3D flow is directly utilized as constraints for robot action generation.
1. **Keypoint Selection:** N key points are selected on the object surface using farthest point sampling, and their corresponding 3D optical flow trajectory is obtained.
2. **Optimization Procedure:** An optimization process minimizes the Euclidean distance in 3D space between the selected initial keypoints and the keypoints corresponding to the predicted flow at each time step.
3. **End-Effector Pose Derivation:** This minimization directly yields a sequence of end-effector poses (in SE(3)) as the final execution actions. The optimization procedure (using SciPy's Dual Annealing and SLSQP) implicitly handles the rigid connection between the gripper and the manipulated object, treating the object's predicted motion as the gripper's desired trajectory. IK and collision detection are incorporated into the objective function. This avoids the complexities and inaccuracies of code-based constraints from VLMs used in prior works.

This comprehensive methodology, from data creation to intelligent action planning, demonstrates a robust and innovative approach to generalizable robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate 3DFlowAction's capabilities across various dimensions, including comparison with existing world models and imitation learning methods, cross-embodiment adaptation, generalization to out-of-domain scenarios, and the impact of key architectural components.

#### 4.1. Comparisons with Manipulation World Models (Table 1)

- **3D vs. 2D Flow:** 3DFlowAction (70% total success rate) significantly outperformed Im2Flow2Act* (25%), a 2D flow world model, across all four complex tasks (Pour tea, Insert pen, Hang cup, Open drawer). This definitively highlights the critical limitation of 2D optical flow in capturing intricate 3D motion trajectories, such as rotations and depth changes.
- **Object-centric 3D Flow vs. Video Models:** 3DFlowAction (70%) also strongly surpassed AVDC (20%), a video world model. The findings suggest that AVDC's low-resolution, non-object-centric video generation leads to inconsistencies and hampers effective action policies. In contrast, 3DFlowAction's object-centric 3D motion trajectories provide precise descriptions, minimizing irrelevant scene interference.
- **3D Flow vs. VLM Code-based Constraints:** 3DFlowAction (70%) outperformed Rekep (20%), a VLM code-based world model. This indicates that 3D optical flow is a more effective and natural representation for complex object motion compared to code-based constraints that primarily describe relationships in terms of distance.

#### 4.2. Cross-Embodiment Experiments (Table 2)

- **Unified Action Feature:** 3DFlowAction demonstrated consistent performance when directly deployed on two distinct robotic platforms (Franka and XTrainer) without any robot-specific fine-tuning. The total success rates were comparable (67.5% on Franka, 70% on XTrainer). This crucial finding strongly supports the claim that 3D optical flow serves as an effective, unified, and embodiment-agnostic action representation, enabling reliable adaptation across different hardware.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Imitation Learning Methods (Table 3)

- **Efficiency of Optimization Policy:** Despite being trained without robot action labels (relying on optimization guided by 3D flow), 3DFlowAction (70%) achieved competitive performance against imitation learning baselines (PI0 at 50%, Im2Flow2Act at 27.5%). Notably, these baselines were fine-tuned using 30 teleoperated demonstrations per task. This highlights that a well-guided optimization policy leveraging comprehensive 3D optical flow can be more efficient and performant than traditional imitation learning methods requiring extensive teleoperation data.

#### 4.4. Experiment on Generalization of Different Objects and Backgrounds (Table 4)

- **Out-of-Domain Robustness:** 3DFlowAction exhibited competitive generalization capabilities to out-of-domain objects (55% total success) and backgrounds (50% total success). In contrast, the video world model (AVDC) struggled significantly (15% object, 0% background), and even the imitation learning method (PI0) showed a decline (40% object, 32.5% background). This success is attributed to 3DFlowAction's object-centric action representation and large-scale pretraining, which makes it less sensitive to irrelevant scene changes.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Table 5)

- **Closed-Loop Planning (Flow-Guided Rendering):** Disabling closed-loop planning (Variant 1) led to an average 20% decrease in task success rate (from 70% to 50%). This confirms the effectiveness of the self-correcting mechanism, where GPT-4o's assessment of rendered predicted states ensures planning accuracy and system stability.
- **Large-Scale Pretraining:** Without large-scale pretraining on ManiFlow-110k (Variant 2), the task success rate drastically decreased by an average of 40% (from 70% to 30%). This demonstrates that pretraining the flow world model on diverse 3D flow data is critical for learning object physical dynamics and achieving strong generalization, enabling the model to learn downstream tasks with minimal human demonstration data (10-30 instances).

Overall, the findings consistently demonstrate that 3DFlowAction, by utilizing 3D optical flow as a unified action representation, excels in generalization across tasks, robot embodiments, and environmental variations, outperforming existing world models and even competitive imitation learning methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "3DFlowAction" carries significant implications for the field of robotic manipulation and broader AI research, offering solutions to long-standing challenges and opening new avenues for future development.

#### 5.1. Novel and Unified Action Representation

The most significant contribution is the proposition and validation of **3D optical flow as a robust, unified, and embodiment-agnostic action representation**. Unlike previous approaches that relied on robot-specific joint angles, end-effector poses, or limited 2D representations, 3D flow intrinsically captures the desired movement of objects in 3D space, which is universal irrespective of the robot's kinematics or end-effector type. This fundamentally changes how manipulation tasks can be learned and executed across diverse hardware.

#### 5.2. Overcoming Data Scarcity and Generalization Limitations

- **Reduced Robot-Specific Data Needs:** By learning from a large-scale, synthesized dataset (ManiFlow-110k) derived from diverse human and robot videos, the method significantly reduces the need for costly and labor-intensive robot teleoperation data. For new tasks, only a small number of human demonstrations are needed for fine-tuning, not robot-specific action labels.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The object-centric nature of 3D flow and the large-scale pretraining enable strong generalization to novel objects, backgrounds, and tasks, a critical capability for deploying robots in unstructured, real-world environments. This moves beyond the narrow domain-specificity that often plagues current robot learning systems.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The demonstrated ability to transfer skills seamlessly between different robotic platforms (Franka and XTrainer) without hardware-specific fine-tuning is a major breakthrough. This implies that a skill learned once could be potentially applied to any robot capable of grasping and manipulating objects, dramatically accelerating robot deployment and reducing development costs.

#### 5.3. Robust and Intelligent Planning

- **Closed-Loop Planning:** The integration of a flow-guided rendering mechanism with a VLM (GPT-4o) for closed-loop planning is a crucial innovation for robustness. This self-correction capability allows the robot to verify its predicted plans against task instructions and environmental feedback, leading to more reliable task completion even in the presence of disturbances or initial inaccuracies.
- **Optimization-Based Action Generation:** Using 3D flow as direct constraints for an optimization policy simplifies the action generation process and bypasses the complexities of learning direct policies from noisy robot action data. This allows for flexible and accurate trajectory generation.

#### 5.4. Potential Impact

- **Accelerated Robotic Deployment:** By simplifying data requirements and enabling cross-embodiment transfer, 3DFlowAction could significantly accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robots in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to service robotics and assistive technology.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes 3D optical flow as a powerful intermediate representation for robot learning, potentially inspiring future research into more sophisticated 3D world models, multi-modal learning for manipulation, and more generalized AI agents.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** Reducing the reliance on expensive robotic hardware for data collection could make advanced robot learning more accessible to a broader research community and smaller organizations.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Work (as noted by authors)

The paper acknowledges limitations, particularly concerning flexible objects, where severe occlusions and non-rigid deformations challenge the accurate modeling of 3D optical flow. This suggests a clear direction for future research, perhaps incorporating specialized models for deformable objects or advanced perception techniques to handle complex occlusions.

In conclusion, 3DFlowAction represents a substantial leap forward in robotic manipulation by proposing a highly generalizable and efficient learning paradigm. Its focus on embodiment-agnostic 3D object motion, coupled with robust planning mechanisms, positions it as a foundational work with the potential to significantly impact the future of intelligent robotic systems.
