# FoldNet: Learning Generalizable Closed-Loop Policy for Garment Folding via Keypoint-Driven Asset and Demonstration Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> FoldNet introduces a framework for generating diverse synthetic garment data with semantic annotations and an iterative learning approach. This enables the training of robust closed-loop policies for garment folding, demonstrating direct sim-to-real transfer with a 25% increase in real-world success rates through improved error recovery.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable objects like garments faces challenges due to their high degrees of freedom and complex dynamics.
- A scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality demonstration data limits the generalizability of learning-based approaches for garment manipulation.
- Current synthetic data generation methods often feature restricted garment assets and lack rich annotations, while policies struggle to recover from out-of-distribution states arising from execution errors.

## Method

- A novel framework synthesizes diverse, high-quality garment meshes with automatic semantic keypoint annotations, utilizing keypoint-based geometry, generative models for textures, and VLM-based filtering.
- Keypoint-Gated DAgger (KG-DAgger), an iterative imitation learning strategy, generates and incorporates error recovery demonstrations by automatically detecting grasp failures and applying keypoint-based recovery in simulation.
- Closed-loop vision-action policies are trained using ==Diffusion Policy== on this synthesized dataset and augmented with error-recovery trajectories, enabling direct transfer from simulation to real-world robots.

## Results

- FoldNet assets facilitated superior keypoint detection on real-world images, achieving an average mAP of **47.2** and AKD of **15.6**, outperforming existing texture generation baselines.
- Policies trained with KG-DAgger achieved the highest success rates, reaching **90%** in simulation and **75%** in real-world deployment on unseen garments, a **25%** improvement over policies trained without error recovery.
- The synthetic dataset also proved effective for fine-tuning large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling a pre-trained π0 model to achieve **70%** real-world success on an unseen robot without any real-world task-specific data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining keypoint-controlled geometry with generative AI for texture synthesis and VLM-based filtering creates highly diverse and realistic synthetic garment assets efficiently.
> - Explicitly training on automatically generated error recovery demonstrations, as with KG-DAgger, substantially enhances policy robustness and allows robots to recover from failures during real-world execution.
> - High-quality synthetic data, especially when augmented with semantic annotations and error recovery examples, can effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap for complex deformable object manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation of deformable objects, particularly garments, presents significant challenges due to their high degrees of freedom and complex dynamics. Traditional approaches to garment manipulation often rely on modular perception and control pipelines, which can be sensitive to camera calibration, depth information, and require numerous hand-designed hyperparameters, thereby limiting their generalizability.

In recent years, data-driven learning approaches, such as imitation learning, have emerged as a prominent paradigm for acquiring robotic skills. These methods have shown promise in garment manipulation. However, a major impediment to their advancement and generalization to novel environments and objects is the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality demonstration data. Collecting such data in the real world is labor-intensive and expensive.

Synthetic data generation offers an alternative to real-world data collection, enabling flexible modification of environments and object properties for improved generalization. Despite this potential, generating high-quality synthetic data for deformable objects remains a challenge. Existing limitations include:
1. **Limited Garment Assets and Annotations:** Many datasets feature a restricted number of garment meshes, often lacking the rich annotations necessary for high-quality demonstration generation and policy learning. Prior template-based methods for geometry generation struggle with realistic texture application, leading to visual inconsistencies or poor performance on layered meshes.
2. **Limited Error Recovery Capabilities:** Garment manipulation tasks are often long-horizon, involving complex dynamics where small errors can accumulate. While closed-loop control allows for error correction, policies trained solely on perfect demonstrations may fail when encountering out-of-distribution states arising from minor execution errors. The ability to recover from errors is crucial for robust policy deployment.

This paper addresses these limitations by focusing on enhancing the quality and diversity of synthetic garment data and incorporating explicit mechanisms for learning error recovery. It builds upon the established field of imitation learning and synthetic data generation by proposing a novel framework for scalable asset creation with semantic annotations and an iterative learning approach that explicitly incorporates error recovery demonstrations. The work aims to bridge the sim-to-real gap, a critical aspect of robotic learning, by developing policies that transfer directly from simulation to real-world deployment without extensive real-world fine-tuning. It also explores the potential for fine-tuning large vision-language-action (VLA) models using the synthetic dataset, situating itself within the growing trend of generalist robot policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to overcome the data scarcity and robustness challenges in robotic garment folding by developing a comprehensive synthetic dataset and an improved imitation learning strategy. The motivation stems from two central limitations identified in the broader research landscape:

**Motivation 1: Addressing the Scarcity of Diverse and High-Quality Garment Assets and Annotations.**
- **Problem:** Existing synthetic garment datasets often lack diversity in garment geometry and texture, and crucial semantic annotations like keypoints are frequently absent. This limits the generalization capabilities of learned policies and increases the manual effort required for demonstration generation. Current texture generation methods for template-based meshes often yield unrealistic results or struggle with complex garment structures.
- **Objective:** To propose a novel framework for generating a large amount of diverse, high-quality garment meshes with automatic semantic keypoint annotations. This includes:
 - Designing geometric garment templates controllable by keypoints to enable scalable geometry generation.
 - Leveraging pre-trained generative models to synthesize realistic and varied texture patterns for these garments.
 - Developing a method to ensure consistency between generated geometry and texture, and to automatically annotate keypoints on the generated meshes.
- **Expected Outcome:** A robust synthetic garment dataset (FoldNet) with rich assets suitable for physical simulation and rendering, providing automatically generated keypoint annotations that can facilitate subsequent demonstration generation and policy learning.

**Motivation 2: Enhancing Policy Robustness by Learning from Error Recovery Demonstrations.**
- **Problem:** Garment manipulation tasks are long-horizon, and policies trained only on perfect demonstrations are prone to failure when real-world execution inevitably deviates, leading to out-of-distribution states. Standard imitation learning struggles to teach a robot how to recover from these errors, which limits the real-world applicability of learned policies.
- **Objective:** To introduce a strategy for generating and incorporating error recovery demonstrations into the training data to improve the robustness of closed-loop folding policies. This involves:
 - Developing Keypoint-Gated DAgger (KG-DAgger), an iterative process that leverages keypoint annotations to detect potential failure cases during policy execution in simulation.
 - Implementing a keypoint-based recovery strategy that intervenes upon failure detection to correct the garment state.
 - Integrating these corrected trajectories into the training dataset, allowing the model to learn how to recover from specific failure modes.
- **Expected Outcome:** A more robust and generalizable closed-loop policy that can handle unexpected deviations and recover from errors during real-world garment folding tasks, leading to higher success rates compared to policies trained without error recovery data.

In summary, the core objective is to create a synthetic data generation and learning pipeline that facilitates the training of generalizable and robust closed-loop policies for garment folding, capable of direct transfer from simulation to real-world robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of FoldNet is structured around three main components: synthetic garment asset generation, keypoint-based demonstration generation with error recovery, and policy training.

**A. Garment Mesh Synthesis**
The process for generating high-quality garment meshes suitable for physical simulation and rendering involves three stages:

1. **Geometry Generation:**
 - **Template-based Approach:** Four garment categories (t-shirt, vest, hoodie, trousers) are supported. For each category, a template is defined by manually specifying a set of 2D semantic keypoints (x, y coordinates). These keypoints implicitly define the garment's structure and serve as semantic manipulation points.
 - **Shape Variation:** By randomizing the positions of these keypoints, a wide variety of garment shapes can be generated efficiently.
 - **Mesh Creation:** Keypoints are connected using Bezier curves to define the garment border. Triangulation within the xy-plane forms the mesh. Z-coordinates and UV coordinates for the vertices are heuristically defined.
 - **Automatic Annotation:** During this process, keypoint indices are automatically saved, associating semantic keypoints with the generated triangular mesh.

2. **Texture Generation:**
 - **Generative Models:** Pre-trained generative models are utilized to produce realistic textures.
 - **Prompt Generation:** A large language model (e.g., ChatGPT) is used to generate textual descriptions (prompts) of desired textures for each garment type.
 - **Image Synthesis:** These prompts are fed into a Text2Image model (e.g., Stable-Diffusion-3.5) to quickly synthesize a large number of texture images.

3. **Combining and Filtering:**
 - **Consistency Enhancement:** To ensure visual consistency between the generated geometry and texture, an additional filtering step is applied.
 - **Rendering and Selection:** Each generated garment mesh (geometry only) is combined with different synthesized texture images and rendered. A vision-language model (VLM) then automatically evaluates and selects the most suitable texture for that specific mesh, enhancing the plausibility of the final asset.

**B. Demonstration Generation**
Using the synthesized garment assets, high-quality closed-loop folding demonstrations are automatically collected in simulation.

1. **Keypoint-Based Demonstration Generation:**
 - **Folding Policies:** A simple, yet effective, keypoint-based policy is designed for each garment category to fold the garment in a predefined manner (e.g., for a t-shirt: rotate, drag, fold sleeves, fold bottom).
 - **Action Derivation:** Grasping points and target placement positions for the two grippers are derived from the ground-truth keypoint locations, with intermediate positions obtained via interpolation.
 - **Generalizability:** The use of keypoint annotations makes this policy unified across shape variations within the same garment category. Different folding strategies can be generated by modifying initial and target positions.

2. **Error-Recovery Demonstration Generation (KG-DAgger):**
 - **Iterative Learning:** KG-DAgger is introduced as a variant of DAgger, an iterative imitation learning algorithm.
 - **Failure Detection:** During model inference in simulation, a keypoint-based strategy monitors garment keypoints and gripper states to detect grasp failures or other potential errors. This automates the role of a human expert in traditional DAgger.
 - **Recovery Mechanism:** When a failure is detected (e.g., gripper fails to grasp), a keypoint-based recovery strategy is invoked. This strategy attempts to correct the error (e.g., reattempting the grasp).
 - **Trajectory Augmentation:** The recovery actions and subsequent successful steps are incorporated into the dataset. These "corrected" trajectories teach the model how to respond to and recover from common failure modes.
 - **End-to-End Deployment:** KG-DAgger is used only during training in simulation. The final trained policy is end-to-end, directly mapping observations to actions without requiring explicit keypoint detection or error detection during real-world deployment.

**C. Model Training**

1. **Vision-Action Model:** Diffusion Policy is chosen as the vision-action model due to its compact size and ability to model multi-modal behaviors and produce coherent action sequences. The architecture consists of a ResNet50 encoder for RGB images and a mapping for robot state, concatenated as conditional input to the diffusion policy.
2. **Data Processing:** Only successful episodes (garments fully folded) are retained, with "no-op" actions padded at the end to indicate termination.
3. **Modified Loss Function:** The training loss is a modified version of the original diffusion loss. A coefficient `m_i` is introduced for each action in an action chunk. If a zero-weight action (indicating a grasp failure) occurs, `m_i` for that action and all subsequent actions in the chunk is set to 0. Otherwise, `m_i` is 1. This weighting mechanism prevents the model from learning incorrect actions during failure states while still utilizing the recovery data.
4. **Inputs and Outputs:** The model takes a masked RGB image and the robot's current state (XYZ coordinates of both end-effectors and gripper open-close status) as input. It outputs an action sequence (length 16), with the first four actions executed during inference.
5. **Sim2Real Transfer:** The models are trained purely in simulation and then directly transferred to real robots without real-world fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of FoldNet was validated through experiments on two main tasks: keypoint detection and garment folding.

**A. Keypoint Detection**

1. **Setup:** A U-Net inspired architecture with a pre-trained MaxViT nano encoder was trained on synthetic garment images generated by FoldNet (1,500 instances per category). The model predicted keypoint positions as Gaussian blobs on 2D heatmaps. Evaluation was performed directly on a real-world dataset (480 t-shirts, 82 trousers, 96 vests, 96 hoodies) without fine-tuning. Grounded-SAM was used for real-world segmentation. Metrics included Mean Average Precision (mAP) and Average Keypoint Distance (AKD).
2. **Findings:**
 - **Superior Texture Generation:** Models trained on FoldNet assets (Ours) achieved higher mAP and lower AKD compared to models trained with textures generated by aRTF and Paint-it. FoldNet obtained an average mAP of 47.2 and AKD of 15.6, outperforming aRTF (mAP 36.6, AKD 15.4) and Paint-it (mAP 38.0, AKD 31.4). Notably, FoldNet performed well across different garment categories.
 - **Effectiveness of VLM-based Filtering:** An ablation study demonstrated that the VLM-based filtering step in texture generation improved performance. "Ours w/o filter" showed a slight decrease in mAP (45.8 vs. 47.2) and increase in AKD (16.7 vs. 15.6) on average, indicating that VLM filtering enhances the consistency and realism of textures.
 - **Qualitative Results:** Visual examples showed that FoldNet produced more plausible textures and more accurate keypoint predictions on real images compared to baselines.
 - **Data Generation Cost:** Generating a single cloth instance (mesh, texture, rendering) took approximately 30 seconds on the specified hardware.

**B. Folding Policy Learning**

1. **Setup:** Experiments were conducted in PyFlex (physics) and Blender (rendering). The robot used RGB images from a head-mounted D436 camera. The action space included XYZ coordinates and gripper open/close states (8 dimensions). Models were trained with 1,000 garment instances and 15,000 demonstration trajectories (approx. 2M image-action pairs) per category. Success in simulation was determined by average vertex distance (below 0.4 mm) to a perfectly folded ground truth mesh, while real-world success was human-evaluated on 10 unseen garments.
2. **Findings:**
 - **Impact of Demonstration Generation Pipelines (KG-DAgger):**
 - Training with error correction demonstrations (Noised, KG-DAgger) significantly improved performance over models trained exclusively with "Perfect" demonstrations.
 - KG-DAgger achieved the highest success rates. In simulation, KG-DAgger resulted in a 90% average success rate, surpassing "Noised" (85%) and "Perfect" (65%) methods.
 - Qualitative analysis demonstrated that models trained with error recovery (KG-DAgger) could reattempt grasps after initial failures, whereas models trained on "Perfect" data often entered out-of-distribution states and failed.
 - **Adaptability to Different Folding Strategies:** The framework demonstrated its ability to learn various folding patterns. Evaluation of T-Shirt-1, T-Shirt-2, and Trousers-1 tasks yielded comparable high success rates (around 85-90% in simulation), indicating that the pipeline is not limited to specific folding methods.
 - **Data Scale and Performance:** Performance generally increased with the amount of training data (both number of meshes and demonstrations). For instance, using 100% of the training data (1000 garments, 15K trajectories) resulted in higher success rates compared to using 25% or 50% of the data.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer Performance:**
 - The model trained with KG-DAgger achieved a 75% success rate in real-world deployment on unseen garments, representing a 25% improvement over models trained with "Perfect" demonstrations (50% success rate). The "Noised" method achieved a 65% success rate in the real world.
 - This demonstrated the effectiveness of the synthetic data and error recovery strategy for direct transfer to real robotic systems without real-world fine-tuning.
 - **Fine-tuning VLA Models:** Fine-tuning a pre-trained π0 VLA model on the FoldNet dataset enabled it to generalize to an unseen robot in both simulation (80% success rate) and the real world (70% success rate). This indicates the value of FoldNet data for adapting large foundation models to specific manipulation tasks, even without any real-world task-specific data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces FoldNet, a comprehensive synthetic dataset and an associated methodology for robotic garment folding, making several significant contributions and holding substantial potential impact:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Diversity:** FoldNet directly tackles one of the primary bottlenecks in robotic garment manipulation – the lack of diverse, high-quality, and annotated training data. The proposed garment mesh generation framework, leveraging keypoint-based geometry and generative models for textures with VLM-based filtering, enables scalable synthesis of realistic and varied garment assets. This significantly reduces the reliance on costly and labor-intensive real-world data collection.

2. **Enhancing Policy Robustness through Error Recovery:** The introduction of Keypoint-Gated DAgger (KG-DAgger) provides a principled and automated mechanism for generating error recovery demonstrations. By explicitly training policies to handle and recover from grasp failures and other deviations, the work addresses a critical challenge in deploying robots in dynamic, uncertain real-world environments. The demonstrated 25% improvement in real-world success rate underscores the practical utility of this approach.

3. **Demonstrated Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The research successfully demonstrates the direct transferability of policies trained entirely in simulation to real-world robotic systems, even for complex deformable object manipulation tasks. This achievement is crucial for accelerating robot learning, as it minimizes the need for extensive real-world experimentation and fine-tuning, which are often time-consuming and prone to hardware wear.

4. **Generalizability of Policies:** The keypoint-driven asset and demonstration synthesis methods enable the training of policies that generalize across different garment shapes within a category and can be adapted to various folding patterns. This flexibility is vital for creating robots capable of handling a wide range of tasks and objects.

5. **Integration with Foundation Models:** The successful fine-tuning of a large vision-language-action (VLA) model (π0) using FoldNet data demonstrates the dataset's utility in the context of emerging foundation models for robotics. This suggests that synthetic datasets like FoldNet can play a pivotal role in adapting powerful pre-trained models to specific manipulation tasks, potentially reducing the need for domain-specific real-world data collection even further.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement in Domestic Robotics:** The ability of robots to reliably fold clothes has significant implications for automating household chores, contributing to more capable and autonomous home robots.
- **Industrial Automation:** Improved garment manipulation capabilities can benefit industries such as apparel manufacturing, logistics, and laundry services, where handling deformable materials is a common but challenging task.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** FoldNet provides a valuable public dataset and a robust methodology that can serve as a benchmark and a starting point for future research in deformable object manipulation, robot learning, and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Development of More Robust Robotic Systems:** By highlighting the importance and providing a solution for learning error recovery, this work contributes to the development of more resilient and adaptable robotic systems capable of operating effectively in unstructured environments.
