# RoboTacDex: A Dexterous Visual-Tactile-Action Dataset for Humanoid Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboTacDex introduces a large-scale, multi-modal dataset for dexterous upper-body humanoid manipulation, comprising 6,000 trajectories with synchronized multi-view RGB-D and tactile data across 19 tasks and 22 objects. The dataset aims to address the scarcity of high-quality humanoid manipulation data, enabling the development and evaluation of advanced robotic policies.

## Problem

- A significant scarcity of high-quality, large-scale, and multi-modal datasets specifically for humanoid robot manipulation, unlike the more mature datasets for fixed-base manipulators.
- Existing humanoid datasets often lack comprehensive multi-modal inputs (e.g., multi-view RGB-D, tactile feedback) crucial for complex physical interactions, generalization, and dexterous manipulation tasks.
- Challenges in collecting temporally consistent multi-modal data from high-DoF humanoid robots performing intricate bimanual tasks in diverse environments.

## Method

- Designed and implemented a data acquisition system centered on a ==Unitree G1 humanoid robot== with dual dexterous hands, integrating multi-view RGB-D cameras and ==tactile sensors==.
- Developed a ==hardware-software co-synchronization== method to ensure millisecond-level temporal consistency across all recorded modalities (visual, tactile, proprioception) at 30Hz.
- Collected **RoboTacDex**, a dataset of 6,000 trajectories (25 hours) covering 19 tasks, 23 skills, and 22 objects, with diversity in task types, object properties, and environmental configurations.

## Results

- RoboTacDex contains **6,000** high-quality trajectories (**25 hours**) across **19 tasks**, **23 skills**, and **22 objects**, emphasizing complex dual-arm and dexterous hand operations.
- VLA models like GROOT N1.5 generally achieved higher success rates (e.g., **9/10** for PickAndPlacePear) compared to ACT and Diffusion Policy (average **3/10**) on some tasks, but struggled with less common tasks and precise spatial constraints.
- Ablation studies showed that additional wrist or third-person cameras did not yield significant performance improvements for ACT, DP, or GROOT N1.5, and tactile sensing shifted failure patterns rather than directly increasing overall success for the Diffusion Policy on the 'UnscrewBottle' task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Humanoid dexterous manipulation requires substantially more training data (around 200 trajectories per task) compared to robotic arms for models like ACT and Diffusion Policy to achieve reasonable performance.
> - Simply concatenating images from multiple viewpoints without architectural support for geometric reasoning or explicit multi-view matching did not significantly improve policy performance for the evaluated models.
> - Tactile sensing altered failure modes in a tactile-rich task (UnscrewBottle), enabling better grasping and revealing hidden state variables, even if it did not immediately translate to higher overall success rates in the evaluated setup.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning, particularly through imitation learning, has experienced substantial advancements, driven by the availability of large-scale datasets. Prior efforts have largely focused on fixed-base robotic manipulators, leading to the development of numerous datasets (e.g., RoboTurk, BridgeData, RT-1) that have propelled various manipulation policies. However, the application of these methodologies to humanoid robots faces distinct challenges and exhibits a notable scarcity of high-quality, comprehensive datasets.

Humanoid robots, with their high degrees of freedom (DoF) and potential for complex tasks in unstructured environments, demand data collection systems capable of precisely recording intricate bimanual coordination. The existing datasets for humanoid manipulation, such as Nvidia Gr0Ot, RoboMIND, Agibot World, and Humanoid Everyday, either lack multi-modality (e.g., only single-view RGB, no tactile data) or extensive dexterous hand data, or fail to cover multi-view perspectives comprehensively. This limitation is particularly critical as contemporary robot learning models, including diffusion models and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, increasingly rely on rich, multi-modal sensory inputs—such as multi-view RGB and depth information for complete spatial geometry, and tactile feedback for dexterous manipulation and object property perception—to achieve generalization and handle complex physical interactions.

This work addresses this specific gap by providing a large-scale, multi-modal, and diverse dataset tailored for dexterous upper-body humanoid manipulation. It integrates visual (multi-view RGB and depth) and tactile information, alongside semantic annotations, aiming to support the development of more general-purpose and robust robotic manipulation models for humanoid platforms. By focusing on challenging dual-arm and dexterous hand tasks, the dataset seeks to advance research beyond simpler tabletop scenarios and single-modality approaches, thereby complementing and extending the capabilities offered by existing datasets in the broader robot manipulation landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the creation of RoboTacDex are detailed as follows:

- **Address the Scarcity of High-Quality Humanoid Manipulation Datasets:** The current research landscape in robot learning has a significant deficit of large-scale, high-quality manipulation datasets specifically for humanoid robots. While fixed-base robot datasets are relatively mature, humanoid platforms present unique data collection challenges due to their high degrees of freedom, complex bimanual coordination requirements, and the necessity to perform intricate tasks in diverse, open-world environments. The project aims to fill this gap by providing a substantial dataset for humanoid manipulation.

- **Integrate Multi-Modal Information for Comprehensive Perception:** Existing humanoid datasets often rely on single-view visual input or lack critical sensory modalities. The authors emphasize that pure visual input from a single viewpoint is frequently insufficient for complex physical interactions and achieving real generalization. Multi-view RGB and depth information offer a more complete spatial geometric representation, while tactile feedback is crucial for dexterous manipulation, compensating for visual occlusions, and perceiving object physical properties. A key motivation is to construct a dataset that comprehensively integrates multi-modal information, including multi-view RGB, depth maps, and tactile sensing, to serve as a foundation for universal embodied intelligence in humanoids.

- **Mimic Human-like Operational Logic and Real-World Complexity:** The project aims to collect data from tasks that are more complex and challenging than typical pick-and-place scenarios, requiring dual arms and dexterous hands. The motivation is to simulate authentic human-like operational logic and real-world manipulation complexity, thereby enabling humanoid robots to learn a wide range of skills, including articulated, dual-arm collaborative, and fine manipulation tasks. This moves beyond basic manipulation skills which are already well-covered by datasets for simpler manipulators.

- **Establish a Robust Data Collection and Synchronization System:** Accurate temporal alignment across heterogeneous sensors (multiple cameras, tactile sensors, proprioception) is critical for multi-modal data consistency. Misaligned observations can lead to incorrect modeling of physical states. Therefore, a significant motivation was to develop an improved hardware-software co-synchronization method to ensure millisecond-level temporal consistency for all recorded modalities, thereby guaranteeing high data quality.

- **Provide a Verification Benchmark for Imitation Learning Policies:** Beyond data provision, the work aims to establish a benchmark for evaluating existing imitation learning models on high-dimensional humanoid action spaces. By conducting extensive experiments with representative policies (e.g., ACT, Diffusion Policy, VLA models), the authors intend to verify the dataset's utility in enhancing robotic policy performance and to identify the strengths and limitations of current models when faced with the complexities of humanoid dexterous manipulation. This provides a standardized performance reference for future research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for creating RoboTacDex involved the design of a data collection platform, a detailed pipeline for recording multi-modal trajectories, and a structured approach to ensure dataset diversity.

**A. Hardware Design and Setup:**
The data acquisition system was built around a **Unitree G1 humanoid robot**. Key hardware components include:
- **Robot:** Unitree G1 humanoid robot, equipped with dual arms (14 DoF) and dual Brainco Revo2 Tactile version dexterous hands (12 DoF each). The robot's lower limbs and waist were fixed to stabilize its position during manipulation, maintaining a consistent head-mounted camera view of the desktop operations.
- **Teleoperation:** A **Meta Horizon VR headset** was used by an operator to capture human upper-body movements, which were then mapped onto the robot's arm and finger joints for control.
- **Multi-View Cameras:** Four RGB-D cameras captured visual observations:
 - One head-centric camera.
 - Two wrist-mounted cameras, focused on the dexterous hands.
 - One third-person perspective camera, providing an overview of the operational scene.
 - The first-person and third-person cameras were **RealSense D435i** models.
- **Tactile Sensing:** The Brainco Revo2 hands were capable of recording touch information, specifically normal forces, tangential forces (including direction) on each fingertip, and proprioceptive signals from self-capacitance proximity sensing.

**B. Data Collection Pipeline:**
Trajectories were recorded at a high frequency of **30Hz**. Each frame included:
- **Low-dimensional State Data:** Joint positions for arms and fingers.
- **Visual Information:** RGB and depth information from all four cameras, with a resolution of 640x480.
- **Tactile Information:** Touch data from both dexterous hands, including normal forces, tangential forces, and proximity sensing.
- **Action Data:** Joint positions for arm and finger movements.
The collected data could be replayed in both simulated (IsaacSim) and real environments to verify recording accuracy.

**1. Camera Synchronization:**
To mitigate temporal misalignment, crucial for tasks with significant temporal sequentiality and dynamic characteristics, a **hardware-software co-synchronization method** was implemented:
- **Hardware Synchronization:** The RealSense D435i cameras (first-person and third-person) supported hardware synchronization.
- **Software Synchronization:** This was applied between the head/third-person cameras and the wrist-mounted cameras to ensure consistent timestamps across all recorded video streams.

**2. Tactile Information Transmission:**
- Tactile information and joint state data from the dexterous hands were published at **100Hz** using DDS messages.
- A local computer received these messages and recorded them at **30Hz**, synchronized with the other modalities.

**C. Dataset Analysis and Diversity Design:**
The dataset, RoboTacDex, comprises **6,000 high-quality trajectories**, totaling approximately 25 hours of data, covering **19 tasks, 23 skills, and interactions with 22 objects**. Diversity was ensured across multiple dimensions:
- **Task and Skill Types:** Tasks were categorized into five manipulation types: Basic, Articulated, Dual-arm Collaborative, Fine, and Humanoid Interactive Manipulations. The dataset was designed to feature a balanced distribution, with an emphasis on challenging dual-arm and dexterous hand tasks (e.g., unscrew, shielded grasp), moving beyond basic pick-and-place.
- **Horizon Diversity:** Trajectory durations varied, with a majority between 10-40 seconds, indicating multi-step coordinated actions, and some longer trajectories involving sustained manipulation.
- **Object Diversity:** 22 distinct object types were included, categorized into Articulated, Constrained, Containers, Functional, and Graspable Objects. These objects varied in shape, material, and size, from kitchen items to office supplies, including both rigid and deformable objects. The use of objects with different colors further contributed to visual diversity.
- **Environmental Diversity:** To study the impact of scene factors, data was collected across four environmental configurations for most tasks: robot-to-table distances of 5cm and 15cm, combined with white and green grid backgrounds.

**D. Experimental Validation:**
To validate the dataset quality and understand policy performance, three representative imitation learning models were evaluated on RoboTacDex:
- **Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT)**
- **Diffusion Policy (DP)**
- **GROOT N1.5 (a VLA model)**
Evaluations were conducted on four specific tasks: `PickAndPlacePear`, `TurnPage`, `InsertBook`, and `UnscrewBottle`. Models were trained on approximately 200 trajectories per task, with baseline observations including head image and joint state. Ablation studies were performed to assess the impact of multi-view visual configurations and the inclusion of tactile sensing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented RoboTacDex, a multi-modal dataset for humanoid manipulation, and evaluated its utility through experiments with imitation learning policies.

**A. Dataset Characteristics and Diversity:**
- RoboTacDex consists of 6,000 demonstration trajectories, spanning approximately 25 hours of data.
- It covers 19 distinct tasks, involves 23 identified skills, and interacts with 22 different objects.
- The tasks are distributed across five manipulation categories: Basic, Articulated, Dual-arm Collaborative, Fine, and Humanoid Interactive Manipulations, with a focus on more complex, dual-arm, and dexterous hand operations.
- Trajectory durations vary, with most requiring multiple coordinated actions rather than single primitive executions.
- Objects range from kitchen items to office supplies, varying in type (articulated, constrained, containers, functional, graspable), material, and size.
- Environmental variations include two robot-to-table distances (5cm, 15cm) and two background types (white, green grid), designed to assess generalization.

**B. Performance of Imitation Learning Policies:**
Three policies (ACT, Diffusion Policy, GROOT N1.5) were evaluated on four tasks, with 10 trials per task.
- **GROOT N1.5 (VLA model):** This model generally exhibited higher success rates compared to ACT and Diffusion Policy. For `PickAndPlacePear`, GROOT achieved 9/10 successes, indicating strong generalization, potentially due to its pretraining on large-scale manipulation datasets. However, for less common tasks like `InsertBook` and `UnscrewBottle`, GROOT's performance was comparable to or slightly better than the others (4/10 and 6/10 respectively), and it sometimes failed to learn critical semantic requirements and spatial constraints (e.g., misalignment in `InsertBook`).
- **ACT and Diffusion Policy (DP):** These models, which map observations to action spaces, showed lower success rates, particularly for tasks where object spatial positions in training data were widely scattered. Their inference often resulted in actions deviating from actual object locations.
 - ACT demonstrated a lower average success rate (3/10) and sometimes led to both hands executing actions without reaching the target position, attributed to the stochasticity and multi-modality of human demonstrations challenging its observation-to-action mapping.
 - DP also showed an average success rate of 3/10 but was noted for its ability to better express multimodal action distributions due to its gradient-based learning mechanism.
- **Overall:** Humanoid robots and dexterous hands (26 DoF) required substantially more data (approximately 200 trajectories per task) compared to robotic arms for models to achieve reasonable performance.

**C. Analysis of Multi-view and Tactile Perception:**

**1. Multi-view Configurations:**
- Ablation studies on `PickAndPlacePear` and `InsertBook` showed that adding wrist cameras or a third-person perspective camera did not lead to significant performance improvement across any of the models (ACT, DP, GROOT N1.5).
- For ACT and DP, this was attributed to their lack of explicit mechanisms for multi-view image matching or additional supervision, suggesting that simply concatenating images from multiple viewpoints is ineffective without architectural support for geometric reasoning across views.
- For GROOT, already high single-view success rates on simple tasks like `PickAndPlacePear` meant additional views did not contribute to further improvement.

**2. Tactile Sensing:**
- Ablation on tactile sensing was conducted using Diffusion Policy on the `UnscrewBottle` task, which is tactile-rich. Tactile information was incorporated as part of the low-dimensional state.
- While the overall success rate for `UnscrewBottle` did not improve with tactile sensing (both with and without tactile feedback for DP were 2/10), a shift in failure patterns was observed.
- Without tactile sensing, `Idle Spinning Failure` (hand spinning without engaging the cap) was more prevalent.
- With tactile feedback, failure modes shifted towards `Stuck/Adjusts Failure` (hand getting stuck or failing to adjust grip) and `Cap Dropped`.
- This indicates that tactile feedback enabled the hand to better grasp the cap, revealing latent state variables that are visually ambiguous or hidden, even if it did not translate to an overall increase in task success within the evaluated context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RoboTacDex represents a contribution to the field of robot learning and humanoid manipulation, with several potential impacts:

- **Bridging the Data Gap for Humanoid Robots:** RoboTacDex directly addresses the identified scarcity of high-quality, multi-modal manipulation datasets for humanoid robots. By providing 6,000 diverse trajectories, it offers a substantial resource that can facilitate the development and training of more advanced manipulation policies specifically tailored for the complexities of humanoid platforms and dexterous hands.

- **Enabling Research in Multi-modal and Dexterous Manipulation:** The dataset's comprehensive inclusion of multi-view RGB, depth information, and tactile feedback provides a rich basis for exploring multi-modal fusion techniques. This is particularly significant for tasks requiring fine manipulation, robust object interaction, and perception under occlusion, where tactile feedback complements visual data. It can drive research into how different sensory modalities can be effectively integrated for robust control.

- **Advancing Generalization and Embodied Intelligence:** The dataset's diversity in tasks (including dual-arm collaborative, articulated, and fine manipulations), skills, objects, and environmental configurations aims to foster the development of more generalizable manipulation skills. By mimicking human-like operational logic and real-world complexity, RoboTacDex can support the training of models capable of adapting to novel scenarios and objects, moving closer to universal embodied intelligence in humanoids.

- **Establishing a Benchmark for Policy Evaluation:** The included evaluation of representative imitation learning policies (ACT, Diffusion Policy, GROOT N1.5) on a subset of tasks establishes an initial benchmark. This provides a standardized reference point for future research to compare the performance of new algorithms and model architectures, particularly in handling high-dimensional humanoid action spaces and multi-modal inputs. The analysis of model strengths, weaknesses, and failure modes offers insights that can guide future policy design.

- **Driving Hardware-Software Co-design:** The improved hardware integration scheme and synchronization system developed for data collection demonstrate a successful approach to resolving challenges across heterogeneous sensors. This methodological contribution can serve as a technical reference for researchers building similar multi-modal data acquisition platforms.

- **Facilitating Future Research in Specific Areas:** The observations from the ablation studies, such as the need for dedicated architectural components for multi-view image matching and geometric reasoning, and the shift in failure modes with tactile sensing, highlight specific areas for future research in model design. The dataset's open-source availability is expected to stimulate further investigation into advanced multi-modal fusion, long-horizon tasks, and sim-to-real transfer for humanoid robots.

While the study acknowledges limitations, such as the relatively straightforward integration of tactile information and the ongoing need for increased dataset scale and environmental diversity, RoboTacDex provides a foundation for addressing current bottlenecks in humanoid manipulation, offering a valuable resource for advancing the capabilities of humanoid robots.
