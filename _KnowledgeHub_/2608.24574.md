# PhysMLLMs: Spatial Priors for Unified Referring Segmentation and Grounded Reasoning of Images and Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysMLLMs injects a physics-inspired spatial continuity prior into Video MLLMs via REPA-Global, a training-time distillation term aligning the student's global visual embedding to a frozen DINOv2 teacher through an offline cache and warmup-ramp-hold schedule. This stabilizes cross-frame masks under occlusion and distractors with zero added inference cost, gaining +1.35 J&F on ReVOS.

## Problem

- Video MLLMs learn semantic grounding from image-text alignment but lack direct training signal for temporal consistency, causing jitter, drift, and identity switches across frames.
- Memory-based association modules reuse past cues but cannot fully fix instability when upstream Video MLLM features are not well regularized in representation space.
- Sa2VA-InternVL3-2B baseline J&F drops from 54.61 overall to 33.65 on small targets and 31.29 on occlusion/disappearance, showing frame-level accuracy alone is insufficient.

## Method

- **REPA-Global** distillation aligns the student's pooled global visual embedding to a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 teacher embedding via cosine distance, injecting object-permanence priors without touching inference.
- An ==offline teacher embedding cache== precomputes DINOv2 global features per frame so training reads cached targets instead of running repeated teacher forward passes.
- A ==warmup-ramp-hold schedule== for the distillation weight lambda(t), combined with calibrated Vision-LoRA and mask-decoder-only fine-tuning, controls prior strength and limits multimodal drift.

## Results

- **PhysMLLMs** (dw012) reaches **57.4** J&F on ReVOS and **55.1** on MeVIS U, beating Sa2VA-InternVL3-2B baselines of **56.2** and **53.9** respectively and outperforming Sa2VA-8B, VISA-13B, and GLUS-7B.
- Complexity-stratified ReVOS analysis shows largest gains on hard subsets: **+2.26** J&F on small targets, **+2.02** on distractor-heavy cases, **+1.92** on occlusion/disappearance, versus **+1.30** overall.
- Ablation shows global-only alignment (**57.41** J&F) beats token-only (**56.26**) and global+token (**56.07**); RefCOCO stays at **81.9** percent and MMBench at **0.7878**, near baseline, with no capability collapse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A training-only representation-level regularizer can fix temporal consistency failures without any inference-time cost, since the teacher is discarded after training.
> - Global embedding alignment outperforms dense token-level distillation, since patch-level constraints over-regularize and interfere with language-conditioned target selection.
> - Distillation weight is a control variable, not a constant, since applying the prior too early or too strongly can weaken grounding and cause over-smoothing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the rapidly evolving field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), particularly their application to video understanding tasks such as language-guided video segmentation. While Video MLLMs have demonstrated robust semantic grounding capabilities through large-scale image-text alignment and instruction tuning, they frequently exhibit spatio-temporal inconsistencies in their predictions. These inconsistencies manifest as temporal jitter, drifting identity or shape, and identity switches, especially when objects are partially occluded or similar objects are present nearby. This issue limits their reliability in dynamic video environments.

Traditional approaches to referring video object segmentation often employ memory-based designs (e.g., STM, STCN, XMem) to improve temporal association. While effective for re-using past cues, these methods may not fully address instability originating from upstream Video MLLM feature representations. Concurrently, self-supervised pretraining, particularly methods like DINOv2, has demonstrated the ability to learn robust, object-centered, and geometry-aware visual features from unlabeled data. Knowledge distillation techniques have also advanced, moving beyond simple logit matching to feature-level and representation alignment, enabling the transfer of structural guidance from a teacher model to a student. Furthermore, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods, such as LoRA, have become crucial for adapting large pre-trained models to specific tasks while preserving their broad capabilities and mitigating representational drift.

This paper positions itself by proposing to address the root cause of temporal inconsistencies—the lack of explicit spatial priors during training—by leveraging the strengths of self-supervised teachers and advanced distillation techniques. Instead of relying solely on stronger mask decoders or post-processing rules, PhysMLLMs aims to guide the representation dynamics of Video MLLMs towards more stable, object-centered features during the training phase. This approach integrates principles from self-supervised learning, knowledge distillation, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning to enhance spatio-temporal consistency in video segmentation without incurring additional inference costs or compromising the general multimodal reasoning capabilities of the underlying MLLM.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatio-temporal consistency and stability of language-guided video segmentation performed by Video Multimodal Large Language Models (Video MLLMs). The authors observe that despite advancements in MLLMs combined with strong mask priors and lightweight decoders, current systems frequently produce masks that are unstable across video frames. This instability manifests as temporal jitter, undesirable drifting in object identity or shape, and complete identity switches, particularly when target objects are partly hidden (occlusion), move rapidly, or are surrounded by visually similar distractors.

The motivation stems from the hypothesis that these prevalent failures are not merely isolated errors in mask decoding but rather originate from unstable feature changes within the visual representations over time. Current training signals for Video MLLMs often provide limited direct guidance for maintaining predictions consistently across frames. Specifically, the authors identify a lack of explicit spatial priors within the training process as a key reason for the difficulty in maintaining stable spatial identity and shape over time. Quantitative analysis of baseline models, such as Sa2VA-InternVL3-2B on the ReVOS validation split, reveals significant performance drops on challenging cases like small targets (20.96 points J&F decrease) and occlusion/disappearance cases (23.32 points J&F decrease), reinforcing that maintaining stable object identity and spatial continuity under difficult video conditions is a major bottleneck.

Therefore, the research aims to introduce a training-time prior that regularizes visual representations towards temporally coherent, object-centric features. This "physics-inspired spatial consistency prior" is based on the principles of object permanence, spatial continuity, and temporal coherence: once an object is identified, its representation should remain coherent over time unless real physical changes provide clear evidence otherwise. The motivation is to inject this prior at the representation level during training, thereby guiding the internal feature dynamics, rather than relying on external heuristics, stronger prediction heads, or post-processing at inference time. This approach seeks to improve both mask quality and cross-frame consistency for language-guided video segmentation, while concurrently preserving the general multimodal capabilities of the Video MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces **PhysMLLMs**, a training-stage prior injection architecture designed to infuse physics-inspired spatial continuity priors into Video MLLMs. The core of the approach involves **representation distillation** from a frozen, self-supervised teacher model, specifically DINOv2, into a student Video MLLM.

### 4. Architecture Overview

PhysMLLMs operates by aligning the student model's global visual representation with that of a pre-computed teacher model's global embeddings during training. The overall objective function combines the standard segmentation loss ($L_{seg}$) with a distillation loss ($L_{distill}$), weighted by a scheduled parameter $\lambda(t)$:
$L = L_{seg} + \lambda(t)L_{distill}$

### 5. REPA-Global Distillation

The core mechanism, **REPA-Global (Global Representation Prior Alignment)**, instantiates $L_{distill}$ as a global prior alignment term.
1. **Teacher Model:** A frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 model serves as the teacher. For any input frame $x$, DINOv2 produces patch tokens, which are then mean-pooled to generate a global teacher embedding $z_t(x) \in \mathbb{R}^D$.
2. **Student Model:** The student Video MLLM (built on Sa2VA-InternVL3-2B) extracts visual tokens $S(x)$. These tokens are pooled (mean-pooling is the default) to form a student global embedding $z_s(x) \in \mathbb{R}^C$. Crucially, this student embedding is extracted *before* any text-conditioned mask prediction, ensuring that the distillation injects a visual prior rather than a text-conditioned multimodal state.
3. **Alignment:** Since the dimensions $C$ and $D$ may differ, a lightweight projector $P(\cdot)$ (implemented as a linear layer) maps the student embedding to the teacher's dimension. The $L_{distill}$ term is then calculated as the cosine distance between the L2-normalized projected student embedding and the L2-normalized teacher embedding:
 $L_{global\_distill} = E_x [1 - \cos(P(z_s(x)), z_t(x))]$
 Cosine alignment is used for robustness to scale and improved optimization stability. This global alignment encourages object-level temporal coherence and stability, differing from token-level alignment which might over-constrain local details or interfere with language-conditioned target selection.

### 6. Scalable Training and Deployment

To make the distillation practical at scale and avoid repeated teacher forward passes during training, the teacher global embeddings $z_t(x)$ for all training frames are **precomputed offline and stored in a cache**. During training, the student retrieves these cached embeddings. At inference time, the teacher model and the cache are completely removed, resulting in **zero inference-time overhead** for the deployed student model.

### 7. Calibrated Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)

To prevent unintended drift in the multimodal alignment and preserve general capabilities, a calibrated PEFT strategy is employed:
1. The language model and most pre-trained modules of the student backbone are kept frozen.
2. Updates are confined to two specific parts: **Vision LoRA** (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied to the visual backbone, and only the **mask decoder parameters** are calibrated. LoRA parameterizes weight updates as $W = W_0 + BA$, where $W_0$ is frozen, and only low-rank matrices $A$ and $B$ are learned. This strategy directs most injected gradients into the visual and decoding pathways, minimizing interference with the core vision-language alignment.

### 8. Training Workflow

The training workflow is structured into phases:
1. **Cache Generation (Offline):** All teacher global embeddings are computed and cached before training.
2. **Warmup Phase:** For an initial number of training steps ($t < t_w$), $\lambda(t)$ is set to 0, and only $L_{seg}$ is optimized, allowing the student to learn basic segmentation.
3. **Ramp and Hold Phase:** For subsequent steps ($t \ge t_w$), $\lambda(t)$ follows a progressive schedule, gradually increasing from 0 to a maximum value $\lambda_{max}$ (e.g., 0.012) and then holding. During this phase, both $L_{seg}$ and $\lambda(t)L_{distill}$ are optimized, with updates restricted to the calibrated PEFT parameters.
4. **Deployment:** After training, the teacher and cache are discarded, and only the fine-tuned student model is used for inference.

This integrated approach aims to imbue the Video MLLM with stable, physics-inspired spatial priors efficiently and without compromising its broader functionalities.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

PhysMLLMs demonstrated consistent improvements in language-guided video segmentation performance, particularly in challenging scenarios, while maintaining competitive performance on image-level grounding and general VLM benchmarks.

### 10. Improvements in Video Segmentation

- **Overall Performance:** On the main video segmentation benchmarks, PhysMLLMs consistently outperformed existing Video-MLLM systems. For instance, on MeVIS U, it achieved 55.1 J&F, compared to the baseline Sa2VA-InternVL3-2B's 53.9. On ReVOS, it reached 57.4 J&F (baseline 56.2), and on Ref-DAVIS17, it achieved 76.0 J&F (baseline 74.5). These improvements indicate that the representation-level prior injection is effective across diverse video datasets.
- **Challenging Scenarios:** A stratified analysis on ReVOS showed that PhysMLLMs achieved more substantial gains in difficult video conditions:
 - Small targets: +2.26 J&F (from 33.65 to 35.91)
 - Occlusion or disappearance: +1.92 J&F (from 31.29 to 33.22)
 - Distractor-heavy scenes: +2.02 J&F (from 55.95 to 57.97)
 - Fast motion: +1.37 J&F (from 49.57 to 50.94)
 - Reasoning queries: +1.76 J&F (from 51.74 to 53.50)
 These results support the hypothesis that the injected physics-inspired spatial prior specifically enhances temporal identity preservation, cross-frame spatial continuity, and robustness to visual distractors.
- **Effect of Prior Injection:** Ablation studies confirmed that the gains were directly attributable to the teacher spatial prior. Enabling distillation consistently improved segmentation scores on ReVOS and MeVIS U across various peak distillation weights ($\lambda_{max}$), suggesting that the improvements are mechanism-driven rather than incidental optimization effects. The generalization to MeVIS U, which differs from the training distribution, further indicates improved robustness.
- **Global vs. Token Alignment:** Comparisons between global and token-level DINOv2 alignment on ReVOS showed that global-only REPA-Global yielded superior performance (57.41 J&F) compared to token-only (56.26 J&F) and global+token variants (56.07 J&F). This suggests that dense patch-level constraints might over-regularize the student or interfere with the language-conditioned target selection, making a compact global constraint more effective for video segmentation.

### 11. Preservation of General Capabilities

- **Image Grounding:** On RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, benchmarks for referring image segmentation, PhysMLLMs maintained competitive accuracy (e.g., 81.9% on RefCOCO vs. baseline 81.4%). This indicates that the video-oriented prior does not degrade image-level grounding capabilities.
- **General VLM Capabilities:** Evaluations on general VLM benchmarks such as MMBench, MME, POPE, and TextVQA showed that PhysMLLMs' checkpoints (e.g., dw012) achieved scores comparable to or slightly better than the official Sa2VA-InternVL3-2B baseline. For example, MME total score was 2152.880 for PhysMLLMs dw012 compared to 2151.195 for the baseline. These results collectively suggest that the injected spatial prior improves video consistency without causing a clear collapse in general multimodal reasoning ability.
- **Distillation-Weight Sensitivity:** An analysis of distillation weight sensitivity confirmed that moderate distillation weights (e.g., $\lambda_{max}=0.012$) provided the best balance between J&F performance and temporal stability, as indicated by tIoU mean and variance.

### 12. Limitations and Failure Cases

Despite improvements, PhysMLLMs exhibited limitations in scenarios involving dense similar distractors, persistent occlusion, and extremely small targets. While gains were observed in these challenging strata, the absolute J&F scores remained significantly lower than the overall average, indicating that these problems are not fully resolved. The global nature of REPA-Global, which provides a soft, physics-inspired regularizer, may be insufficient for query-specific identity disambiguation under highly ambiguous visual conditions or for explicitly modeling complex physical forces, 3D dynamics, or object interactions.

### 13. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces PhysMLLMs, a method for injecting physics-inspired spatial continuity priors into Video MLLMs, addressing critical issues of temporal inconsistency in language-guided video segmentation. This work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Temporal Consistency:** By addressing the root cause of temporal jitter, drift, and identity switches at the representation level during training, PhysMLLMs produces more stable and coherent segmentation masks across video frames. This is particularly impactful for applications requiring reliable object tracking and consistent identity over time in dynamic environments.
2. **Improved Performance in Challenging Scenarios:** The concentrated gains in scenarios with small targets, fast motion, occlusion, distractors, and reasoning queries demonstrate the method's effectiveness where existing Video MLLMs typically struggle. This advances the robustness of video segmentation models in real-world, complex visual settings.
3. **Zero Inference-Time Overhead:** The training-stage prior injection, enabled by an offline teacher embedding cache and the removal of the teacher at inference, ensures that the improved performance does not come at the cost of increased computational load during deployment. This makes the approach practical for real-time or resource-constrained applications.
4. **Preservation of General Multimodal Capabilities:** The use of calibrated parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Vision LoRA and mask decoder calibration) is crucial. It allows the model to specialize for video consistency without significantly degrading its pre-existing image-level grounding capabilities or general multimodal reasoning abilities. This ensures the versatility of Video MLLMs, which are often expected to perform well across a broad spectrum of visual and language tasks.
5. **Conceptual Advance in Prior Injection:** The "physics-inspired spatial continuity prior" offers a novel perspective on how to imbue deep learning models with fundamental physical principles without explicit simulation. By distilling global, object-centered features from a robust self-supervised teacher (DINOv2), the method demonstrates a way to encourage object permanence and temporal coherence implicitly.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The findings regarding global versus token-level alignment suggest that carefully considering the granularity of prior injection is important for task-specific performance. The identified limitations in highly ambiguous or extremely challenging scenarios also point towards future research directions, such as incorporating more explicit 3D dynamics, object interaction modeling, or query-aware prior injection mechanisms.

In conclusion, PhysMLLMs represents a step towards developing more robust and reliable Video MLLMs for understanding and interacting with dynamic visual content, with practical implications for areas like augmented reality, video editing, surveillance, and human-computer interaction where consistent object understanding is paramount.
