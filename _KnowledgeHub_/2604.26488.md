# Featurising Pixels from Dynamic 3D Scenes with Linear In-Context Learners

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Google, TU Munich, and Munich Center for Machine Learning developed LILA, a method that learns high-resolution, pixel-accurate feature descriptors for dynamic 3D scenes by fusing semantic and geometric information from uncurated videos. LILA's novel linear in-context learning approach significantly improves performance on video object segmentation by nearly 10% JF, surface normal estimation, and semantic segmentation tasks compared to baselines, while enabling single-image inference.

## Problem

- Existing vision foundation models often produce low-resolution, patch-level feature maps, making it difficult to obtain high-resolution pixel-accurate features efficiently for fine-grained tasks.
- Current methods struggle to effectively capture spatio-temporal properties at the pixel level from dynamic 3D scenes, either being limited by static image training or focusing on higher-level video tasks.
- Utilizing abundant uncurated video data for pre-training is challenging due to the inherent noise and imperfections in automatically generated geometric and motion cues like depth and optical flow.

## Method

- **LILA** employs an encoder-decoder architecture where a frozen ==Vision Transformer== (e.g., DINOv2) provides low-resolution features, and a trained DPT decoder upsamples them to dense, pixel-level representations.
- It introduces a 'linear in-context learning' objective, where a linear mapping derived from context frame features to automatically generated cue maps (optical flow, depth, refined encoder features) is consistently applied to query frames, promoting spatio-temporal coherence.
- The training process leverages uncurated video datasets with a combined L1 reconstruction loss and an edge consistency loss, allowing the model to learn robustly from noisy, geometrically-grounded supervisory signals.

## Results

- LILA achieved substantial improvements in Video Object Segmentation on DAVIS-2017, outperforming DINOv2 baselines by nearly **10%** JF and surpassing prior methods like LoftUp and FlowFeat by 4-**5%** JF.
- The model demonstrated enhanced performance in Surface Normal Estimation on NYUv2, showing consistent improvements in RMSE and inlier ratios across various angular thresholds, effectively encoding geometric scene properties.
- LILA consistently boosted semantic segmentation quality on COCO-Stuff and ADE20K, with mIoU improvements of up to +**4.1%** for larger backbones, and also showed improved zero-shot semantic segmentation performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The linear in-context learning strategy effectively suppresses frame-specific, temporally unstable noise from automatically generated cues, resulting in more robust and consistent feature representations than direct cue prediction.
> - A synergistic combination of self-distillation, depth, and optical flow cues enables the model to jointly embed semantic, geometric, and dynamic properties into high-resolution pixel descriptors.
> - Training with video-based spatio-temporal cues allows the learned features to capture dynamic scene information, yet the model retains the ability to extract rich, dense features from a single static image during inference for practical application.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in computer vision related to obtaining high-resolution, pixel-accurate feature representations that capture both semantic and geometric properties of visual scenes, particularly in dynamic 3D contexts. Existing vision foundation models, such as the DINO family, primarily provide feature maps at a low, patch-level resolution. While upsampling techniques exist, they are often computationally inefficient or do not effectively bridge the resolution gap between training and inference.

Previous approaches to representation learning can be broadly categorized:
- **Image-based self-supervised learning:** Methods like DINO, MAE, BYOL, and CLIP pre-train on static image data. While these models learn strong semantic features, their outputs are typically patch-level, necessitating additional techniques for dense pixel-level tasks. Upsampling strategies like FeatUp and LoftUp attempt to address this but may introduce computational overhead or rely on additional mask supervision (e.g., from SAM).
- **Video-based self-supervised learning:** Acknowledging the abundance of video data, other works have explored learning from video sequences. However, many focus on action-level reasoning (e.g., VideoMAE, V-JEPA) or patch-level correspondence (e.g., CRW), which do not inherently scale to dense pixel-level prediction. Methods like FlowE and PooDLe extended concepts like BYOL to pixel-level video training by enforcing representation similarity for temporally corresponding pixels, often relying on optical flow. DORA and SAVi++ explore object-centric or slot-based learning from videos.

LILA positions itself within this landscape by:
- **Leveraging uncurated video data:** It capitalizes on the vast availability of videos for pre-training, similar to some prior video-based methods.
- **Focusing on dense pixel-level features:** It aims to directly produce high-resolution feature maps for each input pixel, addressing the limitation of low-resolution outputs from many foundation models.
- **Incorporating spatio-temporal cues:** Unlike purely image-based methods, LILA explicitly uses depth and motion (optical flow) as supervisory signals, derived from off-the-shelf networks. This allows it to embed geometric and dynamic properties.
- **Introducing a novel training strategy:** The core novelty, "linear in-context learning," differentiates it from other self-supervised approaches. This method aims to learn representations that remain consistent under a linear projection to cue maps across video frames, effectively handling the inherent noise of automatically generated cues.
- **Enabling single-image inference:** Despite training on videos and spatio-temporal cues, LILA is designed to operate on a single image at inference time, offering a practical advantage over models requiring spatio-temporal inputs during deployment.

Thus, LILA contributes to the field by proposing an efficient and effective method for learning high-resolution, spatio-temporally consistent pixel-level features, bridging the gap between powerful image-based foundation models and the need for dense predictions in dynamic scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of learning pixel-accurate feature descriptors from dynamic 3D scenes. These descriptors are intended to effectively embed spatio-temporal properties, encompassing both semantic and geometric information, in a temporally consistent manner.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and limitations in the current state of computer vision models:

1. **Demand for Pixel-Level Reasoning:** Many advanced computer vision applications require fine-grained, pixel-level understanding of visual scenes. This necessitates feature representations that can resolve details at the individual pixel level.
2. **Limitations of Existing Foundation Models:** While modern vision foundation models, such as the DINO family, have demonstrated strong capabilities in encoding semantic and geometric properties, they typically produce feature maps at a low patch-level resolution. Directly upsampling these low-resolution features to pixel-level often proves computationally inefficient and not task-effective, as it creates a discrepancy between training and inference input resolutions. The goal is to natively compute a feature vector for each input pixel.
3. **Ineffective Use of Dynamic Scene Information:** Existing frameworks often fall short in capturing spatio-temporal properties at the pixel level:
 - **Image-based pretext tasks:** Models trained on static images do not inherently account for dynamic elements, leading to representations that may lack temporal consistency and motion-awareness crucial for video understanding.
 - **Video-based pretext tasks:** While some methods leverage video sequences, they frequently focus on higher-level action recognition or sparse patch-level reasoning, failing to scale effectively to dense pixel-level prediction.
4. **Leveraging Abundant Uncurated Video Data:** Videos are an abundant data source. The research is motivated to harness this resource for pre-training, recognizing that video data can provide complementary properties (e.g., motion cues) compared to purely image-based pre-training.
5. **Addressing Noisy Supervisory Signals:** The framework proposes to utilize readily available off-the-shelf networks for estimating spatio-temporal cues like depth and optical flow. While these networks generalize well to diverse videos, their outputs are inherently noisy and imperfect. A key motivation is to design a learning strategy that can effectively train despite these imperfect supervisory signals, avoiding the need for expensive, high-quality annotations (like dense masks).
6. **Enabling Single-Frame Inference:** A practical motivation is to develop a model that, once trained on videos, can extract rich pixel-level features from a *single static image* during inference. This makes the model widely applicable without requiring continuous video streams at deployment.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the resolution and spatio-temporal limitations of existing vision models by introducing a novel video-based pre-training strategy, Linear In-Context Learning (LILA), that can effectively learn robust, high-resolution, temporally consistent pixel features from noisy, geometrically-grounded cues in uncurated videos, while maintaining single-image inference capability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LILA (Linear In-Context Learning) is a framework designed to learn pixel-accurate feature descriptors from unlabelled videos. The approach centers on an encoder-decoder architecture and a novel training strategy.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

The model consists of a frozen pre-trained vision transformer (e.g., DINOv2) acting as an encoder. This encoder produces low-resolution, patch-level feature tokens. A DPT (Dense Prediction Transformer) decoder is then attached to this encoder. The decoder utilizes skip connections from intermediate encoder feature blocks to upsample the patch-level tokens, aiming to produce a high-resolution, pixel-level feature map. Only the decoder's parameters are trained, while the encoder remains frozen.

#### 3.2. Linear In-Context Learning Principle

The core of LILA is its linear in-context learning objective. During training, the system processes pairs of adjacent video frames: a "context frame" ($I_t$) and a "query frame" ($I_{t+\Delta}$). The fundamental idea is that an optimal linear mapping ($W^*$) derived from the context frame's feature representation to its associated "cue maps" should also consistently apply to the query frame's feature representation to predict its corresponding cue maps.

Mathematically, $W^*$ is found by minimizing a ridge regression objective:
$W^* = \arg \min_W \|x_0 W - G_{context}\| + \lambda\|W\|$
where $x_0$ is the feature map produced by LILA for a crop of the context frame $C_0(I_t)$, and $G_{context}$ are the cue maps for that context frame crop. This optimization has a closed-form solution and is computationally efficient.

Once $W^*$ is determined from the context frame, the training objective for the query frame is to minimize the distance between the linearly projected query features and their corresponding cue maps:
$L_{L1} = \|x_\Delta W^* - G_{query}\|_1$
Here, $x_\Delta$ is the feature map from LILA for a crop of the query frame $C_\Delta(I_{t+\Delta})$, and $G_{query}$ are its cue maps. This formulation enforces that the learned features maintain consistency under this linear transformation across different frames, thereby promoting spatio-temporal coherence.

#### 3.3. Cue Map Construction

The cue maps ($G_{context}$, $G_{query}$) are multi-modal signals that supervise the learning process. They are derived from off-the-shelf pre-trained networks and are cropped to align with the LILA network's input crops. The cue maps combine three modalities:

1. **Optical Flow:** Forward flow ($U_\uparrow$) from $I_t$ to $I_{t+\Delta}$ and backward flow ($U_\downarrow$) from $I_{t+\Delta}$ to $I_t$. The backward flow is sign-inverted for consistency.
2. **Depth Maps:** Depth estimates ($D_t$ and $D_{t+\Delta}$) for each frame.
3. **Refined Encoder Features (Self-Distillation):** Low-resolution features from the encoder, refined using Pixel-Adaptive Map Refinement (PAMR). PAMR is adapted to refine feature grids in a coarse-to-fine manner across an image pyramid, using local affinity-based kernels to enhance image statistics without significant overhead. These refined features ($F_t$, $F_{t+\Delta}$) act as a form of self-distillation, preserving the encoder's semantic properties.

These three components are concatenated along their feature dimensionality to form the final cue maps:
$G_{context} := C_0 \circ (F_t \| D_t \| U_\uparrow)$
$G_{query} := C_\Delta \circ (F_{t+\Delta} \| D_{t+\Delta} \| -U_\downarrow)$

#### 3.4. Training Objective and Details

The total training loss for LILA is:
$L_{LILA} = L_{L1} + \gamma L_\nabla$

- **L1 Loss ($L_{L1}$):** As described above, this is the primary reconstruction loss between the linearly projected query features and the query cue maps.
- **Edge Consistency Loss ($L_\nabla$):** To encourage the network to align features with semantic structures and boundaries, a gradient matching loss is added. This loss penalizes the difference between the gradients of the projected query features and the gradients of the query cue maps, weighted by factors ($\omega_x$, $\omega_y$) that emphasize strong gradients in the cue maps. This is applied independently for horizontal and vertical axes.

**Training Process:**
- **Input:** Batches of randomly sampled adjacent frame pairs ($I_t$, $I_{t+\Delta}$) with varying temporal windows.
- **Crops:** Random aspect-preserving crops $C_0(I_t)$ and $C_\Delta(I_{t+\Delta})$ are fed to LILA in parallel.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$ and weight decay $10^{-5}$.
- **Backbones:** Initialized from DINOv2 (ViT-S14, ViT-B14, ViT-L14) and other variants like MAE and DINOv3.
- **Training Datasets:** YouTube-VOS (uncurated video sequences) and Kinetics-700 (video clips of human actions).
- **Efficiency:** Context cue grid is downsampled by a factor of 7 for faster ridge regression. Training typically converges within 30K iterations (approx. 24 hours on an A100 GPU for smaller models).

#### 3.5. Inference

At inference time, LILA operates on a single image input. The depth and optical flow networks, which were used only for supervision during training, are discarded. The trained encoder-decoder model directly produces a high-resolution feature map of the same spatial resolution as the input image.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive empirical evaluations of LILA across a diverse set of vision tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in generating high-resolution, temporally consistent, and semantically/geometrically rich pixel-level feature maps.

#### 4.1. Video Object Segmentation (VOS) on DAVIS-2017

LILA consistently achieved significant improvements over baselines and prior state-of-the-art methods in VOS, measured by mean Jaccard index ($J_m$), boundary F-score ($F_m$), and their mean ($JF$).
- **Against Baselines:** LILA based on DINOv2-S14 outperformed the DINOv2 baseline by nearly 10% $JF$ (linear probing).
- **Against Prior Work:** It surpassed methods like LoftUp by 4.3% $JF$ and FlowFeat by 4.7% (linear probing) and 5.2% (k-NN) $JF$ using DINOv2-B14. This is notable as LILA does not rely on mask supervision, unlike LoftUp.
- **Scalability:** LILA demonstrated scalability with larger backbones (DINOv2-L14) and training on larger datasets (Kinetics), further improving performance.
- **Qualitative Results:** LILA produced feature maps with high spatial detail and strong temporal consistency, enabling accurate segmentation of fine-grained and dynamic structures, such as a bicycle.

#### 4.2. Surface Normal Estimation on NYUv2

LILA exhibited enhanced performance in estimating surface normals, evaluated using Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and inlier ratios at various angular thresholds ($\delta_1$, $\delta_2$, $\delta_3$).
- **Consistent Improvement:** LILA improved all metrics across different DINOv2 backbones compared to the baselines and FlowFeat.
- **Geometric Detail:** Qualitatively, LILA's feature maps revealed finer surface structures for furniture and household items in predominantly static indoor scenes, despite being trained on dynamic outdoor scenes. This indicates effective encoding of geometric scene properties.

#### 4.3. Semantic Segmentation on COCO-Stuff and ADE20K

LILA showed consistent and notable improvements in semantic segmentation quality, measured by mean IoU (mIoU) and average pixel accuracy (pAcc).
- **Overall Gains:** LILA significantly boosted segmentation quality across all baselines and FlowFeat. The absolute improvement in mIoU increased with model size (e.g., +3.0% for DINOv2-S14, +4.1% for DINOv2-L14).
- **Scalability:** Training LILA on the larger Kinetics dataset further improved mIoU and pAcc, suggesting its capacity to scale semantic representation from uncurated videos.
- **Qualitative Results:** LILA yielded feature maps with improved spatial detail and semantic granularity, particularly enhancing accuracy at semantic boundaries for both static and dynamic elements.
- **ADE20K:** A consistent improvement in mIoU was also observed on the ADE20K benchmark.

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot Semantic Segmentation on COCO-Stuff

In a zero-shot setting, where the probe was trained on seen classes and evaluated on unseen categories, LILA outperformed the DINOv2 baseline across seen, unseen, and harmonic mIoU metrics. This indicates LILA's capacity to learn generalizable visual patterns that transfer to novel, unseen semantic categories.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Further Analysis

- **Cue Modalities:** The study confirmed a synergistic effect of the three cue modalities (self-distillation, depth, optical flow). Removing geometric and motion cues led to a significant drop in VOS-KNN accuracy (-5.3% $JF$), while each individual cue contributed positively to the overall performance.
- **Training Components:**
 - **Linear In-Context Learning vs. ERM Distillation:** A baseline directly predicting cues from a single frame ("ERM distillation") yielded consistently lower accuracy than LILA. This difference was attributed to LILA's cross-frame formulation, which suppresses frame-specific, temporally unstable noise components inherent in the external cues.
 - **PAMR Refinement:** Removing PAMR from self-distillation resulted in a noticeable drop in VOS-KNN accuracy (-2.0% $JF$), highlighting its importance.
 - **Cropping and Temporal Sampling:** Cropping (creating diverse queries) and temporal sampling (operating on video sequences) were shown to be crucial, with their removal deteriorating accuracy (-1.5% $JF$ for each).
 - **Edge Loss:** The edge consistency loss contributed to promoting semantic structures, with its removal leading to a -1.0% $JF$ drop in VOS-KNN.
- **Temporal Gap ($\Delta$):** The performance exhibited a trade-off with the temporal gap. Small $\Delta$ simplified the task leading to weaker representations, while very large $\Delta$ made cue prediction too challenging. An optimal $\Delta$ existed, and accuracy degraded gracefully for larger values.
- **Backbone Generalization:** LILA consistently improved VOS accuracy when pre-trained with diverse backbones, including MAE-B16, DINOv2-B14-Reg, and DINOv3-B16, demonstrating its generalizability.

#### 4.6. Limitations

The applicability of LILA is constrained by the quality and plausibility of the depth and optical flow maps generated by the underlying off-the-shelf models. For instance, LILA produced reasonable representations for chest X-rays, but representations for aerial imagery were confounded by shadows, indicating limitations in domains where cue estimation is unreliable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work introduces "Linear In-Context Learning" as a novel and effective pre-training strategy for encoder-decoder networks, making several significant contributions to the field of computer vision.

1. **High-Resolution, Pixel-Accurate Feature Generation:** LILA effectively addresses the challenge of producing dense, pixel-level feature maps from foundation models that typically output low-resolution representations. This is critical for tasks requiring fine-grained understanding of visual scenes.
2. **Effective Integration of Spatio-Temporal Properties:** By leveraging geometrically-grounded cues (depth and optical flow) from uncurated videos, LILA successfully embeds both geometric and semantic properties with strong temporal consistency into its pixel-level features. This allows for a more comprehensive understanding of dynamic 3D scenes.
3. **Robustness to Noisy Supervision:** The research demonstrates that linear in-context learning is capable of training effectively despite the inherent noise and inaccuracies present in cue maps estimated by off-the-shelf networks. This reduces the reliance on expensive, clean annotations, making the framework practical for large-scale application.
4. **Broad Applicability and Performance Gains:** LILA consistently achieves substantial and measurable improvements across a diverse suite of downstream tasks, including video object segmentation, surface normal estimation, and semantic segmentation, as well as showing promise in zero-shot segmentation. These empirical benefits validate the learned representation's versatility and quality.
5. **Efficiency and Practicality:** The framework is computationally inexpensive to train, converging within approximately 24 hours on a single A100 GPU for smaller variants. Crucially, it enables single-image inference, meaning that once trained, the model can extract rich spatio-temporal features from individual images without requiring video input or the external cue-generating networks, facilitating widespread deployment.
6. **Enhancement of Foundation Models:** LILA acts as an effective enhancement strategy for existing vision foundation models (e.g., DINOv2, MAE, DINOv3) by extending their capabilities to dense, high-resolution feature extraction, thereby increasing their utility for fine-grained computer vision applications.

The potential impact of LILA is broad. It provides a robust method for developing versatile and high-quality pixel-level feature descriptors that can serve as a foundation for a wide range of applications, from advanced robotics and autonomous systems that require precise environmental understanding to sophisticated video analysis and content creation tools. Its ability to learn from abundant, uncurated video data with minimal supervision also points towards more scalable and data-efficient approaches for future vision model development.
