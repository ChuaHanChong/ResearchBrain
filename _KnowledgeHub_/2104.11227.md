# Multiscale Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Multiscale Vision Transformers (MViT) integrate multiscale feature hierarchies into Transformer models, achieving competitive performance across video and image recognition benchmarks. MViT-B-32x3 performs similarly to ViViT-L on Kinetics-400 with 6.8x fewer FLOPs, and MViT-B surpasses DeiT-B on ImageNet-1K by 1.2% with 2.3x fewer FLOPs, often without requiring extensive external pre-training.

## Problem

- Vanilla Vision Transformers (ViTs) often maintained a fixed spatial resolution and channel capacity, which was suboptimal for the dense nature of visual signals and computationally expensive at high resolutions.
- Existing Vision Transformers frequently required massive pre-training on very large datasets like ImageNet-21K to achieve competitive performance against CNNs.
- Current video Transformers often neglected genuine temporal information, behaving more like "bag-of-frames" models rather than performing true spatio-temporal reasoning.

## Method

- Introduced ==Multi Head Pooling Attention (MHPA)==, which integrates learnable pooling operations into the self-attention mechanism to progressively reduce sequence length for queries, keys, or values.
- Designed a staged, ==multiscale network architecture== that hierarchically reduces spatial and temporal resolution while concurrently expanding channel capacity across its layers.
- Employed an initial =='cubification' layer== for spatio-temporal input projection and incorporated adaptive skip connections to manage varying channel dimensions and sequence lengths throughout the network.

## Results

- MViT consistently achieved state-of-the-art or competitive performance on video benchmarks (Kinetics-400/600, Something-Something-v2), often outperforming concurrent Transformer methods trained with large-scale pre-training.
- MViT models demonstrated superior efficiency; for instance, MViT-B-32x3 achieved similar Kinetics-400 accuracy to ViViT-L with **6.8x** lower FLOPs and **8.5x** fewer parameters.
- MViT showed strong temporal modeling capabilities, with a **7.1%** accuracy drop when input frames were shuffled (unlike vanilla ViT which had **-0.1%**), and achieved **83.0%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, outperforming DeiT-B by **1.2%** with **2.3x** fewer FLOPs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating multiscale feature hierarchies into Transformer architectures significantly improves efficiency and performance for visual recognition.
> - Architecturally sound Transformer designs, with strong inductive biases, can reduce the necessity for large-scale external pre-training datasets.
> - Multi Head Pooling Attention allows Transformers to effectively capture genuine spatio-temporal information, which is crucial for robust video understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of two historically rich and currently dominant paradigms in computer vision: multiscale feature hierarchies (historically associated with Convolutional Neural Networks, CNNs) and the recently popularized Transformer architecture.

Historically, computer vision models, influenced by neuroscientific studies of the visual cortex (e.g., Hubel and Wiesel's work), have long leveraged hierarchical processing. Fukushima's Neocognitron and later LeCun's Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) explicitly adopted principles of spatial downsampling and channel expansion to build progressively more complex and abstract features. Concepts like image pyramids (e.g., Laplacian and Gaussian pyramids) were developed to process visual information at multiple resolutions, reducing computational costs and providing context. CNNs, with their inherent inductive biases of locality and translation equivariance, naturally implement these multiscale hierarchies through pooling layers and increasing channel dimensions in deeper layers (e.g., ResNets, EfficientNets, SlowFast).

In contrast, the Transformer architecture, originally developed for Natural Language Processing (NLP) with its self-attention mechanism, has recently achieved remarkable success in computer vision. The Vision Transformer (ViT) by Dosovitskiy et al. demonstrated that Transformers, by treating images as sequences of patches, could achieve competitive performance with CNNs. However, initial Vision Transformers often maintained a constant spatial resolution and channel capacity throughout their layers, and frequently required massive pre-training on very large datasets like ImageNet-21K to compete with strong CNN baselines trained from scratch. This dependency on extensive pre-training and the "flat" processing of visual information were perceived limitations.

This paper critically observes that a "vanilla" application of Transformers to vision, lacking the inherent multiscale processing of CNNs, might not be optimally designed for the dense nature of visual signals, especially videos. Concurrent works, such as Video Transformer Network (VTN), TimeSformer, and ViViT, adapted ViT for video but largely retained the single-scale paradigm and continued to rely heavily on ImageNet-21K pre-training for competitive performance.

"Multiscale Vision Transformers (MViT)" fundamentally aims to address this architectural gap. By re-introducing multiscale feature hierarchies—a long-established, effective inductive bias in computer vision—into the Transformer framework, the authors seek to build more efficient, robust, and intrinsically vision-aware Transformer models. This work bridges the intellectual history of visual processing hierarchies with the global modeling capabilities of Transformers, offering a more tailored architecture for visual data that aims to reduce reliance on vast external pre-training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **integrate the seminal idea of multiscale feature hierarchies with the Transformer model** to create more effective and efficient architectures for visual recognition, particularly for video and image classification.

The key motivations driving this objective are:

- **Optimizing for Dense Visual Signals:** Visual information, especially in videos (space-time signals), is inherently dense. Traditional Transformers, which often process input at a single, fixed resolution and channel capacity, may not be optimally designed for this density. The authors posit that a multiscale approach, where early layers handle high-resolution, low-complexity features and deeper layers process spatially coarse but semantically rich, high-dimensional features, is more suitable.
- **Improving Computational Efficiency:** The quadratic complexity of self-attention with respect to sequence length makes high-resolution processing computationally expensive. By progressively reducing spatial resolution while expanding channel capacity, MViT aims to maintain a manageable computational footprint across layers, leading to better FLOPs-accuracy trade-offs.
- **Reducing Reliance on External Pre-training:** Many existing vision Transformers require massive datasets (e.g., ImageNet-21K) for pre-training to achieve competitive performance. A significant motivation for MViT is to demonstrate that a more architecturally sound design can achieve superior or competitive results *from scratch*, without such large-scale external pre-training, making the models more broadly applicable and less data-dependent.
- **Enhancing Temporal Modeling in Video:** The authors observed that many existing video Transformers (like vanilla ViT fine-tuned for video) often act as "bag-of-frames" models, neglecting genuine temporal information. A key motivation for MViT's design is to inherently encourage stronger spatio-temporal reasoning, as evidenced by its sensitivity to frame shuffling during inference.
- **Generalizability Across Vision Tasks:** The researchers aim to demonstrate the fundamental architectural advantage of MViT not just for video recognition but also for static image classification, showcasing its versatility as a general-purpose vision backbone.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the MViT architecture lies in its novel **Multi Head Pooling Attention (MHPA)** and a **staged, multiscale network design**.

#### 3.1. Multi Head Pooling Attention (MHPA)

MHPA is a crucial modification to the standard Multi Head Attention (MHA) operation. While MHA maintains fixed channel dimensions and spatio-temporal resolution, MHPA introduces a "pooling operator" P(·; Θ) that can reduce the sequence length of the query (Q), key (K), and value (V) tensors before the attention computation.
- **Pooling Operator:** This operator employs a convolution-like kernel (k), stride (s), and padding (p) to reduce the sequence length (spatial and/or temporal resolution) of an input tensor. By default, overlapping kernels are used.
- **Query Pooling:** Applying the pooling operator to the query tensor P(Q; ΘQ) directly reduces the sequence length (and thus the spatio-temporal resolution) of the attention output. This is primarily used at the beginning of each new scale stage in MViT.
- **Key-Value Pooling:** Applying pooling to key P(K; ΘK) and value P(V; ΘV) tensors reduces the length of the sequences over which attention is computed. This significantly cuts down the quadratic computational and memory costs of attention (O(L^2) becoming O(L/fQ * L/fK)), without changing the output resolution. In MViT, K,V pooling is employed in *all* MHPA blocks within a stage.
- **Multi-head mechanism:** Similar to MHA, the computation is parallelized across multiple heads, each operating on a subset of channels.

#### 3.2. Multiscale Transformer Networks (MViT Architecture)

The MViT network is structured into several "scale stages," departing from the uniform architecture of a vanilla ViT.
- **Initial Projection (cube1 layer):** Instead of large non-overlapping patches (like 16x16 in ViT), MViT starts by projecting smaller, potentially overlapping space-time "cubes" (e.g., 3x7x7) into a relatively small channel dimension (e.g., D=96). This creates a high-resolution, low-channel initial representation, contrasting with ViT's low-resolution, high-channel start.
- **Hierarchical Scaling:** As the network progresses through stages:
 - **Spatial/Temporal Resolution Reduction:** At the *beginning* of each new stage, the resolution of the feature maps is explicitly reduced by applying query pooling (sQ > 1) in the first MHPA block.
 - **Channel Expansion:** Concurrently, the channel dimension of the features is expanded (e.g., by 2x). This design aims to balance computational complexity across stages, allowing early layers to process fine-grained, high-resolution details with fewer channels, and deeper layers to integrate coarser, high-level semantics with more channels. This mimics the successful design principles of CNNs.
- **Skip Connections:** To handle the varying channel dimensions and sequence lengths between and within stages, MViT incorporates adaptive skip connections. At stage transitions where channel dimensions change, an extra linear layer is used on the layer-normalized input to match the output dimension. Within blocks, the skip connection is also pooled to match the reduced resolution of the MHPA output.
- **Positional Embeddings:** The paper explores different positional embedding strategies, finding that separate space and time embeddings (as opposed to joint or no embeddings) are most effective for video.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference Details

- **Training from Scratch:** A crucial aspect of MViT is its ability to be trained "from scratch" on Kinetics datasets, without reliance on large-scale external pre-training like ImageNet-21K. This contrasts sharply with concurrent video Transformers.
- **Optimization:** Synchronized AdamW optimizer, cosine learning rate schedule with linear warm-up, and extensive data augmentation (Mixup, CutMix, Random Erasing, RandAugment) are employed.
- **Inference:** Standard multi-clip and multi-crop testing strategies are used for evaluation.
- **Pooling Function:** Ablation studies show that a learnable channel-wise convolution for pooling (conv-pooling) yields better performance than max or average pooling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling results across a variety of video and image recognition benchmarks, demonstrating the architectural advantages of MViT.

#### 4.1. Video Recognition

- **Kinetics-400 (K400) and Kinetics-600 (K600):**
 - MViT consistently achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance. Crucially, it outperforms concurrent Transformer-based methods (VTN, TimeSformer, ViViT) *without* requiring large-scale ImageNet-21K pre-training.
 - MViT models show significantly better efficiency: for instance, MViT-B, 32x3 achieves similar accuracy to ViViT-L (pre-trained on ImageNet-21K) but with 6.8x lower FLOPs and 8.5x fewer parameters.
 - MViT-B-24 on K600 reaches 83.8% top-1 accuracy, showcasing its scalability and efficiency compared to pre-trained large models.
- **Temporal Modeling Capability:**
 - A key finding is MViT's strong temporal modeling. When input frames are randomly shuffled during inference, MViT-B experiences a significant accuracy drop of -7.1%. In contrast, a vanilla ViT-B baseline shows almost no performance degradation (-0.1%), indicating it largely ignores temporal information and performs "bag-of-frames" classification. This highlights MViT's ability to genuinely learn and utilize temporal context.
- **Something-Something-v2 (SSv2):** A dataset known for requiring strong temporal understanding, MViT-B achieves 67.7% top-1 accuracy (and MViT-B-24 68.7%), outperforming previous state-of-the-art CNNs like SlowFast R101, further validating its temporal modeling strengths.
- **Charades and AVA:** MViT also demonstrates strong performance on these long-range activity and spatio-temporal action localization datasets, showing its versatility across different video understanding tasks.

#### 4.2. Image Recognition (ImageNet-1K)

- By simply removing the temporal dimension and applying the MViT architecture to static images, MViT-B-16 achieves 83.0% top-1 accuracy. This outperforms DeiT-B (a ViT-B trained with an improved recipe) by +1.2% while using significantly fewer FLOPs (2.3x) and parameters.
- Larger MViT models (e.g., MViT-B-24-wide with 320x320 input) further push accuracy to 84.8%, again demonstrating architectural advantages over standard ViT counterparts with better efficiency.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Multiscale Design Benefit:** Even introducing a single additional resolution stage into a ViT-B model boosts accuracy (+1.5%) while significantly reducing FLOPs and memory.
- **Positional Embeddings:** Separate space and time positional embeddings proved most effective for MViT, outperforming joint or no embeddings.
- **Initial Cubification:** Using temporal extent in the initial "cubification" (cT > 1) is beneficial for performance, indicating the importance of initial spatio-temporal feature extraction.
- **Key-Value Pooling Effectiveness:** K,V pooling effectively reduces computational and memory costs while maintaining or even slightly improving accuracy, confirming its critical role in efficiency.
- **Pooling Function:** A learnable channel-wise convolution for pooling consistently outperforms max or average pooling, contributing to better performance.
- **Speed-Accuracy Trade-off:** MViT models exhibit excellent speed-accuracy trade-offs, being both significantly faster and more accurate than vanilla ViT-B baselines and competitive with highly optimized ConvNets like X3D.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Multiscale Vision Transformers" paper represents a significant step forward in the design of vision-specific Transformer architectures, with several profound implications:

- **Architectural Innovation and Convergence:** MViT successfully re-integrates a fundamental and historically proven principle of visual processing—multiscale feature hierarchies—into the modern Transformer paradigm. This marks a significant architectural innovation, moving beyond a direct translation of NLP-centric designs and proposing a more "vision-aware" Transformer, potentially leading to a convergence of insights from CNNs and Transformers.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility:** By drastically improving FLOPs and parameter efficiency compared to existing vision Transformers (e.g., 6.8x less FLOPs than ViViT-L for similar accuracy), MViT makes high-performance visual recognition more accessible. This is crucial for real-world deployment on resource-constrained devices and for researchers with limited computational budgets.
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** Achieving state-of-the-art results for video understanding *without* large-scale external pre-training (like ImageNet-21K) is a paradigm shift. This signifies that MViT's architectural design provides strong inductive biases that reduce the need for massive data-driven learning of fundamental visual features, making it highly valuable for domains where vast pre-training datasets are unavailable or task-specific datasets are small.
- **Enhanced Temporal Modeling:** The clear demonstration of MViT's ability to capture genuine temporal information (unlike "bag-of-frames" ViT models) is a critical advancement for video understanding. This opens doors for more sophisticated spatio-temporal reasoning in applications like action recognition, video generation, and autonomous driving.
- **Broad Applicability:** The success of MViT across various video tasks (classification, detection) and even static image classification highlights its versatility as a powerful general-purpose backbone for computer vision, potentially replacing or complementing traditional CNNs in many applications.
- **Future Research Directions:** MViT's success encourages further exploration into how domain-specific inductive biases can be effectively embedded into general-purpose architectures like Transformers. It paves the way for a new generation of "hybrid" or "specialized" Transformers that combine the best aspects of different deep learning paradigms for specific modalities.
