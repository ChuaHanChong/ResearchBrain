# Multiscale Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Multiscale Vision Transformers (MViT) integrate multiscale feature hierarchies into Transformer models, achieving competitive performance across video and image recognition benchmarks. MViT-B-32x3 performs similarly to ViViT-L on Kinetics-400 with 6.8x fewer FLOPs, and MViT-B surpasses DeiT-B on ImageNet-1K by 1.2% with 2.3x fewer FLOPs, often without requiring extensive external pre-training.

## Problem

- Vanilla Vision Transformers (ViTs) often maintained a fixed spatial resolution and channel capacity, which was suboptimal for the dense nature of visual signals and computationally expensive at high resolutions.
- Existing Vision Transformers frequently required massive pre-training on very large datasets like ImageNet-21K to achieve competitive performance against CNNs.
- Current video Transformers often neglected genuine temporal information, behaving more like "bag-of-frames" models rather than performing true spatio-temporal reasoning.

## Method

- Introduced ==Multi Head Pooling Attention (MHPA)==, which integrates learnable pooling operations into the self-attention mechanism to progressively reduce sequence length for queries, keys, or values.
- Designed a staged, ==multiscale network architecture== that hierarchically reduces spatial and temporal resolution while concurrently expanding channel capacity across its layers.
- Employed an initial =='cubification' layer== for spatio-temporal input projection and incorporated adaptive skip connections to manage varying channel dimensions and sequence lengths throughout the network.

## Results

- MViT consistently achieved state-of-the-art or competitive performance on video benchmarks (Kinetics-400/600, Something-Something-v2), often outperforming concurrent Transformer methods trained with large-scale pre-training.
- MViT models demonstrated superior efficiency; for instance, MViT-B-32x3 achieved similar Kinetics-400 accuracy to ViViT-L with **6.8x** lower FLOPs and **8.5x** fewer parameters.
- MViT showed strong temporal modeling capabilities, with a **7.1%** accuracy drop when input frames were shuffled (unlike vanilla ViT which had **-0.1%**), and achieved **83.0%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, outperforming DeiT-B by **1.2%** with **2.3x** fewer FLOPs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating multiscale feature hierarchies into Transformer architectures significantly improves efficiency and performance for visual recognition.
> - Architecturally sound Transformer designs, with strong inductive biases, can reduce the necessity for large-scale external pre-training datasets.
> - Multi Head Pooling Attention allows Transformers to effectively capture genuine spatio-temporal information, which is crucial for robust video understanding.
