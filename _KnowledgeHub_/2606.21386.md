# VLA-FAIL: Efficient Task Failure Detection for Finetuned Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-FAIL is a runtime task-failure detector for finetuned vision-language-action policies that needs no failure data and almost no extra compute. It fuses last-layer Mahalanobis distance, which flags out-of-distribution internal features using a fixed prior noise sample, with action chunk consistency, which flags velocity-normalized disagreement between overlapping receding-horizon chunks. Either signal crossing a conformal threshold raises an alert, matching or beating far costlier baselines while staying real-time.

## Problem

- Existing runtime failure detectors need many sampled actions or repeated diffusion-loss evaluations per timestep, adding latency that breaks real-time control and itself induces out-of-distribution states.
- Supervised failure detectors require representative failure rollouts, which are costly and unsafe to collect, while other methods depend on external object-centric perception or slow vision-language reasoning models.
- Pure novelty detection is insufficient because out-of-distribution states need not cause failure, and some failures come from internally inconsistent action generation with no visible visual anomaly.

## Method

- ==Last-layer Mahalanobis distance== scores each feature token against per-token training mean and regularized covariance, taking the maximum; token-wise statistics beat one global Gaussian.
- ==Fixed prior noise== evaluates flow-matching features at flow time zero with one frozen noise vector, removing sampling-induced feature variance at the cost of a single parallel forward pass.
- ==Action chunk consistency== takes the velocity-normalized mean absolute error between the unexecuted suffix and the next chunk's prefix, smoothed by an exponential moving average, plus the ==AUCPDT== metric.

## Results

- On six real-world tasks with two policies, the combined detector is the only method in the top three on nearly all tasks, reaching area-under-precision-recall **1.00** on Kitchen and **0.98** on Stack T.
- On Libero-Plus, the fused detector reaches **0.94-0.95** precision-recall area for the smaller policy with the lowest detection-time area, **0.17-0.19**, while baselines exceed **1000 ms** per inference versus near-zero overhead.
- Ablations confirm both design choices: per-token statistics cut the detection-time area from **0.24** to **0.19** on Drawer, and velocity normalization cuts it from **0.38** to **0.28** on the Spatial suite.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feature-space novelty and action-plan inconsistency fail on different tasks and fire at different times, so cheap complementary signals combined beat any single expensive detector.
> - Receding-horizon overlap is a free supervisory signal; consistency degrades gracefully as overlap shrinks but vanishes under fully open-loop chunk execution.
> - Detection rate alone hides latency differences, so failure-monitor evaluation should score precision and detection time jointly and threshold-independently.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a class of policies that integrate pre-trained vision-language representations with large-scale robotic datasets, demonstrating performance in robotic manipulation tasks. However, these models, even after fine-tuning, exhibit various failure modes when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. The safe deployment of VLA-controlled robots in real-world environments necessitates robust runtime failure detection mechanisms to enable timely human intervention or automatic fallback procedures.

Current approaches to task failure detection in robotics and deep learning generally fall into several categories, each with limitations that this work aims to address:
- **Data-dependent methods:** Some detectors require supervised training on explicit failure rollouts (e.g., SAFE), which are costly and potentially unsafe to collect.
- **Computationally expensive methods:** Techniques such as those relying on extensive action sampling (e.g., ACE, Diff) or complex diffusion loss evaluations can introduce significant computational overhead, hindering real-time closed-loop control.
- **Architectural assumptions:** Certain detectors are designed for specific model architectures or require external components like object-centric perception (e.g., RC-NF) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for semantic reasoning (e.g., CycleVLA, FailSafe, Sentinel, VLM-based reward models), which can be slow or limit generalizability across VLA types.
- **Insufficient signals:** Simple novelty or out-of-distribution detection based solely on observations is often insufficient because OOD states do not always imply task failure, and some failures stem from internal policy inconsistencies without obvious visual anomalies. Other methods, such as those relying on ensemble variance or single-sample techniques like action chunk entropy (FIPER), also present trade-offs in accuracy or computational load.

VLA-FAIL positions itself as a lightweight, broadly applicable framework designed to overcome these limitations. It contributes to the field by proposing a method that does not require failure data, avoids computationally intensive action sampling or external models, and is compatible with state-of-the-art VLA architectures, including flow-matching models. By combining two complementary detection signals – internal feature shifts and action plan inconsistencies – it aims for robust and early failure detection while maintaining real-time operational feasibility. The work extends existing out-of-distribution detection techniques, particularly Mahalanobis distance on last-layer features, to the context of continuous action spaces and noisy features prevalent in flow-matching VLAs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and broadly applicable runtime failure detection framework for fine-tuned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, referred to as VLA-FAIL. This overarching goal is supported by several specific objectives:

- **Accurate and Early Failure Detection:** To reliably identify task failures as they occur during robot operation and to do so proactively, before the failure state becomes irreversible or detrimental.
- **Independence from Failure Data:** To develop a detection method that does not require the collection of explicit failure rollouts, thereby mitigating the associated costs, safety risks, and logistical challenges.
- **Minimal Computational Overhead:** To ensure that the failure detection mechanism can operate in real-time alongside VLA inference, without introducing significant latency that could perturb closed-loop control or render the system impractical for real-world deployment.
- **Broad Applicability to VLA Architectures:** To design a framework compatible with diverse and state-of-the-art VLA models, including those employing flow-matching for continuous action generation.
- **Avoidance of External Models or Perception Pipelines:** To prevent reliance on additional, often slower, external modules like separate Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or dedicated object-centric perception systems, which can complicate integration and increase latency.

The motivation for these objectives stems directly from the inherent challenges of deploying sophisticated robotic policies in dynamic and unconstrained environments. While VLAs achieve high performance, their occasional unpredictable behavior in out-of-distribution scenarios poses a significant barrier to safe and reliable real-world application. The limitations of existing failure detection methods — such as their computational expense, need for failure data, or specific architectural dependencies — underscore the necessity for a new approach. A practical failure detector must be lightweight, adaptable, and capable of identifying a wide range of failure modes without prohibitive costs or delays, thereby enabling timely human intervention or autonomous error recovery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-FAIL detects task failures by integrating two distinct yet complementary signals: the Last-Layer Mahalanobis Distance (LLMD) and Action Chunk Consistency (ACC). The framework triggers a failure alert if either detector’s score surpasses its pre-calibrated threshold.

**Problem Formulation:** The work considers a VLA controlling a robotic system. At each environment timestep `t`, the VLA observes `o` and predicts a sequence of `H` actions, `a_1:H`. In a receding horizon control scheme, the first `R` (where `R ≤ H`) predicted actions are executed before the VLA is re-queried. The goal is to predict task failures before the episode’s completion time `T`.

#### 3.1. Last-Layer Token-Wise Mahalanobis Distance (LLMD)

LLMD aims to identify out-of-distribution states by quantifying deviations in the VLA’s internal feature representations relative to the distribution observed during training.
- **Feature Extraction:** The VLA is conceptualized as having a feature extractor `f: O → F`, which maps observations to a high-dimensional last-layer feature space `F` (composed of `H` feature tokens), and a final linear layer `g: F → A^H`, which projects each feature token to an action.
- **Fixed Prior Noise for Flow Matching VLAs:** For flow-matching VLAs, which typically generate actions by iteratively predicting a time-dependent flow velocity `v_t(o, a_t)` from a noisy prior `a_0 ~ N(0, I)`, feature variation is introduced by `a_t`. To isolate the observation's influence and obtain stable feature statistics, a single, fixed noise vector `a_0* ~ N(0, I)` is sampled at `t=0`. This defines a "noise-free" feature representation `f*(o) := f(o, t=0, a_0*)`. This computation can be run in parallel with action sampling or integrated into the action sampling process without additional overhead if multi-modal behavior is not required.
- **Token-Wise Mahalanobis Distance Calculation:** After VLA fine-tuning, a single, gradient-free pre-processing step is performed on the training dataset. For each feature token position `s ∈ {1, ..., H}`, the mean `μ_s` and a regularized covariance matrix `Σ_λ_s` (where `Σ_λ_s = Σ_s + λI` for numerical stability) are computed from the `f*(o_i)_s` features of the training data. During runtime, the LLMD score `s_t^LLMD(o_t)` is calculated as the maximum squared Mahalanobis distance across all token positions `s` for the current observation’s `f*(o_t)_s` features relative to the learned `μ_s` and `Σ_λ_s`. Higher scores indicate greater deviation from the training distribution and thus a higher likelihood of failure. This method does not require temporal smoothing.

#### 3.2. Action Chunk Consistency (ACC)

ACC leverages the temporal overlap inherent in receding-horizon control to detect inconsistencies in predicted action sequences, which are indicative of an unstable or failing policy.
- **Overlap Mechanism:** In receding horizon control, if `R` actions are executed from a chunk of `H` predicted actions, an overlap exists between the unexecuted suffix `a_{t-1}_{R+1:H}` of the previous action chunk and the prefix `a_{t}_{1:H-R}` of the current action chunk.
- **Velocity-Normalized Discrepancy:** ACC measures the disagreement between these overlapping action segments. The discrepancy is normalized by the robot’s average velocity `v_t^d` in each action dimension `d`. This normalization is critical to ensure that a given absolute action mismatch is weighted by the speed of motion, preventing false positives during slow, precise movements. A minimum velocity `v_min` is applied to avoid score inflation for stationary robots. The score `ˆs_t^ACC` is the velocity-normalized mean absolute error across `D` action dimensions, typically using the 3D end-effector position.
- **Temporal Smoothing:** Since transient replanning inconsistencies are normal, ACC applies an exponential moving average with a smoothing parameter `α ∈ [0, 1)` (empirically `α=0.9`) to `ˆs_t^ACC` to produce `s_t^ACC`. Persistent inconsistency, captured by the smoothed score, signals a likely task failure.

#### 3.3. VLA-FAIL

VLA-FAIL combines LLMD and ACC. A failure is detected if `(s_t^ACC ≥ τ_ACC) ∨ (s_t^LLMD ≥ τ_LLMD)`, where `τ_ACC` and `τ_LLMD` are thresholds determined by a time-constant conformal prediction band on calibration data. For evaluation with the AUCPDT metric, a fusion strategy involves rank-transforming individual scores to a uniform scale and taking their minimum, which corresponds to the logical OR operation.

### 4. Evaluation Metrics

- **Area Under Precision-Recall (AUCPR):** A standard threshold-independent metric used to assess overall detection reliability.
- **Area Under Penalized Detection Time (AUCPDT):** A novel, threshold-independent metric introduced to evaluate the trade-off between detection precision, recall, and detection latency. It quantifies the penalized detection time (PDT) for failed episodes, assigning a maximal detection time (1) if a failure is missed. Lower AUCPDT values indicate more precise and earlier failure detection.

**Experimental Setup:** The methodology includes evaluating VLA-FAIL on two state-of-the-art VLAs: `π_0.5` (a 3.6B-parameter model based on PaliGemma) and X-VLA (a 0.9B-parameter model using Florence-2-Large). Experiments are conducted across six diverse real-world manipulation tasks and the large-scale Libero-Plus simulation benchmark. Baselines for comparison include ACE (action chunk entropy), STAC (distributional divergence of overlapping action distributions), and Diff (diffusion loss, adapted to flow matching loss), which generally require multiple action samples (32 in these experiments) and are computationally more intensive.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VLA-FAIL across real-world and simulated manipulation tasks yielded several findings regarding its performance, the complementary nature of its components, and its efficiency compared to existing methods.

- **Robust and Early Detection:** VLA-FAIL, the combined detector, demonstrated performance in failure detection that was comparable to, and often superior to, significantly more computationally expensive baseline methods. The framework consistently ranked among the top three performing methods across nearly all real-world and simulated tasks. A crucial aspect of its performance is its minimal computational overhead, which enables real-time deployment, a characteristic not shared by many baselines.

- **Complementary Failure Modes:** The individual components, LLMD and ACC, were observed to capture distinct types of failure modes.
 - **ACC Effectiveness:** ACC performed well in scenarios where the robot exhibited erratic or jerky motions characteristic of out-of-distribution (OOD) situations. This was evident in tasks such as the real-world Blocks and Kitchen tasks, and across the Libero-Plus simulation suites.
 - **LLMD Effectiveness:** LLMD tended to detect failures earlier than ACC, particularly in situations where the policy might enter repetitive loops, retry actions, or execute "default" actions that do not correspond to the current environment state but where visual anomalies or erratic motion might not be immediately apparent. Examples include the real-world Cups, Stack T, and Mixer tasks, especially for the `π_0.5` model, and for X-VLA on the Libero-Plus task suites. Its ability to detect internal representation shifts without relying on external behavioral cues contributes to earlier detection.

- **Utility of AUCPDT Metric:** The newly introduced AUCPDT metric proved effective in distinguishing detectors based on their detection latency, in addition to precision and recall. While AUCPDT correlated with AUCPR, it revealed that detectors with similar overall detection rates could vary significantly in *when* they detected failures. LLMD, for example, often achieved significantly earlier detections compared to baselines, including ACC, when evaluated at comparable AUCPR levels.

- **Ablation Study Results:**
 - **Token-Wise LLMD:** Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of computing LLMD feature statistics (mean and covariance) on a per-token basis. Using a single global feature mean and covariance across all token positions resulted in significantly reduced OOD detection performance (e.g., AUCPDT increased from 0.19 to 0.24 for X-VLA on the Drawer task). This suggests that different feature tokens capture distinct aspects of the predicted action sequence, requiring individualized statistical models.
 - **Velocity Normalization in ACC:** Velocity normalization in ACC was found to be important for achieving high failure detection rates, particularly in the simulated Libero-Plus benchmark (e.g., reducing AUCPDT from 0.38 to 0.28 for X-VLA on the Spatial suite). This improvement was consistent, though smaller, across real-world tasks.
 - **ACC and Overlap Size:** ACC's performance was observed to degrade with smaller overlaps between action chunks, as anticipated. However, it maintained some failure detection capability even with a single overlapping action. The `π_0.5` model demonstrated greater robustness to shorter overlapping action sequences compared to X-VLA.

- **ACC vs. STAC:** ACC, interpreted as a velocity-normalized single-sample estimator of STAC, generally outperformed STAC on most real-world tasks. On Libero-Plus, while STAC sometimes showed higher AUCPR, ACC consistently achieved earlier failure detection (lower AUCPDT). The paper hypothesizes that ACC's advantage may stem from its focus on planning inconsistencies with *executed* action chunks, potentially reducing false detections during policy mode selection where counterfactual trajectories might otherwise lead to spurious divergence signals.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLA-FAIL framework represents a step toward enabling the safer and more reliable deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in real-world robotic systems. Its significance and potential impact stem from addressing critical limitations of current failure detection methods:

- **Enhanced Robotic Safety and Reliability:** By providing accurate and early detection of diverse failure modes without requiring failure data or incurring significant computational overhead, VLA-FAIL directly contributes to the safety and robustness of robotic manipulation tasks. This is crucial for applications where unpredictable VLA behavior could lead to damage, unsafe conditions, or task failure.
- **Practical Real-World Deployment:** The lightweight nature of VLA-FAIL allows for its integration into existing real-time VLA control pipelines, making it a practical solution for operational environments. This contrasts with many baseline methods that are too computationally intensive for real-time applications.
- **Reduced Development and Calibration Costs:** Eliminating the need for extensive failure rollouts significantly reduces the cost and complexity associated with developing and deploying failure detection systems. This also sidesteps the inherent safety risks involved in purposefully eliciting failure states during data collection.
- **Generalizability Across VLA Architectures:** The framework’s design, which leverages general principles of internal feature distributions and action plan consistency, makes it broadly applicable to different VLA architectures, including flow-matching models. This broad applicability increases its utility across the evolving landscape of robotic foundation models.
- **Improved Human-Robot Collaboration:** Early failure detection can facilitate timely human intervention, allowing operators to regain control or activate recovery procedures before a task completely fails. This enhances the human-robot collaborative experience by making robotic systems more transparent and controllable.
- **Methodological Contribution to Evaluation:** The introduction of AUCPDT provides a more comprehensive, threshold-independent metric for evaluating failure detectors in real-time systems. By jointly considering precision, recall, and detection latency, AUCPDT offers a nuanced assessment that aligns more closely with real-world operational requirements. This metric could influence future research and benchmarks in real-time anomaly and failure detection.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** While VLA-FAIL addresses many limitations, its identified boundaries (e.g., missing failures that are consistent in features and actions, such as confidently ignoring instructions) suggest avenues for future work, potentially by combining it with higher-level semantic reasoning or intent-based monitoring systems. This work lays a strong foundation for hybrid failure detection strategies.

In summary, VLA-FAIL offers a robust, efficient, and practical solution that addresses key challenges in VLA deployment, thereby advancing the state of the art in safe and reliable robotic autonomy.
