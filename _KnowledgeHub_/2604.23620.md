# Move-Then-Operate: Behavioral Phasing for Human-Like Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Move-Then-Operate framework introduces a structurally disentangled Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that explicitly separates robotic manipulation into distinct 'move' and 'operate' phases. This approach achieved an average success rate of 68.9% on the RoboTwin2 benchmark, demonstrating a 24.1% absolute improvement over monolithic baselines, alongside enhanced data and training efficiency.

## Problem

- Monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies conflate coarse relocation movements and fine, contact-critical manipulation within a single objective function.
- The heterogeneous nature of these distinct motor behaviors leads to optimization challenges, instability, and gradient interference.
- Larger action amplitudes from 'move' frames can overwhelm learning signals for finer-grained 'operate' phases, hindering precision in contact-rich interactions.

## Method

- A hierarchically gated dual-expert policy, **Move-Then-Operate**, with disjoint parameters for 'E_Move' (coarse relocation) and 'E_Operate' (fine manipulation).
- A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) router determines expert activation per action chunk, conditioned on a semantic summary from a shared Vision-Language Model backbone.
- Supervised learning with ground-truth phase labels trains experts via Conditional ==Flow Matching== (CFM) and the router via cross-entropy, ensuring orthogonal parameter updates and expert specialization.
- An automated phase-aware auto-labeling pipeline, using a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) with structural constraints and self-refinement, generates high-fidelity ground-truth phase labels.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **68.9%** across 8 RoboTwin2 tasks, representing a **24.1%** absolute improvement over the monolithic π₀ baseline (**44.75%**) with a limited budget of 50 demonstrations per task.
- Demonstrated enhanced data efficiency, outperforming baselines trained on 10 times more data in contact-rich tasks (e.g., 'Press Stapler' at **93%** vs. π*₀.₅ at **80%**, 'Click Bell' at **99%** vs. π*₀.₅ at **75%**).
- Exhibited superior training efficiency, achieving rapid convergence during both multi-task pre-training and task-specific fine-tuning, often reaching near-peak performance in notably fewer training steps.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the learned routing mechanism; random or reversed expert selection led to severe performance degradation (e.g., from **68.88%** to **25.63%** with random selection).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human motor control naturally segregates into distinct 'move' (rapid approach) and 'operate' (precise adjustments) phases, suggesting an effective strategy for robotic manipulation.
> - Explicitly decoupling these heterogeneous behaviors into specialized experts mitigates optimization instability and gradient interference, allowing each expert to master its specific dynamics.
> - Structural inductive biases, such as phase decomposition, can be more impactful than simply scaling up dataset size for certain contact-rich manipulation tasks.
