# Safe Learning of Locomotion Skills from MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Munich Institute of Robotics and Machine Intelligence developed LocoSafeDAGGER, an iterative learning algorithm that enables safer and more sample-efficient acquisition of locomotion policies for quadrupedal robots from a Model Predictive Control (MPC) expert. The algorithm reduces failure rates during training and improves push recovery capabilities by actively intervening with the MPC expert during unsafe states, while matching the robustness and tracking performance of policies trained via Behavioral Cloning.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for legged robots is sample-inefficient and lacks inherent safety mechanisms, making it prone to failures in real-world deployment.
- Behavioral Cloning (BC) from expert demonstrations suffers from distribution shift, leading to compounding errors as policies encounter states not represented in the initial training data.
- Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers robust behaviors but is computationally intensive at runtime and can struggle with handling uncertainties, limiting its direct high-frequency application.

## Method

- Proposed LocoSafeDAGGER, an iterative online learning algorithm that adapts Data Aggregation (DAGGER) and SafeDAGGER for legged robots, combining a neural network policy with an **==MPC==** expert.
- Implemented a safety policy (πsafety) to actively monitor robot states during policy rollouts, enabling the MPC expert to immediately take over and stabilize the robot upon detecting unsafe conditions.
- Targeted data collection for the training dataset by only recording state-action pairs when the MPC expert intervened due to a safety violation or during an additional stabilization phase at the end of rollouts, focusing on critical states.

## Results

- LocoSafeDAGGER demonstrated a consistently low failure rate during data collection (below **5%** after the first iteration), significantly outperforming LocoDAGGER which had higher failure rates as expert influence decayed.
- The algorithm achieved comparable robustness and velocity tracking performance to Behavioral Cloning policies, while also exhibiting superior sample efficiency, requiring four times fewer policy rollouts than LocoDAGGER for similar database sizes.
- Policies trained with LocoSafeDAGGER showed superior push recovery capabilities across most impulse angles compared to both Behavioral Cloning and LocoDAGGER, attributed to the collection of data in specific unsafe or recovery scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active safety monitoring with immediate expert intervention during online learning significantly reduces catastrophic failures, enabling safer policy training for physical robot deployment.
> - Targeted data collection in critical or unsafe states, rather than random expert influence, leads to more sample-efficient learning and policies that are more robust to external disturbances.
> - Iterative learning algorithms can effectively mitigate the distribution shift problem inherent in Behavioral Cloning by continuously expanding the training dataset with relevant out-of-distribution states encountered by the policy.
