# Memory as Plans: World-Action Modeling with Memory-Grounded Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> MaP-WAM splits memory-dependent robot manipulation into memory-grounded planning and plan-conditioned execution. A VLM language planner and a causal video world model turn sparse frames from completed segments into a language-visual plan. A fixed-context World-Action-Progress executor then generates actions plus task progress, which gates segment transitions. It reaches 83.3% on RMBench and 88% on a real-world memory task at constant latency.

## Problem

- Language-summary memories such as MemER and Mem-0 compress history into text, discarding the fine-grained visual and spatial evidence that precise manipulation needs, like object identities and initial locations.
- Causal world-action models with growing visual windows keep rich history, but executor latency and GPU memory scale with context length, eventually running out of memory on long episodes.
- Fixed windows truncate distant history, and continually updated latent memories overwrite earlier evidence, so non-Markovian tasks needing information from long-past observations remain unreliable.

## Method

- ==Memory-grounded language-visual planning==: a fine-tuned Qwen3.5-4B VLM predicts the next segment instruction from keyframes, then a WAN-2.2 causal world model generates a visual plan from long-term sparse context.
- **World-Action-Progress** model: a ==Mixture-of-Transformers== extension of WAN-2.2 that jointly ==flow-matches== future video, action chunks and continuous progress, with the visual plan cached as a static prefix.
- ==Closed-loop progress control==: plan-observation alignment calibrates predicted progress to the best-matching plan frame by pixel difference, and a 0.95 progress threshold triggers replanning and appends real-frame memory.

## Results

- Achieves **83.3%** overall success on RMBench, beating DP, pi0.5, X-VLA, Mem-0 and LingBot-VA; Swap T reaches **96%** versus **88%** for LingBot-VA and **14%** for Mem-0.
- On a real Franka arm, reaches **88%** on Find Button and **68%** on Press Buttons over 50 trials each, where baselines succeed only by random guessing or fail entirely.
- Executor latency stays near **827ms** per chunk, while full-context conditioning reaches about **4x** latency at 1,500 frames and runs out of memory at 1,700; removing progress calibration drops success from **96.7%** to **73.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating long-horizon memory processing from the real-time controller lets history shape decisions through a compact plan, keeping execution cost constant regardless of episode length.
> - Visual plans grounded in real past observations preserve details that language plans lose; world models conditioned only on the current frame cannot substitute for episodic visual memory.
> - Treating task progress as a predicted and conditioned modality disambiguates visually identical phases, but autoregressive progress drifts on long segments unless re-anchored to an external reference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World-Action Models (WAMs). However, a significant portion of these models operates under a Markovian assumption, meaning they base decisions primarily on the current observation or a short, fixed history. This approach is often insufficient for complex, real-world manipulation tasks that are inherently non-Markovian. Such tasks require recalling information from distant past observations, which may no longer be directly perceptible, making long-horizon memory crucial for reliable policy execution.

Existing memory mechanisms in embodied control generally fall into a few categories, each with limitations:
- **Language Summaries:** These methods convert interaction history into compact linguistic abstractions. While compact and interpretable, they risk losing fine-grained visual and spatial details necessary for precise manipulation. For example, methods like MemER, Mem-0, and MEM rely on generating subtask instructions or recursively updated language summaries.
- **Continually Updated Memory:** Approaches that maintain updatable latent states or memory banks can struggle to retain task-relevant information over long horizons, as earlier evidence might be compressed or overwritten.
- **Growing Visual Windows:** These mechanisms retain a window of recent visual observations. Causal WAMs, such as LingBot-VA, extend this by modeling visual dynamics and actions over an increasing prefix of episodic observations. While preserving richer perceptual evidence, this approach leads to rapidly increasing computational and GPU-memory costs as the history length grows, creating a trade-off between history coverage and inference efficiency. Fixed windows, on the other hand, truncate distant history.

This work positions itself to address the limitations of growing visual windows, specifically the increasing computational burden, while also retaining the fine-grained visual evidence often lost in language-only memory systems. It proposes a decoupling of long-horizon visual context processing from short-horizon action generation, aiming for an efficient and visually grounded memory system for non-Markovian robotic tasks. The paper builds upon developments in generalist robotic policies, including VLA models and WAMs, as well as goal- and plan-conditioned policies, but distinguishes itself by grounding plans in accumulated episodic context and incorporating a mechanism for aligning execution progress with generated plans.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic policy capable of robustly handling memory-dependent, non-Markovian manipulation tasks efficiently. The authors identify two main challenges with existing approaches:

1. **Loss of Fine-Grained Visual Evidence:** Memory mechanisms relying on language summaries, while compact, often discard precise visual and spatial information critical for tasks requiring accurate object identification, location recall, or differentiation between visually similar states. This can impair performance on tasks that demand high perceptual fidelity from historical context.
2. **Trade-off between History Coverage and Execution Efficiency:** Methods that maintain a growing window of visual observations (e.g., causal WAMs) preserve rich perceptual detail. However, conditioning action generation on an ever-expanding frame-wise history significantly increases computational load and GPU memory consumption, leading to higher inference latency and potential out-of-memory errors for long-horizon tasks. This necessitates a compromise between the length of history that can be considered and the efficiency of policy execution.

Motivated by these challenges, the paper introduces MaP-WAM, a Memory-as-Plans framework, with the following detailed objectives:

- **Decouple Memory-Grounded Planning from Plan-Conditioned Execution:** The central aim is to separate the processing of long-horizon visual history for planning from the immediate action generation process. This allows the long-term history to influence segment-level plans without directly burdening the real-time executor at every control step.
- **Maintain Fixed Executor Context Length:** By decoupling, the executor's input context length can remain constant regardless of the total task history length. This addresses the computational and memory scalability issues of growing window approaches, aiming for consistent and low execution-time latency.
- **Preserve Fine-Grained Visual Grounding:** The system aims to convert multimodal episodic context into compact plans that include not only language instructions but also fine-grained visual guidance, ensuring that critical perceptual details from long-term memory are retained and utilized for execution.
- **Enable Adaptive Variable-Duration Execution:** Real-world task segments often have variable and initially unknown durations. The policy needs to adaptively determine when a plan segment is complete and subsequently transition to the next planning stage.
- **Mitigate Cumulative Drift in Progress Prediction:** For tasks with long execution horizons, recursively predicting progress can lead to error accumulation. The system aims to introduce a mechanism for calibrating progress to maintain alignment with the planned visual evolution.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance on Memory-Dependent Tasks:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate that the proposed framework can outperform existing methods on benchmarks and real-world tasks specifically designed to test long-horizon memory and complex manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MaP-WAM (Memory-as-Plans World-Action Modeling) decomposes memory-dependent robotic manipulation into two main components: memory-grounded planning and plan-conditioned execution.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Memory-as-Plans Decomposition

The problem is framed as sequential decision-making where a policy $\pi(a_{t+1:t+h} | f_{\le t}, s_t, l)$ predicts an action chunk given the language instruction ($l$), current proprioceptive state ($s_t$), and observation sequence ($f_{\le t}$). The key challenge is that critical information for action generation may reside in historical observations $f_{<t}$.

MaP-WAM addresses this by decoupling the problem. Instead of the executor repeatedly processing a dense, growing sequence of past observations, long-horizon visual context processing is assigned to memory-grounded planning, and short-horizon action generation to plan-conditioned execution.

#### 3.2. Memory-Grounded Planning

This module is responsible for synthesizing a compact, multimodal plan for the next segment using the accumulated history.

- **Structured Multimodal Episodic Context:** Execution history is maintained as segment-level records, $C_i = \{l_i, G_i\}$, where $l_i$ is the language instruction for segment $i$, and $G_i$ is a sparse visual context (fixed-length sequence of 8 frames uniformly sampled from the real execution trajectory of segment $i$). The initial observation $f_0$ is stored as $G_0$. The multimodal context $C_{<k}$ (all records before segment $k$) provides historical evidence.
- **Memory-Grounded Language-Visual Planning:** This process is factorized into two steps:
 1. **Language Planner ($\pi^l_P$):** A Vision-Language Model (VLM, fine-tuned Qwen3.5-4B) predicts the next segment-level language plan $\hat{l}_k$ based on the global instruction ($l$), completed segment instructions ($l_{<k}$), and a compact set of keyframes ($f^\star$) extracted from $G_{<k}$ (initial frame and last frame of each completed segment).
 2. **Visual Planner ($\pi^v_P$):** A Causal World Model (CWM, initialized from WAN-2.2-5B) generates a visual plan $\hat{G}_k$ as fine-grained guidance. This is conditioned on the full long-term sparse visual context $G_{<k}$, the predicted language plan $\hat{l}_k$, and the global instruction $l$. The CWM is trained with a flow-matching objective.
- **Causal Attention for Visual Planning:** The CWM uses a block-causal attention mask. Tokens corresponding to completed segments form a cacheable prefix, and attention within this prefix prevents future leakage across segments. This design allows the key-value states of completed evidence to be computed once and reused in subsequent planning stages, improving efficiency.

#### 3.3. World-Action-Progress (WAP) Modeling

This module executes the memory-grounded plan over an unknown number of control steps.

- **World-Action-Progress Model:** WAP extends a pretrained video Diffusion Transformer (WAN-2.2) with action and progress experts, forming a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture. It jointly models future visual dynamics, robot actions, and execution progress.
 - **Inputs:** The segment plan $C_k = \{\hat{l}_k, \hat{G}_k\}$, current observation ($f_t$), proprioceptive state ($s_t$), and current progress ($p_t$).
 - **Outputs:** Future visual latent, an action chunk ($a_{t+1:t+h}$), and a corresponding progress sequence ($p_{t+1:t+h}$).
 - **Progress as a First-Class Modality:** Progress ($p_t = \frac{t-i}{j-i}$ for a segment from $i$ to $j$) is treated as a continuous coordinate, jointly generated and fed back as a conditioning signal. This provides an explicit temporal anchor, disambiguating visually similar states at different semantic stages without expanding the observation window.
- **Structured Attention and Plan-Prefix Caching:** WAP encodes the visual plan $\hat{G}_k$ as a static clean prefix. The structured attention mask allows dynamic tokens to attend to the plan and current state, while keeping the plan prefix independent and cacheable throughout segment execution. The cache is refreshed only upon a new segment plan generation.
- **Training Objective:** WAP is trained using a conditional flow-matching objective for future visual states, action chunks, and progress sequences, with shared conditions. The future visual prediction serves as an auxiliary objective and can be omitted during inference.

#### 3.4. Closed-Loop Planning and Execution

This mechanism ensures adaptive transitions and context updates.

- **Plan-Observation Alignment for Progress Calibration:** Since ground-truth progress is unavailable during deployment, predicted progress can accumulate errors. The visual plan $\hat{G}_k$ provides a temporally indexed visual reference. At each step, nearby plan frames (from $\hat{G}_k$) are compared with the current observation ($f_t$) using mean absolute pixel difference. The current progress condition is then calibrated towards the progress index of the best-matching plan frame, anchoring execution to the planned visual evolution and improving progress estimation robustness.
- **Progress-Gated Segment Transition:** The predicted progress provides a criterion for segment completion. MaP-WAM averages the predicted progress over the latest actions. If this score exceeds a predefined threshold ($\tau=0.95$), the current segment is deemed complete, execution of the current plan stops, and memory-grounded planning for the next segment is invoked. Crucially, the real execution observations of the just-completed segment are uniformly resampled into sparse visual context $G_k$ and appended to the episodic context $C_{<k+1}$, ensuring memory is grounded in real observations rather than generated predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MaP-WAM was evaluated in both simulation and real-world robotic tasks, demonstrating improvements in performance and efficiency compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Benchmark:** Evaluated on RMBench, a simulation benchmark for long-horizon memory-dependent manipulation, featuring M(1) tasks (requiring one task-relevant past observation) and M(n) tasks (requiring multiple task-relevant past observations).
- **Performance:** MaP-WAM achieved an overall success rate of 83.3% across RMBench tasks, surpassing all representative baselines including DP, $\pi^{0.5}$, X-VLA, Mem-0, WLA-0, and LingBot-VA.
 - **Specific Task Successes:**
 - **Swap T:** Achieved 96% success (compared to 88% for LingBot-VA and 14% for Mem-0), indicating strong performance on tasks requiring precise memory of initial object locations.
 - **Press Button:** Achieved 96% success (compared to 84% for LingBot-VA and 0% for other baselines), demonstrating ability to disambiguate visually similar but semantically distinct execution states.
 - **Observe and Pick Up:** Improved success rate from 9% (strongest baseline) to 19%, despite its high complexity (distinguishing dozens of object types, reasoning over separated observations and spatial relations) and limited demonstrations.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Setup:** Experiments used a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 robot arm with third-person and wrist-mounted cameras.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Find Button:** Robot observes button colors, buttons are occluded, then human provides a color instruction. Robot must find the corresponding button. MaP-WAM achieved an 88% success rate over 50 trials. Baselines could achieve non-zero success only via random selection.
 - **Press Buttons:** Robot observes two numbers, presses left and middle buttons corresponding times, then presses a right confirmation button. MaP-WAM achieved a 68% success rate over 50 trials. Baselines failed on this more demanding task.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis

- **Memory-Grounded Visual Planning:** Ablation studies on "Observe and Pick Up" and "Swap T" demonstrated the importance of visual planning.
 - **"w/o visual plan" variant:** Removing the CWM planner and conditioning WAP only on language plans reduced success rates. This indicates the value of visual guidance.
 - **"w/o visual memory" variant:** Replacing CWM with a current-observation-conditioned world model further reduced success. This showed that visual guidance must be grounded in long-horizon episodic evidence, not just the current observation, to preserve object identities and spatial configurations.
- **Progress Modeling:** Ablation experiments across "Blocks Ranking Try," "Cover Blocks," and "Press Button" highlighted the contributions of different progress modeling components.
 - **"classification" baseline:** Simply predicting segment completion (without explicit progress) yielded the lowest average success rate (37.0%).
 - **"w/o progress condition" variant:** Removing the current progress $p_t$ from WAP's conditioning input substantially degraded performance on "Press Button" (18% success). This task involves visually similar phases (pressing/releasing), and the progress condition provides a crucial temporal state signal for disambiguation.
 - **"w/o progress calibration" variant:** Disabling plan-observation alignment led to a success rate of 73.7% (compared to 96.7% for MaP-WAM), with the largest drop on "Blocks Ranking Try." This task is significantly longer (avg. 423.10 steps vs. 116.32), where autoregressive progress prediction accumulates errors. Progress calibration effectively mitigated this drift.
- **Inference Efficiency:**
 - **Visual Planner Latency:** MaP-WAM's visual planner maintains a sparse visual context (two latent timesteps per completed subtask). With causal attention and KV caching, planning latency remains efficient even after 16 consecutive segments, as completed evidence becomes a cacheable prefix.
 - **Executor Latency:** MaP-WAM's WAP model, by caching a fixed plan prefix, maintained an approximately constant action-chunk latency of 827ms. In contrast, a "Full Context" variant (conditioning the executor on growing frame-wise history) showed rapidly increasing latency. Without KV caching, it grew linearly; even with KV caching, it incurred roughly 4x the zero-history latency with 1,500 historical frames and resulted in an out-of-memory error at 1,700 frames (exceeding 80 GB GPU memory). This highlights MaP-WAM's ability to decouple memory from execution costs effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MaP-WAM framework addresses a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation: enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks that require recalling past information, without incurring prohibitive computational costs. By decomposing memory-dependent world-action modeling into memory-grounded planning and plan-conditioned execution, the paper introduces a novel approach to manage long-term memory efficiently.

The significance of this work lies in several key areas:

- **Enhanced Performance on Non-Markovian Tasks:** MaP-WAM demonstrates superior performance on tasks requiring long-horizon memory and fine-grained visual reasoning in both simulation (RMBench) and real-world settings. This indicates a step towards more capable and reliable autonomous robotic systems that can tackle complex, multi-step operations beyond reactive control.
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The framework's ability to maintain a fixed executor context length and leverage KV caching in both planning and execution is a substantial contribution. This directly addresses the escalating inference latency and GPU memory consumption issues faced by previous approaches that process growing visual histories. This efficiency is critical for deploying robots in real-time environments where consistent, low-latency control is essential.
- **Integration of Multimodal Memory and Planning:** The explicit use of language plans alongside detailed visual guidance (visual plans) provides a robust multimodal representation of intent and desired outcomes. The causal world model's ability to generate fine-grained visual guidance from sparse, long-term visual context is a key innovation for ensuring visual grounding.
- **Robust Execution through Progress Modeling and Calibration:** The introduction of progress as a first-class modality, jointly predicted with actions, allows for adaptive segment transitions and dynamic execution durations. The plan-observation alignment mechanism for progress calibration is particularly important, as it mitigates the cumulative errors inherent in autoregressive progress prediction over extended task horizons, thereby enhancing the robustness of execution.

Potential impacts include:

- **Broader Adoption of AI in Robotics:** By making long-horizon, memory-dependent tasks more tractable and efficient, MaP-WAM could accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robots in diverse applications, from industrial automation to service robotics and assistive technologies.
- **Inspiration for Future Architectures:** The decoupling of planning and execution, along with the structured management of episodic memory, offers an architectural blueprint that could inspire future designs for generalist robotic policies, moving beyond purely reactive systems.
- **Advancements in World Modeling:** The development and application of the Causal World Model for generating visual plans from sparse context contributes to the broader field of world modeling, particularly in how long-term perceptual information can be leveraged for future state prediction.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges that MaP-WAM currently relies on pre-segmented demonstrations for organizing memory and progress. Extending the framework to automatically discover segments from unsegmented demonstrations would be a natural progression. Additionally, the plan-observation alignment mechanism utilizes a simple, training-free matching metric. Future work could explore learned similarity measures to enhance calibration robustness in more visually complex or cluttered environments.
