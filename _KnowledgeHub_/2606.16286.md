# FlowMPC: Improving Flow Matching policies with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces FlowMPC, a framework from Stanford University, that enhances imitation-learned Flow Matching (FM) policies by integrating them with a learned world model for test-time planning. This method significantly improved end-of-episode success rates on robotic manipulation tasks, increasing from 93.14% to 97.44% on PickCube and from 56.81% to 66.41% on PickSingleYCB, without altering the FM policy's original training objective.

## Problem

- Flow Matching (FM) policies, as pure imitation learning methods, are limited by the quality and coverage of demonstration data, often struggling to recover from errors or adapt to out-of-distribution states.
- While FM policies are proficient at generating diverse action trajectories, their test-time actions do not directly optimize for task return, leading to suboptimal performance in maximizing task success and robustness.
- Current approaches to enhance FM policies often involve integrating reinforcement learning directly into the policy's training objective, representing a fundamental shift in how the policy learns.

## Method

- **FlowMPC** combines an imitation-learned Flow Matching (FM) policy with a learned world model and ==Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)== planning to refine actions at inference time.
- During inference, the FM policy proposes candidate action trajectories, which the learned world model evaluates for predicted future outcomes and rewards.
- The world model, an adapted TD-MPC2 framework, includes a behavior cloning action predictor (π_BC), simplified reward/value prediction targets, and a modified representation interface that reuses the FM policy's visual latent.

## Results

- FlowMPC consistently improved performance on robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating enhanced anytime and end success rates on both PickCube-v1 and PickSingleYCB-v1.
- End-of-episode success rates saw substantial gains: from **93.14%** to **97.44%** (an increase of **4.3** percentage points) on PickCube and from **56.81%** to **66.41%** (an increase of **9.6** percentage points) on PickSingleYCB.
- Anytime success rates also improved, from **95.78%** to **98.68%** on PickCube, and from **68.77%** to **69.78%** on PickSingleYCB, with the latter showing overlapping confidence intervals suggesting less clear statistical significance for transient success on that task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World-model-based planning can effectively enhance the robustness and end-of-episode success of imitation-learned FM policies without modifying their original training objective.
> - By leveraging the FM policy as a structured generative prior for action trajectories, the framework combines the strengths of multimodal action generation with the predictive power of world models for lookahead evaluation.
> - The approach provides a complementary strategy to existing RL-based FM refinements, focusing on improving decision-making at inference time rather than through new policy-learning objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the fields of imitation learning, model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), and generative modeling for robotic control. It specifically addresses limitations of Flow Matching (FM) policies within the context of behavior cloning and explores a method for enhancing their performance at inference time.

Flow Matching has emerged as a method for behavior cloning, particularly in multimodal action spaces relevant to robotic manipulation. Unlike traditional regression-based behavior cloning, FM-based policies are designed to capture the diversity and trajectory-level structure of expert demonstrations by learning action distributions. However, as an imitation learning approach, FM policies are inherently limited by the quality and coverage of their training data. They learn to reproduce expert behavior rather than to maximize task-specific returns directly. This can lead to difficulties in recovering from errors or unexpected states not well-represented in the expert dataset, a common challenge in robotic tasks where small errors can compound.

Prior research has attempted to address these limitations by integrating reinforcement learning (RL) into flow-based policies. These approaches typically involve modifying the FM training objective to incorporate RL principles, thereby refining the policy through direct optimization for task return.

Concurrently, the field of model-based reinforcement learning has advanced significantly, with learned world models enabling lookahead planning for complex control tasks. Methods like TD-MPC2 combine learned dynamics, reward, and value models with planning in latent space, demonstrating scalability and robustness in high-dimensional continuous-action problems. In such frameworks, a learned actor typically proposes candidate actions for the planning loop.

This work differentiates itself by exploring a hybrid approach that integrates these two lines of research—flow-based imitation learning and world-model-based planning—without modifying the fundamental training objective of the Flow Matching policy. Instead of refining the policy itself through RL, the proposed method leverages the FM policy as a mechanism to propose rich, multimodal action trajectories, which are then evaluated and refined by a learned world model during test-time planning. This positions the work as complementary to existing RL-based FM refinement methods, focusing on improving decision-making at inference time rather than through new policy-learning objectives. The specific combination of an FM-based imitation policy with a TD-MPC2-style world model for planning is presented as an underexplored area, and the paper evaluates whether this hybrid framework can practically enhance manipulation performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether a learned world model can enhance the performance of Flow Matching (FM) policies without altering the FM training objective. The motivation for this objective stems from specific characteristics and limitations of FM policies when applied to robotic manipulation tasks:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Pure Imitation Learning:** Flow Matching policies are effective for behavior cloning, especially in scenarios with multimodal action spaces where multiple successful trajectories exist. This makes them suitable for robotic manipulation, which often involves high-dimensional observations and diverse solutions. However, as imitation learning methods, FM policies are trained solely to replicate expert demonstrations. They do not directly optimize for expected task return. This dependency on demonstration data means their performance is bounded by data quality and coverage. In practical scenarios, this can lead to policies that perform well on in-distribution states but struggle to recover from minor errors, handle compounding mistakes, or adapt to states not adequately represented in the expert dataset.

2. **Improving Test-Time Policy Actions:** The core problem identified is that while FM policies are proficient at generating diverse action trajectories (their primary strength), there is "still room to improve how FM policies act at test time" in terms of maximizing task success and robustness. Current approaches to improve FM policies often involve integrating reinforcement learning directly into the policy's training objective, which represents a fundamental shift in how the policy learns.

3. **Exploring an Alternative Enhancement Mechanism:** This work proposes a different strategy: rather than modifying the FM policy's learning objective or directly refining it with RL, the aim is to leverage a world model to improve decision-making *at inference time*. The motivation is to allow the FM policy to continue generating its rich, multimodal action trajectories (what it "is already good at"), and then use a separate, learned world model to critically evaluate these proposed trajectories and select the most promising one based on predicted future outcomes.

4. **Leveraging Model-Based Planning for Robustness:** By integrating a learned world model, the policy gains a lookahead capability. This allows it to anticipate the consequences of its actions and plan accordingly, potentially enabling it to recover from unforeseen situations or errors that a purely imitative policy might fail to handle. The specific choice of Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) planning, built upon advancements like TD-MPC2, aims to provide a scalable and robust planning framework for high-dimensional continuous-action problems.

In summary, the key objective is to demonstrate that model-based planning, using a learned world model, can serve as an effective complement to imitation-learned FM policies, enhancing their robustness and end-of-episode success without requiring changes to the underlying flow-matching training paradigm. This approach seeks to combine the strengths of generative imitation learning (multimodal action priors) with the predictive power of world models (evaluating trajectories for future return).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FlowMPC framework integrates an imitation-learned Flow Matching (FM) policy with a learned world model for test-time planning. The methodology involves a multi-stage training paradigm and several architectural adaptations to an existing world model framework.

#### 3.1. Overall Training Paradigm

The training pipeline consists of five sequential stages:
1. **Expert Demonstration Collection:** A Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) policy, with access to privileged state information, is initially trained to serve as a robust expert.
2. **Trajectory Data Collection:** Full expert trajectories are collected. These trajectories include RGB observations, proprioceptive robot state, actions, and rewards.
3. **Flow Matching Policy Training:** An FM policy is trained using these collected expert trajectories. This policy is implemented as a conditional 1D temporal U-Net that predicts a vector field over an action sequence, suitable for multimodal trajectory generation. It is conditioned on proprioceptive state and a learned visual latent from a visual encoder.
4. **Visual Latent Preprocessing:** The expert trajectories are reprocessed. The FM policy's visual encoder is used to generate compact visual latents (z_viz,t) from the RGB observations. These latents are then combined with the robot's proprioceptive state (s_t) to form a combined input x_t = [s_t, z_viz,t] for the world model. This step allows the world model to reuse the FM policy's visual representation, simplifying its training.
5. **World Model Training:** The world model is trained online. Its replay buffer is preloaded with the preprocessed expert data.

#### 3.2. Inference Time Planning

During inference, the FM policy acts as a structured proposal distribution within an MPPI planner:
1. **Observation Processing:** Given the current observation, the FM policy's visual encoder computes a visual latent (z_viz,t) once.
2. **Trajectory Proposal:** The MPPI planner samples K candidate action trajectories. A subset of K_π trajectories are directly proposed by the FM policy, while the remaining K - K_π trajectories are sampled randomly. In the final configuration, K=512 and K_π=24.
3. **World Model Rollout:** Each candidate trajectory is rolled out forward in the learned world model. The world model predicts future states and rewards.
4. **Trajectory Scoring:** Each rolled-out trajectory is scored based on the sum of its predicted rewards across the planning horizon, augmented by a terminal value estimate. This terminal value is calculated using a behavior-cloning action predictor (π_BC) on the predicted terminal state, paired with an action it proposes.
5. **MPPI Refinement:** MPPI reweights and refines the sampled trajectories based on their scores.
6. **Action Execution:** The first action from the optimized action sequence is executed in the environment.

#### 3.3. TD-MPC2 Adaptations

The world model is based on TD-MPC2, but with several modifications to integrate effectively with the FM policy:
1. **Behavior Cloning Action Predictor (π_BC):** A new network, π_BC, is added. It maps the current model state (x_t) to an action (â_t = π_BC(x_t)). This predictor is used during training and, crucially, at the end of planning rollouts to provide an in-distribution action for terminal value bootstrapping, enhancing planning stability.
2. **Modified Representation Interface:** The world model directly consumes the FM visual latent (z_viz,t) concatenated with the proprioceptive state (s_t) as its input x_t. This simplifies the architecture by reusing the FM policy's learned visual features, avoiding redundant visual encoders, and allowing the representation model to leverage the frozen encoder's output.
3. **Simplified Reward and Value Prediction Targets:** For the binary reward structure of the ManiSkill tasks, the complex discretized symlog-transformed rewards and values of standard TD-MPC2 are replaced. A binary cross-entropy loss is used for reward prediction, and a standard mean-squared-error objective is used for Q-value regression.
4. **State-Space Variant of World Model:** In addition to the standard latent-space TD-MPC2 design, a state-space variant is implemented.
 - **Latent Version:** Follows standard TD-MPC2, using a learned representation, supporting multi-step rollouts, and trained from temporally contiguous slices.
 - **State-Space Version:** Omits the learned latent encoder, predicting directly in the space of concatenated proprioceptive state and FM visual latent. This decouples gradients, and it uses a simpler one-step training setup with single-transition tuples (x_t, a_t, r_t, x_t+1).
 - The choice between these variants is task-dependent; PickCube utilized the state-space model (H=1), while PickSingleYCB used the latent-space model (H=3).

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

The world model's components are trained with separate objectives:
- **Dynamics Model:** L_dyn = ||â_t+1 - x_t+1||²₂ (predicts next model state).
- **Reward Model:** L_rew = BCEWithLogits(r_t, r_t) (predicts binary task reward).
- **Value Model (Q-functions):** L_Q = Σ_i=1^N_Q ||Q_i(x_t, a_t) - y_t||²₂. The target y_t is constructed as r_t + γ Q_targ(x_t+1, π_BC(x_t+1)), where Q_targ is the minimum over the target Q-ensemble.
- **Behavior Cloning Head:** L_BC = ||π_BC(x_t) - a_t||²₂ (predicts expert actions from state).

In the state-space implementation, these losses are applied to one-step transitions. In the latent implementation, training uses contiguous slices, unrolls the model across the planning horizon, and includes a consistency loss between predicted and encoder-produced latents. Reward, value, and behavior-cloning losses are applied at each step with a down-weighting schedule.

#### 3.5. Planning Objective

At test time, the planner evaluates each candidate action sequence (indexed by k) using the following objective:
J^(k) = Σ_h=0^H r^(k)_t+h + Q(x^(k)_t+H+1, π_BC(x^(k)_t+H+1)).
This sums predicted rewards over the horizon and adds a terminal value estimate derived from the behavior-cloning policy at the end of the rollout. MPPI then refines trajectories based on these scores, and the initial action of the optimized sequence is executed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated FlowMPC, which combines an FM policy with a learned world model and MPPI planning, against a standalone FM policy baseline. The evaluation was conducted on two robotic manipulation tasks from ManiSkill: PickCube-v1 and PickSingleYCB-v1. Performance was measured using anytime success rate and end success rate over 50-step episodes.

**Overall Performance Improvement:**
Across both tasks, augmenting the FM policy with the learned world model and MPPI planning consistently improved performance compared to the FM policy alone.

**Improvements in End Success Rate:**
The most substantial gains were observed in the end-of-episode success rate.
- **PickCube:** End success rate increased from 93.14% ± 0.77 (FM policy) to 97.44% ± 0.48 (FlowMPC).
- **PickSingleYCB:** End success rate increased from 56.81% ± 1.52 (FM policy) to 66.41% ± 1.45 (FlowMPC).
These results indicate that the planning mechanism, enabled by the world model, contributes to the agent's ability to correct minor action errors and maintain stable control until the episode concludes, rather than only achieving momentary success.

**Improvements in Anytime Success Rate:**
Anytime success rates also showed improvement, though the magnitude of the gains was generally smaller than for end success.
- **PickCube:** Anytime success rate rose from 95.78% ± 0.62 (FM policy) to 98.68% ± 0.35 (FlowMPC).
- **PickSingleYCB:** Anytime success rate increased from 68.77% ± 1.42 (FM policy) to 69.78% ± 1.41 (FlowMPC). Notably, for PickSingleYCB, the 95% confidence intervals for anytime success rates overlapped between FlowMPC and the FM baseline. This suggests that while there was an increase, the statistical significance of the improvement in transient task completion for this specific task was not as clear as for sustained success.

**Comparison to TD-MPC2 Baseline:**
On the PickSingleYCB task, the FM baseline achieved approximately 60% success, which is close to the reported performance of TD-MPC2. FlowMPC, with the addition of the learned world model, surpassed this level of performance.

**Summary of Findings:**
The results demonstrate that world-model-based planning provides a consistent benefit when combined with a fixed FM policy. The most pronounced advantage of FlowMPC lies in tasks requiring precise, sustained control until the end of an episode, where it significantly enhances the policy's ability to maintain success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a framework, FlowMPC, that combines Flow Matching (FM) policies with learned world models for improved control, highlighting several points of significance and potential impact.

**Enhanced End-of-Episode Success:** The most notable finding is the significant improvement in end-of-episode success rates on both evaluated manipulation tasks. This suggests that the integration of a world model and planning is particularly effective in the final, often critical, stages of a task. In manipulation, successfully completing a task often requires not only reaching a goal state but also maintaining stability and control thereafter. The world model's predictive capability allows the agent to anticipate consequences and refine actions, helping it to sustain successful states rather than just briefly achieving them.

**Complementary Approach to Imitation Learning:** The research demonstrates a method to enhance FM policies without altering their original training objective. Traditional approaches to improve imitation-learned policies often involve incorporating reinforcement learning (RL) directly into the policy training, which changes the fundamental learning process. FlowMPC, however, treats the imitation-learned FM policy as a strong generative prior for action trajectories and uses the world model purely for evaluation and refinement during inference. This offers a complementary strategy that leverages the strengths of FM (generating rich, multimodal action sequences) while mitigating its limitations (dependency on demonstration coverage and lack of direct return maximization) through model-based planning. This approach could be beneficial for scenarios where modifying a well-trained generative policy's objective is complex or undesirable.

**Bridging Imitation Learning and Model-Based RL:** This work represents an exploration into a hybrid methodology that connects expressive generative imitation policies with model-based reinforcement learning principles. By demonstrating that a strong multimodal action prior from FM can be effectively combined with a world model for lookahead planning, it suggests a promising direction for creating more robust and adaptive agents. Such hybrid systems could potentially combine the sample efficiency of imitation learning with the adaptability and error recovery capabilities of model-based planning.

**Practical Implications for Robotics:** The application to robotic manipulation tasks in ManiSkill showcases a practical benefit. In robotics, policies must often operate in environments with high degrees of freedom, visual complexity, and the potential for cumulative errors. Improving a policy's ability to maintain stable control and recover from minor errors, particularly towards task completion, is directly relevant to deploying more reliable robotic systems in real-world scenarios.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The study acknowledges its limitations, primarily the evaluation on only two manipulation tasks. The observation that different world model configurations (state-space with H=1 for PickCube, latent-space with H=3 for PickSingleYCB) yielded optimal results suggests that the effectiveness of the method may depend on careful design choices regarding representation and planning horizon, rather than a single universal configuration. This indicates a need for further investigation into the robustness of these design choices across a broader range of tasks and environments. Future work could also explore different world model architectures or alternative planning algorithms to potentially achieve larger gains or more generalizable performance.

In conclusion, FlowMPC provides evidence that world-model-based planning can meaningfully enhance the performance of flow-based imitation policies in robotic manipulation. This is achieved by leveraging the policy's capacity to generate diverse action trajectories and using a learned world model to evaluate and refine these proposals, leading to improved task success, especially in critical end-of-episode phases, without modifying the core imitation learning objective.
