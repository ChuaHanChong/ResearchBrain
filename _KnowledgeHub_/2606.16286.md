# FlowMPC: Improving Flow Matching policies with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces FlowMPC, a framework from Stanford University, that enhances imitation-learned Flow Matching (FM) policies by integrating them with a learned world model for test-time planning. This method significantly improved end-of-episode success rates on robotic manipulation tasks, increasing from 93.14% to 97.44% on PickCube and from 56.81% to 66.41% on PickSingleYCB, without altering the FM policy's original training objective.

## Problem

- Flow Matching (FM) policies, as pure imitation learning methods, are limited by the quality and coverage of demonstration data, often struggling to recover from errors or adapt to out-of-distribution states.
- While FM policies are proficient at generating diverse action trajectories, their test-time actions do not directly optimize for task return, leading to suboptimal performance in maximizing task success and robustness.
- Current approaches to enhance FM policies often involve integrating reinforcement learning directly into the policy's training objective, representing a fundamental shift in how the policy learns.

## Method

- **FlowMPC** combines an imitation-learned Flow Matching (FM) policy with a learned world model and ==Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)== planning to refine actions at inference time.
- During inference, the FM policy proposes candidate action trajectories, which the learned world model evaluates for predicted future outcomes and rewards.
- The world model, an adapted TD-MPC2 framework, includes a behavior cloning action predictor (π_BC), simplified reward/value prediction targets, and a modified representation interface that reuses the FM policy's visual latent.

## Results

- FlowMPC consistently improved performance on robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating enhanced anytime and end success rates on both PickCube-v1 and PickSingleYCB-v1.
- End-of-episode success rates saw substantial gains: from **93.14%** to **97.44%** (an increase of **4.3** percentage points) on PickCube and from **56.81%** to **66.41%** (an increase of **9.6** percentage points) on PickSingleYCB.
- Anytime success rates also improved, from **95.78%** to **98.68%** on PickCube, and from **68.77%** to **69.78%** on PickSingleYCB, with the latter showing overlapping confidence intervals suggesting less clear statistical significance for transient success on that task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World-model-based planning can effectively enhance the robustness and end-of-episode success of imitation-learned FM policies without modifying their original training objective.
> - By leveraging the FM policy as a structured generative prior for action trajectories, the framework combines the strengths of multimodal action generation with the predictive power of world models for lookahead evaluation.
> - The approach provides a complementary strategy to existing RL-based FM refinements, focusing on improving decision-making at inference time rather than through new policy-learning objectives.
