# Structured 3D Latents for Scalable and Versatile 3D Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> TRELLIS generates versatile 3D assets from text or image prompts using Structured Latents (SLAT), a unified representation combining sparse 3D voxel grids with dense DINOv2 multiview visual features, decodable into Radiance Fields, 3D Gaussians, or meshes. Two rectified flow transformers generate structure then latents. Scaled to 2B parameters on 500K assets, it surpasses prior methods and supports tuning-free local editing.

## Problem

- Prior 3D generative models tie themselves to one representation (mesh, point cloud, Radiance Field, or Gaussians), each strong at either geometry or appearance but rarely both.
- Unstructured or representation-specific latents (triplanes, point sets) complicate using a consistent network architecture across different 3D output formats.
- Existing latent approaches either need costly, lossy pre-fitting to align 3D data with a chosen representation or lack a dedicated encoder capturing fine appearance and geometry jointly.

## Method

- ==Structured Latents (SLAT)==: local latent vectors attached to active voxels on a sparse 64-cube grid, fusing multiview ==DINOv2== features per voxel to jointly encode geometry and appearance.
- ==Sparse transformer VAE== with ==shifted-window attention== encodes SLAT and decodes it via representation-specific heads into Gaussians, Radiance Fields, or meshes without per-format retraining of the encoder.
- Two-stage ==rectified flow transformer== pipeline: a sparse-structure generator (G_S) produces active voxel layout, then a sparse latent generator (G_L) fills local latents, plus tuning-free ==Repaint==-based region editing. Together this forms **TRELLIS**.

## Results

- On Toys4k reconstruction, SLAT beats LN3Diff, 3DTopia-XL, and CLAY, reaching PSNR **32.74**, LPIPS **0.025**, Chamfer Distance **0.0083**, and F-score **0.9999**.
- Text/image-to-3D generation on Toys4k shows large FID gains over baselines, e.g. FD-dinov2 of **237.48** (XL model) versus **460.07** for GaussianCube and **756.37** for 3DTopia-XL.
- User study over 100 participants preferred TRELLIS in **67.1%** of text-to-3D and **94.5%** of image-to-3D comparisons; ablations confirm rectified flow and larger 64-cube/**2B**-parameter models improve quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A sparse-structure-plus-dense-feature latent design can decouple 'where geometry exists' from 'what it looks like,' enabling one latent space to serve multiple downstream 3D representations.
> - Reusing a powerful pretrained 2D vision encoder (DINOv2) for 3D feature extraction avoids building a dedicated 3D encoder and removes costly representation-specific data pre-fitting.
> - Rectified flow transformers consistently outperform diffusion baselines at matched model size, suggesting the generative paradigm choice matters even when architecture and data are held fixed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of AI-Generated Content (AIGC) has witnessed tremendous progress, especially in 2D image generation, where models like Diffusion Transformers (DiT) have enabled highly effective tools. However, 3D AIGC has lagged, facing unique challenges due to the inherent complexity and diversity of 3D data representations.

Existing 3D generative models typically fall into two main categories:
- **Native 3D Generative Models:** These models learn directly from 3D data, using various representations such as meshes, point clouds, voxel grids, Triplanes, Radiance Fields (like NeRFs), or 3D Gaussians. While some excel in geometry modeling (e.g., mesh-based methods), they often struggle with detailed appearance. Conversely, models based on Radiance Fields or 3D Gaussians achieve high-quality rendering but find it difficult to extract plausible geometries or are constrained by costly pre-fitting processes. Furthermore, the diverse and often structured/unstructured nature of these representations complicates the development of a unified modeling paradigm.
- **2D-Assisted 3D Creation:** Methods like DreamFusion and its successors leverage powerful pre-trained 2D diffusion models to optimize or generate multiview images for 3D reconstruction. While benefiting from the strong generalization of 2D models, these approaches frequently suffer from multiview inconsistencies, leading to lower geometry quality in the generated 3D assets.

A key limitation across both categories is the **lack of a unified latent space** for 3D data. Unlike 2D generation, where a consensus has emerged around unified latent spaces for diverse image synthesis tasks, 3D generation has been fragmented by representation-specific approaches. This fragmentation hinders quality, versatility (e.g., converting between formats), and efficient high-resolution modeling.

This paper directly addresses this gap by introducing a **unified Structured LATent (SLAT) representation**. By integrating sparse 3D structures with dense visual features, SLAT aims to bridge the divide between geometry and appearance modeling, enabling high-quality generation across multiple 3D formats. Furthermore, the adoption of **rectified flow transformers**, a relatively newer generative paradigm challenging diffusion models in 2D and video synthesis, positions this work at the forefront of exploring efficient and scalable generative modeling for complex 3D data. The paper positions itself as a step towards "3D-representation-agnostic asset modeling," a significant shift from the current representation-specific focus in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a unified and versatile latent space that facilitates high-quality 3D generation across various representations, accommodating diverse downstream requirements.**

The authors are motivated by several critical shortcomings in the existing 3D generative modeling landscape:
- **Quality Gap with 2D AIGC:** Despite recent progress, 3D generative models still fall short in generation quality compared to their highly advanced 2D counterparts. This limits their practical utility in industries like gaming, film, and virtual reality.
- **Lack of Unified Representation:** 3D data exists in myriad formats (meshes, point clouds, implicit fields, volumetric representations like Radiance Fields and 3D Gaussians), each with its strengths and weaknesses for different applications. This diversity prevents a standardized 3D generative modeling paradigm, complicating interoperability and versatile usage.
- **Trade-off between Geometry and Appearance:** Current models often excel at either geometry generation or appearance modeling, but rarely both simultaneously with high fidelity. Methods focused on surface representations (e.g., meshes) struggle with detailed textures, while volumetric methods (e.g., Radiance Fields) may produce less plausible geometries.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** Generating high-resolution 3D data efficiently remains a challenge. Many existing methods are computationally intensive or require costly pre-fitting processes for 3D data.
- **Limited Versatility and Editing Capabilities:** Most models are tied to a specific output format, restricting their application. Flexible 3D editing capabilities, such as local modifications without affecting the entire structure, are often missing or require extensive fine-tuning.

By tackling these challenges, the authors aim to create a **"powerful 3D generation foundation"** that can unlock new possibilities for the 3D vision community, moving towards a more representation-agnostic approach to 3D asset modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method revolves around a novel unified 3D latent representation called **Structured LATent (SLAT)** and a two-stage generative pipeline based on rectified flow transformers.

#### 3.1. Structured Latent Representation (SLAT)

SLAT is designed to comprehensively capture both structural (geometry) and textural (appearance) information of a 3D asset. It consists of a set of local latents `(z_i, p_i)`, where `p_i` are positional indices of "active voxels" (voxels intersecting the object's surface in a 3D grid, typically 64x64x64 resolution), and `z_i` are local latents attached to these voxels. This sparse representation (L ≪ N³) allows for efficient high-resolution modeling and introduces locality for flexible editing.

#### 3.2. SLAT Encoding and Decoding

- **Visual Feature Aggregation:** To derive the local latents `z_i`, the method aggregates visual features from dense multiview images of the 3D asset. Images are rendered from randomly sampled camera views around the object. A powerful pre-trained vision foundation model, **DINOv2**, is used to extract feature maps from these images. Features corresponding to each voxel are projected onto these maps, and their average is used as the visual feature `f_i` for that voxel. This bypasses the need for a dedicated 3D encoder and costly 3D data pre-fitting.
- **Sparse VAE for Structured Latents:** A transformer-based Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is used to encode the voxelized features `f` into structured latents `z` and then decode `z` back into a 3D asset. The VAE encoder and decoder utilize a shared transformer structure adapted for sparse voxels, serializing input features from active voxels and incorporating sinusoidal positional encodings. To handle locality and improve efficiency, **3D shifted window attention** is incorporated.
- **Decoding into Versatile Formats:** SLAT supports decoding into multiple 3D representations via distinct decoders (D_GS, D_RF, D_M):
 - **3D Gaussians (D_GS):** Each `z_i` is decoded into multiple 3D Gaussians with specific position offsets, colors, scales, opacities, and rotations, constrained to the vicinity of their active voxel.
 - **Radiance Fields (D_RF):** Each `z_i` is decoded into CP-decomposed parameters representing a local radiance volume (8x8x8). These local volumes are then assembled to form the complete radiance field.
 - **Meshes (D_M):** Each `z_i` is decoded into parameters for FlexiCubes, including signed distance values for voxel vertices, allowing extraction of meshes from 0-level isosurfaces. This decoder includes convolutional upsampling blocks to increase output resolution to 256x256x256.
 In practice, the VAE is trained end-to-end using 3D Gaussians due to their fidelity and efficiency. Decoders for other formats are trained separately by freezing the learned encoder and structured latents, demonstrating SLAT's strong extensibility.

#### 3.3. Structured Latents Generation (TRELLIS)

A two-stage rectified flow transformer pipeline is employed for generation:
- **Sparse Structure Generation (G_S):** In the first stage, the sparse active voxel structure `{p_i}` is generated. This is achieved by converting the sparse voxels into a dense binary 3D grid `O`, which is then compressed into a low-resolution feature grid `S` using a simple 3D convolutional VAE. A transformer backbone `G_S` generates `S`, which is then decoded back to `O` and finally to the active voxel structure.
- **Structured Latents Generation (G_L):** In the second stage, the local latents `{z_i}` are generated conditioned on the previously generated structure `{p_i}`. A transformer `G_L` designed for sparse structures processes noisy latents, packing them into a shorter sequence before serialization for efficiency. It uses sparse convolutions for downsampling/upsampling and time-modulated transformer blocks.
Both `G_S` and `G_L` are trained separately using the conditional flow matching (CFM) objective. Conditions (text or image prompts) are injected via cross-attention layers using features from pre-trained CLIP (for text) or DINOv2 (for image).

#### 3.4. 3D Editing with Structured Latents

SLAT's design enables tuning-free 3D editing:
- **Detail Variation:** By preserving the generated coarse structure and re-executing the second generation stage with different text prompts, variations in appearance and fine-grained geometry details can be achieved.
- **Region-Specific Editing:** Leveraging the locality of SLAT, specific regions (defined by a bounding box of voxels) can be edited. The method adapts Repaint to modify the flow models' sampling process, generating new content within the target region conditioned on the unchanged areas and new prompts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors present extensive experimental results demonstrating the superiority of their method across reconstruction, generation, and editing capabilities.

#### 4.1. Reconstruction Fidelity

- Compared against latent point clouds (3DTopia-XL), latent vector sets (CLAY), and latent triplanes (LN3Diff), SLAT significantly outperforms all baselines in both appearance fidelity (higher PSNR, lower LPIPS for rendered images) and geometry quality (lower Chamfer Distance, higher F-score for point clouds, higher PSNR-N, lower LPIPS-N for rendered normal maps).
- The results highlight SLAT's strong versatility, as it can faithfully reconstruct 3D assets into diverse formats (3D Gaussians, Radiance Fields, meshes) even when primarily trained with Gaussians.

#### 4.2. Generation Results (TRELLIS)

- **Qualitative Excellence:** Visual examples (Figure 4, 5, 12, 13, 14) showcase unprecedented quality in generated 3D assets. The models produce vivid appearances with vibrant colors and intricate textures (e.g., radio speaker grille, toy blaster scratches) and highly detailed geometries with complex structures and sharp edges (e.g., bulldozer's hollow cab, police robot equipment). The generated assets also exhibit strong adherence to both text and image prompts, including handling challenging features like translucency.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** Comparisons with state-of-the-art methods like InstantMesh, LGM (2D-assisted), GaussianCube, Shap-E, 3DTopia-XL, and LN3Diff (3D generative) consistently show TRELLIS's advantage. 2D-assisted methods suffer from multiview inconsistencies, while other 3D generative models often yield featureless appearances or less plausible geometries due to latent representation limitations.
- **Quantitative Dominance:** On the Toys4k dataset, TRELLIS significantly surpasses all compared methods across all evaluated metrics:
 - **Text-to-3D:** Achieves highest CLIP score (26.70) for prompt alignment, and lowest Fréchet Distance (FD) and Kernel Distance (KD) for quality (FD_incep: 20.48, KD_incep: 0.08, FD_dinov2: 237.48, KD_dinov2: 4.10, FD_point: 5.21), indicating superior overall quality and diversity.
 - **Image-to-3D:** Similarly demonstrates highest CLIP score (85.77) and lowest FD/KD metrics (FD_incep: 9.35, KD_incep: 0.02, FD_dinov2: 67.21, KD_dinov2: 0.72, FD_point: 2.03).
- **User Study Preference:** A user study with over 100 participants confirmed human preference for assets generated by TRELLIS. The method was strongly preferred in 67.1% of text-to-3D cases and 94.5% of image-to-3D cases, significantly outperforming all other methods.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **SLAT Size:** Increasing the resolution of SLAT from 32³ to 64³ provided a significant boost in reconstruction fidelity, justifying the chosen default setting.
- **Rectified Flow vs. Diffusion:** Rectified flow models consistently outperformed diffusion models at both stages of the generation pipeline, demonstrating better generation quality and prompt alignment.
- **Model Size:** Larger models (from 342M to 2B parameters) consistently improved generation performance, indicating the scalability of the architecture.

#### 4.4. Applications

- **3D Asset Variations:** The method successfully generates diverse 3D asset variations while preserving the overall coarse structure, guided by text prompts (e.g., changing material or style of a log cabin).
- **Region-Specific Editing:** Tuning-free local editing, including removal, addition, and replacement of parts (e.g., changing a robot's arm to a track, adding a river to an island), was demonstrated with high coherence to text or image prompts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of 3D AI-Generated Content (AIGC), offering a powerful foundation for scalable and versatile 3D asset creation.

**Key significance points include:**
- **Unified and Versatile 3D Latent Representation:** The introduction of SLAT is a crucial step towards a standardized paradigm for 3D generative modeling. By allowing decoding to various common 3D formats (Radiance Fields, 3D Gaussians, meshes) from a single latent representation, it addresses a long-standing challenge of interoperability and format-agnostic generation. This is particularly impactful as it avoids the need for costly and often lossy conversions between representations.
- **Unprecedented Quality and Detail:** The generated 3D assets achieve a remarkable level of quality, combining vivid appearances with intricate geometric details. This moves 3D AIGC closer to the fidelity seen in 2D image generation, making the outputs more readily usable in professional contexts.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** Leveraging rectified flow transformers and a two-stage generation pipeline tailored for sparsity, the method demonstrates efficiency, generating high-quality assets in approximately 10 seconds. Training on a large dataset of 500K diverse objects with up to 2 billion parameters showcases its scalability.
- **Flexible Editing Capabilities:** The built-in support for tuning-free detail variation and region-specific editing from text or image prompts offers practical utility for creators, enabling iterative refinement and creative control over generated assets, which is a major bottleneck in current 3D workflows.
- **Robustness from Foundation Models:** The integration of powerful 2D vision foundation models like DINOv2 for latent encoding effectively leverages their strong 3D awareness and detailed representation capabilities, circumventing the need for complex 3D-specific encoders.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to release code, models, and data is highly significant. This will foster transparency, reproducibility, and accelerate further research and development in the 3D vision community, potentially leading to widespread adoption and new applications.

**Potential impacts:**
- **Digital Content Creation:** Revolutionize industries like gaming, virtual reality, augmented reality, and film production by enabling rapid prototyping, asset generation, and personalized content at scale.
- **Democratization of 3D Creation:** Lower the barrier for creating high-quality 3D assets, allowing designers, artists, and even casual users to generate complex 3D models from simple text or image prompts without extensive 3D modeling expertise.
- **Foundation for Future 3D AI:** Serve as a powerful baseline and building block for future research in 3D scene generation, virtual world building, and other complex 3D AI tasks.
- **Academic Impact:** The novel SLAT representation and the successful application of rectified flow transformers for 3D generation will likely inspire new research directions in representation learning and generative modeling for 3D data.

In conclusion, this paper presents a compelling solution to critical challenges in 3D AIGC. By providing a unified, high-quality, scalable, and editable 3D generation framework, it lays a strong foundation for the next generation of 3D content creation tools and applications.
