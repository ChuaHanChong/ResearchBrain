# RationalVLA: A Rational Vision-Language-Action Model with Dual System

> [!summary] Summary
> RationalVLA introduces a dual-system vision-language-action model and a new benchmark (RAMA) to enable robots to discern and reject defective natural language instructions while effectively executing valid, unseen commands. The model achieves superior performance in handling both types of instructions, demonstrating enhanced robustness and generalization in simulation and real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models assume perfectly aligned instructions, leading to a lack of robustness and generalization in realistic scenarios with flawed commands.
- Existing dual-system VLA approaches often struggle to fully transmit complex language comprehension to the low-level policy or lack robust mechanisms for handling defective instructions.
- There was no standardized benchmark specifically designed to evaluate a robot's ability to discern and reject problematic natural language instructions in manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Rational Manipulation (RAMA)== benchmark, a new dataset of over 14,000 language instructions designed to evaluate rational decision-making by including six dimensions of defective instructions alongside complex, unseen executable tasks.
- Proposes **RationalVLA**, a novel dual-system Vision-Language-Action model that integrates a pre-trained Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) with a pre-trained robot policy.
- Employs learnable latent space embeddings using special `` and `` tokens in the MLLM's vocabulary to transmit high-level action intent or a clear rejection signal to the low-level policy.

## Results

- RationalVLA significantly outperformed all baselines on the RAMA benchmark, achieving a **14.5%** higher success rate on final tasks and a **0.94** higher average task length.
- In real-world experiments, RationalVLA achieved an **85%** success rate on basic tasks with unseen instructions and an **80%** success rate on long-horizon tasks involving defective instructions, dramatically surpassing baselines (e.g., **16.7%** and **8.3%** respectively).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of CLIP loss for latent space alignment and chat-like expression for preserving MLLM language capabilities, contributing to improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training models to reject defective instructions via a dedicated rejection token is crucial for building rational and robust robotic systems.
> - A dual-system architecture that separates high-level reasoning from low-level control, while allowing for effective information transfer via latent embeddings, can overcome limitations of monolithic VLAs and decoupled VLM-as-identifier systems.
> - Augmenting datasets with diverse defective instructions and using chat-like expressions during MLLM fine-tuning significantly enhances the model's ability to understand and reason about language.
