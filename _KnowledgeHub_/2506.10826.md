# RationalVLA: A Rational Vision-Language-Action Model with Dual System

> [!summary] Summary
> RationalVLA introduces a dual-system vision-language-action model and a new benchmark (RAMA) to enable robots to discern and reject defective natural language instructions while effectively executing valid, unseen commands. The model achieves superior performance in handling both types of instructions, demonstrating enhanced robustness and generalization in simulation and real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models assume perfectly aligned instructions, leading to a lack of robustness and generalization in realistic scenarios with flawed commands.
- Existing dual-system VLA approaches often struggle to fully transmit complex language comprehension to the low-level policy or lack robust mechanisms for handling defective instructions.
- There was no standardized benchmark specifically designed to evaluate a robot's ability to discern and reject problematic natural language instructions in manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Rational Manipulation (RAMA)== benchmark, a new dataset of over 14,000 language instructions designed to evaluate rational decision-making by including six dimensions of defective instructions alongside complex, unseen executable tasks.
- Proposes **RationalVLA**, a novel dual-system Vision-Language-Action model that integrates a pre-trained Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) with a pre-trained robot policy.
- Employs learnable latent space embeddings using special `` and `` tokens in the MLLM's vocabulary to transmit high-level action intent or a clear rejection signal to the low-level policy.

## Results

- RationalVLA significantly outperformed all baselines on the RAMA benchmark, achieving a **14.5%** higher success rate on final tasks and a **0.94** higher average task length.
- In real-world experiments, RationalVLA achieved an **85%** success rate on basic tasks with unseen instructions and an **80%** success rate on long-horizon tasks involving defective instructions, dramatically surpassing baselines (e.g., **16.7%** and **8.3%** respectively).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of CLIP loss for latent space alignment and chat-like expression for preserving MLLM language capabilities, contributing to improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training models to reject defective instructions via a dedicated rejection token is crucial for building rational and robust robotic systems.
> - A dual-system architecture that separates high-level reasoning from low-level control, while allowing for effective information transfer via latent embeddings, can overcome limitations of monolithic VLAs and decoupled VLM-as-identifier systems.
> - Augmenting datasets with diverse defective instructions and using chat-like expressions during MLLM fine-tuning significantly enhances the model's ability to understand and reason about language.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence, particularly language-conditioned robotic manipulation, is a critical area for enabling robots to operate effectively in human environments. Historically, research in this domain has largely focused on equipping robots to understand and execute natural language instructions to perform specific manipulation tasks.

Early works in language-conditioned manipulation, such as those employing text encoders to map language to latent features, laid the groundwork for policy learning from natural language. More recently, the advent of large pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) and large language models (LLMs) has significantly advanced the field, leading to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like RT-2 and RoboFlamingo. These models leverage the immense language generalization capabilities of VLMs, fine-tuning them on robotic datasets to handle complex and previously unseen instructions. However, the paper identifies a key limitation: these VLA models often assume that instructions are perfectly aligned with the environment, leading to a lack of robustness and generalization in realistic scenarios. Issues like "catastrophic forgetting" during fine-tuning (where language abilities degrade) and sub-optimal manipulation performance compared to task-specific policies further highlight the challenges.

To address these limitations, a growing trend in VLA research is the adoption of "dual-system" architectures. These approaches decouple high-level reasoning and planning (often handled by a powerful VLM or LLM) from low-level, high-frequency control (managed by a specialized robot policy). Examples include LCB, HiRT, RoboDual, DP-VLA, GR00T N1, and OpenHelix. While these dual systems improve upon monolithic VLAs by better integrating reasoning and control, the authors point out that they often still fall short in fully transmitting complex language comprehension to the policy or lack robustness against "defective instructions."

Concurrently, a distinct but related area of research has focused on handling defective instructions, particularly in human-robot interaction. Previous works like Ob-VLN and BadNaver have explored scenarios where instructions may be ambiguous, irrelevant, or even malicious, primarily within the context of vision-language navigation tasks. These studies highlight the safety and efficiency implications of robots blindly attempting to execute flawed commands.

This paper's work, "RationalVLA," positions itself at the intersection of these two critical research trajectories. It addresses a significant gap by explicitly introducing and tackling the problem of *defective instructions* within *manipulation tasks*. By proposing a new benchmark, RAMA, that incorporates these defective instructions, and by developing a novel dual-system VLA model, RationalVLA, the authors push the boundaries of robotic intelligence beyond mere instruction following to include critical reasoning about instruction feasibility. This work moves the field closer to developing truly rational and robust embodied AI agents that can operate safely and efficiently in unpredictable real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from a critical observation: for robots to be truly deployable in real-world scenarios, they must possess the ability not only to understand and execute natural language instructions but also to discern when an instruction is problematic and, consequently, to reject it. Current language-conditioned manipulation systems primarily operate under the idealistic assumption that all instructions are perfectly aligned with the environment and are always executable. This assumption severely limits their robustness and generalization in practical settings, where instructions can be ambiguous, irrelevant, or simply infeasible.

To address this fundamental limitation, the paper sets forth two primary objectives:

1. **Introduce a Novel Benchmark for Rational Manipulation (RAMA):** The first key objective is to create a benchmark that accurately reflects the challenges of real-world scenarios. RAMA is designed to evaluate models under conditions where both novel, executable instructions (requiring strong language generalization) and various types of "defective instructions" (requiring reasoning and rejection) coexist. The motivation here is to push robotic models beyond mere instruction following towards a more sophisticated understanding of context and feasibility. The paper meticulously constructs a dataset of over 14,000 samples, encompassing six distinct dimensions of defective instructions: visual, physical, semantic, motion, safety, and out-of-context. This diverse set ensures a comprehensive evaluation of a model's "rationality."

2. **Propose the Rational Vision-Language-Action Model (RationalVLA):** The second objective is to develop a new robotic control model capable of effectively handling the challenges posed by the RAMA benchmark. Existing VLA models, whether fine-tuned end-to-end or employing decoupled VLM-as-identifier approaches, face inherent limitations. Fine-tuned VLAs often suffer from "catastrophic forgetting" of their language understanding capabilities and may underperform task-specific policies in manipulation. VLM-as-identifier systems, while capable of rejecting some defective commands, struggle to fully transfer complex language comprehension to the low-level policy for executing nuanced, unseen instructions. RationalVLA is motivated by the need for an architecture that seamlessly integrates the high-level reasoning prowess of vision-language models with the precise control of low-level manipulation policies, enabling it to accurately interpret instructions, reject infeasible ones, and execute valid actions efficiently.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to bridge the gap between idealized laboratory conditions and the complexities of real-world robotic deployment by fostering "rationality"—the ability to correctly interpret and respond to instructions, even when they are flawed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a two-pronged approach to achieve their objectives: first, the development of the RAMA benchmark, and second, the design of the RationalVLA model.

#### 3.1. Rational Manipulation (RAMA) Benchmark

- **Task Setting:** RAMA is specifically designed to evaluate a robot's language understanding, generalization, and robustness. It introduces defective instructions alongside complex, unseen executable instructions. Defective instructions are defined as those that do not match any executable task in the current scene, categorized along six critical dimensions:
 - **Visual:** Instructions targeting non-existent visual characteristics (color, texture).
 - **Physical:** Instructions referring to non-existent shapes or sizes, or spatially impossible targets.
 - **Semantic:** Instructions for objects not present in the scene.
 - **Motion:** Instructions requiring actions beyond the robot's kinematic capabilities.
 - **Safety:** Instructions that could cause harm to the robot, environment, or humans.
 - **Out-of-Context:** Illogical or nonsensical commands.
 Unseen instructions are generated by GPT-4 as paraphrased versions of training instructions, challenging language generalization.

- **Simulation Environment:** The benchmark is built on the CALVIN environment, a well-established platform for language-conditioned manipulation. CALVIN provides four distinct environments (A, B, C, D), with D reserved for testing generalization (ABC→D setting). Its support for long-horizon tasks is crucial for evaluating the cumulative impact of defective instructions.

- **Dataset Construction:** A new dataset of 14,412 language instructions is created, including 159 test-only defective instructions. A "hindsight approach" is used, where existing CALVIN observations and actions are relabeled with defective instructions.
 - For visual, physical, semantic, and motion defects, instructions are programmatically generated by replacing existing linguistic components (adjectives, nouns, verbs) with non-existent ones. Mixed instructions (multiple perturbations) further enhance diversity.
 - For safety and out-of-context defects, prompts are meticulously crafted for GPT-4o to autonomously generate appropriate instructions.
 - Crucially, all language annotations are converted into a chat-like "User: Can you help me X? Assistant: Sure, I will X <ACT>." format for executable tasks, and "User: Can you help me X? Assistant: Sorry, I can not X <REJ>." for defective tasks. This explicitly trains the model to produce an action or a rejection.

- **Evaluation Protocol:** Each rollout begins with a defective instruction, followed by CALVIN's standard LH-MTLC (Long-Horizon Multi-Task Language-Conditioned) tasks with complex, unseen instructions. This setup assesses both the ability to handle initial defective commands and subsequent task generalization. Success rate (specifically, for the last tasks) and average task length are the primary metrics.

#### 3.2. RationalVLA Model

RationalVLA adopts a novel **dual-system architecture** to effectively bridge high-level reasoning and low-level control, addressing the deficiencies of previous approaches.

- **Architecture Overview (Fig. 6):** It comprises a pre-trained Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) `f_phi` and a pre-trained robot policy `π_theta`, trained end-to-end.
 - **High-level MLLM:** Built upon an LLM (`f_LLM`) and a vision encoder (`f_encoder`) (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5-7b and CLIP-VIT-L/14-336). The vision encoder encodes a third-view RGB image `I` into features, projected by `Φ` into visual tokens `h_I`. Text instructions `S` are tokenized into `h_S`. The MLLM autoregressively generates a text response `y_S`.
 - **Key Innovation: Learnable Latent Space Embeddings:** RationalVLA introduces two special tokens into the MLLM's vocabulary:
 - `<ACT>` token: When the instruction is executable, the MLLM generates this token. Its hidden embedding from the last layer (`y_<ACT>`) is then projected by `Ψ` to become a latent embedding `z`. This `z` serves as a high-level goal and conditioning signal for the low-level policy. This mechanism allows the MLLM to transmit abstract "intent" or "action entities" without loss of detail.
 - `<REJ>` token: When the instruction is defective, the MLLM generates this token. Its projection (`z_0 = Ψ(y_<REJ>)`) results in an all-zero tensor, effectively signaling the low-level policy to halt action. This enables the model to actively reject infeasible commands.
 - **Low-level Policy:** The 3D Diffuser Actor is employed as the low-level policy. It takes visual observations (`o`), proprioception (`p`), and the latent embedding `z` from the MLLM to predict low-level actions (`a`), which include end-effector pose (3D position, 3D orientation) and gripper state. This policy is modified to accept `z` as conditioning input instead of raw language instructions.

- **Training Strategy:**
 - **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** Used for supervised fine-tuning of the MLLM, making training more efficient.
 - **Stage 1: Pre-training for Feature Alignment (Cold Start):** The low-level policy is frozen, and only the MLLM is fine-tuned for 5,000 iterations. This initial phase aligns the MLLM's output embeddings (`z`) with the policy's input feature space, preventing unstable gradients from pre-trained model mismatches.
 - **Stage 2: End-to-End Fine-tuning:** The entire model (MLLM and policy) is unfrozen and fine-tuned for 30,000 iterations.
 - **Loss Function (Equation 3):** A combined loss function is used:
 - `L_policy`: Diffusion denoise loss for the low-level policy.
 - `L_LLM`: Auto-regressive training objective for the MLLM (with special tokens).
 - `L_CLIP`: An auxiliary loss `1 - cos(stop_gradient(clip(g_txt)), z_ACT)` used to regularize the latent embedding `z_<ACT>`, ensuring it remains aligned with the ground truth text description `g_txt`. This prevents the latent space from drifting and helps preserve the semantic meaning of the actions.
 The loss weights (`λ1=1, λ2=100, λ3=1`) emphasize the MLLM fine-tuning due to the new special tokens.

The dual-system design allows for asynchronous operation during inference, where the MLLM performs less frequent, high-level reasoning, while the policy continuously updates actions based on the latest MLLM output and environment changes, leading to efficient inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments thoroughly validate the effectiveness and robustness of RationalVLA across both the newly proposed RAMA benchmark and conventional manipulation tasks, including real-world trials.

- **Performance on RAMA Benchmark (Table II):**
 - RationalVLA significantly outperforms all baselines on the RAMA benchmark (CALVIN D split with unseen and defective instructions).
 - It achieves a 14.5% higher success rate on the last tasks and a 0.94 higher average task length compared to the best baseline (GPT-4o + 3DDA).
 - Baselines, including single policies (3DDA), single VLAs (RoboFlamingo), and VLM-as-identifier systems (LLaVA + 3DDA, GPT-4o + 3DDA), struggle. While VLM-as-identifier models show better first-task success rates by rejecting defective instructions, they fail to significantly improve subsequent task completion, demonstrating their limitation in enhancing the policy's language generalization. RationalVLA's ability to "translate" complex instructions into latent action embeddings for the policy is key here.

- **Performance Across Defective Dimensions (Fig. 7):**
 - RationalVLA demonstrates strong performance across most defective instruction dimensions. It excels in handling "out-of-context" instructions, benefiting from the VLM's robust language capabilities. Its spatial perception improves for "visual" perturbations through RAMA dataset training.
 - "Motion" perturbations prove to be the most challenging, resulting in the lowest average trajectory length for RationalVLA, indicating that assessing the feasibility of actions within the robot's kinematic limits remains a complex task.

- **Qualitative Analysis and Rejection Mechanism (Fig. 8):**
 - Visualizations clearly show the advantage of RationalVLA's rejection mechanism. While a fine-tuned RoboFlamingo fails to correctly interpret a defective instruction (e.g., attempting to manipulate a non-existent "orange block"), leading to environmental disruption and subsequent task failure, RationalVLA successfully recognizes the defect and outputs the `<REJ>` token. This halts the current task and allows the system to seamlessly transition to the next, demonstrating enhanced efficiency and task integrity.

- **Manipulation with Unseen Instructions (Table III, second part):**
 - Even when only unseen, executable instructions are considered (without defective ones), RationalVLA significantly outperforms baselines, achieving an average gain of 1.05 average length over the strongest comparison (GPT-4o + 3DDA). This highlights its superior ability to parse and ground complex, novel language instructions to corresponding actions.

- **Manipulation with Seen Instructions (Table III, first part):**
 - RationalVLA maintains competitive performance on standard, "seen" manipulation tasks (CALVIN ABC→D). It achieves the highest 48% success rate in the fifth task and a 0.18 higher average length than the 3DDA/GPT-4o+3DDA baselines. This indicates that its novel dual-system design and training do not compromise its ability to perform well on conventional tasks, effectively leveraging the policy's control effectiveness and the VLM's understanding.

- **Ablation Study (Table IV):**
 - The **CLIP Loss** is shown to be crucial for performance, as its removal leads to a significant drop (1.22 in average length). This confirms its role in aligning the latent space embeddings with the ground truth text descriptions, preventing drift and ensuring semantic consistency.
 - The **Chat-like Expression** format also contributes significantly to performance (0.78 improvement in average length). This suggests that aligning the training data format with the MLLM's original language distribution helps prevent catastrophic forgetting and preserves its inherent language capabilities.

- **Real-World Experiments (Tables V & VI, Fig. 10):**
 - **Basic Tasks with Unseen Instructions:** RationalVLA achieved a remarkable 85% success rate, dramatically outperforming 3DDA and GPT-4o + 3DDA (both at 16.7%). This validates its strong generalization to real-world complexities and diverse unseen instructions.
 - **Long-horizon Tasks with Defective Instructions:** RationalVLA demonstrated exceptional robustness with an 80% success rate, compared to 0% for 3DDA and 8.3% for GPT-4o + 3DDA. This is a critical finding, showcasing its ability to correctly reject infeasible commands (e.g., non-existent objects, unreachable targets) and maintain task integrity in sequential operations, a crucial step towards reliable real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "RationalVLA: A Rational Vision-Language-Action Model with Dual System" marks a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation, particularly in bridging the gap between research prototypes and practical real-world deployment.

Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap with the RAMA Benchmark:** The introduction of the RAtional MAnipulation (RAMA) benchmark is a pioneering contribution. By explicitly incorporating "defective instructions" across various dimensions (visual, physical, semantic, motion, safety, out-of-context), RAMA moves beyond the idealistic assumption of perfect instructions that has characterized much of previous research. This benchmark is crucial for developing and evaluating more robust, intelligent, and safe robotic systems, as it forces models to reason about the feasibility and relevance of commands rather than simply executing them blindly. It provides a much-needed standardized tool for the community to assess "rationality" in robotic agents.

2. **Novel Dual-System Architecture for Enhanced Rationality:** RationalVLA's dual-system design, which seamlessly integrates a high-level Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) with a low-level manipulation policy via learnable latent space embeddings (specifically, the `<ACT>` and `<REJ>` tokens), is a key innovation. This architecture effectively overcomes the limitations of prior VLA models, such as catastrophic forgetting of language abilities during fine-tuning and the inability of decoupled systems to fully transfer complex language understanding to the action space. The `<REJ>` token, in particular, empowers the robot with a critical "veto" power, allowing it to autonomously recognize and reject infeasible or unsafe commands, which is fundamental for reliable operation.

3. **Demonstrated Robustness and Generalization:** The extensive experimental results, both in simulation on the RAMA benchmark and, critically, in real-world trials, unequivocally demonstrate RationalVLA's superior performance. Its ability to significantly outperform state-of-the-art baselines in handling both unseen executable instructions and defective commands validates the efficacy of its design. The real-world trials are particularly impactful, showing that the model's capabilities translate from controlled simulation environments to unpredictable physical settings, greatly increasing confidence in its practical utility.

4. **Promoting Safer and More Efficient Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling robots to refuse or question problematic instructions, RationalVLA lays the groundwork for more intuitive, safer, and ultimately more effective human-robot collaboration. In industrial settings, domestic environments, or service robotics, a robot that can discern and reject an unsafe or illogical command is far more valuable and trustworthy than one that might blindly attempt it, potentially causing damage or injury. This "rational" behavior leads to increased efficiency by avoiding wasted effort on infeasible tasks and enhances safety for both humans and the robot itself.

5. **Accelerating Real-World Robot Deployment:** The robustness and reasoning capabilities demonstrated by RationalVLA are vital for accelerating the deployment of robot assistants in diverse real-world applications. From complex assembly lines to personalized domestic assistance, robots that can intelligently interact with human commands, even flawed ones, are essential for widespread adoption. This work takes a significant step towards the vision of truly intelligent and adaptable embodied AI agents.

In conclusion, RationalVLA not only introduces a valuable benchmark for a critical, overlooked problem in robotic manipulation but also proposes a highly effective architectural solution. Its contributions pave the way for a new generation of robotic systems that are not just capable of following instructions, but of doing so rationally, safely, and efficiently in dynamic, imperfect real-world environments.
