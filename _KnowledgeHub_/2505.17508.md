# On the Design of KL-Regularized Policy Gradient Algorithms for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new theoretical framework, the Regularized Policy Gradient (RPG) view, unifies KL-regularized policy gradient algorithms for off-policy LLM training, providing exact gradient derivations for various KL divergences. This framework leads to RPG-REINFORCE with RPG-Style Clip, an algorithm that improves LLM reasoning accuracy by up to 6 absolute percentage points over DAPO on AIME24/25, achieving 52% on AIME25 for Qwen models at 8k context.

## Problem

- The design space for KL regularization in reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs) lacks a unified theoretical understanding, especially concerning importance weighting in off-policy settings.
- Existing KL-regularized policy gradient methods often suffer from high variance and instability when applied to LLM reasoning tasks, particularly with off-policy data collection.
- Some current algorithms, such as GRPO, implement KL penalties with importance-weighting mismatches in off-policy settings, leading to biased gradients.

## Method

- The paper introduces the ==Regularized Policy Gradient (RPG) view==, a unified theoretical framework that systematically derives exact policy gradients for various KL divergence types (forward/reverse, normalized/unnormalized) under off-policy sampling.
- It proposes ==RPG-REINFORCE with RPG-Style Clip==, a novel clipping mechanism for importance weights, combined with an ==iterative reference policy update== to stabilize off-policy policy gradient training.
- The methodology includes constructing gradient-equivalent surrogate loss functions and ensuring computational efficiency by allowing pre-computation of log probabilities for the old policy.

## Results

- RPG-REINFORCE, particularly with unnormalized reverse KL (URKL), enhances LLM reasoning accuracy by up to **6 absolute percentage points** over DAPO on AIME24/25 benchmarks.
- The method achieves **52%** accuracy on AIME25 for Qwen3-4B at 8k context length, surpassing the official Qwen3-4B-Instruct model (**47%**) and outperforming other strong baselines.
- RPG algorithms consistently demonstrate more stable training progressions, controlled policy entropy, and appropriate response lengths across various context lengths (2k, 4k, 8k) in mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The widely used k3 estimator for regularization is formally equivalent to the unnormalized KL divergence (UKL), providing theoretical clarity to its empirical success.
> - Under off-policy sampling, precise importance weighting is critical for the surrogate objective to yield the exact gradient of the intended KL-regularized objective, correcting mismatches found in prior methods like GRPO.
> - RPG-Style Clip and the iterative reference policy update are crucial for maintaining stable training dynamics and achieving superior performance in off-policy LLM fine-tuning by managing variance and adapting the KL trust region.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) using reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance their reasoning capabilities. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), particularly policy gradient (PG) methods, has become a prominent framework for aligning LLMs with human preferences and instructions, and for improving complex task performance. Established RL methods such as REINFORCE and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) have been adapted for LLM fine-tuning, with PPO gaining widespread use due to its stability and sample efficiency in off-policy settings.

A common technique for stabilizing policy optimization and controlling policy changes, especially in off-policy scenarios, is Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence regularization. KL regularization penalizes deviations of the learned policy from a reference policy, contributing to more robust updates and preventing issues like catastrophic forgetting. While KL regularization is widely employed, both explicitly (e.g., in Group Relative Policy Optimization, GRPO) and implicitly (e.g., through reward shaping in PPO), the specific design choices—including the direction of KL divergence (forward vs. reverse), normalization status (normalized vs. unnormalized), and the choice of estimator (e.g., k1, k2, k3)—are diverse and often presented without a unified framework. This diversity can lead to subtle differences in how surrogate loss functions are derived and how off-policy importance weighting is applied, potentially affecting training stability and gradient accuracy.

This paper addresses the need for a systematic and unifying treatment of KL-regularized policy gradient methods, particularly focusing on the precise importance weighting required for off-policy updates to ensure that optimized surrogates yield the exact gradient of the intended KL-regularized objective. By clarifying these design considerations, the research aims to provide a more robust and scalable approach to applying RL for LLM reasoning, building upon and refining existing methods like GRPO and DAPO.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically analyze and unify the design choices within KL-regularized policy gradient algorithms, specifically in the context of off-policy learning for enhancing LLM reasoning. The core question motivating this work is: "under the off-policy setting, what weighting is required for each KL variant so that the surrogate we optimize yields the exact gradient of the intended KL-regularized objective?"

This objective is driven by several motivations:

- **Diversity and Lack of Unification in KL Regularization:** Despite the widespread adoption of KL divergence as a regularizer in LLM fine-tuning methods (e.g., PPO, GRPO), there exists a considerable variety in how KL divergence is formulated and estimated. This includes choices between Forward KL and Reverse KL, considerations for unnormalized distributions (Unnormalized KL, UKL), and the use of different estimators (e.g., k1, k2, k3). This diversity is often scattered across the literature and intertwined with off-policy estimation, creating a complex design space without clear guidelines.
- **Importance of Correct Off-Policy Estimation:** In off-policy settings, samples are collected using an older policy (π_old) while training a new policy (π_θ). Accurate importance weighting is crucial to correct for this distribution mismatch and ensure that the estimated gradients align with the true gradients of the target objective. The paper identifies that misapplication or omission of these importance weights, as observed in some existing methods, can lead to unstable updates or gradients that do not correspond to the intended objective.
- **Revisiting REINFORCE for LLM Reasoning:** While PPO-like algorithms are common, REINFORCE-style methods are being revisited for their simplicity. However, REINFORCE traditionally suffers from high variance and is on-policy. The authors aim to explore how principled KL regularization and stabilization techniques can make REINFORCE-style algorithms viable and effective in off-policy, large-scale LLM contexts.
- **Practical Stability and Scalability for LLMs:** Fine-tuning LLMs involves large models and potentially long sequences, requiring algorithms that are not only theoretically sound but also stable and scalable in practice. The motivation includes developing a framework that enables robust training and offers practical throughput on modern LLM stacks.
- **Correction of Existing Methods:** The paper aims to identify and correct potential mismatches in importance weighting within existing algorithms, specifically mentioning GRPO’s KL term, to ensure consistency with the intended KL-regularized objective.

By addressing these points, the research endeavors to provide a compact and unified framework, termed the Regularized Policy Gradient (RPG) view, which offers systematic derivations and clarifies the interplay of KL formulation, off-policy sampling, and surrogate loss design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Regularized Policy Gradient (RPG) framework, which provides a unified approach for deriving policy gradients and corresponding surrogate loss functions for various KL-regularized objectives in an off-policy setting. The methodology systematically explores the design space of KL regularization, encompassing different KL directions, normalization types, and estimator styles.

The core methodology involves:

1. **Unified Derivation of KL-Regularized Objectives:** The authors begin by defining the exact KL-regularized objective functions, combining the expected reward with a KL divergence term: $J(\theta) = E_{\pi_\theta}[R] - \beta \cdot \text{KL}$. This includes four primary variants:
 - **Normalized Forward KL (FKL):** $J_{\text{FKL}}(\theta) = E_{x \sim \pi_\theta}[R(x)] - \beta \text{KL}(\pi_{\text{old}} \parallel \pi_\theta)$.
 - **Normalized Reverse KL (RKL):** $J_{\text{RKL}}(\theta) = E_{x \sim \pi_\theta}[R(x)] - \beta \text{KL}(\pi_\theta \parallel \pi_{\text{old}})$.
 - **Unnormalized Forward KL (UFKL):** $J_{\text{UFKL}}(\theta) = E_{x \sim \pi_\theta}[R(x)] - \beta \text{UKL}(\pi_{\text{old}} \parallel \pi_\theta)$, which includes a mass correction term.
 - **Unnormalized Reverse KL (URKL):** $J_{\text{URKL}}(\theta) = E_{x \sim \pi_\theta}[R(x)] - \beta \text{UKL}(\pi_\theta \parallel \pi_{\text{old}})$, also with a mass correction.

2. **Derivation of Exact Off-Policy Gradients and Fully Differentiable Surrogates:** For each of the four KL variants, the paper systematically derives the exact policy gradient $\nabla_\theta J(\theta)$ under off-policy sampling, where samples are drawn from a reference policy $\pi_{\text{old}}$ (or its normalized version $e\pi_{\text{old}}$). This derivation explicitly incorporates importance weights $w(x) = \pi_\theta(x)/\pi_{\text{old}}(x)$. Concurrently, corresponding fully differentiable surrogate loss functions $L(\theta)$ are formulated such that $\nabla_\theta L(\theta) = -\nabla_\theta J(\theta)$, allowing direct optimization via gradient descent. This process clarifies the specific importance weighting required for each KL variant. For example, for URKL, the loss is $L_{\text{URKL}}(\theta) = Z_{\text{old}}E_{x \sim e\pi_{\text{old}}} [-w(x)R(x) + \beta(w(x) \log w(x) - w(x))]$.

3. **REINFORCE-Style Gradient-Equivalent Estimators:** Recognizing the structural similarity between the derived policy gradients and the REINFORCE algorithm's form, the paper also develops REINFORCE-style surrogate losses. These losses use the stop-gradient (SG) operator, typically formulated as $L_{\text{REINFORCE-style}}(\theta) = -E_{x \sim \pi_{\text{sampling}}}[\text{SG}(\text{Weight}(x, \theta)) \log \pi_\theta(x)]$. Proposition 4.1 establishes the gradient-equivalence of these REINFORCE-style losses to their fully differentiable counterparts, provided the SG operator correctly prevents gradients from flowing through the terms within it that depend on $\theta$.

4. **RPG-Style Clip for Stability:** To address the instability caused by high variance in importance ratios $w(x)$ during off-policy updates, the paper introduces "RPG-Style Clip." This technique adapts the dual-clip method, commonly used in PPO, to both the fully differentiable RPG and REINFORCE-style RPG algorithms. It clips the importance weight $w(x)$ into a range $[1-\epsilon_1, 1+\epsilon_2]$ for positive advantages and imposes an additional lower bound $c$ for negative advantages, thereby stabilizing training without biasing the gradient direction.

5. **Iterative Reference-Policy Updates:** The framework incorporates an iterative training strategy where the reference policy $\pi_{\text{old}}$ is periodically updated to the current policy $\pi_\theta$. This approach helps maintain a practical KL trust region, preventing the policy from over-regularizing towards a potentially outdated initial checkpoint and allowing for more substantial learning.

6. **Correction of GRPO's KL Term:** Through the unified derivations, the research identifies an importance-weighting mismatch in GRPO’s KL penalty under off-policy sampling. A corrected estimator and loss, consistent with the intended objective, are proposed. Furthermore, the paper formally demonstrates the equivalence between the k3 estimator (used in GRPO) and the unnormalized KL divergence (UKL).

7. **Experimental Setup:** Experiments are conducted on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23) using Qwen3-4B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models. The implementation leverages the `verl` framework with `vLLM` for efficient LLM serving. Stabilization techniques such as baseline subtraction, dynamic sampling with group filtering (oversampling challenging prompts, filtering extreme-performing ones), and reward shaping (overlong punishment) are adopted from DAPO to enhance efficiency and data quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical and empirical findings supporting the efficacy and robustness of the Regularized Policy Gradient (RPG) framework.

**Theoretical Contributions:**

- **Unified Derivation of Exact Gradients:** The paper successfully provides a unified and compact derivation, termed the RPG view, for the exact gradients of KL-regularized objectives under off-policy sampling. This covers normalized and unnormalized KL variants (Forward and Reverse KL).
- **Gradient-Equivalence of Surrogates:** It is demonstrated that REINFORCE-style losses incorporating the stop-gradient operator are gradient-equivalent to the fully differentiable surrogate losses of the intended regularized objectives (Proposition 4.1). This provides flexibility in implementation while preserving the correct gradient direction.
- **Equivalence of k3 Estimator and Unnormalized KL:** The research formally establishes the equality between the k3 estimator (Schulman, 2020) and unnormalized KL (Appendix D), clarifying the nature of this commonly used penalty.
- **Correction of GRPO's KL Term:** The analysis identifies that GRPO’s KL penalty term lacks an essential importance weight when operating under off-policy sampling conditions. The paper specifies a corrected estimator and loss function to align with the intended objective.

**Empirical Results on LLM Reasoning Benchmarks (Qwen3-4B):**

The RPG framework, particularly RPG-REINFORCE with RPG-Style Clip, demonstrated strong performance and training stability on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23).

- **Improved Accuracy:** On mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25), RPG-REINFORCE with RPG-Style Clip achieved accuracy improvements of up to +6 absolute percentage points over DAPO, a strong baseline.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** With an 8K context length, RPG-REINFORCE with RPG-Style Clip achieved 52% accuracy on AIME25, surpassing the official Qwen3-4B-Instruct model's reported 47%.
- **Stable and Scalable Training:** The RPG algorithms exhibited stable training progressions as observed through metrics like reward (critic score), policy entropy, and average response length (Figure 4). This stability contributes to their robust benchmark performances compared to baselines which could show more volatility.
- **Context Length Generalization:** Consistent performance improvements were observed across various context lengths (8K, 4K, 2K), indicating the generalizability of the proposed methods (Tables 3, 5, 6; Figures 4, 5, 6).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The implementation benefits from pre-computing and storing $\pi_{\text{old}}$ log-probabilities, meaning only the current policy $\pi_\theta$ needs to reside in GPU memory during training. This approach is more memory-efficient and faster than methods requiring multiple models in GPU memory simultaneously.
- **Ablation Studies:** Investigations into clipping ratios (Figure 8) indicated that a "higher-and-clip-higher" strategy, similar to DAPO, contributed to increased actor entropy, which correlated with performance gains. Ablations on the KL regularization coefficient $\beta$ (Figure 9) showed that $\beta = 1 \times 10^{-4}$ performed better than $1 \times 10^{-3}$, with longer response lengths correlating with improved performance. The iterative reference model update was also shown to be beneficial for stability and performance.
- **Performance on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct:** Experiments on the Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct model (Table 8, Figure 10) further corroborated the competitive or superior performance of RPG and RPG-REINFORCE variants against baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work lies in its systematic and unifying treatment of KL-regularized policy gradient algorithms, which has several potential impacts on the development and application of LLMs, particularly for reasoning tasks:

- **Enhanced Understanding of KL Regularization:** By providing a unified theoretical framework (the RPG view) and explicit derivations for various KL formulations (forward/reverse, normalized/unnormalized) and estimators, the paper clarifies the subtle design choices involved in KL regularization. This reduces ambiguity and provides a foundational understanding for practitioners and researchers.
- **Improved Off-Policy Training Reliability:** The detailed analysis of importance weighting for off-policy settings ensures that surrogate objectives yield exact gradients of the intended KL-regularized objectives. This theoretical correctness can lead to more reliable and predictable training behavior, especially in complex LLM fine-tuning scenarios where off-policy data is crucial.
- **Increased Stability and Scalability of RL for LLMs:** The introduction of RPG-Style Clip and the validation of iterative reference-policy updates contribute to making RL algorithms more stable and scalable for large-scale LLM training. This is critical for practical applications where training large models efficiently and robustly is a challenge.
- **Advancement in LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** The empirical results, demonstrating improved accuracy on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks and outperforming existing models, indicate that the RPG framework can effectively enhance LLMs' reasoning abilities. This has implications for fields requiring complex problem-solving, such as scientific discovery, engineering, and education.
- **Correction and Refinement of Existing Methods:** The identification and correction of importance-weighting mismatches in algorithms like GRPO contribute to the refinement and robustness of the broader landscape of RLHF methods.
- **Practical Implementation Benefits:** The optimization for memory efficiency (requiring only the current policy in GPU memory) offers practical benefits for deploying and training large models, reducing computational resource requirements.
- **Basis for Future Research:** The unified framework provides a strong theoretical and empirical basis for future research in KL-regularized policy gradients, encouraging further exploration of principled design choices and stabilization techniques in RL for LLMs.

In summary, this research contributes to both the theoretical understanding and practical application of reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning, offering a robust, stable, and scalable framework that demonstrates competitive performance on demanding benchmarks. The clarification of KL regularization design principles and the development of effective stabilization techniques are valuable contributions to the ongoing effort to build more capable and reliable AI systems.
