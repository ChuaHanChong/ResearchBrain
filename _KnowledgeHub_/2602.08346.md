# What, Whether and How? Unveiling Process Reward Models for Thinking with Images Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and collaborators introduce ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH, the first benchmark for Process Reward Models (PRMs) in "thinking with images" visual reasoning. The research defines a seven-category error taxonomy and reveals that current Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) perform poorly as PRMs, exhibiting a strong positive bias and limited ability to detect complex multimodal reasoning errors compared to human performance.

## Problem

- Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) utilizing "thinking with images" methods generate complex, multi-step visual reasoning trajectories that are prone to fine-grained errors.
- Existing Process Reward Models (PRMs) and their benchmarks primarily focus on text-based reasoning, lacking specific tools to evaluate multimodal reasoning processes involving interleaved text and image interactions.
- Current LVLMs struggle to reliably self-correct or receive fine-grained feedback due to the absence of a structured approach to identify and assess errors at each step of dynamic visual reasoning.

## Method

- A comprehensive ==seven-category error taxonomy== in "thinking with images" reasoning was developed through analysis of model trajectories.
- ==ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH==, the first human-annotated multimodal benchmark for Process Reward Models, was constructed from 1,206 visual reasoning questions with detailed step-by-step annotations.
- State-of-the-art LVLMs were evaluated on this new benchmark to assess their capabilities as ==Process Reward Models (PRMs)==, focusing on overall accuracy and detection rates for specific error categories.

## Results

- The ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH dataset was created, containing **1,206** questions and **12,714** meticulously human-annotated reasoning steps spanning diverse visual reasoning scenarios.
- Evaluated LVLMs achieved substantially lower accuracy in detecting correct and incorrect reasoning steps than human annotators, with proprietary models also showing limited performance (e.g., GPT-4o at **31.45%** ACC).
- Performance of LVLMs as PRMs was highly variable across different error types and reasoning steps, often degrading during image extraction steps, suggesting challenges in consistently evaluating dynamic visual processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating PRMs, even generic LVLM-based ones, improves the reasoning accuracy of "thinking with images" models by 1-8%, highlighting the utility of process-level supervision.
> - Current LVLMs demonstrate limited capability as PRMs for visual reasoning, with the best open-source model achieving only 41.74% accuracy, considerably below human expert performance.
> - LVLMs exhibit a pronounced "positive bias" in reward assignments and struggle significantly with detecting subtle and complex error types like Tool Invocation and Relationship Understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of multimodal AI, specifically addressing critical challenges in the "thinking with images" paradigm within Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs). The broader research landscape is characterized by:

- **Advancements in LVLMs:** Recent breakthroughs have enabled LVLMs to perform complex visual tasks by integrating language and vision. However, traditional LVLMs are often limited by static image tokenization, leading to information loss and hindering fine-grained visual understanding.
- **Emergence of "Thinking with Images" Paradigm:** To overcome these limitations, the "thinking with images" paradigm has gained traction, allowing models to dynamically interact with and re-encode visual information at each reasoning step. This mirrors human visual processing, where one might zoom in, crop, or re-examine parts of an image during complex problem-solving. Models like OpenAI's o3 exemplify this transformative approach.
- **The Rise of Process Reward Models (PRMs):** In parallel, PRMs have shown remarkable success in enhancing verbal reasoning by providing stepwise supervision rather than just evaluating final answers. This fine-grained feedback helps models learn to generate correct reasoning processes.
- **The Existing Gap:** While PRMs are valuable, existing benchmarks for them are predominantly text-centric. There is a significant void in evaluating PRMs specifically for multimodal reasoning, especially within the dynamic context of "thinking with images" where interleaved text and image processing steps introduce unique error types.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. It acknowledges the power and potential of the "thinking with images" paradigm while simultaneously highlighting its inherent challenges due to the diverse errors that can emerge during dynamic visual reasoning. By introducing the first comprehensive benchmark tailored for PRMs in this multimodal context, the authors aim to bridge the divide between advanced visual reasoning capabilities and the essential tools for ensuring the reliability and correctness of the reasoning *process*. This work is not just about building better visual models, but about building models that can *self-correct* and *reason transparently* in multimodal environments, a crucial step towards more robust, trustworthy, and human-like AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while the "thinking with images" paradigm has significantly advanced LVLMs, it also introduces a new set of challenges in ensuring the correctness of the reasoning process. Errors can occur at various stages, from object detection to logical understanding of visual relationships, making it difficult to assess the quality of intermediate steps. The success of Process Reward Models (PRMs) in text-based reasoning suggests a similar potential for multimodal tasks, but the unique nature of interleaved text and image steps requires a specialized approach.

Against this backdrop, the paper articulates several key objectives:

- **Identify and Taxonomize Fine-Grained Error Types:** A fundamental objective was to systematically analyze reasoning trajectories from current "thinking with images" models and define a comprehensive taxonomy of common visual reasoning failure modes. This aims to provide a clear understanding of *what* issues persist in these advanced LVLMs.
- **Investigate the Necessity and Potential of PRMs:** The authors sought to empirically determine "Whether Process Reward Models Help?" in the "thinking with images" paradigm. This involved preliminary experiments to see if existing LVLMs, acting as surrogate PRMs, could improve reasoning performance, thereby demonstrating the intrinsic value and potential for specialized PRMs.
- **Construct a Comprehensive Multimodal PRM Benchmark:** A crucial objective was to develop the first comprehensive benchmark, named ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH, specifically designed for evaluating PRMs under the "thinking with images" paradigm. This benchmark aims to provide a standardized and fine-grained assessment tool that accounts for the multimodal nature of the reasoning process.
- **Conduct In-Depth Experimental Analysis of Current LVLMs as PRMs:** Using the newly constructed benchmark, the research aimed to thoroughly evaluate the capabilities and limitations of existing state-of-the-art LVLMs when deployed as PRMs. This analysis was intended to uncover performance disparities across different error types, biases in reward assignments, and sensitivities to reasoning step positions, thereby highlighting areas for future improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research is structured into three main phases: identifying error types and assessing PRM necessity, constructing the novel benchmark, and comprehensively evaluating existing models on it.

**Phase 1: Error Type Identification and Assessment of PRM Necessity**

1. **Data Collection for Error Analysis:** The authors began by collecting a large corpus of 7,558 reasoning trajectories. These trajectories were generated by four state-of-the-art "thinking with images" models (ViGoRL-3B, ViGoRL-7B, DeepEyes-7B, and PixelReasoner-7B), applied across five diverse visual reasoning benchmarks (V\*Bench, Blink, SAT-2, HRBench, and MME-RealWorld). This ensured a broad spectrum of visual reasoning scenarios and potential error modes.
2. **Fine-Grained Error Taxonomy:** Through extensive manual analysis and observation of these trajectories, the researchers systematically identified and defined seven distinct error types prevalent in the "thinking with images" paradigm. These categories include:
 - **Object Detection Failure (ODF):** Inability to locate or detect visual elements.
 - **Attribute Recognition Error (AE):** Misidentification of object attributes (e.g., color, material, shape).
 - **Relationship Understanding Bias (RU):** Inaccurate comprehension of spatial, action, or comparative relationships.
 - **Logical Understanding Error (LU):** Failure to grasp the question or task requirements, leading to illogical reasoning.
 - **Answer-Reasoning Inconsistency (ARI):** Final answer not supported by the preceding reasoning steps.
 - **Tool Invocation Error (TE):** Incorrect or failed use of visual processing tools (e.g., cropping, zooming).
 - **Redundant Reasoning (RR):** Superfluous steps taken after the answer has effectively been determined.
3. **Investigating PRM Utility via Guided Search:** To assess whether PRMs could aid visual reasoning, the authors conducted preliminary "Guided Search" experiments. They selected three representative LVLMs (Gemma3-27B, Ovis2-34B, and InternVL2.5-78B) to act as *surrogate* PRMs. In this setup, an upstream "thinking with images" model generated multiple candidate reasoning steps (k=8). The surrogate PRM then scored each candidate, and the highest-scoring step was selected to continue the reasoning trajectory. This process was repeated for a maximum of 10 steps. Performance was measured by the accuracy of the final answer, comparing models with and without PRM integration.

**Phase 2: ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH Construction**

The benchmark construction followed a rigorous four-stage pipeline:

1. **Data Collection:** Question-image pairs were systematically gathered from established open-source datasets (VBench, HRBench, SAT, BLINK) known for aligning with the "thinking with images" paradigm.
2. **Trajectory Construction:** For each collected question-image pair, multiple reasoning trajectories were generated using the diverse pool of "thinking with images" models identified in Phase 1.
3. **Human Annotation:** This critical stage involved two phases:
 - *Data Filtering:* Rule-based methods were applied to deduplicate and retain one valid trajectory per question.
 - *Manual Annotation:* Five expert annotators, utilizing the Label Studio platform and strict guidelines based on the seven defined error types, meticulously annotated each step of the retained trajectories for correctness and error classification over seven days. Low-quality chains were filtered out.
4. **Quality Assurance:** Three independent annotators conducted a final quality verification over three days. Each question and its corresponding trajectory were evaluated against three criteria: accuracy, consistency, and completeness. Only trajectories satisfying all criteria were included in the final benchmark.

The resulting ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH comprises 1,206 questions with corresponding image reasoning chains, totaling 12,714 reasoning steps (52.8% correct, 41.2% incorrect, 6.0% neutral) and 6,544 images. It spans 4 major categories (Recognition & Attributes, Space & Relationships, Dynamics & Actions, Analysis & Reasoning) and 16 subcategories, ensuring comprehensive coverage.

**Phase 3: Evaluation of LVLMs as PRMs on ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH**

1. **Model Selection:** Eleven state-of-the-art LVLMs were chosen for evaluation, including open-source models (LLaVA-OneVision (7B, 72B), Qwen2.5-VL (7B, 72B), InternVL2.5 (8B, 78B), Gemma3 (4B, 27B), Ovis2 (8B, 34B)) and proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash). Human performance was also recorded as a baseline.
2. **Evaluation Metrics:** Models were assessed using overall Accuracy (ACC) and F1 scores, along with specific accuracy rates for detecting each of the seven fine-grained error types (TE, RU, AE, RR, ODF, ARI, LU).
3. **Detailed Analysis:** Beyond overall metrics, the researchers performed an in-depth analysis to uncover specific behavioral patterns of LVLMs as PRMs, focusing on:
 - **Positive Bias:** Examining whether models tend to assign higher rewards (i.e., classify as correct) more often, even for incorrect steps.
 - **Step Sensitivity:** Analyzing performance fluctuations across different steps within a multi-step reasoning trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings that shed light on the current state of Process Reward Models (PRMs) in the context of "thinking with images":

1. **Comprehensive Error Taxonomy:** The study successfully identified and categorized seven fine-grained error types specific to the "thinking with images" paradigm: Object Detection Failure (ODF), Attribute Recognition Error (AE), Relationship Understanding Bias (RU), Logical Understanding Error (LU), Answer-Reasoning Inconsistency (ARI), Tool Invocation Error (TE), and Redundant Reasoning (RR). This taxonomy provides a crucial framework for understanding and addressing failure modes in multimodal reasoning.

2. **Necessity and Potential of PRMs Demonstrated:** Preliminary "Guided Search" experiments showed that existing LVLMs, even when serving as generic PRMs, could improve the reasoning performance of "thinking with images" models. Most models exhibited accuracy gains ranging from 1-8% when PRMs were integrated, indicating that process-level supervision is indeed beneficial and necessary for this paradigm, and there is significant room for improvement with dedicated PRMs.

3. **Introduction of ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH:** The paper successfully constructed and validated the first comprehensive benchmark specifically designed for evaluating PRMs in the "thinking with images" domain. This benchmark comprises 1,206 meticulously human-annotated reasoning trajectories across 4 major categories and 16 subcategories, offering a robust and fine-grained testbed for future PRM development.

4. **Limited Capability of Current LVLMs as PRMs:** A key finding from the extensive evaluation on ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH is that current state-of-the-art LVLMs, both open-source and proprietary, fall significantly short as effective PRMs for visual reasoning processes. The best-performing open-source model, Ovis2-34B, achieved only 41.74% overall accuracy (ACC) and 45.72% F1 score, which is considerably lower than human performance (81.23% ACC). Proprietary models like Gemini-2.5-Flash and GPT-4o also showed limited capabilities (36.81% and 31.45% ACC respectively), highlighting a substantial gap between current model performance and the requirements for reliable process supervision.

5. **Significant Performance Disparities Across Error Types:** The evaluation revealed dramatic variations in model performance across the seven defined error categories. LVLMs struggled particularly with detecting complex and nuanced errors such as Tool Invocation Error (TE) and Relationship Understanding Bias (RU), where detection rates often dropped below 5%. This indicates that current models lack the fine-grained understanding required to identify subtle logical and operational failures in visual reasoning steps.

6. **Consistent Positive Bias in Reward Assignments:** Analysis showed that LVLMs, when acting as PRMs, exhibit a strong "positive bias." They are consistently more accurate at identifying correct reasoning steps than incorrect ones. Many models performed below random chance (50%) when attempting to detect incorrect samples (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Flash achieved 78.89% on correct samples but only 38.81% on incorrect samples), indicating a fundamental deficiency in their ability to accurately pinpoint and penalize errors.

7. **Notable Step Sensitivity:** LVLMs demonstrated severe volatility and significant performance variations across different steps within multi-step reasoning processes. Accuracy often fluctuated dramatically, sometimes dropping from high to near-zero within a single step. Furthermore, performance often degraded during image extraction steps (odd steps) compared to text reasoning steps (even steps), suggesting that the dynamic visual processing part of "thinking with images" is particularly challenging for PRMs to evaluate consistently.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant implications and potential impact across several dimensions of AI development:

1. **Foundational for Multimodal Process Supervision:** By introducing the first comprehensive benchmark and a detailed error taxonomy for Process Reward Models in the "thinking with images" paradigm, this work lays a critical foundation for advancing multimodal process supervision. It shifts the focus from merely evaluating final answers to scrutinizing the intermediate reasoning steps, which is crucial for building more reliable and transparent AI systems.

2. **Guiding Future LVLM Development:** The detailed experimental analysis provides invaluable insights into the specific weaknesses of current LVLMs as PRMs. The identified challenges—such as poor performance on complex error types (TE, RU), prevalent positive bias, and significant step sensitivity—offer a clear roadmap for researchers to focus their efforts on improving the core capabilities of multimodal reward models. This will lead to the development of more robust and accurate PRMs specifically designed for interleaved text-image reasoning.

3. **Enabling More Trustworthy AI:** Process-level feedback is essential for AI alignment and safety. By providing a mechanism to evaluate *how* an AI arrives at its conclusions in a visual context, this work contributes to developing AI systems that are not only capable but also understandable and controllable. This can foster greater trust in AI applications, especially in high-stakes domains where visual reasoning is critical.

4. **Accelerating "Thinking with Images" Paradigm Maturity:** The "thinking with images" paradigm is a powerful approach to overcome static tokenization limitations. However, its widespread adoption hinges on robust error detection and self-correction mechanisms. This research, by providing the tools and insights necessary for effective PRMs, will accelerate the maturity and practical applicability of this paradigm, allowing LVLMs to more reliably mimic human visual thinking.

5. **New Research Agenda for AI Community:** The ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH serves as a valuable public resource, inviting the broader AI research community to engage in developing novel architectures, training methodologies, and evaluation strategies for multimodal PRMs. It establishes new competitive challenges, pushing the boundaries of what LVLMs can achieve in understanding and evaluating complex reasoning processes.

6. **Potential for Improved Generalization and Robustness:** Models trained with effective process rewards are likely to exhibit better generalization capabilities and robustness, as they learn not just to produce correct answers but also correct reasoning strategies. This could lead to a new generation of LVLMs that are more adaptable to novel visual reasoning tasks and less prone to subtle errors.

In conclusion, this paper makes a significant contribution by unveiling the complexities of process supervision in multimodal "thinking with images" models. It not only highlights critical limitations of current LVLMs but also provides the foundational benchmark and analytical framework necessary to spur future innovations in developing highly effective and trustworthy multimodal process reward models.
