# What, Whether and How? Unveiling Process Reward Models for Thinking with Images Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and collaborators introduce ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH, the first benchmark for Process Reward Models (PRMs) in "thinking with images" visual reasoning. The research defines a seven-category error taxonomy and reveals that current Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) perform poorly as PRMs, exhibiting a strong positive bias and limited ability to detect complex multimodal reasoning errors compared to human performance.

## Problem

- Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) utilizing "thinking with images" methods generate complex, multi-step visual reasoning trajectories that are prone to fine-grained errors.
- Existing Process Reward Models (PRMs) and their benchmarks primarily focus on text-based reasoning, lacking specific tools to evaluate multimodal reasoning processes involving interleaved text and image interactions.
- Current LVLMs struggle to reliably self-correct or receive fine-grained feedback due to the absence of a structured approach to identify and assess errors at each step of dynamic visual reasoning.

## Method

- A comprehensive ==seven-category error taxonomy== in "thinking with images" reasoning was developed through analysis of model trajectories.
- ==ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH==, the first human-annotated multimodal benchmark for Process Reward Models, was constructed from 1,206 visual reasoning questions with detailed step-by-step annotations.
- State-of-the-art LVLMs were evaluated on this new benchmark to assess their capabilities as ==Process Reward Models (PRMs)==, focusing on overall accuracy and detection rates for specific error categories.

## Results

- The ThinkWithImages-PRMBENCH dataset was created, containing **1,206** questions and **12,714** meticulously human-annotated reasoning steps spanning diverse visual reasoning scenarios.
- Evaluated LVLMs achieved substantially lower accuracy in detecting correct and incorrect reasoning steps than human annotators, with proprietary models also showing limited performance (e.g., GPT-4o at **31.45%** ACC).
- Performance of LVLMs as PRMs was highly variable across different error types and reasoning steps, often degrading during image extraction steps, suggesting challenges in consistently evaluating dynamic visual processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating PRMs, even generic LVLM-based ones, improves the reasoning accuracy of "thinking with images" models by 1-8%, highlighting the utility of process-level supervision.
> - Current LVLMs demonstrate limited capability as PRMs for visual reasoning, with the best open-source model achieving only 41.74% accuracy, considerably below human expert performance.
> - LVLMs exhibit a pronounced "positive bias" in reward assignments and struggle significantly with detecting subtle and complex error types like Tool Invocation and Relationship Understanding.
