# Let Me Grok for You: Accelerating Grokking via Embedding Transfer from a Weaker Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A method called GrokTransfer accelerates the "grokking" phenomenon in neural networks by transferring embeddings from smaller, weaker models to larger target models, eliminating delayed generalization while maintaining performance across algorithmic tasks like modular arithmetic and sparse parity.

## Problem

- Neural networks often exhibit "grokking" where generalization is significantly delayed after memorization, making training unpredictable and inefficient
- Existing approaches don't address the root cause of delayed generalization or provide principled solutions for accelerating grokking

## Method

- Train a smaller "weak" model until it achieves basic generalization
- Extract and ==linearly map== the weak model's learned embeddings to initialize the target model
- Use the ==transferred embeddings== to enable direct generalization without delay (==GrokTransfer==)

## Results

- Eliminates delayed generalization in synthetic XOR tasks through theoretically proven direct generalization
- Successfully transfers embeddings between different architectures (FNNs to Transformers)
- Reduces computational costs compared to training large models from scratch
- Works across **multiple algorithmic tasks** while maintaining target model performance

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Input embeddings play a crucial role in determining training dynamics and generalization
> - Even very small weak models (e.g., 3 neurons) can provide sufficient embedding information
> - The weak model must achieve some level of generalization for effective transfer

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research fits into the growing body of work aimed at understanding and mitigating the "grokking" phenomenon in neural networks. The study builds upon the initial discovery of grokking by Power et al. (2022) and draws inspiration from subsequent research exploring the phenomenon in various settings, including:

- **Algorithmic Tasks:** Studies on modular arithmetic, group operations, and sparse parity.
- **Real-world Datasets:** Investigation of grokking in image classification tasks (e.g., MNIST).
- **Theoretical Analysis:** Rigorous analysis of grokking in linear regression and binary classification.
- **Acceleration Methods:** Attempts to speed up grokking by manipulating weight initialization or gradient dynamics.
- **Weak-to-Strong Knowledge Transfer:** Techniques that transfer knowledge from smaller, weaker models to larger, stronger ones.

The paper specifically addresses the limitations of delayed generalization associated with grokking. This is a significant problem because it makes training unpredictable and inefficient, especially in safety critical applications. The paper addresses this issue by offering a novel solution, inspired by the transfer learning paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To develop a method for accelerating grokking in neural networks, enabling them to generalize more quickly and efficiently.
- **Motivation:**
 - The grokking phenomenon, while interesting, introduces unpredictability and inefficiency into the training process.
 - Delayed generalization makes it difficult to determine when a model will generalize, even after it has memorized the training data.
 - The authors aim to modify training dynamics to achieve continuous progress, minimizing the gap between training and validation errors.
 - They hypothesize that data embedding plays a crucial role in determining training dynamics, and an informative embedding can facilitate faster generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a method called GrokTransfer, which leverages embedding transfer from a weaker model to accelerate grokking in a stronger target model. The approach consists of the following steps:

1. **Train a Weaker Model:** A smaller, weaker model is trained on the target task until it achieves a non-trivial (but sub-optimal) level of test performance.
2. **Extract Embedding:** The learned input embedding from the weaker model is extracted.
3. **Linear Mapping:** The extracted embedding is linearly mapped to the dimensionality of the target model's embedding layer.
4. **Initialize Target Model:** The target model's embedding layer is initialized using the linearly mapped embedding from the weaker model.

- **Theoretical Justification:** The authors provide a rigorous proof demonstrating that GrokTransfer enables direct generalization (without delay) in a two-layer neural network trained on a synthetic XOR task.
- **Empirical Validation:** The effectiveness of GrokTransfer is verified through empirical studies on various algorithmic tasks that exhibit grokking, including modular arithmetic and sparse parity. Experiments are conducted on both fully-connected neural networks and Transformers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- GrokTransfer effectively eliminates delayed generalization in the synthetic XOR task, enabling the target model to generalize directly without delay.
- Across empirical studies of algorithmic tasks, GrokTransfer reshapes training dynamics, significantly reducing or eliminating the phase transition between memorization and generalization.
- The method works for both fully-connected neural networks and Transformers.
- GrokTransfer can transfer embeddings from FNNs to Transformers.
- Ablation studies indicate that the weak model must have achieved some level of generalization for GrokTransfer to be effective.
- Computational cost analysis suggests that GrokTransfer can be faster than training the target model from scratch, primarily because the smaller, weaker model trains more quickly.
- Empirical evidence suggests that a weak model with three neurons is sufficient to allow the target model to generalize on a synthetic XOR task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research is significant because it provides a novel and principled approach to accelerate grokking and improve the predictability of neural network training. The potential impact of this work includes:

- **Improved Training Efficiency:** GrokTransfer can reduce the time and computational resources required to train neural networks, especially for tasks where grokking is prevalent.
- **Increased Predictability:** By eliminating or reducing delayed generalization, GrokTransfer makes it easier to anticipate when a model will generalize, allowing for more efficient experimentation and optimization.
- **Better Generalization Performance:** The informative embedding transferred from the weaker model can potentially improve the generalization performance of the target model.
- **Weak-to-Strong Generalization:** The study provides further evidence that knowledge can be effectively transferred from smaller, weaker models to larger, stronger ones.
- **Application to Real-World Problems:** The insights gained from this research could be applied to improve the training of neural networks for a wide range of real-world problems, especially those involving complex algorithmic tasks or structured data.

This paper also addresses the current limitations in the understanding of the causes of grokking. The results in this paper indicate that the choice of embedding is very important in the training dynamics, and that finding an informative embedding can close the gap between memorization and generalization.

**Potential Future Directions:**

- Extending the theoretical analysis to more complex tasks and model architectures.
- Investigating the application of GrokTransfer to real-world datasets and problems.
- Exploring alternative methods for constructing informative embeddings, such as unsupervised learning or pre-training on related tasks.
- Studying the relationship between GrokTransfer and other techniques for accelerating grokking, such as weight normalization or gradient amplification.
- Theoretical justification for why a three neuron weak model is sufficient for a synthetic XOR task.
