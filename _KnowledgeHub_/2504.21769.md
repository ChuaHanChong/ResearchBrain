# LLM-based Interactive Imitation Learning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Hamburg researchers demonstrate that large language models can effectively replace human teachers in interactive imitation learning for robotic manipulation tasks, with their LLM-iTeach framework achieving comparable or better performance than human-supervised methods through hierarchical prompting and similarity-based feedback mechanisms.

## Problem

- Interactive imitation learning requires significant human involvement, making it time-consuming and expensive to train robots
- Traditional imitation learning suffers from compounding errors and data mismatch issues in complex tasks
- Whether large language models can serve as effective interactive teachers, providing both corrective and evaluative feedback for training robotic manipulation agents, remained an open question

## Method

- LLM-iTeach framework uses hierarchical prompting to generate task-specific policies in Python code
- Implements similarity-based feedback mechanism where LLM provides corrective or evaluative feedback based on comparing agent actions with generated policy
- ==Similarity threshold== mechanism computes the arccos angle between the agent's predicted action and the CodePolicy's prescribed action, comparing it to a tunable threshold **β** to select feedback type

## Results

- Outperformed traditional behavior cloning and achieved comparable or better success rates than CEILing (human-supervised baseline) across manipulation tasks in RLBench simulator
- Demonstrated effective adaptation to new environments without requiring additional training data
- Identified optimal threshold for balancing corrective vs evaluative feedback through ablation studies

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining both corrective and evaluative feedback from LLMs leads to better performance than using either alone
> - LLMs can effectively break down complex manipulation tasks into sequences of steps with associated action and validation functions
> - The framework generalizes well to new tasks with only task descriptions needed

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work falls squarely within the intersection of several active research areas:

- **Robotics and Automation:** The paper addresses the fundamental challenge of enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks autonomously. This is a core area of research in robotics, with applications spanning manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, and more.
- **Imitation Learning (IL) and Interactive Imitation Learning (IIL):** IL and IIL are established approaches for training robots by leveraging human demonstrations and feedback. This paper builds upon existing IIL techniques, which have shown promise in overcoming limitations of traditional IL.
- **Large Language Models (LLMs):** The use of LLMs in robotics is a relatively new but rapidly growing field. Researchers are exploring how LLMs can provide robots with common-sense reasoning, planning capabilities, and the ability to understand and generate natural language instructions.
- **Code Generation:** This is an emerging area of research for robotics, where LLMs are used to generate code that directly controls robots, providing a bridge between high-level instructions and low-level actions.

The paper contributes to the broader research landscape by:

- **Addressing the cost and time limitations of human involvement in IIL:** Traditional IIL methods require significant human effort for providing feedback, which can be a major bottleneck.
- **Exploring the potential of LLMs as a cost-effective alternative to human teachers:** The paper investigates whether LLMs can effectively provide corrective and evaluative feedback to train robots, reducing the need for human supervision.
- **Introducing a novel framework for LLM-based IIL:** The LLM-iTeach framework combines hierarchical prompting with a similarity-based feedback mechanism to enable LLMs to interactively teach robots.
- **Demonstrating the feasibility and effectiveness of LLM-based IIL:** The paper presents experimental results showing that LLM-iTeach achieves comparable or even better performance than IIL methods that rely on human teachers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary research objective of this paper is to investigate the following research question:

**"Can LLMs interactively teach an imitation learning agent in robotic manipulation by providing corrective and evaluative feedback?"**

The motivation behind this research is driven by the following factors:

- **Limitations of traditional IL:** Imitation learning often suffers from compounding errors and data mismatch issues, leading to poor performance in complex robotic tasks.
- **Cost and time constraints of IIL:** Interactive imitation learning improves performance by incorporating human feedback, but the need for human involvement makes it time-consuming and expensive.
- **Emergent capabilities of LLMs:** Large language models have demonstrated impressive reasoning, planning, and natural language processing abilities, making them potential candidates for automating the teaching process in IIL.
- **Need for cost-effective and scalable solutions for robot training:** As robots become more prevalent, it is essential to develop methods for training them efficiently and affordably.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes a novel interactive imitation learning framework called **LLM-iTeach**, which utilizes an LLM as an interactive teacher to train a robotic agent. The methodology involves the following key components:

- **Hierarchical Prompting:** The LLM is prompted using a hierarchical strategy to generate a task-specific policy in Python code, which is termed CodePolicy. The hierarchy consists of:
 - **Task Planner Prompt:** A first-level prompt where the LLM receives a task description and breaks it down into a sequence of steps.
 - **Action and Check Prompts:** A second-level prompt where the LLM generates two functions for each step:
 - A function to calculate the action to be taken based on the current state.
 - A function to check whether the current step is still valid.

- **Similarity-Based Feedback Mechanism:** The LLM provides corrective and evaluative feedback to the agent based on a similarity comparison between the agent's predicted action and the action prescribed by the CodePolicy. The formula to determine if these angles are within threshold is calculated with arccos and compared to a threshold β.
 - If the agent's action is similar to the CodePolicy's action, the LLM provides positive evaluative feedback.
 - If the agent's action is dissimilar, the LLM provides corrective feedback by directly controlling the agent with the CodePolicy's action.

- **Experimental Evaluation:** The LLM-iTeach framework is evaluated on various robotic manipulation tasks in the RLBench simulator. The performance is compared to the following baselines:
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC):** A traditional imitation learning method.
 - **CEILing:** A state-of-the-art interactive imitation learning method using a human teacher.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the LLM-iTeach framework:

- **LLM-iTeach surpasses BC in success rate:** The LLM-iTeach framework consistently outperforms the traditional imitation learning method (BC) in terms of task completion success rate.
- **LLM-iTeach achieves comparable or even better performance than CEILing:** In some tasks, LLM-iTeach achieves similar or even better success rates compared to CEILing, which relies on human feedback. This highlights the potential of LLMs as cost-effective alternatives to human teachers.
- **Ablation studies highlight the importance of both corrective and evaluative feedback:** The ablation studies show that combining corrective and evaluative feedback leads to better performance than using only one type of feedback.
- **Hyperparameter study reveals the optimal threshold for action similarity:** The hyperparameter study identifies the optimal threshold for determining action similarity, which balances corrective and evaluative feedback.
- **LLM-iTeach generalizes to new tasks:** Experiments on additional tasks demonstrate the ability of LLM-iTeach to adapt to new environments with a simple description of the tasks.

The research presented in this paper has significant implications for the field of robotics and automation:
