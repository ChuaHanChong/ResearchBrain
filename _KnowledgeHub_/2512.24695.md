# Nested Learning: The Illusion of Deep Learning Architectures

> [!summary] Summary
> Nested Learning introduces a paradigm that reinterprets machine learning as nested, multi-level optimization problems with varying update frequencies, addressing the limitations of static models in continual learning. The proposed Hope architecture and M3 optimizer demonstrate enhanced performance in mitigating catastrophic forgetting and improving long-context understanding across various tasks.

## Problem

- Modern Multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) are largely static post-training, experiencing catastrophic forgetting when learning new information continually.
- Deep learning architectures often lack true "computational depth" for complex algorithmic tasks, with simply stacking more layers not equating to enhanced dynamic learning.
- A unifying theoretical framework is missing to integrate diverse machine learning components for effective continual learning and self-improvement.

## Method

- ==Nested Learning (NL)== proposes reinterpreting machine learning as a hierarchy of nested, multi-level optimization problems, with components categorized by their update frequencies.
- The ==Hope architecture== is developed, combining "self-modifying Titans" for dynamic, context-adaptive learning rules with a =="Continuum Memory System (CMS)"== for multi-timescale knowledge retention.
- Expressive optimizers, such as ==Multi-scale Momentum Muon (M3)==, are designed to manage gradient information across multiple timescales for improved parameter updates.

## Results

- Hope-enhanced Llama models consistently improved accuracy on class-incremental learning benchmarks (e.g., CLINC, Banking), demonstrating superior catastrophic forgetting mitigation.
- The Hope architecture achieved competitive performance in long-context understanding, handling up to **10M tokens** on BABILong and outperforming attention-free baselines on Needle-in-a-Haystack tasks.
- The M3 optimizer exhibited superior training and test loss compared to AdamW when pre-training Vision Transformers on ImageNet-21K, supporting the benefits of multi-timescale gradient memory.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - All machine learning components, including neural network layers and optimizers, can be fundamentally viewed as associative memory systems that compress their respective context flows.
> - The diverse appearance of deep learning architectures is an "illusion," as they are all composed of uniform feedforward layers operating at different levels and frequencies with unique objectives.
> - Continual learning and adaptation can be achieved through a multi-level, self-referential optimization process that mimics the human brain's multi-timescale information processing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at a critical juncture in the evolution of artificial intelligence, particularly deep learning and Large Language Models (LLMs). Over the past decade, AI research has largely focused on designing increasingly complex neural architectures and developing sophisticated optimization algorithms to train them effectively. This approach has led to remarkable successes across various domains, from computer vision (Krizhevsky et al., 2012; Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) and computational biology (Jumper et al., 2021) to the advent of powerful LLMs (Brown et al., 2020; Achiam et al., 2023).

However, the authors argue that despite these advancements, fundamental challenges persist, especially concerning how models continually learn, self-improve, and adapt to new information over time. Current LLMs, while exhibiting impressive "in-context learning" abilities for zero- or few-shot tasks, remain largely static after their initial deployment. This static nature leads to limitations such as catastrophic forgetting in continual learning scenarios and an inability to acquire new knowledge beyond their pre-trained data or immediate context. The paper uses the analogy of "anterograde amnesia" to describe this condition in LLMs, where new long-term memories cannot be formed.

Existing efforts to address these limitations often involve computationally expensive retraining, reliance on external components, or susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting. The authors contend that current deep learning models, particularly Transformer-based architectures, primarily excel at "static tasks that require in-distribution predictions over an a-priori fixed set." They critically question whether merely stacking more layers provides genuine "computational depth" or if a new learning paradigm is required to unlock true continual learning capabilities.

"Nested Learning" (NL) emerges as a response to this call for a new paradigm. It seeks to provide a unifying theoretical framework that not only offers new insights into existing algorithms and architectures but also reveals a "new dimension to stacking layers in deep learning" that enhances computational depth and continual learning. By drawing inspiration from neurophysiological insights into the human brain's multi-timescale processing and uniform, reusable structure, this work attempts to move beyond the conventional deep learning mindset of monolithic, task-specific architectures towards a more dynamic, adaptive, and biologically plausible model of intelligence. It implicitly critiques the "illusion of deep learning architecture" by suggesting that the perceived heterogeneity of current models might stem from a limited perspective, and a deeper understanding reveals a more uniform, layered system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Nested Learning (NL) as a novel paradigm for designing and understanding machine learning models, specifically addressing the fundamental limitations of current deep learning approaches in areas such as continual learning, self-improvement, and long-context reasoning.

The core motivations are:

1. **Overcoming Static LLMs and Catastrophic Forgetting:** Existing LLMs are largely static post-training, struggling to continually acquire new capabilities or integrate new knowledge without forgetting old information (catastrophic forgetting). NL aims to provide a framework for models that can dynamically adapt and learn over extended periods.
2. **Enhancing Computational Depth:** The authors question whether merely stacking more layers in deep neural networks truly increases their computational depth or expressivity for complex algorithmic tasks. NL proposes a way to enhance this depth through multi-level, nested optimization processes.
3. **Providing a Unifying Framework:** NL seeks to unify seemingly disparate concepts in machine learning, such as optimizers, architectures, meta-learning, and in-context learning, by representing them as interconnected optimization problems within a hierarchical structure.
4. **Neuro-scientific Inspiration:** The paradigm is heavily motivated by the human brain's remarkable ability for continual learning, neuroplasticity, multi-timescale processing (brain oscillations), and its uniform, reusable architectural structure. The paper explicitly draws parallels to human memory consolidation processes and challenges the traditional long-term/short-term memory dichotomy.
5. **Designing More Expressive Learning Algorithms:** By reinterpreting existing optimizers and architectures as associative memory modules, the authors aim to identify their limitations and propose "more expressive" alternatives that can manage memory and context more effectively across different timescales.

In essence, the paper is motivated by a desire to move beyond the current "deep learning" paradigm, which is perceived as merely "stacking of layers," towards a more nuanced understanding of learning as a multi-level, self-referential optimization process that mimics the adaptive capabilities observed in biological intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of Nested Learning (NL) revolves around reinterpreting a machine learning model and its training procedure as a "set of nested, multi-level, and/or parallel optimization problems, each of which with its own 'context flow'." This perspective suggests that learning, even at different granularities (from parameter updates to architectural choices), can be unified under a single framework where components interact and transfer knowledge across different "levels" based on their update frequencies.

The key methodological components include:

1. **Associative Memory as a Core Concept:**
- **Definition:** Associative memory is defined as an operator M(·) that maps a set of keys (K) to values (V) by optimizing an objective function L(M(K); V).
- **Learning vs. Memorization:** Drawing from neuropsychology, "memory is a neural update caused by an input, and learning is the process for acquiring effective and useful memory." This distinguishes simple information storage from the adaptive process of making that storage useful.
- **Reinterpretation of Existing Components:** The paper argues that both neural network layers and optimization algorithms (like gradient descent) are fundamentally associative memory systems that "compress their own context flow."

2. **Nested Optimization Processes and Update Frequencies:**
- **Levels:** Components of a machine learning algorithm are organized into ordered "levels" based on their "update frequency" (how often they are updated per unit of time). Faster-updating components are in lower levels, while slower-updating components are in higher levels.
- **Neural Learning Module:** This term refers to the integrated system of architecture and its training/optimization process, emphasizing that they are not independent but jointly determine the model's behavior.
- **Knowledge Transfer:** Various mechanisms for knowledge transfer between levels are proposed:
 - **Direct Parametric/Non-Parametric Connection:** One level's output or parameters directly condition another.
 - **Via Backpropagation:** Gradient flow between blocks at different frequencies.
 - **Via Initialization (Meta-learning):** A higher-level problem learns the optimal initial state for a lower-level problem (e.g., MAML).
 - **Via Generation (Hypernetworks, Optimizers):** One block generates weights or context (e.g., gradients for an optimizer) for another.

3. **Reinterpreting Optimizers as Learning Modules:**
- **Backpropagation as Associative Memory:** Gradient descent in backpropagation is seen as an associative memory that maps each layer's input to its "local error signal" or "surprise." It's a "self-referential process" where values are self-generated.
- **Momentum-based Optimizers:** Momentum terms are interpreted as associative memory modules that compress past gradients, providing a "global view of the loss landscape." Adam is shown to be an optimal associative memory for L2 regression on gradients.
- **Expressive Optimizers:** Based on this insight, the paper proposes:
 - **Delta Gradient Descent (DGD):** An update rule derived from L2 regression that adapts based on the current data sample and previous weight state, rather than just current gradient.
 - **Multi-scale Momentum Muon (M3):** An optimizer incorporating a Continuum Memory System for long-term gradient information, combining aspects of Adam, Muon, and CMS.

4. **Reinterpreting Architectures as Neural Learning Modules:**
- **Unified View:** Modern architectures (Transformers, RNNs) are seen as instances of Nested Systems of Associative Memories (NSAMs). Softmax attention is a non-parametric solution to an L2 regression objective on tokens, while RNNs use parametric (Hebbian, Delta rule) learning.
- **"Illusion of Deep Learning Architecture":** The perceived heterogeneity of deep learning models is an "illusion" because, from the NL perspective, all components are fundamentally uniform feedforward layers (MLPs) operating at different levels and frequencies with their own objectives.

5. **Hope: A Self-Referential Learning Module with Continuum Memory:**
- **Continuum Memory System (CMS):** Generalizes the long-term/short-term memory distinction. It's a chain of MLP blocks, each operating at a different, explicitly defined update frequency (e.g., MLP(f1)..., MLP(fk)). This creates a spectrum of memory persistence, where knowledge can be "looped back" to prevent forgetting.
- **Self-Modifying Titans:** A sequence model component where not just the memory itself, but also the projection matrices (keys, values, queries) and even learning rate/retention gates, are self-modifying associative memories, generating their own values to adapt to the context. This allows for higher-order in-context learning.
- **Hope Architecture:** Combines self-modifying Titans (for dynamic, context-adaptive learning rules) with the CMS (for persistent, multi-timescale knowledge storage), resulting in a powerful, adaptive, and continually learning module.
- **Initialization with Pre-Trained Models:** A strategy to initialize CMS blocks with weights from pre-trained models, allowing for rapid adaptation of existing models into the NL framework.

The methodology is inherently theoretical and then validated empirically. The theoretical reinterpretation forms the basis for proposing new, more powerful algorithms and architectures designed to exploit the multi-level, multi-timescale nature of NL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive empirical evaluations to support the efficacy of the Nested Learning paradigm and its derived architectures/optimizers, specifically the Hope model and M3 optimizer.

1. **Continual Learning and In-Context Learning:**
- **Class-Incremental Learning:** On CLINC, Banking, and DBpedia datasets, Hope-enhanced Llama models (Llama3-8B and Llama-3B) consistently achieved the best accuracy compared to baselines like ICL, EWC, and InCA. This highlights Hope's superior ability to continually acquire new knowledge while mitigating catastrophic forgetting.
- **Effect of Memory Levels:** Experiments on LongHealth, QASPER (Q&A), and MK-NIAH (multi-key retrieval) showed that increasing the number of memory levels in Hope significantly improves in-context learning and long-context understanding. Higher numbers of memory levels and appropriately tuned lowest frequencies were beneficial.
- **Continual Translation of a Novel Language (CTNL):** In learning and translating two new languages sequentially, ICL showed catastrophic forgetting, while Hope (especially Hope-3 with three additional memory levels) significantly mitigated this, almost recovering the performance of non-continual learning setups. This strongly supports the importance of CMS for persistent knowledge retention.

2. **Long Context Understanding:**
- **Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH):** Hope, trained from scratch, outperformed all attention-free baselines (RetNet, DeltaNet, RWKV-7, Comba, DLA, Titans) across various NIAH tasks (single, multi-key, multi-query, multi-value), especially in longer contexts. Hope-Attention (Transformer with CMS) also showed improvement over vanilla Transformers. This underscores the benefits of self-referential updates and CMS for maintaining performance in extremely long contexts.
- **BABILong Benchmark:** Hope demonstrated competitive performance with large models (GPT4, GPT4o-mini) and state-of-the-art small models (RMT, ARMT, Titans) at long contexts. Crucially, Hope maintained good performance even up to 10M context length, where other models rapidly degraded, attributing this to its CMS design. Fine-tuning was found essential for small models to adjust lower-frequency levels for optimal memory management.

3. **Language Modeling and Common-Sense Reasoning:**
- On standard benchmarks (Wikitext, LMB, PIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC, SIQA, BoolQ), Hope achieved the best average performance among all baselines (Transformers, various RNNs, deep memory modules) across 760M and 1.3B parameter models. Hope showed greater performance gains with parameter scaling compared to other attention-free models.

4. **In-Context Recall and Synthetic Benchmarks:**
- **In-context Recall Tasks:** Hope showed competitive results on tasks like SWDE, NQ, DROP, FDA, SQUAD, and TQA, outperforming all other attention-free models and closing the performance gap with Transformers.
- **MAD Synthetic Benchmark:** Hope achieved the best results across tasks like compression, in-context recall, fuzzy in-context recall, selective memory, and copying, further validating its advanced memory management capabilities.

5. **Language Recognition Tasks (Non-Parallelizable):**
- Hope achieved perfect scores on all formal language recognition tasks (e.g., Parallel Parity, (aa)*, anbn), demonstrating strong capabilities in non-parallelizable, state-tracking problems, akin to LSTMs and SRWMs, while retaining the benefits of parallelizable training for other tasks.

6. **Expressive Optimizers (M3):**
- **ImageNet-21K (ViT):** The M3 optimizer (Multi-scale Momentum Muon) demonstrated superior training and test loss compared to AdamW and standard Muon when pre-training a Vision Transformer on ImageNet-21K. This supports the idea that more expressive optimizers with multi-timescale gradient memory can find better solutions.
- **Efficiency:** While effective, M3 showed comparable or slightly slower training times compared to AdaMuon and Muon for larger language models (140M and 1.3B parameters), indicating potential computational overhead that needs to be balanced against performance gains.

7. **Ablation Studies on Hope:**
- Removing or altering key components of Hope (DGD, momentum, weight decay, CMS, inner projections) consistently led to a degradation in language modeling perplexity and common-sense reasoning accuracy. This confirms that all proposed design choices contribute positively to Hope's overall performance.

In summary, the empirical findings consistently demonstrate that the NL paradigm, particularly through the Hope architecture and its constituent components (self-modifying Titans, CMS, DGD), offers significant advantages in continual learning, long-context understanding, and overall language model performance. It bridges the gap between the strengths of Transformers (parallelizability, raw power) and recurrent models (sequential processing, memory management) while addressing their respective limitations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Nested Learning: The Illusion of Deep Learning Architecture" paper presents a highly significant theoretical and practical contribution with far-reaching potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence:

1. **A New Paradigm for AI System Design:** NL offers a fresh, unifying conceptual framework for understanding and designing machine learning models. By viewing models as interconnected, multi-level optimization problems with varying update frequencies, it provides a new "design axis of levels" beyond merely increasing depth or width. This could inspire radically different architectures and learning algorithms that are inherently more dynamic and adaptive.

2. **Rethinking Core Deep Learning Concepts:**
- **"The Illusion of Deep Learning Architecture":** A profound implication is the assertion that the perceived heterogeneity of modern architectures (e.g., Transformers vs. RNNs vs. MLPs) is an illusion. NL suggests that at a fundamental level, they are all composed of uniform feedforward layers, differing only in their internal objectives, update frequencies, and knowledge transfer mechanisms. This perspective could simplify architectural design principles by revealing underlying commonalities.
- **Unified View of Optimizers and Architectures:** The paper demonstrates that both neural network components and optimization algorithms can be understood as associative memory modules. This unification emphasizes their interconnectedness, suggesting a future where optimizers are explicitly "architecture-specific" and co-designed with the model.
- **Redefining Learning and Memory:** By aligning with neuropsychological definitions, NL reframes "memory" as any neural update and "learning" as the process of acquiring useful memory. This distributes memory across the entire system rather than isolating it in specific "blocks," fostering a more holistic view of knowledge storage and retrieval.

3. **Advancing Continual Learning and Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting:** This is arguably the most critical practical impact. The Continuum Memory System (CMS) and self-modifying mechanisms of Hope directly tackle the Achilles' heel of current LLMs – their static nature and susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting. By allowing different components to learn and retain information at varied timescales, NL provides a roadmap for building models that can truly learn and adapt continuously from new experiences without degrading past knowledge. The demonstrated empirical success of Hope in continual learning tasks is a strong validation of this potential.

4. **Enhancing Long-Context Understanding:** The ability of Hope to manage and reason over extremely long contexts (e.g., 10M tokens in BABILong) significantly pushes the boundaries of current LLMs. This is crucial for applications requiring deep comprehension of extensive documents, conversations, or datasets. The multi-timescale memory system (CMS) is key to this, allowing models to retain relevant information over vast input lengths.

5. **Boosting Self-Improvement and Adaptability:** The concept of "self-modifying learning modules" like self-referential Titans, where models learn to modify their own parameters, learning rates, and update rules based on context, paves the way for truly autonomous and self-improving AI systems. This could lead to models that can adapt more intelligently to novel tasks and environments without explicit human reprogramming.

6. **Bridging AI and Neuroscience:** The strong neuro-scientific motivation, drawing parallels between NL's multi-level optimization and the brain's plasticity, oscillations, and distributed memory, can foster interdisciplinary research. It provides a computational framework to test hypotheses about biological learning mechanisms and develop AI models that are not just performant but also potentially more interpretable and robust due to bio-inspired design.

7. **Challenging the "Pre-training" Paradigm:** By reinterpreting pre-training as merely "in-context learning with ultra-large context," NL suggests that the sharp distinction between "training time" and "test time" is an artificial construct. In a continually learning system, this boundary dissolves, and models are always in a state of learning and adaptation, which is a significant shift in how AI systems are conceptualized and deployed.

While the computational overhead of some expressive optimizers (like M3) still needs refinement for large-scale deployment, the fundamental insights and architectural designs proposed in this paper provide a compelling vision. "Nested Learning" is presented not as a definitive solution to catastrophic forgetting but as a "roadmap" for future progress, emphasizing the unexplored potential of exploiting the "extra design axis of levels." Its impact lies in redefining what a "deep" learning model truly is and how it can continually interact with and learn from the world.
