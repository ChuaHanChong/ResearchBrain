# TrackGo: A Flexible and Efficient Method for Controllable Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> TrackGo introduces a framework for controllable video generation that enables precise object motion and scene transformations while maintaining computational efficiency. It integrates motion information by manipulating temporal attention maps within Stable Video Diffusion, leading to improved video quality and motion faithfulness with substantially fewer parameters and faster inference speeds compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models struggle to provide precise, fine-grained control over object movements and complex trajectories, often leading to inaccurate actions or unintended shifts.
- Current controllable video generation methods, such as those relying on heavy encoders or extensive additional networks, typically incur significant computational overhead and slow inference times.
- Pose-directed control methods are effective for human-subject videos, but their applicability is constrained to that specific domain, limiting generalization to arbitrary objects and scenes.

## Method

- **TrackGo** preprocesses user-defined masks and motion arrows into structured ==point trajectories==, which serve as explicit motion guidance.
- A lightweight TrackAdapter is introduced into the ==temporal self-attention== modules of ==Stable Video Diffusion==, using a dual-branch attention mechanism to integrate these motion conditions efficiently.
- The TrackAdapter employs an attention mask to selectively suppress the original attention in controlled areas, allowing its dedicated branch to manage specified movements while the base model handles other regions.

## Results

- TrackGo achieved superior quantitative results, with FVD scores of **248.27**, FID of **25.60**, and ObjMC of **191.15** on the VIPSeg dataset, outperforming DragAnything (FVD **294.91**, FID **28.16**, ObjMC **236.02**).
- The method demonstrated significant efficiency gains, requiring only **29.36M** additional parameters and achieving an inference time of 33.94s, notably faster than DragAnything's **685.06M** parameters and 152.98s.
- Qualitative evaluations and a user study (62% preference for TrackGo) confirmed its ability to generate videos with precise target movement and coherent backgrounds, while also offering flexible control over background motion via a adjustable suppression parameter.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal self-attention layers in video diffusion models naturally highlight areas of motion, making them an effective point of injection for precise control signals.
> - A dual-branch attention architecture, combined with an attention mask, allows for a clear separation of concerns, enabling precise control over specified objects without disrupting background coherence.
> - Integrating motion conditions by directly manipulating attention maps, rather than adding heavy network components, offers a computationally efficient pathway to controllable video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced substantial advancements, particularly with the emergence of diffusion models. Initial work focused on text-to-video or image-to-video generation, achieving progress in video quality. Examples include VLDM, AnimateDiff, I2VGen-XL, and Stable Video Diffusion (SVD). While these models produce high-quality videos, they primarily rely on coarse-grained semantic guidance, which can result in generated actions that do not precisely align with user intent.

To address this, controllable video generation has become a significant research direction, aiming for precise control over object movement and scene transformations. Existing approaches in controllable video synthesis can be broadly categorized:

- **Pose-directed methods**: These approaches, such as Disco, MagicAnimate, DreamPose, and Animate Anyone, utilize human pose as a control signal. While effective for videos depicting human subjects, their applicability is constrained to this specific domain.
- **Generalizable control methods**: These methods aim to integrate diverse control signals into pre-trained video diffusion models for broader applicability.
 - AnimateDiff employs Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) within temporal attention mechanisms to learn camera motion. However, its capacity for manipulating complex movements can be limited by the repetitiveness of motion patterns in its training data.
 - MotionCtrl and DragNUWA encode sparse optical flow into dense optical flow as guidance. These often involve heavy encoders, which can increase the number of model parameters and lead to slower inference times.
 - Boximator uses bounding boxes to dictate motion. A limitation of this approach is that bounding boxes may encompass redundant background regions, potentially interfering with the target object's motion and disrupting background coherence.
 - DragAnything employs masks to identify a central point and generate a Gaussian map for tracking. This method has shown limitations in controlling partial or fine-grained object movements and can generate incoherent backgrounds. Furthermore, its reliance on the ControlNet architecture often results in increased parameters and slower inference speed.

TrackGo positions itself within this landscape by addressing the identified gaps in precise control for complex scenarios (including fine-grained object parts, sophisticated motion trajectories, and coherent background movement) and the need for computational efficiency in control implementation. It seeks to provide a flexible and efficient mechanism that overcomes the limitations of previous methods regarding both control granularity and computational overhead.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a method for controllable video generation that offers both precise control over motion and computational efficiency. The authors identify two main challenges that motivated the development of TrackGo:

1. **Precision in Complex Scenarios**: Achieving precise control over target objects, including their fine-grained parts and complex motion trajectories, remains difficult. Current methods often exhibit limitations:
 - DragAnything, for instance, struggles with controlling partial or fine-grained object movements, often causing unintended shifts in the entire object or even the background.
 - Boximator's use of bounding boxes can inadvertently include background elements, leading to interference with the target's motion and potential inconsistencies in the generated background.
 - There is a need for a control mechanism that allows users to specify target areas with high granularity and define complex movement paths clearly.

2. **Computational Efficiency**: Existing approaches for injecting control signals into video generation models frequently lead to a significant increase in the model's parameters and a reduction in inference speed.
 - Models like DragAnything, leveraging architectures such as ControlNet, and DragNUWA, which uses heavy encoders, add substantial computational overhead. This inefficiency hinders their practical deployment in real-world applications requiring rapid video generation.
 - The motivation here is to devise an efficient method for integrating motion conditions that minimizes additional computational cost while maintaining or improving control accuracy.

To address these challenges, TrackGo aims to answer two specific questions:
- What type of control input can accurately describe complex target motion?
- How can this control be efficiently integrated into existing video generation models?

The authors were motivated by the observation that the attention maps within temporal self-attention layers of video generation models inherently highlight areas of motion (as illustrated in Figure 1). This insight suggested that directly manipulating these attention maps could be a mechanism for precise motion control with minimal added computational burden, contrasting with methods that rely on extensive new network components.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TrackGo is a novel framework for motion-controllable video generation. It operates in two main stages: Point Trajectories Generation and Conditional Video Generation, using the Stable Video Diffusion Model (SVD) as its base architecture.

#### 3.1. Point Trajectories Generation

This stage focuses on converting user-defined motion guidance into a structured format suitable for video generation.

- **Inference Process**: When a user provides an initial image, free-form masks defining the target areas (from entire objects to fine-grained parts), and arrows indicating desired movement trajectories, TrackGo preprocesses these inputs to extract point trajectories (P).
- **Training Data Construction**: For training, the process is automated:
 - **Segmentation**: The DEVA tool is used to segment main components in ground truth videos, yielding a sequence of masks for each component (M_j_i).
 - **Control Point Selection**: For each mask in the first frame, K points are selected. To ensure uniformity, 3K points are initially chosen randomly within the mask, followed by K-means clustering to refine them to K points. The number of points K varies from 16 to 64, depending on the mask's area relative to the total image area. Up to 6 significant components (s) are considered.
 - **Trajectory Tracking**: Co-Tracker is then used to track these s*K control points across all frames of the video, generating detailed motion trajectories (T).
 - **Point Trajectory Map Creation**: Each control point is assigned a color corresponding to its component, and its trajectory is plotted to construct the point trajectories map (P), a tensor of dimension L (video length) × H (height) × W (width) × 3 (color channels).
 - This process yielded a training dataset of 110,000 (Video, Point Trajectories, First Frame Masks) triplets.

#### 3.2. Injecting Motion Conditions via TrackAdapter

This stage integrates the generated point trajectories into the base SVD model to guide video generation.

- **Motion Conditions Extraction**: The point trajectories (P) are fed into a lightweight trainable encoder (E), similar in structure to Animate Anyone's encoder (six convolution layers, two pooling layers, one fully-connected layer). This encoder compresses P into a temporal representation (f). The representation `f` is then down-sampled to match the resolutions of various temporal self-attention layers within the U-Net.
- **TrackAdapter Design**: The TrackAdapter is a novel component designed for efficient and effective injection of motion control. It is inserted into each temporal self-attention module of the SVD's 3D U-Net.
 - **Dual-Branch Attention**: The TrackAdapter introduces an additional self-attention branch that runs parallel to the original temporal self-attention branch.
 - **TrackAdapter Attention Map (A')**: This new branch computes its attention map (A') using the compressed motion representation `f` to derive its query (Q') and key (K') matrices: `A' = softmax((Q'(K')^T)/sqrt(d))`. This map specifically focuses on the motion within the target area.
 - **Attention Mask (A_M)**: To ensure the original attention branch does not interfere with the motion specified by the TrackAdapter, an attention mask (A_M) is generated from A'. Regions in A' where values exceed a threshold (α = 0.2) are set to -inf (or -10^4 for fp16 precision) in A_M, effectively suppressing attention in these areas for the original branch. Other regions in A_M are set to 0.
 - **Modified Original Attention**: The original temporal self-attention map (A) is then recomputed by adding A_M to its logits before the softmax: `A = softmax((QK^T)/sqrt(d) + A_M)`. This mechanism ensures a separation of control: the TrackAdapter handles the specified motion areas, while the original branch processes the rest.
 - **Final Output**: The ultimate output of the temporal self-attention block is derived by combining the modified original attention and the TrackAdapter's attention, applied to the value matrix (V): `O = (A + A')V`. This approach allows for accurate and cohesive generation of both the specific target movements and the overall video context with minimal computational overhead.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

- **Training Objective**: The model is optimized using a combined loss function:
 - **Noise Prediction Loss (L_mse)**: The standard denoising diffusion objective, `||ϵ_θ(z_t; E(P), I, t) - ϵ||^2`, where `z_t` is the noisy latent representation, `ϵ_θ` is the predicted noise, and `ϵ` is the ground truth noise. Point trajectories (P) and the input image (I) are provided as conditions.
 - **Attention Loss (L_attn)**: `Σ_q Σ_i (A_q(i,i) * ϕ(M_i))^2`. This loss is applied to the original temporal self-attention maps (A_q) and aims to suppress the areas corresponding to the motion masks (M_i), thereby accelerating the model's convergence and encouraging the original branch to focus on non-motion areas.
 - **Total Loss**: `L = L_mse + λL_attn`, where `λ` is a hyperparameter balancing the two loss terms.
- **Inference Process**: During inference, users provide an initial frame, masks, and arrows. Point trajectories are generated, and `f` is extracted. The TrackAdapter is then engaged to guide video generation. A unique feature during inference is the ability to adjust `τ`, the suppression intensity for unspecified areas. Setting `τ` to a negative value (-inf for areas where A' < α, or specifically -10^4 for fp16) allows users to control how much background elements move or remain static, enhancing fluidity or mitigating visual interference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of TrackGo was evaluated through extensive experiments, comparing it against established controllable video generation methods.

#### 4.1. Experimental Settings

- **Base Model**: Stable Video Diffusion (SVD).
- **Hardware**: 8 NVIDIA A100-80G GPUs.
- **Optimization**: AdamW optimizer, 18,000 training steps, learning rate of 3e-5, batch size of 8.
- **Encoder (E)**: Lightweight, consisting of six convolution layers, two pooling layers, and one fully-connected layer.
- **Dataset**: An internal dataset of 200,000 video clips was filtered to 110,000 high-quality clips for training. Videos were resized to 1024 × 576 pixels and standardized to 25 frames. The test set included the VIPSeg validation set and an additional 300 videos from the internal validation dataset, with trajectories extracted from the first 14 frames for evaluation.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **FVD (Fréchet Video Distance)**: Measures video quality (lower is better).
 - **FID (Fréchet Inception Distance)**: Measures image quality (lower is better).
 - **ObjMC (Object Motion Consistency)**: Measures motion faithfulness by computing the Euclidean distance between predicted and ground truth trajectories (lower is better).
- **Baseline Methods**: DragNUWA and DragAnything, both based on SVD and utilizing trajectory information.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation

Table 1 presents a quantitative comparison of TrackGo with DragNUWA and DragAnything on both the VIPSeg and internal validation datasets.

| Method | VIPSeg FVD ↓ | VIPSeg FID ↓ | VIPSeg ObjMC ↓ | Internal FVD ↓ | Internal FID ↓ | Internal ObjMC ↓ | Parameters | Inference Time |
| :------------- | :----------- | :----------- | :------------- | :------------- | :------------- | :--------------- | :--------- | :------------- |
| DragNUWA | 321.31 | 30.15 | 298.98 | 178.37 | 38.07 | 129.80 | 160.38M | 58.12s |
| DragAnything | 294.91 | 28.16 | 236.02 | 169.73 | 32.85 | 133.89 | 685.06M | 152.98s |
| **TrackGo** | **248.27** | **25.60** | **191.15** | **136.11** | **29.19** | **79.52** | **29.36M** | **33.94s** |

The results indicate that TrackGo consistently achieved lower (better) scores across all video quality (FVD), image quality (FID), and motion faithfulness (ObjMC) metrics on both validation sets.

Furthermore, TrackGo demonstrated superior efficiency:
- **Parameters**: TrackGo required significantly fewer additional parameters (29.36M) compared to DragNUWA (160.38M) and DragAnything (685.06M).
- **Inference Time**: TrackGo exhibited the fastest inference speed (33.94s) for generating a video, substantially quicker than DragNUWA (58.12s) and DragAnything (152.98s).

#### 4.3. Qualitative Evaluation

Visual comparisons (Figure 4, Figure 2, Figure 8) highlighted TrackGo's ability to generate videos with precise target movement and coherent backgrounds.
- **DragNUWA** struggled with accurate perception of control areas, leading to incorrect object movements or fragmented optical flow (e.g., planet not perceived, smoke not following train).
- **DragAnything** demonstrated issues with fine-grained control, often causing entire objects or even backgrounds to shift unintentionally instead of isolated parts.
- **TrackGo**, in contrast, produced videos where target regions moved precisely according to user input, while maintaining visual quality and consistency in the background.

#### 4.4. Attention Mask Control of Background Movement

The `τ` parameter, which controls the suppression intensity of unspecified areas, allowed for flexible background motion adjustment (Figure 5).
- High suppression (`τ = -10^4`) resulted in static unspecified areas, sometimes leading to distortion (e.g., a static steering wheel with moving hands).
- Intermediate suppression (`τ = -10^2`) allowed some background movement but could still yield disharmonious elements (e.g., a steering wheel moving but its logo remaining static).
- No suppression (`τ = 0`) allowed free background movement, often leading to cohesive videos, though sometimes with undesirable extraneous motion. This feature offers users a trade-off between realism and specific artistic control over background dynamics.

#### 4.5. User Study

A user study involving 30 participants assessed video quality based on consistency with conditions and overall visual quality (Figure 6). TrackGo was preferred in 62% of cases, significantly outperforming DragNUWA (21.67%) and DragAnything (16.33%).

#### 4.6. Comparison with SVD

A comparison with the base SVD model (without TrackAdapter) revealed that SVD tended to focus on global dynamic effects (e.g., water surface motion) rather than specific object movements (e.g., a ship's trajectory). TrackGo, with the TrackAdapter, enabled the temporal self-attention layers to concentrate on the intended moving object, demonstrating the adapter's effectiveness in guiding attention.

#### 4.7. Ablation Study

An ablation study (Table 2) investigated the contributions of the attention mask and attention loss to the model's performance and convergence speed.
- Models without attention loss exhibited higher FVD scores, particularly at later training steps, indicating that `L_attn` accelerates training and aids convergence.
- Models without both the attention mask and attention loss showed significantly higher FVD, stabilizing at a suboptimal level compared to the full TrackGo method. This confirms the individual and combined effectiveness of these components.

#### 4.8. Diversity and Camera Motion

TrackGo demonstrated the ability to generate diverse results from the same initial image by varying control conditions (Figure 9), such as making a rabbit's eyes open or closed, or its ears swing. It also showed robustness to minor variations in user input for similar desired effects. Additionally, TrackGo can achieve camera motion effects by selecting the entire image region as the motion area and defining a trajectory (Figure 10).

### 5. Significance

TrackGo introduces a novel approach to controllable video generation that addresses persistent challenges in precision and efficiency. Its primary contributions include:

- **Flexible and Precise Control**: By combining free-form masks and arrows, TrackGo offers users a highly granular and intuitive mechanism for specifying target objects, their fine-grained parts, and complex motion trajectories. This capability is particularly relevant for intricate animation scenarios, distinguishing it from methods that struggle with partial object control or rely on less precise bounding box-based inputs.
- **Efficient Control Integration**: The introduction of the TrackAdapter, designed to integrate motion control information directly into the temporal self-attention layers of a pre-trained diffusion model, represents a significant step towards efficiency. This approach leverages existing model mechanisms, resulting in a substantial reduction in added parameters and a notable increase in inference speed compared to previous methods like DragAnything and DragNUWA.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: Quantitative evaluations demonstrate that TrackGo surpasses existing models in terms of video quality (FVD), image quality (FID), and motion faithfulness (ObjMC). The efficiency gains, coupled with enhanced visual fidelity and control accuracy, indicate an advancement in the practical applicability of controllable video generation.
- **Novel Training and Inference Features**: The attention loss function accelerates model convergence, while the ability to dynamically control the movement intensity of unspecified background areas during inference provides users with enhanced creative flexibility.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Creative Industries**: TrackGo has the potential to significantly impact creative industries such as film production, animation, and video game development. It can streamline the creation of complex animated sequences, facilitate rapid prototyping, and enable artists to precisely control elements within generated videos, thereby reducing manual labor and production timelines.
- **Content Creation**: For general content creators, TrackGo could offer powerful tools for generating dynamic and custom video content, making sophisticated video editing and animation more accessible.
- **Further Research**: The methodology, particularly the TrackAdapter's approach to manipulating temporal attention maps, could inspire new research directions in efficient and fine-grained control for other generative models.

**Limitations:**

The paper identifies two main limitations of the current TrackGo iteration:

- **Handling Large Movements**: The model may struggle with very large object movements, potentially leading to distortions in details of the generated video clips (e.g., a bird's head distortion in later frames). This suggests that the foundational motion prior knowledge might still be insufficient for extreme transformations.
- **3D Rotation Information**: TrackGo currently cannot accurately handle 3D rotation, often misinterpreting it as translation (e.g., rotating a face results in incorrect translational movement). This limits its ability to generate videos depicting complex spatial transformations.

**Societal Impact:**

While TrackGo improves the controllability of video generation, it, like other powerful generative AI models, carries potential societal risks. The enhanced capability to create highly realistic and controllable video content raises concerns about the potential misuse for generating "fake videos" (deepfakes). The authors acknowledge this critical issue, emphasizing the need for responsible deployment and ethical considerations to mitigate negative social impacts. This highlights the ongoing responsibility within the AI research community to develop safeguards and promote ethical use alongside technological advancements.
