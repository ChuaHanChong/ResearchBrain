# OmniTacTune: Policy-Agnostic Real-World RL for Tactile Residual Adaptation of Visual Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> OMNITACTUNE introduces a real-world reinforcement learning framework that enhances pre-trained visual policies for contact-rich robot manipulation by learning tactile-informed residual corrections. The pipeline achieved success rates of 85-100% in tasks such as Peg-in-Hole and Charger Insertion within 40-80 minutes of online practice.

## Problem

- Visual policies, while scalable from abundant data, often fail in contact-rich manipulation tasks that require precise local force and contact geometry measurements.
- Tactile sensing provides crucial interaction signals, but tactile data collection is costly and difficult to generalize, leading to a 'scale gap' compared to visual datasets.
- Training comprehensive visuo-tactile policies from scratch is impractical due to the scarcity of large-scale, paired multimodal datasets.

## Method

- **OMNITACTUNE** implements a two-stage real-world ==RL== pipeline that learns lightweight, policy-agnostic ==tactile residual corrections== on top of frozen pre-trained visual base policies.
- A ==warm-start== stage initializes the system through autonomous rollouts, ==critic bootstrapping==, and fine-tuning of a tactile encoder without requiring offline visuo-tactile demonstrations.
- A ==multi-sensory reward function==, combining object-centric visual flow guidance and tactile contact signals, provides dense feedback for stable and efficient real-world learning.

## Results

- OMNITACTUNE improved initial visual policy success rates (**5-40%**) to **85-100%** across four contact-rich tasks within **40-80 minutes** of online practice, outperforming baselines by over **40%**.
- The framework successfully adapted to five different visual base policies (e.g., Flow, ACT, Diffusion, π0.5) on the Peg-in-Hole task, demonstrating broad compatibility and achieving up to **100%** success.
- It generalized across various tactile representations, including image-based (AnyTouch2) and low-dimensional marker-based tactile data, maintaining high performance with effective encoders.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning tactile residual corrections on existing visual policies is more efficient and effective for contact-rich manipulation than direct visuo-tactile imitation learning.
> - A warm-start phase, including critic bootstrapping and tactile encoder adaptation with data augmentation, is critical for stable and sample-efficient real-world tactile RL.
> - The policy-agnostic design of OMNITACTUNE allows it to generalize across diverse visual base policy architectures and different tactile sensing modalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has made progress in developing visual policies, primarily by leveraging scalable data sources such as teleoperated robot demonstrations and large-scale human videos. These visual policies offer motion priors that can generalize across various tasks and environments. However, their efficacy diminishes in contact-rich manipulation tasks, which depend significantly on precise local force and contact geometry. Visual sensors, such as cameras, provide spatial observations but do not directly measure these physical interaction signals.

Tactile sensing offers complementary information, including normal force, shear force, and contact geometry, which are crucial for tasks requiring fine-grained contact reasoning (e.g., insertion, grasping, tool use). While prior research has demonstrated the benefits of tactile feedback, these methods often necessitate task-specific tactile data collected with particular sensors and setups, which limits their transferability and scalability. Despite ongoing efforts, tactile datasets remain considerably smaller than visual datasets, creating a fundamental disparity. This "scale gap" means that tactile observations are infrequently paired with the extensive visual data from which general robot behaviors are typically derived.

This research addresses this disparity by proposing a framework that integrates the strengths of both modalities. It posits that visual policies can provide high-level, task-oriented guidance from abundant data, while tactile feedback can provide localized, residual corrections during physical interaction. This approach contrasts with efforts to scale tactile data to visual levels or solely rely on visual data for contact tasks. It aims to bridge the gap between scalable visual priors and the precise requirements of contact-rich manipulation by using real-world reinforcement learning (RL) to learn tactile-informed residual corrections on top of existing visual policies, rather than training complex visuo-tactile policies from scratch. This circumvents the challenges associated with simulating tactile signals for real-to-sim-to-real transfer and the repeated expert interventions required by imitation-based correction methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current visual robot policies in contact-rich manipulation tasks. While visual policies are increasingly scalable and capable of learning general motion priors from diverse data sources, they often fail at critical junctures of physical interaction where direct measurement of contact and forces is necessary. Examples include precise insertions or tool use, where subtle contact details determine success or failure.

Tactile sensing provides direct access to these local interaction signals, such as normal force, shear force, and contact geometry. However, tactile data collection is a costly and labor-intensive process, and generalizing tactile information across different sensors, robot platforms, and tasks remains a challenge. This results in a "scale gap," where large-scale visual datasets are abundant, but corresponding large-scale tactile datasets are scarce. Consequently, it is often impractical to train comprehensive visuo-tactile policies from scratch using paired, large-scale multimodal data.

The key objectives of the OMNITACTUNE project are detailed as follows:

1. **Leverage Scalable Visual Priors for Task-Level Behavior**: The work aims to utilize the strengths of visual policies, which excel at understanding global task structure, object movements, and overall action sequences from large datasets. This establishes a foundational "motion prior" for the robot's behavior.
2. **Utilize Tactile Feedback for Local Refinement and Correction**: The intention is to employ tactile sensing to address the "last mile" of physical interaction, providing local, residual feedback for fine-tuning movements when parts need alignment, resistance is high, or small adjustments are required. This approach is conceptually analogous to how humans learn, where initial understanding comes from observation, but mastery through hands-on practice.
3. **Formulate Tactile Adaptation as Residual Correction**: The research proposes a novel formulation where tactile-aware learning is not a complete retraining of the policy but rather a mechanism to generate corrective actions on top of an already functional, but contact-naive, visual base policy. This avoids the inefficiency of learning from scratch.
4. **Develop a Policy-Agnostic Real-World RL Pipeline**: A central objective is to create a robust and plug-and-play pipeline, named OMNITACTUNE, that can efficiently adapt tactile feedback to diverse pre-trained visual base policies. This design aims for compatibility across various policy architectures (e.g., flow-based, transformer-based, diffusion-based) and different visual data sources (e.g., human videos, teleoperation).
5. **Enable Efficient Online Learning without Offline Visuo-Tactile Demonstrations**: A significant challenge is to perform tactile residual learning without requiring extensive pre-collected, paired visuo-tactile demonstrations. The pipeline addresses this by incorporating a warm-start stage that uses autonomous rollouts to initialize the replay buffer, bootstrap the critic network, and adapt the tactile encoder.
6. **Integrate Multi-Sensory Reward Shaping for Stability and Sample Efficiency**: The work aims to enhance the efficiency and stability of real-world RL by designing a reward function that combines object-centric guidance derived from visual flow with tactile contact signals. This dense feedback helps guide exploration towards successful contact-rich states.
7. **Demonstrate Generalization Across Tasks, Base Policies, and Tactile Representations**: The ultimate goal is to validate that OMNITACTUNE can consistently improve manipulation success rates in various challenging contact-rich tasks, maintain compatibility with different visual base policies, and effectively utilize diverse tactile sensing modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OMNITACTUNE is presented as a two-stage real-world reinforcement learning (RL) pipeline designed for policy-agnostic tactile residual adaptation.

#### 3.1. Data Collection and Visual Base Policies

The process begins with the collection of visual data to train initial "base" policies:

- **Human Hand Demonstrations**: These are collected using a third-view RGB camera and a Meta Quest headset. The camera records scene videos, while the headset tracks the 6-DoF hand pose. This human motion data is then retargeted to the robot's base frame using Aruco markers for calibration, yielding robot-aligned hand trajectories. These synchronized videos and trajectories are used to extract object-centric motion priors and train certain base policies.
- **Teleoperation Data Collection**: Robot demonstrations are gathered using a Meta Quest headset via an OpenTeach-style interface. An operator controls the robot's end-effector, and synchronized RGB observations, robot states, and actions are recorded. This data is used for training robot-data-based visual base policies.

Four different types of visual base policies are developed from this data:

1. **Flow-based Policy**: This policy builds on existing work (e.g., Im2Flow2Act) for cross-embodiment visual policy learning. It extracts and tracks sparse object keypoints (using DINOv2, SAM, CoTracker 3) from demonstration videos. A fine-tuned flow generator predicts a complete task-level object flow from an initial observation. This generated flow provides object-conditioned motion guidance and a dense object-centric reward signal. The policy itself is a lightweight MLP that converts these flow representations and robot proprioceptive states into a sequence of 6D delta poses with a binary grasping signal.
2. **Action Chunking Transformer (ACT)**: This policy is trained on teleoperation data, using RGB observations and robot proprioception to predict action chunks via a transformer-based conditional Variational Autoencoder (VAE).
3. **Diffusion Policy (DP)**: Also trained on teleoperation data, DP learns visuomotor policies by predicting future end-effector actions through conditional denoising diffusion, taking RGB observations and proprioception as input.
4. **π0.5**: A Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, fine-tuned on teleoperation data. It takes RGB observations from a single camera and a language instruction to predict low-level robot actions.

These visual base policies are then kept *frozen* during the subsequent tactile adaptation stages.

#### 3.2. Real-World Tactile-Informed Residual RL Adaptation

OMNITACTUNE employs a two-stage real-world RL pipeline to adapt tactile feedback:

- **Stage I: Warm-Start**: This stage addresses the challenge of learning tactile residuals without offline visuo-tactile demonstrations.
 - **Autonomous Rollouts**: The robot executes the frozen visual base policy (with residual action set to zero) to autonomously collect real-world tactile experience, populating a replay buffer.
 - **Critic Bootstrapping**: These collected transitions are used to bootstrap a flow-tactile critic, `Q_η`, which takes robot proprioception, flow features, tactile features, and the executed action as input.
 - **Tactile Encoder Fine-Tuning**: A pre-trained tactile encoder (e.g., AnyTouch2) is adapted to the specific task. Its projection layers and a lightweight adapter are optimized using both the critic loss (`L_Q`) and a reconstruction regularization loss (`L_rec`). This optimization is applied only to contact transitions, detected by tactile marker displacement thresholds.
 - **Tactile Data Augmentation**: To improve data efficiency, trajectory-level tactile augmentation (inspired by ControlTac) is performed after each rollout. This synthesizes new trajectories with temporally consistent tactile images, conditioned on contact force changes, and adds them to the replay buffer. This exposes the critic and encoder to a richer variety of contact conditions.

- **Stage II: Online Tactile Residual Learning**: Following the warm-start, this stage focuses on learning the residual corrections.
 - **Residual Policy Learning**: A lightweight tactile residual policy (`π_r_θ`) is learned to predict contact-aware corrections (`a_r_t`). The final action executed by the robot is a sum of the frozen base policy's action (`a_b_t`) and the scaled residual correction (`s_t * a_r_t`), where `s_t` is a scheduled residual scale.
 - **Policy-Agnostic Input**: The residual policy takes inputs independent of the base policy's internal architecture, specifically: robot proprioception, flow features, a contact-gated tactile representation (`g_t * z_τ_t`), the current base action (`a_t`), and the base policy's action chunk (`a_b_t:t+K`). The contact gate `g_t` ensures tactile feedback is only applied when contact is detected.
 - **Training**: The base policy (`π_b`) remains frozen, while the residual tactile policy (`π_r_θ`) and the flow-tactile critic (`Q_η`) are continuously optimized using a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm.
 - **Safe Exploration**: During execution, a scheduled residual scale (`s_t`) is gradually increased to ensure conservative early updates and stronger corrections later, promoting stable real-world learning.

#### 3.3. Multi-Sensory Reward Design

Real-world RL often struggles with sparse rewards. OMNITACTUNE utilizes a normalized multi-sensory reward to provide dense feedback and guide exploration:

- `r_t = normalize(w_r * r_reach_t + w_g * r_grasp_t + w_f * r_flow_t) - w_s * r_safety_t`.
- **Reaching Reward (`r_reach_t`)**: Guides the end-effector towards the initial 3D object centroid before grasping.
- **Flow Reward (`r_flow_t`)**: Tracks sparse subgoals derived from the generated object flow, providing object-centric motion guidance after grasping.
- **Tactile Grasping Reward (`r_grasp_t`)**: Encourages stable contact based on tactile depth images or average tactile marker displacement, promoting successful interaction.
- **Tactile Slippery Penalty (`r_safety_t`)**: Assigns a large negative reward and resets the robot if excessive tactile marker displacement indicates unsafe or uncontrolled contact.

This comprehensive reward structure facilitates stable and efficient learning during real-world interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates OMNITACTUNE across four challenging real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks: Peg-in-Hole, Charger Insertion, Cap Opening, and Box Opening. The findings highlight the method's efficiency, generalizability, and superiority over several baselines.

- **Efficient Tactile Adaptation with Residual RL**:
 - OMNITACTUNE consistently improved the success rates of visual base policies, which initially ranged from 5% to 40%.
 - Within 40 to 80 minutes of online practice, the success rates increased significantly to 85–100% across all four tasks (100% for Peg-in-Hole and Charger Insertion, 90% for Cap Opening, 85% for Box Opening).
 - This performance substantially exceeded baselines such as PLD\* (adapting PLD to flow-tactile without critic initialization), PLD (visual-only), and ViTAL (learning visuo-tactile from scratch), outperforming them by more than 40% success rate. The results indicated that the warm-start stage (tactile encoder and critic optimization) and tactile residual learning are critical for efficient adaptation.

- **Adaptation to Diverse Visual Base Policies**:
 - On the Peg-in-Hole task, OMNITACTUNE demonstrated broad compatibility by improving the performance of five different base policies: Human Flow Policy (from 40% to 100%), Teleoperation-based Flow Policy (from 50% to 90%), Teleoperation-based ACT (from 20% to 80%), Teleoperation-based Diffusion Policy (from 30% to 90%), and fine-tuned π0.5 (from 15% to 75%).
 - This indicates that the tactile residual adaptation mechanism is not tied to a specific visual policy architecture and can generalize across different visual motion priors. The human flow policy yielded the highest overall performance, suggesting that human demonstrations provide smoother motion priors beneficial for contact-rich refinement.

- **Comparison with Other Visuo-Tactile Robot Learning Methods**:
 - OMNITACTUNE was compared against imitation-learning-based visuo-tactile pipelines (ACT + Tactile, RDP, π0.5 + Tactile) that were trained with additional teleoperation data under the same time budget.
 - OMNITACTUNE achieved 20–30% higher success rates for all backbones and reached 100% success with the human flow policy, outperforming these imitation-based methods.
 - This finding suggests that online tactile residual refinement through trial-and-error is more effective for contact-rich manipulation than directly learning a visuo-tactile policy from additional teleoperated demonstrations, which may be noisy during contact.

- **Adaptation Across Different Tactile Representations**:
 - The method demonstrated generalizability across various tactile representations, including different pre-trained tactile image encoders (AnyTouch2, Sparsh, T3) and low-dimensional Tactile Markers.
 - On both Peg-in-Hole and Charger Insertion tasks, AnyTouch2 and low-dimensional tactile markers achieved comparable high final performance.
 - This indicates that OMNITACTUNE can effectively adapt to both sophisticated pre-trained tactile features and compact marker-based tactile signals. Some tactile encoders (T3, Sparsh) performed less effectively on dynamic contact tasks like Charger Insertion, potentially due to not being pre-trained on dynamic manipulation data.

- **Ablation Studies (Appendix C.2)**:
 - **Reward Shaping**: Removing any component of the multi-sensory reward (reaching, flow, tactile) resulted in slower learning and reduced final performance, underscoring the importance of dense, task-relevant feedback.
 - **Residual Policy Design**: Using trajectory-level keypoint guidance was more effective than per-step keypoint conditions, providing sufficient structure without over-constraining the policy. Contact-aware gating also improved performance by focusing tactile corrections on relevant interaction phases.
 - **Warm-Start Strategies**: Ablations confirmed that warm-start optimization of the tactile encoder and critic, along with ControlTac-style data augmentation, were crucial for stable and efficient early-stage learning, preventing unstable exploration.
 - **Action Space Design**: The scheduled residual scale and action scaling were found to be important for balancing exploration and stable refinement, ensuring that residual actions remained conservative yet effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OMNITACTUNE framework presents a significant advancement in robot manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring precise physical interaction. Its primary contribution lies in offering a policy-agnostic, real-world RL pipeline for efficiently adapting tactile feedback to pre-trained visual policies through residual correction.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Bridging the Visual-Tactile Scale Gap**: By decoupling the learning of high-level visual priors from local tactile refinements, OMNITACTUNE addresses the fundamental challenge of the "scale gap" between abundant visual data and scarce tactile data. This approach allows robots to leverage scalable visual understanding for task-level guidance and then refine execution with tactile information without requiring massive, paired visuo-tactile datasets.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Practicality**: The method demonstrates notable sample efficiency, achieving high success rates (85–100%) in complex contact-rich tasks within relatively short real-world training durations (40–80 minutes). This efficiency contributes to the practicality of deploying tactile-aware robotic systems in real-world scenarios, reducing the cost and time associated with data collection and training.
- **Generalizability Across Architectures and Sensors**: OMNITACTUNE's policy-agnostic design means it can be applied as a plug-and-play solution to diverse visual base policies (e.g., flow-based, ACT, Diffusion Policy, VLA models) and various tactile sensing modalities (e.g., image-based, marker-based). This broad compatibility increases its utility and potential for widespread adoption across different robotic platforms and applications.
- **Improved Robustness in Contact-Rich Tasks**: By enabling robots to actively use tactile feedback for local corrections, the framework significantly enhances robustness in tasks requiring fine-grained contact reasoning, such as insertion, grasping, and tool use. This moves beyond the limitations of purely visual policies that often struggle at the point of physical contact.
- **A New Paradigm for Multimodal Learning**: The work proposes a scalable learning paradigm where vision provides broad supervision, and touch provides targeted, residual feedback. This mirrors human learning processes and offers a promising direction for future research in multimodal robot learning, potentially inspiring similar hybrid approaches for other sensor modalities.

**Limitations and Future Directions**:
Despite these advancements, the paper acknowledges certain limitations inherent to real-world RL. These include the necessity for manual resets, which can be time-consuming, and the wear and tear on hardware, especially fragile vision-based tactile sensors, during repeated contact-rich interactions.

Future research could focus on:
- Developing safer and more automated real-world training methods to reduce reset costs and hardware degradation.
- Further improving motion priors and data augmentation techniques, potentially by incorporating world models to generate more physically plausible visuo-tactile rollouts for pre-training and online policy improvement.
- Extending the framework to other robotic embodiments and tactile sensor types, which the authors suggest as a straightforward extension.

Overall, OMNITACTUNE represents a step toward enabling more dexterous and reliable robotic manipulation by effectively integrating scalable visual intelligence with precise tactile feedback in real-world settings.
