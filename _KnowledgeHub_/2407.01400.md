# GalLoP: Learning Global and Local Prompts for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GalLoP introduces a prompt learning method for Vision-Language Models that integrates global and local visual features to achieve superior performance. The approach consistently improves few-shot classification accuracy by an average of +1.5 percentage points while simultaneously enhancing robustness for domain generalization and out-of-distribution detection.

## Problem

- Existing prompt learning methods for Vision-Language Models often face a trade-off between classification accuracy and robustness, requiring a compromise in performance.
- Local visual features from pre-trained VLMs (e.g., CLIP) are underutilized and suboptimal for fine-grained tasks due to global pre-training and a lack of specific text-vision alignment for local regions.
- Ensembling multiple prompts frequently suffers from a lack of diversity, leading to redundant information and limited improvements in combined predictive power.

## Method

- ==GalLoP== learns specialized ==global and local prompts==, incorporating a sparse local similarity (top-k) mechanism and a learned linear projection to make local features more discriminative and aligned with text.
- It enforces diversity among global prompts through =='prompt dropout'==, selecting a random subset of prompts for each training batch to encourage learning complementary aspects.
- Local prompts are diversified via =='multiscale training'==, where each prompt specializes in a different number of top-k relevant visual regions, capturing varied granularities of information.

## Results

- GalLoP achieved an average of **+1.5 percentage points** higher Top-1 accuracy over 11 diverse datasets compared to previous state-of-the-art prompt learning methods.
- The method demonstrated improved robustness, outperforming specialized domain generalization methods by **+0.5pt** on average and dedicated OOD detection methods by up to **-2.9pt FPR95**, while maintaining higher in-distribution accuracy.
- Ablation studies validated that sparse local feature selection (up to **+18.4pt** accuracy gain), linear projection for local alignment (**+10pt** accuracy gain), prompt dropout, and multiscale training are critical for GalLoP's superior performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective integration of local visual features in VLM prompt learning requires both sparsity (selecting relevant regions) and explicit realignment with textual semantics to overcome limitations of global pre-training.
> - Enhancing diversity across an ensemble of global and local prompts is crucial for achieving high performance in both accuracy and robustness simultaneously.
> - Simple regularization techniques, such as prompt dropout and multiscale training, can effectively induce prompt diversity without needing complex architectures or explicit diversity losses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), exemplified by models like CLIP and ALIGN, has revolutionized computer vision by demonstrating remarkable capabilities in zero-shot image classification. These models learn rich, transferable representations by pre-training on vast datasets of image-text pairs, enabling them to generalize to unseen categories without explicit fine-tuning. However, directly applying these powerful models to specific downstream tasks or adapting them to new datasets often requires efficient mechanisms to leverage their pre-trained knowledge.

**Prompt learning** has emerged as a leading paradigm for this efficient adaptation, particularly in few-shot learning scenarios. Instead of fine-tuning the entire VLM, prompt learning methods introduce a small set of learnable parameters, often called "soft prompts" or "context," into the text encoder. These prompts, typically prepended to class names, guide the VLM's textual understanding to better align with the visual features of the downstream dataset. This approach offers significant computational advantages and can achieve strong performance with limited labeled data.

Despite their success, existing prompt learning methods face a critical challenge: a **trade-off between classification accuracy and robustness**. As illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper, methods optimized for peak classification accuracy often exhibit degraded performance in robustness metrics such as out-of-distribution (OOD) detection or domain generalization (DG). Conversely, methods focusing on robustness might sacrifice in-distribution accuracy. For instance, PromptSRC offers separate versions for accuracy and DG, highlighting this inherent conflict.

Another avenue of research involves **learning multiple prompts** to create an ensemble, with methods specializing prompts for specific classes, Transformer layers, or using probabilistic frameworks. The core challenge here is ensuring sufficient diversity among these prompts to maximize their combined predictive power. However, many of these approaches predominantly rely on global visual representations, limiting their ability to generate truly diverse prompts that capture fine-grained, localized visual information.

More recently, researchers have explored incorporating **local image representations** into prompt learning, as seen in works like LoCoOp and PLOT. While these methods show promise, their overall performance in both accuracy and robustness remains suboptimal compared to leading global-feature-based prompt learning techniques. The paper identifies two key reasons for this limitation:
1. **Dense Local Features:** These methods often utilize all (dense) local features extracted from CLIP, which can include irrelevant or noisy regions (e.g., background elements) for a given concept, thereby diluting the signal.
2. **Suboptimal Local-Text Alignment:** CLIP's pre-training primarily focuses on aligning *global* visual features with text. Consequently, its raw local features are not as discriminative or well-aligned with textual semantics for fine-grained tasks as their global counterparts. This misalignment significantly impacts the performance of prompts trained solely on local features and hinders their effective combination with global features.

GalLoP directly addresses these critical limitations. It seeks to **break the accuracy-robustness trade-off** by developing a novel prompt learning method that effectively leverages *both* global and local visual features. The authors aim to unlock the full potential of local features by introducing mechanisms that enhance their discriminative power and ensure diversity within the prompt ensemble, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in efficient and robust VLM adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel prompt learning method, named **Global-Local Prompts (GalLoP)**, that overcomes the inherent trade-off between classification accuracy and robustness observed in previous VLM adaptation techniques. The authors aim to achieve state-of-the-art performance across multiple criteria simultaneously: in-distribution accuracy, domain generalization, and out-of-distribution detection.

The key motivations driving the development of GalLoP are:

1. **To Resolve the Accuracy-Robustness Dilemma:** Existing prompt learning methods often necessitate a compromise, where optimizing for high classification accuracy leads to reduced robustness, and vice versa. The authors are motivated to design a unified framework that can achieve both high accuracy and strong robustness, making VLM adaptations more reliable and deployable in real-world scenarios. This is a critical need for applications where encountering diverse data distributions or novel inputs is common.

2. **To Effectively Harness the Underutilized Power of Local Visual Features:** The paper identifies that while CLIP's global features are highly discriminative, its raw local features are often suboptimal for fine-grained classification due to CLIP's global pre-training objective. Previous attempts to integrate local features in prompt learning have been hampered by these limitations, leading to compromised performance. GalLoP is motivated to unlock the full potential of these local features by making them highly discriminative and relevant to specific class semantics, thereby enhancing the model's ability to capture fine-grained details.

3. **To Enhance Diversity within Prompt Ensembles:** Learning multiple prompts can boost performance through ensembling. However, if these prompts are not sufficiently diverse, they offer diminishing returns. The authors are motivated to explicitly enforce diversity among learned prompts, particularly between global and local representations, to ensure that each prompt contributes unique and complementary information, maximizing the benefits of their combination. This addresses the problem of prompt redundancy and over-fitting seen in some multi-prompt approaches.

By addressing these motivations, GalLoP aims to establish a new benchmark for prompt learning, demonstrating that it is possible to achieve superior performance across diverse evaluation metrics without compromising on crucial aspects like generalization and OOD detection.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GalLoP proposes a novel approach to prompt learning by systematically integrating and diversifying both global and local visual features. The core idea is to learn two specialized sets of prompts: "global prompts" that leverage the holistic image representation, and "local prompts" that focus on spatially localized features. The method introduces two main methodological contributions to achieve effective and diverse prompt learning: **effective local prompts learning** and **enforcing ensemble diversity**.

**Overall Architecture:**
GalLoP learns a set of *n* local prompts ($P_l$) and *m* global prompts ($P_g$). Each prompt ($p$) consists of learnable embeddings that are prepended to class name embeddings ($c$) before being processed by CLIP's textual encoder to produce a text representation ($t_c$). The visual encoder produces a global visual feature ($z_g$) for global prompts and a set of localized features ($Z_l$) for local prompts. The probability of an image belonging to a class is computed based on the similarity between visual and textual representations.

#### 3.1. Effective Local Prompts Learning

Recognizing the limitations of CLIP's raw local features, GalLoP introduces mechanisms to make them more discriminative and aligned with text:

- **Sparse Local Similarity (sim$_{top-k}$):** Instead of processing all local features, which may include noisy or irrelevant background regions, GalLoP adopts a sparse approach. For a given class prompt $t_c$ and a set of visual local features $Z_l = (z_l^1, \dots, z_l^L)$, the similarity is computed by selecting only the $k$ local features with the highest similarity to $t_c$ and averaging these top-$k$ similarities (Eq. 2). This ensures that only the most semantically relevant regions contribute to the classification decision, reducing noise and focusing on pertinent visual concepts.

- **Improving Local Text-Vision Alignment (Linear Projection $h_\theta$):** CLIP's pre-training primarily aligns global visual features with text, making its zero-shot local features less effective for fine-grained discrimination. To address this, GalLoP introduces a simple linear projection $h_\theta$ that is applied to the local visual features $Z_l$. This projection $h_\theta(Z_l)$ is learned during training to realign the local features with the textual representations of the downstream class labels, thereby enhancing their discriminative power. The linear layer is initialized to identity to ease the learning process. The sparse similarity is then computed using these realigned local features (Eq. 3).

#### 3.2. Enforcing Ensemble Diversity

To maximize the benefits of combining multiple prompts and to ensure complementarity between global and local prompts, GalLoP incorporates explicit diversity-inducing mechanisms:

- **Prompt Dropout (for Global Prompts):** To prevent multiple global prompts from converging to identical solutions, a "prompt dropout" technique is introduced. During training, for each image in a batch, a random subset of global prompts is selected (or equivalently, a random subset of images is assigned to each prompt). This randomization in input exposure forces each global prompt to learn slightly different aspects of the data, fostering diversity and improving generalization (Eq. 4, Fig. 4a).

- **Multiscale Training (for Local Prompts):** To further diversify the local prompts, each local prompt $p_l^j$ is specialized to operate at a different "scale," meaning it considers a different number of top-$k_j$ relevant regions. A set of increasing scales $(k_1, k_1+\Delta_k, \dots)$ is defined, with each local prompt $p_l^j$ being trained with its associated scale $k_j$. This allows different local prompts to capture visual information at varying granularities—some focusing on very specific, small features, others on broader contextual elements—thereby enhancing their diversity and complementarity (Eq. 5, Fig. 4b).

**Overall Training and Inference:**
The total loss function (Eq. 6) is a sum of the global loss (incorporating prompt dropout) and the local multiscale loss. During inference, the similarities obtained from all individual global and local prompts (after their respective processing, including sparse selection and linear projection for local prompts) are averaged to produce a final similarity score for each class. For OOD detection, GalLoP leverages the GL-MCM score, which combines both global and local information to assess the degree of in-distribution match.

By carefully designing how local features are processed (sparsity, alignment) and how diversity is enforced across both global and local prompts (dropout, multiscale), GalLoP aims to build a robust and accurate VLM adaptation framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GalLoP's experimental validation is extensive, demonstrating its superior performance across few-shot image classification, domain generalization, and out-of-distribution (OOD) detection.

#### 4.1. Main In-Distribution Classification Results

- **Superior Accuracy:** GalLoP consistently outperforms state-of-the-art prompt learning methods on a benchmark of 11 diverse datasets using a ViT-B/16 backbone. On average, GalLoP achieves **+1.5 percentage points (pt)** higher Top-1 accuracy compared to PromptSRC$^{\triangleright}$ (the previous best-performing method designed for accuracy). Specifically, on ImageNet, GalLoP surpasses PLOT by +2.5pt and PromptSRC$^{\triangleright}$ by +1.9pt. On datasets like FGVC Aircraft, it shows even more substantial gains, outperforming the next best method by +7.5pt (Table 1).
- **Consistency Across Few-Shot Settings:** GalLoP maintains strong performance across various few-shot settings (1-shot, 2-shot, 4-shot, 8-shot, 16-shot) on ImageNet, consistently outperforming PromptSRC$^{\triangleright}$ in each configuration (Figure 5a, Table 4).
- **Backbone Agnostic:** Unlike some methods (e.g., MaPLe, PromptSRC) that are limited to Transformer architectures, GalLoP performs exceptionally well with both ViT-B/16 and ResNet-50 backbones. With a ResNet-50 backbone, GalLoP outperforms PLOT and CoOp by +3.1pt and +3.9pt respectively, on average (Figure 5b, Table 5).
- **Efficiency Compared to Other Few-Shot Methods:** GalLoP achieves higher Top-1 accuracy than even full fine-tuning or more recent methods like FLYP, while fine-tuning significantly fewer parameters (0.6M for GalLoP vs. 149.7M for FLYP). It also outperforms cache-based methods like DAC-VT with similar or fewer parameters (Table 6).

#### 4.2. Robustness Results

GalLoP significantly improves the trade-off between accuracy and robustness.

- **Domain Generalization (DG):** When trained on ImageNet and evaluated on domain-shifted datasets (ImageNet-V2, -Sketch, -A, -R), GalLoP outperforms PromptSRC$^{\diamond}$ (a method specifically designed for DG) by +0.5pt on average. Critically, GalLoP achieves this while also having +4.9pt higher in-distribution accuracy on ImageNet, demonstrating a superior trade-off compared to specialized methods (Figure 6a, Table 7).
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detection:** GalLoP shows strong OOD detection capabilities, outperforming traditional prompt learning methods (e.g., CoOp) and dedicated OOD detection methods (e.g., LoCoOp, LSN) in FPR95 (lower is better) while maintaining significantly higher in-distribution accuracy. For instance, GalLoP achieves -1.4pt FPR95 better than LoCoOp and -2.9pt FPR95 better than LSN, alongside +3.2pt and +3.6pt higher Top-1 accuracy, respectively (Figure 6b, Table 8).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies rigorously validate the efficacy of GalLoP's key design choices:

- **Complementarity of Global and Local Features:** Naively combining standard CoOp global and local features (without GalLoP's enhancements) actually *degrades* performance compared to using global features alone. In contrast, GalLoP achieves a +3.1pt Top-1 accuracy boost and -8.9pt FPR95 reduction when combining its refined global and local prompts, showcasing the critical role of GalLoP's design in enabling true complementarity (Table 2).
- **The Need for Sparsity:** Using a sparse $top-k$ selection of local features is crucial. It boosts accuracy by +8.5pt (with linear projection) to +18.4pt (zero-shot CLIP) compared to using all local features. The method is also robust to the specific choice of $k$ within a broad range (Figure 7).
- **Improved Local Text-Vision Alignment:** The simple linear projection layer significantly enhances the discriminative power of local features, leading to an additional +10pt accuracy gain (for $k=10$) compared to learning local prompts without this alignment (Figure 7).
- **Prompt Dropout for Global Prompts:** Without prompt dropout, adding more global prompts does not yield performance gains and can even degrade accuracy due to a lack of diversity. With prompt dropout, adding more global prompts consistently improves performance, confirming its role in fostering diversity and generalization (Figure 8a).
- **Multiscale Training for Local Prompts:** Learning local prompts at different scales (i.e., varying $k$ values for each prompt) leads to a steady increase in accuracy (+1pt from 1 to 4 scales), demonstrating the benefit of specializing prompts to capture different visual granularities (Figure 8b).

#### 4.4. Qualitative Study

Qualitative visualizations confirm that CLIP's zero-shot local features are often non-discriminative. In contrast, GalLoP's local prompts accurately identify and segment objects of interest, progressively focusing on different parts of an object as the scale (number of regions considered) increases, leading to correct classification (Figure 9, Figure 10).

In summary, GalLoP presents a robust and effective prompt learning strategy that successfully addresses key limitations in the field, achieving a new benchmark in simultaneous accuracy and robustness for VLM adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GalLoP represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Model (VLM) adaptation, offering a powerful and versatile framework with substantial implications for both academic research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Breaking the Accuracy-Robustness Trade-off

The most profound significance of GalLoP is its ability to simultaneously achieve high classification accuracy and strong robustness across various challenging benchmarks. Previous prompt learning methods have largely struggled with this, forcing researchers and practitioners to choose between an accurate but potentially brittle model or a robust but less precise one. GalLoP demonstrates that this trade-off is not intrinsic to prompt learning, providing a unified solution that excels in in-distribution accuracy, domain generalization, and out-of-distribution detection. This breakthrough paves the way for more reliable and trustworthy VLM deployments in real-world environments.

#### 5.2. Unleashing the Potential of Local Features in VLMs

GalLoP provides a highly effective methodology for leveraging CLIP's local visual features, which were previously shown to be suboptimal for fine-grained tasks in their raw, zero-shot form. By introducing sparse attention mechanisms (top-$k$ selection) and a learnable linear projection for text-vision realignment, GalLoP transforms these underutilized features into highly discriminative components. This opens up new possibilities for VLM adaptation that require fine-grained understanding, moving beyond global image representations to capture nuanced visual details. This contribution is particularly impactful for tasks where subtle differences are critical, such as fine-grained classification or anomaly detection.

#### 5.3. Generalizable Principles for Prompt Diversity

The proposed mechanisms for enforcing diversity—"prompt dropout" for global prompts and "multiscale training" for local prompts—are elegant and effective. Prompt dropout provides a simple yet powerful way to encourage different prompts to learn complementary information without relying on complex, potentially destabilizing explicit diversity losses. Multiscale training allows local prompts to specialize in different granularities of visual information, further enriching the ensemble. These principles could be widely applicable to other multi-prompt or ensemble-based VLM adaptation techniques, fostering more robust and generalizable models across various modalities.

#### 5.4. Enhanced Practicality and Real-World Deployability

The improved robustness metrics (domain generalization and OOD detection) directly translate to more practical and reliable VLM applications. Models adapted with GalLoP are better equipped to handle real-world complexities, such as encountering images from new environments, different lighting conditions, or entirely novel categories. This makes GalLoP highly valuable for critical applications in domains like medical imaging (where OOD detection of unusual conditions is vital), autonomous driving (where robustness to varied road conditions is essential), remote sensing, and industrial inspection. Its ability to work with different backbones (ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16) also enhances its versatility.

#### 5.5. Foundation for Future Research

GalLoP sets a new standard and opens several avenues for future research:
- **Extending Local Alignment:** As suggested by the authors, learning the local feature alignment on even larger vision-language datasets could further boost performance and generalization.
- **Applications Beyond Classification:** The robust local features and diverse prompt ensembles could be extended to other VLM tasks such as object detection, semantic segmentation, or even image generation, where fine-grained control and robustness are crucial.
- **Theoretical Analysis:** Further theoretical work could investigate why sparsity, multiscale representation, and prompt dropout are so effective, potentially leading to deeper insights into VLM learning dynamics.
- **Dynamic Prompt Adaptation:** Exploring dynamic mechanisms for selecting $k$ for sparsity or adapting scales based on image content could offer even greater flexibility and performance.

In conclusion, GalLoP presents a meticulously designed and empirically validated approach that significantly advances the field of VLM adaptation. By integrating global and local features with innovative diversity-enhancing techniques, it delivers a new state-of-the-art in both accuracy and robustness, paving the way for more intelligent, reliable, and versatile AI systems.
