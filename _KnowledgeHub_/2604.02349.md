# OPRIDE: Offline Preference-based Reinforcement Learning via In-Dataset Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> OPRIDE, an algorithm for offline Preference-based Reinforcement Learning, introduces an in-dataset exploration strategy for efficient query selection and a variance-based discount scheduling mechanism to mitigate reward overoptimization. The framework consistently achieves superior performance with significantly fewer human queries on Meta-World, Antmaze, and Atari benchmarks compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Designing appropriate reward functions in real-world reinforcement learning is challenging, leading to reliance on Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL).
- Offline PbRL struggles with inefficient exploration during query selection, often focusing on entire state-action spaces rather than policy-relevant regions.
- Reward functions learned from binary and sparse human preference feedback are prone to overoptimization, which can result in the extraction of suboptimal policies.

## Method

- **OPRIDE** employs a principled ==in-dataset exploration== strategy that maximizes information gain about the optimal policy by selecting trajectory pairs with the greatest disagreement among an ensemble's value functions.
- A variance-based discount scheduling mechanism is introduced to mitigate reward overoptimization, dynamically adjusting the discount factor to a smaller value in state-action regions with high Q-value uncertainty.
- The framework iteratively trains an ensemble of reward and value networks, collects human preferences for informative queries, and then extracts a robust policy using an offline RL algorithm with the adjusted discount factors.

## Results

- OPRIDE consistently outperformed state-of-the-art offline PbRL baselines across Meta-World and Antmaze tasks, achieving strong performance with significantly fewer human queries, including compelling results with as few as ten queries on Meta-World.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the in-dataset exploration strategy (value difference-based) and the variance-based discount scheduling mechanism are crucial for the observed performance gains and improved robustness.
- The algorithm achieved state-of-the-art results on pixel-based Atari environments using real human preference feedback, demonstrating its robustness to complex visual inputs and inherent noise in human labeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prioritizing information gain about the optimal policy through value function differences is more effective for active query selection in offline PbRL than solely refining reward function accuracy.
> - Introducing pessimism into policy extraction via a dynamic, variance-based discount factor effectively regularizes learning and mitigates overoptimization stemming from noisy or sparse preference feedback.
> - Offline querying can be significantly more sample-efficient than online methods, with theoretical analysis showing a `1/(1-γ)` factor improvement in the preference error term.
