# Temporal Difference Learning for Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> TD-MPC integrates model-based planning with model-free temporal difference learning, utilizing a task-oriented latent dynamics model. This framework achieves state-of-the-art sample efficiency and high asymptotic performance on complex continuous control tasks, including previously unsolved benchmarks like Dog locomotion from the DeepMind Control Suite.

## Problem

- Model-free Reinforcement Learning often suffers from poor sample efficiency, requiring vast amounts of interaction data.
- Model-based RL approaches face challenges with learning sufficiently accurate models, leading to compounding errors and hindering asymptotic performance.
- Long-horizon planning in Model Predictive Control (MPC) is computationally expensive, and short-horizon MPC is myopic without a robust terminal value function.

## Method

- **TD-MPC** (Temporal Difference Learning for Model Predictive Control) integrates model-based online ==trajectory optimization== (via ==MPPI==) with model-free ==temporal difference learning== principles.
- A ==Task-Oriented Latent Dynamics (TOLD)== model, comprising representation, latent dynamics, reward, value, and policy networks, is jointly learned.
- Model components are optimized using a temporally weighted objective, incorporating ==TD-losses== for the value function and a ==latent state consistency loss== for the dynamics model.

## Results

- Achieved superior sample efficiency and asymptotic performance on complex DMControl tasks (e.g., Humanoid, Dog locomotion), solving "Dog" in as little as **1M steps**.
- Demonstrated competitive results on image-based and multi-modal RL benchmarks while scaling to high-dimensional continuous action spaces (up to **38-D**).
- Showed strong multi-task learning capabilities on Meta-World and matched or surpassed the wall-time efficiency of state-of-the-art model-free methods like SAC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a "task-oriented" latent dynamics model purely from rewards and latent consistency avoids modeling irrelevant environmental details, improving accuracy and sample efficiency.
> - Jointly optimizing the latent dynamics, reward, and value functions using temporal difference objectives effectively bridges model-based planning and model-free learning.
> - Combining short-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) with a learned terminal value function enables long-term optimality without incurring the high computational costs of long-horizon planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) aims to enable agents to learn optimal behaviors through iterative interaction with an environment. Within RL, two primary paradigms exist: model-free and model-based methods.

- **Model-Free RL:** These algorithms directly learn a policy that maps states to actions, or a value function that estimates future rewards, without explicitly learning a model of the environment's dynamics. Popular examples include Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), DDPG, and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Model-free methods often struggle with sample efficiency, requiring vast amounts of interaction data, but can achieve high asymptotic performance by avoiding model biases.
- **Model-Based RL:** These approaches learn or are provided with a model of the environment's dynamics (how actions affect states) and/or reward function. This model is then used for planning future actions by simulating rollouts in the imagined environment. Examples include PlaNet, Dreamer, MBPO, and MuZero. Model-based methods generally boast superior sample efficiency because they can learn from imagined data, but they face challenges in learning sufficiently accurate models, especially over long horizons. Model inaccuracies can lead to compounding errors during planning, often hindering asymptotic performance compared to model-free counterparts. Planning itself can also be computationally expensive, particularly for long horizons or complex action spaces.

**Model Predictive Control (MPC)** is a classic control strategy that utilizes an internal model to optimize a sequence of actions over a finite, short horizon, executing only the first action, and then repeating the process. While effective for local optimality, extending MPC to achieve global optimality often requires a terminal value function to estimate long-term returns beyond the planning horizon. Data-driven MPC, where the model is learned from data, combines these concepts but still grapples with the challenges of model accuracy and planning costs.

**Hybrid Approaches:** A growing line of research seeks to combine the strengths of both model-free and model-based RL. These methods aim to leverage models for improved sample efficiency while retaining robust performance. Examples include MBPO, POLO, and LOOP. Some methods use learned models to generate synthetic data for model-free policy training, while others augment model-free policies with planning.

**This paper, TD-MPC, critically positions itself within this hybrid landscape.** It addresses the core limitations of existing model-based and hybrid methods:
1. **Cost of long-horizon planning:** MPC over short horizons is myopic. Terminal value functions offer a solution, but learning accurate ones is hard.
2. **Challenge of accurate model learning:** Traditional model-based approaches (e.g., PlaNet, Dreamer) often learn general-purpose models via state or pixel prediction, which can be inefficient as they model irrelevant details, leading to inaccuracies and compounding errors.

TD-MPC explicitly aims to **combine the strengths of model-free and model-based methods.** It leverages a learned model for local trajectory optimization (like MPC) and a learned terminal value function for long-term estimates. The key distinction lies in **how these components are learned: jointly by temporal difference (TD) learning**, a cornerstone of model-free Q-learning. This integrated learning approach, coupled with a "task-oriented latent dynamics model" that focuses only on reward-relevant information, represents a novel contribution to the field, promising improved sample efficiency and asymptotic performance without the prohibitive costs or accuracy issues of prior work. It implicitly argues that previous model-based methods struggled not because the *idea* of a model was flawed, but because the *way* models were learned was inefficient or overly complex for the task at hand.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind TD-MPC are:

1. **Achieve Superior Sample Efficiency:** Model-free methods often require millions of environment steps to learn complex behaviors. TD-MPC aims to significantly reduce this data requirement by leveraging a learned model for planning, which allows for efficient use of gathered experience.
2. **Attain High Asymptotic Performance:** While model-based methods can be sample-efficient, they often plateau in performance due to model inaccuracies. TD-MPC seeks to overcome this by learning a more task-relevant model and integrating it with a robust value function, enabling it to match or exceed the asymptotic performance of state-of-the-art model-free algorithms.
3. **Bridge the Gap Between Model-Free and Model-Based Strengths:** The authors explicitly state the goal of combining the "potential for improved sample efficiency through model learning" (model-based advantage) with "better performance as computational budget for planning increases" (planning advantage, often combined with model-free robustness).
4. **Reduce Computational Cost of Planning:** Long-horizon planning is prohibitively expensive. TD-MPC addresses this by using a short planning horizon in conjunction with a learned terminal value function to estimate long-term returns, making planning tractable.
5. **Develop a "Task-Oriented" Model:** A core motivation is to avoid the inefficiencies of learning a general, reconstructive model of the entire environment (e.g., pixel prediction). Instead, TD-MPC proposes to learn a latent dynamics model purely from rewards, focusing only on information relevant to the task, which is hypothesized to be more sample-efficient and robust to compounding errors.
6. **Ensure Modality-Agnosticism:** By learning dynamics in a latent space and focusing on rewards, the method aims to be robust to different input modalities (e.g., state, image, multi-modal), enhancing its general applicability.
7. **Enable Scalability to High-Dimensional Continuous Control:** The authors aim for a method that can handle tasks with very high-dimensional continuous action spaces, a common challenge in robotics, unlike some prior methods (e.g., MCTS-based) that require action discretization.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to create a practical, robust, and high-performing RL agent that can learn complex continuous control tasks efficiently by intelligently combining the best aspects of model-based planning and model-free temporal difference learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TD-MPC (Temporal Difference Learning for Model Predictive Control) is a novel framework that integrates model-based planning with model-free temporal difference (TD) learning principles. Its core innovation lies in the joint learning of a task-oriented latent dynamics (TOLD) model and a terminal value function, both optimized using TD-style objectives.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework (TD-MPC)

At each decision step, TD-MPC:
1. **Encodes the current observation** into a latent representation.
2. **Performs online trajectory optimization** (planning) using a learned latent dynamics model to predict short-term rewards and a learned terminal value function to estimate long-term returns.
3. **Executes the first action** of the optimized trajectory in the real environment.

The planning process utilizes a variant of **Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control**. MPPI iteratively updates parameters of a distribution over action sequences. In TD-MPC, this involves:
- Sampling `N` trajectories from a multivariate Gaussian distribution over actions.
- Rolling out these trajectories using the learned latent dynamics model.
- Estimating the total return for each trajectory as a sum of short-term rewards predicted by a learned reward model and a long-term estimate from a learned value function at the end of the planning horizon.
- Selecting the top-k performing trajectories and updating the action distribution parameters (mean `μ` and standard deviation `σ`) using an importance-weighted average.
- Crucially, TD-MPC incorporates **policy-guided trajectory optimization** by also sampling a small fraction of trajectories from a learned policy `π_θ`, which helps guide exploration and potentially speed up convergence.
- **Exploration** is managed by linearly annealing the standard deviation of the sampling distribution and gradually increasing the planning horizon in the early stages of training.

#### 3.2. Task-Oriented Latent Dynamics (TOLD) Model

The heart of TD-MPC is the TOLD model, which comprises five jointly learned components, all implemented as deterministic MLPs:
1. **Representation Network (`h_θ`):** Encodes the raw observation `s_t` into a latent representation `z_t`.
2. **Latent Dynamics (`d_θ`):** Predicts the next latent state `z_{t+1}` given the current latent state `z_t` and action `a_t`. (`z_{t+1} = d_θ(z_t, a_t)`)
3. **Reward Predictor (`R_θ`):** Predicts the immediate reward `ˆr_t` given `z_t` and `a_t`. (`ˆr_t = R_θ(z_t, a_t)`)
4. **Value Function (`Q_θ`):** Predicts the state-action (Q) value `ˆq_t` given `z_t` and `a_t`. (`ˆq_t = Q_θ(z_t, a_t)`)
5. **Policy Network (`π_θ`):** Learns an action `ˆa_t` that maximizes the Q-function given `z_t`. (`ˆa_t = π_θ(z_t)`)

#### 3.3. Joint Learning Objective

The TOLD model components are learned jointly by minimizing a temporally weighted objective function `J(θ; Γ)` over trajectories `Γ` sampled from a replay buffer. The overall loss `L(θ; Γ_i)` for each step `i` within a trajectory is a weighted sum of three terms:

1. **Reward Prediction Loss (Eq. 8):** `c_1 * ||R_θ(z_i, a_i) - r_i||^2`
 - This term forces the learned reward predictor to accurately estimate the true environment reward.
2. **Value Prediction Loss (Eq. 9 - TD-objective):** `c_2 * ||Q_θ(z_i, a_i) - (r_i + γQ_θ^-(z_{i+1}, π_θ(z_{i+1})))||^2`
 - This is a standard Q-learning (TD) loss. It trains the Q-function to predict the sum of immediate reward and the discounted value of the next state-action pair, where the next action is chosen by the learned policy `π_θ` and the target Q-value `Q_θ^-` is from a slow-moving target network (common in deep RL to stabilize training). This is crucial for obtaining long-term return estimates.
3. **Latent State Consistency Loss (Eq. 10):** `c_3 * ||d_θ(z_i, a_i) - h_θ^-(s_{i+1})||^2`
 - This is a key innovation. Instead of predicting future raw states or pixels, the latent dynamics model `d_θ` is trained to predict a latent representation `z_{i+1}` that is consistent with the latent representation of the *actual* next observation `s_{i+1}` (encoded by a target representation network `h_θ^-`). This makes the model "task-oriented" by only focusing on what's necessary to predict future rewards and values, rather than irrelevant visual details. Gradients from all three losses are backpropagated through time, improving predictions over multiple rollout steps and alleviating error compounding.

#### 3.4. Policy Learning (Eq. 11)

- The policy `π_θ` is learned separately to maximize the Q-function, `J_π(θ; Γ) = - Σ λ^{i-t} Q_θ(z_i, π_θ(sg(z_i)))`, where `sg` is the stop-gradient operator. This policy serves two purposes: guiding trajectory optimization during inference and providing the `max Q` estimate for the TD-target.

#### 3.5. Training Procedure

The agent alternates between:
1. **Data Collection:** Interacting with the environment using TD-MPC's planning procedure.
2. **Model Update:** Sampling trajectories from the replay buffer and updating the TOLD model (h, d, R, Q, π) using the joint objective.

This integrated approach aims to create a highly efficient and effective RL agent capable of solving complex continuous control problems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments across a wide range of continuous control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite (DMControl) and Meta-World, encompassing diverse challenges such as high-dimensional state/action spaces, sparse rewards, image observations, multi-modal inputs, and multi-task learning.

#### 4.1. Performance on Challenging Continuous Control Tasks (DMControl)

- **Humanoid and Dog Locomotion:** TD-MPC achieved superior sample efficiency and asymptotic performance on these highly complex tasks (up to 38-dimensional continuous action spaces). Notably, it solved the "Dog" tasks, which had not been previously documented as solved by other methods, in as little as 1M environment steps. This is significantly faster than prior work, which often required 30M steps for Humanoid.
- **General DMControl Tasks:** TD-MPC consistently outperformed or matched state-of-the-art model-based (e.g., LOOP, PlaNet, Dreamer) and model-free (e.g., SAC) baselines on a suite of 15 tasks, showing particularly large gains on tasks with complex dynamics like Quadruped and Acrobot.
- **Comparison to Ground-Truth MPC (MPC:sim):** While MPC with a simulator performed well on locomotion, it struggled with sparse reward tasks, highlighting the benefit of TD-MPC's learned components (especially the value function).

#### 4.2. Image-Based Reinforcement Learning

- **DMControl 100k Benchmark & Dreamer Benchmark:** Despite not being specifically tuned for image-based RL, TD-MPC achieved competitive results with state-of-the-art model-free (CURL, DrQ, DrQ-v2) and model-based (PlaNet, Dreamer, Dreamer-v2, MuZero, EfficientZero) algorithms. This is significant because these baselines are often highly optimized for pixel inputs and use more parameters.
- **Scalability to High-Dimensional Actions:** Unlike MCTS-based methods like MuZero/EfficientZero that require action discretization and thus cannot scale to high-dimensional continuous action spaces (e.g., Walker Walk, Cheetah Run), TD-MPC seamlessly handled these tasks due to its sampling-based planning.

#### 4.3. Multi-Task and Generalization Capabilities

- **Meta-World (Goal-Conditioned):** TD-MPC demonstrated strong performance on 50 diverse goal-conditioned manipulation tasks. It showed higher sample efficiency than SAC, especially on complex manipulation tasks like Bin Picking, Box Close, and Hammer.
- **Multi-Task Learning (Meta-World MT10):** The method successfully trained a single policy to perform 10 different tasks simultaneously, benefiting from data sharing, despite its "reward-centric" objective. This suggests that the task-oriented latent dynamics model can generalize across tasks with similar reward structures.
- **Multi-Modal RL:** TD-MPC successfully fused information from proprioceptive data and egocentric camera feeds to solve 3D locomotion tasks (Quadruped Corridor, Quadruped Obstacles), where a "blind" agent (proprioceptive data only) failed. This showcases its modality-agnostic design.
- **Model Generalization (Transfer Learning):** Experiments showed that a TOLD model trained on "Walk" tasks could be finetuned on "Run" tasks with significantly faster convergence. The representation network (`h_θ`) showed strong transferability, while the latent dynamics (`d_θ`) encoded more task-specific behavior.

#### 4.4. Computational Budget and Wall-Time Efficiency

- **Performance vs. Budget:** For complex tasks, increasing the planning horizon or number of CEM iterations generally led to better performance, confirming the benefits of planning. However, performance could be maintained with a 50% reduction in planning cost at inference time compared to training. The jointly learned policy (`π_θ`) offered even faster inference, albeit with lower performance.
- **Training Wall-Time:** TD-MPC significantly closed the "time-to-solve" gap between model-free and model-based methods. It solved Walker Walk 16x faster than LOOP and matched SAC's time-to-solve on both Walker Walk and Humanoid Stand, despite being more sample-efficient. This is a critical finding, indicating that the method is not only data-efficient but also computationally efficient during training.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Latent Dynamics Objective:** The proposed latent state consistency loss (Equation 10) proved superior to "no regularization," a state reconstruction objective, and a contrastive loss, yielding the most consistent results across tasks. This validates the "task-oriented" design principle.
- **Exploration:** The planning procedure inherently balances exploration and exploitation, with the standard deviation of action distribution decreasing as training progresses and converging when tasks are solved, particularly for easier tasks.

In summary, TD-MPC demonstrates state-of-the-art performance, remarkable sample efficiency, and competitive wall-time efficiency across a broad spectrum of challenging continuous control tasks, including those with high-dimensional observations and actions, multi-task, and multi-modal settings. Its core innovation, the task-oriented latent dynamics model learned jointly by TD-learning, appears to be a robust and scalable approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Temporal Difference Learning for Model Predictive Control" holds significant implications for the field of Reinforcement Learning and its applications, particularly in robotics and embodied AI.

#### 5.1. Bridging the Model-Free/Model-Based Divide

The most profound significance of TD-MPC is its effective integration of model-free and model-based paradigms. Historically, researchers have grappled with the trade-offs: model-free methods are robust but data-hungry, while model-based methods are sample-efficient but prone to model inaccuracies and planning costs. TD-MPC successfully demonstrates that it is possible to achieve both superior sample efficiency and high asymptotic performance by intelligently combining these approaches. This could lead to a new generation of RL algorithms that inherit the best of both worlds, accelerating the development of agents for complex real-world tasks.

#### 5.2. Practical Viability for Robotics

- **Sample Efficiency:** Solving complex tasks like Humanoid and Dog locomotion in 1M environment steps is a substantial leap. For real-world robotics, where data collection is expensive and time-consuming, this level of sample efficiency is crucial. It drastically reduces the number of real-world interactions needed for an agent to learn.
- **Wall-Time Efficiency:** The finding that TD-MPC matches or even surpasses the wall-time to solve tasks compared to leading model-free methods (like SAC) is equally impactful. This means not only fewer environmental interactions but also faster overall training times, making it a more practical solution for research and deployment.
- **High-Dimensional Control:** Its ability to handle tasks with up to 38-dimensional continuous action spaces and image-based observations without task-specific tuning or action discretization makes it highly suitable for complex robotic manipulators and locomoting agents.
- **Multi-Modal and Multi-Task Capabilities:** The successful fusion of multi-modal inputs and performance in multi-task settings indicate a pathway towards more general-purpose and adaptable robotic systems that can operate in diverse, unstructured environments and learn multiple skills.

#### 5.3. Novel Model Learning Paradigm

The concept of a "Task-Oriented Latent Dynamics" (TOLD) model, where the latent dynamics and reward/value functions are learned purely from reward signals and consistency in latent space (bypassing explicit state/pixel prediction), is a powerful idea. This shifts the focus from learning a perfect generative model of the environment to learning a compact, reward-predictive representation, which is an inherently easier problem. This approach makes model learning more robust to irrelevant environmental details and potentially less susceptible to compounding errors, opening new avenues for model design in RL.

#### 5.4. Implications for Future Research

TD-MPC lays a strong foundation for several future research directions:
- **Advanced Exploration Strategies:** While TD-MPC's planning-based exploration is effective, integrating more sophisticated exploration techniques could further improve performance on hard-exploration tasks.
- **Architectural Innovations:** Exploring more advanced network architectures for the TOLD components could yield further performance gains.
- **Creative Model Use:** The learned model could be leveraged in other ways, such as for data augmentation, offline policy optimization, or for learning inverse dynamics.
- **Long-Horizon Planning:** While the terminal value function addresses long-term returns, further improvements in integrating longer planning horizons or more advanced planning algorithms could be explored.

In conclusion, TD-MPC represents a significant advance in continuous control, offering a compelling blend of data efficiency, high performance, and computational tractability. Its impact will likely be felt most strongly in applied RL, particularly in robotics, where the ability to learn complex behaviors quickly and robustly from limited data is paramount.
