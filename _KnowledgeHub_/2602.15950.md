# Can Vision-Language Models See Squares? Text-Recognition Mediates Spatial Reasoning Across Three Model Families

> [!summary] Summary
> This research reveals that Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit a substantial and consistent degradation in spatial localization when processing non-textual visual information compared to text symbols, suggesting a reliance on an internal text-recognition pathway for high-fidelity spatial reasoning. Across Claude Opus, ChatGPT 5.2, and Gemini 3 Thinking, F1 scores for black-cell detection dropped by 34.1 to 53.8 points when grids were presented as abstract squares instead of text.

## Problem

- A gap exists in understanding the underlying mechanisms of spatial reasoning in VLMs, particularly whether their capabilities are uniform across diverse visual inputs.
- The prevailing assumption that VLMs possess robust and generalizable spatial reasoning abilities for arbitrary visual content needed to be critically examined.
- There was a need to isolate spatial localization from semantic understanding to probe fundamental limitations in VLM visual processing without confounding factors.

## Method

- Fifteen 15x15 binary grids were programmatically generated with varying densities.
- Each grid was rendered under two ==visual encoding conditions==: as text symbols (periods and hash marks) and as unsegmented abstract squares.
- Three frontier VLMs (Claude Opus, ChatGPT 5.2, Gemini 3 Thinking) were prompted to transcribe these grids, and performance was measured using ==cell accuracy and black-cell F1 score==.

## Results

- All three frontier VLMs showed a substantial performance drop in F1 score for black-cell detection when processing unsegmented squares compared to text symbols, with gaps ranging from **34.1** to **53.8** percentage points.
- Claude Opus and ChatGPT 5.2 maintained stable F1 scores across varying densities in the text condition, while Gemini 3 Thinking experienced a catastrophic performance collapse at higher text densities.
- In the unsegmented squares condition, Gemini 3 Thinking achieved the highest F1 scores on sparse grids (averaging **68.0%** for ≤**20%** density) but rapidly collapsed at higher densities, frequently generating hallucinated geometric patterns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs appear to have two distinct implicit processing pathways for spatial information: a robust text-recognition pathway and a significantly weaker visual-feature pathway for non-textual content.
> - The perceived strong spatial reasoning in VLMs might be largely an artifact of their implicit OCR capabilities, leading to an overestimation of their general visual spatial acuity.
> - Different VLMs exhibit distinct qualitative failure modes in the non-textual condition, ranging from under-counting and displacement (Claude) to massive over-counting (ChatGPT) and hallucinating stereotyped patterns (Gemini).
