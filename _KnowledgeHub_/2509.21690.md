# PACE: Physics Augmentation for Coordinated End-to-end Reinforcement Learning toward Versatile Humanoid Table Tennis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue University developed an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework enabling a humanoid robot to play table tennis with coordinated whole-body motions. The system integrates a learned ball trajectory predictor and physics-based predictive rewards, achieving a 94.1% success rate in simulation and a 61.3% success rate on a physical Booster T1 robot while generating average outbound ball speeds of 6.9 m/s.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to react to fast-moving objects with versatile, coordinated whole-body behaviors in dynamic, interactive tasks like table tennis (TT).
- Traditional model-based TT approaches often rely on restrictive 'virtual hitting-plane' assumptions, limiting adaptability and making it difficult to scale to high-DoF humanoids.
- End-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) for legged humanoids in dynamic tasks faces challenges such as high-dimensional action spaces, locomotion instability, and sparse ball-hitting rewards, which hinder efficient exploration and training.

## Method

- An end-to-end asymmetric actor-critic reinforcement learning framework directly maps ball-position observations and robot proprioception to whole-body joint commands for both arm striking and leg locomotion.
- A lightweight ==Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)== network is trained online to predict the future post-bounce apex of the ball, augmenting the actor's observations to enable proactive decision-making and anticipatory movements.
- Dense, continuous, physics-based 'reaching' and 'returning' rewards are designed to provide immediate feedback, guiding efficient learning and overcoming the sparsity of binary success/failure rewards.

## Results

- In simulation, the policy achieved a **97.6%** hit rate and a **94.1%** success rate for mixed-range serves (**5.2** to **6.5** m/s ball speeds), demonstrating versatile strike and footwork strategies adaptable to varied ball trajectories.
- The learned policy transferred zero-shot to a physical Booster T1 humanoid robot, achieving a **93.5%** hit rate and a **61.3%** success rate across 31 randomized serves.
- The physical robot exhibited coordinated upper and lower body movements, including 2D footwork (lateral and forward-backward), and returned balls at an average outbound speed of **6.9** m/s immediately post-impact, exceeding the incoming ball speed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a learned ball trajectory predictor into the actor's observation space provides crucial proactive guidance, enabling the humanoid to anticipate and position its whole body effectively for fast-paced interactions.
> - Dense, physics-based predictive rewards are essential for efficient exploration and the emergence of coordinated whole-body behaviors in complex tasks with sparse natural rewards.
> - An end-to-end RL approach can successfully learn intricate arm-leg coordination for high-DoF humanoid robots in dynamic sports without relying on restrictive model-based planning or virtual hitting-plane assumptions.
