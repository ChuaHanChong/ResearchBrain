# RewardVerse: Rubric-Guided Policy Optimization for Video Reward Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> RewardVerse is a video reward model that inserts a dynamic rubric between the evaluation query and the scorer instead of scoring videos directly. A generator emits weighted themes and tips from the query alone, and a scorer reads continuous scores from rating-token logits. Its two-stage RGPO training, using only 30 preference pairs per dimension, mitigates scalar drift and reaches state-of-the-art pointwise and pairwise accuracy.

## Problem

- Existing video reward models map videos straight to one scalar without explicit criteria, so scores collapse into a narrow high band and leave near-flat RL gradients.
- Without an explicit anchor, direct scorers shift their internal scale across paraphrased prompts and input orderings, giving inconsistent scores for identical video content.
- Prior rubric-as-reward work in language and image domains treats rubrics as static prompt scaffolds, and existing video reward models need large preference sets or supervised fine-tuning.

## Method

- A **Dynamic Rubric Generator** emits themes, normalized weights and execution tips from the query only, never seeing the candidate video, which keeps criteria query-adaptive yet unbiased.
- A ==soft-logits scorer== takes the expected value over the logits of rating tokens one through five per theme, then aggregates theme scores by their weights into one reward.
- **RGPO** trains in two stages: warm-up on self-evolving seed rubrics built by ==propose-verify-revise== with preference, format and margin-calibration losses, then rubric-level ==GRPO== with a ==BGE-M3== theme-alignment reward.

## Results

- On EvalVerse it reaches the highest PLCC in **14 of 16** dimensions, averaging **0.554** over the 10 shared dimensions against **0.467** for the next-best Q-Scorer.
- On the external VGRB benchmark it beats VisionReward by **7.1** and **7.0** points on seen Visual Quality, and leads all non-oracle models on unseen Text Alignment.
- Ablations drop PLCC by **0.099** without Stage-1 warm-up and **0.074** without Stage-2; rubric-free training at the same 480-pair budget reaches only **0.439**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An explicit rubric is necessary but not sufficient: natural-language score output still ties two-thirds of pairs, so a continuous logit readout is required.
> - Generating evaluation criteria before seeing the candidate prevents the rubric from being bent leniently or strictly toward the item being judged.
> - Give the scorer a different training signal than the generator, and calibrate relative score margins rather than absolute values when data is scarce.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Reward models turn judgments about generated videos into rewards that can guide reinforcement learning. Video quality, though, mixes many subjective criteria. When a model is asked to jump straight to one number, its scale can compress or shift with context, which weakens the signal used to improve the generator. RewardVerse first decides what should be judged. From the evaluation query, it generates a rubric of weighted themes and concrete tips, and only then scores the video against those criteria. The authors frame this intermediate rubric as a semantic anchor. It mitigates unstable or miscalibrated scores, but it does not by itself eliminate them. Three scoring variants leave predictions concentrated in a narrow band, while the rubric-plus-soft-logits variant spreads them across more of the score range.

Predictions and human labels for 240 videos, scored from 1 to 5, under four variants: V1 (no rubric, natural-language float), V2 (no rubric, soft-logits), V3 (rubric, soft-logits), and V4 (rubric, natural-language float). V1’s predictions cluster at the high end. V2 is more distributed but still concentrated. V3 has the broadest prediction spread. V4 remains concentrated toward the high end despite rubric guidance.

### 2. A rubric gives the score something to mean

The authors call the problem “scalar drift.” Score-range collapse leaves little separation between videos, and context shifts can also change scores for the same content.

A rubric is a structured set of evaluation themes, weights, and tips. The rubric generator receives the evaluation query, which consists of a target dimension and the text prompt used to make the video. It does not see the candidate video. The scorer then evaluates the video theme by theme. Its soft-logits readout takes the expected score over the model’s logits for rating tokens 1–5. A weighted average of theme scores gives the pointwise reward. This produces a continuous score without relying on free-form numbers or parsing generated text.

The controlled test used the same 240-video, human-labeled pointwise hold-out set, with Qwen2.5-VL-7B across 16 EvalVerse dimensions. Holding soft-logits constant, adding a rubric increases predicted-score standard deviation from 0.258 to 0.471 (+82.6%). This is evidence of greater score spread. By itself, it does not show greater accuracy or robustness. The rubric with natural-language score generation still shows bias and ties. The authors report that V4 still had ties in two-thirds of comparison pairs. Rubric prompting alone therefore leaves a calibration gap, which motivates the training method, RGPO.

### 3. Train the criteria and the scorer

RewardVerse uses one shared multimodal-model backbone in two prompted roles: rubric generator and scorer. Training proceeds in two stages.

**Stage 1.** An offline frontier model proposes, verifies, and revises seed rubrics from 30 human preference pairs per dimension. The verified rubrics are used to warm up the scorer. The scorer’s learning signal has three parts:

- whether it ranks the preferred video above the less-preferred one,
- a format-validity reward, and
- a calibration loss that matches the predicted score gap to the human-annotated score margin.

**Stage 2.** The generator samples query-adaptive rubrics. A rubric’s reward depends on how well its induced scores separate preferred from less-preferred videos, plus format validity and similarity to seed themes. The optimization is asymmetric. The generator gets the rubric-level policy-gradient update. The scorer is calibrated against human margins, with the sampled rubric treated as fixed context. This prevents the scorer from earning a higher rubric reward merely by inflating score gaps.

Stage 1 (top): a propose/verify/revise loop creates seed rubrics, and a scorer evaluates preferred and less-preferred videos under the seed rubric, with preference and format rewards alongside a human-margin calibration loss. Stage 2 (bottom): the query goes to the generator, which samples dynamic rubrics, and a scorer evaluates both videos under each rubric. The generator’s reward includes preference, format, and seed-theme alignment, while the scorer’s separate signal is calibration.

An ablation on EvalVerse, macro-averaged over 16 dimensions, tests the stages. PLCC measures linear correlation with human ratings, and SRCC measures rank correlation. Full RGPO scores PLCC 0.530 and SRCC 0.416. Removing Stage 1 gives 0.431 and 0.355, and removing Stage 2 gives 0.456 and 0.364. These comparisons support the authors’ claim that both stages contribute. They do not establish that each stage has the same effect in other settings.

### 4. A score needs an anchor

RewardVerse turns explicit, query-specific criteria into a continuous reward that can be calibrated against human preferences. It is more than a more elaborate evaluator. The rubric mitigates scalar drift without eliminating it. The calibration objective also aligns the aggregate score margin, and the authors identify theme-level calibration as a limitation.
