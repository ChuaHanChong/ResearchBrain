# Astra: Efficient Transformer Architecture and Contrastive Dynamics Learning for Embodied Instruction Following

> [!summary] Summary
> Astra is an efficient Transformer architecture for embodied instruction following, introducing trajectory attention and learnable action queries to better process multimodal sequences. It also incorporates Contrastive Dynamics Learning (CDL) for enhanced environmental understanding and multimodal alignment. The model achieves superior performance across challenging benchmarks, including a 70.67% success rate on unseen ManiSkill tasks and faster convergence on VIMA-Bench, by more effectively leveraging multimodal trajectory structure and learning dynamics.

## Problem

- Existing Transformer-based VLA models primarily use causal attention, which is suboptimal for multimodal trajectories where tokens within segments (e.g., state images, action dimensions) lack inherent causal order.
- Autoregressive generation of action dimensions in previous approaches can be inefficient, as the embedding of a preceding token may not optimally encapsulate the most relevant information for predicting the subsequent action dimension.
- Traditional dynamics learning methods for embodied agents are often complex, requiring generative modules (e.g., video generators), and struggle to efficiently align all three modalities: vision, language, and action.

## Method

- Introduces "trajectory attention," a novel self-attention mechanism that allows bidirectional attention within segments (prompt, state, action) while maintaining causal attention between segments.
- Proposes "learnable action queries," where each query is dedicated to extracting information for a specific action dimension, enabling parallel and more targeted action generation.
- Develops "==Contrastive Dynamics Learning (CDL)==," an encoding-based auxiliary objective that trains the model to distinguish between valid and invalid environmental dynamics, improving multimodal alignment with minimal architectural overhead.

## Results

- Astra consistently outperformed baselines (e.g., DT, Gato, VIMA) across VIMA-Bench generalization levels (e.g., **97.08%** on L1, **49.50%** on L4), demonstrating higher accuracy and faster convergence with comparable or fewer parameters.
- Achieved a **70.67%** success rate on "All" unseen ManiSkill tasks, substantially outperforming Gato (**40.00%**) and VIMA (**19.33%**), and exhibited improved robustness with capabilities like "instantaneous regrasp."
- Demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization on CALVIN's challenging ABC→D setting, achieving an average task length of **3.29**, surpassing baselines like GR-1 (**3.06**) and RoboFlamingo (**2.48**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modifying Transformer attention masks to allow bidirectional attention *within* multimodal segments (e.g., visual observations from multiple cameras, multi-dimensional actions) significantly improves contextual understanding of embodied instruction following trajectories.
> - Dedicated, learnable action queries can more efficiently extract relevant information for predicting specific action dimensions in parallel, outperforming sequential autoregressive generation.
> - A lightweight, encoding-based contrastive learning objective can effectively enhance a model's internal representation of environmental dynamics and improve multimodal alignment without requiring complex generative modules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research operates within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on **Embodied Instruction Following (EIF)** tasks, which involve agents interpreting language, perceiving environments visually, and executing actions. This domain has seen the emergence of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**, which build upon the capabilities of **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**.

Traditionally, VLMs (e.g., UniLM, M6, PaliGemma) have been adept at multimodal tasks like visual question answering and image captioning by processing visual and textual inputs. However, VLAs extend this by enabling direct action generation in a physical or simulated environment. The current VLA landscape can be broadly categorized into:
- **"Large VLAs"**: These adapt pre-trained large VLMs to robotics, leveraging vast amounts of web-scale data. Examples include RT-2, OpenVLA, and $\pi^0$. These models often employ causal or cross-attention mechanisms derived from their VLM predecessors.
- **"Generalized VLAs"**: These are built from scratch, framing reinforcement learning as sequence modeling problems. Pioneering works like Decision Transformer (DT) and Trajectory Transformer (TT) laid the groundwork, followed by models like Gato, RT-1, and VIMA. Astra falls into this category.

A key challenge in developing efficient VLAs lies in the nature of multimodal sequences, or "trajectories," which often consist of interleaved segments of different modalities (language prompts, states, actions). Many existing Transformer-based models, especially those for natural language generation, utilize **causal attention**, which restricts information flow to preceding tokens. The paper observes that this causal restriction is suboptimal for processing segments like state images (from multiple cameras) or action dimensions, where tokens within a segment lack inherent causal relationships and are conditionally independent. This limitation can impede the model's ability to fully capture intra-segment information.

Furthermore, prior approaches to action generation in VLAs often generate action dimensions autoregressively. This method can be inefficient, as the embedding of a preceding token may not optimally encapsulate the most relevant information for predicting the subsequent action dimension.

Another area of related work is **dynamics learning**, which aims to model how an environment transitions based on agent actions. Previous methods typically involve decoding tasks, such as forward dynamics (predicting the next state) or inverse dynamics (reconstructing actions between states), often requiring complex generative modules like video generators. The integration of dynamics learning with multimodal representations, particularly for aligning all three modalities (vision, language, action), remains an active area of research, with existing contrastive learning methods often focusing primarily on visual representations (e.g., R3M, VIP).

This work addresses these gaps by proposing a novel Transformer architecture designed to specifically handle the unique properties of EIF trajectories and by introducing an efficient, encoding-based contrastive dynamics learning objective to improve environment understanding and multimodal alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and effective Transformer architecture, named Astra, for embodied instruction following tasks. This objective is driven by two main motivations related to the limitations observed in existing VLA models:

1. **Addressing Suboptimal Causal Attention for Multimodal Trajectories:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing Transformer-based VLAs commonly employ causal attention mechanisms, which are well-suited for autoregressive token generation in natural language tasks. However, in EIF tasks, multimodal trajectories consist of segments (e.g., language prompts, visual states from multiple cameras, and multi-dimensional actions). The tokens within a single state segment (e.g., images from different camera angles) or an action segment (e.g., x, y, z coordinates) do not inherently possess a causal order; they are largely conditionally independent of each other given the preceding context. Causal attention masks information from subsequent tokens within such a segment, thereby hindering the model's ability to capture comprehensive intra-segment relationships and information flow. This can lead to an incomplete understanding of the current state or action.
 - **Objective:** To design a novel self-attention mechanism, "trajectory attention," that optimizes information flow within these segments by allowing bidirectional attention internally, while maintaining causal attention between segments to preserve the temporal order of the trajectory. Additionally, the prompt segment, being an input for encoding rather than generation, is also processed with bidirectional attention.

2. **Improving Action Generation Efficiency and Relevance:**
 - **Motivation:** Many prior VLA models generate action dimensions autoregressively, meaning each action dimension is predicted based on the embedding of the previous token. This approach is considered suboptimal because the embedding of a preceding token, while incorporating historical context through self-attention, is significantly influenced by its direct input. Consequently, it may not aggregate the most pertinent information specifically required for accurately predicting an individual action dimension. This can lead to less precise or efficient action generation.
 - **Objective:** To introduce "learnable action queries," inspired by object queries in object detection, where each query is dedicated to extracting the most relevant information for a specific action dimension. This aims to allow parallel generation of action dimensions, leading to a more efficient and targeted information extraction process for action prediction.

A secondary, complementary objective is to enhance the model's understanding of environmental dynamics and multimodal alignment.

1. **Enhancing Dynamics Learning with Minimal Overhead:**
 - **Motivation:** Dynamics learning is crucial for robots to make informed decisions by understanding state transitions resulting from actions. Previous approaches, such as forward or inverse dynamics, typically involve complex decoding tasks and often require additional generative modules (e.g., video generators), leading to considerable model complexity. There is a need for a simpler, more lightweight approach to dynamics learning that can be effectively integrated.
 - **Objective:** To propose a "contrastive dynamics learning (CDL)" objective that leverages Astra's encoding capabilities. This objective aims to explicitly train the model to distinguish between valid and invalid environmental dynamics, thereby improving its internal representation of how actions affect states. This approach seeks to provide a lightweight alternative to generative dynamics models, serving simultaneously as a representation learning method for aligning vision, language, and action modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Astra’s methodology integrates a novel Transformer architecture with a contrastive dynamics learning objective.

#### 3.1. Astra Architecture

Astra consists of two primary architectural innovations: **trajectory attention** and **learnable action queries**. The model processes multimodal sequences, or trajectories $\tau = (p, s_{t=1}, a_{t=1}, \dots, s_{t=T}, a_{t=T})$, where $p$ is the language prompt, $s_t$ is the state at timestep $t$, and $a_t$ is the action at timestep $t$. Each of these elements comprises a segment of tokens (e.g., $s_{1:M,t}$ for $M$ state tokens).

- **Trajectory Attention:**
 - **Design Principle:** Trajectory attention is characterized by two properties: inter-segment connections are causal, and intra-segment connections are bidirectional. This means tokens can attend to all preceding segments, but crucially, they can also attend to all other tokens *within their own segment*, regardless of their position.
 - **Rationale:** This design is motivated by the observation that tokens within a prompt segment, state segment (e.g., images from different cameras), or action segment (e.g., different action dimensions) do not inherently follow a causal order. For instance, `s_{1,t}` should be able to attend to `s_{2:M,t}` without restriction, and similarly for action dimensions. By allowing bidirectional attention within segments, the model can establish a more complete information flow and contextual understanding compared to purely causal attention. For the prompt, as it is encoded for understanding rather than generated autoregressively, bidirectional attention is also applied.
 - **Implementation:** This is achieved by modifying the attention mask in the Transformer layers. Compared to causal attention, trajectory attention unmasks entries corresponding to `(p_i, p_j)`, `(s_{i,t}, s_{j,t})`, or `(a_{i,t}, a_{j,t})` for `i < j`, enabling these additional information flows.
 - **Segment-level Processing:** This attention mechanism is designed to naturally align with segment-level processing, treating states and actions as cohesive blocks of information rather than discrete, causally ordered tokens.

- **Action Queries:**
 - **Motivation:** To overcome the limitations of autoregressive action generation, where the preceding token's embedding might not be optimally relevant for the current action dimension.
 - **Mechanism:** Astra introduces a set of learnable action queries, `q_{1:N}`, where each `q_i` is dedicated to a specific action dimension `a_i`. These queries are shared across all timesteps (`q_{i,t=1} = q_{i,t=2} = \dots = q_{i,T}`). Each action query independently attends to all preceding tokens (prompt, previous states, and previous actions) to extract the most relevant information for its corresponding action dimension.
 - **Segment-level Decoding:** Unlike autoregressive methods, action queries are independent, allowing for parallel generation of all action dimensions within a segment. This enables a segment-level decoding scheme.
 - **Masking:** To ensure action queries are used solely for information extraction and do not convey trajectory information, they are masked out from the attention matrix, preventing other tokens from attending to them.
 - **Imitation Learning Objective:** The model is trained using a standard behavior cloning objective: `L_BC = min_θ Σ_{t=1}^T -log π_θ(a_t | p, s_{≤t}, a_{<t})`.

#### 3.2. Contrastive Dynamics Learning (CDL)

CDL is an auxiliary objective designed to enhance Astra’s understanding of environment dynamics and multimodal alignment with minimal architectural overhead.

- **Mechanism:** CDL utilizes the InfoNCE objective to train the model to distinguish between valid (positive) and invalid (negative) trajectory samples. Astra encodes the entire multimodal trajectory into a single trajectory embedding, `f(·)`, by pooling the embeddings of the last action tokens through a classification head (a pooling layer followed by a linear layer).
- **Positive Sample Construction:**
 - Given an anchor trajectory `τ`, a positive sample `τ⁺` is created by augmenting it.
 - **Image Augmentation:** Standard computer vision techniques (e.g., random cropping) are applied to state images.
 - **Action Perturbation:** A novel technique involves adding a small amount of random noise to the actions. The intuition is that minor deviations in actions can still lead to the same desired outcome (e.g., a slightly different grasp path still successfully picks up an object).
- **Negative Sample Construction:**
 - Negative samples `τ⁻` are constructed by violating the environment dynamics. This is achieved by mismatching state and action segments from *different* trajectories (`τ'`, `τ''`) with those of the anchor trajectory. For example, `τ⁻ = (p, s'_{t=1}, a_{t=1}, \dots, s_{t=T}, a''_{t=T})`.
 - **Principles for Strong Negatives:**
 1. Avoid inserting entirely random actions or states, as these are easily detectable as negatives.
 2. Utilize augmented versions of states and actions in negative samples to prevent the model from trivially identifying positives solely by detecting augmentation.
 3. Avoid simple shuffling of states and actions along the time axis, as these are also easily recognizable.
- **Overall Training Loss:** CDL is incorporated as an auxiliary objective, combined with the primary behavior cloning loss: `L = L_BC + αL_CDL`. During CDL, action queries are not used for trajectory encoding, and their attention entries are excluded.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated Astra across three large-scale robot manipulation benchmarks: VIMA-Bench, ManiSkill, and CALVIN, and conducted ablation studies and qualitative analyses.

- **VIMA-Bench (Multimodal Robot Learning and Generalization):**
 - Astra (38M parameters) demonstrated performance improvements across all four generalization levels (L1: placement, L2: combinatorial, L3: novel object, L4: novel task generalization) compared to baselines such as DT, Gato, Flamingo, Gato*, and VIMA.
 - Despite being approximately 10% smaller in parameter count than several baselines, Astra achieved higher success rates, indicating architectural efficiency. For example, Astra achieved 97.08% on L1, 94.62% on L2, 86.17% on L3, and 49.50% on L4, outperforming VIMA (87.69% L1, 86.92% L2, 84.17% L3, 47.50% L4).
 - Training curves showed Astra converging faster and achieving lower loss and higher accuracy compared to DT, Gato, and VIMA.

- **ManiSkill (Everyday Object Manipulation and Generalization):**
 - Astra (198M parameters) significantly outperformed RT-1, VIMA, and Gato across various categories of unseen tasks (color, size, shape, container, and combined "All") and seen tasks with varying numbers of distractors.
 - Astra achieved a 70.67% success rate on the "All" unseen tasks, notably higher than Gato (40.00%) and VIMA (19.33%). For seen tasks, Astra scored 90.93% with 0 distractors, 90.53% with 2-4 distractors, and 79.07% with 6-8 distractors, consistently outperforming baselines.
 - Qualitative analysis highlighted Astra's capability for "instantaneous regrasp," where it promptly detected failed grasps and made rapid, repeated attempts until successful, a behavior not observed in baseline models.

- **CALVIN (Long-Horizon Manipulation and Zero-Shot Generalization):**
 - In the challenging ABC→D setting (trained on A, B, C; evaluated on D for zero-shot generalization), Astra (37.8M parameters) demonstrated superior performance in completing longer sequences of tasks.
 - Astra achieved an average task length of 3.29, outperforming all baselines, including GR-1 (3.06), RoboFlamingo (2.48), and Gato (2.38). This indicates enhanced generalization to new environments and improved robustness for multi-stage tasks.

- **Ablation Study:**
 - **Contrastive Dynamics Learning (CDL):** The inclusion of CDL consistently improved Astra's performance. For instance, on VIMA-Bench L1, Astra with CDL achieved 97.08% compared to 94.69% without CDL. On ManiSkill easy tasks, it improved from 91.33% to 93.83%. This suggests CDL enhances environment dynamics understanding and multimodal alignment. The performance gain was less pronounced for VIMA-Bench L4, which was attributed to CDL's training data, focusing on seen nouns and adjectives, potentially limiting generalization to novel meta-tasks.
 - **Trajectory Attention:** Removing trajectory attention resulted in a noticeable performance drop across all benchmarks and generalization levels (e.g., VIMA-Bench L1 dropped from 94.69% to 91.23%), underscoring its importance for processing segmented multimodal trajectories.
 - **Action Queries:** The absence of action queries also led to reduced success rates (e.g., VIMA-Bench L1 dropped from 94.69% to 89.08%), highlighting their role in efficient information extraction for action generation.
 - **Combined Removal:** When both trajectory attention and action queries were removed, the model's performance significantly decreased, reverting to that of a typical token-level autoregressive model, similar to Gato, confirming the individual and combined contributions of these components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Astra represents a notable advancement in the field of embodied instruction following, with several key points of significance and potential impact:

- **Efficient and Effective Transformer Architecture:** Astra's core architectural innovations – trajectory attention and learnable action queries – offer a more suitable and efficient approach for processing multimodal EIF trajectories than traditional causal attention mechanisms. This addresses the inherent non-causal relationships within segments of visual observations or action dimensions, leading to a more comprehensive understanding of context and more precise action generation.
- **Enhanced Understanding of Environment Dynamics:** The proposed Contrastive Dynamics Learning (CDL) objective provides a lightweight yet effective method for explicitly training the model to understand how actions influence environmental states. Unlike complex generative dynamics models, CDL leverages an encoding-based approach, minimizing architectural overhead while simultaneously improving multimodal alignment. This contributes to more informed decision-making by the embodied agent.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalization:** Astra's consistent and substantial performance improvements across diverse and challenging benchmarks (VIMA-Bench, ManiSkill, CALVIN) demonstrate its effectiveness. The model exhibits superior generalization capabilities across novel objects, tasks, and environments, which is critical for deploying robust robotic agents in varied real-world scenarios.
- **Improved Robustness in Manipulation Tasks:** The qualitative observation of "instantaneous regrasp" in ManiSkill highlights Astra's enhanced ability to detect and recover from failed manipulation attempts rapidly. This capability is crucial for practical robotics, where task failures are common, and efficient recovery mechanisms can significantly improve task completion rates and overall system reliability.
- **Scalability and Adaptability:** Astra is demonstrated to be compatible with various vision encoders, language encoders, and action types, showing its adaptability. Its ability to scale from 19M to 198M parameters depending on benchmark complexity, combined with the effectiveness of CDL as a large-scale pretraining method, suggests its potential for broader application in more complex scenarios and with larger foundation models.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The work opens promising avenues for future research, such as integrating Astra as an action prediction head within larger VLA models that are pre-trained on vast datasets. Furthermore, extending Astra to incorporate 3D vision modules could further enhance its performance, indicating its potential as a flexible backbone for advanced embodied AI systems.
