# Mixture of Cognitive Reasoners: Modular Reasoning with Brain-Like Specialization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from EPFL, MIT, and Harvard developed Mixture of Cognitive Reasoners (MICRO), a modular LLM architecture that explicitly incorporates specialized "expert" modules aligned with human brain networks. The model demonstrates interpretable, brain-like functional specialization, enhanced alignment with human behavior on the COGBENCH benchmark, and competitive performance on various reasoning tasks, enabling dynamic control over its cognitive processes.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) possess largely unstructured internal organizations, contrasting with the human brain's clear functional specialization across distinct cognitive networks.
- Existing LLMs, despite exhibiting implicit specialization, lack explicit interpretability and fine-grained controllability over their reasoning processes.
- Bridging the gap between AI models and human cognition requires architectures that not only perform well but also mechanistically align with human brain organization and behavior.

## Method

- A ==Mixture-of-Blocks (MOB) transformer architecture==, MICRO, is introduced, where each layer's entire transformer block is duplicated into four "expert" modules aligned with human cognitive networks (Language, Logic, Social, World).
- A ==three-stage training curriculum== induces specialization: experts are first primed on a small, pseudo-labeled dataset with deterministic token routing, then a router learns to assign tokens, and finally, the whole model is finetuned end-to-end on a large instruction dataset.
- An ==MLP-based router== dynamically assigns each token to a single expert module at every layer, enabling context-dependent activation of specialized reasoning paths.

## Results

- MICRO models exhibit clear domain-specific token routing and an emergent hierarchical processing structure, with router probabilities correlating with human behavioral judgments (e.g., social expert selectivity with mental state content, r=**0.7**).
- Mechanistic ablations confirmed the causal role of individual experts, with Logic expert ablation significantly reducing accuracy on math tasks and Social expert ablation sometimes improving performance on purely logical problems.
- The framework achieved superior alignment with human behavior on the COGBENCH benchmark and matched or outperformed non-modular and general-purpose modular baselines on various reasoning tasks including GSM8K, MATH, MMLU, and BBH.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating cognitive inductive biases into LLM design, coupled with a targeted training curriculum, effectively induces interpretable, brain-like functional specialization in models.
> - The Mixture-of-Blocks (MOB) architecture, where entire transformer blocks function as experts, is crucial for achieving clear and reliable specialization, particularly in larger models, outperforming traditional Mixture-of-Experts.
> - Functionally specialized modules in AI models offer a computational framework to test hypotheses about human brain network interactions and provide enhanced interpretability and dynamic control over AI reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a discrepancy between the organization of human cognition and current Large Language Models (LLMs). Neuroscience research indicates that human cognitive behavior arises from the interaction of specialized brain networks dedicated to distinct functions such as language, logic, and social reasoning. In contrast, the internal organization of contemporary LLMs is generally undifferentiated, with any observed specialization being implicit and difficult to interpret or control.

This paper positions itself within two principal areas of artificial intelligence research: modular language models and brain-inspired computing.
Within modular language models, previous work has explored Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures to promote specialization, mitigate interference, and improve generalization. Approaches have included training experts on domain-labeled datasets, using prompting for domain-specific experts, and frameworks like ModuleFormer that encourage specialization through load-balancing. However, these models have not explicitly aimed to induce specialization aligned with known human cognitive functions.
Concurrently, research into brain-inspired models has investigated aligning LLMs with human brain activity, particularly in the language network. Some studies have integrated biologically inspired principles into model design, drawing from structures like the visual cortex hierarchy or spatio-functional brain organization.

The presented work, **Mixture of Cognitive Reasoners (MICRO)**, distinguishes itself by being, to the authors' knowledge, the first modular language model explicitly designed to induce brain-like specialization. It directly aligns its expert modules with well-studied cognitive networks in the human brain, thus bridging the fields of modular LLMs and brain-inspired AI by incorporating explicit cognitive inductive biases. This approach aims to enhance both the interpretability and controllability of LLMs while also offering a computational framework for formulating testable hypotheses about human brain function.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing MICRO stems from the observation that human cognition is characterized by the interaction of functionally specialized brain networks, while LLMs typically possess a more homogeneous internal structure. The authors aim to introduce this principle of functional specialization into LLM architectures.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **To Induce Interpretable Functional Specialization:** The central objective is to develop an architecture and training curriculum that promotes explicit functional specialization among different modules within a transformer-based LLM. This specialization is designed to mirror well-studied cognitive networks in the human brain: language, logic (multiple demand), social reasoning (theory of mind), and world knowledge (default mode network). The goal is for these specialized experts to be causally meaningful, such that their ablation leads to predictable and domain-specific performance drops.
2. **To Enhance Controllability:** By achieving explicit specialization, the authors aim to enable dynamic steering of the model's behavior at inference time. This involves routing tokens to specific experts (e.g., favoring social over logical reasoning) to provide fine-grained control over the model's outputs.
3. **To Improve Alignment with Human Behavior:** A significant objective is to develop models that exhibit behavior more aligned with human cognitive patterns. This involves evaluating the model on benchmarks designed to capture human behavioral strategies, aiming for higher correspondence compared to non-specialized or non-modular baselines.
4. **To Maintain or Improve Performance on Reasoning Benchmarks:** The research seeks to demonstrate that introducing brain-like functional specialization does not lead to a degradation in overall performance. Instead, the objective is to match or outperform comparable baseline models on standard machine learning reasoning benchmarks, suggesting that interpretability and controllability can be achieved without sacrificing capability.
5. **To Provide a Framework for Cognitive Science:** Beyond engineering improvements, the work aims to offer a computational modeling framework that can be used to formulate and test hypotheses regarding how the relative contributions and interactions of different brain networks support complex human behaviors.

In summary, the motivation is to create LLMs that are not only powerful but also transparent, controllable, and potentially serve as better computational models of human cognition by explicitly integrating brain-like functional modularity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Mixture of Cognitive Reasoners (MICRO) framework, which comprises a modular architecture and a three-stage training curriculum designed to induce functional specialization.

**Model Architecture:**
The MICRO architecture starts with a pre-trained transformer-based backbone. For each layer, the entire transformer block is cloned N times, where N corresponds to the number of experts (N=4 in this study). Each of these experts is designed to mirror a major cognitive network:
- **Language Expert:** Aligned with the human language network (e.g., left-lateralized frontal and temporal regions).
- **Logic Expert:** Aligned with the Multiple Demand (MD) network (e.g., bilateral regions active across diverse cognitively demanding tasks).
- **Social Expert:** Aligned with the Theory of Mind (ToM) network (e.g., bilateral temporo-parietal junction and medial prefrontal cortex).
- **World Expert:** Aligned with the Default Mode Network (DMN) (e.g., medial prefrontal and parietal regions active during internally directed thought and long-timescale information integration).

An MLP-based router is initialized to assign each token to a single expert at every layer, a process referred to as top-1 routing. The authors explicitly term this architecture "mixture-of-blocks" (MOB) to differentiate it from the more common "mixture-of-experts" (MoE), where only feed-forward network (FFN) layers are specialized, and attention is shared. MOB experts are full transformer blocks, including their own attention mechanisms, which was found to induce clearer functional specialization at larger scales.

**Training Curriculum for Inducing Specialization:**
A three-stage curriculum is employed to induce and solidify functional specialization:

1. **Stage 1: Inducing Specialization:**
 - A small, curated dataset named `MICRO_SFT` (3,055 examples) is used. Each example in `MICRO_SFT` is crafted to reflect the functional domain of a specific expert.
 - This stage solely trains the expert parameters using a next-token prediction loss. Tokens attend to all preceding tokens, but only those assigned to a particular expert are processed through its feed-forward network.
2. **Stage 2: Calibrating Router:**
 - The entire model (including experts) is frozen.
 - Only the router parameters are trained on the same `MICRO_SFT` dataset.
 - The objective remains next-token prediction, allowing the router to learn optimal expert assignment based on the initial specialization induced in Stage 1.
 - A soft mixture of the top-2 experts per token is used during this stage to encourage smoother routing decisions.
3. **Stage 3: End-to-End Supervised Finetuning:**
 - The entire model (experts and router) is finetuned end-to-end on a large-scale instruction-tuning dataset (TULU-3, consisting of 939,000 examples).
 - This stage aims to consolidate and improve general performance while preserving the functional specialization established in the earlier stages.

**Constructing the `MICRO_SFT` Dataset:**
The `MICRO_SFT` dataset was constructed as follows:
- Nineteen existing reasoning datasets were selected, categorized into Logic, Social, and World knowledge domains, ensuring diversity within each cognitive domain.
- Approximately 1,000 examples were sampled from each of these three sets.
- For datasets lacking detailed reasoning steps, OpenAI's O1 model was used to generate step-by-step responses.
- GPT-4O was then employed to pseudo-label each sentence in these generated reasoning chains, assigning them to one of the four cognitive experts (Language, Logic, Social, World). Tokens within each sentence inherited the assigned expert label, used for deterministic routing during Stage 1.

**Experimental Setup:**
Five models of varying scales from three families were post-trained within the MICRO framework: LLAMA-3.2-{1B, 3B}, SMOLLM2-{135M, 360M}, and OLMO-2-1B. Each model underwent two epochs of training on `MICRO_SFT` (Stages 1 and 2), followed by one epoch of end-to-end training on the TULU-3 dataset (Stage 3). Next token prediction was the sole learning objective.

**Evaluation:**
The evaluation encompassed:
- **Reasoning Benchmarks:** GSM8K, MATH, BBH, and MMLU, typically using zero- or few-shot chain-of-thought prompting.
- **Behavioral Benchmarks:** COGBENCH, which assesses alignment to human behavior across 10 metrics from 7 cognitive psychology experiments, using a bounded relative error similarity score (S_BRE).
- **Interpretability and Causal Analysis:** Examination of token routing patterns, correlation between router probabilities and human behavioral judgments (using an independently annotated dataset), causal ablations (selectively removing experts), and application of neuroscience localizers (Language, Multiple Demand, Theory of Mind) to identify corresponding expert modules.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the interpretability, behavioral alignment, and performance of the MICRO models.

#### 4.1. Interpretable and Functionally Specialized Experts

- **Token Routing Patterns:** MICRO models exhibited clear domain-specific specialization in their token routing. For example, social cognition tasks primarily routed to the social expert, while arithmetic problems engaged the logic expert. Layer-wise analysis revealed a hierarchical organization: earlier layers predominantly utilized the language expert for linguistic grounding, while deeper layers increasingly delegated to domain-specific experts. This mirrors observations in cognitive neuroscience regarding the sequential engagement of brain networks.
- **Correlation with Human Judgments:** Router probabilities correlated with human behavioral judgments on independently annotated six-word sentences. The social expert’s selectivity, for instance, correlated with human ratings of mental state content (r=0.7). Language expert probabilities correlated with grammaticality and plausibility, particularly in early layers. The world expert correlated with physical objects and places.
- **Causal Meaningfulness via Ablations:** Removing individual experts demonstrated their causal contributions to task performance. Ablating the Logic expert caused substantial drops in accuracy on MATH and GSM8K benchmarks, confirming its role in numerical reasoning. Conversely, removing the Social expert sometimes led to slight performance *improvements* on these tasks, suggesting potential interference in contexts outside its specialization. Ablating the language expert resulted in significant performance drops across most tasks. Test-time ablations were shown to steer model behavior qualitatively, favoring specific modes of reasoning.
- **Neuroscience Localizers:** Neuroscience-inspired localizers successfully identified corresponding expert modules. The language localizer favored the language expert in early layers and the world expert in later layers. The Multiple Demand localizer consistently favored the logic expert. The Theory of of Mind (ToM) localizer was less effective for social experts, but its efficacy improved with model scale.

#### 4.2. Strong Alignment to Human Behavior

- On the COGBENCH benchmark, MICRO models (e.g., MICRO-LLAMA-1B) demonstrated superior or comparable alignment with human behavior compared to both the non-specialized Mixture-of-Blocks (MOB) baseline and the non-modular Dense transformer baseline. This alignment was quantified using the bounded relative error similarity score (S_BRE). Larger MICRO models exhibited stronger average behavioral alignment.

#### 4.3. Competitive Performance on Reasoning Benchmarks

- MICRO models consistently matched or outperformed their comparable MOB baselines on standard reasoning benchmarks such as GSM8K, MINERVA-MATH, MMLU, and BBH.
- Further performance gains were observed when ablating the "least relevant" expert for a given task (e.g., removing the social expert for mathematics benchmarks). This indicates that the induced specialization enables targeted optimization.
- The method demonstrated robustness across different post-training pipelines, including Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and domain-specific instruction tuning, with specialization remaining beneficial.
- Enabling top-2 routing at test time (instead of the top-1 used during SFT) led to consistent performance improvements across most benchmarks, suggesting generalization to increased routing capacity.

#### 4.4. Robustness and Scalability

- The functional specialization, seeded by the small `MICRO_SFT` dataset, was largely preserved throughout the large-scale, end-to-end Stage 3 training, indicating robust inductive biases.
- Experiments with a hybrid MICRO-LLAMA-8B model (experts only in the last 12 layers) confirmed expected routing patterns and competitive performance, though neuroscience localizers were less effective in this hybrid configuration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Mixture of Cognitive Reasoners (MICRO) framework represents a notable contribution to the development of artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of large language models.

**Enhanced Interpretability and Controllability:** By explicitly aligning internal model modules with well-understood human cognitive functions, MICRO offers a pathway toward more transparent and interpretable LLMs. The ability to observe and analyze token routing patterns provides insights into the model's decision-making processes, which is a significant advantage over opaque black-box models. Furthermore, the demonstrated capacity to dynamically steer model behavior at inference time by activating or deactivating specific experts introduces a new level of fine-grained control over LLM outputs, allowing for customized responses that prioritize certain modes of reasoning (e.g., logical over social). This could lead to more reliable and trustworthy AI systems in sensitive applications.

**Advancing Cognitive Science Research:** The framework provides a computational hypothesis space for cognitive neuroscientists. It offers a concrete, testable model for understanding how specialized brain networks might interact to produce complex behaviors. The explicit mapping between model experts and brain networks can facilitate the formulation of new hypotheses about brain function and potentially guide the design of future neuroscience experiments, especially those targeting non-language brain regions where suitable datasets are currently limited. The observed hierarchical routing patterns, for instance, offer a computational parallel to the sequential engagement of brain networks in human cognition.

**Improved Model Performance and Alignment:** The finding that brain-like specialization can be achieved without sacrificing, and in some cases even enhancing, performance on reasoning benchmarks is significant. This suggests that incorporating cognitive inductive biases can be a viable strategy for building more capable AI. The improved alignment with human behavioral patterns on tasks like COGBENCH indicates progress toward creating more human-like AI systems, which could have implications for human-AI interaction and the development of AI that understands and responds to human nuances more effectively.

**Efficiency and Scalability of Specialization:** The research demonstrates that meaningful functional specialization can be induced efficiently using a relatively small, curated dataset (`MICRO_SFT`) and maintained through subsequent large-scale training. This data-efficient approach to specialization is valuable for scaling modular architectures.

**Future Research Directions:** The work opens several avenues for future research. This includes exploring specialization beyond the four cognitive domains studied, such as abstract formal reasoning or intuitive physics. Further investigation into the optimal size and composition of the initial specialization dataset (`MICRO_SFT`) is warranted. A critical future direction involves examining whether the internal representations of these specialized experts align more closely with neural activity in their respective brain networks, which could deepen the understanding of both artificial and biological intelligence. Scaling the MICRO architecture to even larger base models and exploring additional post-training methods also remain important areas of exploration.
