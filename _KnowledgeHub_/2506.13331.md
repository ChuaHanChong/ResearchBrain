# Mixture of Cognitive Reasoners: Modular Reasoning with Brain-Like Specialization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from EPFL, MIT, and Harvard developed Mixture of Cognitive Reasoners (MICRO), a modular LLM architecture that explicitly incorporates specialized "expert" modules aligned with human brain networks. The model demonstrates interpretable, brain-like functional specialization, enhanced alignment with human behavior on the COGBENCH benchmark, and competitive performance on various reasoning tasks, enabling dynamic control over its cognitive processes.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) possess largely unstructured internal organizations, contrasting with the human brain's clear functional specialization across distinct cognitive networks.
- Existing LLMs, despite exhibiting implicit specialization, lack explicit interpretability and fine-grained controllability over their reasoning processes.
- Bridging the gap between AI models and human cognition requires architectures that not only perform well but also mechanistically align with human brain organization and behavior.

## Method

- A ==Mixture-of-Blocks (MOB) transformer architecture==, MICRO, is introduced, where each layer's entire transformer block is duplicated into four "expert" modules aligned with human cognitive networks (Language, Logic, Social, World).
- A ==three-stage training curriculum== induces specialization: experts are first primed on a small, pseudo-labeled dataset with deterministic token routing, then a router learns to assign tokens, and finally, the whole model is finetuned end-to-end on a large instruction dataset.
- An ==MLP-based router== dynamically assigns each token to a single expert module at every layer, enabling context-dependent activation of specialized reasoning paths.

## Results

- MICRO models exhibit clear domain-specific token routing and an emergent hierarchical processing structure, with router probabilities correlating with human behavioral judgments (e.g., social expert selectivity with mental state content, r=**0.7**).
- Mechanistic ablations confirmed the causal role of individual experts, with Logic expert ablation significantly reducing accuracy on math tasks and Social expert ablation sometimes improving performance on purely logical problems.
- The framework achieved superior alignment with human behavior on the COGBENCH benchmark and matched or outperformed non-modular and general-purpose modular baselines on various reasoning tasks including GSM8K, MATH, MMLU, and BBH.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating cognitive inductive biases into LLM design, coupled with a targeted training curriculum, effectively induces interpretable, brain-like functional specialization in models.
> - The Mixture-of-Blocks (MOB) architecture, where entire transformer blocks function as experts, is crucial for achieving clear and reliable specialization, particularly in larger models, outperforming traditional Mixture-of-Experts.
> - Functionally specialized modules in AI models offer a computational framework to test hypotheses about human brain network interactions and provide enhanced interpretability and dynamic control over AI reasoning.
