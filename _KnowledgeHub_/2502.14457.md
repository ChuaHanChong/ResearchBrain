# Watch Less, Feel More: Sim-to-Real RL for Generalizable Articulated Object Manipulation via Motion Adaptation and Impedance Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A robotic system is introduced that manipulates articulated objects by initially using vision for grasping, then relying on proprioceptive feedback and interaction history to adaptively control movements. This approach achieves 80-84% real-world success rates on unseen doors and drawers by opening them to 80% of their joint limits, with the majority of failures attributed to initial grasp pose estimation rather than the manipulation policy.

## Problem

- Manipulating articulated objects is challenging due to their hidden dynamic properties (e.g., pivot points, friction) that are difficult to ascertain without interaction.
- Traditional methods relying on continuous visual feedback suffer from the "vision sim-to-real gap," performing poorly in real environments due to lighting variations, occlusions, and visual noise.
- Existing impedance control implementations often use heuristic parameters, resulting in non-smooth or rigid motions unsuitable for delicate manipulation tasks.

## Method

- A "Watch Less, Feel More" approach that uses vision only for initial grasping, then relies on proprioceptive feedback and interaction history for adaptive manipulation.
- An online policy distillation framework with an adaptation module infers hidden object properties from a history of observation-action pairs without direct access to ground truth.
- Integrates learnable variable impedance control directly into the RL policy, allowing the robot to dynamically adjust its stiffness (compliance) based on learned behavior.

## Results

- Achieved 80% success for OpenDoor+ and 84% for OpenDrawer+ on unseen real-world objects in zero-shot transfer, opening them to 80% of their joint limits.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that policy distillation and variable impedance control are critical, with variable impedance improving OpenDoor+ success from 40% to 80% by preventing emergency stops.
- Most real-world failures (60-88%) occurred during the initial grasping stage due to limitations of off-the-shelf grasp pose estimation modules, rather than the manipulation policy itself.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous high-dimensional visual processing can be reduced, with initial vision and proprioceptive/history-based feedback proving sufficient for manipulation.
> - Learnable variable impedance control is crucial for safe and effective real-world contact-rich manipulation, preventing excessive forces and emergency stops.
> - Inferring hidden object properties from interaction history, rather than direct observation, enables adaptive and generalizable manipulation of objects with unknown dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Manipulating articulated objects like doors and drawers represents one of the most challenging problems in robotics. These objects have hidden dynamic properties—pivot points, friction coefficients, joint stiffness—that can only be discovered through physical interaction. Traditional approaches that rely heavily on continuous visual feedback struggle when transferred from simulation to real-world environments, primarily due to lighting variations, occlusions, and visual noise.

This paper by Do et al. introduces a paradigm shift: "Watch Less, Feel More." Rather than continuously processing high-dimensional visual data, their approach uses vision only initially to determine a grasping pose, then relies on proprioceptive feedback and interaction history to adaptively manipulate articulated objects. The method achieves remarkable real-world success rates of 80-84% on unseen objects when opening doors and drawers to 80% of their joint limits.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Current articulated object manipulation methods can be categorized into three main approaches, each with significant limitations:

**Affordance-based methods** use initial visual information to predict actionable parts and generate open-loop trajectories. While computationally efficient, these methods fail to adapt during execution when unexpected forces or object variations occur.

**RL-based methods with continuous vision feedback** employ reinforcement learning with ongoing visual input (RGB-D or point clouds). However, they suffer from the notorious "vision sim-to-real gap"—policies trained on simulated visual data perform poorly in real environments due to domain shift in visual features.

**Impedance control integration** attempts to address compliance issues but typically relies on heuristic parameters, resulting in jerky, non-smooth motions that are suboptimal for delicate manipulation tasks.

The fundamental insight of this work is that continuous high-dimensional visual processing may be unnecessary and even counterproductive for articulated object manipulation. Instead, the key information can be extracted from:
1. Initial visual assessment for grasping pose
2. Proprioceptive feedback during manipulation
3. Historical interaction patterns that reveal object properties

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Action and Observation Space Design

The proposed system operates with a carefully designed action and observation space that minimizes reliance on continuous vision while maximizing relevant information extraction.

The **action space** ($a_t \in \mathbb{R}^{11}$) includes:
- Target delta position ($\Delta_{xyz} \in \mathbb{R}^3$)
- Target 6D orientation ($R_t \in \mathbb{R}^6$)
- Gripper action ($G_t \in \mathbb{R}^1$)
- Impedance control parameter ($k_p \in \mathbb{R}^1$)

The **observation space** ($o_t \in \mathbb{R}^{30}$) focuses on low-dimensional, relevant information:
- Desired grasping pose from first-frame RGBD image
- Robot proprioception (joint configuration, end-effector pose)
- Robot-object relative distance
- Task-aware guidance (noisy pivot center, radius, hinge orientation)

During simulation training, "privileged observations" including ground-truth object properties like stiffness and mass are available, but these are not accessible during real-world deployment.

#### 3.2. Online Policy Distillation with History

The core innovation lies in the online policy distillation framework that enables the system to infer hidden object properties from interaction history. This approach consists of two neural network modules:

**Privileged Observation Encoder (φ)**: During simulation training, this shallow MLP learns to encode privileged observations (ground-truth object properties) into a latent representation $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{20}$.

**Adaptation Module (σ)**: This temporal architecture processes a history of 10 previous observation-action pairs to estimate the privileged information $\tilde{z}_t$ without access to ground truth.

The key insight is that object properties can be inferred from how the robot's actions affect the environment. For example, if small forces result in large object movements, the object likely has low friction. If the robot must exert significant force to achieve minimal opening, the object may have high joint stiffness.

Both modules are trained simultaneously using a supervision-regularization loss:
$$\mathcal{L} = \lambda\|z - \text{sg}[\tilde{z}]\|^2 + \|\text{sg}[z] - \tilde{z}\|^2$$

where $\text{sg}[\cdot]$ denotes stop-gradient operations to prevent the policy from becoming overly conservative during early training.

#### 3.3. Variable Impedance Control

Traditional position control can be too rigid for articulated object manipulation, potentially causing excessive forces that trigger emergency stops or damage equipment. The system integrates learnable variable impedance control directly into the RL policy.

The impedance controller follows a mass-spring-damper system:
$$M(\ddot{x}_c - \ddot{x}_d) + D(\dot{x}_c - \dot{x}_d) + K(x_c - x_d) = F_{ext}$$

The policy learns to predict the stiffness parameter $k_p$, which is scaled and clipped to form the diagonal stiffness matrix $K$. The damping matrix $D$ is derived using the critical damping condition $D = 2\sqrt{MK}$.

This approach allows the robot to dynamically adjust its compliance: becoming stiffer during reaching phases for precision and softer during interaction phases to accommodate object joint movement. The learned behavior emerges naturally from the RL training without explicit programming of when to be compliant versus rigid.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates significant advances in both simulation and real-world performance compared to existing methods.

**Simulation Performance**: The proposed method achieves success rates of 93-96% across all tasks, including challenging OpenDoor+/OpenDrawer+ tasks requiring 80% joint limit opening. Importantly, there is minimal performance drop between training and test sets, indicating strong generalization within the simulation environment.

**Real-world Transfer**: The most compelling results come from zero-shot transfer to real household objects using a Franka Emika robot:
- **80% success rate for OpenDoor+**
- **84% success rate for OpenDrawer+**

Analysis of failure cases reveals that most failures (60-88%) occurred during the initial grasping stage due to limitations in off-the-shelf grasp pose estimation modules (SAM, GSNet) rather than the manipulation policy itself. When successful grasps were achieved, the manipulation success rate increased to approximately 90%.

**Ablation Studies**: Critical insights emerge from systematic ablation studies:

*Without Policy Distillation*: Real-world success rate drops significantly (e.g., 80% to 62% for OpenDoor+), confirming the importance of learning from interaction history.

*Without Variable Impedance Control*: The most dramatic performance degradation occurs here (80% to 40% for OpenDoor+), with robots frequently triggering emergency stops due to excessive joint torques from rigid position control.

*Without Motion Regularization*: Moderate performance decrease (80% to 64%) with observed jerky motions leading to grasp failures.

*Without Domain Randomization*: Performance drops to 66%, highlighting the importance of diverse training environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Key Findings and Broader Implications

This research establishes several important principles for robotic manipulation:

**Reduced Vision Dependency**: The success of the "Watch Less, Feel More" paradigm demonstrates that continuous high-dimensional visual processing may be unnecessary for many manipulation tasks. This finding has significant implications for computational efficiency and robustness in varying visual conditions.

**Critical Role of Compliance**: The ablation studies unequivocally demonstrate that learnable variable impedance control is not merely beneficial but essential for safe real-world contact-rich manipulation. This challenges the common practice of treating compliance as a secondary consideration in RL-based manipulation.

**History-Based Adaptation**: The ability to infer object properties from interaction history opens new avenues for adaptive manipulation. This approach could generalize beyond articulated objects to other tasks requiring understanding of hidden environmental properties.

**Simulation-to-Reality Success**: Achieving 80-84% success rates on unseen real-world objects represents a significant advancement in sim-to-real transfer for complex manipulation tasks.

#### 5.2. Future Research Directions

The work opens several promising research directions:

**Multi-modal Integration**: While this work demonstrates success with reduced vision dependency, future research could explore optimal integration of tactile feedback, force sensing, and other modalities to further enhance object property inference.

**Broader Task Generalization**: The principles demonstrated here could extend to other manipulation tasks involving unknown object properties, such as tool use, container manipulation, or deformable object handling.

**Learning Efficiency**: Investigating how quickly these adaptive behaviors can be learned with limited real-world interaction could enable more practical deployment scenarios.

**Theoretical Understanding**: Developing formal frameworks for understanding when and why interaction history can substitute for continuous sensory input would provide valuable insights for robot learning more broadly.

This research represents a significant step toward more robust, generalizable robot manipulation systems that can operate effectively in unstructured real-world environments. By demonstrating that strategic reduction of sensory dependency, combined with adaptive learning from interaction, can achieve superior performance, it challenges conventional approaches and opens new pathways for practical robotic systems.
