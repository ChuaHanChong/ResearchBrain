# PhyCo: Learning Controllable Physical Priors for Generative Motion

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyCo endows video diffusion models with the ability to generate motion that is continuously controllable across physical properties like friction, restitution, and applied force. By combining a large-scale physically-grounded simulation dataset with VLM-guided reward optimization, the system significantly enhances physical realism and precise control, reducing directional error in force-conditioned control by over 60% compared to a baseline.

## Problem

- Current video generation models frequently fail to adhere to fundamental physical laws, leading to unrealistic object behaviors and interactions.
- Existing generative models lack continuous, interpretable, and fine-grained control over specific physical attributes like friction, restitution, deformation, or applied force.
- Integrating explicit physics simulators into generative processes often requires complex 3D geometry reconstruction or predefined material properties, limiting scalability and generalization.

## Method

- Developed a large-scale ==photorealistic simulation dataset== (>100,000 videos) with systematically varied physical parameters.
- Fine-tuned **Cosmos-Predict2-2B** using a ==ControlNet-style architecture== to condition denoising on ==spatially aligned, pixel-level physical property maps==.
- Introduced ==VLM-guided reward optimization== as a second training stage for semantic feedback on physical consistency.

## Results

- **PhyCo** outperformed SOTA video models on Physics-IQ, scoring **43.6** under 57-frame conditions.
- User study: **95.5%** preference for friction, **100%** for restitution over CogVideoX across physical attributes.
- Precise force-conditioned control: **15.2°** mean directional error vs. Force-Prompting's **40.5°**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training generative models with dense, pixel-level physical property maps, derived from diverse simulated data, enables continuous and interpretable control over complex motion dynamics.
> - Vision-Language Model (VLM)-guided reward optimization effectively refines the physical consistency and control fidelity of generated videos by providing semantic feedback during training.
> - Learning physical priors from carefully designed synthetic environments can generalize effectively to novel object categories, motion types, and appearance shifts in real-world videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern video generation models, particularly those based on diffusion, have demonstrated capabilities in synthesizing visual appearance, textures, lighting, and motion continuity. However, a limitation of these models is their frequent inability to adhere to fundamental physical laws, leading to phenomena such as objects drifting unnaturally, collisions lacking realistic rebound, and material responses being inconsistent with their intrinsic properties. Furthermore, despite the scale of data used in training foundational video diffusion models, controllable generation of variations in physical properties remains a challenge.

Prior research has addressed this gap through two primary approaches:
1. **Explicit Physics Simulation Integration**: Methods like PhysGen, PhysDreamer, and WonderPlay integrate explicit physics solvers (e.g., rigid-body dynamics, material point methods) with generative models. These approaches can achieve fine-grained motion coherence but typically necessitate reconstructed 3D geometry or predefined material properties during inference, which can limit their scalability and generalization to diverse real-world scenarios.
2. **Implicit Physical Control**: Approaches such as PhysCtrl, VLIPP, PISA, and ForcePrompting embed physical cues or priors directly into diffusion models without explicit simulators. These methods often leverage learned priors, vision-language reasoning, or language-driven prompts to guide motion. While they improve semantic consistency and avoid the inference-time overhead of simulators, they generally offer less continuous control over a broad range of specific physical properties. ForcePrompting, for instance, focuses on a single attribute (force direction).

The work presented in PhyCo positions itself as an advancement that combines strengths from both directions while addressing their respective limitations. It aims to endow generative video models with continuous, interpretable physical property conditioning. Unlike explicit simulation methods, PhyCo operates purely through generative modeling at inference time, avoiding the need for geometric reconstruction or simulator feedback. In contrast to existing implicit methods, PhyCo explicitly trains models to represent and manipulate a diversity of physical properties—friction, restitution, deformation, and applied force—thereby offering richer and more quantitative control. This approach seeks to bridge the gap between visual realism and physical consistency, providing a scalable path toward controllable, physically grounded generative video models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the PhyCo framework is to overcome the limitations of current video diffusion models regarding physical consistency and controllable physical attribute manipulation. While these models excel at visual synthesis, their generated content often violates basic physical laws, leading to unrealistic object behaviors. Humans possess an intuitive understanding of physics, effortlessly predicting how objects react to forces or interact with surfaces (e.g., sliding, bouncing, deforming). Replicating this capacity in artificial intelligence models is a core challenge in developing intelligent visual systems.

The specific objectives of the PhyCo research are detailed as follows:

1. **Introduce Continuous, Interpretable, and Physically Grounded Control**: The central objective is to enable generative video models to produce outputs where physical properties can be controlled continuously and in an interpretable manner. This means not just generating a video, but generating a video where, for example, the "bounciness" of an object can be smoothly varied from low to high, with the visual motion consistently reflecting these changes.

2. **Explicitly Represent and Manipulate Diverse Physical Properties**: The work aims to move beyond generic motion control to specific physical attributes. The authors identify and target four key physical properties: friction, restitution (bounciness), deformation, and applied force. The goal is to train a model that can directly manipulate these attributes to control object dynamics and interactions.

3. **Achieve Control Without Inference-Time Simulators or Geometry Reconstruction**: A crucial objective is to avoid the computational complexity and scalability limitations associated with explicit physics simulators or 3D geometry reconstruction during the video generation (inference) phase. PhyCo intends to embed physical understanding directly into the generative model itself, allowing it to produce physically consistent motion solely through its learned priors.

4. **Provide Quantitative Control Over Motion Behavior**: The framework seeks to offer a level of control that aligns with representations used in physics simulators. This enables direct supervision and interpretable manipulation, moving beyond qualitative consistency to a more precise, quantitative influence on generated physical behaviors.

5. **Enhance Physical Realism and Controllability**: A comparative objective is to demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly improves both the physical realism of generated videos and the fidelity of control over physical attributes, surpassing the performance of existing state-of-the-art video generation models.

6. **Ensure Generalization Beyond Synthetic Training Environments**: Given that the training data is derived from simulations, a critical objective is to ensure that the learned physical priors generalize effectively to novel scenarios, unseen object categories, and appearance shifts, including those characteristic of real-world videos. This generalization capability is vital for the practical applicability of the model.

In essence, the motivation is to advance video generation from purely visual fidelity to a more comprehensive understanding and controlled manipulation of the underlying physical world, making AI models more intelligent and their outputs more aligned with reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PhyCo framework integrates three main technical components to achieve its objectives: a new large-scale simulation dataset, physics-supervised fine-tuning of a diffusion model with ControlNet, and VLM-guided reward optimization.

#### 3.1. Physically Grounded Simulations (PhyCo Dataset)

The foundation of PhyCo is a custom-built, large-scale dataset comprising over 100,000 photorealistic simulation videos. This dataset was designed to provide physically meaningful supervision that generalizes beyond the training domain.
- **Design Principles**: The simulations focus on scenarios where the physical attribute of interest manifests clearly in visual motion, and the scenes are within the competence range of a pretrained diffusion backbone. Overly complex scenes were avoided to prevent unnecessary variance and accelerate learning.
- **Generation Framework**: Videos were generated using the Kubric framework, leveraging PyBullet for physics simulations and Blender for photorealistic rendering.
- **Scenario Diversity**: The dataset includes six controlled scenarios: a brick sliding, a ball rebounding, a vertically bouncing ball, a soft ball dropping, an object impacting a deformable body, and multiple balls colliding on a pool table. These scenarios expose the model to different dynamic regimes.
- **Physical Parameter Variation**: Within each scenario, four key physical parameters—friction, restitution, deformation (using a Neo-Hookean model), and applied force—are systematically varied across continuous ranges.
- **Visual Diversity**: To encourage the diffusion model to disentangle visual variations from underlying dynamics, object colors, surface materials, camera placements, and HDRI illuminations (50 environments) were randomized. High-quality textures from Polyhaven were used to enhance photorealism.
- **Data Outputs**: Each 4-second video (24 FPS, 432x768 resolution) is accompanied by synchronized depth maps, per-frame segmentation masks, and structured metadata detailing scene geometry, object material properties, applied physical parameters, and scene-level text descriptions.

#### 3.2. Physics-Supervised Fine-tuning

This stage fine-tuned a pretrained video diffusion model, Cosmos-Predict2-2B, to incorporate physical property conditioning.
- **Architecture**: A ControlNet-style architecture was employed to condition the denoising process. The base diffusion model and its tokenizer weights were kept frozen to preserve their pretrained representations, while only the ControlNet layers were fine-tuned.
- **Physical Property Encoding**: Physical properties are provided as spatially aligned, pixel-level property maps (circular blobs). These maps are normalized between [-1, 1]. They are decomposed into semantic groups:
 - Friction coefficient ($\mu_f$) and restitution coefficient ($e$).
 - Neo-Hookean deformation parameters ($d_\mu, d_\lambda, d_\gamma$).
 - Force magnitude ($F$) and direction (represented by $\cos \phi, \sin \phi$).
- **Conditioning Mechanism**: Each semantic group of property maps is tokenized by the Cosmos tokenizer and then linearly projected. An adapter network projects these tokenized embeddings to match the dimensionality of the diffusion backbone. Separate ControlNet branches are used for each semantic group, which facilitates faster training and promotes compositionality of various physical properties.
- **Optimization**: Training follows the diffusion score-matching objective, consistent with the Cosmos World Foundation Model, ensuring noise scheduling and temporal supervision across video frames. Each ControlNet branch is trained on a subset of the PhyCo dataset where its corresponding physical property is visually manifest.

#### 3.3. VLM-Guided Reward Optimization

The second stage of training refines the controllability and physical alignment of generated videos using feedback from a Vision-Language Model (VLM).
- **Rationale**: While supervised fine-tuning provides initial physical plausibility, it does not guarantee strong control fidelity. A VLM serves as a generalized critique model to evaluate physical consistency through targeted queries.
- **Inference-Time Proxy**: Standard single-step denoising during training is not ideal for VLM evaluation due to blurry outputs and implicit encoding of ground-truth trajectories. Instead, an N-step denoising rollout (specifically 10 steps) is performed to generate a predicted latent (ˆz0), which is then decoded into a video (ˆx0). This generated video more accurately represents the model's inference-time behavior.
- **VLM Fine-tuning**: A pre-existing VLM, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, was fine-tuned for 200 steps on the synthetic PhyCo dataset. Each video clip was paired with multiple physics-related questions (e.g., "Does the object move in the intended direction of force?"), achieving approximately 85% accuracy in this adapted task.
- **Physical Alignment Reward**: Each query is structured as a binary "Yes"/"No" question. For a generated video ˆx0, a reward is computed by comparing the VLM's logits for the correct and incorrect answer tokens, guided by ground-truth property ranges.
 - Multiple thresholded questions (e.g., "Is friction between X and Y?") are used for each attribute to provide dense feedback.
 - For directional consistency, a blue angular sector is overlaid on the video, and the VLM is queried if the motion lies within this region.
 - The VLM alignment loss is calculated as a binary cross-entropy over the logit difference between correct and incorrect responses.
- **Optimization**: In this stage, only the ControlNet layers corresponding to the physical property maps are fine-tuned, and the diffusion score-matching objective is excluded. The VLM loss is backpropagated end-to-end through the VLM, tokenizer, and DiT backbone. This focused optimization aims for more stable and physically consistent generations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PhyCo encompassed quantitative benchmarks, qualitative comparisons, user studies, and ablation experiments.

- **Quantitative Evaluation on Physics-IQ Benchmark**:
 - On the Physics-IQ benchmark, which assesses physical realism across Solid Mechanics, Fluid Dynamics, Optics, Magnetism, and Thermodynamics, PhyCo significantly outperformed several state-of-the-art video generation models (SVD-XT, LTX-Video-I2V, SG-I2V, Cogvideo-I2V-5B, Cosmos-Predict2-2B, VLIPP, Force-Prompting).
 - PhyCo, particularly with ControlNet and VLM Loss, achieved the highest Physics-IQ scores both when extrapolated to 120 frames (36.3) and when adhering to its 57-frame training conditions (43.6), demonstrating robust performance even with a train-test frame mismatch. This indicates that the physics-aware conditioning contributes to more realistic and coherent dynamics.

- **User Study on Physical Realism**:
 - A 2AFC (two-alternative forced choice) user study with 16 participants comparing 39 video pairs revealed a consistent preference for PhyCo's generations.
 - PhyCo showed higher preference percentages (e.g., 95.5% over CogVideoX for friction, 100% over CogVideoX for restitution, 82.2% over CogVideoX for deformation, and 91.1% over CogVideoX for force) compared to baselines across various physical attributes, indicating improved physical realism and clearer manifestation of controlled variations.

- **Ablation Study on PhyCo Data (Controllability)**:
 - An in-domain test set of 100 synthetic videos was used to quantitatively assess how well generated videos adhere to input physical properties. A fine-tuned VLM predicted properties from generated outputs.
 - Models trained with VLM-based reward optimization ("ControlNet + VLM Loss") demonstrated lower error rates across force magnitude, friction, force direction (angular deviation), restitution, and deformation compared to those without VLM optimization or text-only fine-tuning. This supports the effectiveness of the VLM reward formulation in enhancing controllability and faithfulness to physical conditioning. For instance, angular deviation in force direction decreased from 40.35 degrees (text-only) to 22.53 degrees (ControlNet + VLM).

- **Force Direction Adherence**:
 - When tested on 25 real-world videos, PhyCo achieved a mean directional error of 15.2° for force-conditioned control, substantially lower than Force-Prompting's 40.5°. This indicates more precise and reliable control over induced motion.

- **Qualitative Results**:
 - Representative examples illustrate PhyCo's ability to generate controllable behavior across friction, restitution, deformation, and external forces, as well as compositional control over multiple attributes simultaneously (e.g., force + friction).
 - Despite being trained on synthetic data, the model exhibited generalization to novel object categories, motion types, and appearance shifts in real-world scenes (e.g., a person jumping on a trampoline).
 - Qualitative comparisons with baselines showed PhyCo producing more desired motion, particularly in scenes with complex objects, and demonstrating coherent trajectories and physically plausible deformation (e.g., a pillow compressing under a kettlebell but not under paper).

- **Motion Consistency (Fréchet Video Motion Distance - FVMD)**:
 - FVMD evaluations on Physics-IQ benchmark videos showed that PhyCo achieved the best or second-best scores across most domains. Lower FVMD values signify more realistic motion statistics and temporal coherence.
 - The inclusion of VLM-based reward optimization consistently yielded further gains in FVMD, aligning with improvements observed in Physics-IQ scores.

- **Generalization Across Backbones**:
 - An additional experiment demonstrated that fine-tuning another video model (Wan2.2) with text-only conditioning on the PhyCo dataset resulted in a 4.6% improvement in its average Physics-IQ score. This indicates that the PhyCo dataset itself provides valuable physical priors, even without explicit ControlNet conditioning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PhyCo framework represents an advancement in controllable video generation by integrating physically grounded priors into diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several aspects:

- **Enhancing Physical Realism in Generative AI**: The work contributes to moving generative video models beyond purely aesthetic fidelity toward a more comprehensive understanding and representation of the physical world. This is relevant for applications requiring not just visually plausible, but also physically coherent, simulated scenarios.

- **Scalable and Efficient Physical Control**: A key impact lies in demonstrating a scalable method for incorporating physical priors without the need for complex, computationally intensive simulators or 3D geometry reconstruction during inference. This differentiates PhyCo from many physics-aware generative methods and could facilitate broader adoption in real-time or resource-constrained applications.

- **Interpretable and Fine-Grained Control**: By enabling continuous and interpretable control over specific physical attributes like friction, restitution, deformation, and force, PhyCo offers a more granular level of manipulation than previous methods. This interpretability aligns with how humans or physics engines define and manipulate properties, potentially simplifying user interaction and enabling precise scenario design.

- **Improved Generalization Capabilities**: The demonstrated ability of PhyCo to generalize from synthetic training data to diverse, unseen real-world scenarios, object categories, and appearance variations is crucial for the practical applicability of AI systems. It suggests that targeted synthetic data combined with sophisticated training can yield robust models for real-world dynamics.

- **Foundation for Future Intelligent Systems**: The development of models that can generate physically consistent and controllable motion is a step toward building more intelligent visual models that can reason about and interact with the physical world. This has implications for robotics, virtual reality, simulation for training AI agents, and content creation.

- **Advancement in Reward-Based Learning**: The successful application of VLM-guided reward optimization for physics-aware video generation extends the utility of this paradigm to new domains, providing a method to align generative models with complex, implicit physical behaviors through semantic feedback.

While PhyCo improves controllability and consistency, the generated dynamics remain an approximation. Future work could focus on extending the framework to more complex physical regimes (e.g., articulated motion, fluid-structure coupling, multi-contact dynamics), enforcing strict adherence to conservation laws, and strengthening real-world grounding. Addressing these areas could further enhance the model's robustness and applicability.
