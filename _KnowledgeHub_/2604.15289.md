# Abstract Sim2Real through Approximate Information States

> [!summary] Summary
> ASTRA tackles sim2real when the simulator is deliberately abstract, modeling the task at a coarser state space than the robot. It formalizes abstract sim2real as state abstraction inducing partial observability, then grounds the simulator with a GRU encoder trained under approximate-information-state losses: latent-dynamics NLL, reward prediction, abstract-state correction, plus MMD alignment of a target encoder. Yields 73% NAO navigation and 56% ball-kicking success, beating all baselines.

## Problem

- Standard sim2real assumes simulator and target share one state-action space and differ only in dynamics parameters, so system identification and randomization miss representational mismatch.
- Abstract simulators drop task-relevant detail, so abstract states break the Markov property and naive per-step dynamics correction against real data becomes ill-defined.
- No prior mathematical formalization of the abstract sim2real problem existed, blocking theoretically grounded grounding methods and leaving abstraction-level choices purely empirical.

## Method

- **ASTRA** grounds an abstract simulator with a shared ==GRU== backbone producing a history encoder, latent dynamics model, reward predictor, and abstract-state corrector applied to paired sim-real transitions.
- ==Approximate-information-state== losses combine latent-transition ==negative log-likelihood==, reward-prediction squared error, and abstract next-state correction error, weighted and optimized jointly rather than by prediction accuracy alone.
- A separate target history encoder maps real full-state histories into the same latent space, aligned by ==Maximum Mean Discrepancy== on latent transition distributions, then frozen for deployment.

## Results

- On physical NAO, ASTRA reaches **73%** navigation success versus **53%** for neural-augmented simulation, **50%** COMPASS, **27%** direct transfer, and **56%** ball-kicking versus **40%** best baseline.
- Ablation on Long Maze: full model **0.40** success, dropping reward loss falls to **0.29**, dropping latent-dynamics loss **0.35**, state-correction-only grounding **0.27**.
- Grounding survives morphology change: with Ant leg segments scaled **1.25×**, ASTRA holds **65%** U-Maze success against **21%** for direct transfer; gains plateau past **200** grounding trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State abstraction induces partial observability, so grounding corrections and policies must condition on histories rather than single abstract states to stay well-defined.
> - Shaping the recurrent latent with reward and self-prediction objectives beats optimizing next-state accuracy alone; reward prediction contributed most of the gain here.
> - Adaptation methods built for parametric uncertainty cannot close representational gaps, and grounding data saturates quickly, so collect modest real data and spend effort on abstraction level.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated success across various domains, particularly in robotics when high-fidelity simulators are available. These simulators offer a cost-effective and safe environment for policy training compared to real-world data collection, which is often expensive, time-consuming, and prone to safety concerns. The traditional approach to transferring policies from simulation to the real world (Sim2Real) typically addresses "parametric mismatch," where the simulator and the target real-world domain share the same state-action space but differ in underlying physical parameters (e.g., friction, mass). Methods like domain randomization, system identification, and learned dynamics corrections have been developed to bridge this "reality gap" by adapting to these parametric discrepancies.

However, constructing high-fidelity simulators becomes increasingly challenging as robotic tasks become more complex and operate in diverse environments. Such detailed simulators can be difficult to specify and maintain, and their reliance may lead to policies that overfit to simulator-specific dynamics. An emerging area of research explores the utility of simpler, more abstract simulators. These abstract simulators can significantly accelerate experimentation and simplify the modeling process, potentially making RL development more accessible. Despite the recognized potential benefits of abstract simulators, their use introduces a more fundamental challenge: the simulator might operate over a different, reduced, or "abstracted" state representation compared to the real-world system. This difference implies a structural mismatch beyond mere parametric variations.

Prior work has explored the use of abstraction in Sim2Real, demonstrating benefits such as training high-level policies in abstract state representations, aiding transfer in autonomous driving, and improving visual navigation by training in grid simulators. Some studies even suggest that lower-fidelity simulators can lead to better transfer. While these works highlight the practical advantages, a formal mathematical framework for the "abstract Sim2Real problem" has been largely absent. The existing theory of state abstraction in RL often focuses on model-based RL, but abstract Sim2Real begins with a pre-specified simulator rather than learning a model from scratch. Furthermore, state abstraction is known to induce partial observability, a critical factor for which existing Sim2Real grounding methods are not designed. This research aims to formalize this abstract Sim2Real problem and develop a method that explicitly addresses the challenges posed by partial observability induced by state abstraction, leveraging real-world data to adapt abstract simulator dynamics for successful policy transfer.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether real-world data can be effectively used to modify an abstract simulator, enabling successful Sim2Real transfer of RL-trained behaviors to a real robot. This overarching question is driven by several motivations and challenges:

1. **Addressing the limitations of high-fidelity simulators:** As robots are deployed in increasingly complex and large-scale domains, developing and maintaining fast and accurate high-fidelity simulators becomes difficult and resource-intensive. Such simulators often fail to model all relevant real-world details, leading to a "reality gap" that is difficult to bridge with traditional Sim2Real methods.
2. **Leveraging the advantages of abstract simulators:** Abstract simulators, by design, simplify the modeling process and can significantly speed up RL training and experimentation. They offer a more accessible and efficient pathway for developing RL policies. However, this simplification carries the risk of omitting critical task-relevant dynamics present in the real world, potentially leading to policies that fail upon transfer.
3. **Formalizing the abstract Sim2Real problem:** Before this work, the abstract Sim2Real problem lacked a formal mathematical description in the literature. This absence hinders the development of theoretically grounded methods specifically tailored to address the challenges of abstraction. A formalization is necessary to identify the fundamental properties and difficulties inherent in transferring policies from an abstract simulator.
4. **Overcoming partial observability induced by abstraction:** The core challenge identified by this research is that state abstraction generally induces partial observability. When a simulator operates on a reduced state space (e.g., a 2D point-mass model for a bipedal robot), important details about the robot's internal state or environment that exist in the real world (e.g., joint configurations, balance, detailed contact forces) are lost.
 - **Impact on policy learning:** A policy trained solely on the abstract state might lack crucial information for optimal control in the real world. For instance, an abstract state representing position and velocity cannot distinguish between a stable robot and one about to fall, both mapping to the same abstract state.
 - **Impact on simulator grounding:** Standard simulator grounding methods assume that the Markov property holds for the simulator's state-action space. However, when states are abstracted, the Markov property may no longer hold for the abstract states in the real world. This means that the next abstract state might depend not just on the current abstract state and action, but also on the history of previous abstract states and actions. Consequently, directly applying methods that attempt to correct P(s'|s,a) for an abstract simulator based on real-world data becomes ill-defined and ineffective.

To address these motivations, the paper aims to provide the first formalization of the abstract Sim2Real problem using state abstraction from RL theory. This formalization identifies that history-based grounding and policy learning are crucial to mitigate the effects of induced partial observability. Building on this insight, the research is motivated to develop a novel method, ASTRA (Augmented Simulation with self-predicTive abstRAction), which uses a limited amount of real-world data to ground an abstract simulator by learning history-conditioned corrections and policies. The objective is for ASTRA to implicitly account for unmodeled real-world dynamics, thereby enabling successful policy transfer.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The methodology of this research proceeds in two main stages: formalizing the abstract Sim2Real problem and developing a novel grounding method, ASTRA.

**A. Problem Formalization: Abstract Sim2Real**
The paper first formalizes the problem using the framework of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs).
- **Target Domain (Real World):** Represented by an MDP $M_t = (S_t, A, P_t, r_t, \gamma)$, where $S_t$ is the full real-world state space.
- **Source Domain (Abstract Simulator):** Represented by an MDP $M_s = (S_s, A, P_s, r_s, \gamma)$, where $S_s$ is an abstract state space, deliberately reduced from $S_t$. The paper considers two types of abstract state spaces: compression to a finite set of discrete abstract states or $S_s$ being a subspace of $S_t$.
- **Abstraction Mapping:** A known function $\phi: S_t \rightarrow S_s$ maps real-world states to abstract simulator states. This mapping is also applied to histories, $\Phi: H_t \rightarrow H_s$.
- **Action Space:** The abstract simulator and real world are assumed to share the same action space $A$. If they differ, an action transformation function (e.g., a low-level policy) is assumed to be available.
- **Objective:** To learn a policy $\pi$ using RL in $M_s$ that maximizes the expected return $J_{M_t}(\pi)$ in the real world.

The formalization highlights two primary challenges beyond standard Sim2Real:
1. **Partial Observability:** The state abstraction $\phi$ induces partial observability. A policy $\pi: S_s \rightarrow \Delta(A)$ trained on abstract states may miss critical information available in $S_t$ required for optimal control. The example of a NAO robot with a 2D point-mass simulator is used to illustrate this: identical abstract states (position, velocity) might correspond to vastly different real-world physical configurations (stable vs. unsteady robot).
2. **Failure of Markov Property:** For abstract states in the target domain, the Markov property typically fails to hold. That is, $Pr(s^s_{i+1}|s^s_i, a_i) \neq Pr(s^s_{i+1}|s^s_i, a_i, s^s_{i-1}, a_{i-1})$. This means the future abstract state depends not only on the current abstract state and action but also on the history of previous states and actions. Consequently, standard simulator grounding methods that attempt to match Markovian transitions ($P_s(s^s_{i+1}|s^s_i, a_i)$ to $Pr(s^s_{i+1}|s^s_i, a_i)$ in the target domain) become ill-defined and ineffective. The key insight derived from this formalization is the necessity of history-based methods for simulator grounding and policy learning to account for the induced partial observability.

**B. ASTRA: Augmented Simulation with Self-Predictive Abstraction**
To address the challenges identified by the formalization, the paper introduces ASTRA. ASTRA is designed to use real-world data to align the abstract simulator's dynamics with the real world's dynamics, explicitly accounting for partial observability through history-based processing.
ASTRA augments the abstract simulator with a learned latent dynamics model that preserves task-relevant information through self-predictive constraints. The approach employs three neural components, which share a common recurrent backbone (a GRU in the implementation):
1. **Source history encoder ($\psi_s: H_s \rightarrow Z$):** Maps abstract state-action histories to a latent space $Z$. This encoder produces the latent representation $z^s_i = \psi_s(\Phi(h^t_i))$ that serves as the state for both simulator grounding and policy learning.
2. **Latent dynamics model ($P_{lat}: Z \times A \rightarrow \Delta(Z)$):** Predicts a distribution over the next latent state given the current latent state and action. It outputs parameters of a Gaussian distribution (mean $\mu_i$, log variance $\sigma^2_i$) for $z^s_{i+1}$.
3. **Reward predictor ($R_{pred}: Z \times A \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$):** Estimates rewards from the target environment.
4. **Abstract state predictor ($f_{abs}: Z \times A \times S_s \rightarrow \tilde{S}_s$):** Predicts the next abstract state, correcting the simulator's prediction.

**Training ASTRA (Simulator Grounding - Algorithm 1):**
ASTRA is trained using paired trajectories collected from the target environment. For each real-world transition $(s^t_i, a_i, s^t_{i+1})$, the abstract simulator is reset to $\phi(s^t_i)$ and the same action $a_i$ is executed to get its prediction. The history abstraction operator $\Phi$ constructs abstract histories that reflect real-world dynamics.
The training objective combines three complementary loss functions, inspired by Approximate Information States (AIS) and self-predictive abstractions, to ensure $z^s_i$ contains sufficient information for grounding and policy learning:
- **Latent Dynamics Prediction Loss ($L_{trans}$):** Minimizes the negative log-likelihood of $z^s_{i+1}$ given the predicted distribution from $P_{lat}(z^s_i, a_i)$. This drives $P_{lat}$ to mirror real-world transitions in the latent space: $L_{trans} = - \sum_i \log N(z^s_{i+1} | \mu_i, \sigma^2_i)$.
- **Reward Prediction Loss ($L_{rew}$):** Minimizes the squared error between the predicted reward $R_{pred}(z^s_i, a_i)$ and the actual real-world reward $r^t_i$. This ensures the latent representation can predict task-relevant rewards: $L_{rew} = \sum_i \|R_{pred}(z^s_i, a_i) - r^t_i\|^2$.
- **Abstract State Correction Loss ($L_{abs}$):** Minimizes the squared error between the corrected abstract next state $f_{abs}(z^s_i, a_i, s^s_{i+1})$ and the true abstract next state observed in the real world $\phi(s^t_{i+1})$. This directly corrects the abstract simulator's dynamics: $L_{abs} = \sum_i \|f_{abs}(z^s_i, a_i, s^s_{i+1}) - \phi(s^t_{i+1})\|^2$.
The total training objective is $L = \lambda_1 L_{trans} + \lambda_2 L_{rew} + \lambda_3 L_{abs}$, with all parameters of the shared GRU backbone and output heads optimized jointly.

**Target Environment Abstraction (for policy deployment):**
For deploying policies in the real world, ASTRA learns a separate target encoder $\psi_t: H_t \rightarrow Z$ that maps full real-world histories into the same latent space $Z$ as $\psi_s$. This alignment is achieved by minimizing an MMD (Maximum Mean Discrepancy) loss, $L_{align} = D(p^s_i, p^t_i)$, where $p^s_i = P(z^s_{i+1}|z^s_i, a_i)$ and $p^t_i = P(z^t_{i+1}|z^t_i, a_i)$. During this alignment, $\psi_s, P_{lat}, R_{pred}$ are frozen, and only $\psi_t$ is updated. Once aligned, $\psi_t$ is fixed for real-world policy execution.

**Policy Learning with ASTRA-Grounded Simulator (Algorithm 2):**
After grounding, policies are trained in the augmented abstract simulator using any standard RL algorithm (e.g., PPO or SAC used in experiments). During policy learning, the simulator's state transitions are modified:
1. The current abstract history $h^s_i$ is encoded into a latent state $z^s_i$ using $\psi_s$.
2. The policy $\pi$ takes $z^s_i$ as input to sample an action $a_i$.
3. The next latent state $z^s_{i+1}$ is sampled from the learned latent dynamics $P_{lat}(z^s_i, a_i)$.
4. A reward $\hat{r}_i$ is estimated using $R_{pred}(z^s_i, a_i)$.
5. The action $a_i$ is executed in the abstract simulator to get an initial next state $s^s_{i+1}$.
6. This $s^s_{i+1}$ is then corrected by $f_{abs}(z^s_i, a_i, s^s_{i+1})$ to obtain the final simulator next state.
7. The abstract history $h^s_i$ is updated with the new state and action.
This process effectively trains a policy in a simulator whose dynamics and rewards are implicitly corrected by real-world data and history-based reasoning.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presented empirical studies across sim2sim and sim2real tasks to answer three main questions: (i) efficacy of history-based grounding with recurrent policies, (ii) impact of abstraction level on grounding vs. domain randomization, and (iii) benefits of self-predictive state representation over simple abstract state prediction.

**A. Legged Robot Navigation - Sim2Sim (AntMaze)**
- **Setup:** Transfer from a 2D point-mass abstract simulator to AntMaze (quadruped locomotion) with a substantial abstraction gap. Abstract state: (location, velocity); Target state: 29-dimensional (torso pose, joint angles/velocities). Low-level controller maps abstract velocity commands to joint torques. Evaluated on U-Maze and Long Maze.
- **Results (Figure 3):**
 - **ASTRA's Performance:** ASTRA achieved the highest success rate in both U-Maze (0.90) and Long Maze (0.40), demonstrating its effectiveness for abstract Sim2Real.
 - **Baselines:** Direct Transfer (DT) and most domain randomization (DR, COMPASS) methods performed poorly, often resulting in immediate collisions due to exploiting simplified simulator dynamics.
 - **Parametric Adaptation vs. Abstraction:** Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), designed for parametric uncertainty, performed between DR and COMPASS, confirming that methods for parametric mismatch cannot fully address abstraction gaps.
 - **Direct Learning & Fine-tuning:** Directly training with IQL on target data showed limited performance on complex mazes. Simulator pretraining followed by IQL fine-tuning (DT+IQL) approached NAS but with higher variance, suggesting that explicit dynamics grounding outperforms fine-tuning.
 - **Robustness:** ASTRA policy trained on a standard Ant achieved 65% success rate on U-Maze when evaluated on a morphologically varied Ant (1.25x leg length), compared to 21% for direct transfer, indicating robustness to morphological variations.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 2):** Policies trained without grounding (DT, DR) exploited simplified dynamics, leading to aggressive paths that failed in the real environment. History-based grounding (NAS, ASTRA) enabled policies to learn safer navigation patterns, implicitly accounting for quadruped locomotion dynamics. ASTRA learned policies that identified and navigated high-risk regions where abstract and real dynamics diverged most.

**B. Humanoid Locomotion - Sim2Sim (RL Humanoid Benchmark)**
- **Setup:** Evaluated across three abstraction levels for a humanoid robot:
 - **Walker2D (Most Abstract):** Upper body as rigid link, observations reduced to leg positions, joint angles, foot contacts; omits arm, torso, CoM. 4-dimensional action space for specific leg joints.
 - **Kinematics:** Full humanoid morphology, observations include all joint positions/velocities; same 4-dimensional action space; abstracts force-level dynamics.
 - **Extended Kinematics:** Augments Kinematics with CoM and translational velocity; same 4-dimensional action space.
- **Results (Figure 5):**
 - **Impact of Abstraction Level:** Abstraction level critically impacted transfer success. In the highly abstract Walker2D setting, DT, DR, and COMPASS terminated quickly (within 63 timesteps). NAS showed improvement. ASTRA achieved the best performance (score 2185), maintaining balance longest.
 - **Fine-tuning Trends:** DT+IQL fine-tuning achieved strong performance in Walker2D (score 1950), approaching direct IQL training on target data. However, as simulator fidelity increased (Kinematics and Extended Kinematics), fine-tuning gains diminished, while ASTRA's advantage over DT+IQL grew.
 - **Increased Fidelity:** Full-body Kinematics stabilized all algorithms and narrowed the reality gap, with COMPASS (score 3635) outperforming NAS (score 3140) in this setting. Extended Kinematics brought smaller gains, with NAS (score 3470) and COMPASS (score 3660) approaching the target-trained upper bound, while ASTRA (score 4145) maintained its lead across all abstraction levels.
- **Conclusions:** The results suggest that retaining essential information during abstraction is effective for transfer, and when abstraction is severe, ASTRA's self-predictive grounding is most effective.

**C. Real Robot Evaluation (NAO Bipedal Robot)**
- **Setup:** Physical NAO robot, highly abstract simulators. NAO presents challenges like imprecise odometry, foot slippage, actuator delays, camera noise.
 - **NAO Navigation:** Abstract simulator as 2D point mass with velocity control. Real NAO uses walk engine, onboard localization. Navigate physical maze to goal zone.
 - **NAO Ball-Kicking:** Abstract simulator as 2D point agent with simplified ball physics. Real-world ball tracking from noisy onboard perception. Kick ball into goal within 30s.
- **Results (Table I):**
 - **NAO Navigation:** ASTRA achieved a 73% success rate, significantly outperforming all baselines (17%–53%). ASTRA's policies, while traveling slightly farther on successful trials (12.33m vs. 9.30-12.70m for others), were more conservative and collision-avoiding, leading to higher consistency. Baselines often failed by attempting to take corners too tightly.
 - **NAO Ball-Kicking:** ASTRA achieved a 56% success rate, substantially outperforming all baselines (5%–40%). NAS (37%) showed improvement over DT and DR but fell short of ASTRA, indicating ASTRA's better handling of camera noise and contact uncertainty.
- **Conclusions:** ASTRA enabled successful transfer to real hardware, implicitly accounting for low-level robot dynamics and real-world uncertainties.

**D. Component Analysis (Long Maze)**
- **Setup:** Ablation study on ASTRA's loss components ($L_{trans}$, $L_{rew}$, $L_{abs}$) on the Long Maze navigation task. Using $L_{abs}$ alone is equivalent to NAS.
- **Results (Table II):**
 - **Full ASTRA:** 0.40 success rate.
 - **Without $L_{rew}$:** Dropped to 0.29 success rate with doubled variance, indicating the critical role of task-relevant reward signals for stable, control-oriented representations.
 - **Without $L_{trans}$:** Dropped to 0.35 success rate with stable variance, suggesting self-predictive dynamics provide complementary regularization.
 - **NAS (only $L_{abs}$):** 0.27 success rate.
- **Conclusions:** Both $L_{trans}$ and $L_{rew}$ auxiliary objectives contributed significantly to ASTRA's performance beyond pure state prediction, with reward prediction playing a dominant role.

**E. Data Efficiency Analysis (PointMaze-to-AntMaze)**
- **Setup:** Evaluated ASTRA's data efficiency against NAS across six dataset sizes (25% to 150% of 200 trajectories baseline) on the Long Maze task.
- **Results (Figure 6):**
 - **Diminishing Returns:** Performance showed clear diminishing returns with increased dataset size. ASTRA's steepest improvement occurred between 25% and 75% of the baseline dataset, with performance plateauing beyond 100%. This trend also held for NAS, albeit at lower absolute performance.
 - **Sufficiency:** Doubling the dataset from 75% (150 trajectories) to 150% (300 trajectories) yielded less than 10% improvement in success rate. Performance stability emerged around 100% data, suggesting it provided sufficient state space coverage.
- **Conclusions:** ASTRA is data-efficient, achieving most of its performance gains with a relatively small amount of real-world data.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic reinforcement learning by addressing the fundamental challenge of transferring policies from abstract simulators to the real world. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Formalization of Abstract Sim2Real:** By providing a mathematical formalization of the abstract Sim2Real problem using state abstraction theory, the paper establishes a theoretical foundation for future research in this domain. This formalization explicitly identifies the induction of partial observability as a core consequence of abstraction, which is a critical insight for developing appropriate transfer methods.
2. **Development of History-Based Grounding:** The identification that abstraction induces partial observability necessitates history-based approaches. ASTRA, with its recurrent neural network architecture and self-predictive loss functions, offers a method to implicitly account for unmodeled real-world dynamics by learning from state-action histories. This moves beyond traditional Sim2Real methods that primarily address parametric mismatches.
3. **Enhanced Sim2Real Transfer Performance:** The empirical results across multiple complex tasks (AntMaze navigation, humanoid locomotion, NAO robot navigation, and ball-kicking) consistently demonstrate ASTRA's superior performance compared to various baselines, including direct transfer, domain randomization, and other neural grounding methods. This indicates that ASTRA effectively bridges the reality gap caused by abstraction, leading to higher success rates and more robust policies in real-world environments.
4. **Enabling the Use of Simpler Simulators:** The success of ASTRA suggests that researchers and practitioners may not always need to develop highly complex and accurate simulators. By effectively leveraging abstract simulators and grounding them with real-world data, the barrier to entry for using RL in robotics could be lowered. This can lead to faster iteration cycles, reduced development costs, and broader applicability of RL to new robotic tasks and platforms where high-fidelity simulation is impractical.
5. **Data Efficiency:** The data efficiency analysis indicates that ASTRA can achieve substantial performance gains with a relatively limited amount of real-world data. This is crucial for robotics, where real-world data collection is expensive and time-consuming.
6. **Insights into Abstraction Levels:** The humanoid locomotion experiments provide insights into how different levels of abstraction impact transfer success and the relative effectiveness of various grounding methods. This can guide future decisions on simulator design and the choice of Sim2Real strategies.
7. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies limitations, such as the need to determine acceptable abstraction levels and the assumption of a known state mapping. These limitations provide clear avenues for future research, including investigating methods for discovering state mappings or unifying analyses across a broader spectrum of simulator fidelities.
8. **Broader Impact on Robot Autonomy:** By enabling robots to learn effective policies from abstract models, this work aligns with the idea that generalizable autonomy might benefit from abstract, lower-fidelity simulations. This could pave the way for more flexible and adaptable robotic systems capable of operating in diverse and unpredictable real-world scenarios.

In summary, this work advances the theoretical understanding and practical implementation of Sim2Real transfer, particularly for scenarios involving abstract simulators, which are increasingly relevant for deploying RL in complex real-world robotics.
