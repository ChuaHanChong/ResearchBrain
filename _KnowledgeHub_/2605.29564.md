# VE2VF: Vision-Enabled to Vision-Free Distillation via Real-world Reinforcement Learning for Robust Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at TU Wien developed VE2VF, a real-world human-in-the-loop reinforcement learning framework that distills a vision-enabled teacher policy into a vision-free student for robust contact-rich manipulation. This approach achieved a 95.0% overall success rate across various tasks, including those with visual disturbances and unseen geometries, demonstrating superior generalization compared to vision-enabled baselines which failed on out-of-distribution tasks.

## Problem

- Contact-rich manipulation tasks are challenging due to complex, discontinuous dynamics and the need for high precision and adaptability in uncertain environments.
- Vision-enabled Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies tend to overfit to specific visual conditions, leading to brittleness and poor generalization when environmental appearances change.
- Training robust vision-free policies from scratch using only proprioceptive inputs for complex contact-rich tasks is sample-inefficient and difficult without visual guidance.

## Method

- A two-stage, real-world Human-in-the-Loop ==Reinforcement Learning== (HIL-RL) framework, **VE2VF**, is used, incorporating a teacher-student distillation process.
- A vision-enabled teacher policy is trained first using rich multimodal inputs (images + proprioceptive state) and HIL-RL to efficiently acquire expert reactive behaviors.
- The knowledge from the teacher is then distilled into a vision-free student policy that relies solely on proprioceptive inputs (pose, twist, wrench), utilizing task-relative pose representations to enhance robustness and prevent spatial overfitting.

## Results

- VE2VF achieved the highest overall success rate of **95.0%** across all evaluated task categories, including disturbed training tasks (**100%** success) and unseen out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., **100%** on USB insertion).
- The vision-enabled teacher policy, while achieving **100%** success on training tasks, completely failed on all out-of-distribution tasks (**0%** success) and significantly degraded under disturbed visual conditions.
- Fine-tuning the VE2VF policy for a challenging task (DSUB insertion) with distillation for 10 minutes resulted in **100%** success, whereas fine-tuning without distillation yielded **0%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-enabled policies, while effective for initial skill acquisition, exhibit poor generalization and completely fail when visual conditions deviate from training or tasks are out-of-distribution.
> - Distilling an expert vision-enabled teacher into a vision-free student effectively transfers robust reactive behaviors, overcoming visual overfitting while retaining the benefits of efficient visually-guided exploration during training.
> - Combining all proprioceptive modalities (pose, twist, wrench) is crucial for the robustness of the distilled vision-free policy, as learning from proprioception alone without distillation proves inefficient and prone to failure.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly for contact-rich tasks like assembly, presents significant challenges due to the need for high precision, accuracy, and adaptability in uncertain and diverse environments. Traditional model-based approaches often struggle with the inherent difficulty of accurately modeling contact dynamics, where even minor errors can lead to instability.

Learning-based approaches have gained prominence as an alternative. Imitation Learning (IL) offers data efficiency, allowing policies to be bootstrapped from human demonstrations. However, IL policies are typically constrained to the distribution of demonstration data and often lack the generalization and adaptation capabilities required for novel situations, especially in contact-rich tasks where small deviations can lead to failure. Reinforcement Learning (RL) allows policies to improve through trial and error. A common strategy involves training RL agents in simulators to generate extensive data; however, this introduces a "sim-to-real" gap, which is particularly pronounced for contact interactions involving complex friction, deformation, and surface properties. Deploying model-free RL directly on hardware, while avoiding the sim-to-real gap, typically demands substantial interaction time and careful consideration for safe exploration, making it less practical for real-world scenarios.

Human-in-the-loop RL (HIL-RL) has emerged as a more sample-efficient paradigm for real-world training, leveraging human demonstrations and interventions to provide reward signals and guide exploration. Many HIL-RL implementations utilize vision as a key input modality due to the rich perceptual information it provides. However, a significant limitation of vision-enabled policies is their tendency to overfit to the specific visual conditions encountered during training. This dependence on environmental appearance restricts their robustness and transferability, as changes in lighting, object textures, or backgrounds can degrade performance.

This research addresses this specific limitation. While previous work in teacher-student distillation in robotics often aims to transfer knowledge from a privileged teacher to a more limited student, potentially accepting a performance trade-off, this paper's motivation differs. Here, visual input is hypothesized to be a potential distractor from the fundamental force and geometric relationships governing success in contact-rich manipulation. The work aims to shed this potentially unreliable visual modality while retaining its benefits for accelerated exploration during the training phase. By combining HIL-RL with a cross-modal distillation framework, the paper seeks to achieve robust, vision-free policies that generalize across various task variants without relying on domain randomization or extensive data augmentation. This approach distinguishes itself from other proprioceptive RL methods that can suffer from longer training times or rely on extensive reward shaping.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable control framework for contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks, trained entirely in the real world. Specifically, the authors aim to combine the benefits of efficient, visually-guided learning with the robustness and invariance of policies that rely solely on proprioceptive and force-torque sensing.

This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **Challenges in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Robotic assembly and other contact-rich tasks are inherently difficult. They require precise control, are characterized by discontinuous dynamics, and involve complex force interactions, making them challenging for both traditional model-based and many learning-based approaches.
2. **Limitations of Vision-Enabled Policies:** While visual information can accelerate learning in RL by providing task-relevant context, vision-enabled policies are prone to overfitting to the specific visual conditions present during training. This overfitting leads to brittleness and poor generalization when environmental appearances (e.g., lighting, textures, backgrounds) change, limiting their real-world applicability and robustness.
3. **Inefficiency of Vision-Free Learning from Scratch:** Training policies directly using only proprioceptive inputs (pose, twist, wrench) for complex contact-rich tasks can be challenging. Such policies may suffer from partial observability, and exploration without visual guidance can be difficult and sample-inefficient, leading to prolonged real-world training times.
4. **Desire for Real-World, Generalizable Solutions:** The goal is to develop policies that are robust to real-world variability and can generalize to unseen task variants without relying on simulated training or extensive domain randomization, which can introduce their own complexities and sim-to-real challenges. The authors specifically aim for efficient adaptivity to diverse parts, poses, and environments, as is necessary in high-mix, low-volume assembly settings.
5. **Leveraging Human-in-the-Loop Efficiency:** Human-in-the-loop Reinforcement Learning (HIL-RL) has demonstrated effectiveness in reducing the sample interaction time required for real-world training. The authors intend to build upon this efficiency while addressing the generalization limitations commonly associated with vision-dependent HIL-RL policies.
6. **Addressing Vision as a Potential Distractor:** The authors hypothesize that for contact-rich manipulation, visual input, despite its apparent richness, can sometimes act as a distractor. They suggest that the essential physical relationships governing task success are more directly captured by force, torque, and geometric (pose) information. Therefore, a motivation is to distill the acquired skill into a policy that is grounded in these physical modalities, making it invariant to superficial visual changes.

By addressing these motivations, the authors propose a framework that combines the exploration benefits of vision during training with the robustness of vision-free policies at deployment, thereby offering a practical solution for generalizable contact-rich manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named VE2VF (Vision-Enabled to Vision-Free), is a real-world human-in-the-loop reinforcement learning (HIL-RL) framework that employs a two-stage teacher-student distillation process, with an optional third stage for fine-tuning. The entire training occurs directly on real hardware.

**A. MDP Definition:**
The robotic contact-rich task is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **State Observation Space (S):**
 - For vision-enabled policies: Consists of images from `n` cameras (`I_t`) and a proprioceptive state vector (`x_prop_t`). `x_prop_t` includes end-effector pose (`p_t`), velocity (`v_t`), force (`f_t`), and torque (`τ_t`), all expressed in the end-effector frame.
 - For vision-free policies: Only the proprioceptive state `x_prop_t` is used.
- **Action Space (A):** The robot action `a_t` is an end-effector pose displacement (`Δp_t`) in its local coordinate frame. These commands are executed via a low-level impedance controller, and `Δp_t` is clipped to maintain the robot within a safe region.
- **Reward Function (R):** A sparse binary reward `r_t` is used, assigning 1 upon task success (determined by a classifier `C(s_t)`) and 0 otherwise. This avoids the need for task-specific reward shaping.
- **Initial State Distribution:** Each episode starts from a random pose within the action space.

**B. Policy Optimization (Soft Actor-Critic - SAC):**
Policies are learned using the off-policy maximum entropy RL algorithm, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), known for its sample efficiency in real-world training.
- **Critic Update:** Minimizes temporal difference error using a replay buffer, incorporating both reward and the soft value of the next state.
- **Actor Update:** Maximizes the expected Q-value while maintaining sufficient entropy for exploration, balancing exploitation and exploration.

**C. Human-in-the-Loop Reinforcement Learning (HIL-RL):**
The training leverages HIL-SERL, an SAC variant (RLPD) that symmetrically samples transitions from both policy-generated data (`D_P`) and human-generated data (`D_H`). Human data consists of:
1. **Offline Demonstrations:** Initial collection of human demonstrations.
2. **Online Interventions:** Human teleoperation to correct robot actions during online policy execution, providing immediate guidance.

**D. Training Task Selection:**
A small, representative set of contact-rich manipulation tasks with distinct geometrical profiles and interaction stiffnesses are chosen. Policies are co-trained on these tasks by cycling through them during episodes. For this work, three tasks from the NIST Assembly Board I (medium peg, ethernet, medium gear) were used for training.

**E. VE2VF Framework Stages (Fig. 2):**

1. **Stage I: Vision-Enabled Teacher Training (π_t):**
 - A vision-enabled teacher policy (`π_t`) is trained using the HIL-RL framework.
 - **Observations:** Full multimodal input (`o_t = [I_t, x_t]`), where `I_t` are camera images and `x_t` is the proprioceptive state. The pose `p` within `x_t` is represented *relative to the initial pose of the observed episode* (`T_p0(p)`), enabling reactive visual-servoing behavior.
 - **Training Objective:** The teacher is trained until it achieves 100% success rates on the selected training tasks.
 - **Purpose:** To establish an expert prior policy that benefits from rich perceptual feedback and efficient visual exploration during learning.

2. **Stage II: Vision-Free Student Training via Teacher-Student Distillation (π_s):**
 - The knowledge from the trained vision-enabled teacher (`π_t`) is distilled into a vision-free student policy (`π_s`).
 - **Observations:** The student observes only the proprioceptive state (`o_s = x_s`). To prevent spatial overfitting, the pose `p` within `x_s` is represented *relative to a task-specific coordinate system* (`T_z(p)`).
 - **Distillation Process:** The student is trained using the same HIL-RL framework but with additional distillation terms incorporated into its loss functions:
 - **Critic Loss:** A mean-square error (MSE) term is added to the student's critic loss (`L^s_Q`) to encourage its Q-function (`Q_s`) to match the converged teacher's Q-function (`Q_t`).
 - **Actor Loss:** A KL divergence term is added to the student's actor loss (`L^s_π`) to regularize its action distribution (`π_s`) to match the teacher's action distribution (`π_t`).
 - **Purpose:** To overcome visual task overfitting by shedding the visual modality while retaining the reactive behaviors and exploration benefits acquired by the teacher.

3. **Stage III: Zero-Shot Generalization and Optional Fine-tuning:**
 - **Zero-shot generalization:** The trained vision-free student policy (`π_s`) can be deployed directly on unseen tasks, given an estimated target task pose to provide the task-relative proprioceptive states.
 - **Fine-tuning:** For more challenging, novel tasks where zero-shot performance might be insufficient, the vision-free policy can be further fine-tuned. This involves training a *new* vision-enabled teacher (`π_t'`) specifically for the new challenging task. The previously trained vision-free student is then fine-tuned with distillation from this new task-specific teacher.
 - **Purpose:** To adapt the policy to task characteristics not sufficiently represented in the initial training set, enhancing performance on difficult out-of-distribution tasks.

**F. Experimental Setup:**
- **Hardware:** Franka FR3 robot, two wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D435i cameras, 3D Space mouse for teleoperation, NVIDIA RTX A4000 GPU.
- **Algorithmic Details:** Policies executed at 10Hz; episode lengths 100 steps (training), 150 steps (evaluation). Total training time: 50 minutes (40 minutes for teacher, 10 minutes for student). 5 human demonstrations per task are collected initially, with online interventions during rollouts. Neural networks are MLPs for actor/critic with a shared proprioceptive/visual encoder backbone (pretrained ResNet-10 for vision).
- **Task Benchmark:** NIST Assembly Board I, using a 3D-printed environment for training and a replica of the full board for evaluation.
 - **Training Tasks (3):** Medium peg, ethernet, medium gear (Fig. 3b).
 - **Evaluation Tasks (8):** The three training task types (but distinct sockets on the NIST board) plus five unseen geometries (small gear, large gear, large peg, USB, DSUB) (Fig. 3c).
 - Task-relative poses for vision-free policies are obtained via kinesthetic guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to assess the robustness, generalization capabilities, and the contribution of different sensory modalities within the VE2VF framework.

**A. Robustness and Generalization Compared to Baselines:**
The method was compared against three baselines: HIL-SERL (vision, pose, twist, wrench - VPTW; and pose, twist, wrench - PTW), Dynamic Motion Primitive (DMP), and DMP + Residual RL. Evaluation was conducted on training tasks, disturbed versions of training tasks (with visual distractors and target pose uncertainty), and out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks (unseen geometries).

- **VE2VF Performance:** VE2VF achieved an overall success rate of 95.0%, which was the highest among all tested methods. It demonstrated robust performance across all task categories, including disturbed conditions (100% success on all three disturbed training tasks) and OOD tasks (e.g., 100% on USB, 90% on M Gear). A notable observation was its qualitative reactive recovery behavior during USB insertion (Fig. 5), where the robot successfully re-engaged after slipping off the socket, a behavior attributed to the distilled visual-servoing from the teacher.
- **Vision-Enabled Baseline (HIL-SERL VPTW):** This baseline, which is equivalent to the vision-enabled teacher of VE2VF, achieved near-perfect success on the training tasks (100%). However, its performance significantly degraded under disturbed conditions (e.g., 0% on M Peg, 80% on M Gear) and completely collapsed on all OOD tasks (0% success). This outcome supported the authors' hypothesis that vision-based policies overfit to specific visual features, leading to poor generalization.
- **Vision-Free from Scratch Baseline (HIL-SERL PTW):** This baseline, relying only on pose, twist, and wrench inputs but trained without distillation, showed moderate improvement compared to the purely vision-enabled policy under disturbed and OOD conditions (overall 47.1% success). However, it still struggled to achieve consistently high success rates, particularly on OOD tasks like Ethernet and USB (0% and 50% respectively).
- **DMP Baseline:** The basic open-loop DMP showed poor overall performance (35.7% success), confirming its lack of adaptability for contact-rich manipulation.
- **Residual RL Baseline:** This method, combining DMP with an RL-based residual, performed considerably better than the basic baselines (85.7% overall success). It was comparable to VE2VF on geometrically simpler tasks (e.g., M Peg, L Peg, M Gear), but VE2VF demonstrated superior robustness, especially on tasks involving complex contact dynamics like USB insertion.

**B. Input Modalities Ablation Study:**
An ablation study further investigated the contribution of different sensory inputs under normal and disturbed conditions.

- **Vision-Enabled Policies:** A vision-only (V) policy performed similarly to the vision-enabled teacher (VPTW) under normal training conditions but deteriorated more severely under disturbance. This suggested that the proprioceptive inputs in VPTW provided some compensatory information when visual cues became unreliable.
- **Vision-Free Policies (without distillation):** The PTW policy (trained from scratch) failed to achieve full task completion, even with extended training time (up to 75 minutes, 1.5x standard training). Beyond 50 minutes, it tended to overfit to easier tasks (e.g., M Gear), indicating that proprioceptive input alone was insufficient for the RL agent to learn robust insertion strategies without guidance. This reinforced the necessity of distillation.
- **Vision-Free Student Policies (with distillation):**
 - `sPTW` (distilled pose, twist, wrench) maintained perfect performance (100%) on all training tasks under both normal and disturbed conditions.
 - `sP` (distilled pose-only) degraded significantly under disturbed conditions (e.g., 20% on M Peg), highlighting the importance of twist and wrench information for robustness, especially when pose information is noisy.
 - `sTW` (distilled twist and wrench only, with history) performed poorly, demonstrating the critical role of pose information.
 - These results indicated that combining all proprioceptive modalities (pose, twist, wrench) was crucial for maintaining robustness in the distilled student policy.

**C. Fine-tuning for Challenging Tasks:**
The DSUB insertion task, which yielded 50% zero-shot success for VE2VF, was used to evaluate the effectiveness of fine-tuning.

- **VE2VF Fine-tuning with Distillation:** Fine-tuning the VE2VF policy for 10 minutes *with distillation* from a new DSUB-specific vision-enabled teacher (pretrained for 20 minutes) resulted in 100% success rate on the DSUB task. This demonstrated that the framework could adapt to particularly challenging unseen tasks efficiently. The fine-tuned policy also exhibited reactive recovery behaviors (Fig. 7).
- **VE2VF Fine-tuning without Distillation:** Fine-tuning the VE2VF policy for 10 minutes *without distillation* (i.e., attempting to learn from scratch with only proprioceptive input) resulted in 0% success. This corroborated the ablation study's finding that vision-free policies struggle to learn effectively without the teacher's guidance.
- **Residual RL Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning the Residual RL baseline for a longer duration (30 minutes) yielded only marginal improvement (from 60% to 70% success), suggesting that its architecture had limitations in acquiring the necessary complex reactive behaviors compared to the distilled approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, VE2VF, that addresses key challenges in real-world robotic manipulation, particularly for contact-rich tasks. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The core contribution is a method for generating policies that are robust to environmental appearance changes and generalize effectively to unseen task variants. By distilling a vision-enabled teacher into a vision-free student, the framework mitigates the overfitting common in vision-based RL policies, leading to more reliable deployment in dynamic real-world settings.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The entire training process is conducted directly on hardware, eliminating the complexities and performance degradation often associated with the sim-to-real gap. This increases the practicality and trustworthiness of the learned policies for industrial and service robotics applications.
- **Efficiency in Skill Acquisition:** The integration of human-in-the-loop reinforcement learning significantly reduces the real-world interaction time required for policy training (approximately 50 minutes for generalizable insertion skills). This efficiency makes the acquisition of complex robotic skills more feasible and less resource-intensive.
- **Targeted Modality Usage:** The framework leverages the exploratory advantages of visual input during the initial teacher training phase while deploying a student policy that relies on the more fundamental physical cues (pose, twist, wrench) governing contact interactions. This strategic use of modalities optimizes both learning efficiency and deployment robustness.
- **Adaptive Learning for Challenging Tasks:** The optional fine-tuning stage, using a task-specific teacher for distillation, provides a mechanism to rapidly adapt the generalist policy to highly challenging or novel tasks, achieving full success in situations where zero-shot generalization might be insufficient.
- **Reduced Reliance on Domain Randomization:** The approach achieves strong generalization without requiring explicit domain randomization or data augmentation techniques, simplifying the policy development pipeline and focusing on inherent robustness from physical sensing.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for various domains requiring precise, adaptive, and reliable robotic manipulation. This includes:

- **Industrial Automation:** Particularly in high-mix, low-volume manufacturing and assembly, where robots need to adapt quickly to different parts and environmental conditions without extensive re-training.
- **Service Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform complex tasks in unstructured and variable human environments.
- **Logistics and Handling:** Improving robot capabilities for handling diverse objects and unexpected disturbances.

Future work outlined by the authors includes extending the method to other contact-rich skills like screwing and non-prehensile manipulation, exploring language conditioning or task embeddings for broader applicability, and incorporating passivity constraints to ensure safer physical interaction. These extensions could further broaden the scope and impact of the VE2VF framework.
