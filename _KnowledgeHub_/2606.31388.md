# One Video, One World: Turning Monocular Video into Physical 4D Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> OVOW is a training-free pipeline turning a single monocular video into instance-level, simulation-ready 4D mesh scenes. A vision-language model discovers, labels and motion-classifies objects; category-aware reconstruction yields watertight rigid meshes and topology-consistent deformable sequences; a render-match-optimize loop recovers metric scale and 6-DoF trajectories; physics-grounded assembly enforces ground and inter-object contact. It leads both benchmarks while running at 3.35 s per frame.

## Problem

- Existing 4D reconstruction emits implicit fields, Gaussian primitives or point clouds, lacking the watertight topology, instance separation and URDF interfaces physics simulators and embodied AI require.
- Skeleton-based motion models demand predefined joint topologies and suffer skinning artifacts, while mesh-oriented 4D methods remain single-object instead of scaling to whole multi-object scenes.
- No dataset pairs source video with instance-level 4D mesh annotations, and benchmarks score rendering fidelity such as PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS rather than geometry, separation or physical plausibility.

## Method

- ==VLM-guided scene decomposition==: Qwen3-VL over three keyframes names instances and classifies motion as static, rigid or deformable; text-prompted SAM3 produces per-frame instance masks.
- ==Category-aware reconstruction==: FLUX.2 amodal inpainting plus Hi3DGen image-to-3D for rigid meshes, ==ZoomNorm==-stabilized Motion324 for deformable mesh sequences, VGGT for camera, depth and initial scale.
- ==Iterative render-match-optimize scale and orientation recovery== (FoundationPose rotation, RoMa v2 matching, PnP-RANSAC, L-BFGS-B), bidirectional pose tracking, then ==RANSAC ground fitting== with contact snapping for assembly.

## Results

- On the 4D scene benchmark OVOW leads every quality metric with **0.440** Scene-IoU-OBB, **0.210** Object-IoU, **2.90** photometric loss and **1.43** N-CLIP, beating all **six** baselines.
- Amortizing computation across video drops runtime to **3.35 s** per frame against **103-788 s** for single-image baselines, one to two orders of magnitude faster at **26 GB** VRAM.
- Stage reliability reaches **95.4%** motion-category accuracy, **93.1%/88.7%** rigid/deformable reconstruction success, **92.4%** pose recovery, **86.8%** scene validity and **82.7%** simulation stability; IoU-B saturates at **0.78** after **three** refinement iterations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct per-vertex deformation unifies rigid, articulated and soft motion without rigging or category priors, avoiding the skinning artifacts skeleton-based pipelines inherit.
> - Composing frozen foundation models can beat trained systems when the missing ingredient is output format and geometric grounding rather than learned representation quality.
> - Rendering metrics hide simulator-relevant failures; explicit contact projection and gravity alignment are what convert visually plausible reconstructions into physically stable assets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 4D reconstruction has made advancements in generating visually convincing dynamic scene renderings, often relying on representations such as implicit fields, Gaussian primitives, or point clouds. These outputs are primarily optimized for visual fidelity and rendering quality. However, a gap exists between these rendering-oriented reconstructions and the requirements of applications in robotics, embodied AI, gaming, and virtual reality. These applications, particularly physics simulators like MuJoCo, Isaac Gym, and PyBullet, necessitate structured scene assets that possess watertight mesh topology, clear instance separation, and standardized physical interfaces (e.g., URDF). Current methods generally do not provide these attributes, often producing single-object reconstructions or representations that are not directly usable for physical simulation.

Parallel research areas highlight the demand for and feasibility of more structured 3D/4D outputs. Agent-driven scene synthesis, for example, seeks simulation-ready, instance-level assets. Simultaneously, mesh-oriented 4D and scene-level 3D reconstruction techniques have demonstrated the possibility of recovering temporally consistent meshes from monocular video, albeit primarily at the single-object level. Structured scene generation methods exist, but they typically construct scenes from textual descriptions or procedural rules rather than directly from real-world video. Furthermore, while automatic rigging and articulated object generation aim to imbue individual assets with kinematic structures for simulation, they generally rely on synthetic priors, category templates, or manual design, not direct visual observation from video.

This work addresses the convergence of these trends by proposing a unified pipeline that reconstructs instance-level, simulation-ready 4D mesh scenes directly from monocular video. This approach differentiates itself by providing a comprehensive solution for multi-object scenes, incorporating both rigid and non-rigid motion, and assembling objects into a physically coherent representation. The paper also highlights a deficiency in existing datasets and benchmarks, which often lack paired instance-level 4D mesh annotations with source video and metrics for structural qualities such as geometric correctness, instance separation, and physical interaction, focusing instead on visual fidelity metrics (e.g., PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). By introducing a new benchmark and a scalable data generation engine, this research aims to facilitate further development in 4D world models and embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce OVOW, a training-free system capable of reconstructing instance-level, simulation-ready 4D mesh scenes from a single monocular video. The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the current state of 4D reconstruction and the growing demands of downstream applications:

1. **Bridging the Fidelity-to-Utility Gap in 4D Reconstruction:** Contemporary 4D reconstruction methods, while achieving high visual fidelity for rendering, typically output implicit fields, Gaussian primitives, or point clouds. These representations lack the structural properties (watertight topology, instance separation, standardized physical interfaces) required by physics simulators and embodied AI systems. The researchers aim to close this gap by producing mesh-based outputs that are directly usable for physical interaction and simulation.

2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Scene Representation Models:** Traditional methods for modeling dynamic scenes, such as skeleton-based approaches, often require predefined joint topologies and can exhibit skinning artifacts with complex deformations. OVOW seeks a more generalizable motion model that does not depend on category-specific priors or skeleton rigging. By employing direct mesh vertex deformation, the system aims to uniformly handle a range of motions, including articulated motion, surface deformation, and soft-body dynamics, producing watertight, instance-level meshes suitable for downstream tasks.

3. **Enabling Scalable, Multi-Object Reconstruction from Video:** While progress has been made in mesh-oriented 4D reconstruction for individual objects, a unified pipeline capable of scaling this capability to entire dynamic multi-object scenes from monocular video remains an unmet challenge. The motivation is to develop a system that can decompose complex scenes, reconstruct individual object meshes with their respective motions, and assemble them into a physically coherent scene.

4. **Creating Structured Data and Evaluation Benchmarks:** A significant hurdle in advancing 4D reconstruction for physical applications is the scarcity of datasets that pair instance-level 4D mesh annotations with source video. Additionally, existing evaluation benchmarks primarily focus on visual fidelity metrics, neglecting structural qualities crucial for physical tasks, such as geometric correctness, instance separation, and plausible physical interactions. The authors are motivated to establish the first benchmark for structured Video-to-4D evaluation and to develop a scalable pipeline for synthesizing "video ↔ instance-level 4D mesh scene" pairs to support future research in 4D world models and embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OVOW operates as a training-free system that integrates pre-trained foundation models into a four-stage pipeline to convert a monocular video (V = {I_t}) into a simulation-ready 4D scene (S = { (M_i, {T_t^i}) }), where M_i represents a watertight triangle mesh (or a topology-consistent mesh sequence for deformable objects) and T_t^i is a per-frame 6-DoF pose trajectory.

#### 3.1. VLM-Guided Scene Decomposition

The initial stage involves understanding and segmenting the dynamic scene.
- **Keyframe Selection:** N_key (default 3) keyframes are uniformly sampled from the input video.
- **VLM Processing:** A vision-language model (Qwen3-VL) processes these keyframes as a multi-image prompt. It performs open-vocabulary object discovery, assigns unique instance names (e.g., `<noun>_<index>`), and classifies the motion category of each instance as static, rigid, or deformable. The output is a JSON record for each object instance, containing its label, motion category, and a brief visual description.
- **Dense Video Segmentation:** Using the generated labels, SAM3 with text prompts generates per-frame binary masks ({M_t^i}) for each instance. Instances with a mask area below a threshold (τ_area, default 200 pixels) are filtered out.

#### 3.2. Instance-Level Mesh Reconstruction

This stage focuses on generating 3D meshes for each identified object instance.
- **Static and Rigid Objects:** For static or rigid objects, an anchor frame (t*_i) with the largest mask area is selected. If the object is partially occluded, an inpainting model (FLUX.2) conditioned on the masked RGB, mask, and VLM description is used to hallucinate the complete appearance. A feed-forward image-to-3D model (Hi3DGen) then generates a canonical mesh (ˆM_i).
- **Deformable Objects:** For deformable objects, a per-object masked video is extracted and stabilized using zoom normalization (ZoomNorm) to maintain a consistent object-to-frame ratio (ρ, default 0.65) and resolution (L, default 512). A deformable mesh reconstruction model (Motion324) processes this normalized video to output a topology-consistent mesh sequence ({ˆM_t^i}) and a reference mesh (ˆM_ref_i).
- **Metric Scale Recovery:** A visual geometry foundation model (VGGT) estimates per-frame camera extrinsics ({E_t}), intrinsics ({K_t}), dense depth maps ({D_t}), and a scene-level point cloud (P). For each object, the masked depth from its anchor frame is back-projected to a 3D point cloud (Q_i). An initial scale factor (s^(0)_i) is computed by aligning the bounding box diagonals of Q_i and ˆM_i. This scale is refined in the next stage, and the final scaled mesh (¯M_i = s_i · ˆM_i) is used subsequently, uniformly applied to all frames for deformable objects.

#### 3.3. Spatiotemporal Pose and Deformation Recovery

A unified two-stage procedure recovers object poses and deformations.
- **Iterative Scale and Orientation Recovery:** For each object at its anchor frame (t*_i), the metric scale and orientation are jointly refined through N_iter (default 3) render-match-optimize loops.
 - **Orientation Update:** FoundationPose estimates a 6-DoF pose from the anchor-frame RGBD, mask, and intrinsics. Only the rotation (R^(n)) is extracted and applied to the mesh.
 - **Scale Estimation:** The rotated mesh is rendered from multiple viewpoints. Dense correspondences between the best rendered view and the real masked crop are established using RoMa v2. A pose is obtained via PnP-RANSAC, and a scalar scale (s^(n)) is optimized using L-BFGS-B to minimize the distance between centered point sets. The final scale (s_i) is the product of these iterative scale factors.
- **Per-Frame Pose Tracking:** With the scaled mesh (¯M_i), FoundationPose estimates the 6-DoF pose (T_t^i) for every frame using mask-gated inputs. Full registration is performed at the anchor frame, followed by bidirectional tracking. If tracking fails, full re-registration is performed. The world-space pose (T_t,w^i) is then computed by inverting the camera extrinsics (E_t).
- **Static Pose Locking:** For objects classified as static, all per-frame poses are locked to the pose estimated at their anchor frame.
- **Deformable Object Decomposition:** For deformable objects, the world-space mesh at frame t (M_t,w^i) is expressed as T_t,w^i · ¯M_t^i, effectively decoupling global rigid trajectory from local non-rigid deformation.

#### 3.4. Physics-Grounded Scene Assembly

The independently reconstructed objects are assembled into a physically coherent scene.
- **Ground Plane Estimation:** Candidate planes are extracted from the scene point cloud (P) via RANSAC. The optimal plane is selected based on maximizing the above-plane ratio for static objects and minimizing contact proximity. A camera-up prior resolves normal ambiguity, and the selected plane is aligned to the XY-plane.
- **Contact Projection:** Objects are sorted by their bottom distance to the ground. For each object, the bottom p_bot% (default 10%) vertices are identified. A gravity-axis correction (∆z_t^i) is applied to resolve penetration or floating, ensuring objects rest on the ground.
- **Inter-Object Contact:** Nearest-surface distances are queried between the bottom vertices of an object and other already-settled objects using a KD-tree. If the distance is below a threshold (τ_contact, 3% of scene diagonal), the surface of the settled object acts as a local ground plane, facilitating stacking configurations. This process is iterated N_asm (default 2) times per frame to manage cascading dependencies.
- **Environment Lighting Recovery:** An intrinsic decomposition model recovers an HDR environment map from the input video, which is applied as world lighting for photorealistic rendering of the assembled scene.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of OVOW was evaluated quantitatively and qualitatively on custom benchmarks and real-world videos, demonstrating its capabilities in structured 4D scene reconstruction and its utility for downstream applications.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Setup

The researchers created two synthetic benchmarks, OVOW-3D-Scene-Bench (120 static scenes) and OVOW-4D-Scene-Bench (120 dynamic scenes with at least one rigid-body motion), using a dedicated synthetic pipeline. This pipeline composes scenes from 3D objects (Objaverse-OA), animated 4D assets (Truebones Zoo), and HDRI environments (Poly Haven), providing precise ground-truth data (instance meshes, 6-DoF poses, camera parameters, dense depth maps) for evaluation.
OVOW was compared against state-of-the-art single-image scene reconstruction methods: CAST, SAM3D, VIGA, MIDI, SceneGen, and TabletopGen. These baselines were run on seven uniformly sampled frames per scene, and their scores were averaged, as they do not support video input.
Key metrics included Scene-IoU (volumetric IoU of object bounding boxes, both Axis-Aligned (AABB↑) and Oriented (OBB↑)), Object-IoU (per-object IoU after Hungarian matching↑), Photometric Loss (PL↓), Negative-CLIP score (N-CLIP↓), per-frame time, and peak VRAM.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison

- **OVOW-3D-Scene-Bench (Static Scenes):** OVOW achieved the best Scene-IoU-OBB (0.218), Object-IoU (0.190), Photometric Loss (5.70), and Negative-CLIP score (1.87). While VIGA exhibited a higher Scene-IoU-AABB, it underperformed on other metrics. OVOW's single-image runtime (272 s/frame) was comparable to other feed-forward baselines.
- **OVOW-4D-Scene-Bench (Dynamic Scenes):** OVOW outperformed all baselines across every quality metric, achieving Scene-IoU-OBB (0.440), Object-IoU (0.210), Photometric Loss (2.90), and Negative-CLIP score (1.43). Notably, its amortized computation across video frames resulted in significantly faster per-frame processing (3.35 s/frame), which was one to two orders of magnitude quicker than the baselines (103–788 s/frame).

The quantitative results indicate that OVOW's performance advantage is most pronounced in dynamic 4D scenes, where its ability to leverage video-level temporal cues is critical. The integration of amodal inpainting for occluded objects and physics-grounded assembly for contact and support contributed to reduced interpenetration and floating artifacts, enhancing physical stability. The system also demonstrated graceful degradation in quality with an increasing number of objects, with runtime increasing modestly.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Comparison

Qualitative comparisons highlighted OVOW's ability to reconstruct instance-separated, watertight meshes with accurate geometry, layout, and texture from both dynamic videos and single images. In contrast, feed-forward 3D methods often lacked instance separation, and other scene-reconstruction baselines frequently misplaced objects or distorted geometry. OVOW also surpassed single-object video-to-mesh generation methods (which struggled with unseen viewpoints and lacked metric scale/inter-object contact) and depth-fusion approaches (which produced single fused surfaces without instance separation or physical grounding). The research also presented examples of paired video-to-4D data generated by OVOW, demonstrating its potential as a scalable data engine.

#### 4.4. Sensitivity of Key Design Choices

An ablation study on a held-out synthetic validation set confirmed the robustness of OVOW's key design choices. Hyperparameters such as the number of refinement iterations (N_iter=3), zoom normalization ratio (ρ=0.65), minimum mask area (τ_area=200px), and contact parameters (ϵ=0.2%, δ_max=2%, p_bot=10%) were found to be non-brittle and near-optimal. The pipeline's stages exhibited high reliability: 95.4% motion-category accuracy, 93.1%/88.7% rigid/deformable reconstruction success, 92.4% pose recovery, and 86.8% final scene validity with 82.7% simulation stability, suggesting rare propagation of failures.

#### 4.5. Examples of Simulation and Editing

The reconstructed 4D scenes were directly usable for downstream applications without post-processing.
- **Physics Simulation:** OVOW's watertight, instance-separated meshes were imported into Blender's physics engine, demonstrating realistic rigid-body collision, stacking dynamics, and plausible soft-body deformations under gravity and contact forces. The assets are exportable to mainstream simulators like MuJoCo, Isaac Gym, and PyBullet.
- **Scene Editing:** The instance-level decomposition enabled intuitive editing in standard 3D tools (e.g., Blender). Users could rearrange objects, delete/duplicate instances, replace meshes while preserving motion, retexture objects, and composite them into new scenes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents OVOW as a system that addresses a notable gap in 4D reconstruction by generating simulation-ready assets directly from monocular video. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

1. **Enabling Embodied AI and Robotics:** By producing instance-level, watertight 4D mesh scenes with accurate physical properties, OVOW directly supports the requirements of physics simulators essential for training and evaluating embodied AI agents and robotic systems. This capability can facilitate the development of more sophisticated manipulation, navigation, and interaction tasks in virtual environments.

2. **Advancing Structured Scene Understanding:** The formalization of the structured Video-to-4D reconstruction task and the development of a pipeline to address it contribute to a deeper understanding of dynamic scenes. The output, which includes labeled and motion-classified instances with their specific geometries and trajectories, provides a rich, interpretable scene representation beyond mere visual rendering.

3. **Reducing Reliance on Synthetic Data for Asset Generation:** Unlike previous methods that often rely on synthetic priors, category templates, or manual design to create simulation-ready assets, OVOW extracts these assets directly from real-world visual observations in monocular videos. This approach expands the potential sources of physically plausible 4D content, potentially reducing the manual effort and expertise required for asset creation.

4. **Establishing a New Benchmark and Data Infrastructure:** The creation of the first benchmarks for structured Video-to-4D evaluation (OVOW-3D-Scene-Bench and OVOW-4D-Scene-Bench) with metrics beyond visual fidelity is a crucial contribution. These benchmarks, along with OVOW's capability to serve as a scalable engine for synthesizing "video ↔ instance-level 4D mesh scene" pairs, provide foundational data and evaluation tools for future research in 4D world models and embodied AI.

5. **Efficiency and Training-Free Operation:** OVOW's training-free pipeline, which composes pre-trained foundation models, offers a practical advantage by not requiring extensive, task-specific dataset training. Its ability to process video significantly faster than single-image baselines, particularly for dynamic scenes, demonstrates a step towards more efficient 4D reconstruction workflows.

6. **Direct Usability for Content Creation:** The instance-level decomposition and physical grounding enable direct editing and manipulation of reconstructed scenes in standard 3D tools, offering new possibilities for virtual reality, gaming, and interactive content creation without requiring specialized post-processing.

In summary, OVOW represents a development in turning passive video observations into actionable, physically coherent 4D scene representations, holding implications for the advancement of interactive and intelligent systems.
