# NavOL: Navigation Policy with Online Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> NavOL introduces an online imitation learning framework that leverages GPU-parallel simulation to train robust visual navigation policies for robots. This approach mitigates distribution shift and eliminates the need for manual reward engineering, achieving up to 63% higher success rates on a new benchmark and demonstrating strong sim-to-real transfer on a quadruped robot.

## Problem

- Offline imitation learning policies suffer from distribution shift and compounding errors, leading to unstable navigation in unseen states.
- Reinforcement learning methods for navigation often require extensive, complex reward engineering and exhibit low sample efficiency.
- Scaling DAgger-style online learning algorithms to high-dimensional visual diffusion policies has historically been computationally prohibitive.

## Method

- **NavOL** employs an online imitation learning paradigm with a highly parallelized rollout-update pipeline in a high-fidelity simulator (IsaacLab), continuously collecting expert demonstrations.
- The model uses a multimodal ==diffusion policy== (Trajectory Generator) to predict waypoint sequences, complemented by a Critic network to evaluate trajectory safety.
- Training involves iteratively optimizing the policy with data from recent rollouts, where actions are mixed between the policy's proposal and an optimal expert planner's guidance to stabilize learning.

## Results

- NavOL achieved a mean Success Rate (SR) of **69.0** and Success-weighted Path Length (SPL) of **63.7** on a new, challenging zero-shot benchmark, outperforming NavDP (**42.2** mSR, **37.7** mSPL) by a large margin.
- The policy demonstrated robust sim-to-real transfer and cross-embodiment generalization, successfully navigating a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot in real-world environments with consistently higher success rates than NavDP (e.g., 8/10 vs. 4/10 in an office scene).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of expert rollout guidance, pre-training initialization, and domain randomization for NavOL's performance and generalization, with training from scratch still outperforming NavDP but at a higher compute cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online, on-policy data aggregation from interactive rollouts effectively mitigates distribution shift, leading to more stable and robust navigation behaviors compared to purely offline training.
> - Leveraging GPU-parallel simulation allows for scalable online imitation learning, making DAgger-style interactive policy refinement feasible for complex visual navigation tasks.
> - Initializing with a strong pre-trained behavioral prior (from offline IL) and integrating expert guidance during online rollouts are crucial for efficient and stable policy optimization in complex environments.
