# Uncertainty Quantification for Language Models: A Suite of Black-Box, White-Box, LLM Judge, and Ensemble Scorers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CVS Health developed a framework and an open-source toolkit (`uqlm`) to quantify uncertainty in Large Language Models (LLMs) for real-time, closed-book hallucination detection. The framework standardizes various black-box, white-box, and LLM-as-a-Judge techniques into comparable confidence scores, featuring a tunable ensemble method that consistently outperforms individual scorers.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently generate factual errors (hallucinations), posing significant reliability and safety risks in high-stakes applications like healthcare.
- Current hallucination detection methods, such as ground truth comparison or source content verification, are often not viable for real-time, closed-book inference scenarios.
- A unified and adaptable framework is needed to easily implement, compare, and optimize diverse uncertainty quantification (UQ) techniques for specific LLM deployment contexts.

## Method

- A comprehensive framework integrates and standardizes various UQ methods, including ==black-box==, ==white-box==, and ==LLM-as-a-Judge== approaches, to produce 0-1 confidence scores for real-time, closed-book hallucination detection.
- A ==tunable ensemble scorer== is introduced, allowing any combination of individual UQ methods to be weighted and optimized using a small set of user-provided graded responses, adapting to specific use cases.
- An accompanying open-source Python toolkit, `uqlm`, provides ready-to-use implementations of all presented UQ scorers and the ensemble method, facilitating practical adoption.

## Results

- The tunable ensemble scorer achieved superior performance over individual scorers in **20** out of **24** AUROC scenarios and **17** out of **24** F1-score scenarios, with top AUROC values reaching **0.986**.
- NLI-based black-box methods (NCP, NSN) showed the strongest performance among black-box scorers, leading in AUROC for **13** out of **24** scenarios and F1-score for **18** out of **24** black-box scenarios.
- Applying confidence thresholds to filter low-confidence responses monotonically increased the accuracy of the remaining LLM outputs, demonstrating practical utility (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite accuracy on PopQA improved from **0.35** to **0.61** at τ=**0.6**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The tunable ensemble scorer consistently delivers superior hallucination detection performance compared to individual UQ methods, highlighting the benefit of combining multiple uncertainty signals.
> - Individual UQ scorer performance varies significantly across different LLMs and datasets, indicating that optimal detection strategies require use-case-specific customization.
> - Natural Language Inference (NLI)-based black-box methods (e.g., Non-Contradiction Probability) and LLM-as-a-Judge scorers (when the judge LLM is proficient in the task) are frequently among the top-performing individual techniques.
