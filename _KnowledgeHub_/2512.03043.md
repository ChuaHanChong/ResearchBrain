# OneThinker: All-in-one Reasoning Model for Image and Video

> [!summary] Summary
> OneThinker, an 8B-parameter multimodal reasoning model developed by MMLab, CUHK, and Meituan, unifies image and video understanding across various visual tasks. Trained on a 600k corpus with a novel EMA-GRPO reinforcement learning algorithm, the model achieves strong performance across 31 benchmarks, outperforming baseline and specialized models in areas like image/video QA, grounding, and tracking, while demonstrating knowledge transfer and preliminary zero-shot generalization.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are typically specialized for single tasks or specific modalities (image or video), limiting their versatility and scalability.
- The fragmented nature of specialized models prevents potential knowledge transfer and synergy between different visual tasks and modalities, hindering the development of generalist systems.
- Training unified MLLMs with reinforcement learning is challenging due to the distinct and heterogeneous reward characteristics across various visual tasks, leading to optimization imbalances.

## Method

- The researchers developed **OneThinker**, a ==unified 8B==-parameter MLLM designed to process both image and video across 10 diverse visual reasoning tasks within a single framework.
- They constructed the OneThinker-600k corpus, a large-scale multimodal dataset with approximately 600,000 samples, and used a proprietary model (Seed1.5-VL) to generate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- A novel reinforcement learning algorithm, EMA-GRPO (Exponential Moving Average - Group Relative Policy Optimization), was introduced to adaptively normalize reward advantages, mitigating both intra-task and inter-task imbalances during multi-task training.

## Results

- OneThinker-**8B** achieved consistent high performance across 31 benchmarks for 10 visual understanding tasks, outperforming baseline models like Qwen3-VL-Instruct-**8B** and several specialized models.
- The model scored **70.6%** on MMMU (image QA), **66.2%** on VideoMMMU (video QA), set state-of-the-art results in spatial grounding (e.g., 92.0 val on RefCOCO), and showed significant improvements in temporal and spatio-temporal grounding, tracking, and segmentation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the unified RL framework and specifically the EMA-GRPO algorithm were critical for addressing intra-task and inter-task reward imbalances, leading to superior performance compared to SFT-only and other GRPO variants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single, unified MLLM can effectively perform a wide array of visual reasoning tasks across both image and video modalities, demonstrating the feasibility of an all-in-one generalist model.
> - Reinforcement learning, particularly with the proposed EMA-GRPO algorithm, is crucial for balancing optimization and effectively training MLLMs on a diverse set of tasks with heterogeneous reward structures.
> - Unified training facilitates significant knowledge sharing and transfer across different tasks and modalities, where understanding from one domain (e.g., image QA) can enhance reasoning in another (e.g., video QA or segmentation).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is a significant area of research aimed at advancing artificial general intelligence (AGI). A core component of this advancement involves enhancing reasoning capabilities, enabling models to perform step-by-step inference over complex visual-linguistic inputs. Recent studies have demonstrated the utility of reinforcement learning (RL), particularly algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), in improving the reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), exemplified by DeepSeek-R1. This success has motivated the application of RL to MLLMs, leading to models specializing in various visual tasks.

For instance, Vision-R1 and Video-R1 have shown strong reasoning performance in image and video question answering, respectively. Other models like VLM-R1 and Seg-R1 have extended this paradigm to perception-oriented tasks such as object detection and segmentation. These advancements highlight the effectiveness of RL-based training across a range of visual tasks.

However, a recurring limitation in much of this existing work is the specialized nature of these "thinking models." They are typically designed for a single task and operate exclusively on either images or videos. This specialization restricts their practical versatility and may prevent the realization of potential benefits from knowledge transfer across different tasks and modalities. While some efforts have explored multi-task MLLMs with RL, they are often confined to limited subsets of visual tasks within a single modality and are constrained by small-scale tuning, which can limit their generalization capabilities. For example, VideoChat-R1 performs co-training on a small dataset for a few spatio-temporal tasks, remaining restricted to the video modality. The present work addresses this gap by aiming to create a unified model capable of handling a broader spectrum of visual reasoning tasks across both image and video modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop "OneThinker," an all-in-one multimodal reasoning generalist. This model aims to simultaneously handle both image and video understanding across a diverse range of fundamental visual tasks within a single unified framework.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in the existing research landscape:
- **Limited Scalability and Versatility of Existing Models**: Current MLLMs often require training separate models for different tasks and treat image and video reasoning as distinct domains. This leads to a lack of scalability, as a new model might be needed for each new task or modality, and limits the practical versatility of MLLMs in real-world scenarios that demand integrated understanding.
- **Hindrance of Knowledge Sharing**: The separation of tasks and modalities prevents potential knowledge sharing. Concepts and reasoning patterns learned in one task or modality (e.g., spatial reasoning from images) might be beneficial for another (e.g., spatial-temporal reasoning in videos), but current specialized models cannot fully leverage these synergies.
- **Inherent Nature of Vision**: Vision inherently encompasses both static images and dynamic videos. Real-world applications often require unified reasoning across both, making a single, generalist model more desirable and efficient.
- **Addressing Data and Optimization Challenges**: To achieve a generalist model, the authors are motivated to construct a large-scale, diverse dataset covering various tasks and modalities. Furthermore, they aim to develop an RL algorithm robust enough to handle the distinct reward characteristics and heterogeneity of multiple visual tasks simultaneously, thereby ensuring balanced and stable optimization during training.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond task- and modality-specific models towards a more comprehensive and adaptable MLLM that can generalize across a wide array of visual understanding challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing OneThinker involves three main components: dataset construction, task unification with reward definition, and a novel reinforcement learning algorithm.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

To support the training of a unified multimodal reasoning generalist, the authors constructed the **OneThinker-600k** corpus. This large-scale dataset comprises approximately 600,000 multimodal samples, encompassing both image and video modalities. It covers a diverse range of fundamental visual reasoning tasks, including:
- Rule-based Question Answering (QA)
- Open-ended QA
- Captioning
- Spatial Grounding
- Temporal Grounding
- Spatio-Temporal Grounding
- Tracking
- Segmentation

For perception-oriented tasks, the model is required to output responses in a predefined JSON schema, which facilitates consistent formatting and enables automatic reward computation. The data was collected from a broad range of public training datasets to ensure task diversity, balanced modality coverage, and varying difficulty levels.

To enable effective Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) initialization for reasoning, the authors leveraged a strong proprietary model, Seed1.5-VL, to produce Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations on the OneThinker-600k corpus. Task-specific filtering thresholds were applied to ensure the quality of the CoT traces, resulting in the **OneThinker-SFT-340k** dataset, used for the SFT cold start.

#### 3.2. Task Unification and Reward Mechanisms

All tasks in OneThinker are cast into a unified text interface. The model is designed to first produce its internal reasoning within `<think>...</think>` tags and then output a task-specific result within `<answer>...</answer>` tags. For perception tasks, the `<answer>` block contains structured information (e.g., time spans, bounding boxes, sparse points) in a JSON format.

The overall reward for reinforcement learning is defined as `R = R_acc + R_format`, where `R_acc` is the task-specific accuracy reward and `R_format` checks if the output adheres to the predefined schema for structured outputs.

- **Rule-based QA (Multiple-choice, Numerical, Math, OCR, Regression)**: Correctness is determined by equivalence for multiple-choice, numerical, and math problems. Regression tasks use Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA), and OCR tasks use Word Error Rate for reward computation.
- **Open-ended QA & Caption**: An external reward model, POLAR-7B, provides a similarity score between the predicted and reference answers.
- **Temporal Grounding**: Accuracy is measured using temporal Intersection-over-Union (tIoU) between predicted and ground-truth time intervals.
- **Spatial Grounding**: Accuracy is measured using spatial Intersection-over-Union (sIoU) between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes.
- **Spatial-Temporal Grounding**: Combines temporal IoU and mean spatial IoU across frames.
- **Tracking**: Accuracy is measured as the mean sIoU over all frames in a trajectory.
- **Segmentation (Image/Video)**: The model predicts a bounding box and positive/negative points, which are fed into SAM2 to generate the final mask.
 - **Image Segmentation**: Reward combines sIoU of the bounding box with Gaussian similarities over positive and negative point sets.
 - **Video Segmentation**: Additionally includes a temporal Gaussian kernel applied to a predicted keyframe time. (Mask-based reward is omitted for video segmentation due to computational latency.)

#### 3.3. EMA-GRPO: An Adaptive Reinforcement Learning Algorithm

To address challenges posed by the distinct reward characteristics of heterogeneous visual tasks, the authors propose **EMA-GRPO (Exponential Moving Average - Group Relative Policy Optimization)**. This algorithm aims to mitigate two types of imbalances:

- **Intra-task Imbalance**: Standard GRPO normalizes rewards by the group standard deviation, which can lead to biased weighting, favoring low-variance rollouts and under-optimizing medium-difficulty samples.
- **Inter-task Imbalance**: Removing standard deviation normalization (as in Dr.GRPO) avoids intra-task bias but introduces cross-task imbalance. Sparse-reward tasks (e.g., math) can dominate optimization, while dense-reward tasks (e.g., grounding) are suppressed due to different reward scales.

EMA-GRPO addresses these issues by maintaining task-wise adaptive normalization based on the exponential moving average (EMA) of reward statistics. For each task *τ*, EMA estimates of the first and second moments of its outcome rewards are tracked. A task-wise standard deviation, *σ_τ(t)*, is computed from these EMA moments. The advantage for each sample is then computed as `A_τi(t) = (R_i - mean({R_j})) / σ_τ(t)`. This approach ensures:
- Within a task, all rollouts share the same normalization scale, preventing bias towards specific sample difficulties.
- Across different tasks, each task maintains an independent reward scale, balancing gradient contributions irrespective of differences in reward magnitude or density.

The final policy update follows the standard GRPO objective but utilizes the EMA-normalized advantage, incorporating a KL regularization term. The training process involves an SFT stage using OneThinker-SFT-340k, followed by reinforcement learning on the larger OneThinker-600k corpus.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OneThinker-8B demonstrated consistent performance across a range of visual reasoning benchmarks, covering both image and video modalities. The model was evaluated on 31 benchmarks across 10 fundamental visual understanding tasks, exhibiting improvements over baseline models, particularly Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B, and other specialized models.

#### 4.1. Task-Specific Performance

- **Image QA**: OneThinker-8B achieved 70.6% on MMMU, 77.6% on MathVista, 64.3% on MathVerse, and 70.6% on MMStar. These results indicate superior performance compared to open-source models such as Vision-R1-7B, VAPO-Thinker-7B, and the Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B baseline, across general knowledge, mathematics, science, and multimodal reasoning tasks.
- **Video QA**: The model showed strong performance, achieving 66.2% on VideoMMMU, 70.5% on MMVU(mc), and 66.5% on VideoMME. It also surpassed specialized video reasoning models like VideoChat-R1-7B and VideoLLaMA3-7B on benchmarks such as LongVideo-Reason (79.2%) and VideoMathQA (35.0%).
- **Image and Video Captioning**: OneThinker-8B obtained 25.7 on MMSci-Caption and 57.9 on MMT-Caption for image captioning, and 28.0 on VideoMMLU-Caption for video captioning, outperforming Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B and LLaVA-1.5-7B.
- **Temporal Grounding**: Significant improvements were observed, with the model achieving 68.3 R@0.5 on Charades, 43.6 R@0.5 on ActivityNet, and 43.2 mIoU on ANet-RTL, indicating enhanced ability to localize events in time.
- **Spatial Grounding**: On RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, OneThinker-8B achieved state-of-the-art results (e.g., 92.0 val on RefCOCO, 87.0 val on RefCOCO+, 89.2 val on RefCOCOg), surpassing models like Perception-R1 and DeepEyes.
- **Spatial-Temporal Grounding**: For the STVG benchmark, the model delivered 34.9 tIoU@0.5 and 40.3 sIoU@0.5, representing substantial gains over Grounded-VideoLLM and Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B.
- **Tracking**: OneThinker-8B achieved 73.0 AO and 84.4 R@0.5 on GOT-10k. These results are higher than previous models, including R1-Track and VideoChat-R1, despite being evaluated under a more challenging setting (32 frames vs. 8 frames for prior work).
- **Image and Video Segmentation**: The model attained 75.8 cIoU on RefCOCO, 67.1 cIoU on RefCOCO+, and 70.8 cIoU on RefCOCOg for image segmentation (val set). For video segmentation, it achieved 52.7 J&F on MeViS and 54.9 J&F on ReasonVOS, outperforming previous open-source models.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted with three variants:
- **OneThinker-8B-SFT**: Trained with SFT only, without RL. This variant performed worse across all tasks than RL-trained versions, confirming the effectiveness of reinforcement learning.
- **OneThinker-8B-GRPO**: Replaced EMA-GRPO with the original GRPO algorithm. This variant showed noticeable degradation compared to OneThinker-8B, supporting the claim that intra-task imbalance is a significant issue.
- **OneThinker-8B-DrGRPO**: Adopted the Dr.GRPO algorithm (which removes STD normalization). This variant also performed worse than OneThinker-8B, indicating that inter-task imbalance is an issue when standard deviation normalization is completely removed.

These ablation results collectively confirm the effectiveness of the unified RL framework and the EMA-GRPO algorithm in addressing imbalances during multi-task training.

#### 4.3. Benefits of Unified Training and Zero-shot Generalization

Analysis of unified training benefits indicated that:
- Excluding spatial grounding data during training led to performance drops in image QA and segmentation.
- Removing temporal grounding data negatively impacted video QA and tracking.
- Omitting Image QA samples resulted in a severe performance drop on video QA.

These findings suggest that knowledge is effectively shared and transferred across tasks and modalities during joint training, with higher-quality image data appearing to enhance general reasoning and recognition capabilities that benefit video understanding.

Furthermore, OneThinker-8B demonstrated preliminary zero-shot generalization abilities, outperforming Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B on several unseen visual understanding tasks from MMT-Bench, such as point tracking, image quality assessment, GUI tasks, and rotated object detection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OneThinker project presents a step towards the development of a unified multimodal reasoning generalist. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Unified Multimodal Reasoning**: OneThinker demonstrates the feasibility of a single model capable of performing a wide array of visual tasks across both image and video modalities. This addresses the current fragmentation in multimodal AI research, where models are often specialized for specific tasks or modalities.
- **Enhanced Versatility and Scalability**: By consolidating diverse visual understanding capabilities into one model, OneThinker offers increased practical versatility for real-world applications that require integrated image and video analysis. This approach also suggests a more scalable path for developing advanced MLLMs, reducing the need for numerous specialized models.
- **Improved Knowledge Transfer**: The empirical evidence of knowledge transfer between related tasks and modalities (e.g., spatial grounding benefiting image QA, image QA benefiting video QA) highlights the advantages of joint training. This cross-pollination of learning can lead to more robust and generalizable visual reasoning skills.
- **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for MLLMs**: The introduction of EMA-GRPO provides a methodological contribution by effectively managing reward heterogeneity in multi-task RL. This algorithm's ability to balance optimization across tasks with varying reward characteristics addresses a known challenge in complex multi-task learning environments.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization**: The preliminary demonstration of zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks indicates that OneThinker has learned transferable reasoning capabilities. This capacity is crucial for developing truly intelligent systems that can adapt to novel situations without explicit re-training.
- **Open-Source Contribution**: The release of code, model, and data supports further research and encourages the community to build upon this work, fostering advancements in unified multimodal reasoning.

Overall, OneThinker represents progress towards creating more comprehensive and adaptable MLLMs that can understand and reason about both static and dynamic visual information in a unified manner.
