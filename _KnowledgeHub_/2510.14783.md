# SkyDreamer: Interpretable End-to-End Vision-Based Drone Racing with Model-Based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SkyDreamer introduces an end-to-end vision-based autonomous drone racing policy that achieves champion-level performance in the real world, operating entirely onboard with robust sim-to-real transfer and enhanced interpretability. It is the first system to simultaneously demonstrate full sim-to-real transfer, onboard execution, and champion-level speeds and agility in real-world drone racing.

## Problem

- Classical autonomous drone racing (ADR) systems achieve high performance but lack generalization, relying on precise track maps, calibration, and hand-tuned filters.
- Previous end-to-end vision-based ADR methods struggle with transferring policies from simulation to real-world drones, onboard execution, and achieving champion-level speeds and accelerations.
- End-to-end learning models often function as "black boxes," lacking interpretability which hinders debugging and understanding of their complex learned behaviors.

## Method

- **SkyDreamer** develops an end-to-end vision-based policy utilizing Model-Based ==Reinforcement Learning== (DreamerV3, extended with Informed Dreamer concepts) for efficient actor training within a learned world model.
- The world model is trained to decode not only observations but also privileged information (ground-truth states, camera extrinsics, dynamic parameters), serving as an implicit state and parameter estimator for interpretability.
- The system incorporates visual augmentations (e.g., StochGAN, mask erosion, rolling shutter effect) to bridge the visual reality gap and a flight plan logic to resolve visual ambiguities, along with direct motor command generation for maximal control authority.

## Results

- SkyDreamer achieved champion-level performance in the real world, reaching estimated speeds up to 21 m/s and accelerations up to 6g, with a **100%** success rate on small tracks.
- Demonstrated robust sim-to-real transfer and onboard execution, successfully flying with poor-quality segmentation masks and adapting to online changes such as a **30%** reduction in maximum motor RPM due to battery depletion.
- The world model accurately estimated drone states (position, velocity within 10-15 cm and **0.5** m/s, respectively) and dynamic parameters (e.g., thrust effectiveness, max motor RPM) in real-time, providing crucial interpretability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-based reinforcement learning, when augmented with decoding to privileged information, can achieve both high performance and interpretability in complex, real-world control tasks like agile drone flight.
> - Robust sim-to-real transfer for vision-based systems can be achieved through a combination of sophisticated visual augmentations and online estimation of dynamic and camera parameters.
> - Direct motor command control, bypassing intermediate controllers, allows for more agile and potentially optimal flight trajectories, especially when coupled with an accurate learned world model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous Drone Racing (ADR) has seen remarkable progress, with autonomous systems recently achieving and even surpassing human champion-level performance. However, the paper points out a critical dichotomy: while champion-level performance has been demonstrated, these systems typically rely on **modular pipelines of classical algorithms**. These classical approaches are highly specialized, dependent on accurate track maps, known gate dimensions, precise camera calibration, and hand-tuned filters. They lack generalization capabilities, making them inflexible and limited to structured environments.

In contrast, **end-to-end vision-based learning** promises greater adaptability by mapping pixel-level representations directly to actions, aiming to jointly optimize perception, state estimation, and control. This approach holds the potential to emulate human pilots' remarkable adaptability to unseen tracks and unstructured environments.

SkyDreamer positions itself at the intersection of these two trends, addressing the significant gap in end-to-end vision-based ADR: **no existing system simultaneously achieves full sim-to-real transfer, onboard execution, and champion-level performance**. Previous end-to-end efforts have either relied on offboard computation, hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) simulation (where real-world images are rendered via motion capture) for "real-world" flights, or have been limited to sub-champion-level performance due to capped thrust-to-weight ratios (TWR).

Furthermore, most ADR approaches treat collective thrust and body rates (CTBR) as the control layer, relying on an inner-loop controller (e.g., PID or INDI) for motor commands,–. SkyDreamer, following the G&CNETs concept introduced by ESA and explored by Ferede et al., adopts **direct motor command generation**, aiming for potentially more optimal performance and simpler modeling by bypassing intermediate controllers.

SkyDreamer’s unique contribution to the landscape is its robust integration of **model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL)**, specifically building on the Dreamer algorithms and, more directly, **Informed Dreamer**. MBRL's advantage lies in its sample efficiency, as the actor trains within a learned world model through "latent imagination," reducing reliance on computationally expensive real-world or simulated rollouts. By extending Informed Dreamer, SkyDreamer leverages *privileged information* (ground-truth states, camera/dynamic parameters) during training, which allows its world model to effectively function as an implicit state and parameter estimator. This significantly improves **interpretability**, a critical advancement over black-box end-to-end methods.

In summary, SkyDreamer aims to unify the performance of classical champion-level systems with the flexibility and generalizability of end-to-end learning, while adding crucial elements of interpretability and direct motor control, setting a new benchmark for practical, robust, and deployable autonomous drone racing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SkyDreamer is to develop an **end-to-end vision-based autonomous drone racing (ADR) policy that achieves champion-level performance in the real world, operates fully onboard, and is interpretable and adaptable to various real-world conditions**. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims to address the limitations of existing ADR systems:

- **Bridge the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Previous end-to-end vision-based methods struggle with transferring policies trained in simulation to real-world drones, especially visually. SkyDreamer aims for non-trivial visual sim-to-real transfer by operating on challenging, poor-quality segmentation masks.
- **Onboard Execution without External Aid:** Most prior end-to-end vision-based systems rely on offboard computation or motion capture (MoCap) systems during real-world flight. A key objective is to enable full autonomy by performing all computations onboard the drone.
- **Achieve Champion-Level Performance:** While some systems have achieved high performance, no end-to-end vision-based method has simultaneously demonstrated champion-level speeds, accelerations (e.g., up to 6g), and agile maneuvers (e.g., inverted loops, split-S) in the real world.
- **Direct Control Authority:** Unlike methods relying on intermediate inner-loop controllers (PID, INDI), SkyDreamer aims to map pixels directly to motor commands. This provides maximal control authority, potentially leading to more optimal and agile flight, and simplifies modeling.
- **Enhance Interpretability:** End-to-end learning models often act as "black boxes." A crucial objective is to improve interpretability by having the world model decode to privileged information (ground-truth states, drone parameters), effectively making it an implicit state and parameter estimator. This aids debugging and understanding.
- **Robustness and Adaptability:**
 - **Visual Ambiguity:** Address situations where different parts of a track appear visually similar, requiring the drone to understand its progress along the track.
 - **No Extrinsic Camera Calibration:** Eliminate the need for time-consuming and often unreliable extrinsic camera calibration, enabling rapid deployment across different drones without retraining.
 - **Dynamic Parameter Changes:** Account for real-world phenomena like battery depletion, which alters drone dynamics, by estimating drone-specific parameters on the fly.
 - **Robustness to Sensor Noise:** Operate without an accelerometer, which can saturate under high accelerations and vibrations.
- **Generalizability:** Lay the foundation for policies capable of flying arbitrary, unseen tracks with approximately known gate locations.

By achieving these objectives, SkyDreamer aims to advance autonomous drone racing beyond specialized, inflexible systems, towards truly adaptable, high-performance, and deployable intelligent agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SkyDreamer tackles the complex problem of end-to-end vision-based ADR by framing it as an **informed Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (informed POMDP)**. This framework allows leveraging privileged information (ground-truth states, camera/dynamic parameters) during training, which is unavailable during execution.

**A. Core Reinforcement Learning Framework:**
- **Model-Based RL (DreamerV3/Informed Dreamer):** SkyDreamer is built upon DreamerV3, a state-of-the-art model-based RL algorithm, extended with concepts from Informed Dreamer. This approach involves learning a "world model" that captures environment dynamics and allows for efficient actor training through latent imagination (simulating rollouts in a learned latent space).
- **Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM):** The world model's core is an RSSM, comprising:
 - **Encoder:** Maps observations ($o_t$) and hidden states ($h_t$) to a discrete stochastic latent representation ($z_t$). For image observations, this uses a CNN.
 - **Sequence Model:** A GRU that predicts the next hidden state ($h_t$) from previous hidden states, latent states, and actions.
 - **Dynamics Predictor:** Predicts future latent representations ($\hat{z}_t$) solely from the hidden state, enabling latent imagination without environmental feedback.
- **Decoding to Privileged Information:** A key innovation from Informed Dreamer and central to SkyDreamer is the decoder. Instead of only reconstructing raw observations, the decoder is trained to reconstruct *privileged information* ($i_t$), which includes ground-truth states (position, velocity, orientation), camera extrinsics, and dynamic parameters. This forces the world model to learn meaningful and task-relevant latent features, improving interpretability and parameter estimation.
- **Actor-Critic Learning:** An actor (Gaussian policy) and critic (value estimator) are trained using the Reinforce estimator, entirely in latent space via imagined rollouts generated by the world model.
- **Direct Motor Commands:** Actions ($u_t$) are normalized motor commands for each rotor, directly specifying desired motor power, bypassing intermediate inner-loop controllers.

**B. Observations, State, and Reward:**
- **State Space (S):** Includes world and gate-frame positions and velocities, world and gate-frame orientations (Euler angles), body rates, propeller speeds, camera extrinsics, randomized dynamic parameters, and disturbances.
- **Observations (O):** Binary segmentation mask (X) of gates, measured body rates (IMU), and measured propeller angular velocities (ESC). Significantly, accelerometers are *not* used to avoid saturation issues.
- **Information Variable (I):** During training, this includes ground-truth states, camera extrinsics, and dynamic parameters.
- **Reward Function:** Comprises a progress reward (encouraging movement towards the next gate), a rate penalty (discouraging excessive angular velocities), and a gate reward (maximal at the center of the gate, decreasing towards edges). Crucially, there is *no explicit perception reward*; natural camera orientation towards gates emerges. Episode termination occurs on gate or ground collision.

**C. Sim-to-Real Transfer and Robustness Mechanisms:**
- **Quadcopter Dynamics:** An accurate physics model, including gyroscopic effects, drag, and randomized dynamic parameters ($d$), is implemented in JAX for simulation training. Motor response is modeled with specific parameters, including maximum RPM ($\omega_{max}$).
- **Segmentation Masks:**
 - **Intrinsic Calibration:** Onboard camera images are undistorted and mapped to a fixed-resolution image with a nominal intrinsic matrix, ensuring consistent inputs.
 - **GateNet:** A U-Net based CNN generates binary segmentation masks (X) from the fixed-resolution images. SkyDreamer is intentionally tested with poor-quality GateNet masks to demonstrate robustness to perceptual errors.
- **Visual Augmentations:** To bridge the visual reality gap between idealized simulated masks and noisy real-world masks:
 - **StochGAN (Stochastic CycleGAN):** Translates synthetic segmentation masks to more realistic ones and vice versa without paired data, introducing variation and realism.
 - **Mask Erosion:** Random erosion of masks simulates thinner real-life gate appearances.
 - **Rolling Shutter Effect:** An affine transformation is applied to masks to model horizontal shear (yaw rate) and vertical scaling (pitch rate) caused by the rolling shutter camera.
- **Flight Plan Logic:** To resolve visual ambiguities (visually identical observations requiring different actions), the policy is augmented with a structured flight plan vector ($f$). This vector encodes relative positions and yaw angles of upcoming gates (next three) and their absolute positions/yaws in the world frame. The flight plan index is incremented based on the world model's *decoded* gate-relative position ($\hat{x}_g$).
- **Smoothness Regularization:** A smoothness loss is applied to the mean of the policy output during actor-critic learning to prevent bang-bang control behavior and encourage smoother motor commands, which is safer and more realistic for physical systems.

**D. Deployment:**
- The trained policy is converted from JAX to PyTorch, then exported to ONNX, and optimized with TensorRT for onboard execution on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX 16GB.
- The system uses a low-cost rolling shutter camera operating at 90 Hz, with simulated image (33 ms) and action (11 ms) delays to match real-world latency.

This comprehensive methodology allows SkyDreamer to learn complex, agile maneuvers, handle real-world challenges, and provide insights into its internal state estimation, all within an end-to-end, onboard framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SkyDreamer's performance was rigorously evaluated in both simulation and real-world experiments across various tracks and gate types, demonstrating significant advancements.

**A. Simulation Results (Ladder Inverted Loop Track):**
- **Agility and Speed:** SkyDreamer successfully executed tight maneuvers, including a ladder and an inverted loop, within a confined 6x4m area, reaching speeds up to 13 m/s and accelerations up to 6g.
- **State and Parameter Estimation:**
 - **Accuracy:** SkyDreamer's position and velocity estimates closely aligned with ground truth, converging rapidly (within 10-15 steps) and maintaining a standard deviation of 10-15 cm for position and 0.5 m/s for velocity.
 - **Parameter Estimation:** Crucially, SkyDreamer accurately estimated parameters vital for thrust response, such as thrust effectiveness ($k_w$) and maximum motor RPM ($\omega_{max}$). These converged quickly (within 50-100 steps, ~0.6-1.1 s), enabling adaptive high-speed flight.
 - **Camera Extrinsics:** Camera extrinsics were estimated with a standard deviation of approximately 1 degree, demonstrating accurate online calibration.
 - **Interpretability:** The ability to decode to states and parameters allowed for direct inspection of the world model's beliefs, providing invaluable debugging capabilities and insight into its internal representations, unlike black-box approaches.
- **Perceptual Behavior:** Despite the absence of an explicit perception reward, SkyDreamer naturally learned to orient its camera towards the gates, maximizing visibility—an emergent behavior.
- **Limitations:** Estimated states showed small, high-frequency jumps, possibly due to the discrete latent representations or incomplete physics modeling. Some dynamical parameters (e.g., drag, individual propeller responses) were harder to estimate accurately, and parameter estimates could exhibit drift over long flight sequences.

**B. Real-World Small Tracks (Inverted Loop, Ladder Inverted Loop with Orange and MAVLab Gates):**
- **100% Success Rate:** SkyDreamer achieved a 100% success rate across all 75 laps (5 flights of 5 laps each per track), demonstrating robust performance without crashing.
- **Champion-Level Agility:** Reached measured accelerations of up to 6g and estimated speeds up to 13 m/s in flight areas confined to 6x6m, matching state-of-the-art classical approaches.
- **Robustness to Battery Degradation:** During one flight with a depleting battery, SkyDreamer successfully adapted by accurately estimating the decreasing maximum motor RPM ($\hat{\omega}_{max}$, a 30% reduction outside training bounds). It adjusted its flight path and completed all laps, highlighting adaptive parameter estimation.
- **Non-Trivial Visual Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
 - **Orange Gates:** Achieved excellent performance with high-contrast, uniform orange gates.
 - **MAVLab Gates (Poor Quality Masks):** Crucially, SkyDreamer maintained 100% success even with MAVLab gates, where GateNet produced suboptimal, poor-quality segmentation masks due to challenging lighting, low contrast, and distracting backgrounds (Figure 7). This demonstrated robust operation despite significant perceptual errors and successfully bridged a non-trivial visual reality gap, largely attributed to the StochGAN and other visual augmentations.
- **Consistent Flight Paths:** Composite images of multiple laps showed that SkyDreamer consistently converged to the center of gates, demonstrating precise and repeatable navigation (Figure 6).

**C. Real-World Big Track (MAVLab Gates):**
- **Complex Layout Generalization:** SkyDreamer successfully flew a larger, more complex track with MAVLab gates, combining high-speed flight with challenging maneuvers (split-S, sharp turns, ladder).
- **Higher Speeds:** Achieved significantly higher estimated speeds of up to 21 m/s, while maintaining accelerations around 6g, demonstrating the ability to combine high speed with agility on a larger scale.
- **Robustness to Contact:** In several flights, minor gate contact was audible, but SkyDreamer continued successfully, showing resilience to unmodeled contact forces.
- **Extrinsic Sensitivity:** One crash occurred when the drone's camera extrinsics were outside the training distribution, indicating a limitation in generalization if critical parameters deviate too far from the trained range.

Overall, SkyDreamer convincingly demonstrated that an end-to-end vision-based policy can achieve robust, high-speed, agile, and interpretable flight in the real world, addressing several long-standing challenges in autonomous drone racing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SkyDreamer represents a significant leap forward in the field of autonomous drone racing and, more broadly, in autonomous agile flight. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **First-of-its-Kind End-to-End Performance:** SkyDreamer is, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the first end-to-end vision-based ADR policy to simultaneously achieve **full sim-to-real transfer, onboard execution, and champion-level performance** (up to 21 m/s and 6g accelerations) in the real world. This shatters previous limitations where systems either achieved high performance through classical means or struggled with real-world deployment for end-to-end approaches.
2. **Enhanced Interpretability via Informed Dreamer:** By extending DreamerV3 with the Informed Dreamer concept, SkyDreamer's world model effectively acts as an **implicit state and parameter estimator**. Decoding to privileged information (ground-truth states, camera extrinsics, dynamic parameters) makes the policy more transparent. This interpretability is crucial for debugging and understanding complex learned behaviors, moving away from black-box models common in end-to-end learning.
3. **Direct Motor Command Control:** By mapping pixels directly to motor commands, SkyDreamer maximizes control authority, enabling more agile and potentially optimal flight compared to systems relying on intermediate inner-loop controllers. This approach is highly relevant for high-performance aerial robotics and space applications where precise actuator control is critical.
4. **Robustness to Reality Gap Challenges:**
 - **Visual Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful operation with poor-quality, noisy segmentation masks (MAVLab gates) highlights SkyDreamer's robustness to the visual reality gap, enabled by sophisticated augmentations like StochGAN. This is a critical step towards generalization beyond idealized sensor inputs.
 - **Online Parameter Estimation:** The ability to estimate and adapt to changing drone parameters, such as battery degradation affecting maximum RPM, demonstrates a crucial level of real-world robustness. This reduces the need for constant recalibration and makes the system more practical for long-term deployment.
 - **No Extrinsic Camera Calibration:** Eliminating the need for manual extrinsic camera calibration streamlines deployment and makes the system more robust to slight physical changes (e.g., camera bumps).
 - **Visual Ambiguity Resolution:** The integrated flight plan logic effectively resolves visual ambiguities, laying groundwork for policies capable of flying arbitrary tracks rather than pre-memorized specific paths.
5. **Paving the Way for Generalizable Autonomy:** By demonstrating robustness to various aspects of the reality gap (visual perception, dynamic changes, uncalibrated sensors, ambiguous environments), SkyDreamer lays a strong foundation for future research into highly autonomous drones. This includes:
 - **Flying Unseen Tracks:** The flight plan logic and robust state estimation are key steps towards true generalization to novel track layouts.
 - **Unstructured Environments:** The successful operation on poor-quality segmentation masks hints at the potential for using more generalized pixel-level abstractions (e.g., depth maps) for navigation in complex, unstructured real-world environments.
 - **Hybrid Tasks:** The methodology could be extended to combine drone racing with other tasks like obstacle avoidance, leading to more versatile autonomous systems.

While limitations such as parameter drift, computational demands for training, and sensitivity to false positives in segmentation remain, SkyDreamer represents a significant milestone. It pushes end-to-end vision-based control into the realm of real-world, high-speed, agile, and interpretable flight, demonstrating a viable path towards truly adaptive and robust autonomous systems beyond the confines of specialized tasks.
