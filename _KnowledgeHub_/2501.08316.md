# Diffusion Adversarial Post-Training for One-Step Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ByteDance Seed developed Adversarial Post-Training (APT) to transform pre-trained diffusion models into one-step generators using adversarial fine-tuning against real data. This method allows one-step text-to-video generation for high-resolution videos (1280x720 at 24fps) in under 2 seconds, while enhancing visual fidelity in images by 37.2% compared to 25-step diffusion.

## Problem

- Iterative denoising in diffusion models makes high-fidelity image and video generation computationally expensive and slow, limiting real-time applications.
- Existing diffusion distillation methods for one-step generation often degrade output quality and are limited by the teacher model's performance, especially for high-resolution video.
- Directly applying adversarial training to large, transformer-based diffusion models typically leads to training instability and collapse.

## Method

- Introduces **APT** (==Adversarial Post-Training==), which fine-tunes a pre-trained text-to-video ==diffusion model== into a one-step generator using a ==generative adversarial objective== directly on real data.
- Initializes the generator with a ==consistency-distilled model== and the discriminator with the original diffusion network, enhancing stability through multi-layer architectural modifications.
- Employs an ==approximated R1 regularization== to prevent training collapse and stabilize adversarial learning in large, billion-parameter transformer models.

## Results

- Achieved the first reported one-step text-to-video generation for high-resolution (**1280x720 at 24fps**) videos, with a total inference time of **1.97 seconds** using 8 H100 GPUs for a 2-second clip.
- User studies demonstrated APT improves visual fidelity for images by **37.2%** and for videos by **10.4%** (1-step) to **32.3%** (2-step) compared to 25-step diffusion baselines.
- The approximated R1 regularization proved essential for stable training, preventing training collapse and the generation of visually distorted outputs in the billion-parameter adversarial network.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial training directly against real data can allow generated outputs to surpass the quality ceiling of multi-step diffusion teacher models, particularly in visual fidelity and tone.
> - Stable adversarial training for large transformer-based generators and discriminators is achievable through specific architectural modifications and a computationally efficient approximated R1 regularization.
> - The one-step generator's smoother transitions between semantic modes, while efficient, can lead to degradation in structural integrity and text alignment compared to iterative diffusion with Classifier-Free Guidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI, particularly image and video synthesis, has seen significant advancements with the advent of diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020). These models are capable of generating high-fidelity content but are computationally intensive due to their iterative denoising process. A major area of research focuses on accelerating these models, with "diffusion step distillation" being a prominent technique.

Existing distillation methods for image generation typically involve training a "student" model to emulate the output of a pre-trained "teacher" diffusion model using fewer inference steps. While notable progress has been made in achieving one-step image generation through such methods (Ren et al., 2024; Yin et al., 2024a; Sauer et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2024), challenges persist in maintaining fine-grained details, minimizing artifacts, and preserving structural integrity. These methods often treat the teacher model as an upper bound for quality.

In contrast, accelerated video generation has seen more limited development. Early efforts with Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) (Goodfellow et al., 2014), such as StyleGAN-V (Skorokhodov et al., 2021), were capable of generating high-resolution videos but were often restricted to specific domains and exhibited lower quality by contemporary standards. More recent work has attempted to extend image distillation techniques to video diffusion models, but these have largely focused on smaller scales and lower resolutions (e.g., 512x512 videos for 16 frames) or still require multiple steps (Lin & Yang, 2024; Zhai et al., 2024; Yin et al., 2024b). Generating high-resolution videos (e.g., 1280x720 at 24fps for several seconds) remains computationally demanding, often requiring minutes on advanced GPUs even with iterative diffusion.

This work introduces Adversarial Post-Training (APT) as an alternative to diffusion distillation. Unlike distillation approaches that rely on a diffusion teacher to generate targets, APT initializes a pre-trained diffusion model and then performs adversarial training directly against real data. This paradigm shifts the quality ceiling from the teacher model to the real data distribution, offering the potential to surpass the teacher's performance. The paper addresses the inherent instability of directly applying adversarial training to large transformer-based diffusion models, a known challenge in the broader landscape of generative modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for one-step, high-resolution image and, more significantly, video generation, thereby addressing the computational inefficiency of iterative diffusion models. The specific goals include:

1. **Enabling One-Step Generation:** To transform a pre-trained diffusion model into a generator that can produce high-quality images and videos in a single forward pass, significantly reducing inference time and computational cost.
2. **Achieving High-Resolution Video Generation:** To demonstrate one-step generation for high-resolution videos (specifically 1280x720 at 24 frames per second for a duration of two seconds), surpassing the capabilities of existing few-step or domain-specific video generation methods.
3. **Improving Generation Quality:** To overcome the quality degradation often observed in diffusion distillation methods for one-step generation, particularly in terms of fine-grained details, realism, and exposure, even potentially surpassing the quality of the original multi-step diffusion teacher.
4. **Ensuring Training Stability:** To develop and integrate mechanisms that stabilize the adversarial training process, which is inherently prone to collapse when applied to large generator and discriminator models (both transformer-based with billions of parameters).

The motivation stems from several factors:
- **Computational Cost of Diffusion Models:** The iterative nature of diffusion models makes them slow and resource-intensive, especially for high-resolution video, limiting their real-time application.
- **Limitations of Existing Distillation:** While diffusion step distillation has accelerated image generation, it often suffers from quality degradation for one-step outputs and is constrained by the teacher's performance. For video, progress in distilling large-scale, high-resolution models for one-step generation has been limited.
- **Need for Real-Time High-Resolution Video:** The prohibitive computational cost of generating high-resolution videos (e.g., 1280x720 at 24fps) highlights a significant gap that real-time, one-step generation could fill.
- **Potential to Exceed Teacher Quality:** The authors hypothesize that adversarial training directly on real data, rather than distilled teacher outputs, can yield higher quality samples by allowing the model to learn the true data distribution more effectively, resolving issues like over-exposure that can arise from Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Adversarial Post-Training (APT), converts a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model into a one-step generator by fine-tuning it with a generative adversarial objective against real data.

#### 3.1. Overview

APT operates as a min-max game between a generator (G) and a discriminator (D). The generator aims to produce samples that are indistinguishable from real data, while the discriminator aims to differentiate between real and generated samples. The underlying model architecture for both the generator and discriminator is the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) (Peebles & Xie, 2023), specifically an MMDiT architecture (Esser et al., 2024) trained with a flow-matching objective (Lipman et al., 2023) on a mixture of images and videos. This architecture consists of 36 transformer layers and 8 billion parameters. Training is conducted in the latent space (Rombach et al., 2021).

#### 3.2. Generator

Direct adversarial training on the diffusion model tends to be unstable. To mitigate this, the generator (G) is initialized using a model pre-trained via consistency distillation (Song et al., 2023; Song & Dhariwal, 2024) with a mean squared error loss. This "distilled model" ($\hat{G}$) predicts a velocity field ($\hat{v}$) from a noise sample (`z`) and text condition (`c`), which is then converted into a sample prediction ($\hat{x}$). Although the outputs of this intermediate consistency model can be blurry, this initialization provides a stable starting point for the subsequent adversarial training. During adversarial training, the generator operates in one step, always using the final timestep (T).

#### 3.3. Discriminator

The discriminator (D) is tasked with distinguishing real latent samples (`x`) from generated ones ($\hat{x}$). Several design choices are incorporated to enhance training stability and output quality:
- **Initialization:** The discriminator backbone is initialized using the weights of the *original pre-trained diffusion network* (not the consistency-distilled model used for the generator), and all its parameters are trained without freezing. It also uses the 36-layer, 8-billion parameter transformer architecture.
- **Architectural Modifications:** To produce a scalar logit, new cross-attention-only transformer blocks are introduced at specific layers (16th, 26th, and 36th) of the discriminator backbone. Each block uses a single learnable token as a query, attending to all visual tokens from the backbone to output a single token. These tokens are concatenated, normalized, and projected to yield the final scalar logit. Using features from multiple layers is found to improve the structure and composition of generated samples.
- **Direct Input and Timestep Ensemble:** The discriminator is fed raw, uncorrupted samples directly (without noise corruptions). To compensate for the diffusion model's pre-training objective not being meaningful at `t=0`, an ensemble of different shifted timestep values is used as input to the discriminator. Specifically, `t` is sampled uniformly from [0, T] and then transformed by a `shift` function (Equation 7), with `s=1` for images and `s=12` for videos. A single `t` is sampled per training sample for efficiency.

#### 3.4. Regularized Discriminator

To address the stability issues inherent in training a large, billion-parameter discriminator, an approximated R1 regularization (Roth et al., 2017) is introduced. Standard R1 regularization, which penalizes the discriminator's gradient on real data ($\parallel\nabla_x D(x,c)\parallel_2^2$), is computationally expensive and not well-supported by modern deep learning frameworks (e.g., PyTorch FSDP, gradient checkpointing) for higher-order gradient computation in large transformer models.
The proposed approximated R1 loss ($L_{aR1}$) is defined as $\parallel D(x,c) - D(\mathcal{N}(x, \sigma I), c) \parallel_2^2$, where `x` is perturbed with Gaussian noise of small variance $\sigma$. This encourages the discriminator's predictions to be consistent between real data and its slight perturbations, effectively reducing the discriminator gradient on real data and stabilizing training. The final discriminator loss $L_D$ combines the adversarial loss with this approximated R1 term. Hyperparameters are $\lambda = 100$, $\sigma = 0.01$ for images, and $\sigma = 0.1$ for videos.

#### 3.5. Training Details

Training proceeds in two main stages:
1. **Image Training:** The model is first trained on 1024px images using 128-256 H100 GPUs with a batch size of 9062. A learning rate of 5e-6 is used for both generator and discriminator. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) decay rate of 0.995 is applied to the generator, with the EMA checkpoint adopted after 350 updates.
2. **Video Training:** The generator is then initialized from the image EMA checkpoint, while the discriminator is re-initialized from the original diffusion weights. Training focuses on 1280x720 24fps videos (clipped to 2 seconds). This stage uses 1024 H100 GPUs with gradient accumulation for a batch size of 2048. The learning rate is reduced to 3e-6 for stability, and training continues for 300 updates.
RMSProp optimizer (equivalent to Adam with $\beta_1=0, \beta_2=0.9$) is used, and training is conducted in BF16 mixed precision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research empirically evaluates the Adversarial Post-Training (APT) method through qualitative comparisons, user studies, and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Evaluation

- **Image Generation:** The APT model, in a single step, produces images with a more realistic tone and enhanced fine details compared to the original 25-step diffusion model, which often generates over-exposed and synthetic-looking images, especially when using Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG). Compared to other state-of-the-art one-step image generation methods, APT demonstrates advantages in preserving details and structural integrity.
- **Video Generation:** For videos, one-step and two-step APT results show improvements in details and realism over 25-step diffusion. However, these few-step generations exhibit degradation in structural integrity and text alignment, particularly in complex scenes involving motion. The paper presents examples of successful cases (enhanced realism), average cases (scene produced but with degradation), and failure cases (significant prompt misalignment).

#### 4.2. User Study

Human raters evaluated models based on visual fidelity (texture, details, color, exposure, realism), structural integrity (correctness of objects/body parts), and text alignment (closeness to prompt).
- **Image Generation: One-Step vs. 25-Step Diffusion:** All existing one-step methods showed degradation across all three criteria compared to their 25-step diffusion counterparts. Notably, APT was the only method that achieved a more favorable evaluation in visual fidelity (+37.2%) compared to its own 25-step diffusion model. APT exhibited degradation in structural integrity and text alignment, but generally less severe than most other methods.
- **One-Step Image Generation: Comparison to State-of-the-Art:** APT achieved performance comparable to state-of-the-art one-step image generation models, ranking second in absolute preference and first in relative preference (adjusted for base model performance). It was preferred for visual fidelity and structural integrity but performed less favorably in text alignment.
- **One-Step Video Generation:** Similar to images, APT's one-step and two-step video generation demonstrated improved visual fidelity (+10.4% for 1-step, +32.3% for 2-step) compared to the 25-step diffusion baseline. However, degradation in structural integrity and text alignment was more pronounced in videos. Despite these degradations, the generated 1280x720 videos maintained decent quality.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Discussion

- **Effect of Approximated R1 Regularization:** This regularization was found to be critical for stable training. Without it, the discriminator loss rapidly approached zero, leading to training collapse and the generator producing visually collapsed outputs (e.g., colored plates).
- **Discriminator Design:** Experiments showed that a deeper discriminator, utilizing the full depth of the pre-trained network, led to better image quality. Additionally, employing multilayer features (from the 16th, 26th, and 36th layers) improved structural correctness compared to using only the last layer.
- **Visual Improvement Mechanism:** The visual improvements, particularly in realism and tone, are attributed to APT learning distributions closer to real data. The adversarial objective acts as a perceptual critic, addressing issues like over-exposure and over-saturation that can arise from Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in diffusion models.
- **Structural Degradation Cause:** Analysis of latent traversals revealed that the one-step generator has a smoother transition between semantic modes compared to the sharper switches of diffusion models. This limited capacity to make abrupt changes is hypothesized to be a primary cause of structural incorrectness and occasional hallucinations during mode transitions.
- **Text Alignment Degradation:** The weaker text alignment in APT models compared to CFG-boosted diffusion models is explained by the fact that CFG can push generated distributions towards "canonical" (stronger alignment than real) samples. APT, learning from the real data distribution without CFG's boosting effect, may have naturally weaker alignment, exacerbated by structural degradation issues. Explorations into methods like unmatched conditional pairs or CLIP loss for improved alignment yielded minimal or negative results.

#### 4.4. Inference Speed

The model can generate a 2-second 1280x720 24fps video latent in 6.03 seconds on a single H100 GPU. With 8 H100 GPUs and parallelization, the entire pipeline (text encoder, DiT, VAE) achieves a total inference time of 1.97 seconds, enabling real-time high-resolution video generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in the field of generative AI by demonstrating the feasibility of one-step, high-resolution video generation.
- **Pioneering One-Step High-Resolution Video:** The primary significance lies in being, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the first demonstration of one-step text-to-video generation for high-resolution videos (1280x720 at 24fps), moving beyond previous efforts that were limited to lower resolutions or required multiple steps. This directly addresses the significant computational cost associated with high-fidelity video synthesis.
- **Alternative to Diffusion Distillation:** The introduction of Adversarial Post-Training (APT) offers a novel paradigm for accelerating diffusion models. By training directly against real data rather than distilled teacher outputs, APT has the potential to overcome the quality ceiling imposed by teacher models, leading to outputs that can be more realistic and detailed than the original multi-step diffusion output.
- **Stable Adversarial Training for Large Models:** The proposed architectural enhancements for the discriminator and, critically, the approximated R1 regularization method, provide practical solutions for stabilizing adversarial training in extremely large transformer models. This contribution is significant for enabling the development of large-scale GANs, which are often prone to training instability.
- **Real-Time Generation Potential:** Achieving near real-time generation of 2-second high-resolution videos (under 2 seconds with 8 H100 GPUs) marks a step towards practical applications in areas requiring rapid content creation, such as film production, gaming, and interactive media.

**Limitations:**
Despite these advancements, the authors identify several limitations:
- **Video Duration:** Due to computational constraints, the model was only trained to generate videos up to two seconds in duration.
- **Structural Integrity and Text Alignment Degradation:** One-step generation still exhibits degradation in structural integrity and text alignment, particularly for video, which is a known challenge for few-step generative models.
- **Automatic Metric Inaccuracy:** The paper highlights that traditional quantitative metrics like FID, PFID, and CLIP scores often inaccurately capture the actual perceptual quality and performance, especially when comparing across different base models or steps, necessitating reliance on extensive user studies.

Future work is aimed at addressing the issues of structural integrity and text alignment degradation to further enhance the quality and robustness of one-step video generation.
