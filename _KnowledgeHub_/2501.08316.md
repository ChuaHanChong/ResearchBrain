# Diffusion Adversarial Post-Training for One-Step Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ByteDance Seed developed Adversarial Post-Training (APT) to transform pre-trained diffusion models into one-step generators using adversarial fine-tuning against real data. This method allows one-step text-to-video generation for high-resolution videos (1280x720 at 24fps) in under 2 seconds, while enhancing visual fidelity in images by 37.2% compared to 25-step diffusion.

## Problem

- Iterative denoising in diffusion models makes high-fidelity image and video generation computationally expensive and slow, limiting real-time applications.
- Existing diffusion distillation methods for one-step generation often degrade output quality and are limited by the teacher model's performance, especially for high-resolution video.
- Directly applying adversarial training to large, transformer-based diffusion models typically leads to training instability and collapse.

## Method

- Introduces **APT** (==Adversarial Post-Training==), which fine-tunes a pre-trained text-to-video ==diffusion model== into a one-step generator using a ==generative adversarial objective== directly on real data.
- Initializes the generator with a ==consistency-distilled model== and the discriminator with the original diffusion network, enhancing stability through multi-layer architectural modifications.
- Employs an ==approximated R1 regularization== to prevent training collapse and stabilize adversarial learning in large, billion-parameter transformer models.

## Results

- Achieved the first reported one-step text-to-video generation for high-resolution (**1280x720 at 24fps**) videos, with a total inference time of **1.97 seconds** using 8 H100 GPUs for a 2-second clip.
- User studies demonstrated APT improves visual fidelity for images by **37.2%** and for videos by **10.4%** (1-step) to **32.3%** (2-step) compared to 25-step diffusion baselines.
- The approximated R1 regularization proved essential for stable training, preventing training collapse and the generation of visually distorted outputs in the billion-parameter adversarial network.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial training directly against real data can allow generated outputs to surpass the quality ceiling of multi-step diffusion teacher models, particularly in visual fidelity and tone.
> - Stable adversarial training for large transformer-based generators and discriminators is achievable through specific architectural modifications and a computationally efficient approximated R1 regularization.
> - The one-step generator's smoother transitions between semantic modes, while efficient, can lead to degradation in structural integrity and text alignment compared to iterative diffusion with Classifier-Free Guidance.
