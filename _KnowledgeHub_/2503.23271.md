# Learning Coordinated Bimanual Manipulation Policies using State Diffusion and Inverse Dynamics Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign developed a framework for coordinated bimanual robotic manipulation that explicitly disentangles state prediction using a diffusion model from inverse dynamics modeling. This approach, which models how object states evolve and then computes actions to achieve those states, achieved higher success rates and better generalization in complex simulated and real-world bimanual tasks involving deformable and multiple objects.

## Problem

- Learning coordinated robotic policies for bimanual manipulation, particularly for tasks involving deformable or multiple objects, remains challenging due to difficulties in cooperative force distribution and intricate spatial planning.
- Current imitation learning methods often struggle to generalize to multimodal goal configurations or unseen interactions, and may not fully capture complex object dynamics.
- Robots lack the ability to accurately predict how objects will move or interact under their actions, which is essential for planning long-horizon, precise manipulations.

## Method

- A novel imitation learning framework is proposed that explicitly disentangles state prediction from inverse dynamics modeling.
- A Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) is used to predict future object states, learning how the world evolves from historical observations.
- An inverse dynamics model, implemented as a multi-layer perceptron (MLP), translates the predicted future states into precise robot actions, with both components jointly optimized using human demonstration data.

## Results

- The framework achieved superior success rates in simulation (e.g., 29.3% in Franka Kitchen vs. baselines, 79.3% in Push-L), and demonstrated enhanced data efficiency compared to state-of-the-art imitation learning methods.
- It exhibited robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for 2D pushing and outperformed baselines in complex 3D bimanual real-world tasks involving deformable and multiple objects (e.g., 15/15 for Laundry Cleanup's first pillow vs. 0/15 for baseline on second pillow).
- The policy showed improved generalization, adaptability to out-of-distribution initial conditions, and robustness to human perturbations, indicating the synthesis of complex, emergent behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling object movement via a state prediction diffusion model provides a robust feedback mechanism, directly signaling coordination failures more effectively than action-based losses.
> - Disentangling state prediction from inverse dynamics allows for a deeper understanding of object behavior and enables more effective learning of bimanual coordination policies.
> - Anticipating future object states, mirroring human cognitive processes, is crucial for achieving stable and precise bimanual control in complex manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, yet complex bimanual tasks remain a significant challenge. This work addresses a critical gap in learning robotic policies for coordinated bimanual manipulation, particularly for tasks involving deformable or multiple objects, which are common in human daily life but difficult for robots to execute with stability and precision.

**Current Landscape Challenges:**
- **Single-arm vs. Bimanual Tasks:** While single-arm manipulation has seen considerable progress, bimanual tasks introduce complexities like cooperative force distribution, intricate spatial planning, and interaction-aware control, making end-to-end learning difficult.
- **Limitations of Imitation Learning (LfD):** Many state-of-the-art imitation learning methods rely on direct state-to-action mapping. While successful in many scenarios, these methods often struggle with generalizing to multimodal goal configurations, unseen interactions, and representing complex object dynamics. They may learn to mimic actions without truly "understanding" the underlying physics or object behavior.
- **Model Learning in Robotics:** The development of dynamics models (especially data-driven ones) is crucial for complex robotic tasks. These models aim to predict how objects will respond to robot actions. While successful in modeling various dynamics (deformable, articulated, granular objects), they often require expert design and tend to be task-specific, making generalization challenging. They are typically used within Model Predictive Control (MPC) or to accelerate Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- **Emergence of Diffusion Models in Robotics:** Diffusion models, which have shown remarkable success in generative AI (e.g., image, video generation), are increasingly being adapted for robotics. They offer expressiveness for modeling complex data distributions, including action sequences and state trajectories.

**How This Work Contributes and Differentiates:**
This paper proposes a novel imitation learning framework that explicitly infuses the "predictive nature" of human manipulation strategies into robotic control, directly addressing the limitations of existing approaches.
- **Bridging the Gap:** Unlike traditional end-to-end state-to-action imitation learning, this work *disentangles* state prediction from inverse dynamics modeling. This explicit modeling of future object states and actions is inspired by human cognitive processes, where anticipating object movement precedes action execution.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** By training a diffusion model to predict *future states* and then using an inverse dynamics model to compute actions to achieve those states, the framework gains a deeper understanding of object movement and agent-object interactions. This leads to improved generalization capabilities across multimodal goal configurations and action distributions, which is a significant advancement over methods that simply map states to actions.
- **Robustness via State-centric Feedback:** The key insight is that modeling object movement (state) provides a more effective feedback mechanism than purely action-based losses. If a robot drops an object, a state prediction model registers a large "state loss," directly indicating a coordination failure, whereas a state-to-action model might show only a small "action loss" if the robot's *movements* were superficially similar. This allows the system to capture and correct critical coordination issues more effectively.
- **Distinction from Prior Diffusion Robotics:** The paper clearly differentiates its approach from other applications of diffusion models in robotics:
 - It avoids the need for reward labeling and the extrapolation errors associated with offline RL (e.g., Ajay et al.), making it more suitable for reward-free imitation learning.
 - It goes beyond pure video generation (e.g., Du et al.) by focusing on precise, low-level interaction information (contact dynamics, deformation, force application) critical for dexterous manipulation, and, crucially, demonstrates real-world robotic deployment.
- **Joint Optimization:** The framework's strength lies in its joint optimization of both the state prediction and inverse dynamics models using human demonstrations, allowing it to learn comprehensive knowledge about both object dynamics and the actions required to influence them. This integrated approach enhances versatility and applicability in diverse robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that humans effortlessly perform complex bimanual manipulation tasks, such as laundry or cooking, by naturally coordinating both hands and anticipating how their actions will alter the state of objects. This predictive reasoning is a cornerstone of human dexterity and coordination. In contrast, robots struggle with these tasks due to several interlinked challenges:
1. **Modeling Object Movement:** Accurately predicting how objects, especially deformable or multiple objects, will move and interact under robotic actions is highly complex.
2. **Predicting Future States:** Robots need the ability to "envision" the scene's evolution to plan effective long-horizon actions.
3. **Generating Precise Bimanual Actions:** Coordinating two manipulators requires precise spatial planning, force distribution, and interaction-aware control.

**The key objectives of this work are to:**
- **Infuse Predictive Nature into Imitation Learning:** Develop an imitation learning framework that explicitly incorporates state prediction, mirroring human-like anticipatory reasoning for manipulation.
- **Disentangle State Transition and Inverse Dynamics:** Separate the learning of how the world (object states) evolves from how the robot's actions (inverse dynamics) cause those changes. The hypothesis is that this disentanglement enables more effective bimanual coordination.
- **Improve Bimanual Coordination:** Achieve robust, stable, and precise bimanual control for complex tasks that involve multimodal goal configurations, deformable objects, and multi-object interactions.
- **Enhance Generalization and Data Efficiency:** Create a policy that can generalize beyond the specific demonstrations and synthesize a broader range of behaviors, while also being data-efficient, which is crucial for real-world robotic deployments where data collection is expensive.
- **Validate in Simulation and Real-World:** Demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed method against state-of-the-art baselines in diverse simulation environments and, critically, in challenging real-world bimanual manipulation tasks.

The core insight driving these objectives is that **explicitly modeling object movements (via state prediction) significantly aids in learning policies for bimanual coordination.** By making the evolution of the object's state a direct target of prediction, the model can inherently "understand" and penalize coordination failures (e.g., dropping an object) more effectively than policies that only focus on mimicking actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, as illustrated in Figure 2, strategically decouples the learning process into two principal components: a state prediction model and an inverse dynamics model, which are then jointly optimized.

#### 3.1. State Prediction Diffusion Model

- **Model Type:** The framework utilizes a variant of the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) to predict the future states of the world. This choice is motivated by the strong performance of diffusion models in generating high-quality sequences and their ability to handle multimodal data distributions, which is essential for varied manipulation outcomes.
- **Input and Output:** Given a history of world states $S_{t-T_s:t-1}$, the diffusion model's objective is to predict the future state $S_t$. During inference, it takes a noisy input $S_k^t$ (Gaussian noise added to the true state) and iteratively denoises it over $K$ steps to reconstruct the true state $S_0^t$.
- **Loss Function:** The model is trained using an $L_2$ loss between the actual noise component $\epsilon_k$ and the model’s estimated noise $\epsilon_\theta$. This directs the model to accurately estimate and remove noise, thus predicting the underlying true state sequence.
- **Significance:** This component is crucial because it explicitly models how objects move and interact. As highlighted by the authors, if an object is mishandled (e.g., dropped), the state prediction loss will be significantly large, effectively signaling a coordination failure. This is a key advantage over state-to-action mapping methods, where similar robot trajectories might have small action losses even if the object is dropped, masking critical failures. The state prediction network is based on temporal convolutional neural networks, similar to those used in Diffusion Policy and Janner et al..

#### 3.2. Inverse Dynamics Model

- **Purpose:** This model translates the predicted future states from the diffusion model into executable robot actions.
- **Input and Output:** Unlike standard inverse dynamics models that typically take two consecutive states ($s_t$ and $s_{t+1}$) to predict an action, this modified model takes a *sequence* of historical states ($s_{t-T_h+1:t}$) and future states ($s_{t+1:t+T_f}$) to determine the action $a_t$. This allows the model to leverage temporal context for more informed action generation.
- **Architecture:** The inverse dynamics model is implemented as a multi-layer perceptron (MLP).
- **Significance:** This component ensures that the robot's actions are precisely aligned with the envisioned state transitions. The authors emphasize that its explicit inclusion is vital, as simply relying on the state prediction model to output pusher positions is insufficient to capture the nuanced contact dynamics and agent-object interactions necessary for precise manipulation.

#### 3.3. Policy Composition and Training

- **Joint Optimization:** The framework's total loss ($L$) is a weighted sum of the state prediction loss ($L_{pred}$) and the inverse dynamics loss ($L_{InvDyn}$, an MSE loss between actual and predicted actions). This joint optimization ensures that both the prediction of future states and the generation of actions to achieve those states are learned coherently from human demonstration data.
- **Data Source:** Both models are trained on demonstration datasets, with states randomly sampled from trajectories.
- **Action Consistency:** By predicting actions over an execution horizon ($T_a$), the method encourages temporal consistency in decision-making.

#### 3.4. Real-World Adaptation and Perception Pipeline

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (Push-L):** For the 2D Push-L task, the policy trained solely in simulation is transferred to a real UR5e robot. This involves a perception pipeline that extracts 2D keypoints of objects from real-world camera feeds, transforms them into the simulation coordinate system as input, and then translates the model's simulated actions back into real-world robot commands.
 - **Perception Module:** This module uses four RealSense D415 cameras to capture RGB-D data. Point clouds are generated, aggregated, cropped, and processed using color segmentation, voxel downsampling, outlier removal, normal computation, surface matching, and Iterative Closest Point (ICP) registration to estimate real-time object poses.
- **3D Bimanual Experiments:** For complex 3D tasks, 200 human demonstrations were collected using a Gello teleoperation system on two UR5e robots. The robots were equipped with soft grippers (TPU + anti-slip tape) to handle deformable objects. Observations were point clouds of the scene and robots, and actions involved controlling joint positions and grippers. This extensive real-world data collection emphasizes the practical application of the method.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed framework in both simulated and real-world environments, consistently demonstrating its superior performance over state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarking

The framework was evaluated against Diffusion Policy (DP), Improved Diffusion Policy (IDP), and RNN-GMM baselines across three challenging tasks: Multimodal Block Pushing, Franka Kitchen, and Push-L, considering both position (Pos) and velocity (Vel) control modes.

- **Capability for Task Behavior Synthesis (Franka Kitchen - Table I):**
 - Our method significantly outperformed baselines, achieving a 29.3% success rate in completing 5 out of 7 tasks in position control. Notably, the training dataset only contained trajectories showing success for up to 4 tasks. This highlights the model's ability to **synthesize complex behaviors that extend beyond the explicit scope of demonstrations**, showcasing advanced adaptive and intelligent control in multi-stage environments.
- **Necessity of an Explicit Inverse Dynamics Model (Push-L - Table III):**
 - The proposed framework achieved a 79.3% success rate in the Push-L task. When the inverse dynamics model was removed ("Ours w/o Inv"), the success rate dropped significantly to 61.3%. This result underscores the **critical importance of the explicit inverse dynamics model** in capturing the nuances of agent-object interactions and contact dynamics, which are not fully addressed by state prediction alone.
- **Impact of Control Mode on Policy Performance (Block Push - Table II):**
 - Our method consistently outperformed both DP and IDP in the Multimodal Block Pushing task across both position and velocity control modes. Crucially, our approach maintained stability between these control modes, avoiding the performance degradation observed in end-to-end diffusion policies. This demonstrates the framework's **robustness to different control interfaces**.
- **Data Efficiency (Figure 4):**
 - The proposed method consistently outperformed the Improved Diffusion Policy across all dataset sizes. For example, with 200 demonstration trajectories, our method achieved a success rate of 0.793, compared to 0.502 for IDP. This **superior data efficiency** is attributed to the integration of more comprehensive supervision signals during training, enabling more effective learning from limited data.

#### 4.2. Real-World Bimanual Coordination Experiments

The framework was further validated through zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for the Push-L task and by training on human demonstrations for complex 3D bimanual tasks.

- **2D Push-L Experiment (Real-World Sim-to-Real):**
 - **Quantitative Results:** Our method achieved 9 out of 12 successful outcomes in diverse initial conditions (Figure 7b), significantly outperforming the Improved Diffusion model, which succeeded in only 2 of 12 trials. This demonstrates the model's capability to **synthesize effective plans for object movement** and translate them into successful real-world actions.
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 5):** The model demonstrated intelligent planning by first pushing an orange block to form a joint rectangle with a blue block before efficiently moving the combined unit to the target. In contrast, the baseline often over-rotated and failed.
- **3D Bimanual Experiments (Laundry Cleanup, Fruit Holding, Cluttered Shelf Picking - Table IV):**
 - **Laundry Cleanup:** Our method achieved near-perfect scores for moving the first pillow (15/15) and strong performance for the second (8/15), while IDP failed completely on the second pillow (0/15). This indicates high effectiveness in **whole-body control and modeling interactions with soft, deformable objects**.
 - **Fruit Holding:** Our method significantly outperformed IDP across all fruit transport stages (e.g., 15/15 for p1, p2, p3 vs. IDP's lower scores), demonstrating **robustness in handling multiple, varying-sized objects**.
 - **Cluttered Shelf Picking:** Our method showed superior performance (14/15 successful trials vs. IDP's 0/15), highlighting its ability to model complex **gripper-object interactions and spatial awareness in densely packed environments**, crucial for preventing non-target object disturbance.
 - **Qualitative Failure Cases (Figure 6):** Baselines typically failed by dropping objects or missing targets due to an inability to accurately capture object movements and multi-object interaction dynamics.
 - **Generalization and Robustness Testing (Figure 11, 12):** Our policy adapted successfully to out-of-distribution initial conditions (e.g., only one pillow instead of two) and demonstrated remarkable robustness to human perturbations (e.g., returning a pillow to the table after it was placed in the basket), showcasing **emergent behavior and adaptability to unforeseen circumstances**.

#### 4.3. Runtime and Training Time Analysis

- **Runtime Cost (Table VI):** The proposed model achieved comparable inference times to baseline diffusion policies across all tasks, indicating that the increased complexity of the model does not hinder real-time deployment.
- **Training Time (Table VII):** The training time for our model was longer than for the baselines (e.g., 706 minutes for Franka Kitchen vs. 246 minutes for Diffusion Policy). This is attributed to the larger state space being modeled, which requires more computational effort during training.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that by disentangling state prediction from inverse dynamics and focusing on modeling object movements, the proposed framework achieves significantly higher success rates, better generalization, superior robustness to control modes and perturbations, and the ability to synthesize complex behaviors, all while maintaining comparable inference speeds.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a substantial advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for complex bimanual tasks. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

- **Enabling Advanced Bimanual Coordination:** The core contribution of disentangling state prediction from inverse dynamics modeling provides a powerful paradigm for bimanual robots to achieve unprecedented levels of coordination. This is crucial for tasks requiring cooperative force control, precise spatial planning, and handling of delicate or deformable objects, areas where robots have historically struggled. The ability to implicitly "anticipate" object behavior, much like humans, allows for more robust and dexterous manipulation.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Imitation Learning:** By explicitly modeling object states and their evolution, the framework addresses fundamental limitations of traditional end-to-end state-to-action mapping in imitation learning. It enhances generalization to multimodal goal configurations and action distributions, allowing robots to achieve tasks in varied ways, even synthesizing behaviors beyond those explicitly shown in demonstrations. This makes imitation learning more powerful and applicable to a wider range of scenarios.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The demonstrated success in zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, adaptation to out-of-distribution initial conditions, and robustness to human perturbations are pivotal. These capabilities are critical for deploying robots safely and reliably in unstructured, dynamic real-world environments, moving beyond controlled laboratory settings. The system's ability to recover from unexpected events and exhibit emergent intelligent behavior significantly increases its practical utility.
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** The finding that the method achieves higher success rates with smaller datasets due to "more supervision signals" is highly significant. Data collection in robotics, especially for complex bimanual tasks, is often time-consuming and resource-intensive. Improved data efficiency means that less human effort and fewer costly demonstrations are needed to train effective policies, accelerating research and development.
- **Diagnosable Coordination Failures:** The explicit modeling of object state transitions allows for a more direct and intuitive understanding of failure modes. When an object is dropped or mishandled, the "state loss" directly flags the coordination failure, offering a clearer signal for debugging and refinement compared to abstract "action losses" in state-to-action models. This interpretability can aid in developing more robust and reliable robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions.
 - **Real-world scalability:** Future work can focus on optimizing the training process to reduce time requirements and improving data handling efficiency to scale to even larger, more diverse datasets.
 - **Autonomy in Data Collection:** Streamlining the collection of real-world demonstrations (e.g., through active learning or self-supervision) could further enhance practicality and scalability.
 - **Integration with Planning:** While the paper highlights reactive capabilities, integrating this predictive model with higher-level task planning could lead to truly intelligent, long-horizon manipulation.
 - **Broader Applications:** The principles demonstrated here could be extended to other multi-robot coordination challenges, human-robot interaction, or even tasks involving complex non-rigid object deformations that are currently intractable for robots.

In conclusion, this research presents a robust and effective framework for bimanual robotic manipulation, pushing the boundaries of imitation learning by embedding predictive intelligence. Its demonstrated capabilities in complex real-world scenarios highlight its potential to bring robots closer to performing human-like dexterous tasks, with broad implications for automation in manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics.
