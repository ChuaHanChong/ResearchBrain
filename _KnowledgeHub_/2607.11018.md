# Whole-Body Semantic-to-Actuation Grounding of Elephant-Inspired Soft-Trunk Motion via Lightweight Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University, A*STAR, and the National University of Singapore developed a framework for soft continuum robots that translates open-vocabulary semantic responses from multimodal large language models into real-time, expressive whole-body movements. The system achieved a 77.2% success rate in grounding held-out semantic inputs to actuation trajectories and significantly improved user perceptions of naturalness and expressiveness in human-robot interaction.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic control often focuses on end-effector actions, which underspecify the whole-body expressive behavior of soft continuum manipulators crucial for close-contact human-robot interaction (HRI).
- Translating open-vocabulary, potentially ambiguous, or non-physical responses from multimodal large language models (MLLMs) into feasible, low-level robot actions is challenging for complex soft robot morphologies.
- Generating diverse yet physically valid whole-body actuation trajectories for soft robots in real-time is difficult due to high dimensionality and the computational cost of existing generative models.

## Method

- A **dual-reduction** approach canonicalizes open-vocabulary MLLM responses into morphology-aligned ==intent-intensity tuples==, separating high-level semantics from continuous motion generation.
- High-dimensional soft-trunk actuation trajectories are compacted into a low-dimensional representation using ==Catmull–Rom spline controls==, simplifying the learning target while preserving motion smoothness.
- ==Rectified flow==, a lightweight ==flow-matching== technique, is employed to learn a conditional distribution over these compact spline controls, enabling efficient and diverse sampling of whole-body motion realizations.

## Results

- The full framework achieved a **77.2%** held-out grounding correctness, outperforming a deterministic CNN (**56.2%**) and a DDPM (**71.9%**) baseline, while also demonstrating substantially faster inference (**4.87 ms** vs. DDPM's **7.86 ms**).
- Generated motions exhibited significant diversity (**0.148** mean pairwise RMS distance), indicating the system's ability to produce varied but valid physical expressions for a given semantic intent.
- In a physical HRI study with **100 participants**, the addition of soft-trunk motion dramatically increased positive user ratings for naturalness (**36% to 80%**), expressiveness (**27% to 89%**), engagement (**30% to 79%**), and overall satisfaction (**46% to 82%**), without compromising perceived safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Canonicalizing MLLM responses into a bounded, morphology-aligned semantic representation (intent-intensity tuples) significantly improves the correctness of motion grounding.
> - Representing continuous whole-body actuation with compact spline controls effectively manages high dimensionality, leading to smoother and more structured motion generation.
> - Rectified flow offers a computationally efficient alternative to iterative diffusion models for real-time generative motion, providing a balance of accuracy and speed while maintaining motion diversity for responsive HRI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of human-robot interaction (HRI), specifically focusing on close-contact scenarios involving soft continuum manipulators. The broader landscape of HRI has seen increasing interest in robots that can physically interact with humans, where factors like perceived safety, comfort, and appropriate bodily behavior are paramount. While rigid manipulators offer precision, their use in close proximity often raises concerns about safety and user comfort. Soft robots, particularly trunk-like continuum manipulators, are emerging as a promising alternative due to their inherent compliance and ability to generate expressive, whole-body motions that can be perceived as body language.

A significant challenge in robotic control, especially for advanced HRI, is the "grounding" problem: translating high-level semantic intent (e.g., natural language commands, emotional cues) into low-level, executable robot actions. Traditional vision-language-action (VLA) pipelines in robotics often focus on end-effector or tip-level actions, which are suitable for manipulation tasks requiring precise task-space outcomes. However, for soft continuum robots, particularly those designed for social interaction, expressive behavior emerges from distributed whole-body deformation rather than isolated end-effector movements. This creates a representation mismatch: tip-level actions underspecify the desired whole-body expression, while directly specifying high-dimensional whole-body shapes is computationally complex and difficult to ensure physical feasibility.

Furthermore, with the advent of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs), robots can now generate open-vocabulary, human-like responses to diverse interaction cues. However, these responses can be ambiguous, unsupported by the robot's morphology, or non-physical, posing a challenge for direct translation into robot actions. The "one-to-many" nature of semantic grounding—where a single semantic intent might correspond to multiple physically valid and socially appropriate motion realizations—further complicates the problem, necessitating generative models that can sample from a distribution of possible motions rather than predicting a single deterministic trajectory. While generative models like denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) can address this, their iterative denoising process can be computationally intensive, limiting their utility in real-time, responsive HRI.

This work addresses these challenges by proposing a novel framework for "whole-body semantic-to-actuation grounding" for soft trunks. It departs from conventional VLA paradigms by focusing on translating MLLM-generated semantic responses into executable, whole-body actuation trajectories. The paper contributes by introducing a dual-reduction approach to manage the high dimensionality of soft-trunk motion and by employing a lightweight flow-matching technique (rectified flow) for efficient, distributional motion generation, aiming to bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical embodiment in a safe, expressive, and timely manner for close-contact HRI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that can reliably and efficiently translate open-vocabulary semantic responses from a multimodal large language model (MLLM) into executable whole-body soft-trunk motion trajectories for close-contact human-robot interaction (HRI). This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and addresses key challenges inherent in the design of expressive soft robots:

1. **Bridging the Representation Mismatch in Whole-Body Soft Robotics:**
 - **Motivation:** In close-contact HRI, the expressive behavior of trunk-like soft continuum manipulators is distributed across their entire body, not just at a single end-effector. This whole-body deformation conveys social cues beyond what tip-level actions can achieve.
 - **Challenge:** Conventional vision-language-action (VLA) robot policies typically represent actions through end-effector displacements or low-level commands, which underspecify whole-body expression. Conversely, directly specifying high-dimensional body shapes over-specifies the response, making it difficult to learn and ensure physical feasibility.
 - **Objective:** To move beyond tip-centric action representations and develop a method for grounding language directly into *whole-body trunk actuation trajectories* that align with the robot's morphology.

2. **Handling Open-Vocabulary MLLM Responses for Physical Execution:**
 - **Motivation:** MLLMs can generate nuanced, open-vocabulary responses relevant to HRI. Leveraging these for physical embodiment requires a robust translation mechanism.
 - **Challenge:** Open-vocabulary MLLM responses may contain actions, modifiers, or expressions that are physically unsupported by the trunk morphology, ambiguous, or non-physical. Direct mapping can lead to infeasible or socially inappropriate motions.
 - **Objective:** To canonicalize these open-vocabulary semantic responses into a bounded, morphology-aligned representation (intent–intensity tuples) that defines executable motion families, thereby separating semantic selection from continuous motion realization.

3. **Managing the High Dimensionality of Actuation Trajectories:**
 - **Motivation:** The continuous, distributed nature of soft-trunk motion typically requires dense actuation trajectories with many control points, making them weakly structured and challenging to learn from limited demonstrations.
 - **Challenge:** Directly predicting hundreds of weakly structured values for a dense whole-body actuation trajectory from a semantic condition is computationally intensive and prone to learning difficulties.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a compact, low-dimensional representation for continuous whole-body tendon-actuation profiles using Catmull–Rom spline controls, simplifying the learning target while preserving motion smoothness and structure.

4. **Enabling Distributional Motion Generation for Social Validity:**
 - **Motivation:** Social behaviors often permit multiple valid physical realizations (e.g., a "comforting" gesture can have slight variations in curvature or timing while retaining its meaning). A model should capture this inherent variability.
 - **Challenge:** Deterministic regression models tend to average distinct valid realizations, suppressing within-tuple variation and potentially producing "collapsed" or under-expressive motions.
 - **Objective:** To learn a *conditional distribution* over executable motions, allowing for one-to-many sampling of diverse, smooth, and socially valid trunk-motion realizations for a given semantic condition.

5. **Ensuring Efficient Inference for Real-Time HRI:**
 - **Motivation:** Close-contact HRI demands responsive, low-latency robot behavior.
 - **Challenge:** Generative models like denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs), while effective for distributional generation, can involve iterative denoising processes that are computationally costly and may not meet real-time requirements for responsive HRI.
 - **Objective:** To employ a lightweight flow-matching alternative, specifically rectified flow, which learns a continuous transport field from noise to the expert motion manifold, enabling efficient sampling with a simpler generation process compared to iterative diffusion models.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to provide a feasible and effective interface for grounding high-level social intent into rich, physical expressions for soft-trunk robots, thereby enhancing the quality and naturalness of close-contact human-robot interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for whole-body semantic-to-actuation grounding for elephant-inspired soft-trunk HRI consists of three main components: Morphology-aligned Semantic Reduction, Compact Actuation-space Reduction, and Flow-Matching Grounding via Rectified Flow. This dual-reduction strategy, combined with a generative model, aims to bridge open-vocabulary semantic responses to executable whole-body motion.

**A. Morphology-aligned Semantic Reduction**
This component translates open-vocabulary multimodal reasoning into a bounded and executable semantic condition for soft-trunk motion generation.

1. **Multimodal Response Reasoning:** At each interaction turn, the system receives multimodal observation cues (visual, vocal, tactile) and a history of previous observations, trunk intents, and intensity levels. A pre-trained Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) processes these inputs to generate an open-vocabulary natural-language response plan (r_t), which describes the robot's intended social reaction (e.g., "User seems sad; respond calmly...").

2. **Executable Semantic Reduction:** The open-vocabulary response r_t is not directly executable by the soft trunk due to linguistic variability, morphology-dependent feasibility, and the need to separate high-level intent from continuous motion generation. To address this, the MLLM is guided by a "grounding specification" (C). This specification is a robot-specific, few-shot in-context prompt that encodes the trunk's affordances, feasibility limits, the supported intent–intensity space, canonical tuple descriptions, and examples of response-to-tuple mappings. The MLLM then maps r_t to a bounded intent–intensity tuple (m_t, z_t):
 - **Intent (m_t):** Selects one of sixteen executable, morphology-specific, elephant-inspired social-motion classes (e.g., greeting, comforting, embracing, withdrawal).
 - **Interaction Intensity (z_t):** Selects one of four discrete execution profiles (Subtle, Moderate, Pronounced, Emphatic), representing increasing embodied interaction salience through changes in reach, curvature, orientation, temporal emphasis, and contact proximity.
 This tuple (m_t, z_t) provides a canonical, morphology-compatible semantic condition for the downstream grounding model.

**B. Compact Actuation-space Reduction**
This component parameterizes high-dimensional, continuous actuation trajectories into a compact, low-dimensional representation.

1. **Actuation Trajectory Generation:** Continuous tendon-actuation trajectories (u_i(τ) ∈ R^(N_u)) are initially designed from elephant-inspired kinematic primitives (e.g., bending, curling, extension, retraction, contact-aware deformation) using existing whole-body shape-control strategies. N_u represents the number of actuation channels.

2. **Spline-Control Encoding:** To create a compact and structured representation, each continuous actuation trajectory u_i(τ) is encoded using Catmull–Rom splines. A Catmull–Rom spline is a piecewise cubic interpolating curve known for local support and C1 continuity. Each trajectory is represented by P control points, collected into a compact matrix c_i ∈ R^(N_u × P). This matrix is obtained by minimizing the L2 distance between the spline reconstruction and the original actuation trajectory, effectively learning the optimal control points. This reduced control matrix c_i serves as the low-dimensional training target for the generative model.

**C. Flow-Matching Grounding via Rectified Flow**
This component learns a conditional distribution over the compact spline controls, enabling efficient one-to-many sampling of executable trunk motions.

1. **Reduced Demonstration Set Construction:** An offline demonstration library is constructed from (actuation trajectory, open-vocabulary response) pairs. Each response r_i is processed through the same morphology-aligned semantic reduction (Sec. III-A) to yield an intent–intensity tuple (m_i, z_i). The corresponding actuation trajectory u_i is then fitted into a Catmull–Rom control matrix c_i (Sec. III-B). These (c_i, m_i, z_i) triplets form the raw expert demonstration set. Crucially, candidate c_i matrices are screened for actuation bounds, smoothness, and social appropriateness (using MLLM screening for semantic agreement and manual verification) before being included in the final expert demonstration set D_demo. This ensures that the training data represents valid and appropriate motions.

2. **Training via Rectified Flow:** The goal is to learn a conditional distribution p_θ(c | E_m(m), E_z(z)), where E_m(·) is a pretrained text encoder for the intent label, and E_z(·) is a learnable embedding for the discrete intensity level. Rectified flow (RF) is adopted as a lightweight conditional flow-matching method. It directly learns a continuous velocity field over the compact spline-control space. During training, for each (c_i, m_i, z_i) from D_demo, a noise matrix ϵ_i is sampled from N(0, I), and a flow time λ is sampled from U(0, 1). An interpolated control c_λ,i is created, and the velocity field v_θ is regressed onto the straight-line target (c_i - ϵ_i). The loss function L_RF minimizes the squared difference between the predicted velocity and the target velocity. This approach enables one-to-many generation while being more computationally efficient than iterative diffusion methods.

3. **Inference and Trajectory Reconstruction:** During online interaction, the semantic tuple (m_t, z_t) is obtained from the semantic reduction component. This tuple conditions the learned velocity field v_θ. Starting from a noise matrix c_0_t, the learned flow is integrated over S explicit-Euler steps to obtain the final reduced control matrix ˆc_t. Finally, the Catmull–Rom reconstruction operator R(·; τ) is applied to ˆc_t to generate the continuous whole-body actuation trajectory ˆu_t(τ) for the soft trunk.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated its framework through two main stages: an ablation experiment to quantify the contribution of individual components and a physical HRI study with human participants.

**A. Ablation Experiment: Semantic-to-Actuation Grounding**

This experiment assessed the framework's ability to ground semantic inputs into correct, diverse, and timely actuation trajectories using a held-out dataset of 64 pairs from a total of 256 expert demonstrations. Actuation trajectories were evaluated as 150-step sequences across 4 tendon-driven channels.

1. **Metrics:**
 - **Held-out Grounding Correctness (Success Rate):** Measured by the normalized Root Mean Square (RMS) distance between the inferred trajectory and the target library trajectory. A trajectory was considered "correct" if its RMS was below a calibrated threshold (τ_rms = 0.35).
 - **Valid-Sample Diversity:** Quantified as the mean pairwise RMS distance among 30 generated samples for each held-out condition, averaged over the held-out set.
 - **Inference Time:** Average latency for generating a single trajectory.

2. **Compared Methods (Ablations):**
 - **Ablation 1 (Raw + Dense + CNN):** Conditioned on raw MLLM response, predicts dense actuation trajectory using a deterministic CNN.
 - **Ablation 2 (Tuple + Dense + CNN):** Uses morphology-aligned semantic reduction (Tuple), predicts dense actuation, deterministic CNN.
 - **Ablation 3 (Tuple + Spline + CNN):** Uses semantic reduction (Tuple), compact actuation-space reduction (Spline), deterministic CNN.
 - **Ablation 4 (Tuple + Spline + DDPM):** Uses semantic reduction (Tuple), actuation-space reduction (Spline), a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) for generation.
 - **Ours (Tuple + Spline + RF):** The proposed full framework, using semantic reduction (Tuple), actuation-space reduction (Spline), and Rectified Flow (RF) for generation.

3. **Results Summary:**

| Method | Success Rate | Diversity | Inference Time (ms) |
| :---------------------------- | :----------- | :-------- | :------------------ |
| Ablation 1 (Raw + Dense + CNN) | 25.0% | 0.000 | 0.85 |
| Ablation 2 (Tuple + Dense + CNN) | 48.8% | 0.000 | 0.51 |
| Ablation 3 (Tuple + Spline + CNN) | 56.2% | 0.000 | 1.89 |
| Ablation 4 (Tuple + Spline + DDPM) | 71.9% | 0.165 | 7.86 |
| **Ours (Tuple + Spline + RF)** | **77.2%** | **0.148** | **4.87** |

 - **Impact of Semantic Reduction:** Moving from "Raw" to "Tuple" conditioning (Ablation 1 vs. 2) increased success from 25.0% to 48.8%, indicating that canonicalizing open-vocabulary responses into morphology-aligned semantic tuples significantly improves grounding correctness.
 - **Impact of Actuation-space Reduction:** Incorporating compact spline-control representation (Ablation 2 vs. 3) further improved success from 48.8% to 56.2%. The qualitative examples showed that spline representation led to smoother and more temporally structured tendon commands compared to dense prediction.
 - **Impact of Generative Models:** Distributional generators (DDPM and RF) significantly outperformed deterministic CNNs (Ablation 3 vs. 4 & Ours). CNNs achieved 0.000 diversity, indicating they collapsed multiple valid realizations into an average, less expressive motion. DDPM and RF, in contrast, captured motion diversity (0.165 and 0.148 respectively), leading to higher correctness.
 - **Rectified Flow vs. DDPM:** The proposed **Tuple + Spline + RF** model achieved the highest held-out success rate of **77.2%**, surpassing DDPM's 71.9%. Crucially, RF achieved this with a considerably faster inference time of **4.87 ms** compared to DDPM's 7.86 ms, while maintaining substantial motion diversity (0.148). This demonstrates that RF provides a superior balance of accuracy and efficiency for real-time HRI.

**B. Physical HRI Study**

This study evaluated the user-facing benefits of the generated soft-trunk motion in a close-contact HRI scenario with 100 participants. The robot platform, named ERIC (Elephant-inspired Robotic Interaction Companion), integrated speech, facial display, camera, tactile sensing, and the tendon-driven soft trunk.

1. **Conditions:**
 - **AV-only:** ERIC responded with speech and facial display; the soft trunk remained static.
 - **AV with physical trunk:** ERIC used the same audiovisual response, but additionally executed soft-trunk motion generated by the proposed framework.

2. **Metrics:** Participants rated their interaction on a 5-point Likert scale across six dimensions: naturalness, appropriateness, expressiveness, perceived safety, engagement, and overall satisfaction. Results were reported as the "positive-rating ratio" (fraction of ratings equal to 4 or 5).

3. **Results Summary (Positive Rating Ratio):**

| Dimension | AV-only | AV with physical trunk |
| :------------------ | :------ | :--------------------- |
| Naturalness | 36% | 80% |
| Appropriateness | 50% | 80% |
| Expressiveness | 27% | 89% |
| Engagement | 30% | 79% |
| Overall Satisfaction | 46% | 82% |
| Perceived Safety | 86% | 83% |

 - **Enhanced Social Interaction:** The addition of grounded soft-trunk motion (AV with physical trunk) significantly increased positive ratings across most social metrics.
 - **Naturalness** saw a substantial increase from 36% to 80%, suggesting that the physical embodiment made interactions feel more complete.
 - **Appropriateness** improved from 50% to 80%, indicating better contextual matching of robot responses.
 - The largest gains were observed in **Expressiveness** (27% to 89%) and **Engagement** (30% to 79%), highlighting the trunk's role as an effective embodied communication channel.
 - **Overall Satisfaction** more than doubled from 46% to 82%, underscoring the positive user experience.
 - **Preserved Perceived Safety:** Perceived safety remained high in both conditions (86% for AV-only, 83% for AV with physical trunk), indicating that introducing a moving soft appendage near the user did not significantly compromise the subjective sense of safety.
 - **Qualitative Observations:** Representative interaction episodes demonstrated that ERIC produced distinct whole-body trunk configurations (variations in bending, curvature, reach, proximity) that were smooth, executable, and provided a visible embodied interaction channel.

In conclusion, the ablation experiments validated the technical effectiveness of the dual-reduction approach and the rectified flow generator, showing improvements in correctness, diversity, and inference speed. The physical HRI study provided user-facing validation, demonstrating that the generated soft-trunk motions significantly enhance perceived naturalness, appropriateness, expressiveness, engagement, and overall satisfaction in close-contact human-robot interactions without compromising perceived safety.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of human-robot interaction (HRI) and soft robotics, particularly concerning the embodiment of social communication. The significance and potential impact can be understood through several dimensions:

1. **Advancing Whole-Body Expressive HRI for Soft Robots:** The work addresses a critical gap in HRI by enabling soft continuum manipulators to utilize their entire body for expressive social communication. Traditional robot control often focuses on end-effector tasks, which underspecifies the rich, distributed body language possible with compliant morphology. This framework shifts focus to whole-body semantic-to-actuation grounding, allowing soft robots to convey intent and emotion more effectively through physical presence and movement, akin to how biological organisms use their bodies in social contexts.

2. **Bridging High-Level Semantics and Low-Level Actuation for Complex Morphologies:** The paper provides a robust solution to the complex problem of translating abstract, open-vocabulary semantic intent from MLLMs into feasible, low-level actuation commands for high-dimensional, compliant robotic systems. This is achieved through a novel "dual-reduction" approach—canonicalizing semantic inputs into morphology-aligned tuples and compacting dense actuation trajectories with spline controls. This methodology is significant for managing the inherent complexity and dimensionality of soft robots, making them more amenable to semantic control.

3. **Enabling Real-time, Diverse, and Accurate Generative Motion:** The integration of rectified flow for motion generation is a key technical contribution. It demonstrates a lightweight, efficient, and accurate method for sampling from a distribution of valid motions. This is crucial for real-time, responsive HRI where latency is a concern and where a single semantic intent may have multiple appropriate physical realizations. Outperforming a denoising diffusion baseline in both correctness and speed while maintaining diversity has implications for deploying advanced generative models in latency-sensitive robotic applications.

4. **Enhancing User Experience and Trust in Close-Contact HRI:** The physical HRI study unequivocally demonstrates that adding the generated soft-trunk motion significantly enhances users' perception of naturalness, appropriateness, expressiveness, engagement, and overall satisfaction. This validates the user-facing benefit of the proposed system. Crucially, it achieves this without compromising perceived safety, a paramount concern in close-contact interactions. By fostering more intuitive and engaging interactions, this work could contribute to greater user acceptance and trust in social robots.

5. **Setting a Foundation for Future Adaptive and Bio-inspired Robotics:** The "elephant-inspired" nature of the soft trunk highlights the potential for bio-inspiration in designing robots with rich behavioral repertoires. The framework lays a foundation for future research in several areas:
 - **Closed-loop HRI:** The authors explicitly mention future work on incorporating real-time visual, tactile, and proprioceptive feedback, which would allow for truly adaptive and responsive whole-body soft-trunk behaviors.
 - **Generalization to other Soft Robots:** The principles of morphology-aligned semantic reduction, compact actuation representation, and lightweight flow matching could potentially be generalized to other types of high-dimensional, soft, or continuum robots beyond trunk manipulators, expanding their capabilities for expressive and intuitive interaction.
 - **Embodied AI:** This work contributes to the broader field of embodied AI by demonstrating how large language models can be grounded into complex physical actions in a nuanced and socially intelligent manner, moving beyond symbolic or purely verbal interactions.

In summary, this research is significant for providing a practical and effective solution for enabling expressive, whole-body communication in soft robots within close-contact HRI, supported by strong empirical evidence and technical innovation. Its impact extends from improving the immediate user experience to shaping future developments in adaptive, bio-inspired, and socially intelligent robotics.
