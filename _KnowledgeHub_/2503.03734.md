# OTTER: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Text-Aware Visual Feature Extraction

> [!summary] Summary
> OTTER, a Vision-Language-Action model, employs text-aware visual feature extraction from frozen pre-trained vision-language models to achieve robust zero-shot generalization in robotic manipulation. The model significantly improves success rates on novel objects and environments, outperforming fine-tuned baselines by substantial margins across various tasks.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on relatively small and less diverse robot-specific datasets degrades their inherent semantic alignments.
- This degradation impairs the VLA model's ability to generalize effectively to unseen objects, environments, or task variations, which is critical for real-world robotics.
- Existing VLA approaches often fail to leverage the full zero-shot potential of robust pre-trained VLMs like CLIP due to this fine-tuning interference.

## Method

- **OTTER** freezes the pre-trained vision and language encoders of a VLM (e.g., CLIP) to preserve their rich semantic understanding, preventing degradation during training.
- It introduces a novel text-aware visual feature extraction mechanism that uses temperature-weighted attention to explicitly select and weight visual patches based on their semantic similarity to individual language tokens.
- A causal transformer-based policy network then integrates these text-aware visual features, compressed text features, and proprioceptive states to autoregressively predict robot actions.

## Results

- OTTER achieved **62%** success on unseen real-world pick-and-place tasks, substantially outperforming fine-tuned Octo (**12%**) and OpenVLA (**9%**) and showing strong generalization.
- Across four manipulation primitives, OTTER-OXE-L achieved a mean success rate of **77%** on unseen tasks, while baselines struggled significantly, particularly with complex tasks like pouring (**0%** success).
- Ablation studies confirmed that fine-tuning the CLIP encoder reduced unseen task performance by over **40%** (from **62%** to **15%**) compared to keeping it frozen, validating OTTER's core hypothesis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving the pre-trained semantic alignments of VLMs by keeping their encoders frozen is crucial for achieving superior zero-shot generalization in robot learning, as fine-tuning can compromise these capabilities.
> - Explicitly extracting text-aware visual features, which selectively highlight task-relevant visual information based on language instructions, is more effective than simply passing separate vision and language features.
> - The performance of VLA models can scale positively with the size of the underlying pre-trained VLM and benefit from pre-training on diverse human video datasets, suggesting strong compatibility with foundation models.
