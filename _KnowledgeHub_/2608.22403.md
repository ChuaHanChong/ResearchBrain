# LD4WAM: Learning Latent Dynamics from Human Videos for World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LD4WAM is a world action model that learns motion-aligned latent dynamics as an embodiment-agnostic bridge between human video and robot actions. A Latent Dynamics Model trained with DINOv3 semantic reconstruction plus end-effector motion alignment supplies targets for a mixture-of-transformers policy whose learnable queries distill dynamics from generated future video. Pretrained on 5,086 hours, it reaches 93.4% on RoboTwin and 70.5% real-world average.

## Problem

- Most world action models supervise human video only with pixel-level future-frame reconstruction, so learned dynamics stay in the generative pathway and couple weakly to the control policy.
- Motion retargeting yields directly executable actions but entangles embodiment-specific execution, suffers human-robot kinematic correspondence errors, and leaves the visual embodiment gap unresolved.
- Latent-action methods derived from inter-frame pixel changes explain appearance rather than isolate motion, retaining embodiment-specific detail with no grounding in executable end-effector motion.

## Method

- **Latent Dynamics Model** (LDM): frozen ==DINOv3== features, ==spatio-temporal transformer==, ==soft vector quantization==, supervised by semantic next-feature reconstruction, delta end-effector motion alignment, and codebook usage/entropy regularizers.
- **World Dynamics Action Model** (WDAM): ==mixture-of-transformers== with video, latent-dynamics, and action experts sharing ==asymmetric self-attention==, forming a one-directional video-to-dynamics-to-action pipeline via ==learnable queries==.
- Unified data pipeline plus three-stage training: filtered ==LeRobot==-format corpus of 274.66M frames, then pretraining, robot-data align training, and task-specific post-training with staged loss weights.

## Results

- RoboTwin 50-task unified policy averages **93.4%** success, beating Lingbot-VA by **1.2** and human-data VLA ACE-Ego-0 by **2.5** points; top methods cluster tightly.
- Real robots reach **70.5%** average across seven gripper and dexterous-hand tasks, versus **64.3%** Lingbot-VA, **63.3%** pi-0.5, **47.1%** Fast-WAM, best on **six of seven**.
- Ablations: removing motion alignment inflates latent regression error **3.7-5.0x**; architecture builds from **40.0%** to **63.7%**, with pretraining adding **23.3** points on unseen backgrounds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding an unsupervised latent representation in real end-effector motion, not just pixel or semantic prediction, is what makes video-learned dynamics usable for control.
> - Asymmetric attention visibility can enforce a directional information pipeline, preserving a pretrained generator's priors while still letting downstream policy heads consume its output.
> - World-action models inherit their video generator's weaknesses: unseen textures degrade generated rollouts, so VLAs with internet-scale backbones can still win on background shift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen the emergence of World Action Models (WAMs) as a paradigm for unifying future observation prediction with action generation. These models increasingly leverage large-scale egocentric human manipulation videos due to their diversity and lower collection costs compared to teleoperated robot demonstrations. However, existing approaches face limitations in effectively transferring knowledge from human video to robot control.

Many WAMs primarily utilize human video by predicting pixel-level future frames. This method results in learned dynamics that are not directly actionable by robots, and the connection between the generative pathway and the robot's policy remains weak due to the absence of action labels. Consequently, knowledge acquired from human video in this manner does not consistently translate into effective robot control.

An alternative approach involves retargeting human motion into a robot's action space, often using hand or end-effector annotations from egocentric datasets. While this provides directly actionable movements, it can introduce issues such as the entanglement of transferable physical knowledge with embodiment-specific execution details, correspondence errors arising from kinematic differences between humans and robots, and an unresolved visual gap across embodiments.

A third line of research explores learning latent actions from inter-frame pixel changes without explicit action labels, using these latent codes to condition robot control. However, these codes are typically designed to explain pixel variations rather than to isolate motion, which can lead to the retention of appearance- and embodiment-specific information. This characteristic limits their direct connection to executable motion for robot control.

This research addresses these challenges by proposing a novel intermediate representation: motion-aligned latent dynamics. This representation is intended to bridge the gap between high-dimensional video priors and low-level robot actions by abstracting away appearance and embodiment differences, ensuring transferability across embodiments, and explicitly grounding the representation in real motion for direct robot control. The work aligns with the "learning line" of leveraging human video by focusing on transferable representations, but critically extends it by grounding these representations in real motion, a feature often lacking in prior works. By introducing an embodiment-agnostic, actionable representation within a WAM framework, this study seeks to improve the effectiveness of large-scale human video in training robust and generalizable robot manipulation policies.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance robot manipulation capabilities and generalization by developing a World Action Model (WAM) that can effectively learn from diverse, large-scale egocentric human videos. The paper aims to overcome the limitations of current methods by introducing an embodiment-agnostic representation that bridges visual priors and low-level robot actions.

The specific objectives of the work are:

1. **Develop a novel representation:** To propose "motion-aligned latent dynamics" as a compact, embodiment-agnostic representation that captures inter-frame dynamics in a semantic feature space and is explicitly aligned with observed motion.
2. **Design a Latent Dynamics Model (LDM):** To construct a model responsible for learning this bridge representation. This LDM is trained using next-frame semantic reconstruction and motion alignment to encourage the latent space to encode motion-relevant semantic changes while suppressing appearance-specific variations.
3. **Integrate with a World Dynamics Action Model (WDAM):** To build a WDAM that incorporates the learned motion-aligned latent dynamics. This WDAM is designed as a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture that preserves full future-video generation capabilities while using learnable queries to distill the latent dynamics from generated futures for action conditioning.
4. **Curate a large-scale dataset:** To assemble and process a unified dataset comprising over 5,000 hours of human and robot manipulation data, standardized into a common format to facilitate large-scale pretraining.
5. **Evaluate performance and generalization:** To conduct comprehensive experiments in both simulation (RoboTwin benchmark) and on real robots (with grippers and dexterous hands) to demonstrate the model's manipulation capabilities and its generalization to unseen objects and backgrounds.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several challenges in current robot learning paradigms:

- **Underutilization of Human Video:** While human videos are a rich and cost-effective source of manipulation data, most WAMs fail to fully leverage their potential. Existing approaches often result in dynamics that are not directly actionable or representations that retain embodiment-specific noise, hindering effective knowledge transfer to robots.
- **Embodiment Gap:** Significant kinematic and visual differences between humans and robots create an "embodiment gap." Current methods like motion retargeting struggle to accurately map human movements to robots without introducing errors or losing transferable physical knowledge. A truly embodiment-agnostic representation is needed.
- **Lack of Action Grounding:** Representations learned from video often lack a clear connection to executable robot actions, requiring the policy to learn this mapping from limited robot data independently. There is a need for a representation that is explicitly grounded in real motion.
- **Need for Generalization:** Robots operating in dynamic, unstructured environments require policies that can generalize to novel objects and varying backgrounds. Current WAMs, especially those reliant on pixel-level prediction, can struggle with such generalization.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to establish a more effective and scalable approach for training robot manipulation policies that can learn broadly from human experience and perform robustly in diverse real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The LD4WAM framework integrates a data processing pipeline, a Latent Dynamics Model (LDM), and a World Dynamics Action Model (WDAM), with a three-stage training procedure.

#### 3.1. Data Process and Composition

A unified pipeline processes both human and robot datasets into the LeRobot format. Long videos are segmented into subtask-level clips (3 to 60 seconds), then downsampled to 15 frames per second (FPS).

For **human data**:
- **Action-annotated clips** are filtered to remove instances with large head motion, prolonged periods without hand annotations, or minimal wrist motion. Actions are then standardized into a unified head-camera coordinate frame and a unified wrist coordinate frame.
- **Video-only clips** undergo optical-flow-based camera-shake filtering and MediaPipe hand detection to retain steady segments with active, visible hands.
The robot data undergoes a corresponding format conversion.

The aggregated dataset totals approximately 274.66 million frames (over 5,000 hours) at 15 FPS, with human data constituting 76.4% and robot data 23.6%.

#### 3.2. Latent Dynamics Model (LDM)

The LDM is designed to learn motion-aligned latent dynamics that are embodiment-agnostic. It takes a sequence of frames, sampled with a random temporal skip `n` (to expose the model to varying temporal scales), as input.
1. **Feature Encoding:** Each frame `o_i` is encoded into semantic features `f_i` using a frozen DINOv3 (ViT-L/16) model.
2. **Dynamics Extraction:** A spatio-temporal transformer aggregates these features across space and time. A soft vector quantization (SoftVQ) layer then maps each transition into a latent-dynamics token `z_i`. SoftVQ maintains a smooth and structured latent space while reducing low-level visual noise.
3. **Training Objectives:** The LDM is trained with a combined objective `L_LDM = L_sem + λ_m L_mot + L_vq`:
 - `L_sem` (Semantic Reconstruction Loss): A semantic decoder reconstructs future features `ˆf_{i+n}` from the current frame's features `f_i` and `z_i`. This objective, using cosine distance and a feature-norm matching term, compels `z_i` to encode the semantic evolution rather than appearance.
 - `L_mot` (Motion Alignment Loss): A motion decoder predicts the inter-frame end-effector motion `ˆm_i`. This loss is applied only to frames with action labels, grounding the latent dynamics in executable motion.
 - `L_vq` (Codebook Regularizer): Comprises a KL divergence (`L_use`) to encourage uniform codebook usage and a mean per-token soft-assignment entropy (`L_ent`) to prevent codebook collapse.
At inference, only the encoding path (DINOv3, spatio-temporal transformer, SoftVQ) is used to generate the latent-dynamics tokens `z_i`, which then serve as supervision targets for the WDAM.

#### 3.3. World Dynamics Action Model (WDAM)

The WDAM is a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture comprising three distinct experts: a video expert, a latent dynamics expert, and an action expert. These experts share a self-attention module over a concatenated token sequence but maintain separate weights.
1. **Video Expert:** A flow-matching denoiser responsible for generating future video. It is initialized from a pretrained Wan2.2 video generation model to leverage existing physical priors. This expert attends only to its own stream, preserving the pretrained prior.
2. **Latent Dynamics Expert:** Composed of learnable queries, this expert is trained to reproduce the future latent dynamics `z_i` (defined by the LDM). It attends to the predicted video and its own queries, distilling the visual information into a compact dynamic summary.
3. **Action Expert:** A flow-matching denoiser that generates future robot actions. This expert attends to both the predicted video and the summary provided by the latent dynamics expert at every layer. Text conditioning is also applied through cross-attention.
The WDAM's training objective is `L_WDAM = λ_v L_fm_video + λ_d L_mse_dyn + λ_a L_fm_act`, where `L_fm_video` and `L_fm_act` are flow-matching losses for video and action denoising, and `L_mse_dyn` is a masked mean squared error for latent dynamics.

#### 3.4. Training Procedure

The training of LD4WAM follows a three-stage curriculum:
1. **Stage I (Pre-training):** The LDM is trained on a high-quality subset and then frozen. The WDAM's video and latent dynamics experts are trained on the full unified dataset (human and robot), while the action expert remains frozen due to the absence of action labels in human video. This stage focuses on learning future prediction and latent dynamics extraction at scale.
2. **Stage II (Align Training):** All WDAM experts are unfrozen and trained on general robot data. This stage aims to align the predicted latent dynamics with executable actions.
3. **Stage III (Post-training):** All WDAM experts are fine-tuned on task-specific robot data for deployment, refining performance for specific tasks and embodiments.
Throughout training, the DINOv3 encoder remains frozen, and the video expert is initialized from a pretrained Wan2.2 generator.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results across several evaluation settings, demonstrating the capabilities of LD4WAM.

#### 4.1. Latent Dynamics Retrieval

Qualitative results from latent dynamics retrieval (Fig. 5) indicate that the LDM effectively captures motion semantics. By retrieving nearest neighbors based on cosine similarity in the frozen LDM latent space, the system consistently identified clips with corresponding motion patterns across diverse human and robot datasets. This suggests that the learned latent dynamics are embodiment-agnostic, enabling a shared understanding of motion between human and robot demonstrations.

#### 4.2. Simulation Performance

On the RoboTwin benchmark, LD4WAM achieved an average success rate of 93.4% across 50 manipulation tasks (Table 1). This performance surpassed strong Vision-Language-Action (VLA) baselines like ACE-Ego-0 by 2.5 percentage points and leading World Action Models (WAMs) such as Lingbot-VA by 1.2 percentage points. This indicates that the proposed method is competitive in simulated environments.

#### 4.3. Real-World Manipulation

In real-world experiments, LD4WAM was deployed on two robotic embodiments: a dual-arm PIPER with parallel grippers and a dual-arm Tianji platform with Wuji dexterous hands. Across seven tasks spanning long-horizon, high-precision, and dexterous manipulation, LD4WAM attained the highest success rate on six tasks (Table 2). The average success rate for LD4WAM was 70.5%, outperforming π0.5 (63.3%), Fast-WAM (47.1%), and Lingbot-VA (64.3%). This demonstrates the model's ability to perform complex manipulation skills on physical robots, including tasks like sorting, precise insertion, cloth folding, bimanual handover, and multi-finger tool use.

#### 4.4. Generalization Capabilities

Generalization was assessed under shifts in objects and backgrounds (Table 4).
- **Object Generalization:** LD4WAM retained 88.6% of its in-distribution performance on unseen objects, which was the highest among all compared methods. This result suggests that the motion-aligned latent dynamics effectively abstract away specific object appearances, focusing on transferable manipulation skills.
- **Background Generalization:** While LD4WAM showed improvement over prior WAMs (e.g., +10.0 points over Lingbot-VA and +34.4 points over Fast-WAM), it performed below π0.5 (44.4% vs. 54.5%). The paper attributes this gap to texture-level perturbations, which are particularly challenging for video prediction, thereby affecting the action expert that conditions on generated video. The latent dynamics mitigate some appearance variation but do not entirely eliminate the reliance on the video generation component's robustness to texture changes.

#### 4.5. Ablation Analysis

**Latent Dynamics Model (LDM) Ablation (Table 3):**
- **Motion Alignment:** Removing motion alignment from the LDM's objectives resulted in a significant increase in latent dynamics regression error (3.7 to 5.0 times higher), indicating its critical role in grounding the learned dynamics in real end-effector motion.
- **Soft Vector Quantization (SoftVQ):** Replacing SoftVQ with hard quantization increased regression error by 76–88%, suggesting that maintaining a continuous and smooth latent space is important for capturing continuous motion.
- **Semantic Reconstruction:** Training with semantic feature reconstruction rather than pixel-level reconstruction yielded better performance, implying that abstracting to high-level semantic content is more effective than tying dynamics to noisy, low-level appearance.
- **Multi-stride Sampling:** The use of multi-stride sampling for LDM training contributed to better performance across different motion scales.

**Full Architecture Ablation (Table 4):**
- **Latent Dynamic Expert:** Introducing the Latent Dynamic Expert (with LDM-supervised targets) improved the average success rate from a baseline Video-Action Dual-Expert WAM (40.0%) to 48.1%.
- **LDM Supervision:** The benefit of the Latent Dynamic Expert was dependent on proper LDM supervision; removing motion alignment or using pixel reconstruction instead of semantic reconstruction degraded performance.
- **Pre-training:** Pretraining on the large, unified dataset yielded a significant 10.8 percentage point increase in overall success rate. This gain was largely driven by improved generalization (7.8 points for unseen objects, 23.3 points for unseen backgrounds), indicating that pretraining supplied crucial visual and dynamic coverage missing in task-specific robot data.
- **Align Training:** The alignment middle-training stage further contributed 4.8 percentage points to the overall performance, primarily sharpening execution quality and object generalization. This demonstrates the complementary roles of pretraining (broad coverage) and alignment (refined action grounding).
Collectively, the ablated components of LD4WAM contributed to an overall performance improvement of 23.7 percentage points over the initial baseline.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research on LD4WAM presents several significant contributions to the field of robot learning and manipulation, with potential impacts on future developments.

Firstly, the introduction of **motion-aligned latent dynamics** offers a novel and effective method for distilling actionable information from diverse video data. This representation is specifically designed to be embodiment-agnostic, addressing a core challenge in transferring knowledge from human demonstrations to robots. By abstracting away appearance and embodiment differences while explicitly aligning with real motion, the latent dynamics provide a robust bridge between high-dimensional visual observations and low-level robot actions. This could lead to more efficient and reliable learning from heterogeneous data sources.

Secondly, the development of the **Latent Dynamics Model (LDM)** and its integration into a **World Dynamics Action Model (WDAM)** provides a structured approach for leveraging this representation. The LDM's use of semantic reconstruction and motion alignment is crucial for learning meaningful and actionable dynamics. The WDAM's Mixture-of-Transformers architecture, with learnable queries distilling latent dynamics from generated futures, enables the policy to condition actions on a compact and motion-relevant signal without sacrificing the rich physical priors learned from large-scale video generation models. This methodological innovation could influence the design of future WAMs, promoting action-grounded prediction.

Thirdly, the **curation of a unified, large-scale dataset** comprising over 5,000 hours of human and robot manipulation data is a valuable contribution to the research community. This standardized dataset, processed to support cross-embodiment learning, provides a critical resource for training and evaluating robot learning models at scale, fostering further research in this area.

The empirical results demonstrate **strong performance and generalization capabilities** in both simulation and real-world scenarios, across various manipulation tasks and robot embodiments (grippers and dexterous hands). The high success rates, especially in complex tasks requiring precision, long-horizon planning, and bimanual coordination, highlight the practical applicability of LD4WAM. The robust generalization to unseen objects, as evidenced by the high retention of performance, signifies progress towards deploying robots in unstructured, open-world environments where encountering novel items is common. While background generalization showed room for improvement, the gains over prior WAMs are notable.

In terms of potential impact, LD4WAM could:
- **Accelerate Robot Skill Acquisition:** By enabling robots to learn more effectively from readily available human videos, the cost and effort associated with robot data collection could be significantly reduced, speeding up the development and deployment of new robotic skills.
- **Enhance Robot Autonomy:** The improved generalization capabilities contribute to building more autonomous robots that can adapt to variations in their environment without extensive retraining.
- **Broaden Applications of Robotics:** The ability to handle complex and dexterous manipulation tasks on various platforms suggests potential for applications in industries requiring fine motor control (e.g., manufacturing, assembly), service robotics, and assistive technologies.
- **Advance Fundamental Research:** The concept of motion-aligned latent dynamics and its successful implementation provide a new direction for research into robust, transferable representations for embodied AI, prompting further exploration into how robots can abstract and act upon human experience.
