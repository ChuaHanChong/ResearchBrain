# CODI: Compressing Chain-of-Thought into Continuous Space via Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> CODI introduces a framework for large language models to perform Chain-of-Thought reasoning within a continuous, latent space via self-distillation. This approach matches or exceeds the performance of explicit CoT, particularly at smaller model scales, while achieving substantial efficiency gains with 2.7x to 5.9x speedup and 3.1x to 8.2x compression.

## Problem

- Traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs is inefficient, requiring extensive token generation for natural language steps, increasing inference latency and computational cost.
- Learning explicit, token-by-token CoT annotations can lead to overfitting on superficial linguistic cues, hindering generalization and robustness to varied or out-of-domain inputs.
- Prior implicit CoT methods consistently underperformed explicit CoT, often due to "forgetting" issues inherent in multi-stage curriculum learning strategies.

## Method

- ==CODI (Continuous Chain-of-Thought via Self-Distillation)== employs a ==single-stage, joint learning framework== using self-distillation to compress explicit CoT into a ==continuous latent space==.
- The model is trained with a joint objective function that combines cross-entropy losses for an explicit CoT "teacher" task, an implicit CoT "student" task (generating continuous thoughts), and a ==knowledge distillation loss==.
- A ==feature-level knowledge distillation loss== aligns the hidden activations of a designated "distillation token" between the explicit teacher and implicit student processes across all layers, transferring reasoning capabilities.

## Results

- CODI achieved performance comparable to CoT-SFT, reaching **99%** of its accuracy on GSM8k with GPT-2, marking the first time an implicit CoT method matched explicit CoT at this scale, and substantially outperformed prior implicit methods by over **20%**.
- The framework demonstrated significant efficiency gains, achieving **3.1x** to **8.2x** CoT compression rates and **2.7x** to **5.9x** speedup on mathematical reasoning tasks like GSM8k-Aug and GSM8k-Aug-NL.
- CODI consistently outperformed CoT-SFT and all other implicit CoT baselines across three out-of-distribution mathematical reasoning benchmarks (SVAMP, GSM-Hard, MultiArith) when using GPT-2, demonstrating enhanced robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can effectively perform complex reasoning within their continuous latent space, matching or surpassing explicit natural language CoT performance, particularly at smaller model scales.
> - A single-stage, self-distillation approach, where the model simultaneously learns explicit CoT as a reference and compresses it into latent representations, effectively mitigates "forgetting" issues seen in multi-stage implicit CoT methods.
> - Reasoning in a continuous space, unconstrained by rigid linguistic imitation targets, promotes greater robustness and improved generalization to out-of-domain data compared to fine-tuning on explicit CoT.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have brought their remarkable reasoning capabilities to the forefront. A pivotal technique enabling this has been Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which encourages LLMs to generate explicit, step-by-step natural language reasoning before arriving at a final answer. This "human-like" deliberate thinking, as described by Kahneman's System 2 thinking, significantly improves LLM performance on complex tasks.

However, conventional explicit CoT methods, while effective, suffer from two main drawbacks:
1. **Inefficiency:** Verbalizing every reasoning step in natural language can be token-intensive, leading to increased inference latency and computational cost. Many tokens may be devoted to communication rather than pure computation.
2. **Robustness/Overfitting:** Learning explicit, token-by-token CoT annotations might cause models to overfit superficial linguistic cues in the training data, potentially hindering their generalization to varied or out-of-domain inputs.

This paper addresses these limitations by exploring "implicit CoT" methods, where reasoning occurs in a latent, continuous space rather than through explicit language tokens. The idea is inspired by continuous prompt learning, which has shown that representing discrete prompts in a continuous space can enhance efficiency and effectiveness. Prior implicit CoT methods have attempted to bypass language entirely, but have consistently underperformed explicit CoT. Early efforts like Pfau et al. (2024) and Goyal et al. (2024) involved pretraining models with additional "thinking tokens" from scratch, but with limited success. More recently, methods such as iCoT (Deng et al., 2024) and Coconut (Hao et al., 2024) introduced curriculum learning strategies to gradually replace explicit CoT tokens with continuous representations. While Coconut represented the state-of-the-art for implicit CoT, it still lagged significantly behind standard explicit CoT fine-tuning (CoT-SFT). The authors hypothesize this performance gap is due to "forgetting" issues inherent in multi-stage curriculum learning.

CODI positions itself as a significant advancement in this landscape by being the *first* implicit CoT approach to match or even surpass the performance of explicit CoT, particularly at smaller model scales like GPT-2, while simultaneously offering substantial efficiency gains. It reframes the problem by using a novel self-distillation framework that enables single-stage learning, thus circumventing the forgetting problem of curriculum learning. This work also connects to knowledge distillation, where a larger or more capable "teacher" model transfers knowledge to a "student," and multitask learning, as CODI jointly optimizes for explicit and implicit reasoning. The paper contributes to the broader goal of making LLMs more efficient, robust, and capable of reasoning in more abstract, non-linguistic ways, potentially opening avenues for tasks where reasoning cannot be easily verbalized.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the CODI framework is to **compress natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into a continuous, latent space without sacrificing performance, while simultaneously enhancing efficiency and robustness.**

The key motivations driving this research are:
- **Overcoming Inefficiency of Explicit CoT:** Traditional CoT involves generating lengthy natural language reasoning steps, which increases inference time and computational cost. CODI aims to reduce this overhead by performing reasoning in a compact continuous space.
- **Improving Robustness of Reasoning:** Explicit CoT fine-tuning can lead to overfitting on superficial linguistic cues within annotated CoTs. Reasoning in a continuous space, without needing to mimic exact linguistic targets, is hypothesized to introduce a softer prior that leads to improved robustness and generalization.
- **Addressing Performance Gaps in Prior Implicit CoT Methods:** Existing implicit CoT methods, especially those relying on multi-stage curriculum learning like Coconut, have consistently underperformed explicit CoT. This underperformance is attributed to "forgetting" across training stages. CODI seeks to close this performance gap, aiming to achieve explicit CoT-level accuracy with implicit reasoning.
- **Validating Continuous Space Reasoning:** The overarching motivation is to demonstrate that LLMs can reason effectively not only through natural language but also within their latent continuous representations, potentially unlocking more flexible and versatile reasoning paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CODI (Continuous Chain-of-Thought via Self-Distillation) is a novel training framework designed to compress natural language CoT into a continuous space through a single-stage, joint learning process. The core idea is self-distillation, where the model simultaneously learns explicit CoT (teacher task) and implicit CoT (student task), transferring reasoning knowledge from the former to the latter.

The methodology is structured around three main components:

1. **Joint Training Objective:**
 CODI trains an LLM using a weighted sum of three loss functions:
 $$ L = \alpha L_{student} + \beta L_{KD} + \gamma L_{teacher} $$
 where $L_{student}$ and $L_{teacher}$ are cross-entropy losses for the student and teacher tasks, respectively, and $L_{KD}$ is a knowledge distillation loss. $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are hyperparameters.

2. **Teacher Task (Explicit CoT):**
 - The teacher task (Figure 2, right) is responsible for learning explicit CoT reasoning. It is trained via standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using a cross-entropy loss ($L_{teacher}$).
 - The input to the teacher is the concatenated sequence of question (Q), ground-truth CoT reasoning (c), and the final answer (y), i.e., [Q, c, y]. The model learns to predict $P(r_i | r_{1:i-1}, Q)$, where $r = [c, y]$.
 - Crucially, to prevent the teacher from taking shortcuts, the *final step* of the CoT chain (the part immediately preceding the answer generation) is excluded from the teacher's input during training. This ensures that the teacher's hidden activations fully encode the reasoning process rather than merely copying the final result.

3. **Student Task (Implicit CoT):**
 - The student task (Figure 2, left) learns to perform implicit CoT reasoning in a continuous latent space.
 - This process starts with a learnable `<bot>` (begin-of-thoughts) token appended to the question (Q). The model then autoregressively generates a fixed number of "continuous thoughts" by propagating its last hidden states.
 - A two-layer MLP followed by layer normalization transforms these hidden representations before they are fed into the next step, helping to discriminate between latent and token space.
 - After generating a predefined number of continuous thoughts (e.g., six), a learnable `<eot>` (end-of-thoughts) token is manually inserted to signal the end of implicit reasoning.
 - Finally, the model generates the final answer (y) from the `<eot>` token using a cross-entropy loss ($L_{student}$), optimizing $P(y_i | y_{1:i-1}, Q, Z)$, where Z represents the continuous thoughts. The student's continuous thoughts are *not* constrained to match any specific linguistic target, fostering flexibility.

4. **Self-Distillation (Knowledge Transfer):**
 - The critical component for transferring reasoning ability from explicit to implicit CoT is the knowledge distillation loss ($L_{KD}$).
 - This is a feature-level distillation strategy, aligning the hidden activations of a designated "distillation token" between the teacher and student tasks.
 - Inspired by prior work showing that a query token's hidden activations shift due to CoT reasoning, the authors select the token immediately preceding the answer in the prompt, specifically the colon (":") in "The answer is:", as the distillation token. This token's hidden states are believed to encode essential reasoning information (Orgad et al., 2025).
 - $L_{KD}$ is an L1 loss that minimizes the difference between the teacher's and student's hidden activations across all layers at the selected distillation token position:
 $$ L_{KD} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{l=1}^{M} |sg[h_{teacher}^l] - h_{student}^l| $$
 where $M$ is the number of layers, $sg[\cdot]$ is the stop-gradient operation (ensuring one-way knowledge flow from teacher to student), and $h^l$ represents the hidden activations at layer $l$.
 - The hidden activations are normalized by the standard deviation of the teacher's corresponding activations within the current batch to address norm variations across layers.
 - The same LLM acts as both teacher and student, allowing it to learn explicit CoT as a reference before compressing it, and streamlining training efficiency.

**Training and Inference:**
During training, continuous thoughts are dynamically decoded step-by-step. In inference, the process mirrors the student task: the model autoregressively decodes a fixed number of continuous thoughts following the question and `<bot>` token, then the `<eot>` token is inserted to trigger final answer generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CODI consistently outperforms existing implicit CoT methods and demonstrates strong capabilities across various reasoning tasks and model scales.

1. **Outperformance on Mathematical Reasoning (GSM8k-Aug):**
 - CODI substantially outperforms previous implicit CoT methods. With GPT-2, CODI achieved performance comparable to CoT-SFT, reaching 99% of its accuracy. This marks the *first* time an implicit CoT method has matched explicit CoT at this scale.
 - On LLaMA-1b, CODI also surpassed Coconut by over 20% and achieved 90% of CoT-SFT's performance, indicating effective scaling.
 - Specifically, CODI demonstrated a 28.2% accuracy improvement over the previous state-of-the-art implicit CoT method (Coconut) on GSM8k with GPT-2.

2. **Generalizability to More Verbose CoTs (GSM8k-Aug-NL):**
 - CODI proved capable of compressing more complex, natural language-rich CoTs. On GSM8k-Aug-NL, it surprisingly *surpassed* CoT-SFT for GPT-2 and achieved a higher relative score improvement on LLaMA-1b compared to models trained on structured math expressions.
 - This suggests CODI's self-distillation approach effectively handles the noise and complexity introduced by natural language in reasoning.

3. **Performance on Commonsense Reasoning (CommonsenseQA-CoT):**
 - For GPT-2, CODI outperformed both CoT-SFT and No-CoT-SFT, which the authors attribute to GPT-2's limited capacity for generating coherent explicit CoTs. CODI's ability to reason in continuous space with fewer tokens mitigated this limitation.
 - For LLaMA-1b, where CoT data sometimes hurt performance (due to potential mismatch with GPT-4o-mini's CoT patterns), CODI still significantly outperformed CoT-SFT and achieved accuracy comparable to No-CoT-SFT, demonstrating the benefit of learning latent representations over overfitting specific explicit CoT patterns.

4. **Efficiency and Compression:**
 - CODI achieves significant efficiency gains. Using a fixed set of six continuous thoughts (plus two special tokens), it results in an approximate 3.1x CoT compression rate and a 2.7x speedup on GSM8k-Aug.
 - For the more verbose GSM8k-Aug-NL, CODI achieved an 8.2x CoT compression rate and a 5.9x speedup, highlighting its adaptability to diverse CoT lengths.
 - The optimal number of continuous thought tokens was found to be six, balancing expressiveness and optimization challenges.

5. **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Data:**
 - CODI, trained on GSM8k-Aug, consistently outperformed all other implicit CoT baselines and even CoT-SFT across three OOD mathematical reasoning benchmarks (SVAMP, GSM-Hard, MultiArith) when using GPT-2.
 - With LLaMA-1b, it also showed stronger performance relative to its in-domain results compared to other implicit CoT methods.
 - This robustness is attributed to CODI's reduced tendency to overfit, as it generates continuous thoughts without rigid imitation targets, promoting greater adaptability.

6. **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing the L1 distillation loss ($L_{KD}$) led to a significant performance drop, confirming the critical role of hidden state alignment in transferring reasoning capabilities.
 - Training with a separate, static teacher model without joint learning performed poorly, underscoring the importance of the self-distillation setup where the model learns explicit CoT as a reference internally.
 - Excluding the final CoT step during teacher training was beneficial, validating the hypothesis that it prevents the teacher from taking shortcuts.

7. **Interpretability:**
 - Despite reasoning in continuous space, CODI exhibits a degree of interpretability. By projecting the latent states back into vocabulary space, observable intermediate numerical results can often be decoded.
 - Attention analysis reveals that these latent thought tokens attend to relevant operands from the input, suggesting implicit calculation.
 - Quantitative analysis showed that CODI correctly matches reference intermediate results 97.1% of the time for single-step problems, and over 75% for problems requiring up to three steps, indicating reliable generation of meaningful intermediate reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CODI represents a significant leap forward in the field of LLM reasoning, particularly for implicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods. Its key significance and potential impacts are:

1. **Validation of Latent Space Reasoning:** CODI is the first implicit CoT approach to effectively match and, in some cases, surpass the performance of explicit CoT at the GPT-2 scale. This unequivocally validates the hypothesis that LLMs can perform complex reasoning effectively within their continuous latent space, rather than being confined to verbalized, token-by-token thought processes. This opens new research avenues for exploring and understanding reasoning beyond human language.

2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** By achieving substantial compression rates (3.1x to 8.2x) and speedups (2.7x to 5.9x) with comparable or superior accuracy, CODI offers a more efficient paradigm for deploying LLMs. This is crucial for real-world applications where inference latency and computational resources are limiting factors. It allows for the development of "leaner" reasoning models that can perform complex tasks without the extensive token generation overhead of traditional CoT.

3. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** CODI's ability to outperform CoT-SFT on out-of-domain datasets and handle more verbose, noisy CoTs suggests that reasoning in continuous space can lead to models that are less prone to overfitting superficial linguistic patterns. This increased robustness is vital for deploying LLMs in diverse and unpredictable environments.

4. **Novel Self-Distillation Framework:** The single-stage, self-distillation approach employed by CODI effectively addresses the "forgetting" issues encountered by multi-stage curriculum learning methods. This framework provides a robust blueprint for transferring complex reasoning capabilities from explicit to implicit representations within a single model, offering a powerful technique for knowledge distillation in LLMs.

5. **Interpretability of Latent Reasoning:** While latent space reasoning is inherently less interpretable than explicit language, CODI demonstrates a surprising degree of interpretability by allowing intermediate results to be probed and decoded from its continuous thoughts. This bridges the gap between opaque continuous representations and understandable reasoning steps, potentially fostering trust and enabling better debugging of implicit reasoning processes.

6. **Paving the Way for Non-Verbal Reasoning:** The success of CODI suggests that reasoning doesn't *have* to be expressed in natural language. This has profound implications for future LLM capabilities, especially for tasks that involve non-verbal or difficult-to-verbalize reasoning, such as visual-spatial, emotional, or motor reasoning. It could lead to the development of more versatile and abstract problem-solving agents.

**Potential Limitations and Future Impact:**
The paper acknowledges limitations, such as the difficulty in reconstructing multi-token entities from continuous thoughts and the choice of distillation token. The backpropagation challenge for longer continuous reasoning chains (due to lack of intermediate gradients) also points to future research directions. Scaling CODI to much larger models remains an empirical challenge, though concurrent work shows promise for latent reasoning at greater scales. Overcoming these limitations would further solidify CODI's impact, potentially leading to even more sophisticated and broadly applicable latent reasoning capabilities in future LLM architectures.

In summary, CODI makes a significant contribution by proving the viability and advantages of continuous space reasoning for LLMs. It provides an efficient, robust, and surprisingly interpretable framework that could fundamentally shift how we approach reasoning in artificial intelligence, moving beyond purely linguistic paradigms.
