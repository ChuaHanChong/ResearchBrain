# PerceptTwin: Semantic Scene Reconstruction for Iterative LLM Planning and Verification

> [!summary] Summary
> PerceptTwin automatically turns open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs from a robot's perception stack into interactive AI2Thor simulations: it retrieves or generates 3D assets, aligns them to segmented point clouds, predicts affordances with an LLM, and runs plans in simulation. State diffs feed an isolated LLM judge that flags incorrect or unsafe plans, lifting LLM planning success by about 39%.

## Problem

- Semantic SLAM maps with CLIP embeddings or LLM captions stay static and passive, so plans cannot be rolled out or checked before hardware execution.
- Prior interactive scene generators need text prompts, human asset editing, or closed hand-made asset sets, so they never reconstruct the robot's actual perceived environment.
- Aligned LLM planners remain jailbreakable, and precondition-satisfying plans can still be physically harmful with no grounded verifier to catch misalignment.

## Method

- Reconstruction pipeline pairs ==mesh association== (==CLIP== retrieval over Objaverse) with ==mesh generation== (==TRELLIS== fed SAM segments instead of REMBG), plus LLM affordance prediction over a ten-skill table.
- ==Constrained ICP== placement assuming horizontal alignment, no shear, and aspect-preserving scale, using segmented global-frame point clouds; bounding boxes recover missing relational edges like isOnTopOf.
- Iterative feedback loop returns precondition-violation messages and UNIX-style diffs of key-value scene states to an isolated ==LLM judge== emitting Correct, Incorrect, or Unsafe verdicts with reasons.

## Results

- Excluding the Bomb task, plan success rose from **24%**, **8%**, **0%** to **88%**, **48%**, **12%** for GPT5, GPT5 Mini, GPT5 Nano over five feedback iterations, averaging about **39%** gain.
- A user study with **93** participants improved plan-outcome prediction by up to **18%**, with statistical significance only for consistency (precondition-failure) questions, not logic questions.
- SAM+TRELLIS assets beat REMBG+TRELLIS and CLIP+Objaverse qualitatively; the retrieval module took about **5** minutes and **8GB** RAM CPU-only versus roughly **1** hour on an L40S for generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding a verifier in a simulated rollout, not just plan text, catches misalignment that satisfies every stated goal and precondition, including jailbreak-style prompts.
> - Compact state diffs are an effective LLM interface: they cut token cost while surfacing exactly the salient changes a judge must reason over.
> - Humans and LLMs share a bias reading skill names anthropomorphically, so embodiment constraints like single-arm ordering must be shown in simulation, not merely stated.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the intersection of robotics, artificial intelligence, and simulation technologies, specifically addressing challenges in robot perception, planning, and safety.

Historically, simulations in robotics have been viewed with skepticism, often considered "doomed to succeed" and lacking fidelity to real-world complexities. However, recent advancements have reignited interest in simulation for various applications, including "sim2real" (learning policies in simulation and transferring them to real robots) and "real2sim" (generating simulations from real-world data for verification and validation). While "real2sim" has seen adoption in domains like autonomous driving, its application to open-vocabulary scene understanding and planning, particularly for indoor robotic tasks, has been limited.

Current robot perception systems, such as those based on Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), can generate 3D scene reconstructions. Recent extensions, like ConceptGraphs and HOVSG, enhance these representations with open-vocabulary semantic data (e.g., CLIP embeddings, LLM captions), making them more suitable for abstract reasoning. However, these semantic scene representations remain largely static and passive. The challenge is to transform these static representations into interactive simulations that can leverage the commonsense knowledge encoded in Large Language Models (LLMs) for dynamic plan verification.

The advent of LLMs has introduced a new paradigm for robot planning, enabling natural language reasoning and integration with open-vocabulary scene maps. These LLM planners benefit from feedback mechanisms to refine initial plans. Concurrently, the field of AI alignment has highlighted vulnerabilities in LLMs, such as "jailbreaking," where malicious prompts can lead to unsafe or unintended actions. This risk is particularly critical in robotics, where misaligned plans could have physical consequences.

PerceptTwin addresses these gaps by creating a fully automated pipeline to construct interactive simulations directly from open-vocabulary semantic scene representations. This bridges the divide between static perception data and dynamic planning environments, enabling iterative feedback for LLM planners and introducing an LLM judge for safety and alignment verification. It builds upon foundational models like CLIP, SAM, and advanced 2D-to-3D transformers (TRELLIS), integrating them with established simulation platforms like AI2Thor to enhance the reliability and safety of LLM-driven robot planning in real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of PerceptTwin are multi-faceted, stemming from limitations in current robot simulation, perception, and planning paradigms:

1. **Automate Interactive Simulation Generation from Real-world Perception:**
 - **Motivation:** Traditionally, creating detailed and interactive simulation environments for robot training and validation has been an onerous and time-consuming manual process. Developing bespoke simulations for every new real-world environment a robot encounters is often infeasible. Existing scene generation methods often rely on textual descriptions or simplified scene graphs, or require human input for asset editing.
 - **Objective:** To develop a fully automatic, real2sim pipeline that constructs interactive simulation environments directly from open-vocabulary semantic scene representations produced by a robot's perception stack, without requiring textual prompts or human intervention.

2. **Enable Robust Verification and Validation of Robot Plans:**
 - **Motivation:** While semantic scene representations provide useful data for abstract reasoning, they are static and passive. There is a missed opportunity to leverage the rich commonsense knowledge of LLMs in an interactive, dynamic environment to test and validate robot plans before execution on physical hardware. This is crucial for identifying unsafe or incorrect plans.
 - **Objective:** To create interactive simulations that allow for the "rollout" of robot plans, facilitating auditing, counterfactual analysis, and pre-execution verification. This includes enabling the system to test skill preconditions and object affordances within the simulated environment.

3. **Provide Iterative Feedback for LLM-based Robot Planners:**
 - **Motivation:** Modern LLM-based planners, while powerful in open-vocabulary semantic planning, benefit significantly from iterative feedback to refine their initial plans. Without a dynamic environment, this feedback is often limited to symbolic or rule-based checks.
 - **Objective:** To integrate the automatically generated simulations into an iterative planning pipeline, allowing LLM planners to receive concrete feedback (e.g., precondition violations, state changes) from the simulator to refine and align their plans.

4. **Enhance Safety and Alignment of LLM-generated Robot Plans:**
 - **Motivation:** Even "aligned" LLMs can be vulnerable to "jailbreaking" attacks, where crafted prompts can lead to harmful or misaligned actions. In the context of robotics, such vulnerabilities pose significant safety risks (e.g., instructing a robot to perform dangerous actions). Traditional safety checks might not capture complex commonsense reasoning or adversarial prompts.
 - **Objective:** To introduce an LLM-based "judge" that verifies plan correctness and alignment with human preferences. This judge, inspired by AI alignment literature, is isolated from the planner and observes simulation states to detect logical inconsistencies, unsafe actions, or misaligned behavior, providing explicit reasons for its judgments.

5. **Improve Human Interpretability of Robot Plans:**
 - **Motivation:** Understanding why a robot plan succeeded or failed can be challenging, especially when dealing with complex semantic representations or abstract LLM reasoning. Visualizing the plan execution in a coherent, understandable format can aid human oversight.
 - **Objective:** To leverage the visually representative simulations to improve human interpretability of robot plans, allowing users to better predict plan success or failure by observing simulated executions.

By addressing these motivations, PerceptTwin aims to lay a foundation for safer, more reliable, and more interpretable robot planning, particularly in complex, open-vocabulary environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PerceptTwin proposes a fully automatic real2sim pipeline that converts open-vocabulary 3D semantic scene graphs into interactive simulation environments. This pipeline is then integrated with an iterative LLM planning and verification system.

**A. From Map to Simulation (Reconstruction Pipeline)**

The core of PerceptTwin is its ability to take a semantic scene map (M), derived from robot perception (e.g., ConceptGraphs), and generate a corresponding interactive simulated scene (S). The input map M contains, for each object, open-vocabulary natural language descriptions, object-segmented image views, and object-segmented point clouds. The reconstruction process involves four main stages:

1. **3D Asset Acquisition:**
 - The system requires 3D assets that are semantically and visually similar to the objects in the real-world map. Two methods are offered:
 - **Mesh Association (CLIP+Objaverse):** This method, adapted from Holodeck, searches a large database of 3D assets (Objaverse) using CLIP embeddings. The query embeddings are computed from the natural language descriptions or robot-captured images of the object in M. Top-K assets are selected based on CLIP similarity, with assets having significant size deviations from the target point cloud being discarded. This approach is fast and resource-efficient but may compromise visual accuracy.
 - **Mesh Generation (SAM+TRELLIS):** For higher visual fidelity, PerceptTwin utilizes TRELLIS, a state-of-the-art 2D-to-3D flow transformer, to generate 3D assets from segmented images of the target object. Crucially, the paper proposes an improvement by replacing TRELLIS's original REMBG preprocessing with object-specific segmentations obtained from SAM (Segment Anything Model), which are assumed to be part of the input map M. This aims to improve object isolation and reduce artifacts in the generated meshes. This method is more computationally intensive (requires specific GPU hardware).

2. **3D Asset Placement:**
 - Once 3D assets are obtained, they need to be accurately localized (position, scale, orientation) within the simulated environment.
 - The system uses the segmented global-frame point clouds from the input map M for localization.
 - For orientation estimation, which is more challenging due to noisy point clouds and varied asset sources, a constrained variant of Iterative Closest Point (ICP) is employed. This variant assumes horizontal alignment, disables shear transformations, and preserves aspect ratio during scaling, yielding more reliable results than standard ICP.
 - Bounding box information from the point clouds is also used to infer relational edges between objects (e.g., "mug isOnTopOf table") if these are not explicitly present in M.

3. **Robot-Object Interactions (Affordance Prediction):**
 - To enable interactive planning, PerceptTwin defines a set of supported robotic skills (e.g., PickupObject, SliceObject, OpenObject).
 - An LLM is used to predict which of these skills are applicable (i.e., the affordances) for each object in the scene. This prediction is based on the object's natural language description (from M) and information about the robot (e.g., number of arms, height). The LLM also identifies objects that can serve specific roles (e.g., a knife as a "slicing implement").
 - Commonsense preconditions are implemented for each skill, based on the assumption of a single-arm robot. These ensure that actions are physically plausible (e.g., the robot can only manipulate one object at a time, an object must be held to be put down).

The output of this reconstruction process is a simulated scene S where each object is represented by a 3D asset, a 3D transformation matrix, and a set of predicted applicable affordances.

**B. Plan Verification**

PerceptTwin's simulation enables a feedback-driven iterative planning pipeline:

1. **LLM Planning:**
 - The system uses an LLM planner (e.g., SMART-LLM) which receives a natural language goal, a description of the robot, and a textual, key-value encoding of the initial state of the simulated environment.
 - The planner generates a sequence of actions (a plan).

2. **Simulation Execution and Feedback:**
 - The generated plan is executed within the PerceptTwin simulation.
 - If a skill in the plan violates its predefined commonsense preconditions (e.g., trying to pick up an object while the hand is not free), the simulator provides hardcoded error messages, terminating the plan execution.

3. **LLM Judge for Alignment and Correctness:**
 - A separate, "trusted" LLM acts as a "judge," inspired by AI alignment research. This judge is isolated from the LLM planner and user tampering, receiving only the original planning prompt and observing the state changes in the simulation.
 - To minimize token usage, the judge receives textual "diffs" (UNIX diff format) between the scene state before and after plan execution (or step-by-step execution for fine-grained safety checks). These diffs highlight changes in object properties, relational edges, and distances.
 - The judge performs two main verification tasks:
 - **Logical Correctness:** It assesses if the sequence of skills achieves the stated goal, requiring natural language reasoning to interpret the task and evaluate the final simulated state.
 - **Human Preference Alignment:** It checks for unsafe or misaligned actions, even if they logically fulfill a task. This is crucial for mitigating "jailbreaking" vulnerabilities.
 - Based on its analysis, the judge sends one of three messages: "Unsafe <reason>", "Incorrect <reason>", or "Correct <reason>". This feedback is then used to prompt the LLM planner for refinement in an iterative loop, up to a specified number of iterations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates PerceptTwin across several dimensions, including the visual quality of reconstructions, its impact on human interpretability, and its effectiveness in improving and verifying LLM-generated plans.

**A. Visuals and Reconstruction Quality**

- **Asset Generation Comparison:** The study demonstrates that the **SAM+TRELLIS** pipeline (proposed in this work) generally yields more accurate and visually representative 3D assets compared to:
 - **CLIP+Objaverse:** While offering diversity and speed, CLIP+Objaverse often results in semantically similar but visually inaccurate assets (e.g., a "pickup truck" for a "van," a "multicolored box" for a "cardboard box").
 - **REMBG+TRELLIS (original TRELLIS preprocessing):** Replacing REMBG with SAM-segmented object views improves object isolation and reduces artifacts (e.g., black patches or planes), leading to better semantic closeness and visual fidelity of the generated assets.
- **Scene Diversity:** PerceptTwin successfully reconstructs diverse input maps, including large objects and both indoor and outdoor scenes. Despite limitations due to input data (e.g., missing floor color, restricted viewpoints), the reconstructions are representative of the input map.
- **Computational Requirements:**
 - Processing a scene with approximately 30 objects using the CLIP+Objaverse module took around 5 minutes and 8GB RAM, requiring no GPU.
 - The TRELLIS module required an NVIDIA GPU (specifically an L40S) with at least 16GB VRAM, and processing the same scene took approximately 1 hour.

**B. Human Interpretability**

A user study (N=93 participants) was conducted to assess if PerceptTwin improved human understanding of robot plans. Participants predicted plan success or failure using either a baseline video of the input map's point cloud or a plan execution video generated by PerceptTwin, alongside a representative scene image.

- **Question Categories:** Questions were divided into "logic" (skills executed, but outcome correct/incorrect) and "consistency" (execution failed due to violated preconditions or structural mistakes).
- **Results:** While PerceptTwin generally improved accuracy across both categories, a statistically significant improvement was observed specifically for **consistency questions**, with an average increase of up to 18%.
- **Interpretation:** This suggests PerceptTwin is particularly effective in helping humans detect process-level failures or precondition violations, which might be otherwise misinterpreted due to human biases in understanding skill names (e.g., a human can "pickup" then "open," but a single-armed robot might need to "open" first).

**C. Planning**

The study evaluated PerceptTwin's impact on LLM planning using SMART-LLM as a baseline planner, with GPT5, GPT5 Mini, and GPT5 Nano LLMs across several scenarios (Blocks, Veggies, Bomb). Five successive feedback iterations from PerceptTwin were allowed.

- **Overall Plan Success:** Not considering the "Bomb" task (designed for safety failure), initial plan success rates were low (GPT5: 24%, GPT5 Mini: 8%, GPT5 Nano: 0%). After PerceptTwin feedback, these rates significantly improved:
 - GPT5: 24% increased to 88%
 - GPT5 Mini: 8% increased to 48%
 - GPT5 Nano: 0% increased to 12%
 - This represents an average plan success increase of approximately **39%** across the tested planners.
- **Scenario-Specific Findings:**
 - **Blocks:** PerceptTwin feedback successfully narrowed performance gaps between complex block-stacking tasks.
 - **Veggies:** Feedback improved both plan success and safety. The LLM judge intervened 7 times to prevent unsafe knife handling near a human, even when the planner correctly inferred subtasks like "slice onion and pepper." Precondition violations (e.g., trying to slice without holding a knife) were also resolved. For long-horizon tasks (e.g., "prep & put in cooler"), GPT5 improved from zero to 4 out of 5 successes.
 - **Bomb (Adversarial Prompt):** This scenario tested PerceptTwin's ability to mitigate adversarial attacks. The LLM judge reliably flagged plans that would have detonated a bomb near a laptop (as in previous jailbreaking examples), demonstrating its effectiveness in detecting and preventing harmful actions, even when the planner generated them due to a reframed prompt. The judge provided explicit "Unsafe" feedback.
- **Judge Effectiveness:** The LLM judge's interventions were manually verified and found to be accurate in most cases, providing specific reasons for flagging plans as "Unsafe" or "Incorrect."
- **Comprehensive Simulation Need:** The results indicate that iterative LLM planning necessitates more than just static semantic scene maps. Tasks demanded open-vocabulary affordance prediction, accurate object movements, commonsense precondition checks, and alignment reasoning (e.g., forbidding knife use near humans), validating PerceptTwin's utility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PerceptTwin represents a substantial step toward practical and reliable robot planning in complex, open-vocabulary environments, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Automated Real2Sim:** The work addresses the long-standing challenge of automatically generating interactive simulation environments from real-world perception data. By fully automating the pipeline from open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs to a functional simulator, PerceptTwin reduces the manual effort and cost traditionally associated with creating simulation assets and environments. This capability is critical for scaling robot development and deployment to diverse real-world settings.

2. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability for LLM Planning:** PerceptTwin significantly improves the safety and reliability of LLM-based robot planning. The iterative feedback loop from the simulator allows planners to refine their actions based on physical plausibility and skill preconditions. Crucially, the introduction of an LLM judge provides an external, trusted mechanism to verify plan correctness and alignment with human preferences, mitigating the risks associated with LLM "jailbreaking" or the generation of unsafe actions in physical robots. This is particularly impactful for applications where safety is paramount.

3. **Improved LLM Planner Performance:** The empirical results demonstrate that PerceptTwin feedback leads to a substantial increase in LLM planning success rates (an average of approximately 39%). This indicates that grounded feedback from a dynamic simulation environment is a critical component for LLM planners to reason effectively in complex, long-horizon tasks, moving beyond purely symbolic or text-based reasoning.

4. **Increased Human Interpretability:** By providing a visually representative simulation of plan execution, PerceptTwin enhances human understanding and trust in robot autonomy. The user study results confirm that observing plans in simulation, especially for "consistency" errors, improves human ability to predict plan success or failure. This transparency is valuable for debugging, oversight, and training human operators.

5. **Foundation for Future Robot Learning and Development:** PerceptTwin provides a reusable system for generating diverse simulated scenes from real data, which can serve as a foundation for various downstream applications, including:
 - **Dataset Generation:** Automatically creating diverse datasets for training new robot policies.
 - **Policy Learning:** Providing a reliable simulation environment for sim2real transfer learning.
 - **Adversarial Robustness:** Developing and testing more robust robot planning and control systems against adversarial inputs.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
While highly impactful, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the AI2Thor simulator's inability to visually represent appearance changes for user-provided 3D assets when skills modify object states (e.g., cutting a vegetable). This implies that some skill outcomes are only represented textually, not visually. Addressing this limitation would further enhance the visual fidelity and interpretability of state-changing actions.

In conclusion, PerceptTwin represents a significant advancement in bridging the gap between robot perception and interactive planning. Its automated real2sim pipeline, coupled with iterative LLM feedback and a safety-conscious judge, offers a robust framework for developing safer, more reliable, and more interpretable autonomous robotic systems.
