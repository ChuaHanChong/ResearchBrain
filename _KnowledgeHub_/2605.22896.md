# Agentic-VLA: Efficient Online Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Agentic-VLA framework introduces an agentic training approach to enhance vision-language-action (VLA) models, enabling efficient online adaptation by integrating adaptive reward synthesis, language-guided exploration, and experience memory. This system from Scinetics achieves state-of-the-art performance with a 97.8% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, demonstrating a 2.4x faster convergence and a 2.4x reduction in sample complexity compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models trained via supervised fine-tuning exhibit poor generalization, often memorizing trajectories and failing when environmental conditions deviate from training data.
- Current VLA training paradigms suffer from low efficiency, requiring hundreds of expert demonstrations per task which leads to high data collection costs and limits scalability.
- Prior online adaptation methods for VLAs often rely on noisy reward signals, inefficient random exploration strategies, and lack explicit mechanisms for transferring knowledge across related tasks.

## Method

- An agentic training framework is proposed that dynamically manages reward design, exploration, and knowledge transfer based on the VLA's current learning state.
- Adaptive Reward Synthesis (ARS) generates capability-aware, progress-based dense rewards by decomposing tasks into sub-goals and adjusting reward weights based on the VLA's mastery of each sub-goal.
- Language-Guided Exploration (LGE) replaces random exploration with structured, semantically-informed suggestions from a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), improving sample efficiency.
- Experience Memory (EM) enables cross-task knowledge transfer by storing and retrieving task-relevant policy parameters for warm-starting new tasks.

## Results

- Achieved an average 97.8% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, an 8.6% improvement over the SFT baseline and 2.0% over the previous state-of-the-art RL method (EVOLVE-VLA).
- Demonstrated 2.4x faster convergence, reaching 90% success rate on LIBERO-Long in 700 iterations, and a 2.4x reduction in sample complexity compared to EVOLVE-VLA.
- Showed strong cross-task generalization, achieving 31.2% success in zero-shot transfer (vs. 0% for SFT) and outperforming baselines on the dual-arm RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, particularly under domain randomization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adjusting reward weights based on the VLA's competence in sub-goals creates an effective curriculum, significantly boosting performance on complex, long-horizon tasks.
> - Leveraging a VLM to provide natural language exploration suggestions offers a more efficient and targeted exploration strategy than random sampling for high-dimensional VLA action spaces.
> - Storing and interpolating policy parameters from previously adapted tasks facilitates robust warm-starting, enabling efficient cross-task knowledge transfer and adaptation to new, related tasks.
