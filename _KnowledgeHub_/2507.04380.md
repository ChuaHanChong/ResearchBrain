# Transferring Visual Explainability of Self-Explaining Models through Task Arithmetic

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at STAIR Lab and ZOZO Research developed a method using task arithmetic to transfer visual explainability to self-explaining models without requiring target domain explanation data. The approach achieves explanation quality comparable to Kernel SHAP with 150 times fewer inferences, while maintaining classification accuracy at 90% of a prediction-only baseline model.

## Problem

- Developing and deploying visual explainability in AI systems faces high computational and labeling costs.
- Training self-explaining models, while efficient at inference, demands expensive ground-truth explanation data or numerous model passes.
- Post-hoc explanation methods are computationally intensive during inference, limiting their use in real-time or resource-constrained applications.

## Method

- The method uses ==task arithmetic== to algebraically extract and transfer an =='explainability vector'== from a source self-explaining model to a target domain model.
- It extends vision-language pretrained models (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/32) to generate both predictions and ==patch-level attributions== in a single forward pass.
- Target model parameters are constructed by combining a target-specific ==classification task vector== with the source-learned explainability vector, bypassing the need for explanation data in the target domain.

## Results

- The transferred models maintained high classification accuracy (average **0.876**) close to target-finetuned prediction-only models (**0.890**), while significantly improving explanation quality (e.g., E-RMSE decreased from **1.599** to **0.943**).
- Explanation generation achieved IoU@10 scores comparable to Kernel SHAP with approximately **150 times** fewer model inferences, demonstrating a substantial reduction in computational cost.
- An explainability vector learned from ImageNet+X proved robust, improving explanation quality across 9 out of 10 diverse target datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An 'explainability vector' can be mathematically isolated and transferred between models using task arithmetic, representing specific knowledge for explanation generation.
> - This explainability knowledge can be effectively transferred to models in a target domain that were only trained for prediction, without requiring ground-truth explanations for the target tasks.
> - The similarity between classification-only task vectors from source and target domains can moderately predict the success of explainability transfer.
