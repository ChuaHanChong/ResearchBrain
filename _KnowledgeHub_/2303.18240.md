# Where are we in the search for an Artificial Visual Cortex for Embodied Intelligence?

> [!summary] Summary
> VC-1 is a ViT-L visual encoder pre-trained with masked auto-encoding on 5.6M frames drawn from 4,000+ hours of egocentric video across seven sources plus ImageNet, evaluated on CortexBench, a new 17-task suite spanning locomotion, navigation, dexterous and mobile manipulation. VC-1 reaches 68.7 mean success, beating all prior pre-trained representations on average, and task-specific adaptation matches or exceeds the best published result on every benchmark.

## Problem

- Prior pre-trained visual representation studies are incommensurable: each uses a different self-supervised objective, pre-training corpus, and narrow downstream task suite, preventing apples-to-apples comparison.
- Positive scaling trends for data size and diversity were established only within single narrow domains such as tabletop manipulation, so their generality across embodied tasks was untested and possibly overextrapolated.
- Whether and how to adapt frozen encoders was unresolved: some work reports fine-tuning degrades few-shot imitation, other work reports large gains under large-scale reinforcement learning.

## Method

- **CortexBench** aggregates 17 tasks from seven suites (Adroit, MetaWorld, DMControl, TriFinger, ObjectNav, ImageNav, Mobile-Pick), fixing each domain's policy-learning protocol so only the visual encoder varies.
- Four nested pre-training corpora (Ego4D, +Manipulation, +Navigation, +ImageNet, up to 5.6M frames) train ==ViT==-B and ViT-L with ==masked auto-encoding==, isolating model size, dataset size, and diversity; the largest is named **VC-1**.
- Two adaptation routes: end-to-end fine-tuning of the backbone under the task loss, and ==MAE== adaptation that continues self-supervised reconstruction on task frames before freezing the encoder for policy learning.

## Results

- No existing encoder dominates: R3M leads Adroit, MetaWorld, DMControl; MVP ViT-L leads TriFinger, ImageNav, Mobile-Pick; CLIP leads ObjectNav. VC-1 tops mean success at **68.7** versus MVP **67.5**, R3M **58.0**, CLIP **57.0**.
- Adaptation is domain-split: MAE adaptation lifts Adroit **59.3** to **72.0** and DMControl **66.9** to **80.9**, while end-to-end fine-tuning lifts ImageNav **70.3** to **81.6** and Mobile-Pick **63.2** to **74.0** but collapses Adroit to **15.9**.
- On real hardware, VC-1 reaches **70.0%** on four Franka tasks versus MVP's **55.0%**, rising to **85.0%** with MAE adaptation; on TriFinger push-cube VC-1 scores **45.8** versus MVP **31.9**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling pre-training data helps on average but not uniformly; diversity of data type matters more than raw frame count, and adding static internet images still helps embodied control.
> - Match the adaptation method to the data regime: self-supervised reconstruction on task frames for few-shot imitation, end-to-end fine-tuning only when large-scale imitation or reinforcement data is available.
> - Averaged benchmark scores conceal per-task inversions, so a single aggregate number is insufficient evidence that any representation is a general-purpose foundation model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: **Computer Vision, Reinforcement Learning (RL), Robot Learning, and Embodied Artificial Intelligence (EAI)**. It directly addresses the burgeoning trend of **Foundation Models** – large, pre-trained models that can be adapted to a wide range of downstream tasks – by exploring their applicability and effectiveness in the context of embodied agents.

**Connection to Foundation Models:** The paper explicitly frames pre-trained visual representations (PVRs) as visual 'foundation models' for EAI. This mirrors the success of large language models (LLMs) like GPT and BERT in NLP, and vision-language models like CLIP in general vision tasks. The core idea is to learn generalizable visual features from vast datasets, which can then accelerate learning and improve performance for specific embodied tasks.

**Addressing a Gap in Embodied AI:** Prior to this work, studies on PVRs for EAI were largely "incommensurable." Different self-supervised learning (SSL) algorithms, pre-training datasets, and evaluation tasks made it difficult to compare progress and draw general conclusions. This paper fills this critical gap by providing a comprehensive, systematic benchmark for evaluating and comparing diverse PVRs across a broad spectrum of EAI tasks. It seeks to answer the fundamental question of whether a "universal" visual backbone for EAI already exists or is within reach.

**The "Artificial Visual Cortex" Analogy:** The paper draws inspiration from the biological visual cortex, which converts sight into movement. It operationalizes the search for an "artificial visual cortex" as the design of PVRs that, combined with a policy, enable an agent to convert camera input into actions. This framing highlights the ambition to create a single, broadly capable visual perception module for EAI, analogous to how a biological visual cortex supports various sensorimotor skills.

**Scaling Hypothesis in EAI:** The paper engages directly with the "scaling hypothesis" prevalent in deep learning, which posits that increasing model and data size consistently improves performance. While prior work in robot manipulation (e.g., MVP) showed positive scaling trends, this study rigorously tests this hypothesis across a much broader range of EAI tasks. Its nuanced findings, which often refute a "naive extrapolation" of universal improvement through scaling, provide crucial insights for future research directions.

**Adaptation of Pre-trained Models:** A significant part of the research addresses the critical question of how to effectively adapt foundation models for downstream tasks. This is a general challenge in the field, and the paper explores two distinct adaptation strategies (end-to-end fine-tuning and MAE adaptation) in the EAI context, revealing their respective strengths and weaknesses depending on the data availability and task characteristics.

By systematically curating a diverse benchmark (CORTEXBENCH), conducting large-scale empirical evaluations, and open-sourcing models, this work aims to unify disparate research efforts in embodied AI and provide a common ground for measuring progress toward a generalized artificial visual cortex.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this paper stems from a fundamental question posed by early AI researchers: "how do we design an artificial visual cortex, the module in a computational system that (together with a policy) enables an agent to convert camera input into actions?" In contemporary AI, this translates to the design and evaluation of Pre-trained Visual Representations (PVRs), or visual 'foundation models,' for Embodied AI (EAI).

The paper identifies a crucial gap in the existing literature: prior studies on PVRs for EAI have been "incommensurable," meaning they used different self-supervised learning (SSL) algorithms, varied pre-training datasets, and were evaluated on distinct downstream EAI tasks. This lack of standardized comparison makes it impossible to draw broad conclusions about the effectiveness and generalizability of current PVRs.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives and motivations of the research are:

1. **Systematic Evaluation of Existing PVRs:** To conduct the "largest and most comprehensive empirical study" of visual foundation models for EAI to date. This involves evaluating a representative set of existing open-sourced PVRs (CLIP, R3M, MVP, VIP) across a diverse set of EAI tasks to determine if any single model is universally dominant. The initial motivation is to answer: "Does an artificial visual cortex already exist?"

2. **Curate a Comprehensive Benchmark (CORTEXBENCH):** To establish a standardized evaluation suite consisting of 17 diverse tasks spanning locomotion, navigation, dexterous manipulation, and mobile manipulation. This benchmark aims to provide a robust platform for measuring progress towards a generalized artificial visual cortex for EAI.

3. **Investigate the Scaling Hypothesis in EAI:** To systematically study how scaling pre-training dataset size, diversity, and model size (specifically using Vision Transformers trained with Masked Auto-Encoding) affects performance on a broad range of EAI tasks. This is motivated by the general scaling trends observed in other AI domains and the need to understand if they universally apply to the complex, interactive nature of EAI.

4. **Develop a Strong, Broadly Applicable PVR (VC-1):** Based on the scaling studies, to identify and train the most effective PVR model (named VC-1) for EAI that performs well on average across the diverse CORTEXBENCH tasks.

5. **Explore Adaptation Strategies for PVRs:** To determine if and how adapting a strong general PVR like VC-1 can further improve performance on specific downstream tasks. This involves investigating both end-to-end (E2E) fine-tuning and self-supervised (MAE) adaptation to address task-specialization and domain gap.

6. **Validate Findings in Real-World Hardware:** To provide proof-of-concept hardware experiments, demonstrating the practical applicability and superior performance of VC-1 and its adapted variants on physical robotic platforms.

7. **Open-Source Resources:** To contribute back to the research community by open-sourcing the CORTEXBENCH code and all trained models (including VC-1), facilitating further research and benchmarking in embodied AI.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to move beyond fragmented studies and provide a unified, rigorous understanding of the current state and future directions for building a truly general-purpose visual perception system for embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research is built around a systematic empirical evaluation, involving the curation of a new benchmark, controlled experiments on scaling, and an exploration of adaptation strategies, all supported by significant computational resources.

#### 3.1. CORTEXBENCH: The Evaluation Benchmark

The cornerstone of this study is **CORTEXBENCH**, a curated benchmark designed to systematically evaluate PVRs across a diverse range of Embodied AI tasks.
- **Composition:** It comprises 17 distinct tasks drawn from 7 established EAI benchmarks: Adroit, MetaWorld, DeepMind Control (DMC), TriFinger, Habitat (ImageNav, ObjectNav), and Habitat 2.0 (MobilePick).
- **Diversity:** The tasks span various EAI challenges:
 - **Low-level locomotion and dexterous manipulation:** DMC, Adroit, TriFinger.
 - **Table-top manipulation:** MetaWorld.
 - **Indoor visual navigation:** Habitat (ImageNav, ObjectNav).
 - **Mobile manipulation:** Habitat 2.0 (MobilePick).
- **Environmental and Embodiment Variation:** Environments range from flat planes to photorealistic 3D scans of indoor spaces. Agent embodiments include stationary arms, dexterous hands, and articulated mobile manipulators.
- **Policy Learning Paradigms:** Different learning conditions are employed, ranging from few-shot imitation learning (e.g., Adroit, MetaWorld, DMC, TriFinger) to large-scale reinforcement learning (e.g., ImageNav, ObjectNav, MobilePick). For each task, the study adheres to community-standard policy learning and evaluation protocols to ensure fair comparison and relevance.
- **Metrics:** Two primary metrics are used: Mean Success (average success rate across all benchmarks) and Mean Rank (average rank of a PVR across all benchmarks).

#### 3.2. Initial Evaluation of Existing PVRs

To assess the current state of visual foundation models for EAI, the paper first evaluates several prominent, open-sourced PVRs:
- **CLIP (ViT-B):** Trained on 400M image-text pairs with a contrastive objective.
- **R3M (ResNet-50):** Trained on 5M egocentric video frames with a time-contrastive video-language alignment objective.
- **MVP (ViT-B, ViT-L):** Trained on 4.5M egocentric video frames and ImageNet with a Masked Auto-Encoding (MAE) objective.
- **VIP (ResNet-50):** Trained on 5M egocentric video frames with a goal-conditioned value function objective.
- **Baselines:** Randomly initialized Vision Transformers (ViT-B, ViT-L) with frozen and fine-tuned weights are used to establish lower bounds and assess the necessity of pre-training.
For this initial evaluation, all PVRs are used in a "frozen" manner (i.e., their weights are fixed), to isolate the quality of the learned representations from the downstream task learning.

#### 3.3. Scaling Hypothesis Study

To understand the impact of data and model scaling, the authors conduct a controlled study:
- **Fixed Pre-training Objective:** Masked Auto-Encoding (MAE) is chosen due to MVP's strong initial performance.
- **Varying Visual Backbones:** Two Vision Transformer (ViT) sizes are used: ViT-B (86M parameters) and ViT-L (307M parameters).
- **Curated Pre-training Datasets:** Over 4,000 hours of egocentric videos from 7 sources (over 4.3M images) are combined with ImageNet to create 5 distinct datasets (Table 3), varying in size and diversity:
 - `Ego4D`: Base dataset of daily life egocentric videos.
 - `Ego4D+M` (Manipulation): `Ego4D` + 100DOH, SS-V2, Epic Kitchens (totaling 3.5M frames, manipulation-focused).
 - `Ego4D+N` (Navigation): `Ego4D` + OpenHouse24, Real Estate 10K (totaling 3.5M frames, navigation-focused, more diverse than `Ego4D+M`).
 - `Ego4D+MN` (Manipulation, Navigation): Combination of all previous video datasets (4.3M frames).
 - `Ego4D+MNI` (Manipulation, Navigation, ImageNet): `Ego4D+MN` + ImageNet (5.6M frames).
- **Computational Scale:** Training these models required "over 10,000 GPU-hours," highlighting the immense computational investment.

#### 3.4. Adapting VC-1

After identifying VC-1 (the ViT-L model trained on `Ego4D+MNI`) as the best average performer, the study investigates adaptation strategies:
- **End-to-end (E2E) Fine-tuning:** The VC-1 backbone weights are allowed to be updated during task-specific training (e.g., with policy losses). This aims for both task-specialization and domain gap mitigation.
- **MAE Adaptation:** The VC-1 backbone is continuously trained with the MAE self-supervised objective on task-specific data (e.g., expert demonstrations or sampled training environment frames). After this adaptation, the backbone is frozen for policy learning. This strategy primarily targets domain gap mitigation.
- **Baseline:** "In-domain MAE baseline" involves training MAE from random initialization on task-specific data for comparison with MAE adaptation of VC-1.

#### 3.5. Real-World Hardware Experiments

To provide practical validation, proof-of-concept experiments are conducted on two real robotic platforms:
- **TriFinger:** A three-finger dexterous hand robot, for a `Push-Cube` task.
- **Franka Emika Panda arm:** For tasks like reaching, bottle pickup, close drawer, and toaster plunge.
These experiments use few-shot imitation learning, with demonstrations collected directly in the real world. Comparisons are made between frozen VC-1, adapted VC-1, and the best pre-existing PVR (MVP).

Overall, the methodology is rigorous and multi-faceted, allowing for both broad comparisons and focused analyses of critical factors influencing PVR performance in EAI.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yields several significant findings regarding the current state and future trajectory of visual foundation models for Embodied AI:

#### 4.1. No Universal Visual Foundation Model Exists (Yet)

- **Initial Evaluation (Section 4):** A crucial "negative result" is that no single existing PVR (CLIP, R3M, MVP, VIP) is universally dominant across all 17 CORTEXBENCH tasks. PVRs tend to perform best in the specific domains they were designed for. For instance, R3M excels on Adroit, MetaWorld, and DMControl (manipulation/locomotion), while MVP (ViT-L) performs best on TriFinger, ImageNav, and MobilePick (navigation/mobile manipulation), and CLIP on ObjectNav (visual navigation).
- However, existing PVRs generally outperform learning-from-scratch baselines, confirming the value of pre-training.

#### 4.2. Nuanced Scaling Hypothesis for EAI (Section 5)

The study of scaling model size, dataset size, and diversity reveals a more complex picture than simple "more is better" extrapolations from prior work:
- **Model Size:** Increasing model size (from ViT-B to ViT-L) generally improves average performance on CORTEXBENCH. However, this improvement is not universal; exceptions exist where the smaller ViT-B outperforms ViT-L on specific tasks (e.g., MetaWorld and TriFinger when pre-trained on `Ego4D+MNI`).
- **Dataset Size and Diversity:**
 - Generally, increasing dataset size and diversity leads to improved average performance.
 - **Diversity Matters More than Pure Size:** Adding indoor navigation data (`Ego4D+N`) yielded larger performance gains over the base `Ego4D` than adding an equivalent amount of *more manipulation-focused data* (`Ego4D+M`). Furthermore, `Ego4D+MN` (4.3M frames) showed only marginal improvement over `Ego4D+N` (3.5M frames), suggesting that once diversity is covered, simply adding more data of similar types might offer diminishing returns.
 - **Importance of Static Images:** Surprisingly, adding ImageNet (static internet images) to the diverse video datasets (`Ego4D+MNI`) significantly boosted average performance on EAI tasks, highlighting the importance of broad visual diversity, even from non-egocentric sources.
- **VC-1 Performance:** The largest model, **VC-1 (ViT-L pre-trained on `Ego4D+MNI`)**, achieves the best average rank and mean success across CORTEXBENCH (outperforming existing PVRs by 1.2% on average). However, critically, **VC-1 does not universally dominate individual tasks**. This challenges the naive assumption that a single, large pre-trained model will be optimal for all EAI tasks without further specialization.

#### 4.3. VC-1 Compared to Existing PVRs

- VC-1 significantly outperforms CLIP (despite training on a 70x smaller dataset), R3M, and fine-tuning from scratch baselines on average across benchmarks.
- VC-1 generally outperforms MVP, especially due to its more diverse pre-training dataset.
- R3M, however, still outperforms VC-1 on some manipulation/locomotion tasks (Adroit, MetaWorld, DMControl), suggesting that architectural choices (ResNet-based in R3M vs. Transformer-based in VC-1) or specific pre-training objectives might provide unique inductive biases beneficial for certain task modalities. This indicates room for further architectural innovation.

#### 4.4. The Power of Adaptation for VC-1 (Section 6)

Adapting VC-1 for downstream tasks proves critical for achieving competitive or state-of-the-art results:
- **End-to-End (E2E) Fine-tuning:** Substantially improves performance in **large-scale IL/RL domains** (ObjectNav, ImageNav, Mobile Pick), indicating its effectiveness when ample task-specific data is available. However, it leads to a **significant performance drop** in **few-shot IL domains** (Adroit, MetaWorld, DMC, TriFinger), likely due to overfitting on small datasets.
- **MAE Adaptation:** Significantly improves performance in **few-shot IL domains**, mitigating the domain gap between pre-training data and simulation/robot environments. It also performs well in `DMControl` and `TriFinger` tasks, highlighting the importance of in-domain data. In contrast, it leads to small reductions in performance in large-scale IL/RL domains where E2E fine-tuning is superior.
- **Overall Adaptation:** VC-1 (adapted), through a combination of E2E fine-tuning (for large-scale tasks) and MAE adaptation (for few-shot tasks), becomes competitive with or *outperforms the best known results* on *all* benchmarks in CORTEXBENCH. This highlights adaptation as a powerful paradigm for bridging the gap between a general PVR and task-specific performance.

#### 4.5. Real-World Hardware Generalization (Section 7)

- VC-1 and VC-1 (adapted) substantially outperform the strongest pre-existing PVR (MVP) on real TriFinger and Franka Emika Panda robots in few-shot imitation learning settings.
- The benefits of adaptation observed in simulation (E2E for TriFinger, MAE for Franka tasks) also translate to real-world hardware, further validating the approach.

In summary, while a truly universal visual cortex for EAI does not yet exist, this study demonstrates that systematically designed PVRs, especially when strategically adapted, can achieve high performance across a wide array of embodied tasks, pushing the frontier closer to that ambitious goal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence, with broad implications and potential impact across several domains:

#### 5.1. Standardization and Acceleration of EAI Research

- **CORTEXBENCH as a Community Asset:** The creation and open-sourcing of CORTEXBENCH is a major contribution. It provides a much-needed standardized and comprehensive evaluation suite for PVRs in EAI, enabling researchers to rigorously compare models across diverse tasks. This will undoubtedly accelerate research by providing a common ground for progress, similar to how benchmarks like ImageNet or GLUE have propelled advancements in computer vision and NLP.
- **Open-Sourced Models and Code:** Releasing VC-1 and other trained models, along with the CORTEXBENCH code, offers powerful baselines and starting points for the community. This reduces the immense computational burden for individual researchers and facilitates reproducibility and further innovation.

#### 5.2. Deeper Understanding of Visual Foundation Models for EAI

- **Nuanced Scaling Insights:** The findings on the scaling hypothesis, particularly that naive scaling of data size and diversity does not guarantee universal performance improvement, are crucial. They refute simplistic notions and provide a more nuanced understanding of how to effectively scale PVRs for EAI. The emphasis on *diversity* over sheer volume (beyond a certain point) and the surprising utility of *static ImageNet data* for embodied tasks offer invaluable guidance for future data collection and pre-training strategies.
- **The Role of Adaptation:** The study definitively demonstrates that while a single "frozen" PVR may not be universally dominant, strategic adaptation (either via E2E fine-tuning or MAE adaptation) can bridge domain gaps and specialize models to achieve competitive or superior performance across a broad task landscape. This highlights that the future of EAI foundation models likely involves robust adaptation mechanisms.

#### 5.3. Towards a Generalized Artificial Visual Cortex

- **VC-1 as a Strong Baseline:** VC-1, especially when adapted, represents a powerful general-purpose PVR for EAI. Its ability to achieve high performance across a wide range of tasks moves the field closer to the ideal of a "single visual cortex" for embodied agents, rather than task-specific perception modules. This could simplify agent design and promote more generalizable robotic capabilities.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The successful proof-of-concept hardware experiments are highly significant. They demonstrate that the insights and models developed in simulation (CORTEXBENCH) can effectively transfer to real-world robotic platforms, a critical challenge in robotics. The fact that VC-1 and its adapted versions outperform prior PVRs on hardware further validates their practical utility.

#### 5.4. Identifying Future Research Directions

- The paper clearly articulates that the search for a truly "foundational" PVR is ongoing. It points to the need for innovations in architecture, learning paradigms (e.g., incorporating temporal signals, 3D spatial priors, or objectness more explicitly), and data engineering. This forward-looking perspective will guide future research efforts in the community.
- The contrasting performance of different PVRs (e.g., VC-1 vs. R3M on certain tasks) due to architectural differences (ViT vs. ResNet) or pre-training objectives suggests that hybrid approaches or new architectures combining strengths could be fruitful.

In conclusion, this research provides not only a rigorous evaluation of the current state of visual foundation models for embodied AI but also offers concrete tools, methodologies, and critical insights that will shape the development of more capable and generalizable embodied agents in the years to come.
