# Learning from Failures: Correction-Oriented Policy Optimization with Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Correction-Oriented Policy Optimization (CIPO) introduces a method to enhance large language models' reasoning and error correction capabilities by converting on-policy failed trajectories into explicit, directional supervision. This approach led to a 4.55% improvement in mathematical reasoning and a 1.43% gain in code generation over existing baselines, alongside substantial boosts in error correction performance across various tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods often face challenges due to sparse and binary reward signals, making effective credit assignment difficult in complex, multi-step reasoning tasks.
- Current RLVR approaches underutilize information from failed trajectories, uniformly penalizing all errors without differentiating between error modes or proximity to correct solutions, leading to ambiguous optimization signals.
- Previous solutions to address sparse rewards often rely on auxiliary models or external supervision, which can introduce additional costs, biases, or limit the generalizability of the trained models.

## Method

- **CIPO** transforms ==on-policy failed trajectories== into valuable, correction-oriented supervision by training the policy to refine its own erroneous attempts into correct solutions.
- The method incorporates an ==adaptive replay ratio== to balance learning from failures with retaining existing capabilities and uses ==risk-averse reward shaping== to prevent capability regressions during correction.
- A ==difficulty-aware preference mechanism== prioritizes replaying prompts within a 'medium-difficulty' range, focusing training on the most informative samples for efficient learning.

## Results

- CIPO improved mathematical reasoning accuracy by an average of **+4.55%** (to **64.38%**) across six benchmarks and code generation accuracy by **+1.43%** (to **54.27%**) on LiveCodeBench v6 compared to GRPO.
- The method expanded the intrinsic reasoning capacity of models, demonstrated by a **+6.12%** higher pass@32 on competition-style mathematical benchmarks and a monotonically increasing performance trajectory during training.
- CIPO substantially enhanced error correction ability, boosting correction rates by **+7.74%** on mathematical tasks (CriticBench) and **+4.20%** on code debugging (DebugBench), with these correction capabilities generalizing to out-of-domain reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Converting ambiguous failure signals into precise, directional guidance by requiring the model to 'self-correct' its errors yields a richer and denser learning signal for policy optimization.
> - It is possible to derive fine-grained, corrective supervision directly from the model's own on-policy failed rollouts, eliminating the need for external annotations, process reward models, or LLM-based critics.
> - Adaptive control mechanisms for replay ratios and risk-averse reward shaping are crucial for maintaining learning stability and preventing the model from 'unlearning' correct behaviors while acquiring new error-correction skills.
