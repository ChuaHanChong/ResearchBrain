# Improving Few-Shot Generalization by Exploring and Exploiting Auxiliary Data

> [!summary] Summary
> A method for Few-shot Learning with Auxiliary Data (FLAD) that models dataset selection as a Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) problem enables adaptive auxiliary data utilization. This approach boosts a 3 billion parameter model's performance by 5.6-9.2% over baselines, allowing it to surpass a 175 billion parameter GPT-3 on few-shot benchmarks.

## Problem

- Few-shot learning (FSL) struggles to generalize effectively from very limited labeled examples, hindering real-world applications.
- Previous Few-shot Learning with Auxiliary Data (FLAD) methods face severe scalability issues, typically only handling 1-3 auxiliary datasets due to increasing computational complexity.
- Manually selecting useful auxiliary data or designing training curricula for a large pool of datasets is combinatorially complex and often leads to sub-optimal results.

## Method

- The authors reformulate FLAD as a ==Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) problem==, treating each auxiliary dataset as an 'arm' to be explored and exploited.
- They design efficient ==gradient-based reward functions== (Gradient Alignment, Gradient Magnitude Similarity, Aggregate Reward) computed solely on language modeling head parameters to minimize overhead.
- Two MAB algorithms, ==EXP3-FLAD and UCB1-FLAD==, are adapted to dynamically select auxiliary datasets for model updates, balancing exploration of new data with exploitation of known beneficial data.

## Results

- EXP3-FLAD and UCB1-FLAD consistently outperformed all baselines, achieving absolute performance gains of **5.6%** to **9.2%** over Target-Only fine-tuning.
- A **3 billion parameter** T0-3B model, when utilizing MAB-FLAD with the P3 dataset, demonstrated superior performance to the **175 billion parameter** GPT-3 on the evaluated few-shot benchmarks.
- MAB-based methods exhibited robust scalability, improving performance with a larger number of auxiliary datasets (from **35** to **260**) while maintaining computational efficiency with modest increases in time-to-convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prioritized, adaptive sampling of auxiliary data via MAB methods is more effective than static mixing or loss-scaling approaches, especially with numerous auxiliary datasets.
> - An intelligent balance between exploring diverse auxiliary data and exploiting beneficial subsets is critical for improved few-shot generalization.
> - Effective data interaction strategies can enable smaller models (3 billion parameters) to achieve or exceed the few-shot performance of significantly larger models (175 billion parameters) by leveraging auxiliary data.
