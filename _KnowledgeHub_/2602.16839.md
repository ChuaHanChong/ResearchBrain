# Training Large Reasoning Models Efficiently via Progressive Thought Encoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Microsoft Research and the University of Rochester developed Progressive Thought Encoding, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that significantly improves the efficiency and accuracy of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) during reinforcement learning (RL) training. The method reduces peak GPU memory usage by up to 45.4% and FLOPs by up to 37.8% while achieving up to 10.7% absolute accuracy improvement on mathematical reasoning benchmarks compared to full-cache baselines.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) training for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) is computationally intensive, requiring long reasoning trajectories that lead to high memory and compute consumption due to growing Key-Value (KV) caches.
- Current memory management strategies like sliding-window caches discard older context, degrading LRMs' multi-step reasoning capabilities and overall accuracy.
- Enabling LRMs to perform effective reasoning under strict, fixed-size memory budgets, which is crucial for scalable training and deployment, remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- The paper introduces **Progressive Thought Encoding**, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (==PEFT==) method that encodes information from 'evicted' tokens into a compact, learnable latent representation instead of simply discarding them.
- It uses dynamic Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) weights, which are continuously updated by a 'global query vector' that summarizes all previously evicted context.
- The method integrates a simple sliding-window eviction strategy for generated thinking tokens while permanently retaining question tokens, balancing efficiency with essential context preservation.

## Results

- Achieved up to **10.7%** absolute accuracy improvement (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-**8B** on AIME2024) and **4.5%** (Qwen2.5-**3B**-Instruct) over LoRA baselines on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- Reduced peak GPU memory usage during RL training by up to **45.4%** and total FLOPs by up to **37.8%** compared to full-cache LoRA.
- Demonstrated superior robustness under reduced inference-time KV cache sizes, consistently maintaining higher accuracy across computational budgets than all baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Information from discarded tokens can be effectively preserved by progressively encoding it into dynamically updated LoRA parameters, allowing LRMs to 'remember' long-range context.
> - A global query vector acts as an explicit carrier of evicted-context information, enabling the model to adapt its behavior based on a summarized history of past thoughts.
> - This approach enables more efficient RL training, allowing for longer reasoning rollouts without prohibitive memory costs, which in turn leads to improved reasoning performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), multi-step reasoning, and reinforcement learning (RL) for model alignment and efficiency.

**Large Reasoning Models (LRMs):** The paper introduces Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) as an emerging paradigm extending LLMs with enhanced capabilities for multi-step reasoning, symbolic manipulation, and problem-solving. Unlike traditional LLMs that rely primarily on scale and corpus size, LRMs are explicitly trained with reasoning-oriented signals and architectural designs, making them particularly adept at complex domains like mathematics, science, and programming. The field is rapidly moving beyond merely achieving raw capabilities to focusing on the efficiency of training and deployment.

**Challenges in RL Training for LRMs:** Reinforcement learning, specifically methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Grouped Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO), has become standard for aligning and improving LRMs. However, RL training, especially with outcome-based rewards, suffers from a fundamental efficiency bottleneck. Generating long reasoning trajectories (chain-of-thought, or CoT) for reward computation during the "rollout" stage is computationally intensive. Autoregressive decoding dominates both memory and compute resources, and the length of these trajectories scales with task complexity. This leads to significantly increased post-training and inference costs.

**Memory Constraints and Current Solutions:** To mitigate memory consumption, techniques like sliding-window caches or dynamic pruning of past tokens have been proposed. While these approaches can bound memory usage, they often degrade reasoning quality. Discarding intermediate thoughts weakens the model's ability to integrate long-range context, leading to reduced accuracy during inference and poorer sample quality during RL rollouts, thereby hindering training effectiveness. This creates a critical tension: how to train LRMs efficiently under strict memory budgets without sacrificing reasoning accuracy.

**Contribution to the Landscape:** This paper directly addresses this tension by proposing a novel parameter-efficient fine-tuning method. It aims to bridge the gap between memory-constrained efficiency and the preservation of long-context reasoning, a problem that existing methods like vanilla LoRA or simple sliding-window caches fail to solve adequately. By proposing a method that "learns" from evicted tokens, it seeks to enable LRMs to operate effectively in environments with fixed-size caches, pushing the frontier of scalable LRM training and deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an efficient and effective method for training Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) using reinforcement learning (RL) under strict memory constraints, without compromising their multi-step reasoning capabilities.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Address the Efficiency Bottleneck in RL Training for LRMs:** Complex reasoning tasks require long chain-of-thought (CoT) sequences for high-quality solutions. During RL training, the generation of these long sequences in the "rollout" stage is highly memory and compute-intensive due to autoregressive decoding and the growing KV cache. This makes RL training of LRMs a significant resource hog.
2. **Overcome Limitations of Existing Memory Management Strategies:** Current approaches like sliding-window caches, while bounding memory, often lead to a "forgetting" problem. Discarding older tokens, even if less relevant immediately, can severely degrade the model's ability to maintain long-range contextual understanding, which is crucial for complex reasoning. This degradation impacts both inference accuracy and the quality of training signals during rollouts.
3. **Enable Effective Reasoning under Fixed-Size Caches:** The goal is to allow LRMs to reason effectively, approximating the performance of full-context models, even when operating with fixed-size memory caches. This is crucial for real-world deployment where computational budgets are often tight.
4. **Improve Scalability and Practicality:** By reducing peak memory usage during training and maintaining strong performance under constrained inference budgets, the proposed method aims to make RL training of LRMs more scalable and practical, allowing for the development of more capable reasoning agents on a wider range of hardware.
5. **Maintain or Enhance Reasoning Accuracy:** Crucially, the motivation is not just efficiency at any cost, but efficiency *with* maintained or even improved reasoning accuracy, as demonstrated by the observed performance degradation in baseline sliding-window approaches.

In essence, the paper seeks to answer the critical question: "Can LRMs be trained efficiently under strict memory budgets without sacrificing reasoning accuracy?"

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces **Progressive Thought Encoding**, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) method designed to enable LRMs to reason effectively under fixed-size caches, particularly during reinforcement learning (RL) training. The core idea is to encode information from "evicted" tokens into a compact representation rather than simply discarding them, thereby preserving long-context understanding.

#### 3.1. Notation and Preliminaries

- **KV Cache as Memory:** The transformer architecture relies on a Key-Value (KV) cache for efficient autoregressive decoding. This cache stores `key` and `value` vectors for all previously generated tokens, allowing subsequent tokens to attend over the full history without recomputing these representations. The KV cache grows with sequence length, making long rollouts memory-intensive.
- **GRPO for RL in LLMs:** The paper uses Grouped Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) for fine-tuning. GRPO is a policy gradient method that avoids a critic model by sampling multiple completions per prompt, grouping them, and assigning variance-reduced, group-normalized rewards. The objective is to maximize expected reward while staying close to a reference policy, `π_ref`.

#### 3.2. Challenges for Efficient RL Training (Formalization)

The paper formalizes the efficiency challenge by modifying the GRPO objective to account for a "cache policy" `D` that prunes the KV cache online. The policy `π_D_θ(y|p)` generates tokens based on a pruned context `C_D_t = CachePrune_D(p, y_<t)`. The goal is to optimize `θ_g` (generating model parameters under partial-cache rollouts) such that `π_θ*_g(y|p)` approximates the full-cache policy `π_θ*(y|p)`.

#### 3.3. Progressive Thought Encoding: Learning Think Tokens Prior to Eviction

Instead of naive eviction, the proposed method encodes information from evicted tokens into a learnable, compact latent representation.

1. **Dynamic LoRA Adaptation:** The method integrates with lightweight Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) adapters. LoRA modifies only a small fraction of the model's parameters, making it parameter-efficient.
2. **Global Query Vector (`q_g`):** A crucial component is a "global query vector" `q_g`, which serves as a learnable summary of all evicted context encountered so far. It acts as an explicit carrier of evicted-context information.
3. **Progressive Encoding Process:**
 - During the rollout stage, as the model generates thinking tokens `{y_1..., y_l}` and the KV cache fills up, earlier tokens `{y_e1..., y_em}` are marked for eviction according to a strategy `D`.
 - Instead of discarding them, these evicted tokens (`K_e`, `V_e`) are used in conjunction with `q_g` to compute an "evicted context state" `S_e`. This computation involves projecting `q_g`, `K_e`, and `V_e` into a compressed latent space using specific weight matrices (`W_aQ`, `W_aK`, `W_aV`).
 - The `S_e` is then mapped to an update for the LoRA weights (`ΔW`) using matrices `A` and `B`. This `ΔW` is added to the model's current LoRA weights, effectively adapting the model to the context that is about to be evicted.
 - This process is iterative: each time the cache fills, a new `S'_e` is computed from the newly evicted tokens, which is then normalized and added to the accumulated `S_e`. The `ΔW` is recomputed accordingly.
 - **Initialization:** To bootstrap adaptation, `S_e` is initialized with learnable "global tokens" (`h_g`), from which `q_g`, `k_g`, and `v_g` are derived. This ensures that `q_g` starts with some context.

#### 3.4. Selection of `D` During Training

- **Question Tokens Retention:** All question tokens are permanently retained in the cache. This is inspired by "sink-token" mechanisms, ensuring the model maintains stable grounding to the prompt context.
- **Sliding-Window for Thinking Tokens:** A simple sliding-window eviction strategy is applied only to the generated "thinking tokens." This straightforward design facilitates efficient batch operations, prioritizing training efficiency over more complex (and computationally expensive) importance-based eviction strategies during the RL rollout.

By continuously encoding evicted thoughts into dynamically updated LoRA parameters, the model effectively "remembers" the long-range context in a compact form, enabling it to approximate full-cache reasoning while operating under strict memory budgets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling results demonstrating the effectiveness of Progressive Thought Encoding in improving both the efficiency and accuracy of LRMs under memory constraints.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B. These represent diverse scales and architectures of open-weight instruction-tuned models.
- **Benchmarks:** Six challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks: Math500, Olympiad-Bench, Minerva Math, AMC, AIME2024, and AIME2025. Pass@1 is used for most, while pass@16 is used for the extremely difficult AIME datasets.
- **Compared Methods:**
 - **Baseline:** Original model without RL training.
 - **LoRA:** RL-trained with standard low-rank adaptation.
 - **LoRA_c:** RL-trained with LoRA and a sliding-window cache for token eviction (representing naive memory bounding).
 - **Ours:** RL-trained using Progressive Thought Encoding.
- **Training Details:** Maximum sequence length of 3072, DAPO-Math-17K dataset, Adam optimizer, LoRA rank 32. Sliding-window cache size is dynamic (max question length in micro-batch) with 25% eviction upon saturation for LoRA_c and Ours. Our method uses 32 global tokens.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Math Reasoning Tasks (Table 1)

- **Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct:**
 - LoRA (full cache) achieved 28.2% avg accuracy, with 4.2 TFLOPs and 82.8% peak memory.
 - LoRA_c (sliding-window) reduced peak memory to 38.0% but accuracy dropped to 25.6%.
 - **Ours:** Achieved the highest average accuracy (30.1%), a +1.9% absolute gain over LoRA and +4.5% over LoRA_c, while requiring significantly less computation (2.7 TFLOPs, -35.7% vs. LoRA) and memory (45.3% peak, -45.4% vs. LoRA).
- **Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct:**
 - LoRA achieved 38.1% avg accuracy with high memory (85.8% peak).
 - LoRA_c lowered memory to 63.1% but reduced accuracy to 36.7%.
 - **Ours:** Achieved the best average accuracy (39.6%), +1.5% over LoRA, while cutting FLOPs almost in half (3.6 TFLOPs vs. 5.7 TFLOPs for LoRA) and reducing peak memory (67.2% peak, -21.6% vs. LoRA).
- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B:**
 - LoRA required 7.4 TFLOPs and 88.7% peak memory.
 - **Ours:** Achieved 45.6% avg accuracy, a remarkable +10.7% absolute improvement over LoRA (+15.5% relative) and +10.5% over LoRA_c, consuming only 4.6 TFLOPs (-37.8% vs. LoRA) and 59.8% peak memory (-32.5% vs. LoRA). Improvements were particularly strong on AIME2024 (+36.7%) and AIME2025 (+23.3%).

#### 4.3. Evaluation under Different Computational Budgets (Figure 3 & Table 2)

- **Robustness to Cache Reduction (Figure 3):** When evaluating models under progressively reduced KV cache sizes during inference, Baseline and LoRA rapidly degraded. LoRA_c showed some resilience but remained limited. Our method consistently maintained higher accuracy across all computational budgets, demonstrating superior resilience to cache truncation. Averaged across datasets and cache settings, our approach achieved 39.37% accuracy, compared to 32.99% for LoRA and 30.31% for Baseline, representing +19.3% and +29.9% relative improvements, respectively.
- **Harder Benchmarks (AIME, Table 2):** On AIME2024 and AIME2025, which demand longer reasoning chains, our method achieved the highest average performance across all models and cache sizes. For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, average gains over LoRA were +28.9% on AIME2024 and +21.1% on AIME2025.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study and Discussion

- **Scalable CoT RL Training (Table 3):** Progressive Thought Encoding enabled training DeepSeek-R1 with larger maximum generation lengths (up to 6K tokens) while keeping memory usage stable and significantly lower than vanilla RL training. Longer rollouts consistently improved reasoning performance, demonstrating that memory savings allow for more complex reasoning.
- **Long Sequence Generation (Figure 5):** When testing on MATH-500 with a fixed 1K context window and generation lengths up to 64K tokens, our method showed the strongest scaling behavior, continuing to yield steady gains while other methods plateaued. This indicates its ability to preserve reasoning information far beyond training rollout length.
- **Global Context Tokens (Figure 4c):** Global tokens significantly contribute to performance, especially when combined with evicted token updates. Disabling them or having too many/few global tokens reduces effectiveness, with 32 global tokens performing optimally.
- **Token Dropping Strategy (Figure 4d):** While more advanced token dropping strategies (H2O, PyramidKV, HeadKV) can further improve accuracy, especially under limited cache, they incur significant runtime overhead during batch training. The paper chose a simple sliding-window strategy for training efficiency, acknowledging the potential for future integration of advanced methods.
- **Length of Generated Response (Figure 6):** Our method not only achieves overall best reasoning performance but also maintains strong capability for long-form reasoning, solving more problems with longer response lengths compared to baselines. This supports the claim that encoding evicted tokens helps the model "remember" them throughout generation.
- **Why Better Results?** The consistent gains are attributed to progressive encoding providing a continuous mechanism to preserve long-range reasoning information, acting as incremental distillation and denoising. This strengthens coherent multi-step reasoning, leading to longer, more stable chains of thought.

In summary, Progressive Thought Encoding consistently outperforms baselines by achieving higher reasoning accuracy across various models and benchmarks, significantly reducing training-time memory and compute costs, and demonstrating superior robustness under tight inference-time memory budgets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Progressive Thought Encoding" method presented in this paper carries significant implications for the development, training, and deployment of large reasoning models (LRMs), particularly in resource-constrained environments.

**1. Enabling Scalable and Efficient RL Training:** The most immediate impact is the substantial improvement in the efficiency of reinforcement learning (RL) training for LRMs. By nearly halving peak GPU memory usage and reducing TFLOPs while simultaneously improving accuracy, this method breaks a critical bottleneck. This efficiency gain means that researchers and practitioners can train more capable reasoning models on existing hardware or train current models faster and more cost-effectively. It makes the iterative, reward-based refinement of LRMs more accessible and practical.

**2. Enhanced Reasoning Under Memory Constraints:** The research directly addresses the long-standing challenge of maintaining long-context reasoning capabilities when faced with fixed-size memory caches. Instead of the typical trade-off where memory reduction leads to performance degradation, Progressive Thought Encoding demonstrates that it's possible to achieve *both* efficiency and improved reasoning accuracy. This is crucial for real-world applications where inference often runs on hardware with limited VRAM (e.g., edge devices, consumer GPUs). Models trained with this method will be more robust and performant in deployment scenarios.

**3. Advancing the Capabilities of LRMs:** By enabling longer and more effective rollouts during RL training without prohibitive memory costs, the method allows LRMs to learn more intricate and extended reasoning patterns. The ability to "remember" evicted tokens means models can tackle problems requiring very long chains of thought, pushing the boundaries of what LRMs can achieve in complex domains like advanced mathematics and scientific discovery. The improved performance on challenging benchmarks like AIME underscores this potential.

**4. Novel Approach to Context Management:** The concept of progressively encoding evicted token information into dynamic LoRA weights, guided by a global query vector, offers a novel and effective strategy for managing context in memory-constrained autoregressive models. This moves beyond simple truncation or complex, computationally expensive selective eviction, proposing a more integrated learning mechanism. This approach could inspire similar strategies for other memory-intensive operations in LLMs.

**5. Future Research Directions:** The paper opens several promising avenues for future work:
 - **Adaptive Eviction Strategies:** While the paper used a simple sliding-window for training efficiency, the ablation studies suggest that integrating more sophisticated, importance-aware token dropping strategies could further boost inference accuracy. Developing efficient ways to incorporate these into RL rollouts without incurring significant overhead is a clear next step.
 - **Multimodal Reasoning Tasks:** The principles of preserving long-range context efficiently could be extended to multimodal LRMs, where complex reasoning might involve integrating information from text, images, and other modalities over extended sequences.
 - **Integration with Inference-Time Optimizations:** Combining Progressive Thought Encoding with other inference-time optimization techniques (e.g., quantization, sparse attention) could lead to even greater efficiency gains.

In conclusion, Progressive Thought Encoding represents a significant step towards developing more scalable, efficient, and robust large reasoning models. By addressing a fundamental limitation in their training and deployment, this work paves the way for wider adoption and more sophisticated applications of AI in complex problem-solving scenarios.
