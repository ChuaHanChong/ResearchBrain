# AXIS: A Growable Community-Driven Data Engine for Scalable Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AXIS, a growable community-driven data engine from Axis Robotics and collaborating universities, leverages browser-based teleoperation and automated data augmentation to create a vast repository of robot manipulation demonstrations. This infrastructure generated over 50,000 human trajectories for 207 tasks, enhancing downstream vision-language-action policy robustness by 5.8% on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark and exhibiting significant gains against visual and sensor perturbations.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation datasets are often static and finite, limiting their continuous growth and adaptability to new tasks or evolving model capabilities.
- Data collection pipelines typically require specialized hardware or expert operators, hindering scalability and broad community participation in generating diverse demonstrations.
- Raw teleoperated data often contains noise and artifacts (e.g., human hesitation), and lacks sufficient diversity in visual and physics conditions for robust policy training.

## Method

- **AXIS** introduces a ==three-layered data engine== that integrates automated task generation (==TaskGen==) with a browser-based ==MuJoCo-WASM== teleoperation system for accessible, community-driven data collection.
- It implements a unified data processing pipeline for refinement, including automated cleaning, smoothing, resampling, and extensive visual/physics-based augmentation using a high-fidelity simulator like ==IsaacSim==.
- The system organizes data into ==growable, versioned snapshots== to enable continuous, measurable scaling studies and establish a benchmark for robot learning policies.

## Results

- The AXIS dataset currently contains **207** manipulation tasks, over **50,000** human trajectories, and more than **60,000** augmented scene variants, with data refinement reducing mean acceleration by **63.9%** and mean jerk by **80.8%**.
- Continual pretraining on the AXIS-100% dataset improved the overall LIBERO-Plus success rate of a π0.5 VLA model by **5.8%** (from **83.9%** to **88.8%**) compared to the baseline, with benefits scaling consistently with data volume.
- AXIS pretraining yielded significant robustness gains against specific perturbations, including **+11.3%** for camera variations and **+13.7%** for sensor noise, outperforming a volume-matched RoboCasa365 baseline by **31.3%** overall.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Democratizing robot data collection through a browser-based teleoperation system significantly lowers barriers to entry, enabling large-scale contributions of diverse human demonstrations.
> - A systematic data refinement pipeline, coupled with extensive visual and physics augmentation, effectively transforms raw, noisy teleoperated data into high-quality, diverse training examples for robust policy learning.
> - Continual pretraining on a progressively growing and diverse dataset like AXIS measurably improves the generalization and robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies against various perturbations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation increasingly relies on large, diverse, and high-quality datasets to train effective policies, particularly with the advent of imitation learning and vision-language-action (VLA) models. Current research has demonstrated that policy performance is influenced by the scale, diversity, and quality of robot demonstration data.

However, existing data collection pipelines present several limitations that hinder scalability. Many rely on specialized hardware, requiring significant investment and maintenance, and are typically operated by centralized teams or expert operators. Most public manipulation datasets are released as fixed collections, limiting their ability to expand continuously as robotic capabilities evolve or new tasks emerge. This static nature means that robot datasets have expanded at a slower rate compared to the models they are designed to train. Previous crowdsourced systems have broadened collection efforts but often remain fixed releases or depend on the availability of physical robots and specific laboratory setups. High-fidelity simulators, while offering advantages, often necessitate substantial installation efforts and significant GPU resources.

AXIS addresses these limitations by introducing a "growable" and community-driven data engine. It departs from the paradigm of fixed, centralized datasets by providing mechanisms for continuous generation, collection, validation, processing, and evaluation of new data. This approach aims to make large-scale data collection broadly accessible through browser-based simulated teleoperation, thereby reducing reliance on specialized hardware and expert operators. By organizing data into versioned snapshots, AXIS allows for measurable dataset growth and supports consistent benchmarking over time, differentiating it from previous efforts that often provide one-time releases. Furthermore, AXIS unifies task generation, web teleoperation, data refinement, and augmentation within a single pipeline, integrating components that were often addressed separately in prior work on simulation, task generation, and synthetic augmentation. This comprehensive approach aims to continuously expand data along multiple dimensions, including tasks, objects, scenes, contributors, visual conditions, and physical variations, which is relevant for training and evaluating general manipulation policies and improving robustness against various perturbations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for AXIS stems from the recognized need for diverse and high-quality demonstration data to train effective robot manipulation policies, particularly for general-purpose applications. The authors identify a fundamental limitation in existing robot manipulation data pipelines: their reliance on specialized hardware, centralized operations, or static task suites. This reliance makes them difficult to scale and inhibits broad community participation and continuous expansion. Consequently, the growth of robot datasets has lagged behind the advancement of robot learning models.

The key objectives of the AXIS project are structured to address these challenges:

1. **Enable Scalable and Accessible Data Collection:** The project aims to democratize data collection by developing a browser-based teleoperation system using MuJoCo-WASM. This approach seeks to eliminate the need for specialized hardware or local simulator installations, thereby lowering the barrier to entry and enabling large-scale, community-driven contributions of demonstrations.

2. **Automate Task Generation and Validation:** AXIS seeks to continuously expand the diversity of manipulation tasks by automatically generating and validating new tasks. This includes defining tasks through natural language instructions, parameterized scene configurations, and structured success checkers, moving beyond fixed task suites.

3. **Transform Raw Data into Training-Ready Form:** The project aims to develop a unified data processing pipeline that converts raw, community-collected demonstrations into high-quality, training-ready data. This involves automated success checking, quality filtering, static segment removal, trajectory smoothing, resampling, and visual and physics-based augmentation using a high-fidelity simulator (IsaacSim). The goal is to standardize data representation and enhance data quality despite diverse collection sources.

4. **Establish a Growable Benchmark for Robot Learning:** AXIS intends to serve as a benchmark that supports continuous, measurable growth. This involves organizing data into progressively larger "task snapshots" and employing a systematic, fixed held-out evaluation protocol. This design aims to enable controlled scaling studies where policy architectures, training budgets, and evaluation criteria remain constant, while only the training data volume and diversity expand.

5. **Facilitate the Development of Robust VLA Policies:** By providing a diverse, high-quality, and continuously expanding dataset, AXIS aims to support the training and evaluation of both conventional visuomotor imitation learning policies and modern VLA models. The motivation is to improve policy robustness and generalization across various environmental conditions and tasks.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of static, expensive, and centralized data pipelines by creating an open, community-driven, and continuously expanding data engine that can keep pace with the evolving demands of scalable robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AXIS is structured as a growable, community-driven data engine comprising three integrated layers: an infrastructure layer for collection and generation, a dataset layer for processing, and a model layer for training and evaluation. The system is designed to continuously expand along multiple dimensions, including tasks, objects, scenes, contributors, visual conditions, and physical variations. The current implementation targets tabletop manipulation using a Franka Research 3 robot equipped with a parallel-jaw gripper.

**Infrastructure Layer: Task Generation and Web Teleoperation**

- **Task Generation (TaskGen):** This module automatically generates new manipulation tasks. It takes high-level natural language instructions, object assets, and scene layouts as input. A task manager decomposes requests into task, scene, and object configurations, specifying goals, workspace context, and object roles. An object-model manager retrieves or generates 3D object models, normalizing them to plausible physical scales. A 2.5D layout generator proposes initial scene arrangements, which a layout manager instantiates into full 3D environments with precise object poses and orientations. A layout supervisor then validates the instantiated scene against task constraints, iteratively correcting inconsistencies (e.g., unreachable objects) through relocation, re-orientation, or partial regeneration. Task-specific success checkers are defined for automatic evaluation during both collection and backend validation.
- **Web-Based Teleoperation:** Data collection occurs through a browser-based MuJoCo-WASM frontend, supporting commodity input devices (keyboard, mouse, joystick, gamepad). This design separates interactive data collection (latency-sensitive) from compute-heavy backend processing (throughput-sensitive). The frontend provides an interactive 3D visualization, camera controls, and a dashboard for real-time metrics. Operator commands are mapped to 6-DoF Cartesian end-effector targets and converted to joint-level robot commands via inverse kinematics. For grippers, an interpolation-based grasp control reduces high-dimensional hand configurations to a single scalar control. The system buffers time-aligned state-action samples within the physics loop and serializes successful attempts with metadata for backend upload.

**Dataset Layer: Data Refinement and Augmentation**

- **Unified Trajectory Representation:** Completed demonstrations are serialized into a unified format containing task metadata, robot embodiment, environment identifier, simulator version, timestamps, observations, robot states, actions, and success information. Optional fields include object states, camera streams, and segmentation markers.
- **Offline Data Cleaning & Refinement:** Raw demonstrations undergo a multi-stage process:
 - **Validation:** Structural checks (missing fields, corrupted records), numerical checks (non-finite values), physical checks (implausible frame-to-frame changes), and task-level checks (re-verification of success using backend task checkers) are applied.
 - **Static Segment Removal:** Samples where robot joint variation is below a threshold (5e-3) are removed to eliminate human hesitation and idle segments, improving temporal consistency.
 - **Trajectory Smoothing:** Retained trajectories undergo smoothing using a Savitzky-Golay filter (window size 15, polynomial order 3) to reduce high-frequency teleoperation artifacts, while preserving the geometric structure of manipulation behavior. Discrete gripper transitions are excluded from continuous smoothing. Table 1 indicates that this process reduces mean acceleration by 63.9% and mean jerk by 80.8% with an 86.2% replay success rate for smoothed+resampled data.
 - **Resampling:** Cleaned trajectories are resampled to a fixed control frequency (e.g., 20 Hz from original 6-8 Hz) using cubic spline interpolation to align with policy learning requirements.
- **Realistic Augmentation:** Cleaned demonstrations are replayed in an IsaacSim backend (or IsaacLab) for visual and physics augmentation. Physics randomization varies object poses, clutter configurations, mass, friction, and dynamics parameters. Rendering randomization varies camera viewpoints, lighting, textures, and visual appearance (e.g., table, floor, objects, background, scene layout). This process creates over 60K scene variants from 207 tasks and 50K+ trajectories. The default rendering uses ray-traced lighting at 256x256 resolution for third-view and wrist RGB cameras.

**Model Layer: Policy Learning and Real-World Validation**

- **Benchmarking Platform:** AXIS supports training and evaluation of visuomotor imitation learning and VLA models. It organizes training data into progressively larger "task snapshots" (AXIS-25%, AXIS-50%, AXIS-100%) to enable controlled scaling studies with a fixed held-out evaluation protocol.
- **Training Pipeline:** The experimental setup uses a three-stage pipeline for training VLA policies:
 1. Initialization from a released π$_{0.5}$ checkpoint.
 2. Optional continual pretraining on a simulation corpus (AXIS subsets or volume-matched RoboCasa365).
 3. Fine-tuning on the LIBERO trajectory set provided with LIBERO-Plus.
 - The pretraining stage uses flow matching over action chunks. Images are pad-resized and undergo visual augmentation (random crop, rotation, brightness/contrast/saturation jitter). States and actions are normalized using quantile statistics.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Policies are evaluated on the LIBERO-Plus robustness suite, which includes systematic perturbations across seven axes (Camera, Light, Sensor Noise, Background, Layout, Language, Robot). Success rates are reported over fixed rollout budgets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AXIS focused on its effectiveness as a data engine for continuous learning and its impact on downstream policy performance and robustness.

1. **Dataset Scale and Quality:**
 - The AXIS dataset, as of its current snapshot, comprises 207 diverse manipulation tasks, over 50,000 human demonstration trajectories, and more than 60,000 task or scene variants due to augmentation.
 - The data refinement pipeline demonstrated a reduction in trajectory artifacts. Relative to raw teleoperation, the final smoothed and resampled trajectories reduced mean acceleration by 63.9% (from 1.3539 to 0.4885) and mean jerk by 80.8% (from 11.5899 to 2.2243), while maintaining an 86.2% replay success rate (Table 1). This indicates improved temporal consistency and reduced noise in the action sequences.

2. **Impact of AXIS Continual Pretraining on Downstream Performance:**
 - Continual pretraining of the π$_{0.5}$ VLA model on the AXIS-100% dataset improved the overall LIBERO-Plus success rate by 5.8% compared to the vanilla π$_{0.5}$ baseline (88.8% vs. 83.9%) (Table 2).
 - This improvement scaled consistently with increasing AXIS data volume: AXIS-25% achieved 84.7%, AXIS-50% achieved 85.7%, and AXIS-100% achieved 88.8% overall success rates. This suggests that the benefits had not saturated, and additional data continued to improve robustness.
 - AXIS pretraining yielded a higher overall success rate of 88.8% compared to a volume-matched RoboCasa365 continual pretraining baseline, which achieved 57.5%. This difference of 31.3% indicates that the gains are not solely attributable to increased data volume but also to specific properties of the AXIS pipeline, such as crowdsourced task creation, diverse demonstrations, and large-scale environment randomization.

3. **Per-Perturbation Robustness Analysis:**
 - The largest improvements in success rate were observed on perturbation axes directly targeted by the AXIS IsaacSim augmentation pipeline (Table 3):
 - Camera variations: +11.3% (from 72.5% to 83.8%).
 - Sensor Noise: +13.7% (from 82.5% to 96.2%).
 - Background variations: +3.7% (from 94.4% to 98.1%).
 - Layout variations: +2.6% (from 82.9% to 85.5%).
 - AXIS pretraining also contributed to improvements on language variation (+1.3%) and robot-pose variation (+3.8%), despite these not being explicitly augmented by the IsaacSim pipeline. This suggests that the diverse tasks and interaction contexts provided by AXIS contribute to broader generalization beyond perturbation-specific robustness.
 - Compared to the RoboCasa365 baseline, AXIS-100% showed a notably higher success rate across almost all perturbation axes, including Camera (+48.6%), Sensor Noise (+33.0%), Language (+39.1%), and Robot (+38.8%), further supporting the argument that AXIS's benefits extend beyond mere data volume.

These findings suggest that AXIS provides a practical method for scalable robot manipulation data generation, leading to improved and more robust policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AXIS project introduces a paradigm shift in how robot manipulation datasets can be constructed and utilized, with several implications for the field of robot learning:

1. **Enabling Scalable and Continuous Data Growth:** AXIS addresses a fundamental limitation of static datasets by providing a "growable" data engine. This continuous expansion through automated task generation, community teleoperation, and visual/physics augmentation ensures that datasets can evolve alongside model capabilities and address observed policy failures. This approach positions dataset growth as an ongoing mechanism for improving policy robustness, rather than a one-time release.

2. **Democratizing Data Collection:** By implementing a browser-based teleoperation system with MuJoCo-WASM, AXIS significantly lowers the barrier to entry for data contributors. This accessibility enables broad community participation, which can accelerate the accumulation of diverse human demonstrations, including varied strategies, corrections, and execution styles. This decentralized collection model could lead to datasets that are more representative of real-world human interaction.

3. **Improving Policy Robustness and Generalization:** The systematic data refinement and extensive augmentation pipeline in AXIS are designed to transform raw, noisy community data into high-quality, diverse training examples. The experimental results confirm that continual pretraining on AXIS data significantly improves the overall success rate of VLA policies and enhances their robustness, particularly against visual and geometric perturbations (e.g., camera viewpoints, sensor noise, scene layout). This indicates that the growable and augmented nature of AXIS data contributes to more generalizable manipulation policies.

4. **Establishing a Framework for Measurable Dataset Scaling:** AXIS introduces a structured approach to benchmarking by organizing data into versioned task snapshots (e.g., AXIS-25%, 50%, 100%) and employing a fixed held-out evaluation protocol. This allows researchers to conduct controlled scaling studies, objectively measuring how increased data volume and diversity impact policy performance without confounding variables. This framework can foster a more rigorous understanding of data-driven generalization in robot learning.

5. **Paving the Way for Future Research Directions:** The work suggests paths for future research by highlighting the potential for expanding AXIS beyond simulated Franka tabletop manipulation. Potential impacts include incorporating more diverse robot embodiments, richer sensing modalities, longer-horizon tasks, finer-grained annotations, and integrating active, failure-driven data collection. Such expansions could contribute to the development of even more generalizable and scalable robot learning systems capable of operating in complex, dynamic real-world environments.

Overall, AXIS contributes to addressing the challenge of data scarcity and quality in robot learning by offering a practical, accessible, and continuously expanding data infrastructure. Its emphasis on community contribution, automated processing, and systematic evaluation has the potential to influence the development of more robust and adaptable robot manipulation policies.
