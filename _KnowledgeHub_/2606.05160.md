# GRAIL: Generating Humanoid Loco-Manipulation from 3D Assets and Video Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> GRAIL presents a fully digital pipeline for generating robot-compatible 4D human-object interaction data to train humanoid loco-manipulation policies. The system synthesizes over 20,000 physically plausible sequences from 3D assets and video priors, allowing egocentric visual policies trained exclusively on this synthetic data to achieve up to 90% real-world success rates on a Unitree G1 robot for tasks like stair-climbing and object pick-up.

## Problem

- Generating large-scale, high-quality demonstration data for complex humanoid loco-manipulation tasks is resource-intensive and unscalable using traditional teleoperation or motion capture methods.
- Reconstructing accurate, robot-compatible 4D human-object interaction (HOI) trajectories from unconstrained, in-the-wild videos is an underconstrained problem, leading to ambiguity and physical implausibility.
- Existing data generation approaches often result in morphology mismatches or lack the physical fidelity required for effective sim-to-real transfer and robust robot policy learning.

## Method

- A fully digital pipeline is introduced that first assembles a 3D scene with known camera parameters, metric scale, object geometry, and a robot-proportioned human character, then uses a Video Foundation Model (VFM) to generate interaction videos.
- An interaction-aware 4D HOI reconstruction stack performs joint optimization of human and object trajectories, leveraging the known 3D scene configuration and incorporating losses for keypoint, object projection, depth, and contact alignment to ensure physical plausibility and metric accuracy.
- Task-general loco-manipulation policies are trained by retargeting the reconstructed HOI trajectories to a humanoid robot and using an object-aware ==latent== adaptor for manipulation and a scene-aware height-map conditioning module for locomotion, built upon a pretrained whole-body controller.

## Results

- GRAIL generated HOI sequences with superior geometric quality (lowest contact distance 0.008, **0.90%** penetration) and perceptual realism (VLM Interaction Score 3.58), achieving an **88.9%** tracking success rate in physics simulation, substantially outperforming baselines (15.8-**24.0%**).
- The pipeline created a large-scale dataset of over 20,000 sequences. Policies trained on this data achieved an **81.4%** manipulation success rate, surpassing state-of-the-art baselines (48.5-**49.2%**) on a benchmark of 124 motions.
- Egocentric visual policies trained solely on GRAIL-generated data successfully deployed on a real Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating **90%** real-world success for stair-climbing and **84%** for seen object pick-up (generalizing to **80%** for unseen objects).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-specifying 3D scene parameters (camera, metric scale, object geometry, character morphology) before video generation significantly constrains the 4D HOI reconstruction problem, reducing ambiguity inherent in purely video-based methods.
> - A joint optimization approach integrating various alignment losses (depth, contact, keypoint) is crucial for recovering physically plausible and robot-executable human-object interaction trajectories from generated videos.
> - Developing task-general adaptors on top of a pretrained whole-body controller enables versatile loco-manipulation skills, allowing for scalable policy training across diverse objects and terrains without requiring task-specific controllers.
