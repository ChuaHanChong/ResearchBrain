# GRAIL: Generating Humanoid Loco-Manipulation from 3D Assets and Video Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> GRAIL presents a fully digital pipeline for generating robot-compatible 4D human-object interaction data to train humanoid loco-manipulation policies. The system synthesizes over 20,000 physically plausible sequences from 3D assets and video priors, allowing egocentric visual policies trained exclusively on this synthetic data to achieve up to 90% real-world success rates on a Unitree G1 robot for tasks like stair-climbing and object pick-up.

## Problem

- Generating large-scale, high-quality demonstration data for complex humanoid loco-manipulation tasks is resource-intensive and unscalable using traditional teleoperation or motion capture methods.
- Reconstructing accurate, robot-compatible 4D human-object interaction (HOI) trajectories from unconstrained, in-the-wild videos is an underconstrained problem, leading to ambiguity and physical implausibility.
- Existing data generation approaches often result in morphology mismatches or lack the physical fidelity required for effective sim-to-real transfer and robust robot policy learning.

## Method

- A fully digital pipeline is introduced that first assembles a 3D scene with known camera parameters, metric scale, object geometry, and a robot-proportioned human character, then uses a Video Foundation Model (VFM) to generate interaction videos.
- An interaction-aware 4D HOI reconstruction stack performs joint optimization of human and object trajectories, leveraging the known 3D scene configuration and incorporating losses for keypoint, object projection, depth, and contact alignment to ensure physical plausibility and metric accuracy.
- Task-general loco-manipulation policies are trained by retargeting the reconstructed HOI trajectories to a humanoid robot and using an object-aware ==latent== adaptor for manipulation and a scene-aware height-map conditioning module for locomotion, built upon a pretrained whole-body controller.

## Results

- GRAIL generated HOI sequences with superior geometric quality (lowest contact distance 0.008, **0.90%** penetration) and perceptual realism (VLM Interaction Score 3.58), achieving an **88.9%** tracking success rate in physics simulation, substantially outperforming baselines (15.8-**24.0%**).
- The pipeline created a large-scale dataset of over 20,000 sequences. Policies trained on this data achieved an **81.4%** manipulation success rate, surpassing state-of-the-art baselines (48.5-**49.2%**) on a benchmark of 124 motions.
- Egocentric visual policies trained solely on GRAIL-generated data successfully deployed on a real Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating **90%** real-world success for stair-climbing and **84%** for seen object pick-up (generalizing to **80%** for unseen objects).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-specifying 3D scene parameters (camera, metric scale, object geometry, character morphology) before video generation significantly constrains the 4D HOI reconstruction problem, reducing ambiguity inherent in purely video-based methods.
> - A joint optimization approach integrating various alignment losses (depth, contact, keypoint) is crucial for recovering physically plausible and robot-executable human-object interaction trajectories from generated videos.
> - Developing task-general adaptors on top of a pretrained whole-body controller enables versatile loco-manipulation skills, allowing for scalable policy training across diverse objects and terrains without requiring task-specific controllers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced humanoid robots capable of complex loco-manipulation tasks necessitates vast amounts of high-quality demonstration data. This data is critical for training policies that can coordinate whole-body balance, object contact, and scene-aware locomotion across diverse objects and environments. Historically, two primary approaches have been employed for data generation:
1. **Teleoperation and Motion Capture:** These methods provide high-fidelity demonstrations. However, they are resource-intensive and difficult to scale. Each new object, terrain, or interaction typically requires physical setups, instrumented human actors, and direct human-operated robot demonstrations, leading to high costs, significant human effort, and limited diversity.
2. **Reconstruction from In-the-Wild Videos:** This approach offers the potential for broad visual coverage by leveraging readily available video data. However, reconstructing robot-compatible 4D trajectories from uncontrolled monocular videos presents significant challenges. The inference process is often underconstrained, requiring the system to simultaneously deduce camera parameters, metric scale, object geometry, human shape, contact points, and world-space motion, which can lead to ambiguity and inaccuracies.

Recent advancements in 3D asset generation and video foundation models (VFMs) have opened alternative avenues for data synthesis. Some VFM-based pipelines, such as DAViD and ZeroHSI, utilize generated videos as priors for 4D human-object interaction (HOI) recovery. Nevertheless, these methods often still require inferring critical 3D scene parameters—such as camera intrinsics, metric scale, character morphology, object geometry, or environment structure—*after* video generation, maintaining a degree of ambiguity.

GRAIL positions itself within this landscape by proposing a fully digital data generation pipeline that leverages these generative priors while proactively addressing the ambiguities inherent in reconstruction. By specifying the 3D scene, including object geometry, camera parameters, metric scale, environment depth, and a robot-proportioned character, *before* video generation, GRAIL aims to bypass the underconstrained inference problems of previous video-based reconstruction methods. This approach facilitates the direct synthesis of robot-compatible 4D HOI trajectories that are intrinsically aligned with simulation environments, thus streamlining the sim-to-real transfer for policy training. It complements existing methods by offering a scalable, digital-first alternative that aims to reduce the physical dependency and ambiguity of traditional and purely video-reconstruction-based approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and efficient method for generating high-quality, robot-compatible loco-manipulation demonstration data for humanoid robots. This data should encompass diverse objects, whole-body motions, and various scene geometries, facilitating the training of robust and versatile robot policies.

The motivation for GRAIL stems from several challenges in current data generation paradigms for humanoid robots:
- **Scalability Limitations of Physical Data Collection:** Traditional methods like teleoperation and motion capture provide valuable, high-fidelity data. However, their reliance on physical setups, human operators, and instrumented environments makes them inherently unscalable. Each new task, object, or environmental configuration typically requires a laborious and time-consuming physical collection process, hindering the rapid expansion of demonstration datasets needed for generalist humanoid capabilities.
- **Ambiguity in In-the-Wild Video Reconstruction:** While reconstructing data from unconstrained, in-the-wild videos offers potential for vast data sources, it introduces significant ambiguities. Inferring critical 3D parameters—such as the exact camera pose, metric scale of objects and humans, precise object geometries, human body shapes, and contact dynamics—from monocular 2D observations is an ill-posed problem. This leads to reconstructed 4D trajectories that may lack physical plausibility, metric accuracy, or direct compatibility with robot morphology and simulation environments, thus complicating their use for robot policy learning.
- **Need for Robot-Compatible Trajectories:** For effective sim-to-real transfer, demonstration data must be not only physically plausible but also directly executable by the target robot. Existing methods often struggle with morphology mismatch or require extensive retargeting and adaptation processes that can introduce errors or lose crucial interaction details.

To address these motivations, GRAIL sets out to achieve the following specific objectives, as outlined by its contributions:
1. **Develop a Fully Digital, Humanoid-Centric Data Generation Framework:** This framework integrates video foundation models as interaction priors within a fully specified 3D asset pipeline, enabling the synthesis of over 20,000 physically plausible loco-manipulation sequences without physical collection.
2. **Implement an Interaction-Aware 4D HOI Reconstruction Stack:** This stack is designed to exploit the *known* 3D configuration (geometry, metric scale, camera, environment depth, robot-proportioned character) prior to and during video generation. This privileged setup aims to reduce depth ambiguity and morphology mismatch, allowing for more accurate model-based object tracking, human motion estimation, and interaction-aware optimization.
3. **Create Complementary Task-General Trackers:** These trackers are designed to convert reconstructed 4D HOI trajectories into robot actions. This includes an object-aware latent adaptor for manipulation tasks and a scene-aware height-map conditioning module for terrain traversal and sitting, built on a pretrained whole-body controller. This approach aims to achieve task-generality by pooling similar trajectories, rather than requiring a dedicated controller per sequence or object.
4. **Validate End-to-End Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Demonstrate the practical utility of GRAIL-generated data by training egocentric visual policies exclusively on this synthetic data and deploying them on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, aiming for high success rates on tasks such as object pick-up and stair-climbing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GRAIL pipeline for generating humanoid loco-manipulation demonstrations consists of three primary stages: robot-centric human video generation, interaction-aware 4D HOI reconstruction, and task-general loco-manipulation tracking, followed by an end-to-end sim-to-real deployment.

#### 3.1. Robot-Centric Human Video Generation

This initial stage focuses on synthesizing human interaction videos within a controlled 3D environment.
- **3D Scene Assembly:** Candidate environments (e.g., an indoor floor, a furnished room with a table) are generated using Infinigen. Objects are placed according to their typical affordances, determined by a Vision-Language Model (VLM). A human character asset, prefitted to the target Unitree G1 humanoid's morphology, is positioned in a rest pose alongside the object. Rigid body simulation ensures the object settles into a stable, collision-free initial configuration.
- **Frame Rendering and VFM Conditioning:** The first frame of the scene is rendered in Blender with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics. This rendered environment serves both as visual context for video generation and as a ground-truth point cloud for metric-scale depth alignment during later reconstruction. A VLM generates an interaction prompt from this rendered frame, which then conditions a Video Foundation Model (VFM) (e.g., Kling) to synthesize a reference HOI video under a static-camera setting. This preserves the known camera parameters for the subsequent reconstruction. The choice to generate human, rather than robot, videos is based on the stronger priors of current VFMs for human manipulation and the maturity of human body and hand reconstruction tools.

#### 3.2. Interaction-Aware HOI Reconstruction

Given the generated interaction video, the objective is to recover a coherent 4D HOI trajectory (human and object kinematic motions). This stage involves initial independent motion estimation followed by a joint optimization leveraging the privileged 3D configuration.
- **Initial Motion Estimation:**
 - **Human Motion:** GENMO provides per-frame SMPL-X body pose parameters in camera space from the video, with the body shape fixed to the prefitted character morphology. WiLoR refines per-frame MANO parameters for hands. Missing detections are interpolated and smoothed. Wrist inverse-kinematic (IK) alignment integrates smoothed hand poses into the SMPL-X body.
 - **Object Pose Tracking:** FoundationPose, fine-tuned for RGB-only input, tracks the 6-DoF object pose. It is initialized with the known first-frame object pose and propagates this pose across frames. The availability of known object geometry, texture, and camera parameters ensures tracking accuracy. Tracking quality is further validated by comparing predicted poses against SAM2 segmentation masks, and sequences with inconsistencies are discarded.
- **Joint Optimization:** The independently estimated trajectories are often misaligned. A global optimization refines both human and object trajectories by optimizing residual motion parameters across all frames, keeping hand poses fixed. The objective function $L$ comprises several weighted losses:
 - **Keypoint Alignment ($L_{kp}$):** Minimizes the distance between projected 2D body and hand keypoints (from SMPL-X) and detected 2D keypoints (from estimators like Potamias et al., Xu et al.).
 - **Object Projection Alignment ($L_{proj}$):** Regularizes the optimized object pose to maintain image alignment consistent with FoundationPose's output.
 - **Depth Alignment ($L_{depth}$):** Utilizes MoGe-2 for depth map estimation, aligning it with the ground-truth background depth from the environment to recover metric-scale depth. SAM2 segments human and object regions into point clouds, and a bidirectional Chamfer distance ($CD$) encourages the reconstructed meshes to match these point clouds.
 - **Contact Alignment ($L_{cont}$):** A VLM predicts per-frame contact labels (e.g., which hand is in contact). This loss applies only when contact is detected, using a depth-only bidirectional Chamfer distance to resolve depth discrepancies between relevant human and object vertices whose projections overlap.
 - **Regularization ($L_{reg}$):** Includes terms for foot contact (from GENMO) to suppress skating, pelvis velocity matching GENMO's global-space estimate to suppress depth oscillations, and temporal smoothness (first- and second-order temporal finite differences of vertex positions) for coherence.

#### 3.3. Task-General Loco-Manipulation Tracking

The reconstructed 4D HOI trajectories are retargeted to the Unitree G1 robot using GMR (General Motion Retargeting) to minimize morphology mismatch. These retargeted kinematic motions and object trajectories are then used to train task-general tracking policies built upon SONIC, a pretrained whole-body controller.
- **Object-Aware Tracking:** For object manipulation, a policy ($\pi_{\varphi}$) is trained as an adaptor to SONIC. It modulates SONIC's latent token space and emits hand actions, augmenting the frozen controller with manipulation capabilities while preserving its locomotion prior. The adaptor observes robot proprioception and an object reference (including object pose, hand-to-object transforms, finger contact forces, and a Basis Point Set (BPS) encoding for shape awareness). It outputs a latent residual and binary hand primitives (open/close) mapped to 7 finger DoFs per hand.
- **Scene-Aware Tracking:** For scene-aware interactions (e.g., terrain traversal, sitting), the controller is fine-tuned end-to-end with a local height map ($h_t$) encoder ($\epsilon_h$). The height map, processed by a CNN, provides terrain context, enabling the controller to adapt whole-body motions. An auxiliary kinematic decoder ($G_{rec}$) is trained in parallel to reconstruct motion targets, providing an MSE loss that regularizes the latent space.
- **Reward Design:** Both trackers utilize a motion-tracking reward (sum of exponential terms penalizing discrepancies between reference and simulated quantities) and regularization penalties (e.g., action rate, joint limits). Object-aware tracking additionally incorporates object pose tracking and contact-gated grasp rewards, penalizing object pose deviation, rewarding sustained finger contact, encouraging stable pinch grasps, and drawing fingertips towards contact centroids.
- **Training:** Policies are trained using PPO in Isaac Lab on NVIDIA GPUs. Object-aware training updates only the adaptor ($\pi_{\varphi}$), while scene-aware training fine-tunes the entire controller and height-map encoder. Training occurs jointly across shared task-family pools of 4D HOI trajectories.

#### 3.4. Sim-to-Real Deployment

To validate the generated data, the trained object-aware and scene-aware tracking policies are distilled into separate egocentric visual policies for object pick-up and stair-climbing. These policies consume head-camera RGB inputs and output SONIC latent tokens. Domain randomization is applied during training to aid sim-to-real transfer. The policies are deployed on a real Unitree G1 robot, with visual and proprioceptive input streamed to a desktop GPU for inference, and resulting actions streamed back to the robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GRAIL encompasses three stages: the quality of 4D HOI generation, the effectiveness of task-general loco-manipulation tracking, and the success of sim-to-real deployment on a physical robot.

#### 4.1. Human-Object Interaction Generation

GRAIL's HOI generation component was compared against existing approaches, including training-based (CHOIS, HOIDiff) and training-free (DAViD) methods, using a shared evaluation set of 20 everyday objects.
- **Geometric Quality:** GRAIL achieved the lowest contact distance (0.008) and penetration ratio (0.90%), indicating more realistic physical interactions compared to baselines (e.g., HOIDiff: 0.012 contact, 2.07% penetration; DAViD: 0.246 contact, 1.46% penetration).
- **Perceptual Realism:** The system achieved the highest Interaction Score (3.58 on a 1-5 scale) from VLM ratings, surpassing baselines (e.g., HOIDiff: 1.79; DAViD: 2.74). Object trajectories generated by GRAIL exhibited the highest smoothness (0.0022), with human motion smoothness comparable to other methods. A user study (detailed in Appendix C.1) further indicated that GRAIL was preferred by a significant margin (74.7% for affordance realism, 70.9% for physical realism) over other methods for its more coherent motions and natural hand poses.
- **Physical Executability:** When evaluated using the InterMimic framework for physics-based tracking, GRAIL demonstrated a substantially higher tracking success rate (88.9%) compared to baselines (e.g., HOIDiff: 15.8%; DAViD: 24.0%). It also resulted in the lowest full-body mean per-joint position deviation (0.0913) and mean object surface deviation (0.0851), suggesting that the generated sequences are more amenable to physics-based control and robot execution.
- **Reconstruction Ablation:** An ablation study on reconstruction losses ($L_{proj}$, $L_{depth}$, $L_{cont}$) showed that each loss contributed to the overall quality. Removing any of these losses degraded image-space consistency, metric interaction quality, or both, leading to reduced downstream tracking success rates and increased trajectory deviation in the physics simulation. This indicates the importance of the interaction-aware optimization stack.

#### 4.2. Task-General Loco-Manipulation Tracking

GRAIL generated a large-scale loco-manipulation dataset comprising over 20,000 sequences from 1,000 object assets and 1,000 terrain configurations, categorized into pick-up, whole-body manipulation, sitting, and terrain traversal.
- **Baseline Comparison:** GRAIL's task-general policies were compared against recent humanoid loco-manipulation baselines (HDMI, ResMimic) on a benchmark of 124 motions across 43 objects. GRAIL achieved a significantly higher manipulation success rate (81.4%) compared to HDMI (48.5%) and ResMimic (49.2%). It also showed lower object position error (0.135) and comparable local per-joint position error (41.8), demonstrating its ability to produce more accurate and successful manipulation.
- **Ablation Study of Object-Aware Adaptor:** Ablations of GRAIL's object-aware latent adaptor for manipulation revealed its importance. Training from scratch without SONIC or disabling the latent adaptor ($\pi_{\varphi}$) on SONIC led to substantial degradation in manipulation success rate (39.7% without $\pi_{\varphi}$ vs. 81.4% full model), despite sometimes exhibiting better body tracking. This suggests that accurate body imitation alone is insufficient for successful object interaction, and the latent adaptor is critical for enabling manipulation. Using relative object observations was found to be more effective than absolute observations.

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Deployment

The practical applicability of GRAIL-generated data was demonstrated through the deployment of egocentric visual policies on a real Unitree G1 robot.
- **Stair-Climbing:** A policy trained exclusively on GRAIL's diverse terrain-traversal sequences achieved a 90% real-world success rate on stair-climbing.
- **Object Pick-up:** A policy trained on 200 pick-up sequences per object (cubes, apples, tea boxes, carrots, wet wipes) achieved an 84% real-world success rate on these seen objects. Critically, it transferred effectively to unseen objects (spray can, lint roller, peach, flashlight, medicine bottle), achieving an 80% success rate, indicating robustness and generalization from the generated data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GRAIL research presents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in addressing the challenge of scalable data generation for complex loco-manipulation tasks. Its fully digital pipeline offers several advantages and potential impacts:

- **Scalable Data Generation:** By eliminating the reliance on physical data collection (teleoperation, motion capture) for initial demonstrations, GRAIL provides a method for generating thousands of physically plausible and robot-compatible loco-manipulation sequences across diverse objects and environments at a scale previously difficult to achieve. This digital-first approach significantly reduces the time, cost, and human effort associated with creating large-scale robot datasets.
- **Reduced Ambiguity in 4D HOI Reconstruction:** The methodology's core strength lies in leveraging a privileged 3D setup where scene parameters (object geometry, camera, metric scale, environment depth, robot-proportioned character) are known *before* video generation and *reused* during reconstruction. This transforms ill-posed reconstruction problems into more constrained and solvable ones, yielding more accurate and robust 4D human-object interaction trajectories that are inherently suitable for robot learning.
- **Enhanced Robot Policy Learning:** The generation of a large-scale dataset, coupled with task-general tracking policies (object-aware latent adaptation for manipulation and scene-aware height-map conditioning for locomotion), facilitates the training of more generalist humanoid behaviors. The ability to amortize training costs across pools of related trajectories, rather than requiring per-sequence or per-object controllers, is critical for developing versatile robot skills.
- **Validated Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful deployment of egocentric visual policies trained *only* on GRAIL-generated data onto a real Unitree G1 robot for tasks like object pick-up and stair-climbing demonstrates the high fidelity and practical utility of the synthetic data. This validation highlights the potential of such digital pipelines to accelerate the sim-to-real transfer paradigm, bridging the gap between simulated training and real-world robot performance.
- **Complementary Approach for Robotics:** GRAIL suggests that asset-conditioned generative data can serve as a robust and scalable complement to traditional data sources. This could broaden the scope of tasks that humanoids can learn and perform autonomously, potentially accelerating the development of capable general-purpose robots. The approach pushes towards a future where sophisticated robot behaviors can be iterated and validated largely in simulation, with reduced need for extensive physical experimentation.

While the pipeline assumes the availability of 3D object assets, simulator-ready scenes, and compliant video foundation models, and reconstruction quality can be affected by factors like occlusion, fast motion, or inconsistent VFM output, its demonstrated success on a real humanoid robot positions GRAIL as a notable advancement in scalable data generation for humanoid loco-manipulation.
