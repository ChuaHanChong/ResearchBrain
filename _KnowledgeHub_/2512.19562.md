# REALM: A Real-to-Sim Validated Benchmark for Generalization in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> REALM introduces a real-to-sim validated benchmark for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic manipulation, featuring a high-fidelity simulation with 15 diverse perturbation factors. The benchmark demonstrates a strong Pearson correlation (r > 0.88) with real-world performance and reveals that state-of-the-art VLA models still struggle with generalization, particularly regarding unseen objects and semantic understanding.

## Problem

- Lack of reliable, scalable, and reproducible methods for evaluating the generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotics.
- Existing real-world robot evaluations are costly and challenging to control systematically for specific perturbation factors.
- Current simulation benchmarks suffer from limited perturbation diversity and a significant 'real-to-sim gap' due to low visual fidelity and misaligned control dynamics.

## Method

- Developed **REALM**, a high-fidelity simulation environment aligned with the ==DROID robot platform== through ==system identification== for precise control.
- Integrated a comprehensive set of ==7 manipulation skills==, ==10 tasks==, over ==3,500 objects==, and ==15 distinct perturbation factors== covering visual, semantic, and behavioral challenges.
- Established a ==multi-tiered task progression metric== for granular success evaluation and performed rigorous real-to-sim validation using ==Pearson correlation== and ==Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV)==.

## Results

- **REALM** showed a strong Pearson correlation (**consistently > 0.88**) between real-world and simulated task progression across **7 tasks** and **5 perturbation types**.
- Evaluated VLA models (**π0**, **π0-FAST**, **GR00T N1.5**) exhibited generally low success rates and significant performance degradation under various perturbations, with **unseen objects (VSB-NOBJ)** causing the **largest drops**.
- **π0-FAST** achieved the **highest** overall task progression among tested models, but all models demonstrated noticeable decreases in performance due to purely semantic and specific visual (e.g., viewpoint) changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity simulation, when rigorously validated and aligned with real-world control, can serve as a reliable and informative proxy for evaluating robot learning models.
> - Despite extensive pre-training, VLA models exhibit significant sensitivity to semantic perturbations and changes in camera viewpoint, indicating limits in language understanding and visual robustness.
> - Generalization to new and unseen objects, particularly in behavioral contexts, remains the most challenging aspect for current VLA models, causing the largest performance drops.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics: the need for reliable, scalable, and reproducible evaluation of generalization capabilities. VLA models aim to enable robots to understand and execute tasks from natural language, leveraging insights from large-scale pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs). While these models have shown impressive progress in various tasks, their ability to generalize to novel environments, objects, and conditions—a cornerstone for real-world deployment—remains difficult and expensive to assess.

Historically, progress in fields like NLP and computer vision has been accelerated by standardized benchmarks (e.g., GLUE, ImageNet, COCO). In robotics, particularly for VLA models, a major bottleneck for evaluating generalization is the cost and logistical complexity of real-world experiments. Real-world evaluation platforms, such as RoboArena, offer valuable insights but often lack the systematic control over individual perturbation factors needed for rigorous generalization testing and struggle with reproducibility due to dynamic setups.

Simulation offers a promising alternative, providing controlled environments and cost-effectiveness. However, existing simulation benchmarks for robotic manipulation often fall short in two critical aspects:
1. **Limited Perturbation Variety:** Many benchmarks focus on a narrow range of environmental changes, failing to stress-test models comprehensively.
2. **Real-to-Sim Gap:** This fundamental challenge comprises two parts:
 - **Visual Fidelity:** Low-fidelity visuals in simulations create a distribution shift, causing vision-based policies trained on real data to perform poorly.
 - **Control Alignment:** Mismatched physics and control dynamics between simulation and reality lead to diverging robot behavior, even for identical actions.

Previous efforts, such as SIMPLER, have made strides in mitigating the real-to-sim gap for policy evaluation. However, SIMPLER, along with other benchmarks like GemBench, VLABench, and COLOSSEUM, typically offer limited diversity in skills, objects, viewpoints, or types of perturbations.

REALM directly tackles these limitations. By developing a high-fidelity simulation with aligned robot control and a significantly expanded set of diverse perturbations, it aims to provide a "good proxy" for real-world performance. This positions REALM as a crucial step forward, enabling systematic, reproducible, and scalable evaluation of VLA models' generalization abilities, moving beyond the constraints of limited real-world testing and the deficiencies of prior simulation benchmarks. It represents a methodological advancement in how robot learning models are evaluated, fostering a more rigorous and data-driven approach to understanding their strengths and weaknesses.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind REALM stems from the recognized need for robust and scalable evaluation of generalization in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While VLA models are becoming increasingly powerful, their ability to perform reliably outside their training distribution—a prerequisite for real-world deployment—is poorly understood due due to evaluation challenges.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a High-Fidelity, Reproducible Simulation Environment:** To create a simulation platform that can accurately reflect real-world robotic manipulation scenarios, ensuring both visual realism and precise robot control. This directly addresses the limitations of existing simulation benchmarks that often suffer from low visual fidelity and a significant "real-to-sim" control gap.
2. **Establish a Strong Real-to-Sim Correlation:** To empirically validate that performance measured within the REALM simulation environment strongly correlates with real-world robot performance. This objective is crucial for ensuring that insights gained from simulation are genuinely indicative of how VLA models would behave in physical settings, thus making the benchmark a trustworthy proxy.
3. **Create a Comprehensive Generalization Benchmark:** To design a benchmark capable of stress-testing VLA models against a wide array of factors that impact generalization. This includes implementing a diverse set of manipulation skills, a large variety of objects, and crucially, 15 distinct perturbation factors spanning visual, semantic, and behavioral categories. The goal is to systematically identify specific failure modes and weaknesses.
4. **Evaluate State-of-the-Art VLA Models:** To apply the newly developed benchmark to evaluate prominent VLA models (π0, π0-FAST, GR00T N1.5), providing initial insights into their current generalization capabilities and robustness. This serves both to demonstrate the benchmark's utility and to highlight areas where current models fall short.
5. **Quantify Weaknesses and Drive Future Research:** To systematically quantify the impact of various perturbations on VLA model performance, thereby providing clear signals for future research directions aimed at improving generalization and robustness in robot learning.

In essence, the project aims to overcome the "reproducibility and cost" barrier associated with real-world robotic evaluation, providing a standardized, systematic, and validated tool for accelerating progress in generalist robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors designed REALM as a high-fidelity simulation environment and benchmark focused on evaluating generalization in VLA models, particularly those trained on real-world data like DROID. Their approach is multi-faceted, combining a robust simulation platform with rigorous validation procedures and a comprehensive evaluation framework.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design

- **Embodiment:** The benchmark targets the DROID robot platform, chosen for its large-scale open-source dataset and widespread use in the robot learning community.
- **Skills:** REALM supports 7 fundamental manipulation skills: picking, putting, pushing, rotating, stacking, opening, and closing.
- **Tasks:** Two main task sets are defined:
 - **REALM-base:** Comprising 8 tasks primarily testing pick-and-place skills.
 - **REALM-articulated:** Consisting of 2 tasks involving opening and closing articulated cabinet drawers.
- **Diversity:** The environment supports over 3,500 objects and 10 scenes, providing substantial variety for generalization testing.
- **Extensibility:** The design is modular, allowing for future expansion with more skills, tasks, perturbations, and robot embodiments.

#### 3.2. Choice of Perturbations

To comprehensively assess robustness, REALM incorporates 15 distinct perturbation factors, largely derived from the ⋆-Gen taxonomy and augmented with V-LIGHT (scene illumination). These are categorized as:
- **Visual Perturbations (6):** V-AUG (blur/contrast), V-SC (distractors), V-VIEW (camera pose shifts), V-LIGHT (illumination color/intensity).
- **Semantic Perturbations (8):** S-PROP (object properties), S-LANG (language phrasing), S-MO (spatial relationships), S-AFF (human affordances), S-INT (internet knowledge), SB-NOUN (reference other objects), SB-VRB (change skill for compatible one). Semantic perturbations leverage an off-the-shelf VLM for instruction generation.
- **Behavioral Perturbations (7):** B-HOBJ (object mass), VB-POSE (object pose), VB-MOBJ (object size/shape), SB-NOUN, SB-VRB, VSB-NOBJ (new unseen object). Note that some perturbations span multiple categories (e.g., VSB-NOBJ is visual, semantic, and behavioral).

#### 3.3. Metrics

- **Tiered Task Progression:** Instead of binary success, a granular progression rubric is defined for each skill. This rubric breaks down a task into an ordered sequence of discrete states (e.g., Reach → Grasp → Lift), with each stage equally weighted, yielding a score between 0.0 (complete failure) and 1.0 (full completion). This allows for a more detailed understanding of where failures occur.
- **Real-to-Sim Validation Metrics:**
 - **Pearson Correlation Coefficient (r):** Measures the linear correlation between real and simulated task progression (higher is better).
 - **p-value:** From a two-sided t-test, indicating statistical significance (lower is preferred).
 - **Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV):** Assesses the consistency of policy rankings between real and simulated setups (lower is preferred).
- **Generalization Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Absolute Task Progression:** Direct comparison of model scores under default vs. perturbed settings.
 - **Root Mean Squared Deviation (RMSD):** Quantifies the overall magnitude of a perturbation's effect on task progression across all models and tasks.

#### 3.4. System Identification for Real-to-Sim Alignment

This is a crucial aspect of REALM's methodology.
- **Control Gap Mitigation:** The authors re-implemented the underlying DROID robot controller in IsaacSim, optimizing 14 free parameters for simulated joint friction and armature. This optimization minimizes the L2 norm between real-world and simulated joint position trajectories over a small dataset of real-world trajectory replays, ensuring high fidelity in robot movement.
- **Visual Gap Validation:** The authors argue that the high-fidelity visuals of IsaacSim are sufficient. This is validated by:
 - The strong correlation observed between real and simulated task progression.
 - Quantifying the cosine similarity (0.85) between attention maps of a VLA model (π0) when processing real and simulated video frames, indicating similar visual processing despite the domain difference.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Methodology

- Three state-of-the-art VLA models were evaluated: π0, π0-FAST, and GR00T N1.5. π models were fine-tuned on DROID, and GR00T was fine-tuned to match the action space.
- The evaluation involved approximately 4,000 simulation rollouts for each model across 10 tasks and 15 perturbations.
- Analysis was structured around four factors: visual, semantic, and behavioral generalization, and overall robustness/task completion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating both the utility of REALM and the current limitations of state-of-the-art VLA models.

#### 4.1. Real-to-Sim Validation

- **Strong Correlation:** The empirical validation showed a strong Pearson correlation (r consistently > 0.88, often > 0.9) between task progression in the real world and in REALM simulation across 7 tasks and 5 visual and behavioral perturbations. This indicates that REALM is a reliable proxy for real-world performance.
- **Consistent Ranking:** A low Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) suggested that the relative performance rankings of VLA models are largely consistent between simulation and reality.
- **Visual Fidelity:** The cosine similarity of attention maps from the π0 model (0.85) between real and simulated frames further supported the claim that REALM's high-fidelity visuals adequately mitigate the visual gap, preventing drastic prediction differences.

#### 4.2. VLA Generalization Capabilities

Overall, the evaluation demonstrated that **generalization and robustness remain open challenges for VLA models**, despite their large-scale training.

- **Model Performance Comparison:**
 - **π0-FAST** achieved the highest overall task progression across all perturbations, clearly outperforming other models.
 - **π0** showed intermediate performance.
 - **GR00T N1.5** exhibited substantially lower task progression, making its perturbation effects less informative.

- **Factor I: Visual Generalization:**
 - Purely visual perturbations had a noticeable impact on performance (average RMSD ≥ 0.12).
 - **Least Impactful:** Blur & Contrast (V-AUG) and Scene Illumination (V-LIGHT) were the least severe, likely due to visual diversity in DROID training data and pre-trained VLM backbones.
 - **More Impactful:** Viewpoint change (V-VIEW) and Distractors (V-SC) had a stronger effect, sometimes leading to unexpected improvements in performance, suggesting complex sensitivities.

- **Factor II: Semantic Generalization:**
 - **Surprisingly Challenging:** Semantic perturbations significantly challenged both π models, particularly π0. This suggests that the diffusion training objective of π0 might harm language understanding capabilities, leading to larger drops in task progression for these factors.
 - **Most Profound Effect:** Perturbations requiring world knowledge (S-INT, S-AFF) had the most significant impact on performance.
 - **Low Effect:** Understanding spatial relationships (S-MO) had a surprisingly low effect, despite not being explicitly represented in datasets like DROID.

- **Factor III: Behavioral Generalization:**
 - **Most Challenging Category:** Behavioral perturbations, which often combine visual and semantic changes with the requirement for motor control adaptation, proved to be the most difficult.
 - **Relatively Good:** Models generalized relatively well across *different skills on the same manipulated object* (SB-VRB).
 - **Significant Struggle:** Models struggled significantly with *translating skills across multiple objects* (SB-NOUN, VSB-NOBJ), especially **unseen objects** (VSB-NOBJ), which caused the largest performance drops and ranked as the most impactful perturbation overall.
 - **Moderate Effect:** Changes in object properties like shape (VB-MOBJ) or mass (B-HOBJ) negatively affected performance but to a lesser extent.
 - **High Impact:** Changing the pose of the manipulated object (VB-POSE) had a substantial effect, ranking 2nd most impactful, causing a 0.12 drop in progression for both π models.

- **Factor IV: Robustness and Task Completion:**
 - **Low Success Rates:** Binary success rates for the evaluated models were generally low, even for π0-FAST, on several tasks.
 - **Long Completion Times:** Models took relatively long to complete tasks (avg. 20-30 seconds), indicating continued struggle in novel environments rather than efficient, robust execution.

#### 4.3. Conclusions and Lessons Learned

The authors draw six key conclusions:
1. High-fidelity simulation with aligned control is a valuable and informative proxy for real-world performance.
2. Purely semantic perturbations cause noticeable performance drops, despite Internet-scale VLM pre-training.
3. VLA models exhibit sensitivity to camera view changes.
4. Behavioral generalization, especially to new objects and their properties, is the most challenging.
5. Models generalize relatively well across known skills if the manipulated object remains constant.
6. Overall reliability and robustness, particularly under perturbations, remain low, with low success rates.

The paper also hypothesizes that extensive fine-tuning on the DROID dataset might inadvertently harm the inherent generalization capabilities of the VLM backbones.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The REALM benchmark represents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with the potential for wide-ranging impact:

#### 5.1. Accelerating Research and Development in Generalization

- **Standardized, Reproducible Evaluation:** By providing a highly correlated real-to-sim environment, REALM offers a standardized and reproducible platform for evaluating VLA models. This overcomes the prohibitive cost and logistical challenges of real-world testing, enabling researchers to quickly iterate and rigorously compare different approaches. This will be crucial for fostering rapid progress in the development of truly generalist robot policies.
- **Systematic Identification of Failure Modes:** The comprehensive suite of 15 diverse perturbations allows for systematic probing and quantification of model weaknesses. Researchers can pinpoint whether models struggle with visual changes, semantic understanding, or behavioral adaptation. This granular insight is invaluable for targeted model improvements, leading to more robust and reliable VLA systems.
- **Driving Focused Innovation:** The clear identification of challenging areas (e.g., behavioral generalization to unseen objects, specific semantic perturbations) will guide future research. It highlights the need for novel architectural designs, training methodologies, and data augmentation strategies that explicitly address these generalization gaps.

#### 5.2. Bridging the Real-to-Sim Gap

- **Demonstrated Validation Methodology:** REALM provides a rigorous example of how to validate a simulation environment as a reliable proxy for real-world performance. The emphasis on both control alignment through system identification and visual fidelity validation (attention map similarity) sets a new standard for building trustworthy simulation benchmarks. This methodology can be adopted and adapted by others developing simulation platforms for robotics.
- **Enhanced Trust in Simulation:** By demonstrating a strong correlation with real-world results, REALM increases confidence in using simulation as a primary evaluation tool. This makes research more accessible and democratic, as advanced robotics hardware is not strictly necessary for early-stage evaluation.

#### 5.3. Practical Implications for Robotic Deployment

- **Realistic Assessment of VLA Readiness:** The findings underscore that current state-of-the-art VLA models, despite impressive advances, are still far from being autonomously deployable in unstructured real-world environments. They lack the necessary robustness and generalization capabilities across a spectrum of perturbations. This provides a realistic assessment for industry and policymakers regarding the current state of robotic AI.
- **Guiding Design of Future Robots and AI:** The insights from REALM can inform the design of future robotic systems and AI architectures. For instance, the observed sensitivity to object pose or unseen objects suggests that perception systems need to be more resilient to variations, and action policies require better adaptive control mechanisms.

#### 5.4. Broader Scientific Impact

- **Foundation for Future Research:** REALM itself is presented as an extensible and modular platform, encouraging the community to build upon it by adding more skills, tasks, perturbations, and robot embodiments. It serves as a living benchmark that can evolve with the field.
- **Understanding Generalization in AI:** Beyond robotics, the systematic study of generalization under various types of perturbations provides insights into the fundamental challenges of AI models, particularly in embodied settings where interaction with the physical world is paramount.

In summary, REALM is not just another benchmark; it's a meticulously validated framework that empowers the robot learning community to systematically confront and overcome the critical challenge of generalization in VLA models, thereby accelerating the path towards truly intelligent and capable robots.
