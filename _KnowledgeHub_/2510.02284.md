# Learning to Generate Rigid Body Interactions with Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> KineMask enhances Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) to generate physically realistic rigid body interactions from an initial image and object velocity, leveraging a two-stage training strategy with velocity masks and textual conditioning. The model significantly outperforms existing baselines in user studies on motion fidelity and physical consistency, demonstrating an emergent causal understanding of object dynamics.

## Problem

- Current Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) struggle to generate physically plausible object interactions, often failing to capture fundamental physical traits like object permanence and realistic collisions.
- Existing VDMs lack precise object-level control mechanisms, limiting their utility as 'world simulators' for applications in robotics and embodied decision-making.
- Prior physics-based video generation methods typically rely on explicit 3D scene reconstruction or pre-defined trajectories, which are computationally intensive or restrict the inference of causal effects from initial conditions.

## Method

- **KineMask** integrates a **ControlNet** with a VDM backbone, using novel low-level conditioning via 'velocity masks' that encode instantaneous object velocity for initially moving objects.
- A two-stage training strategy is employed: first, training with full ground-truth velocity masks, then fine-tuning with a mask dropout strategy to compel the model to infer future motion from initial conditions.
- The system utilizes synthetic data generated in ==Blender== for videos and per-frame velocity masks, complemented by high-level textual descriptions generated by a Vision-Language Model to capture interaction effects.

## Results

- KineMask was preferred by users for motion fidelity (88% vs. CogVideoX's 72%) and physical consistency (77% vs. CogVideoX's 59%) in real-world scene evaluations, outperforming all baselines.
- The two-stage training strategy achieved significantly better quantitative metrics on synthetic data (FVD 160.3, IoU 0.367) compared to single-stage training (FVD 288.8, IoU 0.237), confirming its efficacy in learning causal inference.
- The model demonstrates an emergent understanding of causality, producing different interaction outcomes based on varying initial velocities and showing implicit awareness of physical properties like mass in collision dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Withholding future motion supervision through a mask dropout strategy during training effectively forces VDMs to learn and infer complex, causal rigid body interactions from minimal initial conditions.
> - The synergistic combination of precise low-level kinematic control (velocity masks) and high-level textual conditioning enables VDMs to generate both accurate object movements and rich, causally consistent physical effects.
> - Training VDMs with synthetic data explicitly depicting object collisions is crucial for developing a robust understanding of physical dynamics that generalizes well to real-world scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in video generation, particularly with Video Diffusion Models (VDMs), have led to their deployment in various creative industries such as film, social media, and advertising. Beyond content creation, VDMs are being explored as "world simulators," with potential applications in robotics and embodied decision-making. However, a significant limitation within the current landscape of VDM research is their struggle to generate physically plausible object interactions and their lack of precise object-level control mechanisms. Current large-scale models often fail to capture fundamental physical traits such as object permanence, realistic collisions, and causal interactions.

This research addresses this gap by aiming to improve the physical realism and control capabilities of VDMs for object interactions. Prior work has attempted to integrate physics-based simulators into data-driven video generation, such as WonderPlay, PhysGen, and PhysGen3D. These approaches, however, typically rely on explicit 3D scene reconstruction, which presents its own set of computational challenges. Another line of research involves drag-based methods (e.g., DragDiffusion, Motion Prompting, TORA) that enable object movement but often require pre-defined target points or full trajectories, thereby limiting their ability to infer causal effects solely from initial conditions. More recent methods like Force Prompting have introduced physics-guided controls but have been noted to offer limited fine-grained control and sometimes produce unrealistic dynamics or distorted object shapes.

The work presented in this paper, KineMask, seeks to overcome these limitations by introducing an approach that enables VDMs to generate realistic rigid body interactions and effects from minimal initial conditions (a single image and specified object velocity). It differentiates itself by focusing on inferring future object interactions directly within the VDM framework, without explicit physics simulations, and by integrating both low-level kinematic control and high-level textual conditioning. This positions KineMask as a contribution towards establishing VDMs as more reliable and physically accurate world models, a critical step for their application in fields requiring predictive dynamics and robust interaction understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified shortcomings of current Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) in generating physically accurate object interactions and their limited object-level control. Despite progress in visual quality, existing VDMs often produce physically implausible outcomes, such as objects ignoring collisions, exhibiting incorrect motion dynamics, or demonstrating a lack of understanding of causal effects. These deficiencies significantly hinder their utility as "world simulators" for applications in robotics, planning, and embodied artificial intelligence, where even minor deviations from realistic physics can lead to accumulating errors in predicted dynamics. The authors specifically highlight the inability of current models to capture object permanence, collisions, and causal interactions, which are fundamental traits of the physical world.

Against this background, KineMask aims to address two central research questions:
1. **Can a video diffusion model generate realistic interactions between rigid bodies given initial dynamic conditions?** This objective focuses on developing a VDM-based framework capable of synthesizing physically plausible interactions, such as collisions and subsequent movements, based solely on an initial image and the specified velocity of an object. The goal is to move beyond simple object movement to infer complex, multi-object dynamics.
2. **How do data and textual conditioning influence the emergence of causal physical effects in generated videos?** This objective explores the roles of different conditioning signals—specifically, a novel low-level motion control mechanism (velocity masks) and high-level textual descriptions of scene dynamics—in enabling VDMs to learn and express causal physical effects. The research investigates how these conditioning types contribute to the model's understanding and generation of complex phenomena resulting from interactions.

In pursuing these objectives, KineMask aims to provide low-level kinematic control over parameters like object direction and speed, while simultaneously enabling the VDM to infer object interactions directly. It also seeks to integrate this low-level control with high-level textual conditioning to support the synthesis of complex dynamical phenomena, such as a liquid spilling due to a collision. The overarching goal is to enhance the physical realism and controllability of video generation models, thereby making them more suitable as predictive tools for physical AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KineMask is designed to synthesize realistic rigid body interactions in complex scenes, leveraging an input image and a specified object velocity. The methodology involves a two-stage training strategy for a ControlNet integrated with a Video Diffusion Model (VDM) backbone, along with a novel conditioning mechanism and a synthetic data generation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Backbone Architecture

The approach builds upon existing Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) for image-to-video (I2V) generation, such as CogVideoX, Wan2.2, or Cosmos2.5. These models typically operate by reversing a noising process, learning to denoise a noisy video given an initial image and textual conditioning. KineMask integrates a ControlNet branch (ψ_ϕ) into this VDM architecture. The ControlNet is a plug-and-play component that allows for additional dense control signals, such as object motion masks, while keeping the parameters of the pre-trained VDM backbone frozen during its training.

#### 3.2. Enabling Motion Control

The core of KineMask's motion control mechanism lies in its unique conditioning signal and two-stage training.

- **Low-Level Conditioning (Velocity Masks):** The control signal `u` is an object mask `m` of resolution `H×W` across `F` frames, with three channels encoding the instantaneous velocity vector (red for x-axis, green for y-axis, blue for z-axis) within the pixels of the segmented object. Importantly, these velocity masks are initially provided *only* for objects moving in the first frame of the rendered video. This design choice compels the model to infer the motions and interactions of other objects without explicit pixel-level control for subsequent frames, thereby learning causal relationships.

- **High-Level Conditioning (Textual Descriptions):** In addition to low-level control, KineMask incorporates high-level textual descriptions `c` that summarize the effects of physical interactions in the video.

- **Two-Stage Training Strategy:**
 1. **First-Stage Training (ϕ'):** A ControlNet ϕ' is trained using a dataset `D` of captioned videos depicting objects in motion. For each video, the full stack of per-frame velocity masks `m` (representing ground-truth dynamics) is provided as the dense control signal `u`. This stage teaches the ControlNet to map dense pixel-wise motion supervision into structured guidance for object motion throughout the video sequence.
 2. **Second-Stage Training (ϕ'' with Mask Dropout):** To enable inference of motion and interactions from initial conditions only, the ControlNet ϕ' is fine-tuned into ϕ'' using a mask dropout strategy. During this stage, parts of the velocity masks `m` are randomly erased (denoted as `m⊙`). This means that for some frames, the velocity mask is set to zero, forcing the VDM to generate the motion and interactions without direct future pixel-level supervision. The sampling of frame indices for dropout (`f*`) is non-uniform, biased towards earlier frames where rigid body interactions are most frequent, helping the model focus on interaction-rich representations.

#### 3.3. Data Generation

Since real-world videos with explicit ground-truth velocity annotations and detailed interaction captions are difficult to acquire, KineMask relies on synthetic data generated in Blender.
- **Video and Mask Generation:** Scenes with simple rigid bodies (boxes and cylinders) on textured surfaces are rendered. Each controlled object is assigned an initial velocity with random direction and magnitude. This process yields:
 - `x0`: The rendered video.
 - `y`: The first frame of the video, used for image-to-video conditioning.
 - `m`: The stack of per-frame velocity masks for the initially moving objects.
- **Textual Caption Generation (`c`):** For each rendered video, a vision-language model (VLM) like Tarsier is used to generate detailed textual descriptions `c`, specifically prompted to focus on object interactions and their effects.

#### 3.4. Inference Procedure

At inference time, KineMask operates as follows:
- **User Input:** An unseen image `y` is provided. The user specifies a target object and its desired initial velocity (e.g., by drawing an arrow on the object).
- **Mask Construction:** An object segmentation mask for the target object is obtained (e.g., using SAM2). This mask, combined with the user-specified initial velocity, forms the initial velocity mask `m0`. For subsequent frames, the velocity mask `m⊙` is set to zero, consistent with the mask dropout strategy used in training.
- **Predicted Scene Description (`c_infer`):** A large language model (LLM) like GPT-5 is prompted with the input image and initial object motion information. The LLM then generates a textual description `c_infer` predicting the potential outcomes and effects of the object's motion within the scene, including likely interactions.
- **Video Generation:** The VDM, equipped with the fine-tuned ControlNet ϕ'', processes the input tuple `{xT, y_input, m⊙, c_infer}` (where `xT` is pure Gaussian noise) to synthesize the output video, inferring realistic object interactions and their causal effects from the initial conditions and high-level textual guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding KineMask's capabilities, its performance against baselines, and the impact of its design choices.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Improvements and User Study

Qualitative evaluations demonstrate that KineMask generates realistic rigid body interactions in real-world scenes, including plausible collisions, causal effects (e.g., liquid spilling, glass breaking), and multi-object motions. In contrast, baseline models such as CogVideoX often exhibit unrealistic interactions, hallucinations, or object disappearances. A user study involving 30 participants, performing pairwise comparisons on real-world scenes, indicated that KineMask significantly outperformed all baselines (CogVideoX, Wan, TORA, MotionI2V, Force Prompting) across three metrics: motion fidelity to the control signal, realism of object interactions, and overall physical consistency. For instance, KineMask was preferred for motion fidelity 88% of the time against CogVideoX's 72%, and for physical consistency 77% of the time against CogVideoX's 59%.

#### 4.2. Low-Level Motion Control Analysis

- **Fine-Grained Control:** KineMask demonstrates disentangled control over various aspects of motion, including direction, speed, and the specific object to be moved. This control generalizes to complex real-world scenes despite training on basic synthetic data, indicating the model's capacity for fine-grained dynamic manipulation.
- **Two-Stage Training Efficacy:** Ablation studies confirm the importance of the proposed two-stage training strategy. When evaluated on the "Simple Motion" synthetic dataset, KineMask with two-stage training achieved significantly better quantitative metrics (e.g., FVD 160.3, IoU 0.367) compared to direct single-stage training (FVD 288.8, IoU 0.237), approaching the performance of an "upper bound" model with privileged, full-frame mask supervision.

#### 4.3. Impact of Training Data

The choice of training data significantly influences KineMask's ability to generate realistic interactions.
- **Data Influence:** When trained on the "Simple Motion" dataset (without collisions) and applied to real-world data designed to induce collisions, KineMask failed to render realistic interactions, often resulting in hallucinations. However, when trained on the "Interactions" dataset (which includes explicit object collisions), the model accurately produced interactions for the same input velocities. This suggests that while VDMs can be robust to visual domain shifts, the understanding of motion and interactions is more sensitive to the presence of relevant dynamics in the training data.
- **Quantitative Evaluation:** Quantitative results on the "Interactions" synthetic dataset showed that KineMask trained on the "Interactions" dataset achieved the best performance across all metrics (e.g., FVD 250.7, FMVD 143.8, IoU 0.355), outperforming both the CogVideoX baseline and KineMask trained on "Simple Motion."

#### 4.4. Emergence of Causality

KineMask demonstrates an understanding of causality in physical interactions:
- **Velocity-Dependent Interactions:** Varying the initial velocity of an object results in different interaction outcomes, suggesting the model captures the causal relationship between initial force/speed and subsequent dynamics.
- **Implicit Mass Awareness:** In scenarios where the same initial velocity is applied to an object interacting with different environmental objects (e.g., by inpainting and replacing with objects of varying perceived mass), the final position of the moving object differs. This indicates an implicit understanding of physical properties, such as mass, which influences collision outcomes, a challenging capability for drag-based methods that predefine final positions.

#### 4.5. Generalization Capabilities

- **Beyond Object-Object Interactions:** KineMask's learned knowledge of interactions generalizes beyond simple object-object collisions to include plausible hand-object and hand-tool-object interactions, demonstrating the model's ability to apply acquired principles to novel interaction types.
- **Generalization to Different VDMs:** The KineMask approach was successfully applied to other VDM backbones, specifically Wan2.2-5B and Cosmos2.5-2B. In human studies, KineMask-enhanced versions of these models consistently outperformed their original backbones in terms of motion fidelity, interaction quality, and overall physical realism. This highlights the generalizability of the proposed methodology across different VDM architectures.

#### 4.6. High-Level Text Conditioning Analysis

- **Qualitative Impact of Rich Captions:** Training KineMask with rich textual captions `c` (generated by a VLM) enables the model to generate effects beyond those explicitly present in the synthetic training data (e.g., a vase breaking or water ripples forming). This suggests that detailed textual conditioning allows the model to leverage the VDM's broader prior knowledge to synthesize complex, nuanced physical effects, even when inferring novel interactions from high-level `c_infer` prompts.
- **Quantitative Impact:** On synthetic data, training with `c` improved object consistency (IoU) and overall realism (FVD) compared to training with generic captions, despite a slight decrease in motion consistency (FMVD).

#### 4.7. Identified Limitations/Failure Cases

KineMask exhibits some limitations, including failures to generate collisions with objects of insufficient perceived height and in ambiguous scenes with many similar objects, which can lead to object duplication or disappearance. The authors speculate that textual prompts in complex scenarios might also contribute to ambiguities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

KineMask represents a significant step towards enabling physically plausible video generation, a capability that is crucial for advancing Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) beyond purely artistic applications into roles as predictive "world models." The work contributes to the field in several ways:

#### 5.1. Enhanced Physical Realism and Control

By introducing a mechanism for object motion conditioning based on velocity masks and a two-stage training strategy, KineMask significantly improves the physical realism of generated object interactions. It allows for fine-grained control over initial object velocity while enabling the VDM to infer complex, causal interactions without explicit physics simulations. This moves VDMs closer to accurately predicting how objects behave in response to dynamic inputs, addressing a major limitation of prior generative models.

#### 5.2. Implications for World Modeling and Robotics

The ability to generate realistic and causally consistent rigid body interactions has direct and substantial implications for artificial intelligence subfields that rely on understanding and predicting the physical world.
- **Robotic Manipulation:** KineMask's capacity to simulate how objects collide and react can inform the training of robotic agents, allowing them to anticipate outcomes of their actions and plan movements in dynamic environments.
- **Planning and Embodied Decision Making:** For embodied AI agents, the framework offers a tool to generate plausible future states based on initial conditions, facilitating better decision-making and strategic planning in interactive scenarios.
- **Physics-Grounded AI:** The work provides valuable insights into how VDMs can learn and generalize physical principles from synthetic data to real-world scenes, fostering the development of more robust and physically intelligent AI systems.

#### 5.3. Complementary Role of Low- and High-Level Conditioning

KineMask highlights the synergistic relationship between low-level video control (via velocity masks) and high-level textual guidance (via LLM-generated scene descriptions). This demonstrates that combining precise kinematic control with contextual, descriptive text enhances both the accuracy and the richness of generated physical effects, allowing models to leverage both visual and semantic priors. This finding is particularly relevant for the future development of multimodal AI systems that aim to reason about and interact with the physical world.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The authors identify several promising avenues for future work, including the incorporation of more comprehensive physical parameters beyond velocity (e.g., friction, mass, shape, air resistance) to achieve even greater physical accuracy. Extending the framework to handle soft-body interactions would further expand its world modeling capabilities. Furthermore, the reliance on advanced multimodal language models for generating accurate textual descriptions underscores the potential for integrating sophisticated physical reasoning capabilities directly into generative models, a direction that could lead to more nuanced and intelligent world simulators.
