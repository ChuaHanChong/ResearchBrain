# Learning to Generate Rigid Body Interactions with Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> KineMask enhances Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) to generate physically realistic rigid body interactions from an initial image and object velocity, leveraging a two-stage training strategy with velocity masks and textual conditioning. The model significantly outperforms existing baselines in user studies on motion fidelity and physical consistency, demonstrating an emergent causal understanding of object dynamics.

## Problem

- Current Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) struggle to generate physically plausible object interactions, often failing to capture fundamental physical traits like object permanence and realistic collisions.
- Existing VDMs lack precise object-level control mechanisms, limiting their utility as 'world simulators' for applications in robotics and embodied decision-making.
- Prior physics-based video generation methods typically rely on explicit 3D scene reconstruction or pre-defined trajectories, which are computationally intensive or restrict the inference of causal effects from initial conditions.

## Method

- **KineMask** integrates a **ControlNet** with a VDM backbone, using novel low-level conditioning via 'velocity masks' that encode instantaneous object velocity for initially moving objects.
- A two-stage training strategy is employed: first, training with full ground-truth velocity masks, then fine-tuning with a mask dropout strategy to compel the model to infer future motion from initial conditions.
- The system utilizes synthetic data generated in ==Blender== for videos and per-frame velocity masks, complemented by high-level textual descriptions generated by a Vision-Language Model to capture interaction effects.

## Results

- KineMask was preferred by users for motion fidelity (88% vs. CogVideoX's 72%) and physical consistency (77% vs. CogVideoX's 59%) in real-world scene evaluations, outperforming all baselines.
- The two-stage training strategy achieved significantly better quantitative metrics on synthetic data (FVD 160.3, IoU 0.367) compared to single-stage training (FVD 288.8, IoU 0.237), confirming its efficacy in learning causal inference.
- The model demonstrates an emergent understanding of causality, producing different interaction outcomes based on varying initial velocities and showing implicit awareness of physical properties like mass in collision dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Withholding future motion supervision through a mask dropout strategy during training effectively forces VDMs to learn and infer complex, causal rigid body interactions from minimal initial conditions.
> - The synergistic combination of precise low-level kinematic control (velocity masks) and high-level textual conditioning enables VDMs to generate both accurate object movements and rich, causally consistent physical effects.
> - Training VDMs with synthetic data explicitly depicting object collisions is crucial for developing a robust understanding of physical dynamics that generalizes well to real-world scenes.
