# RoboManipBaselines: A Unified Framework for Imitation Learning in Robotic Manipulation across Real and Simulation Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at AIST, Japan, developed RoboManipBaselines, an open-source framework that unifies the imitation learning pipeline for robotic manipulation across simulation and real-world environments. The framework enabled benchmarking of policies, with Diffusion Policy achieving average success rates up to 52.0% in simulation and 47.9% in real-world tasks, and supports diverse research applications including multimodal sensing and interactive systems.

## Problem

- A disparity exists in software framework maturity between model-based and learning-based robotics, with the latter's ecosystem still evolving and lacking unified tools.
- The distinct processing pipelines for learning-based methods (data collection, training, execution) make it challenging to reuse software assets from established model-based robotics frameworks.
- Researchers face barriers in consistently evaluating and reproducing imitation learning methods across diverse environments, robot platforms, and policy models.

## Method

- Introduced **RoboManipBaselines**, an open-source, unified framework for ==imitation learning== in robotic manipulation, designed for integration, generality, extensibility, and reproducibility.
- Implemented an ==OpenAI Gym-compatible interface== for consistent interaction with various environments (MuJoCo, Isaac Gym, PyBullet, and real robots like UR5e and xArm 7).
- Developed a custom data format for efficient compression and I/O, supporting diverse teleoperation interfaces and releasing public datasets to facilitate reproducibility.

## Results

- In simulation, **Diffusion Policy** achieved the highest average success rate of **52.0%**, with **SARNN** closely following at **50.8%** across eight manipulation tasks.
- Real-world evaluations demonstrated **Diffusion Policy**'s highest average success rate of **47.9%** across eight tasks, affirming the framework's capability for unified benchmarking.
- **RoboManipBaselines** facilitated various research applications, including improving success rates in data augmentation from **36%** to **82%**, integrating tactile foundation models, and developing interactive language-controlled robotic systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified framework can streamline the entire imitation learning pipeline, from data collection to policy deployment, enhancing research efficiency across simulation and real-world robotics.
> - Efficient data handling, including specialized depth image compression, is crucial for improving training speed and overall performance in robot learning.
> - Simpler policy architectures like SARNN can achieve performance comparable to more complex models (e.g., Diffusion Policy) in manipulation tasks while offering computational benefits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has traditionally relied on model-based methods, which involve detailed environmental modeling, motion planning, and control based on robot kinematics and dynamics. This approach has a mature software ecosystem supported by frameworks like Pinocchio, MoveIt!, and OMPL. In contrast, recent advancements in learning-based approaches, particularly imitation learning (IL), have enabled robots to acquire complex manipulation skills from expert demonstrations. These methods often employ visuomotor policies, neural networks, action chunking, diffusion models, and multimodal sensing (e.g., 3D point clouds, tactile data). While several open-source frameworks have emerged for learning-based robotics, such as robomimic, LIBERO, ManiSkill, RoboHive, RoboCasa, D3IL, COLOSSEUM, LeRobot, and RoboVerse, the authors note that the ecosystem remains in a "fluid state" compared to model-based robotics. Many existing frameworks exhibit limitations in areas such as comprehensive real-world support, compatibility with multiple simulators, diverse robot embodiments, or integrated multimodal sensing.

RoboManipBaselines aims to address these gaps by providing a unified, open-source framework that seamlessly supports the entire imitation learning pipeline across both simulation and real-world environments. It seeks to consolidate diverse functionalities and promote standardized evaluation, akin to the established frameworks in model-based robotics. The authors position their work as a foundation that can integrate with existing benchmarks (e.g., RLBench, RoboArena) and large-scale datasets (e.g., DROID, Open X-Embodiment), while offering distinct advantages in integration, generality, extensibility, and reproducibility across the full spectrum of IL research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RoboManipBaselines is to establish an open-source, general-purpose software framework that streamlines imitation learning research in robotic manipulation. The authors highlight the significant divergence in processing pipelines between conventional model-based and contemporary learning-based robotics. This divergence creates a barrier for researchers and practitioners, as software assets developed for model-based systems are often not directly reusable in learning-based contexts. The motivation stems from the perceived need for a reliable and comprehensive software ecosystem for learning-based approaches, one that can support the diverse requirements of academic research and real-world applications.

To achieve this overarching goal, the framework is designed around four core principles, which also serve as specific objectives:

- **Integration**: To provide an end-to-end workflow covering all phases of imitation learning, from data collection to policy training and subsequent rollout (execution) of learned skills. This aims to minimize friction and enable a continuous research cycle.
- **Generality**: To ensure compatibility with a wide array of robotic manipulation environments, encompassing both high-fidelity simulators and diverse real-world robot platforms. This objective seeks to enable broad applicability and facilitate sim-to-real transfer.
- **Extensibility**: To facilitate the straightforward addition of new robotic platforms, manipulation tasks, and imitation learning policy models with minimal development effort. This design choice aims to support the evolving nature of research in the field.
- **Reproducibility**: To enable systematic benchmarking and comparative evaluations of different methods using publicly available datasets. This objective underscores the importance of transparent and verifiable research outcomes.

The framework's implementation of these principles specifically targets a unified interface that supports multiple simulators and real robot environments, accommodates multimodal sensors, and integrates a variety of policy models, thereby acting as a common infrastructure for the imitation learning community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboManipBaselines is structured around three core components of imitation learning: the Environment, the Dataset, and the Policy. The design of each component reflects the principles of integration, generality, extensibility, and reproducibility.

**A. Environment**
The framework implements various environments, including both simulation and real-world setups, using an interface compatible with OpenAI Gym. This standardization allows for consistent handling and seamless switching between different environments. Programs developed and validated in simulation can be directly deployed on real robots, and data from both sources can be combined for training and evaluation. Extensibility is achieved by allowing developers to inherit an abstract environment class and override a minimal set of methods for new simulators or robots.

- **Simulators**: RoboManipBaselines supports MuJoCo, Isaac Gym, and PyBullet.
 - **MuJoCo**: Includes manipulators (Universal Robots UR5e, UFACTORY xArm 7, Franka Research 3, Kinova Gen3, FANUC CRX-5iA), a dual-arm manipulator (ALOHA), a mobile manipulator (TOYOTA HSR), and a humanoid robot (Unitree G1, with legs fixed for upper-body manipulation).
 - **Isaac Gym**: Utilized for parallel simulation capabilities, enabling parallelized data collection via teleoperation.
 - **Advanced Features**: The framework integrates features like a MuJoCo tactile simulation plugin for grid-based tactile sensing.
- **Real-world Robots**: Currently supports UR5e and xArm 7, with plans for Franka Research 3 and Kinova Gen3. The design permits users to implement robot-specific components as closed-source code by inheriting the framework, ensuring separation of public and private code.
- **Tasks**: Provides over ten simulation tasks, including manipulation of deformable objects (cables, cloth, granular materials) and articulated objects (doors, cabinets).

**B. Dataset**
RoboManipBaselines emphasizes public release of collected datasets to promote research and benchmarking. Simulation datasets are particularly highlighted for their reproducibility.

- **Dataset Content**:
 - 1,000 episodes for 10 single-task manipulation scenarios (100 episodes each).
 - 540 episodes for language-conditioned Pick&Place tasks (30 episodes for 18 combinations of 9 objects and 2 baskets).
 - Demonstrations are collected from experienced operators using a 3D mouse, resulting in smooth and efficient robot trajectories.
- **Data Collection Interfaces**: Supports keyboard, 3D mouse, and the GELLO leader-follower system, with an extensible design for integrating new interfaces (e.g., VR-based systems).
- **Custom Data Format**: Designed for efficient compression and I/O performance. It uses Hierarchical Data Format 5 (HDF5) for low-dimensional data and MP4 video files for RGB images.
 - **Depth Image Encoding**: Depth values (in millimeters, up to 65m with 1mm resolution) are stored as uint16, then split into upper and lower 8-bit components to form a 3-channel uint8 image. This image sequence is compressed into MP4 video format, allowing standard video codecs for high compression ratios and fast I/O. For example, a 640x480 depth image sequence at 22 Hz for 10 seconds can be stored in approximately 14 MB with millimeter-level reconstruction error.
- **Multiple State/Action Representations**: The dataset automatically stores various representations of robot state and action in parallel, including measured (q_msr, p_msr) and commanded (q_cmd, p_cmd) joint angles and end-effector poses, as well as their relative differences (∆p, ∆q). This flexibility allows for systematic comparison of different state/action spaces during policy training.

**C. Policy**
The framework offers a unified interface for training and rolling out a range of policy models using the collected datasets. It reuses original code implementations from prior work for policy definitions while standardizing dataset loading, parameter management, and I/O for rollout.

- **Supported Policies**: Includes MLP Policy, Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT), Diffusion Policy, Spatial Attention RNN (SARNN), 3D Diffusion Policy, ManiFlow Policy, Flow Policy, Multi-Task ACT (MT-ACT), π₀ (vision-language-action flow model), and GR00T (diffusion Transformer with VLM).
- **Extensibility**: Users can add new policies by inheriting abstract training and rollout classes and overriding a minimal set of methods, preserving the framework's generality.
- **Performance Optimization**: An option to load the entire dataset into CPU memory is provided to accelerate data retrieval during policy training, potentially speeding up the process by several times on systems with sufficient memory.

**Basic Usage (Section V)**
The paper outlines the workflow with simple Python commands for data collection, policy training, and policy rollout. This section highlights how users can switch environments, input devices, and policy models by modifying command-line arguments, demonstrating the framework's integrated and flexible design.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents benchmark evaluations in both simulation and real-world environments, along with several research applications illustrating the framework's utility.

**A. Evaluation in Simulation**
1. **Task Setup and Experimental Results**: Eight manipulation tasks were evaluated using a Universal Robots UR5e in MuJoCo environments (Fig. 7A), including tasks with deformable (Cable, Ring, Particle, Cloth) and articulated objects (Door, Toolbox, Cabinet, Insert). Four policies were compared: MLP Policy, ACT, Diffusion Policy, and SARNN.
 - MLP Policy demonstrated an average success rate of 16.4%, substantially lower than the other policies.
 - ACT, Diffusion Policy, and SARNN achieved average success rates of 45.0%, 52.0%, and 50.8%, respectively. Diffusion Policy showed the highest average performance by a narrow margin.
 - SARNN, despite a simpler architecture, achieved comparable performance to more computationally intensive policies.
2. **Comparison of Camera Number and Viewpoints**: ACT was evaluated on the eight simulation tasks using seven different camera configurations (Front, Side, Hand, and combinations).
 - The highest average success rate (52.1%) was achieved with a two-camera setup (Front+Side).
 - Using only the wrist-mounted Hand camera resulted in the lowest average success rate (17.1%).
 - Configurations including the Hand camera generally yielded lower success rates, suggesting that large viewpoint changes from egocentric views may complicate policy learning.
3. **Learning Curves and Data Efficiency**: Success rates were analyzed against training epochs and the number of training episodes for ACT, Diffusion Policy, and SARNN on five tasks.
 - Success rates generally increased with training epochs, though convergence speed and final performance varied by policy and task.
 - For many policy-task combinations, success rates did not monotonically increase with the number of training episodes; in some cases, they decreased. This suggests that in single-task settings, sufficient performance can be achieved with relatively small datasets, and that increased trajectory variability from larger datasets does not always lead to improved learning.

**B. Evaluation in Real-world Environments**
Eight manipulation tasks were conducted with a Universal Robots UR5e in a real-world setup (Fig. 7B), involving deformable, transparent, and reflective objects (Handkerchief, Sheet, Bag(L), Bag(S), ChainInTube, RingOnCone, RingOnHook, Gloves). ACT, Diffusion Policy, and SARNN were evaluated.
- Diffusion Policy achieved the highest average success rate (47.9%), aligning with the simulation results.
- ACT and SARNN achieved average success rates of 43.8% and 39.6%, respectively.
- These results demonstrate the framework's capability for unified benchmarking across simulation and real-world robotics.

**C. Research Applications**
The paper presents five research applications to illustrate RoboManipBaselines' versatility:
1. **Data Augmentation**: The framework was used to collect a single reference demonstration (Phase 1), train an MLP policy (Phase 3), and integrate an inherited teleoperation class for autonomous trajectory augmentation (Phase 2). This pipeline improved success rates from 36% to 82% across multiple tasks in both real and simulation environments.
2. **Integration with a Tactile Foundation Model**: Diffusion Policy was integrated with Sparsh, a pretrained tactile foundation model, for a spoon manipulation task requiring tactile perception. The integrated model achieved higher success rates than models using only camera observations or naive tactile image encoding (Fig. 11). The TACTO simulator was integrated for consistent sim-to-real evaluation.
3. **Interactive Robotic System**: An interactive system was developed by integrating speech recognition (Whisper), language models (GPT-4o), and text-to-speech with RoboManipBaselines. The system enabled natural language dialogue for sequential manipulation tasks using a Multi-Task ACT policy trained on 1000 episodes for 20 subtasks (Fig. 12B). The policy performed subsequent tasks despite changes in object arrangement from the training scene.
4. **Depth Camera Comparison for 3D Diffusion Policy**: The performance of 3D Diffusion Policy was compared using an Intel RealSense D455 (stereo-based) and an ORBBEC Femto Bolt (time-of-flight, ToF) depth camera. The ToF camera produced cleaner point clouds and resulted in higher task success rates (Fig. 13A). 3D Diffusion Policy, using colorless point clouds, showed robustness to visual disturbances (e.g., color changes, random lighting) that degraded RGB-based policies (Fig. 13B).
5. **Hardware Extension for Contact Sensing**: A prototype hardware extension utilized a compact keyboard with 2x3 keys mounted on a parallel gripper as a grid-based contact sensor (Fig. 14A). This was integrated by adding key states as contact observations to the real-world environment. A Diffusion Policy trained with this contact information exhibited re-grasping behavior when an object was forcibly removed (Fig. 14C), suggesting the policy leveraged contact measurements. This demonstrated the framework's support for rapid hardware prototyping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboManipBaselines represents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a unified and comprehensive software framework for imitation learning. Its systematic implementation of environment, dataset, and policy components, guided by principles of integration, generality, extensibility, and reproducibility, addresses existing challenges in the learning-based robotics ecosystem.

The framework's ability to support the entire imitation learning pipeline—from data collection and policy training to rollout—in a unified manner across diverse simulation and real-world environments is significant. This unification simplifies the development and validation cycle for researchers, allowing them to leverage the same codebase for different robots and policy types. The public release of datasets and the support for systematic benchmarking enhance the transparency and verifiability of research results within the community.

The benchmark evaluations provide comparative insights into various imitation learning policies and highlight the impact of factors like camera configurations and data volume on task success. The demonstrated research applications illustrate the framework's practical utility for diverse investigations, including:
- **Efficient Data Utilization**: Facilitating data augmentation strategies.
- **Multimodal Perception**: Enabling integration with advanced sensory modalities like tactile foundation models and comparative analysis of 3D sensing technologies.
- **Human-Robot Interaction**: Supporting the development of interactive robotic systems through natural language.
- **Hardware Innovation**: Allowing rapid prototyping and evaluation of novel hardware extensions, such as simple contact sensors.

The expanding user community, including industry collaborations, indicates that RoboManipBaselines is being adopted for practical applications beyond academic research. While acknowledging limitations such as computational resource demands, platform dependencies, and current scope restrictions, the authors plan to enhance interoperability with other frameworks and extend the system's capabilities. This initiative is positioned to accelerate research, experimentation, and ultimately, the deployment of advanced robotic manipulation capabilities across various domains.
