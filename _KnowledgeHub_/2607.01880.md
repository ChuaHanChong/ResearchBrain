# Learning the Supports for Categorical Critic in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel algorithm, Dynamic Support Endpoint Learning (DySEL), allows reinforcement learning agents to dynamically learn the lower and upper bounds of support intervals for categorical critics, moving beyond the limitations of fixed, pre-defined ranges. This approach, implemented with TD3, demonstrates competitive or superior performance on DeepMind Control Suite tasks, notably improving results on complex humanoid environments.

## Problem

- Existing categorical and histogram-based distributional RL methods require pre-defined, fixed support intervals for value functions.
- The actual range of returns in RL environments is often unknown a priori, can be highly non-stationary, and varies significantly during learning.
- Incorrectly chosen fixed supports lead to either truncation of valuable return information or dilution of representational resolution, hindering policy convergence.

## Method

- Introduces **Dynamic Support Endpoint Learning (DySEL)**, an algorithm to dynamically learn the lower and upper bounds ($[\nu_{min}, \nu_{max}]$) of categorical critics' support intervals.
- Reformulates the problem as a ==constrained min-max optimization==, leveraging a theoretical connection between HL-Gauss loss and the Mean-Squared Bellman Error — explicitly notes PopArt "does not naturally extend to the preservation of distributional shape in categorical critics."
- Uses a ==Lagrangian multiplier== to balance a 'width penalty' (encouraging narrower support) with a 'mass coverage' constraint (ensuring sufficient probability mass is captured).

## Results

- TD3+DySEL demonstrated performance competitive with or superior to vanilla TD3 and TD3+HL-Gauss on **11** DeepMind Control Suite continuous control tasks.
- Achieved substantial performance improvements on complex humanoid tasks (humanoid-run/stand), attributed to the finer granularity of dynamically learned support intervals.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the absolute width penalty and the mass coverage constraint are necessary for stable and effective dynamic support learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The mean-squared Bellman error is theoretically upper-bounded by the HL-Gauss loss, with the tightness of this bound directly related to the absolute values of the support interval.
> - An adversarial min-max formulation with a specific Lagrangian objective allows a neural network to simultaneously narrow the support for tighter bounds and expand it to cover leaked probability mass.
> - Both the absolute width penalty term and the mass coverage constraint are essential components to prevent degenerate support interval learning (either unbounded widening or insufficient narrowing).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) frequently relies on value functions to guide agent behavior. Historically, these functions have been learned via regression, typically minimizing the mean squared error (MSE) between predicted values and bootstrapped targets. A significant development in this area is Distributional Reinforcement Learning, which shifts focus from learning a scalar expected return to modeling the full probability distribution of returns. This paradigm often employs methods like quantile regression or categorical approximations.

More recently, approaches have emerged that reframe value estimation as a classification problem. The Gaussian Histogram Loss (HL-Gauss) is one such method, encoding scalar Bellman targets as Gaussian-smoothed categorical targets. While HL-Gauss has demonstrated benefits such as smoother gradients and scalability in some RL contexts, it shares a fundamental limitation with other histogram-based or categorical distributional RL methods: the necessity of defining a fixed support interval. This requirement poses challenges because the actual range of returns in RL environments is often unknown a priori, can be highly non-stationary, and may vary significantly throughout the learning process. An incorrectly chosen fixed support can lead to truncation of valuable return information or dilution of representational resolution, potentially hindering policy convergence.

This work directly addresses this limitation by proposing a mechanism to dynamically learn the support interval for categorical critics. It positions itself as an advancement within the classification-based value learning and distributional RL frameworks, aiming to enhance their applicability and performance by removing the reliance on a manually specified, static support. By enabling adaptive support intervals, this research seeks to improve the robustness and efficiency of these methods, particularly in complex and dynamic environments where return distributions can evolve substantially.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an algorithm that dynamically learns the lower and upper bounds of the support interval for categorical critics in reinforcement learning, thereby eliminating the need for a pre-defined, fixed interval. This objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of existing approaches:

**1. Addressing Limitations of Fixed Support Intervals:** Current histogram-based value learning methods, including HL-Gauss, necessitate a fixed support interval (e.g., [ν_min, ν_max]). In practical RL scenarios, the true range of expected returns is rarely known in advance. Environments characterized by sparse rewards, unbounded state spaces, or highly non-stationary target values (as the policy evolves) make pre-specification problematic. The return magnitudes induced by early exploratory policies can differ substantially from those induced by a final, converged policy, meaning no single fixed interval is optimal across the entire learning trajectory.

**2. Mitigating Trade-offs between Truncation and Resolution:**
 - **Narrow Support:** If the pre-defined support interval is too narrow, the target distribution will experience boundary truncation, discarding information about extreme returns. This truncation can lead to biased value estimates and potentially suboptimal policy convergence.
 - **Broad Support:** Conversely, an excessively broad support interval, with a fixed number of bins (k), dilutes the representational resolution of each bin. This reduction in granularity weakens the learning signal, making it difficult for the critic to discern subtle differences in value distributions.

**3. Theoretical Connection to Mean-Squared Bellman Error:** The authors establish a theoretical link demonstrating that the mean-squared Bellman error, a common objective in scalar Q-value regression, is upper-bounded by the HL-Gauss loss. Crucially, the tightness of this upper bound is directly correlated with the absolute values of the support interval. This theoretical insight provides a strong motivation: enforcing a narrower support interval leads to a theoretically tighter bound on the MSE, thus encouraging the development of an approach to actively seek the narrowest feasible interval while maintaining sufficient coverage of the target distribution. This objective aims to achieve a balance between maintaining high resolution (narrower support) and avoiding truncation (sufficient breadth).

**4. Enhancing Learning Stability and Performance:** By allowing the support interval to adapt dynamically, the research aims to foster more stable and efficient learning. An adaptive mechanism can better track the evolving return distributions during training, preventing issues arising from either severe truncation or overly diffuse representations. The proposed method seeks to achieve empirical performance that is competitive with or superior to fixed-support HL-Gauss baselines, particularly in tasks where the optimal return range changes significantly.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Dynamic Support Endpoint Learning (DySEL), addresses the challenges of fixed support intervals by dynamically learning the bounds [ν_min, ν_max] for categorical critics. The approach is built upon a theoretical analysis connecting the HL-Gauss loss to an upper bound of the mean-squared Bellman error and formulates the dynamic support learning as a constrained optimization problem.

**1. Theoretical Foundation (Upper Bound of MSE Bellman Error):**
The core of the methodology is based on the derivation that the mean-squared Bellman error (MSE_Bellman) is upper-bounded by a term that can be decomposed into three components (Equation 11):
- **Absolute Width Term:** `max(|ν_min|, |ν_max|)^2`, which is influenced by the magnitude of the support interval.
- **Distribution Matching Term:** Related to the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between the target distribution and the predicted distribution, which can be approximated by the cross-entropy loss.
- **Truncation Bias Term:** `(E_q_y[z] - (TQ)(s,a))^2`, representing the error incurred when target values fall outside the support interval.

This decomposition reveals an inherent adversarial tension: minimizing the absolute width term encourages a narrower support for a tighter theoretical bound, while minimizing the truncation bias term (by maximizing probability mass coverage) incentivizes the expansion of the support to fully encapsulate target returns.

**2. Reformulation as a Constrained Optimization Problem:**
To resolve this tension and achieve a tractable objective, the authors reformulate the problem into a constrained optimization.
- **Addressing the Product Term:** Directly minimizing the product of absolute width and cross-entropy (as implied by the upper bound) is known to be non-convex and potentially unstable. The AM-GM inequality `2√XY <= (X+Y)` is applied, introducing a positive scaling constant `α > 0` to transform `max(|ν_min|, |ν_max|)^2 * CE` into a sum: `α * max(|ν_min|, |ν_max|)^2 + (1/α) * CE`. This effectively introduces a "width penalty" term.
- **Addressing Truncation Bias:** The truncation bias is simplified and translated into minimizing the "leaked mass" (1 − Z), where Z is the covering mass (the integral of the truncated Gaussian PDF over the support interval). By minimizing `(1 - Z)`, the objective aims to maximize `Z`, ensuring sufficient coverage of the target distribution.

These transformations lead to the following constrained optimization problem (Equation 15):
`J(θ, ϕ) = α max(|ν_min|, |ν_max|) + (1/α) * Cross-Entropy`
subject to the truncation constraint: `(1 − Z) ≤ ϵ`
Here, `θ` represents the critic network parameters and `ϕ` represents the parameters of the support interval network.

**3. Min-Max Optimization Objective:**
The constrained optimization problem is then formulated as an adversarial min-max game using a Lagrangian multiplier `λ`. The final learning objective is given by the Lagrangian (Equation 16):
`L(θ, ϕ, λ) = α max(|ν_min|, |ν_max|) + (1/α) * Cross-Entropy + λ((1 − Z) − ϵ)`
- The parameters `θ` (critic network) and `ϕ` (support network) are trained to minimize `L`.
- The Lagrangian multiplier `λ` (treated as a trainable parameter) is trained to maximize `L`.

This min-max formulation allows the neural network to automatically expand the support interval to capture leaked probability mass (when `(1 − Z) > ϵ`) while simultaneously penalizing excessive width (`α max(|ν_min|, |ν_max|)`) to maintain a tight theoretical bound and high resolution. The hyperparameter `α` controls the trade-off between the width penalty and the cross-entropy loss. The hyperparameter `ϵ` defines the maximum allowed leaked mass. The number of bins `k` and the Gaussian standard deviation `σ` (derived from a sigma-to-width ratio) are kept fixed.

**4. Experimental Setup:**
- **Baselines:** The proposed DySEL algorithm is built on top of TD3, and compared against vanilla TD3 and TD3 with fixed-support HL-Gauss (TD3+HLG).
- **Hyperparameters:** For TD3+HLG, a fixed support interval of `[-100, 100]` and `k = 128` bins are used. For DySEL, the initial support interval is set to `[-10, 10]`, `k = 128`, sigma-to-width ratio of `0.75`, and `ϵ = 0.005`. The `α` parameter is tuned per task via a grid search.
- **Benchmark:** Evaluation is conducted on 11 continuous control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Inter-Quartile Mean (IQM) returns over 10 random seeds, along with 95% bootstrapped confidence intervals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of DySEL on DeepMind Control Suite tasks yielded several findings regarding its performance, adaptiveness, and the importance of its objective components.

**1. Performance Against Baselines:**
- **Overall Competitiveness:** TD3+DySEL demonstrated performance generally competitive with or superior to TD3 and TD3+HLG across the evaluated continuous control tasks.
- **Significant Gains on Humanoid Tasks:** DySEL showed substantial performance improvements on humanoid tasks (e.g., humanoid-run, humanoid-stand). This is hypothesized to be due to the finer granularity enabled by the dynamically learned support interval, which allows the critic to capture subtle variations in returns that might be obscured by fixed, pre-defined supports.
- **Tasks with No Improvement:** On tasks such as `finger-turn_hard` and `hopper-stand`, both HL-Gauss-based methods (TD3+HLG and TD3+DySEL) either underperformed or showed no significant improvement over vanilla TD3. Analysis suggested that this underperformance might be linked to the cross-entropy objective itself rather than the dynamic support learning mechanism, as both HL-Gauss variants exhibited similar behavior.

**2. Dynamics of Learnt Support Intervals:**
- **Task-Dependent Adaptation:** The evolution of the support interval was highly task-dependent. Two main patterns were observed:
 - **Stable Convergence:** For some tasks (e.g., `finger-turn_hard`, `hopper` tasks), the support interval quickly converged to a stable value and remained relatively unchanged throughout training.
 - **Gradual Expansion:** For other tasks (e.g., `cheetah-run`, `fish-swim`, most `humanoid` and `quadruped` tasks), the support interval gradually increased as training progressed, adapting to potentially larger return magnitudes.
- **Symmetry:** The learned support interval tended to be almost symmetric around zero.
- **Influence of `α`:** The hyperparameter `α`, which balances the width penalty against the cross-entropy loss, was found to be crucial. A larger `α` generally favored a narrower support interval initially, while a smaller `α` allowed for greater expansion. This parameter enabled a dynamic scheduling of support interval width without requiring manual pre-specification.

**3. Efficacy of Learnt Supports for Fixed-Support Baselines:**
- When the final support interval learned by DySEL was used as a fixed reference for vanilla HL-Gauss, performance varied. For tasks where DySEL's support interval remained stable, using this learned fixed support improved HL-Gauss performance (e.g., `finger-turn_hard`, `humanoid-run`).
- However, for tasks where the support interval exhibited gradual expansion (e.g., `fish-swim`, `humanoid-stand`), DySEL still outperformed or remained competitive with HL-Gauss using the "learned fixed" support. This indicates that the continuous adaptation throughout training, rather than just identifying an optimal final interval, is a key benefit of DySEL.

**4. Importance of Objective Components (Ablation Study):**
An ablation study confirmed the critical role of both the absolute width penalty and the mass coverage constraint in the proposed objective (Equation 16):
- **Removal of Absolute Width Penalty:** Without the width penalty, the algorithm diverged. The support interval expanded to exceedingly large values, indicating that the cross-entropy loss "cheated" by artificially minimizing its objective through unbounded expansion of the support, rather than genuinely matching the target distribution. This suggests that the width penalty is essential to prevent degenerate widening.
- **Omission of Mass Constraint:** Without the Lagrangian penalty enforcing sufficient mass coverage, the absolute width penalty often overpowered other terms. For tasks requiring support expansion, this led to a support interval that was too narrow to cover the full target distribution, resulting in insufficient probability mass coverage and potentially unstable learning. This highlights that the mass constraint is vital to prevent degenerate narrowing.
Both components are therefore necessary for stable and effective learning of dynamic support intervals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning by addressing a fundamental limitation in classification-based value learning and distributional RL: the reliance on a pre-defined, fixed support interval. The development of DySEL (Dynamic Support Endpoint Learning) provides a principled and empirically validated mechanism for agents to dynamically adapt their value function representation.

**Key Significance:**
- **Elimination of Hyperparameter Tuning Burden:** DySEL substantially reduces the burden of hyperparameter tuning associated with manually selecting appropriate support bounds. This is particularly relevant in complex or novel environments where the range of returns is unknown or highly variable.
- **Improved Robustness and Adaptability:** By allowing the support interval to evolve with the policy, DySEL enhances the robustness of categorical critics to non-stationary and stochastic return distributions. This adaptability prevents issues like truncation of critical return information or loss of resolution, which can plague fixed-support methods.
- **Enhanced Performance:** The demonstrated performance improvements on challenging continuous control tasks, especially humanoid environments, indicate that dynamic support learning can lead to more accurate value estimates and subsequently better policy performance.
- **Theoretical Foundation:** The derivation connecting the mean-squared Bellman error to the HL-Gauss loss and the subsequent formulation as a constrained min-max optimization problem provide a solid theoretical basis for the proposed approach. This theoretical grounding offers insights into the trade-offs involved in defining value function supports.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Broader Adoption of Categorical Critics:** By simplifying the setup and improving the reliability of categorical critics, DySEL could encourage their broader adoption in a wider range of RL applications, particularly those with highly variable or unknown return scales.
- **Advancements in Distributional RL:** This work contributes to the ongoing efforts in distributional RL to develop more expressive and robust representations of return distributions, moving beyond scalar expectations. It complements other approaches like quantile regression by making categorical methods more practical.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the sensitivity to the dual learning rate and the symmetric nature of learned supports, open avenues for future research. Exploring techniques like PID-controlled Lagrangian updates, asymmetric support learning, or adaptive Gaussian sigma parameters could further refine and extend the capabilities of dynamic support learning.
- **Practical Applications:** In real-world robotic control, financial modeling, or game AI, where rewards can be sparse, delayed, or highly variable, an RL agent capable of adaptively scaling its value representation could lead to more stable and effective learning.
