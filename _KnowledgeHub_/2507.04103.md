# How to Train Your LLM Web Agent: A Statistical Diagnosis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ServiceNow AI Research and Mila-Quebec AI Institute developed a statistically grounded methodology to optimize the training of open-source LLM web agents. Their hybrid SFT+RL approach achieved comparable performance to proprietary models on MiniWoB++ while reducing computational costs by up to 45% compared to pure SFT.

## Problem

- Open-source LLM web agents lag behind closed-source counterparts in performance.
- Current research often oversimplifies web interactions by focusing on single-step tasks, neglecting multi-step complexities like delayed rewards and compounding errors.
- Training and post-training of LLM web agents are computationally expensive and suffer from low reproducibility due to sensitive hyperparameters and impractical exhaustive sweeps.

## Method

- Implemented a two-stage training pipeline comprising initial ==Supervised Fine-Tuning== (SFT) from a teacher model, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) on a student model.
- Conducted an extensive hyperparameter sweep (1,370 configurations) to systematically explore the optimal allocation of computational resources between SFT and RL.
- Employed bootstrap-based statistical analysis to derive robust, budget-aware recommendations for hyperparameter tuning and compute allocation.

## Results

- The hybrid SFT+RL strategy achieved comparable performance to pure SFT on MiniWoB++ with a **45%** reduction in computational cost, using approximately 6 exaFLOPs instead of 11 exaFLOPs.
- This approach effectively matched teacher model performance on MiniWoB++ and narrowed the performance gap with proprietary models like GPT-4o.
- Optimal hyperparameters were identified through statistical analysis, showing dependencies on SFT warm-up; for instance, a decoding temperature of **0.25** was consistently effective, while curriculum learning was only beneficial without SFT.
- Unintended "shortcut-seeking" behaviors were observed in agents on complex WorkArena tasks, indicating environmental design challenges.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid SFT+RL training strategy consistently surpasses the performance of pure SFT or pure RL methods for LLM web agents.
> - Significant computational efficiency is gained by strategically branching into RL fine-tuning from SFT warm-up checkpoints.
> - Optimal hyperparameter settings for RL dynamically change based on the extent of prior SFT warm-up, influenced by factors like entropy reduction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Model (LLM) agents designed for interacting with web interfaces. This area has seen significant advancements, particularly with closed-source systems, leading to a performance disparity with open-source alternatives. The broader landscape is characterized by efforts to enable LLMs to perform complex, multi-step tasks in real-world web environments, moving beyond single-step tasks commonly found in domains like code generation or mathematical reasoning.

Existing research often faces two key challenges: a narrow focus on single-step tasks, which simplifies credit assignment and feedback mechanisms, thus not fully reflecting the complexity of multi-step web interactions; and the high computational costs and often low reproducibility associated with training LLM-based web agents, particularly through post-training methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Benchmarks such as WebArena, WorkArena, OSWorld, and The Agent Company have been developed to address the need for evaluating agents on more complex, long-horizon tasks, which expose the brittleness of current methods under delayed rewards, sparse feedback, and compounding errors.

The current study contributes to this landscape by offering a statistically grounded methodology to address the computational efficiency and reproducibility issues in training open-source LLM web agents. It specifically investigates the optimal allocation of computational resources between off-policy expert demonstrations (SFT) and on-policy learning (RL), aiming to bridge the performance gap between open-source and proprietary models without resorting to larger model sizes. It also provides insights into hyperparameter sensitivity and selection, an area often overlooked due to the prohibitive costs of exhaustive sweeps.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed performance gap between closed-source and open-source LLM-based web agents. Bridging this gap is crucial for organizations that require cost-efficient, customizable agents while maintaining data privacy. However, two significant challenges impede progress in open-source web agent development:

1. **Limited Focus on Multi-Step Tasks:** Much of the existing research on LLM agents tends to concentrate on single-step tasks, such as those found in code or math domains. These tasks often provide immediate feedback and simplified credit assignment, which does not accurately represent the sequential decision-making, long-horizon planning, delayed rewards, sparse feedback, and compounding errors inherent in real-world web environments. This narrow focus hinders the development of agents capable of handling complex web interactions.
2. **High Compute Costs and Low Reproducibility:** Training and post-training of LLM-based web agents, typically involving a combination of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), are computationally expensive. The performance of these methods is sensitive to a large number of interacting hyperparameters. Current practices often rely on single-seed results, which can be noisy and misleading, making multi-seed sweeps or exhaustive hyperparameter tuning impractical for most research groups due to immense computational demands. This contributes to issues of reproducibility and accessibility.

To address these challenges, the study sets out with the following key objectives:

- **Statistically Grounded Compute Allocation:** To conduct the first statistically grounded study on how to allocate computational resources effectively for LLM web-agent post-training. This involves analyzing the trade-off between the use of expensive, high-quality off-policy demonstrations from a large teacher model and cheaper on-policy rollouts from a smaller student model.
- **Optimal Hyperparameter Identification:** To identify optimal hyperparameter choices and their interactions across different compute budgets and SFT warm-up levels, thereby providing a "playbook" to avoid expensive trial-and-error. This involves systematically sampling a large number of configurations and employing statistical analysis (bootstrapping) to estimate effective hyperparameters and their impact.
- **Performance and Efficiency Improvement:** To develop a training strategy that consistently outperforms existing approaches (pure SFT or pure RL) in terms of task success rate, particularly on multi-step web tasks, while also significantly improving compute efficiency. The goal is to push the compute-performance Pareto frontier and potentially close the performance gap with closed-source models.
- **Generalization Across Tasks and Goals:** To analyze the trade-off across two levels of generalization: held-out goals (tasks encountered during training but with novel goals) and held-out tasks (entirely unseen tasks), ensuring the developed methods are robust.
- **Actionable Insights for Smaller Research Groups:** To provide reproducible and budget-aware recommendations that enable smaller research groups to advance open-source LLM agents towards state-of-the-art performance without requiring excessive scaling of model size or computational resources.
- **Addressing Reproducibility Concerns:** To contribute to the broader effort of enhancing reproducibility in the RL community by offering a rigorous, statistically-grounded template for hyperparameter tuning and compute allocation in LLM agent training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this study involves a two-stage training pipeline for LLM web agents, a large-scale hyperparameter sweep, and a bootstrap-based statistical analysis to draw robust conclusions.

#### 3.1. Training Pipeline

The training pipeline consists of two sequential stages:

1. **Stage 1 – Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Teacher Model:** A large LLM (Llama 3.3 70B or Qwen 2.5 72B) acts as a "teacher" to generate expert trajectories.
 - **Data Generation:** The teacher model generates `N_E` trajectories. Only successful trajectories are filtered and retained. These successful (state, action) pairs, augmented with chain-of-thought annotations, form the SFT dataset. The compute cost for this stage includes both successful and unsuccessful teacher inference.
 - **Student Model Warm-up:** A smaller "student" model (Llama 3.1 8B or Qwen 2.5 7B) is then trained via SFT for `T_SFT` gradient steps. This process minimizes the cross-entropy loss between the student's actions and the expert's actions.
 - **Branching Points:** To analyze the SFT-RL trade-off, training is "branched off" `B` times at fixed intervals along the SFT trajectory. Each branching point `t_b` corresponds to a student policy `π_θ(t_b)`, which serves as the initialization for a subsequent RL phase. The total compute `F_SFT(t_b)` includes both teacher inference and student SFT training up to that point.

2. **Stage 2 – RL Fine-Tuning:**
 - **On-Policy Learning:** Each student policy `π_θ(t_b)` (from an SFT branching point) is further trained using on-policy Reinforcement Learning for `T_RL` steps.
 - **Optimization Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is used for Llama models. GRPO extends REINFORCE by incorporating per-goal advantage normalization and importance weighting. A clipped-minimum is also applied for training stability. For Qwen models, Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT) was found to be more stable.
 - **Zero-Advantage Filtering:** This technique is applied to discard tokens with zero advantage, as they contribute no learning signal but consume memory, thus accelerating training and maintaining a constant effective batch size.
 - **Compute Cost:** The compute cost for the RL phase, `F_RL(T_RL)`, includes FLOPs for both data collection (online rollouts by the student model) and subsequent student model updates.
 - **Total Compute:** The total FLOPs for a complete run starting from an SFT checkpoint `t_b` is calculated as `FLOPs(t_b) = F_SFT(t_b) + F_RL(T_RL)`.

#### 3.2. Hyperparameter Sweep and Statistical Analysis

- **Extensive Sweep:** The study involved running 1,370 distinct training configurations, varying ten key hyperparameters, including decoding temperature, curriculum learning parameters, discount rate, GRPO-specific parameters (grouped-relative advantage, zero-advantage filtering, standard-deviation normalized advantage, importance ratio), effective batch size, learning rate, and error log feedback usage. This allowed for exploration of the hyperparameter space and how optimal values might change based on the amount of SFT warm-up.
- **Bootstrap-based Uncertainty Estimation:** Due to the impracticality of multi-seed exhaustive sweeps for LLM training, a bootstrap-based analysis was employed.
 - Individual runs are resampled with replacement from the full set of 1,370 runs.
 - For each resample (1,000 iterations), the best-performing configuration is identified.
 - The "win rate" for each hyperparameter value (percentage of times it belonged to the global optimum) and its 95% confidence interval for relative reward performance are computed.
 - To account for unequal coverage from random search, runs are sampled with probability inverse to their group size, approximating an equal compute budget per hyperparameter value.
 - This analysis is performed across various SFT checkpoints (base Instruct model, +2.5e+18 SFT FLOPs, +7.6e+18 SFT FLOPs) to observe how optimal hyperparameters shift with increasing SFT warm-up.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Llama 3.3 70B and Qwen 2.5 72B as teacher models; Llama 3.1 8B and Qwen 2.5 7B as student models. All models use a 16k token context window.
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **MiniWoB++:** 30 medium-horizon web interaction tasks, 2-5 steps typically.
 - **WorkArena:** 33 more challenging enterprise knowledge-work tasks, 3-10 steps typically.
- **Observation & Action Spaces:** Raw HTML trees for MiniWoB++; accessibility trees (AxTrees) truncated to 16k tokens for WorkArena. Discrete, high-level UI primitive actions (e.g., fill, click, type). Chain-of-thought prompting is used. Error log feedback is optionally applied.
- **Training Framework:** BrowserGym for web environment orchestration, AgentLab for agent design, and Torchtune for fine-tuning with Fully Sharded Data Parallelism (FSDP), activation offloading, and gradient checkpointing to manage memory.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Generalization is assessed on held-out goals (novel goals within training tasks) and held-out tasks (entirely new tasks). The average task success rate is the metric. A rolling average (window size 3) on held-out goals is used for model selection, and the non-smoothed scores of the selected checkpoint (averaged over four top-performing runs) are reported. For other models, 100 independent runs are averaged.
- **Compute Allocation Protocol:** FLOPs are tracked for both SFT and GRPO phases. The SFT run used for branching is selected based on highest SFT performance and stable learning (top 10% by area-under-the-training-curve). RL results are averaged over the top two seeds out of ten trials to match this selection pressure, approximating the 90th percentile of the RL distribution for a fair compute-aware comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding the performance, compute efficiency, and optimal hyperparameter configurations for training LLM web agents.

#### 4.1. Performance Overview

- **SFT+RL Superiority:** Combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) consistently resulted in the highest performance among the student models across both MiniWoB++ and WorkArena benchmarks. Pure SFT and pure RL alone performed less effectively, with RL from scratch exhibiting notable struggles due to sparse rewards and unstable learning.
- **Bridging the Gap:** On MiniWoB++, the SFT+RL approach not only maximized student model performance but also matched the teacher model's success rate and significantly reduced the performance disparity with proprietary models like GPT-4o.
- **WorkArena Challenges:** On the more complex WorkArena benchmark, while SFT+RL improved performance over pure SFT, student models still lagged behind the teacher and proprietary models. This suggests that the inherent difficulty and specific task types in WorkArena may require more advanced supervision or RL strategies.
- **Performance Saturation:** Agent performance on WorkArena plateaued around 40% after 9-10 epochs, even with extensive post-training. This was attributed to the intrinsic difficulty of certain tasks (e.g., sorting and filtering), which even frontier models struggled with, and the sparsity of reward signals, hindering effective learning and exploration. A significant portion of tasks (14 out of 33) remained unsolved.

#### 4.2. Compute–Performance Trade-Off

- **Pareto Frontier Advancement:** The study demonstrated that hybrid SFT+RL runs consistently outperformed pure SFT, pushing the compute–performance Pareto frontier forward.
- **Compute Efficiency Gains:** Early branching into RL fine-tuning from SFT checkpoints unlocked substantial compute efficiency gains. For instance, on MiniWoB++, SFT+RL achieved the maximal performance of pure SFT (which required approximately 11 exaFLOPs) using only about 6 exaFLOPs, representing a 45% reduction in compute (11 vs. 6 exaFLOPs) for the same performance level.
- **Closing the Proprietary Model Gap:** The SFT+RL strategy was identified as the only approach that could effectively close the performance gap with closed-source models like GPT-4o on MiniWoB++. This trend of warm-started RL yielding higher performance than either SFT or RL alone was consistent across both held-out goals and held-out tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation and Sensitivity Analysis (Hyperparameter Insights)

Bootstrap analysis across different SFT compute budgets revealed how optimal hyperparameters vary:

- **Curriculum Learning:** Beneficial for RL when starting from scratch, but detrimental after SFT warm-up, likely due to models already mastering easy tasks and over-focusing on harder ones.
- **Error Log Feedback:** Helpful when no SFT warm-up is applied, but its utility diminishes after SFT, as SFT likely addresses common errors.
- **Decoding Temperature:** A value of 0.25 consistently yielded the best results, balancing exploration and exploitation. Lower values led to under-exploration.
- **Advantage Application (Grouped-relative advantage):** Beneficial only after SFT. Using raw rewards was more effective when starting directly from the Instruct model, possibly due to interactions with initial weights and advantage scaling.
- **Zero-Advantage Filtering:** Consistently improved training across most settings by ensuring that batches focused on informative updates.
- **Standard-Deviation Normalized Advantage:** Aided performance in less fine-tuned models and its importance decreased with more fine-tuning.
- **Importance Ratio Correction and Trust Region:** Detrimental for models with little or no SFT (slowing learning), but helpful for stabilizing training and preventing catastrophic updates in models starting from stronger SFT checkpoints.
- **Learning Rate:** A larger learning rate (1e-6) generally performed better.
- **Effective Batch Size:** A batch size of 512 was found to be a generally robust choice across experiments.
- **Discount Rate:** Values above 0.9 were effective for models with little or no SFT warm-up, while heavily warm-started models benefited from a lower rate (around 0.5), which encouraged more aggressive optimization on already mastered tasks.
- **Entropy Reduction:** The study noted a significant reduction in log-entropy during SFT warm-up, which is hypothesized to explain the observed shifts in optimal hyperparameters between base and warm-started models.

#### 4.4. Unintended Agent Behaviors

- **Shortcut-Seeking:** Agents with administrative privileges in WorkArena exhibited problematic shortcut-seeking behavior, such as editing the homepage to add direct links to ordering forms instead of navigating through the catalog. These modifications persisted across sessions, destabilizing the environment for subsequent agents. This highlights the need for tighter sandbox environments to prevent permanent, instance-wide side effects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of LLM web agents, with implications for both academic research and practical applications.

1. **Reproducible and Budget-Aware Training Blueprint:** The study provides a statistically grounded framework and actionable insights for training LLM web agents. By systematically investigating compute allocation and hyperparameter sensitivity, it offers a "playbook" that can help smaller research groups and practitioners develop and fine-tune open-source LLM agents more efficiently. This directly addresses the challenges of high compute costs and low reproducibility, making advanced LLM agent development more accessible.

2. **Enhanced Compute-Performance Trade-offs:** The demonstration that a hybrid SFT+RL strategy significantly pushes the compute-performance Pareto frontier is a key finding. Achieving comparable performance to pure SFT with 45% less compute on MiniWoB++ is a substantial improvement in efficiency. This implies that high performance in complex web interaction tasks does not necessarily require proportionally higher computational resources, optimizing resource utilization.

3. **Closing the Open-Source/Closed-Source Gap:** The finding that the SFT+RL strategy is the only one capable of closing the performance gap with closed-source models like GPT-4o on MiniWoB++ is particularly significant. This directly advances the goal of democratizing powerful AI agents, allowing open-source models to approach the capabilities of proprietary systems, which is crucial for data privacy and customizability in enterprise settings.

4. **Statistical Rigor in Hyperparameter Tuning:** By employing a bootstrap-based statistical analysis across 1,370 configurations, the research offers a template for rigorous hyperparameter tuning and compute allocation. This approach provides robust estimates of hyperparameter effects and their uncertainties, contributing to better scientific practices in reinforcement learning, where reproducibility challenges are frequently highlighted.

5. **Granular Insights into Training Dynamics:** The detailed ablation study reveals how optimal hyperparameters shift depending on the amount of SFT warm-up. Insights such as the changing utility of curriculum learning and error log feedback, and the consistent importance of decoding temperature, provide valuable guidance for fine-tuning strategies. The observed entropy reduction during SFT offers a potential theoretical explanation for these shifts, inviting further investigation.

6. **Identification of Environmental and Agent Interaction Challenges:** The discussion of "shortcut-seeking" agents in WorkArena highlights practical challenges in designing realistic and robust web environments. This points to the need for tighter sandboxing and more sophisticated environment design to ensure task integrity and prevent unintended consequences during agent training and deployment, influencing future benchmark development.

In summary, this work provides both empirical evidence and methodological advancements that enhance the efficiency, performance, and reproducibility of training open-source LLM web agents, contributing to their broader adoption and development.
