# Dealing with Synthetic Data Contamination in Online Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A study investigates the impact of synthetic data contamination on online continual learning (CL) performance, proposing ESRM to maintain model robustness. The method, which uses entropy-based memory selection and real-synthetic similarity maximization, significantly improves accuracy in contaminated online CL environments compared to baseline methods, even at high contamination ratios.

## Problem

- The proliferation of AI-generated content on the internet contaminates datasets, leading to performance degradation, bias, and reduced diversity in machine learning models.
- Online Continual Learning (CL) is particularly vulnerable to this contamination because models process data sequentially in a single pass, hindering comprehensive data quality assessment.
- Existing online CL methods, primarily replay-based, lack strategies to effectively deal with synthetic data in their memory buffers, making them susceptible to catastrophic forgetting and overall performance decline when fed contaminated streams.

## Method

- Developed ==Entropy Selection (ES)== to manage the replay buffer, prioritizing samples with higher ==entropy== to store more realistic data and actively reduce synthetic content.
- Introduced ==Real-synthetic similarity Maximization (RM)==, a ==contrastive loss== that aligns the ==feature embeddings== of presumed real and synthetic samples to bridge their feature gap.
- Combined ES and RM with ==self-distillation== into a novel replay-based online CL method, **ESRM**, designed to perform robustly in streams with high synthetic data contamination.

## Results

- ESRM consistently demonstrated superior robustness against synthetic data contamination, outperforming six state-of-the-art online CL baselines across all tested datasets and contamination ratios.
- For example, on ImageNet-100/SDXL with **95%** contamination, ESRM's average accuracy drop was limited to **6.86 percentage points**, while baselines often experienced drops exceeding **20 points**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Entropy Selection for memory management and Real-synthetic similarity Maximization loss terms independently and synergistically contributed to ESRM's improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthetic data contamination severely degrades the average accuracy, plasticity, and stability of current online Continual Learning methods.
> - Synthetic data can be characterized by lower entropy distributions and distinct, often better-clustered, feature representations compared to real data.
> - Prioritizing real data in the memory buffer using cues like entropy distribution is crucial for mitigating the negative effects of contamination on continual learners.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advent of high-fidelity image generation models, particularly diffusion-based architectures, has significantly increased the volume of AI-generated content available online. While these models demonstrate remarkable capabilities, their widespread use introduces a challenge for the machine learning community: the contamination of datasets collected from the internet with synthetic images. Previous research has indicated that training machine learning models on such contaminated datasets can lead to performance degradation, bias amplification, and a reduction in data diversity in non-continual learning settings.

This paper addresses this emerging issue by specifically investigating its implications for **Online Continual Learning (CL)**. Continual learning, by definition, involves learning from a continuous stream of data without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. Online CL represents a particularly challenging scenario within this paradigm, as models process data in a single pass, making it difficult to assess data quality or implement sophisticated contamination detection and mitigation strategies. The existing landscape of online CL methods primarily relies on replay-based strategies, which store a small subset of past data in a memory buffer to alleviate catastrophic forgetting. This work positions itself at the intersection of generative AI's impact on data integrity and the robust development of continual learning systems, highlighting a novel vulnerability for online CL and proposing a solution within the established replay-based framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing prevalence of AI-generated images on the internet and the potential negative consequences for data-driven machine learning. As generative models become more sophisticated, distinguishing synthetic content from real content becomes increasingly difficult for human observers and automated systems alike. This trend poses a significant threat to the integrity of future datasets collected from the internet, which have historically been a critical resource for training deep learning models in computer vision.

The paper identifies online continual learning as a domain particularly susceptible to this data contamination. In online CL, models encounter data sequentially and process each sample typically only once. This characteristic makes it impractical to perform comprehensive data validation or filtering at the point of ingestion, leaving models vulnerable to any synthetic content present in the data stream. The degradation observed in prior studies using contaminated static datasets motivates a specific investigation into the more dynamic and constrained online CL setting.

The key objectives of this study are threefold:

- **Investigate the Impact:** To experimentally determine how synthetic data contamination affects the performance of existing, representative online Continual Learning methods. This involves quantifying the degree of performance degradation as the contamination ratio increases.
- **Characterize Synthetic Data Properties:** To empirically observe and document specific characteristics of synthetic data within the context of continual learning. The researchers aim to identify distinct properties, such as entropy distribution and feature space representation, that differentiate synthetic data from real data when processed by continual learners. These observations are intended to inform the design of effective mitigation strategies.
- **Propose a Mitigation Method:** To develop and evaluate a novel method designed to alleviate the performance deterioration caused by synthetic images in online CL. This method should aim to maintain satisfactory learning performance even in scenarios with high levels of data contamination.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

To achieve its objectives, the research adopted a systematic approach encompassing simulated data contamination, empirical observation, and the development of a new replay-based method.

#### 3.1. Synthetic Dataset Generation and Contamination Simulation

To simulate contaminated datasets, the researchers utilized five diffusion-based generative models: Stable Diffusion XL, Stable Diffusion v1.4, Stable Diffusion v2.1, VQDM, and GLIDE. These models were used to create "synthetic twin" datasets corresponding to four benchmark datasets commonly used in online CL: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, and ImageNet-100. Synthetic images were generated using simple, class-guided prompts (e.g., "an image of a helicopter") and resized to match the original dataset dimensions.
Two experimental settings for synthetic data generation were established:
- **SDXL-only contamination:** All synthetic images for a given dataset were generated exclusively by Stable Diffusion XL.
- **Mixed contamination:** Synthetic images were generated by an equal contribution (20% each) from the five generative models.
Contamination was simulated by replacing a specific portion `P` (the contamination ratio) of the original dataset's images with their synthetic counterparts. This class-wise and source-wise proportional replacement ensured consistent contamination levels.

#### 3.2. Empirical Observations of Synthetic Data Properties

Prior to method development, the study conducted experiments with existing online CL methods on contaminated datasets to derive insights:
- **Observation 1 (Performance Degradation):** Experiments demonstrated that as the contamination ratio `P` increased, the performance of all tested online CL methods significantly decreased.
- **Observation 2 (Memory Buffer Importance):** By providing synthetic status information to a baseline method (ER), it was shown that storing only real data in the memory buffer could maintain performance comparable to clean data scenarios, even at high contamination ratios. This highlighted the critical role of the memory buffer's content quality.
- **Observation 3 (Lower Entropy Distribution):** Analysis of entropy distributions showed that synthetic data samples, when processed by continual learners, tended to exhibit lower entropy compared to real data samples. Synthetic samples often presented a peak in entropy close to zero.
- **Observation 4 (Feature Gap):** t-SNE visualizations of feature embeddings revealed that synthetic samples were often better clustered than real data. This clustering indicated limited diversity and led to a misalignment between the embeddings of synthetic and real samples, potentially contributing to performance deterioration on real test data.

#### 3.3. Proposed Method: Entropy Selection with Real-synthetic similarity Maximization (ESRM)

Guided by these observations, the researchers proposed ESRM, a replay-based method designed to mitigate synthetic data contamination. ESRM integrates two primary components:
- **Entropy Selection (ES):** This memory management strategy, motivated by Observations 2 and 3, aims to prioritize the storage of more "realistic" samples in the replay buffer. Upon receiving a new batch of data, ES discards 50% of the samples with the lowest entropy. For the remaining samples, if the buffer is not full, they are added. If the buffer is full, Reservoir Sampling determines if an incoming sample replaces an existing one. If a replacement occurs, ES selects a sample from the buffer of the same class as the incoming sample that has the lowest entropy, thus replacing potentially less "real" or less diverse examples. Entropy values in the buffer are updated after each task.
- **Real-synthetic similarity Maximization (RM):** This component addresses the feature gap (Observation 4) using a contrastive learning approach. The continual learner incorporates a feature extractor (`f`), a projection head (`g`), and a classifier (`ϕ`). For each incoming batch, RM splits it into two groups: `X_new+` (50% highest entropy, presumed more real) and `X_new-` (50% lowest entropy, presumed more synthetic). The RM loss (`L_RM`) is designed to maximize the cosine similarity between samples in `X_new+` and `X_new-` of the same class, effectively bridging their embedding space. Additionally, `L_RM` promotes alignment between the current stream data (`X_new`) and data in the memory buffer (`X_mem`).
- **Self-Distillation (L_SDC):** Following established practice in replay-based CL, ESRM employs a self-distillation technique to mitigate overconfidence in the model's predictions, using Kullback-Leibler divergence between predictions on augmented and non-augmented data.
- **Total Loss:** The overall training loss for ESRM (`L_ESRM`) is a weighted sum of the standard cross-entropy loss (`L_CE`), the self-distillation loss (`L_SDC`), and the Real-synthetic similarity Maximization loss (`L_RM`).

#### 3.4. Experiment Setup

- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet, and ImageNet-100, with classes split into non-overlapping tasks. A Domain-Incremental Learning (DIL) scenario was also tested on CIFAR-100's coarse labels.
- **Baselines:** ESRM was compared against six state-of-the-art online CL methods: ER, DER++, ERACE, OCM, GSA, and OnPro.
- **Architecture:** A ResNet-18 (full-width, not pre-trained) was used as the backbone for all experiments to ensure fair comparison.
- **Hyperparameters:** Hyperparameters for all methods were determined through a search on the clean CIFAR-100 dataset (memory size 5,000) and then applied consistently across other settings. Specific attention was given to DER++ on TinyImageNet due to performance issues with default optimizers.
- **Buffer Sizes:** Memory buffer sizes varied by dataset: 1,000 for CIFAR-10, 5,000 for CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100, and 10,000 for TinyImageNet.
- **Metrics:** Performance was primarily evaluated using Final Average Accuracy. Learning Accuracy (LA) and Relative Forgetting (RF) were used to assess plasticity and stability, respectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation yielded several key findings regarding synthetic data contamination and the efficacy of ESRM:

- **Significant Performance Degradation in Baselines:** All six baseline online Continual Learning methods (ER, DER++, ERACE, OCM, GSA, OnPro) experienced a notable decline in average accuracy when trained on datasets contaminated with synthetic images. This degradation became increasingly pronounced as the contamination ratio (P) increased, particularly for P ≥ 70%. For instance, on CIFAR-100 with P=95%, some baselines showed accuracy drops exceeding 20 percentage points.

- **ESRM's Robustness to Contamination:** ESRM demonstrated significantly higher robustness against synthetic data contamination compared to all baseline methods. Its performance degradation was consistently lower across all tested datasets and contamination ratios. In several instances, particularly with severe contamination (e.g., P=90% or 95% on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100), ESRM maintained a substantial level of accuracy, while most baselines yielded unsatisfactory results. For example, on ImageNet-100/SDXL with P=95%, ESRM's accuracy drop was 6.86 percentage points, whereas some baselines experienced drops over 20 points.

- **Improved Plasticity and Stability with ESRM:** The study found that synthetic data contamination negatively impacted both the plasticity (measured by Learning Accuracy) and stability (measured by Relative Forgetting) of baseline models. ESRM alleviated these issues, exhibiting higher learning accuracy and better control over forgetting in contaminated environments. The results suggested that ESRM achieved a favorable trade-off, implicitly boosting plasticity while managing stability degradation through the integrated RM loss and self-distillation.

- **Effectiveness in Domain-Incremental Learning:** Beyond the primary Class-Incremental Learning (CIL) setting, ESRM also demonstrated robust performance in Domain-Incremental Learning (DIL) scenarios. Tested on a DIL-CIFAR20 dataset, ESRM showed minimal performance degradation even with increasing contamination, validating its applicability beyond CIL.

- **Ablation Study Results:**
 - **Entropy Selection (ES) Efficacy:** When comparing different memory strategies on C100/SDXL, ES significantly outperformed random sampling. Storing "Real Only" (an idealized scenario with perfect knowledge of synthetic status) achieved the highest accuracy, with ES closely trailing and outperforming random sampling by a clear margin, confirming the benefit of entropy-based selection for buffer management. The memory buffer dynamics further illustrated that ES actively reduced the proportion of synthetic data over training.
 - **Contribution of Loss Terms:** Ablation studies on ESRM's loss functions (L_SDC and L_RM) confirmed that both self-distillation and real-synthetic similarity maximization individually contributed to improved average accuracy. Their combined application yielded the best performance, indicating their complementary roles in mitigating contamination effects.

- **Alleviation of Feature Misalignment:** t-SNE visualizations of memory data revealed that ESRM, specifically through its RM component, effectively aligned the feature embeddings of synthetic and real samples. This contrasted with baseline methods, where a clear misalignment and superior clustering of synthetic data were observed, which was a contributing factor to performance degradation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of continual learning by systematically investigating and addressing a nascent, yet significant, challenge posed by the proliferation of AI-generated content. The increasing inability to discern synthetic images from real ones implies that future datasets curated from the internet are likely to be contaminated. This paper's significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Addressing an Emerging Threat:** The work is one of the first to comprehensively analyze the detrimental effects of synthetic data contamination specifically within the online continual learning paradigm. By quantifying the performance degradation in existing CL methods, it highlights a critical vulnerability that researchers and practitioners must consider.
- **Empirical Characterization of Synthetic Data:** The identification of four key observations regarding synthetic data properties in online CL (harmfulness, the importance of memory buffer quality, lower entropy, and feature space misalignment) provides foundational insights. These empirical characteristics can guide future research in developing more robust detection and mitigation strategies for synthetic content.
- **Practical Solution for Robust CL:** The proposed ESRM method offers a practical and effective solution to alleviate the performance deterioration caused by synthetic data contamination. Its ability to maintain satisfactory performance even under severe contamination ratios improves the reliability and applicability of online CL models in real-world environments where data purity cannot be guaranteed. This enhances the practical utility of continual learning systems.
- **Broader Implications for Data Integrity and AI Governance:** Beyond the immediate scope of continual learning, this research implicitly underscores a larger concern for the integrity of internet data as a resource for AI development. The paper's conclusion advocating for improved regulation and systematic control over generated data, such as watermarking AI-generated content, highlights a crucial societal and ethical dimension. Ensuring data integrity is presented as fundamental to the future soundness and responsible development of AI.
- **Reproducibility and Future Research:** The public availability of the source code fosters reproducibility and encourages further investigation into this critical area. This facilitates the development of more advanced solutions and a deeper understanding of the interplay between generative AI and robust learning systems.

In summary, this paper not only identifies a growing problem at the intersection of generative AI and continual learning but also offers a method to mitigate its effects, contributing to the development of more resilient AI systems and prompting a discussion on data integrity in the age of synthetic media.
