# RoboAtlas: Contextual Active SLAM

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboAtlas introduces a contextual Active SLAM framework that enables mobile robots to perform language-conditioned tasks in unknown environments by adaptively balancing geometric exploration and semantic reasoning. The system, leveraging real-time 3D semantic mapping (OpenRoboVox) and a Contextual Multi-Armed Bandit policy, achieved a 90.6% success rate on the GOAT-Bench benchmark, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods.

## Problem

- Traditional Active SLAM relies on low-fidelity geometric maps and heuristic exploration, limiting high-level reasoning for complex semantic tasks like object search.
- Existing contextual Active SLAM approaches using foundation models are often suboptimal across exploration phases and face deployment challenges on resource-constrained physical robots due to memory and computational demands.
- Many vision-language navigation systems decouple semantic reasoning from metrically consistent 3D mapping, impacting reliable interaction and navigation.

## Method

- Develops **RoboAtlas**, a "mixture-of-experts" framework coordinated by a ==Contextual Multi-Armed Bandit (CMAB)== policy for adaptive goal selection.
- Introduces OpenRoboVox, a real-time, memory-efficient 3D instance-level open-vocabulary semantic mapping system, optimizing it for deployment on physical robots.
- Employs CMAB to dynamically choose between frontier exploration, global semantic map reasoning (LLM), and egocentric VLM experts, guided by environmental context and a reward function.

## Results

- OpenRoboVox generated large-scale 3D semantic maps on hardware in real-time, covering approximately 1803 m² across two floors and identifying 29,588 unique semantic instances.
- The CMAB policy achieved a 100% success rate finding target objects in both unexplored and pre-explored maps, outperforming any single-expert strategy (frontier, semantic map, or egocentric VLM alone).
- On GOAT-Bench's Val Unseen split, RoboAtlas with GPT-4o reached 90.6% success rate, beating the strongest prior baseline (HIMM, 72.8%) by 17.8 points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive decision-making through a contextual multi-armed bandit framework effectively transitions between geometric exploration and semantic exploitation strategies as environmental understanding improves.
> - Real-time, large-scale, instance-level 3D semantic mapping is feasible on resource-constrained physical robots through architectural optimizations like asynchronous processing and partitioned memory management.
> - Integrating foundation models with a robust 3D semantic map and a context-aware expert fusion policy is crucial for open-vocabulary navigation, with architectural design contributing more to performance than just model scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is progressing towards autonomous agents that can navigate and interact with unknown environments without human intervention. A core component of this autonomy is Active Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (Active SLAM), which involves robots intelligently selecting actions to simultaneously improve their understanding of the environment (mapping) and their own position within it (localization). Traditional Active SLAM approaches often rely on low-fidelity geometric representations, such as occupancy grids, and heuristic objectives for exploration. While sufficient for basic coverage tasks, these methods lack the high-level reasoning capabilities required for tasks demanding semantic understanding, such as searching for specific objects or navigating based on contextual cues.

The integration of Large Vision/Language Models (LLM/VLM) has emerged as a strategy to address this limitation, giving rise to "contextual Active SLAM." Recent works leverage foundation models for zero-shot inference of out-of-view content, enriching semantic maps, and processing complex goal specifications without task-specific training. However, existing zero-shot strategies may not fully account for platform-dependent constraints (e.g., localization reliability, map fidelity, planning feasibility) and can be suboptimal at different phases of exploration. For instance, early exploration benefits from geometric coverage, while later stages require targeted, semantically informed navigation.

This paper positions RoboAtlas as an advancement in contextual Active SLAM by bridging the gap between low-fidelity geometric exploration and high-level semantic reasoning. It distinguishes itself from prior work by integrating instance-level 3D semantic mapping with an adaptive decision-making framework, capable of transitioning between exploration-driven and semantics-driven behaviors. Unlike approaches that primarily rely on image-based perception or predefined planning abstractions, RoboAtlas embeds foundation model reasoning directly within a robust 3D semantic map and couples it with a dynamic, context-adaptive policy. The work also addresses the practical challenges of deploying such systems on resource-constrained physical robots, which many existing semantic mapping systems, often evaluated in simulation or offline, tend to overlook.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of RoboAtlas is to enable mobile robots to autonomously navigate previously unexplored environments while executing high-level language-conditioned tasks. This involves simultaneously localizing the robot, constructing a rich semantic representation of the environment, and adaptively selecting navigation goals to maximize the likelihood of task completion.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges identified in current Active SLAM formulations:
- **Limitations of Geometric Representations:** Traditional approaches often rely solely on geometric maps (e.g., occupancy grids), which are insufficient for tasks requiring high-level reasoning, such as semantic search, inventory tracking, or goal-directed exploration for specific objects. Effective decision-making in these scenarios requires semantic priors (e.g., object co-occurrence) and contextual cues.
- **Suboptimality of Fixed Navigation Strategies:** The utility of different navigation strategies changes throughout a mission. In an unknown environment, geometric exploration is critical for acquiring initial map coverage and semantic observations. As the environment becomes better understood, decisions should leverage semantic context and object relationships. Existing systems typically employ fixed policies or rely on semantic representations that are not sufficiently rich or adaptive across these different stages.
- **Practical Deployment Challenges:** While recent works integrate foundation models, many are validated in idealized simulations or small-scale environments, assuming perfect localization and abundant computational resources. Real-world deployment on resource-constrained robots introduces significant bottlenecks related to GPU memory, noisy sensor data, and the need for real-time, continuous operation over large areas.
- **Decoupling of Reasoning and Mapping:** Many vision-language navigation systems decouple semantic reasoning from geometric localization and mapping, often relying on image-based perception and topological representations that do not maintain metric consistency. This can hinder reliable navigation and interaction.

To address these motivations, RoboAtlas aims to develop a contextual Active SLAM framework that:
1. **Balances Exploration and Exploitation:** Dynamically transitions between geometric exploration and semantically guided navigation as environmental understanding improves.
2. **Enables Large-Scale Semantic Mapping:** Develops a real-time, memory-efficient 3D semantic mapping system (OpenRoboVox) suitable for deployment on physical robots in large, complex environments.
3. **Integrates Foundation Model Reasoning:** Leverages LLMs and VLMs to reason over both global 3D semantic maps and egocentric observations, supporting open-vocabulary queries and contextual understanding.
4. **Provides Adaptive Goal Selection:** Employs a contextual multi-armed bandit framework to intelligently select among different navigation strategies based on the robot's current state and task context.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboAtlas is designed as a "mixture-of-experts" framework coordinated by a Contextual Multi-Armed Bandit (CMAB) policy. The system comprises two main components: OpenRoboVox for 3D semantic mapping and a CMAB-based goal selection policy that leverages outputs from OpenRoboVox and other perception modules.

**A. OpenRoboVox: Real-Time 3D Semantic Mapping**
OpenRoboVox is an instance-level open-vocabulary semantic mapping framework, built upon the architecture of OpenVox but engineered for real-time deployment on physical robots. It maintains a large-scale 3D geometric representation and continuously distills raw voxel-level observations into a structured semantic memory called the Scene-Dictionary ($S_t$).
- **Core Functionality:** Combines Truncated Signed Distance Fields (TSDF) for geometry with a probabilistic voxel grid for instance semantics. It uses YOLO-World for object detection and TAP for segmentation, fusing these 2D outputs into 3D space via Bayesian updates.
- **Architectural Improvements for Real-time Deployment:**
 - **Scene-Dictionary Manager:** An incremental background process decouples high-level semantic reasoning from geometric tracking. It updates the Scene-Dictionary ($S_t$), a compact, queryable representation of objects (ID, caption, centroid, bounding box), using a priority-based scheduling algorithm. This algorithm processes a bounded batch of instances based on temporal recency, active observation, and rotational consistency, ensuring O(1) update cost with respect to map size. Batched mask computation further accelerates instance property extraction.
 - **GPU Optimization & Memory Safety:** Addresses VRAM bottlenecks during map expansion by using a partitioned transfer protocol. This decomposes the 3D map into cubic blocks and transfers them one at a time, bounding peak VRAM overhead and preventing out-of-memory errors on resource-constrained GPUs.
 - **Asynchronous Parallel Architecture:** Runs geometric integration (15 Hz) and the open-vocabulary perception stack (5 Hz) on independent threads. A concurrency control manager uses fine-grained mutual exclusion primitives to allow the high-bandwidth geometric stream to operate without blocking, while masking the latency of semantic processing.

**B. 2D Pillar Map & Similarity Maps**
To facilitate efficient high-level navigation planning without direct access to raw 3D voxel data, OpenRoboVox projects its 3D semantic information into a 2D Pillar Map.
- **2D Pillar Map Construction:** Collapses 3D occupied voxels onto a 2D grid, with each pillar maintaining a ranked hierarchy of top-K dominant instances based on voxel count. This preserves vertical context and instance-level granularity in a 2D representation.
- **Similarity Potential Fields:**
 - **Target Object(s) Similarity ($T_{sim}$):** Computes a dense similarity field representing the semantic relevance of instances to a natural language query ($Q_T$). It uses precomputed SBERT embeddings for the query and map instances, calculating the maximum cosine similarity among instances in each pillar.
 - **Neighbor Similarity ($N_{sim}$):** Identifies regions containing objects ($Q_N$) that are expected to be spatial neighbors of the target, aiding in contextual filtering.
 - **Constraint Similarity ($C_{sim}$):** Highlights regions with objects ($Q_C$) to be avoided, acting as a dynamic "semantic cost layer" for path planning.

**C. Contextual Multi-Armed Bandits (CMAB)**
RoboAtlas employs a Disjoint LinUCB algorithm to dynamically select among a set of navigation experts.
- **Experts (Arms):**
 1. **Frontier Exploration Expert ($E_{Frontier}$):** Selects navigation goals in unexplored regions that maximize information gain (new map coverage) while considering travel cost and energy feasibility.
 2. **Semantic Map Expert ($E_{SemanticMap}$):** Reasons over the global Scene-Dictionary ($S_t$) using an LLM (GPT-4o or Qwen2.5-VL-7B). It filters instances based on target and neighbor similarity thresholds, constructs a textual prompt (including instance metadata and similarity scores), and queries the LLM to rank candidate instances by semantic relevance and spatial plausibility. The highest-scoring instance's centroid becomes the navigation goal.
 3. **Egocentric VLM Expert ($E_{EgoVLM}$):** Interprets the robot's current RGB image and user query ($L_t$) using a VLM (GPT-4o or Qwen2.5-VL-7B). It estimates the relevance of visible candidate targets or determines if a reorientation action (turn left/right/around) is needed. VLM-generated scene descriptions are accumulated and provided to the semantic map expert.
- **Context Vector ($c_t$):** Summarizes the robot's current exploration and semantic state, including grid coverage ($m_{occ}$), change in grid coverage ($\dot{m}_{occ}$), backtracking indicator ($B_t$), previous action ($a_{t-1}$), VLM confidence ($V^{vlm}_t$), and a similarity-based metric ($V^{sim}_t$) derived from object counts exceeding similarity thresholds.
- **Reward Function ($r_t$):** A weighted combination of contextual state variables, including map coverage rate, backtracking penalty, VLM confidence, similarity weight, and a terminal task success reward. This function guides the CMAB to balance exploration and semantic exploitation. For GOAT-Bench, a more complex, recency-weighted reward structure is used, combining dense per-turn rewards with sparse per-subtask success rewards.

**D. Navigation and System Integration**
The overall system runs on a desktop with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, communicating via WiFi with an onboard Jetson AGX Orin on a Unitree Go2 robot. The robot runs a ROS2-based navigation stack (SLAM Toolbox for localization, Nav2 with SMAC planner for path planning, Unitree API for motion). RoboAtlas generates (x, y) navigation goals at 0.07–0.2 Hz, which Nav2 converts into velocity commands. Validation is performed in Isaac Sim, Habitat simulator, and on hardware.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of RoboAtlas was conducted across various platforms, demonstrating its performance in semantic mapping, adaptive decision-making, and open-vocabulary navigation.

**A. OpenRoboVox Performance:**
- **Large-Scale Semantic Mapping:** OpenRoboVox demonstrated the capability to create large 3D semantic maps on hardware in real-time. In a single run, it mapped approximately 1803 m² across two floors of an office building, identifying 29,588 unique semantic instances. This showcases its ability to operate continuously on resource-constrained platforms. (Figure 3)

**B. Contextual Multi-Arm Bandits (CMAB) Validation:**
- **Adaptive Strategy:** In an Isaac Sim environment, CMAB dynamically selected between frontier exploration, semantic map, and egocentric VLM experts. Initially, in unexplored environments, CMAB favored frontier exploration to expand map coverage. As the map became more complete, it transitioned towards egocentric VLM and semantic map experts for semantically guided navigation.
- **Improved Task Success and Efficiency:** In trials for finding a target object in an unexplored map, CMAB achieved a 100% success rate (SR), outperforming single-expert strategies (Semantic Map 0%, Ego-VLM 66.7%, Frontier 53.3%). It also yielded shorter path lengths (SPL) and faster completion times. In pre-explored maps, CMAB maintained 100% SR, again outperforming other single experts in SR and time. (Figure 6, Tables (a) and (b))
- **Policy Adaptation Analysis:** The CMAB's action distribution varied with map coverage:
 - **Low Coverage:** Frontier exploration dominated (63.6%), emphasizing geometric expansion.
 - **Mid Coverage:** Egocentric VLM use increased (33.3%), suggesting reliance on local semantic cues.
 - **High Coverage:** CMAB heavily favored semantic map (55.9%) and egocentric VLM (41.2%) experts, with minimal frontier exploration (2.9%), indicating a shift to semantic exploitation. (Table II)
- **Reward Structure Analysis:** The reward function guided this adaptive behavior. Cosine similarity contributed the most (46%) to the total reward, followed by VLM confidence (29%) and task completion (15%). Coverage rate contributed 13%, while backtracking incurred a -4% penalty. This indicates that CMAB increasingly prioritizes semantically informative actions as exploration progresses. (Table III, IV)

**C. Hardware Validation:**
- RoboAtlas was validated on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot in real-world environments. It achieved a 100% task success rate across multiple language-conditioned directives, including multi-stage tasks. The average standoff distance to target objects was 0.22 ± 0.16 m, attributed to minor depth estimation inaccuracies and the robot's safety margin for obstacle avoidance. (Figure 8, Table V)

**D. Baseline Comparisons on GOAT-Bench:**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** On the "Val Unseen" split of the GOAT-Bench benchmark, RoboAtlas with GPT-4o achieved a 90.6% Success Rate (SR) and 53.4% Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) at a 1.0 m threshold. This represents a significant improvement of 17.8 percentage points in SR over the strongest prior GPT-4o baseline, HIMM (72.8% SR). It also outperformed 3D-Mem (GPT-4o) by 21.7 points in SR. (Table VI)
- **Foundation Model Resilience:** Even with the smaller Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, RoboAtlas achieved 88.8% SR and 53.1% SPL. This result notably surpassed all Qwen2.5-VL-7B baselines by a wide margin (e.g., ReEXplore by 35.6 points on SR) and outperformed every GPT-4o baseline on both metrics except HIMM. This suggests that the architectural improvements in semantic mapping and contextual expert selection contribute more significantly to performance than merely using a larger underlying foundation model.
- **Consistent Modality Performance:** Performance remained consistent across object, description, and image query modalities for both GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL-7B. (Table VII)
- **CMAB Expert Allocation:** Analysis of CMAB decisions on GOAT-Bench showed distinct roles for each expert:
 - Egocentric VLM received the largest share of picks (45.0%) but contributed less to total path length (30.6%), acting as a fine-grained local corrector.
 - Semantic Map expert was chosen for 38.7% of decisions and accounted for the majority of total path length (46.8%), serving as the primary exploitation channel for long-range semantic goals.
 - Frontier expert was least picked (16.4%) but contributed a disproportionate 22.6% of path length, indicating its role in preliminary and genuine exploration requiring substantial distances. (Table VIII)
- The marginal lag in SPL compared to the best prior method (HIMM) is attributed to RoboAtlas's Active SLAM formulation, which maintains exploration pressure to continuously build the semantic map, yielding slightly longer paths in exchange for robust representation building beneficial for long-horizon, multi-subtask episodes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboAtlas represents an advancement in the field of contextual Active SLAM by demonstrating a unified framework that adaptively balances geometric exploration and semantic reasoning. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Robust and Flexible Autonomous Navigation:** By dynamically selecting between distinct navigation strategies, RoboAtlas enables robots to operate effectively in environments ranging from completely unknown to partially explored and semantically rich. This adaptability is critical for real-world robotic deployments where environmental knowledge evolves. The high success rates across diverse benchmarks and real-world scenarios indicate a step towards more reliable autonomous agents.
- **Scalable 3D Semantic Mapping:** The introduction of OpenRoboVox addresses critical practical challenges in real-time, large-scale 3D semantic mapping on resource-constrained platforms. Its memory-efficient design, asynchronous architecture, and Scene-Dictionary abstraction allow for continuous instance-level semantic mapping and querying over extensive environments (e.g., 1800 m² with ~30k instances). This development can facilitate more widespread adoption of advanced semantic capabilities in mobile robotics.
- **Effective Integration of Foundation Models:** RoboAtlas provides a robust methodology for grounding LLMs and VLMs with large-scale 3D semantic maps. By integrating global map reasoning with egocentric perception through a contextual decision policy, the framework harnesses the reasoning power of foundation models for open-vocabulary, language-guided navigation without requiring task-specific training. The ability to achieve high performance with a smaller model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) suggests that the architectural integration is a primary driver of success, not just the scale of the foundation model.
- **Interpretability of Adaptive Policies:** The use of Contextual Multi-Armed Bandits (CMAB) provides a framework for adaptive decision-making that can be analyzed and understood. The reward function and action distribution analyses offer insights into how the system transitions from exploratory to exploitative behaviors based on environmental context, contributing to the development of transparent and predictable autonomous systems.
- **Advancement in Multi-Modal Navigation Benchmarks:** The strong performance on the GOAT-Bench benchmark, surpassing prior state-of-the-art methods in success rate, establishes a new baseline for multi-modal lifelong navigation tasks. This contributes to the ongoing development of more capable embodied AI agents.

While the framework demonstrates significant progress, some limitations offer avenues for future research, including improving robustness to semantic mapping errors, reducing dependence on remote LLM/VLM inference latency (e.g., by consolidating computation onboard more powerful edge devices), and exploring more principled or learned reward formulations for the CMAB. Nevertheless, RoboAtlas highlights that the combination of efficient semantic mapping, context-aware expert fusion, and foundation model reasoning can enhance the capabilities of robots in complex, dynamic, and semantically rich environments.
