# Diversity-Aware Policy Optimization for Large Language Model Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University introduce R1-zero-Div, a diversity-aware policy optimization method that enhances Large Language Model reasoning by selectively promoting solution diversity. The approach achieved an average 3.5% Pass@1 accuracy improvement over the R1-zero baseline on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, while also generating more varied and robust problem-solving paths.

## Problem

- The role of diversity in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning has been largely underexplored, despite its established importance in traditional RL for exploration and avoiding local optima.
- Existing lightweight RL algorithms for LLMs, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), may converge to suboptimal policies or exhibit limited exploration due to a lack of explicit diversity mechanisms.
- A formal investigation and empirical validation were needed to establish the relationship between the diversity of an LLM's generated solutions and its inherent 'reasoning potential'.

## Method

- Empirically investigated the correlation between LLM ==solution diversity== (using Div-Equ) and reasoning potential (quantified by a novel Potential@k metric) across 12 diverse LLMs on the MATH benchmark.
- Developed R1-zero-Div, a diversity-aware policy optimization method that integrates a token-level entropy objective into the efficient R1-zero training framework.
- Strategically applied the diversity promotion objective *exclusively to positive samples* (correct solutions) to mitigate the quality-diversity trade-off and ensure diversity enhances high-quality reasoning paths.

## Results

- R1-zero-Div achieved an average Pass@1 accuracy improvement of **3.5%** across four mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, Olympiad Bench, College Math) compared to the standard R1-zero baseline.
- The method effectively preserved and enhanced solution diversity, as measured by Div-Equ, N-gram diversity, and Self-BLEU, demonstrating its ability to generate more varied and robust reasoning paths compared to other RL fine-tuning baselines.
- Ablation studies validated that applying diversity exclusively to positive samples and using an optimal diversity weight (λ=**0.01**) yielded the best performance, and the method generalized successfully to a smaller Qwen2.5-Math-**1.5B** model with a **2.3%** improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-performing LLMs (Pass@1 > 0.4) show a clear positive correlation (R²=0.81) between solution diversity and reasoning potential, empirically validating the benefit of diverse solution generation for performance improvement.
> - Promoting diversity only on positive samples is critical for effective policy optimization; applying it to all samples can lead to increased diversity in incorrect solutions, thereby degrading overall reasoning quality.
> - The R1-zero-Div method successfully maintains higher entropy during training, preventing the rapid entropy collapse observed in standard R1-zero, which enables better exploration and ultimately leads to improved reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models, exemplified by models such as OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, and Kimi-k1.5, have significantly pushed the boundaries of AI. This progress has been largely attributed to two critical innovations: the adoption of rule-based reward systems that simplify training by focusing on correct final answers, and the introduction of lightweight reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that reduce computational overhead. The success of DeepSeek-R1, in particular, has spurred extensive follow-up research. This subsequent work broadly falls into two categories: enhancing the quality of training data through meticulous curation (e.g., filtering, deduplication) and refining RL algorithms themselves. The latter includes optimizations for PPO-based methods (e.g., VCPPO, VAPO), improvements to GRPO for stability and speed (e.g., DAPO, Dr.GRPO, SRPO), and explorations of alternative RL approaches (e.g., REINFORCE++).

Within this dynamic landscape, the current paper identifies a crucial gap: while diversity is a well-established and vital component in traditional RL tasks—facilitating exploration, preventing local optima, and smoothing the optimization landscape—its specific influence and utility in RL-based training for LLM reasoning remain "largely underexplored." Prior research on diversity in LLMs has touched upon areas like supervised fine-tuning, exploration-exploitation trade-offs in self-improvement, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), and LLM ensembles. However, a dedicated and systematic investigation into how promoting diversity during RL training *directly impacts* an LLM's reasoning capabilities, especially in complex mathematical or logical tasks, has been lacking.

This work positions itself as the "first formal investigation" into this specific intersection of diversity, RL, and LLM reasoning. By drawing lessons from conventional RL where diversity aids exploration and convergence, the authors propose to apply these principles to LLM training. The paper aims to bridge this knowledge gap by empirically demonstrating the correlation between solution diversity and reasoning potential, and subsequently, by introducing a principled, diversity-aware policy optimization method tailored for LLM reasoning tasks. This positions the work as a foundational step toward more robust, creative, and generalizable LLM reasoning systems, capable of exploring a wider array of problem-solving strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate the role of diversity in Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based training for Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning and to propose a novel policy optimization method that explicitly promotes this diversity.

The motivation for this study stems from several key observations and hypotheses:

1. **Underexplored Role of Diversity:** Despite the widely acknowledged importance of diversity in traditional RL research for improving exploration, avoiding local optima, and accelerating convergence, its application and impact in the specific context of RL for LLM reasoning have been largely overlooked.
2. **Intuitive Benefit for LLM Reasoning:** The authors hypothesize that an LLM capable of generating a diverse range of responses for a given reasoning problem could broaden its exploration of potential solution paths. This broader exploration would, in turn, help the model avoid overfitting to narrow or specific solution patterns, particularly in mathematical or logical tasks, potentially leading to more robust and accurate outcomes.
3. **Empirical Justification:** To move beyond intuition, a core motivation was to empirically validate this hypothesis. The researchers aimed to establish a formal link between the diversity of solutions generated by an LLMs and its inherent "reasoning potential" – i.e., its capacity for performance improvement after RL training. This led to the development of a novel metric, Potential@k, specifically designed to quantify this potential.
4. **Enhancing Current RL Paradigms for LLMs:** The success of lightweight RL algorithms like GRPO (used in DeepSeek-R1) highlights the efficiency benefits of these methods. However, without explicit diversity mechanisms, such methods might converge to suboptimal policies or exhibit limited exploration. The motivation is to enhance these efficient training paradigms by integrating diversity, thereby improving reasoning capabilities while maintaining computational efficiency.
5. **Addressing Quality-Diversity Trade-off:** A common challenge in diversity-promoting RL is balancing the desire for varied solutions with the need for high-quality, correct answers. The authors were motivated to design a diversity objective that strategically mitigates this trade-off, ensuring that diversity enhancement contributes positively to overall reasoning performance.

In essence, the key motivation is to bridge the conceptual gap between traditional RL's emphasis on diversity and the emerging field of RL for LLM reasoning, ultimately leading to more capable, robust, and creative LLMs for complex problem-solving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed R1-zero-Div, enhances the DeepSeek-R1's R1-zero training framework by integrating a novel diversity-aware policy optimization objective.

#### 3.1. Foundation: R1-zero Training Method

The backbone of this work is the R1-zero training method, which is known for its computational efficiency. It relies on two core components:
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** This algorithm streamlines the RL process by eliminating the need for a separate critic model. Instead, it estimates baselines using group scores from multiple sampled outputs ({o1..., oG}) for a given question. The objective function maximizes a clipped advantage term, similar to PPO, while including a KL divergence regularization term against a reference policy (π_ref). The advantage (Ai) for each sample is computed based on its reward (ri) relative to the mean and standard deviation of rewards within its group.
- **Rule-Based Reward Functions:** In line with DeepSeek-R1, the training uses simple, rule-based rewards: an accuracy reward (binary, based on comparing the predicted answer to the golden reference) and a format reward (ensuring the answer is in a specific \boxed{} format).

#### 3.2. Investigating Diversity in LLM Reasoning

Before proposing their method, the authors conducted an empirical investigation to understand the relationship between solution diversity and an LLM's reasoning potential.
- **Solution Diversity Metric (Div-Equ):** They adopted Div-Equ, a metric from prior work, which quantifies the ratio of distinct equations among k sampled responses for a question.
- **Reasoning Potential Metric (Potential@k):** A novel metric proposed in this paper, Potential@k, quantifies an LLM’s capability to correct answers within k trials on its Pass@1 failure samples. It essentially measures the potential performance gain achievable through further RL training by focusing on questions where the model initially fails but shows some capability to solve with multiple attempts.
- **Experimental Setup:** 12 diverse LLMs were evaluated on the MATH benchmark, assessing Pass@1 accuracy, Div-Equ, and Potential@16.

#### 3.3. Diversity-Aware Policy Optimization (R1-zero-Div)

Motivated by the empirical findings, the core of the proposed method is an entropy-based diversity objective designed for targeted application.

- **Token-Level Entropy:** A straightforward approach to promoting diversity, like average entropy H(πθ(·|q)), suffers from length bias (longer responses intrinsically have higher entropy). To counteract this, the authors introduce a token-level entropy objective. This calculates the entropy for each token generated from the old policy (π_old) and averages it over the output length T.
- **Tractable Reformulation:** The token-level entropy objective (bJDiv) is reformulated using importance sampling to make its gradient tractable for backpropagation:
 bJDiv(πθ) = E_q∼Q,o∼π_old(·|q) [ − (1/T) * Σ_t=1^T (πθ(o_t|q, o_<t) / π_old(o_t|q, o_<t)) * log πθ(o_t|q, o_<t) ]
- **Targeted Diversity Promotion on Positive Samples:** A critical design choice is to promote diversity *exclusively on positive samples* (i.e., outputs that yield a reward r=1).
 - **Rationale:** Direct application of the diversity objective to all samples can inadvertently increase diversity in incorrect solutions, potentially skewing the optimization process away from quality. Focusing on positive samples ensures that exploration is guided by task-relevant performance criteria, enriching high-quality solutions.
 - **Gradient Analysis Justification:** The gradient analysis reveals that for low-probability tokens, the diversity component encourages increasing their probability. While beneficial for positive samples (to explore alternative correct paths), this tendency is undesirable for negative samples, where it might lead to more diverse incorrect responses rather than converging to correct ones.
- **Integrated Training Objective:** The final training objective for R1-zero-Div combines the original GRPO objective with the selectively applied diversity objective:
 J(πθ) = JGRPO(πθ) + λ ⋅ JDiv(πθ), where λ is the diversity weight (set to 0.01 in practice) and I(r_i=1) ensures diversity is applied only to positive samples.

#### 3.4. Experimental Validation

- **Base Models:** Qwen2.5-Math-7B (primary) and Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (ablation).
- **Benchmarks:** GSM8K, MATH500, Olympiad Bench, and College Math (all with ≥ 500 problems for stable evaluation).
- **Baselines:** Base model, R1-zero (without diversity), and state-of-the-art "R1-zero-like" methods (SimpleRL-Zoo, Eurus-2-7B-PRIME).
- **Metrics:** Pass@1 accuracy, Avg@8 accuracy (with standard errors), and diversity metrics (Div-Equ, N-gram diversity, Self-BLEU diversity).
- **Computational Setup:** Training on GSM8K, AdamW optimizer, 2 epochs, 6 responses sampled with temperature 0.9, utilizing TRL and DeepSpeed on 8x NVIDIA A6000 GPUs.

This systematic approach, from empirical correlation discovery to targeted algorithmic design and comprehensive evaluation, forms the robust methodology of this paper.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various LLMs and mathematical reasoning benchmarks yielded several significant findings:

- **Positive Correlation Between Solution Diversity and Reasoning Potential:**
 - The initial investigation, evaluating 12 LLMs on the MATH benchmark, revealed a bifurcated pattern. For LLMs with limited reasoning ability (Pass@1 < 0.4), no significant relationship was observed between solution diversity (Div-Equ) and reasoning potential (Potential@k).
 - However, for high-performing models (Pass@1 > 0.4), a "clear positive correlation" emerged. Linear regression on this subset showed a strong relationship with an R² value of 0.81, confirming that increased solution diversity directly correlates with higher reasoning potential. This finding provided strong empirical motivation for the proposed diversity-aware policy optimization.

- **Enhanced Reasoning Abilities with R1-zero-Div:**
 - R1-zero-Div demonstrated superior reasoning performance compared to the standard R1-zero baseline. It achieved an average Pass@1 accuracy improvement of 3.5% across the four mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, Olympiad Bench, College Math).
 - Despite being trained with more limited computational resources (8x A6000 GPUs) compared to some state-of-the-art methods (e.g., SimpleRL-Zoo using 2x8 H100-80G GPUs), R1-zero-Div achieved comparable or even slightly better results in some cases, highlighting its efficiency and effectiveness.
 - The results were consistent when evaluated with Avg@8 accuracy, further reinforcing the effectiveness of the approach.

- **Generation of More Diverse and Robust Solutions:**
 - While standard RL fine-tuning methods (like Eurus-2-7B-PRIME, SimpleRL-Zoo, and R1-zero) tend to significantly reduce the diversity of generated solutions compared to the base model, R1-zero-Div effectively preserved and, in some cases, enhanced diversity.
 - Measured by Div-Equ, N-gram diversity, and Self-BLEU diversity on the GSM8K test set, R1-zero-Div consistently produced more varied solutions than other RL-finetuning baselines. Qualitative examples further illustrated the model's ability to generate distinct, yet mathematically valid, reasoning paths for the same problem.

- **Ablation Studies Validate Design Choices:**
 - **Diversity Weight (λ):** An analysis of different diversity weights (λ) showed that small values (λ ≤ 0.02) were most effective, with λ = 0.01 yielding optimal performance. Larger values could lead to diminished returns or even degraded performance.
 - **Targeted Diversity Promotion:** The strategic choice to apply diversity enhancement *only to positive samples* was empirically validated. This "pos" strategy consistently outperformed applying diversity to "pos+neg" (all samples), affirming that focusing diversity on high-quality outputs is crucial for balancing quality and exploration.
 - **Scalability to Smaller Models:** R1-zero-Div successfully generalized to a smaller Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B base model, achieving an average improvement of 2.3% over R1-zero. This demonstrates the method's consistency across different model sizes.

- **Entropy Dynamics:**
 - Analysis of entropy during training showed that the baseline R1-zero (λ=0) suffered from rapid entropy collapse, indicating a loss of exploration.
 - Applying diversity to all samples (λ=0.01, pos+neg) resulted in late-stage exploration but often at the cost of overall solution quality.
 - The proposed R1-zero-Div (λ=0.01, pos) achieved an optimal balance, maintaining higher entropy without sacrificing solution accuracy, leading to the best performance.

In summary, the research firmly establishes the importance of diversity in LLM reasoning, provides a novel metric to quantify reasoning potential, and introduces an effective and principled method (R1-zero-Div) that enhances both the accuracy and solution diversity of LLMs in mathematical reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research "Diversity-Aware Policy Optimization for Large Language Model Reasoning" makes several significant contributions and holds substantial potential for impact in the field of artificial intelligence:

#### 5.1. Significance of Contributions

- **Pioneering Formal Investigation:** This work marks the first formal and systematic investigation into the role of diversity within the context of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Model reasoning. By addressing this underexplored area, it bridges a critical gap between established RL principles and the burgeoning field of LLM capabilities.
- **Empirical Validation of Diversity's Value:** The strong positive correlation identified between solution diversity and an LLM's "reasoning potential" (quantified by the novel Potential@k metric) provides robust empirical evidence. This finding moves beyond intuitive assumptions, offering a solid foundation for actively incorporating diversity promotion into LLM training pipelines.
- **Novel and Principled Policy Optimization:** The introduction of R1-zero-Div, a token-level diversity objective selectively applied to positive samples, is a key methodological advancement. This design elegantly addresses the challenges of length bias and the quality-diversity trade-off, ensuring that diversity fosters productive exploration rather than diluting solution quality. The gradient behavior analysis further provides theoretical insight into this strategic design.
- **Enhanced LLM Performance:** The consistent performance gains (3.5% average improvement) over state-of-the-art R1-zero training across diverse mathematical benchmarks, coupled with the generation of more diverse and robust solutions, demonstrates the practical efficacy of the proposed method.
- **Compatibility and Generalizability:** The method's compatibility with the R1-zero framework implies it can be integrated into and potentially enhance other existing and future RL-based LLM training approaches. Its demonstrated effectiveness on both 1.5B and 7B parameter models also suggests a degree of generalizability across model scales, albeit with a recognized need for further exploration on larger models.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **More Robust and Creative LLMs:** By explicitly promoting solution diversity, LLMs can become less prone to overfitting to specific reasoning patterns. This could lead to more robust models that can tackle problems from various angles and potentially discover novel, non-obvious solutions, thereby enhancing their creativity and problem-solving versatility in complex tasks.
- **Improved AI Tutoring and Education:** In educational applications, LLMs capable of generating multiple, distinct yet correct reasoning paths could revolutionize AI tutoring systems. Instead of simply providing a single solution, an AI tutor could offer students alternative strategies, explain different approaches to a problem, and cater to diverse learning styles, leading to a deeper understanding of concepts.
- **Advancements in Scientific Discovery and Research:** For scientific problem-solving, an LLM that can explore diverse hypotheses or derive solutions through different logical frameworks could significantly aid researchers. This could accelerate the generation of novel research questions, facilitate multi-perspective analysis, and contribute to breakthroughs in fields requiring complex logical deduction.
- **Broader Applicability Across LLM Tasks:** While demonstrated in mathematical reasoning, the principles of diversity-aware policy optimization could extend to other LLM tasks that benefit from varied outputs, such as code generation (diverse algorithmic approaches), creative writing (varied narratives), or complex decision-making scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a strong foundation for future research directions, particularly in exploring the scalability of diversity-aware methods to much larger LLMs and investigating more advanced forms of "semantic diversity" that align with user-intended variety (e.g., specific types of reasoning strategies) rather than just statistical variance.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge a primary limitation: computational constraints restricted experiments to 1.5B and 7B parameter-scale models. The generalizability of this method to significantly larger LLMs (e.g., 70B parameters or more) remains an open question and a crucial area for future research, as optimization landscapes and dynamics can differ substantially across model scales.

Furthermore, the paper highlights the distinction between the entropy-based "statistical diversity" currently employed and the more challenging concept of "user-intended semantic diversity." Future work could delve into explicitly modeling and optimizing for scenario-specific diversity, such as requiring both algebraic and arithmetic solutions to a math problem, or generating code with distinct algorithmic paradigms. Bridging this gap with Quality-Diversity RL methods could unlock more controllable and context-aware generative capabilities, aligning LLM outputs more closely with human-intentional variety in complex tasks.

Overall, this research is a significant step towards developing more intelligent and versatile LLMs, providing both empirical evidence and a practical method to harness the power of diversity in the pursuit of advanced reasoning.
