# Hierarchically Robust Zero-shot Vision-language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a framework that enhances adversarial robustness in zero-shot Vision-Language Models by leveraging hyperbolic geometry to embed and align hierarchical class information. The method improves both clean and robust accuracy across 15 diverse datasets, consistently outperforming previous state-of-the-art techniques against various attack types, and demonstrates successful application in medical imaging.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are susceptible to adversarial attacks, where subtle input perturbations can lead to confident but incorrect predictions.
- Existing adversarial fine-tuning methods for VLMs primarily focus on base-class attacks, resulting in degraded robustness against attacks targeting broader 'superclasses' (parent categories).
- Adversaries generated against superclasses are more generic and can effectively transfer to attack base classes, presenting a significant unaddressed vulnerability.

## Method

- Develops an ==adversarial fine-tuning== framework that projects class hierarchies into a ==hyperbolic embedding space== (==Poincaré ball model==) to explicitly represent multi-level semantic relationships.
- Introduces **Hierarchy-preserving Image-Text Alignment (HITA)** and **Hierarchy-aware Negative Set Augmentation (HNSA)** to robustly align image and text embeddings across all hierarchical levels.
- Incorporates novel loss components, including ==hierarchical norm gap constraints== and ==intra-class vicinity alignment==, to maintain hierarchical integrity and account for individual image variability within the hyperbolic space.

## Results

- Achieved average clean accuracy of **62.14%** and robust accuracy (PGD-20) of **44.34%** across **15 datasets**, outperforming state-of-the-art adversarial fine-tuning methods like FARE and AoS.
- Demonstrated superior robustness against image-level, text-level (BERT-Attack, GBDA), and bi-level (Co-Attack, SGA) adversarial attacks across various perturbation intensities.
- Successfully extended to other Vision-Language Models (BLIP, CoCa) for tasks like image-text retrieval and captioning, and significantly improved **robust AUC** scores for chest X-ray classification in medical imaging.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hyperbolic geometry is intrinsically suited for low-distortion representation of class hierarchies, where vector norms within the Poincaré ball naturally correspond to hierarchical levels.
> - The varying margin sizes inherent to hyperbolic classifiers, which can grow exponentially, facilitate the generation of 'universal' adversarial perturbations that robustify models across different hierarchical levels.
> - Robustness against superclass-level adversaries is crucial for overall VLM security, as these generic attacks effectively transfer to and compromise base-class performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in various zero-shot tasks, including classification, by aligning image and text modalities in a shared embedding space. Prominent examples include models like CLIP. However, these models, despite their performance, exhibit susceptibility to adversarial attacks, where subtle perturbations to inputs can lead to incorrect predictions with high confidence. This vulnerability limits their deployment in applications requiring high security or reliability, such as safety-critical systems.

Existing research has explored adversarial fine-tuning as a strategy to enhance the robustness of VLMs. These approaches typically involve aligning adversarial features of base classes (e.g., "cat") across image and text modalities. Methods like TeCoA, PMG-FT, and FARE fall into this category, aiming to improve robustness by generating adversaries at the level of specific, or "leaf," categories.

This paper identifies a limitation in these existing adversarial fine-tuning methods: their focus on base classes results in a degradation of robustness when models encounter adversarial attacks targeting superclasses (parent categories, e.g., "mammal"). Such superclass-level adversaries, being more generic, can also transfer effectively to attack base classes, posing a broader threat. Furthermore, prior methods often align fixed text embeddings with image embeddings, potentially sacrificing both natural performance and general robustness.

This work addresses this identified gap by introducing a framework that explicitly incorporates the hierarchical nature of class relationships into the adversarial fine-tuning process. By moving beyond a flat class structure and leveraging hyperbolic geometry, the paper aims to enable VLMs to acquire "hierarchical robustness," meaning the ability to withstand attacks across multiple levels of abstraction in class taxonomies. This approach seeks to provide a more comprehensive defense against adversarial threats, acknowledging that adversarial perturbations can originate or generalize from different semantic granularities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the adversarial robustness of zero-shot Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by leveraging the inherent hierarchical properties of class space.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Addressing VLM Vulnerability to Adversarial Attacks**: VLMs are acknowledged as susceptible to adversarial examples. While adversarial fine-tuning can improve robustness, existing methods primarily focus on robustifying models against attacks targeting specific, or "base," categories (e.g., a particular breed of dog). The authors empirically demonstrate that these models exhibit poor robustness when faced with adversarial attacks generated against "superclasses" (e.g., "mammal").
- **Highlighting Superclass Attack Generalization**: The paper shows that adversarial samples generated by targeting superclasses are "more generic" and can effectively transfer to attack base classes. For instance, Figure 1a illustrates that prior methods trained on base classes perform poorly on superclass classification under adversarial conditions. Figure 1b further shows that superclass-generated adversaries can successfully attack base classes, indicating a critical vulnerability not adequately addressed by current methods. This suggests that robustifying only against base-class attacks is insufficient for comprehensive security.
- **Leveraging Class Hierarchy for Robustness**: The research posits that the richness of hierarchical decision boundaries is currently underutilized in adversarial robustification. By explicitly incorporating the relationships between base classes and their superclasses, the model can potentially learn more generalized defenses.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Fixed Text Embeddings**: Traditional adversarial fine-tuning often aligns image embeddings with fixed, pre-computed text embeddings. The authors argue that this approach can constrain the learning process, potentially compromising both natural performance and the breadth of robustness.
- **Exploiting Hyperbolic Geometry**: The paper proposes that hyperbolic embeddings are naturally suited to represent hierarchical structures with low distortion (Figure 2a). Specifically, the norm of a vector in the Poincar´e ball model can serve as a proxy for its hierarchical level (Figure 2b), with superclasses typically having lower norms (closer to the origin) and base classes having higher norms (closer to the boundary). This intrinsic property allows for a novel way to organize and align embeddings hierarchically.
- **Realizing Diverse Margin Sizes for Generalization**: A key theoretical motivation is presented in Figure 2c, which illustrates that a hyperbolic classifier allows for a maximum viable margin size that can grow exponentially to infinity as embedding norms approach the Poincar´e ball boundary. In contrast, Euclidean classifiers have a finite margin limit. By deploying image and text features hierarchically based on their norms, the proposed hyperbolic design naturally realizes several varying margin sizes. This varying margin is hypothesized to expose adversarial perturbations to a spectrum of generalization levels, leading to more "universal" perturbations during robustification. These universal perturbations, generated across different hierarchical levels, are intended to provide both general-to-specialized robustification capabilities.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Develop a novel adversarial fine-tuning framework for VLMs that accounts for class hierarchies.
2. Enhance VLM robustness against both base-class and superclass-level adversarial attacks.
3. Improve the generalization of adversarial robustness by exploiting the unique properties of hyperbolic geometry, particularly its ability to represent hierarchies and induce varying classifier margins.
4. Maintain or improve clean accuracy alongside robust accuracy, avoiding the common trade-off.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces a novel adversarial fine-tuning framework for zero-shot Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that integrates hierarchical class information using hyperbolic geometry. The core idea is to align image and text modalities across multiple levels of abstraction, from specific base classes to general superclasses, within a hyperbolic embedding space.

#### 3.1. Hyperbolic Embedding and Hierarchy Construction

The framework employs the Poincar´e ball model, a type of hyperbolic space, to embed class hierarchies.
- **Hierarchy Definition**: Class levels are defined from `l=0` (base classes) to `l=L` (root).
- **Text Prompt Generation**: For each base class or superclass `c_l` at level `l`, a tokenized text prompt `t_c^l` is created (e.g., "[Context][SUPERCLASS_c^l]"). For ImageNet, WordNet is used to extract hypernyms for superclasses. For other datasets lacking predefined taxonomies, LLMs like ChatGPT-4o are prompted to generate contextually appropriate superclasses, followed by manual review.
- **Mapping to Hyperbolic Space**: Clean or adversarial image/text features (from CLIP's Euclidean embedding space) are projected into the Poincar´e ball `D_r^d` using an exponential map `exp_0^r(·)`. A projection function `Proj(z)` is applied for numerical stability, preventing vectors from reaching the Poincar´e ball's boundary. This results in hyperbolic image embeddings `ϕ(x)` and text embeddings `ψ(t_c)`.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Image-Text Alignment

A central aspect of the method is the alignment of image and text embeddings across hierarchical levels.
- **Superclass Image Embeddings**: Since a single image does not represent an entire superclass, image embeddings for superclasses `ϕ_c^l` are constructed by applying hyperbolic averaging (HypAvg) over individual image embeddings `ϕ(x)` of base classes that share that superclass within a mini-batch. This process is applied recursively up the hierarchy, effectively yielding more generic embeddings from more specific ones, which have lower `ℓ2` norms (closer to the Poincar´e origin) (Figure 2b).
- **Hierarchy-aware Negative Set Augmentation (HNSA)**: The probability `p_c^l` of an image belonging to class `c` at level `l` is computed based on the Riemannian distance `d_r(·,·)` between the image embedding `ϕ_c^l` and the text embedding `ψ(t_c^l)`. An additional augmentation term `η_c^l` is introduced, which incorporates "negative" classes from both the adjacent higher (`l+1`) and lower (`l-1`) hierarchical levels that are not direct ancestors or descendants, enriching the semantic context for alignment (Eq. 9).
- **Hierarchy-preserving Image-Text Alignment (HITA)**: The standard adversarial fine-tuning objective is extended to its hierarchical counterpart (Eq. 10). It maximizes a weighted sum of cross-entropy (CE) losses `L_CE` across all hierarchical levels `l=0..., L`. The weights `ω_l = 1 - l/(L+1)` prioritize lower (more specific) levels. Adversarial perturbations `δ_X` (for images) and `δ_T` (for texts) are generated universally across all hierarchy levels.
- **Label Norm Gap Constraint (`L_Label_gap`)**: A soft constraint (Eq. 11) is applied to text embeddings to maintain hierarchical integrity in the hyperbolic space. It encourages higher-level superclass embeddings to have smaller `ℓ2` norms than lower-level superclass embeddings, ensuring they are positioned closer to the Poincar´e origin, consistent with their more general nature.

#### 3.3. Individual Images and Intra-class Variability

To account for individual image variations within a base class and ensure hierarchical consistency:
- **Intra-class Vicinity Alignment (`L_vic`)**: Individual adversarial image embeddings `ϕ(ˆx_c)` are encouraged to remain within a certain radius `ζ_vic` of their corresponding base class text embedding `ψ(ˆt_c)` (Eq. 12). This prevents image embeddings from collapsing onto the exact text embedding, acknowledging inherent visual variability.
- **Intra-class Norm Gap Constraint (`L_Intra_gap`)**: Another constraint (Eq. 13) encourages individual adversarial image embeddings `ϕ(ˆx_c)` to have `ℓ2` norms smaller than their base class text embedding `ψ(ˆt_c)`. This helps maintain a hierarchical order where individual instances are more specific than their class concept, reinforcing the idea that image embeddings should be "below" their base class in the hierarchy (i.e., closer to the Poincar´e boundary).

#### 3.4. Objective Function and Theoretical Analysis

The overall objective function (Eq. 14) combines `L'` (HITA), `L_vic`, and the gap penalties (`L_Label_gap` + `L_Intra_gap`) with tunable factors `λ1` and `λ2`. The model fine-tunes the image encoder and, unlike previous methods, also the text encoder's projection layer.

The theoretical analysis (Theorem 1, Figure 2c) highlights that the maximum viable margin size in hyperbolic space can range from `(0, ∞)` as the feature norm `η` approaches the Poincar´e boundary (corresponding to "leaves" or specific concepts). In contrast, Euclidean classifiers have a finite margin limit. By deploying features across hierarchical levels, each with different norms, the model achieves multiple margin sizes. This varying margin allows adversarial perturbations `δ_X` and `δ_T` to encounter diverse decision boundary properties during generation, leading to more "universal" perturbations. It's suggested that increasing adversarial risk for parent classifiers (smaller margin) yields more universal perturbations, which then specialize for children classifiers, facilitating a general-to-specific robustification.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

Adversarial samples are generated using PGD with a specified number of iterations and `ℓ∞`-norm threat models for both images and texts. The method also explores parameter-efficient fine-tuning (VPT) for computational efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental validation of the proposed Hierarchically Robust Zero-shot Vision-language Models (our method) across various datasets, architectures, and attack types.

#### 4.1. Benchmarking on 15 Datasets

- **Clean Accuracy**: On a suite of 15 diverse datasets, our method consistently achieved higher clean accuracy compared to state-of-the-art adversarial fine-tuning techniques (TeCoA, PMG-FT, FARE, AoS). Table 1 shows an average improvement of 2.5% over FARE and also surpassed the recent AoS (e.g., 62.14% vs. 61.70% average).
- **Robust Accuracy (PGD-20)**: Against PGD-20 attacks, our method demonstrated enhanced robustness, achieving a mean gain of 6.4% over FARE and outperforming AoS (e.g., 44.34% vs. 43.88% average) (Table 2). The "Ours (5 trees)" variant, which integrates multiple hierarchical trees, further improved these metrics to 62.49% clean and 45.39% robust accuracy.

#### 4.2. Robustness across Multiple Attack Intensities

- Our method consistently outperformed other approaches across a spectrum of increasing perturbation radii (ϵ_X = 1/255, 2/255, 3/255, 4/255) during evaluation (Table 3), indicating sustained robustness against stronger adversarial attacks.

#### 4.3. Fine-tuning with Diverse Perturbation Radii

- When models were fine-tuned with larger ℓ∞-norm perturbation radii (ϵ_X = 2/255, 3/255, 4/255) and evaluated against matching attack intensities, our method exhibited superior performance on both clean and adversarial samples compared to prior approaches (Table 4).

#### 4.4. Robustness on Different Architectures

- Experiments with different CLIP backbones, including ViT-L and ResNet-50, showed that our approach consistently surpassed previous adversarial fine-tuning methods, providing improved clean and adversarial robustness across 15 datasets (Table 5).

#### 4.5. Adversarial Fine-tuning with Parameter-Efficient VPT

- When employing Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, our method achieved superior zero-shot clean and robust accuracy across various perturbation radii, demonstrating its effectiveness even with computational constraints (Table 6).

#### 4.6. Robustness to Text-level and Bi-level Adversarial Attacks

- The model exhibited significant robustness improvements against text-level attacks (BERT-Attack, GBDA) and bi-level attacks (Co-Attack, SGA), outperforming competitors in these multimodal adversarial scenarios (Table 7).

#### 4.7. Extension to BLIP/CoCa

- Our method was successfully extended to other Vision-Language Models like BLIP and CoCa. It consistently achieved better clean and robust performance in image-text retrieval (Flickr30k) and image captioning (Nocaps) tasks (Table 8).

#### 4.8. Extension to Medical CLIP

- Applying the method to medical imaging, specifically for chest X-ray classification, resulted in superior Area Under the Curve (AUC) scores for both clean and adversarial samples across datasets like ChestXray14, CheXpert, and PadChest. This indicates improved robustness in critical medical diagnosis tasks (Table 9).

#### 4.9. Analysis and Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Loss Components (Table 10)**: Hierarchical Image-Text Alignment (HITA) improved both clean and robust accuracy. The intra-class vicinity alignment (`L_vic`) and label/intra-class norm gap penalties (`L_Label_gap`, `L_Intra_gap`) further contributed to performance, particularly in mitigating embedding collapse and promoting hierarchical integrity.
- **Negative Set Augmentation (Table 11)**: Integrating superclasses from both higher and lower hierarchical levels (as in HNSA) yielded the best performance compared to no augmentation or only one level.
- **Adversary Generation Strategies (Table 12)**: A "universal hierarchical perturbation" strategy, where a single perturbation is applied across all hierarchy levels, offered a favorable trade-off between robustness and computational efficiency during training.
- **LLMs for Superclass Generation (Table 13)**: The method's performance remained stable when using different powerful LLMs (LLaMA-2, Claude-2) for superclass generation, indicating robustness to the choice of LLM.
- **Fine-tuning Target VLM vs. Replacing Image Encoder (Table 14)**: Fine-tuning the entire target VLM (our approach) yielded better natural performance and adversarial robustness compared to merely replacing the image encoder.
- **Image-text vs. Image-only Adversaries (Table 15)**: Fine-tuning with adversaries generated at both image and text levels provided superior robustness compared to image-level adversaries only.
- **Hyperbolic vs. Euclidean Model (Table 16)**: The hyperbolic space modeling approach significantly outperformed Euclidean-based superclass classification, especially in terms of adversarial transferability and robustness across both base and superclasses. Euclidean models, even with hierarchical classification, failed to achieve comparable base category robustness or transferability. This underscores the advantage of hyperbolic geometry for hierarchical robustification.
- **Other findings from supplementary material**: The method showed moderate degradation under noisy label hierarchies (Table 25), stable performance across prompt lengths (Table 26) and epochs (Table 27), and benefits from augmenting hierarchical trees into "forests" (Table 18) and specific weighting mechanisms (Table 19). Hyperbolic averaging proved the most effective strategy for representing base classes (Table 20), and superior robustness was observed against black-box multimodal adversaries (Table 21).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Vision-Language Models by addressing a critical vulnerability: the susceptibility of zero-shot VLMs to adversarial attacks, particularly those targeting broader, "superclass" categories. The introduction of a hierarchically robust framework based on hyperbolic geometry represents a significant advancement over existing adversarial fine-tuning methods that primarily focus on base-level classifications.

The potential impacts of this work include:

- **Enhanced Security and Reliability of VLMs**: By providing a mechanism to robustify VLMs against adversaries across multiple levels of abstraction, the proposed method increases the overall security and trustworthiness of these models. This is crucial for their deployment in sensitive applications where reliability under adversarial conditions is paramount.
- **Broader Generalization of Robustness**: The theoretical and empirical demonstration that hyperbolic geometry enables diverse margin sizes for classifiers at different hierarchical levels implies a more generalized form of adversarial learning. This "general-to-specific" robustification capability suggests that the model learns to defend against a wider range of perturbations, potentially improving resilience against unforeseen attack variations.
- **Improved Performance Across Diverse Tasks**: The consistent outperformance on 15 diverse datasets, including general image classification, fine-grained classification, domain-specific tasks, and scene recognition, indicates the broad applicability and effectiveness of the method.
- **Advancements in Multimodal Robustness**: The demonstrated robustness against not only image-level but also text-level and bi-level adversarial attacks highlights its importance for the security of multimodal systems, which are inherently vulnerable across different input modalities.
- **Impact on Critical Domains like Medical Imaging**: The successful extension to medical CLIP and its superior performance in robust chest X-ray disease classification has direct implications for computer-aided diagnosis. Improving VLM robustness in medical contexts is vital for ensuring diagnostic accuracy and preventing malicious interference.
- **Theoretical Insights into Hyperbolic Geometry for ML Robustness**: The theoretical analysis regarding margin sizes in hyperbolic space provides valuable insights into how non-Euclidean geometries can be leveraged to address challenges in machine learning robustness. This could stimulate further research into the application of hyperbolic and other Riemannian geometries for learning robust representations.
- **Practical Framework for Hierarchy Construction**: The method's ability to utilize LLMs for generating class hierarchies in datasets lacking predefined taxonomies makes it adaptable and practical for a wide array of real-world scenarios, extending its utility beyond datasets with existing structured knowledge bases.

Overall, this research moves beyond incremental improvements in adversarial robustness by offering a foundational change in how VLMs perceive and defend against attacks, emphasizing semantic hierarchy. This approach could pave the way for more secure, robust, and trustworthy AI systems across various applications.
