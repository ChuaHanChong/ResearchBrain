# Legged Robot State-Estimation Through Combined Forward Kinematic and Preintegrated Contact Factors

> [!summary] Summary
> A state-estimation method for legged robots integrates forward kinematic and preintegrated contact factors into a factor graph framework, reducing reliance on visual sensors. This approach significantly reduces positional drift and improves robustness, achieving an end-to-end translation error of approximately 1.3 meters over a 100-second real-world robot trajectory.

## Problem

- Existing legged robot state estimation systems rely heavily on visual data, making them susceptible to failure in challenging environments with poor lighting, occlusions, or feature scarcity.
- Traditional leg odometry suffers from unbounded drift in position and yaw due to encoder noise, foot slippage, and a lack of external absolute corrections.
- Factor graph smoothing, while robust, primarily utilizes visual input, leaving a gap for the direct integration of unique odometry information derived from legged robot kinematics and contact dynamics.

## Method

- A ==Forward Kinematic (FK)== factor is developed to incorporate noisy joint encoder measurements into the factor graph, accurately relating the robot's base frame to its end-effector (contact frame).
- Preintegrated contact factors are formulated, including both rigid and point contact models, to describe the movement of the contact frame between time steps while explicitly accounting for foot slippage noise.
- These novel FK and contact factors are integrated alongside existing IMU factors within a unified factor graph smoothing framework, which is then optimized incrementally using the iSAM2 solver.

## Results

- Simulations showed that combining FK, contact, and IMU factors significantly reduced translational and rotational drift compared to IMU-only estimation, and in some cases, outperformed visual loop closure combined with IMU for translational error.
- Real-world experiments on a Cassie-series biped robot demonstrated substantial improvement over IMU-only estimation, achieving an end-to-end translation error of approximately **1.3** meters over a 100-second walking loop without visual odometry or loop closure.
- The proposed method successfully provided accurate state estimates and enabled effective IMU bias estimation in real-world scenarios where visual tracking systems might fail, validating its potential for enhanced robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leg odometry, when properly modeled and integrated into a factor graph, provides a robust and complementary source of information to IMUs, reducing drift and enhancing state estimation, especially in the absence of reliable visual data.
> - Preintegration techniques, typically applied to IMUs, can be effectively adapted for contact measurements, allowing for efficient fusion of high-frequency leg odometry data within the factor graph.
> - By fusing diverse sensor modalities (IMU, joint encoders, contact sensors) probabilistically, the framework can more accurately estimate IMU biases and maintain robust state estimation without relying on external visual inputs for absolute corrections.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic perception heavily relies on accurate state estimation for autonomous operation, particularly in complex and unstructured environments. State-of-the-art robotic systems frequently utilize Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs), cameras, and advanced nonlinear optimization techniques, often within Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) or Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) frameworks. These methods have achieved considerable success, leading to their deployment in various technological applications.

However, a significant limitation of many contemporary state estimation systems, especially for legged robots, is their dependence on visual information. Such systems are susceptible to failure when visual tracking is compromised due to challenging lighting conditions, visual occlusions, or environments with a scarcity of distinctive features. Legged robots, distinct from ground, flying, or underwater platforms, engage in direct and switching contact with the environment, offering an alternative source of odometry information: leg odometry.

Traditional leg odometry, which leverages kinematic and contact information from joint encoders, presents its own challenges. Encoder noise and foot slippage can introduce inaccuracies. While filtering methods, such as Extended Kalman Filters (EKFs), are often employed to fuse high-frequency inertial and contact measurements, they typically suffer from unbounded drift in absolute position and yaw due due to unobservability in these states without external corrections. The accumulation of landmarks in EKF-based SLAM can also lead to computational intractability over long durations.

Smoothing methods, particularly those based on factor graphs, offer a robust alternative by estimating entire trajectories and allowing for absolute position and yaw corrections through loop closures. These graphical models, coupled with nonlinear optimization, exploit sparse structures for real-time performance and incorporate high-frequency sensor data through preintegration, notably for IMUs. While effective, most existing factor graph methods still primarily rely on visual input.

This research addresses the gap by integrating leg odometry directly into the factor graph smoothing framework. By doing so, it aims to reduce dependence on visual information, enhance robustness, and provide improved state estimation, particularly in scenarios where visual tracking is lost. The work endeavors to combine the strengths of factor graph optimization and IMU preintegration with the unique properties of legged locomotion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the vulnerability of current vision-dependent state estimation systems for legged robots. When visual tracking fails due to environmental factors such as poor lighting or a lack of distinct features, these systems can experience significant performance degradation or complete failure. This limitation restricts the adaptive operation of legged robots in unstructured and unknown environments, despite their inherent mobility advantages. Accurate and reliable state estimates are foundational for stable control, effective motion planning, and online decision-making in such robots.

The overarching objective of this work is to develop a state-estimation technique for legged robots that explicitly incorporates the robot's kinematic model and its interaction (contact) with the environment into a factor graph framework. This integration is designed to reduce reliance on visual sensors and mitigate drift in situations where visual data is unavailable or unreliable.

Specific objectives and contributions outlined by the authors include:

- **Development of a Forward Kinematic (FK) Factor:** To incorporate noisy joint encoder measurements into the factor graph, enabling the estimation of an end-effector (specifically, a contact frame) pose at any given time step relative to the robot's base.
- **Formulation of Preintegrated Contact Factors:** To describe the movement of the contact frame between successive time steps. This involves developing both rigid contact factors (assuming a fixed contact point in the world) and point contact factors (allowing for orientation changes), while accounting for noise attributed to foot slippage.
- **Integration of Leg Odometry into Factor Graph Smoothing:** To incorporate these newly developed FK and contact factors alongside existing IMU factors within a unified factor graph smoothing framework, thereby providing a more comprehensive and robust state estimation solution.
- **Real-Time Implementation and Validation:** To implement the proposed FK and preintegrated contact measurement models on a Cassie-series biped robot and evaluate their performance in real-time using both simulated and real-world sensory data.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to enhance the overall localization accuracy and robustness of legged robots, enabling them to operate more reliably in diverse and challenging environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on augmenting a factor graph-based state estimation framework with novel factors that leverage the kinematic model and contact information of a legged robot.

**Overall Framework:**
The system utilizes a factor graph, which is a graphical model representing a nonlinear least-squares problem, to estimate the robot's trajectory. The optimization problem is formulated as finding the Maximum-A-Posteriori (MAP) estimate of the robot's state by minimizing a sum of squared errors (residuals) weighted by their respective covariance matrices. The state at each key-frame (`S_i`) includes the robot's base orientation (`R_i`), base position (`p_i`), base velocity (`v_i`), the contact frame poses for both feet (`C_i`, `d_i`), and IMU biases (`b_i`). The optimization is performed incrementally using iSAM2, a common solver for factor graphs.

**Sensor Fusion:**
The framework fuses measurements from an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU), joint encoders, and binary contact sensors on the feet. The system is designed to integrate these sensor modalities probabilistically.

**Novel Factors Introduced:**

- **Forward Kinematic (FK) Factor:**
 - **Purpose:** To relate the robot's base frame to an end-effector frame (defined as a "contact frame" on the robot's foot) using noisy joint encoder measurements (`α`).
 - **Modeling:** The forward kinematics (FK) function `H(α)` calculates the relative pose (rotation `f_R(α)` and position `f_p(α)`) of the contact frame with respect to the base frame through a product of homogeneous transforms across the robot's links. Each revolute joint's angle `α_n` is measured by an encoder.
 - **Noise Handling:** Encoder measurements (`˜α`) are assumed to be corrupted by additive Gaussian noise (`η_α`). The paper derives how to isolate this noise from the FK functions, resulting in a rotation noise `δf_R` and position noise `δf_p`. These noise quantities are shown to be approximately zero-mean Gaussian.
 - **Residuals:** The FK factor introduces unary residuals `r_fR_i` (for rotation) and `r_fp_i` (for position) at each time step `t_i`, computed as the difference between the measured FK value and the predicted value from the current state estimate.
 - **Covariance:** The covariance matrix `Σ_F_i` for the FK factor is computed by linearizing the noise model, relating `δf_R` and `δf_p` to the encoder noise `η_α`.

- **Preintegrated Contact Factors:** These factors relate the contact frame pose between successive time steps, accommodating foot slip noise.
 - **Rigid Contact Factor:**
 - **Assumption:** During rigid contact, the contact frame is assumed to remain fixed with respect to the world frame. This implies zero angular (`C_ω_WC`) and linear (`W_v_C`) velocities of the contact frame, with additive Gaussian noise (`η_ω`, `η_v`) to model slippage.
 - **Preintegration:** Similar to IMU preintegration, high-frequency contact measurements are integrated between two keyframes `t_i` and `t_j` (where contact is maintained). This yields relative transformations `ΔC_ij` (rotation) and `Δd_ij` (position).
 - **Noise Isolation:** The noise terms `δθ_ij` (for rotation) and `δd_ij` (for position) are isolated and approximated as zero-mean Gaussian.
 - **Residuals:** The residuals `r_ΔC_ij` and `r_Δd_ij` are derived from the preintegrated relative measurements.
 - **Covariance:** The covariance `Σ_C_ij` for constant contact noise is proportional to the contact duration `Δt_ij`. For time-varying noise, an iterative propagation method is used.
 - **Point Contact Factor:**
 - **Assumption:** For a point contact, the contact frame's position remains fixed in the world, but its orientation is unconstrained and can change (a 3-DOF constraint). This means `ΔC_ij` is not simply identity and is unobservable by encoders alone.
 - **Integration with IMU:** To address the unobservability of `ΔC_ij`, the factor leverages preintegrated IMU measurements (`ΔR_ik`) to account for the robot base's rotation between frames. The rotational aspect of the contact factor is effectively removed, and the focus shifts to the linear position constraint.
 - **Position Preintegration:** The relative position `R_i^T (d_j - d_i)` is preintegrated using IMU rotations and FK measurements. The resulting preintegrated position measurement `Δ˜d_ij` is ideally zero.
 - **Noise Decoupling:** The noise term `δd_ij` in this factor is shown to be decoupled from encoder and IMU noise (up to a first-order approximation).
 - **Residual:** The point contact factor contributes a residual `r_C_ij = R_i^T (d_j - d_i)`.
 - **Covariance:** The covariance `Σ_C_ik+1` is propagated iteratively based on the contact velocity noise `Σ_vd`.

**Implementation and Evaluation:**
The proposed factors were implemented in GTSAM (Georgia Tech Smoothing and Mapping), an open-source C++ library for factor graphs, utilizing its iSAM2 solver for incremental optimization. The IMU preintegration within GTSAM 4 was used.
- **Simulated Data:** A SimMechanics model of a Cassie robot performing a walking gait was used to generate ground-truth data, along with simulated IMU, joint encoder, and contact measurements, corrupted with Gaussian noise and biases. Simulated local loop closure (LC) measurements were also used for comparative analysis.
- **Real-world Data:** Experiments were conducted on a physical Cassie-series biped robot. IMU and joint encoder data were collected at 2 KHz. Binary contact detection was achieved by monitoring spring deflections in the robot's legs. The robot performed a walking loop in a laboratory setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed state estimation method was conducted using both simulated and real-world data from a Cassie-series biped robot.

**A. Simulated Evaluation using SimMechanics:**
The simulation experiments compared different combinations of factors: IMU-only, Local Loop Closure (LC) and IMU, IMU and Contact/FK (the proposed method), and LC and IMU and Contact/FK (all factors combined). The performance was assessed by analyzing the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the norm of translation and rotation errors between consecutive estimated poses relative to ground truth.

- **Drift Reduction:** The combination of forward kinematic, contact, and IMU factors demonstrably outperformed IMU-only estimation in terms of reducing drift. This improvement was attributed to the constraints provided by the FK and contact factors, which facilitated more accurate estimation of IMU biases.
- **Robustness to Visual Tracking Loss:** Notably, in approximately 20% of the cases for translational error, the proposed combination of FK, contact, and IMU factors exhibited superior performance compared to the combination of LC and IMU. This indicates that the developed leg odometry approach can offer a meaningful alternative or complement when visual odometry or loop closures are absent or unreliable.
- **Optimal Performance with All Factors:** The most accurate and robust state estimation was achieved when all available factors—LC, IMU, FK, and Contact—were combined. This suggests that leg odometry serves as a valuable additional modality that enhances overall localization accuracy.

**B. Real-world Experimental Results:**
Real-world data was collected from a Cassie-series robot performing a 100-second walking loop around a 4.5-meter section of a laboratory, beginning and ending at approximately the same location.

- **Significant Improvement over IMU-only:** When comparing the trajectory estimated using IMU-only data against the trajectory estimated with the addition of the proposed contact and forward kinematic factors, a substantial improvement was observed. The IMU-only estimate exhibited rapid and considerable drift due to integration errors and uncorrected IMU biases.
- **Reduced End-to-End Error:** With the integration of the contact and kinematic factors, the state estimate was significantly improved, resulting in an end-to-end translation error of approximately 1.3 meters for the entire loop. This contrasts sharply with the unacceptably large drift observed in the IMU-only scenario.
- **Validation of Core Hypothesis:** The preliminary real-world experiments validated the primary motivation of the work. Even in the absence of visual odometry or loop closure constraints, the inclusion of leg odometry enabled the system to estimate IMU biases and reduce positional drift, thereby providing a more accurate state estimate.

These findings suggest that the proposed method can enhance the overall localization accuracy of legged robots and provide a mechanism for maintaining state estimation robustness in situations where visual tracking systems fail.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of legged robotics by addressing a critical limitation in current state estimation methodologies: their dependence on visual data. The development and integration of forward kinematic and preintegrated contact factors into a factor graph framework offer several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Robustness in Challenging Environments:** By providing an alternative and complementary source of odometry, the proposed method increases the robustness of legged robot state estimation. Robots equipped with this system can maintain more accurate localization in environments where visual sensors may fail, such as in poor lighting, featureless terrains, or situations with occlusions. This broadens the operational envelope of legged robots, making them more suitable for deployment in diverse, unstructured, and unpredictable real-world settings (e.g., disaster response, exploration, industrial inspection).
- **Improved Localization Accuracy and Drift Reduction:** Both simulated and real-world evaluations demonstrate that incorporating leg odometry significantly reduces drift and improves the absolute localization accuracy of the robot. This enhanced precision is crucial for stable robot control, precise navigation, and effective interaction with the environment. The ability to estimate IMU biases without relying on visual input further contributes to long-term accuracy.
- **Foundation for Advanced Capabilities:** The reliable state estimation provided by this method forms a critical foundation for developing more advanced robotic capabilities.
 - **Mapping:** It enables the creation of local and global maps, which are essential for path planning, obstacle avoidance, and understanding the robot's surroundings.
 - **Control:** More accurate state information can directly inform the robot's controller, leading to more stable and agile locomotion across varying terrains.
 - **Sensor Fusion Architecture:** The factor graph approach allows for seamless integration with other sensor modalities, such as vision and LiDAR, potentially leading to even more robust and comprehensive SLAM systems that can gracefully handle the failure of individual sensors.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The stated intention to develop an open-source implementation of these techniques can facilitate wider adoption within the robotics community, enabling other researchers and developers to integrate these capabilities into their own legged robot platforms and further advance the state of the art.
- **Applicability to Legged Systems:** While demonstrated on a bipedal robot (Cassie), the underlying principles of incorporating kinematic chains and contact dynamics are generalizable to other multi-legged platforms (e.g., quadrupedal robots), suggesting broader applicability of the methodology.

In summary, this work represents a step towards more autonomous and resilient legged robots by providing a robust state estimation solution that can operate effectively even when visual perception is compromised, thereby expanding their potential for real-world deployment.
