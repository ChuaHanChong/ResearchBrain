# Legged Robot State-Estimation Through Combined Forward Kinematic and Preintegrated Contact Factors

> [!summary] Summary
> A state-estimation method for legged robots integrates forward kinematic and preintegrated contact factors into a factor graph framework, reducing reliance on visual sensors. This approach significantly reduces positional drift and improves robustness, achieving an end-to-end translation error of approximately 1.3 meters over a 100-second real-world robot trajectory.

## Problem

- Existing legged robot state estimation systems rely heavily on visual data, making them susceptible to failure in challenging environments with poor lighting, occlusions, or feature scarcity.
- Traditional leg odometry suffers from unbounded drift in position and yaw due to encoder noise, foot slippage, and a lack of external absolute corrections.
- Factor graph smoothing, while robust, primarily utilizes visual input, leaving a gap for the direct integration of unique odometry information derived from legged robot kinematics and contact dynamics.

## Method

- A ==Forward Kinematic (FK)== factor is developed to incorporate noisy joint encoder measurements into the factor graph, accurately relating the robot's base frame to its end-effector (contact frame).
- Preintegrated contact factors are formulated, including both rigid and point contact models, to describe the movement of the contact frame between time steps while explicitly accounting for foot slippage noise.
- These novel FK and contact factors are integrated alongside existing IMU factors within a unified factor graph smoothing framework, which is then optimized incrementally using the iSAM2 solver.

## Results

- Simulations showed that combining FK, contact, and IMU factors significantly reduced translational and rotational drift compared to IMU-only estimation, and in some cases, outperformed visual loop closure combined with IMU for translational error.
- Real-world experiments on a Cassie-series biped robot demonstrated substantial improvement over IMU-only estimation, achieving an end-to-end translation error of approximately **1.3** meters over a 100-second walking loop without visual odometry or loop closure.
- The proposed method successfully provided accurate state estimates and enabled effective IMU bias estimation in real-world scenarios where visual tracking systems might fail, validating its potential for enhanced robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leg odometry, when properly modeled and integrated into a factor graph, provides a robust and complementary source of information to IMUs, reducing drift and enhancing state estimation, especially in the absence of reliable visual data.
> - Preintegration techniques, typically applied to IMUs, can be effectively adapted for contact measurements, allowing for efficient fusion of high-frequency leg odometry data within the factor graph.
> - By fusing diverse sensor modalities (IMU, joint encoders, contact sensors) probabilistically, the framework can more accurately estimate IMU biases and maintain robust state estimation without relying on external visual inputs for absolute corrections.
