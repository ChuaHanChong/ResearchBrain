# LLaDA-V: Large Language Diffusion Models with Visual Instruction Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaDA-V introduces a purely diffusion-based Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) capable of end-to-end training and sampling across modalities. It achieves competitive performance against and often surpasses autoregressive MLLMs like LLaMA3-V across various multimodal benchmarks, demonstrating enhanced data scalability in several key domains.

## Problem

- Most state-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are built upon autoregressive (AR) language models, limiting exploration of alternative probabilistic modeling approaches.
- Previous attempts at purely diffusion-based MLLMs suffered from limited language modeling capacity, performing suboptimally compared to AR-based counterparts.
- The potential of discrete language diffusion models, like LLaDA, to handle visual inputs and multimodal instruction following remained largely unexplored.

## Method

- LLaDA-V integrates a ==large language diffusion model (LLaDA-8B-Instruct)== with a ==vision tower (SigLIP 2)== via a two-layer MLP connector.
- It employs a ==masked diffusion process== for training, learning to predict masked tokens in responses given clean image features and prompts, differing from autoregressive next-token prediction.
- Inference uses an ==iterative refinement process== starting from a fully masked sequence, employing low-confidence re-masking, and is trained via a ==three-stage strategy== for language-image alignment, visual instruction tuning, and multimodal reasoning enhancement.

## Results

- LLaDA-V achieved state-of-the-art performance among existing hybrid and pure diffusion MLLMs across **18** diverse multimodal tasks.
- Outperformed LLaMA3-V on **11** of 18 tasks, particularly excelling in multidisciplinary knowledge, mathematical reasoning, multi-image, and video understanding benchmarks.
- Demonstrated superior data scalability on MMMU and MMMU-Pro, with LLaDA-V trained on **1M** samples outperforming LLaMA3-V with **9M** samples on MMMU-Pro.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Purely diffusion-based MLLMs can achieve competitive performance against strong autoregressive MLLMs, challenging the dominant paradigm.
> - LLaDA-V demonstrates superior data scalability in certain domains like multidisciplinary knowledge and mathematical reasoning compared to its autoregressive counterpart, LLaMA3-V.
> - Fully bidirectional attention across all tokens in the diffusion process proved more effective for multimodal understanding than a dialogue causal mask.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen rapid advancements, enabling models to process and generate responses across various modalities, including text, images, audio, and video. The vast majority of these state-of-the-art MLLMs, such as LLaVA, Qwen2-VL, and LLaMA3-V, are built upon **autoregressive (AR) language models**. This autoregressive paradigm, where tokens are generated sequentially one after another, has proven highly effective for language generation and understanding due to its inherent causal structure.

However, the authors point out that relying solely on autoregressive models leaves substantial room for exploring alternative probabilistic modeling approaches. This paper specifically focuses on **diffusion models**, which have gained prominence in generative AI, particularly in image and video generation. While some prior work has attempted to integrate diffusion models into MLLMs, these efforts have generally fallen into two categories:
1. **Hybrid Autoregressive-Diffusion Models:** These models leverage autoregressive components for strong language modeling while incorporating diffusion for other modalities or specific generative tasks (e.g., MetaMorph, Show-o, JanusFlow, Orthus). They don't represent a "purely" diffusion-based approach for language.
2. **Pure Diffusion Models with Limited Language Capacity:** A few attempts have explored purely diffusion-based approaches for MLLMs (e.g., D-DiT, Unified Multimodal Discrete Diffusion), but they have often suffered from limited language modeling capacity, leading to suboptimal performance compared to AR-based counterparts.

LLaDA-V directly addresses this gap by proposing a **purely diffusion-based MLLM for both training and sampling**. It leverages the recent breakthroughs in **discrete language diffusion models**, specifically LLaDA, which has already demonstrated strong language modeling capabilities comparable to leading autoregressive LLMs. By extending LLaDA to handle visual inputs through a visual instruction tuning framework, LLaDA-V aims to challenge the prevailing autoregressive dominance in MLLMs. This work represents a significant step towards validating diffusion models as a powerful and competitive paradigm for end-to-end multimodal understanding and generation, moving beyond their traditional strength in visual synthesis. It explores whether the unique properties of diffusion models, such as their non-autoregressive generation process, can offer advantages in multimodal contexts, potentially leading to better data scalability or different types of emergent capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate a fundamental question: **"Can a purely diffusion-based MLLM (both training and sampling) achieve performance comparable to autoregressive-based models?"**

This objective is motivated by several factors:
1. **Exploring Alternative Paradigms:** The current landscape of MLLMs is heavily dominated by autoregressive models. The authors are motivated to explore and validate alternative probabilistic modeling approaches, specifically diffusion models, which offer a different generative mechanism (iterative refinement of noisy data rather than sequential token prediction).
2. **Leveraging Advances in Language Diffusion Models:** The recent success of LLaDA in achieving language modeling performance competitive with strong autoregressive LLMs (like LLaMA3-8B) provides a strong foundation. This breakthrough suggests that the limitations of earlier discrete diffusion models in language tasks might be overcome, making them viable backbones for MLLMs.
3. **Extending LLaDA's Capabilities:** While LLaDA showed remarkable progress in pure language modeling, its potential in multimodal understanding remained largely unexplored. This work aims to bridge that gap by effectively extending LLaDA to integrate visual information and multimodal instruction following.
4. **Investigating Architectural Effectiveness and Data Scalability:** The authors seek to demonstrate that a purely diffusion-based MLLM can not only perform well but also exhibit favorable properties like data scalability, which is crucial for building large, general-purpose MLLMs. They aim to show that even with a language backbone that is "slightly weaker" in pure text generation, the overall multimodal framework can be highly competitive.
5. **Paving the Way for Future Research:** By demonstrating the promise of diffusion models in multimodal contexts, the authors aim to inspire further investigation and development in this less-explored area, potentially leading to new architectures and capabilities that differ from autoregressive designs.

In essence, the paper aims to prove the viability and competitiveness of diffusion models as a foundational technology for general-purpose MLLMs, offering a fresh perspective beyond the prevailing autoregressive paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LLaDA-V is designed as a purely diffusion-based MLLM, integrating a large language diffusion model (LLaDA) with visual instruction tuning. Its methodology comprises several key components:

#### 3.1. Architecture

- **Language Tower:** The core of LLaDA-V is **LLaDA-8B-Instruct**, a representative large language diffusion model. LLaDA's architecture is based on LLaMA3 but crucially replaces the causal transformer with a **bidirectional transformer**, removing the causal mask and not supporting KV caching. It retains common LLM techniques like RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and RoPE.
- **Vision Tower:** **SigLIP 2 (siglip2-so400m-patch14-384)** is used to convert images into visual representations. It processes images at 384x384 pixels, producing 729 visual tokens per image.
- **MLP Connector (Projector):** A randomly initialized two-layer MLP connects the vision tower to the language model. Its role is to project the visual features into LLaDA's language embedding space, enabling joint processing of visual and textual inputs. This setup follows the "visual instruction tuning" framework, known for its effectiveness and data efficiency in MLLMs.

#### 3.2. Training Objective

LLaDA-V's training objective extends LLaDA's masked diffusion process to handle multi-turn multimodal dialogues. Unlike autoregressive models that use next-token prediction, LLaDA-V's objective is to **predict masked tokens within the response** given clean image features and prompts.
- **Forward Process:** Each token in a response (`x0`) is replaced by a special mask token `[M]` with a probability `t` (sampled uniformly from), yielding a corrupted sentence `xt`. The probability `alpha_t = 1-t` signifies the probability of a token remaining unmasked.
- **Reverse Process & Training Objective:** The model learns to reconstruct the original response (`x0`) from the masked one (`xt`). The formal training objective (Eq. 1) is an upper bound of the negative log-likelihood for masked tokens. Intuitively, it trains the model to predict the original masked tokens, conditioned on the unmasked image features and prompts.
- **Attention Mechanism:** The authors explore attention mechanisms within the language tower for multi-turn dialogues. They conducted ablation studies between a "dialogue causal mask" (causality between turns, bidirectional within turns) and a "no mask" (fully bidirectional attention across all tokens). The **fully bidirectional attention ("no mask")** was chosen for LLaDA-V due to its superior performance, hypothesized to stem from a more comprehensive understanding of the entire dialogue context during mask prediction.

#### 3.3. Inference Process

Inference in LLaDA-V involves iterative response generation using the **reverse process of the masked diffusion model**, rather than next-token prediction.
- When a new prompt is given, the model generates responses turn-by-turn.
- For each response, generation starts with a **fully masked sequence** (all `[M]` tokens).
- The sequence is iteratively refined by transitioning from a noisy state (`xt`) to a less noisy state (`xs`, where `s < t`).
- In each step, LLaDA-V predicts all `[M]` tokens.
- A fraction `s/t` of these newly predicted tokens are then **re-masked** to `[M]`, while the rest (`1-s/t`) are kept.
- Crucially, LLaDA-V employs LLaDA's **"low-confidence re-masking strategy"**, preferentially re-masking predictions with the lowest confidence scores to improve quality, rather than random re-masking.

#### 3.4. Multi-Stage Training Strategy

A three-stage training paradigm is adopted, building on established MLLM practices:
- **Stage 1: Language-Image Alignment:** Only the MLP projector is trained to align visual representations from SigLIP 2 with LLaDA's word embeddings. The language and vision towers remain frozen. This stage uses the LLaVA-Pretrain dataset (558K samples).
- **Stage 2: Visual Instruction Tuning:** The entire model (language tower, vision tower, projector) is fine-tuned on large-scale multimodal instruction data from MAmmoTH-VL.
 - **Single Image Training:** 10M single-image multimodal data to establish basic image understanding.
 - **OneVision Training:** ~2M diverse multimodal samples (single-image, multi-image, video) to expand capabilities to complex scenarios.
- **Stage 3: Multimodal Reasoning Enhancement:** Focuses on improving reasoning for complex tasks.
 - **Reasoning Training:** Training on 900K QA pairs with reasoning chains from VisualWebInstruct.
 - **Balanced Reasoning Training:** A mixed dataset of VisualWebInstruct and MAmmoTH-VL's OneVision data is used. Prompts are tagged (e.g., '/no_think' or '/think') to encourage direct answers or explicit reasoning, enhancing response flexibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical investigation of LLaDA-V yielded several intriguing and significant results:

#### 4.1. Data Scalability

- LLaDA-V consistently shows performance improvements with increasing training data, demonstrating that it **benefits from data scalability**.
- Despite using a "slightly weaker language tower" (LLaDA-8B) compared to LLaMA3-V (LLaMA3-8B), LLaDA-V exhibits **superior data scalability on several benchmarks**, particularly excelling in multidisciplinary knowledge (e.g., MMMU, MMMU-Pro) and mathematical reasoning tasks. For instance, LLaDA-V trained with only 1M samples outperformed LLaMA3-V trained with 9M samples on MMMU-Pro.
- However, LLaDA-V lagged behind LLaMA3-V on benchmarks assessing chart/document understanding (e.g., AI2D) and real-world scene understanding (e.g., RealworldQA) when comparing data scalability.

#### 4.2. Comprehensive Benchmark Evaluations

LLaDA-V was extensively benchmarked across 18 diverse multimodal tasks, categorized into multidisciplinary knowledge & mathematical reasoning, chart & doc understanding, real-world scene understanding, and multi-image & video understanding.

- **Comparison with Hybrid and Pure Diffusion MLLMs:** LLaDA-V achieved **state-of-the-art performance** among existing hybrid autoregressive-diffusion models (e.g., MetaMorph, Show-o, JanusFlow, Orthus) and pure diffusion models (e.g., D-DiT). This is a strong validation of its purely diffusion-based architecture and training strategy.
- **Comparison with Autoregressive Baseline (LLaMA3-V):**
 - Despite LLaDA's pure language modeling performance being marginally inferior to LLaMA3-8B, LLaDA-V demonstrated **superior performance across 11 of 18 tasks** compared to LLaMA3-V (which uses LLaMA3-8B as its language tower and shares identical training protocols).
 - Specifically, LLaDA-V outperformed LLaMA3-V on most multidisciplinary knowledge and mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MMMU, MMMU-Pro), and also showed superior performance in multi-image and video understanding tasks (e.g., MuirBench, MLVU).
 - Its performance remained less competitive on tasks focused on chart/document understanding (e.g., AI2D, DocVQA) and real-world scene comprehension (e.g., RealworldQA) when directly compared to LLaMA3-V.
- **Comparison with Strong Autoregressive MLLMs (Qwen2-VL):**
 - LLaDA-V generally underperformed Qwen2-VL, which uses the more powerful Qwen2-7B language backbone.
 - However, LLaDA-V significantly **narrowed the performance gap** on certain tasks, achieving comparable results on specific benchmarks like MMStar (60.1 for LLaDA-V vs. 60.7 for Qwen2-VL), which is notable given LLaDA-8B's weaker textual performance compared to Qwen2-7B. This suggests the effectiveness of LLaDA-V's multimodal architecture despite its language backbone's limitations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Attention Mask

- The study compared "dialogue causal" (causal between turns, bidirectional within) and "no mask" (fully bidirectional) attention strategies.
- The **"no mask" (bidirectional attention)** strategy achieved superior performance, outperforming the "dialogue causal" approach on 7 out of 12 benchmarks. This finding suggests that a comprehensive understanding of the entire dialogue context (enabled by bidirectional attention) is beneficial for multimodal understanding in LLaDA-V.

Collectively, these findings highlight the effectiveness of the LLaDA-V framework and, more broadly, the significant promise of large language diffusion models in multimodal contexts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LLaDA-V represents a substantial contribution to the field of MLLMs and generative AI, with several key significances and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Validation of Diffusion Models for Multimodality

The most significant impact of LLaDA-V is its demonstration that a **purely diffusion-based MLLM** can achieve highly competitive performance, even rivaling and in some cases surpassing strong autoregressive baselines (like LLaMA3-V), especially considering its relatively weaker language backbone. This challenges the long-held dominance of autoregressive models in MLLMs and validates diffusion models as a viable and powerful alternative for end-to-end multimodal understanding and generation. It indicates that the unique properties of diffusion processes, such as iterative refinement and potentially different scaling behaviors, can be leveraged effectively in complex multimodal tasks.

#### 5.2. Opens New Research Avenues

By proving the concept of a performant diffusion-based MLLM, LLaDA-V opens up entirely new research directions. Future work can now explore:
- **Optimized Architectures for Diffusion MLLMs:** Designing diffusion-specific architectures that might inherently benefit from the non-autoregressive nature.
- **Advanced Training Strategies:** Developing novel training objectives and techniques tailored for multimodal diffusion, potentially leading to faster convergence or more robust multimodal alignment.
- **Scaling Laws for Diffusion MLLMs:** Investigating how diffusion-based MLLMs scale with data, model size, and compute, which might differ from autoregressive models.
- **Addressing Current Limitations:** Research into better image processing strategies (e.g., native dynamic resolution support as seen in Qwen2-VL) and more effective mitigation of hallucinations within a diffusion framework.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Data Scalability

The observation that LLaDA-V exhibits superior data scalability on key benchmarks (like MMMU-Pro) compared to its autoregressive counterpart (LLaMA3-V) is a promising result. This suggests that diffusion-based MLLMs might be more efficient in learning from large-scale multimodal instruction data in certain domains, potentially leading to more robust models with less data or faster convergence.

#### 5.4. Diverse Multimodal Capabilities

LLaDA-V's strong performance across diverse tasks, particularly in multidisciplinary knowledge, mathematical reasoning, and multi-image/video understanding, showcases the versatility of the diffusion paradigm. This suggests that diffusion models can effectively capture complex reasoning and contextual understanding required for various multimodal scenarios.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact and Ethical Considerations

- **Inspiration for Probabilistic Modeling:** LLaDA-V can inspire broader exploration of alternative probabilistic modeling approaches in AI, fostering innovation beyond current paradigms.
- **Addressing Hallucinations:** Like all advanced MLLMs, LLaDA-V may generate hallucinations (factually incorrect or non-existent content). The authors acknowledge this limitation and suggest that future work on scaling up data and developing more advanced alignment techniques could help mitigate this issue. This highlights a persistent challenge in generative AI that requires ongoing research.
- **Resource Implications:** Developing and training such large models requires significant computational resources, which raises questions about environmental impact and accessibility for smaller research groups.

In conclusion, LLaDA-V serves as a critical proof-of-concept, shifting the perception of diffusion models from primarily visual generation to a strong contender in the general-purpose multimodal AI space. Its findings underscore the potential of this paradigm to offer a competitive and innovative alternative to autoregressive MLLMs, paving the way for a more diverse and robust landscape of AI models.
