# Autoregressive Image Generation without Vector Quantization

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method enables autoregressive models to generate images using continuous-valued representations, bypassing the need for vector quantization. It integrates diffusion processes to model per-token probability distributions, achieving competitive FID scores as low as 1.55 on ImageNet 256x256 while maintaining fast generation speeds of less than 0.3 seconds per image.

## Problem

- Autoregressive (AR) models for image generation conventionally rely on discrete, vector-quantized (VQ) tokens, which are challenging to train and can lead to information loss.
- The discretization step in VQ-based AR models limits reconstruction quality and subsequently the fidelity of generated images.
- There was a lack of effective methods for applying autoregressive models directly to continuous-valued image representations without an intermediate quantization step, constraining AR models primarily to discrete domains.

## Method

- Introduces **Diffusion Loss** as a novel ==loss function== that enables ==autoregressive models== to operate directly on ==continuous-valued tokens==, eliminating the need for vector quantization.
- The ==Diffusion Loss== models the ==per-token probability distribution== by training a small ==denoising MLP== jointly with the main autoregressive network, leveraging principles from ==denoising diffusion probabilistic models==.
- Proposes a generalized **Masked Autoregressive (MAR)** framework that unifies standard autoregressive and masked autoregressive variants, allowing for efficient generation by predicting multiple tokens simultaneously using ==bidirectional attention==.
- The approach utilizes existing pre-trained ==continuous-valued tokenizers== (e.g., ==KL-16== from LDM) and supports ==Classifier-Free Guidance== for improved sample quality, while the denoising MLP is designed to be lightweight and efficient.

## Results

- Diffusion Loss consistently and significantly outperforms traditional cross-entropy loss across all AR and MAR variants, reducing FID by **50-60%** (e.g., from **8.79** to **3.50** without Classifier-Free Guidance) for the default MAR setup.
- The proposed MAR-H model achieves a best FID of **1.55** with Classifier-Free Guidance on ImageNet 256x256, demonstrating competitive performance against leading generative models including state-of-the-art latent diffusion models.
- Masked Autoregressive (MAR) models with Diffusion Loss can generate high-quality images in less than **0.3 seconds**, offering an excellent speed-accuracy trade-off.
- Random-order autoregressive generation provides better quality than fixed raster order, and using bidirectional attention in Masked Autoregressive (MAR) leads to substantial quality gains (e.g., from **13.07** to **3.43** FID without Classifier-Free Guidance).
- The framework allows for effective sampling control via a temperature parameter, influencing image diversity and fidelity in a manner analogous to discrete autoregressive models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autoregressive models do not inherently require discrete tokens; their power lies in sequential prediction, which can be applied to continuous data.
> - Diffusion processes can effectively model complex continuous per-token probability distributions, serving as a powerful loss function for continuous autoregressive generation.
> - Masked Autoregressive (MAR) generation with bidirectional attention, when combined with continuous token modeling, significantly enhances image quality and generation speed.
> - The proposed Diffusion Loss is flexible and works effectively with various pre-trained continuous-valued tokenizers, including those from Latent Diffusion Models and modified VQ-tokenizers, indicating adaptability to different representation spaces.
> - The small denoising MLP enabling the Diffusion Loss adds minimal computational overhead, making the approach scalable and efficient for large models and high-resolution generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in the field of generative AI, particularly concerning the application of autoregressive (AR) models to continuous-valued data like images.

**Autoregressive Models in Context:** Autoregressive models have been the cornerstone of natural language processing (NLP), exemplified by models like GPT. Their success stems from their ability to model sequences by predicting the next token based on all preceding ones. In NLP, tokens are inherently discrete (words, sub-word units), making categorical distributions and cross-entropy loss natural fits.

**AR for Image Generation – The Conventional Approach:** When applying AR models to images, the conventional wisdom, as stated by the authors, has been to first discretize the image data. This typically involves training a discrete-valued tokenizer, such as Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAEs) or VQ-GANs (e.g., used in VQGAN, DALL-E 1). These tokenizers convert continuous pixel data into a finite vocabulary of discrete tokens, allowing AR models to then operate in this discrete space, analogous to language. However, this approach introduces several challenges:
- **Tokenizer Difficulty:** Discrete tokenizers are complex and sensitive to train.
- **Reconstruction Quality:** The quantization step can lead to a loss of information, impacting reconstruction quality compared to continuous-valued counterparts.

**Emergence of Diffusion Models:** In parallel, diffusion models (e.g., DDPM, ADM, Latent Diffusion Models (LDM), Diffusion Transformers (DiT)) have emerged as a dominant paradigm for high-fidelity image generation. These models operate by progressively denoising a continuous latent representation, effectively modeling the joint distribution of all pixels or latent tokens simultaneously. LDMs, in particular, achieve efficiency by performing diffusion in a compressed continuous latent space.

**Bridging the Gap:** This paper proposes to bridge the gap between autoregressive modeling and the continuous nature of images *without* relying on discrete quantization. It challenges the inherent assumption that AR models *must* operate on discrete tokens. Instead of discretizing the image, it aims to keep the underlying representation continuous and leverage the power of diffusion models to model the *per-token probability distribution*.

**Distinction from Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs/DiT):** A crucial distinction highlighted by the authors is that while LDMs/DiT use diffusion to model the *joint distribution of all tokens* (or pixels), their method uses diffusion to model the *conditional probability distribution of each individual token* within an autoregressive sequence. This allows them to maintain the sequential prediction nature of AR while benefiting from diffusion's ability to model complex continuous distributions.

**Comparison with Related Work:**
- **GIVT:** Acknowledged as a similar effort towards continuous-valued tokens in sequence models. However, GIVT uses Gaussian mixture models for token distribution, which might be less flexible than the proposed diffusion process for arbitrary distributions.
- **Diffusion for Representation Learning (DiffMAE, DARL):** The paper differentiates itself from works that use diffusion for self-supervised representation learning, where diversity in generated samples is not the primary goal. This work specifically focuses on high-quality *image generation*.
- **Masked Generative Models (MaskGIT, MAGE):** The paper innovatively generalizes these models, which predict masked subsets of tokens, into a unified "Masked Autoregressive (MAR)" framework. This demonstrates a broader understanding of autoregression than just strict left-to-right generation.

In essence, this work represents a significant step in expanding the applicability and performance of autoregressive models for continuous data, by integrating the strengths of diffusion modeling at a finer, per-token granularity, distinguishing itself from both traditional VQ-based AR and full-latent diffusion models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to fundamentally challenge and overcome the "conventional wisdom" that autoregressive models for image generation necessitate vector-quantized tokens. The authors explicitly state their aim to address the question: "Is it necessary for autoregressive models to be coupled with vector-quantized representations?"

This core objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Overcoming Limitations of Discrete Tokenizers:**
 - **Training Difficulty:** Vector-quantized tokenizers (e.g., VQ-VAE, VQ-GAN) are notoriously difficult to train, often requiring complex strategies to handle gradient approximations.
 - **Reconstruction Quality:** The discretization process inherent in VQ tokenizers can lead to a loss of information and lower reconstruction quality compared to continuous-valued representations, which can subsequently limit the quality of the generated images.
 - The paper seeks to eliminate the need for such discrete tokenizers, thereby simplifying the pipeline and potentially improving generation fidelity.

- **Leveraging Autoregressive Power for Continuous Data:**
 - Autoregressive models have proven immensely powerful in sequential data modeling, particularly in natural language. The authors recognize that the "predicting next tokens based on previous ones" nature of AR is conceptually independent of whether those tokens are discrete or continuous.
 - The motivation is to unlock the full potential of autoregressive sequence modeling for continuous-valued domains like image generation, unburdened by an artificial discretization step.

- **Harnessing Diffusion Models for Distribution Modeling:**
 - The success of diffusion models in modeling arbitrary and complex probability distributions is well-established. Instead of using a simple categorical distribution for discrete tokens, the authors seek to leverage the expressive power of diffusion processes to model the per-token probability distribution in a continuous space. This is expected to lead to more accurate and flexible distribution modeling.

- **Achieving High-Quality and Efficient Image Generation:**
 - The ultimate goal is to develop an image generator that achieves strong results in terms of image quality (low FID, high IS) while enjoying the inherent speed advantages of sequence modeling, especially when compared to computationally intensive pixel-space diffusion models.
 - The unification of standard autoregressive and masked autoregressive (MAR) variants into a generalized framework further aims to explore more efficient and effective generation strategies, such as predicting multiple tokens simultaneously.

In essence, the paper is motivated by a desire to push the boundaries of autoregressive generative modeling by making it more versatile and powerful for continuous data, drawing on the strengths of diffusion models, and addressing the practical limitations of prior discrete tokenization approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology revolves around replacing the traditional categorical cross-entropy loss in autoregressive (AR) models with a novel "Diffusion Loss" that operates on continuous-valued tokens. This allows AR models to predict continuous quantities directly, eliminating the need for vector quantization.

#### 3.1. Rethinking Discrete-Valued Tokens

The authors begin by deconstructing the role of discrete tokens. They argue that the essential requirements for an AR model are a loss function to measure the per-token probability distribution and a sampler to draw from it. While discrete tokens conveniently lead to categorical distributions with simple cross-entropy loss and sampling, this convenience does not imply necessity. If an alternative mechanism for continuous distributions exists, AR models can operate without discretization.

#### 3.2. Diffusion Loss (The Core Innovation)

- **Modeling Per-Token Probability:** For a continuous-valued ground-truth token `x`, the autoregressive model produces a conditioning vector `z`. The goal is to model `p(x|z)`.
- **Loss Function Definition:** This is achieved by leveraging principles from denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs). The Diffusion Loss `L(z, x)` is defined as the mean squared error between a sampled noise vector `ε` and the predicted noise `ε_θ(x_t|t, z)`. Here, `x_t` is a noise-corrupted version of `x`, `t` is a time step, and `ε_θ` is a small denoising MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) conditioned on `t` and `z`.
 - `L(z, x) = E_{ε,t}[||ε - ε_θ(x_t|t, z)||^2]`
- **Joint Training:** Crucially, the small denoising MLP (`ε_θ`) is trained *jointly* with the main autoregressive network. The gradient from `L(z, x)` is backpropagated to `z` (the output of the AR network), thus updating the AR model's parameters. Conceptually, this MLP acts as a learnable loss function for `z`.
- **Sampler:** At inference time, samples `x ~ p(x|z)` are drawn using a reverse diffusion procedure, starting from random noise and iteratively denoising it conditioned on `z`. This process can also incorporate a temperature parameter `τ`, analogous to discrete AR models, to control sample diversity by scaling the noise variance during sampling.

#### 3.3. Generalized Autoregressive Models

The paper unifies various sequential generation paradigms under a generalized autoregressive framework compatible with Diffusion Loss:
- **Standard Autoregressive (AR):** Predicts tokens in a fixed order (e.g., raster scan), one by one. Can use causal attention in a Transformer.
- **Random-Order Autoregressive:** Predicts one token at a time, but the order is a random permutation. Positional embeddings are used to convey the target position.
- **Masked Autoregressive (MAR):** This is the most generalized form. It predicts *multiple* tokens simultaneously based on known/predicted tokens. This is akin to masked generative models (like MaskGIT). The process is still autoregressive in that it predicts a "next set-of-tokens" given previous sets.
 - **Bidirectional Attention:** For MAR, an MAE-style Transformer with bidirectional attention is employed. This allows all known tokens to communicate with each other and with all unknown tokens, leading to richer context. At training, a random masking ratio is used. At inference, tokens are progressively predicted (denoised) in a random order, iteratively reducing the mask.
 - Unlike some masked generative models that sample *locations* based on confidence, MAR samples tokens in a fully randomized order and applies temperature *to each token's distribution*.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Tokenizer:** The method utilizes existing *pre-trained, continuous-valued tokenizers*, specifically the KL-16 version from Latent Diffusion Models (LDM). It also demonstrates flexibility by adapting to VQ-16 (treating its pre-quantization latents as continuous) and even tokenizers with mismatched strides (e.g., KL-8).
- **Transformer:** A standard ViT-style Transformer architecture is used, with varying sizes (B, L, H) for scaling experiments. Positional embeddings and class tokens are incorporated.
- **Denoising MLP:** A small MLP with residual blocks is used for `ε_θ`, conditioned on `t` and `z` via AdaLN. It is designed to be efficient, adding minimal computational overhead.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** The framework naturally supports CFG by conditioning on a dummy class token during training and combining conditional and unconditional noise predictions at inference.

In summary, the methodology creatively marries the sequential nature of autoregressive models with the expressive power of diffusion models, applying the latter at a granular, per-token level. This enables AR models to operate directly on continuous data, opening up new avenues for high-quality and efficient generative modeling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling experimental results, primarily on ImageNet 256x256 resolution, demonstrating the effectiveness, flexibility, and efficiency of their proposed Diffusion Loss and generalized autoregressive framework.

#### 4.1. Superiority of Diffusion Loss over Cross-Entropy Loss

- **Consistent Outperformance:** Across all evaluated AR and MAR variants (raster-order AR, random-order AR with causal attention, MAR with bidirectional attention predicting one token, and default MAR predicting multiple tokens), Diffusion Loss consistently and significantly outperforms the traditional cross-entropy loss.
- **Quantitative Improvement:** For the default MAR setup, Diffusion Loss reduces FID (Fréchet Inception Distance, lower is better) by a substantial 50-60% (e.g., from 8.79 to 3.50 w/o CFG), indicating a drastic improvement in generation quality. This is attributed to the continuous-valued tokens and the diffusion process's superior ability to model distributions.

#### 4.2. Flexibility and Adaptability of Diffusion Loss

- **Tokenizer Agnostic:** Diffusion Loss proves highly flexible, working effectively with various types of tokenizers:
 - **Continuous-valued (KL-16):** Achieves the best performance.
 - **VQ-tokenizers (VQ-16):** Even when using a VQ tokenizer, treating the *pre-quantization continuous latents* as tokens for Diffusion Loss still outperforms using cross-entropy on the quantized tokens (7.82 FID vs. 8.79 FID), highlighting the loss's inherent advantages.
 - **Mismatched Strides (KL-8):** Successfully applied to a tokenizer with a different stride by grouping tokens, demonstrating robustness.
 - **Alternative Architectures (Consistency Decoder):** Compatible with tokenizers from different design paradigms.
- This flexibility implies that future improvements in continuous-valued tokenizers can directly benefit their autoregressive generation framework.

#### 4.3. Efficiency and Impact of Denoising MLP

- **Lightweight Component:** The denoising MLP, which facilitates the Diffusion Loss, is found to be small (e.g., 2M parameters) and efficient.
- **Minimal Overhead:** It adds only about 5% extra parameters to the MAR-L model and contributes only around 10% to the overall inference time, despite its crucial role.
- **Scalability:** Increasing MLP width (from 256 to 1536 channels) improves quality with negligible impact on inference time, suggesting it's not the bottleneck.

#### 4.4. Sampling Control with Diffusion Loss

- **Sufficient Steps:** Similar to full diffusion models, 100 sampling steps are sufficient for strong generation quality, even when trained with 1000 steps.
- **Temperature Control:** The temperature parameter (τ) for diffusion sampling significantly influences FID and Inception Score (IS), confirming its critical role in balancing diversity and fidelity, analogous to its role in discrete autoregressive models.

#### 4.5. Efficacy of Generalized Autoregressive Models

- **Random Order Superiority:** Moving from a fixed raster order to a random order in AR models significantly improves FID (e.g., from 19.23 to 13.07 w/o CFG).
- **Bidirectional Attention's Power (MAR):** Replacing causal attention with bidirectional attention (Masked Autoregressive, MAR) yields a "massive gain" in quality (e.g., from 13.07 to 3.43 FID w/o CFG), indicating the benefits of enhanced token communication.
- **Speed-Accuracy Trade-off:** MAR, by predicting multiple tokens per step, offers an excellent speed/accuracy trade-off. It can generate images at a rate of `< 0.3 seconds per image` while achieving an FID of `< 2.0`. This is notably faster and often more accurate than state-of-the-art latent diffusion models like DiT-XL/2.

#### 4.6. State-of-the-Art Benchmarking (ImageNet 256x256)

- **Competitive Performance:** The proposed MAR-H model with Diffusion Loss achieves a best FID of **1.55** with Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG). This places it among the leading generative models on ImageNet 256x256, outperforming most previous token-based methods and proving competitive with highly optimized pixel-based and latent diffusion models (e.g., DiT).
- **Scaling Behavior:** The paper observes encouraging scaling behavior with larger model sizes (MAR-B, -L, -H), suggesting potential for even better results with further scaling.

#### 4.7. Other Observations

- **Pixel-space Applicability:** The concept can be directly applied to pixel space (demonstrated on ImageNet 64x64, achieving FID 2.93), though it's computationally more expensive at higher resolutions.
- **L2 Loss Inadequacy:** A naive L2 loss for continuous tokens leads to disastrous results, underscoring the necessity of a sophisticated distribution modeling approach like Diffusion Loss.

In summary, the results unequivocally support the paper's central hypothesis: autoregressive image generation does not require vector quantization, and by leveraging Diffusion Loss within a generalized autoregressive framework (especially MAR), it can achieve high fidelity, flexibility, and impressive generation speed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work carries significant implications for the field of generative AI, particularly in bridging the gap between sequence modeling paradigms and continuous data generation.

#### 5.1. Challenging Conventional Wisdom and Opening New Research Avenues

The most profound impact of this paper lies in its direct challenge to the long-held assumption that autoregressive models require discrete tokens for image generation. By demonstrating the efficacy of "Autoregressive Image Generation without Vector Quantization," it fundamentally redefines the scope of AR models beyond discrete domains. This opens up a "largely uncharted realm" where the interdependence of tokens is modeled by autoregression, while their individual continuous probability distributions are handled by diffusion. This conceptual breakthrough could inspire entirely new architectures and training paradigms.

#### 5.2. Advancing Autoregressive Image Generation Performance

By achieving state-of-the-art or highly competitive results on ImageNet (e.g., 1.55 FID), the paper pushes the boundaries of what is possible with autoregressive models for images. The proposed Diffusion Loss significantly improves generation quality compared to traditional cross-entropy, and the generalized Masked Autoregressive (MAR) framework offers an excellent speed-accuracy trade-off, outperforming other leading generative models (like DiT) in certain aspects. This makes AR models a more viable and attractive option for high-fidelity image synthesis.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Flexibility and Modularity

The Diffusion Loss's compatibility with various pre-trained tokenizers, including continuous-valued, VQ-based (when treating latents as continuous), and even those with mismatched strides, signifies a high degree of flexibility. This modularity means that advancements in tokenizer design can directly translate into improvements in the autoregressive generator, fostering independent progress in different components of the generative pipeline.

#### 5.4. Potential for Broader Applications beyond Images

The authors explicitly hope that this work "will motivate the use of autoregressive generation in other continuous-valued domains and applications." This includes, but is not limited to, video generation, 3D data synthesis, audio generation, and robotics (as conceptually related to Diffusion Policy). By demonstrating a robust way to apply sequence models to continuous data, the paper lays groundwork for innovations in diverse fields where continuous output is required.

#### 5.5. Efficiency Gains for Large-Scale Models

The observed speed advantage of MAR, particularly its ability to generate high-quality images in less than 0.3 seconds, is critical for large-scale applications. The potential to "significantly reduce the training and inference cost of these large models" (e.g., text-to-image or text-to-video generation) suggests a path towards more accessible and deployable generative AI systems.

#### 5.6. Fundamental Research Contribution

Beyond immediate applications, the paper contributes a novel method for modeling probability distributions (Diffusion Loss) that could potentially replace traditional loss functions in various contexts. It refines our understanding of autoregression, demonstrating that its core principle is independent of the discrete/continuous nature of tokens.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as reliance on existing tokenizers and potential artifacts in generated images, its fundamental contribution in decoupling autoregression from discrete quantization marks a significant step forward, offering a powerful and flexible new approach to generative modeling.
