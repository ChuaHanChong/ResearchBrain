# Diffusion Policy Attacker: Crafting Adversarial Attacks for Diffusion-based Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces DP-Attacker, the first comprehensive framework to analyze the adversarial vulnerability of diffusion policies in robotic control. The framework demonstrates that both imperceptible digital perturbations and physical adversarial patches can effectively disrupt robotic task completion, often reducing success rates by over 50%.

## Problem

- Diffusion policies have shown strong performance in multi-modal behavior generation for robotic control, but their vulnerability to adversarial attacks was largely unexamined.
- The potential safety and security risks of deploying diffusion-based robotic policies in real-world environments without understanding their susceptibility to manipulated inputs remained unknown.
- Traditional adversarial attack methodologies were not specifically tailored for the unique architecture and internal workings of diffusion models, particularly their noise prediction mechanism.

## Method

- Researchers at ==Georgia Institute== of Technology introduced DP-Attacker, a comprehensive framework for exposing and analyzing the vulnerability of diffusion policies to adversarial manipulations.
- The framework devises various attack methodologies categorized as global vs. patched, online vs. offline, and targeted vs. untargeted, providing a structured approach to assessing vulnerabilities.
- DP-Attacker formulates adversarial optimization to disrupt the internal noise prediction mechanism of diffusion models by maximizing a noise prediction loss, causing incorrect denoising and erroneous actions.

## Results

- DP-Attacker reduced the success rate of diffusion policies by over **50%** across six robotic manipulation tasks using imperceptible digital perturbations.
- Small adversarial patches placed in the environment significantly degraded policy performance, demonstrating the feasibility of physical world attacks.
- The proposed noise prediction loss approach for crafting attacks was more effective and computationally efficient than end-to-end optimization, requiring substantially less time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The encoder component, which maps raw observations to latent representations, is the most susceptible part of diffusion policies to adversarial attacks, exhibiting significant feature distortion from small input perturbations.
> - Attacks that target the internal noise prediction mechanism of diffusion models are both more computationally efficient and effective than end-to-end optimization through the entire diffusion process.
> - Offline, pre-computed adversarial perturbations can effectively transfer across different frames and timesteps, demonstrating that real-time, computationally intensive attacks are not always necessary to disrupt policy performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful class of generative models, achieving remarkable success across various domains, including recent applications in robotic control through diffusion policies. These policies have demonstrated superior performance in multi-modal behavior generation for robotic manipulation tasks. However, a critical aspect of these systems remains largely unexamined: their vulnerability to adversarial attacks.

The paper "Diffusion Policy Attacker: Crafting Adversarial Attacks for Diffusion-based Policies" by researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology introduces DP-Attacker, the first comprehensive framework designed to expose and analyze the vulnerability of diffusion policies to adversarial manipulations. This work is significant as it reveals that despite their advantages in handling complex distributions, diffusion-based policies remain susceptible to carefully crafted adversarial perturbations, posing potential safety and security risks in real-world robotic deployments.

#### 1.2. Background and Context

Behavior Cloning (BC) is a fundamental approach in imitation learning where policies are trained to mimic expert demonstrations. Traditional BC methods often struggle with multi-modal action distributions, which are common in complex robotic manipulation tasks. Diffusion policies address this limitation by leveraging the powerful generative capabilities of diffusion models.

A diffusion policy operates by learning a denoising process to generate actions based on input observations. The process begins with random noise and iteratively refines it into an appropriate action for the given context. This approach enables the model to capture complex, multi-modal action distributions more effectively than deterministic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Research Gap and Motivation

Adversarial attacks have been extensively studied in the context of image classification and other machine learning tasks, but their impact on diffusion-based policies remains largely unexplored. Understanding these vulnerabilities is crucial as robots increasingly operate in uncontrolled environments where they might encounter deliberately manipulated inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The DP-Attacker Framework

DP-Attacker provides a comprehensive suite of attack methodologies specifically tailored for diffusion policies. The framework introduces several key attack variants, as illustrated in Figure 2.

The attacks are categorized along multiple dimensions:

1. **Global vs. Patched Attacks**:
 - Global attacks perturb the entire input image
 - Patched attacks modify only a small region of the input

2. **Online vs. Offline Attacks**:
 - Online attacks compute a new perturbation for each observation in real-time
 - Offline attacks pre-compute a single perturbation that works across multiple frames

3. **Targeted vs. Untargeted Attacks**:
 - Targeted attacks aim to force the policy toward a specific undesired action
 - Untargeted attacks simply disrupt the normal policy behavior

This taxonomy provides a structured approach to exploring different attack vectors against diffusion policies, with each variant presenting unique challenges and implications.

#### 3.2. Attack Methodologies

The core innovation in DP-Attacker lies in its formulation of the adversarial optimization objective. Unlike traditional adversarial attacks that directly target output actions, DP-Attacker focuses on disrupting the internal noise prediction mechanism of diffusion models.

For a diffusion policy with parameters θ, the noise prediction function εθ aims to predict the noise added during the diffusion process. The authors introduce a noise prediction loss:

```
L_noise(I, δ) = ||εθ(I) - εθ(I + δ)||_2^2
```

Where I is the input observation and δ is the adversarial perturbation. By maximizing this loss, the attacker causes the diffusion process to follow an incorrect denoising trajectory, ultimately leading to erroneous actions.

This approach offers two key advantages:
1. It is more computationally efficient than end-to-end optimization through the entire diffusion process
2. It targets a fundamental component of the diffusion model, yielding more effective attacks

For patch-based attacks, the framework constrains the perturbation to only affect a small, localized region of the input:

```
δ_patch = M ⊙ δ
```

Where M is a binary mask defining the patch location and ⊙ represents element-wise multiplication.

The optimization problem for generating adversarial perturbations can be formulated as:

```
max_δ L_noise(I, δ)
subject to ||δ||_∞ ≤ ε
```

Where ε constrains the magnitude of the perturbation to ensure visual imperceptibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

The authors evaluated DP-Attacker on six robotic manipulation tasks from the Robosuite environment:

These tasks represent a diverse set of manipulation challenges with varying complexity, objects, and goals. For each task, a diffusion policy was pre-trained using demonstration data following the approach from the original Diffusion Policy paper.

The experimental evaluation included:

1. **Digital Perturbation Attacks**: Small, imperceptible perturbations added to the entire observation image
2. **Adversarial Patch Attacks**: Visible patches placed at strategic locations in the environment
3. **Online and Offline Attack Variants**: Testing both real-time and pre-computed perturbations
4. **Physical World Applicability**: Demonstrating how adversarial patches could be deployed in real-world scenarios

Success rates were measured by the robot's ability to complete tasks under attack conditions, compared against baseline performance with clean inputs.

#### 4.2. Key Results

The experimental results revealed several important findings about the vulnerability of diffusion policies:

1. **High Attack Effectiveness**: DP-Attacker successfully reduced the success rate of diffusion policies across all tasks, often by more than 50% with imperceptible perturbations.

2. **Offline Attack Transferability**: Pre-computed perturbations could effectively transfer across different frames and timesteps, showing that computationally expensive online attacks aren't always necessary.

These results demonstrate that while diffusion policies represent an advancement in robotic control, they inherit the vulnerability to adversarial attacks observed in other deep learning systems.

#### 4.3. Attack Analysis

The authors conducted a detailed analysis to understand which components of the diffusion policy are most vulnerable to attack. Their findings revealed that:

1. **Encoder Susceptibility**: The encoder component, which transforms raw observations into latent representations, is the most vulnerable part of the system. Small perturbations to the input cause large distortions in the encoded features, as shown in the feature distortion analysis (Figures 5 and 6).

2. **Targeted vs. Untargeted Effectiveness**: Untargeted attacks generally achieved higher success rates in disrupting policy performance, suggesting that causing general confusion is easier than forcing specific undesired behaviors.

3. **Offline vs. Online Efficiency Tradeoff**: While online attacks achieved slightly higher success rates in some tasks, offline attacks offer a much better computational efficiency with only a small drop in effectiveness.

4. **Patch Location Importance**: The position of adversarial patches significantly impacted attack success. Patches placed near task-relevant objects or in the robot's field of view were more effective.

The vulnerability extends beyond digital attacks to physical world scenarios. Figure 8 shows an example of how an adversarial patch in the physical environment can cause the robot to fail the Lift task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Implications for Robotic Systems

The vulnerabilities exposed by DP-Attacker have several important implications for deploying diffusion policies in real-world robotic systems:

1. **Safety Concerns**: The ability to disrupt policy behavior with small, often imperceptible perturbations raises significant safety concerns, particularly for robots operating in sensitive or human-adjacent environments.

2. **Security Considerations**: The effectiveness of physical patch attacks demonstrates that malicious actors could potentially sabotage robot operations without needing direct access to the control system.

3. **Robustness Requirements**: These findings highlight the need for robust defenses against adversarial attacks before diffusion policies can be widely deployed in critical applications.

4. **Visual Perception Dependence**: The susceptibility to visual perturbations underscores a fundamental challenge of vision-based control systems and suggests that multi-modal sensing approaches might be necessary.

5. **Transferability Concerns**: The success of offline attacks that transfer across frames indicates that attackers might not need sophisticated real-time capabilities to disrupt robotic systems effectively.

These implications call for increased attention to adversarial robustness in the development of diffusion-based policies for robotics.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

The introduction of DP-Attacker represents a significant contribution to understanding the security vulnerabilities of diffusion policies. The work demonstrates that despite their advantages in handling complex action distributions, diffusion policies remain susceptible to adversarial attacks—potentially more so than some traditional approaches due to the complexity of their generative process.

Key takeaways include:

1. Diffusion policies can be effectively attacked using both imperceptible perturbations and visible, localized patches.

2. The encoder component represents a critical vulnerability point that future defensive measures should address.

3. Both online and offline attack strategies pose significant threats, with different tradeoffs between effectiveness and computational efficiency.

4. Physical world attacks using adversarial patches are practical and effective, highlighting real-world security concerns.

This research opens several important directions for future work, including developing robust defenses against such attacks, exploring multi-modal sensing approaches to reduce vulnerability to visual perturbations, and investigating whether similar vulnerabilities exist in other diffusion-based control systems.

By highlighting these vulnerabilities, the work contributes to the development of more secure and reliable robotic systems based on diffusion models, helping to ensure their safe deployment in real-world applications.
