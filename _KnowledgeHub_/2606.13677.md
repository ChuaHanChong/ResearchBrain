# Mana: Dexterous Manipulation of Articulated Tools

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed MANA, a framework for dexterous robotic manipulation of articulated tools, leveraging an animation-inspired data generation pipeline and reinforcement learning for sim-to-real transfer. The system consistently achieved approximately 70% success rates for grasping and in-hand manipulation across diverse tools like tongs and syringes, outperforming teleoperation and open-loop policies.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation of articulated tools with multi-fingered hands remains largely underexplored due to complex coordination, precise force requirements, and contact-rich interactions.
- Existing control paradigms, like position-based human teleoperation, struggle to generate sufficient and accurately directed pinch forces required for tool actuation and stability.
- End-to-end reinforcement learning faces immense exploration challenges for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks involving millimeter-scale functional contacts and high force generation.

## Method

- The **MANA** framework reinterprets dexterous manipulation as a ==coarse-to-fine== animation problem, generating diverse and physically plausible manipulation data in simulation.
- It combines a grasp generator (with collision-aware Inverse Kinematics and dense keyframe sampling) and a trajectory generator using GPU-accelerated RRT-Connect for pre-grasping and Reinforcement Learning for grasping and in-hand actuation.
- A point-cloud-conditioned diffusion policy, trained entirely on simulated data with extensive domain randomization, enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a robot equipped with custom compliant fingertips.

## Results

- Mana achieved approximately **70%** real-world success rates for both grasping and in-hand manipulation across four categories of articulated tools (tongs, pliers, clothespins, syringes), significantly outperforming open-loop policies and human teleoperation (often below **30%**).
- Ablation studies demonstrated that real-world performance scales positively with the quantity of simulated data, the diversity of grasp keyframes, and the intensity of force randomization during training.
- The system successfully demonstrated functional tool-use workflows, achieving 50-**70%** success rates for multi-stage tasks like "Tong Pick" and "Syringe Inject" using only onboard perception for manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An 'animation-inspired' coarse-to-fine data generation pipeline effectively decomposes complex, long-horizon articulated tool manipulation tasks, mitigating exploration difficulties for RL while ensuring contact-rich behaviors.
> - Dense sampling of grasp keyframes and extensive force-related domain randomization in simulation are critical for learning stable multi-point position-force control and achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
> - Custom-designed compliant fingertips increase contact patches and passively compensate for small pose errors, significantly improving contact stability and robust force distribution for thin articulated objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous robot manipulation, particularly grasping and reorienting rigid objects, has seen significant advancements, often leveraging sim-to-real reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning. Prior research has demonstrated success in grasping everyday items and performing in-hand reorientation of rigid bodies using various sensory inputs like proprioception, tactile sensing, and vision.

However, the manipulation of *articulated tools* with multi-fingered hands remains a substantial challenge, largely underexplored. This is due to the inherent complexity of coordinating a robot hand's internal degrees of freedom with the tool's articulation, coupled with the necessity for precise, contact-rich interactions. While some work has addressed large articulated objects (e.g., doors, laptops) or tools initialized in stable hanging configurations, the problem of precisely grasping and manipulating small, thin articulated tools from a flat surface, such as a tabletop, remains largely unsolved. Existing approaches like human teleoperation often fall short because they are typically position-based rather than force-based, making it difficult to generate the precise and substantial contact forces required for tool actuation. Similarly, end-to-end RL from scratch is problematic for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks due to the immense exploration space and the sensitivity to contact locations and forces.

Previous closely related works by Xu et al. and Atar et al. explored in-hand tool manipulation but typically started from a pre-established, fixed, and stable in-hand grasp. In contrast, this research aims to address the entire pipeline of articulated tool use, encompassing initial object acquisition from a tabletop, stable grasping, and functional in-hand manipulation, including the challenges of hybrid position-force control. The work positions itself as a scalable, data-driven framework that bridges this gap by reinterpreting dexterous manipulation as an "animation problem" to facilitate data generation for sim-to-real transfer, specifically targeting small, thin, and difficult-to-manipulate articulated tools.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general and scalable framework, termed Mana (Manipulation Animator), for dexterous robotic manipulation of articulated tools, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for both grasping and in-hand manipulation.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges inherent in articulated tool manipulation:

- **Ubiquity and Utility of Articulated Tools:** Many essential tools in human environments, such as tongs, pliers, clothespins, and syringes, are articulated. These tools require not only grasping but also the actuation of their internal degrees of freedom while maintaining stable contact with often thin and moving handles. Enabling robots to autonomously use such tools is crucial for practical applications in various domains.
- **Physical Complexity of Articulated Interactions:** Dexterous manipulation of articulated tools is a complex physical problem. It demands simultaneous stabilization of the tool and application of specific, functional actuation forces. These two objectives often require challenging force configurations within the friction cone, where forces optimal for actuation may not align with those best for preventing slip, and vice-versa. This dynamic interplay, compounded by multiple fingers and complex hand kinematics, means that even minor execution errors can lead to contact changes, altered moment arms, and overall instability.
- **High Force Requirements:** Many articulated tools, such as pliers for cutting or syringes for dispensing, require substantial force (e.g., 3-7 Newtons in the experiments). Generating and controlling such forces precisely, while maintaining stability and preventing slippage, is a significant robotic challenge that amplifies the difficulty of the task.
- **Limitations of Existing Control Paradigms:**
 - **Teleoperation:** Current dexterous teleoperation systems are predominantly position-based, retargeting hand poses or fingertip positions. This makes it difficult, or even infeasible, to generate sufficient and accurately directed pinch forces required to actuate stiff tool joints or maintain stable contact with thin tools. Workarounds like moving fingertip targets slightly inside objects yield limited force and may not align with desired actuation directions.
 - **End-to-End Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While powerful, end-to-end RL from scratch faces severe exploration challenges for long-horizon articulated tool manipulation. The policy needs to discover precise functional contact points on centimeter-scale surfaces, maintain these contacts through changing tool configurations, and generate high task-specific forces within a high-dimensional robotic hand action space. This makes learning from exploration exceedingly difficult and brittle.
- **Need for Scalable Data Generation:** To train robust policies for diverse articulated tools, a scalable method for generating high-quality manipulation data is essential, especially when real-world demonstrations are difficult or impractical to acquire.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to provide a robust, generalizable, and scalable framework for complex dexterous manipulation tasks, pushing the capabilities of robotic systems beyond rigid object interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Mana framework (Manipulation Animator) is designed as a sim-to-real pipeline that reinterprets dexterous manipulation as a coarse-to-fine animation problem. It aims to generate robust manipulation data in simulation and transfer the learned policies to real robots with zero-shot capabilities.

#### 3.1. Mana Data System Overview

The core of Mana is a data generation system that takes a tool mesh with an articulated joint model and minimal human input (functional affordance annotations) to produce a dataset of successful simulated manipulation trajectories. These trajectories contain wrist motion, hand joint commands, object state, and semantic phase labels (e.g., grasping, opening, closing).

#### 3.2. Grasp Generator (Coarse Phase)

The first step involves creating a set of "grasp keyframes" that define the essential poses in a manipulation sequence.
- **Human Annotation:** A user briefly annotates the functional regions (e.g., handles, plunger) on the tool mesh in a 3D interface, taking less than a minute per tool.
- **Lightning Grasp+:** This system builds upon the Lightning Grasp pipeline and introduces a *collision-aware Inverse Kinematics (IK) process* (Appendix A.1). This enhancement is crucial for generating physically plausible grasps for thin objects resting on a surface, where traditional IK might lead to collisions with the environment. It optimizes fingertip contact locations within the annotated domains and finger joint positions.
- **Stability Filtering:** The generated grasps are filtered for stability within the IsaacLab simulation environment.
- **Dense Keyframe Sampling:** Unlike rigid object grasping, articulated tool manipulation requires a sequence of stable grasps across varying tool configurations. Therefore, the keyframe planner samples a dense set of grasp states, covering functional contact regions and relevant tool configurations (e.g., different plunger depths for a syringe). This dense coverage addresses the millimeter-scale sensitivity of articulated tool use. The output is a graph of keyframes: pre-grasp, stable grasp, and in-hand actuation states.

#### 3.3. Trajectory Generator (Fine Phase - Inbetweening)

The trajectory generator connects these keyframes into full manipulation trajectories, decomposing the process into distinct phases based on their physical requirements:

- **Pre-grasping:** This phase moves the robot hand from an initial configuration to a collision-free approach pose near the target grasp. Since forceful interaction is not required, Mana uses a **GPU-accelerated RRT-Connect algorithm** with path smoothing for trajectory planning. To prevent premature collisions, pre-grasp poses are generated by displacing fingertips away from final contact locations along local surface normals.
- **Grasping:** This phase transitions from the pre-grasp pose to a stable tool grasp.
 - **Procedural Generation:** For many cases, a simple procedural strategy is used: fingertips move inward along contact normals to squeeze and secure the tool, then lift it.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** For challenging scenarios (e.g., thin tools, unstable initial contacts, misaligned required forces), where geometric strategies are insufficient, Mana trains a short-horizon RL policy to connect pre-grasp and grasp keyframes.
- **In-hand Tool Actuation:** Once the tool is grasped, this phase generates trajectories to change the tool's internal configuration while maintaining stable contact. This is an inherently contact-rich problem requiring precise force application.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** This phase is framed as a goal-reaching problem. RL episodes are initialized from stable grasp keyframes, with the target being a desired tool configuration (e.g., open to closed).
 - **Reward Function:** The reward `r = r_tool + w_1*r_hand + w_2*r_contact` encourages tool and hand pose matching and maintenance of contact points for stability (Appendix A.2).
 - **Domain Randomization:** Critical for sim-to-real transfer, diverse force-related randomizations are applied during RL training (Appendix A.3). These include action noise, randomization of robot and tool PD controller gains, object mass, friction coefficients, and random Gaussian force perturbations on the object. This ensures policy robustness against real-world uncertainties.
 - Successful trajectories from these RL training sessions are stored with semantic labels.

#### 3.4. Robot System

The system comprises hardware and a visuomotor policy for real-world deployment.
- **Dexterous Manipulator Platform (Sec 4.1):** A 7-DoF xArm7 robot arm equipped with a 16-DoF Allegro hand.
- **Custom Fingertips:** Designed with a flattened, compliant silicone contact surface (Appendix A.7). This increases the contact patch for thin tools (approx. 1 cm thickness) and passively deforms to compensate for small pose errors, distributing normal forces more robustly during high-pressure actuation.
- **Perception (Sec 4.1):** An Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D camera captures visual input. SAM 3 segments the image, and Fast Foundation Stereo produces an object point cloud, which is then transformed into the wrist frame. The system operates at approximately 10 Hz.
- **Visuomotor Policy (Sec 4.2):**
 - A point-cloud-conditioned diffusion policy is trained entirely on Mana-generated simulated data.
 - **Architecture (Appendix A.5):** A Perceiver-style transformer encodes the 512-point tool point cloud, and an MLP encodes robot proprioception. These encoded features are then passed to a transformer-based diffusion model head.
 - **Action Output:** The policy outputs delta 6D wrist poses and delta hand joint position targets.
 - **Control:** A differential IK solver controls the wrist, and a low-level PD controller manages hand joint motors.
 - **Training:** The policy is trained using a standard sample-denoising objective on the Mana dataset. Point-cloud randomizations (noise, random part masking) are applied during training to enhance tolerance to real-world segmentation imperfections, missing points, and depth noise.
- **Force Simulation (Appendix A.6):** Emphasizes accurate calibration of simulation force parameters via system identification for the robot hand and spring-mass-damper models for tools. It uses the Implicit Euler method for actuator simulation and increased simulation iterations (position, velocity) with a smaller timestep in IsaacLab to ensure stability with high forces on small objects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the Mana framework on four categories of articulated tools: tongs, pliers, clothespins, and syringes, each with two instances. These objects represent a range of sizes, shapes, and joint properties, with thicknesses of 0.8–1.5 cm and requiring 3–7 N of force for actuation.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Comparison (Table 1)

The Mana system is compared against two baselines: an open-loop Mana policy and human teleoperation using the Geometric Retargeting (GeoRT) system. Each evaluation consists of 10 trials for distinct phases (Grasp, Open, Close, Use).

- **Mana (Ours):** Achieves approximately 70% success rates across all tool categories for both grasping and in-hand manipulation. For instance, tongs achieve 0.8/0.8 success rates for grasping, opening, and closing. Syringe "use" (plunger actuation) achieves 0.6/0.6. This demonstrates the system's ability to consistently perform dexterous tasks with various articulated tools.
- **Open-loop Mana Policy:** This baseline shows lower success rates, typically ranging from 0.0 to 0.7 depending on the task and instance (e.g., tongs grasp 0.5/0.6, syringe use 0.0/0.3). Failures are attributed to the sensitivity of dexterous manipulation to small perception errors and imperfect object meshes, which can lead to contact errors or perturbations if the policy lacks reactive control.
- **Teleoperation (GeoRT):** This baseline exhibits the lowest performance, with success rates generally below 0.3 across all tasks and often 0.0 for more complex manipulations like clothespin opening/closing or syringe use. The paper attributes this to the inherent difficulty of precisely controlling forces with position-based teleoperation systems, especially for thin articulated objects. Operators struggled with slippage, bouncing, and generating sufficient force for stiff joints, even after practice.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Figure 7)

Ablation studies investigate the impact of data design choices on real-world performance.
- **Data Quantity (Number of Trajectories):** Performance, specifically grasping success rate for tongs, scales positively with the number of trajectories generated in simulation.
- **State Diversity (Number of Grasp Keyframes):** The manipulation success rate for syringes improves with an increased number of grasp keyframes used. This indicates that dense sampling of diverse grasp configurations is critical for learning stable multi-point position-force control, given the millimeter-level sensitivity of contact points in articulated manipulation.
- **Force Randomization:** Manipulation success rate for pliers scales with the intensity of force randomization applied during training. This highlights the importance of perturbing object and action dynamics in simulation to build robustness against real-world noise, actuator variability, and torque degradation.

#### 4.3. Demonstrating Articulated Tool Use (Table 2)

The research demonstrates the composition of learned skills into complex, functional tool-use tasks (e.g., Tong Pick, Plier Cut, Clothespin Use, Syringe Inject).
- For these multi-stage tasks, the policy's ability to perform overall workflows is evaluated. To isolate the policy's performance from perception limitations in precise tool-to-site alignment (e.g., aligning pliers with a 0.5mm wire), manual wrist teleoperation is employed exclusively for this fine-alignment phase, after the object has been grasped and lifted by the policy.
- The system achieves success rates of 50-70% for these combined tasks (e.g., Tong Pick 7/10, Syringe Inject 5/10).
- Performance degradation in these complex tasks is attributed to unseen perturbations when tools interact with other objects, potentially leading to non-recoverable poses. This suggests areas for further improvement in force and contact physics modeling in simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to dexterous robotics and holds significant potential impact:

- **Addressing a Grand Challenge:** The work tackles a fundamental and largely unsolved challenge in dexterous robotics: the manipulation of articulated tools. By demonstrating consistent zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for various complex articulated objects, it advances the state of the art beyond rigid object manipulation.
- **Novel "Animation-Inspired" Framework:** The Mana framework's coarse-to-fine pipeline, combining procedural grasp keyframe generation, motion planning for coarse motions, and reinforcement learning for contact-rich phases, represents a novel approach to data generation for complex manipulation. This decomposition effectively mitigates the exploration difficulties of end-to-end RL for long-horizon tasks while preserving the necessary contact-rich behaviors.
- **Scalable Data Generation:** The ability to generate diverse and physically plausible manipulation trajectories with minimal human input (a few clicks per tool) makes the approach highly scalable. This alleviates the bottleneck of collecting real-world demonstration data for complex dexterous tasks, which is often time-consuming and difficult.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The achievement of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer is a key indicator of the framework's robustness. This is facilitated by a multi-faceted approach including dense state space coverage in simulation, extensive force-related domain randomizations, point-cloud-specific randomizations, and careful simulation physics calibration.
- **Practical Hardware Contributions:** The custom-designed, compliant fingertips are a practical hardware innovation that specifically addresses the challenges of grasping and manipulating thin articulated objects. This hardware adaptation contributes to the stability of contacts and the robustness of force application.
- **Enabling Complex Tool Use:** The successful demonstration of functional tool-use workflows, even with limited teleoperation for precise alignment, indicates the potential for robots to perform more sophisticated tasks in human environments. This opens avenues for applications in domestic robotics, industrial assembly, and specialized tasks requiring intricate manipulation of tools.
- **Insights into Dexterity Requirements:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the critical factors for achieving dexterous manipulation, emphasizing the importance of diverse data, extensive state coverage, and rigorous force randomization in simulation. These findings can guide future research in designing robust policies for contact-rich tasks.

The limitations, such as insufficient motor strength for very stiff tools, the focus on precision grasps over power grasps with the Allegro hand, and the reliance on wrist teleoperation for fine alignment, also highlight important directions for future research. Overcoming these could lead to even more capable and autonomous systems for general articulated tool use.
