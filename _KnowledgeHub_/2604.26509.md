# 3D Generation for Embodied AI and Robotic Simulation: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper provides a structured overview of 3D generation techniques specifically tailored for embodied AI and robotic simulation, emphasizing the concept of "simulation readiness." It categorizes existing methods into three functional roles: Data Generator for simulation-ready objects, Simulation Environments for interactive scenes, and Sim2Real Bridge for connecting virtual and real worlds, while identifying key challenges and future research directions.

## Problem

- Embodied AI and robotic systems require scalable, diverse, and physically accurate 3D content for perception, reasoning, and action in complex physical environments.
- Traditional 3D generation research often prioritizes visual realism or static geometry, neglecting crucial requirements for physical simulation, kinematic structures, and real-world robotic deployment.
- The literature on 3D generation for robotics is fragmented across various communities, lacking a unified framework to address the specific needs of "simulation-ready" content.

## Method

- Introduces a novel ==taxonomy== organizing 3D generation by functional role: ==Data Generator==, ==Simulation Environments==, and ==Sim2Real Bridge==.
- Establishes ==simulation readiness== as a primary evaluation criterion: geometric validity, physical parameterization, kinematic executability, and simulator format compatibility (URDF, MJCF).
- Systematically reviews foundational concepts, existing methods, datasets, and evaluation metrics across object-level generation, scene synthesis, and sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- Identifies a shift toward **interaction readiness** and **simulation deployability** over visual fidelity for embodied AI.
- Details progress in generating ==simulation-ready articulated objects== exportable to physics engines via semantic priors and end-to-end pipelines.
- Highlights ==generative digital twins== from real data and 3D-grounded data augmentation to bridge sim-to-real, supporting zero-shot policy deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - There is a crucial distinction between conventional 3D generation (focused on visual appearance) and embodied-oriented 3D generation, which demands interaction readiness, physical grounding, and simulator compatibility.
> - The use of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) is increasingly enabling more controllable and agentic 3D scene generation, translating high-level intent into structured, task-oriented environments.
> - Despite advancements, key bottlenecks include the scarcity of physical annotations, trade-offs between geometric quality and physical validity, the complexity of generating deformable assets, and the persistent sim-to-real domain gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI and robotic systems is progressively relying on 3D content for training and real-world application. While 3D generative modeling has advanced significantly, the requirements for embodied applications extend beyond visual realism. Such applications demand 3D content that possesses kinematic structures, material properties, and scenes that facilitate interaction and task execution, along with the capacity to bridge the gap between simulation and reality.

Existing literature on 3D generation is fragmented across various communities, including computer vision, computer graphics, robotics, embodied AI, and simulation systems. Previous work often focuses on isolated aspects, such as object generation without considering downstream executability, scene generation lacking interaction semantics, or simulation platforms without generative scalability. Recent methods, however, are increasingly integrating asset generation, scene synthesis, simulator feedback, and agentic planning into unified pipelines.

This survey addresses this fragmentation by providing a structured review of 3D generation specifically for embodied AI and robotic simulation. It distinguishes itself from prior surveys by focusing on the "simulation readiness" of generated 3D content, which encompasses geometric validity, physical parameterization, kinematic executability, and simulator compatibility, rather than solely visual metrics or generative backbones. Other surveys often cover general 3D generation (without embodied AI focus), scene generation (omitting object-level and sim-to-real aspects), embodied AI (treating 3D assets as infrastructure), or generative AI for manipulation (focusing on policy rather than asset generation). This work aims to provide a unified perspective on how 3D generation contributes to embodied AI across various levels of abstraction and integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to comprehensively review 3D generation for embodied AI and robotic simulation, organizing the literature around the specific roles 3D generation plays within embodied systems. The authors aim to clarify the distinct requirements of embodied-oriented 3D generation compared to traditional 3D generation, establishing "simulation readiness" as a central evaluation criterion.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations:

- **Growing Dependence on 3D Content:** Embodied AI and robotics increasingly require scalable, diverse, and physically grounded 3D content for simulation-based training and real-world deployment.
- **Beyond Visual Realism:** Unlike conventional 3D generation, embodied applications demand assets that are not only visually realistic but also manipulable, physically simulable, and transferable across tasks. This includes objects with plausible kinematic joints, deformable materials, and scenes that support navigation, interaction, and task execution under physical constraints.
- **Convergence of Fields:** Rapid advancements in 3D generative modeling (e.g., diffusion models, LLMs) are converging with the needs of robotics-oriented simulation. This convergence highlights the need for generated outputs to encode kinematic structure, physical parameters, material properties, and compatibility with execution formats (e.g., URDF, MJCF, USD).
- **Fragmented Literature:** The existing body of work is dispersed across multiple research communities, leading to isolated studies that often address only one layer of the problem without considering the end-to-end requirements for embodied systems.
- **Need for a Unified Taxonomy:** A structured review is needed to connect these disparate research threads and refocus the field on practical utility for robotic simulation.

The survey's contributions are explicitly defined as:
- Clarifying the distinction of embodied-oriented 3D generation by centering on "simulation readiness."
- Providing the first taxonomy organized around three core roles: Data Generator, Simulation Environments, and Sim2Real Bridge, thus connecting diverse research threads.
- Identifying and systematizing key technical gaps, such as limited physical annotations, the tension between geometric quality and physical validity, fragmented evaluation, and the persistent sim-to-real divide.
- Translating these gaps into concrete research directions for the community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This survey adopts a thematic organizational approach, structuring the literature around the functional roles that 3D generation plays within embodied AI and robotic simulation systems, rather than by underlying generative techniques. This approach aims to highlight how different generative methods contribute to the overarching goal of creating "simulation-ready" content.

The methodology is organized into the following components:

1. **Preliminaries (Section 2):** The survey establishes foundational knowledge by reviewing:
 - **3D Representations:** Discussing explicit (voxel grids, point clouds, polygonal meshes), structured and implicit (scene graphs, Signed Distance Functions), and neural rendering representations (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting), emphasizing their relevance for embodied AI.
 - **Generative Models:** Summarizing the main paradigms used for 3D content synthesis, including Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Diffusion and Flow-Based Models, and Autoregressive Models.
 - **Simulation Platforms and Assets:** Defining "simulation readiness" based on geometric validity, physical parameterization, kinematic executability, and simulator compatibility. It also reviews standard simulation-ready formats (URDF, MJCF, USD) and major robotic simulation platforms (e.g., MuJoCo, Isaac Sim, Habitat).
 - **Embodied AI Fundamentals:** Briefly introducing downstream robot learning paradigms such as Imitation Learning, Sim-to-Real Transfer, World Models, and Vision-Language-Action Models, to contextualize the demand for scalable 3D generation.

2. **Three Core Roles of 3D Generation (Sections 3, 4, 5):** The literature is categorized into three primary functions, each addressed in a dedicated section:
 - **Data Generator (Section 3):** This role focuses on producing simulation-ready objects and assets. It covers articulated objects (structural topology, semantic reasoning, programmatic synthesis, multi-state dynamics), physically grounded objects (physical property understanding, post-hoc refinement, simulation-aligned training, native unified physical generation), deformable objects (cloth and garment generation, elastoplastic and soft-body generation), and end-to-end simulation-ready pipelines.
 - **Simulation Environments (Section 4):** This role examines methods for constructing interactive and task-oriented 3D worlds. It distinguishes between structure-driven scene generation (procedural-based and scenario-prior–guided), controllable scene generation (instruction-conditioned, vision-conditioned, and physics-conditioned), and agentic scene generation (modular and self-reflection approaches, task-oriented generation).
 - **Sim2Real Bridge (Section 5):** This role addresses how 3D generation facilitates the transfer between simulation and real-world deployment. It includes generative digital twin creation (automated scene reconstruction, kinematic reconstruction, physics perception and visual-dynamics alignment), generative data augmentation (viewpoint and geometric, dynamic and temporal, physics-aware consistency), and generative task and demonstration scaling (trajectory synthesis, world model-based generation, foundation-scale synthetic training).

3. **Datasets and Evaluation (Section 6):** The survey consolidates relevant datasets (object assets, scene datasets, robot demonstration datasets) and evaluation protocols. It proposes a three-level evaluation hierarchy: geometry and appearance quality, physical plausibility and simulation readiness, and embodied task performance.

4. **Challenges and Future Directions (Section 7):** Based on the comprehensive review, the authors identify key bottlenecks and propose future research directions, covering physical grounding at scale, deformable and dynamic asset generation, efficiency-controllability trade-offs in scene generation, evaluation standardization, and closing the sim-to-real loop.

By organizing the literature around these functional roles and explicitly defining "simulation readiness," the survey aims to provide a cohesive understanding of the field, highlight interdependencies between different research areas, and pinpoint critical areas for future development.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey identifies a significant shift in the field of 3D generation for embodied AI, moving from a primary focus on visual realism towards interaction readiness and physical deployability. This overarching finding is substantiated by detailed reviews across three core roles: Data Generator, Simulation Environments, and Sim2Real Bridge.

#### 4.1. Data Generator (Simulation-Ready Objects and Assets)

The survey finds that 3D generation is increasingly focused on producing assets with explicit kinematic structures and physical properties.
- **Articulated Objects:** Early methods focused on reconstructing articulation from observation; however, generative approaches now learn priors for scalable asset creation. Key developments include hierarchical learning for part connectivity, diffusion models for joint geometry and kinematics, LLM/VLM integration for semantic reasoning, and programmatic synthesis (e.g., URDF-Anything, PhysX-Anything) for directly simulator-executable formats. The field is moving towards interaction-driven dynamics, where generated objects respond plausibly to contact.
- **Physically Grounded Objects:** Generated assets must comply with physical constraints beyond mere geometry. The progression is from understanding physical properties (e.g., NeRF2Physics) and post-hoc refinement (e.g., Atlas3D) to integrating simulator feedback during training (e.g., DSO, PhyCAGE), and finally to native unified physical generation where geometry, appearance, and physical properties are modeled jointly (e.g., PhysX-3D, PhysGaussian).
- **Deformable Objects:** This area is less mature due to the complexity of continuum mechanics. Progress is mainly in cloth and garment generation (e.g., Dress-Code, Dress-1-to-3) using sewing patterns as simulation-compatible intermediates, and elastoplastic/soft-body generation (e.g., PhysGaussian) which focuses on material field estimation. A bottleneck is the lack of large-scale deformable datasets with material annotations.
- **End-to-End Pipelines:** A crucial finding is the emergence of integrated pipelines (e.g., TRELLIS, Seed3D, EmbodiedGen) that automate the entire process from generation to simulator-ready output, including geometry, texture, PBR estimation, UV mapping, and format serialization. This addresses the integration gap and improves interface alignment for multi-simulator deployment.

#### 4.2. Simulation Environments (Interactive Worlds)

The survey identifies a progression in scene generation from static plausible layouts to interactive, task-oriented worlds.
- **Structure-Driven Generation:** Early methods either used procedural rules (e.g., ProcTHOR, Infinigen Indoors) or learned statistical regularities from scene data (e.g., ATISS, DiffuScene). These provide coarse control and often rely on external asset libraries.
- **Controllable Scene Generation:** Recent efforts focus on flexible conditioning signals, including natural language instructions (e.g., InstructScene, SceneFoundry), visual inputs (e.g., SceneGen, MIDI), and explicit physics constraints (e.g., PhyScene, DynScene). This improves specificity but highlights challenges in integrating multiple modalities consistently.
- **Agentic Scene Generation:** Foundation models enable agentic approaches (e.g., SceneWeaver, SAGE, SceneSmith) that translate high-level intent into structured scene specifications with iterative correction through tool use and feedback. This improves robustness but incurs high computational costs, especially with physics-in-the-loop validation (e.g., PAT3D, PhyScensis). Task-driven methods (e.g., MarketGen, MesaTask) directly align generation with downstream training objectives.

#### 4.3. Sim2Real Bridge (Simulation-to-Reality Transfer)

The survey highlights generative models as key enablers for bridging the sim-to-real gap across three dimensions:
- **Generative Digital Twin:** The automation of creating simulation-ready replicas from real-world observations is critical. This includes automated scene reconstruction (e.g., DRAWER, RoboSimGS), kinematic reconstruction of articulated objects (e.g., Ditto, PARIS, ArticulatedGS), and physics perception (e.g., PhysTwin, TwinAligner) to infer mass, friction, and elasticity from visual dynamics. Real-is-Sim demonstrates closed-loop real-time digital twins.
- **Generative Data Augmentation:** The field has moved from 2D image-space augmentation to 3D and 4D spatiotemporal augmentation using NeRF and 3DGS. This includes synthesizing novel-viewpoint observations (e.g., RoboSplat, SplatSim) and dynamic trajectories with varied execution (e.g., ExoGS, GAF). A key finding is the emphasis on physics-aware consistency to prevent non-physical artifacts during augmentation (e.g., RoboTransfer, Splat-MOVER).
- **Generative Task and Demonstration:** Methods are scaling demonstration data by adapting existing human demonstrations (e.g., MimicGen, DemoGen) or generating entirely new tasks and trajectories. LLMs are used to automatically construct simulation tasks with physical parameters (e.g., Gen2Sim, GenSim2). World model-based generation (e.g., DreamGen, PhysWorld) embeds structural and kinematic constraints to produce physically plausible motions. GraspVLA demonstrates foundation-scale synthetic training using billion-frame datasets, enabling open-vocabulary robot learning.

#### 4.4. Datasets and Evaluation

A critical finding is the evolution of datasets from purely geometric collections (e.g., ShapeNet, PartNet) to those with deep physical, kinematic, and interaction annotations (e.g., AKB-48, PhysXNet, ManiTwin). Scene datasets similarly progressed from passive collections (e.g., ScanNet, Matterport3D) to active, procedurally or agent-generated, and task-oriented environments (e.g., ProcTHOR-10K, SAGE-10K, MesaTask-10K).
Evaluation protocols remain fragmented, spanning geometric (CD, EMD), physical (stability rate, joint accuracy, penetration volume), and embodied task performance metrics (grasp success rate, navigation success rate). The survey highlights the need for standardization and cross-simulator consistency tests.

#### 4.5. Challenges and Future Directions

The survey identifies several bottlenecks:
- **Physical Grounding at Scale:** Lack of large-scale datasets with physical and kinematic annotations, and generative architectures that integrate differentiable physics constraints efficiently.
- **Deformable and Dynamic Assets:** Limited maturity compared to rigid/articulated objects due to high-dimensional state spaces and reliance on continuum mechanics.
- **Efficiency-Controllability Trade-off in Scene Generation:** Agentic methods provide high control but are computationally expensive, hindering large-scale training.
- **Evaluation Standardization:** Fragmented metrics and simulator dependence limit meaningful comparisons.
- **Closing the Sim-to-Real Loop:** Persistent gaps in appearance, dynamics, and semantics, often exacerbated by the modular and disjoint nature of generative models, physics engines, and robot learning systems.

Overall, the survey concludes that 3D generation is transitioning into a core infrastructure layer for embodied learning, with "simulation readiness" as its defining characteristic.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey contributes to the fields of embodied AI, robotics, and 3D generation by providing a structured and forward-looking analysis of their convergence. Its significance and potential impact can be understood across several dimensions:

- **Clarification and Unification of the Research Landscape:** By introducing a novel taxonomy centered on the functional roles of 3D generation (Data Generator, Simulation Environments, Sim2Real Bridge), the survey unifies fragmented research threads from diverse communities (computer vision, graphics, robotics). This structured perspective helps researchers understand how their work fits into the broader ecosystem of embodied AI development, fostering interdisciplinary collaboration.
- **Redefinition of "Quality" for 3D Generation:** The explicit definition and emphasis on "simulation readiness" as the central evaluation criterion reorients the field. It moves beyond traditional metrics of visual plausibility to prioritize geometric validity, physical parameterization, kinematic executability, and simulator compatibility. This provides a clearer, more practical target for future generative models, ensuring their outputs are directly usable in embodied applications.
- **Identification of Critical Bottlenecks and Research Gaps:** The survey systematically identifies key challenges that impede the practical deployment of 3D generation in embodied AI. These include the scarcity of large-scale, physically annotated datasets, the tension between geometric fidelity and physical soundness, the limited capabilities for generating deformable and dynamic assets, the computational cost versus control trade-off in agentic scene generation, and the lack of standardized evaluation protocols. By articulating these gaps, the survey provides a roadmap for future research efforts, directing resources towards high-impact problems.
- **Guidance for Future Research Directions:** Beyond identifying bottlenecks, the survey translates these challenges into concrete research directions. Examples include integrating differentiable physics into generative model training, developing simulation-native intermediate representations for deformable objects, amortizing verification in agentic scene generation, and advocating for a multi-level, cross-simulator evaluation protocol. These proposed directions offer actionable insights for researchers seeking to advance the state of the art.
- **Acceleration of Embodied AI Development:** By enabling more scalable, diverse, and physically grounded 3D content, the advancements outlined in this survey are crucial for training more capable and generalizable embodied AI agents. Generating high-fidelity digital twins, augmenting demonstration data, and synthesizing task-relevant environments at scale directly addresses the data bottleneck in robot learning, potentially accelerating progress in areas like policy learning, vision-language-action models, and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Inspiration for Unified Architectures:** The survey highlights the fragmentation between generative models, physics engines, and robot learning systems. It implicitly and explicitly advocates for the development of unified foundation models and representations (e.g., PhysGaussian) that jointly reason over geometry, physics, and task semantics. Such unification could simplify the current brittle conversion pipelines and lead to more robust and integrated embodied intelligence systems.

In summary, this survey is significant for synthesizing a rapidly evolving, interdisciplinary field, providing a much-needed framework for understanding current capabilities, pinpointing critical limitations, and guiding future research toward building dependable 3D generation foundations for advanced embodied intelligence.
