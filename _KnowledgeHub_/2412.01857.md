# Planning from Imagination: Episodic Simulation and Episodic Memory for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> An embodied agent called SALI (Space-Aware Long-term Imaginer) was developed at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, integrating a reality-imagination hybrid memory system to enhance Vision-and-Language Navigation in unfamiliar environments. This system, inspired by human episodic simulation, enabled the agent to generate and leverage future scene information, leading to state-of-the-art results on the R2R and REVERIE benchmarks, with R2R's Success rate weighted by Path Length (SPL) improving by 6-8% in unseen test splits.

## Problem

- Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents exhibit significant performance degradation when deployed in unfamiliar or unseen environments.
- Existing imaginative mechanisms in VLN agents produce transient predictions that are not integrated into a persistent, long-term memory system, limiting robust reasoning.
- There is a need for embodied agents to better align unfamiliar visual observations with navigation instructions and generalize knowledge, similar to human cognitive abilities like episodic memory and simulation.

## Method

- A **SALI** (Space-Aware Long-term Imaginer) agent is proposed, incorporating a ==reality-imagination hybrid memory== system that fuses real-time observations with generated future scenes, organized as a dynamically-updated ==topological memory map== (G_t) with visited, current, navigable, and imagination nodes.
- A ==recurrent imagination module==, organized as a tree, iteratively generates future semantic, depth, and RGB images, alongside probable waypoints, which are then integrated into the memory map.
- Dynamic action planning utilizes a ==multimodal transformer== and an ==adaptive fusion policy== to weigh real and imagined information when selecting the next navigation step.

## Results

- SALI achieved state-of-the-art performance on both the R2R benchmark, improving Success rate weighted by Path Length (SPL) by **6-8%** (SPL of **74**) on Test Unseen, and REVERIE, with the highest Remote Grounding Success (RGS) of **34** and RGSPL of **25** on Test Unseen.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the reality-imagination hybrid memory system significantly outperformed reality-only or imagination-only approaches, and dynamic fusion of imagined information yielded better results than a fixed weighting.
- Quantitative analysis revealed the largest performance gains on complex instructions involving both room and object terms (S3), indicating enhanced commonsense reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a hybrid memory system that combines real observations with imagined future scenarios significantly enhances an agent's ability to generalize and navigate effectively in unseen environments.
> - Episodic simulation, by generating fine-grained visual features and higher-level spatial structures, allows agents to perform complex commonsense reasoning about object-room associations.
> - Dynamically weighting imagined information's influence as navigation progresses outperforms a fixed fusion factor; auxiliary models for room-type refinement and waypoint prediction further improve imagined-information fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN). VLN tasks involve guiding an autonomous agent through an environment to a target location using natural language instructions and visual inputs. A recognized challenge in VLN is the performance degradation of agents when navigating in environments they have not previously encountered (unseen environments). This limitation is particularly evident in the agents' ability to effectively align unfamiliar visual observations with navigation instructions.

Current VLN approaches have explored various mechanisms, including multimodal visual-linguistic input alignment, and the construction of memory for long-term decision support. Memory mechanisms in embodied agents have encompassed Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), 2D bird-eye-view maps, ego-centric grid maps, and topological maps to represent spatial information. Some prior works have also incorporated "imaginary mechanisms" to support navigation decisions by generating fine-grained visual information (e.g., RGB-D images) or spatial structural information. However, a consistent limitation across these existing methods is the transient nature of their imaginative predictions; these predictions are often isolated and not integrated into a persistent, time-series memory system. This prevents the formation of long-term episodic simulations necessary for robust navigation and holistic reasoning.

This work draws inspiration from human cognitive neuroscience, specifically the concepts of episodic simulation and episodic memory. Humans utilize these mechanisms to recall past experiences, mentally simulate uncertain information or future scenes, and integrate these simulations into their decision-making process, particularly in unfamiliar surroundings. By integrating a form of "imaginative memory" into an embodied agent, this research aims to bridge the gap between transient visual predictions and durable, context-aware memory representations, thereby enhancing navigation performance in unseen environments and addressing the limitations of existing VLN agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the navigation performance of embodied agents, particularly in unseen environments, by developing an imaginative memory system inspired by human cognitive mechanisms.

The motivation for this objective stems from several observations:

- **Challenge in Unseen Environments:** Existing Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents demonstrate a notable decline in performance when deployed in unfamiliar environments compared to those they have encountered during training. This suggests a fundamental limitation in their ability to generalize knowledge and adapt to novel visual and spatial contexts.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** Humans effectively navigate unfamiliar spaces by leveraging episodic simulation and episodic memory. These cognitive processes allow individuals to mentally reconstruct past experiences, anticipate future scenarios, and understand complex relationships between objects and their environment, even without direct prior exposure. For instance, humans can imagine fine-grained visual features (e.g., an RGB image of a washing machine) and higher-level spatial structures (e.g., the likely adjacency of a laundry room to a bathroom). This ability to generate and integrate anticipatory knowledge is a critical component missing in many artificial agents.
- **Limitations of Current VLN Memory Mechanisms:** While some existing VLN approaches incorporate mechanisms for predicting potential object locations or RGB features, these predictions are typically transient. They are often overwritten at each navigation step and are not integrated into a persistent, time-series memory structure. This lack of a "long-term episodic simulation" capability prevents agents from accumulating and leveraging imagined information for future reasoning or decision-making, leading to isolated and short-lived imaginative outputs that lack continuity and compositional depth.
- **Need for a Hybrid Memory System:** There is a recognized need for a memory system that can dynamically fuse both real-time observations and imagined future scenarios. Such a hybrid system would enable agents to reason more holistically about their environment and adapt more effectively to novel situations, much like humans combine sensory input with mental simulations.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the research are:
- To propose a novel architecture that equips embodied agents with a reality-imagination hybrid memory system.
- To enable agents to maintain and continuously expand their memory through both imaginative mechanisms and direct navigation actions.
- To design and integrate tailored pre-training tasks specifically aimed at developing and refining the agent’s imaginative capabilities, allowing it to generate high-fidelity RGB images for future scenes.
- To demonstrate that such a system can achieve improved navigation performance, specifically state-of-the-art results in metrics like Success rate weighted by Path Length (SPL), particularly in unseen environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed agent, named Space-Aware Long-term Imaginer (SALI), is designed to emulate human-like episodic simulation and memory by employing a reality-imagination hybrid memory system. The methodology involves constructing and dynamically updating a topological memory map, incorporating both real observations and imagined future scenes, and using this memory for action planning.

#### 3.1. Real-Imaginary Hybrid Memory

SALI maintains a topological map, denoted as G_t, which serves as its memory. This map stores mixed-granularity information, combining both realistic and imaginative data.

- **Memory Map Representation:** G_t consists of nodes (N_t) and edges (E_t). Edges record Euclidean distances between neighboring nodes. Nodes contain visual inputs (V_t = {r_t, d_t, s_t} for RGB, depth, and semantic images), position information (p_t), and node features (f_t). Node features are extracted from visual inputs using a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) and ResNet encoder.
 - **Node Categories:** Nodes are classified into four types: `visited nodes`, `current nodes`, `navigable nodes`, and `imagination nodes`.
 - Visited and current nodes store complete and fixed image information.
 - Navigable nodes store partial visual inputs observed from neighboring real nodes and can incorporate potential information from imagination nodes.
 - Imagination nodes' V_t is entirely generated from imagined images.
 - **Memory Efficiency:** To manage efficiency, a pruning operation is applied to newly imagined nodes. If two nodes are sufficiently similar (based on cosine similarity of features and negative position Mean Square Error (MSE)), they are merged, retaining a single node whose feature is an average pooling. The new node's type (imagination or navigable) is determined by the types of the merged nodes. An upper bound (N_bar) is set on the number of imagination nodes.

- **Dynamic Action Planning:** The topological memory G_t is processed by a multimodal transformer to derive contextual representations for navigation decisions.
 - **Encoders:** The system uses pre-trained encoders for real nodes, fine-tuned encoders for imagination nodes (sharing structure, called "node encoder"), and a pre-trained instruction encoder.
 - **Node Embedding:** Node embeddings are generated from node features, location encoding, and navigation step encoding (marking visited nodes with their last visit time). These embeddings are categorized as `real` (V_r_t) or `imagined` (V_i_t). A 'stop' node is added to represent the stop action.
 - **Instruction Encoder:** A multi-layer transformer processes word embeddings from instructions (augmented with positional and type embeddings) to generate contextual instruction embeddings (I_hat).
 - **Cross-modal Transformer:** Node and instruction embeddings are fed into a multi-layer cross-modal transformer incorporating a Graph-Aware Self-Attention (GASA) layer to capture environment layout. This outputs navigation scores (s_k_t) for each navigable and imagination node.
 - **Action Fusion Policy:** An adaptive framework dynamically integrates navigation scores. Scores from imagination nodes are multiplied by a fusion factor (γ_t) and added to the scores of their nearest navigable nodes (as imagination nodes are not directly reachable). The fusion factor (γ_t) is generated by an FFN layer from concatenated imagination and real node embeddings, indicating the dynamic weighting of imagined information.

#### 3.2. Recurrent Imagination Tree

SALI utilizes a recurrent imagination module with a tree structure to generate future information (spatial knowledge and image features).

- **Initialization:** At step t, a history (H_t) of length K (depth, semantic, and position images), current RGB image (r_t), and neighboring positions (p_g_t) from G_t initialize an imaginary tree (T^0_t).
- **Iterative Generation:** The imagination tree grows iteratively for M steps. At each imagination step (N+1), from the input queue (T^N_t):
 - **Inpaint Model:** Converts historical data into structural maps via a point cloud projector. An encoder-decoder network (based on RedNet and ResNet) then generates one-hot semantic codes (e_t+1) and depth images (d_t+1) for each navigable position.
 - **Room Type Model:** To address ambiguity in semantic image generation, this model refines one-hot semantic codes. It uses commonsense knowledge about room-object correlations (e.g., refrigerator-kitchen) and a predefined object weight dictionary to adjust semantic predictions. This model consists of two ResNet-50 networks encoding semantic and depth images, outputting room type one-hot codes.
 - **Spade Model:** An image-to-image translation GAN (Wang et al., 2018a) generates high-resolution RGB images (r_N+1_t) using embeddings of the previous RGB image, a guidance image (generated from a point cloud projector), and the newly generated semantic and depth images as inputs.
 - **Waypoint Model:** A BERT-based model predicts the next navigable position (p_g,N+1_t) by taking the generated RGB-D images as input and producing a heatmap of probable waypoints.
- **Cross-correction:** The global memory and local imagination mechanisms are designed to be complementary, with imagination enhancing node features and map expansion, and the global map providing historical context and trajectory options to reduce imagination information loss.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

- **Multimodal Transformer Pre-training:** The multimodal transformer is pre-trained using expert behavior and imitation learning, incorporating auxiliary tasks such as masked language modeling (MLM), masked region classification (MRC), single-step action prediction (SAP), and object grounding (OG).
- **Imagination Model Pre-training:**
 - **Inpaint Model:** Trained by minimizing a combination of cross-entropy loss (for semantics) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) (for depth). The room-type model uses cross-entropy loss between predicted and true room type labels.
 - **Spade Model:** The generator is trained with GAN hinge loss, feature matching loss, and perceptual loss. The discriminator (PatchGAN-based) is trained with its own loss for ground-truth and generated images.
 - **Waypoint Model:** Trained by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between a predicted heatmap (m) and a ground-truth heatmap (m_hat) representing neighboring waypoints.
- **Inference:** During inference, SALI constructs an imagination-based global memory map on-the-fly, reasons about the next action over this map, and stops when the 'stop' node is selected or maximum action steps are reached.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SALI on two standard Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) benchmarks: R2R (fine-grained instructions) and REVERIE (coarse instructions with object grounding). The performance is assessed using common metrics including Navigation Error (NE), Trajectory Length (TL), Success Rate (SR), Success rate weighted by Path Length (SPL), Oracle Success Rate (OSR), Remote Grounding Success (RGS), and RGSPL (RGS penalized by path length).

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art (SoTA)

- **R2R Benchmark:** SALI demonstrates superior performance across all evaluation metrics on the R2R benchmark, particularly in unseen environments. For instance, on the Test Unseen-R2R split, SALI achieves an SPL of 74, surpassing the previous SoTA (Lily, 68; ScaleVLN, 68) by a notable margin of 6-8%. Its SR also reaches 79, higher than previous bests. This indicates improved alignment between scenes and instructions, leading to more efficient navigation.
- **REVERIE Benchmark:** SALI also outperforms all prior models on the REVERIE benchmark, achieving the highest RGS (34 on Test Unseen) and RGSPL (25 on Test Unseen) scores. This improvement is attributed to the imagination module's ability to enhance object recognition and association within the environment, which is crucial for object grounding tasks.

#### 4.2. Quantitative and Qualitative Analysis

- **Quantitative Study (Commonsense Reasoning):** To assess SALI's ability to process complex environment-related commonsense, the R2R validation unseen instruction set was divided into three categories:
 - S1: Instructions with over two room-related terms (e.g., "hallway," "restroom").
 - S2: Instructions with over two object-related terms (e.g., "oven," "fridge").
 - S3: Complex instructions with over four combined room and object terms.
 SALI exhibited the largest performance improvement (in SR and SPL) on the S3 instruction set compared to the previous best space-aware model, BEVBert. This suggests that SALI's imagination-memory mechanism effectively interprets intricate object-room associations and spatial relationships, reflecting enhanced commonsense reasoning capabilities akin to human episodic simulation.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations illustrate the imagination mechanism's generation of spatial and image information. Imagined RGB images for future goal positions show reasonable fidelity when compared to real images, as quantified by Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Pearson correlation coefficient. The waypoint prediction heatmaps also visually represent neighboring navigable positions effectively.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies on the R2R val-unseen split investigate the contribution of different components:

- **Imagination vs. Reality:**
 - A "reality only" agent achieved an SPL of 61.
 - An "imagination only" agent performed worst, with an SPL of 50.
 - The full "Reality + Imagination" hybrid memory mechanism achieved the highest SPL of 70 (note: this SPL is from the ablation, the full model achieves 78-74 on different splits as per table 1). This demonstrates that the hybrid approach significantly outperforms memory based solely on reality or imagination, highlighting the necessity of long-term memory to effectively leverage imagination results.
- **Temporal and Spatial Imaginary (Memory Range):**
 - Increasing the imagination output time step (M) and the upper node limit (N_bar) generally improved navigation performance (e.g., from M=0, N_bar=0 with SPL 61 to M=2, N_bar=4 with SPL 71).
 - However, excessively large ranges (e.g., M=2, N_bar=8 leading to SPL 68) resulted in a decline in SPL, possibly due to agents at different positions imagining the same destination, leading to decision interference.
- **Auxiliary Model Effectiveness:**
 - The "None" baseline (without room or waypoint models) had an SPL of 61.
 - Including only the "Room" type model improved SPL to 64.
 - Including only the "Waypoint" model improved SPL to 66.
 - The combination of "Room + Waypoint" models yielded the optimal SPL of 71. This indicates that both the room type model, which refines semantic predictions, and the waypoint model, which aids in predicting navigable positions, are essential for effective decision-making.
- **Dynamic Decision-making (Fusion Weight):**
 - A "Fixed" fusion factor (γ_t = 0.5) resulted in an SPL of 67.
 - The "Dynamic" fusion factor achieved an SPL of 71. This demonstrates the effectiveness of dynamically adjusting the weight of imagined information, with the weight decreasing over time as navigation progresses, paralleling human memory mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SALI, an agent equipped with an imagination-integrated memory mechanism, marking a significant step in the field of embodied AI, particularly Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN).

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Novelty in Memory Architecture:** SALI introduces a novel reality-imagination hybrid memory system that explicitly integrates generated future scenes into a persistent topological map. This is distinct from prior imaginative approaches where predictions were largely transient.
- **Cognitive Inspiration:** The work is explicitly inspired by human episodic memory and episodic simulation, providing a computational model for how such cognitive abilities can enhance an agent's understanding and interaction with complex, unfamiliar environments. This bridges a gap between neuroscience insights and practical AI system design.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** By enabling agents to "imagine" and reason about unseen environments, SALI demonstrates improved robustness and performance in novel scenarios, a critical challenge for current VLN systems. The significant performance gains on unseen splits of R2R and REVERIE benchmarks validate this.
- **Commonsense Reasoning:** The quantitative analysis on complex instructions (S3) suggests that SALI's imaginative capabilities facilitate a deeper level of commonsense reasoning, allowing it to interpret intricate object-room associations and spatial relationships more effectively than previous models.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advanced Embodied AI:** This work contributes to the development of more intelligent and adaptable embodied agents. Agents capable of anticipatory knowledge through imagination are better equipped for real-world tasks where complete prior knowledge is often unavailable.
- **Improved Navigation Systems:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in VLN tasks, especially in unseen environments, indicates the potential for more reliable and efficient autonomous navigation systems in complex indoor environments. This could benefit applications such as domestic robots, industrial inspection bots, and virtual assistants.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** The hybrid memory architecture and recurrent imagination module provide a foundation for future research into cognitive robotics. This includes exploring more sophisticated imaginative mechanisms, incorporating different modalities of imagination (e.g., auditory, tactile), and developing more computationally efficient ways to achieve high-fidelity scene generation.
- **Bridging AI and Cognitive Science:** By operationalizing concepts from human cognition, this research also offers a practical framework that can inform and inspire further interdisciplinary studies between AI, robotics, and cognitive neuroscience.
- **Adaptability in Dynamic Environments:** The dynamic fusion policy, which adjusts the reliance on imagination over time, suggests a model for agents to adapt their decision-making strategies as they gather more real-time information, mirroring human cognitive flexibility.

Future work outlined by the authors focuses on optimizing the computational efficiency of the imagination process, which is a practical consideration for real-world deployment of such cognitively-inspired agents.
