# Exploring Synthesizable Chemical Space with Iterative Pathway Refinements

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST and NVIDIA developed ReaSyn, an iterative generative framework that projects input molecules onto synthesizable chemical space by refining synthetic pathways through bidirectional decoding and holistic editing. ReaSyn achieved an 87.9% molecule reconstruction rate on the ZINC250k dataset, outperforming previous methods like SynFormer (18.0%), and demonstrated superior performance in synthesizable goal-directed optimization and hit expansion tasks.

## Problem

- Molecular generative models frequently output molecules that are not practically synthesizable, hindering their application in drug discovery.
- Existing methods using heuristic synthesizability scores are often insufficient, as they fail to account for complex factors like available building blocks and reaction selectivity.
- Current synthesizable projection methods suffer from poor coverage and difficulty in reliably navigating the vast combinatorial chemical space to generate diverse and accurate synthetic pathways.

## Method

- Developed **ReaSyn**, an iterative generative framework employing a simple, non-hierarchical, ==SMILES-based== sequential representation for synthetic pathways.
- Utilized a single autoregressive Transformer model trained for both bottom-up (from building blocks) and top-down (from product) pathway generation.
- Implemented an iterative refinement cycle combining bidirectional decoding with a novel discrete flow model, Edit Bridge, for holistic pathway editing, to explore and refine generated synthetic routes.

## Results

- ReaSyn achieved a **87.9%** reconstruction rate on the ZINC250k dataset, significantly outperforming SynFormer (**18.0%**), and consistently demonstrated superior performance in synthesizable molecule reconstruction across various benchmarks.
- The framework surpassed existing synthesis-based baselines in goal-directed molecular optimization tasks, finding synthesizable chemical optima with high sampling efficiency (e.g., 5k oracle calls compared to 300k for baselines).
- In synthesizable hit expansion, ReaSyn showed superior analog (**75.7%**) and improve (**11.8%**) rates compared to prior methods, successfully generating diverse and potent synthesizable analogs of hit molecules.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A non-hierarchical sequential representation using SMILES strings for building blocks reduces information loss and complexity compared to molecular fingerprint-based approaches.
> - Unifying bidirectional (bottom-up and top-down) pathway generation within an iterative refinement cycle greatly improves the exploration and navigability of synthesizable chemical space.
> - Incorporating a holistic editing mechanism, Edit Bridge, further refines generated pathways by applying sequence-wide edit operations, enhancing overall quality and synthesizability.
