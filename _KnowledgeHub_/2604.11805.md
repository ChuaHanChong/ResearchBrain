# Solving Physics Olympiad via Reinforcement Learning on Physics Simulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Carnegie Mellon University and Lambda researchers developed Sim2Reason, a framework that leverages physics simulators to generate scalable, high-fidelity question-answer pairs for training large language models in physical reasoning. This approach enables LLMs to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and improve performance on real-world physics benchmarks, including up to a +17.9% gain on JEEBench and competitive accuracy on IPhO mechanics.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) lack robust multi-step physical reasoning skills due to a scarcity of high-quality, scalable question-answer datasets in physics.
- Current approaches that use physics simulators as external tools often require LLMs to generate complex, simulator-specific code, which is challenging and unscalable.
- Directly using raw simulator outputs is difficult because physics problem-solving requires accurate, inverse, symbolic, and counterfactual reasoning, which differs from the simulator's continuous, forward-time numerical traces.

## Method

- The **Sim2Reason** framework procedurally generates diverse physics scenes using a ==Domain Specific Language (DSL)==, then simulates their dynamics to create rich, verifiable time-series data.
- Natural language question-answer pairs (numeric, reverse, symbolic) are automatically formulated from the simulated data, and a crucial ==data filtration== step removes =="shortcut solutions"== by using ==scene ablations==.
- Large language models are post-trained using ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== on this high-fidelity, synthetically generated and filtered dataset, bypassing the need for human-annotated data.

## Results

- LLMs post-trained on Sim2Reason's synthetic data achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, improving performance on real-world benchmarks by **+5-10%** on IPhO Mechanics, **+17.9%** on JEEBench, and **+3.67%** on PHYSICS.
- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) consistently outperformed Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), yielding robust improvements on out-of-distribution benchmarks (**+5.4%** on IPhO for Qwen2.5-32B) without catastrophic forgetting.
- Sim2Reason models, using only synthetic data, achieved **40.0%** accuracy on IPhO mechanics with Qwen3-30B, outperforming several models post-trained on real-world physics QA corpora and showing strong correlation with real-world performance (Spearman rho = **0.79**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics simulators, when paired with a structured data generation and filtration pipeline, can serve as a scalable source of high-fidelity, verifiable supervision for training LLMs in complex physical reasoning.
> - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) on synthetic data enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, allowing LLMs to generalize physical reasoning skills to real-world problems like the International Physics Olympiad.
> - Explicitly filtering out "shortcut solutions" from the generated data, which do not require deep multi-entity reasoning, is essential for improving the quality of LLM training and enhancing out-of-distribution generalization.
