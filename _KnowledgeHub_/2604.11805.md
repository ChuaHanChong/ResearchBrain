# Solving Physics Olympiad via Reinforcement Learning on Physics Simulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Carnegie Mellon University and Lambda researchers developed Sim2Reason, a framework that leverages physics simulators to generate scalable, high-fidelity question-answer pairs for training large language models in physical reasoning. This approach enables LLMs to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and improve performance on real-world physics benchmarks, including up to a +17.9% gain on JEEBench and competitive accuracy on IPhO mechanics.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) lack robust multi-step physical reasoning skills due to a scarcity of high-quality, scalable question-answer datasets in physics.
- Current approaches that use physics simulators as external tools often require LLMs to generate complex, simulator-specific code, which is challenging and unscalable.
- Directly using raw simulator outputs is difficult because physics problem-solving requires accurate, inverse, symbolic, and counterfactual reasoning, which differs from the simulator's continuous, forward-time numerical traces.

## Method

- The **Sim2Reason** framework procedurally generates diverse physics scenes using a ==Domain Specific Language (DSL)==, then simulates their dynamics to create rich, verifiable time-series data.
- Natural language question-answer pairs (numeric, reverse, symbolic) are automatically formulated from the simulated data, and a crucial ==data filtration== step removes =="shortcut solutions"== by using ==scene ablations==.
- Large language models are post-trained using ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== on this high-fidelity, synthetically generated and filtered dataset, bypassing the need for human-annotated data.

## Results

- LLMs post-trained on Sim2Reason's synthetic data achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, improving performance on real-world benchmarks by **+5-10%** on IPhO Mechanics, **+17.9%** on JEEBench, and **+3.67%** on PHYSICS.
- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) consistently outperformed Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), yielding robust improvements on out-of-distribution benchmarks (**+5.4%** on IPhO for Qwen2.5-32B) without catastrophic forgetting.
- Sim2Reason models, using only synthetic data, achieved **40.0%** accuracy on IPhO mechanics with Qwen3-30B, outperforming several models post-trained on real-world physics QA corpora and showing strong correlation with real-world performance (Spearman rho = **0.79**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics simulators, when paired with a structured data generation and filtration pipeline, can serve as a scalable source of high-fidelity, verifiable supervision for training LLMs in complex physical reasoning.
> - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) on synthetic data enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, allowing LLMs to generalize physical reasoning skills to real-world problems like the International Physics Olympiad.
> - Explicitly filtering out "shortcut solutions" from the generated data, which do not require deep multi-entity reasoning, is essential for improving the quality of LLM training and enhancing out-of-distribution generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a current limitation in the development of reasoning capabilities in large language models (LLMs), particularly in scientific domains beyond mathematics. Recent advancements in LLM reasoning, often driven by techniques like Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), have largely relied on the availability of extensive high-quality question-answer (QA) pairs. However, such data is finite, unevenly distributed across subjects, and particularly scarce in physical sciences like physics and chemistry compared to mathematics. This scarcity acts as a bottleneck for training LLMs to acquire robust multi-step scientific reasoning skills.

Existing approaches to integrate physics into LLMs include using simulators as external tools, but this often shifts the challenge to complex code generation and requires human intervention for phenomena not natively supported by simulators. Prior work in synthetic data generation for LLMs has largely focused on domains with clean symbolic structure and canonical forms of supervision, such such as geometry (e.g., AlphaGeometry) or mathematical problems (e.g., MetaMath, Self-Instruct). This paper extends the paradigm of synthetic data generation to physical reasoning, where supervision is derived from continuous physics simulations rather than pre-existing symbolic problem sets. It positions itself as a method for turning physics simulators into scalable generators of verified QA pairs, thus removing the dependency on human-annotated data or the complexities of LLM-driven simulator tool use. The research seeks to demonstrate that simulator-derived data can enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, allowing LLMs to generalize physical reasoning skills to real-world physics problems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the data scarcity bottleneck that limits the development of advanced physical reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models. The motivation stems from several key observations and challenges:

- **Scarcity of High-Quality Physics QA Data:** While LLMs have shown progress in reasoning, largely attributed to large datasets of QA pairs, domains like physics lack comparable large-scale, high-quality datasets. Existing internet-derived QA data is sparse, unevenly distributed, and not systematically varied, leading to incomplete supervision for scientific reasoning tasks. This deficiency restricts the effectiveness of methods like Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in physics.
- **Limitations of Current Approaches to Physics Reasoning with LLMs:**
 - **Reliance on Internet Data:** The reliance on existing internet QA data primarily fuels progress in domains like mathematics, leaving other sciences underserved. For example, less than 1% of QA pairs in large reasoning datasets are STEM-related, leading to poor generalization on physics benchmarks.
 - **Physics Simulators as Tools (Code Generation Challenge):** While physics engines encode physical laws and can generate rich data, directly using them as LLM tools for problem-solving is non-trivial. It often requires LLMs to master complex, simulator-specific APIs for code generation, a task they frequently struggle with, and which is unscalable due to the need for human-in-the-loop engineering for unsupported phenomena.
 - **Simulator Output Mismatch:** Simulator outputs are typically approximate, continuous, forward-time numerical traces. In contrast, physics problem-solving often requires accurate, inverse, symbolic, and counterfactual reasoning, making direct use of simulator data challenging for LLMs.
- **Need for Scalable, High-Fidelity Supervision:** To address these limitations, there is a need for a scalable source of high-fidelity supervision that systematically covers diverse physical phenomena and provides verifiable answers.

The paper aims to demonstrate that physics simulators can serve as this alternative source of supervision. The specific objectives include:

- Developing a framework, Sim2Reason, that transforms physics simulators into scalable generators of physically grounded question-answer pairs.
- Generating diverse physical scenes and simulating their dynamics to automatically produce verified QA pairs without human annotation.
- Creating a Domain Specific Language (DSL) to structure scene randomization, allowing for procedural generation of complex and varied reasoning problems across classical mechanics.
- Training LLMs using reinforcement learning on this synthetically generated data to improve their physical reasoning skills.
- Evaluating the zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities of these models on real-world physics benchmarks such as the International Physics Olympiad (IPhO), JEE-Bench, and PHYSICS.
- Investigating the effectiveness of different QA formats and the importance of data filtration strategies (e.g., shortcut solution pruning) for training quality.
- Assessing the scalability and portability of the proposed data generation pipeline across different simulators and for novel scene types.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to enable LLMs to acquire deeper physical reasoning skills, moving beyond the constraints of internet-scale QA data and facilitating advancements in AI for scientific discovery and education.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Sim2Reason framework proposes a four-stage pipeline for generating synthetic physics data and training LLMs: Scene Generation, Physics Simulation, QA Pair Generation, and Data Filtration, followed by RL Training.

#### 3.1. Scene Generation

To create diverse and physically meaningful random scenes, the authors developed a Domain Specific Language (DSL). This DSL abstracts away low-level simulator details, focusing on physically relevant parameters for randomization.
- **Abstraction Levels:** The DSL operates at three levels:
 - **Body:** The fundamental element (e.g., a mass, sphere, pulley). Each body has a name, type-specific parameters (e.g., mass of a block, radius of a sphere), a template MuJoCo XML snippet, and a natural language description template.
 - **Entity:** A set of bodies connected in a physically meaningful way (e.g., `MassWithFixedPulley`, `StackedMassPlane`). Entities expose defined connection points for interaction.
 - **Scene:** Formed by randomly selecting and connecting multiple entities.
- **Procedural Generation:** The DSL allows for the dynamic composition of heterogeneous physical scenes (e.g., combining pulley systems with rotational dynamics). Scenes are compiled into MuJoCo XML by concatenating entity-specific XML templates, enabling scalable generation without human intervention. The DSL covers kinematics, rotational mechanics, orbital motion, variable-mass systems, and basic electromagnetism.

#### 3.2. Physics Simulation

Generated scenes are simulated in MuJoCo, a physics engine.
- **Data Recording:** Key physical quantities for each body are recorded as time-series data. This includes proprioceptive quantities for masses (displacement, velocity, acceleration, mass, momentum, net force, kinetic/potential energy, inertia) and string-related data (length, velocity, force, stiffness). The simulator was extended to support variable-mass systems, Newtonian gravitation, and collisions with a specified coefficient of restitution.
- **Unmodeled Transition Detection:** Simulation traces can contain unpredictable dynamics due to unmodeled events (e.g., block colliding with a pulley, falling off a plane). A filtering mechanism truncates unstable segments of the trace by detecting spikes in acceleration, comparing sliding-window mean and standard deviation against a threshold `k` (set to 5). Only stable prefixes are used for QA generation.

#### 3.3. QA Pair Generation

From the recorded time-series data and scene configurations, natural language question-answer pairs are automatically generated.
- **Scene Description:** A natural language description of the scene is created by concatenating descriptions of its entities and their connections.
- **Question Formulation:** Questions are generated by randomly selecting a body, a recorded physical quantity, and a timestep. Three reasoning modes are supported:
 - **Numeric Questions:** Forward reasoning (e.g., "What is the velocity of block A at time 3 s?"). These were primarily used for RL training.
 - **Reverse Questions:** Inverse reasoning, where a scene parameter is masked (e.g., "What is the mass of block A if its velocity after 3 s is 5 m/s?").
 - **Symbolic Questions:** Symbolic reasoning, where numeric parameters are replaced by symbols (e.g., "What is the velocity of block A after time t?").
- **Answer Retrieval:** The ground-truth answer is directly retrieved from the recorded sensor data.

#### 3.4. Data Filtration

A crucial step is filtering the generated data to remove "shortcut solutions."
- **Shortcut Detection:** These are cases where an LLM could obtain a correct numeric answer by ignoring parts of the scene or oversimplifying multi-body interactions, thus not requiring complex physical reasoning.
- **Ablation Strategy:** To detect shortcuts, controlled "ablations" of each scene are created:
 - **Entity-removal ablations:** Sub-scenes are generated by removing one entity at a time while maintaining connectivity, and then re-simulated.
 - **Joint-removal ablations:** Individual joints or constraints are replaced by rigid components.
- **Pruning:** If the ground-truth answer for a given question remains unchanged between the original scene and any ablated variant, the QA pair is discarded. This ensures that training focuses on problems requiring genuine multi-entity dynamics reasoning, with approximately 15% of generated QA pairs being filtered.

#### 3.5. RL Training

LLMs are post-trained using Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).
- **Reward Function:** For each prompt, a group of responses is sampled from the current policy. A scalar reward is assigned based on the correctness of the final answer, allowing for a 5% relative error tolerance to account for numerical approximations in the simulator.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) is used, with group-relative advantages computed by normalizing rewards within each group. The GSPO loss is a clipped, sequence-level policy-gradient objective.
- **Dynamic Sampling:** DAPO-style dynamic sampling is incorporated to improve efficiency in sparse-reward settings. If a prompt yields near-zero reward standard deviation across its group, additional prompts are resampled to fill the batch with more informative examples.
- **Models and Training Steps:** Qwen2.5 Instruct checkpoints (3B, 7B, 14B, 32B) and Qwen3-30B-Instruct were used. Qwen2.5 models were trained for 200 RL steps, while Qwen3-30B was trained for 100 steps due to higher computational cost associated with longer responses. No real-world physics QA pairs were used during this post-training phase.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness of Sim2Reason in training LLMs for physical reasoning.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** LLMs post-trained solely on synthetic Sim2Reason data demonstrated consistent zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to real-world physics benchmarks.
 - **IPhO Mechanics:** Improvements of 5–10 percentage points were observed across Qwen2.5 model sizes (3B to 32B). For instance, Qwen2.5-3B saw a +7.5% increase, and Qwen2.5-32B achieved a +5.4% gain. Even the stronger Qwen3-30B-Instruct model improved by +4.4%.
 - **JEEBench:** Qwen2.5-32B showed the largest gain, an increase of +17.9%.
 - **PHYSICS:** Qwen2.5-32B improved by +3.67%.
 - **OlympiadBench:** Qwen2.5-32B saw a +3.12% increase.
- **Generalization Across Reasoning Modes:** Although trained on numeric questions, Qwen2.5 models also showed improvements on synthetic reverse (+4.0% to +4.8%) and symbolic (+4.8% to +6.2%) reasoning modes, indicating the learning of reusable physical reasoning patterns.
- **Impact on Math Benchmarks:** Improvements were also observed on out-of-domain math benchmarks like AIME 2025 (+1.67% for Qwen2.5-32B) and MATH 500 (+4.4% for Qwen2.5-32B), suggesting that physics reasoning training strengthens underlying algebraic and multi-step quantitative skills.

#### 4.2. Training Strategies and Data Composition

- **RLVR vs. SFT:** Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) significantly outperformed Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). While SFT on strong teacher models (GPT-4, o3, o4-mini) yielded modest in-distribution gains, it led to a substantial degradation (-3.9%) in out-of-distribution performance on IPhO mechanics for Qwen2.5-32B, potentially due to catastrophic forgetting. RLVR, by contrast, provided robust improvements both in-distribution (+18.0% on synthetic data) and out-of-distribution (+5.4% on IPhO).
- **Ablations on QA Format and Data Filtration:**
 - **QA Format:** Training with numeric questions yielded the strongest transfer to IPhO mechanics compared to reverse or symbolic questions.
 - **Shortcut Filtering:** The data filtration strategy, which removes shortcut-solvable questions through scene ablations, was critical. Training without filtering yielded substantially smaller gains on IPhO mechanics (+7.14% baseline vs. +13.15% with filtering for Qwen2.5-3B), highlighting its importance in focusing training on problems requiring true multi-entity dynamics reasoning.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Real-World Post-Training Data

- **Outperforming Real-World QA:** Sim2Reason, using only synthetic data, achieved 40.0% accuracy on IPhO mechanics with Qwen3-30B, outperforming models post-trained on curated real-world physics QA corpora, such as Prime P1 30B (38.6%), DAPO-32B (24.7%), and LIMO-32B (25.5%). This suggests that simulator supervision can be competitive with or exceed the effectiveness of real-world corpora for physics reasoning.
- **Comparison with DAPO-17K:** Training on Sim2Reason's synthetic mechanics data yielded substantially better IPhO transfer for Qwen2.5-3B (+13.15%) than training on the larger (17K samples) but generic math-focused DAPO-17K dataset (+9.98%). Combining both datasets showed a minor additional improvement (+10.35%), indicating domain-aligned simulator data provides a higher-signal training distribution for physics.

#### 4.4. Simulator as a Benchmark

- **Correlation with Real-World Performance:** Accuracy on Sim2Reason synthetic questions showed a strong correlation (Spearman $\rho$ = 0.79) with IPhO mechanics accuracy across various models. This finding suggests that simulator-based evaluation can serve as a scalable and lightweight proxy for real-world physics reasoning performance, enabling rapid iteration and fine-grained diagnostics.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Capabilities

- **Scalability of the Pipeline:** The DSL abstraction proved effective for extending the pipeline to new scene types. LLM agents tasked with implementing real-world physics problems in MuJoCo succeeded in 100% of cases using DSL-based generation (with minor corrections), compared to only 33% success for direct MuJoCo XML generation. This indicates the DSL offers a more effective abstraction.
- **Simulator Portability:** LLMs successfully ported a subset of DSL entities from MuJoCo to NVIDIA Omniverse, demonstrating the portability of the DSL across different simulator backends.
- **Coverage Across Difficulty Levels:** RL post-training on Sim2Reason data improved performance across all difficulty tiers of the PHYSICS benchmark. The largest gains (+5.6%) were observed at the Postgraduate tier, suggesting the approach particularly strengthens harder multi-step quantitative reasoning.
- **Generalization Beyond Simulation:** Improvements transferred to physics problems not directly covered by the simulator's current entity library (e.g., a rocket-planet-star system). For an advanced problem not explicitly modelable with current entities, the base Qwen2.5-32B model failed in all 8 trials, while the RL-finetuned model achieved a 50% success rate (4/8). This suggests the model learns transferable abstractions rather than merely overfitting to simulated scenes.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Qualitative analysis revealed improvements in arithmetic (reducing calculation errors), physical reasoning (mapping text to correct equations and boundary conditions), and strategic planning (e.g., unit conversions, intermediate checks).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents Sim2Reason as a significant contribution to advancing the scientific reasoning capabilities of large language models, particularly in physics.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** The work demonstrates a scalable and automated pipeline for generating high-quality, verifiable physics question-answer pairs, effectively addressing the critical bottleneck of data scarcity in scientific domains for LLM training. This removes the reliance on limited, human-annotated internet QA data.
- **Enabling Deeper Physical Reasoning:** By leveraging the executable nature of physical laws in simulators, the approach provides LLMs with a rich, high-fidelity supervision signal that goes beyond superficial pattern matching. The consistent zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to complex, real-world benchmarks like IPhO indicates that LLMs can acquire and generalize fundamental physical reasoning skills.
- **Validation of RLVR in New Domains:** The successful application of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to physics, a domain characterized by continuous dynamics rather than purely symbolic structures, expands the scope and utility of this training paradigm.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The findings highlight that carefully designed synthetic data, combined with appropriate training strategies (RLVR and data filtration), can lead to models that not only perform well on in-domain tasks but also generalize to novel scenarios, harder problems, and even related out-of-domain mathematical reasoning tasks.
- **New Benchmarking Paradigm:** The strong correlation between performance on synthetic questions and real-world benchmarks suggests that physics simulators can serve as a scalable and automated tool for evaluating and diagnosing LLM capabilities in scientific reasoning, accelerating research and development.
- **Scalable and Portable Framework:** The Domain Specific Language (DSL) offers an effective abstraction for extending the pipeline to new scenarios and porting entities across different simulator backends, indicating the flexibility and future-proofing of the approach.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Positive Impacts:**
 - **Improved Scientific Tutoring and Problem-Solving Tools:** The development of more capable physics reasoning LLMs could lead to advanced AI-powered tools for education, research, and engineering, providing accessible and high-quality assistance for learning and problem-solving in science.
 - **Reduced Dependence on Human Annotation:** Automating the data generation process significantly reduces the labor and cost associated with curating large-scale datasets, democratizing access to high-quality training data for LLM development in scientific fields.
 - **Advancements in Scientific AI:** The methodology could be extended to other scientific domains beyond classical mechanics (e.g., electromagnetism, thermodynamics, chemistry), accelerating progress in diverse areas of scientific AI.
- **Potential Risks and Mitigations:**
 - **Misuse of Stronger Reasoning Models:** Enhanced reasoning capabilities in AI could potentially be misused, such as assisting in harmful engineering applications.
 - **Over-reliance on Simulator-Generated Supervision:** Simulators inherently embody modeling assumptions and may exhibit failure modes that do not perfectly reflect the real world. Over-reliance on such data could lead to models inheriting these limitations or misrepresenting real-world physics.
 - **Mitigation Strategies:** The authors emphasize continuous evaluation on real-world benchmarks to ensure fidelity, reporting the limitations of simulator fidelity and coverage, and encouraging the inclusion of safeguards, monitoring, and domain-specific validation in downstream deployments to address these risks.

Future work could focus on combining simulator-generated data with curated real-world QA to further enhance robustness and coverage, and expanding the approach to other branches of physics and physical sciences.
