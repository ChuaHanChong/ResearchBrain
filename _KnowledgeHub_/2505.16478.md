# Unified Multi-Rate Model Predictive Control for a Jet-Powered Humanoid Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed a unified multi-rate Model Predictive Control framework for jet-powered humanoid robots. This framework explicitly integrates second-order jet engine dynamics and accommodates varying actuator update rates, enabling stable flight, disturbance recovery, and accurate trajectory tracking in simulations with real-time performance.

## Problem

- Prior control strategies for jet-powered humanoids often neglected the highly nonlinear, second-order dynamics of jet engines, leading to inaccurate models and suboptimal control.
- Complex robotic systems like humanoids have diverse actuators (e.g., high-frequency joints, low-frequency jet engines) with disparate update rates, which traditional MPC frameworks struggle to handle natively.
- Real-time control of nonlinear dynamic systems with MPC is computationally intensive, limiting control frequency and practical implementation on physical robots.

## Method

- Proposed a ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework that unifies a linearized centroidal momentum model with a second-order nonlinear jet dynamics model.
- Employed a Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) approach to linearize the combined dynamics around the current operating point, transforming the problem into a computationally efficient Quadratic Program (QP).
- Developed a multi-rate MPC formulation that natively handles different actuator frequencies by adjusting control input computations within the prediction horizon based on actuator update rates.

## Results

- The controller successfully recovered from external disturbances (300 Nm torque, 50 N force), with transient tilts of 15 degrees roll and displacements of about 1 meter before stabilization.
- Demonstrated accurate tracking of minimum jerk trajectories, achieving Mean Absolute Errors (MAE) of less than **0.01** radians for Roll and Yaw orientation, and approximately **0.03** radians for Pitch.
- Achieved real-time feasibility with an average MPC computation time of **2.18** ms (max **4.45** ms), well within the 5 ms control period for 200 Hz operation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling the complex, nonlinear dynamics of jet engines within the MPC is critical for achieving stable flight in jet-powered humanoids, as demonstrated by the failure of controllers that omitted these dynamics.
> - Continuous linearization (LPV) of the system dynamics around the current operating point is essential for maintaining robust control performance during dynamic maneuvers, unlike static linearization.
> - Embedding multi-rate actuation capabilities directly into the MPC framework, rather than using hierarchical methods or discarding commands, significantly improves tracking performance and reduces oscillations in orientation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditionally, humanoid robots have been engineered for terrestrial locomotion, replicating human-like walking and manipulative capabilities. The emergence of flying humanoid robots introduces a novel paradigm, integrating human morphology with aerial mobility. This hybrid capability expands the scope of potential applications into domains such as disaster response, search and rescue, and exploration, where both agile ground interaction and aerial locomotion are beneficial.

The existing literature presents various examples of aerial robots equipped with manipulation or locomotion functionalities. These include aerial manipulators (flying platforms with robotic arms or grippers), hexapod-quadrotor hybrids, ground-and-air capable quadrotors, insect-inspired biobots combining flight and walking, and shape-shifting robots designed for multi-modal locomotion. A specific area of research involves humanoid robots fitted with thrusters to support bipedal motion. The iRonCub project, which serves as the experimental platform for this research, aims to integrate manipulation, aerial mobility, and terrestrial locomotion onto a single system by equipping the iCub 3 humanoid robot with four jet engines on its arms and shoulders.

Control strategies for aerial vehicles have been extensively investigated, encompassing a range from classical Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) control to advanced nonlinear and optimal control techniques, as well as data-driven methods, including those that leverage thrust-vectoring for tilting-propeller quadrotors. However, much of this prior research has focused on single-body aerial systems, rather than articulated robots.

Previous work on the control of jet-powered humanoids, particularly on the iCub platform, primarily involved momentum-based strategies. These include frameworks for asymptotically stabilizing the centroidal momentum using a vectored-thrust approach within a hierarchical control structure, and task-based controllers capable of tracking full position and attitude trajectories. More recently, nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) has been applied to stabilize both legged and aerial phases of robot trajectories.

A notable gap in these prior control strategies for jet-powered humanoids is the general neglect of the specific dynamics of the jet engines, which are characterized by high nonlinearity and second-order behavior. While some research has addressed jet dynamics in trajectory generation, such methods have typically been offline and reliant on external whole-body controllers for stabilization, rendering them unsuitable for real-time applications. A recent Reinforcement Learning framework incorporated jet dynamics for walking and flying, but it requires precise torque-level control, which may not be universally available, and did not explicitly address the limitations imposed by the low bandwidth actuation of jet engines.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing an online flight controller that explicitly accounts for the complex, nonlinear dynamics of jet propulsion and the varying actuation rates of the robot’s components. By integrating a linearized centroidal momentum model with a second-order nonlinear jet model within a multi-rate Model Predictive Control framework, the authors aim to overcome the identified limitations in real-time control for jet-powered humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose a novel Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework designed for a jet-powered flying humanoid robot. This framework aims to enable stable and precise flight maneuvers while addressing several specific challenges inherent to such complex robotic systems.

The key motivations and objectives underpinning this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Incorporation of Jet Engine Dynamics:** A significant motivation stems from the observation that previous controllers for jet-powered humanoids often neglected the intrinsic dynamics of the jet engines. These dynamics are characterized by high nonlinearity and second-order behavior, as documented in prior research. The paper aims to explicitly integrate a comprehensive model of these jet dynamics directly into the predictive control framework. This is motivated by the necessity for a more accurate representation of the robot's propulsion system to achieve robust and effective control, especially given the observed discrepancies between simplified models and actual jet behavior.

2. **Real-Time Computational Feasibility:** Solving nonlinear MPC problems can impose a substantial computational burden, which limits the achievable control frequency and thus hinders real-time implementation on physical robots. To address this, a core objective is to formulate the MPC problem in a manner that allows for high-frequency execution. This is achieved through the adoption of a Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) approach, where the combined centroidal momentum and jet dynamics are linearized around the current operating point. The motivation here is to transform the complex nonlinear control problem into a computationally tractable linear quadratic program (QP), thereby facilitating real-time control.

3. **Management of Multi-Rate Actuation:** Jet-powered humanoid robots, such as iRonCub, possess diverse actuators, specifically high-frequency joints and low-frequency jet engines. Traditional MPC frameworks often assume a single, uniform control rate. The paper is motivated to develop a unified MPC formulation that natively handles these disparate actuation rates. Unlike hierarchical or cascaded control architectures that decompose the problem, or approaches that focus solely on sampling and actuation mismatches, this work seeks to embed the multi-rate capability directly within the MPC, allowing it to compute and apply control inputs at distinct frequencies for different actuator types. This objective aims to enhance control performance by respecting the hardware limitations and operational characteristics of each actuator.

4. **Demonstration of Robustness and Stability:** The authors aim to validate the proposed framework's capability to maintain stable flight and recover from external disturbances. This motivation reflects the practical requirement for aerial robots to operate reliably in dynamic and unpredictable environments. The objective includes demonstrating the controller's ability to execute stable, non-abrupt flight maneuvers and to track reference trajectories with acceptable accuracy.

5. **Application to the iRonCub Platform:** The research is directly motivated by and conducted in the context of the iRonCub project, which aims to enable the iCub 3 humanoid robot to fly. The goal is to develop a control solution that can be transitioned from simulation to the real iRonCub robot, addressing specific hardware-related limitations such as the slow dynamics and low control frequency of the jet turbines.

In summary, the key objectives are to develop a unified, real-time capable MPC for jet-powered humanoids that explicitly models complex jet dynamics and accommodates multi-rate actuation, ultimately enabling robust and stable flight control for platforms like iRonCub.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a novel Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework designed for a jet-powered flying humanoid robot, integrating a linearized centroidal momentum model with a second-order nonlinear jet dynamics model. The methodology addresses real-time computational demands and the differing actuation rates of robot components.

**Robot Modeling:**
The robot is modeled as a multi-body system using a floating-base formalism. To simplify the control problem and reduce computational load, the authors opt for a **centroidal momentum equation** rather than the full equation of motion. This approach models the robot’s dynamics with fewer variables, enhancing suitability for real-time implementation. The centroidal momentum is defined in the robot's body frame and includes linear and angular momentum components. The time derivative of this momentum is expressed in terms of thrust forces from the jet turbines, joint positions, gravity, and the robot's angular velocity.
To further simplify, two key assumptions are made:
1. **Assumption 1:** The angular component of the centroidal momentum is approximated as the product of the robot's total inertia and the body-frame angular velocity, assuming small joint velocities during flight. This allows a direct relationship between Euler angle rates and angular momentum.
2. **Jet Dynamics:** The dynamics of each jet turbine are modeled as a second-order nonlinear system, where thrust (T_i) is the output of a differential equation driven by an auxiliary input (v_i) related to the throttle command.

**Unified Dynamical Model:**
The overall state vector `z` for the robot's dynamics is composed of the center of mass position, linear momentum, Euler angles, angular momentum, jet thrust, and the rate of change of jet thrust. The control input vector `u` consists of joint positions (`s`) and the auxiliary jet throttle inputs (`v`). These components are combined into a compact nonlinear system of equations `˙z = f(z, u)`.

**Linearization Strategy:**
To enable the use of a linear MPC formulation, which is computationally less demanding and allows for higher control frequencies, the nonlinear dynamic model is linearized. A **Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) approach** is employed, where the system dynamics are linearized around the current state (`z_c`) and control input (`u_c`) using a first-order Taylor series expansion.
**Assumption 2:** In the linearization process, the rotation matrix between the world and body frames, the skew-symmetric matrix of the body angular velocity, and the robot's total inertia matrix are assumed to remain constant over the MPC's short prediction horizon. This is justified by the expectation of slow orientation changes and joint movements during flight maneuvers. The linearization yields a time-varying linear system `˙z ≈ A(z, u)z + B(z, u)u + c(z, u)`, where `c(z, u)` accounts for bias terms. Detailed computation of linearization matrices related to joint position derivatives is provided in the appendix.

**MPC Formulation:**
The core of the control strategy is a linear MPC. To improve reference tracking and mitigate steady-state offset, the state vector `z` is augmented with two integral error variables: one for the center of mass position (`e_x`) and another for Euler angles (`e_ϕ`).
The MPC problem is formulated as a quadratic program (QP) that minimizes a weighted sum of:
- Tracking errors for center of mass position (`x_G`), linear momentum (`h_p`), Euler angles (`ϕ`), and angular momentum (`h_w`).
- The change in control input (`∆u`) for regularization.
- The augmented integral error variables (`e_x`, `e_ϕ`).
The optimization is subject to:
- The discretized linear system dynamics (using the Forward Euler method).
- The initial state at the current time.
- Box constraints on joint positions (`s_min` ≤ `s_k` ≤ `s_max`) and jet throttle auxiliary inputs (`v_min` ≤ `v_k` ≤ `v_max`).
The problem is solved using the OSQP solver.

**Multi-Rate MPC:**
A key contribution is the **multi-rate MPC formulation** to handle the different actuation rates of the robot’s joints and jet engines. Assuming the MPC runs at a frequency `f_MPC` (e.g., 200 Hz) and an actuator operates at a lower frequency `f_s` (e.g., 10 Hz), the MPC is adapted to generate control inputs at frequencies matching the actuators. If `f_MPC` is an integer multiple of `f_s` (`N_r = f_MPC / f_s`), the number of control inputs for the slow actuator within the prediction horizon is adjusted.
The algorithm dictates that control inputs for slow-frequency actuators (`u_s`) are only recomputed at MPC iterations that align with their slower sampling periods (i.e., every `N_r` steps). For intermediate MPC iterations, the control input for the slow actuator is constrained to be equal to the last computed value. This ensures consistency and effectively embeds the multi-rate behavior directly into the MPC optimization without relying on hierarchical decomposition.

**Simulation Environment and Parameters:**
- **Simulator:** MuJoCo, running at 1000 Hz.
- **Operating System:** Ubuntu 22.04, Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-12700H processor.
- **Jet Dynamics in Simulation:** The jet dynamics in the simulator are based on a neural network model identified from real turbine data, which creates a controlled mismatch with the linearised nonlinear model used internally by the MPC.
- **Actuator Frequencies:** Joint low-level controller at 1000 Hz; jet engine low-level controller at 10 Hz.
- **MPC Frequency:** 200 Hz.
- **Prediction Horizon:** 1 second.
- **Variable Sampling:** To manage the long prediction horizon with high MPC frequency, a variable sampling interval approach is adopted, with shorter time steps at the beginning of the horizon and progressively longer ones towards the end, enabling 200 Hz operation with 17 knots.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed multi-rate MPC framework was evaluated through simulations in the MuJoCo environment, focusing on disturbance recovery, trajectory tracking, real-time feasibility, and ablation studies.

**Disturbance Recovery:**
The controller’s robustness was assessed by applying an external torque of 300 Nm along the y-axis and an external force of 50 N along the x-axis to the robot’s root link for 0.1 seconds at t = 2 s. The results indicated that the robot successfully recovered from this disturbance. During the recovery, the robot experienced a transient tilt of approximately 15 degrees in the roll axis, a displacement of about 1 meter along the x-axis, and a vertical drop of approximately 1 meter. The system subsequently regained its equilibrium and stabilized.

**Minimum Jerk Trajectory Tracking:**
The MPC's performance in tracking a reference trajectory was tested using a minimum jerk trajectory generated by interpolating a set of points for the center of mass (CoM) position. The reference orientation was kept constant. The linear momentum reference was derived from the CoM velocity, while the angular momentum reference was set to zero.
- **CoM Tracking:** The x and y components of the CoM position were tracked with reasonable accuracy. The z component, however, exhibited a consistent offset of approximately 0.15 meters.
- **Orientation Tracking:** For orientation, the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for Roll and Yaw was less than 0.01 radians, while for Pitch, it was approximately 0.03 radians.
The observed tracking errors were attributed to the inherent discrepancies between the complex neural network model used to simulate the jet dynamics and the simplified, linearized nonlinear model employed within the MPC’s prediction framework.

**Real-Time Feasibility:**
The real-time operational compatibility of the MPC was evaluated by measuring the computation time per iteration. The MPC problem, formulated as a quadratic program (QP), was solved using the OSQP solver.
- **Average Computation Time:** During the minimum jerk trajectory tracking task, the average computation time per MPC iteration was 2.18 ms, with a standard deviation of 0.16 ms.
- **Maximum Computation Time:** The maximum computation time recorded was 4.45 ms.
These computation times are within the targeted MPC frequency of 200 Hz, which corresponds to a control period of 5 ms. This suggests that the proposed control strategy is compatible with real-time operation on systems with comparable computational resources to those used in the simulation setup.

**Ablation Studies:**

1. **Jet Dynamics Study:** To highlight the necessity of embedding jet dynamics within the MPC, an ablation study was conducted where jet dynamics were removed from the MPC model. Instead, the controller was allowed to choose thrust (subject to regularization), and a Feedback Linearization controller from prior work was used for jet turbine control. This setup failed to stabilize the robot, resulting in the robot falling to the ground during minimum jerk trajectory tracking. This outcome confirms that explicit modeling of jet dynamics within the MPC is crucial for stable flight.

2. **LPV Study:** The importance of the Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) formulation was investigated by comparing it to a scenario where the model was linearized only once at the robot's initial position. When commanded to follow a minimum-jerk trajectory with this static linearization, the robot was unable to track the trajectory and subsequently failed. This demonstrates that continuous linearization around the current state (LPV) is essential for maintaining control performance during dynamic maneuvers.

3. **Multi-Rate Study:** The impact of the multi-rate scheme was assessed by comparing the proposed Multi-rate MPC with a Single-rate MPC variant. In the single-rate setup, the MPC computed jet thrust commands at every iteration (200 Hz), but only commands at 10 Hz were actually applied, mimicking the low-level jet controller's behavior of discarding intermediate commands. Both controllers were tested on the minimum jerk trajectory.
 - **Position Tracking:** Both MPC variants achieved comparable tracking performance for the x and y components of the CoM position.
 - **Orientation Tracking:** The Single-rate MPC exhibited significantly greater oscillations in orientation compared to the Multi-rate MPC. For instance, the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for Roll was 0.0765 rad for the Single-rate MPC versus 0.0076 rad for the Multi-rate MPC. Similarly, for Yaw, the Single-rate MPC had an MAE of 0.0319 rad compared to 0.0036 rad for the Multi-rate MPC. Overall MAE values across all tracked states (position and orientation) were consistently lower for the Multi-rate MPC (e.g., Z-position MAE 0.1508 m vs 0.2034 m).
 This study demonstrates that explicitly accounting for the differing actuator update rates within the MPC formulation leads to improved tracking performance, particularly in orientation control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the control of highly agile, complex robotic systems, specifically jet-powered humanoid robots. The proposed Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework offers a method for addressing several critical challenges in this emerging field, thereby enhancing the feasibility and robustness of such platforms.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to **integrate the complex, nonlinear dynamics of jet propulsion directly into an online predictive control framework**. Previous control strategies often neglected these crucial dynamics or only incorporated them in offline planning, limiting their applicability for real-time, reactive control. By explicitly modeling the jets as second-order nonlinear systems and subsequently linearizing them using an LPV approach, the authors have developed a controller that operates with a more accurate representation of the robot's actuation capabilities. This enhanced fidelity is crucial for achieving stable and precise flight, especially for systems where propulsion dynamics are not instantaneous.

Another key contribution and area of impact is the **introduction of a unified multi-rate MPC formulation**. Robotic systems, particularly humanoids, typically feature actuators operating at vastly different frequencies (e.g., high-bandwidth joints versus low-bandwidth jet engines). Prior approaches often resorted to hierarchical or cascaded control, or simply discarded intermediate commands for slower actuators, which can lead to suboptimal performance and oscillations. This paper's multi-rate MPC natively generates control commands tailored to the specific update rates of each actuator type, ensuring that the control system respects hardware limitations without compromising overall performance. The ablation study demonstrated a substantial reduction in orientation oscillations compared to a single-rate approach, highlighting the practical benefits of this method.

The successful **demonstration of robust disturbance recovery and stable trajectory tracking** in simulation, despite using an approximated jet model within the MPC, indicates the framework's potential for real-world application. The capability to recover from significant external forces and torques is vital for aerial robots operating in dynamic and unstructured environments, such as those encountered in disaster response or exploration.

Furthermore, the rigorous analysis of **real-time feasibility** is critical for any online control strategy. Achieving average computation times well within the target control frequency (2.18 ms for a 5 ms cycle) suggests that the proposed MPC framework is computationally efficient enough for onboard implementation on hardware with comparable processing capabilities. This moves the development closer to actual deployment on a physical robot like iRonCub, overcoming a major hurdle for complex optimal control algorithms.

The research directly contributes to the **iRonCub project**, a broader initiative aiming to unify manipulation, aerial mobility, and terrestrial locomotion on a single humanoid platform. By addressing specific hardware limitations of the iRonCub, such as the slow dynamics and low control frequency of its jet engines, this work provides a foundational flight controller. This is a crucial step toward realizing a versatile robot capable of both agile ground interaction and aerial maneuvers.

In terms of potential impact, advancements in flying humanoid control, as demonstrated by this paper, could **expand the operational envelope of humanoid robots** into new application areas. These include:
- **Disaster Response:** Robots that can fly over obstacles and then interact with their environment on the ground could provide critical assistance in search and rescue missions.
- **Exploration:** Combining aerial reconnaissance with dexterous manipulation or locomotion on varied terrain could enable more comprehensive exploration in challenging environments.
- **Industrial Inspection:** The ability to navigate complex structures both terrestrially and aerially could improve safety and efficiency in inspecting large industrial facilities.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the use of Euler angles with potential singularities and assumptions in linearization, the demonstrated capabilities and addressed challenges position this research as a significant step forward. The defined future work, including testing on the real robot and developing dedicated controllers for take-off and landing, indicates a clear path toward translating these simulation results into practical robotic capabilities.
