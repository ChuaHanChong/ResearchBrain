# Unified Multi-Rate Model Predictive Control for a Jet-Powered Humanoid Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed a unified multi-rate Model Predictive Control framework for jet-powered humanoid robots. This framework explicitly integrates second-order jet engine dynamics and accommodates varying actuator update rates, enabling stable flight, disturbance recovery, and accurate trajectory tracking in simulations with real-time performance.

## Problem

- Prior control strategies for jet-powered humanoids often neglected the highly nonlinear, second-order dynamics of jet engines, leading to inaccurate models and suboptimal control.
- Complex robotic systems like humanoids have diverse actuators (e.g., high-frequency joints, low-frequency jet engines) with disparate update rates, which traditional MPC frameworks struggle to handle natively.
- Real-time control of nonlinear dynamic systems with MPC is computationally intensive, limiting control frequency and practical implementation on physical robots.

## Method

- Proposed a ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework that unifies a linearized centroidal momentum model with a second-order nonlinear jet dynamics model.
- Employed a Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) approach to linearize the combined dynamics around the current operating point, transforming the problem into a computationally efficient Quadratic Program (QP).
- Developed a multi-rate MPC formulation that natively handles different actuator frequencies by adjusting control input computations within the prediction horizon based on actuator update rates.

## Results

- The controller successfully recovered from external disturbances (300 Nm torque, 50 N force), with transient tilts of 15 degrees roll and displacements of about 1 meter before stabilization.
- Demonstrated accurate tracking of minimum jerk trajectories, achieving Mean Absolute Errors (MAE) of less than **0.01** radians for Roll and Yaw orientation, and approximately **0.03** radians for Pitch.
- Achieved real-time feasibility with an average MPC computation time of **2.18** ms (max **4.45** ms), well within the 5 ms control period for 200 Hz operation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling the complex, nonlinear dynamics of jet engines within the MPC is critical for achieving stable flight in jet-powered humanoids, as demonstrated by the failure of controllers that omitted these dynamics.
> - Continuous linearization (LPV) of the system dynamics around the current operating point is essential for maintaining robust control performance during dynamic maneuvers, unlike static linearization.
> - Embedding multi-rate actuation capabilities directly into the MPC framework, rather than using hierarchical methods or discarding commands, significantly improves tracking performance and reduces oscillations in orientation.
