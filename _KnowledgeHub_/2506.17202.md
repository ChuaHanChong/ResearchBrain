# UniFork: Exploring Modality Alignment for Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> UNIFORK, developed by HKUST, Shanghai AI Lab, and SJTU, introduces a Y-shaped Transformer architecture for unified multimodal understanding and generation that resolves the inherent representational conflict between these tasks. The model achieves competitive performance across vision-language benchmarks like MME-P and VQAv2 for understanding, and GenEval and MJHQ for generation, by separating deeper layers into task-specific branches based on observed distinct modality alignment patterns.

## Problem

- Existing unified multimodal models using fully shared Transformer backbones face a "representational compromise," where understanding and generation tasks have conflicting demands on feature alignment in deeper layers.
- Image understanding benefits from monotonically increasing modality alignment across network depth, while image generation requires alignment to decrease in deeper layers for synthesizing spatial details.
- Prior unified approaches often introduce architectural complexity or compromise performance by forcing a single, suboptimal alignment strategy across inherently divergent tasks.

## Method

- A ==Y-shaped Transformer backbone== that features shared early layers for joint semantic representation and independent deeper branches specialized for understanding and generation.
- A shared ==visual tokenizer based on residual vector quantization== is used for both tasks, converting images into 1D token sequences for the Transformer.
- A ==three-stage training pipeline== is employed: visual alignment pretraining, joint optimization of the full model, and task-specific fine-tuning where only the respective branches are updated.

## Results

- UniFork consistently outperformed fully shared Transformer backbones on both understanding (e.g., MME-P, VQAv2) and generation (e.g., GenEval, MJHQ) benchmarks.
- Achieved performance comparable to or surpassing task-specific expert models, with up to **10%** gains on MME-P over some unified baselines and a FID score of **10.6** on MJHQ-30K for generation.
- The final UniFork model's modality alignment analysis empirically confirmed its successful recovery of the desired distinct patterns: monotonically increasing for understanding and "rise-then-fall" for generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image understanding models exhibit a progressively increasing modality alignment with network depth, crucial for comprehensive semantic comprehension.
> - Image generation models show a "rise-then-fall" alignment pattern, where early layers establish semantic grounding, and deeper layers reduce alignment to enable high-fidelity spatial detail synthesis.
> - Fully shared unified models trained on both tasks demonstrate a "representational compromise," where their internal modality alignment patterns converge, preventing optimal performance for either task.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements, with a particular focus on models capable of both understanding and generating information across different modalities, predominantly vision and language. The paper "UNIFORK" directly addresses a core challenge within this emerging paradigm: designing optimal architectural structures for unified multimodal understanding and generation.

Historically, vision and language tasks have often been approached with specialized models. Image understanding tasks (e.g., visual question answering, image captioning) typically focus on extracting high-level semantic information from visual inputs. Conversely, image generation tasks (e.g., text-to-image synthesis) prioritize creating detailed, pixel-level visual outputs. While recent efforts have aimed to unify these disparate tasks within a single Transformer architecture, often leveraging the next-token prediction (NTP) paradigm borrowed from large language models (LLMs), a fundamental conflict arises due to the intrinsically different objectives of understanding and generation.

Prior works have attempted to bridge this gap through various strategies:
- **Diffusion-based methods** (e.g., LDM, SDv1.5) excel at photorealistic image generation but introduce architectural complexity when integrated into discrete token-based language models.
- **Autoregressive (AR) models** (e.g., DALL·E, LlamaGen) discretize images into token sequences, aligning more naturally with LLMs but potentially struggling with the high-fidelity demands of generation.
- **Dual-pathway approaches** (e.g., Janus series) explicitly decouple visual encoding into semantic and spatial encoders, which can enhance task-specific performance but increase architectural complexity and might undermine the "simplicity and elegance" of a unified NTP paradigm.
- **Task-specific or modality-specific parameters** within the LLM-based Transformer (e.g., Chameleon, BAGEL) distribute parts of the network to different tasks, but the optimal design for this distribution remains an open question.

UniFork distinguishes itself by investigating the *modality alignment behaviors* as a guiding principle for architectural design. Instead of solely focusing on encoding strategies or external models, it delves into the internal dynamics of how visual and textual features align across Transformer layers for different tasks. The paper identifies a crucial "representational compromise" in existing fully shared Transformer backbones, where a uniform flow of information hinders optimal performance for both understanding and generation. This analytical approach, followed by a principled architectural solution (the Y-shaped UniFork), offers a fresh perspective that aims to achieve high performance while maintaining the unified framework of the NTP paradigm. It directly challenges the notion that a single, fully shared backbone can optimally serve inherently divergent tasks, proposing a more nuanced sharing strategy. By providing empirical evidence for its effectiveness, UniFork contributes significantly to the ongoing quest for truly unified and efficient multimodal foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a critical observation regarding the internal workings of existing multimodal models: the inherent conflict between the representational needs of image understanding and image generation tasks within a fully shared Transformer backbone.

The authors thoroughly analyze the modality alignment patterns—how visual and textual features interact and align across different layers of a neural network—in both task-specific expert models and unified multimodal models. Their key observations are:
1. **Divergent Alignment Patterns:**
 - **Image Understanding:** Expert models for understanding (e.g., LLaVA-1.5) exhibit a progressively *increasing* modality alignment across network depth. This suggests that understanding tasks benefit from continuously building up semantic information and strengthening cross-modal connections in deeper layers for comprehensive comprehension.
 - **Image Generation:** Expert models for generation (e.g., LlamaGen) show a different trend: modality alignment *increases in early layers* (for semantic grounding) but then *decreases in deep layers*. This latter decrease is crucial for recovering fine-grained spatial details and pixel-level fidelity, where too strong an alignment might overly constrain visual synthesis.
2. **Representational Compromise in Unified Models:** When a single, fully shared Transformer backbone (e.g., Emu3-base) is trained for both tasks under a next-token prediction (NTP) objective, the alignment curves for understanding and generation tend to converge. This convergence signifies a "representational compromise," where the model cannot simultaneously optimize for the conflicting alignment requirements of both tasks, leading to performance compromises in one or both. The authors confirm this by showing that task-specific fine-tuning of such a model recovers the distinct expert alignment patterns.

Motivated by these profound insights, the key objectives of this work are:
- **To systematically analyze and articulate the distinct modality alignment patterns** required by image understanding and generation tasks.
- **To design and propose a novel architectural paradigm, UniFork**, that explicitly accounts for these divergent alignment patterns. This architecture aims to resolve the representational conflict by balancing shared learning (for common semantic grounding) and task-specific specialization (for distinct deeper-layer needs).
- **To empirically demonstrate that UniFork outperforms conventional fully shared Transformer architectures** in unified multimodal settings.
- **To show that UniFork can achieve performance on par with or even surpass task-specific expert models**, despite operating within a unified framework and often with a more modest parameter count.
- **To maintain the simplicity and elegance of the NTP paradigm** while enhancing performance, avoiding the increased complexity of external modules or significantly different objectives.

In essence, the paper seeks to provide an architecturally principled solution to the fundamental tension between unifying multimodal tasks and optimizing their individual performance, guided by a deep understanding of feature alignment dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of UniFork is characterized by a two-pronged approach: rigorous empirical analysis of modality alignment patterns, followed by the design and implementation of a novel Y-shaped architecture and a flexible training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Observation and Analysis (The Foundation)

The authors began by investigating how visual and textual features align across different layers of a Transformer network for both image understanding and generation.
- **Modality Alignment Metric:** They employed mutual k-nearest neighbors (mutual-kNN), a standard metric for evaluating representation alignment, to quantify the alignment score between vision features (extracted from each Transformer layer) and final textual prompt features.
- **Analysis of Expert Models:** They first analyzed models trained exclusively for a single task: LlamaGen (for generation) and LLaVA-1.5 (for understanding). This revealed the "ground truth" alignment patterns:
 - **Generation:** A "rise-then-fall" pattern, where alignment increases in early layers (for semantic understanding) but decreases in later layers (to allow for spatial detail synthesis).
 - **Understanding:** A "monotonically increasing" pattern, indicating the importance of strong, progressively enhanced cross-modal alignment in deeper layers for semantic comprehension.
- **Analysis of Unified Models:** Next, they examined Emu3-base, a unified model pretrained jointly on both tasks. They observed that the alignment curves for generation and understanding nearly overlapped and both largely followed the "rise-then-fall" pattern characteristic of generation. This suggested a "representational compromise," where the understanding task was potentially subdued.
- **Validation:** To confirm this compromise, they analyzed Emu3-Gen (generation-specific finetune) and Emu3-Chat (understanding-specific finetune) variants from Emu3-base. Emu3-Chat recovered the monotonically increasing trend, while Emu3-Gen retained the rise-then-fall pattern, strongly supporting their hypothesis of divergent optimal alignment dynamics and compromise in fully shared backbones.
- **Further Validation on MJHQ-30K:** To ensure the findings were not dataset-specific, they replicated the alignment analysis on MJHQ-30K, a dataset with longer, more complex prompts, confirming the consistency of the observed patterns.

#### 3.2. Architecture (UniFork - The Solution)

Motivated by the alignment analysis, UniFork proposes a Y-shaped Transformer backbone:
- **Visual Tokenizer:** A single, shared image tokenizer (from VILA-U-256) is used for both tasks. This tokenizer uses residual vector quantization, compressing images by 16x16 and flattening them into 1D token sequences, which are then passed through a lightweight MLP. This choice prioritizes reconstruction quality and text-image alignment.
- **Transformer Backbone:** The core innovation. It comprises:
 - **Shared Early Layers (M layers):** These initial layers are shared across both understanding and generation tasks. Their purpose is to facilitate joint semantic representation learning, establishing a common grounding between modalities.
 - **Task-Specific Deeper Branches (N layers):** After the shared layers, the architecture forks into two independent branches:
 - **Understanding Branch:** Structurally identical to the generation branch but with independently parameterized modules (QKV projections, FFNs). This branch focuses on refining semantic representations for better comprehension.
 - **Generation Branch:** Also with independently parameterized modules. This branch specializes in reconstructing spatial details and high-frequency visual information.
- **Generation Vision Head:** An image head predicts the discrete codes from the residual vector quantization in an autoregressive manner, taking the output features from the generation branch.
- **Initialization:** The entire backbone is initialized with weights from the Qwen2.5-0.5B LLM, leveraging its strong language modeling capabilities.

#### 3.3. Training Pipeline (Three Stages for Flexibility and Performance)

UniFork employs a three-stage training process designed to align modalities, enhance joint capabilities, and specialize task-specific branches:
- **Stage I: Visual Alignment Pretraining:**
 - **Objective:** Align visual representations with the frozen LLM.
 - **Data:** ImageNet-1K (for pixel-level dependencies), Laion-En, COYO (larger-scale image-text pairs).
 - **Tasks:** Image captioning and text-to-image generation. Only the visual connector and image head are trained.
- **Stage II: Joint Optimization:**
 - **Objective:** Enhance overall understanding and generation abilities, unfreezing the LLM.
 - **Data:** Mixture of JourneyDB, SAM, Unsplash, and internal datasets for generation; InternVL-1.5 for understanding.
 - **Tasks:** Multitask pretraining and instruction tuning (BLIP3o-60k for generation, InternVL-1.5 SFT for understanding). All components (backbone, connector, head) are jointly optimized.
- **Stage III: Task-Specific Fine-Tuning:**
 - **Objective:** Refine task-specific performance without interference.
 - **Key Feature:** Only the parameters of the *task-specific branches* (understanding or generation) are updated using their respective instruction-tuning datasets. All shared components remain frozen. This unique stage is a direct advantage of the UniFork architecture, eliminating the need for delicate data ratio adjustments typical in fully shared SFT.

#### 3.4. Training Objective

The model uses a standard cross-entropy loss for autoregressive token prediction, applied only to visual tokens for generation and only to the response portion of text tokens for understanding, without any complex task-specific loss weighting.

This systematic approach, from insightful analysis to a tailored architecture and flexible training, underpins UniFork's effectiveness in balancing the conflicting demands of unified multimodal understanding and generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented in the paper provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness of the UniFork architecture, demonstrating its ability to mitigate representational conflicts and achieve strong performance across both image understanding and generation tasks.

#### 4.1. Validation of UniFork Structure through Ablation Study

- A crucial ablation study (Table 2) compared UniFork against a "Fully Shared LLM" and task-specific "Gen Expert" and "Und Expert" models, all initialized from Qwen2.5-0.5B LLM and trained with consistent configurations.
- **Key Finding:** UniFork consistently **outperformed the "Fully Shared LLM"** on all evaluated benchmarks (MME-P, VQAv2, SEED-I for understanding; GenEval, MJHQ for generation). This directly validates the core hypothesis that separating deeper layers into task-specific branches alleviates representational compromise.
- Furthermore, UniFork achieved **comparable or even better performance than the task-specific expert models**, indicating that its Y-shaped architecture effectively balances shared semantic learning with task-specific specialization without sacrificing performance in either domain. For instance, UniFork surpassed "Und Expert" on MME-P (1229 vs. 1177) and was competitive on VQAv2 and SEED-I, while also outperforming "Gen Expert" on GenEval (0.33 vs. 0.36, smaller gap) and MJHQ (16.3 vs. 15.1, smaller FID is better).

#### 4.2. Image Understanding Evaluation

- **Performance:** UniFork (using only 0.5B active parameters for understanding) delivered strong results across a suite of benchmarks: MME-P, POPE, SEEDv1, VQAv2, and GQA (Table 3).
- **Comparison to Unified Models:** It showed relative gains of 10% on MME-P and 7.3% on POPE compared to the recent unified model Show-o (1.3B parameters). It also outperformed other unified models like LWM (7B) and Chameleon (7B) in various metrics.
- **Competition with Understanding-Only Models:** UniFork remained competitive even against significantly larger understanding-only models. It matched MobileVLM (2.7B) on POPE (85.8 vs. 84.9) and outperformed IDEFICS-9B (8B) on SEEDv1 (55.2 vs. 45.0).
- **Conclusion:** The Y-shaped architecture allows UniFork to achieve high-quality understanding capabilities, even with a limited parameter budget for its understanding branch, by reducing task interference.

#### 4.3. Image Generation Evaluation

- **Performance:** UniFork (using 0.76B active parameters for generation) demonstrated substantial improvements in generation quality (Table 4 & 5).
- **Quantitative Gains:** It achieved an overall 46% accuracy on GenEval, representing a 39% improvement over its smaller ablation variant. On MJHQ-30K, UniFork achieved a FID score of 10.6, a 35% improvement over the smaller variant, with lower FID indicating better image quality.
- **Comparison to Unified and Generation-Only Models:** UniFork significantly outperformed prior unified models like Show-o (FID 15.18) and LWM (FID 17.77) on MJHQ-30K, despite using considerably fewer parameters (0.76B vs. 1.3B and 7B+). It also surpassed several generation-only baselines, including LDM and LlamaGen, across most GenEval categories.
- **Qualitative Results:** Figures 1 and 6 provide visual examples of UniFork's high-fidelity text-to-image generation, showcasing its ability to produce detailed and accurate images consistent with prompts.
- **Conclusion:** The dedicated generation branch within UniFork effectively handles the spatial detail reconstruction necessary for high-quality image generation, leading to substantial performance gains.

#### 4.4. Modality Alignment Analysis of UniFork

- **Confirmation:** The modality alignment analysis of the final UniFork model (Figure 8) confirmed that it successfully recovered the desired distinct alignment patterns:
 - The understanding branch showed a steady *increase* in alignment score with network depth.
 - The generation branch exhibited the characteristic *rise-then-fall* alignment trend.
- **Significance:** This empirical validation confirms that UniFork's architecture effectively reconciles the divergent representational needs of the two tasks within a unified framework, without forcing a compromise.

In summary, the main findings unequivocally support the UniFork architecture as an effective solution for unified multimodal understanding and generation, offering a principled way to manage task-specific representation learning within a shared framework and achieving competitive or superior performance across both modalities with relatively efficient parameter usage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniFork paper presents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal AI, particularly in the domain of unified understanding and generation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Principled Resolution of Fundamental Architectural Conflict

The most profound impact of UniFork lies in its principled approach to resolving the inherent architectural conflict between image understanding and generation. By rigorously analyzing modality alignment patterns, the authors identified that these tasks have fundamentally divergent representational needs within deeper network layers. UniFork's Y-shaped architecture directly addresses this by allowing shared learning in shallow layers for semantic grounding and then decoupling into task-specific branches for specialized refinement. This design is not merely an arbitrary split but a data-driven solution based on observed internal dynamics, offering a robust blueprint for future unified multimodal models.

#### 5.2. High Performance with Parameter Efficiency

UniFork demonstrates that achieving state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance in both understanding and generation does not necessarily require massive, monolithic shared backbones. By intelligently distributing parameters (e.g., 0.5B active for understanding, 0.76B for generation), UniFork outperforms or rivals models with significantly larger total parameter counts (e.g., 7B, 13B, 34B) on various benchmarks. This efficiency is crucial for deploying multimodal AI in resource-constrained environments or for scaling models more sustainably.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Training Flexibility and Simplicity

The three-stage training pipeline, particularly the Stage III task-specific fine-tuning, introduces significant practical advantages. By allowing independent optimization of task-specific branches with their respective datasets while freezing shared layers, it eliminates the complex and often delicate process of data balancing required in traditional fully shared instruction-tuning. This simplifies the training process, making it more robust and potentially faster for achieving optimal performance.

#### 5.4. Deeper Insights into Multimodal Representation Learning

The initial analysis of modality alignment patterns itself is a valuable contribution. It provides critical insights into how different multimodal tasks leverage cross-modal information at various depths of a Transformer network. This understanding can guide the design of future architectures, not just for understanding and generation, but potentially for other multimodal tasks as well. It underscores the importance of examining internal model dynamics beyond just input-output performance.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Multimodal AI Systems

UniFork establishes a strong baseline and architectural paradigm for the next generation of unified multimodal models. Its "shared-then-split" design provides a flexible foundation for further exploration:
- **Optimal Parameter Ratios:** Future work can investigate the ideal split point (M and N layers) and parameter allocation based on task complexity and data characteristics.
- **Scalability:** The architecture is amenable to scaling with larger base LLMs, more advanced visual tokenizers, and higher-quality/interleaved vision-language data, promising further performance gains.
- **Extension to Other Modalities:** The core idea of identifying and accommodating divergent alignment patterns could be generalized to integrate additional modalities like audio, video, or 3D data, paving the way for truly "any-to-any" multimodal AI systems.

#### 5.6. Democratization of Advanced Multimodal Capabilities

By making the code publicly available (as indicated in the abstract), UniFork contributes to the open-source community. This allows other researchers and practitioners to build upon its architectural insights and potentially democratize access to advanced unified multimodal capabilities.

In conclusion, UniFork offers a highly impactful approach by providing a theoretically grounded and empirically validated architectural solution to a fundamental challenge in unified multimodal AI. Its contributions span improved performance, enhanced efficiency, simplified training, and crucial insights into representation learning, positioning it as a significant advancement in the pursuit of general-purpose multimodal intelligence.
