# Latent Action Diffusion for Cross-Embodiment Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a framework for cross-embodiment policy learning by creating a semantically aligned latent action space that unifies diverse robotic end-effector action spaces. The method achieves up to 25.3% improvement in manipulation success rates through skill transfer and enables a single policy to control multiple robots with heterogeneous morphologies.

## Problem

- End-to-end robotic manipulation learning is hampered by data scarcity and the challenge of effectively pooling data from robots with diverse action spaces, known as the 'embodiment gap'.
- Existing methods either constrain action spaces, limit transfer to one-way retargeting, or lack architectural inductive biases for efficient skill transfer between highly varied robot morphologies.
- Developing generalist robots requires a scalable approach to unify distinct robot action spaces to enable a single policy to control multiple, diverse robotic platforms.

## Method

- A three-stage pipeline is proposed: generating paired action data using existing retargeting functions as an alignment prior for human and various robotic hands/grippers.
- A shared, semantically aligned latent action space is learned using contrastive learning, where modality-specific encoders project actions into this space, and decoders reconstruct ground truth actions.
- A diffusion policy is trained within this frozen latent action space to map observations to latent end-effector actions and wrist poses, enabling multi-robot control from a single policy.

## Results

- The co-trained cross-embodiment policies achieved up to a 25.3% (average 13.4%) improvement in manipulation success rates compared to single-embodiment policies across various tasks.
- A single diffusion policy successfully controlled both dexterous hands (mimic, Faive) and parallel jaw grippers (Franka) for tasks like block stacking and plush toy pick & place.
- Ablation studies confirmed that finetuning encoders during decoder training was the most impactful component for improving self-reconstruction (e.g., mimic hand SR-Loss 0.762) and cross-reconstruction losses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing retargeting techniques can be effectively leveraged as a prior to create aligned action pairs, which is crucial for learning semantically coherent latent action representations across heterogeneous embodiments.
> - Learning a unified latent action space through contrastive learning allows for both precise reconstruction of individual embodiment actions and a shared representation that facilitates cross-embodiment skill transfer.
> - Training a policy within this shared latent space enables a single model to control multiple robots with diverse end-effectors, simplifying deployment and reducing the need for embodiment-specific policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

End-to-end learning represents a paradigm for robotic manipulation, aiming to create adaptable and generalist robots. However, its implementation is challenged by data scarcity and the inherent heterogeneity of observation and action spaces across diverse robotic embodiments, a phenomenon referred to as the "embodiment gap." Scaling data volume and diversity to meet the requirements of advanced models necessitates the pooling of data from various robotic platforms.

Existing approaches to cross-embodiment learning have addressed this challenge with varying strategies:
- **Constrained Action Spaces:** Some works, such as RT-1 and those building on the Open-X-Embodiment collaboration, achieve positive skill transfer by co-training on multi-robot datasets that share a constrained action space (e.g., a 7-dimensional space). This approach, however, necessitates discarding data from end-effectors with more complex action spaces, such as dexterous hands. This work differentiates itself by employing a learned latent action space, where expressivity is not limited by the physical constraints of any single embodiment.
- **One-Way Retargeting:** Several methods focus on transferring skills from human demonstrations or videos by explicitly aligning human action spaces to a specific robot's action space. This limits the utility of the resulting policy to the particular embodiment for which the retargeting was designed. In contrast, the presented work develops a shared, semantically aligned latent action space that utilizes retargeting as a prior for alignment, enabling reconstruction between any two heterogeneous end-effectors and facilitating policies that can control multiple embodiments.
- **Alignment-Free Methods:** Other research explores cross-embodiment learning without explicit alignment between action spaces. These approaches often rely heavily on large data volumes for skill transfer, as they lack architectural inductive biases to encourage transfer. Such large datasets are frequently unavailable for dexterous end-effectors with high-dimensional action spaces. This paper's methodology incorporates retargeting as an alignment prior, which supports skill transfer between complex action spaces without requiring extensive data volumes.
- **Video Representations as Actions:** Some methods propose using video representations as embodiment-agnostic actions. These typically capture coarse motion primitives and often require embodiment-specific fine-tuning before deployment. The reported approach, by learning a latent space directly from explicit action spaces rather than video, encodes precise low-level actions, allowing for direct deployment of the same model on different robots without further fine-tuning.

This research addresses the fundamental challenge of unifying diverse action spaces across different end-effectors to enable more efficient cross-embodiment learning and skill transfer. It contributes a new method for leveraging heterogeneous robot data to improve manipulation performance and scalability in robotic learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable effective skill transfer and multi-robot control across diverse robotic embodiments, particularly those with heterogeneous action spaces. This overarching goal is motivated by several challenges and desired advancements in end-to-end robotic learning:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity:** End-to-end learning for robotic manipulation is often limited by the availability of sufficient and diverse training data. The motivation is to overcome this by enabling the pooling and sharing of manipulation data collected from different robotic setups and end-effectors.
2. **Bridging the Embodiment Gap in Action Spaces:** A significant barrier to cross-embodiment learning is the variability in action spaces across different robot morphologies (e.g., dexterous hands versus parallel grippers). The work aims to develop a method to unify these disparate action spaces, allowing a single learning framework to interpret and generate actions for multiple robot types.
3. **Learning Semantically Aligned Latent Representations:** A key objective is to construct a latent action space that is semantically aligned, meaning that similar manipulation intents or poses are represented similarly in the latent space, regardless of the physical embodiment. This alignment is crucial for facilitating meaningful skill transfer between embodiments.
4. **Enabling Multi-Robot Control with a Single Policy:** The research seeks to demonstrate that by learning policies within this unified latent action space, a single policy model can effectively control multiple robots with different end-effectors. This would streamline policy deployment and reduce the need for training separate policies for each robot.
5. **Achieving Positive Skill Transfer:** A core motivation is to show that co-training on data from diverse end-effectors, through the proposed latent action space, leads to positive skill transfer. This implies that learning from one embodiment can improve the manipulation capabilities of another, even in the presence of significant morphological differences. The paper specifically aims for improved manipulation success rates.
6. **Reducing Data Collection Burden:** By enabling data sharing and generalization, the work seeks to reduce the extensive data collection effort currently required for each new robot morphology or task. This contributes to more efficient and scalable robotic learning systems.
7. **Accelerating Generalization:** The development of a unified representation is intended to accelerate the generalization of learned skills across a broader range of robotic embodiments.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for a scalable and efficient approach to robotic learning that can leverage heterogeneous data sources and control diverse hardware from a single, generalizable policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for cross-embodiment policy learning consists of two main components: learning a latent action space and training policies within this space. The approach is designed for single-arm robots equipped with different end-effectors. The core insight is that aligning different end-effector action spaces can be treated as a multimodal representation learning problem. The methodology involves a three-stage pipeline: generating paired action data, learning encoders and decoders for the shared latent space, and latent policy learning.

**A. Creating Aligned Action Pairs**
To facilitate multimodal representation learning, the approach first generates tuples of paired action data, establishing cross-modal correspondences between action spaces. This is achieved by utilizing existing retargeting functions, primarily from human hands to robotic end-effectors, as an alignment prior.

1. **Action Representations:**
 - **Human Hands:** Represented by a 189-dimensional pose vector, derived from local transformations between 21 joints, using a continuous 6D rotation representation.
 - **Anthropomorphic Robotic Hands (Faive, mimic):** Represented by 11- or 16-dimensional joint angle vectors, respectively.
 - **Parallel Jaw Grippers (Franka):** Represented by a normalized one-dimensional gripper width, `θ_P ∈`.
2. **Retargeting:** The retargeting technique, based on prior work, employs "keyvectors." These vectors, extending from the palm to each fingertip and between fingertips, provide a unifying representation applicable to any hand with defined fingertips.
 - **Human to Complex Robotic Hands (e.g., mimic hand):** Retargeting is formulated as a minimum-energy solution. This involves optimizing the robot hand's joint angles (`θ_M`) to minimize the squared difference between the human hand's keyvectors (`v_H`) and the robot hand's scaled keyvectors (`s_i v_F`). Scaling factors (`s_i`) are introduced to account for varying finger lengths.
 - **Human to Parallel Jaw Grippers:** The gripper width (`θ_P`) is determined by taking the minimum of all keyvectors originating at the human thumb and normalizing this value by a standard gripper width (`W`).
 - **Extensibility:** Adding new robotic end-effectors to the system only requires defining a retargeting function from either a human hand or an already integrated robotic end-effector.

**B. Contrastive Latent Space Learning**
This stage aims to learn a shared latent action space that is both informative for precise pose reconstruction and semantically coherent. It involves a two-step learning procedure:

1. **Encoder Training:** Multiple modality-specific encoders (`q_m`) are trained to project actions (`x_m`) from each input modality into a shared latent space. A pairwise InfoNCE loss (`L_contrastive`) is employed to ensure semantic alignment among the `B` aligned end-effector poses within a batch.
2. **Decoder Training and Encoder Finetuning:** Following encoder training, `M` modality-specific decoders (`p_m`) are trained to reconstruct the ground truth actions (`ˆx_i`) from their latent representations. Concurrently, the encoders (`q_m`) are fine-tuned with a lower learning rate to enhance reconstruction quality. The total loss (`L_total`) combines a reconstruction loss (`L_recon`) and the contrastive loss (`L_contrastive`), weighted by a hyperparameter `λ`.
 - **Validation Metrics:** Self-reconstruction (SR) loss measures reconstruction within the same modality, while cross-reconstruction (CR) loss evaluates reconstruction from one modality's latent representation to another's ground truth.
 - **Architecture:** Encoders and decoders are implemented using standard Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), featuring linear layers, ReLU activations, dropout, and layer normalization.

**C. Policy Learning**
With the learned latent action space, Diffusion Policy is utilized to map shared observations to both latent end-effector actions and non-latent wrist poses of the robot arm.

1. **Frozen Action Model:** The encoders and decoders of the contrastive action model remain frozen during policy learning. The diffusion objective is applied to the denoised latent end-effector actions and wrist poses.
2. **Inference:** During inference, the output from the latent policy (a latent end-effector action) is fed into the appropriate embodiment-specific decoder to yield a physical end-effector pose.
3. **Diffusion Policy Implementations:** The method is validated using two diffusion policy implementations: a transformer-based model and a U-Net-based model, each with specified hyperparameters for vision encoder, batch size, horizon, noise schedule, learning rate, and training steps.
4. **Co-training Strategy:** For co-training on datasets of varying sizes, normalized weights are assigned to each dataset. During training, batches are assembled by sampling sub-batches from each dataset according to their weights, projecting actions into the latent space, normalizing, and concatenating. In the experiments, all datasets are assigned equal weights.

**Experimental Setups:**
The framework was evaluated across three real-world manipulation tasks using different combinations of end-effectors:
- **Block Stacking:** mimic hand and Franka gripper. 200 demonstrations per embodiment, single external camera.
- **Block Pick & Place:** mimic hand and Franka gripper. 200 demonstrations per embodiment, with asymmetric observations (wrist camera for mimic hand, zero-padded for Franka).
- **Plush Toy Pick & Place:** Faive hand and Franka gripper. 100 demonstrations per embodiment, single external RGB camera.

For each task and embodiment, performance of a single-embodiment policy was compared against a cross-embodiment policy co-trained on data from both embodiments.

**Ablation Study:** An ablation study was conducted on the contrastive action model to assess the impact of design choices such as temperature annealing and finetuning on self-reconstruction and cross-reconstruction losses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated that co-training policies within the learned latent action space enables multi-robot control from a single policy and yields performance improvements through cross-embodiment skill transfer.

**A. Ablation Study: Contrastive Action Model**
The ablation study for the contrastive action model provided insights into the effectiveness of specific design choices:
- Both temperature annealing (TA) and finetuning (FT) of the encoders significantly improved both self-reconstruction (SR) and cross-reconstruction (CR) metrics.
- Finetuning the encoders during the decoder training phase was identified as the most impactful component of the pipeline. The full model achieved SR-Loss values of 0.762 for the mimic hand and 3.7e-8 for the Franka gripper, with CR-Loss values of 0.002 (mimic→Franka) and 214.20 (Franka→mimic). The higher dimensionality and complexity of the mimic hand's action space resulted in comparatively larger loss values.

**B. Experimental Results and Discussion**

### 5. Multi-Robot Control

- The approach successfully enabled multi-robot control, with a single cross-embodiment diffusion policy capable of controlling both a dexterous hand (mimic or Faive) and a parallel jaw gripper (Franka) for a given task. This capability was qualitatively demonstrated through policy rollouts across the three evaluated tasks.

### 6. Skill Transfer and Performance Gains

- **Block Stacking Task (mimic hand and Franka gripper):** This task exhibited the most substantial skill transfer.
 - For the "Pick block" stage, the co-trained policy improved success rates by 25.3% for the mimic hand and 13% for the Franka gripper compared to single-embodiment policies.
 - In the subsequent fine-grained stages ("Stack blocks" and "Put into box"), the Franka gripper showed performance improvements of 13% and 11% respectively, suggesting it learned more precise manipulation strategies from the dexterous hand data.
 - The mimic hand experienced a minor performance decrease in the final "Put into box" stage, potentially due to camera occlusion by the hand after grasping.
- **Block Pick & Place Task (mimic hand and Franka gripper with asymmetric observations):**
 - The mimic hand, which had a wrist camera, showed a 13% improvement in success rate, indicating benefits from co-training.
 - Conversely, the Franka gripper's performance decreased. This was attributed to the policy potentially relying on the wrist camera view, which was absent for the Franka gripper (replaced by zero-padding). This highlights that skill transfer remains challenging in scenarios with significant observational asymmetries.
- **Plush Toy Pick & Place Task (Faive hand and Franka gripper):**
 - The co-trained policy outperformed single-embodiment versions.
 - The Faive hand achieved a 10% higher success rate.
 - The Franka gripper showed a 7.5% improvement.
 - These results suggest the policy learned a more robust, shared representation of the task, where skills from the dexterous hand (e.g., nuanced object handling) improved the simpler gripper's performance, and vice versa.

### 7. Overall Performance Summary

- Across the experiments, the proposed method resulted in up to 25.3% (with an average of 13.4%) improvement in manipulation success rates when comparing co-trained policies to single-embodiment diffusion policies.
- The results confirmed that the policies learned helpful shared representations, contributing to improved success rates in both coarse and fine-grained manipulation.

**C. Limitations Identified by the Authors:**
- **Asymmetric Observations:** The policy's performance consistency across embodiments is negatively affected by differing sensor modalities (e.g., a wrist camera present for only one robot).
- **Latent Space Regularization:** The current latent space lacks explicit regularization, which may lead to non-smooth regions that are more challenging for downstream policies to model.
- **Dataset Scale Imbalance:** The method showed limited gains when incorporating large-scale, pre-existing datasets with highly imbalanced sizes or significant environmental differences, indicating a remaining challenge for skill transfer under such conditions.

### 8. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers advancements in addressing fundamental challenges within robotic manipulation, particularly concerning data efficiency and generalization across diverse hardware.

**1. Unification of Diverse Action Spaces:** The primary significance lies in introducing a general framework that unifies diverse end-effector action spaces into a single, semantically aligned latent space through contrastive learning. This moves beyond limitations of constrained or one-way retargeted action spaces, offering a more flexible approach to integrating heterogeneous robot data.

**2. Enhanced Cross-Embodiment Learning and Skill Transfer:** The demonstrated ability to achieve up to 25.3% (average 13.4%) improvement in manipulation success rates through cross-embodiment co-training highlights a practical method for facilitating skill transfer. This is particularly relevant for end-effectors with significant embodiment gaps, such as dexterous hands and parallel grippers. Such transfer implies that knowledge gained from one robot can effectively benefit others, potentially accelerating the development of new robotic capabilities.

**3. Multi-Robot Control with a Single Policy:** The approach enables a single diffusion policy to control multiple robots with substantially different morphologies. This factorized policy architecture, separating an embodiment-agnostic latent policy from embodiment-specific decoders, streamlines policy deployment and management in multi-robot environments, reducing the need for numerous specialized policies.

**4. Increased Data Efficiency and Scalability:** By allowing data from different embodiments to be pooled and jointly leveraged, the method contributes to alleviating the problem of data scarcity in robot learning. This reduces the requirement for extensive, embodiment-specific data collection for each new robot, thereby accelerating generalization across hardware and promoting more scalable and efficient robotic learning pipelines.

**5. Future-Proofing for Dexterous Manipulation:** As the development and adoption of increasingly dexterous and anthropomorphic end-effectors progress, this methodology provides a path for effectively sharing and reusing datasets across these complex embodiments. This can help to overcome the data challenges typically associated with high-dimensional dexterous control.

**6. Directions for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as challenges with asymmetric observations, the need for latent space regularization, and skill transfer across highly imbalanced or visually distinct datasets, also outline clear avenues for future research. Addressing these could further enhance the robustness and applicability of the proposed framework.

In conclusion, this work offers a method for bridging the embodiment gap in action spaces, facilitating effective data sharing, improving manipulation performance, and enabling multi-robot control from a single policy. These contributions have implications for making robotic learning more efficient, scalable, and adaptable to a wider range of robotic platforms and tasks.
