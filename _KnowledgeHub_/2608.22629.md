# Enhancing Sim2Real Transfer for Torque-Controlled Robots through Real2Sim Dynamics Estimation and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A Real2Sim2Real pipeline for torque-controlled robot arms. Real joint trajectories identify asymmetric friction, link inertias, and gravity-compensation offsets via genetic-algorithm trajectory matching; the calibrated MuJoCo model plus domain randomization trains a TQC agent for Cartesian reaching. Calibration removes the torque mismatch seen without tuning, and the policy transfers to Gazebo and a real 7-DOF Franka Panda with matching reward curves.

## Problem

- Torque-level control amplifies small modelling errors into unstable or unsafe behaviour, yet training directly on real hardware is slow, costly, and safety-critical.
- Prior sim-to-real work leans on generic domain randomization or default description-file friction and damping, which miss directional asymmetry and static breakaway effects in real joints.
- Physics differs between simulators themselves, so a policy validated in one engine gives no evidence it survives another engine or the real robot.

## Method

- **Real2Sim** identification: ==genetic algorithm== minimizes summed error between real and simulated joint trajectories to fit link inertia tensors and a ==sigmoid velocity-dependent dynamic friction model== per joint.
- Hand-added dynamics terms: per-joint ==gravity-compensation== torque offsets, plus a ==static-friction dead zone== that nullifies commanded action below a measured breakaway torque near zero velocity.
- ==TQC== agent trained in ==MuJoCo== on the calibrated model with ==domain randomization== and a reward mixing normalized goal distance with tanh-bounded joint velocity and acceleration penalties.

## Results

- TQC beat DDPG, SAC, and TD3 on episode mean reward over **2.5K** steps under grid-searched hyperparameters, and showed lower hyperparameter sensitivity, so it was chosen for transfer.
- Calibration changed parameters substantially, for example link-1 Ixx from **0.703** to **0.512** and joint-1 friction gain from **0.55** to **3.40**, and aligned joint-1 and joint-2 torque curves that mismatched before tuning.
- Domain randomization of **21** friction parameters at plus-minus **10 percent** and **42** inertia elements at plus-minus **5 percent** gave reward traces on the real Panda closely tracking MuJoCo across **four** random goals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Identifying a custom friction model with directional asymmetry and a static breakaway dead zone beats trusting the robot description file's default friction and damping coefficients.
> - Randomization width is a tuned quantity, not a free choice: too wide destabilized training, too narrow cost generalization, hence the asymmetric 10 percent and 5 percent bands.
> - A second simulator with different physics is a cheap intermediate test bed, exposing engine-specific disturbances before hardware and evidencing calibration that generalizes beyond one engine.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The transfer of control policies developed in simulation to real-world robotic systems, commonly known as the Sim2Real transfer problem, represents a significant challenge in robotics research. This issue is particularly pronounced in scenarios involving low-level torque control, where minor inaccuracies in physical modeling can lead to unstable or unsafe robot behaviors. Traditional methods of training control policies directly on physical robots are often constrained by the time, resources, and safety risks involved. Simulations offer a safer and faster alternative for policy development; however, discrepancies between simulated and real-world environments, termed the "reality gap," frequently result in a decline in performance when policies are deployed on hardware.

Existing research has explored various strategies to mitigate this reality gap. Domain Randomization (DR) is a prominent technique that introduces controlled variability in simulated physical parameters, such as mass, friction, and inertia, during agent training. This approach aims to enhance the robustness of learned policies to real-world uncertainties. Another method is trajectory matching, which uses real-world data to refine simulation dynamics, making them more closely resemble observed physical behavior.

This work specifically addresses the challenge of accurately encoding realistic dynamics for torque-controlled robots by reducing the reality gap through careful fine-tuning of parameters, particularly friction and inertia. It extends upon prior efforts to improve simulation fidelity by integrating parameter optimization with Reinforcement Learning (RL) within a comprehensive Real2Sim2Real pipeline. By focusing on detailed physical parameter estimation, the research contributes to the broader goal of enabling more reliable and effective deployment of simulation-trained RL policies on physical robots, especially in the context of advanced torque control that is crucial for complex robot-environment interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the effectiveness of Sim2Real transfer for torque-controlled robotic arms. This overarching goal is pursued through a Real2Sim2Real pipeline designed to bridge the reality gap, particularly for low-level torque control where modeling inaccuracies have substantial impact.

More specific objectives include:
- **Parameter Identification:** To identify critical physical parameters, including friction, inertia, and gravity compensation, for a 7-Degrees of Freedom (DOF) Franka Emika Panda robot. This identification is achieved by minimizing the error between real-world and simulated joint trajectories.
- **Optimization via Genetic Algorithms:** To implement a genetic algorithm for parameter optimization, systematically calibrating the simulated dynamics to match real-world behavior more accurately.
- **Reinforcement Learning Policy Training:** To train an RL agent, specifically a TQC (Truncated Quantile Critic) based policy, using the calibrated dynamics in simulation. This aims to develop a control policy that outputs joint torque commands for reaching Cartesian target positions while maintaining smooth and stable motion.
- **Robustness Enhancement through Domain Randomization:** To apply domain randomization during RL training by perturbing the estimated physical parameters. This is intended to improve the generalization capabilities and robustness of the learned policy to uncertainties encountered in the real world.
- **Multi-Environment Evaluation:** To evaluate the trained policy in various simulated environments (MuJoCo and Gazebo) and subsequently deploy it on the physical Franka Emika Panda robot to assess its transferability and performance.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several challenges in current robotic control:
- **High Cost of Real-World Training:** Training RL agents directly on physical robots using joint torques is time-consuming, resource-intensive, and poses safety risks, especially for exploratory learning.
- **The "Reality Gap":** Simulations, while offering a safer training ground, inherently possess discrepancies with the real world due to differences in physical parameters, contact forces, and the implementation of physics engines across various simulators. This "reality gap" hinders the direct transfer of policies that perform well in simulation.
- **Importance of Torque Control:** Torque control is recognized as a promising approach for improving robot-environment interaction, enabling precise responses to physical forces and constraints. However, its effectiveness heavily relies on accurate physical modeling.
- **Need for Accurate Dynamics:** The performance of an agent trained in simulation is highly dependent on how accurately the physical parameters of the robot and its environment are modeled. Without precise parameter estimation, policies may fail to generalize effectively in real-world scenarios.
- **Filling the Control Gap:** The research aims to address the challenge of accurately encoding realistic dynamics to achieve simulated robot behavior that closely reflects real-world dynamics, particularly concerning friction and inertia, before the robot engages in complex interactions.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to establish a reliable methodology for developing torque-controlled RL policies that are directly deployable on physical robots, thereby advancing the state of Sim2Real transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a Real2Sim2Real pipeline designed to enhance Sim2Real transfer for torque-controlled robotic arms. The methodology integrates parameter estimation, reinforcement learning, and domain randomization.

**A. Real2Sim Parameter Estimation**
The initial phase focuses on reducing the Sim2Real gap by calibrating critical dynamics parameters of the robot.
- **Robot Dynamics Model:** The robot's dynamics are described by the standard equation of motion for an n-DOF manipulator: $\tau = M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + G(q)$, where $\tau$ is joint torque, $M(q)$ is the inertia matrix, $C(q, \dot{q})$ represents Coriolis and centrifugal effects, and $G(q)$ is the gravity vector. Inertial and geometric parameters are initially taken from the robot's description file.
- **Friction Modeling:** Friction and damping coefficients are not directly from the robot description file but are modeled separately, accounting for directional asymmetry.
 - **Dynamic Friction:** A velocity-dependent dynamic friction torque ($\tau_{dyn_{f,j}}$) is calculated for each joint using a non-linear function involving three parameters ($\phi_{1,j}$, $\phi_{2,j}$, $\phi_{3,j}$) specific to each joint (Eq. 5).
 - **Static Friction:** Static friction is introduced manually as a joint-wise term. A dead-zone around zero velocity (i.e., $|\dot{q}_j| < \dot{q}_{min}$) is defined, within which a constant breakaway torque ($\tau_{st_{f,j}}$) is applied, with its sign determined by the desired motion direction (Eq. 6). This breakaway torque is empirically tuned for each joint.
- **Gravity Compensation:** A torque offset is introduced for each joint to compensate for gravity, determined through a manual adjustment process to make positive and negative trajectories visually similar.
- **Parameter Optimization:** The core of the parameter estimation problem involves minimizing the error between simulated and real-world trajectories. The error function $E(p)$ (Eq. 7) quantifies the discrepancy between the simulated trajectory $y_{sim}(t, p)$ (based on a parameter set $p$) and the corresponding real-world trajectory $y_{real}(t)$.
- **Genetic Algorithm:** A genetic algorithm is employed to iteratively tune the parameter set $p$ for each joint, which includes inertia, friction (parameters $\phi$), and gravity compensation offsets. The algorithm operates by:
 1. Sampling a real trajectory.
 2. Initializing a population of parameter sets.
 3. For each parameter set, simulating a trajectory and computing the error using Eq. 7.
 4. Selecting top-performing individuals based on the error.
 5. Applying mutation to generate new individuals.
 6. Updating the population.
 This process continues until convergence or a maximum number of generations is reached, yielding an optimized parameter set.

**B. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Training (Sim2Sim)**
After parameter calibration, the refined simulator is used to train an RL control policy.
- **RL Agent:** The agent is a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda arm, tasked with outputting joint torque commands.
- **Task:** The policy is trained to reach Cartesian target positions in the workspace while ensuring smooth and stable motion.
- **Reward Function:** The reward function $r$ (Eq. 2) is designed to balance accuracy in reaching the goal with smooth and energy-efficient joint movements. It includes terms for distance to goal, velocity penalty, and acceleration penalty, with hyperbolic tangent (tanh) used to bound contributions and encourage stability. The goal is to maximize the expected cumulative reward $R_t$ (Eq. 1).
- **Policy Update:** The policy parameters $\Theta$ are updated iteratively using gradient descent to minimize a loss function $L$ (Eq. 3).
- **RL Algorithm Selection:** Four RL algorithms were evaluated: DDPG, SAC, TD3, and TQC. TQC (Truncated Quantile Critic) was selected for subsequent Sim2Real transfer due to its observed robust and stable performance and lower sensitivity to hyperparameters.
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** During training in the MuJoCo simulator, DR is applied to enhance the agent's robustness. All 21 joint-specific friction parameters ($\phi$) are perturbed within a range of $\pm10\%$, and all 42 elements of the joint inertia matrices are perturbed within a range of $\pm5\%$ of their previously estimated values. These ranges were determined empirically to balance variability and training stability.
- **Sim2Sim Validation:** The trained TQC agent is validated across different simulators. After training in MuJoCo, it is tested in Gazebo with varying Panda model parameters within the training domain, simulating diverse real-world conditions.

**C. Sim2Real Transfer and Evaluation**
The final phase involves deploying and evaluating the trained policy on the physical robot.
- **Deployment:** The RL policy, trained with calibrated dynamics and domain randomization, is deployed on the physical Franka Emika Panda robot.
- **Real-World Evaluation:** The agent's performance is assessed on real hardware for target-reaching tasks, observing its ability to generalize from simulation to the physical environment. Performance metrics include tracking accuracy, policy robustness, and overall stability of motion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning parameter estimation, algorithm performance, and Sim2Real transfer efficacy.

**A. Reinforcement Learning Agents Comparison**
An initial evaluation compared four deep reinforcement learning algorithms: DDPG, SAC, TD3, and TQC. The results, illustrated in Fig. 2, indicated that TQC consistently achieved higher mean rewards during training. TQC also demonstrated greater robustness and stability across metrics and exhibited lower sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning, which made it the preferred algorithm for subsequent Sim2Real deployment.

**B. Parameters Estimation**
The parameter estimation phase significantly refined the robot's dynamic model.
- **Initial State:** Before calibration, the robot exhibited issues such as inadequate gravity compensation and unmodeled joint dynamics, particularly in load-bearing configurations, as observed in joint torque comparisons (Figs. 3a and 3b).
- **Gravity Compensation:** Manual adjustment led to the introduction of torque offsets for each joint to address gravity compensation. The specific offset values determined for each joint are detailed in Table I.
- **Static Friction:** Static friction was identified by applying a linearly increasing torque until joint movement was detected. This established minimum breakaway torque values for both positive and negative directions, which are presented in Table II. These values were then incorporated into the simulation as an action threshold to replicate static friction's effect.
- **Inertia and Dynamic Friction Optimization:** The genetic algorithm successfully optimized the inertia and dynamic friction parameters. Table III presents a comparison of inertia tensor components for the 7 links "before" (OLD) and "after" (NEW) optimization, showing distinct changes. Similarly, Table IV details the "OLD" and "NEW" friction parameters ($\phi_{1,j}$, $\phi_{2,j}$, $\phi_{3,j}$) for each joint, indicating the calibration process resulted in updated values that more accurately reflect real-world dynamics. The authors specifically noted the importance of tuning a custom function of friction and inertia rather than relying on default simulation parameters.

**C. Sim2Sim Validation**
The TQC agent, trained in MuJoCo with the calibrated dynamics and domain randomization, was validated in the Gazebo simulator.
- **Random Target Reaching:** The agent's primary task was to guide the robotic arm to random target points within its workspace.
- **Domain Randomization:** During training, friction parameters were perturbed within $\pm10\%$ and inertia matrix elements within $\pm5\%$ of their estimated values. This randomization aimed to build robustness against real-world uncertainties.
- **Performance Across Simulators:** Comparisons between MuJoCo and Gazebo (Fig. 4) for multiple random goal scenarios indicated variations in reward performance, attributing these differences to the distinct dynamics and properties of each simulation environment. This Sim2Sim testing served as an intermediate validation step, demonstrating the agent's adaptability before real-world deployment (Figs. 5 and 6 illustrate goal reaching in MuJoCo and Gazebo, respectively).

**D. Sim2Real Transfer**
The policy trained in MuJoCo, using calibrated parameters and domain randomization, was deployed on the physical Franka Emika Panda robot.
- **Robust Generalization:** The training methodology, particularly the application of domain randomization, facilitated effective generalization of the learned policy from simulation to the real robot, requiring minimal manual adjustments.
- **Real-World Performance:** Real-world evaluations (Fig. 8) demonstrated that the model performed consistently well. The end-effector consistently approached and maintained its position near the randomized goals (Fig. 7).
- **Minor Deviations:** While overall performance was robust, some minor uncertainties were observed in precisely reaching the target, as depicted in Fig. 8 (b) and (c), suggesting residual gaps. Despite these, the overall results supported the effectiveness of the trained model in a real-world setting. The comparison between MuJoCo and Real-World performance (Fig. 8) highlighted the quality of the transfer, with MuJoCo showing smoother transitions, and the real world exhibiting some disturbances mimicking physical inconsistencies, which the agent handled effectively.

These findings collectively demonstrate a significant improvement in tracking accuracy and policy robustness for torque-controlled robots after systematic parameter tuning and robust RL training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotics by proposing and validating a comprehensive Real2Sim2Real pipeline for torque-controlled robots, specifically addressing the persistent challenge of the "reality gap" in reinforcement learning.

The key significance of this work lies in:
- **Enhanced Sim2Real Transfer Fidelity:** The methodology demonstrates a notable improvement in transferring policies from simulation to real-world robots, particularly for low-level torque control, which is often sensitive to minor modeling inaccuracies. This is achieved through a combination of detailed physics-aware system identification, trajectory matching, genetic algorithm-based parameter optimization, and domain randomization.
- **Importance of Accurate Physical Modeling:** The findings underscore the critical role of accurate physical modeling, especially concerning friction and inertia, for the success of Sim2Real transfer in torque-controlled systems. The shift from relying on default simulation parameters to tuning custom friction and inertia functions through real-world data is identified as a crucial factor in increasing simulation fidelity.
- **Robustness Across Simulation Environments:** The validation across multiple simulation environments (MuJoCo and Gazebo) highlights the generalizability and robustness of the proposed calibration and training procedure. This suggests that the approach is effective beyond a single simulation domain, offering adaptability to different simulation platforms.
- **Development of Stable and Generalizable Policies:** By integrating a carefully designed reward function that balances task accuracy with smooth actuation, the learned policies exhibit stable and physically plausible motions when deployed on the real robot. This capability is crucial for practical applications where energy efficiency and minimal wear-and-tear are important.
- **Practical Applicability:** The successful deployment of the simulation-trained policy on a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda robot for target-reaching tasks demonstrates the practical applicability of the pipeline. It confirms that policies can be developed in a safe and efficient simulated environment and then reliably transferred to physical hardware, even under practical disturbances such as backlash, cable drag, and unmodeled delays.

The potential impact of this research is broad, facilitating:
- **Accelerated Robotic Development:** By reducing the need for extensive real-world training, the proposed pipeline can significantly accelerate the development and iteration cycles for torque-controlled robotic applications.
- **Safer Policy Learning:** Shifting the majority of policy training to simulation inherently enhances safety by minimizing the risks associated with unproven control strategies on physical robots.
- **Expansion of Torque Control Applications:** With more reliable Sim2Real transfer, advanced torque-based control strategies, which are critical for complex interaction tasks, compliant manipulation, and human-robot collaboration, can be more readily adopted and deployed.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust foundation for future investigations into more complex robotic tasks, such as contact-rich manipulation. It also opens avenues for exploring online system identification, generalizing the approach to other robot platforms, and integrating safety-aware reinforcement learning and compliance control strategies.

Overall, this research represents a step towards making simulation-trained reinforcement learning policies more reliable and practical for real-world torque-controlled robotic systems.
