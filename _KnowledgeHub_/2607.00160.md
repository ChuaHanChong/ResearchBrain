# Distributed Multi Robot Lunar Cargo Transportation via Phase Decomposed Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tohoku University developed a phase-decomposed reinforcement learning framework for modular robots to cooperatively transport lunar cargo, enabling stable execution across distinct task phases and varied payload masses in a lunar-analog environment, where a single monolithic policy failed to converge.

## Problem

- Enabling robust and adaptable cooperative cargo transportation for sustained lunar surface missions using modular reconfigurable robots.
- Managing complex dynamics from physical coupling, morphology changes, and multi-stage contact transitions in cooperative modular robotic systems.
- Existing monolithic reinforcement learning approaches struggle with the sequential nature of such tasks, which involve distinct phases with heterogeneous dynamics and coordination requirements.

## Method

- A **phase-decomposed reinforcement learning** framework divides the cargo transport task into distinct lifting, transportation, and placement phases, each with its own ==PPO==-trained policy.
- Policies are trained centrally with full joint-state observations and ==domain randomization==, then executed distributively on modular wheel-arm robots.
- A high-level coordination layer, including a discrete ==MDP-based phase controller== and a ==synchronization clamp==, manages robust phase transitions and ensures coordinated movement of coupled robots.

## Results

- Phase-specific policies successfully converged in simulation, demonstrating stable lifting (steady-state tilt **< 2.5°**), precise transportation (height deviation **< 1.58mm**), and controlled placement (impact velocity **0.030 m/s**).
- A monolithic control policy failed to converge for the full task, exhibiting unstable behavior and underscoring the critical role of phase decomposition.
- Hardware validation in a lunar-analog environment confirmed robust multi-phase operation for modular robots transporting payloads from **5.3 kg** to **9.5 kg**, with consistent vertical regulation despite increased height undershoot on heavier loads.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex, multi-stage robotic tasks into phase-specific reinforcement learning problems significantly improves learning stability and convergence, avoiding gradient interference inherent in monolithic approaches.
> - A hybrid coordination layer combining discrete phase gating with a synchronization mechanism is essential for robustly managing transitions and maintaining coordinated control in physically coupled modular robot systems during real-world deployment.
> - Centralized training with comprehensive joint-state observations, coupled with domain randomization, facilitates effective sim-to-real transfer for policies executed distributively on modular robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Sustained lunar surface missions require robotic systems capable of performing diverse tasks such as transporting construction materials, instruments, and logistical payloads. These operations must contend with communication delays, terrain uncertainties, and stringent safety protocols. Modular reconfigurable robots are considered a suitable solution for such environments due to their inherent scalability, reusability, redundancy, and adaptability, allowing for task-specific configurations and simplified repair.

However, cooperative cargo transportation using these modular systems presents several challenges. These include morphology-dependent topology changes, strong payload-induced dynamic coupling between units, the need for long-horizon decision-making, and critical safety constraints. Existing approaches in decentralized reinforcement learning often assume fixed system topologies or optimize a single control policy for structurally static teams. Such methods generally do not address the complexities introduced by physical reconfiguration, multi-stage contact transitions, or the varying dynamics encountered during different phases of a task. Attempting a unified policy optimization across heterogeneous dynamics and contact regimes can lead to gradient interference and may fail to explicitly model configuration-dependent coupling.

Specifically for lunar cargo transport, the problem is compounded by the sequential nature of operations, which typically involve distinct phases: lifting, transportation, and placement. Each phase presents unique contact conditions and objective structures, requiring different coordination strategies from the robotic modules (e.g., synchronized actuation during lifting, compliant steering during transit, precise alignment during placement). The current work aims to address these identified gaps by developing a framework that explicitly accounts for phase-specific dynamics and coupling through a structured decomposition of the learning problem, integrated with synchronization mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the requirements of future sustained lunar surface missions, which necessitate robust and adaptable robotic systems for critical tasks such as cargo transportation. Modular reconfigurable robots are identified as a promising solution due to their scalability and reconfigurability, which can enhance mission flexibility and resilience. However, realizing cooperative cargo transport with these systems introduces significant technical challenges, particularly in managing the complex dynamics arising from physical coupling with payloads and the sequential nature of the task.

The key objectives of this work are to develop and validate a phase-decomposed reinforcement learning framework designed to address these challenges for cooperative lunar cargo transportation. The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Decomposition of Complex Tasks:** The task of cargo transportation is inherently multi-stage, involving distinct phases such as lifting, transportation, and placement. Each phase presents different control objectives, contact dynamics, and coordination requirements. A single, monolithic control policy often struggles to learn effectively across such heterogeneous regimes due to gradient interference. Therefore, a core objective is to decompose the overall mission into these distinct operational stages, with each stage being optimized by a dedicated, joint-state policy. This aims to simplify the learning problem and improve policy stability and convergence.
- **Handling Payload-Induced Coupling:** When multiple modular robots collectively transport a shared payload, they form a mechanically coupled system. This coupling results in strong dynamic interactions that necessitate coordinated control to prevent instability, ensure load balance, and maintain payload integrity. The framework aims to capture this inter-agent coupling within each phase's policy optimization and through a higher-level synchronization mechanism.
- **Centralized Training with Distributed Execution:** To leverage the benefits of multi-agent reinforcement learning, the objective is to train the phase-specific policies in a centralized manner using full joint-state observations. This approach typically promotes stable convergence in multi-agent settings. For real-world deployment on modular robots, the objective is to execute these policies in a distributed fashion, relying on onboard proprioception and relative state information to maintain operational autonomy.
- **Robust Phase Transition and Safety Coordination:** Real-world robotic operations, especially in hazardous environments like the lunar surface, require robust mechanisms for transitioning between task phases and ensuring safety. The research aims to implement a deterministic phase controller, expressed as a Markov state representation, to regulate transitions between stages based on measurable task events. Furthermore, a failure-sensitive synchronization mechanism is designed to ensure coordinated progression among units and enable safety-aware halting during real-world execution, mitigating risks from uncoordinated movements or system faults.
- **Hardware Validation in Lunar-Analog Environment:** A crucial objective is to validate the proposed framework not only in simulation but also through controlled field experiments. This involves using a physical modular reconfigurable robotic platform in a lunar-analog test facility. This validation aims to demonstrate the framework's reliability and robustness under real-world conditions, including terrain uncertainty, contact dynamics, and varying payload masses, thereby bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- **Scalability and Adaptability for Modular Systems:** The motivation to use modular reconfigurable robots implies a need for a control framework that supports their inherent advantages. While this study focuses on a specific transport configuration, the underlying phase-structured approach is intended to be scalable and potentially adaptable to other configurations and mission profiles, supporting the long-term vision of adaptable lunar robotics.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for robust, adaptive, and safe cooperative cargo transport capabilities for future lunar missions, specifically leveraging modular robotic systems while addressing the challenges of complex task decomposition, inter-robot coupling, and practical deployment in uncertain environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a phase-decomposed reinforcement learning framework for cooperative cargo transportation using modular wheel-arm robotic units. This framework integrates phase-specific policy optimization, a high-level coordination layer, and a synchronization mechanism for robust sim-to-real deployment.

**Modular Robotic System:**
The hardware platform consists of physically distinct locomotion and manipulation modules.
- **Wheel Module:** A differential drive base with two independently actuated wheels, providing nonholonomic planar mobility.
- **Arm Module:** A 7-degree-of-freedom (DOF) serial articulated arm, enabling 3D end-effector control for grasping and manipulation.
- **Integrated Configuration:** For cooperative transport, a mobile manipulation unit is formed by mechanically coupling one wheel module with one arm module. Two such units are used, physically engaging a shared payload to form a closed kinematic chain: Wheel1–Arm1–Cargo–Arm2–Wheel2. Each unit controls its two wheel velocities and seven arm joint positions.

**Modular Multi-Phase Reinforcement Learning Policies:**
The cooperative cargo transport task is formulated as a sequential decision-making process decomposed into three operational stages:
1. **Lifting (L):** Raising the cargo to a target height while maintaining balanced load distribution and minimizing oscillations.
2. **Transportation (T):** Governing cooperative planar motion from a starting point to a destination, preserving payload stability.
3. **Placement (P):** Ensuring controlled lowering and precise alignment of the payload at the target configuration.

Each stage `k ∈ {L, T, P}` is modeled as an independent Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a shared joint state (`S`) and action (`A`) space, phase-specific reward function (`r_k`), and a discount factor (`γ`).
- **System State (s_t):** At time `t`, the state includes the pose, velocity, and joint configuration of all `N` robots, plus the pose and velocity of the cargo (`s_t = [x^(1)_t, ..., x^(N)_t, x^c_t]`).
- **Joint Action (a_t):** Comprises the individual actions `u^(i)_t` for each robot `i`, which include two differential-drive wheel velocity commands and seven arm-joint position commands (total 18 dimensions for two robots).
- **Observation Model:** During centralized training, robots observe the full joint state (`o_t = s_t`). During distributed execution, each robot `i` observes its own state, relative cargo pose, and relative neighboring robot states (`o^(i)_t = [x^(i)_t, ∆x^c_t, ∆x^(j)_t]`).
- **Reward Functions:**
 - `r_L` (Lifting): Penalizes deviations from target height and vertical velocity, variance of interaction forces across arms (to encourage balanced load sharing), and cargo tilt angle.
 - `r_T` (Transportation): Penalizes deviations from target planar position and commanded planar velocity, cargo tilt, and deviations from the desired lifting height. This formulation aims to decouple translational tracking from stability and height regulation.
 - `r_P` (Placement): Penalizes residual height, vertical velocity, and cargo tilt to ensure smooth descent and upright alignment.
- **Policy Optimization:** Each phase policy `π_θk` is optimized independently using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in NVIDIA IsaacLab. Policies are implemented as multilayer perceptrons with ELU activations. Centralized observation (`o_t = s_t`) is used during training, with domain randomization applied to friction, terrain, and actuation noise to enhance sim-to-real transferability.

**Policy Coordination and Sim-to-Real Deployment:**
To manage the transition between phases and ensure robust hardware execution, a hybrid coordination layer is introduced:
1. **Phase Coordination via MDP Gating:** A discrete phase variable `m_t ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}` (Idle, Lift, Transport, Place) augments the continuous system state. This variable gates the active stage policy, allowing only one policy to be active at a time. Transitions between phases (`m_t+1`) are triggered by measurable task events (e.g., cargo reaching a minimum height, proximity to goal) and include safety-stop overrides (e.g., fault, timeout, operator intervention) that return the system to an Idle state.
2. **Joint Synchronization for Cargo Mode:** This mechanism addresses the strong mechanical coupling formed by the shared payload and aims to mitigate sim-to-real discrepancies by bounding per-step progression. The sensed states of the two arms and wheel bases (`Y_k`) are stacked. The policy's raw target state (`F_k`) is derived from the previous command and the current action. Instead of directly applying `F_k`, the commanded stacked state (`U_k`) is clamped to an "admissible set" (`V_k`) centered on the sensed state `Y_k`. This set is defined by an L∞ maximum distance metric over the four subsystems (two arms, two wheels), normalized by their individual tolerances (e.g., 0.03m translation / 5° rotation for arms; 0.02m planar deviation for wheels). This effectively ensures that no subsystem advances significantly ahead of others, preventing asynchronous motion and internal cargo stress. If the admissible step is undefined or a safety stop is active, the controller defaults to a hold command.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Simulation:** Policies were trained in NVIDIA IsaacLab using articulated rigid-body simulation with contact dynamics. 2048 to 4096 parallel environments were used. Domain randomization included friction variation, terrain perturbations, actuation noise, and initial joint variations.
- **Hardware:** Validation was conducted at JAXA’s Advanced Facility for Space Exploration in a lunar-analog environment (regolith-like sand test field). The experimental platform consisted of two integrated wheel-arm units engaging a shared payload. Ground-truth pose was measured by an OptiTrack system for offline evaluation, while onboard encoders provided proprioceptive feedback for control.
- **Payloads:** Three configurations were tested: a Sledge (5.3 kg, elongated geometry), a Cargo box (7.5 kg), and Cargo with bricks (9.5 kg). The Sledge allowed for evaluation of generalization to unseen object shapes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the phase-decomposed reinforcement learning framework was conducted in both simulation and through hardware experiments in a lunar-analog environment.

**Simulation Results (Phase-Specific Policy Performance):**
- **Lifting Policy:** Training convergence was observed across different target heights (0.3m, 0.5m, 0.75m), with mean episodic reward converging within 1.72 x 10^4 to 2.43 x 10^4 timesteps. Higher target heights resulted in slightly lower final plateau rewards and larger height tracking errors (5.9mm for 0.3m to 36.0mm for 0.75m), indicating increased control difficulty. Transient tilt during mid-lift reached up to 25.4°, but the payload stabilized with a steady-state tilt below 2.5° in all cases.
- **Transportation Policy:** The policy demonstrated rapid convergence, reaching 95% of its final plateau reward in approximately 1.74 x 10^4 timesteps. During execution with a 0.2 m/s velocity command, the RMS velocity tracking error was 0.023 m/s (approximately 11% of the command). Simultaneously, the payload height was maintained at 0.30m with a maximum deviation of 1.58mm, suggesting effective decoupling of planar motion from vertical support.
- **Placement Policy:** This policy converged fastest, reaching 95% of its final plateau reward within 1.42 x 10^4 timesteps. It achieved a controlled descent to ground contact, with a final placement height error of 13.3mm. The impact velocity at contact was 0.030 m/s, indicating a soft touchdown. Transient tilt during descent was limited to 6.0°, and steady-state tilt after contact was 0.6°, demonstrating stable and upright placement.

**Baseline Training Without Phase Decomposition:**
A crucial comparison was made by training a single, monolithic policy for the entire mission without phase separation or synchronization. This monolithic policy failed to converge even after 7.5 x 10^4 timesteps, which was significantly longer than the training horizons for individual phase policies. The reward remained oscillatory, and the entropy did not exhibit stable decay, indicating an inability to learn a coherent strategy. Qualitative observations in simulation revealed unstable behavior, where the robot attempted to translate while simultaneously lifting or placing the payload. These results provide empirical evidence for the necessity of phase decomposition in achieving stable learning for this complex, multi-stage task.

**Hardware Evaluation:**
Hardware experiments were conducted in a lunar-analog test facility with three different payload masses (5.3 kg, 7.5 kg, 9.5 kg).
- **Transportation Height:** During transportation, the steady-state payload height decreased monotonically with increasing mass: 272.6±3.8 mm (5.3 kg), 243.0±5.5 mm (7.5 kg), and 209.1±7.8 mm (9.5 kg). These values corresponded to approximately 91%, 81%, and 70% of the 0.30m target height, respectively. The increased undershoot and variance observed with heavier payloads were attributed to terrain sinkage and amplified arm torque coupling in the granular terrain.
- **Time to Steady-State Height:** The time required to reach 95% of the steady-state height also increased with payload mass, ranging from 21.02±0.61 s for the 5.3 kg payload to 31.18±0.56 s for the 9.5 kg payload, indicating slower vertical convergence under higher loads.
- **Placement Accuracy:** For all payloads, the placement phase demonstrated controlled descent with small residual post-contact heights. The final settled heights after lowering were 8.1±0.7 mm (5.3 kg), 8.0±1.0 mm (7.5 kg), and 12.1±1.0 mm (9.5 kg). The slightly higher residual height for the heaviest payload was attributed to deeper sand penetration and localized terrain deformation.
- **Overall Hardware Performance:** The hardware results confirmed stable multi-phase execution across varying payload masses and in a challenging granular terrain environment. Despite a reduction in absolute lifting height under heavier loads, the vertical regulation remained consistent and bounded across trials, demonstrating robust task completion even with degraded height regulation under increased load.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a phase-decomposed reinforcement learning framework for cooperative cargo transport using modular reconfigurable robots, demonstrating its utility and robustness through both simulation and hardware validation in a lunar-analog environment.

**Key Contributions and Technical Significance:**
The central methodological contribution lies in the phase-structured formulation itself. By decomposing a long-horizon, contact-rich cooperative transport task into phase-specific MDPs with dedicated reward functions, the framework effectively mitigates gradient interference that would otherwise destabilize a monolithic policy, as evidenced by the baseline comparison. The integration of centralized joint-state training with distributed execution allows for stable policy learning while enabling practical deployment on physically independent, yet mechanically coupled, modular robots. Furthermore, the discrete MDP-based phase gating, coupled with a synchronization-aware safety coordination layer (the "synchronization clamp"), provides a robust mechanism for managing sequential multi-stage execution and enhancing sim-to-real stability. This approach specifically addresses the challenges posed by configuration-dependent dynamics and payload-induced coupling in modular systems.

**Implications for Future Lunar Missions:**
This work offers a scalable and adaptable solution for complex cooperative surface operations, which are essential for future sustained human presence and infrastructure development on the Moon. Robotic systems capable of reliably transporting construction materials, instruments, and logistical payloads under communication delays, terrain uncertainty, and strict safety constraints are critical. The modular nature of the robots, combined with the adaptable control framework, supports principles of repairability, redundancy, and functional adaptability, enhancing mission resilience. The framework's demonstrated robustness across varying payload masses and in regolith-like terrain conditions indicates its potential for real-world deployment in lunar exploration and construction scenarios.

**Broader Impact and Future Directions:**
Beyond lunar applications, the proposed phase-decomposed reinforcement learning methodology has potential applicability to other complex robotic tasks in diverse environments that involve sequential stages, strong inter-agent coupling, and reconfigurable robot morphologies. The ability of the policies to generalize to previously unseen object shapes (as indicated by the Sledge payload experiment) suggests a pathway for increased adaptability to novel cargo types. Future research directions could include investigating policy transfer across alternative robot configurations and dynamic reconfigurations, further enhancing the adaptability and versatility of modular robotic systems for a wider range of cooperative tasks.
