# Rethinking Progression of Memory State in Robotic Manipulation: An Object-Centric Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce LIBERO-Mem, a benchmark for non-Markovian robotic manipulation, and Embodied-SlotSSM, an object-centric Vision-Language-Action framework. This work enables robots to retain object-specific memory for long-horizon tasks and partially observable environments, demonstrating improved performance over baselines in complex manipulation scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing robotic benchmarks predominantly assume Markovian processes, failing to evaluate tasks requiring persistent object-specific memory or historical reasoning.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack mechanisms to encode and recall object-specific histories, leading to failures in non-Markovian tasks with visual ambiguities.
- Token-based representations in VLA models face scalability issues for long temporal sequences, limiting their applicability in tasks requiring extensive memory.

## Method

- Introducing **LIBERO-Mem**, a non-Markovian benchmark suite featuring 10 tasks designed to stress-test object-centric memory, temporal dependencies, and identity ambiguities.
- Proposing Embodied-SlotSSM, a slot-centric VLA framework that integrates object-centric learning with State-Space Models (SSMs) to maintain structured, persistent object identities.
- Implementing temporal coherence with Slot Attention and a temporal contrastive loss, along with a Slot Dynamics Encoder predicting short-term static latents, to enable context-aware action prediction.

## Results

- Embodied-SlotSSM achieved an **83.0%** average success rate on the general LIBERO-Goal benchmark, outperforming SlotVLA (**75.5%**) and OpenVLA (**32%**) baselines.
- On the new non-Markovian LIBERO-Mem benchmark, Embodied-SlotSSM showed a **14.8%** average subgoal completion rate, a notable improvement over baselines (**5.0%** for OpenVLA/SlotVLA h=8, **0%** for SlotVLA h=1).
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated that the model consistently tracks the same object instance across time, even during occlusion, indicating robust object permanence for long-horizon reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating structured, object-centric memory provides a strong inductive bias for robust state estimation and decision-making, even in general manipulation tasks.
> - Slot-based State-Space Models (SSMs) can effectively maintain spatio-temporally consistent object identities over extended periods, offering a scalable approach for long-horizon tasks.
> - Object permanence and historical reasoning are critical for handling non-Markovian scenarios where identical visual inputs require different actions based on prior interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and perform complex tasks using visual input. However, the authors identify a critical limitation in current VLA models and robotic benchmarks: they predominantly operate under a *Markovian assumption*. This means they typically infer the next action solely from the current observation, disregarding past interactions or the history of specific objects. This simplification, while enabling efficient learning, fundamentally limits their applicability in real-world environments.

**Gaps in Existing Benchmarks:**
Most existing robotic benchmarks, such as RLBench (James et al. 2020), LIBERO (Liu et al. 2023), and RoboCasa (Nasiriany et al. 2024), focus on short-horizon, atomic tasks where the current visual state is often sufficient for decision-making. While some recent efforts like MemoryBench (Fang et al. 2025) and MIKASA-Robo (Cherepanov et al. 2025) acknowledge the need for memory, they often simplify settings, lack object-level ambiguities, or do not thoroughly stress-test temporal scaling and compositional challenges inherent in real-world scenarios. Specifically, they often overlook partially observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) where identical visual inputs can have different semantic meanings based on prior interactions.

**Limitations of Current VLA Models:**
State-of-the-art VLA models like OpenVLA (Kim et al. 2024), Octo (Octo Model Team et al. 2024), and ECoT (Zawalski et al. 2024) have achieved strong performance in reactive policy learning, often leveraging powerful pre-trained visual encoders. However, their reliance on an image-based Markovian assumption prevents them from encoding and recalling object-specific histories. This results in poor performance when tasks require remembering past actions, object locations, or interaction sequences, leading to failures or redundant actions. Furthermore, scaling token representations for long video sequences quickly becomes intractable, limiting their temporal capabilities.

**The Contribution of This Work:**
This paper directly addresses these gaps by:
1. **Introducing LIBERO-Mem:** A novel benchmark explicitly designed to stress-test object-centric memory in non-Markovian, partially observable robotic manipulation tasks. It introduces object identity ambiguities, long-horizon temporal dependencies, and explicit memory requirements.
2. **Proposing Embodied-SlotSSM:** A new VLA framework that integrates object-centric learning with state-space modeling (SSM) to build structured, persistent memory. This architecture aims to enable temporal scalability and context-aware action prediction, moving beyond purely reactive policies.

By providing both a challenging benchmark and a memory-augmented architectural solution, this work pushes the boundaries of robotic manipulation towards more intelligent, temporally aware, and adaptable embodied agents capable of operating in complex, ambiguous environments. It aligns with a growing trend in AI and robotics to imbue agents with more human-like cognitive abilities, particularly the ability to recall and reason about past experiences with specific entities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the observation that humans effortlessly use object-level memory to perform complex, long-horizon tasks, whereas current robotic systems struggle in analogous situations. In dynamic, partially observable environments, robots frequently encounter scenarios where the current visual input alone is insufficient to determine the correct action. Tasks involving sequenced interactions with visually similar objects, or actions that depend on an object's hidden state (e.g., whether it has been picked up multiple times or where it was last placed when occluded), are inherently non-Markovian.

The authors identify the following key problems:
1. **Lack of Persistent Object-Specific Memory:** Existing visuomotor policies primarily rely on instantaneous sensory inputs, lacking mechanisms to encode and recall the history of interactions associated with individual object instances. This leads to failures, redundant actions, or inability to track completed subtasks.
2. **Inadequate Benchmarking for Non-Markovian Tasks:** Most robotic manipulation benchmarks are designed for short-horizon or atomic tasks under Markovian assumptions, failing to stress-test memory-augmented models under object-level partial observability, temporal dependencies, and ambiguity.
3. **Scalability Issues in Current VLA Models:** While VLA models show promise, their token-based representations quickly become intractable for long temporal sequences, making them unsuitable for tasks requiring extensive memory.

To address these challenges, the paper outlines two main objectives:
1. **To introduce LIBERO-Mem:** A novel, non-Markovian robotic manipulation benchmark suite. This suite is specifically designed to evaluate an agent's ability to retain and reason about object-centric interactions over extended periods, under conditions of ambiguity in object identity, location, and relational history. Its tasks demand reasoning beyond the current visual frame, emphasizing object permanence, historical reasoning, and structured memory retention.
2. **To propose Embodied-SlotSSM:** A new slot-centric VLA framework built for temporal scalability. This model aims to maintain spatio-temporally consistent slot identities, leveraging them through slot-state-space modeling to reconstruct short-term history and a relational encoder to align input tokens with action decoding. This framework is intended to enable temporally grounded, context-aware action prediction in non-Markovian settings.

Ultimately, the motivation is to bridge the gap between reactive, short-term robotic policies and the development of memory-centric capabilities, paving the way for more reliable and intelligent general-purpose robots in complex, real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces both a new benchmark, LIBERO-Mem, and a novel model architecture, Embodied-SlotSSM, to tackle the challenge of object-centric memory in non-Markovian robotic manipulation.

#### 3.1. LIBERO-Mem: Non-Markovian Benchmark

LIBERO-Mem is designed to systematically evaluate memory-augmented models by introducing object-level partial observability and temporal dependencies.
- **Task Design for Object-Centric POMDPs:** The benchmark comprises 10 tasks categorized into four memory dimensions:
 - **Object Motion (OM):** Requires remembering the last action performed on an object (e.g., pick up or place down).
 - **Object Sequence (OS):** Success depends on recalling how many times an object has been manipulated when visual cues are insufficient.
 - **Multi-Object Relations (OR):** Involves tracking the temporal order of object relations and interactions (e.g., reordering visually identical bowls).
 - **Multi-Object Occlusion (OO):** Necessitates memory of past placements to identify targets when objects are occluded.
 - Crucially, all tasks are designed such that identical visual observations at different timesteps can correspond to different required actions, thus enforcing the need for historical reasoning.
- **Data Collection:** Expert demonstrations (120 trajectories per task, 200-700 frames each) are collected, with 100 trajectories refined for training and 20 for validation. This supports both short- and long-horizon evaluation.
- **Subgoal-Aware Evaluation:** Tasks are decomposed into symbolic subgoals (using Sequence and Or operators) for fine-grained assessment of progress, beyond a simple binary success/failure.
- **Object Identity Ambiguities:** Visually identical objects (e.g., bowls or plates) are used, requiring agents to resolve object identity based on interaction history rather than appearance.
- **Object & Subgoal Annotations:** Each timestep includes object instance IDs, masks, and subgoal completion flags per object, providing rich supervision for memory-centric learning.
- **Stress-testing by Temporal Scaling:** The benchmark allows for evaluating policy robustness over extended episodes, addressing a key gap in existing benchmarks.

#### 3.2. Embodied-SlotSSM: A Slot-Centric VLA Framework

Embodied-SlotSSM is a novel architecture designed to maintain structured, object-centric memory and enable temporally grounded action prediction. It builds upon SlotSSM (Jiang et al. 2024), adapting it for visuomotor control (Figure 2).

- **SlotSSM Formulation:** The model leverages State-Space Models (SSMs) to approximate object histories. Unlike traditional SSMs, Embodied-SlotSSM decomposes visual embeddings into *K* individual slot representations (`s_k_t`) using a slot encoder. The SSM's hidden state (`h_t`) and output (`y_t`) are also modular, with input-conditioned matrices (`A`, `B`, `C`) designed to be block-diagonal, each block conditioned on a corresponding slot input. By default, K=16 slots are used.
- **Transient Memory via Temporal Localization:**
 - **Slot Attention for Object Localization:** A Slot Attention module (Locatello et al. 2020) is used to transform dense visual embeddings into discrete, object-centric tokens (`s_t`). This module iteratively binds spatial features to learnable object queries, yielding disentangled representations.
 - **Temporal Coherence:** To ensure consistent object identity tracking, slots at each timestep (`s_t`) are initialized with the final slot outputs from the previous timestep (`s_t-1`), propagating identity across the sequence.
 - **Temporal Contrastive Loss:** A contrastive objective is applied over a fixed temporal horizon, treating corresponding slots in nearby frames as positives and other slots as negatives. This loss further enforces temporal consistency and improves slot identity.
- **Slot Dynamics Encoder with SlotSSM:**
 - Rather than predicting a single next-step embedding, the SlotSSM predicts a window of `P` static latents (p past, q future) centered at the current timestep for each slot. This captures short-term motion continuity and provides rich supervision for both forward dynamics and backward reconstruction, enhancing representation stability.
 - For the *Naive E-SlotSSM* (the empirical version tested), an *oracle text-embedding subgoal* (`g_j_t`) is used for each relevant object to guide the model, acknowledging a current limitation in autonomous subgoal inference.
- **Action Control through Slot-Conditioned Decoding:**
 - **Slot Fusion Module:** A Slot Fusion module combines the current slot (`s_j_t`), the predicted next slot (`s_hat_j_t+1`), and the oracle subgoal (`g_j_t`) to create a latent state (`d_j_t`) that captures current dynamics and temporal context at the object level.
 - **Relation Encoder:** A lightweight relational encoder performs cross-attention between the fused slot latents (`d_j_t`) and raw visual features (`v_t`) to produce relational tokens (`r_j_t`). This enables context-aware reasoning about object states and interactions.
 - **VLA Head:** The final action (`a_hat_t`) is decoded by a VLA head conditioned on the relational tokens, slot dynamics, and the current task language query embedding (`l`).

By integrating these components, Embodied-SlotSSM aims to ground actions in object-centric memory and task semantics, enabling robust reasoning over identity, relational context, and temporal dependencies in partially observable, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation focuses on comparing Embodied-SlotSSM (specifically, its "Naive E-SlotSSM" variant which uses oracle subgoal information) against several baselines on both the standard LIBERO-Goal benchmark (for general task performance) and the newly introduced LIBERO-Mem benchmark (for object-level POMDP tasks). Baselines include OpenVLA (referred to as π0) and SlotVLA (Hanyu et al. 2025) with varying input frame horizons (h=1, h=8).

#### 4.1. General Task Performance on LIBERO-Goal

- **Superior Performance:** Naive E-SlotSSM achieved an average success rate of **83.0%** on LIBERO-Goal tasks, significantly outperforming SlotVLA (h=8, 75.5% avg) and SlotVLA (h=1, 32% avg).
- **Robustness Across Tasks:** The model demonstrated particular strength in tasks involving multi-object interactions and occlusions (e.g., "mid drawer open," "top drawer open → bowl in"). For instance, it achieved 100% success on "bowl in stove" and "stove turned on," and 90% on "bowl in cabinet," "bottle on cabinet," and "bowl on plate."
- **Effectiveness of Structured Memory:** These results highlight that even in general manipulation tasks, integrating structured object-centric memory provides a strong inductive bias, enabling more robust state estimation and decision-making compared to policies relying on shorter temporal contexts or dense token representations.

#### 4.2. Object POMDP Task Performance on LIBERO-Mem

- **Significant Improvement in Subgoal Completion:** On the more challenging non-Markovian LIBERO-Mem tasks, Naive E-SlotSSM achieved an average subgoal completion rate of **14.8%**. This is a notable improvement over baselines, with π0 (h=1) and SlotVLA (h=8) both scoring 5.0%, and SlotVLA (h=1) scoring 0%.
- **Handling Repetitive and Complex Sequences:** Naive E-SlotSSM showed partial success in tasks requiring long-horizon repetitive actions and object sequence tracking:
 - T1 (pick and place bowl 1x): 50.0% subgoal completion.
 - T3 (pick and place bowl 3x): 33.3% subgoal completion.
 - T5 (pick and place bowl 5x): 14.3% subgoal completion.
 - T9 (put bowl in basket, move basket to center): 30% subgoal completion.
 - T10 (put bowl in basket, move *empty* basket to center): 20% subgoal completion.
- **Open Challenges Remain:** While improved, the overall subgoal completion rate of 14.8% still suggests that persistent memory modeling remains challenging, especially in complex tasks like swapping multiple bowls (T7, T8) where Naive E-SlotSSM showed 0% completion. The reliance on an oracle subgoal monitor for Naive E-SlotSSM indicates that autonomously inferring and grounding subgoals in real-world non-Markovian settings is a critical open problem.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analyses

- **Object Permanence and Tracking:** Visualizations of slot attention demonstrated that the model consistently attends to and tracks the same object instance across time, even during occlusion or motion. This indicates the emergence of robust object permanence, a critical capability for long-horizon reasoning in dynamic environments.
- **Scalability:** The paper qualitatively discusses the token scaling problem, illustrating that concatenation-based methods (e.g., OpenVLA with 256 dense tokens or SlotVLA with 128 slot tokens) quickly become intractable. Embodied-SlotSSM, operating with 32 slot tokens, offers a more scalable solution for long-horizon tasks, addressing computational memory constraints. The figure (Fig 7) highlights that baselines struggle with visually ambiguous states (e.g., "pick up" vs. "place down" when the gripper is at the same height), while Embodied-SlotSSM can distinguish them using its memory.

In summary, the results demonstrate that Embodied-SlotSSM, with its structured, object-centric memory, provides a more scalable and effective approach for general and, particularly, non-Markovian robotic manipulation tasks compared to memory-less or short-horizon VLA baselines. The LIBERO-Mem benchmark successfully exposes the limitations of existing methods and highlights the importance of dedicated memory architectures for complex robot control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in developing more capable and intelligent robotic systems, particularly those operating in complex, dynamic, and partially observable environments. The contributions are twofold, each addressing a critical need in the field of robot learning:

1. **Introduction of LIBERO-Mem:** This novel benchmark is a crucial contribution. By systematically stress-testing object-centric memory, temporal dependencies, and identity ambiguities, LIBERO-Mem directly addresses the shortcomings of existing benchmarks that largely adhere to the Markovian assumption. It provides a much-needed platform for evaluating and fostering the development of memory-augmented visuomotor policies. Its features, such as subgoal-aware evaluation, object identity ambiguities, and temporal scaling, push the boundaries of what robot learning models need to achieve for real-world applicability. The public release of LIBERO-Mem will enable broader research into non-Markovian reasoning, setting a new standard for evaluating robotic intelligence.

2. **Proposal of Embodied-SlotSSM:** This framework offers a scalable and principled approach to integrating structured, persistent memory into VLA models. By leveraging slot-based state-space modeling, it tackles the challenge of maintaining spatio-temporally consistent object identities over extended periods, a capability essential for long-horizon tasks. The model's ability to reconstruct short-term history and use a relational encoder for context-aware action prediction represents a significant architectural innovation. The experimental results, though demonstrating modest performance on some of the most challenging LIBERO-Mem tasks, clearly show that Embodied-SlotSSM outperforms existing memory-less baselines. This provides a strong inductive bias for reasoning under partial observability and offers a promising direction for developing truly scalable memory-based VLAs.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** By enabling robots to recall past interactions and reason about object histories, this work contributes directly to more autonomous and robust robotic systems. Robots equipped with such memory capabilities could operate more reliably in domestic, industrial, or service settings, avoiding redundant actions, correctly identifying target objects amidst visual similarity, and completing multi-step tasks that require sequential reasoning.
- **Bridging Simulation to Reality:** While currently in simulation, the principles of structured object memory and temporal reasoning are fundamental for real-world deployment where perfect observability is rare. The insights gained from LIBERO-Mem and Embodied-SlotSSM can guide the design of future real-world robotic systems.
- **Advancement in AI Research:** The approach highlights the importance of structured representations (slots) and temporal modeling (SSMs) for complex sequence prediction and decision-making in embodied AI, inspiring further research at the intersection of computer vision, natural language processing, and reinforcement learning.
- **Scalability for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The explicit focus on temporal scalability through slot-centric representations addresses a critical bottleneck in current VLA models, making long-horizon robot control more feasible from a computational perspective.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge important limitations. The empirical version, Naive E-SlotSSM, relies on *oracle subgoal representations*. This means the model is "told" the subgoal state rather than inferring it autonomously. This is a significant challenge in real-world POMDP settings, where self-discovered subgoal reasoning at the object level is crucial. Future work will need to address this limitation to enable fully autonomous memory-driven task execution. Additionally, the current work is in a simulated environment, and extending it to physical robots will introduce new challenges related to real-world perception noise, actuation inaccuracies, and sim-to-real transfer.

Despite these limitations, this paper lays foundational groundwork. By introducing a rigorous benchmark and a principled memory-augmented architecture, it encourages the robotics community to rethink conventional Markovian assumptions and move towards developing more context-aware, temporally intelligent, and truly general-purpose embodied agents.
