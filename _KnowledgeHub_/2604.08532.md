# Self-Improving 4D Perception via Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> SelfEvo, a framework from researchers including UIUC and Impossible Research, enables pretrained multi-view reconstruction models to continually improve their 4D perception capabilities using only unlabeled videos. It leverages a self-distillation mechanism based on spatiotemporal context asymmetry, demonstrating up to a 19.7% reduction in Absolute Relative Error for depth and a 20.1% increase in AUC@30 for camera estimation on new domains like OmniGeo, while maintaining or improving performance on original datasets.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art 4D perception models rely heavily on costly and scarce ground-truth geometric annotations, particularly for dynamic, in-the-wild scenes.
- The traditional "train-freeze-deploy" paradigm prevents models from adapting to new data distributions and continually improving after initial training.
- Existing self-supervised 3D learning methods often struggle with large viewpoint changes, dynamic scenes, or strong view-dependent effects, limiting their practical applicability.

## Method

- **SelfEvo** employs a ==self-distillation framework== with a teacher-student setup, where a teacher model receives rich spatiotemporal context to generate reliable pseudo-targets.
- A student model, provided with a ==context-reduced subset== of inputs, learns by matching the teacher's predictions through an ==output-level self-distillation loss==.
- The teacher's parameters are continuously updated as an ==Exponential Moving Average (EMA)== of the student's parameters, creating an online self-improving loop.

## Results

- **SelfEvo** achieved substantial improvements on new domains, with up to a **19.7%** reduction in Absolute Relative Error for depth and a **20.1%** increase in AUC@30 for camera estimation on OmniGeo.
- The framework demonstrated generality across different base models (VGGT, "pi^3") and diverse unlabeled training data (OmniWorld-Game, BEDLAM2.0, DROID), yielding consistent performance gains.
- Improvements learned via SelfEvo generalized effectively to unseen domains like DROID and HOI4D, indicating the framework enhances the robustness and adaptability of 4D perception systems beyond the adaptation domain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-view reconstruction models inherently produce more reliable geometric predictions with richer spatiotemporal context, allowing self-supervision by creating context asymmetry between teacher and student inputs.
> - An online EMA teacher provides more consistent and progressively stronger supervision than a fixed teacher, leading to better cross-domain generalization and original-domain retention.
> - Freezing the camera decoder while updating other model components (backbone and depth decoder) provides optimal stability and plasticity for effective self-improvement, preserving camera prediction accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D and 4D (dynamic 3D) perception and reconstruction has seen advancements, particularly with the emergence of learning-based multi-view reconstruction models. Recent models such as DUSt3R, VGGT, $\pi^3$, and DA3 have demonstrated the ability of large-scale supervised training to embed strong geometric priors into networks, enabling robust feedforward 3D prediction. These methods represent a shift from classical optimization-based approaches to unified, feedforward reconstruction paradigms using transformers.

However, a significant limitation within this landscape is the reliance on dense, ground-truth geometric annotations for fully supervised training. Such annotations are expensive to acquire for static scenes and become increasingly prohibitive and scarce for dynamic, in-the-wild 4D environments. This dependency on labeled data restricts the scalability of current systems and imposes a rigid "train-freeze-deploy" model, preventing systems from adapting to shifts in target data distributions after initial training.

Previous work has explored self-supervised 3D learning, often leveraging photometric consistency to jointly learn geometry and camera motion from unlabeled videos. However, these methods frequently encounter challenges under large viewpoint changes, dynamic scenes, or strong view-dependent effects, often performing optimally only on continuous, mostly static video streams or requiring category-specific constraints. Another direction involves learning representations rather than explicit geometry. The presented work distinguishes itself by focusing on the self-improvement of *pretrained* geometric foundation models, leveraging existing 3D supervision while enabling continued enhancement using only unlabeled data. This approach aims to push performance beyond the initial models on new and diverse domains.

The concept of self-distillation, where a model learns from its own predictions, has precedents in areas like representation learning (e.g., BYOL, DINO) and bootstrapping existing vision models (e.g., Depth Anything for monocular depth, BootsTAP for 2D point tracking). This paper extends self-distillation to multi-view reconstruction models, exploiting their flexibility to process continuous videos and unstructured photo collections. A concurrent effort, Selfi, also proposes a self-improving pipeline based on geometric feature alignment, but its reliance on multi-view consistency limits it to static scenes. In contrast, this research introduces a self-distillation formulation that is presented as more general and scalable, enabling continual online self-improvement in dynamic scenes without requiring static scene assumptions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable multi-view reconstruction models to continually improve their 4D perception capabilities using unlabeled videos, thereby eliminating the need for geometric annotations during the improvement phase.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key challenges and observations in the current state of 4D perception:

- **Annotation Scarcity and Cost:** Existing state-of-the-art learning-based multi-view reconstruction models heavily depend on dense ground-truth 3D/4D annotations for supervised training. Acquiring these annotations is a costly and time-intensive process, especially for complex dynamic scenes (4D), where data is particularly scarce. This limits the scalability and real-world applicability of these models.
- **Lack of Adaptability:** Current models are typically trained once on curated datasets and then "frozen" for deployment. This "train-freeze-deploy" paradigm prevents models from adapting to new or unseen data distributions that may arise in real-world scenarios. As data distributions shift, fixed models may experience performance degradation.
- **Leveraging Context Asymmetry:** The authors identify a core observation: multi-view reconstruction models inherently produce more reliable geometric predictions when provided with richer spatiotemporal context (i.e., a denser set of input views) compared to sparser inputs. This fundamental characteristic suggests a potential self-supervision signal. By strategically creating an asymmetry in the spatiotemporal context provided to different instances of the same model, one instance (with richer context) can generate more reliable "pseudo-targets" to supervise another instance (with reduced context). This mechanism allows the model to learn from itself without external ground-truth labels.
- **Continual Learning:** The aim is to move beyond static model deployment towards a framework that allows models to continuously learn and refine their understanding of 4D geometry directly from unlabeled video streams, fostering robustness and generalization over time.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to develop a scalable, annotation-free method for improving 4D perception models, addressing the limitations of supervised learning and rigid deployment paradigms by harnessing an intrinsic property of multi-view reconstruction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named SelfEvo (Self-Evolving 4D Perception), is designed for annotation-free, continual self-improvement of pretrained multi-view reconstruction models using unlabeled videos. It operates on the principle of self-distillation, leveraging spatiotemporal context asymmetry.

#### 3.1. The SelfEvo Framework Overview

SelfEvo instantiates two copies of a pretrained multi-view reconstruction model: a teacher and a student. The core idea is that the teacher receives a richer set of input views, enabling it to produce more reliable geometric predictions. These predictions then serve as pseudo-targets to supervise the student, which is provided with a restricted subset of the input views. The teacher's parameters are updated as an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's parameters, creating an online self-improving loop.

#### 3.2. Setup for Context Asymmetry

Given an unlabeled video clip $\mathbf{x} = \{I_t\}_{t=1}^S$, inputs for the teacher and student are constructed as:
- $\mathbf{x}_T = a_T(\mathbf{x})$: The teacher's input, representing a context-rich sequence.
- $\mathbf{x}_S = a_S(\mathbf{x}_T; \beta)$: The student's input, a context-reduced subset derived from the teacher's input, where $\beta$ defines the reduction method.

The teacher model $f_{\bar{\theta}}$ (with parameters $\bar{\theta}$) and student model $f_{\theta}$ (with parameters $\theta$) are initially identical in architecture and initialization. They produce outputs:
- $O_T = f_{\bar{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_T)$
- $O_S = f_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_S)$

#### 3.3. Objective Function

The student parameters $\theta$ are optimized using a loss function that primarily consists of an output-level self-distillation term:
$\min_{\theta} L(\theta; \beta, \pi, \tau, \gamma) = L_{base}(O_S, \text{sg}[O_T])$
- **Output-level self-distillation ($L_{base}$):** The student's predictions ($O_S$) are trained to match the teacher's predictions ($O_T$). The stop-gradient ($\text{sg}[\cdot]$) operator ensures that gradients do not flow back to the teacher, making its predictions fixed during a student update step.
- **Optional feature matching ($L_{feat}$):** An additional term $\gamma L_{feat}(F_S, \text{sg}[F_T])$ can be included to match intermediate feature representations ($F_S, F_T$) from student and teacher. $\gamma \ge 0$ is a weighting factor.

In the online setting, the teacher's parameters are updated via EMA: $\bar{\theta} \leftarrow \lambda \bar{\theta} + (1 - \lambda)\theta$, where $\lambda$ is the decay rate.

#### 3.4. Design Axes and Ablation Studies

The paper systematically investigates five design axes to optimize the self-improvement process:

- **Inducing Context Asymmetry ($a_T, a_S$):**
 - **Photometric Perturbations:** Applying augmentations (e.g., color jitter, grayscale) to the student's input only, or independently to both teacher and student.
 - **Frame Cropping:** Teacher observes full frames, student receives spatially cropped views.
 - **Frame Dropping:** Teacher observes a longer sequence (e.g., $m$ frames), student observes a shorter subsequence (e.g., $n$ frames, $n < m$).
 - **Finding:** Frame dropping was identified as the most effective method for inducing context asymmetry.

- **Student Frame Selection (scheme $\beta$):**
 - When using frame dropping, how the $n$ student frames are selected from the $m$ teacher frames.
 - **Random Sampling:** Randomly selects $n$ frame indices from the teacher's sequence.
 - **Attention-Guided Sampling:** Selects frames based on per-frame importance scores derived from teacher attention (e.g., 'keep-top' for high-score frames, 'keep-bottom' for low-score frames).
 - **Finding:** Random sampling proved to be the most robust and highest-performing strategy.

- **Teacher Update Rule ($\tau$):**
 - **Fixed Teacher (Offline):** Pseudo-targets are generated once by a fixed pretrained model.
 - **Online Teacher (EMA):** The teacher is an EMA-smoothed copy of the student and co-evolves throughout training.
 - **Finding:** An online updated EMA teacher consistently outperformed fixed-teacher (offline) pseudo-label fine-tuning, providing more consistent and progressively stronger supervision.

- **Parameter Update Rule ($\pi$):**
 - To manage adaptation and stability under pseudo-supervision, different model components were selectively updated or frozen.
 - **Freezing:** Camera decoder, depth decoder, shared backbone, or combinations (e.g., freezing both camera and depth heads, freezing only the aggregator).
 - **Training All:** Updating all modules.
 - **Finding:** Freezing only the camera decoder while updating the rest of the model yielded the best results, balancing stability and plasticity, particularly as camera estimation relies on global cues sensitive to frame subset changes.

- **Supervision Form:**
 - **Output-level distillation only:** $L_{base}$ term.
 - **Adding intermediate feature matching loss ($L_{feat}$):** Matching per-frame representations from aggregator tokens at multiple layers.
 - **Finding:** The feature matching loss did not provide significant additional gains, suggesting output-level supervision was sufficient.

#### 3.5. Default Instantiation

Based on these analyses, the default SelfEvo implementation utilizes random frame dropping, an online EMA teacher updated every step, freezes the camera decoder, and applies only output-level self-distillation ($\gamma=0$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence for the effectiveness of the SelfEvo framework across various benchmarks, base models, and data domains.

#### 4.1. New-Domain Adaptation and Original-Domain Retention

Using VGGT as the base model and OmniWorld-Game (specifically OmniGeo and OmniVideo subsets) for unlabeled training, SelfEvo demonstrated substantial gains:

- **Video Depth Estimation (OmniGeo & OmniVideo):** SelfEvo achieved improvements in Absolute Relative Error (Abs Rel) and threshold accuracy ($\delta < 1.25$). For example, on OmniGeo, Abs Rel decreased by up to 19.7% (from 0.346 to 0.278, scale alignment) and $\delta < 1.25$ increased by 18.8% (from 0.592 to 0.703, scale alignment). Similar gains were observed on OmniVideo.
- **Camera Estimation (OmniGeo & OmniVideo):** Improvements in Area Under the Curve (AUC) at various angular thresholds were also noted. On OmniGeo, AUC@30 increased by 20.1% (from 72.649 to 87.285).
- **Original-Domain Retention:** Critically, these new-domain gains did not compromise performance on established original benchmarks (Sintel, Bonn, KITTI for video depth; RealEstate10K for camera estimation). In many cases, performance on these original domains was maintained or even further improved, indicating that the framework enhances general capabilities rather than overfitting. For instance, VGGT's Abs Rel on KITTI decreased from 0.074 to 0.047 (scale alignment), showing improvement.

#### 4.2. Generality Across Base Models and Training Data

SelfEvo's applicability was tested across different base models (VGGT and $\pi^3$) and unlabeled training sources (BEDLAM2.0 and DROID):

- **$\pi^3$ on BEDLAM2.0 and DROID:** SelfEvo applied to $\pi^3$ showed consistent gains. On BEDLAM2.0, camera estimation improved substantially (e.g., AUC@30 from 96.08 to 96.72), while depth changes were minor. On DROID (wrist-camera videos), consistent video depth gains were observed (e.g., Abs Rel decreased from 0.271 to 0.249, scale alignment).
- **VGGT on BEDLAM2.0 and DROID:** VGGT also benefited from self-improvement on these datasets, further supporting the framework's generalization across model architectures and data regimes. For example, on DROID, VGGT's Abs Rel (scale alignment) decreased from 0.306 to 0.254.

#### 4.3. Unseen-Domain Generalization

To assess transferability, VGGT self-improved on OmniWorld-Game was evaluated on unseen domains: DROID (robot manipulation) and HOI4D (egocentric video).

- SelfEvo consistently outperformed the pretrained baseline on both unseen datasets, demonstrating that the learned improvements generalize beyond the adaptation domain. For example, on DROID, VGGT's Abs Rel (scale alignment) decreased from 0.306 to 0.268. On HOI4D, $\delta < 1.25$ (scale alignment) increased from 0.957 to 0.988.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Outcomes

The systematic study of design choices yielded specific conclusions:

- **Context Asymmetry:** Frame dropping was found to be the most effective method for creating teacher-student asymmetry.
- **Student Frame Selection:** Random sampling of student frames from the teacher's sequence was robust and yielded the best performance.
- **Teacher Update Rule:** An online EMA teacher, co-evolving with the student, consistently outperformed a fixed (offline) teacher baseline. Furthermore, it demonstrated better cross-domain generalization and original-domain retention compared to fully supervised fine-tuning.
- **Parameter Update Rule:** Freezing the camera decoder while updating other components (backbone and depth decoder) provided the most consistent gains and stability, particularly in preserving camera prediction accuracy.
- **Supervision Form:** Adding an intermediate feature matching loss ($L_{feat}$) did not yield significant additional improvements, indicating that output-level self-distillation provided a sufficient learning signal.

#### 4.5. Continuous Improvement Throughout Training

Checkpoint-wise evaluations demonstrated that the model consistently improved on held-out benchmarks (OmniGeo and OmniVideo) for both depth and camera estimation throughout the training process, with rapid gains in early stages followed by milder saturation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SelfEvo framework represents a methodological contribution to the field of 4D perception by addressing the challenges of data annotation and model adaptability.

- **Scalability and Reduced Annotation Dependency:** By enabling multi-view reconstruction models to continually improve using unlabeled videos, SelfEvo mitigates the high cost and scarcity of ground-truth 3D/4D annotations, particularly for dynamic scenes. This allows for greater scalability in model development and deployment.
- **Enhanced Model Adaptability and Generalization:** The framework moves beyond the rigid "train-freeze-deploy" paradigm, allowing pretrained models to adapt and refine their capabilities on new, unlabeled target domains. The demonstrated retention of performance on original domains and strong generalization to unseen domains suggests the creation of more robust and broadly applicable 4D perception systems.
- **Novel Self-Supervision Mechanism:** The core insight of leveraging spatiotemporal context asymmetry for self-distillation provides a new self-supervision signal for geometric prediction. This mechanism is shown to be effective for learning-based 4D perception, even in complex dynamic environments.
- **Systematic Design Principles:** The comprehensive study of design choices, including asymmetry construction, frame selection, teacher updating, and parameter freezing, offers practical guidance for implementing effective self-improving frameworks in computer vision. The finding that simple strategies like random frame dropping and selective freezing are crucial highlights the importance of systematic investigation.
- **Broader Applicability:** The framework's demonstrated generality across different base models (VGGT, $\pi^3$) and diverse data regimes (synthetic game environments, human-centric videos, robot manipulation, egocentric videos, in-the-wild internet videos) suggests its potential for wide adoption in various applications requiring robust 3D/4D understanding.
- **Foundation for Continual Learning in Geometry:** SelfEvo provides a foundation for developing continually learning geometric models, which can adapt to evolving real-world conditions without human intervention or expensive relabeling efforts.

Despite its advancements, the framework has identified limitations. Its effectiveness relies on a base model possessing non-trivial predictive ability, as it cannot bootstrap from severely degraded initial predictions. The reliance on frame dropping for asymmetry may also limit its effectiveness in static camera scenarios. Future work could explore token-level selection for greater flexibility and investigate strategies to sustain improvement over extended training horizons to mitigate potential model collapse, though this was not observed as a significant issue in the reported experiments. Overall, SelfEvo advances the development of autonomous and adaptable 4D perception systems.
