# Impossible Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Singapore introduce IPV-BENCH, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating video models' ability to understand and generate impossible scenarios, including 902 high-quality videos across four domains (Physical, Biological, Geographical, and Social Laws) and 260 text prompts for testing models' reasoning capabilities with counterfactual events.

## Problem

- Existing video benchmarks focus primarily on real-world scenarios, lacking evaluation tools for impossible or counterfactual scenes
- Current models' ability to reason about and generate physically impossible content remains poorly understood and evaluated

## Method

- Development of a structured taxonomy covering 14 categories of impossible scenarios across four domains
- Creation of IPV-**BENCH** with curated impossible videos, text prompts, and three evaluation tasks (Judgment, **MCQA**, **OpenQA**)

## Results

- Proprietary models (Gemini, GPT-4o) outperform open-source alternatives but still struggle with impossible video identification
- Current video generation models achieve relatively low IPV-Scores, indicating difficulties in balancing visual quality with prompt adherence
- Video-LLMs particularly struggle with OpenQA tasks requiring independent identification of impossible phenomena

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Models exhibit unbalanced capabilities between visual quality and faithful representation of impossible scenarios
> - Temporal reasoning and world knowledge are crucial but lacking in current video understanding models
> - Physical Laws domain poses the greatest challenge for both generation and understanding tasks

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a gap in the current landscape of video understanding and generation research. Existing datasets and benchmarks primarily focus on replicating real-world scenarios. This work introduces the novel concept of "impossible videos," which depict counterfactual, anti-reality scenes, directly challenging the limitations of current models.

- **Video Understanding:** The paper acknowledges the progress in video understanding tasks such as action recognition, captioning, question answering, and video-based world modeling. However, existing benchmarks lack specifically designed datasets to evaluate the generalization, robustness, and reasoning abilities of video models when presented with impossible or counterfactual scenarios.
- **Video Generation:** While there has been significant advancement in text-to-video generation, research has been primarily focused on achieving high visual quality, temporal consistency, and semantic coherence within the bounds of physical plausibility. Recent benchmarks have started evaluating models' ability to adhere to physical laws. However, the paper argues that the ability to generate *impossible* scenes is crucial for creative applications and has been largely overlooked. This work fills this gap by creating a benchmark (IPV-BENCH) to evaluate video understanding and generation models regarding this specific challenge.

The paper positions itself in contrast to existing benchmarks like:

- **GenVideo and GenVidBench:** AI-generated video detection, real-world and generated videos.
- **LOKI:** AIGC detection, video understanding, real-world videos, text prompts, and text descriptions.
- **VBench, VideoPhy, PhyGenBench:** Video generation, generated videos, text prompts, and text descriptions.
- **SEED-Bench, Video-Bench, MV-Bench, TempCompass:** Video understanding, real-world videos, text prompts, and text descriptions.

The main difference is that IPV-BENCH includes AI-generated video detection, video understanding, and video generation tasks, generated and impossible videos, text prompts, and impossible text.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold:

- **Evaluate Video Generation Models:** To investigate whether current video generation models can effectively follow prompts to create impossible video content. This goes beyond simply generating visually appealing videos and delves into the models' ability to "break the rules" while remaining faithful to the given instructions.
- **Assess Video Understanding Models:** To determine if today's video understanding models, especially Video-LLMs, are capable of understanding and reasoning about impossible videos. This requires temporal reasoning and world knowledge, as the models need to identify and explain why a given scene is impossible.

The motivation stems from the belief that impossible videos can serve as an effective testbed for assessing video models' ability to genuinely learn from real-world data and reason upon new, out-of-distribution inputs. This is crucial for advancing AI capabilities and enabling wider applications of video models in creative domains like film and advertising.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces IPV-BENCH, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate video models on impossible video understanding and generation. The methodology involves the following key steps:

- **Taxonomy Development:** A comprehensive taxonomy is created to categorize various types of impossible scenes, encompassing four domains (Physical Laws, Biological Laws, Geographical Laws, and Social Laws) and 14 categories. This taxonomy serves as the foundation for the benchmark.
- **Prompt Suite Construction (IPV-TXT):** Based on the taxonomy, a prompt suite consisting of 260 high-quality text prompts is created. These prompts describe various counterfactual and unusual scenarios, challenging video generation models to create impossible scenes. A combination of LLM generation, crowdsourcing, iterative refinement, and quality control were used to create prompts.
- **Video Dataset Curation (IPV-VID):** A video dataset of 902 high-quality videos (including synthetic and real) is curated, depicting impossible scenes. The videos are carefully annotated with information about spatial/temporal anomalies, taxonomy categories, and explanations. Text-to-video generation, web video collection, and real-world video collection were used to create the video dataset.
- **Evaluation Tasks:** Three evaluation tasks are designed to assess video understanding models:
 - **Judgment Task:** Classifying videos as synthetic or real.
 - **Multi-Choice Question Answering (MCQA):** Identifying the description that best captures the impossible phenomenon in the video.
 - **Open-Ended Question Answering (OpenQA):** Independently identifying and explaining the impossible phenomenon in the video.
- **Model Evaluation:** Mainstream video generation and understanding models are evaluated using IPV-BENCH, and the results are analyzed to identify limitations and future research directions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluations conducted using IPV-BENCH revealed several key findings:

- **Limitations of Video Generation Models:** Current video generation models struggle to generate high-quality videos that faithfully depict impossible events. The IPV-Score, a metric combining visual quality and prompt following, is relatively low for most models.
- **Unbalanced Capabilities in Video Generation:** Models exhibit unbalanced capabilities between visual quality and impossible prompt following. Some models excel in generating visually appealing videos but fail to accurately depict impossible scenes, while others prioritize prompt adherence at the expense of visual quality.
- **Challenges for Video Understanding Models:** Current Video-LLMs struggle with impossible videos, particularly in the OpenQA task, indicating a gap in their ability to independently identify and explain counterfactual phenomena.
- **Unbalanced Capabilities in Video Understanding:** Video understanding models also exhibit unbalanced capabilities across domains and tasks. "Physical Laws" emerges as the most challenging domain, and models often perform differently on the Judgment, MCQA, and OpenQA tasks.
- **Importance of Temporal Reasoning:** Understanding impossible videos requires both world knowledge and temporal reasoning. However, current models, including video expert models, face significant challenges in temporal dynamic reasoning.
- Proprietary Models vs. Open-Source Models: Generally, proprietary models such as Gemini and GPT-4o perform better than open-source models, but their ability to identify impossible videos remains suboptimal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research is significant because it:

- **Addresses a Critical Gap:** Identifies and investigates the underexplored area of impossible videos, which is crucial for advanced video understanding and generation research.
- **Introduces a Novel Benchmark:** Provides IPV-BENCH, a comprehensive benchmark with a well-structured taxonomy, a diverse prompt suite, and a high-quality video dataset, enabling researchers to evaluate and advance video models in this new direction.
- **Reveals Limitations and Future Directions:** Unveils the limitations of existing models in handling impossible videos and highlights the importance of temporal reasoning, world knowledge, and balanced capabilities across domains and tasks.

The potential impact of this research is substantial:

- **Advancing AI Capabilities:** By pushing video models beyond simple memorization and retrieval, this work can contribute to the development of more robust, generalizable, and creative AI systems.
- **Enabling New Applications:** The ability to generate and understand impossible videos can open up new possibilities in creative domains like film, advertising, gaming, and virtual reality.
- **Inspiring Future Research:** IPV-BENCH can serve as a valuable tool for researchers to explore new architectures, training strategies, and evaluation methods for video models, ultimately leading to significant advancements in the field.

**In conclusion, this research makes a valuable contribution to the field of computer vision and machine learning by introducing the concept of impossible videos and providing a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating video models on this challenging task. The findings and insights from this work have the potential to inspire future research and drive significant advancements in the development of more capable and creative AI systems.**
