# HyperEyes: Dual-Grained Efficiency-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Parallel Multimodal Search Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> HyperEyes introduces a dual-grained reinforcement learning framework for parallel multimodal search agents, enhancing both accuracy and inference efficiency by enabling concurrent visual grounding and information retrieval. The agent reduces tool-call turns by 5.3 times compared to strong baselines while improving average accuracy by 9.9 percentage points.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal search agents process queries sequentially, leading to redundant interactions, increased latency, and context contamination for decomposable multi-entity tasks.
- Models are primarily optimized for accuracy, lacking explicit efficiency objectives, which often results in "over-searching" even when parallel capabilities are available.
- Current benchmarks do not adequately evaluate inference cost or the agent's ability to perform parallel searches.

## Method

- Developed HyperEyes, a parallel multimodal search agent, utilizing a Unified Grounded Search (UGS) action space that fuses visual grounding and retrieval into a single atomic action for concurrent processing.
- Implemented a three-stage data curation pipeline, including a Parallel-Amenable Data Synthesis Pipeline and Progressive Rejection Sampling, to generate high-fidelity, efficiency-oriented training trajectories.
- Introduced a Dual-Grained Efficiency-Aware Reinforcement Learning framework, combining macro-level TRACE (Tool-use Reference-Adaptive Cost Efficiency) with micro-level On-Policy Distillation (OPD) to optimize both accuracy and efficiency.

## Results

- HyperEyes-30B (RL) achieved open-source state-of-the-art, surpassing VDR by 9.9 absolute percentage points in average accuracy (64.0% vs. 54.1%) while requiring 5.3 times fewer tool calls (2.2 vs. 11.6 turns).
- The Reinforcement Learning stage, incorporating TRACE and OPD, simultaneously improved both accuracy and efficiency over the Supervised Fine-Tuning-only stage, demonstrating the framework's dual optimization capability.
- HyperEyes-30B attained the highest Cost-Aware Score (CAS) on the BCVL and IMEB benchmarks, outperforming competitors by 4.3 and 7.6 times respectively, indicating superior information density and operational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing visual grounding and retrieval into a unified, atomic action (UGS) is crucial for enabling efficient parallel search and mitigating early localization errors in multi-entity scenarios.
> - Explicitly incentivizing efficiency with a dynamically adapting reward mechanism (TRACE) prevents over-retrieval and guides the agent toward more concise, noise-robust information fusion.
> - Augmenting sparse outcome rewards with dense, token-level corrective signals from an efficiency-aligned teacher (OPD) on failed rollouts effectively addresses the credit-assignment problem, improving both accuracy and efficiency simultaneously.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) inherently face limitations due to their finite training data cutoff. This constraint has spurred the development of search agents designed to augment these models by actively invoking external retrieval tools, thereby grounding responses in real-time and verifiable information.

The prevailing paradigm for multimodal search agents relies on sequential tool invocations to construct reasoning chains. While effective for complex multi-hop reasoning tasks, this sequential approach introduces redundancy and inefficiency when queries can be naturally decomposed into independent sub-retrievals. Such decomposable queries, particularly those involving multiple visual entities, necessitate numerous interaction rounds, leading to increased latency and potentially noisy, elongated contexts.

Although the concept of parallel tool invocation has been explored in text-based agents and some recent visual models, merely possessing parallel capability does not inherently guarantee efficient search behavior. Existing models are primarily optimized for accuracy, often lacking explicit objectives to favor concise, parallel trajectories over verbose ones. Consequently, without dedicated efficiency objectives, models may resort to over-searching, incurring unnecessary interaction rounds to maintain accuracy.

This work addresses this inefficiency bottleneck by introducing a parallel multimodal search agent. It aims to shift the paradigm from "searching longer" (sequential depth) to "searching wider" (concurrent breadth) for decomposable queries. By integrating explicit efficiency objectives into the training process and extending parallel search capabilities to the visual modality through a unified action space, the research seeks to improve both the accuracy and operational efficiency of multimodal search agents, particularly in multi-entity scenarios where current benchmarks and methodologies fall short in evaluating efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed inefficiencies in existing multimodal search agents when processing queries that are naturally decomposable into independent sub-retrievals. Current agents typically employ a sequential "crop-then-search" pipeline for visual entities, leading to several limitations:
1. **Interaction Redundancy:** Each entity requires a separate tool call, accumulating redundant rounds for multi-entity queries.
2. **Increased Latency:** The sequential nature prolongs the overall search process.
3. **Context Contamination:** Accumulation of intermediate observations can make the context longer and noisier, potentially hindering downstream reasoning.
4. **Brittle Dependencies:** Errors in early localization steps can cascade and corrupt subsequent search results.
5. **Lack of Parallelism:** The separation of visual grounding and retrieval prevents concurrent processing of multiple entities.

The authors argue that for such decomposable queries, effective multimodal agents should prioritize "searching wider rather than longer," meaning they should dispatch multiple grounded queries concurrently within a single round. However, existing models, optimized predominantly for accuracy, do not inherently prefer efficient, parallel trajectories, often resorting to "brute-force over-searching" to recover accuracy when parallel capabilities are present but not explicitly incentivized for efficiency.

To address these issues, the key objectives of the HyperEyes project are:

1. **Develop a Parallel Multimodal Search Agent (HyperEyes):** To create an agent capable of fusing visual grounding and retrieval into a single atomic action, enabling concurrent search across multiple entities and modalities within a single interaction round.
2. **Integrate Inference Efficiency as a First-Class Training Objective:** To move beyond sole accuracy optimization and explicitly train the agent to prefer compact, non-redundant parallel search trajectories.
3. **Establish Robust Cold-Start Supervision:** To develop a `Parallel-Amenable Data Synthesis Pipeline` capable of generating both visual multi-entity and textual multi-constraint queries, and to curate efficiency-oriented trajectories using `Progressive Rejection Sampling` for initial model fine-tuning.
4. **Introduce a Dual-Grained Efficiency-Aware Reinforcement Learning Framework:**
 - **Macro-level Optimization:** To propose `TRACE (Tool-use Reference-Adaptive Cost Efficiency)`, a trajectory-level reward mechanism that monotonically tightens its efficiency reference during training, thereby suppressing superfluous tool calls without compromising genuine multi-hop search.
 - **Micro-level Optimization:** To adapt `On-Policy Distillation (OPD)` to the multimodal agentic search setting, injecting dense, token-level corrective signals from an external teacher model specifically on failed rollouts. This aims to mitigate the credit-assignment deficiency inherent in sparse outcome rewards.
5. **Create a Joint Efficiency and Capability Benchmark:** To introduce `IMEB (Image Multi-Entity Benchmark)`, a human-curated dataset designed to jointly evaluate multimodal search capability and efficiency, addressing the limitation of existing benchmarks that solely focus on accuracy and omit inference cost and parallel-search capabilities.

These objectives collectively aim to develop a multimodal search agent that is not only accurate but also highly efficient, particularly in scenarios requiring concurrent information retrieval from diverse entities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HyperEyes agent operates on an iterative reasoning-and-acting paradigm, following the structure of the ReAct framework. Given a query, the agent generates a reasoning trace, selects a tool call, and receives an observation from the retrieval environment, iterating until a final answer is produced or a maximum turn limit is reached.

**Multimodal Search Tools:** The agent is equipped with two primary tools:
- **Image search:** Retrieves visually relevant results for a grounded visual image.
- **Text search:** Retrieves textual evidence for a natural language query.

**Unified Grounded Search (UGS):** A core methodological innovation is the reformulation of visual grounding. Instead of a two-stage "crop-then-search" pipeline, UGS treats visual grounding (predicting bounding boxes) as a parameter of the retrieval action itself. This allows the policy to simultaneously predict bounding boxes for all target entities and dispatch parallel search queries across modalities within a single turn, mitigating early localization errors and enabling concurrency.

**Training Data Curation:** A comprehensive three-stage pipeline supports cold-start supervision and efficiency-aware optimization:
1. **Task Formulation and Synthesis:**
 - A foundational pool of 246,000 multi-hop reasoning and visual recognition queries is compiled from public datasets and internal annotations.
 - This is augmented with 25,000 novel synthetic queries designed to strictly enforce parallel search behaviors:
 - **Visual multi-entity synthesis:** Fine-grained visual classification datasets are used. Knowledge retrievers build per-class knowledge bases. Images from distinct classes are composited via mosaic augmentation into multi-entity scenes. A question synthesizer generates QA pairs requiring integration of information across all co-occurring entities, ensuring parallel localization and retrieval are necessary. This yields 20,000 QA pairs.
 - **Textual multi-constraint synthesis:** Wikidata is used to perform multi-hop random walks and collect candidate entities. Questions are constructed to require answers satisfying multiple independent attribute constraints, preventing chain-style reasoning. This contributes 5,000 queries.
 - **Tool-necessity filtering:** Qwen3-VL-235B is used to filter out QA pairs that can be resolved without external tools, finalizing a pool of 271,000 genuinely tool-dependent tasks.
2. **SFT Trajectory Curation:**
 - **Progressive Rejection Sampling (PRS):** To generate clean, efficiency-oriented training signals, PRS samples trajectories across an ascending schedule of turn budgets (e.g., {2, 4, 8} turns). For each query, it strictly retains the shortest successful trajectory, thus inherently promoting single-turn precision and parallel execution. Gemini-3.0-Flash serves as the policy model for sampling.
 - **Quality filtering:** Surviving trajectories are further filtered to eliminate format invalidity, zero information gain, ungrounded reasoning, unimodal shortcuts, or inefficient sequential querying for parallelizable tasks. This process distills the initial pool to 30,000 high-fidelity, zero-redundancy trajectories.
3. **RL Data Selection:** A subset of medium-difficulty queries is curated from the PRS pipeline. These are queries where the initial model fails to find an answer under the tightest `pass@1` setting but succeeds under relaxed `pass@5` constraints. The successful trajectories from these samples establish dynamic efficiency boundaries for the subsequent RL phase (6,056 for the 30B model, 9,337 for the 235B model).

**Agentic Training:** A two-stage paradigm is employed:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The base MLLM is fine-tuned via next-token prediction on the curated SFT trajectory corpus. This phase instills basic one-shot parallel dispatch behaviors due to the strict efficiency filtering of the trajectories.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** This phase addresses explicit inference efficiency optimization and token-level credit assignment. It uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a `Dual-Grained Efficiency-Aware RL framework`:
 - **TRACE (Tool-use Reference-Adaptive Cost Efficiency):** This macro-level, trajectory-based reward mechanism dynamically adjusts its efficiency reference. The total reward combines accuracy (`R_acc`), format validity (`R_fmt`), and tool-use efficiency (`R_tool`). `R_tool` considers both the number of tool-call rounds (`t_c`) and total invocations (`t_s`). An initial reference (`ˆt_c^(0)`, `ˆt_s^(0)`) is derived from successful trajectories in the RL dataset. `ˆt_c` is monotonically tightened each epoch, anchoring the reward boundary at the minimum achievable efficiency by the current policy. Positive rewards are given only for strictly efficient, successful trajectories (`t_c ≤ ˆt_c` and `t_s ≤ ˆt_s`), with linear interpolation for continuous signals. The `t_s` constraint prevents reward hacking by minimizing rounds through excessive parallel calls.
 - **On-Policy Distillation (OPD):** This micro-level approach addresses the credit-assignment deficiency of sparse outcome rewards on failed rollouts. It injects dense token-level corrective signals from a frozen, stronger teacher model (`π_teacher`, e.g., an RL-trained HyperEyes-235B) into the student model (`π_θ`). This distillation is applied exclusively to failed rollouts (`R_acc = 0`) by minimizing the reverse KL divergence, ensuring that the parallel-dispatch behaviors learned from TRACE on successful rollouts are preserved.

**IMEB Benchmark Construction:** A new benchmark, the `Image Multi-Entity Benchmark (IMEB)`, was introduced to jointly evaluate accuracy and search efficiency. It comprises 300 human-curated instances across diverse domains, averaging 4.6 entities per image. Each question rigorously requires concurrent localization and retrieval across multiple entities. A `Cost-Aware Score (CAS)` is proposed: `CAS = (Acc^2 * 100) / (N_tok + 2*N_tool + 1)`, penalizing token consumption and sequential tool-call rounds while prioritizing accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HyperEyes was conducted across six multimodal search benchmarks: MMSearch, FVQA, LiveVQA, BrowseComp-VL (BCVL), MMSearch-Plus, and the newly introduced IMEB. Performance was measured by accuracy (Acc) and average tool-call turns (Turns).

**Open-Source State-of-the-Art and Pareto-Dominance:**
- HyperEyes-235B (RL) demonstrated performance comparable to proprietary models, approaching Gemini-3.1-Pro and outperforming Claude-Opus-4.6 and Kimi-K2.5. The remaining performance gap with Gemini is attributed to the latter's richer parametric knowledge.
- HyperEyes-30B (RL) surpassed the strongest open-source search agent of comparable scale, VDR, by 9.9 absolute percentage points in average accuracy (64.0% vs. 54.1%).
- HyperEyes exhibited Pareto-dominance in both accuracy and operational efficiency. For instance, HyperEyes-30B achieved superior accuracy while requiring 5.3 times fewer tool calls than VDR (2.2 average turns vs. 11.6). This efficiency is attributed to the Unified Grounded Search (UGS) action space, which replaces conventional serial "crop-then-search" loops with unified, parallel operations.

**Reinforcement Learning for Dual Improvement:**
- The RL stage, incorporating TRACE and OPD, simultaneously improved both accuracy and efficiency over the SFT-only stage. For example, HyperEyes-235B improved its average accuracy to 66.6% while reducing tool-call turns on complex benchmarks like BCVL. This suggests that TRACE's adaptive efficiency reference and OPD's token-level signals provide complementary supervision for optimizing both objectives.
- Empirical analysis indicated that simply increasing tool calls does not consistently improve accuracy and can often degrade it due to the accumulation of distractor evidence. By explicitly penalizing over-retrieval, the RL framework guided the policy toward more holistic and noise-robust information fusion.

**Cost-Aware Accuracy (CAS):**
- Under the proposed CAS metric, which jointly quantifies reasoning correctness and search efficiency, HyperEyes-30B achieved the highest scores on both BCVL and IMEB, surpassing the second-best open-source competitors by 4.3 times and 7.6 times, respectively. This implies that HyperEyes delivers substantially higher information density per unit of compute, achieving efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Training Data Curation:** Rigorous quality filtering of training trajectories proved more impactful than raw data volume. Reducing the dataset from 121,000 to 30,000 high-quality trajectories (via Progressive Rejection Sampling and subsequent filtering) improved average accuracy by 7.2 absolute points.
- **RL Reward Mechanisms:**
 - Using only outcome-based rewards led to inflated tool calls without accuracy improvement.
 - A static tool-call reference improved efficiency and accuracy.
 - The adaptive TRACE formulation further enhanced accuracy by 1.6 points and minimized tool calls, affirming the benefit of dynamic efficiency constraints.
- **On-Policy Distillation (OPD):**
 - Augmenting TRACE with OPD from an efficiency-aligned teacher (RL-trained HyperEyes-235B) provided an additional 1.3 points average accuracy gain without increasing tool calls.
 - Conversely, using a vanilla MLLM as the teacher or skipping the initial SFT phase entirely resulted in significant performance degradation, demonstrating that effective distillation necessitates an efficiency-aligned teacher and a parallel-amenable cold start.
- **Unified Grounded Search (UGS) Validity:** A controlled comparison showed that UGS consistently achieved higher average accuracy and fewer tool-call turns compared to alternative grounding paradigms like LLM Crop and Code Crop, validating the efficiency and effectiveness of the unified action space.
- **Robustness to Random Seed:** Evaluations across multiple random seeds for the RL stage of HyperEyes-30B (RL) showed minimal standard deviation in both accuracy and tool-call turns, indicating the stability and reliability of the reported results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of multimodal AI agents:

1. **Novel Parallel Multimodal Search Agent:** The introduction of HyperEyes, with its Unified Grounded Search (UGS) action space, represents a notable advancement in multimodal search. By fusing visual grounding and retrieval into a single atomic action, it directly addresses the inefficiency of sequential processing for decomposable, multi-entity queries. This shifts the paradigm from "searching longer" to "searching wider," which is beneficial for tasks requiring concurrent information gathering.

2. **Advanced Efficiency-Aware Reinforcement Learning:** The `Dual-Grained Efficiency-Aware RL framework` is a methodological contribution. `TRACE` provides an adaptive, macro-level reward that dynamically tightens efficiency constraints, guiding the agent towards optimal tool usage without stifling multi-hop reasoning. `On-Policy Distillation` offers a micro-level correction mechanism, mitigating the credit-assignment problem in sparse reward environments by providing dense supervision on failed rollouts. This dual-grained approach sets a new standard for optimizing both accuracy and operational efficiency in agentic systems.

3. **New Benchmark for Joint Evaluation:** The creation and release of `IMEB` (Image Multi-Entity Benchmark) fills a critical gap in current evaluation methodologies. Existing benchmarks primarily focus on accuracy, neglecting inference cost and parallel-search capabilities. IMEB, with its `Cost-Aware Score (CAS)`, provides a comprehensive tool for jointly assessing reasoning correctness and search efficiency, encouraging the development of more practically viable agents.

4. **Established Performance Benchmark:** HyperEyes-30B achieves open-source state-of-the-art performance, demonstrating a Pareto-dominance over existing models in both accuracy and efficiency. Its ability to achieve comparable accuracy with significantly fewer tool-call rounds (e.g., 5.3 times fewer than VDR) highlights a substantial improvement in resource utilization. The performance of HyperEyes-235B also brings open-source models closer to proprietary frontier models like Gemini-3.1-Pro, reducing the performance gap.

5. **Broader Accessibility and Sustainability:** By reducing the per-query tool cost by approximately five-fold and shortening end-to-end latency, HyperEyes contributes to lower energy consumption and faster response times. This makes grounded multimodal QA more accessible to users on resource-constrained devices and to researchers with limited computational resources, promoting broader adoption and more sustainable AI practices.

6. **Enabling Robust Multimodal Applications:** The robust performance and efficiency-aware design of HyperEyes offer a generalizable recipe for building reliable multimodal assistants. Such assistants are crucial for applications where verifiable, source-grounded answers are essential, including educational tools, scientific literature exploration, accessibility solutions for visually impaired users, and consumer-facing visual question answering systems.

In conclusion, this work demonstrates that accuracy and efficiency can be complementary objectives in agentic multimodal search when optimized through a dedicated, dual-grained framework and evaluated with appropriate metrics. It provides both a novel architectural approach and a comprehensive training methodology to achieve this synergy.
