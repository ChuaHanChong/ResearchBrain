# Refining Almost-Safe Value Functions on the Fly

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, San Diego, developed REFINECBF and HJ-PATCH, algorithms enabling real-time, online adaptation of formally safe value functions for robotic control. These methods demonstrated collision avoidance against unforeseen obstacles and adaptation to unmodeled wind disturbances on ground robots and quadcopters, often achieving convergence in under 2 seconds.

## Problem

- Learning-based robot control often lacks robust adaptability to unforeseen real-world changes and distribution shifts, limiting deployment in safety-critical applications.
- Formal safety methods like Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) provide strong guarantees but suffer from computational intractability ('curse of dimensionality') and are typically offline.
- Approximate methods (e.g., learning-based CBFs) offer scalability but frequently lack formal guarantees and struggle with post-deployment adaptation to environmental variations.

## Method

- A formally-grounded ==adaptive safety framework== using ==warm-started HJR-based refinement== (**REFINECBF** and **HJ-PATCH**) enables online, in-the-loop adaptation to sensed changes.
- **REFINECBF** iteratively refines any given value function using ==Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs variational inequality (HJI-VI)== formulations, continuously incorporating real-time environmental changes.
- **HJ-PATCH** provides a computationally efficient approach for ==localized corrections== using a ==contractive HJI-PDE== formulation and an ==active set strategy== for rapid patching.

## Results

- **REFINECBF** and **HJ-PATCH** successfully adapted to new obstacles and unmodeled wind disturbances in simulations and hardware, preventing collisions where non-adaptive baselines failed.
- Hardware experiments on mobile ground robots and quadcopters demonstrated real-time adaptation, with safety value functions re-converging in **under 2 seconds** upon detecting changes.
- **HJ-PATCH** provided a CPU-efficient solution for obstacle avoidance, demonstrating robust safety in **9 out of 10** rollouts, while **REFINECBF** (GPU-accelerated) enabled deadlock avoidance for quadcopters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Warm-starting Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability with an approximate value function allows for rapid, iterative refinement and convergence while preserving formal safety guarantees.
> - A two-phase algorithm, SAFEADAPT-REFINECBF, can ensure strict safety during adaptation by first retracting to a provably safe set before expanding for improved performance.
> - Localized patching (HJ-PATCH) significantly improves computational efficiency by only updating relevant state-space regions, making online adaptation feasible for resource-constrained platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly integrating learning-based modules for perception and control, which has expanded capabilities in areas such as dynamic locomotion and complex navigation. However, the effectiveness of these learning-based approaches often depends on the conditions under which they were trained, limiting their adaptability to uncertain or evolving real-world scenarios. This limitation poses a significant challenge for deployment in safety-critical applications, where unforeseen environmental changes or distribution shifts can lead to failures. Addressing this, the paper positions itself within the context of developing safety modules capable of real-time, online adaptation at the control level.

A common strategy for ensuring safety in learning-enabled systems is the use of safety filters. These modules monitor control actions from a primary decision-making system and intervene only when necessary to prevent unsafe states. Many safety filters are constructed upon a safety value function, a scalar field that quantifies the system's safety margin at a given state. Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) are a prominent example, facilitating the formulation of quadratic programs (QPs) to minimally modify control inputs to maintain safety. However, a core challenge in CBF-based methods is the initial design or learning of a valid safety value function that guarantees persistent safety.

Two primary approaches to generating safe value functions exist:
1. **Constructive methods**, such as Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) Reachability, offer formal safety guarantees by numerically solving for the value function. Their main drawback is poor scalability with state dimensionality, typically limiting them to systems with fewer than six state dimensions, and they are generally performed offline.
2. **Approximate methods**, which use function approximators like neural networks, offer scalability but often sacrifice formal guarantees and exhibit limited generalization to novel scenarios.

Both paradigms struggle with adaptation to post-deployment variations, such as sim-to-real gaps or unexpected dynamic changes. This paper seeks to bridge the gap between the formal guarantees of constructive methods and the scalability of approximate methods. It proposes using data-driven approximations as warm-starts for a formal, constructive refinement process that leverages HJ Reachability.

Related work in safety ranges from optimal control and model predictive control (MPC) to formal verification. While optimal control can incorporate safety constraints, it often faces computational intractability for complex nonlinear systems. Safety filters, on the other hand, aim for minimal intervention. CBF-based methods are computationally efficient for online enforcement but require a pre-designed safe value function. Learning-based CBFs offer scalability but lack formal guarantees. Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) provides formal characterization of worst-case safety for general nonlinear systems with disturbances, yielding strong guarantees, but its computational burden and offline nature limit online deployment. Various efforts have been made to improve HJR scalability or link CBFs and HJR (e.g., Control Barrier Value Functions (CBVFs)). Previous work by the authors introduced REFINECBF and HJ-PATCH for offline refinement using approximate initial value functions. This current work extends these methods to online, in-the-loop adaptation to real-world changes while maintaining theoretical safety and convergence guarantees.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the limitations of existing safety verification and control methods in dynamic, uncertain real-world robotic environments. While learning-based control offers enhanced capabilities, its performance is often tied to training conditions, making systems vulnerable to environmental changes or distribution shifts that were not encountered during training. This lack of robust adaptability can lead to catastrophic failures in safety-critical applications. Therefore, a primary objective is to develop safety modules that can adapt online and in real-time, directly at the control level.

The paper identifies specific challenges with current approaches to generating safe value functions:
- **Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR):** Provides strong formal safety guarantees but suffers from the "curse of dimensionality," making it computationally intractable for higher-dimensional systems and typically restricting its application to offline computation. This offline nature prevents real-time adaptation to dynamic environments.
- **Control Barrier Functions (CBFs):** Offer efficient online enforcement of safety via simple optimization problems, but the initial design or learning of a valid CBF is a significant hurdle, especially for complex nonlinear dynamics or under disturbances. Learning-based CBFs, while scalable, often lack formal guarantees and their reliability depends on data quality.
- **Lack of Adaptability Post-Deployment:** Both constructive (HJR) and approximate (learning-based CBFs) methods generally struggle to adapt to variations encountered after deployment, such as sim-to-real gaps or unexpected changes in system dynamics or environment (e.g., new obstacles).

Recognizing that formal and approximate methods are complementary rather than mutually exclusive, the core objective of this work is to bridge this gap. The authors propose to leverage the scalability of data-driven approximations by using them as "warm starts" for a formal, constructive refinement process based on HJ Reachability.

Specifically, the key objectives and contributions are:
1. **Develop a formally-grounded adaptive safety framework:** This framework aims to enable online, in-the-loop adaptation to sensed changes in the system and environment. The core idea is to iteratively refine any given value function until convergence, while continuously adapting to changes.
2. **Extend REFINECBF for disturbance-affected systems:** To formally guarantee safety for systems subject to unknown but bounded external disturbances, extending previous control-only formulations.
3. **Propose a unified algorithmic framework:** This framework should encompass both offline and in-the-loop refinement, along with theoretical analysis and implications for online deployment. This includes two main algorithms: REFINECBF for global refinement and HJ-PATCH for computationally efficient, localized updates.
4. **Conduct rigorous comparative analysis:** Evaluate the proposed methods against typical CBF approaches (disjoint CBFs, backup CBFs) in realistic simulated experiments with real-time requirements.
5. **Perform extensive hardware experiments:** Demonstrate real-time adaptation to online detection of unforeseen obstacles and wind disturbances on multiple robotic platforms (ground vehicles and quadcopters). This aims to validate the practical applicability of the framework in real-world settings.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for robotic safety guarantees that are not only formal but also practical and adaptable to the inherent uncertainties and dynamics of real-world operation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper builds upon the foundations of Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) and Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) Reachability, integrating them into a framework for online, adaptive safety refinement.

**System Model and Safety Preliminaries:**
The paper considers a control- and disturbance-affine dynamical system: $\dot{x} = F(x, u, d) \triangleq f(x) + g(x)u + w(x)d$, where $x$ is the state, $u$ is the control input, and $d$ is an unknown but bounded disturbance. The constraint set $L$ denotes non-failure states, and the goal of a safety filter is to keep the system within $L$. Key concepts include robust control invariant sets and the viability kernel $S(K)$, which is the largest control invariant set within a given set $K$.
A Lipschitz continuous value function $h(x)$ defines a 0-superlevel set $H = \{x | h(x) \geq 0\}$. Based on Nagumo's theorem, $H$ is control invariant if $L_{F}^* h(x) \geq 0$ for all $x \in \partial H$, where $L_{F}^* h(x) := \inf_{d \in D} \sup_{u \in U} L_F h(x, u, d)$ is the robustified Lie derivative. CBFs extend this condition to the interior of $H$ with a Lyapunov-like inequality: $L_{F}^* h(x) \geq -\alpha(h(x))$ for an extended class $\mathcal{K}$ function $\alpha$. This condition defines an admissible control set $G_h(x)$, which can be enforced online through a quadratic program (QP) (Equation 5) that minimally modifies a nominal control input. The paper categorizes CBFs based on their properties: Invalid, Unsafe, Conservative (safe), and Desired (safe).

**HJR-based Control Barrier Value Functions (CBVFs):**
HJR provides a formal method to characterize safety by computing the set of states from which failure is inevitable. This approach can yield a CBVF, $h_\lambda(x, t)$, defined as the minimum over disturbances of the maximum over controls of the minimum over time of a constraint function, discounted by $\lambda$ (Equation 6). The paper primarily focuses on the infinite-time safety scenario where $\lambda=0$, allowing any positive decay rate $\gamma$ to be used online. HJR problems are typically solved by numerically solving Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs (HJI) partial differential equations (PDEs) or variational inequalities (VIs) on a discretized state-space grid. The paper details two discrete-time dynamic programming update rules:
1. **Single-Boundary PDE Formulation (Equation 11):** A contractive mapping that recovers the largest control invariant subset of the initial value function's 0-level set.
2. **Variational Inequality (VI) Formulation (Equation 13):** Recovers a control invariant set that is a subset of the constraint set $L$.

**REFINECBF Algorithm:**
REFINECBF is designed to refine a candidate value function $h_0$, potentially an analytically derived, learned, or even unsafe CBF, by warm-starting the HJI-VI formulation.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** Theorem 1 proves that if the HJI-VI formulation (Equation 15) converges, the result $h^*(x)$ is a safe CBVF. This relies on lemmas establishing that the converged 0-superlevel set $H^*$ is control invariant (Lemma 1), a subset of the viability kernel $S(L)$ (Lemma 2), and that $h^*(x)$ satisfies the CBF inequality (Lemma 3). Lemma 4 further guarantees that refining a control-invariant CBVF preserves safety.
- **Algorithm 1 (REFINECBF):** Iteratively applies the HJI-VI update rule (Equation 13), initialized with $h_0$. In the online setting, it continuously observes and incorporates real-time environmental changes (new obstacles, modified actuation limits, changed disturbances) into the refinement process. The most current value function is published for use by a safety filter (Algorithm 2), which queries the value and gradient to solve a QP for safe control.
- **SAFEADAPT-REFINECBF (Algorithm 3):** A two-phase variant addressing the need for stricter safety assurances during refinement.
 1. **Retraction Phase:** Uses the contractive HJI-PDE update rule (Equation 11) to shrink the safe set until it represents a provably safe, control-invariant subset of the viability kernel, thereby emptying the set of false positive states.
 2. **Refining Phase:** Switches to the HJI-VI update rule, allowing the safe set to expand for improved performance while guaranteeing that safety, once established, is never lost (Theorem 2). This algorithm explicitly handles safety-decreasing updates by forcing a return to the retraction phase.

**HJ-PATCH Algorithm:**
HJ-PATCH is a computationally efficient alternative to REFINECBF, designed for localized corrections rather than global refinement.
- **Core Idea:** It uses the contractive HJI-PDE formulation (Equation 10) to find the largest control invariant subset within the 0-superlevel set of the initial guess $h_0$. This is efficient when the initial CBF is "mostly safe" but requires minor localized corrections.
- **Localized Dynamic Programming:** Computational speedup is achieved through an active set strategy. Only states in an "active set" $R$ are updated at each iteration. This set includes value-decreasing states near the boundary $\partial_\zeta H$ and is expanded to include their neighbors, ensuring correct propagation of patches while maintaining efficiency.
- **Algorithm 4 (HJ-PATCH):** Initializes an active set $R^{(0)}$ around the boundary of $H^{(0)}$, optionally removing oracle-certified safe cells. It iteratively applies the HJI-PDE update to states in $R^{(k)}$. The active set is then updated by identifying value-decreasing states and padding them with neighbors. For online adaptation, safety-decreasing updates (reduced actuation, increased disturbance) conservatively reset the active set to the entire current boundary. New obstacles clip the value function and expand the active set to include boundary states affected by the obstacle.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** Theorem 3 proves that HJ-PATCH, upon convergence, recovers the viability kernel of the initial candidate safe set, $S(H_0)$. This relies on Lemma 6 (convergence to a discrete-approximated control-invariant set), Lemma 7 (optimistic global warm-start HJR recovers viability kernel), and Lemma 8 (converged safe set is a superset of the viability kernel). Theorem 4 states that HJ-PATCH monotonically decreases the rate of false positive states with every iteration due to its contractive updates.

Both algorithms leverage spatial discretization on grid points and approximate Hamiltonians using finite difference methods (WENO schemes).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core contribution of this work is the validation of REFINECBF and HJ-PATCH for online, in-the-loop adaptation of safety value functions in non-stationary environments. The experiments demonstrate that warm-starting from a prior value function allows for rapid refinement and convergence, even with real-time environmental changes.

**A. Simulation Experiments (Ground Robot - Jackal):**
- **Static Environment Analysis:** In a two-obstacle environment, a naive combination of individual CBFs (jointCBF) resulted in an overly optimistic and unsafe value function. Both REFINECBF and HJ-PATCH produced safe value functions with boundaries comparable to the ground truth from full HJR computation. HJ-PATCH's value function closely matched the HJR solution, while REFINECBF was more conservative.
- **Online Adaptation with Obstacle Detection:** When the Jackal robot discovered new obstacles online, REFINECBF (with GPU acceleration) and HJ-PATCH (on CPU) successfully adapted and avoided collisions. Baseline methods (jointCBF and re-solving HJR from scratch) consistently failed. jointCBF was inherently unsafe, and full HJR re-computation was too slow for real-time. HJ-PATCH demonstrated robust safety in 9 out of 10 rollouts on a CPU, highlighting its computational efficiency for resource-constrained platforms.

**B. Simulation Experiments (Quadcopter - Backup CBF Comparison):**
- **Deadlock Avoidance:** In a planar quadcopter task with an obstacle occluding the goal, REFINECBF successfully discovered and navigated a path over the obstacle by progressively incorporating newly visible free space into its safe set. The backupCBF method, relying on a fixed "stabilize to hover" backup maneuver, resulted in deadlock as it could not find a path to the goal. REFINECBF's ability to characterize a safety filter over the full dynamics (including velocity) provided more informative gradients, nudging the drone upwards to avoid the obstacle. REFINECBF seamlessly expanded the safe set online as new free space was revealed.

**C. Hardware Experiments (Mobile Robots - Obstacle Appears):**
- **Real-time Adaptation to New Obstacles:** A Clearpath Jackal UGV was tasked with reaching a goal. When an obstacle was unexpectedly knocked over, blocking its path, the fully adaptive REFINECBF immediately perceived the change, updated its safety map, backtracked, and found a new safe path to the goal. A non-adaptive baseline (operating on a stale map) failed to detect the change and collided with the new obstacle. This outcome was consistent across multiple trials, demonstrating the criticality of online adaptation.

**D. Hardware Experiments (Drone - Sensing Limitations):**
- **Optimistic Planning with Dynamic Obstacles:** A quadcopter navigated an environment where a second circular obstacle was initially hidden behind a rectangular one. The system initially planned optimistically, assuming unseen space was free. The fully adaptive REFINECBF successfully perceived the new obstacle upon discovery, recomputed its constraint function, updated its value function (re-converging in under 2 seconds), and adjusted its trajectory to safely reach the goal in all 10 trials. In contrast, the non-adaptive baseline consistently collided with the unexpected obstacle.

**E. Hardware Experiments (Quadcopter - Varying Disturbances):**
- **Adaptation to Unmodeled Wind Disturbances:** A quadcopter flew through a narrow passage with a localized, unmodeled wind disturbance from a fan. Equipped with a flow sensor to detect airflow, the adaptive REFINECBF rapidly updated its safety value function to account for the new dynamics. This allowed the drone to successfully counteract the disturbance and traverse the passage in 9 out of 10 attempts. The non-adaptive baseline, assuming nominal dynamics, was consistently pushed into the obstacle. This experiment leveraged SAFEADAPT-REFINECBF, showing an initial contraction upon wind detection followed by safe expansion.

Overall, the experiments consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of warm-started HJ reachability methods for real-time, in-the-loop adaptation. REFINECBF typically requires GPU acceleration for rapid updates, while HJ-PATCH provides a CPU-efficient solution for localized "patching" scenarios. The ability to incorporate full state-space information (including velocities) in the value function proved crucial for effective obstacle avoidance and deadlock resolution in dynamic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a significant contribution to the field of robotic safety by demonstrating a practical approach to integrating formally guaranteed safety into real-time, online robotic operations. Traditionally, Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) Reachability, while offering strong formal safety guarantees, has been largely confined to offline analysis due to its computational intensity and the "curse of dimensionality." Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), while efficient for online control, often suffer from challenges in principled design or learning with formal guarantees. This paper effectively bridges this gap.

The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:
- **Enabling Real-time, Adaptive Safety Guarantees:** The primary impact is enabling robotic systems to maintain formal safety guarantees in dynamic, uncertain environments. By refining "almost-safe" value functions on the fly, robots can adapt to unforeseen changes such as new obstacles, altered system dynamics (e.g., wind disturbances), or updated operational constraints, a critical capability for real-world deployment.
- **Increased Robustness and Reliability:** The framework provides a principled way to incorporate real-time sensor data and environmental changes into the safety analysis, leading to more robust and reliable autonomous systems. This is particularly impactful for safety-critical applications like search-and-rescue, autonomous logistics, and long-term mobile robot operations where unexpected events are common.
- **Leveraging Approximate Solutions with Formal Backing:** The approach demonstrates that approximate or learned CBFs, which are scalable but may lack formal guarantees, can be effectively utilized as warm-starts for a formal refinement process. This allows for the benefits of scalability while systematically addressing potential unsafe regions with the rigorousness of HJ Reachability.
- **Computational Efficiency through Warm-starting and Localization:** The introduction of warm-starting for REFINECBF and the localized patching strategy of HJ-PATCH addresses a major practical limitation of traditional HJR: computational cost. HJ-PATCH, in particular, offers a CPU-efficient method for localized corrections, making it suitable for resource-constrained platforms.
- **Addressing Limitations of Fixed Safety Policies:** The comparison with Backup CBFs highlights a key advantage: the proposed methods generate state-dependent safety policies derived from the full system dynamics, avoiding deadlocks or overly conservative behaviors that can arise from fixed, expert-chosen backup maneuvers.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies key limitations—idealized sensors, model dependency, and scalability to very high-dimensional systems—which serve as clear avenues for future research. This includes integrating realistic sensor modalities and uncertainty, and merging the framework with advanced learning-based function approximators to extend its applicability to complex robots like manipulators and humanoids.

In conclusion, this research moves beyond theoretical discussions of formal safety to demonstrate its practical viability for dynamic robotic systems. By providing tools for rapid, in-the-loop adaptation of safety value functions, it offers a concrete path toward deploying formally guaranteed safety in complex, real-world robotic applications, thereby enhancing their autonomy, safety, and trustworthiness.
