# Long-Horizon Manipulation via Trace-Conditioned VLA Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The LoHo-Manip framework enables robots to reliably execute complex, multi-step, long-horizon manipulation tasks through a hierarchical approach that separates high-level task management from low-level control. This method, featuring receding-horizon planning and trace-conditioned execution, demonstrates superior performance on embodied reasoning and manipulation benchmarks in both simulation and real-world settings, notably achieving a 97.5% average score on LIBERO and significantly outperforming baselines on VLABench and real-world out-of-distribution tasks.

## Problem

- Long-horizon manipulation tasks are challenging for robots due to multiple interdependent steps, cumulative execution errors, and the need for progress-dependent decision-making.
- Existing monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies suffer from fragility under compounding errors and limited modularity, making it difficult to adapt to environmental changes or swap components.
- Current approaches struggle with implicit progress tracking, robust error recovery, and generalization to unseen objects or environments in complex, multi-step scenarios.

## Method

- **LoHo-Manip** is a modular hierarchical framework with a ==VLM task manager== and a ==VLA executor==.
- The VLM task manager, conditioned on the current observation, predicts a ==remaining plan== with linguistic subtasks and a ==spatial visual trace== in a ==receding-horizon== manner.
- The VLA executor performs short-horizon actions by ==conditioning on the visual trace==, translating abstract plans into spatially grounded instructions.

## Results

- LoHo-Manip achieved RoboVQA BLEU **63.1** and EgoPlan-Bench2 accuracy **56.7%**, leading on embodied reasoning and trajectory prediction.
- Outperformed strong VLA baselines: **97.5%** average on LIBERO; **0.39** vs **0.24** for base π0.5 on VLABench.
- Enhanced robustness and higher success rates in real-world out-of-distribution manipulation tasks vs fine-tuned base VLAs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level task management (VLM) from low-level control (VLA) significantly enhances robustness, generalization, and task management capabilities for long-horizon manipulation.
> - A visual trace, rendered as an actionable prompt, effectively translates abstract VLM plans into precise spatial conditioning signals for a VLA executor, simplifying complex long-horizon planning into manageable short-horizon problems.
> - Receding-horizon planning, continuously predicting the 'remaining plan' based on current observations and a compact language memory, enables implicit progress tracking and dynamic replanning without explicit failure detectors or brittle visual history.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has experienced notable progress in short-horizon tasks such as picking, placing, and pushing, largely propelled by advancements in large-scale imitation learning and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. However, a substantial challenge remains in extending these capabilities to long-horizon manipulation. Real-world tasks frequently involve multiple interdependent steps, require progress-dependent decision-making, and are susceptible to cumulative execution errors. Current approaches often attempt to address this by scaling monolithic VLA policies or embedding high-level planning directly within the same model responsible for action generation.

This monolithic strategy often encounters two primary limitations: fragility under compounding errors and limited modularity. Long sequences of actions can amplify minor errors, leading to policy failures as one-shot plans struggle to account for partial failures, occlusions, or dynamic object movements. Furthermore, tightly coupling planning and execution makes system modifications challenging; upgrading or swapping low-level VLA components (e.g., changing robot embodiments, action spaces, or training domains) typically necessitates a complete re-engineering of the entire system. This issue is particularly pronounced in embodied settings, where task complexity, environmental variability, and distribution shifts are common.

This paper proposes a departure from monolithic approaches by advocating for a modular, hierarchical framework. It aims to separate the concerns of high-level task management ("what remains to be done") from low-level control ("how to do the next short-term action"). This addresses the limitations of fragility and poor modularity by introducing a dedicated task-management Vision-Language Model (VLM) that operates above a VLA executor, thereby facilitating robust, adaptable, and generalizable long-horizon manipulation. This work aligns with efforts to integrate foundation models into robot planning, provide explicit intermediate guidance to policies, and enhance memory mechanisms for long-horizon tasks, but distinguishes itself by its specific decomposition and receding-horizon trace-conditioned execution strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to reliably execute complex, multi-step, long-horizon manipulation tasks by addressing the limitations of existing monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. The authors aim to enhance robustness, generalization, and task management capabilities in dynamic, real-world environments.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Bridging the gap between short-horizon successes and long-horizon requirements:** While VLA models excel at atomic actions, real-world tasks (e.g., "refill the kettle") involve intricate sequences of interdependent sub-tasks. Existing models struggle with the extended temporal consistency and error recovery demanded by such scenarios. The paper seeks to provide a structured approach to decompose and manage these long sequences.
- **Addressing fragility under drift and compounding errors:** Monolithic policies, which generate a complete plan upfront, are prone to failure. Small errors in early execution steps can accumulate and lead to significant deviations, as the initial plan cannot dynamically adapt to unexpected changes or partial failures in the environment. The motivation is to create a system that can implicitly track progress and replan in response to actual world states, rather than relying on brittle, pre-generated plans.
- **Improving modularity and reusability:** Tightly coupled planning and execution within a single policy limits flexibility. Changes to low-level control (e.g., a different robot arm, new action space, or training domain) often necessitate retraining or re-engineering the entire system. A key motivation is to decouple these components, allowing a high-level task manager to interface with various low-level VLA executors, thereby promoting reusability and easier upgrades.
- **Facilitating implicit progress tracking and failure recovery:** Hand-crafted recovery logic for various failure modes can be complex and extensive. The authors are motivated to develop a system that can automatically identify uncompleted subtasks and adjust its plan without explicit failure detectors or complex visual history buffers, leading to more robust operation.
- **Enhancing generalization to unseen objects and environments:** VLA models can overfit to the visual and semantic statistics of their training data. By externalizing high-level grounding and spatial planning through a visual trace, the aim is to enable the low-level executor to operate on novel objects or in new environments, provided the task manager can correctly identify and "point" to them.
- **Efficiently representing and utilizing history:** Feeding long visual histories to a model can increase latency and introduce distribution shift if real-world execution deviates from training demonstrations. The motivation is to maintain task progress through a compact, shift-resilient language memory, allowing the manager to condition on the current observation while still retaining necessary historical context.

In summary, the motivation is to create a robust, modular, and generalizable framework for long-horizon robotic manipulation that can reliably break down complex instructions, track progress, recover from errors, and adapt to new scenarios by intelligently decoupling high-level reasoning from low-level control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LoHo-Manip framework adopts a hierarchical decomposition approach, separating high-level task management from short-horizon control. This system consists of two main components: a task manager (Vision-Language Model, VLM) and an executor (Vision-Language-Action, VLA).

**System Overview:**
The core idea is that the task manager, given a high-level instruction and the current observation, predicts a "remaining plan" which includes both linguistic subtasks and a visual trace. The VLA executor then performs low-level actions, conditioned on this guidance. This interaction operates in a closed-loop, receding-horizon manner.

**Task Manager (VLM):**
The task manager is a VLM designed to reason about task progress. For a given instruction (x) and current observation (o_t), it produces two outputs:
1. **Subtask Description (s_t):** Natural language outlining the next short-horizon objective.
2. **Visual Trace (τ_t):** A sequence of 2D keypoints indicating the spatial trajectory or interaction target for the subtask. This trace is not merely for visualization but serves as an actionable conditioning signal for the executor.

A key design choice is that the manager is *generalist* and *executor-agnostic*, meaning it can interface with different VLA backbones without modification.

**Executor (VLA):**
The executor is a standard action policy (e.g., π0, GR00T, π0.5) that translates observations into robot actions. Modern VLA models are proficient at short-horizon instructions, making them suitable for following the subtasks and traces provided by the manager. The visual trace (τ_t) predicted by the manager is rendered into the observation input for the executor, providing a spatial conditioning signal that the VLA learns to follow through fine-tuning.

**Closed-Loop Execution and Receding-Horizon Management:**
Unlike systems that generate a complete plan at the beginning, LoHo-Manip operates in a receding-horizon manner. The task manager is invoked periodically (or at every step) to predict the *remaining plan* from the *current observation*. This yields an implicit closed-loop property: if a subtask fails, the world state reflects this, and the manager continues to include the uncompleted item in subsequent plans, often with an updated trace. This enables implicit progress tracking, replanning, and recovery without hand-crafted failure logic.

**Sub-Task and Trace Construction:**
- **Progress-aware plan representation:** For a given instruction and trajectory, the task is represented as a sequence of atomic interaction primitives. At each time step `t`, the manager predicts a progress-aware plan text that explicitly includes a "completed prefix" (`C*_t`) and a "remaining suffix" (`R*_t`). `C*_t` acts as a compact language memory, summarizing what has been done, while `R*_t` specifies the future plan.
- **Visual trace representation:** The end-effector's 2D pixel coordinates (`p_t`) are extracted from demonstration videos. The remaining trace label (`τ*_t`) is defined as the future portion of the end-effector trajectory, stored compactly (e.g., resampled waypoints) and rendered as a visual prompt.
- **Current-frame conditioning and Shift-resilient language memory:** The task manager is conditioned solely on the current frame (`o_t`) during both training and inference. History is maintained via the compact textual summary (`C_t-1`), which summarizes completed primitives. This prevents reliance on potentially noisy or distribution-shifted visual history frames during deployment.

**Training Paradigm:**
The training process decouples the manager from the executor:
1. **Training Data:** Two main sources are used:
 - **Real-robot demonstrations:** From the Bridge subset (Open X-Embodiment format), providing video trajectories for extracting atomic interaction primitives and end-effector traces. Off-the-shelf vision-language models are used for temporal segmentation and mapping segments to primitives, and end-effector localization provides pixel locations for traces.
 - **Auxiliary long-horizon reasoning/planning data:** RoboVQA and EgoPlan-BenchIT provide additional signals for instruction understanding and progress reasoning, enhancing the manager's generalization.
 - **Synthesized failure-recovery samples:** Augmented training data, created by replacing grasped objects with other items in grasp-and-place episodes, is used to improve the manager's robustness to execution errors.
2. **Task Manager Training:** Initialized from a pre-trained VLM (e.g., Qwen3-VL), its vision encoder is frozen, and the language model is fine-tuned to predict the progress-aware plan text (completed and remaining primitives) and the associated 2D trace. Conditioning is always on the current frame, with history provided via the textual summary.
3. **Executor Adaptation:** A base VLA architecture (e.g., π0.5) is initialized and fine-tuned to condition on the rendered trace prompt (and optionally the current subtask text). This enables the VLA to reliably track the manager-provided trajectory and execute short-horizon primitives.

**Inference Loop:**
During inference, a receding-horizon closed loop operates. At each step (or fixed interval), the manager takes the current observation and textual progress summary to predict an updated (completed, remaining) plan and trace. The executor then acts for a short horizon, guided by the trace. The textual memory is updated based on the latest manager output, ensuring efficiency and robustness by avoiding long visual histories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental validation across embodied planning, long-horizon reasoning, trajectory prediction, and end-to-end manipulation in both simulation and real-world settings. The main findings indicate that LoHo-Manip consistently achieves performance gains over existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Embodied Reasoning and Trajectory Prediction

- **Benchmarks:** RoboVQA (long-horizon reasoning), EgoPlan-Bench2 (human-level planning), ShareRobot-T and VABench-V (2D trajectory prediction).
- **Results:** LoHo-Manip consistently outperformed proprietary models (e.g., Gemini-3.0-Flash), general-purpose open-source VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL), and specialized embodied foundation models (e.g., ThinkAct).
 - On RoboVQA and EgoPlan-Bench2, LoHo-Manip-4B achieved BLEU scores of 63.1 and an average accuracy of 56.7% respectively, surpassing all compared models (Table 1).
 - For trajectory prediction (ShareRobot-T, VABench-V), LoHo-Manip-4B showed lower Discrete Fréchet Distance (DFD), Hausdorff Distance (HD), and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) values, indicating more accurate spatial trajectory generation (e.g., DFD 0.2309 on ShareRobot-T, RMSE 0.1469 on VABench-V) compared to baselines like Qwen3-VL-4B and MolmoAct-7B (Table 2).
- **Key takeaway:** The task manager demonstrated superior accuracy in both high-level semantic planning and low-level spatial trajectory generation, and exhibited generalization across diverse robotic setups and environments, including varying manipulators and object categories (Fig. 8).

#### 4.2. Embodied Agent Planning

- **Benchmark:** EmbodiedBench (EB-Alfred for household tasks, EB-Habitat for semantic navigation). This evaluates high-level planning by requiring atomic commands given egocentric observations.
- **Results:** LoHo-Manip-4B achieved leading performance across both EB-Alfred and EB-Habitat, particularly on complex configurations. For instance, on EB-Alfred, it scored an average of 0.38, compared to 0.24 for GPT-4o mini and 0.19 for Qwen3-VL-4B (Table 3).
- **Key takeaway:** The hierarchical task reasoning design enabled effective interpretation of long-horizon instructions and accurate sub-task plan generation.

#### 4.3. VLA in Simulation

- **Benchmarks:** LIBERO (foundational manipulation and atomic capabilities) and VLABench (long-horizon reasoning and generalization, implicit instructions, visual deviations).
- **Results:**
 - On LIBERO, LoHo-Manip achieved the highest average score of 97.5%, outperforming strong baselines like StarVLA (96.6%), MolmoAct (86.6%), and π0-fast (85.5%) across spatial, object, goal, and long-horizon tracks (Table 5).
 - On VLABench, LoHo-Manip consistently outperformed its base VLA (π0.5) across all tracks, including In-Distribution, Cross-Category, Common-Sense, Semantic-Instruction, and Unseen-Texture, achieving an average of 0.39 compared to π0.5's 0.24 (Table 4).
 - Analysis using Intention Score (IS) and Progress Score (PS) on VLABench further showed LoHo-Manip's superior performance in metrics requiring robust vision-language reasoning, especially in completing intermediate steps (Fig. 7, right).
- **Key takeaway:** The framework successfully bridged high-level reasoning with precise low-level control for complex manipulation tasks, even under significant visual shifts and distractor objects.

#### 4.4. VLA in Real-World

- **Setup:** Franka arm with top-view and wrist-view Intel RealSense cameras.
- **Evaluation:** Single-step and multi-step tasks, with a focus on Out-of-Distribution (OOD) scenarios (novel object categories, novel spatial arrangements, unseen language combinations).
- **Results:** LoHo-Manip significantly outperformed its base VLA (π0.5, fine-tuned on the same 100-sample dataset) in OOD settings. This indicates that the decoupled architecture enhanced the robot's ability to maintain a higher success rate when faced with novel visual and linguistic arrangements, where the baseline often failed to localize objects or correctly sequence sub-tasks (Fig. 7, left). Qualitative examples (Fig. 6, Fig. 10 in supplementary material) demonstrated successful execution of complex multi-step instructions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Sub-Task and Trace Curation:** The quality of the curated sub-task and trace data was shown to significantly improve the model’s trajectory prediction ability (Table 6).
- **Integration of Different VLA Executors:** The task manager successfully interfaced with an alternative VLA model, StarVLA, demonstrating the modularity and flexibility of the framework (Table 7).
- **Inference Efficiency:** The hierarchical execution scheme, with the task manager invoked at a lower frequency (e.g., every 100 executor steps), maintained efficient inference, with comparable overall latency to using the executor alone (Table 8).

Overall, the findings suggest that the modular, hierarchical design of LoHo-Manip, with its receding-horizon progress-aware planning and trace-conditioned execution, contributes to robust, generalizable, and accurate long-horizon manipulation across a range of benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LoHo-Manip framework presents a significant advancement in long-horizon robotic manipulation by addressing several core limitations of existing approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be understood across several dimensions:

- **Enhanced Robustness to Execution Errors:** The receding-horizon task management, which continuously predicts the "remaining plan" from the current observation, introduces an implicit closed-loop system. This enables automatic progress tracking and replanning without explicit, hand-crafted recovery logic or brittle visual history buffers. This inherent robustness is critical for deploying robots in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments where execution errors are inevitable, potentially leading to more reliable and autonomous robotic systems.
- **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** By decoupling high-level semantic planning (task manager) from low-level execution (VLA executor), the system exhibits stronger generalization. The task manager can provide abstract spatial guidance (visual traces) that allows the executor to operate on novel objects or in unseen environments, even if the executor has not encountered those specific visual characteristics during its own training. This enhances the practical applicability of robots to out-of-distribution scenarios and diverse tasks.
- **Increased Modularity and Reusability:** The framework's modular architecture separates the VLM-based task manager from the VLA executor. This means the high-level manager can be reused across different low-level robot policies, action spaces, or embodiments without requiring a complete system overhaul. This modularity reduces development complexity, facilitates easier upgrades, and allows for the integration of future advancements in either planning or control components independently.
- **Efficient and Stable Long-Horizon Reasoning:** The use of a compact, shift-resilient language memory to summarize completed actions, coupled with conditioning the task manager solely on the current visual frame, provides explicit progress context while avoiding the computational overhead and potential distribution shift associated with long visual histories. This design ensures that long-horizon decision-making remains efficient and stable.
- **Bridging High-Level Intent and Low-Level Control:** The introduction of the visual trace as an actionable prompt effectively translates abstract linguistic goals into concrete, spatially grounded instructions for the VLA executor. This mechanism simplifies the challenging problem of long-horizon planning into a sequence of more manageable short-horizon control problems, leveraging the strengths of modern VLAs in local control.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI and Foundation Models:** LoHo-Manip demonstrates a practical pathway for integrating large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for high-level reasoning with high-frequency robot control policies. This contributes to the broader field of embodied AI by showcasing how foundation models can be effectively leveraged to enhance complex robotic capabilities beyond basic manipulation.

In conclusion, LoHo-Manip's hierarchical and trace-conditioned approach offers a scalable and robust framework for long-horizon manipulation. Its impact lies in enabling robots to perform more complex, multi-step tasks reliably in varied environments, while also fostering greater modularity and generalization in robotic system design.
