# Long-Horizon Manipulation via Trace-Conditioned VLA Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The LoHo-Manip framework enables robots to reliably execute complex, multi-step, long-horizon manipulation tasks through a hierarchical approach that separates high-level task management from low-level control. This method, featuring receding-horizon planning and trace-conditioned execution, demonstrates superior performance on embodied reasoning and manipulation benchmarks in both simulation and real-world settings, notably achieving a 97.5% average score on LIBERO and significantly outperforming baselines on VLABench and real-world out-of-distribution tasks.

## Problem

- Long-horizon manipulation tasks are challenging for robots due to multiple interdependent steps, cumulative execution errors, and the need for progress-dependent decision-making.
- Existing monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies suffer from fragility under compounding errors and limited modularity, making it difficult to adapt to environmental changes or swap components.
- Current approaches struggle with implicit progress tracking, robust error recovery, and generalization to unseen objects or environments in complex, multi-step scenarios.

## Method

- **LoHo-Manip** is a modular hierarchical framework with a ==VLM task manager== and a ==VLA executor==.
- The VLM task manager, conditioned on the current observation, predicts a ==remaining plan== with linguistic subtasks and a ==spatial visual trace== in a ==receding-horizon== manner.
- The VLA executor performs short-horizon actions by ==conditioning on the visual trace==, translating abstract plans into spatially grounded instructions.

## Results

- LoHo-Manip achieved RoboVQA BLEU **63.1** and EgoPlan-Bench2 accuracy **56.7%**, leading on embodied reasoning and trajectory prediction.
- Outperformed strong VLA baselines: **97.5%** average on LIBERO; **0.39** vs **0.24** for base π0.5 on VLABench.
- Enhanced robustness and higher success rates in real-world out-of-distribution manipulation tasks vs fine-tuned base VLAs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level task management (VLM) from low-level control (VLA) significantly enhances robustness, generalization, and task management capabilities for long-horizon manipulation.
> - A visual trace, rendered as an actionable prompt, effectively translates abstract VLM plans into precise spatial conditioning signals for a VLA executor, simplifying complex long-horizon planning into manageable short-horizon problems.
> - Receding-horizon planning, continuously predicting the 'remaining plan' based on current observations and a compact language memory, enables implicit progress tracking and dynamic replanning without explicit failure detectors or brittle visual history.
