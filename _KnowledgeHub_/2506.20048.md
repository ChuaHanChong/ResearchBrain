# A Principled Path to Fitted Distributional Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> A principled framework for Fitted Distributional Evaluation (FDE) was developed, identifying functional Bregman divergences as theoretically sound discrepancy measures for distributional Off-Policy Evaluation. This approach consistently outperformed existing baselines like Fitted Likelihood Evaluation, QRDQN, and IQN on Linear Quadratic Regulators and Atari game benchmarks, exhibiting improved accuracy and stability.

## Problem

- Lack of a unified theoretical framework for designing Fitted Distributional Evaluation (FDE) methods, particularly regarding the choice of discrepancy functions for comparing distributions.
- Many existing distributional Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) methods lacked robust theoretical guarantees, especially in non-tabular (infinite state-action space) environments.
- Limitations in existing approaches, such as potential for biased estimates, incompatibility of quantile-based methods with multi-dimensional returns, and common assumptions of bounded distributions.

## Method

- Extended the ==Fitted Q-Evaluation== (FQE) algorithm to the distributional setting (FDE), using an iterative minimization procedure with a distributional Bellman backup.
- Proposed three guiding principles (P1: contraction-inducing metrics, P2: functional Bregman divergences as discrepancy measures, P3: quantifiable relationship between metric and divergence) to systematically construct theoretically grounded FDE methods.
- Developed a unified statistical convergence analysis for a broad class of FDE methods in both tabular and general (non-tabular) settings, using non-overlapping data partitions for rigorous analysis.

## Results

- FDE methods (e.g., PDF-L2, RBF, Energy, KL) consistently outperformed the baseline Fitted Likelihood Evaluation (FLE) on Linear Quadratic Regulator tasks.
- On Atari games, FDE methods, particularly KL and Energy FDE, demonstrated superior performance with lower mean inaccuracy and improved stability compared to FLE, QRDQN, and IQN.
- The Total Variation Distance, empirically shown not to improve accuracy across iterations in Atari, validated the theoretical prediction that it is not a functional Bregman divergence and thus unsuitable for effective FDE.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Functional Bregman divergences are uniquely suited as discrepancy measures for FDE, as the true distributional Bellman operator's output is their unique minimizer for the population objective function.
> - The research rigorously defined and analyzed two classes of contraction-inducing metrics (supremum-extended and expectation-extended) for the distributional Bellman operator, essential for convergence guarantees.
> - A systematic framework allows for deriving and validating a wide range of discrepancy measures for FDE, some of which were previously unexplored in distributional OPE, offering options for unbounded and multi-dimensional return distributions.
