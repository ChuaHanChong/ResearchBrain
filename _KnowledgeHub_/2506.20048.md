# A Principled Path to Fitted Distributional Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> A principled framework for Fitted Distributional Evaluation (FDE) was developed, identifying functional Bregman divergences as theoretically sound discrepancy measures for distributional Off-Policy Evaluation. This approach consistently outperformed existing baselines like Fitted Likelihood Evaluation, QRDQN, and IQN on Linear Quadratic Regulators and Atari game benchmarks, exhibiting improved accuracy and stability.

## Problem

- Lack of a unified theoretical framework for designing Fitted Distributional Evaluation (FDE) methods, particularly regarding the choice of discrepancy functions for comparing distributions.
- Many existing distributional Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) methods lacked robust theoretical guarantees, especially in non-tabular (infinite state-action space) environments.
- Limitations in existing approaches, such as potential for biased estimates, incompatibility of quantile-based methods with multi-dimensional returns, and common assumptions of bounded distributions.

## Method

- Extended the ==Fitted Q-Evaluation== (FQE) algorithm to the distributional setting (FDE), using an iterative minimization procedure with a distributional Bellman backup.
- Proposed three guiding principles (P1: contraction-inducing metrics, P2: functional Bregman divergences as discrepancy measures, P3: quantifiable relationship between metric and divergence) to systematically construct theoretically grounded FDE methods.
- Developed a unified statistical convergence analysis for a broad class of FDE methods in both tabular and general (non-tabular) settings, using non-overlapping data partitions for rigorous analysis.

## Results

- FDE methods (e.g., PDF-L2, RBF, Energy, KL) consistently outperformed the baseline Fitted Likelihood Evaluation (FLE) on Linear Quadratic Regulator tasks.
- On Atari games, FDE methods, particularly KL and Energy FDE, demonstrated superior performance with lower mean inaccuracy and improved stability compared to FLE, QRDQN, and IQN.
- The Total Variation Distance, empirically shown not to improve accuracy across iterations in Atari, validated the theoretical prediction that it is not a functional Bregman divergence and thus unsuitable for effective FDE.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Functional Bregman divergences are uniquely suited as discrepancy measures for FDE, as the true distributional Bellman operator's output is their unique minimizer for the population objective function.
> - The research rigorously defined and analyzed two classes of contraction-inducing metrics (supremum-extended and expectation-extended) for the distributional Bellman operator, essential for convergence guarantees.
> - A systematic framework allows for deriving and validating a wide range of discrepancy measures for FDE, some of which were previously unexplored in distributional OPE, offering options for unbounded and multi-dimensional return distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) traditionally focuses on estimating the *expected* return of a policy. However, distributional RL, an increasingly recognized subfield, aims to model the *full distribution* of returns. This provides a more comprehensive understanding of policy behavior, enabling the assessment of properties beyond the mean, such as risk or multimodality. Applications of distributional RL include risk assessment and risk-sensitive policy learning across various domains.

Within distributional RL, Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) is a critical problem. Distributional OPE seeks to estimate the return distribution of a target policy using offline data collected under a different behavior policy. Many existing distributional OPE methods extend techniques from traditional RL. These include temporal difference (TD) methods and model-based approaches. Fitted Q-evaluation (FQE), an iterative algorithm well-established in traditional RL for estimating Q-functions, has also seen extensions to the distributional setting.

This work specifically addresses a gap in the development of these distributional FQE extensions, which the authors term Fitted Distributional Evaluation (FDE). While previous distributional FQE extensions exist (e.g., using p-powered Wasserstein-p metric or log-likelihood), they are often based on specific discrepancy functions and lack a unified theoretical framework for their design. This absence leaves practitioners without systematic guidance for constructing FDE methods with robust theoretical guarantees, particularly concerning the appropriate choice of discrepancy function when dealing with distributions rather than real numbers. The paper aims to establish such a unified framework, moving beyond ad-hoc choices.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. **Develop a Principled Framework for Fitted Distributional Evaluation (FDE):** The central motivation is the lack of a unified theoretical framework for designing FDE methods. Unlike traditional FQE, where the squared L2-loss is a standard choice for scalar value comparison, distributional FQE operates on entire distributions, making the selection of a discrepancy function non-trivial. The goal is to provide guiding principles for constructing theoretically grounded FDE methods.
2. **Identify Valid Discrepancy Measures:** To address the non-trivial choice of discrepancy functions, the research aims to identify a class of functions that are appropriate for FDE, particularly functional Bregman divergences. This involves systematically deriving and surveying various discrepancy measures, including novel ones not previously considered in distributional OPE.
3. **Provide Unified Statistical Convergence Analysis:** A key motivation is to offer comprehensive theoretical guarantees for a broad class of FDE methods. This includes convergence analysis in both tabular (finite state-action space) and non-tabular (infinite state-action space) environments, addressing a limitation where theoretical support for many distributional OPE methods in non-tabular scenarios is limited.
4. **Address Limitations of Existing Approaches:** The work is motivated to overcome specific challenges faced by existing distributional OPE methods, such as:
 - Lack of a general guideline for discrepancy function selection.
 - Potential for biased estimates in non-tabular settings in some existing FQE extensions.
 - Incompatibility of widely used quantile-based methods with multi-dimensional returns.
 - Conventional assumptions of bounded distributions, with a goal to provide guarantees for unbounded distributions.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to move from ad-hoc extensions of FQE to a systematically designed and theoretically justified family of FDE methods, offering a clearer path for future development and application in complex RL settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology involves extending the Fitted Q-Evaluation (FQE) algorithm, typically used for expectation-based RL, to the distributional setting, resulting in Fitted Distributional Evaluation (FDE). This extension necessitates an iterative minimization procedure where, at each step, a candidate return distribution is updated by minimizing a discrepancy measure between its current estimate for a state-action pair and a "distributional Bellman backup" derived from offline data.

The central challenge addressed is the choice of an appropriate discrepancy measure, denoted as 'd', for comparing distributions. The authors propose a set of three guiding principles (P1, P2, P3) to construct theoretically grounded FDE methods:

- **P1: Contraction-Inducing Metric (˜η):** The chosen evaluation metric, ˜η, must ensure that the distributional Bellman operator (Tπ) is a contraction mapping. This property is fundamental for guaranteeing convergence in iterative algorithms like FDE. The paper identifies two classes of such metrics:
 - **Supremum-extended metrics (η∞):** These are defined as the supremum of a base probability metric (η) over all state-action pairs. They are well-studied and contractive if the base metric satisfies c-sensitivity, location-insensitivity, and q-convexity. However, controlling these metrics can be challenging in large or continuous state-action spaces.
 - **Expectation-extended metrics (¯ηdπ,q):** These involve an expectation of the base probability metric (η) raised to a power, weighted by a stationary distribution (dπ). They are introduced as a more compatible choice for expectation-based objective functions and are shown to be contraction-inducing under specific conditions on η.

- **P2: Functional Bregman Divergences as Discrepancy Measures (d):** The discrepancy function 'd' used in the iterative minimization must be chosen such that the true distributional Bellman operator's output (Tπ˜Υ) is the unique minimizer of the population objective function (E[d(Υ(S,A), Ψπ(R,S', ˜Υ))]). The authors prove that functional Bregman divergences naturally satisfy this property. This class of divergences is broad and encompasses various statistical distances, including those derived from strictly proper scoring rules (e.g., Kullback–Leibler divergence, Maximum Mean Discrepancy with specific kernels).

- **P3: Relationship between ˜η and d:** There must be a quantifiable relationship where a small value of the objective function (measured by 'd') implies closeness between the current estimate and the true Bellman target with respect to the contraction-inducing metric (˜η). This principle ensures that minimizing 'd' effectively reduces the error measured by '˜η'.

For theoretical analysis, the authors modify the FDE objective to use non-overlapping data partitions across iterations, ensuring independent samples at each step. This allows for a more rigorous statistical convergence analysis.

**Specific approaches to deriving theoretical guarantees:**
- **Tabular Setting (finite state-action space):** For divergences that can be expressed as squared metrics (IPS metrics), the authors provide near-minimax optimal convergence rates using techniques from M-estimation theory and McDiarmid’s inequality, extending previous results to a wider range of metrics.
- **General (possibly non-tabular) Setting:** For more general functional Bregman divergences, the analysis relies on empirical process theory. Two main theorems are developed:
 - **Theorem C.5 (general divergence with norm-space association):** This applies when the discrepancy 'd' is associated with a normed space and satisfies certain complexity conditions (entropy conditions).
 - **Theorem C.11 (general divergence with metric-space association):** This is a broader theorem for cases where the specific conditions of Theorem C.5 are not met, potentially yielding slower but still guaranteed convergence rates.
 These analyses incorporate assumptions about data distribution coverage and model class complexity.

**Experimental Approach:** The methodology is validated through extensive experiments on:
- **Linear Quadratic Regulators (LQR):** A parametric setting, comparing FDE methods (Energy, Laplace, RBF, PDF-L2, KL) with Fitted Likelihood Evaluation (FLE).
- **Atari Games:** A common benchmark for distributional RL, comparing FDE methods (KL, Energy, PDF-L2, RBF) with baselines (FLE, QRDQN, IQN) and a non-functional Bregman divergence (Total Variation Distance - TVD). Experiments vary parameters such as number of mixtures/quantiles, environments (deterministic/random reward), behavior policies (strong/weak coverage), and sample sizes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings across theoretical development and empirical validation:

#### 4.1. Theoretical Contributions

1. **Principled Framework for FDE:** The paper successfully establishes a framework for constructing FDE methods based on three guiding principles (P1, P2, P3). This framework clarifies the role of discrepancy function selection, indicating that **functional Bregman divergences are natural candidates** for 'd' due to their property of minimizing the expected discrepancy at the true Bellman operator's output (Theorem 2.4).
2. **Identification of Contraction-Inducing Metrics:** The work rigorously defines and analyzes two classes of contraction-inducing metrics for distributional Bellman operators: supremum-extended (η∞) and expectation-extended (¯ηdπ,q). This expands the theoretical toolkit for analyzing convergence in distributional RL.
3. **Survey of Valid Discrepancy Measures:** Leveraging the framework, the authors derive and validate a range of discrepancy measures (summarized in Tables 1 and 2), including novel ones not previously explored in distributional OPE literature (e.g., specific MMD kernels). This provides a systematic collection of theoretically sound options for FDE.
4. **Unified Statistical Convergence Analysis:**
 - **Tabular Setting (Theorem 3.3, Table 1):** For FDE methods using squared IPS (Inner-Product-Space) metrics as divergences, the paper provides a comprehensive convergence analysis in tabular environments. For certain metrics (e.g., L2-Cramér, MMD-Coulomb for specific p), the achieved convergence rates are near-minimax optimal for bounded distributions.
 - **General/Non-Tabular Setting (Theorem 3.4/C.5, C.11, Table 2):** The research extends theoretical guarantees to a broad class of FDE methods in more general state-action spaces. These theorems accommodate various functional Bregman divergences (e.g., Cramér (L2-CDF), Energy MMD, PDF-L2, Matern MMD, Coulomb MMD, KL divergence). This significantly expands the set of distributional OPE methods with theoretical guarantees in non-tabular scenarios.
 - **Addressing Limitations:** The theoretical framework demonstrates the capability of certain FDE methods to handle:
 - **Unbounded return distributions:** In contrast to previous works that often assume boundedness.
 - **Distributions without densities:** Allowing methods like Energy MMD to be applied where density-based approaches would fail.
 - **Multi-dimensional returns:** Which are incompatible with widely used quantile-based methods.
5. **Improved Convergence Rates:** For instance, Cramér FDE is shown to achieve faster convergence rates compared to the existing Fitted Likelihood Evaluation (FLE) method, uniformly across various Wasserstein metric degrees and model complexities (Corollary B.1, Appendix C.17.1).

#### 4.2. Experimental Results

1. **Superior Performance on LQR:** In simulations on Linear Quadratic Regulators, the proposed FDE methods (PDF-L2, RBF, Matern, Energy, KL) consistently outperformed the baseline FLE. This is attributed to FLE's susceptibility to Monte Carlo errors due to its reliance on sampling, whereas FDE methods (in this setting) can leverage closed-form solutions for Gaussian distributions.
2. **Strong Performance on Atari Games:** Across a wide range of Atari games and experimental settings (varying mixtures/quantiles, environments, behavior policies, sample sizes), the FDE methods (particularly KL and Energy FDE) demonstrated superior performance.
 - **Outperformance of Baselines:** FDE methods generally exhibited lower mean inaccuracy and improved stability (lower variance) compared to baseline methods like FLE, QRDQN, and IQN.
 - **Failure of Total Variation Distance (TVD):** As predicted by the theoretical framework, TVD, which is not a functional Bregman divergence, showed no improvement in accuracy through iterations in Atari simulations. This empirical result strongly supports the theoretical claim regarding the necessity of using functional Bregman divergences for effective FDE.
 - **Effectiveness of Density Modeling:** The experiments suggest that density modeling (used by FDE and FLE) can improve accuracy even in deterministic reward environments, compared to quantile-based methods (QRDQN, IQN), given the same model capacity (number of mixtures/quantiles).
 - **Practical Validation of Other Divergences:** Even functional Bregman divergences not fully analyzed in the main theoretical section (e.g., Hyvärinen divergence) showed good practical performance, further supporting the general utility of the proposed framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes a significant step towards a more rigorous and principled approach to distributional off-policy evaluation in reinforcement learning.

1. **Unified Foundation for FDE:** By introducing a set of guiding principles and rigorously linking FDE to functional Bregman divergences, the paper provides a unified theoretical foundation for designing and analyzing a broad class of FDE methods. This moves the field beyond ad-hoc choices of discrepancy functions, offering systematic guidance for future algorithm development.
2. **Expansion of Theoretically Grounded Methods:** The work substantially expands the catalog of distributional OPE methods with statistical convergence guarantees, especially in complex non-tabular settings and for conditions such as multi-dimensional and unbounded returns. This addresses long-standing limitations in theoretical support for such methods.
3. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** The demonstrated empirical superiority of the proposed FDE methods over existing baselines in both controlled (LQR) and complex (Atari) environments suggests practical advantages in terms of accuracy and stability. This could lead to more reliable policy evaluation in real-world applications where risk and distributional properties are crucial.
4. **Guidance for Practitioners:** The established principles and the survey of valid discrepancy measures offer concrete guidance for practitioners to select or design appropriate FDE methods tailored to specific problem characteristics, data properties, and desired performance metrics. For instance, the empirical failure of TVD highlights the importance of theoretical considerations in discrepancy function choice.
5. **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies several avenues for future work, including investigating the necessity of functional Bregman divergences, extending analyses to scenarios with data reuse, and relaxing the completeness assumption. These discussions suggest that the proposed framework is a fertile ground for further theoretical and algorithmic advancements in distributional RL.

Overall, this work provides a robust theoretical backbone and empirical validation for a new generation of Fitted Distributional Evaluation methods, thereby strengthening the foundation of distributional reinforcement learning and expanding its applicability to more complex and safety-critical domains.
