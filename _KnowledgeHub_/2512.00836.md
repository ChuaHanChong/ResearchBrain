# Assessing model error in counterfactual worlds

> [!summary] Summary
> Research from Princeton University and the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill presents a framework to assess model error in unobserved counterfactual scenarios, differentiating between scenario deviation and model miscalibration. The paper introduces three approaches, with simulation experiments demonstrating that statistically inferring error distributions or estimating counterfactual observations provides more accurate and less biased error assessments than direct comparisons.

## Problem

- Retrospective evaluation of counterfactual scenario projections is challenging because the assumed conditions for these scenarios are rarely observed in reality.
- Differences between a model's counterfactual projection and observed reality can stem from both the real world not matching the scenario (scenario deviation) and the model being inaccurate for that scenario (model miscalibration).
- A lack of robust methods for systematically evaluating scenario projections hinders model improvement and reduces trust among end-users.

## Method

- A conceptual framework was established to clearly separate ==scenario deviation error== from ==model miscalibration error==.
- Three distinct approaches were proposed to estimate model miscalibration error in unobserved ==counterfactual worlds==: evaluating only plausible scenarios, inferring the error distribution directly, and estimating observations in modeled scenarios.
- A simulation experiment using an ==SIR epidemiological model== was conducted to test the performance, benefits, and limitations of each proposed evaluation approach under controlled conditions where true counterfactual outcomes were known.

## Results

- The 'evaluate only plausible scenarios' approach (Approach 1) consistently showed systematic bias, often misattributing errors or appearing accurate due to canceling effects between scenario deviation and model miscalibration.
- Approaches 2 and 3, which involve statistical modeling of errors or observations, yielded more accurate mean error estimates and captured the overall error distribution better than Approach 1.
- Approach 2 (inferring error distribution directly) slightly outperformed Approach 3 (estimating observations in modeled scenarios) in capturing the full error distribution across various model-scenario comparisons, and both benefited significantly from the inclusion of covariates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically distinguishing between scenario deviation and model miscalibration is fundamental for a meaningful evaluation of scenario projections, as these error sources can confound or even cancel each other out.
> - Directly comparing model projections to observed realities, as in the 'evaluate only plausible scenarios' approach, leads to biased and misleading error estimates because it fails to adequately separate scenario deviation from model miscalibration.
> - Approaches that statistically infer error distributions (Approach 2) or estimate counterfactual observations (Approach 3) provide more accurate assessments of intrinsic model miscalibration, especially when incorporating relevant covariates.
