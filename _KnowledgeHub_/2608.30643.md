# Temporal Forcing: 4D Representation Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Temporal Forcing is a training-time 4D representation alignment method for vision-language-action models. A lightweight history pathway compresses past frames into gist tokens injected via zero-init gated cross-attention, and its latent representations are aligned with causal geometric features from a pretrained streaming 4D foundation model. It reaches 98.8% on LIBERO and lifts real-robot full-task success from 20.0% to 43.3%, adding no foundation model at inference.

## Problem

- Framewise 3D geometric alignment supervises only current-observation geometry, so it cannot represent object-state transitions or how a scene evolved over time.
- Visually similar states alias: once an object is hidden, the current frame cannot disambiguate task progress needed for long-horizon multi-stage decisions.
- History-augmented policies extend what a model observes or stores, but never supervise whether its latent representations actually preserve temporal information.

## Method

- ==History pathway==: frozen ==DINOv2== plus ==Q-Former== compress K=7 past frames into ==gist tokens==, a ==causal transformer== yields 16 history tokens, injected by ==zero-init gated cross-attention==.
- ==Temporal alignment loss== combines state, change (weighted 4x), and readout cosine terms matching history latents to pooled causal geometric features from pretrained ==StreamVGGT==.
- ==Current-frame alignment== matches backbone image-token features to context-conditioned dense geometric targets; the 4D model and all alignment heads are training-only, removed at inference.

## Results

- LIBERO average **98.8%** versus **96.6%** for the same base model, largest gain on the Long suite (**93.8** to **97.2**); RoboTwin 2.0 mean rises **53.5%** to **62.8%**.
- Ablations: history pathway alone drops **2.5** points below base, adding it without temporal alignment scores **83.7** versus **93.6** with it; 3D targets lag 4D by **8.6** points.
- Real robot: full sequential task **13/30** versus **6/30** for base, while isolated stages stay comparable (**78/90** versus **75/90**); LIBERO-Plus pooled **75.0** to **77.8**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feeding history to a policy is not enough; without direct supervision the injection gate stays near zero and the pathway remains an inert, noisy channel.
> - Supervision targets must be temporally contextualized: aligning to framewise geometry while carrying a history pathway is actively worse than no pathway.
> - Aligning representations at training time buys temporal reasoning without deployment cost, since the teacher foundation model and heads are discarded at inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which translate language instructions and visual observations into robot actions. A recent direction in VLA research involves improving manipulation performance by aligning model representations with geometric features derived from pretrained 3D models. Methods such as Spatial Forcing, GLaD, and ROCKET exemplify this approach by using framewise 3D geometry to supervise latent representations within VLA models. These methods typically derive geometric supervision from the current observation.

However, a recognized limitation of these 3D-aligned methods is their struggle with long-horizon manipulation tasks and observation aliasing. This occurs when visually similar states can represent different stages of a task, or when crucial information about past object states or task progression is no longer visible in the current frame. For instance, if an object is placed inside an opaque container, its prior state transition becomes unobservable, leading to ambiguity for the robot. This limitation stems from the fact that 3D scene geometry, in these approaches, captures only the current state and lacks information about how the environment has evolved over time.

Other lines of work aim to augment VLA models with historical context. Some methods explicitly feed observation history into VLA models, using techniques such as recurrent modules, retrieval memory banks, or aggregating 3D/4D history at the input side. Another approach involves world-model-based VLA methods that introduce temporal supervision through future prediction or generation. While these methods extend the scope of what the model observes or stores, they do not consistently supervise whether the latent representations within the VLA model effectively encode temporal information.

Temporal Forcing addresses the limitations of framewise 3D geometric alignment by extending the training-time representation alignment paradigm from static 3D geometry to dynamic 4D representations. It integrates a lightweight history pathway with 4D representation alignment, distinguishing itself from prior work that primarily focuses on extending observation windows or explicit future prediction. This work aims to enable VLA models to capture the evolving 3D world through temporally consistent geometric representations, thereby improving their understanding of dynamic environments and performance in complex, multi-stage tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to identify and address specific limitations of existing VLA models, particularly those that rely on framewise 3D geometric alignment for representation learning. The motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Framewise 3D Geometric Alignment:** The paper identifies two main limitations of current VLA models that use 3D geometric alignment:
 - **Inability to represent object-state transitions:** When an object's state changes (e.g., being moved or hidden), and this change is no longer visible in the current observation, existing models struggle to account for it. The current 3D scene geometry only describes the present, not the past.
 - **Difficulty in disambiguating task progress:** In long-horizon, multi-stage tasks, the completion of earlier stages might not be evident from the current visual input. This leads to observation aliasing, where visually similar states can correspond to different points in the task sequence, preventing the model from tracking its overall progress.

2. **Improving Performance on Long-Horizon Manipulation Tasks:** These limitations directly impact the ability of VLA models to succeed in complex tasks that require sequential steps, memory of past actions, or reasoning about occluded objects. The paper highlights that decisions in such tasks, such as resolving "the other" of two identical blocks after one is hidden, or knowing when to close a drawer after multiple placements, necessitate an understanding of how the scene has evolved over time.

3. **Equipping VLA Models with Temporally Aware Latent Representations:** To overcome the aforementioned challenges, the central objective is to integrate temporal awareness into the latent representations of VLA models. This involves moving beyond static 3D understanding to a dynamic 4D understanding, where the model's internal representations reflect the temporal evolution of the environment.

4. **Developing a Training-Time Representation Alignment Method for Temporal Information:** The motivation is to achieve this temporal awareness without increasing the complexity or latency of the VLA model at inference time. The authors propose a training-only alignment mechanism that guides the VLA model to learn temporally consistent representations, but is then removed for deployment, retaining only a lightweight history pathway.

5. **Leveraging Pretrained 4D Foundation Models:** The research is motivated by the availability of pretrained 4D foundation models, such as StreamVGGT, which are capable of processing observation streams causally and producing temporally consistent geometric representations of an evolving 3D world. The objective is to harness these external models to provide rich, dynamic supervision for VLA model training.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to enhance the robustness and capabilities of VLA models in dynamic and long-horizon robotic manipulation scenarios, where a pure current-frame visual understanding is insufficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Temporal Forcing is presented as a 4D representation alignment method designed to equip vanilla VLA models with temporally aware latent representations. The approach integrates a history pathway with two distinct alignment objectives supervised by a pretrained 4D foundation model during training.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation and Base VLA Model

The base VLA model observes images from a set of cameras and a language instruction, then predicts a chunk of future actions. The model utilizes a Qwen3-VL-4B vision-language backbone for input tokenization and an MLP head for action regression. It is primarily trained with an L1 action loss. Existing alignment-based methods augment this with a framewise alignment loss, matching backbone features to 3D geometric features from the current observation. Temporal Forcing builds upon this architecture.

#### 3.2. 4D Representation Acquisition

To provide temporal supervision, the method uses the pretrained StreamVGGT model to acquire 4D representations offline from a long causal context of each training trajectory. StreamVGGT reconstructs observations in a shared reference frame, generating intermediate features that represent each frame as part of a temporally consistent scene history. For each anchor timestep:
- **Causal Geometric Features:** These are pooled from StreamVGGT for various history offsets and cameras. They form the targets for Temporal Alignment.
- **Dense Geometric Features:** These represent the current frame's geometry, but are contextualized by the preceding temporal information processed by StreamVGGT. They form the targets for Current-Frame Alignment.
All extracted 4D features are used as stop-gradient targets, meaning they provide supervision without receiving gradients themselves. The 4D foundation model and its feature extraction process are exclusively used during training.

#### 3.3. History Pathway

The base VLA model is modified with a history pathway to process past observations, as it natively accepts only current frames.
- **History Window and Gist Tokens:** The pathway samples K strictly past frames per camera at uniform, decreasing offsets (oldest to most recent). A frozen DINOv2 encoder and a Q-Former compress each past frame into two gist tokens. Each gist token is augmented with time and camera embeddings. The current frame is excluded from this pathway.
- **Temporal Summary and Gated Injection:** A causal transformer processes the sequence of gist tokens, ensuring that the representation of each timestep depends only on earlier timesteps. This transformer produces M (e.g., 16) learned output queries, forming the "history tokens" that serve as a fixed-budget summary. These history tokens are then injected into the input of the VLA model's first decoder layer via a single gated cross-attention block. The gate is initialized to zero, making the block an identity at initialization, allowing the model to initially reproduce the base model's outputs.

#### 3.4. 4D Representation Alignment Objectives

The training objective supervises both the history latent representations (from the history pathway) and the current-frame representation.
- **Temporal Alignment (L_temp):** This loss guides the history pathway to encode temporal information by aligning the history latent representations with the Causal Geometric Features. It consists of three terms:
 - **State Term (L_state):** Matches the mean of camera-c gist tokens (after the temporal transformer and projection) to the normalized Causal Geometric Features at each offset.
 - **Change Term (L_change):** Matches the differences between adjacent projected gist tokens to the differences between adjacent Causal Geometric Features. This term specifically encourages the history pathway to capture temporal variations and disregard static content. Pairs with near-zero target differences are masked.
 - **Readout Term (L_read):** Ensures the mean of the history tokens can reproduce the Causal Geometric Feature of the most recent history timestep, providing a gradient path for the output queries.
 The combined temporal loss is a weighted sum of these three terms: L_temp = (1/6) * (L_state + 4 * L_change + L_read).

- **Current-Frame Alignment (L_cur):** This loss anchors the VLA model's current-frame understanding to contextually rich 4D geometry. Backbone features from an intermediate VLA layer, corresponding to current-frame image tokens, are projected and matched to the Dense Geometric Features. These targets are extracted by the 4D model after processing the preceding context, providing temporally contextualized geometry.

#### 3.5. Full Objective and Inference

The total training objective is a weighted sum of the action loss, current-frame alignment loss, and temporal alignment loss: L = L_act + λ_cur L_cur + λ_temp L_temp, with λ_cur = λ_temp = 0.5.
At inference time, the pretrained 4D foundation model, both alignment losses, and their projection heads are removed. Only the history pathway and its gated cross-attention block remain added to the base VLA model. The backbone sequence length is unchanged, and per-frame features from the history pathway are cached to maintain low latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from evaluations on simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0), controlled ablation studies, and real-robot experiments.

#### 4.1. Performance on LIBERO Benchmark

- Temporal Forcing achieved an average success rate of **98.8%** across the four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), representing a **2.2-point improvement** over its base model (StarVLA-OFT), which scored 96.6%.
- The most substantial gain was observed on the **Long suite**, where Temporal Forcing improved from 93.8% to **97.2%**. This suite contains multi-stage tasks that are highly dependent on historical information, aligning with the method's design.
- Performance on other suites was also high: 99.6% on Spatial (vs. 97.8%), 99.8% on Object (vs. 98.6%), and 98.4% on Goal (vs. 96.2%).

#### 4.2. Performance on RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark

- On twelve bimanual RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, Temporal Forcing increased the mean success rate from 53.5% to **62.8%**.
- Improvements were observed on **nine of the twelve tasks**.
- Notable gains occurred on handover tasks (e.g., "handover block" from 0% to 44%, "handover mic" from 39% to 79%). These tasks involve objects being repeatedly occluded during transfer, indicating the benefit of temporal awareness.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Compute-Constrained Protocol)

The ablation studies clarified the contribution of individual components:
- **History Pathway Alone (without alignment):** Adding only the history pathway resulted in a performance drop (88.2% avg success vs. 90.7% for base model). The injection gate remained largely closed, indicating the pathway was inert without direct supervision.
- **4D Targets Alone (Current-Frame Alignment only):** Using only current-frame alignment with 4D targets improved precision suites but caused an 8.0-point drop on the Long suite, suggesting that the model could not recover the temporal context encoded in the targets from a single frame.
- **Combining History Pathway and Current-Frame 4D Alignment (without Temporal Alignment):** This configuration yielded the lowest performance (83.7% avg success), indicating that an unsupervised history pathway, even with context-rich 4D targets for current-frame alignment, is detrimental. The history pathway failed to effectively encode useful information.
- **Temporal Alignment's Role:** The addition of temporal alignment (making the full Temporal Forcing) significantly increased performance from 83.7% to **93.6%**. This 9.9-point gain over the previous configuration demonstrated that temporal alignment activated the history pathway and made its representations useful.
- **Complementary Alignments:** Removing either L_temp or L_cur from the full Temporal Forcing configuration was detrimental. Dropping L_temp cost 9.9 points, while dropping L_cur cost 2.8 points. This indicates that both current-frame and temporal alignments contribute synergistically to the overall performance.
- **Temporally Consistent 4D Targets Necessity:** Replacing the 4D targets with framewise 3D targets (even with the history pathway and both alignment losses) resulted in lower performance (85.0% avg success vs. 93.6% for Temporal Forcing). This highlights that the temporally consistent nature of the 4D targets is crucial for supervising the history representations and achieving performance gains, particularly in long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Experiments (Hidden-Placement Task)

A physical multi-stage task (place one block in an opaque box, the other in a drawer, then close the drawer) was used:
- **Isolated Skills:** On individual stages evaluated in isolation, Temporal Forcing and its base model showed comparable success rates (78/90 vs. 75/90 total trials, respectively). This suggests that the improvement is not solely due to better fundamental manipulation skills.
- **Sequential Task:** In the full sequential task, Temporal Forcing significantly improved cumulative task completion. It raised full-task success from 20.0% (6/30 trials) for the base model to **43.3%** (13/30 trials). Success for the second placement stage (after the first block is hidden) also notably increased from 12/30 to 20/30 trials.
- These results indicate that Temporal Forcing enables the model to effectively track task progress and infer necessary actions even when critical information is no longer visually present, a direct consequence of its temporal awareness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Temporal Forcing: 4D Representation Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models" contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing a fundamental limitation of current VLA models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in VLA Capabilities:** By recognizing and directly tackling the issues of long-horizon manipulation, observation aliasing, and the inability to represent object-state transitions or task progress from current visual frames, Temporal Forcing pushes the boundaries of VLA model applicability. This is particularly relevant for complex, multi-stage tasks in dynamic environments.

2. **Introduction of a Novel 4D Representation Alignment Paradigm:** The paper introduces a method that effectively supervises VLA models with temporally consistent geometric features extracted from a pretrained 4D foundation model. This moves beyond static 3D alignment, providing a framework for teaching VLA models to understand the evolution of a scene over time.

3. **Enhanced Task Success in Complex Scenarios:** The demonstrated improvements on LIBERO (especially the Long suite), RoboTwin 2.0 (particularly handover tasks), and the physical hidden-placement task underscore the practical benefits of the proposed approach. Doubling the full-task success rate on a real-robot, multi-stage task is a notable achievement, indicating a more robust and capable robotic agent.

4. **Efficient Integration without Inference Overhead:** A key impact is that Temporal Forcing achieves its gains through a training-only alignment mechanism. The pretrained 4D foundation model and alignment heads are discarded at inference, meaning the deployed VLA model retains a minimal, lightweight history pathway without introducing significant computational overhead or increasing latency during real-time control. This makes the method practical for real-world robotic applications.

5. **Insights into Latent Representation Learning:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the learning process, demonstrating that merely providing historical observations is insufficient. The explicit temporal alignment, particularly the "change term" in the loss function, is shown to be crucial for making the history pathway encode useful, trajectory-specific temporal information. This highlights the importance of targeted supervision in learning abstract representations.

6. **Potential for Broader Applications:** The principles of 4D representation alignment could extend beyond manipulation to other robot learning tasks that require temporal reasoning, such as navigation in dynamic environments, human-robot interaction involving sequential cues, or even higher-level planning. The reliance on pretrained foundation models also suggests a pathway for leveraging increasingly sophisticated spatio-temporal representations developed in general computer vision research.

In summary, Temporal Forcing represents an advance in VLA model design, enabling robots to reason more effectively about dynamic environments and long-horizon tasks by explicitly incorporating temporal awareness into their internal representations. Its design, which separates complex supervision during training from efficient deployment, offers a pathway for developing more intelligent and capable robotic systems.
