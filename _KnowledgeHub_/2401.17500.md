# LeTO: Learning Constrained Visuomotor Policy with Differentiable Trajectory Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> LeTO integrates differentiable trajectory optimization into visuomotor imitation learning, enabling policies to intrinsically generate safe, constraint-satisfying, and smooth robotic movements. The framework achieves high success rates while significantly reducing accelerations and contact forces compared to existing methods across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor imitation learning methods often produce uncertain actions and lack explicit safety guarantees, leading to system instability and reduced robustness in robotic applications.
- Current imitation learning approaches frequently overlook critical requirements for trajectory smoothness and adherence to physical safety constraints (e.g., position, velocity, acceleration limits).
- The 'black box' nature of neural network policies in robot learning hinders interpretability and makes it difficult to intrinsically incorporate and enforce explicit physical constraints.

## Method

- **LeTO** integrates a ==differentiable trajectory optimization (DTO)== layer directly into the neural network architecture, enabling end-to-end learning of constrained actions.
- The DTO layer formulates action generation as a ==Quadratic Program (QP)== that optimizes for an action sequence while explicitly enforcing position, velocity, and acceleration constraints, alongside promoting trajectory smoothness.
- An ==LSTM-based observation encoder== maps raw visual and robot state inputs to a latent embedding, which then serves as a ==linear cost term== for the DTO layer, blending data-driven representation with model-based optimization.

## Results

- LeTO achieved success rates comparable to DiffusionPolicy (e.g., **100%** in 'Can' tasks) in simulations while significantly reducing maximum linear acceleration (e.g., **2 m/s²** for LeTO vs. **15.5 m/s²** for LSTM-GMM).
- In real-world tasks, LeTO demonstrated improved robustness to out-of-distribution observations and higher success rates (e.g., **13/20** for 'Move-the-stack') with significantly reduced peak acceleration and contact forces (e.g., **16.1 N** peak contact force vs. **27.3 N** for DiffusionPolicy).
- An ablation study confirmed that tighter acceleration constraints directly lead to smoother trajectories and LeTO maintains task success, showcasing its direct control over output characteristics and interpretability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating differentiable optimization into imitation learning creates a 'gray box' model that combines neural network's representational power with optimization's safety, interpretability, and constraint guarantees.
> - Embedding explicit physical constraints directly within the policy's learning process intrinsically generates safe and smoother trajectories, outperforming post-hoc clipping or external safety modules.
> - The framework offers interpretability and controllability, as output trajectory characteristics (e.g., smoothness) directly correlate with specified constraint values, a feature absent in purely black-box neural networks.
