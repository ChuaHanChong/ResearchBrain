# Self-Supervision Enhances Instance-based Multiple Instance Learning Methods in Digital Pathology: A Benchmark Study

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive benchmark study reveals that simple instance-based Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) methods combined with self-supervised learning achieve comparable or superior performance to complex embedding-based approaches in digital pathology, establishing new state-of-the-art results on the BRACS and Camelyon16 datasets while providing better interpretability through patch-level predictions.

## Problem

- Whole Slide Images (WSIs) are extremely large and lack patch-level annotations, making direct application of deep learning challenging
- Conventional wisdom suggests embedding-based MIL methods are superior, but they lack interpretability and are computationally expensive

## Method

- Evaluate ==10 MIL methods== (both instance and embedding-based) with ==6 self-supervised learning strategies== across **4** datasets
- Introduce **4** novel ==instance-based MIL methods== adapted from sound event detection
- Compare performance using different ==backbones== and ==foundation models==

## Results

- New state-of-the-art results achieved on BRACS and Camelyon16 benchmarks
- Instance-based methods provide better interpretability through patch-level predictions
- Findings are robust across different datasets, backbones, and SSL methods
- Foundation models perform well but highlight importance of domain-specific data

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simple instance-based MIL methods with strong SSL features perform as well or better than complex embedding approaches
> - MOCOv3, DINO and Barlow Twins provide the most stable SSL performance
> - Domain-specific pre-training consistently outperforms ImageNet pre-training
