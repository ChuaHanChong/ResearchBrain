# Learning Humanoid Arm Motion via Centroidal Momentum Regularized Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT developed a limb-level multi-agent reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to generate functional arm motions during locomotion. The approach, using centroidal angular momentum (CAM) regularization, led to improved whole-body stability, reduced ground reaction moments, and a 23% higher success rate in recovering from external disturbances on a physical humanoid platform.

## Problem

- Coordinating arm motion with leg locomotion in humanoid robots presents challenges due to the complexities of whole-body dynamics and the difficulty in designing effective, physically-grounded guidance for arm movements.
- Traditional unified control approaches (e.g., imitation learning, single-agent RL) struggle with unclear functional benefits beyond trajectory matching or conflicting reward signals requiring extensive heuristic tuning.
- Model-based optimization methods for whole-body control are often computationally intensive, limiting their scalability for online, closed-loop execution with sufficient bandwidth.

## Method

- A limb-level multi-agent reinforcement learning (RL) framework with separate actor-critic policies for arm and leg agents, employing a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm.
- Arms are guided by rewards that encourage vertical centroidal angular momentum (CAM) tracking and horizontal CAM damping, promoting anti-phase arm swing to reduce whole-body angular momentum and vertical ground reaction moments.
- The framework leverages centroidal dynamics modeling (e.g., Centroidal Momentum Matrix computation) to provide physically-grounded feedback, allowing agents to coordinate implicitly through shared global base and CAM observations.

## Results

- The method achieved more accurate vertical CAM tracking and demonstrated emergent anti-phase arm-leg coordination, where arm and leg angular momentum contributions were consistently out of phase.
- The proposed approach reduced peak vertical ground reaction moments and resulted in an approximately 23% higher success rate in recovering from random torque disturbances compared to a 'Fixed Arms' baseline.
- The learned policies successfully transferred to a physical humanoid robot, enabling robust locomotion on flat ground (up to 1.3 m/s), rough terrain, and during vision-free stair climbing without modification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Emergent anti-phase arm-leg coordination, guided by CAM regularization, effectively cancels out angular momentum contributions, enhancing overall balance and reducing undesirable wrenches on the robot.
> - Multi-agent RL with CTDE design improves learning stability and convergence speed by allowing decentralized actors to operate on local observations while centralized critics leverage global information for robust value estimation.
> - Uncontrolled or passively swinging arm motion can introduce instability, indicating that functional arm movements require explicit physical guidance rather than simply allowing free movement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of enabling coordinated whole-body control, specifically focusing on the emergent arm motion in humanoid robots during locomotion. This area is situated at the intersection of biomechanics, robot control, and reinforcement learning.

Biomechanically, human arm swing during locomotion is recognized for its role in regulating whole-body dynamics, reducing angular momentum, and maintaining balance. However, the exact mechanisms and optimal coordination for robotic platforms remain an area of active investigation. Prior research has approached arm and leg coordination in humanoid robots through several paradigms:

- **Unified Control Approaches:**
 - **Imitation Learning:** Methods such as those in attempt to mimic human-like behaviors by matching reference data. A limitation noted by the authors is the unclear functional benefit of such imitation for robot robustness or performance, as the primary objective is often trajectory matching rather than task-specific optimization.
 - **Heuristic-based Reinforcement Learning:** Other RL approaches plan arm and leg motions simultaneously but often rely on heuristics for arm motion regularization, which may lack a clear physical basis or require extensive stylistic tuning.
- **Model-Based Optimization Approaches:**
 - Techniques leveraging full-body optimization, including centroidal momentum tracking, relative angular-acceleration control, or full-body model predictive control (MPC), can generate physically consistent arm motion. However, these methods often face computational scalability challenges for online, closed-loop control, frequently requiring approximations or specialized treatments to achieve sufficient bandwidth. Additional complexities arise from collision avoidance, state estimation noise, and environmental constraints.

The paper contrasts these whole-body approaches with biological evidence suggesting that arm and leg commands originate from separate cortical areas and are only coupled for stabilization. This biological observation suggests that a decoupled control architecture could offer engineering advantages, akin to "sectioning" physics in complex system optimizations or abstracting limb motions in loco-manipulation tasks.

This work introduces a multi-agent reinforcement learning (RL) framework that leverages the Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm. This approach aims to address the non-stationarity inherent in multi-agent environments while providing benefits in convergence and stability. The specific contribution is applying this paradigm to humanoid locomotion, where arms and legs are treated as distinct agents, connected via centroidal dynamics, to achieve coordinated whole-body movement guided by physically-grounded centroidal angular momentum (CAM) objectives.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective control strategy for humanoid robots that enables coordinated whole-body locomotion through emergent, physically-informed arm movements. The authors aim to overcome limitations observed in existing humanoid control methods by drawing inspiration from human biomechanics and leveraging advancements in multi-agent reinforcement learning.

The key motivations driving this work include:

- **Mimicking Human Arm Swing Functionality:** Humans naturally swing their arms during locomotion, and biomechanical studies suggest this motion is crucial for regulating whole-body dynamics, reducing angular momentum, and maintaining balance. The authors are motivated to imbue humanoid robots with similar functional arm movements to enhance their stability and robustness during locomotion.
- **Addressing Challenges in Robotic Arm Coordination:** Coordinating arm motion with leg locomotion in humanoid robots presents significant challenges. Traditional optimization-based or learning-based control methods struggle with the complexities of whole-body dynamics, the variability of locomotion tasks, and the difficulty in designing effective, physically-grounded guidance for arm movements.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Unified Control Approaches:**
 - **Imitation Learning:** While capable of producing human-like behaviors, imitation-based methods often prioritize mimicking trajectories, which may not directly translate to improved robustness or performance for the specific robotic platform. The functional advantages for locomotion, beyond aesthetics, are often unclear.
 - **Single-Agent Reinforcement Learning:** Planning arm and leg motions simultaneously with a single agent can lead to conflicting reward signals, making training difficult and requiring sensitive heuristic-based regularization that may lack physical justification.
- **Addressing Computational Burden of Model-Based Control:** Full-body optimization schemes, while capable of generating physically consistent arm motions, are often computationally intensive, making them difficult to scale for online, closed-loop control with sufficient bandwidth.
- **Leveraging Biological Insights for Decoupled Control:** The biological observation that commands for arms and legs arise from separate cortical areas, with coupling for stabilization, suggests that a modular or multi-agent control architecture could be advantageous from an engineering perspective. This motivates exploring a limb-level control paradigm where arms and legs are treated distinctly yet coordinated.
- **Enhancing Learning Stability and Performance:** The authors are motivated to employ the Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) framework in multi-agent RL to stabilize training by providing centralized critics with global information, while allowing decentralized actors to operate on local observations for efficient execution.
- **Incorporating Physically-Grounded Principles:** A key motivation is to guide arm motion using centroidal angular momentum (CAM) rewards, rooted in biomechanical studies. This is intended to promote arm movements that actively reduce overall angular momentum and vertical ground reaction moments, thereby contributing to improved balance and robustness, especially under external perturbations.

In summary, the objective is to develop a modular, learning-based control framework that enables humanoids to generate functional, emergent arm motions during locomotion, thereby improving stability, disturbance recovery, and overall task performance by adhering to physically consistent centroidal dynamics principles.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a limb-level multi-agent reinforcement learning (RL) framework for humanoid whole-body control, featuring separate agents for the arms and legs. This framework operates on a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm.

**A. Multi-Agent RL Architecture (CTDE)**
The core of the approach involves two distinct actor-critic policies: one for the arm agent and one for the leg agent.
- **Decentralized Actors:** Each actor receives only local, limb-specific observations. Critically, both actors share global base states and Centroidal Angular Momentum (CAM) observations, which serve as implicit coupling signals for coordination without requiring direct knowledge of the other agent's internal state.
- **Centralized Critics:** During training, each critic has access to global information, encompassing the full state of both arm and leg joints, all base information, and the actions of both agents. This centralized critic design aims to stabilize learning by providing a consistent value estimate in a non-stationary multi-agent environment.
- **Execution:** For deployment, only the decentralized actors are used, allowing for efficient, modular control.

The training algorithm employed is Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), extended for multi-agent settings (MAPPO), implemented in the IsaacLab simulation environment with parallel rollouts across 4096 environments. Policies operate at 100 Hz, outputting residual joint position commands which are then fed to a low-level 1 kHz joint PD controller.

**B. Centroidal Dynamics Modeling and Computation**
The research leverages centroidal dynamics, a representation that balances simplification and full-body complexity.
- **Centroidal Momentum (h_G):** Defined as comprising centroidal linear momentum (l_G) and centroidal angular momentum (k_G) with respect to the robot's center of mass (CoM), calculated as h_G = A_G(q) * dot(q) (Eq. 3), where A_G is the centroidal momentum matrix (CMM).
- **Rate of Change of Centroidal Momentum (dot(h_G)):** Calculated as dot(h_G) = A_G(q) * ddot(q) + dot(A_G(q)) * dot(q) (Eq. 5). This quantity is exploited for reward shaping as external disturbances directly affect it.
- **Limb-wise Decomposition:** Total centroidal momentum is decomposed into contributions from the base, legs, and arms (h_G = h_G,base + h_G,legs + h_G,arms) to isolate the impact of each limb group.
- **Reference Centroidal Momentum (hat_h_G):** A target momentum profile (Eq. 7) is defined by projecting desired base linear and angular velocities through the CMM.
- **Computation:** Dynamic quantities like CMM are computed using Pinocchio, a rigid-body dynamics library. CasADi and CusADi are used for symbolic expression generation and GPU parallelization to achieve high-throughput evaluation.

**C. Arm Agent Design**
The arm agent's primary function is to regulate CAM.
- **Observation Space (O_arm, R^41):** Includes base height, heading, linear/angular velocities (base frame), normalized gravity vector projection (base frame), arm joint positions/velocities, previous arm action, and current/target CAM (x,y in base frame, z in world frame).
- **Action Space (A_arm, R^8):** Residual arm joint position commands (Delta_hat_q_arm).
- **Reward Structure:**
 - **Vertical CAM tracking (r_CAM, Eq. 8):** Encourages anti-phase arm swing by aligning the vertical CAM (k_zG) with a reference vertical CAM (hat_k_zG).
 - **Horizontal CAM damping (r_dCAM, Eq. 9):** Penalizes the undesirable build-up of horizontal angular momentum (k_xG, k_yG), promoting arm responses that dissipate perturbation-induced momentum.

**E. Hardware Platform**
Experiments are conducted on a humanoid platform weighing 24.89 kg, with arms contributing 5.71 kg (22.8% of total mass). The robot has 10 actuated leg joints and 8 actuated arm joints.

**F. Baselines for Comparative Analysis**
To validate the approach, the authors compare their method against several baselines:
- **For CAM-regularized arm motion:**
 - *Fixed Arms:* Only legs are actuated, arms remain static.
 - *With Arms w/o CAM:* Full-body policy with the same multi-agent architecture but without CAM-related rewards.
- **For effectiveness of Multi-Agent RL framework:**
 - *Single Agent:* A single actor-critic policy with full global observation and joint arm/leg actions.
 - *Multi-Agent DTDE:* Decentralized Training and Decentralized Execution, where both actors and critics use only local observations.
 - *Multi-Agent CTCE:* Centralized Training and Centralized Execution, where both actors and critics receive full global observations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the benefits of CAM-regularized arm motion and the effectiveness of the limb-level multi-agent RL framework.

**A. CAM-Regularized Arm Motion**
- **Improved Vertical Centroidal Angular Momentum (CAM) Tracking:** The proposed method ("With Arms (Ours)") tracks the reference vertical CAM more accurately compared to the "Fixed Arms" baseline (Fig. 4a). This indicates that the learned arm motions effectively contribute to regulating whole-body angular momentum.
- **Emergent Anti-Phase Arm-Leg Coordination:** Decomposition of the total vertical CAM shows that the angular momentum contributions from the arms and legs are consistently out of phase, effectively canceling each other (Fig. 4b). This mechanism helps in better tracking the reference vertical CAM, corroborating biomechanical observations from.
- **Reduced Vertical Ground Reaction Moment (GRM):** The proposed method exhibits a narrower distribution of vertical GRM and achieves the lowest peak vertical moment during walking, in contrast to "Fixed Arms" and "With Arms w/o CAM" (Fig. 5). This suggests that CAM-guided arm movements reduce undesirable wrenches on the body, thereby improving stability and potentially reducing foot slippage.
- **Negative Impact of Uncontrolled Arm Motion:** The "With Arms w/o CAM" policy, where arms swing passively without explicit CAM regularization, performs worse than both "Fixed Arms" and the proposed method, displaying higher peak vertical moments and a wider GRM distribution. This indicates that uncontrolled arm motion can introduce instability rather than improve it.
- **Enhanced Disturbance Recovery:** Under external torque disturbances (3 N·m along x, y, z-axes), the emergent arm motion quickly generates counter-momentum, stabilizing the overall CAM. The robot demonstrates rapid recovery to a stable walking gait within approximately one second (Fig. 6).
- **Increased Robustness to Perturbations:** The proposed method shows an approximately 23% higher success rate in recovering from random torque disturbances (ranging from [−15 N·m, 15 N·m] along x, y-axes) compared to the "Fixed Arms" baseline (Fig. 7). This validates the contribution of emergent arm motion to disturbance rejection.

**B. Effectiveness of Limb-Level Multi-Agent RL**
- **Faster and Higher Reward Convergence:** The proposed "Multi-Agent CTDE" approach and the "Multi-Agent DTDE" baseline exhibit faster and achieve higher asymptotic reward (velocity tracking term) convergence compared to the "Single Agent" and "Multi-Agent CTCE" baselines (Fig. 8).
- **Limitations of Single-Agent Control:** The "Single Agent" approach, which aggregates all rewards, leads to conflicting objectives and results in significantly lower final reward and slower convergence, indicating the difficulty in balancing diverse limb control tasks simultaneously.
- **Inefficiency of Centralized Actor Observations:** The "Multi-Agent CTCE" variant performs slightly worse than the proposed method. This is attributed to actors receiving full global observations, which increases input dimension and can introduce irrelevant noise, hindering sample efficiency and convergence.
- **Importance of CTDE Design:** The results support the CTDE design, where actors operate on local, limb-specific observations while sharing only essential global information like base state and CAM. This allows for effective coordination without unnecessary input noise.
- **Improved Advantage Function Stability:** The "Multi-Agent CTDE" method demonstrates significantly lower variance and faster convergence of the advantage function for the arm agent compared to "Multi-Agent DTDE" (Fig. 9). This indicates that centralized critics, by leveraging global observations, produce more reliable advantage estimates, thereby contributing to stable and consistent policy updates in multi-agent settings.

**C. Hardware Experiment**
- **Successful Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The learned policy successfully transfers to a physical humanoid platform, demonstrating its real-world applicability (Fig. 1, Fig. 10).
- **Generalization to Diverse Tasks:** The policy generalizes to various locomotion tasks, including flat-ground walking (up to 1.3 m/s with coordinated out-of-phase arm swing), rough terrain traversal, and vision-free stair climbing, without modification. Adaptive arm motions are observed to assist in recovery from missteps and maintaining balance during stair traversal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework with centroidal momentum regularization that significantly contributes to the field of humanoid robot control. The work holds several points of significance and potential impact:

- **Biologically-Inspired and Physically-Grounded Control:** By drawing inspiration from human biomechanics regarding arm swing and incorporating centroidal angular momentum (CAM) objectives, the framework provides a physically-grounded method for generating naturalistic and functional arm movements. This moves beyond purely aesthetic imitation towards movements that actively contribute to robot stability and dynamics.
- **Enhanced Locomotion Stability and Robustness:** The emergent arm motions, guided by CAM tracking and damping, are demonstrated to reduce whole-body angular momentum fluctuations and lower vertical ground reaction moments. These factors are crucial for stable locomotion, reducing the risk of slippage, and improving overall balance, especially on challenging terrains or under dynamic conditions.
- **Improved Disturbance Rejection:** The study shows that the learned arm policy enables the humanoid robot to effectively counteract external perturbations, significantly increasing its ability to recover from unexpected disturbances. This is a critical capability for deploying humanoid robots in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments.
- **Advancement in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Robotics:** The effective application and validation of the Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm for a complex, high-degree-of-freedom system like a humanoid robot demonstrate its utility beyond simpler multi-agent tasks. The work highlights how modularizing control tasks for different limb groups (arms and legs) can lead to more stable and efficient learning, overcoming challenges like conflicting reward signals and non-stationarity inherent in single-agent or fully decentralized approaches.
- **Practical Applicability and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful deployment of the learned policies on a physical humanoid platform across diverse and challenging locomotion tasks (flat ground, rough terrain, stair climbing) underscores the practical viability of the approach. This strong sim-to-real transferability is a key indicator of its potential for real-world applications.
- **Modular Control Design:** The limb-level multi-agent framework offers a modular approach to control design. This modularity can simplify reward shaping, prevent interference between different control objectives (e.g., arm swing for balance vs. leg motion for velocity tracking), and potentially allow for easier integration of additional capabilities, such as manipulation, in the future.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for exploring more complex and dynamic humanoid behaviors, such as one-foot balancing, running, or loco-manipulation tasks like dribbling or kicking. The insights into CAM-based regularization and multi-agent architecture can inform the development of highly agile and versatile humanoid robots. Further integration with human motion datasets could also enhance the naturalness and generalizability of learned behaviors.
