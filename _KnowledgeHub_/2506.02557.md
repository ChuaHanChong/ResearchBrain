# Kernel-based Unsupervised Embedding Alignment for Enhanced Visual Representation in Vision-language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Chinese University of Hong Kong and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed Kernel-based Unsupervised Embedding Alignment (KUEA) to improve CLIP's fine-grained visual perception by aligning its visual embeddings with a vision-centric model like DINOv2. This method, which enhanced CLIP ViT-L-14's zero-shot accuracy by 1.28% and boosted downstream Multi-modal Large Language Models like LLaVA-1.5-7B by 1.66% on average, preserves the crucial image-text alignment while being computationally lightweight.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, while strong in semantic understanding, struggle with fine-grained visual perception (e.g., spatial reasoning, counting) due to training with global image-caption supervision.
- This limitation propagates to Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that rely on CLIP as their visual backbone, hindering their performance on visually nuanced tasks.
- Existing solutions to improve fine-grained perception are often computationally prohibitive (requiring full retraining) or risk disrupting CLIP's crucial pre-existing image-text alignment, making them incompatible with downstream MLLMs.

## Method

- Proposes ==Kernel-based Unsupervised Embedding Alignment (KUEA)== to enhance CLIP's visual encoder by aligning its feature space with a vision-centric model like DINOv2.
- The method minimizes the ==squared difference between kernel matrices== of CLIP's visual embeddings and the target model's embeddings, encouraging CLIP to capture more fine-grained visual similarities.
- An ==L2 regularization term== is applied to CLIP's visual embeddings, constraining them to remain close to their original pre-trained state, thereby preserving the vital visual-text alignment without needing text data during fine-tuning.

## Results

- Achieved consistent improvements in CLIP's zero-shot object recognition (e.g., **1.28%** average increase for ViT-L-14 on CLIP-Benchmark) and enhanced performance on tasks requiring fine-grained perception (e.g., **5.51%** average increase for linear probing on counting/spatial tasks).
- Maintained original image-text retrieval performance on datasets like Flickr30K and MSCOCO, confirming that the alignment process does not compromise CLIP's core visual-text compatibility.
- Improved performance of downstream MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B, OpenFlamingo-3B) when integrating the aligned CLIP visual encoder, with LLaVA showing an average score increase of **1.66%** on vision-language benchmarks, especially strong in localization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning embeddings in a higher-dimensional kernel space allows for flexible adjustments of similarity relationships, enhancing fine-grained perception without drastically altering the feature space.
> - An L2 regularization term on visual embeddings is sufficient to maintain cross-modal (image-text) alignment, even without explicit text-based supervision during fine-tuning.
> - Efficient stochastic optimization for kernel alignment is feasible, enabling lightweight and scalable fine-tuning on large image-only datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated at the intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), a rapidly evolving field in artificial intelligence. VLMs like CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) have been foundational in bridging the gap between visual and textual modalities, demonstrating remarkable zero-shot generalization capabilities across various tasks, from classification to segmentation. Their success has led to their widespread integration as visual encoders in MLLMs such as LLaVA, OpenFlamingo, BLIP-2, and Qwen-VL, serving as critical components for extracting visual features that contextualize textual understanding.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation of CLIP: its struggle with fine-grained visual perception. Due to its training with global image-caption supervision, CLIP often emphasizes semantic coherence over intricate pixel-level details, leading to difficulties with tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning, counting, or recognizing subtle visual attributes (Thrush et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024). This deficiency directly impacts the performance of downstream MLLMs that rely on CLIP's visual input.

The existing research landscape has explored several avenues to address this issue:
1. **Multi-Model Encoders:** Some approaches combine CLIP with vision-centric foundation models like DINO (Caron et al., 2021) or DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024) as separate visual encoders for MLLMs (Jiang et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024). While effective in enhancing fine-grained perception, this method introduces significant computational overhead.
2. **CLIP Fine-tuning:** Other studies focus on fine-tuning the CLIP encoder itself using vision-only self-supervised learning protocols (Wu et al., 2023; Covert et al., 2025). While this can improve localization abilities, it often risks disrupting the crucial alignment between image and text representations, making the fine-tuned CLIP incompatible with its original text encoder and requiring costly re-tuning of entire MLLMs from scratch.
3. **Retraining CLIP with Additional Losses:** Methods involving integrating region-based task losses or distillation losses from vision models during CLIP's initial training (Zhong et al., 2022; Salehi et al., 2023). This approach is computationally prohibitive, as it necessitates re-training CLIP from its foundational stage.

This paper addresses a critical gap in this landscape by proposing a novel, *lightweight*, and *unsupervised* fine-tuning strategy that enhances CLIP's fine-grained visual perception *without compromising its vital image-text alignment* or requiring expensive retraining of downstream MLLMs. By focusing on alignment in the kernel space, the authors offer a solution that is both effective and practical, setting it apart from prior works that incur high computational costs or break essential compatibility. It seeks to provide a generalizable solution that can be applied to other VLMs and leverage new, powerful vision encoders as they emerge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the recognized shortcomings of state-of-the-art vision-language models like CLIP. While CLIP excels at aligning global visual and textual representations, enabling impressive zero-shot capabilities, its training on global image captions inherently limits its ability to capture fine-grained visual details, such as precise object attributes, spatial relationships, or counts. This limitation manifests as significant performance degradation in tasks requiring nuanced visual understanding and carries over to Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that rely on CLIP as their visual backbone.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Enhance Fine-grained Visual Perception:** The primary objective is to significantly improve the CLIP visual encoder's ability to discern and represent fine details within images. This is motivated by the observation that vision-centric foundation models (like DINOv2) inherently possess superior fine-grained perceptual capabilities.
2. **Preserve Visual-Text Compatibility:** A critical objective is to ensure that any enhancement to the visual encoder does not disrupt its original alignment with CLIP's text encoder. This is paramount for maintaining CLIP's zero-shot generalizability and for allowing seamless integration into existing MLLMs without the need for extensive, costly re-tuning of the entire multi-modal system. The text encoder is explicitly frozen and untouched during the alignment process.
3. **Develop an Efficient and Scalable Alignment Method:** The method must be computationally lightweight and capable of scaling to large, real-world datasets. This contrasts with approaches that require re-training from scratch or introduce significant architectural overhead. The aim is to enable efficient stochastic optimization with minimal hardware requirements.
4. **Demonstrate Downstream Transferability:** The enhanced visual representations are intended to benefit downstream applications. A key objective is to show that the improvements in the CLIP visual encoder effectively transfer to Multi-modal Large Language Models, leading to enhanced performance in tasks like visual question answering, without requiring comprehensive fine-tuning of the LLM component.

In essence, the paper aims to bridge the "fine-grained gap" in CLIP's visual understanding by leveraging the strengths of vision-only models, while innovatively overcoming the practical challenges (computational cost, compatibility breakage) that have hindered previous attempts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Kernel-based Unsupervised Embedding Alignment (KUEA), offers an innovative approach to enhance CLIP's visual representation by aligning it with a vision-centric model like DINOv2, all while preserving compatibility with CLIP's text encoder. The core of the methodology lies in the use of kernel functions for alignment and a carefully designed optimization objective.

#### 3.1. Kernel Function and Kernel Matrix

The method leverages kernel functions, `k(x, y)`, which measure the similarity between two vectors `x` and `y` in a high-dimensional feature space. A key property of kernel functions is the "kernel trick," where `k(x, y) = ⟨ϕ(x), ϕ(y)⟩`, implicitly mapping input features into a potentially much higher-dimensional Hilbert space (`R^s`) via a feature map `ϕ`. This allows for operations in a richer space without explicit computation of `ϕ(x)`. For this work, the authors primarily utilize a normalized polynomial kernel of degree 3, given by `k_polynomial(γ,c,d)(x, y) = (γx^T y + c)^d`, noting its common adoption in related literature. For DINOv2, the hyperparameters `γ` and `c` are fixed, while for CLIP, they are made trainable, allowing for flexible adaptation.

#### 3.2. Kernel-based Embedding Alignment Objective

The central idea is that while CLIP's visual representations excel in semantic alignment, DINOv2's representations are superior in capturing fine-grained visual details. These differences are reflected in their respective kernel matrices: DINOv2's matrix would show higher similarity for visually similar samples, even if their semantic meanings are broad, whereas CLIP's might show high similarity for semantically similar but visually distinct samples.

To address this, the authors propose to align the kernel matrix of CLIP's visual encoder (`f_θ`) with that of the target model DINOv2 (`g`). The alignment objective is formulated as:
`min_θ E_{I_i,I_j ~ D_train} [(k_1(f_θ(I_i), f_θ(I_j)) - k_2(g(I_i), g(I_j)))^2]`
Here, `k_1` is the kernel for CLIP's visual embeddings and `k_2` is for DINOv2's. This objective encourages CLIP's visual encoder to adjust the pairwise similarities of images in its feature space to better reflect the fine-grained visual similarities captured by DINOv2.

A crucial aspect of this objective is its computational feasibility. Proposition 3.1 demonstrates that the gradient of this objective can be accurately estimated using limited sample pairs, enabling efficient stochastic optimization (e.g., mini-batch gradient descent) and allowing the framework to scale to large datasets.

#### 3.3. Regularization for Visual-language Alignment

To prevent the fine-tuning process from inadvertently disrupting the critical pre-existing alignment between CLIP's visual and text embeddings, a regularization term is introduced. This term is the L2 distance between the current (fine-tuned) visual representation `f_θ(I_i)` and the original (pre-trained, frozen) visual representation `f_{θ_0}(I_i)`. The final optimization objective becomes:
`min_θ w · E_{I_i,I_j ~ D_train} [ (k_1(f_θ(I_i), f_θ(I_j)) - k_2(g(I_i), g(I_j)))^2 ] + E_{I_i ~ D_train} [ ||f_θ(I_i) - f_{θ_0}(I_i)||_2^2 ]`
The coefficient `w` balances the two loss terms. Proposition 3.2 theoretically supports that this L2 regularization effectively preserves the visual-text alignment (measured by cosine similarity) without requiring any text data during the fine-tuning phase. This is a major advantage, as it avoids the complexities and computational burden of multimodal data for alignment.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Models:** The method is applied to various CLIP visual encoders (ViT-B-16, ViT-L-14, ViT-L-14-336) and DINOv2 with a ViT-L-14 backbone (after registration). This demonstrates flexibility across different architectures and resolutions.
- **Training Data:** Fine-tuning is performed solely on the ImageNet-1K training set (1.28M images), highlighting the image-only nature and efficiency of the approach.
- **Training Scheme:** The process is lightweight, taking approximately 30 hours for ViT-L-14 on two NVIDIA 4090 GPUs. AdamW optimizer and CosineAnnealingLR scheduler are used.

In summary, the methodology offers a novel, kernel-based alignment strategy that judiciously balances enhancing fine-grained visual perception (by aligning with DINOv2) with preserving the crucial visual-text alignment (via regularization), all within an efficient and scalable image-only fine-tuning framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various benchmarks consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and practical benefits of the proposed Kernel-based Unsupervised Embedding Alignment (KUEA) method.

#### 4.1. Improvements on Vision-centric Tasks

- **Zero-shot Object Recognition:** Evaluating on 12 diverse datasets from CLIP-Benchmark (including common objects, fine-grained, domain-specific, and out-of-distribution), the aligned CLIP models show consistent improvements in average zero-shot accuracy. For example, ViT-L-14 saw an increase of 1.28%. These gains are particularly notable for tasks involving low-resolution images or tiny objects, indicating enhanced fine-grained perception. The improvements are architecture-agnostic and maintain CLIP's generalizability. Critically, KUEA outperforms straightforward baselines like linear projection and direct feature-based alignment, which often lead to performance drops due to the significant differences in feature spaces. It also shows better zero-shot performance than DIVA (Wang et al., 2025).
- **Image-to-text and Text-to-image Retrievals:** Experiments on Flickr30K and MSCOCO datasets confirm that the alignment process *does not compromise* CLIP's original image-text alignment. The aligned models maintain strong retrieval performance, reinforcing the efficacy of the regularization term in preserving compatibility.
- **Counting, Spatial Reasoning, and Caption Recognition:** For tasks where CLIP traditionally struggles (SVHN, GTSRB, CLEVR Distance, CLEVR Counts), KUEA significantly enhances performance. For ViT-L-14, average accuracy improved by 1.58% for zero-shot classification and 5.51% for linear probing on these tasks. This directly validates the method's ability to alleviate CLIP's fine-grained perception limitations in text- or spatial-related contexts.
- **Localization Ability:** Using the probing benchmark on MSCOCO, the aligned CLIP visual encoder demonstrates improved localization abilities, reflected in higher macro-averaged recall for both local and global probing. Visualizations of attention maps (Fig. 2) show that the aligned model produces sharper attention maps, highlighting more fine-grained features. This enhancement is achieved without sacrificing textual alignment, a common drawback in prior localization-focused fine-tuning methods.

#### 4.2. Improvements on MLLMs

- **LLaVA Integration:** When the aligned CLIP visual encoder replaces the original in LLaVA-1.5-7B, performance on diverse vision-language benchmarks (VQAv2, TextVQA, RefCOCO, etc.) improves even when the LLM component remains frozen. This indicates that the enhanced visual representations are directly compatible and beneficial for downstream MLLMs. Further parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) of the LLM component (without re-aligning visual-text embeddings) amplifies these gains, leading to an average score increase of 1.66% for ViT-L-14-336, with particular strength in localization benchmarks (3.05% improvement).
- **OpenFlamingo Integration:** Similar positive results are observed when integrating the aligned CLIP visual encoder into OpenFlamingo-3B (Instruct). Across various VQA benchmarks and different in-context learning settings (0, 4, 16 shots), the aligned encoder leads to improved metrics, further demonstrating the generalizability of the proposed framework to other prominent MLLMs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Training Epochs:** Optimal performance is achieved with very short training durations (e.g., 2 epochs), highlighting the method's efficiency.
- **Regularization Term:** The regularization term for preserving L2 distance to the original CLIP embeddings is crucial. Without it, performance significantly drops, confirming its role in maintaining visual-language alignment.
- **Coefficient `w`:** The method is robust to the choice of the balancing coefficient `w` for the loss terms, with stable performance across a range of values.
- **Kernel Function:** While all tested kernel functions (polynomial, Gaussian, cosine) yield improvements, the polynomial kernel consistently performs best, suggesting its flexibility in capturing desired similarities.
- **Dataset Size:** Performance gains are observed even with smaller fine-tuning datasets (e.g., 25% of ImageNet), and improve further with larger data, confirming efficiency and scalability.

#### 4.4. Generalizability to Other VLMs and Target Models

The appendix further demonstrates the generalizability of KUEA by showing improvements when aligning other VLMs (SigLIP, DFN, MetaCLIP) with DINOv2, and when using alternative vision encoders like MLCD as the target model instead of DINOv2.

Overall, the findings strongly support that KUEA successfully enhances CLIP's fine-grained visual perception and localization capabilities while critically preserving its zero-shot image-text alignment, making these improvements directly transferable and beneficial to downstream MLLMs without costly re-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Kernel-based Unsupervised Embedding Alignment for Enhanced Visual Representation in Vision-language Models" holds significant implications for the field of multi-modal AI, addressing a critical bottleneck in the utility of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

#### 5.1. Addressing a Core VLM Limitation with Novelty

The most significant contribution is the effective and elegant solution to CLIP's well-documented deficiency in fine-grained visual perception. Unlike prior attempts that either incur prohibitive computational costs or, more critically, break the vital image-text alignment, this work introduces a novel kernel-based approach that enhances visual detail recognition *while preserving* zero-shot generalizability and compatibility with frozen text encoders. This is a substantial advancement, as it allows for specialized visual capabilities without sacrificing the broad utility that makes CLIP so valuable. The use of kernel space for alignment is a sophisticated methodological choice that enables flexible adjustments of sample similarities without drastic, incompatible changes to the underlying feature space.

#### 5.2. Practical Efficiency and Accessibility

The proposed method stands out for its practical efficiency. The image-only fine-tuning on a relatively small dataset like ImageNet-1K (compared to CLIP's original training scale) and its short training duration (e.g., 30 hours on consumer-grade GPUs) make it a remarkably lightweight and hardware-friendly approach. This significantly lowers the barrier for researchers and practitioners to adopt and benefit from enhanced visual encoders without needing vast computational resources or extensive re-training efforts. This aspect is particularly impactful in accelerating research and deployment of advanced multi-modal systems.

#### 5.3. Seamless Integration and Reduced Development Cost for MLLMs

A major practical benefit is the demonstrated seamless transferability of the enhanced visual representations to downstream Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like LLaVA and OpenFlamingo. The fact that MLLMs show improved performance simply by replacing their visual encoder with the aligned CLIP version, even *without* fine-tuning the LLM component, is highly impactful. This eliminates the need for expensive and time-consuming full MLLM re-alignment or re-training, which has been a major hurdle for incorporating visual improvements into complex multi-modal architectures. This facilitates faster iteration and deployment of more capable MLLMs.

#### 5.4. Opening New Research Avenues

The paper explicitly states that it "opens up a new research avenue to facilitate improvements through lightweight adaptation." This signifies a potential paradigm shift away from solely relying on massive pre-training or complete re-training for model enhancements. Instead, it advocates for targeted, efficient adaptation strategies that maintain the integrity of pre-trained foundation models while injecting specialized capabilities. This could inspire further research into similar lightweight adaptation techniques for various modalities and model architectures.

#### 5.5. Broad Societal and Industrial Impact

As highlighted in the impact statement, the improved visual perception of VLMs and MLLMs can have wide-ranging benefits across various industries and societal applications. Enhanced fine-grained understanding is crucial for:
- **Autonomous Systems:** Better perception of intricate details in real-world environments.
- **Assistive Technologies:** More accurate interpretation of visual information for aiding individuals.
- **Healthcare:** Improved performance in tasks like medical image analysis and diagnosis (consistent with some authors' background).
- **Transportation, Education, and beyond:** Leading to more accurate, reliable, and context-aware AI systems.

#### 5.6. Identified Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge two main limitations:
1. **Computational Resources:** Due to resource constraints, the alignment was performed on a relatively small dataset (ImageNet-1K) and evaluated on smaller MLLMs (e.g., 7B parameter models). Future work could explore scaling to larger datasets (e.g., DataComp) and larger MLLMs (e.g., 70B parameter models) to verify performance gains at greater scales.
2. **Scope of Alignment:** The current work focuses primarily on aligning CLIP with DINOv2. Future research could extend the pipeline to align other pairs of embeddings, especially for MLLMs that utilize visual encoders other than CLIP (e.g., InternVL) or leverage other vision-centric models (e.g., BEit). This indicates the potential for broad applicability of the kernel-based alignment paradigm.

In conclusion, this paper presents a significant methodological advancement that efficiently resolves a key weakness in CLIP's visual understanding. Its practicality, preservation of core VLM functionalities, and demonstrable impact on downstream MLLMs position it as a highly influential work that could reshape how visual capabilities are integrated and enhanced within the rapidly evolving landscape of multi-modal AI.
