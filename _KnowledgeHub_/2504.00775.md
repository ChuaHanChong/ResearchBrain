# Visual Environment-Interactive Planning for Embodied Complex-Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Xidian University developed a framework that enables embodied agents to answer complex, multi-step questions by integrating sequential planning, a structured semantic space, and continuous visual feedback. It consistently improved accuracy over one-step planning methods in both simulated and real-world environments, particularly for tasks involving abstract language and dynamic observation.

## Problem

- Embodied Question Answering (EmbodiedQA) systems typically struggle with complex human questions that involve intricate grammatical structures, abstract semantics, or require multi-step reasoning.
- Current LLM-based planning methods for embodied agents often generate 'once-for-all' plans, leading to limited understanding of dynamic visual environments and susceptibility to LLM hallucinations.
- The black-box nature of solely LLM-based planning makes it challenging to interpret an agent's actions, diagnose errors, or refine strategies in complex, dynamic scenarios.

## Method

- A sequential '==Observation-Planning-Action==' framework that allows for multi-turn feedback and dynamic adjustment of plans based on real-time visual perception.
- A structured semantic space that iteratively integrates hierarchical visual scene graph representations with chained linguistic expressions from parsed complex questions.
- Specialized tools for language parsing to standardize complex human questions, and a hybrid task planning system combining rule-based reasoning with LLM-based guidance to reduce direct LLM reliance.

## Results

- The proposed framework achieved an LLM-Match score of **65.4%** using ChatGPT-4O, outperforming the ReAct baseline (**61.8%**) in simulated environments.
- It consistently surpassed ReAct on both template-based (**66.8%** vs. **59.1%**) and multi-step questions (**62.0%** vs. **52.6%**) within the newly introduced ECQA dataset.
- The framework demonstrated practical applicability in a real-world home environment, successfully answering complex, multi-step questions involving small objects and people while mitigating issues like visual occlusion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating external, rule-based planning derived from human observation habits with LLM-based reasoning significantly improves robustness and interpretability for embodied task execution.
> - A hierarchical visual scene graph, dynamically updated with transient information about small objects, provides essential prior knowledge and supports fine-grained interaction with the environment.
> - Parsing complex natural language questions into standardized patterns within a structured semantic space clarifies intent, enabling more precise and multi-turn task planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied Question Answering (EmbodiedQA) focuses on enabling robots to understand natural language commands, navigate indoor environments, and respond to questions based on visual observations. Historically, EmbodiedQA tasks have primarily addressed relatively simple, template-based questions due to limitations in the generalization capabilities of early models. These questions typically involve clear intentions and basic object attributes.

With the emergence of large language models (LLMs), there has been an expansion into open-vocabulary datasets and more complex scenarios. LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in abstract reasoning and task planning for embodied robots. However, current LLM-based planning methods for embodied agents often generate plans in a "once-for-all" or one-step manner. These approaches typically lack a sufficiently deep understanding of the dynamic visual environment, rendering them susceptible to LLM hallucinations and making error diagnosis challenging due to their black-box nature. This limitation becomes particularly pronounced when faced with human questions that possess intricate structures and abstract semantics, which the authors term "embodied complex-question answering" (ECQA).

Research into visual scene representation has focused on providing intelligent agents with structured understanding of their environments. Approaches range from layered hierarchical graphs describing entire buildings to agent-centric scene graphs focusing on local information. While some methods pre-construct complete scene graphs, others incrementally build them during exploration. This paper contributes to the latter by adopting a global scene graph perspective while dynamically inferring transient information.

In task planning, LLMs have been utilized either directly as planners to generate action sequences or as translators to convert natural language into formal planning languages like PDDL. Both approaches, particularly the former, often exhibit an over-reliance on LLMs, which can lead to issues in complex, dynamic environments. More recent work has explored "AI Agents" that augment LLMs with abilities like perception, decision-making, memory, and tool use, mimicking human cognitive processes to improve task execution and enable self-optimization through feedback. Examples include ReAct, LATS, and Reflexion, which integrate reasoning and acting. This paper positions its work within this trajectory, drawing inspiration from AI Agents but specifically addressing the limitations of one-shot planning by proposing a multi-step, environment-interactive planning framework grounded in a structured semantic space. It aims to reduce the direct reliance on LLM reasoning for every planning step by incorporating external rules and continuous environmental feedback, thereby improving robustness and interpretability for ECQA tasks. The work also addresses the need for more complex benchmarks by introducing a new dataset.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized limitations of existing embodied agents in handling complex human natural language instructions within indoor environments. While current EmbodiedQA systems can address basic queries, they often falter when questions involve intricate grammatical structures, abstract semantics, or require multi-step reasoning and dynamic interaction with the visual world. The paper highlights several specific challenges:

1. **Complexity of Human Questions:** Real-world human-robot interaction often involves questions that are more complex than the template-based queries typically addressed by current EmbodiedQA systems. These questions demand a deeper comprehension of intent and context.
2. **Limitations of One-Step Planning:** Many LLM-based task planning methods, while powerful, generate plans in a "once-for-all" manner. This approach often lacks sufficient real-time understanding of the dynamic environment, making the robot prone to errors, particularly hallucinations from the LLM, and difficult to diagnose when failures occur.
3. **Lack of Interpretability and Debugging:** The black-box nature of solely relying on large models for planning makes it challenging to pinpoint the source of errors or to refine action strategies when tasks go awry.
4. **Need for Continuous Environment Interaction:** Effective task execution in dynamic environments requires an agent to continuously perceive, plan, and adjust its actions based on real-time feedback, rather than adhering to a rigid, pre-generated plan.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Sequential Planning Framework:** To propose an embodied agent framework that formulates plans in a sequential, step-by-step manner, allowing for continuous feedback and adjustment of actions based on visual perception.
2. **Create a Structured Semantic Space:** To design and implement a structured semantic space where hierarchical visual perceptions (from a scene graph) and chained linguistic expressions (from question parsing) can interact iteratively. This space is intended to enable granular, multi-turn task planning.
3. **Enhance Language Parsing:** To develop a mechanism for parsing complex human natural language instructions into standardized patterns within the structured semantic space, thereby clarifying the intention of the question for subsequent planning.
4. **Integrate Rule-based and LLM-based Planning:** To combine external rules (derived from human observation habits) with LLM-based reasoning for task planning. This approach aims to weaken the sole reliance on large models, making planning more robust, interpretable, and less susceptible to hallucinations.
5. **Contribute a New Dataset:** To create "ECQA," a new EmbodiedQA dataset specifically designed to include more complex, multi-step, and real-world-inspired questions, providing a challenging benchmark for embodied agents.
6. **Validate Real-World Applicability:** To demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed framework in both simulated and real-world indoor environments, particularly for tasks involving small objects, people, and multi-step reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces an embodied agent framework that performs visual environment-interactive planning for Embodied Complex-Question Answering (ECQA) tasks. This framework operates on a "Observation-Planning-Action" cycle, enabling multi-turn feedback and sequential plan adjustment.

**A. Structured Semantic Space**
Central to the framework is a structured semantic space, denoted as S = {S1, S2, S3, S4}, designed to facilitate continuous interaction between linguistic instructions and visual perception. This space is hierarchical, with each layer Si = {Vi, A} containing a set of nodes (Vi) and attributes (A). The visual environment is represented within this space as a hierarchical visual scene graph, while linguistic instructions are mapped into a chain structure that interacts with the graph.

**B. Indoor Visual Scene Representation**
The scene graph (Gv = (Vv, Ev)) serves as a structured representation of the indoor environment, offering prior knowledge to the robot. It is organized into four hierarchical layers:
- **V1 (Floor Layer):** The top level, representing the entire floor.
- **V2 (Room Layer):** Rooms within each floor (e.g., bedrooms, living rooms).
- **V3 (Big Object Layer):** Fixed furnishings within each room (e.g., coffee table, sofa). These nodes encode detailed positional information.
- **V4 (Small Object Layer):** Movable objects often associated with big objects (e.g., potted plant on a coffee table). Crucially, to enhance adaptability to dynamic environments, no information about V4 objects is pre-defined; the robot must infer it through observation.

The edges (E) in the graph represent both abstract relations between adjacent layers (Eij where i ≠ j) and spatial relations within the same layer (Eij where i = j). The scene graph for the first three layers (V1-V3) is pre-constructed upon entering a new environment, while V4 information is dynamically updated through active perception.

**C. Approach: Tools and Workflow**
The embodied agent leverages a suite of specialized tools to process complex questions and execute tasks (Algorithm 2):

1. **Language Parsing Tool:**
 - This tool's function is to interpret the human's natural language question (Q) and project its intent into the structured semantic space.
 - It utilizes a fine-tuned LLM (specifically, a 7-billion parameter model for efficiency) to convert complex questions into standardized patterns (e.g., "V2 → V3 → V4(A) → A" for "What is the title of the book open on the table in the living room?").
 - This parsing provides a logical path on the hierarchical scene graph, clarifying the target object or attribute and its hierarchical position, which guides subsequent planning.

2. **Task Planning Tools (Rule-based Plan & LLM-based Plan):**
 - These two tools collaborate to formulate action plans (Algorithm 1).
 - **Rule-based Plan Tool:** Infers the robot's optimal movement path based on the parsed language pattern, the hierarchical scene graph, and predefined rules inspired by human observation habits. The rules dictate movement based on the target's layer and attribute type:
 - If the target is a V4 (small) object, move to its parent V3 (big object).
 - If the target is a V3 (big) object, move to its parent V2 (room).
 - If the target is a V3 attribute (e.g., color) requiring remote perception, move to V2. If it requires close-range perception (e.g., material), move to V3.
 - If the target is a V4 attribute (e.g., color) requiring remote perception, move to V3. If it requires close-range perception, move to V4.
 - **LLM-based Plan Tool:** Leverages the commonsense knowledge and language processing capabilities of LLMs to:
 - Pre-judge whether an attribute requires remote or close-range perception, informing the Rule-based Plan.
 - Generate specific content to be observed in each step, simplifying complex queries (e.g., "What is the title of the book?") to prevent LLM hallucinations from overly complex grammar.
 - Think through decision history and current visual scene feedback.
 - The collaboration follows a chain: Language Parsing output guides the Rule-based Plan for navigation, then the LLM-based Plan refines observation goals, leading to sequential movement.

3. **Observation Tool:**
 - This tool performs visual perception.
 - It assesses whether each navigation step has been correctly executed, providing feedback to the embodied agent to ensure path accuracy.
 - It integrates the specific perceptual requirements from the LLM-based Plan to observe the target object/attribute and extract the answer.
 - In real-world scenarios, it utilizes Capsule Network methods to handle object occlusion, ensuring robust visual information gathering. If a perception error is detected, it triggers a secondary perception using the scene graph and robot's position for verification.

**D. Workflow Summary**
Upon receiving a question, the agent first uses the Language Parsing tool to standardize its intent. Then, in an iterative loop, it combines the Rule-based Plan and LLM-based Plan tools to formulate the current step's action plan. After executing the plan, the Observation tool perceives the environment, checks task completion, and provides feedback to guide the next planning step. This cycle continues until the question is answered.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed framework through experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, presenting a new dataset and comparing performance against an existing method.

**A. ECQA Dataset Contribution**
The paper introduces the Embodied Complex-Question Answering (ECQA) dataset, which expands upon previous EQA datasets. ECQA enriches template-based questions and adds complex multi-step questions inspired by real-world human-robot interactions. It also incorporates questions specifically about small objects and people, primarily for real-world scenarios. The dataset categorizes questions into four types:
1. **Template-based questions:** Inquire about object properties (location, quantity, material, color, existence) in the Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) environment.
2. **Multi-step questions:** Involve complex grammar and require multi-stage reasoning.
3. **Questions related to small objects:** Focus on properties (number, color, state, location, existence) of small, often movable, objects.
4. **Questions related to people:** Address the state, location, and existence of individuals.

**B. Evaluation Metric**
The correctness of answers provided by the embodied robot is evaluated using the LLM-Match score, an adaptation of an existing protocol. Given a question, a correct answer, and the model's output, an LLM (ChatGPT-4o) assigns a score from 1 (wrong) to 5 (correct), which is then normalized to a 0-100% scale.

**C. Implementation Details**
Experiments were first conducted in the HM3D simulation environment, comparing the proposed method with ReAct, a one-step planning approach. Various LLMs (ChatGPT-4O, Qwen2.5-72B, 32B, 14B, 7B) served as backbones. For real-world deployment, emphasizing practicality, the framework was ported to a robot equipped with an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, utilizing Qwen2.5-14B for planning due to its balance of performance and parameter size. To address object occlusion in real environments, Capsule Network methods were employed. The first three layers of the visual hierarchical scene graph (floor, room, big object) were pre-built as prior knowledge, while the fourth layer (small object) was dynamically inferred.

**D. Experimental Results in Simulated Environments**
Two main comparative experiments were performed:

1. **LLM-Match evaluation with different LLM backbones (Table II):**
 - The proposed framework consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to ReAct across all tested LLM backbones.
 - ChatGPT-4O yielded the highest scores for both methods (Ours: 65.4%, ReAct: 61.8%), indicating its strong reasoning capabilities.
 - With the open-source Qwen2.5-72B, a performance gap with ChatGPT-4O was observed. However, the proposed approach (64.4%) maintained a more stable performance compared to ReAct (55.8%), which was more significantly impacted by the change in backbone.
 - As the parameter size of Qwen2.5 models decreased (72B to 7B), the performance of both methods declined, with a notable sharp drop at 7B. However, the proposed method consistently maintained a higher score and a larger performance margin over ReAct (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B: Ours 57.9%, ReAct 48.7%).
 - The Qwen2.5-14B model was identified as a suitable backbone, offering a balance between performance and computational requirements for practical deployment.

2. **LLM-Match evaluation on template-based and multi-step questions (Table III, Figure 6):**
 - The proposed framework outperformed ReAct on both question categories within the ECQA dataset.
 - **Template-based questions:** The framework achieved an LLM-Match score of 66.8%, significantly higher than ReAct's 59.1%. Analysis (Figure 6a) indicated that the proposed method generated more high-quality answers, while ReAct produced more low-scoring matches. For instance, ReAct failed to answer "Is the person in bed asleep?" accurately due to visual occlusion and lack of fine-grained planning, whereas the proposed method used its Rule-based and LLM-based plans to approach the object for close-range observation.
 - **Multi-step questions:** The framework scored 62.0%, exceeding ReAct's 52.6%. For ReAct, the number of wrong answers (2285) was nearly equivalent to correct ones (2281), indicating difficulty with multi-step reasoning. For example, for "What color is the phone held by the person sitting on the sofa in the living room?", ReAct only navigated to the living room, failing to gather specific object attribute information. The proposed method, through sequential planning based on fine-grained observation requirements, guided the robot to the optimal observation position.
 - The superior performance of the proposed method is attributed to its sequential planning and multi-turn feedback mechanism, which allows for dynamic adjustment based on environmental observations, enhancing error diagnosis and task execution.

**E. Real-World Demonstration**
The framework was deployed on a real embodied robot in a self-constructed indoor home environment, testing questions related to small objects, people, and multi-step tasks from the ECQA dataset (Figure 7).
- **Successes:** The robot successfully navigated to appropriate observation points for small objects (e.g., "Is there a bag on the bed"), attributes requiring close-range perception, and person-related questions (e.g., "Is the person in bed asleep?"). For multi-step questions, the hierarchical scene graph and sequential reasoning enabled the robot to avoid distractions and focus on relevant elements.
- **Failure Cases (Figure 8):** One instance involved the robot failing to count cushions on a sofa accurately due to viewpoint occlusion. Despite moving near the sofa, the pre-defined location for the "sofa" node in the scene graph did not allow for a comprehensive view of all objects, leading to an incorrect answer. This highlights a limitation regarding the static nature of pre-defined large object locations and the need for active perception capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces an embodied agent framework designed to address the challenges of Embodied Complex-Question Answering (ECQA), representing a contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence.

**A. Addressing Complex Human-Robot Interaction:** The work directly targets the gap between current embodied agent capabilities and the complexities of real-world human-robot communication. By focusing on intricate question structures and abstract semantics, it pushes the boundaries of what embodied agents can comprehend and execute.

**B. Novel Planning Paradigm:** The proposal of a sequential, visual environment-interactive planning framework departs from traditional one-step planning methods, particularly those solely reliant on large language models (LLMs). This multi-turn feedback mechanism allows for continuous adaptation and error correction based on real-time environmental observations, contributing to more robust and reliable task execution.

**C. Structured Semantic Space for Robust Interaction:** The creation of a structured semantic space, unifying hierarchical visual scene graphs with chained linguistic expressions, provides a principled approach for integrating multi-modal information. This structured interaction facilitates more precise planning and understanding of complex queries.

**D. Mitigating LLM Limitations:** By incorporating external, rule-based planning alongside LLM-based guidance, the framework reduces the direct reliance on LLMs for every planning decision. This approach potentially lessens susceptibility to LLM hallucinations and enhances the interpretability of the robot's actions, making diagnosis and debugging more manageable.

**E. Contribution of ECQA Dataset:** The ECQA dataset serves as a valuable new benchmark for the community, providing a more challenging and diverse set of questions. Its inclusion of multi-step questions and scenarios involving small objects and people pushes research towards more practical and realistic embodied AI applications.

**F. Demonstrated Practical Applicability:** The successful deployment and validation of the framework in real-world scenarios, beyond simulations, underscore its practical feasibility. This suggests potential for real-world applications in household environments or other settings where robots interact with complex questions and dynamic surroundings.

**G. Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies limitations, such as handling flexible oral instructions, automatically constructing scene graphs in unknown environments, and perceiving dynamic events. These highlight critical avenues for future research, indicating the framework's role as a foundation for further advancements in adaptive and versatile embodied robots.
