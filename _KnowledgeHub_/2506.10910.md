# Magistral

> [!summary] Summary
> Mistral AI introduces Magistral, its inaugural reasoning model, which leverages a custom Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) pipeline to significantly enhance the reasoning abilities of its base models. Magistral Medium, trained purely with RL, achieved a nearly 50% increase on AIME '24 (pass@1), while Magistral Small demonstrated that RL effectively boosts distilled models and both models showed surprising improvements in multimodal reasoning despite text-only training.

## Problem

- Traditional large language models (LLMs) often struggle with multi-step logical deduction and complex problem-solving tasks.
- Developing scalable and efficient reinforcement learning pipelines for LLMs, particularly from scratch, poses significant engineering and algorithmic challenges.
- Ensuring that specialized reasoning training does not degrade other essential LLM capabilities like instruction following and function calling.

## Method

- Developed Magistral, Mistral AI's first reasoning model, using a custom-built, scalable ==Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== pipeline.
- Employed a modified ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== algorithm with key changes like eliminating KL divergence and relaxing the trust region's upper bound to foster exploration.
- Implemented a meticulous reward system considering formatting, correctness (math/code verification), length penalties, and a novel ==language consistency reward== for multilingual reasoning.

## Results

- Magistral Medium achieved a nearly **5**0%** increase** on AIME '24 (pass@1), improving from **2**6.8%** to 73.6%**, and a **3**0%** increase** on LiveCodeBench (v5), from **2**9.1%** to 59.4%**.
- Magistral Small, a **24B model**, demonstrated that RL provided substantial performance boosts even after distillation (SFT) from a larger teacher model, achieving **70.7%** on AIME '24 pass@1 with SFT+RL.
- Despite being trained on text-only data, Magistral models showed improved performance on multimodal reasoning benchmarks like MathVista, MMMU, and MMMU-Pro.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) alone can achieve substantial reasoning performance gains on large models without initial distillation from other reasoning models.
> - Text-only RL training not only preserves but surprisingly improves multimodal reasoning capabilities, suggesting deep generalization of learned reasoning patterns.
> - Detailed ablations revealed that controlling exploration via the PPO-like clip-higher threshold was more stable than entropy bonuses, and specific batching strategies influenced training stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) is a central and highly active frontier in current AI research. Traditional chatbots often struggle with multi-step logical deduction or complex problem-solving. This paper directly addresses this challenge by focusing on "reasoning models" that leverage techniques like "chains-of-thought" to improve performance on intricate tasks.

Magistral positions itself within the emerging paradigm of Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), a technique that has shown significant promise in imbuing LLMs with enhanced reasoning abilities. The authors explicitly reference and build upon the "seminal work by DeepSeek-AI et al.," which provided crucial insights into the RLVR recipe for creating scalable reasoning models. This indicates that Magistral is a direct advancement or alternative approach within this specific RLVR landscape, aiming to contribute new insights and potentially superior methods.

A key differentiator highlighted by the authors is their "ground up approach," which contrasts with methods that might rely on existing RL implementations or "RL traces distilled from prior models." This suggests an effort to establish a robust, independent RL pipeline, which could offer greater flexibility, efficiency, and novel discoveries compared to adapting existing frameworks. The paper also directly compares its findings to those of DeepSeek-AI et al., particularly regarding whether RL can improve upon distillation for smaller models, indicating a direct engagement with and sometimes contradiction of established RLVR literature.

Furthermore, the paper explores aspects beyond pure reasoning performance, such as multilingual reasoning, multimodal understanding (even when training on text-only data), and the preservation of instruction following and function calling capabilities. This broader scope signifies a move towards creating more generally capable and robust LLMs through RL, rather than just optimizing for a narrow set of reasoning benchmarks. The open-sourcing of Magistral Small also contributes significantly to the research landscape by providing the community with a high-performing, RL-trained reasoning model, fostering further research and development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the reasoning abilities of Mistral AI's large language models through a novel and scalable reinforcement learning pipeline.** This overarching goal is driven by several more specific motivations and objectives:

- **Develop a Scalable Reinforcement Learning Pipeline:** A core motivation was to build their "own scalable reinforcement learning (RL) pipeline" from the ground up, avoiding reliance on external implementations or pre-existing RL traces. This aimed to provide Mistral AI with full control and optimization capabilities over their RL training process.
- **Explore the Limits of Pure RL Training:** The authors sought to investigate "the limits of pure RL training of LLMs," particularly for reasoning tasks, without the aid of "cold-start" distillation from existing reasoning models. This objective aims to understand the intrinsic potential of RL alone.
- **Introduce Mistral's First Reasoning Models:** The paper officially introduces "Magistral Small and Magistral Medium," the first dedicated reasoning models from Mistral AI, built upon their Mistral Small 3 and Mistral Medium 3 base models.
- **Demonstrate Significant Performance Boosts:** A clear objective was to achieve substantial performance improvements on complex reasoning benchmarks, exemplified by the target of a "nearly 50% boost in AIME-24 (pass@1)" for Magistral Medium.
- **Preserve and Improve Broader Capabilities:** The research aimed to demonstrate that RL training on text data alone could "maintain or improve multimodal understanding, instruction following and function calling," ensuring that specialized reasoning training does not degrade other essential LLM capabilities.
- **Force Reasoning Language Consistency:** A practical objective was to implement "a simple method to force the reasoning language of the model," ensuring that both the chain-of-thought and the final response are in the user's specified language, crucial for real-world multilingual applications.
- **Contribute to RLVR Literature:** By sharing insights, including "unsuccessful experiments" and findings that "add to, or contradict, existing RLVR literature," the authors aimed to advance the collective understanding of reinforcement learning for LLMs.
- **Open-Source a Capable Model:** A specific objective was to "open-source Magistral Small (Apache 2.0)," providing the broader AI community with access to a high-quality, RL-trained reasoning model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Magistral models were trained using a sophisticated and highly customized Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework, built entirely in-house by Mistral AI.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning Algorithm: Modified GRPO

The core RL algorithm utilized is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) [Shao et al., 2024], an offshoot of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that eliminates the need for a separate critic model by using average rewards from multiple generations per prompt. The authors introduced several key modifications to GRPO to enhance stability and effectiveness:
- **Eliminating KL Divergence:** The standard KL divergence penalty, which keeps the policy close to a reference policy, was removed entirely. The authors found it computationally costly and observed that the policy diverged substantially regardless, rendering the penalty ineffective.
- **Loss Normalization:** The loss was normalized by the total length of generations within a group, mitigating length biases.
- **Advantage Normalization:** Advantages were simplified to `r_i - mu` (reward minus group mean) and further normalized per minibatch by mean and standard deviation.
- **Relaxing Trust Region's Upper Bound (Clip-Higher):** To foster exploration and prevent entropy collapse, the upper clipping threshold (`ε_high`) in the PPO-like objective was increased (tuned between 0.26 and 0.28). This allows low-probability tokens to have more room for growth, crucial for exploring rare but insightful reasoning paths.
- **Eliminating Non-Diverse Groups:** Groups where all generations had identical rewards (either all correct or all wrong, resulting in zero advantage) were filtered out from training batches to avoid noisy and ineffective gradient updates.

#### 3.2. Reward Shaping

A meticulous reward system was designed to guide the model towards desired reasoning behaviors, considering four axes:
- **Formatting:** Responses had to adhere to a strict format (e.g., `<think>` tags, `\boxed{}` for math answers, Markdown code blocks for code). Failure to meet these conditions resulted in a 0 reward, halting further evaluation. Formatted responses received an initial 0.1 reward.
- **Correctness:**
 - **Math Correctness:** Extracted answers were compared against ground truth using a rule-based verifier, normalizing semantic equivalents. Correct answers received an additional 0.9 reward (total 1.0).
 - **Code Correctness:** Code extracted from markdown blocks was compiled (for C++) and executed against 20 randomly selected test cases (consistent within a group). Passing all tests earned an additional 0.9 reward.
- **Length Penalty:** A soft length penalty was introduced based on `l_max` (maximal completion length) and `l_cache` (length before penalty starts). Responses exceeding `l_max - l_cache` incurred a penalty, signaling proximity to a hard cutoff.
- **Language Consistency Reward:** To ensure multilingual reasoning, 10% of English problems were translated into French, Spanish, Italian, German, Chinese, and Russian. A `fastText` classifier checked if the problem, thoughts, and answer were all in the same language. If consistent, an additional 0.1 reward was given. A custom system prompt (Figure 2) also specified format and language requirements, including a "Be as casual and as long as you want" part to encourage exploration.

#### 3.3. Infrastructure

The training system is a distributed online RL pipeline, coordinating three types of workers:
- **Trainers:** Maintain model weights and perform gradient updates.
- **Generators:** Perform "roll-outs," using the latest policy to generate completions from training prompts.
- **Verifiers:** Evaluate completions from generators and assign rewards.
To address challenges like heterogeneous generation times and the need for on-policyness, an **asynchronous generation** approach was implemented. Generators operate continuously, sending finished completions to verifiers. Trainers gather verified groups, perform updates, and broadcast new weights to generators via NCCL (GPU-to-GPU transfer). Crucially, generators continue in-flight generations with slightly outdated KV-caches, which was found to be sufficient. Batches are defined by a fixed number of completions, distributed across data parallel ranks. A greedy collation algorithm sorts sequences by descending size to minimize padding and ensure homogeneous workload across training workers.

#### 3.4. Data Curation

The training relied exclusively on problems with verifiable solutions (math problems with numerical/expression answers, code problems with tests).
- **Math Data:** Started with 700k noisy samples.
 - **Format Filtering:** Removed proof-based and multi-part problems difficult to verify, and reformulated multiple-choice problems. Reduced to 501k samples.
 - **Difficulty Filtering:** A two-stage process using LLMs. First, Mistral Large 2 filtered out too-easy/too-hard problems (by sampling 16 solutions). This curated set trained a small RL model for grading. Second, this stronger RL-trained model re-graded the entire original dataset, filtering out easiest/unsolved problems and those where model consensus contradicted ground truth. This resulted in a highly curated, 'goldilocks' difficulty dataset of 38k samples.
- **Code Data:** Gathered from contest data. Removed problems without solutions or sufficient tests. Ensured test agreement, updating incorrect tests where necessary. Generated additional tests for problems lacking them. Duplicated problems for Python or C++ requirements. Resulted in 35k code problems.

#### 3.5. Training Stages (for Magistral Medium and Small)

- **Magistral Medium:** Trained with pure RL on top of Mistral Medium 3 Instruct. Training was staged: increasing data difficulty, allowing longer completions (`l_max - l_cache`), and scaling down concurrent requests/batch sizes to manage KV-cache memory.
- **Magistral Small:** 'Cold-started' with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using correct traces from Magistral Medium's RL training and augmented with diverse prompts from OpenThoughts and OpenR1. This SFT checkpoint (Mistral Small 3 Instruct) was then further trained with RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Magistral Medium – Reasoning RL From Scratch

- **Significant Performance Gains:** Magistral Medium, trained solely with RL, showed substantial improvements over its Mistral Medium 3 base:
 - AIME ’24 (pass@1): 26.8% to 73.6% (nearly 50% increase).
 - LiveCodeBench (v5): 29.1% to 59.4% (30% increase).
- **Competitive with DeepSeek-R1:** Achieved performance comparable to or even surpassing DeepSeek-R1 (which uses SFT before RL) on some benchmarks (e.g., AIME’24, AIME’25, LiveCodeBench v5), demonstrating the strength of their pure RL pipeline. Notably, Magistral Medium reached 90% accuracy on AIME-24 with majority voting (maj@64).

#### 4.2. Magistral Small – RL on top of Reasoning SFT Bootstrapping

- **RL Boosts Distillation Baseline:** Contrary to some prior findings (e.g., DeepSeek-AI et al.), RL yielded substantial boosts even on a smaller 24B model (Magistral Small) *after* distillation (SFT) from a larger teacher (Magistral Medium).
 - SFT + RL (Magistral Small) consistently outperformed SFT alone and RL-only on most reasoning and coding benchmarks (e.g., AIME’24 pass@1: SFT 65.4%, RL-only 65.8%, SFT+RL 70.7%).
- **Open-Source Release:** Magistral Small (24B) weights were open-sourced under Apache 2.0.

#### 4.3. Multilingual Benchmarks

- Magistral Medium demonstrated strong multilingual capabilities, reasoning and answering in French, Spanish, German, Italian, Russian, and Chinese versions of AIME 2024.
- Performance degradation was observed (4.3-9.9% lower than English), but it was comparable to the base model's degradation, indicating that the language constraint for reasoning did not disproportionately harm performance.

#### 4.4. Ablations and Analysis

- **Cross-Domain Generalization:** Training exclusively on math data showed performance improvements on code benchmarks, and vice versa, indicating strong generalization abilities of the RL.
- **Distillation vs. RL for Small Models:** Pure RL on Mistral Small 3 achieved performance similar to or even outperforming distillation on some benchmarks (MATH, GPQA), contradicting previous observations that RL might be less effective for smaller models without a distillation cold-start. RL *on top of* SFT provided the best performance.
- **Batch and Minibatch Size:** Performance was robust as long as `n_batch = n_minibatch` and `n_batch` was sufficiently large. Degradation occurred if `n_minibatch` was significantly smaller than `n_batch` (more than 2 minibatches per batch), suggesting that excessive off-policy updates within a batch hurt training.
- **Advantage Normalization:** Different normalization methods (minibatch, group, no normalization) showed no significant differences in evaluation performance or length growth, leading to the use of minibatch normalization.
- **Weight Trajectory Analysis (PCA):** Visualizing the loss landscape showed a clear "length" direction in the weight space, where mean reward and output length increased up to a point, confirming that increasing completion length is a primary driver of performance improvement. A logarithmic scaling between raw reward and output length was observed.
- **Multimodal Free Lunch:** Despite being trained on text-only data, Magistral models (derived from multimodal base models) surprisingly *improved* their multimodal reasoning capabilities on benchmarks like MathVista, MMMU, and MMMU-Pro, with significant gains. This suggests that textual reasoning improvements generalize to multimodal contexts.
- **Impact on Other Capabilities:** RL training maintained or slightly improved tool calling and instruction following capabilities.

#### 4.5. Unsuccessful Approaches

- **Partial Reward for Code:** Using proportional rewards based on tests passed (instead of binary pass/fail) led to faster training (less data discarded) but slightly lower final performance (-2% on LiveCodeBench) and slower length growth. The authors hypothesize that partial rewards might introduce false signals.
- **Entropy Targeting (Entropy Bonus):** Adding an entropy bonus term to the loss for exploration was found to be unstable, with its effect varying drastically between datasets (dropping entropy for math-only, exploding for math+code). Manually adjusting `ε_high` was found to be a more effective and stable way to control entropy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Magistral paper presents several significant contributions to the field of large language model research, particularly in the realm of advanced reasoning and reinforcement learning:

- **Demonstration of Pure RL Efficacy at Scale:** The most prominent finding is the substantial boost in reasoning capabilities achieved by Magistral Medium through *pure reinforcement learning alone*, without relying on distillation from pre-existing reasoning models. This contradicts some prior assumptions about the necessity of distillation for large-scale RL-trained reasoning models and highlights the immense potential of RL to directly imbue complex reasoning. This is a crucial validation for researchers exploring direct RL approaches.
- **Robust and Scalable RL Pipeline:** The detailed description of Mistral's in-house, asynchronous, distributed RL infrastructure is a significant methodological contribution. Its design choices, such as GPU-to-GPU weight transfer and intelligent batching, address practical challenges in large-scale online RL, offering valuable insights for other research groups developing similar systems.
- **Open-Sourcing of a High-Performing Model:** The release of Magistral Small under an Apache 2.0 license is a major practical impact. It provides the AI community with a strong, openly accessible baseline for reasoning tasks, fostering further research, fine-tuning, and application development, potentially accelerating innovation in this domain.
- **Surprising Multimodal Generalization:** The discovery that text-only RL training not only preserves but *improves* multimodal reasoning capabilities is a counter-intuitive yet highly impactful finding. It suggests a deep generalization of learned reasoning patterns across modalities, implying that focused textual reasoning training can yield broader AI capabilities "for free." This could simplify future multimodal model training by reducing the need for extensive multimodal RL datasets.
- **Insights into RL Optimization:** The detailed ablations on GRPO modifications, batching strategies, and the analysis of weight dynamics provide critical empirical insights into what works and what doesn't in large-scale LLM RL. The discussion of "unsuccessful approaches" (e.g., partial rewards for code, entropy bonus instability) is particularly valuable for guiding future research, helping others avoid similar pitfalls.
- **Advancement in Multilingual Capabilities:** The method to maintain language consistency during reasoning, coupled with successful performance on multilingual benchmarks, demonstrates progress towards truly global AI systems that can reason and respond effectively in diverse languages.
- **Holistic Capability Preservation:** The confirmation that RL training for reasoning does not degrade other vital LLM capabilities like instruction following and function calling is important for developing versatile and robust foundation models. It ensures that specialized training does not lead to unwanted performance trade-offs.

In conclusion, Magistral represents a significant step for Mistral AI into the domain of advanced LLM reasoning. By pioneering a scalable, ground-up RL pipeline and rigorously evaluating its impact, the authors not only deliver high-performing reasoning models but also contribute profound insights into the mechanics of reinforcement learning for LLMs. Their work paves the way for future research into optimizing RL algorithms, leveraging self-generated reasoning traces, and scaling these techniques to achieve even more generally capable AI systems across various applications, including integrated multimodality and agentic behaviors.
