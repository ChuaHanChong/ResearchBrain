# RGB: RL Guided Whole-Body MPPI for Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KIST and collaborators developed RGB, a control framework that integrates a robust, pretrained reinforcement learning (RL) policy as a sampling prior for a whole-body Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller. This approach enables precise task execution and flexible objective specification for humanoid robots without retraining the RL policy, reducing lateral drift RMSE from 0.339 m to 0.022 m and successfully tracking prescribed base height references.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning (RL) policies for humanoid locomotion often exhibit systematic errors, drift, and lack precision, requiring costly retraining for new tasks or adjustments.
- Vanilla Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) struggles with high-dimensional humanoid systems, showing sensitivity to initialization and inefficient exploration in contact-rich environments.
- A modular control solution is needed that combines the robustness of learned policies with the precision and flexible task specification of model-based methods, without constant retraining.

## Method

- A hybrid '==RL Guided Whole-Body MPPI==' (RGB) framework, where a pretrained RL policy provides a dynamically feasible motion prior for MPPI's sampling distribution.
- MPPI performs asynchronous, physics-engine-based rollouts in MuJoCo, refining control sequences by minimizing modular task-specific cost functions.
- A low-level PD controller executes the MPPI's desired joint commands, operating on a 29-DoF humanoid model in real-time at an average effective update rate of ~280 Hz.

## Results

- The framework achieved an average effective update rate of approximately 280 Hz in simulation, demonstrating real-time predictive control for high-DoF humanoids.
- Lateral drift during straight walking was significantly suppressed, with the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of the base lateral position reduced from **0.339** m (pure RL) to **0.022** m (RGB).
- RGB successfully enabled the humanoid to track new vertical task objectives, such as a piecewise-linear base height reference, by adapting whole-body posture through MPPI cost functions alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learned policy's role as a robust motion prior from task-specific objectives, allowing new tasks to be specified via MPPI costs without RL retraining.
> - Guiding MPPI's sampling with an RL policy significantly improves sample efficiency and biases exploration towards dynamically feasible whole-body behaviors in high-dimensional spaces.
> - Leveraging physics-engine-based rollouts implicitly handles complex contact dynamics and constraints, eliminating the need for explicit derivative calculations or hand-crafted contact penalties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of whole-body controllers for humanoid robots faces the challenge of achieving both robust stability in contact-rich and uncertain environments and precise execution of complex, human-like motions. The existing research landscape for humanoid control is broadly categorized into two paradigms: model-based control and learning-based control, primarily deep reinforcement learning (RL).

Model-based methods, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC), offer advantages in interpretability, stability guarantees derived from system dynamics, and the ability to explicitly enforce constraints. Recent advancements in sampling-based MPC, particularly Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), are relevant for contact-rich scenarios as they can optimize non-smooth objectives through parallel rollouts without requiring analytic derivatives. Physics-engine-based predictive control frameworks, like MuJoCo MPC (MJPC), further enhance practicality by leveraging high-fidelity forward dynamics and efficient rollout computation. However, model-based methods, especially vanilla MPPI, often struggle with high-dimensional humanoid systems due to sensitivity to initialization, sampling distributions, and the challenge of generating dynamically feasible trajectories in a vast state-action space, particularly under rapidly changing contact conditions.

Conversely, deep reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated success in achieving robust whole-body locomotion skills, adaptability, and the ability to handle high-dimensional behaviors. By exposing robots to diverse simulated environments, RL policies can acquire robust behaviors. Nevertheless, RL-based approaches exhibit their own limitations. Robustness achieved through extensive training and domain randomization can sometimes come at the expense of motion precision and fine-grained control performance. Moreover, learned policies are intrinsically tied to their training objectives and command interfaces. Incorporating new task objectives (e.g., explicit swing foot clearance modulation or pelvis height regulation) typically necessitates redesigning rewards and retraining the policy, which is resource-intensive and limits scalability across diverse tasks. Furthermore, RL policies do not naturally enforce strict constraint satisfaction, such as torque limits, which is critical for safe robot operation.

This research aims to bridge the gap between these two paradigms by proposing a hybrid approach. It seeks to integrate the robustness and adaptability demonstrated by learned RL policies with the precision and explicit objective shaping capabilities of model-based predictive control. Prior efforts have explored hybrid control for legged locomotion, but a unified whole-body control framework that simultaneously achieves high precision, robustness, and strong generalization, while also being modular and extensible without retraining, remains an active area of research. This work positions itself as an "add-on" feedback controller, leveraging a pretrained RL policy not as a final controller, but as a sampling prior to guide MPPI, thereby preserving RL's robust foundation while augmenting it with model-based precision and flexible task specification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations observed in applying pure deep reinforcement learning (RL) and vanilla model predictive path integral (MPPI) to the whole-body control of humanoid robots. The authors identified a need for a control framework that can combine the robust stability offered by RL with the precision and flexible task specification of model-based methods, without requiring extensive retraining for every new objective.

Specifically, the key motivations include:

1. **Addressing RL's precision and adaptability limitations:** While deep RL policies can achieve robust locomotion, they often exhibit systematic errors or drift, and their behavior is tightly coupled to the training objectives and command interfaces. Adding new feedback objectives or fine-tuning existing behaviors (e.g., regulating pelvis height or controlling specific end-effector positions) typically requires significant redesign of the reward function and costly retraining, limiting their generalizability and ease of deployment for diverse tasks.
2. **Mitigating vanilla MPPI's sensitivity in high-dimensional, contact-rich environments:** Standard MPPI, despite its derivative-free nature, struggles with high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) humanoid locomotion. Its performance is highly dependent on initialization and sampling distributions. Generating dynamically feasible trajectories in a complex, contact-rich state-action space is challenging, often leading to inefficient exploration, high variance in rollout costs, and suboptimal convergence, particularly under real-time constraints.
3. **Developing a modular and "plug-and-play" control solution:** The goal was to create a framework where a robust, learned locomotion policy could be augmented with additional task objectives without modification or retraining. This would allow for flexible task composition and rapid deployment across various scenarios by simply adjusting cost terms in a model-based component.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **To propose an RL guided whole-body MPPI framework:** This framework would function as an add-on feedback controller on top of a pretrained RL policy. The RL policy's role is re-envisioned from a final controller to a sampling prior that biases MPPI rollouts towards dynamically feasible behaviors.
2. **To establish a modular objective formulation:** This would enable flexible task-level command augmentation without requiring modifications or retraining of the underlying learned policy. Task objectives would be specified through modular MPPI cost terms.
3. **To implement an asynchronous, physics-engine-based MPPI on a 29-DoF humanoid:** This implementation aims to achieve real-time operation in simulation, leveraging parallel computation to efficiently refine the control sequences.
4. **To demonstrate improved task-level precision over a pure RL baseline:** The framework aims to show that it can correct systematic drift during locomotion and track additional whole-body reference signals imposed through the MPPI cost, all while preserving the robust locomotion behavior provided by the RL prior under the same command interface.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed RL Guided Whole-Body MPPI (RGB), integrates a pretrained reinforcement learning (RL) policy with a physics-engine-based Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) framework. The core concept is to use the RL policy not as the final controller, but as a structured sampling prior to guide the MPPI's exploration, enabling precise control and flexible task specification without retraining the RL policy.

The control stack operates asynchronously at three distinct rates:

1. **RL Policy Module (50 Hz):** A pretrained RL policy, which can be any locomotion policy outputting nominal joint position references (q_RL) with a compatible action interface, runs at 50 Hz. This policy is trained independently, often for command velocity tracking, and is assumed to provide robust locomotion. Crucially, the policy is neither modified nor retrained within this framework. Its observation inputs are shared with the MPPI for consistency.
2. **MPPI Refinement Module (Asynchronous, average ~280 Hz):** This module serves as the primary feedback controller. Upon completion of its planning cycle, it outputs an updated desired joint command (q_des_t).
 - **RL Policy as a Sampling Prior:** At each MPPI update, the RL policy provides a nominal joint position reference (q_RL). This reference acts as the mean of the sampling distribution for MPPI. Gaussian perturbations (ϵ) are applied around this mean to generate candidate control sequences (Equation 5: z^(n) = π_RL(o_t) + ϵ^(n)).
 - **Knot-Based Interpolation:** To manage the high dimensionality of the control space, a knot-based sampling approach is employed. The MPPI optimizes a sequence of `H` knot points (joint position targets), where `J` is the number of actuated DoFs. These knots are interpolated using cubic splines to reconstruct a high-resolution, temporally smooth control sequence for the entire prediction horizon. This reduces the effective sampling dimension and enhances computational efficiency.
 - **Physics Engine-Based Rollouts:** For each of `N` sampled candidate control sequences, a forward simulation is performed in a physics engine (MuJoCo, utilizing MJPC for parallel computation). The physics engine acts as a "rollout oracle," propagating the system dynamics, including contact, and returning the resulting trajectory and its accumulated cost. This derivative-free approach is advantageous for contact-rich humanoid locomotion, where analytical derivatives are complex or unreliable.
 - **Cost-Based Optimal Solution:** For each rollout `n`, a finite-horizon trajectory cost `S^(n)` is computed, comprising a terminal cost `ϕ` and a running cost `ℓ` (Equation 7). Task objectives and soft constraints are encoded in these modular cost terms, allowing for multi-objective optimization.
 - **Importance-Weighted Update:** Importance weights `w^(n)` are calculated based on the cumulative costs of the sampled trajectories (Equation 8), with lower-cost trajectories receiving exponentially higher weights. The control sequence is then updated by computing an importance-weighted average of the sampled perturbations, leading to a refined knot vector `z*`. The first element of the interpolated `z*` becomes the desired joint command `q_des_t` for immediate execution in a receding-horizon fashion.
3. **Low-Level PD Controller (500 Hz):** A joint-space Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller receives the `q_des_t` from the MPPI module and computes the desired joint torques `Γ_des` (Equation 9). These torques are then applied to the robot's actuators to track the desired joint positions. The PD gain matrices are fixed and identical to those used during RL policy training.

**Design Rationale:**

- **Decoupling Learning from Task Specification:** The framework explicitly separates the role of the RL policy (providing a robust motion prior) from task specification (defined by modular MPPI cost functions). This allows for new tasks to be introduced or modified without necessitating retraining of the RL policy.
- **RL Guided Sampling and Sample Efficiency:** By centering MPPI's sampling distribution on the learned RL policy's output, rollouts are biased towards dynamically feasible whole-body behaviors. This significantly improves sample efficiency, especially for high-dimensional humanoids where random exploration would yield many infeasible trajectories.
- **Implicit Contact Handling:** Contact-rich feasibility is implicitly enforced by the physics engine during rollouts. The simulator naturally handles contact dynamics and constraint-consistent state propagation, eliminating the need for explicit, hand-crafted contact constraint penalties in the cost function. Infeasible contact behaviors lead to higher rollout costs, which are then rejected through importance weighting.

**System Setup for Evaluation:**

The method was evaluated in MuJoCo using a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid model. Rollout evaluation utilized MJPC for CPU-based parallel computation. The system comprised an Intel Core i9-14900KF CPU, 32 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 Ti GPU. The RL policy used was trained independently in IsaacLab with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), focused on command velocity tracking, and outputted desired joint positions for 12 lower-body DoFs. MPPI parameters included `N=128` rollouts, a 0.02s prediction horizon, a control timestep of 0.002s (500 Hz), `H=2` spline knot points, and a Gaussian exploration noise standard deviation `σ=0.2`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed RL guided whole-body MPPI (RGB) framework focused on demonstrating its ability to augment the behavior of a pretrained RL policy, improving task-level precision and enabling new objectives without retraining the policy. The framework was tested on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid in MuJoCo.

**1. Real-time Operation:**
The asynchronous implementation achieved an average effective update rate of approximately 280 Hz in simulation, leveraging CPU-based parallel rollouts. This demonstrates the framework's capability for real-time predictive optimization for high-DoF humanoids.

**2. Straight-Walk Drift Suppression:**
- **Problem Context:** Humanoid locomotion policies trained with RL, even with symmetric observations and rewards, can develop persistent biases due to stochastic optimization or contact discontinuities, leading to lateral drift or turning despite a zero yaw command.
- **Experiment Design:** A pretrained RL policy exhibiting this straight-line tracking deficiency was used. The same locomotion command (forward velocity `v_x` = 1 m/s, lateral velocity `v_y` = 0 m/s, yaw rate `ψ` = 0 rad/s) was provided to both the RL policy and MPPI rollouts. MPPI was augmented with a cost term (Equation 10) penalizing lateral base position deviation (`y_base`) and heading deviation (`ψ_base`) from zero, with weights `β_y, β_ψ = 25`.
- **Results:**
 - The RGB method significantly suppressed drift accumulation compared to the pure RL policy. The Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of the base lateral position error was reduced from 0.339 m for the pure RL policy to 0.022 m for the RGB method over an 11.265 s trial.
 - Forward velocity tracking accuracy remained comparable between the two methods (RMSE: 0.773 for RGB vs. 0.806 for pure RL).
 - Lateral velocity tracking showed noticeable improvement with RGB (RMSE: 0.021 m/s vs. 0.046 m/s for pure RL).
- **Conclusion:** The added task-space feedback through MPPI effectively compensated for systematic left-right biases in the pretrained policy, enabling near-straight locomotion without degrading the nominal velocity-tracking performance of the learned policy.

**3. Squat Task (Base-Height Regulation):**
- **Problem Context:** The pretrained RL policy was designed for planar velocity tracking and typically accepts limited command inputs. Modulating vertical objectives like base height is usually outside its direct command interface.
- **Experiment Design:** The MPPI stage cost was augmented with a base-height tracking term (Equation 11) that penalized the deviation of the robot's base height (`z_base`) from a piecewise-linear height reference `z_des(t)`, with a weight `β_z = 100`.
- **Results:**
 - The RGB method successfully tracked the prescribed base-height reference throughout the entire motion, including descent and ascent phases.
 - In contrast, the pure RL policy, without the MPPI augmentation, maintained its nominal base height and was unable to follow the `z_des(t)` reference.
- **Conclusion:** This demonstrates that the RGB framework can introduce and accurately regulate additional vertical task objectives through cost composition alone, synthesizing whole-body posture adaptations without requiring retraining or modification of the underlying RL policy or its command interface. The robot adjusted knee and hip flexion to achieve the desired height while maintaining balance.

These results indicate that the proposed framework effectively leverages a robust RL prior for dynamic feasibility while utilizing MPPI for precise, online refinement and task augmentation, thereby addressing limitations of both standalone approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "RL Guided Whole-Body MPPI" (RGB) framework presents a significant contribution to humanoid robot control by integrating complementary strengths of reinforcement learning (RL) and model predictive path integral (MPPI).

The primary significance lies in its **"add-on" or "plug-and-play" architecture**. This design allows a pretrained RL policy to be utilized as a robust motion prior without modification or retraining. Instead of being the final controller, the RL policy intelligently biases the MPPI's sampling distribution towards dynamically feasible whole-body behaviors. This directly addresses a critical limitation of pure RL policies: their tight coupling to training objectives and command interfaces, which typically necessitates costly retraining for new tasks or adjustments. The RGB framework decouples learning from task specification, enabling flexible task composition through modular MPPI cost terms.

The **improved task-level precision** demonstrated by the RGB framework is another significant aspect. By continuously correcting the RL prior online, the method successfully mitigates systematic tracking errors, such as lateral drift during straight walking. It also allows for the enforcement of additional whole-body reference signals (e.g., base-height regulation) that are not part of the original RL policy's command interface. This capability enhances the practical utility of learned policies by providing a mechanism for fine-grained control and adaptation to specific mission requirements without compromising the underlying robust locomotion.

The **enhanced sample efficiency** for MPPI, achieved by guiding exploration with the RL prior, is also notable. In high-dimensional and contact-rich environments characteristic of humanoids, vanilla MPPI can struggle with inefficient exploration and sensitivity to initialization. The RL guidance concentrates MPPI's search in regions of the state-action space known to yield stable and feasible motions, leading to more efficient computation and better convergence under real-time constraints. The reported average update rate of 280 Hz in simulation, even with CPU-based parallelism, supports the real-time viability of this approach for complex humanoid systems.

The **implicit handling of contact dynamics** through physics-engine-based rollouts further streamlines the control design. By leveraging high-fidelity simulators like MuJoCo, the framework avoids the need for explicit, hand-crafted contact constraint penalties, allowing infeasible behaviors to be naturally rejected through their higher simulated costs.

Potential impacts of this work include:

- **Accelerated Deployment and Adaptability:** Robotics researchers and developers could deploy learned locomotion skills more rapidly and adapt them to new, specific tasks without the extensive computational resources and time required for retraining deep RL policies. This could facilitate more versatile robot applications in diverse and changing environments.
- **Bridging the Gap between Learning and Model-Based Control:** The framework offers a practical paradigm for combining the strengths of data-driven learning (robustness, complex behaviors) with the benefits of model-based control (precision, interpretability, constraint satisfaction), fostering further research into hybrid control architectures.
- **Advancements in Humanoid Capabilities:** By enabling precise and flexible control over complex whole-body motions, this method could contribute to humanoids performing more sophisticated manipulation, navigation, and interaction tasks in unstructured human environments.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as supporting larger behavioral changes through multiple priors or adaptive distributions, real-hardware deployment, and improving robustness to modeling errors, indicates clear pathways for extending the framework's capabilities and broader applicability.
