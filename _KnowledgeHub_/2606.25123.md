# RGB: RL Guided Whole-Body MPPI for Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KIST and collaborators developed RGB, a control framework that integrates a robust, pretrained reinforcement learning (RL) policy as a sampling prior for a whole-body Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller. This approach enables precise task execution and flexible objective specification for humanoid robots without retraining the RL policy, reducing lateral drift RMSE from 0.339 m to 0.022 m and successfully tracking prescribed base height references.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning (RL) policies for humanoid locomotion often exhibit systematic errors, drift, and lack precision, requiring costly retraining for new tasks or adjustments.
- Vanilla Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) struggles with high-dimensional humanoid systems, showing sensitivity to initialization and inefficient exploration in contact-rich environments.
- A modular control solution is needed that combines the robustness of learned policies with the precision and flexible task specification of model-based methods, without constant retraining.

## Method

- A hybrid '==RL Guided Whole-Body MPPI==' (RGB) framework, where a pretrained RL policy provides a dynamically feasible motion prior for MPPI's sampling distribution.
- MPPI performs asynchronous, physics-engine-based rollouts in MuJoCo, refining control sequences by minimizing modular task-specific cost functions.
- A low-level PD controller executes the MPPI's desired joint commands, operating on a 29-DoF humanoid model in real-time at an average effective update rate of ~280 Hz.

## Results

- The framework achieved an average effective update rate of approximately 280 Hz in simulation, demonstrating real-time predictive control for high-DoF humanoids.
- Lateral drift during straight walking was significantly suppressed, with the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of the base lateral position reduced from **0.339** m (pure RL) to **0.022** m (RGB).
- RGB successfully enabled the humanoid to track new vertical task objectives, such as a piecewise-linear base height reference, by adapting whole-body posture through MPPI cost functions alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learned policy's role as a robust motion prior from task-specific objectives, allowing new tasks to be specified via MPPI costs without RL retraining.
> - Guiding MPPI's sampling with an RL policy significantly improves sample efficiency and biases exploration towards dynamically feasible whole-body behaviors in high-dimensional spaces.
> - Leveraging physics-engine-based rollouts implicitly handles complex contact dynamics and constraints, eliminating the need for explicit derivative calculations or hand-crafted contact penalties.
