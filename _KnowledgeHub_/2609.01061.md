# Accelerating Reinforcement Learning via MPC Solver-Gradient Guidance for Weights-varying MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> SG-RL (Solver-Gradient Guided Reinforcement Learning) learns an online cost-weight adaptation policy for constrained nonlinear MPC by keeping sampled closed-loop return as the PPO objective while injecting bounded, normalized differentiable-solver sensitivities as auxiliary guidance at four injection points: update scaling, an auxiliary policy loss, advantage shaping, and the critic. On two full-scale racing platforms it matches PPO's best return with up to 70.6% fewer samples.

## Problem

- Fixed offline MPC cost weights are context-independent, so a single tuning is conservative in some operating regimes and overly aggressive in others as conditions shift.
- Reinforcement-learning weight adaptation optimizes realized closed-loop return but is sample-inefficient and ignores the first-order sensitivity information the differentiable MPC solver already provides.
- Gradient-based policy learning optimizes a differentiable surrogate on open-loop predicted trajectories, so it is biased under model mismatch, lacks exploration, and cannot handle sparse or non-smooth objectives.

## Method

- **SG-SCA** scales the ==PPO== update magnitude by an alignment coefficient between the lifted solver-gradient signal and the PPO policy gradient, acting as a clipped dynamic learning rate.
- **SG-LOS** adds a performance-gated target-following loss pulling the policy mean toward a solver-suggested weight target, activated only on negative-advantage transitions; **SG-ADV** shapes advantages by solver-consistent displacement.
- **SG-CRT** augments the critic with a residual branch encoding the normalized solver sensitivity; a history-aware ==MLP== policy with ==tanh-bounded constrained cost parameterization== supplies reactive, proactive and adaptive observations.

## Results

- On AV-24/Monza all four variants beat PPO's best mean return earlier: SG-SCA at **60.0%** fewer samples with **33.2%** higher peak reward; on EAV-24/Yas Marina SG-SCA and SG-LOS saved **70.6%**.
- Best-return gains over the authors' GB-PL baseline were **54.3-59.5%** (**68.5-72.0%** over the reduced GB-PL); SG-SCA scored **-33.17** total return with **810K** samples versus PPO's **-38.31** with **1.94M**.
- Zero-shot transfer to Laguna Seca and Suzuka improved over GB-PL by **65.6-68.6%** and **56.1-62.1%**; ablations show removing anticipation or history degrades reward by roughly **80-171%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-based solver sensitivities and environment-based policy gradients are complementary: biased low-variance guidance can steer early learning while sampled return stays the true objective.
> - Guidance works best as bounded, normalized, gated and annealed auxiliary signal, not as a replacement objective, so the update remains structured rather than merely larger.
> - Benefit hinges on predictive-model fidelity; under severe mismatch solver guidance may mislead, and each injection point adds its own hyperparameters and platform-dependent behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in Model Predictive Control (MPC) related to the online adaptation of cost-function weights in dynamic and safety-critical applications. MPC is a control framework that optimizes a sequence of control actions over a prediction horizon, subject to system dynamics and constraints. Its closed-loop performance is significantly influenced by the weighting of competing objectives within its cost function. Traditionally, these weights are tuned offline and remain fixed. However, changing operating conditions often render fixed parametrizations suboptimal, motivating the need for context-dependent online adaptation, a concept referred to as Weights-varying MPC.

Learning policies for such online weight adaptation is complex due to several factors:
1. **Bilevel Optimization Structure**: The overall system performance depends indirectly on policy parameters through the numerical solution of a constrained MPC problem.
2. **Long-horizon Dependencies**: Decisions at one time step influence future states and subsequent MPC solutions, leading to complex credit assignment.
3. **Nonlinear and Non-smooth Mappings**: The MPC solver introduces implicit, potentially non-smooth, and nonlinear mappings between policy parameters and system behavior.
4. **Closed-loop Interaction**: Policies must be learned through interaction with the controlled system, which can be expensive or unsafe in real-world scenarios.

Existing approaches to MPC cost-weight optimization can be broadly categorized into:
- **Offline Tuning**: Methods like genetic algorithms or Bayesian optimization that identify fixed weights, but do not address online adaptation.
- **Online Adaptation**: Approaches that learn state-dependent weight-adaptation policies. Within this category, two complementary sources of learning signals are prevalent:
 - **Environment-Based Gradients (Black-box Learning)**: Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods that optimize actual closed-loop return from environment samples. These methods benefit from long-horizon credit assignment and can handle broad reward signals (including non-smooth/sparse), but are often sample-inefficient and subject to approximation biases.
 - **Model-Based Solver Gradients (White-box Learning)**: Gradient-Based Policy Learning (GB-PL) methods that differentiate through the MPC solver to optimize surrogate losses on predicted trajectories. These methods exploit local first-order gradients, offering higher sample efficiency when the surrogate model is accurate. However, their gradients are only as accurate as the predictive model (leading to bias under model mismatch) and they typically lack exploration mechanisms and long-horizon value estimation characteristic of RL.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these two online adaptation paradigms. It identifies a gap where environment-based RL and model-based GB-PL offer complementary strengths and weaknesses. The high variance of RL updates versus the potential bias of GB-PL updates under model mismatch motivates a hybrid approach. The work aims to combine the sample efficiency of solver gradients with the robustness to model mismatch and long-horizon optimization capabilities of RL. This aligns with a broader trend in control and machine learning that explores guided, structured, or hybrid reinforcement learning, combining model-free updates with model-based components, auxiliary losses, or teacher signals to improve learning efficiency. Specifically, it extends prior work on differentiable MPC and RL for MPC by introducing a novel way to integrate solver sensitivities as auxiliary guidance *within* an RL framework, rather than replacing RL or using sensitivities for different objectives.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that can learn online MPC cost-weight adaptation policies more efficiently and robustly than existing standalone RL or gradient-based approaches, especially in scenarios with intentional model mismatch.

The key motivations driving this research are:
1. **Suboptimality of Fixed MPC Weights**: Standard MPC often relies on fixed cost weights, which become suboptimal in real-world applications where operating conditions, disturbances, and task requirements change dynamically. A single static tuning must compromise across different regimes, necessitating online adaptation to maintain optimal performance.
2. **Challenges in Learning Adaptive Policies**: Learning policies for online weight adaptation is a bilevel optimization problem. The explicit dependence on numerical MPC solutions introduces nonlinearities and potential non-smoothness, complicating gradient computation. Furthermore, the long-horizon consequences of weight choices and the need for closed-loop interaction with the system present significant credit assignment and sample efficiency challenges.
3. **Limitations of Existing Paradigms**:
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: While RL (specifically Proximal Policy Optimization - PPO) can optimize realized closed-loop return directly from environment samples and implicitly handle model mismatch and long-horizon effects, it is notoriously sample-inefficient. It relies on stochastic gradient estimates that can suffer from high variance.
 - **Gradient-Based Policy Learning (GB-PL)**: These methods leverage differentiable optimization to compute low-variance solver gradients from MPC. This typically leads to high sample efficiency. However, GB-PL optimizes model-based surrogate objectives, meaning its gradients are only as accurate as the predictive model. Under model mismatch, these gradients can be biased and potentially misleading, preventing true closed-loop optimality. They also generally lack explicit exploration mechanisms.
4. **Complementary Strengths**: RL and GB-PL possess complementary strengths: RL's robustness to model mismatch and long-horizon optimization versus GB-PL's sample efficiency due to local, first-order information. This complementarity motivates combining the two.

The central question addressed in the paper is: "how can environment-based and model-based solver gradients be combined effectively for learning online MPC cost-weight adaptation policies?" The overarching objective is to harness the sample efficiency of solver gradients while maintaining the direct optimization of the true closed-loop performance, which is a strength of RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on Solver-Gradient Guided Reinforcement Learning (SG-RL), a framework that integrates model-based NMPC solver loss gradients as auxiliary guidance into the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm.

**Problem Formulation:**
The problem is framed as a hierarchical decision-making process for Weights-varying Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC). A low-level NMPC controller solves a constrained finite-horizon optimal control problem at each time step `t`. A high-level learning policy, $\pi_\phi$, adapts the NMPC cost weights $\theta_t$ online, mapping an observation $o_t$ to a distribution over NMPC weight vectors. The NMPC objective function $J(x, u, \theta_t)$ includes a stage cost $l(x_k, u_k, \theta_t)$ and a terminal cost $l_N(x_N, \theta_t)$, where the weights $\theta_t$ parameterize quadratic cost terms. The internal prediction model $f(\cdot)$ and constraints $h(\cdot)$ are fixed.

The objective is to find optimal policy parameters $\phi^*$ that minimize the expected cumulative user-defined performance loss $L_{perf}(s_t, u_t)$ over a closed-loop episode:
$\phi^* = \arg \min_\phi E_{\tau \sim p_{cl}^\phi} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^T L_{perf}(s_t, u_t) \right]$.
This performance loss $L_{perf}$ can differ from the NMPC's internal cost and may include non-smooth or sparse terms.

**Neural Policy Design (for all methods):**
A feed-forward neural network $\pi_\phi$ acts as a meta-controller. Its input, $o_t$, is structured into three functional groups:
1. **Current measurable context (Reactive)**: Task-relevant local state information.
2. **Reference preview / task context (Proactive)**: A short-horizon lookahead of reference quantities (e.g., target speed, path geometry).
3. **Performance history (Adaptive)**: Recent closed-loop tracking terms (e.g., lateral/velocity errors).
The policy output $\theta_t$ (NMPC cost weights) is constrained within strictly positive limits via a smooth saturation function $\Gamma(q_t) = \frac{\theta_{max} - \theta_{min}}{2} \tanh(q_t) + \frac{\theta_{max} + \theta_{min}}{2}$. This ensures valid weights and regularizes the search space.

**Baseline Approaches:**
1. **Gradient-Based Policy Learning (GB-PL)**:
 - **Differentiating the NMPC Solution**: Leverages sensitivity analysis of nonlinear programs, as implemented in `acados` software. It computes the Jacobian $\nabla_{\theta_t} z^*$ (solver sensitivity) of the optimal trajectory $z^*$ with respect to the cost weights $\theta_t$ by differentiating the KKT conditions.
 - **Gradient Propagation**: Optimizes a differentiable surrogate loss $\tilde{L}_{perf}(z^*)$ defined on the NMPC-predicted open-loop trajectory $z^*$. The end-to-end gradient $\nabla_\phi \tilde{L}_{perf}$ is computed using the chain rule, combining the loss gradient $\frac{\partial \tilde{L}_{perf}}{\partial z^*}$, the solver sensitivity $\frac{\partial z^*}{\partial \theta_t}$, and the policy Jacobian $\frac{\partial \theta_t}{\partial \phi}$.
 - **Limitations**: Suffers from local descent without explicit exploration, requires a differentiable surrogate loss (precluding sparse/non-smooth objectives), and is prone to bias under model mismatch as it optimizes predictions rather than true closed-loop behavior. The authors implemented an improved GB-PL baseline with batching, constrained outputs, and a history-aware observation space compared to prior work.

2. **Reinforcement Learning (PPO Baseline)**:
 - Models the problem as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The plant plus NMPC controller constitutes the environment, and the agent adapts NMPC weights $\theta_t$ (actions) through interaction.
 - The reward $r_t$ is defined as the negative of the instantaneous high-level performance loss $L_{perf}(s_t, u_t)$, directly aligning the objective with realized closed-loop performance.
 - PPO, an on-policy actor-critic method, estimates policy updates from sampled closed-loop returns. It implicitly handles exploration, stochasticity, and model mismatch but is typically sample-inefficient.

**Solver-Gradient Guided Reinforcement Learning (SG-RL) Framework:**
The core idea is to inject the model-based NMPC solver loss gradient, $g_{\theta_{SG,t}} := \nabla_\theta \tilde{L}_{perf}(z^*(\theta))|_{\theta=\theta_t}$, as auxiliary guidance into PPO. This solver gradient is normalized to $g_{SG,t}$ to provide a direction-only signal, bounded to a magnitude of $\le 1$. If sensitivities are unavailable, $g_{SG,t}=0$. The PPO policy gradient is denoted $g_{RL} := \nabla_\phi L_{PPO}(\phi, \psi)$.

Four modular algorithms are proposed to integrate solver guidance into PPO components:
1. **Adaptive PPO Update Scaling (SG-SCA)**:
 - Lifts the weight-space solver gradient $g_{SG,t}$ to policy parameter space as $\tilde{g}_{SG,t} := \nabla_\phi \ell_{SG,t}(\phi)$ where $\ell_{SG,t}(\phi) := \mu_\phi(o_t)^\top g_{SG,t}$.
 - Computes an alignment $\rho$ between $\tilde{g}_{SG,t}$ and the PPO policy gradient $g_{RL}$.
 - Scales the PPO update step by an adaptive factor $\alpha = \text{clip}(1 + \lambda \cdot \rho, 0, \alpha_{max})$. This means larger steps are taken when solver guidance confirms the PPO direction, and smaller steps when they conflict.

2. **Performance-Gated Loss (SG-LOS)**:
 - Adds an auxiliary supervised loss $L_{guide}(\phi)$ to the standard PPO loss.
 - $L_{guide}(\phi) = \hat{E}_t[w_t \|\mu_\phi(o_t) - \theta_{target,t}\|_2^2]$, where $\theta_{target,t} = \bar{\theta}_t - \eta_{guide} g_{SG,t}$ is a local target weight.
 - The gating weight $w_t = \text{clip}(-\hat{A}_t, 0, w_{max})$ activates the guidance loss only for transitions with negative advantage (underperforming samples), pulling the policy towards solver-suggested corrections.

3. **Advantage Shaping (SG-ADV)**:
 - Modifies the PPO advantage estimate $\hat{A}_t$ with a gradient-based correction: $\hat{A}_{SG,t} = \hat{A}_t + \beta \Delta_{SG,t}$.
 - The correction term $\Delta_{SG,t} = - (\mu_\phi(o_t) - \bar{\theta}_t)^\top g_{SG,t}$ encodes consistency between the policy's mean weight displacement and the local loss landscape. Positive $\Delta_{SG,t}$ (displacement towards lower surrogate loss) increases the advantage, biasing exploration.

4. **Augmented Critic (SG-CRT)**:
 - Augments the critic's input with the solver-derived weight-space loss gradient $g_{SG,t}^-$ (from previous MPC solve).
 - The value function becomes $V_\psi(o_t, g_{SG,t}^-) = V_{\psi,base}(o_t) + c_\psi(g_{SG,t}^-)$, where $c_\psi$ is a small neural network that maps $g_{SG,t}^-$ to a residual correction.
 - $g_{SG,t}^-$ is treated as a detached, action-independent critic feature, aiming to reduce advantage variance without modifying the actor's gradient path.

**Optimization Interpretation:**
SG-RL aims to exploit a complementary bias-variance trade-off. Solver gradients, derived from a potentially biased surrogate model, offer informative local guidance early in training. RL gradients, directly optimizing closed-loop return, exhibit high variance initially. SG-RL methods introduce a controlled bias towards the surrogate objective, which is gradually annealed to allow PPO to dominate as learning progresses.

**Application Example: High-Speed Autonomous Racing:**
The framework is validated on two full-scale autonomous racing simulation platforms (Dallara AV-24 on Monza, Super Formula EAV-24 on Yas Marina).
- **Motivation**: Autonomous racing is characterized by tight constraints, nonlinearity, and rapid regime shifts (straights, braking, corners), making fixed weights suboptimal and motivating adaptation.
- **Vehicle Model and NMPC**: Employs a nonlinear single-track model with Pacejka Magic Formula tires, explicit aerodynamics, and state/actuation constraints. The NMPC tracks a time-optimal trajectory, and the learnable weight vector $\theta_t \in \mathbb{R}^7_{>0}$ parameterizes the quadratic stage and terminal costs (e.g., weights on lateral/yaw/velocity errors, longitudinal/lateral acceleration errors, jerk, steering rate).
- **Model Mismatch**: Intentional differences between the plant simulator and NMPC prediction model (e.g., input definitions, tire models, load transfer, aerodynamics) are introduced to test robustness.
- **Observations**: The policy receives current motion state (stacked velocity/yaw rate), performance history (stacked lateral/velocity deviations), and task anticipation (reference velocity/curvature look-ahead).
- **Reward**: The reward $r_t$ is defined as the negative of a fixed per-step performance loss $L_{perf}$, including squared lateral/velocity errors and exponential penalties for high jerk/steering rate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides quantitative evidence for the effectiveness of SG-RL in improving sample efficiency and closed-loop performance in autonomous racing.

**Performance and Sample Efficiency (Fig. 7, 8 & Table IV):**
- **Sample Efficiency**: All four SG-RL methods consistently reached PPO's best mean closed-loop return with significantly fewer training samples.
 - On AV-24/Monza, SG-SCA achieved this with 60.0% fewer samples (4.86 $\times 10^5$ vs PPO's 1.215 $\times 10^6$). SG-LOS/SG-ADV with 53.3% fewer, and SG-CRT with 46.7% fewer.
 - On EAV-24/Yas Marina, SG-SCA and SG-LOS achieved this with 70.6% fewer samples (4.05 $\times 10^5$). SG-ADV and SG-CRT still offered 47.1% and 35.3% sample savings, respectively.
- **Peak Performance**: SG-RL methods also improved PPO's peak mean reward.
 - On AV-24/Monza, SG-SCA improved PPO's peak mean reward by 33.2%, SG-LOS by 20.4%, SG-CRT by 18.5%, and SG-ADV by 14.1%.
- **Comparison to GB-PL Baselines**: All SG-RL variants significantly outperformed both the authors' improved GB-PL baseline and the reduced GB-PL baseline from prior work. Best-return gains over their GB-PL were 54.3%–59.5%, and 68.5%–72.0% over the reduced GB-PL.
- **Training Stability**: The min–max bands in the learning curves suggest improved training stability for SG-SCA and SG-LOS, exhibiting tighter concentration around high-return regimes earlier in training compared to PPO.

**Closed-Loop Performance Metrics (Table V):**
- SG-RL methods maintained or improved closed-loop quality. For instance, SG-SCA achieved tightest tracking (mean $|e_{lat}|$ of 0.034m vs PPO's 0.035m) and lowest maximum steering rates (0.34 rad/s vs PPO's 0.35 rad/s) on Monza, while handling higher peak jerk (42.55 m/s$^3$ vs PPO's 26.60 m/s$^3$). The human expert showed lower peak jerk but significantly worse tracking. GB-PL methods exhibited more aggressive control, particularly in peak jerk, but generally worse tracking than RL/SG-RL.

**Generalization in Unseen Environments (Fig. 9 & Table VI):**
- Policies trained on one track (Monza) demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer to unseen tracks (Laguna Seca, Suzuka).
- On Laguna Seca, SG-RL methods improved upon PPO's already strong performance over GB-PL (63.7% gain for PPO vs GB-PL), with SG-CRT achieving a 68.6% gain, SG-LOS 67.0%, and SG-SCA 66.4%.
- On Suzuka, SG-SCA achieved a 62.1% gain over GB-PL, surpassing PPO's 55.8% gain.
- These gains were achieved with policies trained on 50%–58% fewer samples than PPO.

**Explanation of Cost-Weight Policies via Correlation Structure (Fig. 10 & 11):**
- All policies strongly conditioned on velocity signals.
- GB-PL showed a strong, almost direct, velocity-aware structure, with velocity weights aligned with reference velocity and acceleration weights anti-correlated, suggesting an optimization-like trade-off.
- RL (PPO) exhibited a "see-saw" effect between aggressiveness/smoothness and lateral correction.
- SG-RL methods displayed distinct, method-specific structures:
 - SG-SCA primarily modulated steering smoothness based on curvature and showed a systematic lateral trade-off.
 - SG-LOS concentrated its strongest velocity dependence on steering smoothness and linked longitudinal aggressiveness to curvature, suggesting geometry-aware adaptation.
 - The analysis indicated coordinated velocity/smoothness scaling (e.g., $r_j$ with $q_v$) and objective rebalancing (e.g., $q_\psi$ with $q_\ell$) in guided methods.

**Closed-Loop Weight Adaptation Dynamics (Fig. 12):**
- **PPO**: Exhibited coarse, event-wise adaptation, keeping some weights (e.g., $q_\ell, q_a$) high and relying on bursts of others (e.g., $r_j, r_{\dot{\delta}}, q_v$) around braking/turn-in.
- **SG-SCA**: Showed sparser, targeted reweighting with sharper, localized activations for $q_\psi, q_v, q_{ay}$ near high-curvature/deceleration zones.
- **SG-LOS**: Demonstrated coordinated co-scheduling of smoothness-related objectives ($q_a, r_j, r_{\dot{\delta}}$ rising and falling together), indicating synchronized updates in critical performance regions.
- **GB-PL**: Displayed continuous variation in $q_v$ and $r_{\dot{\delta}}$, with $q_v$ closely tracking the reference-velocity profile, suggesting velocity-weight overfitting.

**Ablations: Removing Future and Past Context (Fig. 13 & Table VII):**
- **No Anticipation**: Removing reference lookahead (proactive context) systematically degraded performance across all methods, confirming the necessity of anticipatory information for effective weight adaptation. SG-SCA was most robust to this loss, and SG-CRT still outperformed pure RL.
- **No History**: Removing performance history (adaptive context) also degraded reward, though heterogeneously. PPO and SG-SCA suffered more without history than without anticipation. SG-LOS, SG-ADV, and SG-CRT retained more performance in this case. The GB-PL baseline degraded modestly, consistent with its myopic update but at the cost of overall optimality.
- **Solver Gradients under Information Scarcity**: Even with reduced temporal context, the strongest SG-guided methods continued to outperform the pure PPO baseline in peak reward.

**Training-Dynamics Analysis (Table VIII & IX):**
- Winning SG methods achieved better performance not through indiscriminately larger updates, but by more structured, regularized updates. They led to lower KL divergence and clip fractions (indicating more stable trust-region behavior), lower value loss, policy gradient loss, and entropy loss in performance-relevant windows.
- Method-specific analysis (Table IX) showed that SG-LOS's early gains reflected solver-biased shaping, while SG-SCA exhibited selective dampening of PPO steps diverging from solver directions in peak performance. The impact of SG-ADV and SG-CRT was more platform-specific.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Solver-Gradient Guided Reinforcement Learning (SG-RL) framework represents a significant advancement in learning online MPC cost-weight adaptation policies.

**Key Significance:**
1. **Bridging Model-Free and Model-Based Learning**: The work successfully demonstrates that environment-based policy gradients (model-free) and model-based solver gradients are complementary signals. SG-RL effectively combines the strengths of both, addressing the limitations of each standalone approach. This "grey-box" approach offers a path for leveraging rich model information without sacrificing the ability to optimize true closed-loop performance in the presence of model mismatch.
2. **Improved Sample Efficiency**: By using solver sensitivities as auxiliary guidance, SG-RL methods substantially reduce the sample requirements for RL to reach high performance. This is critical for real-world applications where data collection can be costly, time-consuming, or unsafe. The reported reduction of up to 70.6% in training samples is a notable achievement.
3. **Enhanced Performance and Stability**: SG-RL consistently achieved higher peak closed-loop returns compared to both PPO and GB-PL baselines. Furthermore, the analysis of training dynamics suggests improved stability (lower variance) in learning, as indicated by tighter performance bands and more controlled PPO metrics.
4. **Robustness to Model Mismatch and Generalization**: The validation on full-scale autonomous racing platforms with intentional model mismatch, combined with robust zero-shot generalization to unseen tracks, highlights the practical applicability and transferability of the learned policies. This indicates that SG-RL can learn effective adaptation strategies rather than overfitting to specific environments or relying solely on a perfect model.
5. **Interpretability**: The ability to analyze the correlation structure and time evolution of learned cost weights provides insights into *how* the policies adapt, fostering trust and facilitating debugging or further design.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Autonomous Systems**: The framework has direct and high impact on autonomous vehicles, robotics, and other complex, safety-critical systems where adaptive control is crucial. It can lead to more robust, efficient, and higher-performing autonomous agents capable of handling dynamic operating conditions.
- **Control Systems Engineering**: It provides a new paradigm for designing adaptive controllers, particularly for MPC, by offering a principled way to integrate first-order model information into data-driven learning. This could reduce the manual tuning effort often associated with MPC deployment.
- **Reinforcement Learning Research**: The work contributes to the broader field of RL by demonstrating a successful strategy for guided exploration and learning in continuous control tasks. The modular nature of the four SG-RL variants also provides a foundation for further research into specific mechanisms of guidance.
- **Differentiable Optimization**: It expands the practical utility of differentiable optimization in control, showing how solver sensitivities can be used not just for direct optimization but also to accelerate and stabilize model-free learning.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
- **Model Fidelity Dependence**: While robust under intentional mismatch, the effectiveness of SG-RL might diminish under severe or pathological model mismatch conditions. A systematic study of different mismatch types and magnitudes is warranted.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning**: Each SG-RL variant introduces method-specific hyperparameters, which may require careful tuning.
- **Algorithm Specificity**: The current work focuses on PPO. Extending SG-RL to off-policy algorithms like SAC and TD3 would broaden its applicability.
- **Theoretical Understanding**: A stronger theoretical foundation for how solver guidance interacts with stochastic policy-gradient optimization is an open research direction.
- **Generalization Beyond NMPC Cost Weights**: Applying SG-RL to other differentiable parametric controllers could unlock further potential.
- **Physical Validation**: The ultimate validation would involve deploying these learned policies on physical vehicles.

In conclusion, SG-RL offers a pragmatic and effective method for accelerating the learning of adaptive MPC policies, promising more capable and efficient autonomous systems through a synergistic combination of model-based and model-free intelligence.
