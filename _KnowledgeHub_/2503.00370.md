# Scalable Real2Sim: Physics-Aware Asset Generation Via Robotic Pick-and-Place Setups

> [!summary] Summary
> A fully automated Real2Sim pipeline turns real objects into simulation-ready assets inside a standard robotic pick-and-place setup. The robot re-grasps objects before a static RGBD camera for object-centric photometric reconstruction (alpha-transparent training plus gripper-mask exclusion), convex-decomposes the mesh for collision geometry, and identifies inertial parameters from joint torques along an information-maximizing excitation trajectory, reaching millimeter geometry and 1.34% mass error.

## Problem

- Building simulation assets demands manual geometry curation and hand-tuned mass and inertia, requiring expertise and scaling poorly to large object sets.
- Existing Real2Sim work targets either dynamic parameter identification or geometric reconstruction alone, and often needs custom scanning hardware or human-in-the-loop interfaces.
- Photometric reconstruction assumes static scenes with known camera poses, so moving, gripper-occluded objects cause density bleeding and wrongly transparent occluded regions.

## Method

- ==Object-centric reconstruction recipe==: object frame as world frame, ==alpha-transparent training== to kill background density bleeding, and gripper masks excluded from the photometric loss to preserve occluded surfaces.
- ==Autonomous scanning==: antipodal grasp pairs scored for perpendicularity and separation drive re-grasps along two axes, with ==SAM2== video segmentation and ==BundleSDF== tracking supplying masks and camera poses.
- ==Inertial identification== via ==SDP== with pseudo-inertia positive-definiteness constraints, plus excitation trajectories from a custom black-box ==augmented Lagrangian solver== optimizing condition number and E-optimality under collision and joint limits.

## Results

- Chamfer reconstruction error against YCB scanner models was **0.93mm**, **1.68mm**, **5.58mm**, and **0.80mm** for mustard bottle, potted meat can, bleach cleanser, and gelatin box.
- On the replicated benchmark, joint-torque identification gave **1.34%** mass and **2.15%** center-of-mass error, beating a force-torque baseline's **4.45%** and **4.50%**, though inertia error was worse at **42.35%**.
- An end-to-end 3D-printed mustard bottle (**427.1g** ground truth) yielded **1.23%** mass error and **6.33%** center-of-mass error, but **358.6%** rotational inertia error; **20** assets were released as a dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Alpha-transparent training plus explicit foreground-occlusion masking generalizes across NeRF, Gaussian Splatting, and surface-reconstruction backbones for object-centric meshes from dynamic scenes.
> - Mass and center of mass are recoverable to a few percent from joint torques alone, but rotational inertia stays poorly conditioned and should not be trusted without external sensing.
> - Physical-feasibility constraints must be reimposed on parameter differences, since the feasible lumped-parameter set is closed under addition but not subtraction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the intersection of several critical research areas in robotics and artificial intelligence, most notably **Sim2Real transfer, Real2Sim asset generation, 3D reconstruction, and robot system identification.**

**Sim2Real and the Need for Real2Sim:** The core motivation for this work stems from the growing paradigm of Sim2Real (simulation-to-reality), where robotic policies are trained extensively in simulation before being deployed in the real world. This approach is highly appealing, especially for data-hungry machine learning methods like reinforcement learning and the emerging field of foundation models in robotics, which require massive interaction datasets that are prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to collect in reality. However, a significant bottleneck in Sim2Real is the **Sim2Real gap**, often caused by inaccurate simulation models that do not faithfully represent the physical properties of real-world objects. This is where **Real2Sim** comes in: the process of generating accurate digital replicas (digital twins) of real-world objects for use in simulation. This paper directly addresses this gap by providing an automated Real2Sim pipeline.

**Advancements Beyond Prior Real2Sim Work:** The authors acknowledge prior work in Real2Sim but differentiate their contribution by offering a more comprehensive and automated solution. Previous methods often focused on either geometric reconstruction (e.g., Wang et al. for static objects, Downs et al. requiring custom hardware and manual handling, Torne et al. needing human input for modeling) or dynamic parameter identification in isolation. This paper uniquely integrates both aspects—visual geometry, collision geometry, and physical properties (mass, CoM, inertia)—into a single, autonomous pipeline, creating truly "complete" simulation assets.

**3D Reconstruction and Neural Rendering:** The geometric reconstruction component builds upon recent advances in 3D reconstruction, particularly implicit representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and Gaussian Splatting. While these methods excel at high-quality rendering, they are typically designed for static scenes and struggle with generating structured geometry from dynamic, object-centric views, especially with occlusions. This paper makes a specific contribution by proposing a general recipe to adapt these SOTA photometric methods for robust, object-centric mesh extraction, even when objects are moving and partially occluded by a robot gripper. This pushes the boundaries of how neural rendering techniques can be utilized for practical robotics applications requiring explicit geometric models.

**Inertial Parameter Estimation and Optimal Experiment Design:** The physical parameter identification component is rooted in the well-established field of robot system identification. The paper leverages techniques for identifying payload parameters using joint-torque sensors, contrasting it with methods requiring force-torque sensors. A critical challenge in this area is ensuring physically feasible parameter estimates and collecting informative data. The authors adopt approaches that enforce physical consistency via Linear Matrix Inequal (LMIs), and tackle the "informative data" problem through **optimal excitation trajectory design**–. They contribute by integrating these methods into `Drake` for arbitrary robots and by developing a custom augmented Lagrangian solver to overcome numerical challenges in optimizing information-maximizing objectives under complex constraints (like collision avoidance), a limitation of prior gradient-based methods.

**Contrast with Generative AI:** The paper also briefly contextualizes its approach against generative AI methods for asset creation (e.g., Gen2Sim, Zero-1-to-3). While generative models can create large datasets quickly, they often produce approximations or "AI-imagined" objects. The authors argue that Real2Sim is preferable when high accuracy reflecting real-world objects and their physics is paramount, citing evidence that accurate physical parameters are crucial for successful Sim2Real transfer in complex manipulation tasks.

In essence, this work integrates sophisticated perception, rigorous dynamics modeling, and advanced optimization into a cohesive, automated system. It provides a crucial missing piece for scaling up data generation for model-based robotics, particularly relevant for the next generation of foundation models in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **automate and scale the creation of high-fidelity, simulation-ready digital twins for real-world objects.** This objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from the challenges in contemporary robotics research:

1. **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap:** The primary motivation is to address the "Sim2Real gap." While training robots in simulation offers significant advantages (safety, speed, data collection scale), the effectiveness of Sim2Real transfer heavily depends on the fidelity of the simulation. Inaccurate object models—both geometrically and physically—lead to policies that fail when deployed in the real world. The authors aim to close this gap by ensuring that simulation assets are faithful representations of their real-world counterparts.

2. **Enabling Scalable Dataset Creation for Foundation Models:** The rise of data-intensive machine learning paradigms, such as reinforcement learning and, more recently, large foundation models in robotics, demands unprecedented amounts of interaction data. Manually creating digital assets for a diverse range of objects is prohibitively time-consuming, expensive, and requires specialized expertise. This manual bottleneck severely limits the scale and diversity of datasets available for training advanced robotic policies. The paper's goal is to remove this bottleneck through automation, enabling the creation of large-scale, diverse datasets.

3. **Generating Complete Simulation Assets:** Existing Real2Sim methods often focus on either geometry or physical properties in isolation. However, a truly "simulation-ready" asset requires a comprehensive description:
 - **Visual Geometry:** A detailed, textured mesh for realistic rendering.
 - **Collision Geometry:** A simplified, convex representation suitable for efficient physics simulations.
 - **Physical Properties:** Accurate mass, center of mass, and rotational inertia for correct dynamic behavior.
 The authors aim to deliver all these components within a single, integrated pipeline.

4. **Minimizing Human Intervention and Hardware Customization:** A crucial practical motivation is to make the asset generation process autonomous and compatible with existing robotic setups. Unlike prior work that might require manual object handling, specialized scanning hardware, or human input for modeling, this pipeline is designed to integrate directly into standard robotic pick-and-place workstations. This "no-extra-hardware, no-human-intervention" requirement is vital for industrial adoption and true scalability.

5. **Robust Object-Centric Reconstruction from Challenging Data:** A specific technical motivation is to adapt advanced 3D reconstruction techniques, typically designed for static scenes, to the more challenging scenario of a moving object that is partially occluded by the robot's gripper. This involves developing novel techniques to handle background subtraction, density bleeding, and gripper occlusion during photometric reconstruction.

6. **Reliable Physical Parameter Identification under Constraints:** For dynamic parameter identification, a key objective is to generate "informative" robot trajectories that maximally excite the object's physical parameters. This needs to be done robustly, considering arbitrary robot specifications and practical constraints such as collision avoidance, joint limits, and smooth motion profiles, which are often difficult for existing optimization methods to handle.

In summary, the research is motivated by the critical need for high-quality, scalable, and automated digital asset creation to accelerate advances in simulation-driven robotics, particularly for data-intensive AI models, while ensuring practical deployability in real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed "Real2Sim" pipeline is a fully automated, multi-stage system designed to convert real-world objects into complete, simulatable digital assets using a standard robotic pick-and-place setup. The process for each object involves geometric reconstruction, collision geometry derivation, and physical parameter identification, followed by placement into a second bin.

**A. Overall Workflow (Figure 1):**
The pipeline begins with objects in a "first bin." A robotic arm picks an object, performs a sequence of interactions for data collection, then places it into a "second bin." This repeats until the first bin is empty. This structure ensures full autonomy and integration into existing robotic workflows.

**B. Geometric Reconstruction for Visual Geometry (Section III-B):**
This stage focuses on creating a detailed, textured 3D mesh (visual geometry V) of the object.

1. **Data Collection and Processing (III-B.1):**
 - **Robotic Interaction:** The robot grasps the object and moves it in front of a static RGBD camera. To capture all surfaces and reduce occlusions, the robot performs a series of **re-grasps**, rotating the object along two perpendicular axes. Grasp pairs are selected based on antipodal quality and a "pair score" to ensure perpendicularity and spatial separation.
 - **Image Acquisition:** Redundant RGB images are collected, then downsampled for computational efficiency. A diverse set of frames is selected using DINO feature space cosine distance.
 - **Segmentation:** Object masks ($M_O$) and gripper masks ($M_G$) are extracted using SAM2 video segmentation. These masks are crucial for isolating the object from the background and the robot's gripper.
 - **Object Tracking:** BundleSDF is used to track the object's pose ($X_C$) relative to the camera throughout the dynamic motion.

2. **Geometric Reconstruction (III-B.2):**
 - **Object-Centric Adaptation of SOTA Methods:** The paper introduces a general "recipe" to adapt state-of-the-art implicit reconstruction methods (e.g., NeRF, Gaussian Splatting, Neuralangelo) for dynamic, object-centric scenes, which typically assume static scenes and known camera poses.
 - **Dynamic to Static:** The object frame is redefined as the world frame, and background pixels are masked out. The object's tracked poses ($X_C$) effectively become the camera poses relative to this new, static object-world frame.
 - **Alpha-Transparent Training:** To combat "density bleeding" (where the model assigns non-zero density to empty space), alpha-transparent training is employed. This method replaces background pixels with random colors during training, forcing the model to assign zero density to transparent regions by minimizing the loss against these random, unpredictable colors.
 - **Gripper Occlusion Handling:** Gripper masks ($M_G$) are used to explicitly exclude pixels occluded by the gripper from the reconstruction objective. This prevents the model from incorrectly making occluded object regions transparent.
 - **Depth Supervision:** For featureless objects, depth data is optionally used to improve accuracy and resolve photometric ambiguities.

**C. Collision Geometries (Section III-C):**
The detailed visual geometry (V) is converted into a simplified, computationally efficient collision geometry (C) suitable for physics simulations. This is achieved using **approximate convex decomposition algorithms**, which split the visual mesh into several nearly convex components and then simplify each component using convex hulls. This process aims to balance simulation accuracy with computational speed.

**D. Physical Parameter Identification (Section III-D):**
This stage determines the object's inertial properties (mass, center of mass, rotational inertia).

1. **Parameters to Identify (III-D.1):** For the object, these are mass ($m_B$), center of mass ($p_{com_B}$), and rotational inertia ($I_B$). The physical feasibility of these parameters is enforced by ensuring the pseudo-inertia matrix ($J_B$) is positive definite. For the robot itself, individual link inertial parameters, joint friction coefficients ($\mu_v, \mu_c$), and reflected rotor inertia ($I_r$) are identified.

2. **Robot Identification (III-D.2):**
 - **Manipulator Equations:** The robot's dynamics are described by the manipulator equations (Eq. 2), which are affine in the dynamic parameters.
 - **Least-Squares Optimization:** By collecting joint torque ($\tau$) and kinematic state ($q, \dot{q}, \ddot{q}$) data, a linear least-squares problem (Eq. 3) is formed to solve for the robot's parameters ($\alpha$).
 - **Physical Feasibility:** This problem is formulated as a Semi-Definite Program (SDP) (Eq. 4) to impose physical consistency constraints (e.g., positive definite pseudo-inertia for each link, non-negative friction/rotor inertia). The authors use a modified version to exclude unidentifiable parameters and add regularization.

3. **Optimal Excitation Trajectory Design (III-D.3):**
 - **Informative Data:** To ensure accurate parameter identification, the robot must follow trajectories that sufficiently "excite" all parameters. This is achieved by designing trajectories that maximize the information content of the regressor matrix $W(q)$.
 - **Optimization Objective:** The optimization minimizes a weighted combination of the condition number (to balance information distribution) and the E-optimality criterion (to minimize the largest uncertainty) of the $W(q)^T W(q)$ matrix eigenvalues.
 - **Constraints:** The trajectory optimization considers critical robot constraints: collision avoidance, joint position/velocity/acceleration limits, and zero start/end velocities/accelerations.
 - **Solver:** The problem is challenging due to the eigenvalue-dependent cost function and complex constraints. The authors introduce a **custom augmented Lagrangian solver** that uses **black-box optimization** for its subproblems. This approach, while computationally more intensive, is less prone to local minima compared to gradient-based methods, which is acceptable for an offline process. The implementation in `Drake` allows for arbitrary robots and constraints.

4. **Object Identification (III-D.4):**
 - **Lumped Parameter Linearity:** After the robot's parameters are identified, the robot re-grasps the object, and the identification process is repeated for the robot-payload composite system. The object's parameters are then calculated as the difference between the composite and robot-only parameter sets (Eq. 6), leveraging the linearity of lumped parameters.
 - **Physical Feasibility of Difference:** A pseudo-inertia constraint ($J_p \succ 0$) is added for the object's parameters to ensure physical feasibility, as estimation errors can lead to invalid results (e.g., negative mass) when subtracting parameter sets.
 - **Error Mitigation:** The same excitation trajectory is used for both robot-only and robot-with-object identification to mitigate systematic errors. Gripper pose variations are handled by identifying the robot with multiple gripper openings and matching the closest one during object identification.

**E. Implementation Details (Section IV):**
- Grasp and motion planning utilize methods from and.
- Object tracking uses BundleSDF.
- Geometric reconstruction uses Nerfstudio, Gaussian Frosting, and Neuralangelo with the proposed recipe.
- Physical parameter identification involves collecting multiple trajectories, averaging data to improve SNR, double differentiating and low-pass filtering kinematics, and using ICP for aligning reconstructed meshes with known grasp positions to express inertial parameters in the object frame.

This comprehensive methodology integrates perception, control, and dynamics, emphasizing automation, robustness, and physical fidelity to generate complete digital twins.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive experimental validation for both the geometric reconstruction and physical parameter identification components, as well as qualitative demonstrations of the full pipeline's performance in simulation.

**A. Geometric Reconstruction (Section V-A):**
1. **Accuracy:** Quantitative evaluation using Chamfer distance on selected YCB objects (mustard bottle, potted meat can, bleach cleanser, gelatin box) against their 3D scanner models shows **millimeter-level accuracy**, with errors ranging from 0.80mm to 5.58mm. This confirms that the system produces accurate and complete object reconstructions.
2. **Completeness:** Qualitative results (Figures 5 and 6) visually demonstrate the pipeline's ability to reconstruct entire objects, including regions that were initially occluded by the robot's gripper or faced away from the camera. This highlights the effectiveness of the re-grasping strategy and the proposed object-centric reconstruction recipe.
3. **Topology Considerations:** While BundleSDF (used for tracking and initial mesh generation) yields good visual reconstructions, the authors note it produces meshes with poor topology (e.g., scattered boundary faces, non-manifold vertices), which can complicate rendering in simpler engines or pose challenges for simulators requiring watertight meshes. They demonstrate that using higher-quality reconstruction methods like Neuralangelo with their recipe significantly improves mesh topology and consistent rendering quality across different renderers (Figure 7).

**B. Physical Parameter Identification (Section V-B):**
1. **Benchmark Comparison:** The authors replicate a benchmark from and compare their method (using joint-torque sensors) against a SOTA method ('PMD-25K') and an OLS baseline (both using force-torque sensors).
 - **Mass and CoM:** Their method achieves **superior accuracy for mass (1.34% error vs. 4.45%) and center of mass (2.15% error vs. 4.50%)** compared to 'PMD-25K'.
 - **Rotational Inertia:** It performs slightly worse for rotational inertia (42.35% error vs. 29.11%), but still significantly outperforms the OLS baseline.
 - **Sensor Type:** It's noteworthy that these results are achieved using only joint-torque sensors, which provide indirect force measurements and are generally considered more challenging for identification than direct force-torque sensors.
2. **End-to-End Validation:** An end-to-end experiment with a 3D-printed mustard bottle (with known ground truth inertia) showed mass error of 1.23%, CoM error of 6.33%, and a higher rotational inertia error of 358.6%. The high inertia error is attributed to challenges introduced by camera calibration errors and limitations of joint-torque sensing, but the overall results remain encouraging for an automated system.
3. **Robustness:** The use of optimal excitation trajectories, physical feasibility constraints (SDP, pseudo-inertia), and explicit handling of gripper pose variations contributed to the robustness of the identification process.

**C. Simulation Performance (Section V-C):**
1. **Simulatable Assets:** The pipeline successfully generates assets that can be directly imported and used within physics simulators like `Drake`.
2. **Qualitative Correspondence:** Due to the inherent sensitivity of simulation rollouts to initial conditions, a direct quantitative comparison with real-world rollouts is challenging. Instead, the authors provide qualitative evaluations and side-by-side comparisons (Figure 8), demonstrating plausible and visually convincing simulated behaviors for tasks like pick-and-place, knocking over, and falling down a ramp. Interactive simulations are available on the project page.
3. **Benchmark Dataset:** As a significant result, the authors generated a **benchmark dataset of 20 assets** using their pipeline, including raw sensor observations. This dataset is a valuable contribution, allowing other researchers to evaluate and improve individual components of the pipeline (tracking, reconstruction, inertia estimation) without requiring access to a physical robotic system.

In summary, the main findings validate the pipeline's ability to accurately reconstruct object geometry and identify key physical properties fully autonomously, making it a viable and scalable solution for creating simulation-ready digital twins. While challenges remain, particularly with rotational inertia estimation and robust simulation-to-reality correspondence, the overall performance represents a substantial step forward in automated Real2Sim.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Scalable Real2Sim" pipeline represents a significant advancement in robotics and AI, addressing a critical bottleneck that has hindered the widespread adoption and scaling of simulation-driven research. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Enabling Scalable Robotics Datasets for Foundation Models:** This is arguably the most profound impact. The current surge in interest in foundation models for robotics necessitates vast, diverse, and high-fidelity datasets. Manually creating these datasets is infeasible. By automating the generation of complete digital twins (visual geometry, collision geometry, and physical properties) from real objects, this pipeline unlocks the potential for generating massive datasets needed to train and validate next-generation robotic AI. This could accelerate progress in areas like general-purpose manipulation, dexterous grasping, and complex task execution.

2. **Accelerating Sim2Real Transfer:** By providing more accurate simulation assets, the pipeline directly reduces the Sim2Real gap. When simulated objects closely mirror their real-world counterparts, policies learned in simulation are more likely to transfer effectively to physical robots, reducing deployment costs and time. This improves the reliability and practicality of Sim2Real learning for real-world applications.

3. **Democratizing Digital Twin Creation:** The elimination of manual intervention, specialized expertise, and custom hardware makes the process of creating digital twins far more accessible. Any lab or industry setup with a standard pick-and-place robot can leverage this pipeline. This democratization could foster innovation by allowing more researchers and developers to create rich, physically accurate simulation environments.

4. **Integration into Industrial Workflows:** The design to integrate seamlessly into existing pick-and-place setups has significant implications for industries like warehouse automation (where Amazon Robotics' involvement is highly relevant). Robots could passively scan new objects as part of their routine operations, continuously updating their digital inventory without disrupting workflows. This real-time, automated asset generation capability is a game-changer for maintaining digital twins of dynamic and evolving physical environments.

5. **Advancements in 3D Reconstruction and System Identification:** Beyond the integrated pipeline, the paper contributes novel techniques and practical implementations in its constituent sub-fields:
 - **Object-centric 3D Reconstruction:** The general recipe for adapting SOTA photometric reconstruction methods (like NeRF) to dynamic, occluded, object-centric scenarios is a valuable contribution to computer vision, extending the applicability of these powerful rendering techniques to robotics.
 - **Robust Dynamic Parameter Identification:** The practical implementation of optimal excitation trajectory design, especially the custom augmented Lagrangian solver for handling arbitrary constraints, pushes the boundaries of robot system identification, making it more robust and applicable to diverse robot platforms.

6. **Benchmark Dataset for Future Research:** The release of a benchmark dataset of 20 generated assets, including raw sensor observations, is a crucial contribution to the research community. It provides a standardized resource for evaluating and comparing future work on individual pipeline components (e.g., object tracking, geometric reconstruction, inertia estimation), fostering iterative improvements without requiring access to expensive robotic hardware.

**Limitations and Future Work (acknowledged by authors):** While significant, the paper also points to areas for future improvement:
- **Contact Parameter Estimation:** Currently, only inertial parameters are identified. Estimating contact parameters (e.g., friction coefficients, restitution) is essential for even more realistic physics simulations.
- **Handling Slippage:** The current approach assumes a rigid grasp during parameter identification; accounting for slippage would improve robustness.
- **Collision Geometry Representation:** Exploring more optimal collision geometry representations (e.g., primitive shapes, better convex decomposition for hydroelastic contact) could enhance simulation speed and accuracy.
- **Robust Simulation Evaluation:** Developing more robust methods for comparing simulation performance with reality (e.g., comparing distributions of trajectories rather than individual rollouts) is an important avenue.

In conclusion, "Scalable Real2Sim" offers a compelling solution to a long-standing challenge in robotics. Its potential to automate the creation of high-fidelity digital twins is transformative, promising to accelerate the development of more intelligent and capable robots by bridging the critical gap between simulated and real-world interactions.
