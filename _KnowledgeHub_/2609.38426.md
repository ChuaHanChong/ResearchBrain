# LoopVL: Recurrent Visual Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> LoopVL is a 1B-scale recurrent vision-language model. It repeatedly applies two shared 16-layer Transformer stacks to a joint image-text state: a fast low-level L module and a slower high-level H module. This executes 128 layer calls from only 32 unique layers. Trained on only 0.14T tokens, it matches or beats dense VLMs trained on 11-36T tokens, and its visual attention shifts sharply at cycle boundaries.

## Problem

- Looped Transformers, which deepen computation by reusing shared parameters, have been studied almost exclusively in language models, leaving their extension to vision-language models largely untested.
- Conventional VLMs gain capability by stacking more independently parameterized layers or widening them, demanding proportionally more parameters, FLOPs and trillions of training tokens.
- Prior recurrent models ignore that visual-token importance changes after each vision-language interaction, so it is unclear whether loops should keep updating visual states or reread fixed evidence.

## Method

- Nested ==Module-Loop== and ==Model-Loop== recurrence: the shared L stack runs three times under a fixed H condition, then H integrates the refined state, repeated over two cycles (==H2L3 schedule==).
- ==Visual anchor re-injection==: each model cycle re-injects the initial projected visual embeddings into H's visual positions, controlled by a ==query-conditioned token-wise gate== and a learnable cycle-specific scale.
- **LoopVL** staged from-scratch training: 75B-token HRM-Text-based language pretraining, frozen-encoder alignment and mid-training, SFT, then ==GRPO== with progressive reasoning length, backpropagating only through the final recurrent invocations.

## Results

- At an equal **0.14T-token** budget, LoopVL beats a 32-layer Transformer-VL 1B (**MMStar 63.47 vs 55.33**, **ChartQA 74.52 vs 51.12**) and rivals 4B deep and wide baselines with fewer FLOPs.
- Against compact VLMs trained on 11-36T tokens, LoopVL-1B leads on **LogicVista (45.19 vs 36.91)**, **MMK12-Math (61.60 vs 54.80)** and **MMMU-Pro (38.67 vs 38.30)**.
- H2L1 beats H1L3 at the same 64 layer calls. Extra inference cycles hurt (**H4L3 MMStar 24.00 vs 63.47**), and visual-attention Gini jumps from **0.405 to 0.858** at the cycle boundary.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - How recurrent computation is organized, meaning the ordering of fast local refinement and slow global updates, matters as much as total unrolled depth. Equal compute can give very different accuracy.
> - A looped model trained at a fixed schedule does not automatically benefit from more test-time loops. Training and inference recurrence must match unless depth extrapolation is explicitly trained.
> - Letting visual tokens keep updating inside the language backbone, instead of treating them as fixed context, lets the same shared layers reread and reallocate visual evidence across cycles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

LoopVL tackles a practical bottleneck in vision–language modeling. Adding depth conventionally means storing more independently parameterized layers. Yet answering a question about an image, such as **"How many cyan squares?"**, may require locating the relevant evidence rather than processing the image once. LoopVL lets the same internal image-and-language processing modules revisit an updated representation. In this selected example, the attention maps at the ends of the two cycles show a changed allocation. The authors report more attention on the annotated target in the color and cyan-square examples.

### 2. Revisiting an image with shared modules

A visual encoder turns image patches into spatially aligned visual tokens. A projector then places these tokens into the language model's sequence alongside the instruction. LoopVL uses two distinct stacks, L and H, each with 16 Transformer layers.

- **L** is invoked three times under the current H state.
- **H** then integrates those updates.

The two-cycle H2L3 schedule is L→L→L→H, repeated twice. Each stack reuses its own parameters across invocations, but L and H do not share parameters with each other. The model therefore executes 128 Transformer-layer calls while storing two 16-layer stacks.

The encoder's image features are not recomputed at every cycle. Instead, visual-token states keep changing inside the recurrent backbone. At the start of each model cycle, the model also reinjects an anchor from the initial projected visual embeddings. A query-conditioned token-wise gate and a learnable cycle-specific scale control its strength.

(a) The visual encoder and projector supply visual-token positions that join instruction tokens. A complete L module repeats three times before H, and that sequence repeats for two model cycles. (b) An L/H module, with ×16 Transformer layers. This is an architecture schematic, not a data-flow measurement.

### 3. What the score comparison shows

On **MMStar**, a general-understanding benchmark scored by accuracy, LoopVL-1B scores 63.47% and Transformer-VL 1B scores 55.33%. Both models were trained on 0.14T tokens. The paper says they share the number of unique Transformer layers and hidden dimension, but LoopVL executes 128 layer calls rather than 32.

This is not an equal-compute comparison. Reported training FLOPs are 2.47 × 10²¹ for LoopVL and 0.92 × 10²¹ for Transformer-VL 1B. The result therefore supports a performance difference under the same token budget and comparable stored backbone. It does not show lower training compute or faster inference.

A second test restricts second-cycle visual updates. The normal model is compared with two inference-only controls and with a separately trained read-only variant that permits visual updates only in cycle one. Normal achieves the highest accuracy, and the read-only trained variant remains below it. In these controls, visual tokens remain readable and nonvisual states continue updating. The result supports the importance of visual updates under the tested setup. It does not identify visual updating as the only cause.

Accuracy (%) on LogicVista, RealWorldQA, VMCBench-DEV, and MMStar under four conditions: Normal, Block L visual updates, Freeze visual states, and Read-only trained. Normal is highest in all four panels. Read-only trained is a separately trained variant.

### 4. A shift, not a solved mechanism

The authors' "Visual Aha Moment" names a sharp change in visual-attention concentration at the boundary between the two model cycles. In a 32-example diagnostic set, mean Gini concentration rises from 0.405 to 0.858 between layer indices 63 and 64. This describes a concentration shift, not proof that the most-attended patches caused correct answers.

The authors lack a separately trained, more general Model-Loop vision–language control. Their results characterize one hierarchical framework, image tasks, and a frozen visual encoder.

LoopVL adds recurrent computation without storing separate parameters for every executed layer, and it makes the changing visual state inspectable across cycles. The authors frame this as evidence that recurrent computation can be extended to vision–language models. They do not present it as proof that recurrence universally improves multimodal reasoning.
