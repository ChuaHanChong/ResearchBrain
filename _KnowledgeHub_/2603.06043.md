# Learning to Generate via Understanding: Understanding-Driven Intrinsic Rewarding for Unified Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced Generate via Understanding (GvU), a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework that leverages a Unified Multimodal Model's internal visual understanding to provide token-level intrinsic rewards for its generative component. This approach improved compositional text-to-image generation by 43.3% on the GenEval++ benchmark and concurrently enhanced fine-grained visual understanding.

## Problem

- Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) exhibit an imbalance where visual understanding often outperforms visual generation, limiting their ability to produce semantically coherent images from complex text prompts.
- Current methods for improving UMM generation often rely on external, coarse reward signals from human preferences or separate models, which can be susceptible to reward hacking and lack fine-grained guidance for intricate semantic details.
- The joint optimization of understanding and generation in UMMs can lead to "negative transfer," hindering performance in one or both tasks due to intrinsic decoupling during training.

## Method

- A self-supervised reinforcement learning (RL) framework was developed that uses an ==autoregressive== UMM's own understanding branch to evaluate generated images against the original text prompts.
- A token-level intrinsic reward mechanism, named Generate via Understanding (GvU), was introduced to quantify the fine-grained alignment between a generated image and its text prompt by computing the model's intrinsic probability of the ground-truth text tokens conditioned on the generated image.
- The intrinsic reward was integrated into a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm to iteratively update the UMM's generation policy, maximizing an objective function based on normalized intra-group rewards.

## Results

- GvU achieved a **43.3%** relative improvement in compositional text-to-image generation on the GenEval++ benchmark and strong performance across GenEval and DPG-Bench, demonstrating enhanced alignment and coherent spatial layouts.
- The framework improved fine-grained visual understanding, with notable gains in MMT-Bench subtasks such as Visual Illusion (+5.06) and Visual Commonsense Reasoning (+5.0), suggesting a synergistic benefit.
- Ablation studies showed that GvU yielded substantially larger performance gains on base models with a wider initial gap between understanding and generation, confirming its efficacy in knowledge transfer and guiding learning for less capable models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A UMM's internal visual understanding capabilities can effectively serve as a fine-grained, self-sufficient intrinsic reward signal to guide and enhance its generative performance.
> - Token-level intrinsic feedback is crucial for driving improvements in complex compositional generation tasks, enabling UMMs to better reflect spatial relationships, quantitative reasoning, and attribute bindings.
> - Enhancing a UMM's generative abilities through understanding-driven intrinsic rewards can create a synergistic effect, leading to a concurrent improvement in its fine-grained visual understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the evolving field of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs). UMMs represent an area of artificial intelligence research that seeks to integrate both visual understanding and generation capabilities within a single model architecture and shared parameters. This approach aims to enable models to perform complex tasks, particularly in text-to-image (T2I) generation, where fine-grained instruction following is crucial. Unlike traditional T2I models, UMMs leverage their advanced visual comprehension to potentially generate images that accurately reflect intricate spatial relationships, quantitative reasoning, and attribute bindings described in textual prompts.

Despite the theoretical promise of UMMs to achieve a synergistic relationship between understanding and generation, a persistent performance imbalance has been observed. Current UMMs frequently demonstrate stronger capabilities in visual understanding tasks (e.g., image-to-text mapping) compared to visual generation tasks (e.g., text-to-image mapping). This "understanding–generation gap" is attributed to several factors, including training pipelines that may prioritize understanding, and the phenomenon of "negative transfer" where joint optimization of both tasks can lead to hindering performance in one or both. The intrinsic decoupling of these processes during training further exacerbates this asymmetry.

Existing approaches to enhance UMM generation often involve reinforcement learning (RL) guided by external reward signals, such as text-image alignment metrics or human preference ratings. However, these methods can be susceptible to reward hacking and may lack the fine-grained granularity needed to address subtle semantic details in complex compositional generation tasks. This paper positions itself by proposing an internal, self-supervised mechanism that leverages the UMM's *own* understanding capabilities to provide intrinsic, fine-grained feedback to its generation component, thereby moving towards a more integrated and self-improving multimodal system. It explores the dualistic nature of understanding and generation to turn the model's internal capabilities into a source of self-supervision, without relying on external annotation or models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generative capabilities of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) by harnessing their inherent visual understanding capacity. This is motivated by the observed discrepancy in current UMMs, where visual understanding typically outperforms visual generation.

Specifically, the key objectives and their underlying motivations are:

1. **Bridge the Understanding–Generation Gap:** The central motivation is to overcome the performance imbalance in UMMs. While UMMs can accurately interpret fine-grained visual details (understanding), they often struggle to produce semantically coherent images that faithfully reflect complex textual prompts (generation). The goal is to narrow this gap, enabling UMMs to translate their comprehension into improved generative outputs.
2. **Leverage Intrinsic Understanding for Generation:** The researchers hypothesize that a UMM's visual understanding branch can serve as an internal evaluator for its generation branch. Since the text input to a T2I task provides a comprehensive semantic blueprint for the desired image, the understanding component can assess the alignment between a generated image and the conditioning text. This eliminates the need for external supervision, promoting data efficiency and computational autonomy.
3. **Develop a Fine-Grained, Token-Level Intrinsic Reward Mechanism:** Traditional reward functions in multimodal reinforcement learning often provide image-level feedback, which may be too coarse to guide the generation of intricate semantic details, spatial relationships, or multiple object attributes. A key objective is to propose a novel reward mechanism, termed GvU (Generate via Understanding), that provides token-level text-image alignment signals. This fine-grained feedback is intended to allow the generation branch to refine its outputs with greater precision and semantic accuracy.
4. **Design a Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning Framework:** To operationalize the intrinsic reward, a self-supervised reinforcement learning (RL) framework is required. This framework would allow UMMs to iteratively improve their generation quality by using the understanding-based intrinsic reward signals. The motivation here is to enable continuous self-improvement within the UMM, fostering a "self-teaching" paradigm where the understanding branch acts as a "teacher" guiding the generation branch as a "student."
5. **Promote Cross-Task Synergy:** The paper also aims to demonstrate that enhancing generative performance through understanding-driven intrinsic rewards can, in turn, strengthen the UMM's fine-grained visual understanding. This objective directly addresses the challenge of "negative transfer" sometimes observed in joint training, aiming to transform potential antagonism between tasks into a synergistic relationship where both capabilities mutually benefit and advance.

In summary, the motivation stems from the fundamental division within UMMs and the belief that the inherent duality between understanding and generation can be exploited to create a self-evolving system that requires minimal external guidance, ultimately leading to more capable and unified multimodal AI models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a self-supervised reinforcement learning (RL) framework that leverages a UMM's internal understanding capabilities to enhance its generative performance. This framework introduces a token-level intrinsic reward mechanism called Generate via Understanding (GvU).

#### 3.1. Self-Generation Pipeline in UMMs

The core of the approach involves a closed-loop training pipeline that operates without external image resources or reward models. It uses an autoregressive (AR) backbone with a diffusion head for the UMM architecture, specifically building upon the pretrained parameters of X-Omni.

1. **Data Generation:** Given a dataset of text prompts, T = T_{1:L}, the model's generation branch takes these prompts as input. It autoregressively models the causal distribution over text tokens (Eq. 1) and then, conditioned on the complete text sequence, generates image tokens causally (Eq. 2). These image tokens are subsequently decoded into a pixel-space image through the diffusion head.
2. **Image Understanding and Reward Computation:** On the understanding branch, the *same* UMM processes the newly generated images (I) along with the *original* text prompts. Instead of sampling new text, the model computes the intrinsic probability of the ground-truth prompt tokens conditioned on the generated image (Eq. 3). This process forms a self-contained generation-evaluation cycle.

#### 3.2. Token-level Model-Intrinsic Reward (GvU)

To provide fine-grained feedback, the GvU mechanism quantifies the alignment between a generated image (I) and its corresponding text prompt (T) by exploiting the UMM's intrinsic generation probabilities.

1. **Token-level Probability Calculation:** For a given text prompt T = T_{1:L} and a generated image I, the image is first converted into a discrete representation compatible with the AR architecture. This representation, concatenated with special instruction tokens, forms the input sequence X_0 = [I]. The model then autoregressively computes the probability of each token T_j in the prompt T, conditioned on the preceding input sequence X_{j-1} (Eq. 4). This probability, p_θ(T_j | X_{j-1}), is derived from the softmax of the model's logits for the ground-truth token T_j.
2. **Overall Intrinsic Reward:** To mitigate length bias, the overall probability P(T_{1:L} | I) is calculated as the geometric mean of all individual token probabilities (Eq. 5). This probability value serves as the fine-grained intrinsic reward signal, reflecting the UMM's internal assessment of the visual-textual alignment of the {T, I} pair.

#### 3.3. Self-Supervised RL Optimization

The intrinsic reward is integrated into a self-supervised RL framework based on the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm. This choice aims to improve computational efficiency by not requiring a separate value function or external reward models.

1. **Trajectory Generation:** For each textual prompt T, the policy model π_θ generates a group of G trajectories, where each trajectory corresponds to the generation process of an image I_i.
2. **Reward and Advantage Estimation:** For each generated image I_i in the group, the GvU mechanism provides a model-intrinsic reward R_i(T, I_i) = P(T | I_i) (Eq. 6). An advantage A_i for each generated process is then estimated by normalizing the individual reward against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the group (Eq. 7). This intra-group comparison helps in understanding relative performance.
3. **Policy Update:** The policy model θ is updated by maximizing the GRPO objective function (Eq. 8). This objective function incorporates a probability ratio r_i(θ) between the current and old policies, a clipping mechanism to stabilize updates (similar to PPO), and a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term to regularize the policy updates and prevent large deviations from a reference model (π_ref). This ensures stable and controlled learning.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Model Architecture:** The study employs a unified multimodal model with an autoregressive (AR) module for semantic token processing and a diffusion head for image reconstruction. The base model uses pretrained parameters of X-Omni, and LoRA is used during RL training.
- **Training Data:** A text-only prompt dataset of 50,000 samples, focusing on compositional aspects like positional, quantitative, and fine-grained attribute relationships.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - **Visual Generation:** GenEval, DPG-Bench, and GenEval++ for compositional generation, object recognition, counting, colors, positions, and complex instruction following.
 - **Visual Understanding:** POPE, GQA, MMB, SEED, DocVQA, and OCRB for general understanding tasks, plus MMT-Bench and its fine-grained subtasks (VR, VI, Ha, VCR, DKR) to specifically assess the impact on fine-grained visual comprehension.

This comprehensive approach allows the UMM to learn from its own internal "judgment" regarding text-image alignment, iteratively improving its generative fidelity without external human feedback, while also investigating the reciprocal benefits for understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings across visual generation, visual understanding, and ablation studies, demonstrating the efficacy and characteristics of the GvU mechanism and self-supervised RL framework.

#### 4.1. Visual Generation Evaluation

- **Quantitative Performance:**
 - **GenEval Benchmark (Table 1):** GvU achieved an overall score of 0.84 (with LLM prompt rewriter) and 0.81 (without), marking a 19.1% relative improvement over the base X-Omni model (0.68 without rewriter). This indicates substantial enhancement in general text-to-image generation.
 - **DPG-Bench Benchmark (Table 2):** GvU attained an overall score of 85.68. It exhibited strong performance in the Entity and Relation subcategories, suggesting that the understanding module effectively contributes to improved spatial and relational comprehension in the generative process.
 - **GenEval++ Benchmark (Table 3):** GvU achieved an overall score of 0.404, representing a 43.3% improvement from the base X-Omni model (0.282). Significant gains were observed in mixed-category evaluations (e.g., color/pos, pos/count, pos/size, multi-count), indicating the token-level fine-grained reward effectively guides the generation of more complex and compositionally consistent images.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Figure 3 illustrates that GvU-generated images demonstrate better text-image alignment and more coherent spatial layouts compared to other methods, aligning with the quantitative improvements.
- **Cumulative Effects of T2I Performance (Figure 4 & 5):** Analysis of training curves showed a steady and consistent improvement in visual generation performance across GenEval, DPG-Bench, and GenEval++ benchmarks as RL training steps increased. This suggests that the fine-grained intrinsic rewards provide dense and informative feedback, driving continuous and robust performance gains in complex T2I tasks. Qualitative results (Figure 5) further supported this, showing progressively better alignment with input text and more coherent compositional layouts.

#### 4.2. Visual Understanding Evaluation

- **General Understanding Benchmarks (Table 4):** GvU achieved performance comparable to other models on six diverse image understanding benchmarks (POPE, GQA, MMB, SEED, DocVQA, OCRB), despite being trained without explicit image-understanding supervision. This suggests that the generation-enhancing process did not negatively impact general understanding capabilities.
- **Fine-Grained Understanding (MMT-Bench, Table 5):** Crucially, the study observed improvements in fine-grained visual understanding subtasks of MMT-Bench (Visual Recognition (VR), Visual Illusion (VI), Hallucination (Ha), Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR), and Discipline Knowledge Reasoning (DKR)). For example, VI score increased by +5.06 and VCR by +5.0. This indicates a synergistic effect where enhancing generative capabilities, guided by intrinsic rewards, concurrently facilitates improvements in the model's fine-grained visual comprehension.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Ablation on Base Models (Figure 6):** The study compared GvU's impact on a "weak" base X-Omni model (weaker generation, slightly stronger understanding) versus a "regular" base X-Omni model (stronger generation, slightly weaker understanding). GvU yielded a substantially larger improvement on the weak base (e.g., +138.1% on GenEval vs. +19.1% on the regular base). This suggests that a wider initial gap between understanding and generation in the base model enhances the effectiveness of GvU in guiding model training and knowledge transfer.
- **Ablation on Intrinsic Reward:**
 - **Training Curves (Figure 7):** The intrinsic reward consistently increased in a smooth and stable manner during RL training for both weak and regular base models. The regular base started with a higher initial reward (0.045 vs. 0.018 for weak base), reflecting its stronger initial generation. The weak base, despite starting lower, showed a larger proportional increase in reward (0.018 → 0.038), indicating the framework's ability to facilitate learning even with limited initial generative performance.
 - **Semantics Sensitivity (Figure 8):** To assess the fine-grained nature of the intrinsic reward, specific semantic terms (count, color, region) were systematically removed from evaluation prompts. Removing these descriptors consistently led to a decrease in the intrinsic reward, with region terms causing the largest decline. Removing multiple descriptors resulted in larger drops. This finding confirms that the intrinsic reward effectively captures and reflects fine-grained semantic correspondences between text and images.

Overall, the results indicate that GvU effectively leverages a UMM's internal understanding to drive substantial improvements in its generative capabilities. Furthermore, these generation enhancements foster a synergistic relationship, leading to positive effects on fine-grained visual understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the development of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) by proposing a novel, self-supervised mechanism to bridge the gap between their visual understanding and generation capabilities. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Core UMM Challenge:** The work directly tackles the observed performance asymmetry in UMMs, where generation often lags behind understanding. By demonstrating a viable method to leverage internal understanding to guide generation, it advances the fundamental goal of UMMs to achieve truly unified and balanced multimodal intelligence.
2. **Novel Self-Supervised Learning Paradigm:** The introduction of the GvU (Generate via Understanding) token-level intrinsic reward mechanism and its integration into a self-supervised reinforcement learning (RL) framework offers a new paradigm for training UMMs. This approach significantly reduces reliance on external supervision, large annotated datasets, or separate reward models, thereby enhancing data efficiency and computational autonomy. This has implications for scaling UMM development and reducing the high cost associated with human-labeled data or external model integration.
3. **Enhanced Fine-Grained Generative Control:** The token-level nature of the intrinsic reward is critical. Unlike coarser image-level rewards, it provides dense and precise feedback, enabling UMMs to refine their generation at a much finer granularity. This leads to demonstrable improvements in generating images that accurately reflect complex compositional elements, intricate spatial relationships, quantitative specifications, and fine-grained attribute bindings, as evidenced by the strong performance on GenEval++ and DPG-Bench. This level of control is essential for practical T2I applications that require high fidelity to detailed prompts.
4. **Demonstrating Mutual Enhancement (Synergy):** A key finding is the observation that improved generative capabilities, guided by the intrinsic reward, subsequently strengthen the UMM's fine-grained visual understanding. This establishes a virtuous cycle where understanding informs generation, and enhanced generation, in turn, refines understanding. This synergistic effect moves beyond merely co-existing tasks within a UMM towards a mutually beneficial relationship, fulfilling a long-term aspiration in multimodal AI research.
5. **Foundation for Robust and Adaptive UMMs:** By providing a mechanism for UMMs to evaluate and improve their own outputs, this research lays a foundation for developing more robust, adaptive, and self-improving multimodal systems. Such systems could potentially learn from their own "experience" and continually refine their capabilities without constant human intervention or external feedback loops.
6. **Practical Implications for T2I and Beyond:** The ability of UMMs to generate images that are highly aligned with complex textual instructions has direct practical implications for various applications, including content creation, design, virtual reality, and accessibility tools. Furthermore, the demonstrated synergy between understanding and generation suggests potential for advances in other multimodal tasks where similar interdependencies exist.

In conclusion, this work offers a method for UMMs to learn from their internal representations, promoting a more integrated and self-sufficient multimodal intelligence. It contributes both a novel methodological framework and empirical evidence for the synergistic potential within unified models, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in multimodal AI.
