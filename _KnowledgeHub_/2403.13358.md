# GeRM: A Generalist Robotic Model with Mixture-of-experts for Quadruped Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Westlake University developed GeRM, a generalist robotic model for quadruped robots that integrates a Mixture-of-Experts Transformer with offline reinforcement learning, achieving superior multi-task performance and emergent adaptive path planning using automatically collected mixed-quality data.

## Problem

- High cost and labor associated with collecting large-scale, high-quality human demonstration data for complex, multi-degree-of-freedom quadruped robots.
- Imitation Learning (IL) methods are limited by the quality and diversity of expert demonstrations, preventing robot performance from surpassing the demonstrated capabilities.
- Scaling deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies for multi-task generalization often leads to increased computational costs or performance degradation.

## Method

- Developed QUARD-Auto, an automatically collected dataset of 257K episodes for five distinct quadruped tasks, comprising both successful (optimal) and failed (sub-optimal) trajectories.
- Designed GeRM, a Transformer decoder-only Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that replaces traditional Feed-Forward Networks with sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layers for enhanced capacity and efficiency.
- Trained GeRM using an offline Deep Q-learning objective, incorporating Conservative Q-learning (CQL) to robustly learn from the mixed-quality QUARD-Auto dataset and handle potential out-of-distribution actions.

## Results

- GeRM consistently outperformed baseline methods (RT-1, GeRM w/o RL, GeRM w/o MoE) across 99 sub-tasks, achieving success rates ranging from 71.00% to 90.50% in multi-task scenarios.
- The MoE module allowed GeRM to achieve superior performance with 167M total parameters while only utilizing 39.31M 'active' parameters during inference, demonstrating an efficient balance of capacity and computation.
- GeRM showed faster convergence and superior data utilization efficiency compared to IL methods, even when trained exclusively on demonstrations, and exhibited emergent skills like dynamic adaptive path planning in unseen scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mixture-of-Experts architectures enable substantial increases in total model parameters for enhanced learning capacity while maintaining a relatively low 'active parameter' count during inference, balancing performance and computational cost.
> - Offline Reinforcement Learning, when combined with conservative Q-learning, effectively utilizes automatically collected mixed-quality data (including sub-optimal trajectories) to improve performance beyond human demonstrations.
> - Learning from diverse and large-scale datasets via offline RL can lead to the acquisition of emergent skills, allowing robots to generalize to novel scenarios not explicitly present in the training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to several active areas within robotics and artificial intelligence, particularly focusing on robust and generalizable control for quadruped robots.

Quadruped robots are recognized for their agility and ability to navigate complex terrains, making them a subject of extensive research in autonomous navigation, locomotion, and manipulation. A significant challenge in this field is enabling these robots to handle diverse, multi-task scenarios, which requires processing multi-modal inputs (e.g., language instructions, visual data) and generating appropriate actions.

Current approaches often rely on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which typically employ Imitation Learning (IL) trained on expert demonstrations. However, a primary limitation of IL is the high cost and difficulty associated with collecting large-scale, high-quality expert datasets, especially for complex robots like quadrupeds with many degrees of freedom. This dependency on expert data also caps the performance of IL policies to the quality of the demonstrations.

To address the data scarcity and performance ceiling of IL, offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as an alternative, enabling learning from pre-collected, potentially sub-optimal datasets without requiring constant interaction with the environment. Integrating offline RL with high-capacity VLA models, particularly Transformer-based architectures, is a developing area, building on works like Q-Transformer which adapt Q-learning for large Transformer-based Q-functions on mixed-quality data.

Another parallel development in AI, particularly in natural language processing and increasingly in deep RL, is the use of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures. MoE models allow for an increase in total model parameters, enhancing capacity and generalization, while maintaining a relatively constant computational cost during inference by only activating a sparse subset of experts for any given input. This approach has shown effectiveness in scaling deep learning models and has recently been explored for parameter scalability in deep RL.

This work specifically aims to combine these advancements: applying a Transformer-based VLA model for quadruped control, leveraging offline RL to utilize automatically collected mixed-quality data (including sub-optimal trajectories), and integrating a Mixture-of-Experts architecture to enhance model capacity and efficiency for multi-task learning. By doing so, it attempts to advance the field towards more generalist robotic models that are less reliant on expensive human demonstrations and can exhibit emergent behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives and motivations driving the GeRM research are multifaceted, addressing critical challenges in multi-task robot learning for quadruped robots:

#### 2.1. Overcoming Limitations of Human Demonstration Data

- **High Cost of Data Collection:** The primary motivation stems from the significant financial and labor costs associated with manually collecting large-scale, high-quality expert demonstration datasets for complex robots like quadrupeds. This difficulty is exacerbated by the high degrees of freedom in quadruped robots, making expert teleoperation challenging. The paper seeks to reduce this burden by exploring automated data collection methods.
- **Performance Ceiling of Imitation Learning:** Existing VLA models, predominantly trained with Imitation Learning (IL), are limited by the quality and diversity of expert demonstrations. This means that the robot's performance cannot surpass the capabilities or coverage of the human expert. The objective is to utilize Reinforcement Learning (RL) to learn beyond these limitations, optimizing data utilization and extracting value from sub-optimal data, potentially leading to superhuman performance.

#### 2.2. Enhancing Model Capacity and Efficiency for Multi-Task Learning

- **Generalization Across Diverse Tasks:** To enable quadruped robots to handle multi-task scenarios, they require models capable of processing various human instructions and visual inputs to generate appropriate actions. The aim is to develop a generalist model that can perform effectively across a wide range of tasks and varying scenarios.
- **Scaling RL Policies without Performance Degradation:** While increasing model parameter count often improves generalization in other AI domains, it frequently has a negative impact on the performance of deep RL policies. The research aims to find a method to augment model capacity for multi-task RL without incurring prohibitive computational costs or degrading performance. This involves exploring architectures that can scale effectively.

#### 2.3. Advancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models for Quadruped Robots

- **Integration of Multi-modal Inputs:** The paper aims to build upon the concept of VLA models, exemplified by RT-1, to effectively combine language instructions and visual perceptions for quadruped robot control, enabling intuitive human-robot interaction and task specification.
- **Effective Application of Transformer-based VLA to Offline RL:** A specific objective is to adapt Transformer-based VLA models for effective training with offline RL algorithms, particularly by integrating Deep Q-Learning principles to handle mixed-quality datasets.

#### 2.4. Contribution of a Novel Dataset

- A key motivation is to provide the research community with a new, automatically collected dataset, QUARD-Auto, which includes both successful and sub-optimal trajectories. This dataset is designed to support offline RL approaches and address the lack of diverse, readily available data for multi-task quadruped robot learning, thereby fostering further advancements.

In summary, the motivation behind GeRM is to develop a robust, scalable, and generalist robotic model for quadruped robots by integrating offline reinforcement learning with a Mixture-of-Experts Transformer-based VLA architecture, trained on an automatically collected dataset containing diverse quality data, thereby overcoming the constraints of traditional imitation learning and manual data collection.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeRM (Generalist Robotic Model) integrates several advanced techniques to achieve multi-task learning for quadruped robots, specifically a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model based on a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Transformer decoder, trained using an offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) objective on an automatically collected dataset.

#### 3.1. Auto-collected Quadruped Robot Datasets (QUARD-Auto)

To address the high cost and limitations of human demonstrations, the authors developed a fully automated data collection approach in Nvidia’s Isaac Gym simulator.
- **Tasks:** The QUARD-Auto dataset comprises trajectories for five distinct task categories: "Go to Object," "Go to Object and avoid the obstacle," "Stop Object," "Distinguish Letter," and "Go through Tunnel." These tasks cover navigation, obstacle avoidance, and simple manipulation, totaling 257K episodes (trajectories).
- **Collection Process:** Data was collected autonomously using a pre-trained VLA policy (specifically, GeRM without MoE, pre-trained on demonstrations) as the high-level controller. This policy receives instructions (from simple templates) and images and outputs commands. A low-level control strategy (based on) translates these commands into actual robot joint angles. The process records images, instructions, and executable command data for each frame.
- **Data Composition:** The dataset includes a mix of successful (optimal) and failed (sub-optimal) trajectories. Out of 258,418 collected trajectories, 120,128 were successful, and 138,290 were failures. This mixed-quality data is crucial for offline RL, allowing the model to learn from both positive and negative experiences. For training GeRM, a specific ratio of demonstration to sub-optimal data (75.69% optimal, 24.31% sub-optimal) was utilized, where demonstrations received a reward of 1.0 and sub-optimal data 0.0.

#### 3.2. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Network Architecture

GeRM's core architecture is a Transformer decoder-only model that replaces the conventional Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) with sparse MoE layers.
- **Transformer Base:** The model employs 8 self-attention layers and has a total of 167M parameters. It processes multi-modal inputs and outputs discretized action tokens.
- **MoE Layer Details:** Each MoE layer consists of 8 distinct "expert" groups of parameters. For every token at each layer, a "router network" (gating network G) selects a predetermined number of these experts (Top-K experts, where K=2 in this implementation) to process the token. The outputs from these selected experts are then additively combined.
- **Gating Mechanism:** The gating network G determines which experts to activate. It uses a linear layer followed by a Softmax function over the Top-K logits. Tunable Gaussian noise is added before the Softmax, which aids in load balancing among experts and ensures differentiability for gradient backpropagation.
- **Efficiency:** This sparse activation mechanism allows the model to leverage a high total parameter count (167M) for increased capacity while maintaining a relatively low "active parameter" count (39.31M in this case, meaning only 1/2 of total parameters and 1/8 of FFN layers are active during inference). This strategy aims to increase model capacity without significantly increasing computational cost or inference speed.

#### 3.3. Vision-Language-Action Model in Reinforcement Learning

GeRM operates as a VLA model trained with an offline RL objective.
- **Input Processing:**
 - **Text Encoder:** Language instructions are first encoded into 512-dimension vectors using a Universal Sentence Encoder.
 - **Vision-Language Feature Encoder:** These text embeddings are combined with a history of 6 (or 7 for Q-value calculation) images, processed by an ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B3 with FiLM conditioning, to generate vision-language tokens.
 - **TokenLearner:** A TokenLearner then compacts these vision-language tokens into a smaller set of tokens.
- **Action Generation:** The MoE Transformer decoder attends over these compact tokens and produces discretized action tokens. These tokens are then de-tokenized into a 12-dimensional continuous robot command vector, including linear and angular velocities, gait parameters, robot height, pitch angle, foot width, foot height, and a termination signal.
- **Offline RL Objective:** The training leverages a deep Q-learning approach, similar to Q-Transformer, adapted for Transformer-based VLA models.
 - **Discretization and Autoregression:** Each action dimension is treated as a separate, discretized component, and Q-values are learned autoregressively across action dimensions and time steps.
 - **Conservative Q-learning (CQL):** To address potential out-of-distribution actions in offline datasets, a conservative penalty is added to the Q-learning objective. This term regularizes Q-values towards zero for actions not present in the dataset, ensuring that the learned policy favors in-distribution actions. The objective minimizes the temporal difference error while applying this conservative regularization.

This integrated approach aims to leverage the representational power of Transformers, the efficiency and capacity scaling of MoE, and the data utilization capabilities of offline RL to train a generalist quadruped robot controller from diverse, automatically collected data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate GeRM's effectiveness as a generalist model, the importance of its specific architectural designs (MoE and Q-learning), its efficiency, data utilization, and its potential for emergent skills.

#### 4.1. Multi-task Performance and Design Efficacy (Q1 & Q2)

GeRM demonstrated performance across 99 sub-tasks, evaluated on varying gaits and object settings (seen and unseen).
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** GeRM consistently outperformed all baseline methods (RT-1, GeRM w/o RL, GeRM w/o MoE) across most of the five main task categories ("Go to," "Go avoid," "Stop," "Distinguish," "Go through"). For example, GeRM with sub-optimal data achieved success rates ranging from 71.00% to 90.50%, significantly higher than RT-1 (0% to 48.67%) and GeRM w/o MoE with sub-optimal data (45.67% to 63.55%).
- **Impact of Offline RL and Sub-optimal Data:** The results indicate that Imitation Learning (IL) methods (RT-1, GeRM w/o RL) struggled with the limited demonstrations (8610 trajectories). Offline RL methods (GeRM w/o MoE and GeRM) showed better performance, as they could leverage both demonstrations and failed episodes. GeRM, specifically, showed substantial performance improvement when trained on demonstrations, and further enhancement across most tasks, particularly in "Stop" tasks, with the inclusion of sub-optimal data. This highlights the effectiveness of GeRM in improving upon human demonstrations by utilizing autonomously collected mixed-quality data.
- **Significance of MoE and Q-Learning:** The comparison between GeRM and its ablations (GeRM w/o RL, GeRM w/o MoE) directly supports the significance of both the MoE module and the Q-learning methodology in achieving the observed performance gains.

#### 4.2. MoE Module Efficiency and Performance (Q3)

The MoE module contributed to balancing computational cost and performance.
- **Sparse vs. Active Parameters:** GeRM, despite having 83.48M total parameters, only utilized 39.31M "active" parameters for each token during inference. This implies that only half of its total parameters and 1/8 of its FFN layers were actively engaged.
- **Cost-Performance Balance:** This sparse activation allowed GeRM to achieve superior performance compared to RT-1 (33.50M total and active parameters) with only a modest increase in active parameters (5.81M). GeRM w/o RL, also an MoE model with 39.31M active parameters, also outperformed RT-1 in most categories. This suggests that MoE effectively increases model capacity without proportionally increasing inference computational cost, though hardware utilization and training costs for MoE layers were noted as potential considerations.

#### 4.3. Training Efficiency and Data Utilization (Q4)

GeRM demonstrated commendable training efficiency.
- **Faster Convergence:** In a comparison experiment on the "Go to the red cube" task using only demonstration data, GeRM achieved higher success rates and converged much faster than IL methods (GeRM w/o RL, RT-1). GeRM reached performance levels similar to RT-1's 20th epoch by its 2nd epoch and essentially converged by the 7th epoch. GeRM w/o MoE (also offline RL) converged around 8 epochs, while IL methods failed to converge by the 10th epoch.
- **Superior Data Utilization:** This rapid convergence, even when exclusively trained on demonstrations, indicates GeRM's proficiency not only in effectively harnessing sub-optimal data but also in leveraging demonstrations with higher efficiency compared to alternative methodologies.

#### 4.4. Emergent Skills (Q5)

GeRM exhibited emergent skills, particularly in dynamic adaptive path planning, beyond what was explicitly present in the training demonstrations.
- **Dynamic Adaptive Path Planning:** An example scenario presented involved the task "Go to the fan and avoid the obstacle." When the robot's initial vision was limited, GeRM demonstrated the ability to make an exploratory turn, and upon receiving new visual input, execute a significant reorientation to align with the correct target outside its original field of view, successfully completing the task. These types of trajectories were considered out-of-distribution of the training dataset.
- **Contrast with IL:** In contrast, IL-based methods frequently failed in similar scenarios by choosing an incorrect direction and directly heading towards a wrong target.
- **Decision-Making and Planning:** This suggests that GeRM, through learning from large-scale mixed-quality data via RL, acquired novel capabilities for decision-making and planning future paths based on dynamic visual perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GeRM research presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic learning, particularly for quadruped robots and generalist AI models.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Robot Data Collection and Utilization

- **Automated Data Collection:** The introduction of the QUARD-Auto dataset and its automated collection methodology is a substantial step towards alleviating the high cost and labor intensity of obtaining large-scale robot training data. This offers a new paradigm for scaling up datasets without continuous human intervention.
- **Learning from Sub-optimal Data:** By effectively leveraging offline Reinforcement Learning to learn from a mixture of successful demonstrations and sub-optimal (failed) auto-collected data, GeRM demonstrates a method to transcend the limitations imposed by expert-only demonstrations. This capability suggests that robots can learn from their own failures and imperfect experiences, akin to how biological systems learn, potentially enabling performance levels beyond human expert capabilities.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Generalization and Efficiency in Multi-Task Robotic Control

- **Generalist Robotic Model:** GeRM’s architecture, combining a Transformer-based VLA with MoE, facilitates the development of a generalist model capable of understanding diverse language instructions and visual inputs to perform a wide array of tasks. This moves closer to robots that can adapt to various scenarios without requiring task-specific retraining.
- **Scalability with Controlled Computation:** The integration of the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) structure is a critical innovation. It enables GeRM to significantly expand its total model capacity for enhanced learning and generalization while controlling the active parameter count during inference. This addresses a fundamental challenge in deep RL where increasing parameters often negatively impacts performance or computational efficiency, offering a path for more powerful yet practical robotic policies.

#### 5.3. Advancements in Reinforcement Learning for Robotics

- **Effective Offline RL with VLAs:** GeRM demonstrates a robust framework for applying offline RL principles (like Q-learning with conservative regularization) to high-capacity, multi-modal VLA models. This is crucial for training complex robotic agents from pre-recorded datasets, making RL more accessible and less dependent on online interaction.
- **Emergent Skill Acquisition:** The observation of emergent skills, such as dynamic adaptive path planning, implies that GeRM can learn more abstract and transferable knowledge than simple imitation, enabling it to generalize to novel and out-of-distribution scenarios. This is a key indicator of true intelligence and adaptability in robotic systems.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

- The work sets a foundation for extending these capabilities from simulation to real-world quadruped robots, which is a common and challenging next step in robotics research.
- The potential for expanding the dataset with a wider array of task categories could further solidify GeRM's robustness and generalization abilities, pushing towards even more versatile robotic agents.

Overall, GeRM advances the state of the art in quadruped robot control by offering a robust, efficient, and data-agnostic learning framework. It points towards a future where complex robots can learn a broad range of skills from diverse data, requiring less human intervention and potentially exhibiting intelligent behaviors not explicitly programmed or demonstrated.
