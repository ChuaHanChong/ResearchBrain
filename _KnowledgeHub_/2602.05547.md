# Multi-Task GRPO: Reliable LLM Reasoning Across Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCL, Huawei Noah's Ark Lab, UNIST, Edinburgh, and Basel developed Multi-Task GRPO (MT-GRPO), an algorithm for large language model post-training that optimizes for balanced performance across diverse reasoning tasks. MT-GRPO enhances worst-task accuracy by 16-28% over standard GRPO and 6% over DAPO, achieving faster convergence to reliable multi-task reasoning.

## Problem

- Current LLM post-training methods create "specialist" models, failing to ensure reliable, balanced performance across diverse reasoning tasks required for general-purpose reasoners.
- Multi-task optimization often results in imbalanced outcomes, with some tasks dominating and others suffering from negative transfer or stagnation.
- In GRPO, tasks exhibit varying rates of "zero advantage" prompts, which distort actual gradient contributions and make the GRPO loss an unreliable signal for effective task reweighting.

## Method

- Formulated a ==multi-task post-training objective== that maximizes average reward while explicitly constraining performance disparity across tasks, using a ==max-min optimization framework==.
- Introduced ==Improvement-Aware Task Reweighting (IWU)== to dynamically adapt task weights based on both current task performance and recent learning progress, preventing weight collapse and ensuring balanced optimization.
- Developed a ==Ratio-Preserving Sampler (RP SAMPLER)== to guarantee that intended task weights translate into accurate gradient contributions by correcting for task-dependent rates of zero-gradient prompts.

## Results

- MT-GRPO achieved **16-28%** absolute improvement in worst-task accuracy over standard GRPO and **6%** over DAPO in a 3-task reasoning benchmark.
- The algorithm demonstrated faster convergence to robust performance, reaching **50%** worst-task accuracy in **50%** fewer training steps than baselines.
- The "lambda" parameter in improvement-aware reweighting effectively controlled the trade-off, allowing tunable emphasis on worst-task robustness versus overall average accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust multi-task LLM reasoning necessitates an explicit objective that balances average performance with worst-task guarantees, moving beyond single-task specialization.
> - The varying rates of "zero advantage" prompts in GRPO significantly bias effective task gradient contributions, making naive multi-task sampling ineffective.
> - Dynamic task reweighting benefits from incorporating both absolute task performance and observed improvement signals to prevent optimization from collapsing onto a single task.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself at the intersection of large language model (LLM) development, reinforcement learning (RL) post-training, and multi-task learning, addressing a critical emerging challenge: enabling LLMs to perform reliably across a *diversity* of reasoning tasks rather than specializing in just one.

**Current Landscape:**
Recent advancements in LLM post-training, particularly those employing policy optimization methods like Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have significantly enhanced LLM performance on specific, individual reasoning benchmarks. This includes impressive capabilities in mathematical problem-solving, code generation, and structured reasoning. However, the vast majority of these post-training pipelines are tailored and optimized for single tasks, essentially creating "specialist" models.

**Identified Research Gap:**
The paper highlights a crucial gap between current research and real-world LLM deployment. As LLMs are increasingly deployed as "general-purpose reasoners," their reliability hinges on broad competence across diverse reasoning skills, not just peak performance on a narrow set of benchmarks. A model that excels in complex math but fails at basic logical inference is ultimately unreliable. The core problem is that existing multi-task adaptation approaches often lead to:
1. **Imbalanced Outcomes:** Optimizing for average performance causes some tasks to dominate the optimization process, achieving strong gains, while others stagnate or even suffer from "negative transfer" (progress on one task hindering another).
2. **GRPO-Specific Challenges:** When using GRPO, prompts whose rollouts yield identical rewards contribute "zero advantages" and thus no gradient signal to the policy update. The prevalence of these zero-gradient prompts varies significantly across tasks, leading to distorted effective gradient contributions. This means that even if a "weaker" task is intentionally upweighted, its actual influence on the model update might be minimal due to a high rate of zero-gradient prompts. Furthermore, the GRPO loss itself is not a reliable scalar signal for comparing task performance to adapt weights, as it can be zero for both fully correct and fully incorrect rollouts.

**Connecting to Existing Fields:**
The authors relate their work to several established areas but emphasize the unique challenges in their specific context:
- **Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) and Multi-Task Learning (MTL):** These fields have explored robust objectives that balance average performance with guarantees for underperforming groups or domains, often adapting weights based on loss. However, these methods typically operate in supervised learning paradigms and do not account for the peculiarities of RL post-training, such as the zero-gradient issue or the unreliability of GRPO loss for direct reweighting.
- **Multi-objective Optimization and Gradient Conflict Methods:** Research in MTL has developed techniques to mitigate gradient conflicts and ensure balanced progress. While relevant, these also often focus on supervised settings and do not directly address the distinct RL-specific challenges encountered with GRPO.
- **Multi-task LLM Fine-tuning and RL Post-training:** While multi-task supervised fine-tuning and various forms of RL fine-tuning (e.g., for preference alignment, self-training, GRPO-based reasoning) exist, very few works directly tackle multi-task RL post-training with a focus on *robustness* across heterogeneous tasks under the GRPO framework. Approaches like curriculum sampling or reward normalization have been explored, but none fully address the specific issues of task-dependent zero-gradient rates and the ambiguity of GRPO loss for weight adaptation.

This paper distinguishes itself by directly incorporating a robustness-aware objective into multi-task GRPO and proposing algorithmic solutions (improvement-aware reweighting, ratio-preserving sampling) that specifically address the aforementioned RL-peculiar limitations, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art towards truly general-purpose and reliable LLM reasoners.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the growing demand for Large Language Models to function as versatile, general-purpose reasoners in real-world applications, moving beyond their current status as specialists in isolated benchmarks. The authors identify critical limitations in current RL post-training methodologies, particularly when applied to multiple reasoning tasks simultaneously.

**Overarching Motivation:**
- **The Need for General-Purpose Reasoners:** Modern LLM deployment scenarios require models capable of reliably performing across a diverse spectrum of reasoning tasks. Current approaches, which primarily optimize for individual tasks, produce "specialist" models that lack balanced competence and reliability across a broad range of skills. An LLM that is brilliant at coding but struggles with basic logical puzzles is not truly reliable in a general context.
- **Addressing the "Worst-Task" Problem:** A fundamental question arises: "How should we post-train a single model to improve reasoning across tasks while ensuring that no task is left behind?" This emphasizes the importance of robustness and worst-case performance guarantees over mere average performance maximization.

**Specific Problems and Challenges that Drive the Objectives:**

1. **Imbalanced Outcomes in Multi-Task Optimization (Lack of Task-Wise Robustness):**
 - Optimizing for average performance often leads to imbalanced results, where strong gains on easy tasks can mask stagnation or even degradation on more difficult ones. This violates the goal of achieving broad competence.
 - The problem is compounded by "negative transfer" and "task interference," where learning on one task negatively impacts others.

2. **Uneven Zero-Gradient Rates Across Tasks in GRPO:**
 - A core limitation of GRPO-style RL post-training is that if all sampled responses for a given prompt receive identical rewards (e.g., all correct or all incorrect), the "advantage" for that prompt is zero, meaning it contributes no gradient to the policy update.
 - Tasks inherently vary in how frequently their prompts yield these "zero advantage" scenarios. This means that even if a weaker task is assigned a higher sampling weight, the *effective* gradient contribution from that task might be disproportionately low if it has a high zero-gradient rate, fundamentally skewing the optimization signal.
 - Furthermore, the GRPO objective itself can be zero for both perfectly solved and completely failed tasks, making it an unreliable signal for directly comparing tasks to determine reweighting strategies.

**Key Objectives of MT-GRPO:**

To address these challenges, the authors propose MT-GRPO with two core objectives:

1. **Robustness-Aware Multi-Task RL Post-Training Objective:**
 - Develop a novel objective that explicitly incorporates task-wise robustness, promoting balanced competence across tasks.
 - This objective aims to maximize average performance while simultaneously constraining the performance disparity between any two tasks (formalized as `max (avg J_k(θ))` s.t. `|J_k(θ) - J_j(θ)| <= ε`).
 - The design should allow for a tunable trade-off between strict worst-task robustness and overall average performance.

2. **Algorithmic Solutions for Practical and Effective Multi-Task GRPO:**
 - **Improvement-Aware Task Reweighting:** Introduce a mechanism that dynamically adapts task weights not just based on absolute task rewards, but also on *task-wise improvement signals*. This prevents optimization from collapsing onto a single worst task and ensures balanced progress by prioritizing tasks that are both underperforming *and* improving slowly.
 - **Ratio-Preserving Batch Construction:** Develop a sampling mechanism that guarantees that the learned task weights (which reflect robustness and improvement priorities) translate accurately into *actual gradient contributions* during training. This sampler must explicitly account for and correct the distortion caused by varying rates of "zero advantage" prompts across tasks.

In essence, the motivation is to build more reliable and generally capable LLMs, and the objectives outline a principled, practical, and RL-specific approach to achieve this by tackling the limitations of existing multi-task learning paradigms when applied to GRPO-based post-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is the development of **Multi-Task GRPO (MT-GRPO)**, an algorithm designed to overcome the limitations of standard GRPO in multi-task settings by explicitly optimizing for task-wise robustness and ensuring accurate gradient contributions. The methodology involves a novel objective formulation and two key algorithmic innovations.

#### 3.1. Multi-Task Post-Training Objective Formulation

The authors first formalize their objective to promote balanced competence.
- **Task-level Performance:** For each task `k`, the performance `J_k(θ)` is defined as the KL-regularized expected reward, penalizing deviations from a reference policy `π_ref`.
- **Constrained Optimization:** The goal is to maximize the average task reward `(1/K) Σ J_k(θ)` subject to a constraint `|J_k(θ) - J_j(θ)| ≤ ε` for all task pairs. Here, `ε ≥ 0` controls the allowed performance disparity; `ε=0` enforces strict equality, while larger `ε` relaxes it towards average reward optimization.
- **Max-Min Equivalence:** This constrained problem is transformed into an equivalent max-min objective via Lagrangian relaxation: `max_θ min_z (Σ z_k J_k(θ) - ε Ω(z))`. Here, `z ∈ Δ_K` is a learned distribution over tasks, and `Ω(z) = (K/2) ||z - (1/K)1||_1` is an ℓ1-regularizer that penalizes deviations from uniform task weighting. This formulation encourages the policy to perform well even for the worst-case task weighting chosen by `z`, which implicitly pushes for better worst-task performance.

#### 3.2. Addressing GRPO's Limitations with Algorithmic Innovations

The paper identifies two critical limitations of applying standard GRPO directly to multi-task objectives: (i) the GRPO loss's unreliability for task reweighting and the tendency for weights to collapse on a single task, and (ii) uneven zero-gradient rates across tasks. MT-GRPO proposes distinct solutions for each.

##### 3.2.1. Improvement-Aware Task Reweighting (IWU - Subroutine 1)

- **Problem:** The GRPO objective can be zero when all rollouts are correct or all are incorrect, making it an unreliable signal for comparing task performance to adapt weights. Strict worst-task optimization (ε=0 in the objective) can lead to "weight collapse," where a single worst-performing task dominates optimization, ignoring others.
- **Solution:** Decouple task reweighting from the GRPO loss. Instead, use *true task-level rewards* `J_k(θ)` and crucially, *task-level improvement signals* `I^(t)_k = J_GRPO,k(θ_t+1) - J_GRPO,k(θ_t)`.
- **Mechanism:** Task weights `z_t` (parameterized via softmax over logits `ξ_t`) are updated through gradient descent on `ξ_t`. The gradient signal for `ξ_k` is proportional to `z_k (s^(t)_k - Σ z_j s^(t)_j)`, where `s^(t)_k = I^(t)_k + λJ_k(θ_t)`.
 - `J_k(θ_t)` reflects current task performance.
 - `I^(t)_k` captures how much task `k` has improved from the last policy update.
 - The tunable parameter `λ` controls the trade-off: larger `λ` emphasizes absolute task reward (closer to strict worst-task), while smaller `λ` prioritizes balanced improvement across tasks (balancing performance and progress).
- **Benefit:** This approach prevents weight collapse by considering both current performance and learning progress, directing optimization effort to tasks that are both underperforming and/or improving slowly.

##### 3.2.2. Ratio-Preserving Sampler (RP SAMPLER - Algorithm 2)

- **Problem:** Different tasks exhibit widely varying rates of "zero-gradient prompts" (where all sampled rollouts for a prompt yield identical rewards). If prompts are sampled purely based on task weights `z_t`, the *effective* gradient contributions from tasks with high zero-gradient rates will be much lower than intended, distorting the optimization.
- **Solution:** The RP SAMPLER explicitly enforces the target task proportions in the *post-filtered* batch (i.e., after zero-gradient prompts are removed).
- **Mechanism:**
 1. **Desired Counts:** Samples desired number of informative prompts `(n_1..., n_K)` for the batch size `B` based on current task weights `z`.
 2. **Acceptance-Aware Sampling:** Maintains an estimate `ρ_k` of the zero-gradient filtering rate for each task `k`. It then *inflates* task weights `z_k` to `ˆz_k` during initial prompt generation, oversampling tasks with higher expected filtering rates. This increases the likelihood that the initial generation yields enough informative samples from typically "difficult" tasks.
 3. **Resampling:** If, after initial generation and filtering, a task `k` still has fewer informative samples (`c_k`) than desired (`n_k`), the sampler resamples prompts specifically for those deficient tasks, prioritizing based on deficiency and expected acceptance. This process continues for a fixed number of rounds or until targets are met.
- **Benefit:** Ensures that the actual gradient signal observed by the policy closely aligns with the dynamically learned task weights, overcoming the "zero-gradient" distortion unique to GRPO.

#### 3.3. Overall MT-GRPO Algorithm (Algorithm 1)

MT-GRPO iteratively performs the following steps:
1. **Batch Construction:** Uses RP SAMPLER to create a training batch `(x, ρ)` that respects the current task weights `z_t` and accounts for zero-gradient prompts.
2. **Policy Update:** Updates the LLM policy `θ` using the `z_t`-weighted GRPO objective on the constructed batch.
3. **Task Weight Update:** Calculates task-level improvements `I^(t)_k` and then updates task weights `z_t` using the IWU (Subroutine 1), based on `J_k(θ_t)` and `I^(t)_k`.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen-2.5-3B.
- **Tasks:** Reasoning tasks from Chen et al., categorized into:
 - **Planning:** Countdown, Zebra puzzles (various difficulty levels).
 - **Inductive Reasoning:** Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC, string-based, various lengths/difficulties).
- **Settings:** Controlled 3-task setting (medium difficulty Countdown, Zebra, ARC) and a larger 9-task setting (easy, medium, hard variants of all three).
- **Baselines:** Standard GRPO (uniform sampling), SEC-GRPO (self-evolving curriculum), DAPO (uniform sampling with dynamic adjustments), SEC-DAPO.
- **Metrics:** Worst-task accuracy (robustness), average accuracy, and average per-task relative change (normalized gains over DAPO, emphasizing improvements on weaker tasks).

This comprehensive methodology addresses both the objective-level formulation for robustness and the algorithmic intricacies required to make such an objective practical and effective within the GRPO framework for LLM post-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MT-GRPO on both 3-task and 9-task reasoning benchmarks consistently demonstrates its superior performance in achieving reliable LLM reasoning across diverse tasks compared to existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Experiment 1: Controlled Three-Task Setting (Countdown, Zebra, ARC - Medium Difficulty)

- **Superior Worst-Task Accuracy:** MT-GRPO substantially outperforms all baselines in terms of worst-task accuracy. Specifically, it achieves 16–28% absolute improvement over standard GRPO and 6% absolute improvement over DAPO. This is a crucial finding, indicating MT-GRPO's effectiveness in bolstering the performance of the weakest tasks, a direct measure of robustness.
- **Competitive Average Accuracy:** Despite focusing on worst-task performance, MT-GRPO maintains competitive average accuracy. This highlights that the gains in robustness do not come at the expense of overall model capability.
- **Balanced Improvements Across Tasks:** MT-GRPO achieves the highest average per-task relative change, meaning it delivers stronger and more balanced improvements, particularly on tasks where baselines struggle. This metric, which normalizes gains, underscores MT-GRPO's ability to uplift underperforming tasks.
- **Faster Robustness Gains:** MT-GRPO reaches target worst-task accuracy thresholds significantly faster, requiring 50% fewer training steps to achieve 50% worst-task accuracy. This demonstrates improved efficiency in establishing reliable performance.
- **Adaptive Weight Dynamics:** Analysis of task weights reveals that MT-GRPO dynamically reallocates optimization effort. For instance, it shifts weight towards Zebra and ARC when they underperform relative to Countdown, unlike baselines which continue to prioritize high-performing tasks. This adaptive reweighting is key to its robust performance.
- **Validation of Ratio-Preserving Sampler (RP SAMPLER):** Experiments without RP SAMPLER show that while MT-GRPO tries to compensate for under-represented tasks (like ARC, which has many zero-gradient prompts) by increasing their theoretical weight, their actual representation in training batches remains lower. This confirms the critical role of RP SAMPLER in ensuring that intended task weights translate into actual gradient contributions. Acceptance-aware sampling also significantly improves sampling efficiency by reducing resampling rounds.

#### 4.2. Experiment 2: Scaling to Nine Tasks (Easy, Medium, Hard variants of Countdown, Zebra, ARC)

- **Scalability Confirmed:** The positive findings from the 3-task setting generalize to the larger and more diverse 9-task setting, demonstrating MT-GRPO's ability to handle increased task heterogeneity.
- **Effective Control via Trade-off Parameter (λ):** The trade-off parameter `λ` in the improvement-aware reweighting mechanism (Subroutine 1) provides effective control over the balance between worst-task robustness and average performance.
 - Increasing `λ` consistently improves worst-task accuracy (e.g., 16% over GRPO and 6% over DAPO at `λ = 1.2`).
 - However, increasing `λ` also leads to a reduction in average accuracy, illustrating the intended trade-off encoded in the objective.
- **Difficulty-Wise Analysis:**
 - For smaller `λ` values (e.g., 0.1, 0.3), MT-GRPO shows negative relative change on easy tasks but positive relative change on hard tasks. This indicates a strategic reallocation of optimization effort towards more challenging and slower-improving tasks, aligning with the "improvement-aware" aspect.
 - For larger `λ` values, performance gains become more concentrated on the single worst-performing task (e.g., Zebra-hard), maximizing worst-task accuracy at the cost of a slightly lower average relative change.
- **Faster Convergence in Large Scale:** Similar to Experiment 1, MT-GRPO reaches target worst-task accuracy thresholds in fewer steps than baselines, even in the 9-task setting, further confirming its efficiency.

In summary, MT-GRPO consistently achieves superior worst-task performance and faster convergence to robustness, all while maintaining competitive average accuracy. Its adaptive reweighting and specialized batch sampling mechanisms are crucial for these gains, demonstrating a practical and principled approach to building more reliable and general-purpose LLM reasoners.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Multi-Task GRPO (MT-GRPO) represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model (LLM) post-training, particularly for developing more reliable and broadly capable AI systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling True General-Purpose LLMs:** By directly addressing the challenge of multi-task learning for reasoning capabilities, MT-GRPO helps shift LLMs from being "specialists" on individual benchmarks to becoming more "general-purpose reasoners." This is crucial for real-world deployments where LLMs are expected to handle a diverse range of problems without catastrophic failure on any specific task.

2. **Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness:** The explicit focus on optimizing worst-task performance significantly improves the reliability and trustworthiness of LLMs. Users can have greater confidence that the model will not "fail silently" or perform unacceptably poorly on certain tasks, even if it excels on others. This robustness is vital for critical applications and broader user adoption.

3. **Novel Algorithmic Solutions to RL-Specific Multi-Task Challenges:** MT-GRPO introduces innovative mechanisms that directly tackle limitations unique to RL-based LLM post-training with GRPO:
 - **Improvement-Aware Task Reweighting:** This goes beyond traditional loss-based weighting by incorporating task-wise improvement signals, preventing weight collapse and ensuring optimization effort is intelligently directed to tasks that are truly stagnating or underperforming. This idea could inspire similar robust optimization strategies in other complex RL settings.
 - **Ratio-Preserving Sampling:** This mechanism precisely solves the issue of "zero-gradient prompts," a previously unaddressed problem in multi-task GRPO. By ensuring that theoretical task weights translate into actual gradient contributions, it makes the optimization truly effective and robust. This approach to batch construction under varying data informativeness could be valuable in other fields.

4. **Improved Training Efficiency:** The demonstrated ability of MT-GRPO to achieve target worst-task accuracies in significantly fewer training steps translates to substantial savings in computational resources and time. This makes the expensive process of LLM post-training more accessible, scalable, and environmentally friendly, fostering faster iteration and development.

5. **Principled Trade-off Control:** The `λ` parameter offers a clear and effective way to control the trade-off between maximizing worst-task robustness and overall average performance. This flexibility allows developers to tailor models to specific deployment requirements, where different levels of robustness might be prioritized.

6. **Broader Impact on AI Research:** The paper contributes to the growing body of knowledge on multi-objective optimization, distributionally robust learning, and meta-learning, especially in the context of deep reinforcement learning. The identified challenges and proposed solutions could serve as a foundation for future research in training complex AI models for diverse, real-world tasks.

7. **Practical Application and Open Science:** The empirical validation on a standard LLM (Qwen-2.5-3B) and reasoning benchmarks, combined with the release of code, ensures that the research findings are immediately applicable and reproducible. This fosters further research and adoption by the wider AI community.

In conclusion, MT-GRPO addresses a fundamental challenge in LLM development by enabling robust and reliable reasoning across diverse tasks. Its innovative algorithmic contributions and strong empirical results pave the way for more capable, trustworthy, and broadly intelligent AI systems, pushing the frontier towards truly general-purpose LLMs.
