# Neuromorphic Reinforcement Learning for Quadruped Locomotion Control on Uneven Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Penn State University developed an Equilibrium Propagation-based Proximal Policy Optimization (EP-PPO) framework to train a bio-inspired controller for quadruped locomotion. This method achieved performance on uneven terrain comparable to backpropagation-trained controllers while demonstrating approximately 4.3 times lower GPU memory consumption for gradient estimation.

## Problem

- Conventional reinforcement learning (RL) policies for quadruped locomotion struggle with real-world adaptation to unforeseen conditions like terrain changes, requiring extensive offline retraining.
- High computational energy costs associated with backpropagation-trained RL and Model Predictive Control (MPC) policies limit their deployment on power-constrained robots.
- Existing CPG-RL approaches still primarily rely on computationally expensive backpropagation, hindering local, energy-efficient, on-chip learning.
- Application of Equilibrium Propagation (EP) in reinforcement learning, particularly for high-dimensional embodied control, remains largely unexplored.

## Method

- Proposed an Equilibrium Propagation-based Proximal Policy Optimization (EP-PPO) framework to train a bio-inspired quadruped controller, replacing conventional backpropagation-trained policy and value networks with EP-enabled networks.
- Derived and implemented an EP-compatible output-layer nudging signal for continuous control policy learning, incorporating a two-sided PPO ratio clipping mechanism and adjusted gradient scaling to stabilize updates.
- Integrated the EP policy networks into a two-branch Central Pattern Generator (CPG) and Residual (RES) controller architecture, trained in two stages for flat ground and uneven terrain traversal.

## Results

- The EP-PPO controller achieved locomotion performance on randomized uneven terrain comparable to a backpropagation-trained PPO baseline across metrics like success rate, forward velocity, and body stability.
- The EP algorithm consumed approximately **4.3** times less GPU memory for gradient estimation (3,110 MB) compared to backpropagation through time (13,486 MB) during training.
- Both EP and backpropagation-trained PPO controllers demonstrated stable policy convergence and comparable sample efficiency in learning stable locomotion and adapting to uneven terrain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Equilibrium Propagation can effectively train high-dimensional continuous-control policies and value functions within a PPO framework for complex embodied robotics tasks.
> - Stabilizing EP-based continuous control training requires specific mechanisms, including two-sided PPO ratio clipping and scaling the objective gradient with 1/σ for the log-standard deviation update.
> - Observation preprocessing, such as Inverse Discrete Cosine Transform (iDCT), accelerates convergence to equilibrium within EP networks, reducing the required relaxation steps and improving stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic locomotion has advanced significantly, enabling quadruped robots to traverse complex and uneven terrains. This progress has been largely driven by sophisticated control strategies such as Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). While these methods have demonstrated robust and agile locomotion, they typically rely on computationally intensive processes, often involving offline training in massively parallel simulations and deployment as fixed policies. This conventional approach presents several limitations for real-world, long-duration robot autonomy:
- **Limited On-Robot Adaptation:** Fixed policies struggle to adapt to unforeseen real-world conditions like terrain variations, payload changes, or actuator wear, necessitating laborious simulation-to-real transfer and offline retraining.
- **High Computational Energy Costs:** Conventional MPC and backpropagation-trained RL policies demand significant computational resources (CPUs/GPUs) for frequent high-dimensional inference and gradient propagation, which is a major constraint for onboard power budgets, especially for smaller robots or those operating in remote environments.

Biological locomotor systems offer inspiration for addressing these limitations, featuring hierarchical motor organization where central pattern generators (CPGs) create rhythmic movements, modulated by higher-level commands and adapted by sensory feedback. CPG-based robotic controllers exploit this by structuring the action space, reducing the burden on learned policies. However, traditional CPG-RL approaches still primarily use backpropagation for their trainable neural components.

This work integrates into the growing field of Neuro-AI, which seeks to develop more robust, adaptive, and energy-efficient artificial agents by drawing principles from biological intelligence. A key aspect of Neuro-AI for embodied robots is shifting from merely accelerating inference to designing learning rules compatible with local information processing and limited onboard resources. Backpropagation, the dominant training method, is biologically implausible and computationally expensive due to its reliance on global credit assignment and activation storage. Alternative local learning algorithms, such as feedback alignment, target propagation, predictive coding, and equilibrium propagation (EP), have emerged as promising alternatives.

Among these, Equilibrium Propagation (EP) is particularly relevant because both inference and learning are formulated through network relaxation dynamics, avoiding explicit global backpropagation graphs. EP is inherently compatible with in-memory and neuromorphic hardware. While EP has shown promise in supervised learning benchmarks and recent variants have improved its scalability, its application in reinforcement learning, particularly for high-dimensional embodied control tasks like quadruped locomotion on uneven terrain, remains an underexplored area. This paper specifically addresses this gap by demonstrating EP-based policy and value learning for such a complex task.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the gap between high-performance, simulation-trained quadruped locomotion and low-power, local neuromorphic learning. The authors propose an Equilibrium Propagation-based Proximal Policy Optimization (EP-PPO) framework to train a bio-inspired quadruped controller. This framework aims to enable energy-efficient local, on-chip learning while maintaining locomotion performance comparable to conventional backpropagation-trained controllers.

The key motivations and specific objectives of this work are:
1. **Enabling Energy-Efficient On-Robot Adaptation:** Current RL methods for quadruped locomotion are trained offline, limiting their ability to adapt to real-world variations (e.g., terrain, payload, actuator wear) under onboard power constraints. The motivation is to develop an algorithmic foundation for local learning that can support adaptation directly on the robot, reducing reliance on external infrastructure or power.
2. **Developing an EP-based PPO Framework for Continuous Control:** A core objective is to replace the backpropagation-trained policy and value networks in PPO with EP-enabled networks. This involves formulating EP-based updates for both policy learning and value regression, thereby offering a local-learning alternative to conventional actor-critic training and establishing a pathway for neuromorphic implementation of RL in high-dimensional robotics.
3. **Deriving an EP-Compatible Policy Objective Gradient:** To enable EP to train stochastic continuous-control policies, the authors aim to derive and implement a specific output-layer nudging signal. This signal maps the PPO policy objective onto EP nudge phase equilibrium dynamics. Furthermore, they seek to introduce a two-sided PPO ratio clipping mechanism and an adjusted gradient-scaling rule to stabilize policy updates during the relaxation process, which is critical for successful training of continuous control policies with EP.
4. **Integrating EP into a Bio-Inspired Controller:** The research aims to combine EP policy networks with a two-branch bio-inspired controller architecture. This architecture consists of a Central Pattern Generator (CPG) policy for structured rhythmic gait generation and a residual postural adjustment policy for joint-level corrections, particularly for uneven terrain traversal. This integration seeks to connect biological locomotion organization principles with the benefits of local neuromorphic learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-branch Central Pattern Generator (CPG) and Residual (RES) controller architecture, implemented on a 12-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) Unitree A1 quadruped robot model simulated in MuJoCo. The core methodology involves adapting the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learning framework to utilize Equilibrium Propagation (EP) networks instead of conventional backpropagation-trained feedforward networks for both policy and value functions.

#### 3.1. Controller Architecture

The controller integrates two main components:
- **CPG Policy Network:** This network provides parameters (amplitude, frequency, phase offset) to a bio-inspired Hopf oscillator for each leg. The oscillator generates rhythmic CPG states ($r_i, \theta_i, \phi_i$) that determine CPG foot Cartesian coordinates, which are then converted into CPG joint angles ($q_{j,i,CPG}$) via inverse kinematics.
- **RES Policy Network:** This network learns to output residual angle derivatives ($\dot{q}_{j,i,RES}$) for each joint. These derivatives are integrated over time to yield residual angles ($q_{j,i,RES}$), which are added to the CPG joint angles to form the final target joint angles ($q_{j,i,target} = q_{j,i,CPG} + q_{j,i,RES}$).
A proportional-derivative (PD) controller then converts these target joint angles into joint torques ($\tau_{j,i}$), which are applied to the simulated robot.
The RL policies operate at 100 Hz, while the CPG oscillator, residual integrator, PD controller, and MuJoCo simulation run at 1000 Hz, implying each high-level policy action is held for 10 low-level control steps. The CPG policy uses a 63-D observation vector and outputs a 12-D action vector, while the RES policy uses an 87-D observation vector and also outputs a 12-D action vector.

#### 3.2. EP-PPO for Continuous-Control Learning

The conventional backpropagation-trained policy and value networks within PPO are replaced with EP networks.
- **Value Network Training:** The value network is trained as a regression task, minimizing the L2 loss between its output and the generalized advantage estimation (GAE) return, using EP for gradient computation.
- **Policy Network Training:**
 - **EP-Compatible Nudging Signal:** The standard PPO action surrogate objective function ($L_{CLIP}$) needs to be maximized. For EP, this translates to applying a "nudging" signal to the output layer neurons during the nudge phases. The authors derive this signal by considering the gradient of the PPO objective with respect to the policy network's output mean ($\mu_t$).
 - **Stabilization Mechanism:** Initial attempts with a direct application of the PPO objective gradient led to training instability (large update steps and policy collapse). To address this, two modifications were introduced:
 1. **Two-sided PPO Ratio Clipping:** The original PPO clips the probability ratio only on one side depending on the advantage sign. This work introduces a two-sided ratio clip coefficient ($\epsilon_{rev}$) that bounds the ratio from both sides regardless of the advantage sign, preventing unbounded movement of the output neuron states.
 2. **Reduced Gradient Scaling:** The original PPO gradient uses $\sigma^2$ in the denominator for scaling. Experiments showed that using $\sigma$ instead ($1/\sigma$ scaling) significantly improved training stability, as EP's equilibrium shift is sensitive to gradient magnitude.
 - **Log-Standard Deviation (Log-std) Update:** To avoid using backpropagation entirely, the log-std vector (which determines the exploration noise in the continuous action space) is updated separately using explicitly calculated gradients of the PPO objective and an entropy loss with respect to each dimension of the log-std vector.
- **EP Network Structure:** Both policy and value networks are energy-based networks with two hidden layers, each containing 768 neurons. A hard-sigmoid activation function is used for hidden layers. Weights are initialized from a uniform distribution scaled by input fan-in. EP training involves a free phase (where the network settles to an equilibrium without external perturbation) followed by two nudge phases (positive and negative, where a small perturbation proportional to the objective gradient is applied to the output neurons). The parameter gradients are estimated by comparing the equilibrium states from these two nudge phases.
- **Observation Preprocessing:** An inverse discrete cosine transform (iDCT) is applied to the raw observation vector, converting it into a higher-dimensional space (1024-D). This empirically observed to accelerate convergence to equilibrium during EP relaxation and reduces the required inference iterations.

#### 3.3. Locomotion Training Paradigm

The training proceeds in two stages, mirroring existing CPG-RES approaches:
1. **Stage 1 (CPG Policy Training):** Only the CPG policy is trained on flat ground. The objective is to learn stable forward locomotion and accurate velocity tracking. The RES policy is disabled during this stage.
2. **Stage 2 (RES Policy Training):** The trained CPG policy is fixed. The RES policy is then trained on randomized uneven terrain to learn joint-level postural corrections, enabling robust traversal over obstacles.
The reward function encourages forward-velocity tracking, lateral/yaw tracking regularization, penalizes body instability (z-velocity, roll/pitch angular velocity), and regularizes actuator power consumption. Rollouts are collected from parallel MuJoCo environments. Domain randomization is applied to environment parameters (friction, limb mass, torso load, body height, swing clearance, stance penetration, random pushes) and uneven terrain generation (random box heights). Robot initial states, including CPG oscillator phases and joint angles, are also randomized, and a free-fall initialization process is used to ensure valid starting poses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPUs, with CPU memory consumption reaching approximately 200 GB for stage-2 training due to parallel MuJoCo environments. The proposed EP-based PPO controller was compared against a backpropagation (BP)-trained PPO baseline under identical task settings, PPO configurations, rollout sizes, and reward functions, with the primary difference being the neural network architecture and observation preprocessing (BP uses normalized observation without iDCT).

#### 4.1. Training Convergence and Sample Efficiency

- Both EP and BP-trained PPO controllers demonstrated stable policy convergence in both stage-1 (CPG on flat ground) and stage-2 (RES on uneven terrain) training, reaching similar final reward levels over 100 million samples.
- EP and BP exhibited comparable sample complexity in stage-1, meaning they learned to a similar performance level using a similar number of environmental interactions.
- The value networks for both EP and BP continuously minimized the value loss (Mean Squared Error), indicating effective learning of the state-value function.
- Analysis of per-update KL-divergence indicated that BP generally resulted in a smoother increment of KL-div. EP-based training occasionally exceeded the early stop KL threshold, leading to more frequent update rollbacks.

#### 4.2. Locomotion Performance on Uneven Terrain

A comprehensive walking test, extended from previous work, evaluated the control policies on randomized uneven terrain. This test measured success rate, average forward velocity, average actuator total power, average absolute roll/pitch angle, and average absolute roll/pitch angular velocity across three target velocities (0.1, 0.3, 0.5 m/s) and six levels of terrain height ($H_{max}$ from 0.02 m to 0.12 m).
- **Overall Performance Comparability:** The EP-based controller achieved locomotion performance comparable to the BP-trained PPO baseline across all evaluated indicators. This suggests that local EP learning can support robust, high-dimensional locomotion in complex environments.
- **Success Rate and Velocity Tracking:** Both EP and BP showed similar success rates in reaching target distances within the time limit, and comparable average forward velocities, indicating effective velocity tracking even on challenging terrain.
- **Actuator Power:** The average actuator power consumption for the EP controller was similar to that of the BP baseline, suggesting no significant energy overhead from the EP learning mechanism itself in terms of motor effort.
- **Body Stability:** Both controllers demonstrated similar levels of body stability, as indicated by comparable average absolute roll/pitch angles and angular velocities across different terrain difficulties and target speeds.

#### 4.3. GPU Memory Consumption

A significant finding related to computational efficiency was the GPU memory consumption during gradient estimation.
- During stage-1 training, the backpropagation through time (BPTT) algorithm consumed 13,486 MB of GPU memory.
- In contrast, the EP algorithm consumed 3,110 MB for the same task.
- This indicates that EP is approximately **4.3 times more memory-efficient** compared to BPTT, primarily due to its reliance on local computations rather than global gradient graphs.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (summarized from Appendix E)

- **Effect of $\epsilon_{rev}$ (Reverse PPO Ratio Clip Coefficient):** Experiments demonstrated that values of $\epsilon_{rev}$ = 0.3 and 0.7 led to stable convergence, while $\epsilon_{rev}$ = 1.0 resulted in training failure and numerical instability. This highlights the importance of the two-sided clipping mechanism in bounding output neuron state shifts during EP nudge phases.
- **Effect of Gradient Scaling ($1/\sigma$ vs. $1/\sigma^2$):** Using a scaling factor of $1/\sigma$ in the objective gradient led to more stable training and value loss minimization compared to $1/\sigma^2$, which caused frequent KL-divergence spikes and unstable value updates.
- **Effect of Dynamic vs. Static Gradient Mask:** The proposed dynamic gradient mask, which re-calculates the probability ratio at each relaxation step, was crucial for policy improvement. A static gradient mask (calculated once based on free-phase equilibrium) failed to improve the policy.
- **Effect of iDCT Observation Preprocessing:** iDCT preprocessing was shown to accelerate convergence to equilibrium within EP networks, requiring fewer relaxation steps for both policy and value networks in their free and nudge phases. It also maintained a higher fraction of samples converging within a maximum number of steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work demonstrates a significant step toward developing energy-efficient, adaptive robotic controllers. By successfully integrating Equilibrium Propagation (EP) with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to train a high-dimensional quadruped locomotion controller, the authors have shown that learning can occur without direct backpropagation through the policy and value networks.

The core significance and potential impact are:
- **Validation of Local Learning for Complex Embodied Control:** The results establish that local, equilibrium-based learning, traditionally studied on simpler supervised benchmarks, can effectively support contact-rich embodied control tasks like quadruped locomotion on uneven terrain. This expands the perceived applicability of neuromorphic learning algorithms to more challenging real-world robotics problems.
- **Algorithmic Foundation for Low-Power On-Robot Adaptation:** The EP-PPO framework provides an algorithmic pathway for energy-aware continual locomotion learning. By eliminating the need for global backpropagation, EP aligns naturally with the principles of neuromorphic and in-memory computing substrates, which are designed for parallel, local computations with high energy efficiency. This could enable robots to adapt and fine-tune their behaviors directly on board, using limited power resources, without needing to offload computation or rely on external infrastructure.
- **Improved Computational Efficiency:** The demonstrated 4.3x reduction in GPU memory consumption compared to Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) for gradient estimation highlights the practical computational benefits of EP's local learning rules, which could translate into lower energy consumption on specialized hardware.
- **Inspiration for Bio-Inspired Robotics and Neuro-AI:** By combining a bio-inspired CPG-RES controller architecture with a biologically plausible learning mechanism like EP, the work reinforces the utility of drawing inspiration from biological systems for advanced robotic control and learning. It contributes to the broader Neuro-AI initiative by offering a concrete example of how brain-inspired computing principles can address current limitations in artificial intelligence for robotics.

Despite these advances, the authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Sample Efficiency:** The current method inherits PPO's limited sample efficiency, which is a bottleneck for real-world robot learning where environmental interactions are costly and potentially unsafe. Future work will need to explore combining EP with more sample-efficient and safety-aware RL methods.
- **System-Level Implementation:** While the learning rule is local and neuromorphic-compatible, the overall implementation still relies on digital computation for elements like rollout storage, advantage estimation, normalization, and batch updates. Practical on-robot deployment will necessitate a holistic system-level neuromorphic design encompassing both EP network dynamics and the surrounding RL infrastructure.

In conclusion, this research provides a concrete bridge between local neuromorphic learning algorithms and modern RL-based quadruped locomotion, motivating future efforts to optimize for sample efficiency, safety, relaxation and memory costs, and ultimately, to realize hardware-oriented EP implementations for true low-power, on-robot adaptation.
