# Neuromorphic Reinforcement Learning for Quadruped Locomotion Control on Uneven Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Penn State University developed an Equilibrium Propagation-based Proximal Policy Optimization (EP-PPO) framework to train a bio-inspired controller for quadruped locomotion. This method achieved performance on uneven terrain comparable to backpropagation-trained controllers while demonstrating approximately 4.3 times lower GPU memory consumption for gradient estimation.

## Problem

- Conventional reinforcement learning (RL) policies for quadruped locomotion struggle with real-world adaptation to unforeseen conditions like terrain changes, requiring extensive offline retraining.
- High computational energy costs associated with backpropagation-trained RL and Model Predictive Control (MPC) policies limit their deployment on power-constrained robots.
- Existing CPG-RL approaches still primarily rely on computationally expensive backpropagation, hindering local, energy-efficient, on-chip learning.
- Application of Equilibrium Propagation (EP) in reinforcement learning, particularly for high-dimensional embodied control, remains largely unexplored.

## Method

- Proposed an Equilibrium Propagation-based Proximal Policy Optimization (EP-PPO) framework to train a bio-inspired quadruped controller, replacing conventional backpropagation-trained policy and value networks with EP-enabled networks.
- Derived and implemented an EP-compatible output-layer nudging signal for continuous control policy learning, incorporating a two-sided PPO ratio clipping mechanism and adjusted gradient scaling to stabilize updates.
- Integrated the EP policy networks into a two-branch Central Pattern Generator (CPG) and Residual (RES) controller architecture, trained in two stages for flat ground and uneven terrain traversal.

## Results

- The EP-PPO controller achieved locomotion performance on randomized uneven terrain comparable to a backpropagation-trained PPO baseline across metrics like success rate, forward velocity, and body stability.
- The EP algorithm consumed approximately **4.3** times less GPU memory for gradient estimation (3,110 MB) compared to backpropagation through time (13,486 MB) during training.
- Both EP and backpropagation-trained PPO controllers demonstrated stable policy convergence and comparable sample efficiency in learning stable locomotion and adapting to uneven terrain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Equilibrium Propagation can effectively train high-dimensional continuous-control policies and value functions within a PPO framework for complex embodied robotics tasks.
> - Stabilizing EP-based continuous control training requires specific mechanisms, including two-sided PPO ratio clipping and scaling the objective gradient with 1/\sigma for the log-standard deviation update.
> - Observation preprocessing, such as Inverse Discrete Cosine Transform (iDCT), accelerates convergence to equilibrium within EP networks, reducing the required relaxation steps and improving stability.
