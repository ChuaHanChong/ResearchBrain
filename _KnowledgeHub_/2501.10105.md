# Universal Actions for Enhanced Embodied Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> UniAct introduces a novel framework that learns a universal action space to abstract away embodiment-specific details, enabling embodied foundation models to effectively utilize diverse robot data. The approach allows a 0.5B-parameter model to outperform much larger state-of-the-art models in cross-embodiment generalization and adaptation, requiring minimal fine-tuning for new robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Action heterogeneity across diverse robotic embodiments (different DoF, mechanical structures, control interfaces) makes it difficult to aggregate and leverage large-scale robot demonstration data.
- Existing embodied foundation models often struggle with cross-embodiment generalization, either by oversimplifying action spaces or requiring extensive human engineering for common representations.
- The lack of a universal action representation hinders the development of generalist robotic agents capable of learning from diverse, internet-scale datasets, unlike LLMs or VLMs.

## Method

- UniAct learns a discrete "==Universal Action Space==" (UAS) as a vector-quantized codebook that captures generic, atomic behaviors universal across diverse robotics platforms.
- A shared Vision-Language Model (VLM) is fine-tuned as a universal action extractor, predicting the most relevant universal action for a given observation and task goal, using Gumbel-Softmax for differentiability.
- Lightweight, embodiment-specific MLP decoder heads translate the extracted universal action back into precise control signals for individual robots, ensuring efficient and targeted execution.

## Results

- UniAct-0.5B, a 0.5B-parameter model, outperforms **14x** larger state-of-the-art models (e.g., OpenVLA-7B, LAPA-7B) in cross-embodiment generalization on real-world and simulated benchmarks (e.g., WidowX, LIBERO).
- It demonstrates superior fast adaptation to new, unseen robots (e.g., AIRBOT with 4 controller types) by fine-tuning only **0.8%** of its parameters (4M out of 500M), compared to **1.4**-**2%** for baselines.
- Manual inspection and statistical analysis confirm that universal actions correspond to semantically meaningful, consistent behaviors across different robots, indicating the extractor effectively focuses on task progression rather than robot-specific details.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot actions, despite physical heterogeneity, can be distilled into a shared, discrete Universal Action Space (UAS) of atomic behaviors, enabling cross-embodiment generalization.
> - A shared Vision-Language Model (VLM) can act as an effective information bottleneck to extract universal actions, forcing the model to learn embodiment-agnostic behaviors.
> - Lightweight, embodiment-specific decoder heads allow for efficient translation of universal actions back into precise control signals for diverse robots, maximizing generalization while minimizing new parameters for adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Universal Actions for Enhanced Embodied Foundation Models" directly addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of large foundation models and embodied artificial intelligence. The recent successes of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), exemplified by GPT-4 and Qwen-VL, have largely stemmed from their training on vast, diverse, internet-scale datasets. This paradigm has driven remarkable generalization capabilities in natural language processing and computer vision.

Inspired by these successes, the AI community is increasingly focused on developing "embodied foundation models" – versatile agents capable of generalizing across tasks, environments, and, crucially, different physical embodiments. However, the paper points out a fundamental hurdle: **action heterogeneity**. Unlike text or images, robot action data is inherently diverse. This heterogeneity arises from:
1. **Distinct Physical Embodiments:** Robots differ in their Degrees of Freedom (DoF) and mechanical structures (e.g., robotic arms, quadrupeds, cars).
2. **Diverse Control Interfaces:** Even similar robots might use different control signals (e.g., end-effector position vs. velocity, joint positions).
3. **Multimodality in Human Behaviors:** Demonstrations from different human operators can lead to varied action sequences for the same task.

This heterogeneity means that action data collected from different robots or labs often resides in largely disjoint manifolds, making it extremely difficult to aggregate and leverage for training general-purpose models.

**Existing approaches and their limitations:**
- **Ignoring Heterogeneity:** Many prior works (e.g., RT-X series, Octo, OpenVLA) either implicitly or explicitly treat different action spaces as equivalent or apply uniform normalization/discretization. This can lead to a "conflicted action space" where similar encodings represent physically different meanings, hindering effective data sharing and generalization. These models, while achieving state-of-the-art results within their more homogeneous datasets (e.g., RT-X focuses on 7-DoF robots), struggle when confronted with truly diverse embodiments.
- **Naive Aggregation:** Some efforts (e.g., CrossFormer, RDT, π0, Yang et al.) attempt to aggregate all action spaces. However, these often require extensive human engineering to design physically interpretable common spaces and fail to automatically uncover inherent connections across diverse action types, thus impeding the development of truly general-purpose models.
- **Latent Action Spaces:** Other research (e.g., LAPA, IGOR, LAPO) develops latent action spaces, often by inferring dynamic changes between video frames. However, these latent actions may capture irrelevant visual changes (e.g., new objects appearing) rather than direct causal control signals, making them suboptimal for actual robot control. Furthermore, models like BeT, VQ-BeT, and QueST, while building discrete action codebooks, primarily focus on simpler domains with a single embodiment type, enhancing modeling of human multi-modal behaviors but not addressing cross-embodiment heterogeneity.

**UniAct's Contribution to the Landscape:**
UniAct positions itself as a direct solution to the action heterogeneity problem. It proposes to **learn a "Universal Action Space"** that abstracts away embodiment-specific details and captures *generic atomic behaviors* across diverse robots. By doing so, it aims to:
- Enable the effective utilization of vast, heterogeneous crowd-sourced embodied data.
- Facilitate robust cross-domain data sharing.
- Achieve superior cross-embodiment generalization.
- Allow for rapid, parameter-efficient adaptation to new robot platforms.

This approach signifies a shift from trying to homogenize heterogeneous actions in the physical space to learning a higher-level, abstract action representation that is inherently universal. It combines the strengths of VLM reasoning capabilities with a novel mechanism to learn generalizable robotic skills, making it a significant step towards truly versatile and scalable embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to advance the field of embodied artificial intelligence by enabling the development of **versatile embodied foundation models**. These models are envisioned to possess strong generalization capabilities, specifically across various tasks, environments, and most critically, **diverse robotic embodiments**.

The primary motivation stems from observing the remarkable success of large foundation models in fields like Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV). Their ability to learn general-purpose representations from vast, diverse, internet-scale data has led to unprecedented generalization. The research hypothesizes that applying a similar "recipe" to embodied AI—i.e., training on large, diverse robot demonstration datasets—could unlock similar breakthroughs for general-purpose robotic agents.

However, the authors identify a significant bottleneck that has prevented this direct translation of the foundation model paradigm to robotics: **action heterogeneity**. This problem manifests in several ways:
- **Physical Differences:** Robots have distinct mechanical structures and degrees of freedom (e.g., robotic arms vs. quadruped robots vs. mobile platforms).
- **Control Interfaces:** Even similar robots can be controlled via fundamentally different interfaces (e.g., end-effector position, joint velocities, torques).
- **Behavioral Multimodality:** Human demonstrations for the same task can vary significantly across individuals, further contributing to action diversity.

These factors result in embodied action data from different sources residing on largely disjoint manifolds, making it nearly impossible to directly combine and learn from them effectively. Existing methods either oversimplify this problem by treating diverse action spaces as equivalent (leading to conflicting interpretations) or require extensive human engineering to create common action representations, which hinders scalability and the discovery of inherent shared patterns.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the UniAct framework are:
1. **To introduce a "Universal Action Space":** This novel space is designed to capture generic, atomic behaviors that are universal across diverse robotics platforms, abstracting away embodiment-specific mechanics and control interfaces.
2. **To enhance cross-domain data utilization:** By mapping heterogeneous robot actions into a common universal space, UniAct aims to effectively leverage large, diverse datasets collected from various robots and institutions, overcoming the challenges of data sharing and integration.
3. **To enable enhanced cross-embodiment generalization:** The learned universal actions should facilitate the transfer of skills and control policies from one robot to another, even when the target robot has an unseen embodiment or control interface.
4. **To facilitate fast adaptation to new robots:** The framework should allow for efficient and straightforward deployment on novel robotic systems with minimal additional data and parameter tuning, by simply translating universal actions back to embodiment-specific commands.
5. **To demonstrate the practical benefits:** Through extensive evaluations on both real-world and simulated robots, the authors aim to showcase that learning and operating in a universal action space yields significant performance improvements and superior generalization compared to existing state-of-the-art embodied foundation models, especially those operating in heterogeneous physical action spaces.

In essence, the motivation is to unlock the full potential of large-scale, diverse robot data by abstracting actions into a learnable, common language of behaviors, thereby paving the way for truly generalist robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniAct introduces a novel framework for embodied foundation modeling that operates within a **Universal Action Space (UAS)**. This approach is designed to overcome the challenge of action heterogeneity across diverse robotic platforms.

**Core Idea: The Universal Action Space (UAS)**
The central tenet of UniAct is that all complex robot movements, despite being driven by heterogeneous control signals, can be distilled into a set of shared, latent atomic behaviors. These abstract representations are termed "universal actions." The paper opts for a **discrete universal action space**, motivated by the success of discrete representations in complex reasoning and planning within LLMs and VLMs. This space is implemented as a **vector-quantized codebook**, denoted as `U = (u1, u2..., uN)`, where `N` is the size of the codebook (e.g., 256 in their 0.5B instantiation) and each `ui` is a `D`-dimensional vector embedding (e.g., 128 dimensions). Each `ui` encapsulates a generic atomic behavior.

The authors contrast their UAS with prior latent action spaces (e.g., LAPA, IGOR), which often infer actions from visual changes between frames. UniAct argues that such methods are prone to noise, as observational changes can result from factors unrelated to the robot's control (e.g., new objects appearing). Instead, UniAct's universal actions are designed to be directly related to **task progression** and **actual control signals**.

**Universal Action Extraction**
To derive this desirable universal action space, UniAct employs a **shared Vision Language Model (VLM)** as the universal action extractor. The VLM is fine-tuned to predict the likelihood `p(u|o, g)` of selecting a universal action `u` given an observation `o` and a task goal `g` (e.g., a language instruction). The objective is for the extractor to infer the most relevant universal action `u*` that matches the atomic behavior demonstrated in the embodied data, thereby directly connecting universal actions to task completion.

Since the selection of `u*` via `argmax` is non-differentiable, UniAct utilizes the **Gumbel-Softmax technique** for categorical reparameterization during training. This allows for gradient propagation through the discrete action selection. The Gumbel-Softmax operation introduces Gumbel noise and a temperature parameter `τ`, which is gradually decayed during training to promote exploration early on and then stabilize convergence.
This VLM-based extractor acts as a crucial **information bottleneck**. By forcing diverse robots to map their actions into the *same* discrete codebook, the VLM is compelled to identify and exploit shared primitive behaviors across different action spaces, thus enhancing cross-domain generalization.

**Heterogeneous Decoding**
Once a universal action `u*` is extracted, it needs to be translated back into precise, embodiment-specific control signals for actual robot execution. This is achieved through a series of **lightweight decoder heads**, `H = (h1..., hK)`, where `K` is the number of distinct training domains/embodiments. Each decoder head `hk` is specifically designed for a particular embodiment.
The operation of a decoder head `hk` is formulated as `â(k) = hk(u*, o)`, where `â(k)` is the predicted control signal for embodiment `k`. These heads are implemented as **simple MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) networks**. They take the selected universal action `u*` and visual features extracted by a shared vision backbone as inputs.
The design choice of keeping these decoding heads lightweight is critical. It ensures that the majority of the learning capacity is dedicated to refining the universal actions and the extractor (the VLM), rather than allowing the decoders to overfit to specific embodiment details. This strategy maximizes the generalization potential across different embodiments, as the core, transferable behaviors are encoded in the universal action space.

**Training Procedure**
UniAct is trained end-to-end on a comprehensive collection of `K` heterogeneous datasets, `D = (D1..., DK)`. Each dataset `Dk` comprises robotic control trajectories, including observations, actions, and goals. The primary learning objective is a **behavior cloning loss** (`Lk`) that minimizes the difference between the predicted control signals `â(k)` and the ground-truth actions `a(k)` from the demonstrations. The loss function is customizable (e.g., Cross-Entropy for discrete actions, MSE/Huber/diffusion for continuous actions).

During training, both the universal action codebook `U` and the parameters of the universal action extractor (VLM) are concurrently updated. The heterogeneous decoder heads `hk` are updated based on domain-specific sampled training batches. This meta-learning-like strategy allows UniAct to learn globally shared parameters (universal actions) that facilitate adaptation across tasks, alongside embodiment-specific components (decoders) that ensure high performance for individual robots.

**Implementation Details (UniAct-0.5B)**
The authors present a 0.5B-parameter instantiation of UniAct. It is built upon `LLaVA-OneVion-0.5B`, a well-trained VLM, which provides robust multi-modal representations. The universal action space uses 256 codes, each 128-dimensional. Training was substantial, involving 64 A100 GPUs with DeepSpeed over 10 days, utilizing 1 million demonstrations from 28 distinct embodiments. Critically, the training data (from Open-X Embodiment, Libero, Droid) was standardized for third-person visual observations and language instructions, but its **action heterogeneity was deliberately preserved**, directly confronting the problem UniAct aims to solve.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of UniAct-0.5B demonstrates its significant capabilities in cross-embodiment generalization and efficient adaptation, often outperforming larger state-of-the-art models.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Against State-of-the-Art Baselines

UniAct-0.5B, despite being a 0.5B-parameter model, shows strong performance compared to models 14 times larger, such as OpenVLA (7B parameters) and LAPA (7B parameters). This highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of learning within a universal action space. The authors compare UniAct against Octo (0.1B), CrossFormer (0.1B), OpenVLA (7B), and LAPA (7B), with baselines either directly evaluated or fine-tuned according to official protocols.

#### 4.2. Real-World Robot Evaluation (WidowX Robot)

Evaluations on a real-world WidowX robot assess five dimensions of generalization: visual, motion, physical, semantic, and language grounding, across 19 tasks (190 rollouts).
- **Visual, Motion, and Physical Generalization:** UniAct-0.5B significantly outperforms 14X larger OpenVLA-7B and LAPA-7B. This demonstrates its superior robustness to visual distractions (varying lighting, backgrounds, object textures, distractors) and its ability to generalize low-level control for unobserved object positions, orientations, and physical properties (size, weight, material).
- **Semantic Generalization and Language Grounding:** UniAct-0.5B achieves comparable performance to OpenVLA-7B, even though OpenVLA leverages a much larger backbone (7B) known for its strong semantic understanding. This underscores UniAct's efficiency in handling novel objects and interpreting language commands.

#### 4.3. Simulation Evaluation (LIBERO Benchmarks - Franka Robot)

UniAct-0.5B is evaluated on the LIBERO Benchmarks, comprising 130 tasks across five suites. Notably, baseline models were fine-tuned on the LIBERO data for fair comparison.
- UniAct-0.5B surpasses all baseline models (Octo, CrossFormer, OpenVLA, LAPA) across all suites, achieving a significant overall average accuracy improvement.
- This superior performance is attributed to UniAct's core ability to bridge domain gaps and extract generalizable atomic behaviors by leveraging demonstrations from diverse domains.

#### 4.4. Fast Adaptation to New Embodiment (AIRBOT Robot)

To test adaptability, models are fine-tuned on an entirely new, unseen real robot, AIRBOT, with four distinct controller interfaces (relative/absolute EEF position and relative/absolute joint position). Only 100 demonstrations are used for fine-tuning.
- UniAct-0.5B consistently shows strong generalization across all control signal types, outperforming both OpenVLA and Octo.
- Crucially, UniAct's adaptation is highly parameter-efficient: only 0.8% of its total model size (4M out of 500M parameters) is used for fine-tuning (training new MLP decoder heads), whereas OpenVLA uses 1.4% (97M/7000M) and Octo uses 2% (2M/100M). This highlights UniAct's inherent generalizability, requiring minimal new parameters to adapt.

#### 4.5. Fast Adaptation to New Decoder Head (Bi-manual AIRBOT and ACT)

The paper further demonstrates UniAct's flexibility by integrating a more advanced decoder architecture (ACT) for a bi-manual AIRBOT robot, using richer observations (top view, two wrist views, proprioception).
- UniAct-0.5B with an ACT decoder significantly outperforms a pre-trained VLM (LLaVA-OV-0.5B) on complex bi-manual tasks like "sweep plate," "fold towel," "cup on plate," and "transport pen."
- Further improvements are observed when fine-tuning UniAct with the ACT decoder on the LIBERO benchmark, validating its adaptability to advanced decoders and richer observational data.

#### 4.6. In-depth Analysis of Universal Actions

The paper provides qualitative and quantitative evidence that UniAct learns a meaningful universal action space:
- **Interpretation of Universal Actions:** Manual inspection reveals that at least 40% of the 256 universal actions consistently decode into semantically meaningful behaviors across different robots, even with varying viewpoints and significant sim-to-real gaps. For instance, the same universal action can consistently translate to "moving forward" despite different robot mechanics.
- **Direct Control with Universal Actions:** The learned universal actions are so interpretable that a robot can be controlled by simply selecting a sequence of universal action IDs, without needing complex robotic knowledge (e.g., kinematics). This suggests the potential for universal actions to serve as an "action tokenizer" for planning in future embodied foundation models.
- **Statistical Analysis (JS Divergence):** Quantitatively, the utilization distributions of universal actions (measured by JS divergence) are highly similar for the *same tasks performed by different robots*, but distinct for *different tasks on the same robot*. This crucial finding indicates that the universal action extractor effectively focuses on task progression and extracts embodiment-agnostic behaviors, rather than merely encoding robot-specific details.

In summary, UniAct-0.5B provides compelling evidence that explicitly modeling a universal action space is a highly effective strategy for building scalable and generalizable embodied foundation models, offering superior performance and adaptation capabilities with remarkable parameter efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of UniAct and its concept of a Universal Action Space (UAS) represents a significant advancement in embodied artificial intelligence, holding substantial potential impact across research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Significance of the Research

- **Addressing Action Heterogeneity:** UniAct directly tackles one of the most fundamental and persistent challenges in embodied AI: the inherent heterogeneity of action spaces across different robotic platforms. By learning an abstract, universal representation of atomic behaviors, it circumvents the limitations of prior approaches that either ignored this heterogeneity or aggregated it crudely.
- **Enabling Data Scale-Up:** The core bottleneck for developing generalist embodied agents, akin to the success of LLMs and VLMs, has been the inability to effectively utilize vast, diverse robot demonstration data collected from various sources due to incompatible action spaces. UniAct's universal action space unlocks this potential, making it feasible to train on internet-scale robot data.
- **Superior Generalization and Adaptation:** The empirical results demonstrate that UniAct-0.5B, a relatively compact model, significantly outperforms much larger (14x) state-of-the-art embodied foundation models in cross-embodiment generalization and adaptation tasks. This suggests that the quality of the action representation (universal actions) can be more impactful than simply scaling up model size.
- **Parameter Efficiency in Adaptation:** The ability to rapidly adapt to new robots and control interfaces by training only lightweight, embodiment-specific decoders, while keeping the core universal action extractor frozen, is a major advantage. This drastically reduces the data and computational resources required for deploying robots in new settings.
- **Interpretability of Actions:** The finding that universal actions correspond to consistent, semantically meaningful behaviors across diverse robots (e.g., "moving forward") is profoundly significant. It not only aids in understanding the learned representations but also opens doors for more intuitive human-robot interaction and control.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Accelerated Robotic Skill Acquisition:** By enabling the effective pooling of diverse robot data, UniAct can accelerate the pace at which robots learn new skills. Instead of learning from scratch or from limited domain-specific datasets, future robots could benefit from a vast "experience library" encoded in the universal action space.
- **Lowering Barriers to Robot Deployment:** For new robotic platforms or specialized tasks, the need for extensive, costly, and time-consuming data collection and fine-tuning can be dramatically reduced. Robots could quickly acquire new capabilities by connecting to pre-trained universal action models and training minimal decoding components.
- **Foundation for Advanced Embodied AI:** The concept of universal actions as a discrete, semantically meaningful "skill library" or "action tokenizer" can serve as a fundamental building block for future embodied foundation models. This could facilitate more sophisticated planning, reasoning, and hierarchical control, where high-level cognitive models operate in the universal action space, while low-level decoders handle embodiment-specific execution.
- **Towards Truly Generalist Robots:** This work is a crucial step towards realizing the vision of general-purpose robots that can seamlessly operate across a wide variety of tasks and physical forms, moving beyond narrow, task-specific or robot-specific policies.
- **Cross-Domain Knowledge Transfer:** The principles demonstrated in UniAct could inspire similar approaches in other domains facing data heterogeneity challenges, fostering more efficient and generalizable AI systems.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors)

The authors acknowledge several avenues for future work, indicating ongoing research directions and potential improvements:
- **Broader Embodiment Coverage:** Currently, UniAct is primarily evaluated on single-arm robotic platforms with varied control interfaces. Future work aims to extend the universal action space to encompass a wider range of embodiments, including bi-manual robots, dexterous hands, quadrupeds, and even autonomous driving cars, exploring the commonalities across vastly different mechanical structures.
- **Flexible Network Design for Decoders:** While current decoders are lightweight MLPs, future work will explore more complex and specialized decoder heads tailored to the control complexity and data richness of specific embodiments, potentially incorporating proprioceptive data and multiple camera views more dynamically.
- **Scaling Laws for Universal Action Training:** A critical future direction is to systematically study the relationship between data quantity, embodiment diversity, and the performance of the universal action space, to optimize data collection strategies for maximal impact.
- **Enhanced Utilization of Universal Actions:** The learned universal actions can be leveraged beyond direct control, for instance, as "tokenizers" for world models, enabling more accurate prediction and simulation, or for more advanced planning algorithms.

In conclusion, UniAct presents a compelling solution to a long-standing problem in embodied AI, with immediate practical benefits and a clear path toward developing the next generation of versatile and adaptable robotic systems.
