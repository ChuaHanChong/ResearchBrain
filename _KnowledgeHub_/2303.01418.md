# Human Motion Diffusion as a Generative Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> PriorMDM treats a pretrained Motion Diffusion Model as a generative prior and composes it three ways: DoubleTake stitches short clips into arbitrarily long prompted sequences at inference time, ComMDM adds a slim learned communication block for two-person interaction from a dozen examples, and DiffusionBlending blends fine-tuned control models. It beats dedicated task-specific baselines on transition FID and joint control.

## Problem

- Annotated motion datasets are tiny and homogeneous, mostly short single-person clips, so long-sequence and multi-person generation remain poorly served by existing generators.
- Prior long-motion methods generate autoregressively conditioned only on the previous suffix, and rely on explicitly annotated transitions, producing sliding artifacts and needing post-hoc interpolation.
- Existing multi-person predictors learn smoothness-promoting transforms that lower L2 error but yield static unrealistic motion, and no multi-person dataset carries text annotations.

## Method

- **DoubleTake**: a zero-shot two-take inference scheme where batched intervals share averaged handshake frames, then a partial re-noising pass with a soft inpainting mask refines transitions.
- **ComMDM**: a single-layer ==transformer== communication block inserted after layer 8 of two frozen ==MDM== instances, outputting a symmetric correction term plus each person's initial pose.
- Fine-tuned control plus **DiffusionBlending**: masking noise on controlled features during training and sampling, then generalizing ==classifier-free guidance== to interpolate two condition-specific models.

## Results

- On BABEL, DoubleTake cuts transition FID from **3.86** to **1.88** at 70-frame margins and **7.93** to **3.45** at 30 frames versus the dedicated TEACH baseline.
- Ablations on HumanML3D show the full two-take scheme reaches **0.60** motion FID and **1.48** transition FID, versus **1.00** and **2.15** for first take only.
- DiffusionBlending drops FID from **1.18** to **0.22** on wrist-plus-trajectory control; users preferred ComMDM over the prediction baseline **79.2%** on quality despite higher L2 error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A pretrained diffusion model can act as a manifold projector, so new tasks only need mechanisms coordinating composed parts rather than retraining on scarce data.
> - Classifier-free guidance generalizes beyond conditional-versus-unconditional: any two aligned diffusion models can be interpolated to combine their control signals.
> - L2 joint error rewards dull low-frequency motion; it disagreed with human preference here, so distribution-level metrics and user studies matter for interaction quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion generation has seen remarkable progress, especially with the advent of denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) integrated with elaborate language models. Recent works like Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) (Tevet et al., 2023), MotionDiffuse (Zhang et al., 2022), MoFusion (Dabral et al., 2023), and FLAME (Kim ets al., 2022) have demonstrated the ability to generate diverse and high-quality human motion sequences from textual or other control signals. These advancements hold significant promise for applications in gaming, animation, and virtual reality, potentially democratizing character animation for a broader audience.

Despite these breakthroughs, the core challenge in human motion generation remains data scarcity. High-quality motion data is typically acquired through expensive motion capture systems or laboriously crafted by artists, leading to relatively small, homogeneous, and often short-duration, single-person datasets (e.g., HumanML3D, BABEL). This inherent limitation severely restricts the capabilities of current models, particularly for complex tasks such as generating long, continuous sequences, simulating multi-person interactions, or achieving fine-grained control over specific body parts. These "out-of-domain" scenarios often result in poor generation quality or are simply unaddressable by existing data-driven models.

This paper addresses these critical limitations by introducing a novel paradigm: leveraging pre-trained diffusion-based motion generation models as **generative priors** for composition. The central idea is to overcome the data consumption reputation of diffusion models by showing that a powerful prior, specifically the state-of-the-art MDM, can be adapted to enable non-trivial tasks in few-shot or even zero-shot settings. The work positions itself as a crucial step towards bridging the gap between existing single-person, short-clip generation capabilities and the more complex, real-world demands of animation and interactive systems.

In comparison to existing literature:
- **Long-Sequence Generation:** Traditional approaches like Motion Graphs (Kovar et al., 2008) are constrained by existing data. More recent auto-regressive methods (e.g., TEACH by Athanasiou et al., 2022) generate sequences incrementally, often relying on learned transitions or explicit annotations. This paper's *DoubleTake* method, in contrast, leverages the diffusion process itself to blend motions at each denoising step, considering both past and future contexts simultaneously, enabling zero-shot generation of arbitrarily long, coherent sequences.
- **Multi-Person Motion Generation:** This area is particularly data-starved. Prior works like MRT (Wang et al., 2021) and SoMoFormer (Vendrow et al., 2022) focused on unsupervised prefix completion, often yielding unrealistic, smooth motions due to DCT transform usage. This paper introduces *ComMDM* to achieve *text-guided* two-person motion generation, a significant advancement, using a novel communication block and demonstrating effectiveness even with a very small, manually annotated dataset.
- **Fine-Grained Motion Control:** While MDM offers motion inpainting, this paper shows it doesn't extend well to complex tasks like trajectory or end-effector tracking. By proposing a specific fine-tuning strategy and *DiffusionBlending*, it offers a surgical and flexible control mechanism, analogous to classifier-free guidance, allowing the combination of multiple control signals, a key capability for any professional animation system.

By effectively extending the capabilities of a fixed, pre-trained MDM, this work challenges the notion that diffusion models are solely data-hungry and demonstrates their versatility as foundational generative priors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the acknowledged limitations of current human motion generation models, particularly the scarcity and homogeneity of motion data. Existing datasets predominantly feature short, single-person sequences, posing significant barriers to achieving more complex, real-world animation goals. The authors succinctly state that "in the absence of data, tasks like multi-person interaction and long sequence generation are left behind, with poor generation quality."

Against this backdrop, the overarching objective of this paper is to demonstrate that pre-trained diffusion-based motion generation models can be effectively leveraged as **generative priors for composition**, thereby enabling "out-of-domain motion generation and efficient control" even in data-scarce settings. The authors aim to introduce three novel forms of composition—sequential, parallel, and model composition—each designed to tackle a specific limitation of current motion generation systems.

The key objectives can be broken down as follows:

- **Long Sequence Generation (Sequential Composition):** To develop a method capable of generating arbitrarily long, coherent human motion sequences. This method must allow for individual textual control over distinct time intervals and ensure realistic, semantically matching transitions between these intervals, all while using a prior model trained only on short clips and without requiring new training data (zero-shot). The challenge here is to overcome the inherent length limitations of training data and models.

- **Multi-Person Interactive Motion Generation (Parallel Composition):** To enable the text-driven, simultaneous generation of motion for two interacting people. This objective is driven by the extreme scarcity of annotated multi-person motion data. The goal is to achieve this with minimal new training examples (few-shot), by focusing on learning the coordination and communication between two independent person-specific priors rather than training a complex multi-person model from scratch.

- **Fine-Grained Motion Control and Editing (Model Composition):** To introduce a flexible and precise control mechanism for human motion, allowing users to dictate specific features such as root trajectory, individual joint movements, or combinations thereof. The motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing inpainting-based control methods, which often fail to produce semantically consistent and physically plausible results when applied to detailed control tasks. The objective is to achieve surgical control over body parts, a crucial capability for professional animation pipelines.

In essence, the paper seeks to push the boundaries of what is possible with existing motion diffusion models by creatively combining their generative power for novel, complex tasks, thereby addressing the persistent challenges of data limitations and control granularity in human motion synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodological principle of this paper is to utilize a pre-trained Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) (Tevet et al., 2023) as a robust generative prior for various motion composition tasks. MDM is a denoising diffusion model based on the DDPM framework, trained to predict clean motion from a noised input, conditioned on text. The authors leverage this powerful base model and introduce three distinct composition techniques:

#### 3.1. Sequential Composition: DoubleTake for Long Sequence Generation

**Objective:** Generate arbitrarily long motions with interval-specific textual control from a model trained on short clips (zero-shot).
**Approach:** DoubleTake is an inference-time, two-stage process that generates long motion in a single batch, differing from auto-regressive methods that generate segments sequentially.
1. **First Take (Handshake Generation):**
 - Multiple short motion intervals, each with its own text prompt, are generated simultaneously within the same batch.
 - A "handshake" mechanism, a short prefix or suffix of the motion, is maintained between neighboring intervals. The prefix of the current motion is forced to be equal to the suffix of the previous motion.
 - This is achieved by overriding the handshake frames with a frame-wise average of the relevant suffix and prefix at each denoising step. This allows each interval to be aware of its past and future context.
2. **Second Take (Transition Refinement):**
 - After the first take, artifacts and inconsistencies may appear at transitions, especially between semantically different motions.
 - To refine these, the batch is reshaped into "transition sandwiches" (current sequence, handshake, next sequence).
 - These sandwiches are partially noised and then denoised back using a novel *soft-masking* feature. Unlike binary inpainting masks, the soft mask assigns a value between 0 and 1 to each frame, dictating the degree of refinement (blending between original and newly generated content).
 - The final long sequence is constructed by unfolding these refined intervals and transitions.

#### 3.2. Parallel Composition: ComMDM for Two-Person Generation

**Objective:** Enable few-shot, text-driven two-person motion generation.
**Approach:** The key insight is to dedicate a fixed MDM instance for each person, ensuring the individual motions remain within the human motion distribution. The challenge then becomes learning only the coordination between them.
1. **Frozen Priors:** Two instances of the pre-trained MDM are kept frozen.
2. **Slim Communication Block (ComMDM):** A lightweight, single-layer transformer model, ComMDM, is introduced.
 - It is inserted after a specific transformer layer (e.g., layer 'n') of both MDM instances.
 - ComMDM takes the output activations from this layer for both actors ($O_{1,(n)}^t, O_{2,(n)}^t$) as input.
 - It then predicts a correction term for these activations (${\Delta O}_{i,(n)}^t$), which is added back to the original activations. To reduce parameters, symmetry is exploited: it learns one correction term based on the order of input.
 - **Initial Pose Learning:** To mitigate issues where MDM (trained on normalized data) might place both characters at the origin, ComMDM additionally learns the initial pose ($D$) for each person at the first frame as part of the diffusion process.
3. **Few-Shot Training:** Only ComMDM is trained using the $L_{simple}$ loss on a very limited dataset (e.g., 10 training examples from 3DPW, manually annotated with text). It is evaluated for both prefix completion and text-to-motion tasks.

#### 3.3. Model Composition: Fine-Tuned Motion Control and DiffusionBlending

**Objective:** Achieve fine-grained, self-coherent control over specific joints, trajectories, or combinations thereof, which MDM's original inpainting struggles with.
**Approach:**
1. **Single Control Fine-Tuning:**
 - The pre-trained MDM is fine-tuned for specific control tasks (e.g., root trajectory, left wrist position).
 - During the training's forward pass, the noise applied to the ground-truth control features (e.g., trajectory) is masked out. This propagates the clean control features to the model's input, teaching it to rely on them for reconstruction.
 - During sampling, the user-defined control features are injected into the predicted $X_0$, and noise is masked out in these features during the forward process to $X_{t-1}$. This ensures the control signal is cleanly maintained. This fine-tuning is computationally inexpensive (less than 20K steps).
2. **DiffusionBlending for Composite Control:**
 - To combine multiple control signals (e.g., trajectory + left wrist), the authors generalize the concept of classifier-free guidance.
 - DiffusionBlending interpolates or extrapolates between two "aligned" fine-tuned models ($G_a$ and $G_b$), each trained for a specific control condition ($c_a$ and $c_b$).
 - The combined prediction is computed as $G_{a,b}^s(X_t, t, c_a, c_b) = G_a(X_t, t, c_a) + s \cdot (G_b(X_t, t, c_b) - G_a(X_t, t, c_a))$, where 's' is a scale parameter balancing the influence of the two control signals. This allows for flexible and surgical control with any cross-combination of joints.

In essence, the methodology revolves around augmenting a powerful motion diffusion prior with specialized, lightweight modules and innovative inference-time or fine-tuning strategies to unlock capabilities previously constrained by data or model design.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of its three composition methods in extending the capabilities of a pre-trained Motion Diffusion Model (MDM).

#### 4.1. Long Sequences Generation (DoubleTake)

- **Quantitative Performance:** On the BABEL dataset, DoubleTake consistently outperformed TEACH (a dedicated long-sequence generation model) in terms of Frechet Inception Distance (FID), a measure of how similar the distribution of generated motions is to the ground truth. This was true for both individual motion intervals and, significantly, for transitions between intervals, where DoubleTake showed "even larger" gaps in its favor. R-precision (motion-to-text alignment) and Diversity metrics were competitive.
- **Qualitative Performance:** DoubleTake successfully generated "10-minute long fluent motions" from a model trained on only 10-second sequences. Visual comparisons (Figure 10) highlight that DoubleTake produces "coherent motion with realistic transitions," whereas TEACH often suffers from "sliding" artifacts. This demonstrates robust temporal coherence despite limited training data for long sequences.
- **Ablation Studies:** Experiments on the HumanML3D dataset confirmed the optimal hyperparameters for DoubleTake, including the effectiveness of the second take (transition refinement), soft masking, and a one-second handshake size, which all contribute to superior results (Table 2, Figure 5).

#### 4.2. Two-Person Generation (ComMDM)

- **Prefix Completion:** While ComMDM showed slightly higher L2 errors compared to MRT (a prior multi-person model) in quantitative metrics (Table 3), a crucial user study revealed a different picture.
 - **User Study:** 30 unique users overwhelmingly preferred ComMDM's generated motions over both MRT and the original MDM in terms of "quality," "completion" of the prefix, and "interaction" level (Figure 8). MRT tended to generate "static and unrealistic motions," fixating on the prefix pose, whereas ComMDM produced "lively and semantically correct completions" (Figure 6). This highlights that quantitative L2 error might not perfectly capture perceptual quality for complex motion.
- **Text-to-Motion:** ComMDM demonstrated the first-ever text-guided two-person motion generation. Given unseen text prompts like "A Capoeira practice. One is kicking and the other is avoiding the kick," the model generated diverse and interactive two-person motions (Figures 1, 7). The authors acknowledge that, due to the extremely small dataset (10 training examples from 3DPW, manually annotated by them), generalization is somewhat limited to interaction types seen during training.
- **Ablation Studies:** ComMDM's performance was found to be optimal when the communication block was placed in higher layers of the MDM transformer and when it consisted of fewer layers itself, suggesting that fine-grained, high-level interaction information is more beneficial (Table 3).

#### 4.3. Fine-Tuned Motion Control (DiffusionBlending)

- **Single Control Tasks:** Fine-tuned MDM models dramatically improved control adherence compared to the original MDM's inpainting method.
 - **Quantitative:** For tasks like "Trajectory control" or "Left Wrist control," fine-tuned models achieved significantly lower FID and higher Diversity scores, moving closer to ground truth distribution (Table 4).
 - **Qualitative:** The original MDM often "completely ignore[d] the input features," leading to artifacts like "massive foot sliding" for trajectory control or "unrealistically bend[ing] [a] hand behind the back" (Figure 9). Fine-tuned models, conversely, generated "natural motions that semantically and physically match the input features," such as a walking motion tailored to a circular trajectory or a golf swing motion for specific hand control.
- **Composite Control (DiffusionBlending):** This method successfully combined multiple control signals.
 - **Quantitative:** For blended tasks like "Left Wrist + Trajectory" or "Left Wrist + Right Foot," DiffusionBlending achieved the best FID and Diversity scores among all methods, significantly outperforming MDM (Table 4).
 - **Qualitative:** DiffusionBlending enabled "surgical and flexible control" over multiple body parts, demonstrated by tasks such as "writing 'hello' in mid-air" (Figure 1, Right).

In summary, the results strongly validate the paper's central hypothesis: by strategically composing and adapting a pre-trained MDM, complex and data-intensive motion generation tasks can be effectively tackled, often outperforming dedicated prior art and yielding high-quality, controlled, and diverse motions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant conceptual and practical advancement in the field of human motion generation, with broad implications for animation, gaming, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction.

**1. A New Paradigm for Generative Priors:** The most significant conceptual contribution is the formalization and demonstration of using a powerful motion diffusion model as a "generative prior" for the human motion manifold. This challenges the conventional wisdom that diffusion models are inherently data-hungry. Instead, the paper shows that by creatively composing existing priors, new, complex tasks can be addressed with minimal or no additional data, essentially extending the applicability of existing models far beyond their initial training scope. This paradigm shift could inspire similar approaches in other generative AI domains.

**2. Overcoming Data Scarcity Challenges:** The paper offers concrete, effective solutions to the long-standing problem of data scarcity in human motion capture.
 - **Zero-shot Long Sequence Generation:** DoubleTake's ability to generate arbitrarily long, coherent motion from models trained only on short clips eliminates the need for vast datasets of extended activities, which are prohibitively expensive and rare. This significantly reduces the barrier to creating continuous, immersive character animations.
 - **Few-shot Multi-Person Interaction:** ComMDM’s success in text-guided two-person generation with only a handful of examples is a breakthrough. Multi-person interaction data is extremely rare and challenging to annotate. This method paves the way for generating dynamic and interactive multi-character scenes without extensive new data acquisition, making such complex scenarios more accessible for content creators.

**3. Enhanced and Flexible Control for Animation:** The fine-tuned motion control and DiffusionBlending techniques offer unprecedented flexibility and precision, which are critical for professional animation pipelines.
 - **Surgical Control:** The ability to precisely control root trajectory, individual joints, or combinations thereof, ensuring semantic and physical plausibility, provides animators with powerful tools for crafting specific movements and expressive performances. This goes beyond simple text-to-motion, enabling detailed artistic direction.
 - **Democratization of Animation:** By simplifying complex control tasks and requiring less data expertise, these methods could lower the entry barrier for novices and greatly enhance the efficiency of professionals, accelerating content creation in various media.

**4. Broad Extensibility Beyond Motion:** The authors explicitly note that the proposed techniques (DiffusionBlending, DoubleTake, ComMDM) are not inherently domain-specific. This suggests a powerful blueprint for adapting pre-trained generative models across other modalities (e.g., images, audio, 3D objects) for novel compositional tasks. Investigating these cross-domain applications represents a promising avenue for future research and could unlock new capabilities in diverse generative AI fields.

**5. Open Science and Community Impact:** The provision of code and trained models online (via GitHub and the project page) is a crucial contribution. This fosters reproducibility, allows other researchers to build upon their work, and enables wider adoption of these advanced generation and control methods by the community, accelerating further innovation.

While acknowledging limitations, such as potential inconsistencies in very long sequences or generalization issues in multi-person interaction without more diverse data, this paper lays a strong foundation. It firmly establishes diffusion models as versatile, adaptable priors capable of tackling complex, data-scarce generative challenges through intelligent composition, opening up exciting possibilities for the future of AI-driven content creation.
