# JEPA4Rec: Learning Effective Language Representations for Sequential Recommendation via Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture

> [!summary] Summary
> VinUniversity researchers introduced JEPA4Rec, a framework that applies the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to learn language representations for sequential recommendation. The model consistently outperformed existing baselines by an average of 6.22% in Recall@10 and 10.06% in NDCG@10, demonstrating enhanced data efficiency and robustness in various recommendation scenarios.

## Problem

- ID-based sequential recommendation systems face cold-start issues and limited knowledge transfer across domains.
- Existing text-based methods often struggle with a semantic gap between general Pretrained Language Model (PLM) knowledge and specific item descriptions, or require extensive pre-training data.
- A need exists for flexible item text representation, common-sense user preference learning, and efficient training strategies in data-sparse environments.

## Method

- **JEPA4Rec** integrates the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with a ==Longformer== encoder and specialized embeddings for word tokens, positions, and token types to create rich item text representations.
- A two-stage training strategy is employed, consisting of a pre-training phase with Mapping Representation, Masked Language Modeling (MLM), and Sequence-Item (S-I) Contrastive tasks.
- The fine-tuning stage focuses on the target domain, utilizing the pre-trained Context Encoder to predict next items with a cross-entropy loss over a dynamic item matrix.

## Results

- JEPA4Rec consistently outperformed state-of-the-art ID-only, ID-text, and text-only baselines across six diverse datasets, showing average improvements of **6.22%** in Recall@10 and **10.06%** in NDCG@10.
- The model achieved strong zero-shot performance on unseen domains, outperforming some fully supervised ID-only methods, and demonstrated competitive results using only **35%** of the pre-training data volume compared to previous studies.
- It exhibited robustness in low-resource training scenarios and when item descriptions were incomplete, maintaining stable performance and outperforming baselines under data sparsity and partial information conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - JEPA's ability to predict abstract representations and its asymmetric encoder design effectively prevent representation collapse without requiring negative samples, proving beneficial for learning robust item embeddings.
> - The multi-objective pre-training framework (Mapping, MLM, S-I Contrastive) efficiently bridges the semantic gap between general PLM knowledge and specific recommendation contexts, learning generalizable item representations and common-sense user preferences.
> - A moderate masking ratio (around 50%) for next-item sentences during pre-training provides an effective learning signal for generalizable representations, enhancing the model's ability to handle incomplete information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict a user's next interaction based on their historical behavior. Historically, these systems have largely relied on ID-based methods, such as those employing Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), or Transformers (e.g., SASRec, BERT4Rec). While effective at capturing sequential patterns, ID-based methods face challenges with cold-start items and limited transferability of knowledge across different domains due to their reliance on discrete, non-transferable item identifiers.

To address these limitations, several research directions have emerged. Cross-domain methods attempt to leverage overlapping users or items to transfer knowledge, but their real-world applicability is often constrained by the scarcity of shared data. Another avenue involves utilizing item modalities like text or images, yet a semantic gap between these modalities and user preferences can hinder effectiveness. The advent of Pretrained Language Models (PLMs), such as BERT, has shown promise in learning generalizable representations from textual data. However, applying PLMs directly to recommendation tasks faces challenges, including the potential mismatch between general pre-training data (e.g., Wikipedia) and specific item descriptions, and the difficulty of generating sentence-level embeddings that accurately reflect nuanced user preferences. Existing text-only methods (e.g., UniSRec, VQ-Rec, RecFormer) often require extensive pre-training data and sometimes decouple textual understanding from sequential modeling.

This paper introduces a novel approach that integrates the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) into the domain of language representation learning for sequential recommendation. JEPA, while previously successful in computer vision tasks, had remained largely unexplored in natural language processing and recommendation systems. JEPA's mechanism of predicting abstract representations rather than raw inputs, and its asymmetric encoder design to prevent representation collapse without requiring negative samples (unlike contrastive learning), positions it as a potential solution to learn robust and generalizable item embeddings. This work seeks to bridge the identified gaps by leveraging JEPA to create flexible item text representations, learn common-sense user preferences, and devise an efficient training strategy suitable for data-sparse environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance sequential recommendation by effectively leveraging language representation learning and knowledge from Pretrained Language Models (PLMs). This overarching goal is driven by the need to overcome several persistent challenges in the field.

The authors identify three specific key challenges that motivate their work:
1. **Flexible Item Text Representation:** Existing methods often simplify item representations through basic attribute concatenation. A key objective is to develop a more flexible and semantically rich method for representing items as text, capable of capturing detailed descriptive information.
2. **Learning Common-Sense User Preferences:** Beyond explicit interaction patterns, understanding inherent user behaviors such as brand loyalty or category preferences (referred to as "common sense" in this context) is crucial for improved generalization. The aim is to design a framework that can learn both item sequences and these common-sense user preferences.
3. **Efficient Training Strategy:** There is a need for an efficient training strategy that can effectively bridge the semantic gap between general text understanding (from PLMs) and the specific context of recommendation data. This strategy must also maintain effectiveness in sparse data settings, which are prevalent in real-world recommendation scenarios.

The motivation to employ the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) stems from its inherent properties:
- **Abstract Representation Learning:** JEPA's ability to predict abstract representations rather than raw tokens allows it to focus on capturing meaningful semantic features while disregarding low-level noise. This is considered beneficial for learning robust item embeddings.
- **Avoiding Negative Samples:** Unlike contrastive learning methods, JEPA eliminates the need for explicit negative samples, simplifying the training process and potentially avoiding issues related to suboptimal negative sampling strategies.
- **Prevention of Representation Collapse:** The asymmetric encoder design within JEPA helps to prevent representation collapse, ensuring that the learned embeddings remain distinct and informative.

By integrating JEPA, the proposed JEPA4Rec framework aims to transform item metadata into semantically rich text sentences, effectively learn these sentence representations, and capture common-sense user preferences through structured and transferable representation learning. This is intended to improve recommendation performance, particularly in scenarios characterized by data sparsity, cold-start items, and the need for cross-domain or cross-platform knowledge transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, JEPA4Rec, integrates the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with language modeling for sequential recommendation. It operates through a two-stage training strategy: pre-training and fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The model handles sequential recommendation with multi-domain interaction data. Each user's history in a domain is a sequence of items $s = \{i_1, i_2, \dots, i_n\}$. Instead of item IDs, items are represented by flattening their textual attributes (e.g., title, category, description) into a single text sentence $S_i = \{a_{i1}, v_{i1}, \dots, a_{im}, v_{im}\}$. The objective is to learn user preferences from the sequence of item sentences $s = \{S_1, \dots, S_n\}$ and predict the next item sentence $S_{n+1}$.

#### 3.2. Item Representation

To process item information, JEPA4Rec constructs input sequences and leverages specialized embeddings:
- **Model Inputs:** The interaction history is formulated as $X = \{[\text{CLS}], S_n, \dots, S_1\}$, where $[\text{CLS}]$ is a special token for sequence generalization, and items are ordered from most recent to oldest.
- **Encode Item Mechanism:** Four types of embeddings are summed and layer-normalized to form the input representation for each word $w$: $E_w = \text{LayerNorm}(A_w + B_w + C_w + D_w)$.
 - **Token Embeddings (A):** Represent the meaning of each word token, independent of item IDs, offering flexibility.
 - **Token Position Embeddings (B):** Capture the positional information of tokens within a sentence.
 - **Token Type Embeddings (C):** Distinguish whether a token belongs to $[\text{CLS}]$, an attribute key, or an attribute value, which is important for handling repetitive metadata structures.
 - **Item Position Embeddings (D):** Encode the position of items within a user's interaction sequence, where all tokens of an item $S_i$ share the same vector $D_i$.
- **Encoder:** A Longformer, a bidirectional Transformer optimized for long sequences, is used. The $[\text{CLS}]$ token has global attention, while other tokens use local windowed attention. The output is $d$-dimensional word representations, with $h_{CLS}$ serving as the sequence representation. Item embeddings $h_i$ are derived by treating each item $S_i$ as a single-item sequence.
- **Context and Target Encoders:** The same embedding layer and Longformer encoder are used for both the Context Encoder ($f_\theta$) and the Target Encoder ($f_{\theta'}$). The Target Encoder's parameters are updated via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the Context Encoder to ensure stable training and prevent representation collapse.

#### 3.3. Pre-training Framework

The pre-training phase establishes a robust parameter initialization through three self-supervised learning objectives:
- **Masking Strategy:**
 - For the next item $S_{n+1}$, 50% of its tokens are masked, and the model predicts its full representation.
 - For historical sentences ($S_1, \dots, S_n$), a 15% token masking rate is applied.
 - Masking follows the BERT-style: 80% replaced with $[\text{MASK}]$, 10% with a random token, and 10% kept unchanged.
 - One masked item per sequence is sampled. A learnable zero vector is used if no tokens are masked.
 - The full representations of a masked history item $h_n$ and the next item $h_{n+1}$ are obtained from the Target Encoder.
- **Mapping Representation Task:** A lightweight MLP-based Predictor ($g_\phi$) reconstructs full item representations from partial information.
 - To predict a masked history item $S_n$, the predictor uses $h_{CLS}$ concatenated with the Item Position Embedding $D_n$: $\hat{h}_n = \text{FFN}_1(h_{CLS} \oplus D_n)$.
 - To predict the next item $S_{n+1}$, the predictor uses $h_{CLS}$ concatenated with the masked next item embedding $h_{n+1,M}$: $\hat{h}_{n+1} = \text{FFN}_2(h_{CLS} \oplus h_{n+1,M})$.
 - The **Mapping Loss** ($L_{map}$) is defined as the sum of L2 distances between predicted and target representations: $L_{map} = \|h_n - \hat{h}_n\|^2 + \|h_{n+1} - \hat{h}_{n+1}\|^2$.
 - The **Masked Language Modeling (MLM) Loss** ($L_{MLM}$) is a standard cross-entropy loss for masked token prediction.
- **Sequence-Item (S-I) Contrastive Task:** This objective facilitates next-item prediction.
 - Ground truth next items are positive instances.
 - Negative instances are selected as in-batch negatives (ground-truth items from other sequences in the same batch). This strategy is used to reduce computational costs and enhance cross-domain generalization.
 - The **S-I Loss** ($L_{S-I}$) is a contrastive loss based on cosine similarity.
- **Total Pre-training Loss:** $L_{PT} = L_{S-I} + \lambda_1 \cdot L_{MLM} + \lambda_2 \cdot L_{map}$, where $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ are hyperparameters.

#### 3.4. Finetuning Framework

After pre-training, only the Context Encoder is fine-tuned on the target domain.
- All item sentences are encoded to create a dynamic, learnable item matrix $I$. This matrix is updated per epoch.
- Next-item prediction probabilities are computed using a softmax over the entire dataset: $P_I(i_{n+1}|h_{CLS}) = \text{Softmax}(h_{CLS} \cdot h_{n+1})$.
- The fine-tuning loss ($L_{FT}$) is a cross-entropy loss derived from a sequence-item contrastive learning task, using full softmax over all items in the dataset based on cosine similarity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of JEPA4Rec was conducted on Amazon review datasets for pre-training and fine-tuning, including cross-domain and cross-platform scenarios. Pre-training utilized approximately 35% of the data volume typically seen in prior studies.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- JEPA4Rec consistently outperformed all baseline methods—including ID-only (SASRec, BERT4Rec), ID-text (S3-Rec, LlamaRec), and text-only (UniSRec, VQ-Rec, RecFormer) models—across six diverse datasets (Scientific, Pet, Instruments, Arts, Office, Online Retail).
- On average, JEPA4Rec showed improvements of 6.22% in Recall@10 and 10.06% in NDCG@10.
- Text-only methods generally performed better than ID-only or ID-text approaches on Amazon datasets, where rich textual metadata is available. ID-based methods remained effective on the dense Online Retail dataset.
- The performance indicated the efficacy of JEPA4Rec's two-stage training, where pre-training facilitates the learning of generalizable item representations and fine-tuning enhances adaptability to new domains.

#### 4.2. Efficient Learning Representation Performance

- **Universal Pre-training:** Experiments demonstrated that JEPA4Rec, when pre-trained on three datasets, surpassed models pre-trained on a single dataset. It also outperformed the fine-tuned public checkpoint of RecFormer, which was pre-trained on a larger set of seven Amazon datasets. This finding suggests that JEPA4Rec achieves competitive results with a reduced volume of pre-training data, highlighting its data efficiency.
- **Zero-shot Performance:** In zero-shot settings, where models were evaluated on domains not seen during pre-training, JEPA4Rec outperformed other text-only models (RecFormer, UniSRec) by 1-5% across five datasets. Notably, on the Scientific dataset, JEPA4Rec's zero-shot performance exceeded that of SASRec, an ID-only method, even when SASRec was fully supervised on the target domain. This indicates JEPA4Rec's ability to learn and transfer common-sense user preferences effectively.
- **Low-Resource Training:** Under conditions of limited training data (e.g., 1% or 5% of the full dataset), text-only models, particularly JEPA4Rec, consistently outperformed ID-based models. As the training data increased, ID-based models showed improvement, but JEPA4Rec maintained its position as the top-performing model across all data scales, demonstrating robustness in data-scarce environments.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted on the Scientific and Online Retail datasets to evaluate the contributions of JEPA4Rec's components:
- **Without MLM Loss:** Removing the Masked Language Modeling (MLM) loss led to a reduction in performance across all metrics, indicating its importance in bridging the semantic gap between general PLM knowledge and recommendation-specific textual data.
- **Without Pre-training:** A significant performance drop was observed when pre-training was omitted, underscoring the necessity of this stage for learning generalizable item embeddings and common-sense user preferences.
- **Without Token Type Embeddings:** This component's absence had a minor impact on the Scientific dataset but notably reduced Recall@10 on the Online Retail dataset, suggesting its role in distinguishing patterns within item sentences, especially in diverse datasets.
- **Masking Ratio Analysis:** An analysis of the next-item sentence masking ratio revealed that NDCG@10 scores improved as the masking ratio increased from 10% to 50%, and then degraded at 80%. A moderate masking ratio around 50% was found to provide an effective learning signal for generalizable representations.

#### 4.4. Additional Experimental Results (Appendix)

- **Case Studies:** JEPA4Rec exhibited strong adaptability to partial item information. In a zero-shot setting on the Instruments dataset, its performance in Recall@10 doubled with only 10% of item text information, outperforming RecFormer with larger gains. This indicates robust inference capabilities even with minimal textual cues.
- **Robustness Analysis:** When items had incomplete descriptions (e.g., 20-60% of tokens masked), JEPA4Rec maintained stable performance with only modest degradation, consistently outperforming RecFormer by 3-5% in NDCG. This robustness is attributed to learning item representations in the embedding space via its mapping loss pre-training.
- **Token Embedding Component Analysis:** Further ablation confirmed the importance of MLM loss and all token embedding components (token type, token position). Combined removal of multiple components led to progressively worse performance.
- **Loss Components Ablation:** Dropping the contrastive loss during pre-training or replacing it with BPR loss during fine-tuning resulted in performance degradation, particularly on the Online Retail dataset. This suggests that the multi-negative sampling strategy of contrastive learning provides richer training signals compared to single-negative BPR.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of JEPA4Rec presents several significant contributions to the field of sequential recommendation and broader machine learning applications involving language representations.

Firstly, the consistent outperformance of JEPA4Rec against state-of-the-art methods across multiple real-world datasets indicates a notable advancement in recommendation accuracy. This improvement is particularly pronounced in challenging scenarios such as cross-domain, cross-platform, and low-resource environments, which are common in practical applications where data sparsity and cold-start items remain significant hurdles.

Secondly, the framework demonstrates enhanced generalizability and data efficiency. By leveraging the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) and a tailored two-stage training strategy, JEPA4Rec learns robust and transferable item representations. Its capability to achieve strong performance with only a fraction (approximately 35%) of the pre-training data typically required by previous state-of-the-art models highlights its efficiency. This reduced reliance on extensive pre-training data makes the approach more scalable and applicable in settings where vast domain-specific datasets are unavailable. The robust zero-shot capabilities, allowing the model to perform well on unseen domains without explicit fine-tuning, further underscore its generalizability.

Thirdly, JEPA4Rec's robustness to incomplete item information signifies its practical utility. Its ability to infer item representations and make accurate recommendations even when item descriptions are partially masked or sparse suggests that the model can function effectively in real-world systems where metadata quality may vary. This resilience is attributed to the model's ability to learn in the embedding space through its mapping loss pre-training, which facilitates a deeper semantic understanding rather than relying on surface-level textual cues.

Finally, this work marks a novel application of JEPA, an architecture previously successful primarily in computer vision, to the domains of Natural Language Processing and sequential recommendation. This integration opens new avenues for exploring self-supervised learning techniques that predict abstract representations in these fields. The findings from JEPA4Rec could potentially inspire future research into similar architectures for learning more generalizable, data-efficient, and robust representations for a wide range of tasks involving textual data and sequential patterns, moving beyond traditional token-level or contrastive learning paradigms.
