# JEPA4Rec: Learning Effective Language Representations for Sequential Recommendation via Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture

> [!summary] Summary
> VinUniversity researchers introduced JEPA4Rec, a framework that applies the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to learn language representations for sequential recommendation. The model consistently outperformed existing baselines by an average of 6.22% in Recall@10 and 10.06% in NDCG@10, demonstrating enhanced data efficiency and robustness in various recommendation scenarios.

## Problem

- ID-based sequential recommendation systems face cold-start issues and limited knowledge transfer across domains.
- Existing text-based methods often struggle with a semantic gap between general Pretrained Language Model (PLM) knowledge and specific item descriptions, or require extensive pre-training data.
- A need exists for flexible item text representation, common-sense user preference learning, and efficient training strategies in data-sparse environments.

## Method

- **JEPA4Rec** integrates the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with a ==Longformer== encoder and specialized embeddings for word tokens, positions, and token types to create rich item text representations.
- A two-stage training strategy is employed, consisting of a pre-training phase with Mapping Representation, Masked Language Modeling (MLM), and Sequence-Item (S-I) Contrastive tasks.
- The fine-tuning stage focuses on the target domain, utilizing the pre-trained Context Encoder to predict next items with a cross-entropy loss over a dynamic item matrix.

## Results

- JEPA4Rec consistently outperformed state-of-the-art ID-only, ID-text, and text-only baselines across six diverse datasets, showing average improvements of **6.22%** in Recall@10 and **10.06%** in NDCG@10.
- The model achieved strong zero-shot performance on unseen domains, outperforming some fully supervised ID-only methods, and demonstrated competitive results using only **35%** of the pre-training data volume compared to previous studies.
- It exhibited robustness in low-resource training scenarios and when item descriptions were incomplete, maintaining stable performance and outperforming baselines under data sparsity and partial information conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - JEPA's ability to predict abstract representations and its asymmetric encoder design effectively prevent representation collapse without requiring negative samples, proving beneficial for learning robust item embeddings.
> - The multi-objective pre-training framework (Mapping, MLM, S-I Contrastive) efficiently bridges the semantic gap between general PLM knowledge and specific recommendation contexts, learning generalizable item representations and common-sense user preferences.
> - A moderate masking ratio (around 50%) for next-item sentences during pre-training provides an effective learning signal for generalizable representations, enhancing the model's ability to handle incomplete information.
