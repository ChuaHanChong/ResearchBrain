# Self-Harmony: Learning to Harmonize Self-Supervision and Self-Play in Test-Time Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Self-Harmony framework enhances Large Language Model reasoning at test-time without external supervision by leveraging cooperative self-play and a theoretically grounded harmonic mean for robust pseudo-label selection. This method, which utilizes a single LLM in dual roles, consistently outperforms existing approaches across diverse benchmarks and models.

## Problem

- Enhancing Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning capabilities traditionally requires costly human-curated datasets for supervised fine-tuning.
- Existing Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL) methods using majority voting for pseudo-labeling are prone to reinforcing systematic reasoning errors, creating an 'echo chamber' effect.
- A lack of robust, self-contained mechanisms for generating reliable, label-free learning signals within test-time adaptation paradigms without external verifiers.

## Method

- A ==cooperative self-play== framework where a single LLM dynamically takes on two roles: a =='Solver'== for problem-solving and a =='Reframer'== for generating semantically equivalent paraphrases.
- Generates multiple 'views' of a problem by having the Reframer create stylistically diverse reformulations, acting as an ==online data augmentation== mechanism.
- Applies a theoretically justified ==harmonic mean== for pseudo-label selection, identifying answers that are both frequently generated and consistent across original and reframed problem views.

## Results

- Self-Harmony achieved state-of-the-art performance across **28 of 30** configurations, encompassing **5** open-source LLMs and **6** challenging reasoning benchmarks.
- Performance improved substantially; for example, Llama-3.1-8B's accuracy on GSM8K increased from **60.5%** to **91.6%** with only **16** original and **16** reframed rollouts.
- The framework demonstrated robust training stability with zero failures and consistently produced pseudo-labels with higher accuracy (**80-85%**) throughout training compared to competing methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate answers demonstrate inherent stability and robustness when confronted with semantically equivalent yet stylistically varied problem rephrasings.
> - The harmonic mean score, theoretically justified by a View-Invariant Infomax objective, offers a principled method for selecting high-quality pseudo-labels by rewarding consistency across diverse problem views.
> - Optimizing the cooperative self-play process by fusing reframing and solving actions into a single generative step enhances computational efficiency at test-time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) with advanced reasoning capabilities has historically relied on extensive, human-annotated datasets for supervised fine-tuning (SFT). The significant cost and effort associated with creating such datasets have driven research towards more autonomous model adaptation paradigms. Test-time adaptation (TTA) allows models to adjust at inference time using only unlabeled problems and available computational resources. Within TTA, Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL) represents a notable advancement, enabling models to improve their reasoning by generating and leveraging self-generated feedback signals, thereby eliminating the need for external supervision.

Existing TTRL methods frequently generate multiple solution candidates and infer a "pseudo-label" for training, often employing self-consistency or majority voting mechanisms. However, this approach carries a critical vulnerability: if an LLM exhibits systematic reasoning flaws, it may consistently produce a specific incorrect answer more often than the correct one. In such scenarios, majority voting not only fails to rectify the error but can inadvertently amplify it by selecting the erroneous solution as a learning target, a phenomenon described as the "majority-vote trap." This limitation underscores a demand for TTRL methodologies capable of generating robust reward signals that can reliably guide models beyond their inherent biases and systematic errors.

Self-Harmony addresses this core challenge within the TTRL landscape by proposing a novel framework for generating reliable pseudo-labels. It builds on the premise that a correct answer should be stable across different semantic representations of a problem, thereby providing a robust learning signal that mitigates the risks associated with single-view pseudo-labeling. This work extends the concept of self-play, typically used in adversarial contexts, into a cooperative framework. It also draws inspiration from unsupervised signal selection methods that enforce consistency across multiple data views, but applies a more stringent criterion (harmonic mean) compared to traditional majority voting. By doing so, Self-Harmony aims to advance TTRL by enabling more stable and effective model adaptation without human intervention or reliance on external verifiers, making it a fully self-contained, label-free solution for improving LLM reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and stable test-time reinforcement learning (TTRL) framework that enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to improve their reasoning capabilities without relying on human-annotated labels or external auxiliary models. The authors motivate this work by identifying a critical vulnerability in current TTRL approaches: the generation of unreliable learning signals.

Specifically, the core motivations and objectives are:

1. **Addressing the "Majority-Vote Trap"**: Current TTRL methods often use majority voting or self-consistency to derive pseudo-labels from multiple generated answers. The authors observe that if an LLM consistently produces a specific incorrect answer due to a systematic flaw, majority voting will reinforce this error, leading to a degradation rather than an improvement in model performance. The motivation is to overcome this "echo chamber" effect.
2. **Leveraging View Invariance for Robustness**: The fundamental intuition guiding Self-Harmony is that a correct answer to a problem should remain stable and consistent even when the problem is rephrased or viewed from a different perspective, provided its underlying semantics are preserved. Conversely, spurious or incorrect answers are hypothesized to be more fragile and sensitive to stylistic changes. The objective is to operationalize this intuition to distinguish robust, correct answers from view-dependent, flawed ones.
3. **Developing a Novel Self-Play Mechanism**: The authors propose a cooperative self-play framework where a single LLM dynamically assumes two complementary roles: a "Solver" that generates answers to problems, and a "Reframer" that rephrases the input problem to create diverse, semantically equivalent perspectives. The motivation is to enable the model to generate its own diverse training data and consistency checks internally, without external resources.
4. **Introducing a Principled Pseudo-Label Selection Method**: Building on the dual-view approach, the research aims to replace standard majority voting with a more robust pseudo-label generation strategy. The authors propose using the harmonic mean of answer frequencies across the original and reframed problem views. The motivation here is that the harmonic mean naturally favors answers that appear consistently and frequently in *both* distributions, thereby effectively filtering out solutions that arise from biased reasoning in only one view.
5. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance and Stability**: A key objective is to demonstrate that the Self-Harmony framework, with its novel pseudo-labeling and cooperative self-play, can achieve superior reasoning performance compared to existing label-free TTRL methods across various benchmarks and LLM sizes. Furthermore, the framework aims to exhibit unprecedented training stability, preventing common failure modes observed in other adaptation techniques.

In essence, the motivation is to create a fully autonomous, reliable, and effective test-time adaptation paradigm that can self-correct an LLM's reasoning abilities by internally generating robust learning signals based on a principle of semantic consistency across diverse problem representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Self-Harmony framework is designed to address the limitations of pseudo-label selection in Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL) by generating robust learning signals from multiple, self-generated views of a problem.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Motivation

The task is to adapt a pre-trained LLM (π_θ) to an unlabeled test dataset. For each question `x`, the model generates `n` rollouts. Since ground-truth labels are unavailable, a pseudo-label `y*` must be inferred from these rollouts to guide policy optimization. Standard majority voting, as a dominant approach, selects the most frequent answer. However, the authors argue that if the probability of a correct answer `p(Correct | x)` is less than that of a wrong answer `p(Wrong | x)`, majority voting can amplify incorrect solutions. To counter this "majority-vote trap," Self-Harmony draws inspiration from human problem-solving heuristics, where solutions are cross-checked against reformulated versions of the problem. This motivates the principle that pseudo-labels should be evaluated based on their invariance across multiple views, rather than mere popularity within a single problem description.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analysis: From Majority Voting to Harmonic Mean

The methodology includes a theoretical foundation for its pseudo-label selection mechanism.
- **Dual-view majority voting**: A simple extension to multiple views, `y* = arg max_a [p_0(a) + p_1(a)]`, where `p_0(a)` is the probability of answer `a` for the original query `x`, and `p_1(a)` for a reformulated query `x'`. This approach can still fail if different wrong answers dominate each view, potentially overwhelming the correct answer's combined probability.
- **View-Invariance Assumption**: The core assumption is that the correct answer `C` depends only on the underlying semantics of a query, implying `p(A = C | x) = p(A = C | x')` for semantically equivalent `x` and `x'`. Incorrect answers, conversely, are assumed to vary across views due to their dependence on superficial artifacts.
- **View-Invariant Infomax Objective**: To formalize this, the authors define an objective `J_λ(a) = I(Z_a; A) - λI(Z_a; X)`. This objective maximizes mutual information between the answer indicator `Z_a` and the actual answer `A`, while penalizing dependence on the specific view `X` (original or reframed).
- **Theorem 3.2 (Harmonic Mean Selector)**: Under conditions including non-degeneracy, balanced confidence, and uniform view prior, the authors prove that for a penalty weight `λ = 2`, the pseudo-label that maximizes the second-order approximation of the View-Invariant Infomax objective is given by the harmonic mean of the two view-probabilities: `y* = arg max_a [2 * p_0(a) * p_1(a) / (p_0(a) + p_1(a))]`. This theorem establishes the harmonic mean as a principled regularizer that enforces view-invariance, penalizing solutions that are strong in one view but weak in another.

#### 3.3. Self-Harmony Framework

The framework operationalizes the harmonic mean principle through structured self-play:
- **Single Model, Dual Roles for View Generation**: A single LLM (`M_θ`) dynamically assumes two roles via prompting: a "Solver" (`π_θ`) to generate answers, and a "Reframer" (`ρ_θ`) to create semantically equivalent paraphrases (`x'`).
- **Conceptual Sequence**: The process conceptually follows a "solve → reframe → solve" sequence. The Solver first generates answers for `x`. Then, the Reframer generates `x'`. Finally, the Solver generates answers for `x'`.
- **Pseudo-Label Selection via Harmonic Mean Score (HMS)**: Using empirical frequencies `p_0(a)` and `p_1(a)` from rollouts on `x` and `x'`, the pseudo-label `y*` is chosen as the answer `a` that maximizes its Harmonic Mean Score.

#### 3.4. From Conceptual Framework to Practical Implementation

To enhance computational efficiency, the conceptual three-step process is optimized:
- **Fused Actions**: The reframing and the second solving steps are fused into a single generative action. A system prompt instructs the model to first paraphrase the question and then immediately solve its own paraphrase within one generation. This reduces the process to two efficient model calls: one for the initial solution and one for the joint "reframe-and-solve" trajectory.
- **Policy Optimization with Fused Actions**: The reward structure is designed for this fused dynamic:
 - **Reward for Initial Solving Action**: `R_solve(y) = I[y = y*]`, where `y*` is determined after both trajectories.
 - **Reward for Fused Reframing-and-Solving Action**: `R_fused(y') = (1 - w_f R_penalty_format(y'))(1 - w_d R_penalty_div(y', y))I[y' = y*]`.
 - `I[y' = y*]` acts as a success gate, ensuring only correct answers receive reward.
 - `R_penalty_format` penalizes structural violations in the rephrased problem.
 - `R_penalty_div` is a Jensen-Shannon divergence between the answer distributions of the original and reframed queries, incentivizing the Reframer to generate meaningfully diverse paraphrases.
- **Algorithm**: The overall algorithm involves iterating through minibatches, generating solutions and reformulations, calculating pseudo-labels via HMS, computing rewards for both actions, and updating model parameters (`θ`) via policy optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Self-Harmony's effectiveness was evaluated through extensive experiments across multiple reasoning benchmarks and open-source LLMs.

#### 4.1. Dominant Performance Across Benchmarks

Self-Harmony demonstrated consistent superiority, achieving state-of-the-art results in 28 out of 30 tested configurations (5 models across 6 reasoning benchmarks), and ranking second in the remaining 2.
- **Example Performance Gains**:
 - On MATH500, Self-Harmony improved Qwen3-1.7B's accuracy from 42.70% to 69.60%, nearly matching the oracle GT-Reward score of 71.80%, while no baseline exceeded 65%.
 - For Llama-3.1-8B on GSM8K, performance increased from 60.5% to 91.6% (with 16 original and 16 reframed rollouts).
 - Qwen3-4B on MATH500 saw an improvement from 60.2% to 78.5%.

#### 4.2. Superior Training Stability

The framework exhibited high robustness and stability throughout all experiments, with zero reported training failures. This indicates the reliability of its reward modeling and training paradigm, particularly when compared to other TTRL methods that can suffer from unstable learning curves. Figure 3a shows Self-Harmony maintaining the highest training stability with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on MATH500.

#### 4.3. Pseudo-Label Quality Evaluation

Self-Harmony consistently generated pseudo-labels with higher accuracy compared to baselines. On MATH500 (Level 3), its pseudo-label accuracy stabilized at 80-85% (Figure 3b). Further analysis in Appendix L indicated that the harmonic mean score provided more calibrated supervision by effectively penalizing spurious, view-dependent answers, as supported by F1-score and Spearman correlation metrics.

#### 4.4. Module Ablation Study

An ablation study on the MATH500 benchmark confirmed the necessity of each component:
- **Reframer Rewards**: Removing either the diversity reward or the format reward from the Reframer consistently led to performance degradation. For instance, on Qwen3-8B, removing the diversity reward reduced accuracy from 79.80% to 78.90%, emphasizing the importance of generating meaningfully different problem views.
- **Pseudo-label Selection**: Replacing the Harmonic Mean Score (HMS) with simpler strategies like Majority Voting or Cross Selection resulted in lower performance. Qwen3-8B's accuracy dropped to 79.00% with Majority Voting and 78.40% with Cross Selection, compared to 79.80% with HMS.

#### 4.5. Sensitivity to Reward Coefficients

A grid search demonstrated that Self-Harmony is robust to the choice of the format (`w_f`) and diversity (`w_d`) reward coefficients. Performance remained consistently high (above 89.5% accuracy on Math500) across a wide range of values (Figure 4), suggesting that the method does not require extensive hyperparameter tuning.

#### 4.6. Reframed Question Examples

Examples provided (Figure 5) illustrate the Reframer's ability to rewrite problems by introducing stylistic diversity while rigorously preserving semantic constraints, thus ensuring the consistency checking mechanism is valid.

#### 4.7. Additional Experiments

- **Convergence of Pseudo-Label Selectors**: HMS initially selected more accurate labels, particularly when the model was uncertain, before converging with dual-view majority voting as the policy stabilized (Figure 9).
- **Semantic Consistency Validation**: External LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 4 Sonnet) showed high consistency rates (around 88%) between solutions to original and Reframer-generated problems, validating the Reframer's ability to preserve semantic meaning (Table 4).
- **Sensitivity Analysis on Semantic Shift**: Self-Harmony showed robustness even when using smaller (0.6B) Reframers that might produce noisier paraphrases, demonstrating that the HMS selector can still recognize valid answers despite imperfect semantic equivalence (Table 5).
- **Harmonic Mean Pseudo Label Selector vs. Harmonic Mean Reward**: Using the harmonic mean as a pseudo-label selector yielded better performance than using it as a direct reward signal (Figure 10).
- **Additive vs. Multiplicative Reward**: The multiplicative reward formulation for fused actions demonstrated slightly faster convergence compared to an additive reward formulation, while achieving similar final performance (Figure 11).
- **Generalized Mean Comparison**: Empirical results across fine-grained confidence/correctness categories (Table 8) and general inference-only comparisons (Table 9) reinforced the superiority of the harmonic mean over other generalized means and majority voting, particularly in challenging "Confident-Incorrect" and "Not-Confident-Correct" scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Self-Harmony framework presents a significant advancement in the field of Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL) and self-supervised learning for Large Language Models (LLMs). Its primary significance lies in addressing a critical limitation of existing TTRL approaches: the vulnerability of pseudo-label generation to an LLM's systematic reasoning flaws, often amplified by mechanisms like majority voting.

The potential impacts of this work include:

1. **Enhanced Reliability of LLM Adaptation**: By introducing a principled, theoretically grounded pseudo-label selection mechanism (Harmonic Mean Score) that emphasizes view-invariance, Self-Harmony provides a more robust and reliable signal for model self-correction. This can lead to more consistent and trustworthy performance improvements in deployed LLMs.
2. **Reduced Reliance on Costly Human Supervision**: Self-Harmony operates in a completely label-free paradigm, performing adaptation solely at test time using self-generated signals. This significantly reduces the dependency on expensive, human-curated datasets for fine-tuning or verification, which is a major bottleneck in scaling LLM capabilities.
3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness**: The framework's core intuition—that correct answers should be stable across different problem phrasings—promotes the learning of more robust reasoning patterns. By penalizing view-dependent, spurious solutions, Self-Harmony encourages LLMs to internalize deeper semantic understanding rather than relying on superficial cues, potentially leading to better generalization across diverse problem formulations.
4. **Broader Applicability of TTRL**: The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across various open-source models (Qwen3, Llama-3 series) and diverse reasoning benchmarks (mathematics, multi-subject, multi-task) highlights the broad applicability of Self-Harmony. It shows that effective TTRL is feasible even for models of varying sizes and across different types of complex reasoning tasks.
5. **Foundation for Future Self-Correction Mechanisms**: The cooperative self-play mechanism, where a single model takes on dual roles as a Solver and a Reframer, offers a novel blueprint for how LLMs can internally generate diverse perspectives and self-verify their outputs. This approach could inspire future research into more sophisticated self-correction and self-improvement techniques for AI systems.
6. **Practical Deployment Benefits**: The framework's superior training stability (zero failures reported) and robustness to hyperparameter choices are practical advantages for deployment. Furthermore, its ability to yield further accuracy gains at inference time in a plug-and-play manner, without parameter updates, offers immediate utility for enhancing existing LLM applications.
7. **Theoretical Contributions**: The theoretical derivation of the harmonic mean as an optimal selector from the View-Invariant Infomax objective provides a strong scientific basis for the approach, moving beyond heuristic methods and contributing to the theoretical understanding of robust unsupervised learning signals.

In conclusion, Self-Harmony addresses a fundamental challenge in autonomous LLM adaptation, providing a robust, efficient, and theoretically justified method for self-correction that promises to significantly advance the reliability and capability of reasoning-focused LLMs without external supervision.
