# PreferThinker: Reasoning-based Personalized Image Preference Assessment

> [!summary] Summary
> PREFERTHINKER introduces a reasoning-based framework for personalized image preference assessment that predicts visual preference profiles to provide interpretable, multi-dimensional evaluations. The method demonstrates accuracy improvements of up to 8.8 percentage points on multi-preference user data compared to prior methods, while also enabling personalized image generation.

## Problem

- Personalized image preference assessment suffers from data scarcity, as individual user data is limited and not easily scalable for training.
- Existing methods often provide a single numerical score without interpretability, failing to capture or explain the complex, multi-dimensional nature of individual aesthetic tastes.
- Current models do not effectively leverage the crucial prior information contained in a user's specific preferred and non-preferred reference images.

## Method

- A "==visual preference profile==" was defined, comprising five common visual elements (e.g., art style, color) with a rich vocabulary, to formalize complex personalized preferences and enable cross-user learning.
- The PreferImg-CoT dataset, a large-scale Chain-of-Thought (CoT) style dataset, was constructed by simulating 80,000 users and using Claude 3.7 for structured "predict-then-assess" reasoning annotations.
- A two-stage training strategy was employed: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) for cold-start initialization of structured reasoning, followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a hybrid reward for enhanced generalization.

## Results

- PreferThinker achieved the highest assessment accuracy on the PreferImg dataset, with **96.4%** on unseen single-preference users (+3.0 percentage points over ViPer) and **92.8%** on unseen multi-preference users (+6.8 percentage points over Claude 3.7).
- The framework provides interpretable, multi-dimensional scores and reasoning for image preferences, explicitly justifying assessments based on predicted user profiles.
- The method demonstrated robustness to varying numbers of reference images and achieved competitive performance (**65.7%** accuracy) on the general preference PickaPic dataset, second only to a model trained directly on it.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A common visual preference profile can effectively bridge the gap between scarce personalized data and abundant general data, allowing for element-level characterization and scalable training.
> - A "predict-then-assess" paradigm, where the user's preference profile is explicitly predicted before evaluating candidate images, provides a robust foundation for multi-dimensional, interpretable reasoning.
> - Designing a hybrid reward for RL that accounts for profile prediction accuracy, output format, and final assessment correctness is crucial for guiding the model to learn complex and interpretable personalized preferences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image preference assessment is a critical area within artificial intelligence, particularly for evaluating and aligning generative models with human preferences, which has applications in fields such as content recommendation. Existing research primarily addresses *general preference assessment*, focusing on tasks like text-image alignment and aesthetics. These approaches typically rely on large-scale, easily scalable general preference data to train models, often employing methods based on CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) or Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

CLIP-based methods, such as those that fine-tune CLIP models on extensive text-image pairs, assess general preferences by measuring similarity. However, they generally lack interpretability, often yielding only a numerical score without explaining the underlying reasons for the preference. MLLM-based methods, which fine-tune MLLMs on large-scale visual question-answering (VQA) pairs, offer more interpretable assessments by providing reasoning. Despite their advancements, both categories of methods face significant challenges when applied to *personalized image preference assessment*.

The core challenge in personalized assessment stems from the nature of individual user data: it is typically scarce, not easily scalable, and highly distinct between users. Furthermore, personalized preferences are often complex and multi-dimensional, encompassing various visual elements like art style, color, and texture, which are more intricate than general preferences like overall aesthetics. Most existing methods are not designed to handle this scarcity and complexity. While some recent efforts, such as ViPer, have attempted personalized assessment by feeding reference and candidate images into an MLLM for score regression, they often lack explicit reasoning steps and may not fully leverage the critical prior information contained in reference images.

PreferThinker positions itself to address these limitations by focusing specifically on reasoning-based *personalized* image preference assessment. It aims to bridge the gap between abundant general data and scarce personalized data, provide interpretable, multi-dimensional assessments, and effectively utilize user-specific reference images. This work contributes to the development of more sophisticated and human-centric AI systems capable of understanding and reflecting individual aesthetic tastes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel reasoning-based framework, named PreferThinker, for personalized image preference assessment. This framework aims to accurately evaluate an individual user’s image preferences by relying on a small set of reference images, while also providing interpretable, multi-dimensional insights.

The motivation for this work arises from several key challenges inherent in personalized preference assessment:

1. **Data Scarcity and Scalability:** Unlike general preference data, where assessment criteria are often shared across users, personalized data is typically limited for each individual. This makes large-scale, user-specific training difficult and renders existing general preference models ineffective for personalized tasks. The project is motivated to find a way to leverage large-scale data for training while still accommodating user-specific preferences.
2. **Complexity and Diversity of Individual Tastes:** Personalized preferences are often complex, diverse, and multi-dimensional, encompassing various visual attributes such as art style, color, art medium, saturation, and detail. Existing methods that output a single numerical score struggle to capture or explain these intricate individual tastes, necessitating a more granular and interpretable approach.
3. **Lack of Interpretability in Existing Methods:** Many current image preference assessment models operate as "black boxes," providing only a score without explaining *why* an image is preferred or not. For personalized applications, understanding the underlying reasoning for a preference is crucial for user trust and for providing actionable feedback to generative models. The authors are motivated to develop a system that offers clear, multi-dimensional explanations.
4. **Ineffective Use of Prior Information:** While personalized reference images contain critical prior information about a user's taste, existing methods often utilize them implicitly or superficially, failing to extract and leverage this information effectively to guide the assessment of new images. The research aims to explicitly model and utilize this prior information.

To overcome these challenges, the central motivation behind PreferThinker is the introduction of a **common preference profile**. This profile, comprising multiple shared visual elements, acts as a bridge across diverse users. This key insight offers several advantages:
- It allows for the characterization of complex personalized preferences at an element level, simplifying the challenge of diverse individual tastes.
- By identifying common visual elements that shape preferences, large-scale user data can be leveraged to train models for effective profile prediction, thereby mitigating the issue of limited personalized data for individual users.
- The structured nature of the preference profile provides a direct foundation for generating interpretable and multi-dimensional assessments, directly addressing the interpretability gap.

Therefore, the work is driven by the need for a robust, interpretable, and data-efficient framework for personalized image preference assessment that can generalize across various user tastes and effectively utilize limited user-specific data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PreferThinker proposes a reasoning-based personalized image preference assessment framework that operates on a **predict-then-assess paradigm**. Given a user's personalized reference images (both preferred and non-preferred) and two candidate images, the system first predicts the user's preference profile and then uses this profile to provide an interpretable, multi-dimensional assessment.

The methodology involves several key components and a two-stage training strategy:

#### 3.1. Visual Preference Profile

The core of PreferThinker is the **visual preference profile**, designed to bridge user-specific preferences.
- **Definition:** Based on analysis of Lexica text prompts and a user study with 100 participants, five most representative visual elements were identified: art style, color, detail, art medium, and saturation. A rich vocabulary of 288 related terms was collected for these elements to ensure diversity in profile characterization.
- **Purpose:** This element-level profile formalizes complex personalized preferences, enables cross-user learning from larger datasets, and provides a basis for interpretable, multi-dimensional scoring.

#### 3.2. Large-Scale CoT-Style Personalized Preference Assessment Dataset (PreferImg-CoT)

To facilitate the model's structured reasoning capabilities, a new dataset was constructed.
- **Personalized Preference Assessment Data Generation (PreferImg):**
 - To address the lack of personalized preference data, a simulation pipeline was developed. It involved randomly sampling five visual elements to assign visual preference and non-preference profiles to 80,000 simulated users (20,000 with multiple profiles).
 - These profiles were combined with 190,000 initial prompts (from Lexica, DiffusionDB, COCO) and fed into a text-to-image (T2I) model to generate 1.36 million reference images (preferred and non-preferred) and candidate images for each user.
- **CoT-style Dataset Construction (PreferImg-CoT):**
 - The advanced Claude 3.7 model was employed for annotation. A detailed prompt guided Claude 3.7 to follow the "predict-then-assess" structure:
 1. Predict the user's visual preference and non-preference profiles from the reference images.
 2. Generate a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) style analysis, providing interpretable scores (1-5) and assessments for candidate images across the five visual dimensions.
 3. Output a final choice based on total scores.
 - A data filtering stage removed illogical or inconsistent CoT responses to ensure high quality. The final dataset, PreferImg-CoT, contains 60,000 user samples.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

A two-stage training strategy was adopted to instill structured reasoning and enhance generalization:

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) for Cold-start Initialization:**
 - The Qwen2.5-VL-7B MLLM was used as the base model.
 - It was fine-tuned on the PreferImg-CoT dataset using a standard autoregressive language modeling objective with a token-level cross-entropy loss.
 - This phase teaches the model the "predict-then-assess" structured reasoning: predicting profiles, then generating multi-dimensional scores and assessments.

- **Stage 2: GRPO-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Post-training:**
 - Following SFT, reinforcement learning was applied to enable the model to explore more diverse and reasonable assessment paths and improve generalization beyond the supervised data.
 - **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** This algorithm was chosen for its efficiency in generating diverse reasoning responses without a critic model. For each input query, the MLLM generates a group of `G` CoT-style outputs. Each output receives a scalar reward, which is then normalized to estimate a group-relative advantage. The policy gradient is optimized using this advantage, incorporating a KL divergence term to control policy updates and maintain stability.

#### 3.4. Similarity-aware Preference Profile Prediction Reward

Recognizing that accurate profile prediction is crucial for reasonable assessments, a specific reward was designed:
- **Prediction Accuracy Measurement:**
 - `s_text`: Text semantic similarity between the predicted and ground-truth preference/non-preference profiles, computed using SBERT.
 - `s_img`: Image similarity between images generated using predicted profiles and images generated using ground-truth profiles. This involves recaptioning an initial text with the respective profiles, generating images via a T2I model (Flux), and then computing similarity using DreamSim.
- **`r_predict` Calculation:** A weighted combination of `s_text` and `s_img` is used for the profile prediction reward.
- **Hybrid Reward Modeling:** The total reward `r` for an RL response is a weighted sum of three components:
 - `r_predict`: The similarity-aware prediction reward.
 - `r_format`: A binary reward (1 or 0) for adhering to the specified CoT-style output format (e.g., correct tag usage for profiles, thinking, and answer).
 - `r_accuracy`: A binary reward (1 or 0) for producing the correct final assessment answer (Image A or B).
 This hybrid reward guides the model towards accurate profile prediction, correct output format, and precise assessment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate PreferThinker demonstrated its performance and the effectiveness of its various components.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Comparison

- **Superiority on PreferImg Dataset:** PreferThinker achieved the highest assessment accuracy on the newly constructed PreferImg dataset across all categories: single-preference (SP) and multi-preference (MP) users, for both seen and unseen data.
 - On seen SP data, PreferThinker achieved 96.6% accuracy, outperforming the previous SOTA (GPT-4o) by 2.4 percentage points.
 - On unseen SP data, it achieved 96.4% accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA (ViPer) by 3.0 points.
 - For multi-preference users, the performance gains were even more substantial. On seen MP data, PreferThinker achieved 92.0%, an 8.8-point improvement over the previous SOTA (Claude 3.7). On unseen MP data, it reached 92.8%, a 6.8-point improvement over Claude 3.7.
 - Compared to its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), PreferThinker showed substantial improvements across these categories, ranging from 21.2 to 30.0 percentage points, underscoring the impact of the proposed methodology.
- **Performance on PickaPic Dataset:** On the real-user, general preference PickaPic dataset, PreferThinker achieved 65.7% accuracy, placing it second only to PickScore (67.9%), which had PickaPic as a "seen" dataset during its training. This result indicates PreferThinker's strong generalizability to real-world user preferences, even when the dataset labels reflect general rather than strictly personalized preferences.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** CLIP-based models and most MLLM-based methods performed poorly on personalized tasks, highlighting their limitations in handling personalized data scarcity and complexity. ViPer, designed for personalized assessment, performed well on SP but its accuracy decreased on MP data and lacked interpretability. PreferThinker outperformed open-source MLLMs significantly and was competitive with, or superior to, closed-source MLLMs (e.g., Claude 3.7, GPT-4o) on personalized tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

- **Effectiveness of Cold-start SFT and RL Post-training:** The ablation study confirmed the critical roles of both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). Removing either phase led to a notable degradation in model performance. The RL phase, in particular, significantly improved performance on unseen user data, especially for multi-preference users, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing model generalization. Direct application of RL without SFT failed to provide correct reasoning structure.
- **Impact of Prediction Reward (PR):** Incorporating the similarity-aware prediction reward (PR) effectively improved the accuracy of preference profile prediction. Qualitative analysis further showed that better profile prediction facilitated more reasonable multi-dimensional assessments and led to correct final answers, whereas models without PR sometimes produced unreasonable assessments due to inaccurate profile predictions.

#### 4.3. Qualitative and Discussion Points

- **Interpretable Assessments:** PreferThinker demonstrated the ability to provide interpretable, multi-dimensional scores and assessments. The system explicitly predicted user preference and non-preference profiles and then justified its scores for candidate images based on these profiles, addressing the black-box nature of many existing models.
- **Personalized Image Generation:** The predicted preference profiles by PreferThinker were shown to be beneficial for personalized image generation. By optimizing initial text prompts with the predicted profiles, the system could generate images that aligned well with user preferences reflected in their reference images.
- **Efficiency without Explicit CoT:** PreferThinker exhibited the ability to implicitly leverage its learned reasoning capabilities for direct preference assessment without explicit CoT output. In this mode, it still outperformed SOTA methods on PreferImg and maintained comparable accuracy on PickaPic, while achieving the fastest inference speed among evaluated models.
- **Robustness to Reference Image Count:** The method proved robust to variations in the number of personalized reference images provided, consistently outperforming baselines across different input sizes on both seen and unseen data.
- **Limitations:** The primary limitation identified is the reliance on synthetically generated personalized preference datasets. While extensive, these synthetic profiles may not fully capture the nuanced and complex nature of real human preferences, which can be influenced by factors beyond the five defined visual attributes (e.g., content semantics, user demographics like age, gender, and culture). This can lead to difficulties in assessing highly complex or subtle real-world user preferences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PreferThinker represents a notable advancement in the field of image preference assessment, particularly for personalized applications. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Addressing Core Challenges in Personalized Preference Assessment:** The work directly tackles the critical issues of data scarcity, the complexity of individual tastes, and the lack of interpretability in personalized image preference assessment. By introducing the concept of a common preference profile and a predict-then-assess paradigm, it offers a scalable solution that can leverage large-scale data while still catering to unique user preferences.
2. **Enhancing Interpretability:** A major contribution is the provision of interpretable, multi-dimensional scores and reasoning. Unlike black-box models, PreferThinker explains *why* an image is preferred or not, based on a user's explicit visual profile. This interpretability is crucial for building user trust, enabling fine-grained feedback for generative models, and facilitating more transparent AI systems.
3. **Novel Dataset for Structured Reasoning:** The creation of PreferImg-CoT, the first large-scale CoT-style personalized assessment dataset with annotated preference profiles and high-quality reasoning, is a significant contribution. This dataset can serve as a foundational resource for future research in reasoning-based multimodal preference learning.
4. **Robust and Generalizable Framework:** The two-stage training strategy (SFT for structured reasoning and GRPO-based RL for generalization) and the similarity-aware prediction reward contribute to a robust framework that demonstrates strong performance on both seen and unseen personalized data, as well as competitive performance on general preference datasets. This generalizability suggests its potential applicability in diverse real-world scenarios.
5. **Broader Applications and Future Directions:**
 - **Content Recommendation Systems:** PreferThinker could significantly enhance personalized content recommendation by understanding individual aesthetic tastes at a granular level, moving beyond simple click-through rates or implicit feedback.
 - **Generative AI Alignment:** It offers a powerful tool for aligning text-to-image models with specific human preferences, allowing for more controlled and personalized image generation. The demonstrated ability to use predicted profiles for personalized image generation directly supports this.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** The interpretable output can foster better human-computer interaction by making AI's decision-making process more transparent and understandable to users.
 - **Foundation for Comprehensive Preference Modeling:** The established framework, while currently focused on visual attributes, provides a strong foundation for future research to incorporate more complex factors influencing human preferences, such as semantic content understanding, emotional impact, and user demographic or cultural backgrounds.

In summary, PreferThinker pushes the boundaries of personalized preference assessment by combining structured reasoning with effective data utilization strategies, promising more intelligent, interpretable, and user-centric AI applications in visual domains.
