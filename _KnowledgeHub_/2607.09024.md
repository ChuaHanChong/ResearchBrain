# Video Generation Models are General-Purpose Vision Learners

> [!summary] Summary
> GenCeption, a generalist video perception model from Google DeepMind, repurposes a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model into an efficient, feed-forward architecture. It achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance across diverse vision tasks like depth estimation, surface normal prediction, segmentation, and 3D keypoint estimation, exhibiting robust generalization from synthetic to real-world data and out-of-distribution categories.

## Problem

- Computer vision largely remains in a 'specialized model' stage, lacking a generalist foundation model comparable to Large Language Models (LLMs) in Natural Language Processing (NLP).
- Existing visual representation learning methods often struggle to capture comprehensive spatio-temporal understanding, achieve deep vision-language alignment, or scale effectively for video data.
- A universal pre-training objective is needed that compels models to learn 4D temporal causality, physical interactions, vision-language alignment, and is scalable to foster emergent general visual intelligence.

## Method

- **GenCeption** utilizes a pre-trained ==text-to-video generative diffusion model== (==WAN 2.1==) as a universal backbone, leveraging its inherent ==generative objective== to internalize robust spatio-temporal priors, 3D geometry, and vision-language alignment.
- It reformulates diverse vision perception tasks as unified ==sequence-to-sequence== mappings within a single, shared ==feed-forward architecture==, avoiding task-specific components and using text instructions for task specification.
- The inherently iterative diffusion backbone is transformed into an efficient ==single-step, feed-forward perception model== by feeding ==clean video latents== and fixing the ==conditioning timestep== to zero, enabling faster inference.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art or competitive performance across a diverse suite of vision tasks, including geometric understanding (depth, surface normal, camera pose), segmentation, and 3D keypoint prediction, often outperforming specialized models.
- Demonstrated significant inference efficiency, processing an **81-frame** video (**480x832** resolution) in **5.92 seconds** (**13.6 FPS**) for the **1.3B** parameter model and **10.03 seconds** (**8.0 FPS**) for the **14B** parameter model.
- Exhibited robust emergent generalization capabilities, including effective transfer from synthetic to real-world videos, generalization to multiple instances in a scene, and adaptability to out-of-distribution object categories (e.g., non-human entities).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale text-to-video generation implicitly forces models to learn a 'universal world model' encompassing spatio-temporal dynamics, 3D geometry, physical causality, and deep vision-language alignment, making it a powerful pre-training paradigm for general visual perception.
> - Pre-trained generative diffusion models can be effectively repurposed as efficient, feed-forward perception architectures, overcoming the slow iterative sampling limitations typically associated with generative inference.
> - A unified, task-agnostic architecture, primarily trained on synthetic data, can achieve state-of-the-art or competitive performance across a wide range of dense and sparse vision tasks, demonstrating robust generalization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a recognized disparity between the evolution of Natural Language Processing (NLP) and computer vision. In NLP, large-scale next-token prediction has led to the development of generalist foundation models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs), which can handle a wide array of tasks with a unified architecture and exhibit emergent behaviors. In contrast, computer vision is described as largely remaining in a "specialized model" stage. While notable advancements like Segment Anything (SAM) and Depth Anything have emerged, these are characterized as task-specific models that often require customized architectures. The broader research landscape in computer vision is actively seeking a unified, task-agnostic architecture—a vision foundation model analogous to LLMs.

Existing approaches to visual representation learning, such as masked autoencoders (MAE, VideoMAE) and self-distillation methods (DINO), primarily focus on visual data alone, often lacking explicit multimodal vision-language alignment. Contrastive learning methods like CLIP and SigLip provide vision-language alignment but face scaling limitations, particularly for video data, due to computational costs and the complexity of modeling temporal dynamics.

A growing area of research involves repurposing pre-trained diffusion models for perception tasks. Previous works have adapted image diffusion models for single-image tasks like depth estimation (e.g., Marigold, GenPercept, Diception) or extended them with temporal layers for video (e.g., BufferAnytime). Other studies have explored native video diffusion models for specific dense vision tasks like depth (DepthCrafter), normals (NormalCrafter), or 4D scene reconstruction (Geo4D), and even open-world segmentation (ReferEverything). However, these efforts often focus on a single task, maintain slow iterative sampling processes inherent to generative models, or use rigid architectural modifications for multitasking.

GenCeption is positioned within this landscape as a step towards a unified, generalist video perception model. It proposes that large-scale text-to-video generation, rather than simpler self-supervised or contrastive learning paradigms, provides the necessary spatiotemporal priors, vision-language alignment, and scalability to serve as a foundational pre-training objective. The paper seeks to demonstrate that by repurposing such a generative model into an efficient feed-forward perception architecture, it can achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse vision tasks and exhibit emergent generalization capabilities, moving beyond the limitations of task-specific or single-modality diffusion repurposing methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify a "universal pre-training objective" for computer vision that can serve as a catalyst for developing general-purpose foundation models, mirroring the success observed in NLP with next-token prediction. The authors explicitly state this quest as a search for a visual analog to next-token prediction, aiming to unify diverse visual challenges into a single generalist intelligence capable of exhibiting emergent behaviors.

This overarching objective is motivated by three core imperatives that any such pre-training paradigm should satisfy:
1. **Spatio-Temporal Evolution**: The pre-training objective must compel the model to internalize the 4D temporal causality and physics of a dynamic world. This is crucial for understanding how objects move, interact, and change over time, which is fundamental to video understanding.
2. **Vision-Language Alignment**: To enable instruction-following capabilities and incorporate common-sense knowledge, visual features must be intrinsically aligned with linguistic semantics during both pre-training and post-training phases. This allows for text-steered control over vision tasks.
3. **Scalability**: The paradigm needs to be scalable in terms of both data volume and computational resources. This scalability is considered essential for the emergence of advanced vision intelligence, echoing the scaling laws observed in LLMs.

The authors posit that large-scale text-to-video generation uniquely satisfies these three requirements. The objective of generating high-fidelity video sequences inherently forces models to learn detailed spatio-temporal world priors, including 3D geometry and object permanence. Furthermore, as these models are conditioned on text, they naturally establish vision-language alignment. Finally, the commercial value and relatively low annotation cost of text-to-video generation enable the use of massive datasets and compute, providing a path for scaling.

The motivation is to shift computer vision from an era of specialized, task-specific models with customized architectures and losses to a unified, task-agnostic paradigm where a single backbone can flexibly handle a vast array of perception tasks, steered by text instructions, ultimately leading to general visual intelligence for the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GenCeption's methodology is founded on the premise that large-scale video generation models function as universal visual representation learners. The approach reformulates video perception as a post-training phase built upon a pre-trained video generative model, guided by three principles:

**1. Multimodal Generative Pre-training as Representation Learning:**
A pre-trained text-to-video generative diffusion model serves as the universal backbone. The generative objective—producing high-fidelity video conditioned on text—is hypothesized to enforce the internalization of robust spatio-temporal priors, 3D geometry, physical laws, and deep visual-language alignment. The design prioritizes minimal modifications to the pre-training regime to preserve these foundational representations.

**2. Task-Agnostic Post-Training in Unified Architecture:**
Diverse vision tasks are framed as unified sequence-to-sequence mapping problems. The architecture avoids task-specific encoders, decoders, or loss functions, instead employing a single, unified architecture and shared weights for both the backbone and decoder. Task specifications are communicated via text instructions. Different target modalities are mapped to a shared representational space, typically the standard RGB space for dense tasks. This data-driven approach allows for integrating new visual capabilities by adjusting data representation rather than architectural changes.

**3. Feed-Forward Reformulation:**
A critical design element is transforming the inherently iterative, multi-step generative diffusion backbone into a single-step, feed-forward perception model. This addresses the need for efficient and accurate predictions in perception tasks, contrasting with the slower sampling processes typically used for synthesis.

**Instantiation of GenCeption:**
- **Base Model:** GenCeption utilizes a text-to-video diffusion model, specifically WAN 2.1, consisting of a VAE encoder-decoder, a text encoder, and a transformer-based latent diffusion model (DiT).
- **Feedforward Perception Formulation (Section 3.2):**
 - The multi-step DiT is repurposed into a single-forward-pass prediction model.
 - Instead of noisy latents, the clean latent of the input video is fed directly to the DiT.
 - The conditioning timestep `t` is fixed to `t=0`, signifying a noise-free input, which corresponds to the termination of the Rectified Flow generative process used in the DiT.
 - Since the DiT predicts velocity `v = ε - x0` (noise minus original input), the raw output is negated (`-v = x0 - ε`) before passing to loss functions or the decoder. This negation aligns the output with the target video's latent representation, accelerating convergence and improving performance.
 - The DiT is interpreted as a feature extractor, with features harvested from its final layer to maintain alignment with the decoder without architectural modifications.
- **Unified Task Representation (Section 3.3):**
 - **Dense Tasks:** All dense perception tasks (e.g., normal, depth, foreground segmentation, dense human semantic estimation, raymap estimation) are unified in a 3-channel RGB space, constrained to the range. Single-dimensional outputs replicate channels, while 3D tasks use distinct channels. For higher-dimensional modalities like camera poses (traditionally matrices), a pixel-space "Rothko" raymap representation is used, spatially partitioning 6-channel data into 3 channels to fit the unified framework. This minimizes architectural changes and leverages pre-trained visual priors.
 - **Sparse Tasks:** For structured outputs like 2D/3D keypoint predictions, a lightweight token-based extension is introduced. `T` learnable tokens (one per frame) are appended to the video latents. After processing by the model, an MLP decodes each token into a `K`-dimensional target. To maintain compliance with the base DiT, its native 3D RoPE (Rotary Positional Embedding) is applied to these tokens, with learnable spatial positions and temporally down-scaled frame indices for positional interpolation.
- **Scalable Synthetic Data Generation (Section 3.4):**
 - To address ground-truth data scarcity, a synthetic data generation workflow is designed. This involves animating 800 RenderPeople assets with 200 CMU motion capture dataset motions, utilizing diverse 3D scenes or HDRI backdrops, and varying camera conditions.
 - A dataset of 7,500 synthetic videos is generated. For dense tasks, Blender is used to render video normals, depth, and segmentation masks. Human joint positions from rigged assets provide ground truth for 2D/3D keypoint tasks.
 - An offline preprocessing stage precomputes and caches video latents and text embeddings.
- **Training Recipe (Section 3.5):**
 - A fully unified `L2` loss is employed, applied in latent space for dense tasks and output space for sparse tasks, eliminating the need for manually balanced task-specific loss weights.
 - Task-specific customizations are handled at the data representation level. For example, monocular depth maps are normalized using scene median depth to remove scale ambiguity and then mapped to the RGB range using a non-linear function. This data-centric harmonization principle ensures streamlined multi-task training, where balancing is managed through data mixture ratios.
- **Implementation Details (Section 4.1):**
 - The model is trained on video samples at 480x832 resolution, 81 frames, 24 FPS.
 - Training is conducted on 256 v6e TPUs with a batch size of 64 for 15,000 steps, using Adam optimizer (learning rate 5e-5) and linear warmup.
 - Gradient clipping and dropping are applied for stability.
 - Synthetic training data is augmented with publicly available datasets (TartanAir, Virtual KITTI, MVS Synth for depth/camera pose) and real-world datasets for expression-referring segmentation (MeViS, Ref-COCO, YouTube-VOS).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents GenCeption, a generalist video perception model, demonstrating its capabilities through empirical evaluations and analysis of emergent behaviors.

**State-of-the-Art Performance:**
GenCeption achieves competitive or superior performance across a diverse suite of vision tasks when compared to specialized, state-of-the-art models. This is notable given its unified, simple architecture and primary reliance on synthetic training data (with the exception of expression-referring segmentation).
- **Geometric Understanding:** GenCeption surpasses specialized models such as NormalCrafter and Lotus-2 in surface normal estimation. For depth estimation and camera pose estimation, it outperforms or matches dedicated foundation models like DepthAnything 3, D4RT, and VGGT Ω.
- **Segmentation:** The model achieves competitive results in soft foreground segmentation across various video and image datasets. In expression-referring segmentation, GenCeption demonstrates a stronger ability to interpret complex sentences compared to models like SegmentAnything3.
- **Sparse Tasks:** For 3D keypoint prediction, GenCeption delivers stronger performance than specialized models including Genmo and TRAM.

**Inference Efficiency:**
The feed-forward reformulation of the diffusion model eliminates the slow, iterative sampling process typical of standard diffusion models. This results in faster inference times. For an 81-frame video at 480x832 resolution, the 1.3B parameter model takes 5.92 seconds (13.6 FPS) and uses 15.3GB VRAM, while the 14B parameter model takes 10.03 seconds (8.0 FPS) and uses 42.8GB VRAM.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Joint Training vs. Task-Specific Training:** Joint training as a generalist yields mixed results. While foreground segmentation consistently benefits, and expression-referring segmentation remains largely unchanged, dense prediction modalities (surface normal, depth, camera pose) can experience regression or stability. Importantly, joint training severely degrades 3D human keypoint estimation and negatively impacts other dense tasks. This is attributed to the divergence of token-based coordinate regression from the pixel-space pre-training and the disruption of native attention mechanics by additional learnable tokens, suggesting the importance of minimal architectural modifications.
- **Importance of Pre-training:** Comparative analysis shows that the generative diffusion pre-training of WAN 2.1 significantly outperforms other visual pre-training approaches like V-JEPA and VideoMAE V2 for depth estimation under comparable fine-tuning data. This indicates that the generative diffusion objective itself contributes to superior spatiotemporal priors. Additionally, randomly initialized DiT models exhibit a flat learning curve, confirming that pre-training is essential for subsequent convergence and performance.
- **Scaling Properties and Data Efficiency:** GenCeption demonstrates preliminary scaling properties, with performance generally improving with increased model size and data volume. The model also exhibits exceptional data efficiency, achieving performance comparable to leading models such as D4RT and VGGT-Ω with significantly less training data (7x to 500x less).

**Emergent Behaviors:**
Beyond quantitative performance, GenCeption exhibits several emergent behaviors:
- **Generalization from Synthetic Training Data:** Despite being trained predominantly on synthetic videos, GenCeption generalizes effectively to real-world videos, even setting new state-of-the-art performances on real benchmarks. The qualitative output details (e.g., cat whiskers, soft hair segmentation) often surpass the quality of the synthetic training data.
- **Generalization to Multiple Instances:** The model, trained on synthetic videos featuring a single object per video, generalizes in a zero-shot manner to real-world videos containing multiple objects.
- **Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Classes:** Although trained exclusively on synthetic videos of human entities, GenCeption generalizes well to real-world videos of humans and other categories, including animals and anthropomorphic characters.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper carries several significances and potential impacts for the field of computer vision.

Firstly, it proposes and provides empirical evidence that large-scale text-to-video generation can serve as a foundational pre-training paradigm for general visual perception. This represents a conceptual shift from previous approaches that often viewed video generation primarily as a synthesis tool. The research suggests that the complex task of generating high-fidelity, temporally consistent video, conditioned on natural language, inherently forces models to internalize rich spatio-temporal priors, 3D geometry, physical causality, and deep vision-language alignment. These learned representations are shown to be directly transferable and beneficial for diverse perception tasks.

Secondly, the introduction of GenCeption demonstrates a practical pathway to instantiate this paradigm. By repurposing a pre-trained video diffusion backbone into an efficient, feed-forward perception model, the research challenges the notion that generative models must rely on slow, iterative sampling for perceptual inference. This architectural innovation significantly improves inference efficiency, making the approach more practical for real-world applications requiring timely predictions.

Thirdly, the model's performance, matching or surpassing specialized state-of-the-art models across a wide range of tasks (depth, surface normal, camera pose, segmentation, 3D keypoint estimation), highlights the potential for a unified architecture. This contrasts with the current landscape of specialized models, suggesting a move towards a more generalist vision intelligence that can handle heterogeneous tasks within a single framework, steered by text instructions.

Fourthly, the observed emergent behaviors—including robust generalization from synthetic to real-world data, to multiple instances, and to out-of-distribution object categories—underscore a deeper understanding of the physical world learned during generative pre-training. These emergent properties suggest the existence of a "universal world model" embedded within large generative video backbones, capable of reasoning beyond its direct training data. This has implications for reducing the reliance on massive, labor-intensive real-world datasets for fine-tuning. The exceptional data efficiency demonstrated further reinforces this potential, indicating that powerful perception capabilities can be achieved with significantly less fine-tuning data compared to other leading models.

The potential impact of this research is a paradigm shift in computer vision development. By advocating for scalable generative pre-training as the foundation, it could lead to the creation of truly unified and generalist vision intelligences for interacting with and understanding the physical world, analogous to the transformative impact of LLMs in NLP. This could streamline the development of new vision systems, reduce the need for task-specific engineering, and unlock new capabilities in areas like robotics, augmented reality, and complex scene understanding, where a comprehensive understanding of spatio-temporal dynamics and context is crucial.
