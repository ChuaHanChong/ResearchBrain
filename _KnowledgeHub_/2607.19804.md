# V2F: Vision-Informed Grasp Force Prediction for Damage-Aware Robotic Handling of Date Fruits

> [!summary] Summary
> V2F predicts a **safe grasp force** for date fruits directly from vision, replacing slow, potentially damaging tactile-exploration-based force calibration. It combines geometric feature extraction from segmented images with a physics-informed residual neural network layered on top of the Hertz contact equation, and validates that the predicted force avoids observable bruising across unseen cultivars — but scores exactly one grasp per fruit, with no treatment of force effects from prior or subsequent handling.

## Problem

- Date fruits present a narrow safe-force window: enough force is needed to detach and hold the fruit reliably, but they have a low bruise threshold, so even a single miscalibrated grasp can cause visible damage.
- Determining a safe grasp force per fruit conventionally requires tactile exploration (probing the fruit to find its compliance), which is slow and can itself bruise the fruit before the "real" grasp even happens.
- Fruit mechanical properties vary substantially across cultivars, so a fixed force threshold generalizes poorly; a vision-only estimate needs to transfer to unseen cultivars without added instrumentation.

## Method

- A ==Vision-to-Force (V2F)== pipeline: image segmentation + active-contour refinement extracts the fruit's geometric profile (size, curvature) from a single image.
- Geometric features and cultivar metadata feed a **physics-informed residual neural network** that augments the classical Hertz contact equation — the network learns the *residual* between the idealized elastic contact prediction and the fruit's true force-response, rather than learning the force mapping from scratch.
- The predicted safe grasp force is applied open-loop per grasp; the paper does not incorporate feedback from, or influence on, any subsequent grasp of the same fruit.

## Results

- Mean validation performance of **R² ≈ 0.7** across unseen cultivar groups, showing the physics-informed residual generalizes better than a purely geometric or purely learned baseline would be expected to.
- Experimental grasps using the predicted force produced **residual deformations below 1 mm** and **no observable damage**, with stable manipulation demonstrated across different date-fruit types.
- All reported results are single-grasp: the paper establishes that a *safe force for one grasp* is predictable from vision alone, not whether that safety margin holds up under repeated handling of the same fruit.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - V2F is a genuine damage-*aware* force model (it predicts force conditioned on physically-grounded, cultivar-specific fragility), which is rarer than pure force-magnitude bounding — but it is scoped to exactly one grasp, with no state carried from a prior handling event into the next.
> - The physics-informed-residual-on-Hertz-contact recipe is a plausible substrate for a *cumulative* extension: nothing in the architecture prevents feeding a running deformation/compliance estimate (updated after each grasp) back in as an additional input, but the paper does not attempt this.
> - Because a single miscalibrated grasp can already bruise the fruit, V2F implicitly assumes the "first grasp is representative" — it never asks whether a fruit that clears the safe-force bound on grasp 1 still clears it on grasp 5 after cumulative compression.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The agricultural sector faces increasing demands for robotic automation to address labor shortages, enhance post-harvest quality, and mitigate labor risks. While significant advancements have been achieved in robotic handling and harvesting of rigid fruits, the manipulation of delicate organic produce in unstructured environments remains a complex challenge. Date fruits, in particular, present a unique mechanical paradox: they necessitate a relatively high gripping force to detach from bunches but possess a low threshold for mechanical deformation, rendering them highly susceptible to bruising.

Existing robotic harvesting systems predominantly employ uniform clamping forces or simplified vacuum suction for rigid fruits. Recent developments in specialized end-effector designs for soft-fruit manipulation often rely on tactile exploration. However, these tactile-based methods typically react to contact or slip *after* it occurs, potentially causing micro-bruising before corrective action can be taken. Current vision-based systems for fruit classification and maturity assessment primarily operate in the visual domain and lack mechanisms to translate visual predictions into mechanically safe grasping decisions. Consequently, these systems do not provide a predictive framework for determining optimal grasping forces based on individual fruit characteristics prior to contact. This absence of pre-contact force prediction results in a lack of adaptive grasping strategies, which can lead to fruit slippage or irreversible tissue damage during robotic handling.

Previous research in grasp state classification and safe force inference has advanced capabilities for monitoring ongoing grasps and adapting force reactively. Studies have explored sensor fusion techniques, integrating IMU, IR reflectance, GelSight tactile images, and external RGB camera data to classify grasp states and infer safe forces. Some approaches use optical flow from tactile images to capture dynamic contact information and employ transformer architectures for improved generalization. However, these methods typically operate during or post-contact and often depend on high-resolution tactile sensors and online exploratory actions, which may be impractical for widespread deployment in field harvesting robots due to cost, complexity, and the inherent risk of initial damage.

This research distinguishes itself by focusing on *pre-contact* vision-driven prediction of safe grasp forces. Instead of inferring force from rich contact sensing during or after interaction, the presented framework estimates yield-level grasp stress before contact. It leverages compact geometric descriptors and fruit type, grounding its predictions in analytical Hertzian contact mechanics and refining them through physics-guided residual learning. This approach aims to complement execution-stage feedback systems by providing planning-time force estimations, thereby offering a proactive strategy to minimize fruit damage during robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a vision-informed framework capable of predicting safe grasping forces for robotic handling of date fruits. This addresses the dual challenge posed by dates, which require substantial force for detachment but are highly prone to bruising.

The motivation for this research stems from several factors:
- **Addressing Agricultural Labor Shortages:** The increasing demand for robotic solutions in agriculture is driven by a need to mitigate labor shortages in harvesting and handling operations.
- **Improving Post-Harvest Quality:** Current manual and non-adaptive robotic handling methods often result in significant fruit damage, leading to economic losses and reduced product quality. Robotic systems capable of damage-aware manipulation could preserve fruit integrity.
- **Overcoming the "Mechanical Paradox" of Date Fruits:** Date fruits present a specific challenge due to their unique biomechanical properties. They require a relatively high gripping force to overcome the detachment resistance from the palm bunch but possess a low threshold for mechanical deformation, making them highly susceptible to bruising from excessive pressure.
- **Bridging the Gap in Predictive Grasping:** Existing robotic systems often rely on uniform forces or reactive tactile sensing. There is a lack of predictive frameworks that can determine the optimal grasping force *before* contact based on individual fruit characteristics. This absence leads to non-adaptive grasping strategies, increasing the risk of fruit slippage or irreversible tissue damage.
- **Enabling Adaptive and Damage-Free Manipulation:** The goal is to develop a system that can pre-emptively estimate the required force for each fruit, allowing the robot to adapt its grip to the specific mechanical properties of individual dates and thus ensure stable manipulation without causing damage.

The specific contributions outlined by the authors to achieve these objectives include:
- Developing a Vision-to-Force (V2F) framework designed to predict safe grasp forces directly from pre-contact visual observations.
- Proposing a physics-guided residual learning model that integrates Hertzian contact mechanics with data-driven learning to enhance force prediction accuracy.
- Conducting experimental validation on a robotic gripper equipped with a force sensor to demonstrate the feasibility of using a machine learning-driven approach for damage-free handling of delicate fruits.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Vision-to-Force (V2F) framework employs a three-stage, physics-guided residual learning architecture to map visual descriptors to mechanical grasping constraints.

#### 3.1. Mechanical Characterization and Force Modeling

The framework is grounded in a biomechanical understanding of date fruits, establishing a "safe grasping envelope."
- **Biomechanical Properties:** Date fruits exhibit viscoelastic behavior. Force-deformation curves are characterized by three points: the Linear Limit (LL), the Bioyield Point (y) (onset of microstructural cell rupture, representing the functional limit for damage-free handling), and the Rupture Point (R) (macrostructural failure). The objective is to keep the maximum contact stress (σ_max) below the bioyield threshold (σ_bioyield) to ensure elastic deformation.
- **Parameters:** Mechanical resistance is characterized by Young’s modulus (E), Poisson’s ratio (ν), and shear modulus (G). These are crucial for the physics-informed model.
- **Feature Mapping:** The core hypothesis is that fruit morphology, specifically its projected surface area, serves as a reliable proxy for its mechanical constraints. This allows the problem to be framed as a predictive vision task rather than a reactive tactile sensing task.

#### 3.2. Data and Inputs

- **Dataset:** The dataset comprises approximately 500 date fruit samples at the Tamar ripeness stage, collected under controlled laboratory conditions. It includes three Saudi cultivars (Ajwa, Barhi, Sagai) prepared under normal, moistened, and dried conditions to capture variability in texture and moisture.
- **Mechanical Labels:** Supervisory labels were generated from synchronous force and deformation recordings during compression tests, identifying safe grasping limits prior to permanent tissue damage.
- **Coordinate System and Dimensions:** A right-handed x-y-z coordinate system is defined, with the z-axis aligned longitudinally (fruit height Z_0) and the y-axis corresponding to the maximum transverse diameter (Y_0). The cross-sectional area (A_0) is measured on the zy-plane, and fruit volume (V_0) is approximated using an ellipsoidal formula.
- **Input Vector (x):** The input to the V2F framework consists of:
 - Geometric features: Y_0, Z_0, A_0, V_0.
 - One-hot encoded categorical indicators for cultivar identity (DT) and hydration level (HS).
 - Cultivar-specific elastic strain limit (ε_y) from experimental data, serving as a biomechanical prior.
- **Target:** The target for prediction is the measured yield-level engineering stress (σ_y_kPa).

#### 3.3. Vision Model Architecture

- **Object Detection and Segmentation:** A Mask Region-Based Convolutional Neural Network (Mask R-CNN) with a ResNet-50 Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) backbone is used for date fruit detection and segmentation in static images and video sequences. The model was fine-tuned on the custom dataset and initialized with COCO pre-trained weights for binary classification (background or date fruit).
- **Hyperparameters:** Key hyperparameters include 100 training epochs, Stochastic Gradient Descent optimizer, an initial learning rate of 0.001, 0.9 momentum, 0.0005 weight decay, batch size of 2, mask loss weighting of 1.5, early stopping patience of 5 epochs, NMS threshold of 0.4, and a detection score threshold of 0.3.
- **Contour Refinement:** To improve measurement accuracy, an Active Contour Algorithm refines the initial Mask R-CNN segmentation contour. This algorithm uses grayscale conversion with Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) for contrast enhancement and incorporates contours from previous frames for temporal consistency. The refined contour is constrained within the initial model’s boundaries.
- **Dimensional Measurement:** A ZED stereo camera captures images and videos, from which the refined model’s detected contour is used to extract physical dimensions in pixels, later converted to real-world units.

#### 3.4. Stress Prediction ML Model Theoretical Formulation

The stress prediction model is a hybrid framework combining an analytical baseline with a learnable residual term.
- **Analytical Baseline:** An initial baseline stress (σ_Hertz) is computed using Hertzian contact theory. Mechanical parameters (estimated Young's modulus Ê and Poisson’s ratio ν̂) are regressed from physical features to define an effective modulus Ê* = Ê/(1 - ν̂^2). The equivalent fruit radius (R_eq) is approximated as Y_0, and indentation (δ) is modeled as (ε_y/100) * Y_0.
- **Residual Learning:** Due to the biological nature of fruits (viscoelasticity, heterogeneity), which deviates from ideal elastic assumptions, the predicted stress is decomposed into the Hertzian term plus a learnable residual r_φ(x). The overall prediction is σ̂ = σ̂_Hertz(x) + r_φ(x) + η, where η accounts for measurement noise.
- **Physics-Guided Loss Function:** A loss function (L) enforces consistency between data-driven predictions and underlying contact mechanics. It combines a supervised data loss (L_data) that penalizes the mismatch between predicted and ground-truth residual stress, and a physics loss (L_phys) that penalizes deviations of the total predicted stress from the Hertzian contact stress. A trade-off parameter α controls the balance between these two components.

#### 3.5. Validation and Generalization

- **Cross-Validation Strategy:** To assess robustness across diverse conditions, a five-fold GroupKFold cross-validation (CV) strategy was implemented. This ensured that samples from the same cultivar-hydration state group were entirely contained within either the training or validation set, forcing the model to generalize to unseen date varieties and conditions.
- **Performance Metrics:** Model performance was evaluated using the mean validation R^2 score and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) in kPa.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Vision Model Performance

- **Accuracy:** The Mask R-CNN model achieved an initial classification accuracy of 0.67 at epoch 5, which improved to a stable accuracy of 1.0 by epoch 10. This indicated robust detection performance and accurate segmentation of date fruits, including compressed samples.
- **Refinement:** The Active Contour Algorithm further refined the segmentation contours, improving measurement accuracy and effectively handling the irregular textures and natural asymmetry characteristic of date fruits.
- **Conclusion:** The automated segmentation process provided by the vision model effectively measures date fruit dimensions, surpassing the limitations of manual caliper-based methods.

#### 4.2. Stress Prediction ML Model Performance

- **Comparison to Baselines:** The proposed V2F model significantly outperformed standard baseline regression models (e.g., Linear Regression, Ridge, Lasso, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, SVR, KNN) in predicting safe grasping stress.
- **Overall Performance:** The V2F model achieved a mean validation R^2 of approximately 0.74 and a Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of 1.13 kPa across the five-fold GroupKFold cross-validation.
- **Generalization:** While one fold (Fold 5: Sagai Dried/Normal) presented a greater generalization challenge with an R^2 of 0.69, the model demonstrated consistent performance, indicating its ability to provide physically reasonable force set-points even when encountering novel cultivar-condition combinations that were entirely unseen during training. The consistently low training RMSE (0.56 kPa) suggested that while internal features were learned effectively, high inter-group variability necessitated the hybrid approach for stability.

#### 4.3. Experimental Validation

- **Procedure:** Physical grasping experiments were conducted using a UR10e robotic manipulator, a ZED stereo camera, a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, and an integrated load cell. The vision model calculated fruit area, which, along with variety and ripeness, was fed into the V2F model to predict a target force (F_pred). The gripper then applied F_pred, executed a pick-and-place task, and the vision system re-scanned the fruit to quantify residual deformation (δ_res) after release.
- **Cultivars Tested:** Experiments were performed on Ajwa, Barhi, and Sagai date cultivars at their natural hydration state.
- **Predicted Forces:** The predicted grasp forces varied by cultivar: 1.25 N for Ajwa, 0.60 N for Barhi, and 0.30 N for Sagai.
- **Damage-Free Handling:** All grasping trials were successful, with no fruit dropping or observable bruising. The measured post-release residual deformation remained below 1 mm for all cultivars (0.8 mm for Ajwa, 0.6 mm for Barhi, and 0.4 mm for Sagai). These low residual deformations indicated that the predicted forces maintained the fruit within its elastic deformation regime, thereby preventing permanent tissue damage.
- **Grasp Stability:** Grasp stability was further evaluated using the Coulomb friction model. The minimum normal force required for static slip-free grasping was calculated to be approximately 0.15 N, assuming a conservative static friction coefficient of 0.3 and an average fruit mass of 9 g. All predicted grasp forces (minimum 0.30 N for Sagai) were well above this theoretical minimum, providing a safety margin of at least two. Consistent with this analysis, no observable slip or fruit loss occurred during any pick-and-place experiment.
- **Conclusion:** The experimental results demonstrated that the V2F framework achieved both damage-free handling and reliable grasp stability simultaneously, validating its effectiveness in embedding physics-guided residual learning within a vision-based force prediction pipeline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation of delicate agricultural products, particularly date fruits, by introducing a predictive and damage-aware grasping framework.

- **Proactive Damage Prevention:** Unlike conventional reactive tactile sensing methods that can cause micro-bruising before corrective action, the V2F framework enables pre-contact prediction of optimal grasp forces. This proactive approach minimizes the risk of fruit damage, which is crucial for maintaining post-harvest quality and commercial value.
- **Addressing a Critical Agricultural Challenge:** The work specifically addresses the "mechanical paradox" of date fruits—their requirement for high detachment forces combined with low bruise thresholds. By providing cultivar-specific and condition-aware force predictions, the system can adapt to the inherent variability of biological tissues, a capability often lacking in generic robotic solutions.
- **Integration of Vision, Machine Learning, and Physics:** The methodology's strength lies in its hybrid design, combining robust computer vision for geometric feature extraction (Mask R-CNN with active contour refinement), data-driven machine learning (residual neural network), and fundamental physics principles (Hertzian contact mechanics). This integration results in a more robust and physically interpretable model compared to purely data-driven approaches.
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy and Efficiency:** By enabling robots to autonomously determine safe grasping parameters from visual input, the framework has the potential to increase the efficiency and throughput of agricultural handling operations. It reduces reliance on human labor for delicate tasks and allows for more consistent quality control.
- **Generalizability and Robustness:** The use of GroupKFold cross-validation, specifically holding out entire cultivar-hydration state groups, demonstrates the model's ability to generalize to unseen conditions, a key requirement for real-world agricultural deployment where fruit characteristics can vary significantly.
- **Foundation for Future Adaptive Systems:** The success of this vision-driven force estimation method provides a foundation for the development of more sophisticated adaptive robotic handling systems. The stated future work, exploring closed-loop integration with tactile feedback, suggests a path towards even greater robustness for rare or out-of-distribution cases where vision-only predictions might be insufficient. This framework contributes to the broader goal of making agricultural robotics more capable and reliable in handling complex and fragile biological materials.
