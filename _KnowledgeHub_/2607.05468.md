# Learning 4D Geometric Priors for Inference-Efficient World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MECo-WAM enables World Action Models to acquire action-relevant 4D geometric priors during training without incurring additional inference cost at deployment. The method improved manipulation success rates by up to 1.38 percentage points in simulation and reduced real-world task completion times by 12% with 39% fewer corrections on an ARX-R5 robotic arm.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) often rely on appearance-oriented video prediction, which inadequately captures precise spatial and temporal geometry crucial for intricate manipulation tasks.
- Directly integrating explicit 4D reconstruction or dense geometric prediction modules into WAMs increases inference costs, making them impractical for real-time robotic control.
- Generic geometric supervision may overlook specific visual relations among manipulated objects, targets, and their dynamic interactions that are causally relevant to robot actions.

## Method

- A **Multi-Expert Co-Training** architecture is introduced, consisting of parallel Video, Action, and a training-only ==4D expert== that processes clean current and future geometry targets from a frozen ==VGGT== encoder.
- ==Decayed 4D read-mask attention== establishes temporary, stochastic read edges from future video/action queries to the current-frame geometry token, which are gradually decayed over training to zero at deployment.
- ==Action-aware temporal geometric distillation== supervises the 4D expert's predictions by aligning pairwise feature relations with VGGT targets, using action-aware weights to emphasize relevant regions and a temporal loss for evolving relations.

## Results

- MECo-WAM achieved an average success rate of **98.2%** across LIBERO benchmarks, a **0.6 percentage point** improvement over Fast-WAM, with gains most pronounced in geometry-sensitive tasks like Spatial (**+0.6 pp**).
- On RoboTwin 2.0, the model obtained an overall average success rate of **92.62%**, surpassing the non-pretrained Fast-WAM baseline by **0.79 percentage points**, and exhibiting enhanced robustness under visual randomization.
- In real-world robotic tasks, MECo-WAM improved average success rate by **5.0 percentage points** and progress rate by **2.5 percentage points**, while reducing correction counts by **39%** and completion times by **12%** compared to Fast-WAM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-relevant 4D geometric priors can be effectively instilled into a WAM's core representations during training and then completely removed at inference, maintaining computational efficiency while enhancing geometric understanding.
> - Stochastic and decaying attention mechanisms provide a viable method to transfer knowledge from auxiliary experts without creating permanent dependencies or increasing deployment-time complexity.
> - Focusing geometric supervision on action-aware relational and temporal changes between objects and the gripper improves a robot's ability to reason about and execute precise manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is advancing rapidly, with a significant focus on developing policies that can map visual observations and language instructions to precise action trajectories. World Action Models (WAMs) represent a class of approaches that aim to achieve this by jointly learning the dynamics of visual states under interaction and the generation of robot actions. These models leverage video-action co-training to acquire richer motion and interaction priors, enabling reasoning about temporal changes.

A common challenge in many existing WAMs is that their visual representations tend to be dominated by appearance-oriented video prediction. While sufficient for plausible future video synthesis, these representations may inadequately capture the precise spatial and temporal geometric relations crucial for intricate manipulation tasks (e.g., grasping, object alignment, contact stability). To address this, recent research has explored integrating 3D or 4D structural information into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and WAM frameworks to strengthen spatial grounding.

Previous attempts to incorporate geometry often involve introducing explicit 4D reconstruction or dense geometric prediction modules into the deployment pipeline. This direct approach can lead to increased inference costs and may divert optimization efforts towards generic geometric reconstruction that is not strongly coupled with action generation. Furthermore, generic geometric supervision might overlook the specific visual relations among manipulated objects, target regions, and their dynamic interactions that are causally relevant to robot actions.

This work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome the trade-off between geometric richness and inference efficiency. It aims to integrate action-relevant temporal geometry into WAMs without incurring additional computational cost at deployment time, thus distinguishing itself from methods that add permanent 4D components. It builds upon efficient WAM principles, such as those that decouple future video prediction from test-time action generation, by exploring a novel source and method of supervision: using a training-only 4D expert to transfer geometric priors into the shared video-action representation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a World Action Model (WAM) to acquire action-relevant temporal geometric priors during its training phase, while maintaining a lightweight video-action inference graph that does not include any explicit 4D geometry components at deployment.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in current robotic manipulation models:
- **Insufficient Geometric Capture:** Existing video-action co-training methods in WAMs primarily optimize for appearance-oriented video latents. While effective for generating visually plausible future video frames, these representations often fail to capture the temporally evolving 4D geometry essential for precise manipulation. For instance, successfully grasping an object requires understanding its spatial reachability and alignment, which appearance alone might not adequately convey.
- **High Inference Cost of Explicit Geometry:** A direct approach to inject geometry involves integrating explicit 4D reconstruction or dense geometric prediction into the WAM pipeline. However, making geometry an explicit output during deployment significantly increases inference cost, which is undesirable for real-time robotic control. Moreover, such explicit reconstruction might lead to optimization biases towards generic geometric accuracy rather than action-relevant features.
- **Lack of Action-Awareness in Generic Geometry:** Generic geometric supervision may not differentiate or emphasize the specific visual relations crucial for a robot's current action. This can lead to overlooking critical interactions between manipulated objects, target locations, and their dynamic changes over time, which are vital for successful task completion.
- **Need for Inference-Efficient Solutions:** To facilitate practical deployment on robots, it is critical for policies to operate with low inference latency. Incorporating complex 4D processing at inference time contradicts this requirement. The research aims to find a method that enriches the model's understanding of geometry without sacrificing deployment efficiency.

The central question addressed by the paper is whether a WAM can gain a deeper understanding of action-relevant temporal geometry during training without introducing a permanent 4D dependency or increasing inference cost. MECo-WAM proposes to achieve this by employing a temporary, training-only 4D expert, which acts as a guide to instill geometric knowledge into the core video-action representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MECo-WAM, a Multi-Expert Co-Training World Action Model, introduces a training-time architecture to inject 4D geometric priors into the WAM representations. The core principle is that the 4D geometry serves as a training-time representation constraint, and all auxiliary 4D components are removed at deployment, preserving the original lightweight video-action inference graph.

The methodology involves three main components:

#### 3.1. Multi-Expert Co-Training Architecture

The model utilizes a transformer-based architecture with three distinct experts operating in parallel during training:
- **Video Expert:** Processes current and future video frames encoded by a VAE. It denoises noisy future VAE target slots to predict future video outputs (f_p1, f_p2, f_ph) supervised by a video loss.
- **Action Expert:** Uses the current robot state (a_0) as an anchor and denoises noisy future action targets to predict future action chunks (a_p1, a_p2, a_ph) conditioned on the current visual context (f_0) and language instruction (ℓ).
- **4D Expert:** This is a lightweight, training-only expert. It receives tokens from a frozen VGGT encoder (Wang et al. 2025a), which provides clean current and future geometry targets (g_0 and g_1, g_2, g_h). The 4D expert denoises noisy future geometry slots to predict future geometry (g_p1, g_p2, g_ph). Its objective is applied only on selected keyframe predictions.

During training, these experts operate on a mixed-attention sequence, where each expert has separate query, key, and value projections. A specific attention mask governs information flow between experts.

#### 3.2. Decayed 4D Read-Mask Attention

To transfer geometric knowledge without creating a permanent dependency, MECo-WAM introduces a decayed 4D read-mask attention mechanism.
- **Mask Design:** The attention mask restricts information flow to prevent non-causal shortcuts. Current anchors (f_0, a_0, g_0) can only attend to themselves. Future video tokens can read from the video branch but not action tokens. Future action tokens can read clean visual context (f_0) and the action branch but not noisy future video slots. Crucially, future 4D slots remain within the 4D branch and serve only as auxiliary prediction targets.
- **Temporary Read Edges:** The mechanism introduces temporary read edges from future video/action queries to the *current-frame geometry token* (g_0). This is considered "safe" because g_0 is encoded solely from the current RGB frame.
- **Decay Schedule:** These read edges are stochastic and gradually decay over the course of optimization. The activation probability (p_4d(s)) follows a linear decay schedule, starting from a high probability (p_start) and progressively reducing to a low or zero probability (p_end) after a certain number of optimization steps (S_decay). This schedule exposes the video-action pathway to current-frame geometric guidance early in training and progressively removes this dependency. At deployment, p_end is set to 0, ensuring no 4D tokens are instantiated, and the original video-action graph is recovered.

#### 3.3. Action-Aware Temporal Geometric Distillation

This distillation objective leverages the frozen VGGT encoder as a geometry teacher to supervise the training-time 4D expert's predictions, focusing on action-relevant geometry:
- **VGGT-Aligned Geometry (Relational Matching):** For selected keyframes, the student's predicted geometry features are aligned with the clean VGGT target geometry. Geometry is represented by pairwise feature relations (L2 distance between features) rather than absolute coordinates. This relational matching approach ensures supervision focuses on relative object-gripper-target structure, avoiding assumptions about shared absolute feature coordinate systems.
- **Action-Aware Weights:** Not all visual regions are equally pertinent to a given action. The methodology computes action-aware weights (w_k_i) that indicate the relevance of visual tokens to the contemporaneous robot motion. This is done by computing a similarity score between a selected video token and a pooled action representation, then applying a softmax and mixing with a uniform prior (controlled by η) to prevent collapse onto a single region. These weights emphasize manipulated objects, targets, and contact regions in the within-frame geometric loss (L_act_geo).
- **Temporal Geometry:** Manipulation involves dynamic changes. A temporal loss (L_act_tem) is introduced to supervise how action-relevant relations evolve across keyframes. This loss calculates normalized relation changes between consecutive selected keyframe pairs for both student predictions and VGGT targets, weighted by combined action-aware weights. This captures approach, contact, transport, and release dynamics, rather than just static scene geometry.

#### 3.4. Training Objective and Inference

The overall training objective combines the conditional flow-matching losses for the video (L_video) and action (L_action) experts with the 4D objective (L_4d), which is a weighted sum of the action-aware geometric (L_act_geo) and temporal (L_act_tem) losses.
During inference, all auxiliary 4D components—the 4D expert, the frozen VGGT encoder, and the alignment modules—are entirely removed. This means the deployed policy maintains the exact same observation-to-action interface as the base WAM, incurring no additional inference cost.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates MECo-WAM across various benchmarks, including simulated environments and real-world manipulation tasks, to demonstrate its efficacy in improving manipulation performance without increasing inference cost.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

- **LIBERO (Liu et al. 2023):** MECo-WAM achieved an average success rate of 98.2% across the Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long suites. This represents a 0.6 percentage point improvement over Fast-WAM and a 0.5 percentage point improvement over Motus, both non-pretrained WAMs. The gains were most pronounced in geometry-sensitive tasks: 98.8% on Spatial (0.6 points over Fast-WAM), 100.0% on Object (matching Fast-WAM), and 98.2% on Goal (1.2 points over Fast-WAM). This suggests that the action-aware 4D co-training specifically enhances spatial and object-centric reasoning.
- **RoboTwin 2.0 (Mu et al. 2025):** MECo-WAM obtained an overall average success rate of 92.62%, surpassing the non-pretrained Fast-WAM baseline (91.83%) by 0.79 percentage points. The improvement was greater in the clean setting (93.26% vs. 91.88%, +1.38 points) and remained positive under randomization (91.98% vs. 91.78%, +0.20 points). This indicates enhanced robustness to visual perturbations. Notably, MECo-WAM slightly outperformed the strongest pretraining-based WAM (LingBot-VA, 92.20%) without requiring extensive embodied-policy pretraining.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation

Experiments were conducted on an ARX-R5 robotic arm for two tabletop tasks: "Stack three sponge cubes vertically" and "Sort three cubes into a line by size."
- **Stack Cubes:** MECo-WAM matched Fast-WAM in success rate (SR) and progress rate (PR) at 60.0% and 75.0% respectively. However, MECo-WAM significantly reduced the average correction count from 1.67 to 0.83 and shortened the average completion time from 27.06s to 25.71s. This indicates a more efficient and stable execution, likely due to better vertical alignment.
- **Sort Cubes by Size:** MECo-WAM improved SR from 60.0% to 70.0% and PR from 75.0% to 80.0% compared to Fast-WAM. It also reduced correction counts from 1.33 to 1.00 and completion time from 38.49s to 31.96s.
- **Overall Real-World Performance:** Averaged across both tasks, MECo-WAM showed a 5.0 percentage point gain in SR and a 2.5 percentage point gain in PR over Fast-WAM, with approximately 39% fewer corrections and 12% shorter completion times. This provides empirical evidence that the learned 4D prior translates to improved real-robot spatial grounding and execution efficiency.

#### 4.3. Representation Probing

To investigate whether geometric priors were indeed transferred to the deployed video-action features, the researchers probed the shared WAM representation:
- **Depth Structure:** Training a DPT-style depth prediction head on tokens from the final layers of MECo-WAM (with the backbone frozen and auxiliary 4D tokens excluded) revealed sharper depth maps compared to Fast-WAM. This suggests that the 4D co-training enriches the deployed video-action representation with more detailed geometric information.
- **Grasp Sensitivity:** A comparison of grasp attempts showed that MECo-WAM exhibited stronger sensitivity to action-relevant 3D position and pose, and better preserved contact geometry during grasping. This further supports the transfer of fine-grained geometric understanding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

A systematic ablation study on RoboTwin 2.0 identified the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Isolated 4D Expert:** Adding only an isolated 4D expert yielded a minimal task gain (91.83% to 91.87%) but did not improve action MSE, suggesting limited transfer if geometry is confined.
- **Decayed 4D Read-Mask Attention:** Incorporating decayed 4D read access increased average success to 92.14% and significantly reduced action MSE, demonstrating the importance of temporary geometry-to-policy transfer.
- **Action-Aware Geometric and Temporal Losses (L_act_geo, L_act_tem):** Sequential addition of these losses progressively reduced action MSE to 0.022 and 0.019, respectively, and increased task success above 92.2%.
- **Full MECo-WAM vs. Uniform Weights:** The full MECo-WAM achieved the highest PSNR (30.72), lowest action MSE (0.013), and highest average success (92.62%). A variant without action-aware weighting (using uniform weights) reached 92.38%, indicating that the action-aware weighting contributed an additional performance gain by focusing on task-relevant geometry.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in MECo-WAM introduces an approach for enhancing the geometric reasoning capabilities of World Action Models without increasing their inference complexity at deployment. This has several implications and potential impacts:

- **Inference-Efficient Geometric Grounding:** The core contribution lies in demonstrating that action-relevant 4D geometric priors can be effectively injected during training and then completely removed at inference. This addresses a critical trade-off in robotic manipulation, where rich environmental understanding often comes at the cost of real-time performance. By decoupling training and inference time architectures, MECo-WAM offers a pathway to develop policies that are both geometrically aware and computationally efficient.
- **Improved Manipulation Precision and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains observed across LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world tasks (including reduced correction counts and faster completion times) suggest that enhanced geometric priors lead to more precise and robust robot control. This is particularly relevant for tasks requiring fine motor skills, accurate object placement, and stable interactions.
- **Leveraging Foundational Models for Robotics:** By utilizing a frozen VGGT encoder as a source of 4D geometric priors, the work demonstrates an effective strategy for transferring knowledge from powerful foundational vision models into robot control policies. This method avoids the need for robots to learn geometry from scratch or to perform complex 3D reconstruction themselves at runtime.
- **Advancement in WAM Design:** MECo-WAM contributes to the ongoing evolution of World Action Models by proposing a novel method for enriching their internal representations. It suggests that WAMs can move beyond solely appearance-oriented video prediction to implicitly incorporate sophisticated spatial and temporal geometric understanding, leading to more capable and generalizable robot policies.
- **Facilitating Real-World Deployment:** The focus on maintaining a lightweight inference graph makes MECo-WAM highly relevant for practical robotic applications. Reduced latency and computational demands are crucial for deploying advanced manipulation skills on resource-constrained robotic platforms in real-world environments.
- **New Directions for Knowledge Distillation:** The action-aware temporal geometric distillation mechanism introduces a sophisticated form of knowledge transfer. It highlights the importance of not just *what* geometry is learned, but *which parts* of the geometry are most relevant to the task and *how* those relevant relations change over time. This could inspire similar approaches for distilling task-specific information into general-purpose models.

In summary, MECo-WAM represents an advancement in the field of embodied AI, offering a method to imbue robot policies with a deeper understanding of the 4D world dynamics critical for manipulation, without compromising the efficiency required for real-world robotic deployment.
