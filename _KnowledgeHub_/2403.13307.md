# LaserHuman: Language-guided Scene-aware Human Motion Generation in Free Environment

> [!summary] Summary
> LaserHuman is a Scene-Text-to-Motion dataset of real LiDAR/IMU-captured human motion in 11 large indoor and outdoor scenes, with free-form language and dynamic interacting people. It pairs this with a multi-conditional diffusion model whose parallel cross-attention fuses point-cloud and CLIP text features, reaching state-of-the-art FID, contact, and text-matching scores on both LaserHuman and HUMANISE.

## Problem

- Existing scene-text motion data relies on synthesized, fitting-adjusted motion, compromising physical plausibility and authenticity of human-scene contact.
- Prior datasets use templated language over four basic postures in static indoor rooms, excluding outdoor terrain, dynamic obstacles, and person-to-person interaction.
- Existing multi-condition generators use naive concatenation or scene-queried attention, overlooking cross-modal interplay and collapsing latents in VAE-based formulations.

## Method

- LaserHuman capture pipeline: synchronized 128-beam LiDARs, RGB cameras, and 17 wearable IMUs, registered to pre-scanned ==SLAM== scene maps, fit to ==SMPL== with semi-automatic alignment.
- Free-form annotation protocol: two annotators of varied age and gender describe each 1-4 second clip, then ChatGPT augmentation expands text count and diversity.
- **Multi-condition fusion module**: point Transformer scene features plus ==CLIP== text features, self-attention, then ==parallel bidirectional cross-attention== concatenated with raw text to condition an ==MDM==-style diffusion denoiser.

## Results

- Dataset holds **3,374** motion sequences, **12,303** descriptions averaging **28.5** words (four times prior length), **92,955** point-cloud frames, scenes spanning **50** to **2000** square metres.
- On LaserHuman the model reaches FID **0.987** and R-score **0.326** versus MDM **1.014/0.313** and cVAE **1.595/0.195**, with best contact **0.523** and translation diversity **2.035**.
- Ablations show parallel cross fusion beats scene-queried (FID **4.063**), text-queried (**2.782**) and no-query (**2.191**) variants; user study plausibility **4.1** versus **2.8-3.5** baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional cross-modal attention outperforms one-sided queried fusion: each modality must query the other, or the neglected condition silently dominates generation.
> - Diversity metrics can mislead - high translation diversity with low pose variance signals sliding artifacts, so read pose, marker, and translation diversity jointly.
> - Physics-based motion trackers degrade on noisy reconstructed meshes and uneven terrain, so simulator refinement needs curriculum learning over increasing geometry noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic human motions from natural language descriptions within 3D scenes, referred to as Scene-Text-to-Motion, is an interdisciplinary challenge spanning computer vision, computer graphics, natural language processing, and robotics. Prior research in human motion generation predominantly focused on single-condition inputs.

One major category is **Text-to-Motion**, where models generate motions based on action classes or natural language descriptions. Datasets like KIT, HumanML3D, and BABEL have facilitated advancements in digital human applications. However, these methods typically lack scene constraints, limiting their applicability in contexts requiring interaction with an environment, such as robotics.

Conversely, **Scene-to-Motion** methods aim to produce physically plausible human motions coherent with contextual scenes. Datasets such as PROX, GTA-IM, and CIRCLE are utilized for evaluating human-scene interaction. While these methods are relevant for simulation and automated navigation, they generally do not incorporate precise control through language instructions.

The recent work, HUMANISE, introduced a platform for Scene-Text-to-Motion research by offering both textual and scene-based conditions. However, HUMANISE has several identified limitations:
1. It relies on synthesized motion data, which can compromise the authenticity and physical plausibility of movements.
2. Its language descriptions are based on fixed templates and restricted action types, which may not capture the diversity of real-world language and actions.
3. It is confined to indoor scenes, limiting the variety of activity spaces and terrains.
4. Its environments consist solely of static furniture, whereas real-life scenarios often involve dynamic elements like moving humans or objects.

These limitations in existing datasets and methodologies highlight a gap in the ability to generate human motions that are simultaneously realistic, semantically consistent with diverse language, and physically plausible within complex, dynamic 3D environments. The work presented aims to address these identified gaps by introducing a new dataset and a corresponding generative model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of language-guided, scene-aware human motion generation, particularly in free and unconstrained environments. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Address Data Limitations:** Existing datasets for Scene-Text-to-Motion research suffer from critical limitations, including reliance on synthesized motion, templated language descriptions, confinement to static indoor scenes, and exclusion of dynamic environmental elements. These constraints hinder the development of models that can generate realistic and versatile human motions for real-world applications. The paper aims to create a new dataset that overcomes these issues.
2. **Enhance Authenticity and Plausibility:** Synthesized motion data often lacks the authenticity and physical realism observed in natural human movements. A key motivation is to provide real human motion data captured in genuine 3D environments to ensure that generated motions are physically plausible and naturally expressive.
3. **Increase Diversity and Generalizability:** Previous datasets and methods were limited in the diversity of actions, language expressions, and environmental settings they supported. The research seeks to enable the generation of a broader range of human motions, interactions, and trajectories, described by free-form natural language, across varied indoor and outdoor scenarios, including dynamic scenes with moving objects or other individuals.
4. **Facilitate Robotics and Entertainment Applications:** The ability to generate realistic and contextually appropriate human motions from language instructions in 3D scenes has direct applications in domains such as humanoid robotics (for generating robot action sequences), animation, virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR), and simulations. The goal is to provide tools that can replace laborious frame-by-frame 3D modeling with an end-to-end generation paradigm, thereby improving efficiency and enabling user-specific requirements.
5. **Develop an Effective Generative Model:** Beyond data provision, the research is motivated to develop a robust generative model capable of leveraging the richness of the proposed dataset. This model needs to effectively integrate multi-modal conditions (language and 3D scene information) to produce motions that are both semantically consistent with textual instructions and physically plausible within the interactive 3D environment. This involves addressing the challenge of combining diverse contextual inputs without losing information or creating inconsistencies.

In summary, the motivation is to fill the existing data and methodological gaps to enable the generation of more realistic, diverse, and controllable human motions in complex, real-world 3D environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a new dataset, LaserHuman, and proposes a multi-conditional diffusion model to address the task of language-guided, scene-aware human motion generation.

#### 3.1. LaserHuman Dataset Acquisition and Pre-processing

**Data Collection:**
The LaserHuman dataset focuses on capturing real human motions and interactions in diverse, unconstrained 3D environments.
- **Scene Data:** Pre-scanned dense point clouds of 11 diverse scenes (5 outdoor, 2 small indoor, 4 complex first-floor halls) were obtained using a Stereye Polar 1S and SLAM algorithms, with meshes constructed subsequently. These scenes range in size, with the largest spanning over 2000 m².
- **Dynamic Scene and Human Motion Data:** Synchronized and calibrated multiple 128-beam LiDARs and RGB cameras were used to capture dynamic scenes from different views. For human motion, a target person wore 17 Noitom IMU devices and interacted naturally with static objects or up to three other persons. To ensure diversity, target persons of different ages and genders were involved.

**Data Pre-processing:**
- **Scene Alignment:** Pre-scanned static scene maps were aligned with dynamic LiDAR point clouds using a registration algorithm.
- **Human Motion Extraction:** LiDAR data provided accurate depth information for segmenting human points. The global translation (T) in world coordinates was obtained using optimization algorithms. SMPL models were used to represent human body motion, defined by translation (T), body rotation parameters (θ), and shape parameters (β). The alignment process was semi-automatic, involving manual segmentation and position adjustment for occlusions, achieving an average Chamfer Distance of 4.5mm between the point cloud and the mesh. SMPL models for interacting participants were obtained using LIP and optimized from multi-view LiDAR point clouds.
- **Motion Segmentation:** Motion data was segmented into clips ranging from 1 to 4 seconds.
- **Language Annotation and Augmentation:** For each motion sequence, two different annotators of various ages and genders provided free-form linguistic descriptions detailing the person’s movements and interactions. ChatGPT was employed for text augmentation to expand the number and diversity of descriptions. Consistency and quality were verified by cross-checking motion-text pairs.
- **Multi-modal Data Output:** LaserHuman provides videos, 3D scene maps, dynamic LiDAR point clouds, and global human motions (SMPL parameters) for the target person and other participants. Three types of scene data are prepared:
 1. Integration of cropped scene map and LiDAR point cloud sequences of interactive participants.
 2. Cropped scene map and SMPL sequences of interactive participants.
 3. Only the cropped scene map.

#### 3.2. Multi-conditional Diffusion Model

The research proposes a multi-conditional diffusion model, building on the denoising diffusion probabilistic models and MDM, to generate human motion sequences. The architecture incorporates a specific "Multi-condition Fusion Module."

**Human Motion Representation:**
Human motion is represented as a sequence of poses x = {x_n}^N_m, where x_n is a single pose in the HumanML3D format, and N_m is the sequence length.

**Multi-condition Fusion Module:**
This module is designed to integrate scene data and textual descriptions into a joint conditional embedding (z_c) that guides the diffusion process. The first type of scene data (registered point cloud of static scene map and dynamic interactive participants) is used as input.
1. **Feature Extraction:**
 - **Scene Features (F_p):** A Point Transformer extracts features from the downsampled scene point cloud (f_p ∈ R^(Np×D), where D=6 for (x,y,z,r,g,b)). The scene center is normalized to (0,0) and serves as the motion sequence's starting position.
 - **Language Features (F_l):** A pre-trained CLIP model extracts features from the natural language description.
2. **Feature Enhancement and Interaction:**
 - Self-attention layers enhance F_p and F_l, yielding F'_p and F'_l.
 - F'_p is concatenated with the original position feature f_p and processed by a fully-connected layer to get F'_pc.
 - A parallel cross-attention mechanism is then applied to learn feature interactions between modalities. F'_l acts as a query for F'_pc (keys and values), and F'_pc acts as a query for F'_l (keys and values), resulting in mutually enhanced features F_L and F_P, respectively.
 - The final condition feature z_c is obtained by concatenating F'_l, F_P, and F_L, followed by a fully-connected layer. This design aims to leverage both fine-grained text features and rich geometric/dynamic scene information.

**Learning and Sampling:**
- **Diffusion Process:** The model learns to denoise a noisy motion sequence x_t back to a clean motion x_0, conditioned on a noise step t and the fused scene-text condition feature z_c. The denoising process is modeled by a Transformer network, M(x_t, t, z_c), which predicts the clean motion ^x_0.
- **Loss Function:** The training objective includes the motion loss (L_motion = E_{t,x_0}[|x_t - M(x_t, t, z_c)|]) and common geometric losses from MDM to encourage natural and coherent motion.
- **Sampling:** During inference, the model iteratively predicts a clean motion ^x_0, which is then re-noised to generate x_{t-1}, repeating this for T steps to synthesize the final motion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a new dataset, LaserHuman, and evaluates the proposed multi-conditional diffusion model against various baselines on both LaserHuman and HUMANISE datasets.

#### 4.1. LaserHuman Dataset Characteristics and Statistics

- **Novelty:** LaserHuman is characterized by real human motions in 3D scenes, free-form natural language descriptions, a mix of indoor and outdoor scenarios, and both static and dynamic environments.
- **Scale:** It contains 11 diverse 3D scenes (up to 2000 m²), 3,374 high-quality motion sequences (1-4 seconds each), and 12,303 language descriptions (4,495 original, augmented to 12,303), with an average description length of 28.5 words. The total point cloud frames amount to 92,955 over 3 hours of collection.
- **Diversity:** The dataset includes detailed interactions with static objects, diverse terrains (e.g., stairs), complex sports activities, and person-to-person interactions (e.g., shaking hands, hugging), categories often absent or limited in previous datasets.
- **Multi-modal:** It provides videos, 3D scene maps, dynamic LiDAR point clouds, and global human motions.

#### 4.2. Quantitative and Qualitative Evaluation

The model's performance was evaluated using standard metrics: non-collision score, contact score, Average Pairwise Distance (APD-t/p/m) and standard deviation (std-t/p/m) for diversity, FID for distribution distance, and R-score for text-motion matching accuracy. A user study assessed diversity, scene consistency, text alignment, smoothness, and overall plausibility (p-score).

**Comparison with Baselines on LaserHuman (Table 2 & 3):**
- **Single-modality Baselines:** Text-only methods (MDM, MLD) exhibited lower non-collision and contact scores, indicating poor physical plausibility in scenes. Scene-only methods (SceneDiff) achieved high non-collision but lower R-scores, showing a lack of semantic consistency with text.
- **Multi-conditional Baselines:** cVAE showed less parameter variance but higher marker and translation diversity, suggesting plausible global locations but limited local pose variation leading to sliding motions. sd-Text, a variant of SceneDiff with a simple fusion, showed improved scene consistency but still a modality gap.
- **Proposed Method (ours):** The proposed multi-conditional diffusion model consistently achieved higher contact scores (0.523 vs. 0.426-0.512), better FID (0.987 vs. 1.014-10.067), and R-score (0.326 vs. 0.089-0.313) compared to all baselines. It also demonstrated higher diversity in translation and SMPL parameters.
- **User Study:** The user study (Table 3) confirmed these quantitative findings, with the proposed method achieving the highest total plausible score (4.1), driven by superior scene consistency (3.9) and text consistency (3.1) while maintaining high diversity and smoothness.

**Evaluation on HUMANISE Dataset (Table 4):**
The proposed method also achieved superior performance on the HUMANISE dataset, notably higher contact score (0.881 vs. 0.785-0.846), lower FID (0.388 vs. 0.392-2.525), and higher R-score (0.320 vs. 0.121-0.208), as well as a higher overall plausible score (4.2).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Multi-condition Fusion (Table 5 & 6)

The ablation studies demonstrated the effectiveness of the parallel cross-attention mechanism in the fusion module:
- **Scene-Queried Dual Fusion:** This strategy, which over-emphasizes scene information, resulted in high FID and low R-score, indicating poor text consistency.
- **Text-Queried Dual Fusion:** This approach lacked fine-grained text information for local motion and interaction from text to scene.
- **Triple Fusion:** This method, attempting a more complex concatenation, performed less effectively due to a potential domain gap between language and point features.
- **Parallel Cross Fusion (ours):** The proposed method, through mutual enhancement of text and scene features via parallel cross-attention, demonstrated superior performance in integrating both action/direction knowledge from text and dynamic scene information, leading to more consistent and plausible generation. An additional experiment (sd-Text(w cf) in Table 6) showed that integrating the proposed fusion module into SceneDiff significantly improved its R-score (0.196 vs 0.210 for w/o qf) and p-score (3.7 vs 3.5), validating its efficacy.

#### 4.4. Discussion of Failure Cases

The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Text Consistency:** The model partially aligns with long, fine-grained text descriptions, often focusing on dominant actions rather than intricate details.
- **Motion Complexity:** The model struggles with less common or complex movements (e.g., "arch") due to data distribution imbalances.
- **Scene Plausibility:** Challenges remain with complex terrains (stairs, irregular ground), leading to penetration and floating issues.
- **Dynamic Interaction Precision:** Point-level contact in dynamic interactions often lacks precision.

Attempts at physical refinement using physics-based trackers also faced challenges, attributed to noisy input meshes from the capture system and the limited diversity of training data for complex landforms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LaserHuman and the proposed multi-conditional diffusion model represent a contribution to the field of human motion generation with several potential impacts:

1. **Advancement in Data for Scene-Text-to-Motion:** LaserHuman addresses critical limitations of existing datasets by providing a large-scale collection of real human motions in diverse, unconstrained 3D environments, complete with free-form language descriptions and dynamic scene elements. This dataset is positioned to serve as a benchmark, enabling researchers to develop and evaluate models that generate more authentic, diverse, and physically plausible human motions.
2. **Enhanced Realism and Plausibility:** By utilizing real human motion data and explicitly modeling interactions with complex 3D scenes, the research contributes to generating motions that are physically plausible and semantically consistent with natural language instructions. This is crucial for applications where motion realism is paramount.
3. **Improved Multi-modal Integration:** The proposed multi-conditional diffusion model, with its parallel cross-attention fusion module, demonstrates an effective strategy for integrating textual and 3D scene information. This methodological advancement can inform future work on combining diverse modalities for generative tasks, potentially reducing modality gaps and improving overall generation quality.
4. **Applications in Robotics:** The ability to generate complex human action sequences from language instructions, while respecting 3D scene constraints, holds implications for humanoid robotics. It facilitates a shift towards end-to-end generation paradigms for robot action sequences, potentially reducing error accumulation and computational redundancy compared to traditional modular perception-prediction-planning systems.
5. **Impact on Entertainment and Simulation:** For industries like animation, virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR), and simulation, the work offers a method to automate the creation of detailed and nuanced human movements and interactions, replacing labor-intensive manual 3D modeling. This could lead to more efficient content creation and richer user experiences.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** LaserHuman's multi-modal nature (videos, point clouds, SMPL, text) and varied scene types provide a foundation for exploring several future research directions, including:
 - Development of models that handle finer-grained text details and less common, complex movements.
 - Improved physical simulation integration and robust control policies for physically plausible motions on complex terrains.
 - Decoupling static scene maps from dynamic objects for more sophisticated dynamic scene-conditioned generation.
 - Exploring different input modalities for dynamic environment representation.

In summary, this research provides a resource and a methodological approach that addresses known challenges in language-guided human motion generation within 3D environments, thereby offering potential benefits across various application domains.
