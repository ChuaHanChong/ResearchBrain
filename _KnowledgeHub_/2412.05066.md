# BimArt: A Unified Approach for the Synthesis of 3D Bimanual Interaction with Articulated Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> BimArt synthesizes 3D bimanual hand motions for manipulating articulated objects from object trajectories alone, without a reference grasp or coarse hand path. A diffusion model first predicts distance-based bimanual contact maps from an articulation-aware BPS object representation, then guides a second diffusion model to generate hand motion, refined by contact-aware optimization. It sets new state of the art on ARCTIC and HOI4D in interaction plausibility and diversity.

## Problem

- Prior bimanual hand-object synthesis methods for articulated objects require a known reference or goal grasp, restrictive for non-expert users and limiting generative flexibility.
- Existing methods handle either object articulation or global root translation/rotation, but not both simultaneously, or train separate models per object category.
- Standard BPS or sparse contact-point representations under-sample small articulated parts relative to larger rigid parts and lack fine-grained bimanual contact detail for motion synthesis.

## Method

- ==Normalized part-based Basis Point Set (BPS)== representation that maps basis points separately to each articulated part in a canonicalized, scale-normalized object frame for category-agnostic encoding.
- **Bimanual Contact Generation Model**: a ==transformer-based denoising diffusion model== predicting per-frame distance-based left/right hand contact maps conditioned on object BPS features and global states.
- **Bimanual Hand Motion Model**: a second ==diffusion model== conditioned on contact maps and object features, using ==classifier-free guidance== plus ==contact-map discrepancy guidance==, followed by ==MANO fitting== and ==penetration/acceleration optimization==.

## Results

- On ARCTIC, BimArt reduces hand-vertex penetration to **2.03%** versus **30-67%** for MDM-B, OMOMO-B, and CAMS-B baselines, while raising articulation contact to **85.57%**.
- On HOI4D cross-category setting, BimArt outperforms the unified CAMS-X baseline on contact and articulation scores by a large margin while matching category-specific baselines.
- A **55**-person user study across **40** animation pairs found statistically significant preference (**p < 10^-3**) for BimArt over MDM-B and OMOMO-B on every tested object.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting dense, per-frame contact maps as an intermediate generative target captures richer bimanual interaction patterns than sparse or stage-wise contact targets.
> - Part-aware, scale-normalized object features prevent small articulated components from being under-represented relative to larger rigid parts in a unified cross-category model.
> - Decoupling contact prediction from motion generation, then fusing them via diffusion guidance, avoids over-constraining the model and preserves motion diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on 3D human motion synthesis and hand-object interaction is a rapidly evolving field, driven by advancements in deep learning and the availability of large-scale datasets. Traditionally, this landscape can be broadly categorized into:

- **3D Human Motion Synthesis:** Early works focused on synthesizing full-body motions, often conditioned on text, audio, or scene context. However, these methods typically overlooked the fine-grained details of hand movements and their interaction with objects. Recent advancements, particularly with diffusion models, have enabled high-quality and diverse motion generation.
- **Hand-Object Interaction (HOI):** This sub-field focuses specifically on the challenges of realistic hand and object pose estimation, static grasp synthesis, and dynamic interaction. A significant portion of this research, however, has concentrated on:
 - **Unimanual interactions:** Many methods simplify the problem by considering only one hand interacting with an object.
 - **Rigid objects:** Most approaches assume the objects are rigid, simplifying contact dynamics and affordances.
 - **Specific tasks or inputs:** Some methods require restrictive inputs like known reference grasps, coarse hand trajectories, or are trained in a category-specific manner, limiting their generalizability.

BimArt directly addresses several critical gaps in this landscape. While there has been progress in 3D character animation and even some works tackling hand-object interactions, very few studies simultaneously address **3D bimanual interactions with articulated objects**. Articulated objects (e.g., scissors, laptops, bottles with caps) introduce a new layer of complexity, as interactions must account for their internal degrees of freedom (e.g., rotation of a hinge or a screw joint) in addition to global translation and rotation. Bimanual interaction further compounds the problem, requiring coordination between two hands to achieve a functional manipulation.

Previous attempts for articulated objects often had significant limitations: they might be category-specific, only handle unimanual motions, or separate grasping and articulation into distinct stages, leading to unnatural transitions. Critically, many also rely on strong assumptions, such as a known initial or goal grasp, or require noisy interaction sequences as input for refinement rather than generation from scratch.

BimArt positions itself as a unified generative approach that overcomes these limitations. It seeks to generate diverse and plausible bimanual hand motions for articulated objects *without* relying on reference grasps, coarse hand trajectories, or category-specific training. By doing so, BimArt significantly advances the state-of-the-art by tackling a more realistic and complex scenario of human-object interaction, moving towards more generalizable and user-friendly animation synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of BimArt is to **synthesize realistic, diverse, and contact-aware 3D bimanual hand interactions with articulated objects from a sequence of articulated object states**. This overarching goal can be broken down into several specific motivations and sub-objectives:

1. **Overcome Restrictive Assumptions:** A primary motivation is to move beyond the limitations of prior work. This includes eliminating the need for:
 - A known reference grasp (initial or goal hand pose).
 - A coarse hand trajectory as input.
 - Separate modes for grasping and articulating, aiming for seamless, simultaneous actions.
 - Category-specific training, striving for a unified model that can handle diverse articulated objects.
2. **Handle High-Dimensional Complexity:** Human interaction with articulated objects is inherently complex. It involves coordinating two hands, understanding the object's geometry, its articulated parts, and the functional affordances of these parts, all within a high-dimensional space of potential hand poses and movements. The goal is to "tame" this complexity computationally.
3. **Generate Meaningful Articulations:** The method aims not just to move an object rigidly but also to generate meaningful and plausible articulations (e.g., twisting, tilting, opening) driven by the hand interactions.
4. **Ensure Physical Plausibility:** Generated motions must be physically plausible, meaning hands should maintain realistic contact with the object, avoid penetration, and exhibit smooth, natural movements.
5. **Achieve Diversity:** For applications in animation or virtual environments, it's crucial for the model to generate diverse interaction patterns for the same object trajectory, offering artists and users multiple plausible options.
6. **Develop Unified Object Representation:** To enable category-agnostic synthesis, a key objective is to devise a novel feature representation for articulated objects that consistently encodes geometric and articulation information across a wide array of geometries, including those with unbalanced part sizes.
7. **Leverage Contact Priors:** Motivated by the observation that accurate contact understanding is crucial for realistic finger placement, the method aims to learn and utilize contact maps as an intermediate, guiding feature.

In essence, BimArt seeks to bridge the gap between abstract object trajectories and detailed, realistic bimanual hand animations for complex articulated objects, making the process more automated, generalizable, and less constrained.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BimArt proposes a novel three-stage generative approach, centered around denoising diffusion probabilistic models, to synthesize 3D bimanual hand motions from articulated object trajectories. The input is a sequence of object states, $\xi = \{ \xi_i \}_{i=1}^N$, where each $\xi_i$ includes the object's 6D global state (orientation and translation) and a 1D articulation angle. The output is a corresponding N-frame bimanual hand motion parameterized by MANO.

The core methodology comprises:

1. **Articulation-Aware Canonicalized Feature Representation:**
 - **Object Representation:** To handle diverse object types and articulate structures uniformly, BimArt uses a novel **part-based Basis Point Sets (BPS)** representation. Unlike standard BPS, this approach normalizes the object's scale and computes BPS features for each articulated part *independently*. This ensures that smaller articulated parts are not under-sampled, providing a denser and more detailed geometric description. The BPS features encode the object's shape and articulation state. Additionally, the model incorporates the object's 6D global states (relative translation and global rotation) as lower-dimensional vectors to provide awareness of global movement and gravity direction.
 - **Hand Representation:** Hand motion is encoded in an object-centric canonical frame using a dense set of **surface keypoint positions** (from MANO) and **direction vectors** from these keypoints to their nearest object vertices. This richer representation (compared to skeletal joints) aids in contact reasoning and MANO parameter recovery.
2. **Bimanual Contact Generation Model:**
 - This is the first generative stage. A denoising diffusion probabilistic model (with a transformer-encoder architecture) is trained to generate **distance-based bimanual contact maps** ($C$) for the left and right hands.
 - The model is conditioned on the object's BPS features, global states, and scale.
 - The contact map for each hand and object vertex is defined as the minimum distance from the object vertex to any hand vertex. This "distance-based" map captures nuanced interaction patterns without over-constraining specific hand-object correspondences, allowing for diversity.
 - By generating contact maps first, the method removes the need for a reference grasp, as the contact prior guides subsequent motion synthesis.
3. **Bimanual Hand Motion Model:**
 - This second generative stage uses another denoising diffusion model (also a transformer encoder) to synthesize hand animations ($X = [H|D]$) conditioned on the object features ($O, G, s_o$) and, crucially, the **generated contact maps ($C$)** from the previous stage.
 - Object features and contact maps are encoded into latent embeddings using MLPs and concatenated as conditioning inputs for the diffusion denoiser.
 - **Classifier-free guidance** is employed during training to enhance motion diversity and quality, by randomly replacing contact features with a null token.
 - A key innovation is the **contact map discrepancy guidance** applied at each denoising timestep. This encourages the predicted noisy hand motion to precisely align with the predicted contact map by minimizing the L1 distance between a *derived* contact map (from the current hand estimate) and the *predicted* contact map. This fine-grained guidance ensures accurate contact.
4. **Physically Plausible Hand Motion Post-Processing:**
 - After denoising, an optimization-based post-processing step refines the generated hand motions to ensure physical realism.
 - First, MANO parameters are fitted to the generated sparse hand keypoints.
 - Then, these MANO parameters are optimized using a loss function with three terms:
 - `l_proj`: Encourages hand keypoints (projected along their direction vectors) to lie on the object surface, resolving "floating" artifacts.
 - `l_pen`: Penalizes hand-object penetration, drawing inspiration from existing penetration losses.
 - `l_acc`: Penalizes high acceleration of hand vertices to promote smoother, more natural movements and reduce jitter.

This multi-stage approach, particularly the intermediate contact map generation and the detailed contact guidance, allows BimArt to effectively decompose the complex problem, leading to more controlled, diverse, and physically plausible bimanual interactions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BimArt demonstrates state-of-the-art performance through comprehensive quantitative experiments, qualitative comparisons, perceptual user studies, and detailed ablations.

1. **Quantitative Superiority:**
 - **ARCTIC Dataset:** BimArt outperforms modified baselines (MDM-B, OMOMO-B) significantly across various metrics. It achieves notably lower penetration (2.03% at 1cm threshold vs. 30.4%–66.7% for baselines), higher contact percentage (99.6% vs. 93.6%–98.9%), and higher articulation percentage (85.5% vs. 73.7%–80.0%). It also shows good multi-modality (6.9cm average pairwise distance), indicating diverse outputs, and reasonable acceleration (0.188 cm/s²). While CAMS-B had higher multi-modality, its penetration rates were unacceptably high, indicating poor interaction plausibility.
 - **HOI4D Dataset:** In the single-hand setting, BimArt's cross-category model performs comparably with category-specific baselines (GraspTTA, ManipNet, CAMS) in terms of contact and articulation scores, while maintaining low penetration. Crucially, in a direct cross-category comparison (Unified CAMS-X), BimArt significantly outperforms CAMS-X in articulation and contact consistency, highlighting its superior generalization to diverse object geometries.
2. **Perceptual User Study:** A user study involving 55 human respondents confirmed BimArt's qualitative advantages. Users showed a statistically significant preference (p < 10⁻³) for BimArt's generated motions over those from MDM-B and OMOMO-B across all evaluated objects, deeming them more natural and better aligned with object trajectories.
3. **Qualitative Realism and Diversity:**
 - BimArt generates highly natural and physically realistic motions, even for challenging scenarios like precisely grasping scissors or opening a box with significant articulation, where baselines struggled with accurate contact or rigid constraints.
 - The method successfully generates diverse bimanual sequences for the *same* object trajectory, as showcased by varying finger placements and grasping patterns for objects like laptops, ketchup bottles, and mixers.
4. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Object and Hand Representation:** The proposed **normalized part-based BPS (NP-BPS)** proved superior, yielding the highest multi-modality and contact percentage compared to unnormalized or part-agnostic BPS, demonstrating its effectiveness in detailed object geometry encoding. The chosen hand representation (surface keypoints + direction vectors) also outperformed MANO 6D pose parameters, especially in contact and articulation metrics. Including global states (`G`) was critical for physical plausibility.
 - **Contact Information Utilization:** The ablation results clearly show the benefit of the two-stage contact-guided approach.
 - Contact conditioning (`w C`) significantly improved multi-modality, contact, and articulation compared to generating motions without contact information (`w/o C`).
 - Contact map guidance (`w C + CG`) further reduced the discrepancy between derived and predicted contact maps, indicating better alignment.
 - The optimization-based refinement (`w C + CG + Opt`) drastically reduced penetration (from ~20% to ~2%) and improved motion smoothness (lower acceleration), solidifying physical plausibility.

Overall, the findings strongly validate BimArt's design choices, particularly its novel feature representations, the two-stage generative process with contact guidance, and the final optimization, which collectively contribute to producing high-quality, diverse, and plausible bimanual interactions with articulated objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BimArt represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D human motion synthesis, particularly for complex human-object interactions. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Unified and Generalizable Approach:** By eliminating restrictive assumptions like known reference grasps, coarse hand trajectories, and category-specific training, BimArt offers a truly unified approach. This makes it far more practical and scalable for real-world applications where a user might want to animate interaction with arbitrary articulated objects without extensive pre-computation or specialized models.
2. **High-Quality and Diverse Bimanual Interactions:** The method generates physically plausible, contact-aware, and temporally consistent bimanual motions that surpass the state-of-the-art in terms of motion quality and diversity. This is crucial for creating realistic digital content, improving user experiences in virtual environments, and developing more natural human-robot interaction.
3. **Novel Feature Representation:** The introduction of the articulation-aware canonicalized part-based BPS is a notable innovation. It effectively encodes the complex geometry and articulation states of diverse objects, addressing a long-standing challenge in developing generalizable interaction models for articulated objects. This representation ensures consistent spatial encoding regardless of part size discrepancies.
4. **Effective Contact Prior and Guidance:** The two-stage approach of generating distance-based contact maps as an intermediate prior, followed by explicit contact map discrepancy guidance during motion synthesis, is a powerful mechanism. It allows the model to learn and exploit rich bimanual interaction patterns, leading to precise finger placements and maintaining consistent contact, which is often a stumbling block for generative models.
5. **Robustness and Relaxed Inputs:** By requiring only an articulated object trajectory as input, BimArt simplifies the animation pipeline. This makes it more accessible for 3D artists, animators, and non-expert users who can specify desired object movements and have realistic hand interactions automatically generated.
6. **Potential Applications:**
 - **Computer Graphics and Animation:** Animators can rapidly prototype and generate diverse, realistic hand animations for articulated objects in films, games, and virtual experiences, significantly reducing manual effort.
 - **Robotics:** The learned contact priors and motion synthesis capabilities could inform the development of more dexterous robotic manipulation policies, particularly for bimanual tasks involving articulated objects.
 - **Virtual Reality/Augmented Reality (VR/AR):** Enhancing the realism of virtual hand interactions with digital objects, improving immersion and user experience.
 - **Ergonomics and Product Design:** Simulating human interaction with prototype products to assess usability and identify design flaws before physical prototypes are made.

While the current method is restricted to object categories seen in training datasets, the authors acknowledge this limitation and propose leveraging large language models for zero-shot generalization to novel objects. Incorporating faster diffusion sampling techniques could also improve its adoption in time-sensitive artistic creation pipelines. Overall, BimArt represents a substantial step towards automating the synthesis of complex human-object interactions, paving the way for more realistic, diverse, and user-friendly animation tools and interactive systems.
