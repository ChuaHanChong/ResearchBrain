# Benchmarking the Sim-to-Real Gap in Cloth Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A benchmark quantifying the sim-to-real gap in cloth manipulation. Bi-manual Franka robots run a dynamic fling and a quasi-static drag on three household rags, recording segmented point clouds. Four deformable simulators (MuJoCo, Bullet, Flex, SOFA) are parameter-fit via Bayesian Optimisation and CMA-ES, then scored by unidirectional Chamfer and Hausdorff distance, stability, and step time. MuJoCo tracks dynamic motion best and is recommended.

## Problem

- Cloth manipulation research relies heavily on simulators, yet few works quantify how far those deformable-object engines diverge from real-world fabric behaviour.
- Prior reality-gap benchmarks target rigid-body manipulation or contact impacts; none measure the gap for dynamic, high-acceleration deformable-object tasks against real data.
- Existing deformable work either compares algorithms inside one simulator or reports transfer success, leaving simulator fidelity itself unmeasured and zero-shot transfer unpredictable.

## Method

- A real-world dataset of RGB-D and segmented point clouds from two Franka Panda arms executing ==quintic-polynomial via-point trajectories==: a dynamic fling and a quasi-static lower-and-drag, on three rags.
- A simulator-agnostic evaluation framework requiring ==Cartesian pinch-grasp control==, mesh vertex readout, and adjustable step frequency, with coordinate transforms mapping simulated meshes into observation space.
- Metrics: unidirectional ==Chamfer== and ==Hausdorff distance== under an L1 norm (robust to outliers, no correspondences needed), a moving-average stability score, and per-step computational time.

## Results

- On the dynamic task MuJoCo and SOFA reach roughly **0.06-0.08** Chamfer distance versus **0.12-0.17** for Bullet and Flex, about a **two-fold** gap; Hausdorff for Flex reaches **0.28**.
- All four engines perform comparably on the quasi-static task, Chamfer roughly **0.07-0.10** across rags, so contact-dominated motion is far easier to simulate than inertia-dominated motion.
- Frequency sweeps at **10/100/1000 Hz** show Bullet and MuJoCo destabilise at low rates while step times of **milliseconds** at **100 Hz** rule out hardware-in-the-loop control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simulator error on dynamic deformable motion can exceed the measured difference between two physically distinct fabrics, so benchmark numbers need an inter-material reference baseline.
> - Fidelity is frequency-coupled: parameters fitted at one simulation rate degrade at another, so system identification must be redone per timestep rather than transferred.
> - Lowest reality gap does not imply best research tool; missing robot models, camera control, or CPU support can outweigh a modest accuracy advantage.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation of deformable objects, such as garments, presents significant challenges due to their infinite degrees of freedom (DoFs) and complex dynamics. Representing their state and predicting their behavior in real-world environments is difficult. To circumvent these complexities and safety concerns associated with real-world robot deployments, researchers frequently leverage physics-based simulation engines for developing and evaluating robotic controllers.

While simulation offers a controlled environment for extensive experimentation, it operates on approximations of real-world physics, leading to a "reality gap" between simulated and actual outcomes. This gap can hinder the direct transfer of learned policies from simulation to real robots (sim-to-real transfer), particularly for tasks involving dynamic object interactions. Previous research has explored benchmarking this reality gap for rigid object manipulation and specific deformable objects like cables, or for quasi-static cloth manipulation. However, a comprehensive evaluation of the reality gap specifically for *dynamic* cloth manipulation tasks has been largely unaddressed. Existing approaches often rely on techniques like domain randomization to mitigate the sim-to-real gap, but these do not quantify the fidelity of the underlying simulators themselves.

This paper addresses this gap by providing a benchmark dataset and a structured evaluation of popular deformable object simulators in the context of both dynamic and quasi-static cloth manipulation. It seeks to quantify how accurately these simulators model fabric behavior compared to real-world data, thereby informing the selection and parameter tuning of simulation engines for future research in robotic cloth manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the widespread reliance on simulation for developing robotic cloth manipulation capabilities and the recognized yet unquantified challenge of the sim-to-real gap, especially for dynamic interactions. Cloth manipulation is pertinent to various applications, including healthcare, domestic chores, and the textile industry. However, the inherent complexity of deformable objects, characterized by their high DoFs and non-linear dynamics, makes real-world deployment challenging. Simulation platforms offer an alternative by allowing researchers to train and test controllers in a safe, repeatable, and scalable environment.

However, the efficacy of sim-to-real transfer is directly dependent on the fidelity of the simulator. When simulators inaccurately model physical dynamics, particularly under high-acceleration dynamic movements or complex contact conditions, the accumulated errors can lead to a significant reality gap, resulting in controllers that perform poorly when transferred to physical robots without further adaptation. Prior studies had not thoroughly evaluated this gap for dynamic cloth manipulation tasks, leaving a critical blind spot for researchers.

The key objectives of this work were:
1. **To develop a benchmark dataset for cloth manipulation:** This dataset includes real-world point clouds and RGB-D images of cloths manipulated through both dynamic and quasi-static trajectories. It uses three different fabrics with varying material properties from a publicly available dataset.
2. **To quantify the reality gap of popular physics engines:** The research aimed to evaluate the discrepancy between simulated cloth behavior and real-world data for four widely used deformable object simulators: MuJoCo, Bullet, Flex, and SOFA.
3. **To assess simulator performance across different manipulation tasks:** Specifically, the benchmark was designed to evaluate the simulators' capabilities in handling dynamic in-air manipulation (fling motion) and quasi-static manipulation involving sustained contact with a rigid surface.
4. **To analyze computational characteristics of the simulators:** This included evaluating the computational time required for simulation steps and the stability of each simulator across different frequencies.
5. **To provide insights into simulator strengths and weaknesses:** Based on the quantitative and qualitative evaluations, the paper aimed to discuss the benefits and drawbacks of each benchmarked simulator, informing future research and development in robotic cloth manipulation.
6. **To facilitate future benchmarking efforts:** By providing an open-source dataset and code, the work intended to enable other researchers to evaluate new or improved simulation engines against a standardized benchmark.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed benchmark encompasses a real-world dataset, specifically designed manipulation tasks, and a set of performance metrics.

**A. Task Description**
The benchmark utilizes a fabric placement manipulation task executed by a bi-manual robot system. This task involves two distinct pre-defined trajectories:
- **Dynamic Motion Trajectory:** This trajectory simulates a "fling" motion, characterized by high accelerations. It is designed to evaluate the fabric's in-air dynamics, where inertia forces and rapid deformations are dominant. The trajectory involves movement along the Y and Z axes and rotation around the roll angle ($\phi$).
- **Quasi-Static Motion Trajectory:** This trajectory involves slowly lowering the fabric into contact with a rigid surface and then dragging it. Its purpose is to assess the simulators' ability to model contact dynamics, including friction and sustained interaction with a rigid body, in the absence of rapid dynamic forces. This motion primarily involves movements along the Y and Z axes.

Both trajectories are defined using quintic polynomials, ensuring smooth transitions in position, velocity, and acceleration. The task assumes a successful pinch grasp of two corners of a rectangular cloth, simplifying the problem by isolating the cloth dynamics from the complexities of grasping mechanisms.

**B. Real-World Dataset Collection**
The dataset was collected using three types of cloths from a public household dataset: a towel rag, a linen rag, and a checkered rag. Each cloth measured 50x70 cm and possessed distinct weight and elasticity properties. Two Franka Emika Panda robots executed the pre-defined quintic trajectories.
A Microsoft Azure Kinect RGB-D sensor captured RGB-D images at 30 frames per second (fps). The raw data was processed to obtain dense point clouds (P) of the cloth at each time step. This pre-processing involved:
- **Segmentation:** MiVOS was used to segment the RGB images, allowing for interactive refinement and temporally coherent results to filter out non-garment points.
- **Outlier Removal:** An statistical outlier removal process was applied to discard points that were significantly distant from their neighbors.
- **Coordinate Transformation:** Simulated meshes were transformed into the observation space using defined matrices to enable direct comparison with real-world point clouds.
For the dynamic task, the benchmarking focused on the "cloth dynamics phase" (before contact), which typically concluded around 3 seconds into the trajectory. For the quasi-static task, the entire trajectory, including the contact phase, was evaluated.

**C. Simulation Engine Setup**
A simulation-agnostic framework, made open-source, was developed to benchmark the selected physics engines. The framework requires simulators to support rigid and deformable objects, control over cloth points, access to mesh point positions, and adjustable simulation frequencies. The simulated scene mirrored the real-world setup with a rigid table, fabric, and two dummy manipulators performing pinch grasps. The manipulators received Cartesian coordinate trajectories as input, independent of the simulator's internal frequency.
Four popular simulation engines were evaluated: MuJoCo, Bullet, Flex, and SOFA. Their specific features, such as visual feedback capabilities, robot integration, grasp types (point/line), numerical integrators, CPU/GPU acceleration, and deformable object dynamics models (mass-spring, Position Based Dynamics (PBD), Finite Element Method (FEM)), were detailed.
Crucially, the cloth parameters within each simulator (e.g., stiffness, damping, friction) were optimized to best fit the real-world behavior for each fabric and trajectory type. This optimization was performed using Bayesian Optimization (BO) and Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) over 500 sweeps, minimizing the Chamfer Distance. Default numerical integrators were used for Bullet and Flex, semi-implicit Euler for MuJoCo, and implicit conjugate gradient for SOFA.

**D. Performance Metrics**
The benchmark employed metrics to quantify the reality gap, simulator stability, and computational cost:
- **Reality Gap:**
 - **Chamfer Distance (CD):** Measures the average closest point distance between the simulated mesh vertices (V) and the real point cloud (P). Unidirectional CD (CD(V, P) = avg(min(||v-p||1))) was used to account for varying mesh resolutions and occlusions.
 - **Hausdorff Distance (HD):** Measures the maximum closest point distance between V and P. Unidirectional HD (HD(V, P) = max(min(||v-p||1))) captures the worst-case error.
 - The L1 norm (Manhattan distance) was chosen over the squared Euclidean distance for both CD and HD, as it empirically provided more representative and outlier-robust error values.
 - CD_d and HD_d represent metrics for the dynamic (pre-contact) phase, while CD_q and HD_q refer to the quasi-static (entire) trajectory.
- **Simulator Stability (L_s):** Calculated as the average difference between filtered and non-filtered simulated vertices over time, where the filtered vertices were obtained using a moving average of three consecutive time steps.
- **Computational Time:** The average time taken to perform a single simulation step was measured to assess feasibility for real-time hardware-in-the-loop applications.
Reference error metrics between the real cloths themselves were also provided to contextualize the magnitude of the measured reality gap.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the performance of MuJoCo, Bullet, Flex, and SOFA across the two manipulation tasks using three different fabrics, with simulator parameters optimized for each case.

**A. Sim-to-Real Gap Evaluation**
- **Quasi-static Task:** For the quasi-static manipulation task, all evaluated physics engines exhibited comparable performance. The Chamfer Distance (CD_q) and Hausdorff Distance (HD_q) values were generally in the same order of magnitude across all simulators and fabrics. This suggests that current simulators are relatively effective at modeling cloth behavior when motions are slow and involve sustained contact.
- **Dynamic Task:** A more significant disparity in performance was observed for the dynamic manipulation task. MuJoCo and SOFA generally achieved lower Chamfer Distance (CD_d) and Hausdorff Distance (HD_d) values, approximately two times lower than those of Bullet and Flex. This indicates that MuJoCo and SOFA provided a closer approximation to real-world cloth dynamics under high-acceleration movements.
- **Overall Error Magnitude:** The reported metric values for the reality gap were often comparable to, or greater than, the inherent differences measured between the real-world point clouds of distinct fabric types (e.g., checkered vs. linen rags).
- **Qualitative Assessment:** Visualizations confirmed the quantitative results. MuJoCo demonstrated the ability to closely follow the dynamic trajectory of the cloth. While Bullet showed low error at intermediate steps, its identified parameters did not consistently produce stable simulations, leading to inconsistent results at later stages of the dynamic task. For the quasi-static trajectory, all simulators generally matched the real cloth behavior well, with Flex exhibiting the lowest error at the final time step.

**B. Simulator Stability, Computational Time, and Reality Gap**
- **Stability:** Bullet and MuJoCo exhibited instability when operating at lower simulation frequencies (e.g., 10 Hz), leading to greater deviations from the averaged cloth state (higher L_s values). Conversely, Flex and SOFA demonstrated more consistent stability across the tested frequencies (10, 100, and 1000 Hz).
- **Computational Time:** Unstable simulator configurations (such as Bullet and MuJoCo at low frequencies) resulted in a drastic increase in the time taken per simulation step. At a frequency of 100 Hz, the simulation step times for all simulators were in the order of milliseconds, suggesting that real-time dynamic manipulation with hardware-in-the-loop control would be unfeasible given the current performance. While Flex and SOFA had lower step times at 10 Hz and were more stable, their associated reality gap (CD_d/CD_q) remained considerable.
- **Frequency Impact on Reality Gap:** Increasing the simulation frequency significantly improved MuJoCo's performance in terms of stability and reduced the reality gap for both dynamic and quasi-static tasks. However, for Flex and SOFA, increasing the frequency did not consistently reduce the distance metrics, and in some cases, detrimental performance was observed. This indicated that the physics engines, particularly Flex and SOFA, are sensitive to cloth parameters and require specific fine-tuning for each simulation frequency to achieve optimal fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a comprehensive benchmark that quantifies the sim-to-real gap in cloth manipulation, a previously underexplored area, particularly for dynamic tasks. The findings have several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Informing Simulator Selection:** The detailed comparative analysis of MuJoCo, Bullet, Flex, and SOFA offers practical guidance for researchers. While SOFA demonstrated the lowest errors in some tasks, its lack of integrated robotic models limits its utility for learning visual feedback controllers for robots. MuJoCo is recommended for learning cloth manipulation tasks due to its relatively low errors in both dynamic and quasi-static tasks, its capability to integrate robotic models, and its support for both CPU and GPU acceleration.
- **Highlighting the Need for Sim-to-Real Transfer Techniques:** The study's observation that a significant reality gap persists, especially in dynamic cloth manipulation, underscores the continued necessity of sim-to-real transfer techniques. Approaches such as domain randomization, real-to-sim transfer, or integrating real-time visual feedback remain crucial for successfully deploying controllers learned in simulation onto physical robots.
- **Facilitating Future Research and Development:** The provision of an open-source benchmark dataset and code base represents a significant contribution to the robotics community. It enables other researchers to:
 - Objectively evaluate the reality gap of new or improved simulation engines.
 - Fine-tune simulator parameters more effectively for specific fabrics and manipulation tasks.
 - Develop and validate control policies with a clearer understanding of the underlying simulation fidelity.
 - Benchmark different deformable object models or integration schemes within existing simulators.
- **Driving Simulator Improvement:** By clearly delineating the performance differences and limitations of current simulators, this work can motivate the development of next-generation physics engines with improved accuracy and stability for deformable object dynamics, particularly under high-acceleration scenarios and complex contact interactions. Such advancements could lead to robotic controllers that translate more faithfully from simulation to the real world, reducing deployment costs and accelerating research progress.
- **Addressing a Complex Robotics Challenge:** The focus on dynamic cloth manipulation contributes directly to a challenging area of robotics, offering foundational data and tools necessary for advancing autonomous manipulation of soft, non-rigid objects in various real-world applications.

The identified limitations, such as the need to evaluate stiff cloths and other garment types, also provide clear directions for future extensions of this benchmark.
