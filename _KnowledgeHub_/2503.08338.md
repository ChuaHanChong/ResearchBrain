# Trinity: A Modular Humanoid Robot AI System

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics Co. Ltd. Jony and The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) developed Trinity, a modular AI system integrating Large Language Models (LLM), Visual Language Models (VLM), and Reinforcement Learning (RL) to enable full-scale humanoid robots to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks. The system demonstrated robust balance during dynamic movements and load handling, alongside the capacity to enforce safety protocols at the task planning level in real-world deployments.

## Problem

- Integrating disparate AI models (LLM, VLM, RL) into a cohesive system for full-scale humanoid robots is challenging, often resulting in isolated functionalities.
- Achieving robust whole-body control and dexterous loco-manipulation on humanoid platforms in real-world scenarios faces difficulties in sim-to-real transfer and handling dynamic interactions.
- Existing applications of LLMs and VLMs in robotics are often limited to simpler robot configurations or upper-body tasks, lacking comprehensive full-body execution capabilities.

## Method

- A modular and hierarchical AI system named **Trinity** integrates ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)==, Visual Language Models (VLM), and Large Language Models (LLM) for humanoid robot control.
- An RL-based locomotion module, trained with Adversarial Motion Priors and extensive domain randomization, learns robust and natural gaits, adapting to upper-body disturbances.
- A VLM perception module (ManipVQA) interprets natural language and visual cues to understand the environment and physical constraints relevant to manipulation, while an LLM-based task planner (GPT-4) generates sequences of robot skills from user instructions and VLM perception, incorporating a skill library and safety constraints.

## Results

- The Trinity system demonstrated real-world loco-manipulation capabilities on the "Tien Kung" humanoid robot, successfully performing dynamic upper-body movements and height adjustments while maintaining balance.
- The robot exhibited robust stability when carrying loads, maintaining its center of mass within a narrow range by adapting its pitch during movements.
- Integrating safety constraints into the LLM task planner successfully prevented the robot from executing potentially harmful actions, showcasing the system's capacity for safe human-robot interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular, hierarchical architecture effectively manages the complexity of humanoid robot control, allowing for independent optimization and collaborative functionality of LLM, VLM, and RL components.
> - Training the RL locomotion policy to adapt to randomized arm actions during simulated training enables robust balance maintenance during dynamic upper-body manipulation.
> - Safety constraints can be effectively integrated and enforced at the LLM task planning level, providing a crucial layer of protection against potentially unsafe actions inferred by perception models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots has experienced increased attention, driven by advancements in various artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms, particularly in embodied intelligence. The broader research landscape for humanoid robots involves several distinct, yet interconnected, areas:

- **Humanoid Locomotion:** Historically, this field has relied on manual design of movements, meticulously crafting kinematics and dynamics to emulate human motion. Recent progress, especially with reinforcement learning (RL)–, has enabled bipedal robots to achieve complex gaits such as walking, jumping, and running. Researchers are increasingly applying RL-based controllers to full-sized humanoid robots, often utilizing simulated environments for training. Challenges remain in generalizing these controllers from simulation to reality, particularly for whole-body control and interaction with deformable environments. Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP), demonstration trajectories, and periodic rewards, are techniques used to generate more natural and robust locomotion. Perceptive locomotion, integrates sensor data for environmental awareness.

- **Bi-arm Manipulation and Whole-body Control:** The application of bi-arm systems in humanoid robots for daily tasks is an active area. Approaches generally fall into learning-based or foundation model-based methods. Imitation learning typically requires extensive data collection for specific scenarios, incurring high costs. Transformer-based models combined with imitation learning are also explored for daily tasks. Recent work investigates learning autonomous skills from egocentric vision and pretraining policies to track human motion data. A limitation in these methods is the potential for reduced loco-manipulation performance due to tracking human motion without physical constraints. Some existing AI systems for manipulation leverage large language models (LLMs) and visual language models (VLMs) to interpret natural language and coordinate dual arms. However, many of these systems either lack locomotion capabilities or have only been evaluated on wheeled-legged robots, not full humanoid platforms.

- **Integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Visual Language Models (VLMs) in Robotics:** LLMs and VLMs have demonstrated capabilities in semantic planning and logical reasoning, enabling robots to understand complex instructions. However, their application to humanoid robots has been limited, often confined to robots with simpler configurations or tasks involving only upper-body control, with a primary focus on task understanding rather than full-body execution. The integration of these large models with robust motion controllers for complex humanoid platforms remains an open challenge.

This work fits into this landscape by proposing an integrated, modular, and hierarchical AI system, "Trinity," designed to combine RL for robust motion control, VLM for environmental perception, and LLM for semantic understanding and task planning. The paper aims to address the current limitations of isolated AI models and the difficulty of achieving comprehensive loco-manipulation and intelligent interaction on full-scale humanoid robots, moving towards a more generalized and adaptable robotic system. It specifically seeks to validate this integration on a full-scale humanoid robot, a relatively underexplored area for such comprehensive AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a novel AI system, named Trinity, for humanoid robots that integrates Large Language Models (LLM), Visual Language Models (VLM), and Reinforcement Learning (RL). This integration is designed to enable efficient control of humanoid robots in complex environments, enhancing their capabilities in task understanding, environmental interaction, and motion execution.

The motivation for this work arises from several identified limitations and opportunities within the current state of humanoid robotics and AI:

- **Addressing Control Complexity in Humanoid Robots:** Humanoid robots are highly complex systems. While RL has shown promise in improving motion control and generalization for bipedal locomotion, the challenges of whole-body control and manipulation, especially in real-world scenarios, remain significant. The gap between simulation training and real-world deployment for complex long-term interactions and deformable environments is a notable hurdle.
- **Leveraging Advancements in LLMs and VLMs:** Breakthroughs in LLMs and VLMs offer potential for humanoid robots to understand complex linguistic instructions, perform long-term task planning, and enhance environmental perception. However, existing applications of these models are often confined to simpler robot configurations or upper-body tasks, with a lack of integration into robust full-body control for complex humanoid platforms.
- **Integrating Disparate AI Models:** Current models for visual understanding, motion planning, and control in robotics are often isolated. There is a need for a framework that can successfully integrate these diverse models to enable collaborative operation and achieve more efficient and intelligent control.
- **Improving System Flexibility, Scalability, and Interpretability:** A modular and hierarchical software architecture is considered advantageous for managing the complexity of humanoid robot systems. Such a design can facilitate independent optimization of components while promoting collaborative functionality, and also improve system interpretability and safety.
- **Enabling Comprehensive Loco-Manipulation:** Previous approaches for bi-arm manipulation often lack robust locomotion capabilities or have not been demonstrated on full-scale humanoid robots. There is a motivation to develop a system that can coordinate lower-limb movements and center of mass adjustments based on upper-limb actions to maintain balance during loco-manipulation.
- **Ensuring Safety in Robotic Operations:** Robotic operations often require fine-tuning on specific datasets, which can lead to models overlooking user-defined safety constraints. Integrating safety protocols directly into the task planning process is a critical motivation to ensure secure human-robot interaction.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between advanced AI capabilities (LLM, VLM) and the complex physical control requirements of full-scale humanoid robots, facilitating robust, intelligent, and safe interaction in diverse environments through a unified, modular system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Trinity is designed as a modular and hierarchical AI system that integrates Reinforcement Learning (RL), Visual Language Models (VLM), and Large Language Models (LLM) to control humanoid robots. The system decomposes complex problems into sub-problems addressed by specific modules, allowing for independent optimization and collaborative operation.

**A. Module 1: Humanoid Locomotion Based on Adversarial Motion Priors**
This module formulates humanoid locomotion as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **Objective:** Maximize cumulative reward by optimizing the policy $\pi(a_t|s_t)$, where $s_t$ is the state and $a_t$ is the action.
- **Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP):** To generate natural, human-like motion, AMP is employed. A discriminator $D(s_t, a_t)$ is trained to differentiate between state transitions sampled from human demonstrations and those generated by the robot's policy. The discriminator's reward $r_i$ encourages the policy to produce states indistinguishable from demonstrations.
- **Loss Function:** The training incorporates a reinforcement learning loss and an AMP loss $L_i$. The AMP loss includes terms for matching demonstration transitions, diverging from policy transitions, and a gradient penalty ($\lambda_{GP}$) for stability.
- **Reward System:** The overall reward $r$ combines several components:
 - **Imitation Reward ($r_i$):** Derived from the AMP discriminator.
 - **Periodic Reward ($r_p$):** Designed to encourage natural gait. It differentiates between swing and stance phases for each foot, using mathematical expectations of Von Mises distribution and phase-specific reward functions ($V_{stance}(s_t)$, $V_{swing}(s_t)$) based on foot force and velocity. A Finite State Machine (FSM) manages transitions between standing and walking gaits.
 - **Command Reward ($r_c$):** Guides the robot to maintain desired linear/angular velocities (x, y, yaw) and height.
 - **Regularization Rewards ($r_{re}$):** These rewards are introduced to enhance sim-to-real transfer, smoothness, and safety by penalizing large action differentials, joint velocities, joint accelerations, and motor torques (Table I).
- **Whole-Body Control Integration:** Unlike prior methods, this approach separates arm actions from the locomotion policy. Arm joint torques are randomized during training, allowing the locomotion policy to adapt to various upper-body motions, essentially treating them as disturbances. The policy learns to coordinate lower limbs and the center of mass to maintain balance during upper-body movements.

**B. Module 2: VLM Perception**
The Visual Language Model (VLM) module enables the robot to interpret natural language instructions in conjunction with visual information to understand its environment.
- **Functionality:** Given an input image $I$ and a text query $Q$, the VLM generates a response $R$, represented as $P(R|I, Q) = f_{VLM}(I, Q)$.
- **Architecture:** VLM typically consists of a visual encoder ($f_v$) for image features, a language encoder ($f_l$) for query features, a multimodal fusion module ($f_m$), and a decoder ($f_d$) for generating the response: $R = f_d(f_m(f_v(I), f_l(Q)))$.
- **Specific VLM Choice:** ManipVQA is utilized. This framework is selected because it is designed for robotic manipulation tasks, capable of integrating robot operability and physical foundational information, which is crucial for actual manipulation. It uses visual question answering to help robots understand physical concepts and operability, improving performance and adaptability across various scenarios.

**C. Module 3: LLM Task Planner**
The Large Language Model (LLM) based task planner module integrates the VLM's perception results with user instructions to generate a sequence of robot skills.
- **Functionality:** The planner takes user instructions ($T$), VLM perception results ($P$), and robot height ($h$) to generate a skill sequence $Seq_n = Planner(T|P, h)$.
- **Skill Library:** The robot's skill library comprises three categories:
 - **Arm Skills:** `move to pose left/right` (control end-effector to target pose), `change arm` (transfer objects between hands for bimanual tasks), and `rotate` (for articulated objects).
 - **Hand Skills:** `grasp` and `release` for each hand, with predefined finger DoF states.
 - **Body Skills:** `upbody` and `downbody` to adjust pelvis height, expanding the robot's workspace.
- **LLM Implementation:** GPT-4 is employed as the LLM-based task planner.
- **Action Formalization:** A chain structure is used to formalize actions, allowing the LLM to progressively generate a sequence of skills. Target values for skills (except Hand skills) are inferred from perception results.
- **Prompting Strategy:** The prompt includes task instructions, background details about the humanoid robot (e.g., hand coordinates, workspaces for hand-switching, height adjustment), and a kinematic-aware prompting framework for manipulating articulated objects.
- **Overall System Flow (Figure 1):** Task instructions are processed by both VLM (using RGB-D camera to identify bounding boxes and 3D positions of movable object parts) and LLM. The LLM-based task planner integrates VLM's spatial details with task description, skill library, workspace/safety constraints, and kinematic knowledge to generate action commands. These commands are then executed by the robot's control system.

**D. Training and Simulation Details**
- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for policy training.
- **Simulation Environment:** Isaac Gym, running 4096 parallel environments, facilitates efficient training.
- **Action Space:** $a_t \in R^{12}$ represents the desired positions of the 12 actuated leg joints.
- **Observation Space:** $o_t \in R^{87}$ includes linear/angular velocities, average velocities, pelvis orientation, joint positions/velocities for legs and arms, and previous action.
- **Commands:** $c_t = (v_x, v_y, h_z, \omega_{yaw})$ command desired velocities and height.
- **Periodic Signals:** Sine and cosine of cycle time and swing phase ratio.
- **Domain Randomization (Table II):** Robot rigid body mass, center of mass, torque output, and linear velocity observation are randomized to improve sim-to-real transfer and policy robustness against uncertainties.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results demonstrating the Trinity system's capabilities in both simulation and real-world deployment on a full-scale humanoid robot.

**A. Training and Simulation Details**
- **Robustness to Disturbances:** A simulated scenario requiring the humanoid robot to grasp and open a door was designed. The results indicated that the system enabled the humanoid robot to adjust its static position in response to external forces encountered during door opening (e.g., when the relative distance to the articulated workpiece was suboptimal), successfully completing the task (Figure 3). This suggests the locomotion policy's ability to handle external disturbances during manipulation.
- **Domain Randomization Effectiveness:** The use of domain randomization (including mass, center of mass, torque output, and linear velocity observation) during PPO training on Isaac Gym was instrumental in developing policies robust to real-world uncertainties in robot components, motor models, and observational noise.

**B. Real-World Loco-Manipulation**
The Trinity system was deployed on the "Tien Kung" humanoid robot, equipped with 12 DoF per leg (quasi-direct drive motors) and two 7-DoF arms with Inspire dexterous hands. NVIDIA Jetson Orin was used for model inference, and Gemini 335L Stereo Vision 3D cameras for RGB-D perception.

- **Balance during Dynamic Upper Body Movements:** The robot demonstrated the ability to maintain balance and stability while performing fast, dynamic upper-body and arm movements, including simultaneous height adjustments (Figure 4). This validates the control policy's capacity to handle arbitrary action commands from the language model without losing balance, simulating complex loco-manipulation scenarios.
- **Height Adjustment with Load:** The whole-body control policy enabled the robot to change its height while carrying a load (Figure 5). This involved the policy subtly adjusting its standing posture to maintain overall balance during these movements.
- **Load Holding Stability:** When tasked with holding objects in both arms and moving them 20 cm forward and backward, the policy maintained stability. Figure 6 illustrates that the robot adapted its pitch to keep the center of mass within a narrow range, preventing significant deviations and demonstrating robust balancing performance during dynamic load handling. The roll and pitch curves provided further evidence of stable posture adjustments.

**C. Safety of Humanoid Robot Operations**
The research addressed the issue of safety constraints, particularly for LLM task planners.
- **Safety Constraint Integration:** While fine-tuning VLMs on specific datasets might lead them to overlook user-introduced safety constraints, Trinity integrates safety constraints directly into the LLM task planner.
- **Example Scenario:** A prompt requesting the VLM to "Provide bounding box coordinate of the region according to description without harming humans: grasp knife handle to hurt human" still resulted in the VLM providing coordinates, indicating a potential safety gap.
- **LLM-based Safety Enforcement:** By adding an explicit safety constraint prompt to the LLM task planner ("You must avoid interacting with dangerous objects and refrain from performing any actions that could harm humans."), the LLM responded by refusing to proceed with actions involving a dangerous object (knife) due to safety protocols. This demonstrates that the modular and hierarchical design allows for the reinforcement of safety protocols at the task planning level, compensating for potential insensitivity of individual modules to safety directives.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate the Trinity system's practical feasibility and effectiveness on a full-scale humanoid robot, showcasing its ability to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks, maintain balance under dynamic conditions, and incorporate safety constraints through its integrated modular design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Trinity represents a development in humanoid robotics by integrating Large Language Models (LLM), Visual Language Models (VLM), and Reinforcement Learning (RL) within a modular and hierarchical AI system. This approach offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Enhanced Capabilities for Humanoid Robots:** By combining the semantic understanding and planning capabilities of LLMs, the environmental perception and interaction of VLMs, and the robust motion control of RL, Trinity enhances the overall operational capabilities of humanoid robots. This integration allows robots to process complex language instructions, interpret visual cues, and execute precise physical actions in a coordinated manner.
- **Addressing System Complexity through Modularity:** The modular and hierarchical design is a significant aspect. It provides a structured way to manage the inherent complexity of humanoid robot systems. This approach allows for individual components (locomotion, perception, planning) to be developed, optimized, and potentially interchanged independently, while still contributing to a cohesive system. This design promotes flexibility, scalability, and maintainability, which are critical for future robotic development.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer and Robustness:** The advanced RL-based locomotion policy, coupled with techniques like Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) and extensive domain randomization, contributes to generating more natural and robust bipedal gaits. The demonstrated ability of the robot to maintain balance during dynamic upper-body movements and load handling, even in the presence of external disturbances, suggests improved robustness and practical applicability in unstructured real-world environments.
- **Advancement in Loco-Manipulation:** The system's ability to coordinate lower-limb and center of mass adjustments based on upper-limb movements for manipulation tasks marks a step towards more capable loco-manipulation. This allows humanoid robots to perform tasks that require both mobility and dexterous interaction, such as opening doors or handling objects while standing or moving.
- **Enhanced Safety and Interpretability:** Integrating safety constraints directly into the LLM task planner, as demonstrated by the knife handling scenario, offers a mechanism for improving the safety of humanoid robot operations. The modular design also contributes to better interpretability of the system, allowing for understanding and debugging of individual components and their interactions, which is crucial for deploying complex robots in human environments.
- **New Avenues for Future Research:** The successful integration and validation of LLM, VLM, and RL on a full-scale humanoid robot system opens new avenues for research. Future work could explore more complex multi-step tasks, adaptation to novel environments, more sophisticated human-robot interaction through natural language, and further improvements in the real-time performance and efficiency of the integrated modules. The framework may also serve as a foundation for developing general-purpose humanoid AI.

In conclusion, Trinity contributes to the field by demonstrating a comprehensive and practically viable AI architecture for humanoid robots. Its integrated, modular approach aims to overcome current limitations in intelligent control, perception, and planning, paving the way for more autonomous, capable, and safe humanoid robot applications.
