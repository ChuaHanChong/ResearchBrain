# Self-Supervised Learning from Structural Invariance

> [!summary] Summary
> The AdaSSL framework facilitates self-supervised learning by explicitly modeling complex conditional distributions, including heteroscedastic and multimodal noise, inherent in naturally paired data. It yields representations that are robust, disentangled, and generalize effectively across synthetic and real-world benchmarks, improving performance in causal representation learning and stochastic world modeling.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods primarily use handcrafted augmentations that often fail to capture natural real-world variations, limiting representation generalizability.
- Learning from naturally paired data introduces 'one-to-many mapping' or conditional uncertainty, where transformations between pairs are complex, non-deterministic, heteroscedastic, or multimodal.
- Current SSL objectives struggle to model these complex conditional distributions, implicitly assuming simpler noise structures and discarding valuable information.

## Method

- The ==Adaptive SSL (AdaSSL) framework== models conditional uncertainty by introducing a ==latent variable 'r'== that captures non-deterministic information between naturally paired data points.
- AdaSSL reformulates the mutual information objective, incorporating 'r' into the similarity function or predictor and adding a regularization term to control 'r's expressiveness.
- Two variants are developed: ==AdaSSL-V==, which uses variational distributions and KL divergence, and ==AdaSSL-S==, which employs deterministic prediction with an L0 sparsity penalty for 'r'.

## Results

- AdaSSL variants consistently **outperformed** baselines on synthetic data, achieving **higher R² scores** for latent factor recovery, especially with heteroscedastic and multimodal conditionals.
- Experiments on 3DIdent showed AdaSSL achieved **superior disentanglement** and identifiability of ground-truth latent factors, with the 'r' variable enabling interpretable manipulation.
- AdaSSL **improved F1 scores** for facial attribute prediction on CelebA and demonstrated **robustness** to noisy data pairings on iNat-1M, while enabling diverse future trajectory sampling in stochastic world modeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Traditional SSL methods struggle with heteroscedastic and multimodal noise, which are inherent in natural data, leading to suboptimal representation learning.
> - Geometrically, mapping latent variables from a flat space to a normalized embedding sphere necessitates heteroscedasticity in the conditional distribution of paired embeddings.
> - Explicitly modeling this conditional uncertainty through a latent variable 'r' enables SSL to better preserve diverse latent variability and learn more disentangled features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge within **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, specifically within the dominant paradigm of **joint-embedding SSL** for visual representation learning. Historically, joint-embedding SSL methods have relied on crafting "positive pairs" through *handcrafted augmentations* (e.g., cropping, color jittering) to encourage similarity between representations of semantically related data. The underlying assumption is that these augmentations perturb pixels while preserving the core semantics, thereby inducing useful invariances.

However, the authors accurately identify a critical limitation: such augmentations often fail to mimic natural variations in the real world, potentially leading to representations that do not capture the *right* invariances or that discard fine-grained information. This has spurred a shift in the research landscape towards exploiting **naturally-paired data** (e.g., successive video frames, image-caption pairs, class labels) which inherently reflect real-world variations.

This shift naturally connects to **Causal Representation Learning (CRL)**, a field concerned with recovering the underlying latent factors of data generation. When using natural pairs, the differences between `x` and `x+` are often driven by structured changes in these latent factors. While natural pairs offer significant benefits for generalization and visual understanding, they introduce a new challenge: the "one-to-many mapping problem" or **conditional uncertainty**. Unlike simple augmentations where `p(z+|z)` is highly concentrated, natural generative processes can lead to complex conditional distributions that are **heteroscedastic** (input-dependent variance) or even **multimodal** (multiple plausible targets for a given input), as seen in world modeling scenarios (e.g., a car turning left or right).

Existing SSL methods, including contrastive approaches (like InfoNCE) and distillation-based methods (like BYOL), implicitly assume simpler noise structures (constant, isotropic, or anisotropic) in these conditional distributions. They struggle to flexibly capture arbitrary, complex noise patterns without explicit conditioning. Previous attempts to address this include anisotropic noise modeling in contrastive SSL and the use of latent variables in Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs).

This work specifically carves out its niche by providing a solution to model these *complex conditional distributions with unknown shapes*, without requiring *a priori* access to the latent variables that govern these transformations. It proposes a theoretically justified framework that adapts to these challenging distributions, thereby expanding the capabilities of SSL to learn more diverse and generalizable features, a principle termed "SSL from structural invariance." It bridges the gap between the theoretical aspirations of CRL and the practical scalability of modern SSL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **develop a novel self-supervised learning (SSL) framework that can flexibly capture complex conditional distributions, including heteroscedastic and multimodal noise structures, when learning from naturally paired data.** The authors aim to enhance the learning of diverse and generalizable representations by explicitly modeling the inherent uncertainty in prediction targets that arises from real-world generative processes.

The key motivations driving this objective are:

1. **Limitations of Handcrafted Augmentations in SSL:** Current state-of-the-art joint-embedding SSL relies heavily on handcrafted data augmentations. While effective, these augmentations often fail to perfectly mimic the changes observed in natural factors of variation. This can lead to representations that are either too invariant (discarding fine-grained information) or that learn shortcut solutions, ultimately hindering generalization and robust performance on downstream tasks.
2. **Unleveraged Potential of Natural Data Pairs:** Naturally occurring data pairs (e.g., consecutive video frames, related images from the same entity) offer a more authentic source of real-world variations. Such pairs are highly valuable for Causal Representation Learning (CRL), where the goal is to recover underlying latent factors. However, the complex, non-deterministic transformations between these natural pairs pose a significant challenge for existing SSL methods.
3. **The "One-to-Many Mapping" Problem and Conditional Uncertainty:** When data generation involves stochastic processes (e.g., in world modeling, where a current state can lead to multiple plausible future states), the conditional distribution `p(z+|z)` (where `z` and `z+` are latent factors of paired data `x` and `x+`) can be highly complex, multimodal, or heteroscedastic. Standard SSL similarity functions or predictors implicitly assume simpler, unimodal, and homoscedastic noise, causing them to discard unmodeled variance and degrade performance.
4. **Theoretical Imperative for Heteroscedasticity Modeling:** The paper provides a theoretical result (Proposition 2.1) demonstrating that mapping a flat latent space to a curved manifold (like a normalized embedding sphere) *necessarily* induces heteroscedasticity in the conditional distribution of paired embeddings. This highlights a fundamental geometric mismatch that standard SSL approaches, which assume uniform noise scales, fail to address.
5. **Desire for More Diverse and Generalizable Features:** By explicitly modeling the structure of `p(z+|z)`, particularly its uncertainty, the authors hypothesize that SSL can learn representations that better preserve a broad range of latent variability, leading to features that are more diverse, disentangled, and generalizable to various downstream tasks and out-of-distribution scenarios.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of SSL beyond static augmentations, enabling it to robustly learn from dynamic, naturally occurring data transformations by effectively disentangling and capturing the inherent uncertainty in these processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology introduced in this paper, named **Adaptive SSL (AdaSSL)**, centers on modeling the conditional uncertainty inherent in naturally paired data through the explicit incorporation of a **latent variable `r`**. This variable is designed to capture the information about `x+` that cannot be deterministically predicted from `x` alone, effectively transforming a complex conditional `p(z+|z)` into a simpler `p(z+|z,r)`.

The authors begin by leveraging the mutual information (MI) objective, a common theoretical basis for SSL. They reformulate `I(f(x); f(x+))` using the chain rule to include the latent variable `r`: `I(f(x); f(x+)) = I(f(x), r; f(x+)) - I(r; f(x+) | f(x))`. The goal is to maximize the first term (making `f(x)` and `f(x+)` similar given `r`) while minimizing the second (preventing `r` from simply encoding `f(x+)` as a shortcut). This leads to the general AdaSSL objective: `L_AdaSSL = L_SSL((x, r), x+) + βL_Reg(r)`. Here, `L_SSL` is a standard SSL loss (e.g., InfoNCE or BYOL) adapted to condition on `r`, and `L_Reg(r)` is a regularization term for `r`.

The paper proposes two distinct variants of AdaSSL:

1. **AdaSSL-V (Variational):**
 - **Modeling `r`:** This variant models `r` using a variational distribution `q_phi(r | x, x+)` which approximates the posterior of `r`, and a prior `p_theta(r | x)`. Both are parameterized by lightweight MLPs on top of the learned embeddings.
 - **Regularization:** The `L_Reg(r)` term is derived as a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, `D_KL(q_phi(r | x, x+) || p_theta(r | x))`, which acts to limit the information `r` can encode about `f(x+)` given `f(x)`. This term essentially encourages `r` to be predicted from `f(x)` alone, while still capturing residual information from `f(x+)`.
 - **Similarity Function/Predictor:** For contrastive SSL, the similarity function becomes `s(x, x+, r) = psi_1(x, r)^T psi_2(x+)`, where `psi_1(x, r)` is the original embedding `f(x)` *edited* by `r` using an editing function `t(f(x), r)`. For distillation-based SSL (like BYOL), `t(f(x), r)` replaces the input to the online predictor. The editing function `t` can be additive, linear, or an MLP.
 - **Compatibility:** AdaSSL-V is theoretically justified for contrastive SSL but empirically shown to be effective with non-contrastive methods as well.

2. **AdaSSL-S (Sparse):**
 - **Modeling `r`:** In this variant, `r` is predicted deterministically by an MLP `m(f(x), f(x+))` and then regularized for sparsity. This is motivated by the inductive bias that natural transitions often correspond to sparse changes in latent factors.
 - **Regularization:** `L_Reg(r)` is an L0 sparsity penalty, `β||r||_0`, made differentiable using a Gumbel-Sigmoid estimator.
 - **Modular Editing Function:** AdaSSL-S employs a modular editing function: `t(f(x), r) = f(x) + sum(r_i * t_i(f(x)))`, where `t_i` are affine transformations. This design allows specific dimensions of `r` to activate modular edits on the `f(x)` embedding, reflecting sparse latent changes.

**Experimental Approach:**
The authors rigorously evaluate AdaSSL across a diverse set of benchmarks to demonstrate its versatility and effectiveness:
- **Controlled Numerical Data:** Synthetic datasets are generated to systematically vary the complexity (isotropic, anisotropic, heteroscedastic, multimodal) of `p(z+|z)`, allowing for precise analysis of how different methods handle uncertainty and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.
- **Synthetic Images (3DIdent):** A 3D-rendered dataset is used for Causal Representation Learning (CRL), where ground-truth latent factors are known. Metrics include DCI disentanglement score and R2 for latent factor recovery.
- **Natural Images (CelebA, iNat-2021):** Real-world facial attribute prediction and fine-grained classification tasks are used. "Natural pairs" are constructed by matching images of the same identity (CelebA) or same species (iNat-2021), potentially with corrupted labels to test robustness. Performance is measured using F1 scores and classification accuracy.
- **World Modeling (Stochastic Moving-MNIST Videos):** A custom video dataset with stochastic velocity changes is used to evaluate the model's ability to capture both invariant (digit identity) and variant (velocity) factors, as well as its capacity for future trajectory prediction and sampling.

Baselines include standard InfoNCE, AnInfoNCE (for anisotropic noise), a new baseline H-InfoNCE (for heteroscedastic noise), LieSSL, and BYOL. The evaluations consistently involve training linear probes on frozen representations or embeddings to assess representation quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented in the paper consistently demonstrate that AdaSSL outperforms existing self-supervised learning methods, particularly when dealing with complex conditional distributions arising from naturally paired data.

1. **Numerical Data (Controlled Settings):**
 - **Heteroscedasticity is crucial:** Traditional InfoNCE and AnInfoNCE perform poorly, especially on out-of-distribution (OOD) data, when the true conditional distribution `p(c+|c)` is heteroscedastic. The newly introduced H-InfoNCE (specifically the MLP variant) significantly improves performance by modeling input-dependent noise. This validates Proposition 2.1, showing that heteroscedasticity is unavoidable when mapping flat latent spaces to curved embedding manifolds.
 - **AdaSSL excels on complex conditionals:** For synthetic data with complex (multimodal and heteroscedastic) `p(c+|c)`, AdaSSL-V and AdaSSL-S achieve the highest linear regression R² scores for recovering latent factors, particularly under challenging OOD evaluations. This indicates their superior ability to model diverse uncertainty structures.

2. **Synthetic Images (3DIdent - Causal Representation Learning):**
 - **Superior Disentanglement and Identifiability:** AdaSSL variants, especially AdaSSL-V with a linear editing function and AdaSSL-S, demonstrate the best disentanglement (highest DCI score) and latent factor recovery (highest R² score) compared to all baselines, including β-VAE and AdaGVAE. This suggests that AdaSSL effectively disentangles underlying data-generating factors without requiring explicit reconstruction.
 - **Interpretable Latent `r`:** Controllable retrieval experiments show that by sampling specific dimensions of the learned latent `r`, AdaSSL-V can selectively manipulate individual latent factors (e.g., position, spotlight, hue) in the retrieved images, providing evidence for the interpretability of the `r` variable.

3. **Natural Images (CelebA and iNat-1M):**
 - **Capturing Fine-Grained Features:** On CelebA, AdaSSL-V consistently improves F1 scores for facial attribute prediction when trained on natural pairs, outperforming InfoNCE and BYOL baselines, especially with weak augmentations. This indicates its effectiveness in capturing fine-grained semantic features that are often discarded by vanilla SSL objectives under complex conditional noise.
 - **Robustness to Noisy Pairings:** On iNat-1M, AdaSSL-V shows greater robustness to increasing levels of noise in data pairings (where images from the same class are sometimes paired with images from the same superclass). Its fine-grained classification accuracy decays more gracefully than InfoNCE, highlighting its ability to adapt to varying levels of target uncertainty.
 - **Variant-Specific Behavior:** AdaSSL-S, while performing well with InfoNCE, shows reduced benefits when combined with BYOL. The authors hypothesize this is due to the lack of explicit information regularization in distillation-based methods, which may require additional mechanisms to enforce efficient utilization of `r`.

4. **World Modeling (Stochastic Moving-MNIST Videos):**
 - **Enhanced Representation Learning for Dynamics:** AdaSSL-V and AdaSSL-S significantly improve both digit recognition accuracy (invariant factor) and velocity decoding R² (variant factor) from video representations compared to BYOL and BYOL+Future. This confirms AdaSSL's ability to learn more comprehensive representations that simultaneously capture both stable content and dynamic changes.
 - **Improved Future Prediction:** Quantitative retrieval metrics (MRR, Hit@k) show AdaSSL variants achieve superior future trajectory prediction capabilities. Qualitatively, AdaSSL-V can sample diverse, plausible future trajectories, unlike BYOL which yields deterministic predictions, showcasing its capacity to model multimodal uncertainty in sequential data.

Overall, the findings strongly support the paper's central hypothesis: explicitly modeling conditional uncertainty with a latent variable `r` via structural invariance leads to more robust, disentangled, and generalizable representations across various data modalities and tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "SELF-SUPERVISED LEARNING FROM STRUCTURAL INVARIANCE" offers several significant contributions and has the potential for broad impact across multiple subfields of artificial intelligence:

1. **Advancement in Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** The most direct impact is on SSL itself. By addressing the critical limitation of existing methods in handling complex, non-trivial conditional distributions (heteroscedasticity, multimodality) from naturally paired data, AdaSSL significantly expands the applicability and robustness of SSL. This shifts the paradigm beyond reliance on handcrafted augmentations, enabling more powerful learning from real-world, messy data. The finding that heteroscedasticity is unavoidable when mapping to a curved embedding space provides a strong theoretical underpinning for this direction.

2. **Enhanced Causal Representation Learning (CRL):** AdaSSL offers a scalable and efficient (reconstruction-free) pathway for Causal Representation Learning. Its demonstrated ability to disentangle and identify ground-truth latent factors from high-dimensional images (like in 3DIdent) is a crucial step towards achieving the goals of CRL. By effectively modeling how latent factors change structurally between pairs, the method moves CRL closer to practical applications without strong probabilistic generative model assumptions.

3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The results show that AdaSSL learns more diverse, fine-grained, and generalizable features. This translates to better performance on downstream tasks, improved out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, and greater robustness to noise in data pairings. Such improvements are vital for deploying AI systems in real-world scenarios where data is inherently noisy and subject to shifts.

4. **Smarter World Modeling and Planning:** The demonstrated success in world modeling tasks, particularly in capturing stochastic dynamics and enabling diverse future trajectory sampling, is highly impactful. This capability is foundational for agents to learn intuitive physics, plan effectively in uncertain environments, and develop a deeper understanding of sequential data like videos. It paves the way for advancements in areas such as action-free representation learning, controllable video generation, and model-based reinforcement learning.

5. **Versatility and Practicality:** AdaSSL's design as a regularization term compatible with both contrastive (InfoNCE) and distillation-based (BYOL) SSL objectives makes it highly versatile. Researchers and practitioners can readily integrate AdaSSL into their existing SSL pipelines, leveraging its benefits without overhauling their entire framework. The explicit regularization of the latent variable `r` provides a mechanism for controlling its informativeness and promoting efficient representation.

6. **New Research Avenues:** The work opens up several promising directions for future research. These include exploring AdaSSL's performance with more advanced backbone architectures (like ViTs) and on uncurated, large-scale web data; developing more structured transition models for the latent variable `r`; and leveraging the learned world models for advanced tasks like controllable video generation or enabling planning in implicitly learned action spaces. The variant-specific behavior of AdaSSL-S with BYOL also points to interesting theoretical questions about information flow and regularization in different SSL paradigms.

In conclusion, AdaSSL represents a significant step forward in making self-supervised learning more adaptable to the complexities of real-world data, fostering the development of more intelligent, robust, and versatile AI systems capable of deeper understanding and interaction with dynamic environments.
