# Self-Supervised Learning from Structural Invariance

> [!summary] Summary
> The AdaSSL framework facilitates self-supervised learning by explicitly modeling complex conditional distributions, including heteroscedastic and multimodal noise, inherent in naturally paired data. It yields representations that are robust, disentangled, and generalize effectively across synthetic and real-world benchmarks, improving performance in causal representation learning and stochastic world modeling.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods primarily use handcrafted augmentations that often fail to capture natural real-world variations, limiting representation generalizability.
- Learning from naturally paired data introduces 'one-to-many mapping' or conditional uncertainty, where transformations between pairs are complex, non-deterministic, heteroscedastic, or multimodal.
- Current SSL objectives struggle to model these complex conditional distributions, implicitly assuming simpler noise structures and discarding valuable information.

## Method

- The ==Adaptive SSL (AdaSSL) framework== models conditional uncertainty by introducing a ==latent variable 'r'== that captures non-deterministic information between naturally paired data points.
- AdaSSL reformulates the mutual information objective, incorporating 'r' into the similarity function or predictor and adding a regularization term to control 'r's expressiveness.
- Two variants are developed: ==AdaSSL-V==, which uses variational distributions and KL divergence, and ==AdaSSL-S==, which employs deterministic prediction with an L0 sparsity penalty for 'r'.

## Results

- AdaSSL variants consistently **outperformed** baselines on synthetic data, achieving **higher R² scores** for latent factor recovery, especially with heteroscedastic and multimodal conditionals.
- Experiments on 3DIdent showed AdaSSL achieved **superior disentanglement** and identifiability of ground-truth latent factors, with the 'r' variable enabling interpretable manipulation.
- AdaSSL **improved F1 scores** for facial attribute prediction on CelebA and demonstrated **robustness** to noisy data pairings on iNat-1M, while enabling diverse future trajectory sampling in stochastic world modeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Traditional SSL methods struggle with heteroscedastic and multimodal noise, which are inherent in natural data, leading to suboptimal representation learning.
> - Geometrically, mapping latent variables from a flat space to a normalized embedding sphere necessitates heteroscedasticity in the conditional distribution of paired embeddings.
> - Explicitly modeling this conditional uncertainty through a latent variable 'r' enables SSL to better preserve diverse latent variability and learn more disentangled features.
