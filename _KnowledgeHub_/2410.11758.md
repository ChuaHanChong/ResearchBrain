# Latent Action Pretraining from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Action Pretraining (LAPA), developed by researchers from KAIST, University of Washington, Microsoft Research, NVIDIA, and Allen Institute for AI, enables unsupervised pretraining of Vision-Language-Action models from actionless videos, including human manipulation footage. This approach allows for learning generalizable robot policies from abundant, unlabeled web data and demonstrates superior performance and pretraining efficiency compared to current state-of-the-art methods that rely on extensive labeled robot datasets.

## Problem

- Developing generalist robotic policies is constrained by the scarcity and high cost of collecting large, diverse, action-labeled robot datasets via human teleoperation.
- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models still largely depend on labeled robot action data, limiting scalability and diversity.
- Utilizing abundant, unlabeled internet videos is challenging due to the lack of explicit robot action annotations and the embodiment gap between human and robot movements.

## Method

- An unsupervised, multi-stage method called **Latent Action Pretraining (LAPA)** is introduced, which first learns discrete ==latent actions== from raw video frames using a ==VQ-VAE based encoder-decoder model==.
- A pre-trained ==Vision-Language Model== is then trained to predict these ==pseudo-labeled discrete latent actions== from observations and language instructions.
- The **LAPA** model is ==fine-tuned== on a small dataset of robot trajectories with ground-truth actions to map the learned latent actions to ==continuous robot control commands==.

## Results

- LAPA, when pretrained on Open-X, surpassed the state-of-the-art OpenVLA (trained on **970k** action-labeled trajectories) in real-world tabletop manipulation tasks with a **50.09%** average success rate (vs. **43.87%**).
- Pretraining LAPA solely on human manipulation videos (Something-Something V2) successfully enabled positive transfer to robot manipulation tasks, outperforming OpenVLA (Bridge) in real-world scenarios.
- Achieved significantly higher pretraining efficiency, requiring approximately **272 H100-hours** for its best model compared to OpenVLA's **21,500 A100-hours** (estimated **30–40x** more efficient).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning discrete latent actions from raw video observations allows for unsupervised pretraining without explicit action labels or pre-defined action priors.
> - A shared, discrete latent action space can be learned that is robust across different robot embodiments and can transfer from human manipulation videos to robot tasks.
> - Leveraging a pre-trained Vision-Language Model as a backbone and learning a small, discrete latent action space significantly improves pretraining efficiency (30-40x faster than prior methods).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the critical intersection of robotics, computer vision, and natural language processing, specifically within the emerging field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics. The broader landscape of robot learning is currently grappling with a fundamental challenge: bridging the gap between perception and action in real-world scenarios.

Traditionally, training robust robotic policies, especially for complex manipulation tasks, has relied heavily on carefully curated, human-teleoperated datasets with explicit ground-truth robot action labels. This reliance creates a significant bottleneck, as collecting such data is exceptionally expensive, time-consuming, and limits the diversity and scale of available training material. This constraint hinders the development of truly generalist robotic agents capable of performing a wide array of tasks in varied environments.

The rise of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in fields like NLP and computer vision, trained on internet-scale text and image data, has demonstrated the power of vast, diverse datasets. Researchers have sought to extend these successes to robotics by developing VLAs, which aim to leverage the semantic understanding of VLMs and ground them in physical actions. However, these VLAs, such as RT-2 and OpenVLA, still predominantly rely on labeled robot action data for finetuning or pretraining, perpetuating the data scarcity problem.

"Latent Action Pretraining" (LAPA) directly addresses this bottleneck. It proposes a paradigm shift by enabling pretraining of VLA models from abundant, *unlabeled* internet videos, which typically feature human activities rather than robot actions. This approach tackles two major challenges:
1. **Lack of Action Labels:** Internet videos rarely contain explicit robot action annotations. LAPA overcomes this by learning "latent actions" in an unsupervised manner.
2. **Human-Robot Embodiment Gap:** Human motions and environments differ significantly from robot embodiments and operational spaces. LAPA demonstrates positive transfer despite this gap.

The work differentiates itself from related efforts in several ways:
- **Unsupervised Action Learning:** Unlike methods that rely on inverse dynamics models (IDMs) to pseudo-label actions on raw videos (e.g., VPT), LAPA learns a discrete "latent action" space directly from observations, free from pre-defined action priors or ground-truth actions during this critical pretraining phase.
- **Generalist VLA Focus:** While some works explore video generative models to predict future *robot trajectories* (e.g., UNIPI), LAPA focuses on learning latent actions to enable a monolithic VLA to predict *robot actions* after a lightweight finetuning.
- **Broader Data Scope:** LAPA pushes beyond robot-specific datasets by demonstrating effective learning from generic human manipulation videos, opening up the possibility of leveraging truly web-scale data, a vision akin to the foundation models in other AI domains.
- **Shared Latent Space:** The core contribution lies in learning a unified quantized latent action representation that is robust across different robot embodiments, a challenge for traditional action representations that vary significantly between robots.

By offering an unsupervised, data-efficient pretraining strategy that leverages readily available video data, LAPA represents a significant advancement towards scalable, general-purpose robot learning, paving the way for the next generation of robotic foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **Latent Action Pretraining (LAPA)**, an unsupervised method for pre-training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models *without the need for ground-truth robot action labels*. This overarching goal is motivated by critical limitations in the current paradigm of training generalist robot policies.

The core motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:** Existing Vision-Language-Action models largely depend on large, diverse robot datasets that are typically collected through expensive, labor-intensive human teleoperation. This severely limits the scale and diversity of available data, hindering the development of truly generalist and robust robotic foundation models. The paper explicitly states that "diverse real-world robot datasets mostly require human teleoperation, which makes scaling difficult."
- **Leveraging Internet-Scale Video Data:** The internet offers an immense, largely untapped resource of video data depicting human behavior and physical interactions. This data is widely available and diverse, but crucially, it lacks explicit robot action labels. The motivation is to overcome the twin challenges of "much of the raw data on the web lacks explicit action labels" and the "fundamental difference... from the embodiments and environments of typical robotic systems."
- **Unsupervised Learning of Action Priors:** A key objective is to learn meaningful action representations from raw video data in a fully unsupervised manner. This is envisioned as learning to "tokenize atomic actions without requiring predefined action priors (e.g., end-effector positions, joint positions)," analogous to Byte Pair Encoding in language modeling.
- **Enabling Scalable VLA Pretraining:** The aim is to create a pretraining methodology that allows VLAs to benefit from the scale of internet videos, similar to how large language models and vision models are trained. This means moving beyond the constraints of robot-specific datasets during pretraining.
- **Achieving Superior Generalization:** The research seeks to demonstrate that LAPA-pretrained models can achieve superior generalization capabilities compared to existing approaches, especially in scenarios involving cross-environment, cross-embodiment, unseen objects, and novel language instructions.
- **Improving Pretraining Efficiency:** A practical objective is to achieve higher pretraining efficiency compared to current state-of-the-art VLA models that rely on labeled action data.
- **Exploring Human-to-Robot Transfer:** A specific objective is to rigorously test whether pretraining *solely* on human manipulation videos yields positive transfer to robot manipulation tasks, thereby validating the potential of web-scale human video data.
- **Developing a "World Model" Capability:** Qualitatively explore if the learned latent actions and the associated decoder can enable LAPA to act as a general-purpose robotic world model, capable of closed-loop rollouts and anticipating action outcomes.

In essence, the core motivation is to unlock the potential of vast, unlabeled video data to create more capable, generalist, and efficiently trainable robotic foundation models, moving beyond the limitations imposed by expensive labeled robot datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Latent Action Pretraining (LAPA) is an unsupervised, multi-stage approach designed to pretrain Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models from actionless videos, followed by a fine-tuning phase to enable real-world robot control. The methodology consists of two main sequential learning stages and a final fine-tuning stage.

#### 3.1. Latent Action Quantization

- **Goal:** To learn discrete, tokenized "latent actions" directly from raw video frames without any ground-truth action labels. These latent actions are intended to represent the "delta" or change between consecutive observations.
- **Architecture:** A VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder) based encoder-decoder model, specifically a variant of the C-ViViT tokenizer.
 - **Encoder:** Takes a current image frame ($x_t$) and a future frame ($x_{t+H}$) as input. It processes these through patch embeddings, spatial transformers, and a causal transformer to produce continuous embeddings. The difference between these embeddings ($d_t = e_{t+H} - e_t$) is then quantized by finding the nearest representation in a predefined "codebook" of latent actions. This quantized representation becomes the discrete latent action token ($z_t$). The window size $H$ (number of frames ahead) is adjusted based on video FPS (e.g., 0.6 seconds for robot videos, 2.4 seconds for human videos).
 - **Decoder:** Takes the current frame ($x_t$) and the quantized latent action ($z_t$) as input. It is trained to reconstruct the future frame ($x_{t+H}$). A crucial detail is the use of cross-attention to condition $x_t$ on $z_t$ and applying a stop gradient to $x_t$'s patch embedding during decoding to prevent representational collapse.
- **Training Objective:** Primarily an L2 reconstruction loss to minimize the difference between the true future frame and the reconstructed frame.
- **Quantization Technique:** Utilizes NSVQ (Noise Substitution in Vector Quantization) to mitigate gradient collapse issues common in VQ-VAE training and improve codebook utilization with a codebook replacement technique in early training.
- **Role in Downstream Stages:** The encoder of this model effectively serves as an "inverse dynamics model" to pseudo-label latent actions across all frames in the pretraining dataset. The decoder can function as a "world model" for generating future frames conditioned on predicted latent actions.

#### 3.2. Latent Pretraining

- **Goal:** To train a VLA model (referred to as LAPA) to predict the discrete latent actions identified in the first stage. This is essentially a behavior cloning task in the latent action space.
- **Backbone Model:** A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically the 7B Large World Model (LWM-Chat-1M) is used, which has strong capabilities in multimodal understanding.
- **Process:**
 - The encoder from the Latent Action Quantization stage is used to label all frames in the pretraining dataset with their corresponding latent actions ($z_t$).
 - The LAPA model is then trained to predict these $z_t$ tokens, given a current image observation ($x_t$) and a language instruction associated with the video clip.
- **Model Structure:** The pre-trained VLM's existing language model head is replaced with a new, simpler "latent action head" (a single MLP layer) whose output vocabulary size matches the codebook size of the latent actions ($|C|$).
- **Training Strategy:** The vision encoder of the backbone VLM is frozen, while the language model parameters are unfrozen and trained. This leverages the VLM's powerful visual representations while adapting its reasoning capabilities to the latent action space.
- **Granularity:** A key advantage is that this approach does not impose a predefined action granularity; the model learns "atomic actions" that best capture changes in observations, allowing for an end-to-end learning of action hierarchies.

#### 3.3. Action Finetuning

- **Goal:** To bridge the gap between the learned latent actions and the actual continuous robot actions executable on a real robot.
- **Process:** The LAPA model, after latent pretraining, is fine-tuned on a relatively small dataset of robot trajectories that *do* contain ground-truth robot actions (e.g., delta end-effector positions).
- **Action Space Mapping:** The latent action head is discarded, and a new "action head" is attached to generate the ground-truth robot actions. For continuous actions, these are discretized into bins, aligning with common practices in VLA training.
- **Training Strategy:** Similar to latent pretraining, the vision encoder is frozen, and the underlying language model's parameters are unfrozen for fine-tuning.

**Overall Approach:** LAPA intelligently decouples the challenging problem of learning action representations from the even more challenging problem of collecting large-scale, labeled robot data. By learning discrete, semantically meaningful latent actions from abundant unlabeled video data, it provides a strong foundation, which is then efficiently adapted to specific robot embodiments with minimal labeled data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of LAPA demonstrates its significant effectiveness across various benchmarks, outperforming existing baselines and setting new state-of-the-art results for VLA models.

- **Superior Performance over Unlabeled Action Baselines:**
 - Across Language Table (simulation) and SIMPLER (simulation), LAPA consistently and significantly outperforms baseline methods (UNIPI, VPT, SCRATCH) that also train manipulation policies without ground-truth action labels during pretraining.
 - UNIPI, relying on video diffusion models, often struggled with longer-horizon tasks. VPT, which uses an inverse dynamics model (IDM) to pseudo-label actions, showed improvement over SCRATCH but was consistently surpassed by LAPA, highlighting the robustness and effectiveness of LAPA's latent action learning.

- **Outperforming State-of-the-Art Labeled Action Models:**
 - A remarkable finding is that LAPA, when pretrained on Open-X dataset (multi-embodiment robot data), outperforms OPENVLA (the current state-of-the-art VLA model trained on 970k *action-labeled* trajectories) on real-world tabletop manipulation tasks. LAPA achieves an average success rate of 50.09% compared to OPENVLA's 43.87% (+6.22%).
 - This includes strong performance on tasks requiring language conditioning, generalization to unseen objects, and semantic generalization to unseen instructions (Table 2). LAPA (Open-X) outperformed OpenVLA (Open-X) across all generalization settings.
 - For specific tasks, LAPA shows nuanced performance: it excels in pick-and-place and covering tasks but slightly underperforms OPENVLA in early grasping stages, suggesting potential areas for fine-grained skill improvement.

- **Positive Transfer from Human Manipulation Videos:**
 - A critical validation of LAPA's core motivation is its ability to learn effectively from purely human manipulation videos (Something-Something V2 dataset) despite the substantial human-to-robot embodiment gap.
 - LAPA pretrained on human videos outperformed SCRATCH and baselines like UNIPI and VPT on the SIMPLER environment.
 - Even more strikingly, LAPA trained with human videos outperformed OPENVLA (Bridge), a model pretrained on a large robot dataset, in real-world robot tasks. This result strongly indicates the potential of leveraging vast, unlabelled web-scale human video data for robotic foundation models.

- **Exceptional Pretraining Efficiency:**
 - LAPA demonstrates vastly superior pretraining efficiency compared to OPENVLA. The best-performing LAPA model (Open-X pretraining) required approximately 272 H100-hours, while OPENVLA needed 21,500 A100-hours (estimated 30-40x more efficient for LAPA).
 - This efficiency is attributed to LAPA's use of the Large World Model (LWM) as its backbone and its significantly smaller action space (8^4 vs. 256^7) during pretraining, simplifying the perception-to-action learning problem. LAPA often achieved optimal performance in a single epoch of pretraining.

- **Scalability and Latent Action Properties:**
 - Ablation studies confirm that LAPA benefits from scaling model parameters, data size, and the latent action representation space (both sequence length and vocabulary size). This suggests that further scaling with larger models and datasets could lead to even greater performance.
 - Qualitative analysis of latent actions shows that they correspond to distinct, semantically meaningful robot movements (e.g., "move left and forward," "rotate up"). Crucially, the latent actions learned from multi-embodiment data show a shared representation space, meaning a single latent action can correspond to similar high-level movements across different robot types, which aids positive transfer.
 - The latent actions learned from human videos also captured camera movements, indicating a rich representation of dynamic scene changes.

- **World Model Capabilities:**
 - LAPA's ability to generate closed-loop rollouts (predicting future frames based on current observation and predicted latent actions) without any action finetuning demonstrates its potential as a general-purpose robotic world model. This suggests LAPA could be used for neural simulation, task planning, and inferring action outcomes.

In summary, LAPA presents a breakthrough in robot learning by demonstrating that unsupervised pretraining from actionless videos, including human manipulation footage, can yield highly performant and generalizable VLA models, surpassing existing state-of-the-art methods while being significantly more resource-efficient.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Latent Action Pretraining (LAPA) carries profound significance and potential impact for the field of robotics and artificial intelligence:

- **Breaking the Data Bottleneck in Robotics:** The most impactful contribution of LAPA is its ability to bypass the critical data scarcity issue in robot learning. By enabling unsupervised pretraining from abundant, readily available, and unlabelled video data—including vast repositories of human manipulation videos—LAPA unlocks a new paradigm for scaling robot learning. This drastically reduces the dependency on expensive, labor-intensive human teleoperation, democratizing access to large-scale training data.
- **Enabling True Foundation Models for Robotics:** LAPA is a crucial step towards realizing generalist "foundation models" for robotics. Just as large language models (LLMs) and vision models have transformed their respective fields by leveraging web-scale data, LAPA provides a pathway for robots to learn broad, generalizable skills from the diverse interactions captured in online videos. This could lead to robots that are adaptable to unforeseen tasks, objects, and environments, requiring minimal fine-tuning for specific applications.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance with Efficiency:** LAPA's ability to outperform existing state-of-the-art VLA models (like OPENVLA) that *do* rely on extensive labeled robot data is a powerful validation of its effectiveness. Furthermore, achieving this superior performance with vastly greater pretraining efficiency (30-40x) makes the development of advanced robotic AI more sustainable and accessible to a broader research community. This cost-effectiveness accelerates research and deployment cycles.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated robustness across cross-environment, cross-embodiment, unseen object, and semantic generalization scenarios is pivotal. This means robots trained with LAPA are more likely to adapt to real-world variability, a long-standing challenge in robotics. The learned shared latent action space, independent of specific robot embodiments, is key to this generalization.
- **Foundation for Robotic World Models and Planning:** The qualitative demonstration of LAPA's ability to act as a world model, predicting future frames and action outcomes in closed-loop rollouts, opens up exciting avenues. This capability could be leveraged for sophisticated task and motion planning, allowing robots to "mentally" simulate and evaluate potential actions before execution, leading to more robust and intelligent behavior. It also hints at a future where test-time compute scaling (like in LLMs) improves robot performance.
- **New Research Directions:** LAPA's success invites further research into several areas:
 - **Higher-Dimensional Action Spaces:** Scaling LAPA to capture more complex, higher-dimensional actions (e.g., whole-body control) for more dexterous manipulation or humanoid robots.
 - **Real-time Inference:** Investigating hierarchical architectures or other techniques to reduce latency for real-time robotic deployment.
 - **Beyond Manipulation:** Applying the LAPA framework to other domains like navigation, self-driving cars, or complex assembly tasks by leveraging relevant video data.
 - **Quantitative World Model Evaluation:** Moving beyond qualitative analysis to quantitatively evaluate LAPA's capabilities as a predictive world model.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing the model checkpoints and code will significantly accelerate research and development in the field, fostering collaboration and enabling others to build upon this foundational work.

In essence, LAPA represents a significant leap forward in robot learning by democratizing access to training data, enabling more scalable and generalizable robot intelligence, and laying the groundwork for a new generation of robotic foundation models capable of complex, adaptable behaviors in the real world.
