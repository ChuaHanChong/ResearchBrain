# Extracting Multimodal Learngene in CLIP: Unveiling the Multimodal Generalizable Knowledge

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Southeast University developed Multimodal Learngene (MM-LG), a framework that extracts generalizable knowledge from CLIP, enabling efficient initialization of diverse multimodal and unimodal models. The method reduces pre-training costs by 2.8 times and parameter storage by 75% compared to full pre-training, while achieving comparable or superior performance across various tasks, notably improving image captioning.

## Problem

- Large multimodal foundation models like CLIP require significant computational resources for pre-training and necessitate repetitive large-scale pre-training when different model scales or architectures are needed for downstream applications.
- Existing 'Learngene' paradigms, which aim to extract generalizable knowledge for efficient initialization, primarily focused on unimodal scenarios and lacked mechanisms to handle the unique characteristics of multimodal knowledge within models like CLIP.
- There was a need for a method to efficiently extract and transfer the powerful cross-modal understanding capabilities of CLIP into smaller, task-specific models without redundant training.

## Method

- Introduces ==Multimodal Learngene (MM-LG)==, a framework that extracts multimodal generalizable knowledge from a large CLIP model, comprising a 'Block Part' (vision, language, multimodal blocks) and a 'Coefficient Part' (learnable coefficients).
- An auxiliary model is constructed whose layer parameters are formed as a weighted sum of these extracted ==learngene blocks== and trained using knowledge distillation from the ancestry CLIP model, combining CLIP's contrastive loss with a logit distillation loss.
- The extracted learngene is used to numerically initialize descendant models of various scales and modalities, followed by a brief =='parameter activation' training phase== on a small fraction of the pre-training dataset.

## Results

- MM-LG consistently outperforms existing unimodal Learngene approaches (He-LG, TLEG) across cross-modal retrieval, image classification, and image captioning, with up to **+4.13%** I2T Recall@1 on Flickr30k over TLEG.
- The framework achieves performance comparable to or superior to the traditional Pre-training and Fine-tuning (PT-FT) paradigm, notably outperforming PT-FT in image captioning by over **5 CIDEr points**.
- MM-LG significantly reduces parameter storage by **75%** and pre-training costs by approximately **2.8 times** compared to PT-FT, while maintaining or improving task performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalizable knowledge within large multimodal models like CLIP can be effectively decomposed and extracted into distinct unimodal (vision, language) and multimodal interaction components.
> - A knowledge distillation approach can be used to train an auxiliary model that learns to compose these fundamental 'learngene' components, capturing the behavior of the large ancestry model efficiently.
> - A brief parameter activation step using a small subset of data is crucial after numerical initialization from the extracted learngene to effectively prepare descendant models for downstream tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several critical and active areas within artificial intelligence: multimodal learning, foundation models, model efficiency, knowledge distillation, and transfer learning.

**Multimodal Learning and Foundation Models:** Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) has emerged as a groundbreaking foundation model, demonstrating remarkable capabilities in understanding and relating visual and linguistic information. Its success in various downstream tasks, from zero-shot image classification to cross-modal retrieval and image captioning, has revolutionized multimodal AI. However, common to most foundation models, CLIP's impressive performance comes at a significant computational cost, involving billions of parameters and extensive pre-training on massive datasets (e.g., 400 million image-text pairs). This high computational overhead poses challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices and necessitates repetitive large-scale pre-training when different model scales or architectural variations are required for specific downstream applications.

**Model Efficiency and Compression:** The field has seen a surge in research aimed at making large models more efficient, addressing issues of model size, inference speed, and training costs. Techniques like knowledge distillation, pruning, and quantization are common approaches. Previous works, such as CLIP-KD (Yang et al., 2024a) and Clipping (Pei et al., 2023), have explored methods for enhancing the performance and efficiency of lightweight CLIP models through knowledge distillation or layer-wise alignment. However, a key limitation shared by many existing methods is the need for *repetitive large-scale pre-training* whenever the scale or architecture of the downstream model changes. This paper directly confronts this challenge by aiming to extract a universally applicable knowledge core.

**The Learngene Paradigm:** This paper is a direct extension of the "Learngene" paradigm, first proposed by Wang et al. (2022). The core idea of Learngene is to extract a "generalizable component" (the "learngene") from a large pre-trained "ancestry model" and use this compact learngene to efficiently initialize diverse "descendant models" for various downstream tasks. This concept fundamentally shifts from fine-tuning an entire large model or distilling to a fixed smaller model, to creating a reusable, transferable core of knowledge. Prior Learngene works (e.g., He-LG, TLEG, LearngenePool, SWS) primarily focused on unimodal scenarios (e.g., vision models). This paper identifies a critical gap: the lack of research into applying Learngene to *multimodal* models like CLIP, specifically how to extract and leverage generalizable knowledge that spans different modalities (image and text) and their interactions.

By addressing the challenge of extracting multimodal generalizable knowledge within the Learngene framework, this work significantly contributes to making advanced multimodal AI models more accessible, versatile, and sustainable. It proposes a novel approach that aims to overcome the "repetitive pre-training" bottleneck, setting a new direction for efficient knowledge transfer in multimodal contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a novel framework, Multimodal Learngene (MM-LG), capable of extracting and leveraging multimodal generalizable knowledge from CLIP**, which can then be used to efficiently initialize diverse descendant models for both multimodal and unimodal downstream tasks.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key observations and challenges:

- **Computational Burden of CLIP:** While CLIP offers powerful representation capabilities and strong cross-modal generalization, its large number of parameters and the substantial computational resources required for its pre-training are significant barriers. Training different scales of CLIP models for various applications is extremely time-consuming and computationally expensive due to repetitive pre-training.
- **Limitations of Existing Learngene Paradigms:** The "Learngene" concept has proven effective in unimodal contexts for efficient knowledge transfer. However, previous Learngene approaches lack the mechanisms to explicitly handle and extract the generalizable knowledge inherent in *multimodal scenarios*, where interactions between modalities are crucial. They do not account for the unique characteristics of models like CLIP that learn joint representations across different data types.
- **Need for Multimodal Generalizable Knowledge:** CLIP's strength lies in its ability to understand concepts across both images and text. This "multimodal generalizable knowledge" is highly valuable for downstream tasks. The authors are motivated to find a way to encapsulate this specific type of knowledge in a compact, transferable form.
- **Desire for Efficiency and Versatility:** The ultimate goal is to enable the efficient deployment of CLIP's generalizable capabilities across a wide spectrum of downstream tasks, including both multimodal (e.g., cross-modal retrieval, image captioning) and unimodal (e.g., image classification) applications, and importantly, for models of varying scales, without the need for redundant large-scale pre-training.

In essence, the paper aims to combine the efficiency benefits of the Learngene paradigm with the powerful multimodal understanding capabilities of CLIP, addressing a critical gap in current knowledge transfer methods for multimodal foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed MM-LG framework consists of two main stages: **Extraction** and **Initialization**.

#### 3.1. Extraction of Multimodal Learngene

The core challenge in this stage is to design a structure that can capture both unimodal and multimodal generalizable knowledge from a pre-trained CLIP model (referred to as the "ancestry model").

- **Learngene Components:** The "learngene" in MM-LG is designed to be comprehensive. It comprises two main parts:
 - **Block Part ($\theta_{LG}$):** This part stores the actual learnable parameters. Critically, it is divided into two groups of blocks, $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$. Each group contains:
 - Vision blocks ($\theta_v$): For unimodal visual knowledge.
 - Language blocks ($\theta_l$): For unimodal linguistic knowledge.
 - Multimodal blocks ($\theta_m$): For knowledge representing cross-modal interactions and joint representations.
 Each $\theta$ represents the weights and biases of linear processes within a Transformer layer (Multi-Head Self-Attention and Multi-Layer Perceptron).
 - **Coefficient Part ($c_{LG}$):** This part consists of learnable coefficient vectors:
 - $c_v$: For vision blocks.
 - $c_l$: For language blocks.
 - $c_m$: Further split into $c_{mv}$ (multimodal-vision) and $c_{ml}$ (multimodal-language) to modulate the multimodal blocks' contribution to vision and language branches, respectively.

- **Auxiliary Model Construction:** To extract the learngene, an auxiliary model is constructed. The parameters of each layer in this auxiliary model are formed as a *weighted sum* of the extracted learngene blocks. For example, for layer $i$ in the vision encoder, its parameters $P_v_i$ are calculated as $P_v_i = c_v_k \times \theta_v_j + c_{mv}_k \times \theta_m_j$, where $j$ and $k$ are indices determined by an alternating and repeating scheme of block groups and coefficients. This weighted-sum approach allows the auxiliary model to learn how to combine the fundamental unimodal and multimodal components to mimic the ancestry model's behavior. Other components like Layer Normalization and Embedding layers are not decomposed and are shared.

- **Training of Auxiliary Model (Distillation):** The auxiliary model is trained using **knowledge distillation** from the ancestry CLIP model. The training objective is a combined loss function:
 - **$L_{CLIP}$:** A contrastive language-image pre-training loss for the auxiliary model itself, similar to the original CLIP objective, to ensure it learns strong multimodal representations.
 - **$L_{dist}$:** A distillation loss that measures the difference between the logit similarity matrices produced by the auxiliary model and the ancestry CLIP model. This ensures the auxiliary model (and thus the extracted learngene) captures the behavior and knowledge of the large pre-trained CLIP.
 - These two losses are combined with a loss weight $\lambda$.

#### 3.2. Initialization of Descendant Models

Once the learngene (blocks and coefficients) has been successfully extracted from the ancestry model, it is used to numerically initialize descendant models of various scales and modalities.

- **Numerical Initialization:** For a descendant model (which can be a unimodal Transformer or a full CLIP-like multimodal model), its layer parameters are initialized by applying the same weighted-sum mechanism used during auxiliary model construction. The key here is the selection of appropriate learngene group indices and coefficient indices, which are modified from the extraction phase to fit the target descendant model's depth.
- **Scaling for Diverse Depths:** The paper details how to handle descendant models of different layer counts (e.g., 6-layer, 8-layer, 12-layer). The extracted learngene, corresponding to a particular layer depth (e.g., 6 layers implicitly by removing repetitions from the 12-layer auxiliary model's construction), can be expanded. For example, to create an 8-layer model from a 6-layer initialized model, the first two layers of the 6-layer model's initialization are simply replicated and connected with the remaining four layers.
- **Parameter Activation and Fine-tuning:** After initialization, a crucial "parameter activation" step is performed. This involves a brief training phase using a small fraction (e.g., 10%) of the original pre-training dataset for just one epoch. This step is observed to be crucial for effectively initializing the parameters. Following this, the initialized models are fine-tuned on specific downstream tasks (image classification, cross-modal retrieval, image captioning).

The methodology effectively decouples the vast knowledge of CLIP into fundamental multimodal and unimodal components, allowing them to be compactly stored and flexibly recombined to form efficient, task-ready models of varying sizes and types.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of MM-LG across a range of multimodal and unimodal tasks and different model scales.

#### 4.1. Performance Against Existing Learngene Paradigms

MM-LG consistently outperforms existing Learngene approaches (He-LG and TLEG), highlighting its superior ability to handle multimodal generalizable knowledge.
- **Cross-modal Retrieval (COCO, Flickr30k):** MM-LG significantly surpasses TLEG (e.g., +3.78% I2T Recall@1 on COCO and +4.13% I2T Recall@1 on Flickr30k for 12-layer models). He-LG showed inferior performance, often worse than training from scratch, indicating issues with negative transfer in multimodal contexts. This underscores the importance of MM-LG's explicit multimodal block.
- **Image Classification (CIFAR-100, Food-101, Oxford-IIIT PET):** Even for unimodal tasks, MM-LG shows improvements over TLEG (e.g., 0.7-1.4% on CIFAR-100, 0.1-1.0% on Food-101, and 0.9-3.1% on Oxford-IIIT PET). This indicates that MM-LG effectively preserves unimodal generalizable knowledge through the cooperation of its vision and multimodal components, even when only a part of the learngene is used. He-LG again struggles due to negative transfer.
- **Image Captioning (COCO):** MM-LG achieves substantial gains over both He-LG and TLEG across BLEU, CIDEr, and ROUGE-L metrics. For instance, on the 12-layer setting, MM-LG improves BLEU by 16.73 over He-LG and 0.90 over TLEG. This task, heavily reliant on multimodal understanding and generation, further validates MM-LG's comprehensive extraction of multimodal generalizable knowledge.

#### 4.2. Performance Against Pre-training and Fine-tuning (PT-FT) Paradigm

Surprisingly, MM-LG achieves performance comparable to, and in some cases even superior to, the traditional Pre-training and Fine-tuning (PT-FT) paradigm, which serves as an upper bound.
- **Cross-modal Retrieval:** MM-LG often surpasses PT-FT, for example, by +2.50% on COCO and +3.65% on Flickr30k for 12-layer models (I2T Recall@1). For 6-layer settings, MM-LG achieves comparable results.
- **Image Classification:** MM-LG delivers comparable or slightly superior results to PT-FT (e.g., +1.9% on Oxford-IIIT PET for 12-layer, +0.5% for 8-layer), demonstrating its robust initialization capability across diverse model scales.
- **Image Captioning:** Most notably, MM-LG *outperforms* the PT-FT paradigm across all layer settings and all evaluated metrics (BLEU, CIDEr, ROUGE-L). For the CIDEr metric, MM-LG shows significant improvements of over 5 points compared to PT-FT. This suggests that MM-LG's specific extraction of multimodal generalizable knowledge might be more targeted and effective for this generative task than general large-scale pre-training.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Gains

MM-LG demonstrates substantial improvements in efficiency compared to the PT-FT paradigm.
- **Parameter Storage:** MM-LG requires only around 25% of the parameter storage compared to PT-FT (37.4M vs. 151.4M for initializing three different model scales). This is a 75% reduction in storage footprint.
- **Training Costs:** MM-LG significantly reduces pre-training costs. It requires 407.8 GPU-hours for learngene extraction, whereas PT-FT demands 1142.4 GPU-hours (nearly 2.8 times more) due to the necessity of repetitive pre-training for each model scale.
- **Overall Value:** Despite these massive efficiency gains, MM-LG's average image-to-text accuracy on Flickr30k *outperforms* PT-FT by 1.03%.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirms that both the multimodal and unimodal components of the extracted learngene are indispensable for MM-LG's superior performance. Disabling either the multimodal part ("w/oMM") or the unimodal part ("onlyMM") leads to significant performance degradation on both multimodal (e.g., ~40% drop in Flickr30k retrieval) and unimodal (e.g., ~8-9% drop on CIFAR-100 classification) tasks. This validates the design choice of explicitly extracting distinct components for generalizable knowledge across modalities and within each modality, demonstrating their collaborative role in providing robust initialization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Extracting Multimodal Learngene in CLIP" offers several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of artificial intelligence:

- **Pioneering Multimodal Learngene:** This paper is the first to explicitly address and formulate the extraction of *multimodal generalizable knowledge* within the Learngene paradigm. Previous Learngene studies primarily focused on unimodal models. By successfully extending this concept to CLIP, the authors open up a new avenue for efficient knowledge transfer in the rapidly growing domain of multimodal AI.
- **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks of Foundation Models:** The computational cost of pre-training and deploying large foundation models like CLIP is a critical challenge. MM-LG provides a practical and highly effective solution by drastically reducing repetitive pre-training costs (2.8x reduction) and parameter storage (75% reduction). This makes powerful multimodal capabilities more accessible and deployable, particularly in resource-constrained environments or on edge devices.
- **Enhanced Versatility and Flexibility:** MM-LG offers unprecedented flexibility by extracting a single, compact "learngene" that can initialize descendant models of *diverse scales and modalities*. This means developers are no longer forced to either fine-tune an oversized model or re-pre-train entirely new smaller models for different task requirements. A single extraction process yields a knowledge base for a wide range of applications, simplifying the model development lifecycle.
- **Superior Performance with Efficiency:** The finding that MM-LG not only achieves significant efficiency gains but also matches or *surpasses* the performance of the traditional pre-training and fine-tuning (PT-FT) paradigm (especially notably in image captioning) is highly impactful. This suggests that the "learngene" approach, by intelligently distilling and structuring generalizable knowledge, can sometimes lead to more effective task-specific models than broad pre-training followed by fine-tuning.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions. It could inspire similar multimodal learngene extraction methods for other large multimodal models (e.g., large language models with visual capabilities, other vision-language models beyond CLIP). The methodology of disentangling unimodal and multimodal generalizable components could be applied to other complex, multi-faceted foundation models. It also furthers the broader goal of understanding what constitutes "generalizable knowledge" in deep neural networks and how it can be most effectively extracted and transferred.

In summary, MM-LG represents a leap forward in efficient multimodal AI, offering a paradigm shift from repetitive pre-training to intelligent knowledge inheritance. Its practical benefits in terms of cost reduction and deployment flexibility, combined with its strong performance, position it as a significant contribution to making advanced AI more scalable and widely applicable.
