# Visual Jev: Accurate and Efficient Decisions from Shared Visual Context

> [!summary] Summary
> Visual Jev answers many independent forced-choice questions about one image by encoding the image and public context once as a shared KV-cached prefix, running isolated question suffixes as one batch, and reading option probabilities from the backbone's existing LM head. Answer-supervised LoRA raises four-benchmark macro accuracy from 0.706 to 0.761, while shared batched execution is 8.9× faster per question at 32 questions per image.

## Problem

- Standard VLM querying decodes free-form text for fixed-choice decisions and re-encodes the image for every question, ignoring that candidate sets and visual context are known and shared.
- Serving work treats prefix reuse and batching as generic levers, without separating how much each contributes on many-questions-per-image workloads or checking question isolation.
- Decision interfaces often bolt typed classification heads onto VLMs without matched controls, so gains from task adaptation are confounded with gains attributed to the output parameterization.

## Method

- **Shared-prefix batched execution**: the system turn, image and public context are prefilled once, the ==KV cache== is expanded across question branches, and right-padded suffixes run as one batch with separate rows preserving isolation.
- **LM-head candidate readout**: each option maps to a verified single letter token, and candidate logits are ==softmax==-normalized over only the runtime-supplied candidates (up to 16), avoiding free-form decoding.
- **Answer-supervised LoRA** post-training on Qwen3-VL-4B with a frozen vision tower, trained on GQA Choice and SNLI-VE Claim items, compared against matched typed Choice/Claim heads and an evidence-sufficiency head as controls.

## Results

- Answer SFT lifts macro accuracy from **0.706** to **0.761** (8B: **0.747** to **0.780**), gains concentrated on trained GQA (**0.879** to **0.916**) and SNLI-VE (**0.629** to **0.808**); held-out TextVQA and TallyQA barely move.
- At **32** questions per image, amortized time drops from **50.7 ms** (serial) to **19.3 ms** (batching) to **5.7 ms** (prefix sharing), **176** vs **20** questions/s, with peak memory rising **8.40** to **10.10 GiB**.
- Typed decision head matches LM head (both **0.761** macro, overlapping seed ranges); a head trained only on K≤4 collapses to **0.639** versus **0.973** on eight-option TextVQA; the sufficiency head lowers macro to **0.748**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - For forced-choice VLM decisions, adapt the backbone and keep the pretrained LM-head readout; fixed-slot typed heads add no accuracy and fail on unseen slot counts.
> - The serving saving comes from reusing visual context and batching isolated suffixes, not from skipping generation; sharing pays only when several questions arrive together.
> - Evidence-sufficiency signals detect missing evidence yet worsen selective prediction versus plain confidence, since models answer correctly from context and prior; use them to trigger re-observation, not to decide trust.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A vision–language model often has to choose among known options, and several independent questions may concern the same image. Answering each question from scratch repeats work on that image. Generating prose is also unnecessary when the system needs a choice. Visual Jev processes the shared image and context once, then answers the separate questions together without letting them see one another. For 32 questions available together, this shared, batched path takes **5.7 ms per question amortized**, versus **50.7 ms** for independent serial execution. That is an **8.9× throughput gain**.

At N=32, the shared-prefix batched path ends at 5.7 ms/question, compared with 19.3 for batched independent sequences and 50.7 for independent serial execution. At N=1, shared-prefix batching is slower, 82.9 versus 48.1. Values are group time divided by N, not independent-request latency.

### 2. Share the image, isolate the questions

The image and public text context form a shared prefix. The model computes that prefix once and caches its KV state, which is the stored intermediate attention state that lets the model avoid recomputing the already-processed prefix. Each question and its candidate options form a separate suffix. The suffixes run in separate batch rows. Masks prevent cross-question attention and exclude padding. At the answer position, the default system reads candidate-token logits from the backbone's existing language-model head. It normalizes over only the valid options for that question. Sharing computation therefore leaves the questions separate.

Image and shared context enter the vision encoder and form a shared KV-cached prefix, "computed once." The prefix branches into question-plus-options suffixes, each with an "Answer:" position, and the branches never see each other. The readout normalizes candidate-token logits over valid options. A dashed box marks optional experimental heads that the recommended system does not require.

Prefix reuse is established systems practice. It is not Visual Jev's claimed novelty. [SGLang: Efficient Execution of Structured Language Model Programs](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2312.07104) describes its runtime's radix-tree cache for shared prefixes. The paper does not compare Visual Jev directly with SGLang.

### 3. Where the speedup comes from

The synchronized warm benchmark starts with an in-memory decoded image and extracted records. It includes processor/tokenization, transfer, and execution. It excludes image decode, record construction, disk, network, and queueing. Under this scope, at N=32, batching without reuse reduces time from **50.7 to 19.3 ms/question**. Adding prefix reuse to that batched path reduces it further to **5.7 ms/question**. That corresponds to 2.6× for batching, a further 3.4× for adding sharing, and 8.9× combined. Batching and sharing both contribute on this workload.

The 5.7 ms is amortized over a known group of 32, which completes in about **182 ms**. It is not the response time of an independently arriving question. Peak allocated memory rises from **8.40 to 10.10 GiB**. At one question, sharing is slower, **82.9 ms versus 48.1 ms**.

### 4. Quality comes from post-training

The quality result is separate from the serving result. Accuracy is reported per benchmark and as an **equal-weight macro average**, the arithmetic mean of accuracy on four benchmarks. GQA and SNLI-VE supply the training families. TextVQA and TallyQA are held out. On Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, answer-supervised post-training raises macro accuracy from **0.706 to 0.761**. The gain is concentrated on the trained families, and the held-out tasks show little change. This result is not general cross-task improvement.

A matched typed-head control also reaches 0.761 macro. The three-seed ranges overlap: 0.758–0.764 for answer SFT and 0.760–0.763 for typed decision cross-entropy. Under the tested setup, the typed head therefore shows no consistent advantage over the existing language-model head. The paper does not claim the two procedures are strictly equivalent.

Accuracy per benchmark and equal-weight macro, with daggers marking the two held-out tasks. The 4B original row reports .879 GQA.629 SNLI-VE, and .706 macro. Answer SFT reports .916±.002 and .808±.006 on those trained families, with little change on TextVQA and TallyQA, and .761±.002 macro. The decision-CE head row has .761±.001 macro.

### 5. Scope of the result

The throughput gain depends on the workload: one image, several questions known together, and answers kept independent. The workload requires co-available questions. The benchmark excludes queueing latency. The quality gain supports adaptation to the trained decision families. It does not show broad transfer to new visual tasks.
