# Learning Manipulation by Predicting Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> MPI is an interaction-oriented visual representation learning pipeline for robot manipulation. Given initial/final keyframes and language, a Prediction Transformer predicts the unseen transition frame while a Detection Transformer localizes the interaction object, jointly teaching how-to-interact and where-to-interact. Improves 10-64% over prior state-of-the-art across real-robot and simulated benchmarks.

## Problem

- Contrastive and masked-signal pre-training methods capture high-level semantics or pixel details but overlook interactive dynamics between robot and environment during manipulation.
- Video prediction pre-training on random consecutive frames models only temporal continuity, since objects rarely move autonomously, yielding noisy signals irrelevant to interaction.
- Prior egocentric-video pre-training studies show representations from manipulation-relevant data fail to generalize better than standard vision datasets like ImageNet.

## Method

- ==Keyframe triplet sampling==: selects initial state plus either transition or final state via ==Bernoulli-parameterized sampling== to jointly teach consequence prediction and causal transition understanding.
- ==Causality Modeling== module: ==deformable attention== layer relates initial and subsequent-state visual embeddings, fusing them into a unified causality-aware representation before decoding.
- ==Prediction Transformer== and ==Detection Transformer== decoders exchange query information via ==bidirectional attention==, jointly predicting the unseen transition-frame pixels and regressing the interaction-object bounding box.

## Results

- MPI improves average success rate by **26.3%** over prior state-of-the-art across 15 real-world manipulation tasks, and retains only an **8.3%** drop under background distraction versus **54.5%** for Voltron.
- On Franka Kitchen, MPI ViT-Base reaches **78.9%** average success rate versus **71.7%** for MVP and **70.5%** for Voltron, a **7.2%** relative gain over the leading baseline.
- On Referring Expression Grounding, MPI achieves **74.40%** AP at 0.75 IoU versus MVP's **60.37%**, an **11.5-point** improvement, confirming stronger where-to-interact localization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling state-transition causality and object localization jointly yields more transferable representations than treating prediction and detection as separate objectives.
> - Selecting semantically meaningful keyframes (initial/transition/final) for prediction targets beats predicting arbitrary consecutive video frames, filtering out interaction-irrelevant noise.
> - Decoupling vision and language encoders during pre-training but fusing them via a shared aggregator lets language supervision improve representations without requiring language at deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of representation learning for robotic manipulation. Traditionally, visuomotor control for robots has been hampered by the scarcity and high cost of collecting in-domain robot data. This limitation has driven researchers to leverage large-scale, readily available human video datasets (e.g., Ego4D, Something-Something V2, Epic Kitchens) for pre-training visual models.

Prior approaches in this domain can be broadly categorized into:
- **Contrastive Learning (e.g., R3M, LIV, Voltron):** These methods learn representations by pulling similar samples closer and pushing dissimilar samples apart in an embedding space, often aligning visual features with language descriptions. While effective for high-level semantic cues, they can overlook fine-grained interactive dynamics.
- **Masked Signal Modeling (e.g., MVP):** Inspired by MAE, these approaches reconstruct masked portions of images or videos, forcing the model to learn detailed pixel-level information. However, they may still miss the specific interaction patterns in manipulation scenarios.
- **Video Prediction (e.g., conventional methods):** These models learn to predict future frames, thereby acquiring temporal scene evolution. However, the authors argue that simply predicting consecutive frames can introduce interaction-irrelevant noise in manipulation contexts where scene changes are primarily interaction-driven, thus hindering the learning of dynamic interactions.

MPI specifically addresses a critical gap: **prior endeavors disregard the interactive dynamics that capture behavior patterns and physical interaction during the manipulation process.** This results in an inadequate understanding of the relationship between objects and the environment. The paper argues that current methods, while making progress, either focus on high-level semantics or fine-grained pixels, overlooking "crucial interactive dynamics." MPI aims to overcome this by introducing an "interaction-oriented" video prediction method, explicitly modeling "how-to-interact" and "where-to-interact" through a targeted prediction and detection framework. This approach refines video prediction by making it task-aware for manipulation, filtering out irrelevant information and emphasizing key interaction states.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind MPI stems from the observation that existing representation learning methods for robotic manipulation, despite leveraging large-scale human video datasets, often fail to adequately capture the interactive dynamics crucial for effective manipulation. Robotic tasks inherently involve active engagement with the environment and specific physical interactions between the robot and objects. Without a deep understanding of these interaction patterns, the learned visual representations remain sub-optimal for visuomotor control.

The key objectives of MPI are:
1. **Develop an Interaction-Oriented Representation Learning Framework:** To explicitly guide the model to learn about interactive dynamics, thereby strengthening its comprehension of manipulation scenes.
2. **Enhance Understanding of "How-to-Interact":** By predicting the unseen "transition frame" given an initial and final state along with language instructions, the model is compelled to understand the intermediate actions and changes that constitute an interaction. This goes beyond simple temporal progression to focus on the *causality* and *process* of interaction.
3. **Enhance Understanding of "Where-to-Interact":** By simultaneously detecting the "interaction object" in the unseen frame, the model learns to identify the specific entities that are being manipulated. This is crucial for object-centric manipulation tasks.
4. **Achieve Synergistic Learning:** To enable the "how-to-interact" (prediction) and "where-to-interact" (detection) objectives to mutually reinforce each other, leading to a more comprehensive and robust visual representation.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** To demonstrate that the proposed interaction-oriented pre-training framework yields superior performance across a diverse range of challenging downstream robotic tasks, including real-world policy learning and object grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MPI is designed as a general pre-training pipeline that learns Manipulation by Predicting the Interaction. It utilizes a multi-modal transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture.

#### 3.1. Data: A Triplet of Keyframes

The method leverages the Ego4D Hand-Object Interaction dataset, which provides annotations for three keyframes representing the `{F_init, F_trans, F_final}` (initial, transition, and final states) of an interaction, along with language descriptions and bounding boxes for interaction objects.
- `F_init` is always used as an input.
- The second input frame is either `F_trans` or `F_final`, chosen probabilistically (`α ~ Bernoulli(p)`).
- The *unseen* frame (the one not chosen as the second input) becomes the prediction target. This strategy encourages the model to learn both consequences (predicting final from initial+transition) and causality (predicting transition from initial+final).

#### 3.2. Encoder: Representation of Interactive Dynamics

- **Decoupled Multi-modal Encoder:**
 - **Visual Encoder:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) processes input frames into sequences of visual embeddings.
 - **Language Encoder:** DistillBERT is used to encode language descriptions. Critically, the language encoder is frozen during pre-training, and the visual and language encoders are decoupled, allowing the visual representation to be used independently in downstream tasks without language input.
- **Causality Modeling:** A deformable attention layer takes the visual embeddings from the two input frames. `F_init`'s embedding acts as queries, while the second input frame's embedding provides keys and values. This module captures patch-to-patch relations and temporal causality, merging the two frame embeddings into a unified representation `v`. For single-image input in downstream tasks, features from the ViT are simply replicated.
- **Token Aggregator:** A Multi-headed Attention Pooling (MAP) block is used to generate concise, fixed-length aggregated visual (`~v`) and language (`~l`) embedding vectors from the variable-length encoder outputs. This facilitates language-aligned semantic features and provides compact representations for downstream tasks, enabling fair comparisons with methods relying on aggregated features.

#### 3.3. Decoder: Prediction and Detection

The decoder consists of two interconnected transformer modules:
- **Prediction Transformer:** Given `v` and `l` from the encoder, this module predicts the pixels of the *unseen* interaction state. It starts with randomly initialized prediction queries (`q_pred`) corresponding to patches of the target frame. Cross-attention is performed with `v` and `l`. Importantly, `q_pred` is enhanced by bidirectional attention with the detection query (`q_det`), allowing for semantic information exchange. The final `q_pred` is linearly projected to generate the predicted frame `ˆF_pred`. This addresses "how-to-interact".
- **Detection Transformer:** An interaction-oriented design introduces a detection query (`q_det`), initialized with the aggregated visual embedding `~v`. This query primarily regresses the bounding box (`ˆb_det`) of the interaction object in the *unseen* frame, leveraging information from the prediction queries `q_pred` through bidirectional attention. This addresses "where-to-interact". The bidirectional information exchange between prediction and detection queries is crucial for their synergistic learning and mutual reinforcement.

#### 3.4. Optimization Objectives

MPI minimizes a combined loss function `L`:
- **`L_pred`:** Mean-squared error (MSE) between the predicted frame `ˆF_pred` and the ground-truth target frame `F_gt`.
- **`L_det`:** L1 loss and GIoU loss between the predicted bounding box `ˆb_det` and the ground-truth bounding box `b_gt`.
- **`L_con`:** InfoNCE contrastive loss between the aggregated visual embedding `~v` and language embedding `~l`, promoting semantic alignment.
The total loss `L = λ1 * L_pred + λ2 * L_det + λ3 * L_con` is optimized, with uniform balancing factors (λ=1) used in experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MPI consistently demonstrates superior performance across a comprehensive suite of robotic tasks, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Visuomotor Control on Real-world Robots

- **Clean Background Tasks:** MPI achieves consistently high performance across ten diverse manipulation tasks (e.g., "put orange into basket," "stack block"), suggesting a broadly applicable representation, unlike some baselines that excel in specific tasks but falter in others.
- **Complex Kitchen Environment:** In a more challenging environment with interfering objects, MPI shows outstanding performance across five tasks (e.g., "take spatula off shelf," "lift lid"), particularly for tasks requiring precise object perception for small objects.
- **Overall Real-world Performance:** MPI delivers an average success rate increase of **26.3%** across 15 real-world tasks compared to prior approaches (Fig. 1d).
- **Generalization and Robustness:**
 - **Background Distraction and Object Variation:** MPI exhibits remarkable robustness, experiencing only an 8.3% drop in success rate under background distraction and a minimum 37.5% drop under object variation, significantly outperforming baselines (e.g., Voltron saw a 54.5% drop). This validates its generalizability to unseen environments and manipulation objects (Table I).
 - **Object Position and Lighting Condition:** MPI surpasses Voltron and MVP under variations in object position and lighting, showing greater resilience to environmental changes (Table II).

#### 4.2. Visuomotor Control on Franka Kitchen Simulation

- MPI significantly outperforms baselines like R3M, MVP, and Voltron, as well as general computer vision pre-training methods (ImageNet, CLIP).
- With a ViT-Small backbone, MPI shows a **+6%** average success rate improvement over Voltron (76.5% vs. 70.5%).
- With a ViT-Base backbone, this advantage increases to **+7.2%** over Voltron (78.9% vs. 71.7%), maintaining optimal or near-optimal performance across all five complex kitchen tasks (Table III).
- **Robustness Evaluation:** MPI also demonstrates superior robustness against background distractions and varying lighting conditions in Franka Kitchen, outperforming MVP by an average of **3%** and nearly doubling the success rate in darker lighting (Table IV).

#### 4.3. Visuomotor Control on Meta-World Simulation

- In tasks with randomly varying object locations (e.g., "assemble ring," "pick & place block"), MPI with a smaller ViT-Small backbone achieves the highest average success rate (**85.7%**), surpassing MVP (ViT-Base) and leading Voltron (ViT-Small) by **17%**. This highlights its effective generalization to positional object movement (Table V).

#### 4.4. Referring Expression Grounding (R.E.G.)

- This task evaluates "where-to-interact" understanding. MPI achieves superior Average Precision (AP) at various IoU thresholds compared to R3M, MVP, and Voltron.
- With aggregated embeddings, MPI shows improvements of **+3.22%, +6.78%, and +11.5%** over MVP at AP@0.25, @0.5, and @0.75 IoU, respectively (Table VI). This demonstrates the effectiveness of its interaction-oriented learning target in capturing object-centric semantics and spatial relationships.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Further Analysis

- **Input Frames:** Using key interaction states (initial, transition, final) significantly outperforms sequential frames. Alternating between transition and final frames as prediction targets (p=0.5) further boosts performance, supporting the holistic understanding of interaction (Table VIIa).
- **Decoder Design:** Both the Prediction Transformer and Detection Transformer are indispensable. Using either alone improves performance (e.g., +12% success rate in Franka Kitchen), but their synergistic integration yields optimal results, confirming the necessity of interaction-oriented prediction and detection (Table VIIb).
- **Causality Modeling:** The causality modeling module using deformable attention, which captures relationships between two input states, is beneficial for manipulation tasks, especially for Franka Kitchen performance and robustness (Table VIIc).
- **Involvement of Language:** While optional for downstream tasks, including language in pre-training provides a modest **+2%** increase in Franka Kitchen success rate by highlighting relevant visual features (Table VIId). Even without language, MPI outperforms other methods.
- **Data Regime:** MPI shows consistent performance improvement with increased pre-training data scale, indicating potential for further enhancement with larger datasets (Fig. 6). Notably, MPI achieves superior results with a smaller pre-training dataset (Ego4D HoI subset, 93K clips) compared to methods using millions of clips from multiple datasets (Appendix B, Table A-II, A-III).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MPI framework represents a significant step forward in representation learning for robotic manipulation, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Interaction Understanding:** By explicitly modeling "how-to-interact" (transition frame prediction) and "where-to-interact" (interaction object detection), MPI fills a critical gap in existing methods. This deeper comprehension of interactive dynamics is fundamental for robots to perform complex manipulation tasks requiring precise and context-aware interactions.
2. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** MPI consistently achieves superior performance across a diverse range of benchmarks, including real-world robotic platforms, complex simulation environments (Franka Kitchen, Meta-World), and language-conditioned object grounding tasks. This establishes MPI as a new state-of-the-art in interaction-oriented representation learning.
3. **Robustness and Generalizability:** The demonstrated robustness against background distractions, object variations, positional shifts, and lighting changes is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments. MPI's ability to generalize to unseen scenarios and objects highlights the quality of its learned representations.
4. **Sample Efficiency:** By acquiring highly informative representations during pre-training, MPI enables more sample-efficient learning in downstream tasks, which is vital given the high cost of collecting robot demonstration data. This could accelerate the development and deployment of robotic systems.
5. **Unified Prediction-Detection Framework:** The synergistic integration of prediction and detection transformers, with their mutual reinforcement, provides a novel and effective architecture for learning manipulation-specific visual features. This dual objective ensures that the learned representations encode both the process and the targets of interaction.
6. **Foundation for Future Robotics:** The interaction-oriented visual features learned by MPI have the potential to serve as a stronger visual backbone for various advanced robotic applications. For instance, the authors suggest its utility in enhancing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for long-horizon planning and causal reasoning, a critical area for future robotic intelligence.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the reliance on explicit annotations (keyframes, language, bounding boxes) from datasets like Ego4D-HoI. This restricts applicability to broader datasets. Future work could explore leveraging automated annotation tools or web-crawled images to scale up pre-training data. Additionally, the current policy model's reliance on limited expert demonstrations restricts generalization. Integrating MPI's pre-trained model into larger vision-language models could address long-horizon planning and causal reasoning, pushing the boundaries of autonomous manipulation.
