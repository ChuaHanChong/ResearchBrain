# EnerVerse-AC: Envisioning Embodied Environments with Action Condition

> [!summary] Summary
> ENERVERSE-AC (EVAC), developed by AgiBot, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and the Chinese University of Hong Kong, introduces an action-conditional world model that generates realistic video observations for robotic manipulation policies. This model functions as both a data engine for policy learning and an evaluator for trained policies, enabling cost-effective and scalable testing by showing high correlation between its evaluations and real-world performance.

## Problem

- Developing and evaluating robotic manipulation policies traditionally requires resource-intensive physical robots or 3D simulations that often lack real-world physics and visual fidelity.
- Most existing video generation models lack the ability to condition generated content on specific robot actions, limiting their utility for policy development and evaluation.
- There is a need for cost-effective and scalable methods to acquire diverse training data and perform iterative policy testing without extensive physical robot usage.

## Method

- ENERVERSE-AC employs a UNet-based video generation architecture with a chunk-wise ==autoregressive== ==diffusion model== for long-horizon sequences and a sparse memory mechanism for visual stability.
- It incorporates multi-level action condition injection using spatial-aware pose injection and a Delta Action Attention Module to precisely reflect robot movements.
- Multi-view scene representation is achieved via spatial ==cross-attention== modules for inter-view consistency and ray direction map encoding for camera motion context across different viewpoints.

## Results

- The model produces high-fidelity, visually realistic videos that accurately follow input action trajectories and maintain visual stability over long-horizon sequences.
- Evaluations show a high correlation between ENERVERSE-AC assessments and real-world policy performance, confirming its utility for predicting effectiveness.
- Policies trained with ENERVERSE-AC augmented trajectories achieved significantly higher success rates compared to those trained solely on original data, demonstrating improved policy learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly conditioning video generation on robot actions and integrating multi-view consistency is critical for creating useful virtual environments for embodied robotics.
> - Training a world model on both successful and failure trajectories allows it to realistically represent diverse interaction outcomes, improving its utility for policy evaluation.
> - A generative world model can function dually as a data engine to augment limited human-collected trajectories and as an evaluator for iterative policy testing, reducing reliance on physical hardware.
