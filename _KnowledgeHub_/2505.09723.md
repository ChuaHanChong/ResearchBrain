# EnerVerse-AC: Envisioning Embodied Environments with Action Condition

> [!summary] Summary
> ENERVERSE-AC (EVAC), developed by AgiBot, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and the Chinese University of Hong Kong, introduces an action-conditional world model that generates realistic video observations for robotic manipulation policies. This model functions as both a data engine for policy learning and an evaluator for trained policies, enabling cost-effective and scalable testing by showing high correlation between its evaluations and real-world performance.

## Problem

- Developing and evaluating robotic manipulation policies traditionally requires resource-intensive physical robots or 3D simulations that often lack real-world physics and visual fidelity.
- Most existing video generation models lack the ability to condition generated content on specific robot actions, limiting their utility for policy development and evaluation.
- There is a need for cost-effective and scalable methods to acquire diverse training data and perform iterative policy testing without extensive physical robot usage.

## Method

- ENERVERSE-AC employs a UNet-based video generation architecture with a chunk-wise ==autoregressive== ==diffusion model== for long-horizon sequences and a sparse memory mechanism for visual stability.
- It incorporates multi-level action condition injection using spatial-aware pose injection and a Delta Action Attention Module to precisely reflect robot movements.
- Multi-view scene representation is achieved via spatial ==cross-attention== modules for inter-view consistency and ray direction map encoding for camera motion context across different viewpoints.

## Results

- The model produces high-fidelity, visually realistic videos that accurately follow input action trajectories and maintain visual stability over long-horizon sequences.
- Evaluations show a high correlation between ENERVERSE-AC assessments and real-world policy performance, confirming its utility for predicting effectiveness.
- Policies trained with ENERVERSE-AC augmented trajectories achieved significantly higher success rates compared to those trained solely on original data, demonstrating improved policy learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly conditioning video generation on robot actions and integrating multi-view consistency is critical for creating useful virtual environments for embodied robotics.
> - Training a world model on both successful and failure trajectories allows it to realistically represent diverse interaction outcomes, improving its utility for policy evaluation.
> - A generative world model can function dually as a data engine to augment limited human-collected trajectories and as an evaluator for iterative policy testing, reducing reliance on physical hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Developing and evaluating robotic manipulation policies is a resource-intensive process that traditionally requires either physical robots or complex 3D simulations. Both approaches present significant challenges: physical testing is costly and time-consuming, while simulations often fail to capture the complexities of real-world physics and visual appearance. ENERVERSE-AC (EVAC) addresses these challenges by introducing an action-conditional world model capable of generating realistic video observations based on a robot's predicted actions.

This paper, authored by researchers from AgiBot, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and the Chinese University of Hong Kong, presents a novel approach to create a virtual testing environment for robotic policies that maintains visual fidelity while accurately representing the consequences of robot actions. By functioning as both a data engine for policy learning and an evaluator for trained policy models, ENERVERSE-AC offers a more scalable and cost-effective alternative to traditional testing methods.

### 2. Background and Motivation

In recent years, video generation models have emerged as promising alternatives to traditional robotic simulation environments. However, most existing models lack the ability to condition the generated content on specific robot actions, limiting their utility for policy development and evaluation. ENERVERSE-AC addresses this gap by explicitly incorporating action conditioning into the video generation process.

The key motivations behind this work include:

1. Reducing the cost and complexity of testing robotic manipulation policies
2. Developing an action-conditional world model for realistic and controllable inference
3. Improving policy generalization through diverse training data augmentation
4. Creating a data engine for expanding limited human-collected trajectories
5. Providing an evaluator for iterative policy testing without physical robot hardware

The approach builds upon advances in video generation techniques while focusing specifically on the requirements of robotic manipulation tasks, which demand precise action conditioning and multi-view generation capabilities.

### 3. ENERVERSE-AC Model Architecture

ENERVERSE-AC employs a UNet-based video generation architecture that extends previous work in the field. The model is designed to handle the specific challenges of robotics applications, including:

- Long-horizon generation for robotics tasks
- Multi-view scene representation for embodied environments
- Action conditioning for robot movements
- Visual stability and scene consistency during inference

The architecture incorporates specialized modules to address these challenges:

1. A chunk-wise autoregressive diffusion model for generating long-horizon videos
2. Multi-level action condition injection to incorporate robot actions
3. Multi-view condition injection for generating synchronized views from different cameras
4. A sparse memory mechanism to maintain visual stability over extended sequences

The model is trained on a combination of successful and failure trajectories, with the latter being particularly important for learning to recognize and represent unsuccessful interactions realistically.

### 4. Action Condition Injection

A key innovation in ENERVERSE-AC is its approach to action condition injection, which ensures that generated videos accurately reflect the robot's intended actions. The system incorporates two complementary mechanisms:

1. **Spatial-Aware Pose Injection**: This converts end-effector positions to pixel coordinates and uses visual prompting techniques to represent the end-effector's orientation. The mathematical formulation can be expressed as:

 ```
   P_pixel = K * [R|t] * P_world
   ```

 Where P_pixel represents the pixel coordinates, K is the camera intrinsic matrix, [R|t] is the camera extrinsic matrix, and P_world is the world coordinates.

2. **Delta Action Attention Module**: This calculates the delta motion between consecutive frames and integrates this information through cross-attention. The module processes temporal changes using:

 ```
   Δa_t = a_t - a_{t-1}
   ```

 These delta actions are then encoded and integrated via cross-attention mechanisms:

 ```
   Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QK^T/√d) * V
   ```

This dual approach allows the model to understand both the absolute position of the robot and the relative changes in its configuration, enabling more accurate video generation that properly reflects robot movements.

### 5. Multi-View Condition Injection

Robotic tasks typically require multiple camera viewpoints to provide comprehensive visual information. ENERVERSE-AC addresses this need through:

1. **Spatial Cross-Attention Modules**: These enable interaction between different camera views, allowing the model to maintain consistency across perspectives.

2. **Ray Direction Map Encoding**: This provides spatial context for each view and encodes camera motion, which is especially important for dynamic wrist camera views that move with the robot arm.

The multi-view approach is essential for embodied tasks, as it allows the model to generate complete visual information similar to what a real robot would perceive, including fixed external cameras and movable wrist-mounted cameras.

### 6. Applications in Robotics

ENERVERSE-AC serves two primary functions in the robotics pipeline:

1. **Data Engine for Policy Learning**: The model augments limited human-collected trajectories by:
 - Segmenting actions into meaningful units
 - Applying spatial augmentations (e.g., rotations, translations)
 - Generating new video sequences conditioned on these modified actions

 This data augmentation process produces diverse training examples that help improve policy robustness and generalization.

2. **Evaluator for Policy Models**: The model generates realistic, action-conditioned video observations for iterative policy testing. This enables:
 - Rapid assessment of policy performance without physical robot hardware
 - Visualization of potential failure modes
 - Iterative refinement of policies before deployment on real robots

These applications significantly reduce the resources required for robotic policy development while improving the quality and generalization capability of the resulting policies.

### 7. Experimental Results

The experimental evaluation of ENERVERSE-AC demonstrates several important capabilities:

1. **High-Fidelity Video Generation**: The model produces visually realistic videos that accurately follow input action trajectories, maintaining proper physical interactions between the robot and objects.

2. **Visual Stability During Long-Horizon Generation**: The chunk-wise autoregressive architecture and sparse memory mechanism enable the model to generate sharp and reliable videos for extended sequences, avoiding the degradation common in many video generation models.

3. **Accurate Performance Reflection**: Evaluation results show a high correlation between ENERVERSE-AC evaluations and real-world performance, confirming the model's utility for predicting policy effectiveness.

4. **Improved Policy Learning**: Policies trained with ENERVERSE-AC augmented trajectories showed significantly higher success rates compared to those trained only on original data. This demonstrates the value of the model's data augmentation capabilities.

5. **Failure Mode Representation**: The inclusion of failure data in training allows the model to recognize and represent failed grasp attempts and other common failure modes, making it more robust against overfitting to successful trajectories.

These results validate the effectiveness of ENERVERSE-AC as both a training data augmentation tool and a policy evaluation platform.

### 8. Significance and Impact

ENERVERSE-AC represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic imitation learning with several important implications:

1. **Cost and Time Reduction**: By providing a realistic and controllable simulation environment, the model reduces the need for extensive testing on physical robots, cutting both costs and development time.

2. **Improved Policy Generalization**: The ability to generate diverse training data helps policies generalize to new scenarios and handle unexpected situations more effectively.

3. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**: The realistic video generation capabilities help bridge the gap between simulation and reality, potentially facilitating easier transfer of policies from virtual to physical environments.

4. **Enabling New Applications**: The model's capabilities could enable new applications in robotics, such as rapid prototyping of manipulation strategies for novel tasks or environments.

5. **Resource for the Research Community**: By making the code, checkpoints, and datasets available, the authors provide a valuable resource for other researchers working on similar problems.

The approach taken in ENERVERSE-AC offers a complementary alternative to traditional simulation methods, with particular advantages in visual realism and ease of deployment.

### 9. Conclusion

ENERVERSE-AC introduces a promising approach to robotic policy development and evaluation through action-conditional video generation. By addressing the key challenges of action conditioning and multi-view generation, the model provides a practical tool for researchers and developers working on robotic manipulation tasks.

The model's dual role as both a data engine for policy learning and an evaluator for trained policies makes it a versatile addition to the robotics toolkit. Its ability to generate realistic videos conditioned on robot actions enables more efficient policy development cycles and reduces the resource requirements for robotic research.

As the field continues to advance, approaches like ENERVERSE-AC may become increasingly important in bridging the gap between simulation and reality, ultimately accelerating progress in robotic manipulation capabilities. The integration of such world models into the robotics development pipeline represents a promising direction for future research and application development.
