# HandEdit: A Unified Benchmark for Egocentric Human-to-Robot Dexterous Hand Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> HandEdit is a large-scale dataset and benchmark for editing egocentric human hand-object images into robot-embodied ones. A curation pipeline segments and inpaints the human hand, retargets the pose to a target URDF, renders and composites the robot, then harmonizes the result as pseudo-ground-truth. It spans 200M editing instances across 26 embodiments, with two tracks and embodiment-aware metrics benchmarking 11 image editors.

## Problem

- Robot teleoperation and UMI-style data collection are costly and hardware-bound, while abundant egocentric human video cannot be used directly because of the human-robot embodiment gap.
- Prior human-video robotization pipelines insert rendered grippers on fixed platforms with embodiment-specific code, so they never generalize across many dexterous hand and hand-arm morphologies.
- General image-editing benchmarks lack egocentric input, dexterous hands, and URDF conditioning, and their similarity or CLIP metrics never test embodiment identity or contact preservation.

## Method

- A staged curation pipeline: ==SAM3 segmentation==, ==ProPainter background inpainting==, ==MANO-to-URDF retargeting==, camera-matched rendering and compositing, then a self-supervised ==Harmonizer== module producing kinematically grounded ==pseudo-ground-truth== targets.
- For hand-arm targets, a camera-relative virtual base is fixed per sequence via ==embodiment-adaptive local search== over translation and yaw, ranked by ==IK feasibility==, joint limits, and collision checks.
- Two benchmark tracks (Hand-only, Hand-Arm) plus a metric suite: generic similarity, ==GPT-4o SC/PQ judgment==, and embodiment-aware skin-residue removal, ==DINOv2== structure, ==CLIP+CIELAB identity==, ==LPIPS interaction scores==.

## Results

- GPT-Image-2 leads Hand-only with **0.780** structural fidelity, **0.703** interaction, **19.85** full PSNR and best ROI LPIPS **0.482**, beating open-source editors like OmniGen2 (**0.266** interaction).
- VLM judgment diverges from embodiment metrics: GPT-Image-1.5 scores highest VLM (**0.765**) yet only **0.435** interaction; FireRed leads perceptual quality (**0.754**) with the worst semantic consistency (**0.228**).
- The Hand-Arm track is harder: best ID fidelity falls to **0.563** versus **0.852** Hand-only, while blinded human raters show Krippendorff's alpha above **0.60** and metric-human Fleiss kappa **0.554-0.586**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-hand removal is nearly solved (scores above 0.90 everywhere); the hard part is synthesizing a specific target morphology and keeping contact geometry intact.
> - Perceptual realism and VLM judges are poor proxies for embodiment correctness; geometry-grounded metrics anchored to a rendered target are needed for embodied data generation.
> - Rendering a URDF as a visual condition is a cheap, general way to specify embodiment, but pseudo-ground-truth built by compositing inherits retargeting and inpainting errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in HandEdit is situated at the intersection of Embodied AI (EAI), dexterous robotic manipulation, and image editing. Advancements in EAI, particularly for dexterous manipulation, rely on large-scale, diverse datasets. Current data acquisition methods for robotic manipulation, such as robotic teleoperation, are often costly, time-consuming, and platform-specific. While Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI)-style data collection offers improved scalability, it still necessitates specialized equipment and embodiment-specific demonstrations.

Egocentric human videos, which capture a wealth of hand-object interactions in natural environments, represent a potentially abundant and scalable data source for learning dexterous manipulation policies. However, directly utilizing these human-centric videos for robot learning is challenging due to a significant "embodiment gap." This gap arises from fundamental discrepancies between human and robotic dexterous hands in terms of appearance, geometry, articulation, kinematic constraints, and interaction patterns. These differences are further amplified in hand-arm systems, where consistency of the wrist and arm morphology with the target robot and camera viewpoint becomes critical.

Existing general image-editing models, despite their capabilities, lack the embodiment-specific priors required to bridge this human-robot visual gap effectively. For embodied learning, an edited image must not only transform the human hand into a robot hand but also maintain the original scene context, object state, task semantics, and crucially, the precise hand-object contact. The replacement robot must be physically plausible, respecting the geometry and articulation defined by its Unified Robot Description Format (URDF) while cohering with the manipulation context.

Prior research has explored "robotizing" human videos through rendering and compositing. However, these efforts have largely focused on simpler gripper types, fixed robot platforms, or specific, often handcrafted, pipelines. A systematic investigation into human-to-robot dexterous hand editing as a general image-editing problem has been lacking. Specifically, there has been no dedicated large-scale dataset, unified evaluation protocol, or comprehensive benchmark capable of assessing URDF-conditioned editing quality across a diverse range of dexterous hand and hand-arm embodiments. HandEdit addresses this gap by providing these critical resources, thereby facilitating the development of advanced image editing models tailored for robotic applications and enabling scalable data generation for embodied learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of HandEdit is to introduce a unified, large-scale, embodiment-aware image-editing dataset and benchmark specifically designed for transforming egocentric images of human hands and arms into various dexterous robotic embodiments. This overarching goal is motivated by several detailed considerations:

1. **Addressing the Embodiment Gap for Scalable Data**: The high cost and hardware specificity of collecting teleoperation data for dexterous robots significantly impede progress in Embodied AI. Egocentric human videos offer a readily available, diverse, and scalable alternative. However, the profound visual, kinematic, and articulatory discrepancies between human and robotic hands (the "embodiment gap") prevent their direct use. HandEdit aims to bridge this gap by enabling the conversion of human manipulation footage into robot-centric observations, thus providing a scalable source of data for robotic policy learning.

2. **Enabling URDF-Conditioned Dexterous Manipulation Transfer**: The visual transformation must be precise and functionally relevant for robotics. Edited images need to preserve critical elements like the scene, object state, task semantics, and hand-object contact, while depicting a physically plausible robot embodiment consistent with a specified URDF. Current image-editing models often lack this embodiment-specific conditioning. HandEdit explicitly provides a framework for URDF-conditioned editing, which is essential for grounding the visual transformation in robotic kinematics and physics.

3. **Systematic Study of Human-to-Robot Dexterous Hand Editing**: The task of human-to-robot dexterous hand editing has not been systematically studied as a distinct image-editing problem. There has been an absence of a standardized dataset, unified evaluation protocol, and comprehensive benchmark. HandEdit is motivated to fill this void by providing a controlled environment for researching and developing models capable of this specialized form of image manipulation.

4. **Establishing a Unified Benchmark for Diverse Embodiments**: To facilitate research, a standardized evaluation framework is necessary. HandEdit establishes a unified benchmark with two distinct tracks: "Hand-only" and "Hand-Arm," accommodating 26 diverse URDF-specified robotic embodiments (13 hand-only and 13 hand-arm configurations). This enables systematic comparison of editing models across a wide range of robot morphologies and complexities.

5. **Developing Comprehensive Evaluation Metrics**: Generic image similarity metrics are insufficient to assess the success of embodiment-aware editing, as they do not capture aspects critical for robotics, such as embodiment fidelity, interaction preservation, and physical plausibility. The motivation behind HandEdit includes designing a multi-dimensional metric suite that integrates generic similarity, VLM-based judgment, and specific embodiment-aware metrics to provide a more faithful and functionally relevant assessment of editing quality.

By achieving these objectives, HandEdit aims to serve as a critical resource that advances embodiment-aware editing models and enables scalable dexterous robotic learning from abundant human video data, ultimately paving the way for more generalizable Embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of HandEdit involves a multi-stage data curation pipeline, the creation of a large-scale dataset, the definition of a unified benchmark protocol, and the development of a comprehensive suite of evaluation metrics.

#### 3.1. Data Curation Pipeline

The pipeline converts egocentric human hand-object clips into pseudo-ground truth (pseudo-GT) robot-embodied images through a series of processing steps:

1. **Human Hand/Arm Segmentation and Removal**: Off-the-shelf segmentation models, such as SAM3, are used to segment the human hand or hand-arm region. This segmented foreground is then removed from the original image.
2. **Background Restoration**: Video inpainting models, specifically ProPainter, are employed to restore the occluded background. This step ensures appearance and temporal consistency in the regions previously covered by the human hand.
3. **Kinematic Retargeting**:
 - **Dexterous Hands**: Human MANO or 3D hand poses are converted into target robot joint states. This involves optimization- and geometry-based retargeting, which first achieves a coarse alignment by considering differences in kinematic trees, link lengths, joint limits, and contact geometry between human and robot hands. A subsequent refinement step uses position-based retargeting under embodiment-specific kinematic constraints to improve local structural consistency and preserve the hand-object interaction layout.
 - **Hand-Arm Embodiments**: For hand-arm scenarios, the retargeted hand pose is extended to the supporting arm. A camera-relative virtual base is defined for each source sequence and target embodiment, remaining fixed throughout the sequence. An embodiment-adaptive local search is performed for the base's horizontal translation and yaw, initialized using shoulder keypoints or wrist trajectory. Candidate bases are evaluated based on Inverse Kinematics (IK) feasibility, joint limits, collision checks, and trajectory quality. A human operator then selects one of the top three plausible sequence-level solutions.
4. **Robot Rendering and Compositing**: The target URDF, with the retargeted robot pose, is rendered under the egocentric camera view of the original clip. This rendered robot foreground is then composited into the restored background.
5. **Harmonization Post-processing**: The initial composite may exhibit visual inconsistencies (color, illumination, contrast) between the rendered robot and the restored background. A lightweight image-harmonization module, based on Harmonizer, is applied. This module, trained self-supervisedly on egocentric hand images, refines the appearance of the robot foreground and its boundaries to ensure visual integration without altering robot joint configurations or object states. The harmonized output serves as the final pseudo-GT reference.

**Quality Control**: Throughout the pipeline, automatic checks and human screening are implemented at each stage (segmentation, background restoration, retargeting, IK/virtual-base placement, rendering/compositing, and harmonization) to identify and reject samples that fail specific quality criteria.

#### 3.2. HandEdit Dataset

The HandEdit dataset is compiled from the egocentric portions of five publicly available hand-object datasets: EgoDex, ARCTIC, OakInk2, HOI4D, and HO-Cap. These sources contribute diversity in scale, scene types, interaction patterns, and capture quality.
- **Scale**: The dataset comprises over 200 million image-level editing instances, derived from over 300,000 clips, spanning more than 600 scenes, 1,100 objects, and 400 tasks.
- **Embodiment Coverage**: It covers 26 target URDF embodiments, including 13 hand-only and 13 hand-arm configurations. These URDFs represent various hand families (e.g., Ability, Allegro, Shadow Hand) and arm platforms (e.g., Jaka, KUKA, Panda, UR5, xArm). Visual assets for these URDFs are standardized to ensure consistent rendering.
- **Utility**: The dataset provides paired human-source and robot-embodied images, suitable for both training and evaluating human-to-robot editing models.

#### 3.3. HandEdit Benchmark

The benchmark defines two tracks for human-to-robot dexterous hand editing:
1. **Hand-only Track**: Focuses on editing the local hand region, replacing the human hand with a dexterous robotic hand while preserving the hand-object interaction.
2. **Hand-Arm Track**: A more challenging task that additionally edits the forearm or full arm, demanding larger-region background preservation, plausible arm kinematics, and consistent hand-object-scene composition.
Both tracks support URDF-conditioned evaluation, allowing systematic comparison across diverse robotic embodiments using standardized instruction templates.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics

HandEdit employs a multi-dimensional metric suite to assess both generic visual fidelity and embodiment-specific correctness:

1. **Generic Similarity Metrics**: These metrics quantify low-level and perceptual similarity between the edited image and the pseudo-GT target across three regions: the full image, the edited Region of Interest (ROI), and the background.
 - PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio): Measures pixel-level reconstruction fidelity.
 - SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure): Evaluates structural similarity.
 - LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity): Measures perceptual similarity using deep features.
 - FID (Fréchet Inception Distance): Compares the distributional realism of generated images against reference images.

2. **VLM-based Judgment (GPT-4o)**: A protocol using a large vision-language model (GPT-4o) to evaluate semantic-level aspects.
 - **Semantic Consistency (SC)**: Assesses whether the edited result accurately reflects the requested robot embodiment and preserves task conditions (robotness, target-embodiment match, interaction preservation, scene preservation).
 - **Perceptual Quality (PQ)**: Evaluates the visual naturalness and local coherence of the edited image (naturalness, artifact absence, local coherence).
 - The final VLM score is calculated as $\sqrt{\text{SC} \cdot \text{PQ}}$.

3. **Embodiment-Aware Metrics**: These metrics specifically assess correctness related to robotic embodiment and interaction.
 - **Human-hand Removal**: Quantifies the absence of residual human-hand artifacts (e.g., exposed skin) within the edited ROI.
 - **Target-robot Fidelity**: Decomposed into:
 - *Structural Fidelity*: DINOv2 feature similarity between the edited robot ROI and the pseudo-GT robot ROI, measuring geometric consistency.
 - *ID Fidelity*: CLIP feature similarity between the edited robot ROI and a URDF reference render bank of the requested embodiment, combined with CIELAB color distance to the pseudo-GT, to assess identity and local color consistency.
 - **Interaction Consistency**: Measures the preservation of the manipulated object's appearance and local contact neighborhood, using LPIPS on a dilated object-contact region.

#### 3.5. Benchmark Execution

Eleven representative commercial and open-source image editors were benchmarked. Models were evaluated using their native conditioning interfaces and fixed prompt templates. Commercial API-based models were assessed within a consistent timeframe to minimize the impact of API-side updates, while open-source models used official checkpoints and standardized inference settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The benchmark conducted extensive evaluations of 11 image-editing baselines across the Hand-only and Hand-Arm tracks, employing a multi-dimensional metric suite.

**Key Observations from Benchmark Results:**

1. **GPT-Image-2 as a Strong Baseline**: GPT-Image-2 consistently demonstrated strong performance across both generic similarity and embodiment-aware metrics. It achieved the highest LPIPS(ROI) and FID(ROI) scores on the Hand-only track, indicating superior local editing quality within the manipulated region. Furthermore, it attained the highest structural fidelity and interaction scores, suggesting a robust capability in matching the target robot embodiment and preserving hand-object interactions. On the Hand-Arm track, GPT-Image-2 also showed strong performance, achieving the highest or second-highest scores in multiple metrics, including PSNR(Full), LPIPS(ROI), Removal, Structural Fidelity, and SC.

2. **Limitations of VLM-based Judgment Alone**: While VLM-based judgment (Semantic Consistency and Perceptual Quality) provides valuable high-level feedback, it was not always sufficient for a comprehensive evaluation of embodied editing tasks. For instance, GPT-Image-1.5 achieved the highest VLM score on both tracks, but did not necessarily excel in the embodiment-aware metrics such as structural fidelity or interaction preservation. This discrepancy indicates that VLMs, in their current form, tend to capture high-level semantic consistency and general perceptual quality, but may be less sensitive to fine-grained robotic morphology and contact-level interaction details, which are more accurately reflected by the specialized embodiment-aware metrics.

3. **Perceptual Quality Does Not Guarantee Task Success**: The evaluations revealed that high perceptual quality (PQ) in an edited image does not automatically translate to successful fulfillment of the embodied editing task. FireRed-Image-Edit-1.1, for example, achieved the highest PQ score on both tracks, suggesting its outputs often appeared visually plausible and natural. However, its scores for Semantic Consistency (SC) and other embodiment-aware metrics (e.g., structural fidelity, ID fidelity, interaction preservation) were comparatively low. This finding underscores that visually natural edits might still fail to accurately represent the target robot embodiment or preserve the intended manipulation interaction, highlighting the necessity of domain-specific metrics beyond general perceptual realism for evaluating embodied image editing.

4. **Embodiment-Aware Editing as the Core Challenge**: The benchmark indicated that removing the source human hand (Human-hand Removal score) was generally achievable for most models, with relatively high scores across the board. In contrast, metrics related to generating the correct robot embodiment (structural fidelity, ID fidelity) and maintaining physically plausible interaction with objects (interaction preservation) exhibited larger performance gaps among models. This suggests that the primary challenge in human-to-robot dexterous hand editing lies in the faithful generation of the specified robotic embodiment and its coherent integration into the manipulation context, rather than simply erasing the human hand.

5. **Human Evaluation Validation**: A blinded human evaluation study was conducted to corroborate the automatic metrics. The study involved five annotators rating edited outputs on three axes: target-robot correctness, scene-and-interaction preservation, and realism with physical plausibility. The inter-annotator agreement was found to be reliable (Krippendorff’s $\alpha$ > 0.60, ICC(2,k) > 0.83). Furthermore, the agreement between human judgments and the corresponding automatic metrics (ID Fidelity for target-robot correctness, Interaction for scene-and-interaction preservation, and VLM score for realism) was consistent (Fleiss’ $\kappa$ ranging from 0.554 to 0.586). These results support the design and validity of HandEdit's proposed metric suite.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HandEdit represents a significant contribution to both the Embodied AI and image editing communities through its introduction of a unified benchmark and large-scale dataset for egocentric human-to-robot dexterous hand image editing.

**Advancing Embodied AI:**
- **Scalable Data Generation**: The high cost and hardware specificity of collecting real-world robot teleoperation data are major bottlenecks in Embodied AI development. HandEdit provides a mechanism to leverage abundant and diverse egocentric human videos by transforming them into robot-centric observations. This capability offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative for generating training data, which can reduce the reliance on expensive real-robot demonstrations for policy pre-training and co-training.
- **Bridging the Embodiment Gap**: By systematically addressing the visual and kinematic discrepancies between human and robot hands, HandEdit enables the development of models that can bridge this critical "embodiment gap." This is essential for transferring human manipulation skills and experiences to robots, fostering more generalizable and capable embodied agents.
- **Foundation for Robot Learning**: The paired human-source and robot-embodied images in HandEdit provide foundational data for training new models that can directly convert human manipulation frames into a format consumable by robots. This facilitates research into robot-centric data generation and dexterous embodiment transfer.

**Advancing Image Editing:**
- **Specialized Editing Task**: HandEdit defines and systematically studies a novel and challenging image-editing task: URDF-conditioned human-to-robot dexterous hand image editing. This moves beyond generic image manipulation by incorporating complex robotic kinematic and physical constraints, pushing the boundaries of controllable image editing.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation**: The multi-dimensional metric suite, combining generic visual similarity, VLM-based judgment, and embodiment-aware metrics, offers a more rigorous and functionally relevant assessment of editing quality. This suite helps identify specialized failure modes (e.g., embodiment mismatch, interaction failure) that are critical for robotic applications but not captured by traditional metrics, guiding the development of more robust editing models.
- **Benchmarking Current Capabilities**: The comprehensive benchmarking of 11 representative commercial and open-source image editors provides a clear empirical analysis of the current state-of-the-art and highlights specific limitations in existing general-purpose editors when applied to this specialized domain. This offers valuable insights for future research directions.

**Broader Impact:**
HandEdit is designed to serve as a standardized testbed, fostering interdisciplinary research between the fields of computer vision, robotics, and machine learning. By providing a clear framework for evaluation and a rich dataset, it can accelerate the development of advanced robot manipulation capabilities and more adaptable Embodied AI systems. The explicit focus on embodiment fidelity and interaction preservation ensures that advancements are functionally relevant and contribute to building robots that can effectively interact with the physical world. While the dataset uses pseudo-references due to the impossibility of pixel-aligned human-robot pairs in physical capture, it aims to provide scalable pre-training data that complements, rather than replaces, real-robot demonstrations.
