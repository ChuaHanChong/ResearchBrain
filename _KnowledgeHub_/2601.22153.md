# DynamicVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model for Dynamic Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DynamicVLA, from S-Lab at Nanyang Technological University, introduces a compact 0.4B parameter vision-language-action model alongside the Dynamic Object Manipulation (DOM) benchmark, enabling robots to robustly manipulate moving objects in real-world scenarios. The model achieved an average 47.06% success rate on the DOM simulation benchmark, notably outperforming prior baselines, and demonstrated consistent performance on physical robots.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily focus on static manipulation, struggling with the complexities of dynamic objects in real-world environments.
- High inference latency in current VLA models creates a "perception–execution gap," leading to outdated actions and task failures when objects are in continuous motion.
- There is a lack of large-scale, standardized datasets and benchmarks specifically designed for evaluating VLA models in dynamic object manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Developed **DynamicVLA**, a compact ==0.4-billion-parameter VLA model== leveraging a ==convolutional vision encoder (FastViT)== and a ==truncated language backbone== for improved efficiency and speed.
- Introduced two execution mechanisms: =="Continuous Inference"== to pipeline inference and execution, and =="Latent-aware Action Streaming"== to explicitly manage temporal misalignment by prioritizing the most recent action predictions.
- Created the ==Dynamic Object Manipulation (DOM) benchmark==, a large-scale dataset with ==200K synthetic== and ==2K real-world episodes==, collected via automated pipelines to provide diverse dynamic tasks for evaluation.

## Results

- **DynamicVLA** achieved an average success rate of **47.06%** on the DOM simulation benchmark, significantly surpassing the best baseline (GR00T-N1.5 at **13.05%**) and reducing task completion time to **8.53 seconds**.
- In real-world evaluations, DynamicVLA consistently outperformed baselines across various dynamic tasks on Franka and PiPER robots, achieving success rates ranging from **50.0%** to **78.3%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the crucial role of Continuous Inference (increasing SR by **10.95%**) and Latent-aware Action Streaming (increasing SR by **7.34%**) in mitigating latency and temporal misalignment, respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing inference latency and explicitly addressing temporal misalignment are critical factors for achieving robust and responsive dynamic object manipulation with VLA models.
> - Compact model architectures (0.4B parameters) can effectively achieve high performance in dynamic tasks when paired with efficient execution mechanisms, demonstrating that large parameter counts are not always necessary.
> - Automated data collection, including a "real-world simulator" approach for physical robots, is essential for building scalable and diverse datasets for dynamic robotic tasks, bridging the sim-to-real gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly evolving with the integration of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage the representational power of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and generate appropriate actions. Prior VLA models have demonstrated remarkable generalization capabilities in **static manipulation tasks**, where objects remain fixed during the robot's perception and action cycles. Examples include tasks like pick-and-place, pushing, and assembly in controlled, unchanging environments.

However, a significant gap exists in the application of these models to **dynamic object manipulation**. Real-world environments are inherently dynamic; objects can move, be handed over, shift positions, or experience unpredictable disturbances. Manipulating such objects requires rapid perception, temporal anticipation, and continuous, low-latency control – capabilities that current VLA models often lack. The primary limitation of existing VLAs in dynamic settings stems from their **inference latency**. Many state-of-the-art VLAs rely on large, transformer-based backbones (e.g., 3B–7B parameters), leading to slow inference speeds. In dynamic scenarios, these delays cause a "perception–execution (P.E.) gap," where the robot's actions are based on outdated observations, leading to misalignment and task failure. While some works address moving targets (e.g., throwing, soccer, table tennis), they often rely on reactive control, handcrafted perception, or involve tasks tolerant to timing/spatial errors (e.g., returning a paddle to a ball over a wide area, not requiring precise 6DoF control). Efforts to extend manipulation to moving objects (e.g., DBC-TFP, GEM) are often limited to predictable, conveyor-belt–like motions.

DynamicVLA directly addresses this critical research gap by focusing on "open-ended dynamic manipulation, which involves uncertain motion, precise contact, and tight perception–action alignment." It positions itself as a unified framework that overcomes the latency and temporal misalignment issues plaguing current VLA models, thereby extending their utility from static to truly dynamic robotic interaction. Furthermore, the paper highlights the scarcity of large-scale datasets for dynamic object manipulation, an issue it tackles by introducing the **Dynamic Object Manipulation (DOM) benchmark**, a foundational contribution to accelerate research in this underserved area. This benchmark, with its automated data collection, sets a new standard for evaluating VLA models in dynamic, real-world conditions, providing a much-needed resource for consistent and comparable evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this work is to enable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to effectively manipulate **dynamic objects** in real-world scenarios. This entails addressing the fundamental challenges that prevent current VLA models, primarily designed for static environments, from performing robustly under rapidly changing conditions.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be broken down as follows:

1. **Overcome Inference Latency and Temporal Misalignment:** The core motivation is the "perception–execution gap" caused by the inference latency of existing VLA models. In dynamic environments, objects continuously move during the model's reasoning process, leading to a mismatch between the perceived state and the actual state at the time of action execution. DynamicVLA aims to minimize this gap and ensure timely adaptation to object motion.
2. **Achieve Rapid Perception and Continuous Control:** Dynamic manipulation demands real-time responsiveness. The model needs to perceive changes quickly, anticipate future object motion, and generate a continuous stream of appropriate actions. The objective is to enable the VLA model to operate with high-frequency perception and seamless action transitions.
3. **Develop a Compact and Efficient VLA Architecture:** To achieve low latency, a compact model size is crucial. The paper aims to design a VLA model that is significantly smaller and more efficient than existing large-parameter models, enabling faster inference without sacrificing multimodal reasoning capabilities essential for understanding complex instructions and visual scenes.
4. **Bridge the Perception-Execution Gap for Temporally Aligned Actions:** Beyond just reducing inference time, the work aims to develop mechanisms that explicitly address the temporal misalignment between perception and action. This means ensuring that the executed actions are always relevant to the most recent environmental state.
5. **Establish a Standardized Benchmark for Dynamic Manipulation:** Recognizing the lack of large-scale, standardized datasets for dynamic object manipulation, a critical objective is to introduce a new benchmark, DOM, that facilitates rigorous and comparable evaluation of robotic policies on moving objects. This benchmark aims to provide diverse scenarios across interaction, perception, and generalization dimensions, supported by automated data collection in both simulation and the real world.
6. **Demonstrate Robust Generalization:** The work seeks to show that the proposed DynamicVLA framework can generalize effectively to unseen objects, novel scenes, and new motion patterns, and exhibit robustness to unexpected disturbances, which are all critical for deployment in varied real-world settings.

In essence, DynamicVLA is motivated by the need for VLA models to move beyond static, structured tasks into the unpredictable, continuously evolving dynamics of real-world interaction, thereby unlocking a new frontier in robotic capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DynamicVLA addresses the challenges of dynamic object manipulation through a multi-pronged approach encompassing a novel model architecture, innovative execution mechanisms, and a dedicated benchmark.

**A. The DynamicVLA Model Architecture (Compact 0.4B VLA)**
The core of DynamicVLA is a highly compact, 0.4-billion-parameter VLA model designed for speed and efficiency.
- **Vision-Language Backbone:** It utilizes **SmolLM2-360M** as the language backbone, a relatively small language model. Crucially, it employs a **convolutional vision encoder, FastViT**, instead of transformer-based vision encoders. FastViT is chosen for its efficient spatial compression and better structural preservation, which is vital for processing multi-frame visual inputs quickly without quadratic token growth. The language backbone is further truncated to its first 16 transformer layers, as done in SmolVLA, to significantly reduce inference latency while retaining multimodal reasoning capacity.
- **Diffusion-Based Action Expert:** An action expert, instantiated as a conditional **Flow Matching Transformer**, predicts action chunks. This diffusion-style modeling is trained to denoise actions conditioned on multimodal features from the VLM backbone.
- **Multi-modal Fusion and Projection:** Lightweight linear projections are used to align representations from robot states, language instructions, and visual features into a unified multimodal feature space for the action expert.

**B. Execution Mechanisms for Low Latency and Closed-Loop Adaptation**
DynamicVLA introduces two key execution schemes to mitigate the perception-execution gap:
- **Continuous Inference:** Unlike traditional VLA models that wait for the current action sequence to finish before triggering a new inference, Continuous Inference pipelines inference and execution. A new inference cycle is triggered immediately after the previous one completes, irrespective of whether the previous action sequence is fully executed. This scheme overlaps prediction and action execution, eliminating "inter-chunk waiting" and maintaining a continuous action stream, thereby enhancing responsiveness.
- **Latent-aware Action Streaming (LAAS):** This mechanism explicitly handles temporal misalignment caused by inference delay. If an action sequence A_t is predicted at time *t* but becomes available only at *t+m* (due to inference delay *m*), the actions corresponding to timesteps *t* through *t+m-1* are "outdated." LAAS discards these outdated actions and begins execution from *a_{t+m}*. Furthermore, when new action sequences (e.g., A_{t+m}) are generated while an older sequence (A_t) is still being executed, LAAS prioritizes actions from the *newer* sequence for overlapping timesteps, overwriting older predictions. This ensures that the robot's control is always based on the most recent environmental state.

**C. The Dynamic Object Manipulation (DOM) Benchmark**
Recognizing the lack of suitable datasets, the authors built DOM from scratch with a fully automated data collection pipeline:
- **Benchmark Dimensions:** DOM evaluates policies across three dimensions:
 - **Interaction:** Closed-loop reactivity, dynamic adaptation (handling abrupt changes), long-horizon sequencing.
 - **Perception:** Visual understanding, spatial reasoning, motion perception.
 - **Generalization:** Visual generalization (unseen appearance), motion generalization (new speed/friction/trajectories), disturbance robustness.
- **Simulation Data Collection:** Using Isaac Sim, a high-throughput pipeline generates **200K synthetic episodes**. It features 206 diverse objects, 2.8K 3D scenes, multi-view cameras, and ground-truth 6D object states (position, rotation, velocity). A four-stage state-machine controller (Approach Object, Grasp & Lift, Approach Target & Place, Reset) uses these real-time states to generate reactive, prediction-informed trajectories for manipulating moving objects.
- **Real-World Data Collection:** To overcome the limitations of teleoperation for fast-moving objects and the absence of ground-truth 6D states, a "real-world simulator" pipeline is developed. It uses dual RGB views from commodity Azure Kinect DK cameras and a wrist-mounted RealSense D435i for 3D object tracking, estimating 6D pose and inferring velocity (using EfficientTAM for masks and geometric triangulation). This approximated state stream then drives the *same* four-stage state-machine controller autonomously, collecting **2K real-world episodes** across Franka Emika Panda and AgileX PiPER robot embodiments, with humans only initiating object motion.

The combination of a lean architecture, advanced execution strategies, and a comprehensive, automatically collected benchmark represents a holistic approach to tackling dynamic object manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that DynamicVLA achieves significant improvements in dynamic object manipulation compared to existing VLA baselines, particularly in responsiveness, temporal alignment, and generalization.

**A. Simulation Benchmark Results (Table I)**
DynamicVLA significantly outperforms all baselines across the nine sub-dimensions of the DOM benchmark.
- **Overall Performance:** DynamicVLA achieves an average success rate (SR) of **47.06%**, dramatically higher than the best baseline (GR00T-N1.5 at 13.05%). It also exhibits a lower task completion time (**8.53 seconds**) compared to baselines (e.g., VLA-Adapter-Pro at 9.98s), despite having a slightly longer path length (2.50m vs. ~1.3m for baselines), indicating faster, more decisive movements.
- **Interaction:** DynamicVLA achieves remarkable success rates in **Closed-loop Reactivity (60.5%), Dynamic Adaptation (38.5%), and Long-horizon Sequencing (40.5%)**, outperforming the strongest baselines by substantial margins (e.g., +188.1% for CR, +87.8% for DA, +440.0% for LS). This highlights its superior ability to react quickly to varying speeds, adapt to sudden motion changes, and maintain coherent behavior over extended interactions.
- **Perception:** Even in perception-heavy tasks, DynamicVLA excels in **Visual Understanding (51.5%), Spatial Reasoning (48.0%), and Motion Perception (33.5%)**. While performance generally degrades for all models as perceptual demands increase, DynamicVLA's performance remains robust where baselines severely falter, indicating better grounding of visual and linguistic cues in dynamic contexts.
- **Generalization:** DynamicVLA shows strong generalization to unseen conditions, with high success rates in **Visual Generalization (59.5%), Motion Generalization (65.0%)**, and reasonable performance in **Disturbance Robustness (26.5%)**. This suggests robustness to appearance variations, novel motion patterns, and external perturbations.

**B. Real-World Evaluation Results (Figures 4, 5, 6)**
Real-world experiments across Franka and PiPER robot embodiments consistently mirror the simulation findings.
- **Interaction (Figure 4):** DynamicVLA achieves success rates ranging from **50.0% to 78.3%** across tasks like placing coffee cans, conical bottles, pickleballs, and ping pong balls, far surpassing the best baselines (e.g., 10.0-33.3% for SmolVLA, 3.3-26.7% for VLASH). This confirms its ability for real-time reactivity, adaptation, and long-horizon sequencing on physical robots.
- **Perception (Figure 5):** DynamicVLA maintains strong performance in real-world perception tasks (e.g., **31.6% to 73.3%** success rates), while baselines struggle significantly (e.g., 3.3-16.7% for SmolVLA, 5.0-16.7% for VLASH), underscoring its superior spatial-temporal reasoning under real-world dynamics.
- **Generalization (Figure 6):** DynamicVLA demonstrates solid generalization in the real world for unseen objects and irregular motion patterns (e.g., **48.3% to 81.7%** success rates), reinforcing its robustness to distribution shifts.

**C. Ablation Studies (Table II)**
Ablation studies confirm the critical role of DynamicVLA's key design choices:
- **Backbone Capacity:** The 360M parameter model strikes the best balance, outperforming smaller (135M) models due to limited reasoning capacity and larger (1.7B) models due to increased inference latency, confirming the "sweet spot" for dynamic manipulation.
- **Vision Encoder:** FastViT (convolutional) outperforms a transformer-based encoder, highlighting the efficiency gains and structural preservation for visual encoding under real-time constraints.
- **Continuous Inference (CI):** Disabling CI leads to a significant performance drop (SR of 36.11% vs. 47.06%), underscoring its role in eliminating inter-chunk waiting and improving responsiveness.
- **Latent-aware Action Streaming (LAAS):** Disabling LAAS while keeping CI enabled still causes a performance decrease (SR of 39.72% vs. 47.06%), demonstrating that CI alone is insufficient without explicit temporal alignment. LAAS ensures actions are based on the most recent observations.
- **Temporal Visual Context (Table III):** Sparse but sufficiently spaced temporal context (e.g., `o_{t-2}, o_t`) is crucial for implicit velocity perception, outperforming single-frame inputs.
- **LLM Depth (Table IV):** A 16-layer LLM backbone offers the best balance; aggressively truncating (8 layers) sacrifices capacity, while deeper backbones (24, 32 layers) introduce more latency without substantial gains in task success given the continuous execution framework.
- **Cross-Model Analysis of CI and LAAS (Table V):** Integrating CI and LAAS into SmolVLA yielded consistent performance improvements, suggesting general applicability for models with moderate inference latency. However, models with substantially larger backbones (e.g., π0.5) showed only marginal gains, indicating that the benefits of these mechanisms are constrained by fundamental model latency.

In summary, DynamicVLA's compact architecture, coupled with Continuous Inference and Latent-aware Action Streaming, effectively addresses the core challenges of latency and temporal misalignment in dynamic manipulation. The DOM benchmark serves as a crucial tool for validating these improvements and sets a new standard for future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DynamicVLA represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving dynamic objects. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Enabling General Dynamic Object Manipulation:** This work moves VLA models beyond the realm of static, predictable environments into the complex, continuously evolving dynamics of the real world. By directly tackling the perception-execution gap and temporal misalignment, DynamicVLA unlocks the potential for robots to interact with objects that are moving, being handed over, or experiencing unexpected disturbances, which is crucial for real-world applications like household robotics, manufacturing, and assistive care.
2. **Highlighting Latency as a Critical Factor:** The paper incisively identifies inference latency and temporal misalignment as the dominant failure modes for VLA models in dynamic scenarios, a factor often downplayed in static manipulation. This reframing of the problem emphasizes the need for latency-aware design in future VLA architectures and execution schemes, shifting focus from purely representational power to practical real-time performance.
3. **Introducing a Compact and Efficient VLA Design:** DynamicVLA's compact 0.4B parameter architecture, coupled with a convolutional vision encoder, demonstrates that high performance in dynamic tasks does not necessarily require billions of parameters. This efficiency is critical for deploying VLA models on robots with limited computational resources and for achieving the high inference frequencies demanded by real-time control. This approach sets a precedent for developing "small but mighty" models tailored for specific, demanding robotic challenges.
4. **Novel Execution Mechanisms for Real-time Control:** Continuous Inference and Latent-aware Action Streaming are innovative contributions that fundamentally change how VLA models interact with dynamic environments. By overlapping reasoning and execution and explicitly managing temporal alignment, these mechanisms provide a blueprint for achieving robust, closed-loop reactivity, reducing stale actions, and improving overall task success. These principles could be adopted and adapted by other robotic control systems facing similar latency challenges.
5. **Establishing the Dynamic Object Manipulation (DOM) Benchmark:** The creation of DOM is a foundational contribution to the field. By providing a large-scale, automated, and multi-embodiment benchmark for dynamic manipulation, the authors address a critical data scarcity issue. This benchmark will enable standardized, reproducible, and comparable evaluation of future algorithms, accelerating progress in an underexplored but vital area of robotics research. Its automated data collection pipeline also offers a scalable approach to generating complex robotic datasets without relying on labor-intensive teleoperation.
6. **Unifying Simulation and Real-World Data Collection:** The "real-world simulator" approach for collecting real-world data without teleoperation is highly impactful. It bridges the sim-to-real gap by mimicking the simulator's state interface in the real world, allowing the same state-machine controller to be used, thus simplifying the transition from simulation to physical robot deployment.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to promising avenues for future research:
- **More Efficient VLA Architectures:** While DynamicVLA is compact, there's still a trade-off between multimodal understanding and responsiveness. Future work could explore architectures that further optimize this balance.
- **Beyond Short-Horizon Dynamics:** The current formulation focuses on short-to-medium-horizon reactive interaction. Extending dynamic manipulation to multi-stage, longer-horizon tasks requiring planning, memory, and task decomposition is a significant challenge.
- **Beyond Rigid-Body Dynamics:** The current data pipeline assumes rigid-body state estimation. Manipulating non-rigid objects or fluids with continuously evolving states remains an open problem for both VLA models and data pipelines.

In conclusion, DynamicVLA offers a comprehensive solution for bringing VLA capabilities to dynamic robotic tasks. By tackling architectural efficiency, real-time execution, and data generation, it lays a robust foundation for the next generation of generalist robots capable of fluid and responsive interaction in complex, dynamic environments.
