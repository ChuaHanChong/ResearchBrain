# eNavi: Event-based Imitation Policies for Low-Light Indoor Mobile Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> eNavi introduces a real-world indoor person-following dataset pairing hardware-synchronized event streams, RGB frames, and odometry-derived expert velocity labels from a TurtleBot 2, plus ENP, a late-fusion policy with dual MobileNetV3-Small encoders and a transformer fusion block trained by behavioral cloning. Fusion converges faster and cuts validation MAE to 0.0370 versus 0.0707 for RGB-only in the hardest regime, with better zero-shot low-light generalization.

## Problem

- Frame-based RGB cameras degrade under motion blur, extreme dynamic range, and dim indoor illumination, limiting reliable perception for person-following and other service-robot navigation tasks.
- Existing event datasets target SLAM, depth, or outdoor driving, and lack synchronized differential-drive control signals or odometry aligned with vision, so they cannot train navigation policies.
- Event-based work stops at low-level perception like odometry, optical flow, and SLAM, or uses synthetic simulators with sim-to-real gaps, leaving real closed-loop control policies unexplored.

## Method

- **eNavi** dataset: beam-splitter co-axial EVK4 event and Blackfly RGB cameras hardware-triggered at 30Hz by an STM32 oscillator, 175 teleoperated episodes, two hours, three indoor maps, normal and low light.
- Multimodal pipeline: per-RGB-frame event aggregation into two-channel ==ON/OFF polarity histograms==, plus ground-truth actions reconstructed by averaging ==wheel-odometry twists== over each frame interval instead of noisy joystick input.
- **ENP** policy: frozen pretrained ==MobileNetV3-Small== RGB encoder and trainable event encoder, tokens fused by a ==self-attention transformer block== into an MLP head predicting continuous linear and angular velocity, trained with ==L1 behavioral cloning==.

## Results

- On the hardest Multi-Path Mixed-Light regime, ENP-Fusion reaches validation MAE **0.0370** against RGB-only **0.0707**; Fusion or Event-only give the lowest MAE in every one of the **four** regimes.
- Zero-shot low-light transfer on multi-path: Fusion total MAE degrades **0.0335** to **0.0467**, better than RGB-only **0.0463** to **0.0514**; both beat the YOLOv8n plus PID baseline at **0.0998** and **0.1033**.
- Event and Fusion models early-stop around epochs **18** to **36** while RGB-only always exhausts the **50**-epoch budget; low-light trajectory tracking holds linear MAE **0.125** and angular MAE **0.078**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Event streams act as a stronger learning signal than RGB for control regression, cutting epochs to convergence roughly in half, not merely improving final accuracy.
> - Modality benefits appear only under task difficulty: on simple single-path routes RGB matched fusion, so easy benchmarks can hide a sensor's real advantage.
> - Frame-synchronized fusion sacrifices the asynchronous latency advantage of event cameras and burdens edge compute, so single-modality policies may remain preferable for real-time deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Indoor robot navigation presents challenges for service robotics, particularly in environments characterized by clutter, dynamic human motion, and variable lighting conditions. Conventional RGB cameras frequently encounter limitations in such settings, exhibiting degradation due to motion blur, extreme dynamic range, and dim illumination. This necessitates the development of sensing pipelines capable of maintaining perceptual quality in visually degraded environments.

Event cameras have emerged as an alternative sensing modality, offering high dynamic range and microsecond-level temporal resolution by recording asynchronous brightness changes. This technology has facilitated advancements in low-level perception tasks such as visual odometry, optical flow, and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM). However, much of the existing event-based research has focused on reconstructing scene geometry or motion, leaving the potential for end-to-end control policies that exploit the asynchronous nature of event streams largely underexplored.

Existing event-based datasets, such as MVSEC, DSEC, and DDD17, are primarily designed for outdoor environments or perception tasks like 3D state estimation, lacking synchronized action labels suitable for indoor mobile robot navigation. Datasets like MMID and PED-Ro offer synchronized RGB and event streams but do not include differential-drive control signals, rendering them unsuitable for end-to-end control policy learning. Synthetic datasets, while useful, suffer from sim-to-real gaps, particularly regarding noise characteristics and real-world illumination response.

Similarly, while multimodal fusion has improved robot navigation by combining sensors like RGB and LiDAR, the integration of event cameras into end-to-end navigation policies remains uncommon in real-world control applications. End-to-end policy learning, common with RGB cameras, is also limited in the event domain, often relying on decoupled perception-control pipelines or synthetic environments.

This work addresses these identified gaps by introducing a real-world RGB-Event indoor navigation dataset, a multimodal data processing pipeline, and an imitation learning framework designed for end-to-end event-based indoor navigation, specifically focusing on person-following tasks under varying illumination.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were driven by the limitations of conventional RGB cameras in challenging indoor navigation scenarios and the underexploited potential of event cameras for end-to-end robot control. The key objectives and their underlying motivations are as follows:

- **To create a real-world, synchronized RGB-Event indoor person-following dataset:** The motivation for this objective stems from the absence of prior datasets that pair event streams with high-quality action labels in realistic indoor navigation settings. Existing datasets either focus on outdoor environments, lack synchronized control signals, or are purely synthetic, thus limiting their utility for training robust, real-world navigation policies. The authors aimed to provide a comprehensive resource covering diverse indoor layouts, trajectories, and lighting conditions (normal and low-light).

- **To develop a multi-sensor data processing pipeline for temporal alignment and action reconstruction:** To enable effective learning from heterogeneous RGB and event modalities, a robust pipeline was necessary. Raw event streams are asynchronous, while RGB frames are frame-based, requiring careful synchronization. Furthermore, relying solely on raw teleoperation joystick inputs can introduce noise. The motivation was to produce high-quality, temporally aligned input-output pairs by reconstructing ground-truth actions directly from robot odometry, thereby enhancing the fidelity of supervision for imitation learning.

- **To propose and evaluate a late-fusion RGB-Event navigation policy:** Given the complementary strengths of RGB (contextual information) and event cameras (motion cues, low-light robustness), the objective was to design an architecture that could effectively integrate these two modalities. The motivation was to determine if such a fusion policy could achieve improved robustness and lower action prediction error, particularly in challenging conditions like low light, where RGB-only models are known to fail. This involved systematic evaluation of RGB-only, Event-only, and RGB-Event fusion models across various training conditions.

- **To systematically evaluate the contribution of event data to navigation robustness and generalization:** The authors sought to understand the specific conditions under which event data provides advantages. This was motivated by the need to quantify the benefits of event-based sensing, especially for zero-shot generalization to unseen low-light conditions, which is a critical capability for reliable service robots.

In essence, the overarching motivation was to bridge the gap between event-based perception and closed-loop robot navigation by providing both the necessary data infrastructure and a validated architectural framework for end-to-end imitation learning in real-world indoor environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved the creation of a novel dataset, the development of a sophisticated data processing pipeline, and the design and evaluation of an event-based navigation policy using behavioral cloning.

**A. The eNavi Dataset**

1. **Hardware and Camera Setup:**
 - **Platform:** TurtleBot 2 mobile robot (Kobuki base) equipped with an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano for onboard processing running ROS 2.
 - **Cameras:**
 - **Event Camera:** Prophesee Metavision EVK4 (IMX636 sensor, 1280 × 720 resolution, <220 μs latency).
 - **RGB Camera:** Teledyne Blackfly S USB3 (onsemi Python1300 sensor, 1280 × 1024 resolution, up to 170 fps).
 - **Alignment:** Co-axial alignment achieved using a THORLabs CCM1-BS013 non-polarizing cube beam splitter and Kowa 25mm focal length lenses (f/1.4).
 - **Synchronization:** Hardware-level time synchronization was implemented using an STM32F407 microcontroller generating a 30Hz pulse to trigger both cameras and inject markers into the event stream. Spatial calibration used a blinking checkerboard to compute a homography for RGB-to-event frame transformation.
 - **Connectivity:** A TP-Link Wi-Fi dongle enabled remote SSH access and joystick-based teleoperation.

2. **Data Collection Protocol:**
 - **Environments:** Data collected across three distinct indoor environments to ensure generalization, with one used for in-distribution training/testing and two for generalization experiments.
 - **Subjects:** Two human subjects introduced variability in appearance and gait.
 - **Trajectories:** Subjects varied walking speeds and trajectories, resulting in approximately two hours of driving data segmented into over 175 episodes.
 - **Protocols:**
 - **Privacy/Pose:** Target subjects kept their back facing the robot.
 - **Teleoperation:** A human expert teleoperated the robot via joystick using a first-person-view (FPV) from the RGB camera to ensure valid, obstacle-free paths.

**B. Multimodal Data Processing Pipeline**

Raw data, initially recorded in rosbag2 format, was processed into structured HDF5 containers.

1. **Temporal Synchronization:**
 - RGB frame timestamps (`t_i`) served as the temporal reference.
 - **Event Alignment:** For each RGB frame `I_i`, events (`e_k`) occurring between `t_{i-1}` and `t_i` were aggregated into `E_i`.
 - **Event Representation:** Raw events `E_i` were converted into a 2-channel dense tensor (`E_i ∈ R^(H×W×2)`) using a spatial event histogram, separating events by polarity (ON/OFF).
 - **Control Alignment:** Robot control commands (linear `v` and angular `ω` velocities) published asynchronously were aggregated between `t_{i-1}` and `t_i`, and their mean (`a_i`) was computed to represent the action associated with `(I_i, E_i)`.

2. **Odometry-based Action Reconstruction:**
 - To mitigate noise from raw joystick inputs, ground-truth linear (`v`) and angular (`ω`) velocities were derived directly from the robot’s wheel odometry feedback (`/odom`).
 - Instantaneous velocity reports from odometry messages were aggregated over the time window of each RGB frame, and their mean was computed to generate the final action label `a_i`.

**C. Event-based Navigation Policy (ENP)**

The ENP architecture adopted a late-fusion CNN-Transformer design.

1. **Architecture:**
 - **Modality-specific Encoding:**
 - **RGB Encoder:** A pre-trained MobileNetV3-Small backbone processed 3-channel RGB frames. Its weights were frozen during training to preserve pre-trained features.
 - **Event Encoder:** A MobileNetV3-Small backbone processed the 2-channel event representation. Its weights were kept trainable to learn features specific to event data.
 - **Attention-based Fusion and Control Head:** Feature vectors from both encoders were tokenized, concatenated, and processed by a Transformer encoder block. This self-attention mechanism allowed adaptive weighting of RGB context and event dynamics. The fused representation then fed into a multilayer perceptron (MLP) policy head predicting continuous linear (`v`) and angular (`ω`) velocities.

2. **Policy Variants and Training Methods:**
 - **Architecture Variants:**
 - **ENP-RGB:** RGB-only, using the frozen RGB encoder.
 - **ENP-Event:** Event-only, using the trainable event encoder.
 - **ENP-Fusion:** The proposed multimodal model, combining both encoders and the Transformer fusion block.
 - **Dataset Variants (for training):**
 - **Path Complexity:** Single-Path (simple trajectory `P1`) and Multi-Path (union of `P1, P2, P3`).
 - **Lighting Conditions:** Normal-Light (standard indoor lighting) and Mixed-Light (normal and low-light conditions).
 - This factorial design resulted in 12 trained policies (3 architectures × 2 path complexities × 2 lighting conditions).

3. **Behavioral Cloning Objective:**
 - All ENP variants were trained using behavioral cloning, formulated as a supervised regression problem.
 - The Mean Absolute Error (MAE) loss was used to minimize the discrepancy between predicted actions (`π_θ(o_i)`) and expert commands (`u*_i`): `L_BC(θ) = (1/N) Σ |π_θ(o_i) - u*_i|`. MAE was chosen for robustness to outliers and high-frequency noise in expert signals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to validate two hypotheses: multimodal training efficiency and low-light robustness/zero-shot generalization.

**A. Training Setup and Convergence**

All policies were implemented in PyTorch, trained with the AdamW optimizer (learning rate 2 × 10⁻⁴, weight decay 3 × 10⁻⁴), a batch size of 64, and an 80/10/10 training/validation/test split. Early stopping with a patience of 8 epochs was used, with a maximum budget of 50 epochs.

- **RGB Saturation:** RGB-only models (ENP-RGB) consistently utilized the full 50-epoch budget, particularly in complex (Multi-Path, Mixed-Light) settings. This suggests that RGB alone struggled to extract robust control features within the given training period.
- **Event-driven Efficiency:** Models incorporating event information (ENP-Event and ENP-Fusion) converged more rapidly, often triggering early stopping between epochs 20 and 35. This indicates that event cues provide a stronger and more informative learning signal, supporting the multimodal training efficiency hypothesis.
- **Validation Performance Gap:** Fusion and Event-only models achieved lower validation Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across most regimes. For example, in the (Multi-Path, Mixed-Light) setting, ENP-Fusion achieved an MAE of 0.0370, significantly lower than ENP-RGB's 0.0707.

**B. Zero-Shot Generalization to Low Light**

To assess low-light robustness, policies trained only on (Normal-Light) data were tested on both (Normal-Light) and (Low-Light) conditions. Comparisons were made against a YOLOv8n + PID baseline, as well as ENP-RGB and ENP-Event models.

- **Simple Trajectories (Single-Path, Normal-Light trained):**
 - ENP-RGB generalized well to low light, with a minor change in Total MAE (0.0210 to 0.0213). Fusion and Event-only variants showed similar performance, suggesting RGB sensors can retain sufficient contrast for simple paths even under reduced illumination.
 - The YOLOv8n + PID baseline performed worse than all learned policies in both normal and low-light conditions (Total MAE 0.0819 to 0.0713).
- **Complex Trajectories (Multi-Path, Normal-Light trained):**
 - The benefits of fusion were more pronounced. ENP-RGB showed a performance change from 0.0463 (Normal-Light) to 0.0514 (Low-Light).
 - ENP-Fusion exhibited better generalization, with its Total MAE changing from 0.0335 (Normal-Light) to 0.0467 (Low-Light), demonstrating superior low-light robustness compared to ENP-RGB.
 - ENP-Event also performed well, changing from 0.0305 to 0.0534.
 - The YOLOv8n + PID baseline again performed significantly worse (Total MAE 0.0998 to 0.1033).
These findings support the low-light robustness and zero-shot generalization capabilities of policies that incorporate event data, especially the fusion model, for more complex navigation tasks.

**C. Trajectory Analysis**

Analysis of the ENP-Fusion policy trained on (Multi-Path, Mixed-Light) data for a test trajectory (P3) in both normal-light and low-light conditions revealed:

- **Linear Velocity:** In low-light, the ENP-Fusion policy successfully tracked high-speed segments (approx. 0.33 m/s) with a low linear MAE of 0.125, indicating accurate forward motion planning despite illumination changes.
- **Angular Velocity:** Steering predictions aligned with expert turns in low-light, with an angular MAE of 0.078. While slightly noisier, the policy captured major directional changes, confirming sufficient information from the event stream for complex maneuvering.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a notable gap in event-based robotics by providing a real-world dataset, a robust data processing pipeline, and a validated end-to-end control policy. The work's significance lies in its demonstration of the advantages of integrating event camera data for indoor mobile robot navigation, particularly under challenging conditions.

The introduction of the **eNavi dataset** is a direct and substantial contribution to the research community. As a first-of-its-kind resource offering synchronized RGB frames, raw event streams, and expert control actions collected from a physical robot in diverse indoor environments under varying illumination, it provides a crucial foundation for future research in event-based imitation learning. This dataset enables the development and benchmarking of new algorithms that can exploit the unique characteristics of event data.

The **multimodal data processing pipeline** is significant because it overcomes the technical challenges of temporally aligning asynchronous event data with frame-based RGB observations and reconstructing high-fidelity, odometry-based ground-truth actions. This pipeline ensures that the data is suitable for high-quality supervised imitation learning, enhancing the reliability of learned policies.

The **proposed late-fusion RGB-Event navigation policy (ENP-Fusion)** and its systematic evaluation highlight the practical benefits of multimodal sensing. The findings demonstrate that policies incorporating event data, particularly the fusion model, achieve improved robustness, faster convergence, and lower action prediction error compared to RGB-only baselines. Crucially, the ENP-Fusion policy exhibits superior generalization to unseen low-light conditions, where conventional RGB-only models typically degrade in performance. This enhanced performance in visually degraded environments has direct implications for the reliability and autonomy of service robots operating in real-world settings such as homes, offices, and public spaces.

Despite the demonstrated improvements, the authors identify a current limitation in the computational demands of the time-synchronized fusion pipeline for real-time inference on edge devices. This indicates that while the approach shows promise, further engineering efforts are required for practical deployment.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Advancement in Robot Autonomy:** By enabling more robust navigation in low-light and dynamic conditions, the research contributes to the development of more capable and reliable autonomous mobile robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The released dataset and pipeline serve as a stepping stone for the development of new event-based fusion techniques, asynchronous data processing methods, and advanced policy learning approaches (e.g., diffusion-based policy heads, online learning with DAgger).
- **Inspiration for Vision-Language Models:** The work implicitly suggests avenues for integrating event data into more complex AI systems, such as vision-language models, to fully leverage the high temporal resolution of event-based vision for nuanced environmental understanding and decision-making.

In summary, this research effectively bridges a critical gap between event-based perception and closed-loop control, establishing a comprehensive framework that enhances the capabilities of indoor mobile robots and lays foundational groundwork for future innovations in event-driven artificial intelligence.
