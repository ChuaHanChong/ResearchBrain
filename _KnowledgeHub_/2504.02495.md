# Inference-Time Scaling for Generalist Reward Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepSeek-AI and Tsinghua University developed Self-Principled Critique Tuning (SPCT) and DeepSeek-GRM models, a pointwise generative reward modeling approach that achieves state-of-the-art performance in generalist reward benchmarks. This method demonstrates effective inference-time scalability, allowing smaller models to match or exceed the performance of much larger models by leveraging increased compute during inference, thereby improving LLM alignment.

## Problem

- Existing Reward Models (RMs) often lack generalizability and flexibility, struggling with diverse "general queries" where reward criteria are complex and lack explicit references.
- There is limited potential for RMs to improve quality by scaling inference-time compute (e.g., through multiple samplings), particularly for scalar RMs which lack diverse outputs.
- Traditional RM approaches, like pairwise scoring, restrict input flexibility, making it challenging to uniformly evaluate single, paired, or multiple responses.

## Method

- Developed ==Pointwise Generative Reward Modeling (GRM)== to output textual critiques and numerical scores, ensuring input flexibility for single, paired, or multiple responses.
- Introduced ==Self-Principled Critique Tuning (SPCT)==, a two-phase learning method (Rejective Fine-Tuning and Rule-Based Online RL) that trains GRMs to generate adaptive principles and critiques.
- Implemented ==inference-time scaling== strategies, including Voting with Parallel Sampling and ==Meta Reward Model Guided Voting==, to leverage increased compute for finer-grained and more robust reward signals.

## Results

- DeepSeek-GRM-**27B**, trained with SPCT, achieves leading performance on various RM benchmarks, often outperforming or matching larger, state-of-the-art models like Nemotron-4-340B-Reward and GPT-4o.
- The DeepSeek-GRM models show a substantial performance increase (e.g., highest gain over greedy decoding with **8** samples) and significantly reduced domain biases through inference-time scaling with parallel sampling.
- Meta Reward Modeling further enhances accuracy by filtering low-quality sampled critiques, enabling DeepSeek-GRM-27B with MetaRM-guided voting to achieve results better than a **671B** MoE model (DeepSeek-V3) on specific benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proper learning methods, such as SPCT, can instill "scalable behaviors" in RMs, enabling them to consistently generate higher-quality reward signals with increased inference compute.
> - Pointwise generative reward modeling inherently allows for input flexibility and diverse outputs, crucial for effective sampling-based inference-time scaling across general domains.
> - Inference-time scaling with a well-trained GRM can achieve performance comparable to or better than traditional training-time scaling (larger model sizes), offering a more compute-efficient path to high-quality RMs.
