# Inference-Time Scaling for Generalist Reward Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepSeek-AI and Tsinghua University developed Self-Principled Critique Tuning (SPCT) and DeepSeek-GRM models, a pointwise generative reward modeling approach that achieves state-of-the-art performance in generalist reward benchmarks. This method demonstrates effective inference-time scalability, allowing smaller models to match or exceed the performance of much larger models by leveraging increased compute during inference, thereby improving LLM alignment.

## Problem

- Existing Reward Models (RMs) often lack generalizability and flexibility, struggling with diverse "general queries" where reward criteria are complex and lack explicit references.
- There is limited potential for RMs to improve quality by scaling inference-time compute (e.g., through multiple samplings), particularly for scalar RMs which lack diverse outputs.
- Traditional RM approaches, like pairwise scoring, restrict input flexibility, making it challenging to uniformly evaluate single, paired, or multiple responses.

## Method

- Developed ==Pointwise Generative Reward Modeling (GRM)== to output textual critiques and numerical scores, ensuring input flexibility for single, paired, or multiple responses.
- Introduced ==Self-Principled Critique Tuning (SPCT)==, a two-phase learning method (Rejective Fine-Tuning and Rule-Based Online RL) that trains GRMs to generate adaptive principles and critiques.
- Implemented ==inference-time scaling== strategies, including Voting with Parallel Sampling and ==Meta Reward Model Guided Voting==, to leverage increased compute for finer-grained and more robust reward signals.

## Results

- DeepSeek-GRM-**27B**, trained with SPCT, achieves leading performance on various RM benchmarks, often outperforming or matching larger, state-of-the-art models like Nemotron-4-340B-Reward and GPT-4o.
- The DeepSeek-GRM models show a substantial performance increase (e.g., highest gain over greedy decoding with **8** samples) and significantly reduced domain biases through inference-time scaling with parallel sampling.
- Meta Reward Modeling further enhances accuracy by filtering low-quality sampled critiques, enabling DeepSeek-GRM-27B with MetaRM-guided voting to achieve results better than a **671B** MoE model (DeepSeek-V3) on specific benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proper learning methods, such as SPCT, can instill "scalable behaviors" in RMs, enabling them to consistently generate higher-quality reward signals with increased inference compute.
> - Pointwise generative reward modeling inherently allows for input flexibility and diverse outputs, crucial for effective sampling-based inference-time scaling across general domains.
> - Inference-time scaling with a well-trained GRM can achieve performance comparable to or better than traditional training-time scaling (larger model sizes), offering a more compute-efficient path to high-quality RMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has necessitated sophisticated post-training techniques, with Reinforcement Learning (RL) emerging as a critical component for aligning LLMs with human values and complex objectives. Reward Modeling (RM) is the cornerstone of RL in this context, tasked with generating accurate reward signals that guide LLM behavior.

Historically, high-quality reward signals were primarily confined to specific domains with verifiable ground truths, such as mathematical problems or coding tasks, or through human-designed environments. This limitation makes it challenging to apply RL effectively to general domains where reward criteria are diverse, complex, and lack explicit references. The current research landscape for RM can be broadly categorized by reward generation paradigms (scalar, semi-scalar, generative) and scoring patterns (pointwise, pairwise):

- **Scalar RMs** (e.g., Bradley-Terry models, DeepSeek-BTRM): Assign a single numerical score, offering simplicity but limited expressiveness and little to no inference-time scalability, as the output is invariant.
- **Semi-Scalar RMs** (e.g., CLoud): Generate textual critiques alongside a scalar value, offering more interpretability but still struggling with diverse reward signals for sampling-based scaling.
- **Generative RMs (GRMs)** (e.g., LLM-as-a-Judge): Produce textual feedback, potentially including scores or preference order. This approach offers greater flexibility and the potential for diverse outputs through sampling.

Furthermore, scoring patterns distinguish between:
- **Pointwise RMs:** Assign individual scores to each response, offering flexibility for single or multiple responses.
- **Pairwise RMs:** Compare two responses to determine relative preference, often lacking the flexibility to rate single responses or larger sets without extra techniques.

Existing methods often fall short in two key areas for generalist applications:
1. **Generalizability and Flexibility:** Many RMs are domain-specific or lack the input flexibility to handle single, paired, or multiple responses uniformly.
2. **Inference-Time Scalability:** While LLMs have demonstrated training-time scalability (larger models = better performance), improving RM quality by simply increasing inference compute (e.g., by sampling multiple times) remains a challenge, especially for scalar RMs which lack output diversity.

This paper directly addresses these gaps by focusing on **pointwise generative reward modeling (GRM)** to maximize input flexibility and **inference-time scalability for general queries**. It aims to demonstrate that *proper learning methods can enable effective inference-time scalability*, moving beyond mere training-time scaling of model parameters. The novel approach, Self-Principled Critique Tuning (SPCT), and the subsequent DeepSeek-GRM models are positioned to significantly advance the field by creating RMs that are both generalist and can leverage increased inference compute to yield higher quality and finer-grained reward signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **significantly enhance the accuracy and generalizability of reward modeling (RM) for Large Language Models (LLMs) by enabling effective inference-time scalability, particularly for general queries.**

The core motivations driving this work are:

- **The Critical Role of Accurate Reward Signals:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become indispensable for post-training LLMs, facilitating alignment with human values, long-term reasoning, and environment adaptation. The success of RL hinges on the quality and accuracy of the reward signals provided by RMs.
- **Limitations of Current RMs in General Domains:** Existing RMs excel in specific, verifiable domains (e.g., math, coding) where explicit rules or ground truths are available. However, they struggle with "general queries" where reward criteria are diverse, complex, and often lack explicit references, thus limiting the broader applicability of RL to LLMs.
- **The Need for Inference-Time Scalability:** While increasing training compute (e.g., larger models) is a known scaling law for RMs, the potential for improving RM performance by investing more compute *at inference time* (e.g., through multiple samplings or extended reasoning) has been underexplored for generalist RMs. The paper posits that "proper learning methods could enable effective inference-time scalability," implying that current methods don't sufficiently foster behaviors amenable to such scaling.
- **Desire for Input Flexibility:** Traditional RM approaches (e.g., pairwise) often constrain the types of inputs they can handle (e.g., only two responses). A truly "generalist" RM should flexibly evaluate single, paired, or multiple responses.
- **Addressing Performance-Compute Scaling:** The research seeks to design a learning method that not only improves reward quality but also instills "scalable behaviors" in RMs, allowing them to consistently generate higher-quality reward signals as inference compute increases. This includes generating diverse principles and accurate critiques that lead to finer granularity in rewards.
- **Moving Beyond Biased Scalar RMs:** Many scalar and semi-scalar RMs exhibit severe domain biases and lack the diversity in reward generation needed for effective sampling-based inference-time scaling. The motivation is to overcome these biases with a more flexible, generative approach.

In essence, the authors are motivated to develop a new paradigm for reward modeling that is inherently more adaptable, less biased, and capable of leveraging increased inference-time resources to deliver superior performance across a broad spectrum of tasks, thereby pushing the boundaries of LLM post-training and inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel approach centered around **Pointwise Generative Reward Modeling (GRM)** combined with a new learning method called **Self-Principled Critique Tuning (SPCT)**, further augmented by a **Meta Reward Model** for enhanced inference-time scaling.

#### 3.1. Pointwise Generative Reward Modeling (GRM)

The choice of pointwise GRM is fundamental to addressing the challenges of input flexibility and inference-time scalability:
- **Generative Paradigm:** Unlike scalar RMs that output a single numerical score, GRMs generate textual critiques and assign numerical scores within the language representation. This allows for rich, diverse outputs necessary for sampling-based inference-time scaling.
- **Pointwise Scoring:** GRMs assign individual scores to each response, making them highly flexible. They can uniformly evaluate single, paired, or multiple responses using the same language-based representation, overcoming the limitations of pairwise methods.
- **Principle-Guided Generation:** The core idea is that GRMs can generate not just critiques but also *principles* (criteria) that guide the reward generation. This "unpinning principles from understanding to generation" allows for adaptive and high-quality principle formulation based on the input query and responses, which is crucial for general domains without explicit rules.

#### 3.2. Self-Principled Critique Tuning (SPCT)

SPCT is the proposed learning method to foster effective inference-time scalable behaviors in GRMs. It consists of two main phases:

1. **Rejective Fine-Tuning (RFT) - Cold Start:**
 - **Objective:** To train the GRM to generate principles and critiques in the correct format and for various input types, acting as an initial bootstrapping phase.
 - **Data Construction:** Involves general instruction data and "rejective sampled" data. For RM data points (query + one or more responses + ground-truth label), a pre-trained GRM samples principles and critiques multiple times (N_RFT).
 - **Rejection Strategy:** Trajectories are rejected if predicted rewards are incorrect. Additionally, "too easy" trajectories (where all N_RFT samples are correct) are also rejected, encouraging the model to learn from challenging examples.
 - **Hinted Sampling:** To further improve initial alignment, some samples are given a hint (e.g., "The best response is: Response X") during generation, ensuring the predicted rewards align with the ground truth. This is crucial for guiding the model to generate correct critiques, even if it sometimes takes "shortcuts" in reasoning.

2. **Rule-Based Online Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Objective:** To further optimize the GRM to generate adaptive and high-quality principles and critiques by reinforcing correct reward generation behaviors online.
 - **Mechanism:** Uses the GRPO (Generative Reinforcement learning with Policy Optimization) algorithm with rule-based outcome rewards.
 - **Reward Signal:** During rollout, the GRM generates principles and critiques. The predicted rewards are extracted and compared to the ground truth. A reward of +1 is given for correct predictions (identifying the best response or correctly scoring a single response), and -1 otherwise.
 - **KL Penalty:** A larger coefficient for the KL penalty is applied to ensure the generated format is maintained and to prevent severe biases, without needing explicit format-specific rewards.
 - **Purpose:** This online learning encourages the GRM to adaptively posit principles and critiques that lead to accurate reward outcomes across general domains, crucial for effective inference-time scaling.

#### 3.3. Inference-Time Scaling Strategies

SPCT enables GRMs (specifically DeepSeek-GRM) to effectively leverage increased inference compute:

1. **Voting with Parallel Sampling:**
 - **Mechanism:** DeepSeek-GRM generates multiple (k) sets of principles and critiques for the same query and responses by sampling in parallel (using temperature > 0, e.g., 0.5).
 - **Aggregation:** For pointwise GRMs, the final reward for each response is obtained by summing the individual scores from all k samples.
 - **Benefit:** Since individual scores (e.g., 1-10) are discrete, summing them effectively expands the reward space by k times. This allows for finer-grained distinctions between responses and greater robustness. The diversity of principles generated across samples is seen as reflecting a more accurate distribution of judgment perspectives.
 - **Bias Mitigation:** Responses are shuffled before sampling to mitigate positional biases.

2. **Meta Reward Modeling Guided Voting:**
 - **Motivation:** Some principles or critiques generated during sampling might be biased or low-quality due to randomness or model limitations.
 - **Meta RM:** A separate pointwise scalar RM is trained to identify the correctness of the principles and critiques generated by DeepSeek-GRM. It uses binary cross-entropy loss, with labels derived from Equation 10 (correct vs. incorrect). The training data includes trajectories from the RFT stage and DeepSeek-GRM's own generations.
 - **Guided Voting:** The meta RM assigns "meta rewards" to each of the k sampled rewards. Instead of simply summing all k samples, the final outcome is voted only by the top k_meta (a subset of k) samples with the highest meta rewards, effectively filtering out low-quality generations and further boosting scaling performance.

This comprehensive methodology aims to create an RM that is not only powerful in its judgment capabilities but also inherently designed to improve with additional inference-time resources, a critical step towards practical and scalable LLM alignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling empirical evidence supporting the effectiveness of SPCT and the DeepSeek-GRM models.

- **Superior Performance in RM Benchmarks:**
 - DeepSeek-GRM-27B (trained with SPCT) consistently outperforms existing baseline methods (LLM-as-a-Judge, DeepSeek-BTRM-27B, CLoud-Gemma-2-27B, DeepSeek-PairRM-27B) in overall performance across various RM benchmarks (Reward Bench, PPE Preference/Correctness, RMB).
 - It achieves competitive performance with strong public RMs, including Nemotron-4-340B-Reward and GPT-4o, even before inference-time scaling.
 - Unlike many scalar and semi-scalar RMs that show significant domain biases (e.g., excelling in verifiable tasks but failing elsewhere), DeepSeek-GRM demonstrates significantly fewer biases, indicating its generalist nature.
- **Effective Inference-Time Scalability:**
 - DeepSeek-GRM-27B shows a substantial performance increase with inference-time scaling. With 8 samples (Voting@8), it exhibits the highest performance gain over greedy decoding among tested methods.
 - This improvement continues with further scaling; for example, at 32 samples (Voting@32), DeepSeek-GRM-27B achieves even better overall results, surpassing many larger, state-of-the-art models.
 - The effectiveness is attributed to the refined principle generation, which leads to richer, finer-grained outcome rewards within an expanded value space through diverse judgment perspectives.
- **Impact of Meta Reward Modeling:**
 - The introduction of a meta RM further boosts inference-time scaling performance. MetaRM-guided voting effectively filters out low-quality sampled trajectories, leading to even higher accuracy (e.g., achieving the best overall results at Voting@32 with MetaRM).
 - Ablation studies confirm the robustness of meta RM guided voting across different k_meta values.
- **Outperforming Training-Time Scaling:**
 - A key finding is that inference-time scaling with DeepSeek-GRM-27B can achieve comparable or even better performance than scaling model sizes (training-time scaling). For instance, DeepSeek-GRM-27B with meta RM guided voting at 8 samples achieved results better than a 671B MoE model (DeepSeek-V3) on the Reward Bench benchmark, and 32 samples achieved comparable performance. This suggests a more compute-efficient pathway to higher RM quality.
 - Furthermore, models relying on extensive chain-of-thought generation (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-0120) showed limited effectiveness for generalist RM, performing worse than smaller, RFT-trained models, underscoring the specific benefits of SPCT's approach.
- **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - The principle generation component is crucial for both greedy decoding and inference-time scaling.
 - Online RL (rule-based) significantly improves performance even without the initial rejective sampled critique data.
 - Non-hinted sampling during RFT is found to be more important than hinted sampling, potentially due to hinted sampling introducing "shortcuts."
 - General instruction data is confirmed as essential for GRM performance.
- **Input Flexibility Validation:** Empirical tests demonstrate DeepSeek-GRM's flexibility in handling varying numbers of responses (single, paired, multiple) and its ability to judge with or without external references, validating the pointwise GRM design.

Despite these successes, the paper acknowledges limitations in efficiency compared to scalar RMs and occasional lags in specific verifiable tasks, particularly without reference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Self-Principled Critique Tuning (SPCT) and the DeepSeek-GRM models represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment and evaluation, with profound implications across several domains:

- **Enhanced LLM Post-Training:** By providing more accurate, nuanced, and scalable reward signals, DeepSeek-GRM can significantly improve the effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and other RL-based post-training methods. This leads to LLMs that are better aligned with human preferences, more helpful, and safer across a broader range of general-purpose tasks. The ability to generate adaptive principles and critiques allows for a more robust and context-aware reward system.
- **Efficient Resource Utilization:** The demonstration that inference-time scaling can outperform training-time scaling (i.e., scaling model size) offers a paradigm shift in how computational resources are allocated for RM. Instead of always defaulting to training ever-larger models, researchers and practitioners can achieve superior RM quality by intelligently leveraging increased inference compute, potentially leading to more economical and faster development cycles for high-performing RMs. This is particularly impactful for resource-constrained environments or for applications where rapid iteration is crucial.
- **Versatile Interface for Generalist Reward Systems:** DeepSeek-GRM, with its pointwise generative nature and input flexibility, can serve as a highly versatile and unified interface for generalist reward systems. It can evaluate single, paired, or multiple responses from diverse domains, making it a powerful tool for various LLM applications and evaluations. Its capacity to generate explicit principles and critiques also enhances transparency and interpretability in the reward generation process.
- **Advancing LLM Evaluation:** As a robust evaluator, DeepSeek-GRM can provide finer-grained and more reliable assessments of LLM performance. The ability to generate principles explicitly allows for detailed analysis of an LLM's strengths and weaknesses against specific criteria, contributing to more systematic and interpretable evaluation protocols for foundation models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - **Integration with Online RL Pipelines:** DeepSeek-GRM's enhanced scalability and accuracy make it a strong candidate for direct integration into online RL pipelines, potentially leading to more dynamic and adaptive LLM learning.
 - **Inference-Time Co-scaling with Policy Models:** The advancements in scalable reward generation could pave the way for "co-scaling" with policy models, where both the reward model and the LLM agent improve performance by jointly leveraging increased inference-time compute.
 - **Tool Integration:** Future work exploring the integration of tools (e.g., code interpreters, search engines) with GRMs could further enhance their accuracy in tasks requiring strict procedures or extensive external knowledge, mitigating current limitations.
 - **Decomposed Principle/Critique Generation:** Exploring a multi-stage generation process where principles are generated and stored independently before critique generation could improve efficiency.

**Ethical Considerations:** The authors proactively address ethical implications, acknowledging that while SPCT promotes accuracy, automated principle generation can still perpetuate or amplify biases present in training data. They advocate for continued human-in-the-loop frameworks, diligent bias mitigation strategies, and robust validation across varied benchmarks. Open-sourcing the models under public supervision is a crucial step towards ensuring responsible deployment and maintaining trust in these powerful AI systems.

In conclusion, this work significantly pushes the boundaries of reward modeling by providing a robust, flexible, and efficient method for achieving high-quality reward signals through inference-time scaling, setting a new direction for LLM alignment and evaluation.
