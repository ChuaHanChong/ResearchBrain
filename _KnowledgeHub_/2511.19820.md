# CropVLM: Learning to Zoom for Fine-Grained Vision-Language Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> CropVLM introduces an external, lightweight network that leverages reinforcement learning to dynamically generate relevant image crops, thereby enhancing existing Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) fine-grained perception on high-resolution inputs. This method consistently improves VLM performance across diverse benchmarks and target models by mitigating computational overhead and removing the need for direct bounding box supervision.

## Problem

- Mainstream Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with fine-grained visual perception due to low input resolution constraints, leading to loss of crucial details.
- Existing solutions like uniformly increasing resolution or architectural modifications are computationally prohibitive, risk catastrophic forgetting, or are infeasible for proprietary VLMs.
- Many fine-grained perception methods rely on expensive human-labeled bounding box annotations, limiting their scalability and generalizability.

## Method

- **CropVLM** employs a ==lightweight, external cropping network==, initialized from a ==SmolVLM Instruct== model, designed to output ==bounding box coordinates== for a given image-question pair.
- The cropping network is trained using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, which utilizes ==objective reward signals== (accuracy or log-likelihood) from the target VLM's performance, eliminating the need for explicit bounding box supervision.
- The generated ==high-resolution crop==, alongside the original image, is then fed to any VLM (open-source or proprietary) to enhance its fine-grained understanding without altering its internal architecture or weights.

## Results

- **CropVLM** consistently delivered significant performance gains across all evaluated target VLMs (SmolVLM, LLaVA 1.5 7B, Qwen 2.5 VL 3B, GPT 4.1 nano) and various fine-grained VQA benchmarks, including strong out-of-domain generalization.
- The method outperformed alternative cropping strategies like ViCrop and UV-CoT, demonstrating superior results despite **CropVLM** being a much smaller model trained on less data.
- **CropVLM** exhibited lower GPU memory usage compared to ViCrop and UV-CoT, and enabled a **1024x1024** base VLM to achieve higher average performance than a **2048x2048** baseline without CropVLM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning with VLM-derived objective rewards offers a scalable and cost-effective method for training cropping networks, outperforming approaches reliant on explicit bounding box annotations.
> - Log-likelihood based rewards provide more nuanced feedback and consistently yield superior performance compared to accuracy-based rewards during GRPO training.
> - VQA performance does not directly correlate with high Intersection over Union (IoU) scores; instead, achieving sufficient context through larger crops with higher recall proves more beneficial for VQA accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in visual understanding and reasoning. However, a limitation arises in tasks requiring fine-grained visual perception, such as scene-text recognition, document analysis, or detailed object identification. This limitation is often attributed to the relatively low input resolutions (e.g., 224x224 or 336x336 pixels) typically supported by mainstream VLM vision encoders, which can render fine details indiscernible.

The broader research landscape has explored several approaches to address these challenges:
- **Uniform High-Resolution Processing:** A straightforward method of increasing input resolution uniformly. This approach, however, incurs a significant computational burden due to the quadratic scaling of Transformer architectures, and studies suggest that only a small number of image tokens are often critical for a given query, making uniform processing inefficient.
- **Architectural Modifications:** Some methods involve adapting VLM architectures to handle higher resolutions or different visual granularities. Examples include Matryoshka (M3) which uses progressive training, and S2 which adapts models across granularities. PS3 scales pre-training to 4K resolution by contrasting local image regions. While effective, these often require extensive model retraining.
- **Specialized Fine-Tuning:** Approaches like Visual-CoT utilize supervised fine-tuning on task-specific datasets, often relying on bounding box supervision to direct models to relevant regions. These methods can be effective in controlled settings but typically require expensive annotations (human or synthetic) and risk catastrophic forgetting or poor generalization to out-of-domain scenarios. They are also often inapplicable to proprietary models with closed weights.
- **Intention-Driven Visual Models:** These methods dynamically prioritize image regions based on user queries. They can be broadly categorized into:
 - **LLM-Guided Querying:** Using Large Language Models (LLMs) to guide region selection through search, textual semantics, or visual tags (e.g., SEAL, SEMCLIP, SoM-LLaVA).
 - **Cropping-Based Preprocessing:** Cropping inputs before processing the request (e.g., Visual-CoT, ViCrop).
 - **Preference-Based Refinement:** Optimizing cropping mechanisms through reward models or preference feedback (e.g., VisRL, UV-CoT, Visual-RFT, DeepEyes, Chain-of-Focus, Mini-o3). Many of these still rely on explicit region supervision, either human or synthetically generated.

CropVLM positions itself within this landscape as a lightweight, external, and reinforcement learning-based approach to dynamic region selection. It aims to overcome the limitations of existing methods by not requiring human-labeled bounding boxes for supervision, avoiding expensive synthetic evaluations, operating without modifying or fine-tuning the target VLM (thereby preventing catastrophic forgetting and allowing use with proprietary models), and maintaining a low computational overhead. It addresses the fundamental challenge of VLMs' inability to dynamically adjust visual focus by adaptively selecting and processing high-resolution crops alongside a global low-resolution view.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce CropVLM, a method designed to enhance the fine-grained visual perception capabilities of existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This enhancement is achieved by enabling VLMs to dynamically "zoom in" on relevant image regions.

The motivation for developing CropVLM stems from several identified limitations and challenges within current VLM architectures and training paradigms:

- **VLM Limitations in Fine-Grained Understanding:** Despite advances in general visual reasoning, VLMs frequently struggle with tasks that demand precise visual details, such as recognizing small text in scene images (e.g., TextVQA, ST-VQA), analyzing complex documents (DocVQA), or identifying minute objects. This is largely attributed to the low input resolutions typically employed by VLM vision encoders (e.g., 224x224 or 336x336 pixels), which can make crucial fine details indiscernible.
- **Computational Inefficiency of Uniform High-Resolution Processing:** A straightforward solution of increasing the input resolution uniformly for VLMs based on Transformer architectures leads to a prohibitive computational burden, characterized by a quadratic increase in cost with resolution. Existing research suggests that often only a small portion of an image is truly relevant to a given query, rendering uniform high-resolution processing inefficient.
- **Drawbacks of Architectural Modifications and Specialized Fine-Tuning:** While architectural improvements (e.g., Matryoshka, S2) or fine-tuning techniques (e.g., Visual-CoT) can address some limitations, they typically necessitate extensive model retraining. This process is resource-intensive, risks "catastrophic forgetting" of previously learned knowledge, and often results in poor generalization to out-of-domain scenarios. Furthermore, these strategies are generally incompatible with proprietary VLM models due to their closed-source nature.
- **Lack of Dynamic Visual Focus:** Current VLMs often lack the ability to dynamically adapt their visual focus across different spatial regions of an image in response to a task or textual prompt. This limitation highlights the need for a flexible mechanism to allocate computational resources specifically to the most informative parts of an image.
- **Dependency on Expensive Annotations:** Many existing methods for improving visual grounding rely on explicit supervision, such as human-labeled or synthetically generated bounding boxes. Obtaining such annotations is costly and labor-intensive, limiting the scalability and applicability of these approaches.
- **Need for a Modular and Low-Cost Solution:** The research aims to develop a method that functions as an external, lightweight component, requiring no modifications or access to the internal weights of the target VLM. This modularity would allow it to be paired with both open-source and proprietary VLMs, providing a low-cost, generalizable performance boost without retraining the core VLM.

In summary, the motivation is to develop an efficient, adaptable, and annotation-independent method that empowers VLMs to dynamically focus on relevant image details, thereby overcoming their inherent fine-grained perception limitations without incurring high computational costs or requiring internal model modifications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CropVLM is introduced as a lightweight, external cropping network designed to augment the fine-grained perception of target VLMs. It operates by generating a single bounding box per image-request pair, allowing the VLM to focus on a dynamically selected region of interest. The bounding box is represented as text in the format `[x1, y1, x2, y2]`, where coordinates are percentages to ensure generalization across different image sizes.

The core methodology involves two main stages: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for initial capability and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for refinement.

#### 3.1. Training Stages

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Stage:**
 - **Goal:** To enable the CropVLM model to initially generate bounding box-like outputs in the specified format.
 - **Base Model:** The 256M parameter SmolVLM Instruct model is selected due to its compact size and ability to process varying image resolutions.
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** Since SmolVLM does not inherently generate bounding boxes, a synthetic dataset is created. The Qwen 2.5-VL 7B Instruct model is prompted with "Outline the region in the image that would help answer the following question: {QUESTION} Output the coordinates in JSON format." This generates initial bounding box annotations.
 - **Data Preprocessing:** The absolute coordinates from Qwen 2.5-VL are normalized to percentages. An important adjustment involves expanding smaller generated bounding boxes based on their relative area percentiles (e.g., 45x expansion for areas below 0.16%, 10x for 0.16-0.38%), as smaller boxes often failed to encompass the correct region of interest.
 - **Training:** Three separate SmolVLM models are fine-tuned at input resolutions of 512x512, 1024x1024, and 2048x2048 pixels on this synthetic dataset. This provides the models with the basic ability to generate bounding box estimates.

2. **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) Stage:**
 - **Goal:** To refine the SFT-trained models to generate more useful crops for downstream VLM performance, without requiring ground-truth bounding boxes.
 - **Algorithm:** GRPO, a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), is employed. GRPO simplifies training by avoiding the need for a separate value model. It directly compares the quality of multiple candidate responses generated by the policy within a group, normalizing their rewards to determine relative quality (A_i). The policy is then updated to favor outputs similar to those with higher relative rewards.
 - **Reward Design:** The reward signal is computed by a separate target VLM (referred to as the "reward VLM") which receives *both* the original full image and the cropped image as input. Two reward formulations are experimented with:
 - **Accuracy-Based Reward:** The reward VLM provides an answer, and the reward is the accuracy between this prediction and the ground-truth answer. Specific accuracy metrics include VQA accuracy for TextVQA and ANLS for DocVQA, ST-VQA, and InfographicVQA.
 - **Likelihood-Based Reward:** The reward VLM computes the log-likelihood of the ground-truth answer tokens given the combined inputs. This method is faster as it avoids autoregressive generation.
 - **Bounding Box Validation Reward:** An additional reward component (1 for valid, 0 otherwise; scaled differently for accuracy vs. likelihood rewards) is included to ensure generated bounding boxes are well-formed.
 - **Reward Model:** A base 256M SmolVLM Instruct model with an input resolution of 512x512 pixels is used as the reward VLM. This choice is motivated by computational efficiency, as it allows for fast evaluation while providing sufficient signal, especially with group-normalized rewards.
 - **Datasets:** The training mixture consists of TextVQA, ST-VQA, DocVQA, and InfographicsVQA, totaling 124k image-question pairs. This mixture is split into two halves: one for SFT and one for GRPO.

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters

- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning:** LoRA is applied to the SmolVLM models with rank 128, alpha 256, and dropout of 0.05.
- **Optimization:** A batch size of 16 and a cosine decay learning rate scheduler are used. All models are trained for a single epoch.
- **Learning Rates:** 5e-5 for SFT and 5e-6 for GRPO.
- **Gradient Norm:** Maximum gradient norm of 1.0 for SFT and 0.1 for GRPO.
- **GRPO Specifics:** Group size of 6, generation temperature of 0.8, and beta of 0.01.
- **Computational Resources:** Training was performed on a single A100 GPU, leading to approximate training times of 3 GPU hours for SFT and 24 GPU hours for the largest GRPO model (2048px).

#### 3.3. Comparison to Alternative Methods

The proposed reward formulations offer advantages over existing methods like VisRL, which use general-purpose VLMs for textual rewards, by directly measuring performance with quantifiable metrics aligned with the downstream task. The GRPO-based approach is also more data-efficient than DPO-based methods, utilizing all sampled bounding boxes within a group. By grounding rewards in objective measures, the approach aims to reduce reward hacking.

#### 3.4. Ablation Studies (from Appendices)

- **Learning without External Help:** Experiments demonstrate that CropVLM can achieve similar performance without relying on external bounding box supervision for SFT (e.g., using exhaustive search for initial crops), highlighting the robustness of the GRPO stage.
- **Importance of Full Image Context:** Ablations confirm that providing both the full image and the crop to the target VLM is generally more effective than providing only the crop, although crops alone can still improve over baselines in some contexts.
- **Training with Tight Bounding Boxes:** A cropping network trained via SFT on Visual-CoT data (with tight bounding box annotations) struggles to produce effective crops for VQA tasks, suggesting that tight bounding box annotations do not necessarily correlate with VQA accuracy and that CropVLM's tendency to predict larger boxes might be a beneficial strategy when precise localization is difficult. Modest expansion of human-annotated bounding boxes can improve VQA performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CropVLM involved assessing its performance when paired with the same-resolution SmolVLM models, as well as with external, pre-trained VLMs, and comparing it against alternative cropping strategies.

#### 4.1. CropVLM Paired with SmolVLM (Table 3)

- **Post-SFT Performance:** CropVLM models at 512x512 and 1024x1024 resolutions showed performance gains over their SmolVLM baselines, indicating that even synthetic supervised training enables the models to learn useful cropping patterns. The 2048x2048 model, however, experienced a slight performance drop, suggesting potential overfitting at higher resolutions during this stage.
- **Post-GRPO Performance:** The application of GRPO consistently led to substantial performance improvements across all resolutions and both reward types (accuracy and log-likelihood). The 512x512 model achieved the largest relative gains, suggesting that cropping effectively compensates for its lower perceptual capacity. Log-likelihood rewards generally outperformed accuracy-based rewards, likely due to providing more nuanced feedback during training.
- **Key Insights:** The 2048x2048 model, despite an SFT performance dip, surpassed its baseline after GRPO, demonstrating that even high-resolution models benefit from the cropping mechanism through reinforcement learning, even when trained with a lower-resolution reward model. Notably, a 1024x1024 CropVLM model trained with log-likelihood rewards, when paired with a same-resolution SmolVLM, achieved a higher average performance than the baseline 2048x2048 SmolVLM, indicating efficiency gains.

#### 4.2. CropVLM with Other VLMs (Table 4)

CropVLM was evaluated with LLaVA 1.5 7B (336x336 input), Qwen 2.5 VL 3B (448x448 max input), and GPT 4.1 nano (512x512 input), utilizing CropVLM models trained with log-likelihood rewards.

- **Consistent Improvements:** Cropping networks trained at higher resolutions generally outperformed their lower-resolution counterparts across all target VLMs and datasets.
- **Domain-Specific Gains:** Larger relative improvements were observed in in-domain datasets (DocVQA, InfographicVQA) that typically involve smaller areas of interest.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** Performance improvements were also evident on the V*, HR-Bench 4k, and HR-Bench 8k benchmarks, indicating robust out-of-distribution performance for both the cropping network and the answering model. For instance, the baseline GPT 4.1 nano model frequently refused to answer questions on V*, but this refusal rate significantly decreased when paired with CropVLM.
- **Model Agnosticism:** CropVLM demonstrated its ability to enhance external models without requiring access to their weights or fine-tuning them.

#### 4.3. Comparison to Alternative Cropping Strategies (Table 5)

CropVLM was compared against ViCrop (training-free, explainability-based) and UV-CoT (DPO-based training method).

- **CropVLM vs. ViCrop:** CropVLM generally outperformed ViCrop across most datasets, with particularly strong advantages on DocVQA and InfographicVQA when paired with LLaVA 1.5. This performance gap is attributed to these datasets being out-of-domain for LLaVA 1.5, which reduces the reliability of ViCrop's explainability methods. CropVLM also showed superior performance on V* and HR-Bench 8k, datasets that are out-of-domain for both CropVLM and the target model.
- **CropVLM vs. UV-CoT:** CropVLM consistently outperformed UV-CoT across almost all datasets for both LLaVA 1.5 and Qwen 2.5 VL. This is noteworthy as CropVLM is significantly smaller (256M parameters vs. UV-CoT's 7B) and trained on less data (62k VQA examples vs. 249k preference pairs).

#### 4.4. Execution Overhead (Table 6)

- **Memory Efficiency:** CropVLM exhibited significantly lower GPU memory usage compared to ViCrop and UV-CoT. This is attributed to its smaller model size and compatibility with FlashAttention, which ViCrop cannot leverage due to its reliance on full attention scores.
- **Execution Time:** CropVLM was slower in bounding box generation than ViCrop, as it relies on text generation for coordinate values. However, it was faster and less memory-demanding than UV-CoT, despite UV-CoT's FlashAttention compatibility, due to CropVLM's smaller model size.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Additional Findings (Appendices B, D, E, F, G)

- **Bounding Box Characteristics (Table 7):** GRPO training resulted in significantly larger bounding boxes compared to the SFT stage, suggesting that models learn to capture broader regions for comprehensive information. Counter-intuitively, IoU with human annotations did not directly correlate with TextVQA accuracy, while recall and full recall, combined with bounding box size, provided better performance indicators. CropVLM models trained with RL generated progressively smaller boxes at higher input resolutions.
- **Benefits at Higher Resolutions (Table 8):** CropVLM continued to provide improvements when paired with Qwen 2.5 VL at 1792x1792 input resolution (CropVLM processing 2048x2048 images), particularly on in-domain datasets, suggesting visual fragmentation remains an issue even at high resolutions.
- **Learning Without External Help (Table 10):** CropVLM models trained without external bounding box supervision (using noised crops from exhaustive log-likelihood search) achieved performance similar to those trained with Qwen 2.5 VL supervision, demonstrating the robustness of the GRPO stage.
- **Training with Tight Bounding Boxes (Tables 11, 12):** A cropping network of CropVLM's scale struggles to learn effective tight cropping strategies via SFT on Visual-CoT data. IoU was not a strong predictor of VQA accuracy. Modestly expanding human-annotated bounding boxes could improve VQA performance (Figure 3).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CropVLM research offers several points of significance and potential impact on the field of Vision-Language Models:

- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Perception for VLMs:** CropVLM directly addresses a notable limitation of current VLMs: their struggle with tasks requiring fine-grained image understanding due to input resolution constraints. By enabling dynamic "zooming" into relevant image regions, CropVLM allows VLMs to capture crucial details that would otherwise be lost, thereby improving performance on tasks like scene-text recognition, document analysis, and detailed object identification.
- **Computational Efficiency and "Green AI":** The approach achieves the benefits of high-resolution processing without incurring the quadratic computational cost associated with uniformly increasing VLM input resolution. By selectively processing only informative regions, CropVLM reduces computational overhead, enhances inference speed, and lowers memory usage. This efficiency aligns with the principles of "green AI," promoting more sustainable and accessible large model deployment.
- **Model Agnosticism and Broad Applicability:** A key aspect of CropVLM is its design as an external, lightweight cropping network that does not require modifications or fine-tuning of the target VLM. This modularity means CropVLM can be seamlessly paired with both open-source and proprietary VLMs, expanding its applicability across various existing models. This also prevents issues like catastrophic forgetting, which can arise from fine-tuning a VLM for specific tasks.
- **Reduced Annotation Burden:** The training methodology, leveraging reinforcement learning (GRPO) with objective reward signals (accuracy or log-likelihood), eliminates the need for expensive human-labeled bounding boxes as a supervision signal. This significantly reduces the data annotation burden, making the development of fine-grained perception capabilities more scalable and less resource-intensive.
- **Improved Generalization and Out-of-Domain Performance:** CropVLM demonstrates significant improvements on benchmarks that are considered out-of-domain for the target VLM, such as V* and HR-Bench 4k/8k. This indicates a robust ability to generalize and enhance performance even in scenarios not explicitly seen during training of the target VLM.
- **Robustness to Underlying VLM Weaknesses:** The research shows that CropVLM can compensate for the lower perceptual capacity of smaller VLMs and even mitigate issues like VLM refusal to answer questions due to perceived lack of detail (as seen with GPT 4.1 nano on V*).
- **Foundation for Future Research:** CropVLM provides a new direction for research into efficient visual grounding and adaptive attention mechanisms. It highlights the potential of reinforcement learning for spatial reasoning tasks without direct bounding box supervision and encourages further exploration of objective reward designs grounded in VLM language modeling behavior. Future work could investigate alternative cropping network designs, multilingual generalization, and fairness-oriented evaluations in such systems.

In summary, CropVLM offers a scalable, efficient, and broadly applicable solution to a critical VLM limitation, paving the way for more capable and resource-conscious multimodal AI systems.
