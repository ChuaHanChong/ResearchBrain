# On-Policy RL with Optimal Reward Baseline

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research introduces On-Policy RL with Optimal reward baseline (OPO), an algorithm that simplifies large language model alignment by eliminating auxiliary models and explicit regularization terms. OPO achieves superior performance on mathematical reasoning tasks while enhancing training stability and output diversity by adhering to exact on-policy training and a novel variance-reducing baseline.

## Problem

- Current RLHF algorithms for LLMs, such as PPO, require an auxiliary value model, leading to high computational and memory overhead.
- PPO's loose on-policy constraint, where data is collected with an older policy, causes training instability, large policy shifts, and an 'alignment tax' where original model capabilities degrade.
- Policy gradient methods suffer from high gradient variance, and while theoretical optimal baselines exist, their practical application for complex tasks like sequence generation has been elusive.

## Method

- **OPO** employs ==exact on-policy training==, ensuring that all data used for gradient computation is freshly sampled from the very latest version of the policy.
- It derives and applies a ==practically feasible optimal reward baseline== for sequence generation, where the baseline is a ==length-weighted average== of the reward from sampled responses.
- The algorithm omits ==auxiliary value models== and common ==regularization terms== like ==KL divergence constraints== or ==entropy bonuses==, simplifying the objective function and training pipeline.

## Results

- OPO consistently outperforms off-policy and on-policy GRPO baselines on mathematical reasoning `pass@k` metrics, improving accuracy beyond the initial SFT baseline.
- OPO demonstrates remarkably lower KL divergence and higher output entropy during training compared to off-policy methods, indicating enhanced stability and reduced 'alignment tax' without explicit regularization.
- Models trained with OPO generate more diverse (lower Self-BLEU) and less repetitive (lower Rep-5) outputs, reflecting improved output quality stemming from stable, higher-entropy training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strict exact on-policy training inherently stabilizes policy updates and preserves output entropy, mitigating the need for external regularization terms like KL divergence constraints or entropy bonuses.
> - A practically feasible optimal reward baseline, simplified for sequence generation problems based on length-proportional gradient norms, effectively reduces policy gradient variance.
> - Eliminating auxiliary value models and explicit regularization terms simplifies the RL training pipeline, reducing computational overhead and hyperparameter tuning complexity while improving stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models has been profoundly impacted by Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which is a cornerstone for aligning LLMs with human preferences, values, and instructions. The standard RLHF pipeline typically involves initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) followed by a reinforcement learning phase, most commonly utilizing the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. PPO has gained widespread adoption due to its balance of stability and sample efficiency, notably in models like OpenAI's InstructGPT. Beyond general alignment, RL has also proven highly effective in enhancing the specialized reasoning abilities of LLMs, as seen in models focused on mathematics, code generation, and complex problem-solving.

However, the current landscape of RLHF algorithms, particularly PPO, faces several well-documented challenges. Firstly, PPO, as an actor-critic method, necessitates training an additional "value model" (critic) alongside the primary policy model (actor). This introduces significant computational overhead and memory requirements, making the training process less efficient. Secondly, PPO's approach to policy updates often involves collecting a batch of data with an older policy and then performing multiple gradient updates on this fixed batch. While this improves sample efficiency, it inherently introduces an "off-policy" divergence, meaning the policy updates are not strictly based on data from the *current* policy. This "loose on-policy constraint" can lead to training instability, large policy shifts, and a phenomenon known as "alignment tax," where the model's original capabilities (e.g., helpfulness, factuality, diversity) can degrade as it over-optimizes for the reward signal. It also often necessitates explicit regularization terms, such as KL divergence penalties and entropy bonuses, to maintain stability and prevent policy collapse or repetitive outputs.

In response to these challenges, various alternative RL algorithms and modifications have emerged. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO, Shao et al. 2024) is a notable effort that addresses the value model issue by calculating relative advantages within groups of sampled responses, thereby eliminating the need for a separate critic. However, GRPO still suffers from the inherent instability associated with off-policy updates and loose on-policy constraints. Other contemporary works, such as ReMax, RLOO, and Reinforce++, similarly focus on alternative advantage estimation methods without auxiliary value models. Concurrent research also explores ways to mitigate issues like KL or entropy collapse in these loose on-policy settings.

The present work, "On-Policy RL with Optimal Reward Baseline (OPO)," positions itself directly within this evolving landscape. It builds upon the insights of GRPO by aiming to eliminate auxiliary models and regularization terms, but critically distinguishes itself by emphasizing **exact on-policy training**. Furthermore, it tackles the long-standing problem of variance reduction in policy gradients, a fundamental challenge since early algorithms like REINFORCE. While the concept of an "optimal baseline" for minimizing gradient variance has been theoretically established, its practical implementation for complex tasks like sequence generation has remained elusive. OPO's innovation lies in deriving a practically feasible formulation of this optimal baseline tailored for sequence generation, thereby combining strict on-policy adherence with principled variance reduction. By doing so, OPO seeks to offer a simpler, more stable, and more effective RL algorithm for LLM training, addressing the core limitations of existing methods without introducing additional complexities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose **On-Policy RL with Optimal reward baseline (OPO)**, a novel and simplified reinforcement learning algorithm designed to overcome critical limitations in current LLM alignment and reasoning methods.

The key motivations driving the development of OPO are:

1. **Addressing Training Instability:** Current RL algorithms, particularly PPO and even value-model-free methods like GRPO, often suffer from instability due to "loose on-policy constraints." This means that policy updates are not always based on data generated by the *current* version of the policy, leading to divergences that can destabilize training, cause large policy shifts, and necessitate complex regularization. A core motivation for OPO is to achieve inherently stable training through strict adherence to exact on-policy data collection.

2. **Improving Computational Efficiency:** Algorithms like PPO require an auxiliary value model to estimate advantages, introducing significant computational overhead and increasing memory footprint. OPO aims to eliminate the need for such additional models, simplifying the training pipeline and improving efficiency.

3. **Mitigating "Alignment Tax" and Enhancing Output Diversity:** The "alignment tax" refers to the phenomenon where over-optimization for a specific reward signal can lead to a degradation of other desirable model qualities, such as helpfulness, factuality, or diversity. This often manifests as reduced output entropy and repetitive responses. OPO is motivated by the desire to maintain high output entropy and prevent undesirable policy drifts, thereby reducing the alignment tax and fostering more diverse, less repetitive generated text.

4. **Minimizing Gradient Variance:** A fundamental challenge in policy gradient methods is high gradient variance, which can hinder stable and efficient learning. While theoretical optimal baselines exist to minimize this variance, their practical application has been limited. OPO seeks to bridge this gap by deriving and implementing a practically feasible formulation of the optimal reward baseline, thereby enhancing training efficiency and stability.

In summary, OPO is motivated by the desire to develop an RL algorithm that is:
- **Simple:** By eliminating auxiliary models (value/reference models) and external regularization terms (KL divergence, entropy bonus).
- **Stable:** Through exact on-policy training and a principled variance reduction technique.
- **Effective:** Achieving superior performance in LLM reasoning tasks while simultaneously promoting output diversity and mitigating alignment tax.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OPO integrates two core methodological strategies: (1) strict adherence to **exact on-policy training** and (2) the application of a **practically feasible optimal reward baseline** for variance reduction.

#### 3.1. Exact On-Policy Training

The fundamental principle of policy-based reinforcement learning is to optimize a policy $\pi_\theta$ to maximize the expected reward, where this expectation is inherently taken with respect to trajectories sampled from the *current* policy ($\mathbb{E}_{x \sim D, y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}[r(x, y)]$). OPO adheres strictly to this principle by ensuring that for every gradient step, the data (trajectories or responses) used for computation is freshly sampled from the very latest version of the policy.

This contrasts sharply with many popular policy gradient methods, notably PPO, which typically collect a batch of data with a slightly older policy and then perform multiple gradient updates on this fixed "off-policy" batch. While this data reuse can improve sample efficiency, it introduces a divergence between the policy that generated the data and the policy being updated. This divergence is a primary source of instability, often leading to issues like entropy collapse (where the policy becomes overly deterministic) and large, undesirable policy shifts. By maintaining strict on-policy training, OPO aims to inherently stabilize the learning process, preserve policy entropy, and reduce the KL divergence between the current policy and its initial state, thereby mitigating the "alignment tax."

#### 3.2. Optimal Reward Baseline for Variance Reduction

Reducing the variance of policy gradient estimates is crucial for the stability and efficiency of reinforcement learning. A common technique is to subtract a baseline $b$ from the reward:
$g = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim D, y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}[\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y|x) \cdot (r(x, y) - b)]$
The inclusion of $b$ does not change the expected value of the gradient but can significantly reduce its variance. Theoretically, an optimal baseline $b^*$ exists that minimizes this variance. As derived from setting the derivative of the variance with respect to $b$ to zero, $b^*$ is given by:
$b^* = \frac{\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}[(\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y|x))^2 \cdot r(x, y)]}{\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}[(\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y|x))^2]}$
This formulation weights rewards by the squared magnitude of their score function gradients. However, directly computing this for each trajectory is impractical due to the need for individual gradient norm calculations.

OPO introduces a **simplified and practically feasible form of the optimal baseline for sequence generation problems**. The key assumption is that for language models, the gradients of different tokens are approximately orthogonal, and the norm of the gradient for each token follows a similar distribution. Under this condition, the squared magnitude of the policy gradient for a complete trajectory is approximately proportional to its length ($||\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y|x)||^2 \propto l_y$), where $l_y$ is the length of the response $y$. With this simplification, the optimal reward baseline transforms into a length-weighted average of the reward:
$b^* = \frac{\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}[l_y \cdot r(x, y)]}{\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}[l_y]}$
This refined baseline is both theoretically grounded and straightforward to compute empirically from a batch of sampled responses, making it highly practical for LLM training.

#### 3.3. Overall OPO Algorithm

The OPO algorithm integrates these two components:
1. **Data Collection:** For each input prompt $x$, the algorithm samples $K$ responses $\{y_i\}_{i=1}^K$ directly from the *current* policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)$. This ensures exact on-policy data.
2. **Advantage Calculation:** For each sampled response $y_i$, the advantage $A_i$ is computed as $A_i = r(x, y_i) - b^*(x)$. The empirical optimal baseline $b^*(x)$ is calculated across the $K$ samples for that prompt using the simplified length-weighted average (Equation 12 in the paper).
3. **Policy Optimization:** The policy model $\pi_\theta$ is updated by maximizing the objective function:
 $J_{OPO}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim D, \{y_i\}_{i=1}^K \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)} \left[ \frac{1}{K} \sum_{i=1}^K \log \pi_\theta(y_i|x) \cdot A_i \right]$
A key characteristic of OPO is the deliberate **omission of commonly used KL divergence constraints or entropy regularization terms** from its objective function. This simplification is enabled by the inherent stability and exploration-promoting properties of exact on-policy training combined with the variance-reducing optimal baseline.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The effectiveness of OPO is validated on mathematical reasoning benchmarks using the Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B model (and Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B for an auxiliary experiment). The training data consists of a math subset from Skywork-OR1-RL-Data, and a simple rule-based reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) is used, verified by the Math-Verify evaluator.

OPO's performance is compared against:
- **Off-Policy GRPO:** A variant that reuses data, requiring an explicit entropy penalty.
- **On-Policy GRPO:** A direct comparison with GRPO using exact on-policy training, but with GRPO's original relative reward baseline.
- **Initial SFT Policy:** To demonstrate improvements over the supervised fine-tuned baseline.
- **Reinforce++ (in Appendix):** An additional comparison showing OPO's optimal baseline benefits other algorithms.

Evaluation metrics include `pass@k` (k=1, 2, 4, 8, 16) for accuracy, training dynamics (KL divergence and output entropy) for stability and exploration, and output quality metrics like Self-BLEU (for diversity) and Rep-5 (for repetition).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate OPO's superior performance, enhanced training stability, and improved output quality compared to existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Impact of Exact On-Policy Training (vs. Off-Policy)

- **Performance (Table 1):** Exact on-policy training significantly outperforms off-policy training on the `pass@1` metric across MATH-500, AIME 2024, and AIME 2025 datasets. For larger `pass@k` values, the performance is generally comparable or slightly better. This highlights the effectiveness of strict on-policy adherence for achieving direct and precise improvements.
- **Training Dynamics (Figure 1):** Exact on-policy training exhibits remarkably lower KL divergence and higher entropy throughout the training process compared to off-policy training, even though off-policy training explicitly includes an entropy bonus. This indicates that exact on-policy training inherently stabilizes the policy, reduces the "alignment tax" (as shown by lower KL divergence), and encourages broader exploration (as shown by higher entropy) without the need for additional regularization terms. The paper suggests that off-policy methods, despite sometimes achieving higher training rewards in early stages, may suffer from overfitting, leading to inferior generalization.
- **Output Quality (Table 3):** Reflecting the improved training dynamics, exact on-policy training yields significantly better output quality. It results in lower Rep-5 scores (less intra-sequence repetition) and lower Self-BLEU scores (higher sampling diversity) across all evaluated datasets. This confirms that stable training with higher entropy directly translates to more varied and less repetitive model responses.

#### 4.2. Impact of Optimal Reward Baseline (OPO vs. On-Policy GRPO)

- **Performance (Table 2):** When both OPO and GRPO are trained with exact on-policy settings, OPO consistently outperforms GRPO in most `pass@k` metrics, with improvements becoming more pronounced at higher `k` values (e.g., `pass@8` and `pass@16`). Crucially, OPO is shown to improve accuracy *beyond the initial SFT baseline*, whereas GRPO sometimes shows similar or even slightly reduced performance for `pass@16`. This indicates OPO's superior ability to scale performance and generalize.
- **Training Dynamics (Figure 2):** OPO maintains similar high entropy levels as on-policy GRPO but achieves consistently lower KL divergence. The visualization of KL divergence vs. math performance clearly shows that OPO reaches higher performance levels with more stable training dynamics (lower KL). This suggests that the optimal baseline provides a more effective and less disruptive way to reduce variance, allowing for more stable learning without drifting too far from the initial policy.
- **Output Quality (Table 4):** OPO generates outputs that are slightly more diverse (lower Self-BLEU) and less repetitive (lower Rep-5) than on-policy GRPO. While the differences are smaller than those between on-policy and off-policy training, they further support the idea that OPO's refined baseline contributes to better overall generation quality.

#### 4.3. Generalizability of Optimal Baseline (OPO with Reinforce++)

- The appendix demonstrates that the optimal reward baseline component of OPO can be effectively applied to other RL algorithms, specifically Reinforce++. When integrated with Reinforce++ (excluding its default KL reward), OPO's optimal baseline consistently achieves higher training rewards and maintains higher entropy (Figure 3). This suggests the broad applicability and effectiveness of the proposed optimal baseline for stabilizing training and promoting diverse policy exploration across different policy gradient frameworks.

In summary, the results strongly validate OPO's design principles. Exact on-policy training provides inherent stability and diversity, while the practically derived optimal reward baseline further minimizes gradient variance, leading to superior performance and reduced alignment tax.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "On-Policy RL with Optimal Reward Baseline (OPO)" paper presents a significant advancement in the field of reinforcement learning for large language models, with several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Simplification of RLHF Pipelines:** OPO fundamentally simplifies the Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) pipeline. By eliminating the need for auxiliary components like a separate value model (critic) and explicit regularization terms (such as KL divergence constraints or entropy bonuses), OPO reduces computational overhead, memory requirements, and the complexity of hyperparameter tuning. This simplification makes RLHF training more accessible, faster, and less resource-intensive for researchers and practitioners.

2. **Enhanced Training Stability and Efficiency:** The strict adherence to exact on-policy training directly addresses one of the most persistent challenges in current RL algorithms: training instability due to off-policy updates. This intrinsic stability, combined with the theoretically grounded and practically feasible optimal reward baseline for variance reduction, leads to more robust and predictable training dynamics. More stable training means fewer failed runs, faster convergence, and more reliable performance scaling, which is critical for training large and complex LLMs.

3. **Improved Model Quality and Generalization:** OPO demonstrates that a simpler, more stable RL approach can lead to superior model performance. By mitigating the "alignment tax" (evidenced by lower KL divergence) and encouraging greater exploration (higher output entropy), OPO yields models that are not only better at reasoning tasks but also produce more diverse and less repetitive responses. This is crucial for real-world applications of LLMs, where helpfulness, truthfulness, safety, and nuanced, varied outputs are highly valued. The ability to improve accuracy beyond the initial SFT baseline, particularly at higher `pass@k` values, indicates OPO's effectiveness in enhancing a model's underlying capabilities and its potential for stronger generalization.

4. **Bridging Theory and Practice in RL:** The paper's derivation of a practically feasible optimal reward baseline for sequence generation problems is a significant theoretical and practical contribution. While the optimal baseline has been known in theory, its direct application has been challenging. OPO's simplified formulation, based on the intuitive assumption of length-proportional gradient norms, bridges this gap, enabling principled variance reduction in a real-world, high-dimensional setting like LLM training.

5. **Broad Applicability and Future Research:** Although validated on mathematical reasoning tasks, the core principles of exact on-policy training and a robust variance-reducing baseline are general. OPO's methodology could be applied to a broader range of LLM alignment tasks, including summarization, dialogue systems, content generation, and safety fine-tuning, potentially improving performance and stability across these domains. The success of OPO without KL/entropy regularization terms opens new avenues for research into simpler, more fundamental RL objectives for LLMs.

6. **Contribution to the Open-Source Community:** The integration of OPO into the `verl` library (an open-source RLHF training library) is a commendable contribution. This ensures that the research findings are not only theoretical but also readily available for adoption, experimentation, and further development by the wider AI research community, accelerating progress in LLM alignment and reasoning.

In conclusion, OPO represents a promising direction for developing more efficient, stable, and effective reinforcement learning algorithms for large language models. Its emphasis on fundamental RL principles, coupled with practical innovations, positions it as a valuable contribution that could streamline LLM training pipelines and lead to more capable and versatile AI assistants.
