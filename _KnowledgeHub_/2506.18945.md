# Chain-of-Experts: Unlocking the Communication Power of Mixture-of-Experts Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Chain-of-Experts (CoE) architecture enhances Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models by enabling sequential communication and iterative processing among experts within each layer. This approach improves performance, particularly for reasoning tasks, and offers an efficient scaling method that achieves the capabilities of deeper models with less memory.

## Problem

- Traditional Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models feature experts operating independently and in parallel within a layer, limiting their ability to leverage complementary reasoning patterns.
- Existing MoE scaling primarily relies on increasing model depth, width, or the total number of experts, often leading to substantial computational and memory costs.
- Complex tasks that benefit from multi-expert coordination may not fully utilize the capacity of MoE models due to the lack of explicit intra-layer expert interaction.

## Method

- A ==Chain-of-Experts (CoE)== architecture is introduced, which processes tokens through sequential communication steps within a single Transformer layer.
- Each iterative step utilizes a ==dedicated router== to dynamically select a new subset of experts, allowing for adaptive processing based on the progressively refined token representation.
- ==Inner residual connections== are incorporated at each iterative step to ensure stable updates and preserve information throughout the multi-step refinement process.

## Results

- CoE (C=2, K=4 experts/iteration) consistently outperformed traditional MoE (C=1, K=8 experts) under matched per-token compute, achieving faster convergence and reduced validation loss on math reasoning tasks.
- A **4-layer** CoE model with **2** iterations matched the performance of significantly deeper MoE models (**8** or **12** layers) while reducing memory usage by up to **42%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing iteration-specific gating or inner residual connections severely degraded performance, underscoring their necessity for CoE's effectiveness and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enabling sequential communication and dynamic, iteration-specific routing among experts within a layer significantly enhances the model's capacity for compositional reasoning and expert specialization.
> - Introducing 'depth through expert iteration' offers an efficient new axis for scaling MoE models, capable of achieving performance comparable to much deeper or wider models with reduced resource requirements.
> - Both iteration-specific gating and inner residual connections are critical architectural components, preventing performance degradation and ensuring training stability in the iterative expert refinement process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures has become a cornerstone for scaling large language models (LLMs) efficiently. Traditional dense Transformer models require immense computational resources as they grow in size, making MoE a popular alternative by sparsely activating only a subset of expert networks per input token. This sparsity allows for a significant increase in parameter count without a proportional rise in runtime cost, leading to improved memory utilization and inference throughput.

Previous MoE research has largely focused on three main areas:
1. **Scaling expert capacity:** Increasing the total number of experts to boost model scale.
2. **Improving routing algorithms:** Developing sophisticated mechanisms to select the most relevant experts for each token.
3. **Enhancing training stability:** Addressing challenges like expert imbalance and convergence issues inherent in sparse models.

However, a fundamental architectural assumption underpins most existing MoE models: experts operate independently and in parallel, with no explicit interaction or communication *within the same layer*. While this design maximizes parallelism and system efficiency, the authors contend it may limit the model's ability to leverage complementary reasoning patterns across experts, potentially underutilizing its vast capacity for complex tasks requiring multi-expert coordination.

The Chain-of-Experts (CoE) work directly challenges this independence assumption by introducing *sequential communication* and *iterative processing* among experts *within each layer*. This is a significant departure from the prevailing paradigm. While other research has explored iterative architectures (e.g., Universal Transformers, Adaptive Computation Time, ALBERT) and expert reuse (e.g., SparseUT, MoEUT, RMoE, OLMoE), most of these approaches involve iteration or sharing *across different Transformer layers* or introduce recurrent states to influence routing decisions. CoE, in contrast, focuses on establishing a "relay race" of expert processing *inside a single layer*, allowing intermediate representations to be refined through multiple, sequential expert applications. This positions CoE as a novel exploration of intra-layer expert interaction, offering a new dimension for scaling and enhancing the representational power of sparse models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and validate Chain-of-Experts (CoE), a new Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture that integrates sequential expert communication within each layer of a Transformer model. This objective is driven by a critical motivation: to overcome the limitations imposed by the independent and parallel operation of experts in traditional MoE designs.

The authors identify several specific motivations for developing CoE:
- **Unlocking Complementary Reasoning:** Current MoE models may struggle to fully exploit complementary reasoning patterns, especially for complex tasks that benefit from experts building upon each other's outputs.
- **Enhancing Representational Capacity:** By enabling experts to process tokens iteratively, CoE aims to enrich the model's representational capacity and foster more sophisticated expert compositions.
- **Introducing a New Scaling Axis:** The conventional methods of scaling MoE models involve increasing model depth, width, or the number of experts. CoE seeks to introduce "depth through expert iteration" as a novel and potentially more efficient scaling dimension.
- **Improving Performance under Fixed Compute:** The ultimate goal is to achieve better model performance (e.g., lower validation loss, higher accuracy on reasoning tasks) while maintaining or reducing computational cost and memory usage compared to standard MoE models.
- **Fostering Expert Specialization:** By allowing tokens to be re-evaluated and routed to different experts at each iterative step, CoE is motivated to encourage finer-grained expert specialization and dynamic adaptation to evolving token representations.

In essence, the paper aims to shift the focus from merely selecting experts to orchestrating their sequential interaction, believing that this collaborative processing can lead to more expressive and efficient sparse neural networks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Chain-of-Experts (CoE) methodology fundamentally reconfigures the internal operation of a standard Mixture-of-Experts layer. Instead of a single, parallel pass through chosen experts, CoE introduces an iterative, sequential process over `C` communication steps within a single layer.

The core approach is defined as follows:
- **Sequential Processing:** For an input token embedding `x`, an initial hidden state `x^(0) = x` is established. At each iteration `t` (from 1 to `C`), the next hidden state `x^(t)` is computed by processing the previous hidden state `x^(t-1)` through a selected set of experts.
- **Iteration-based Independent Routing:** Crucially, CoE employs a *dedicated router* for each iteration `t`. This means that at each step, the model dynamically re-evaluates the current hidden state `x^(t-1)` and selects a new set of `K/C` experts using a unique gating function `g_t,i`. This mechanism allows experts to be chosen adaptively based on the progressively refined token representation, facilitating dynamic expert specialization.
- **Inner Residual Connections:** To ensure stable updates and preserve information across iterations, a residual connection (`I_r * x^(t-1)`) is added at *each* iterative step. This "inner residual" design helps stabilize the multi-step refinement process, preventing information degradation and facilitating effective gradient flow.
- **Computational Parity:** To ensure a fair comparison with standard MoE, CoE aims to maintain comparable per-token computational cost. If a standard MoE layer activates `K` experts in one pass, CoE with `C` iterations activates `K/C` experts at each step, resulting in a total of `K` expert activations per token per layer, thus preserving FLOPs.

**Experimental Setup:**
The empirical validation of CoE is conducted using a scaled-down version of the DeepSeek-V2-Lite architecture, configured with 544 million parameters (excluding embeddings), 4 Transformer layers, a hidden size of 1024, and 8 attention heads. Each layer is an MoE layer with 63 routed experts and 1 shared expert.
- **Baseline MoE:** Selects 8 routed experts and the shared expert in a single pass (K=8, C=1).
- **CoE Configuration:** Uses 2 iterations (C=2) and selects 4 routed experts per iteration (K=4, effectively processing 8 experts in total across 2 steps), plus the shared expert. This setup ensures matched per-token expert processing between CoE and MoE.
- **Datasets:** Training is performed on general open-domain SlimPajama, reasoning-domain-specific MetaMathQA, and a 1:1 mixture of both.
- **Metrics:** Validation loss, and zero-shot accuracy on reasoning and commonsense benchmarks like ARC-E, HellaSwag, and PIQA.
- **Training Details:** AdamW optimizer, linear learning rate schedule with warmup, gradient clipping, batch size 64, sequence length 512.
- **Infrastructure:** PyTorch, leveraging Fully-Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP) Trainers, on NVIDIA H100 GPUs.

Ablation studies are performed to investigate the importance of iteration-specific gating and inner residual connections, comparing them against variants with shared gating or only outer residual connections. Furthermore, co-activation matrices are visualized to understand expert specialization and interaction patterns across iterations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Chain-of-Experts (CoE) provides significant advantages over traditional Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, primarily by enabling richer expert communication and offering a new, efficient scaling axis.

1. **Improved Performance under Equal Compute:**
 - CoE (K=4 experts/iteration, C=2 iterations) consistently outperforms MoE (K=8 experts, C=1 iteration) when the total number of expert computations per token is matched. For instance, on math reasoning tasks, CoE reduced validation loss from 1.20 to 1.12, showing faster convergence.
 - On general downstream benchmarks (ARC-E, HellaSwag, PIQA), CoE generally achieves comparable or superior performance, indicating its benefits extend beyond specific domains.
 - The performance gain is particularly pronounced in *sparse routing settings*, where increasing communication steps leads to noticeable validation loss improvements. In contrast, under dense settings (all experts active), iterative processing offers limited gains, suggesting that sparsity encourages expert specialization beneficial to CoE.

2. **Efficient Scaling through Iteration Depth:**
 - CoE introduces a novel scaling axis: *depth through expert iteration*. This approach proves more efficient than conventional scaling methods.
 - **Matching Deeper MoE with Lower Memory:** A 4-layer CoE model with C=2 iterations matches the performance of significantly deeper MoE models (8 or 12 layers) while reducing memory usage by up to 42%. This highlights CoE's ability to achieve greater effective depth without increasing parameter count or layer depth.
 - **Outperforming Wider MoE:** CoE (C=2) achieves better convergence and performance compared to MoE variants that increase expert selection width (K=16 or 24), all while consuming comparable memory and compute resources. This suggests that iterative processing of fewer experts is more effective than a single, wider selection.
 - **Matched Performance with Fewer Experts:** CoE with 48 total experts (C=2, K=4) achieves similar validation loss to an MoE with 64 total experts (K=8), reducing memory usage by 17.6%. This demonstrates improved expert reuse and capacity utilization.

3. **Criticality of Key Architectural Components:**
 - **Iteration-specific Gating:** Ablation studies reveal that removing independent, iteration-specific routing (i.e., reusing the same expert selection across all steps) severely degrades performance, plateauing validation loss around 1.5, worse than both standard CoE and MoE. This underscores the necessity of dynamic routing based on evolving token representations for effective sequential processing.
 - **Inner Residual Connections:** Applying residual connections at *each* iterative step ("inner residuals") is crucial for training stability and effective multi-step refinement. An alternative design with only a final "outer residual" leads to slower convergence and higher validation loss, particularly on mathematical data.

4. **Evidence of Step-wise Expert Specialization:**
 - Analysis of expert co-activation patterns between iterations (visualized as KxK matrices) shows highly non-uniform and asymmetric distributions. This indicates that tokens are routed to meaningfully different experts at each step, supporting the hypothesis that CoE leverages iteration-specific gating to perform progressive, compositional refinement. Different iterations foster the emergence of specialized roles for experts.

5. **Theoretical Advantages:**
 - **Combinatorial Flexibility:** CoE significantly increases the number of possible expert combinations. For `n` experts, selecting `K` experts in one shot yields `C(n, K)` combinations. With `C` iterations selecting `K/C` experts per step, the combinations expand to `C(n, K/C)^C`. For `n=64` and `K=8` (split into `k=4` experts over `C=2` iterations), CoE offers 823 times more expert pairings, enabling more diverse interactions.
 - **Implicit Depth Expansion:** The iterative nature of CoE deepens token representations over time. Each expert in a later iteration builds on the output of previous experts, effectively creating a deeper internal computation pathway without increasing the number of physical Transformer layers.

6. **Supplementary Findings:**
 - **Impact of Shared Experts:** Introducing a shared expert marginally improved PIQA normalization scores, especially for smaller CoE configurations, suggesting it can aid generalization.
 - **Extended Training:** CoE's advantages persist and are often amplified with more training steps, with CoE (K=8, C=2) consistently achieving lower final validation loss than MoE (K=8, C=1). However, naively increasing iterations (C > 2) showed diminishing or unstable returns, suggesting an optimal iteration count or the need for more advanced optimization.
 - **Expert Stability:** CoE helps mitigate expert collapse, a common issue in MoE, by encouraging diverse expert usage across iterations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Chain-of-Experts (CoE) architecture presents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in the field of large language models, particularly within the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift in MoE Design:** CoE fundamentally challenges the long-standing assumption of independent, parallel expert processing within MoE layers. By demonstrating the benefits of *sequential communication* and *intra-layer iteration*, it introduces a new perspective on how experts should interact to maximize their collective intelligence. This shift emphasizes the *flow of information* and *compositional reasoning* over mere aggregate capacity.

2. **Novel and Efficient Scaling Axis:** The introduction of "depth through expert iteration" as a scaling factor offers a powerful alternative to traditional scaling methods (increasing model depth or width). CoE achieves comparable or superior performance with substantially reduced memory footprint and parameter counts, making it a highly attractive strategy for developing more resource-efficient and capable sparse foundation models. This is crucial for democratizing access to large models and enabling their deployment in constrained environments.

3. **Enhanced Representational Power and Specialization:** CoE's iterative, dynamically routed process leads to a dramatic increase in combinatorial flexibility, allowing for a much richer space of expert interactions. This enhanced flexibility, combined with the observed step-wise expert specialization, enables models to learn more sophisticated and nuanced representations of input tokens, which is particularly beneficial for complex reasoning tasks (e.g., mathematics).

4. **Improved Performance for Reasoning Tasks:** The empirical results on mathematical reasoning datasets are particularly compelling. The nature of reasoning often involves sequential, multi-step problem-solving. CoE's architecture naturally aligns with this process, allowing experts to progressively refine intermediate representations, thus unlocking greater reasoning capabilities within a sparse framework.

5. **Foundational for Future Sparse Models:** CoE provides a strong argument for integrating more sophisticated intra-layer expert communication into future sparse architectures. It suggests that the quality of expert interaction is as important as, if not more important than, the sheer number of experts. This could guide the development of next-generation modular language models that are both powerful and efficient.

**Potential Impact on Research and Industry:**
- **Academic Research:** CoE opens new avenues for theoretical and empirical research into expert orchestration, dynamic routing mechanisms, and the interplay between sparsity and sequential computation. Future work may explore optimal iteration counts, dynamic halting mechanisms (like ACT), and more complex intra-layer communication topologies.
- **Industry Applications:** For industry, CoE offers a pathway to building more powerful LLMs that are cheaper to train and deploy due to their memory efficiency and lower compute requirements for a given performance target. This could accelerate the development of specialized AI assistants and complex reasoning agents.

While the current implementation has some limitations (e.g., sequential processing overhead, incompatibility with existing checkpoints, single-device testing), the foundational insights provided by CoE are significant. It points towards a future where modularity in LLMs is not just about sparse activation but about intelligent, communicative collaboration among specialized experts.
