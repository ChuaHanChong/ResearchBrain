# NovaFlow: Zero-Shot Manipulation via Actionable Flow from Generated Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> NovaFlow is a robotics framework that performs zero-shot manipulation of rigid, articulated, and deformable objects across various robot embodiments by deriving actionable 3D object flow from large-scale generated videos. It achieves higher success rates compared to existing zero-shot methods and even surpasses demonstration-based policies trained with dozens of examples.

## Problem

- Modern Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for generalist robots demand vast, expensive, robot-specific interaction data, creating a significant data bottleneck.
- Existing robotic manipulation methods struggle with broad generalization across diverse tasks, novel objects, and different robot embodiments without extensive retraining or specific demonstrations.
- Even modular robotic systems frequently rely on predefined skill primitives or learned skills from real demonstrations, which reintroduces the data collection challenge for wide applicability.

## Method

- NovaFlow introduces a modular framework that decouples high-level task understanding from low-level robot control.
- It leverages pre-trained, internet-scale video generation models to synthesize task-solving videos based on natural language instructions and initial robot observations.
- The framework extracts an actionable 3D object flow from these generated videos, which serves as an embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation to guide robot actions without requiring robot-specific training data.

## Results

- NovaFlow achieved superior success rates on real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming both other zero-shot methods and imitation learning policies trained with 10-30 demonstrations.
- The framework demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization across diverse tasks involving rigid, articulated, and deformable objects, using both a Franka arm and a Spot mobile robot.
- Utilizing an optional goal image in video generation improved precision tasks, such as increasing the block insertion success rate from 40% to 80%, by enhancing the validity of the generated actionable flow.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale video generation models implicitly encode extensive common-sense knowledge about object dynamics and plausible task execution, which can be distilled into an actionable format for robotics.
> - An 'actionable 3D object flow' provides an effective, model-free, and embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation that successfully bridges high-level task understanding and low-level robot control.
> - VLM-based rejection sampling is crucial for filtering out physically implausible or hallucinated motions from generated videos, significantly enhancing the reliability of the extracted object flow.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "generalist robots" capable of performing a wide array of manipulation tasks in dynamic, unstructured environments without extensive task-specific training is a central, long-standing goal in robotics. NovaFlow directly addresses a critical bottleneck hindering this ambition: the enormous data requirements of modern Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

Current state-of-the-art VLA models, inspired by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) trained on internet-scale datasets, attempt to learn end-to-end policies for robotic control. However, these models demand vast quantities of robot-specific vision-language-action data, which is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to collect. This "data bottleneck" severely limits their generalizability and scalability, especially when transferring across different robot embodiments or novel tasks.

NovaFlow positions itself as an alternative, modular approach to achieve generalist robotics. Instead of end-to-end training, it advocates for decoupling task understanding from low-level robot control, a strategy that aligns with traditional robotic engineering principles (e.g., inverse kinematics, model predictive control). Previous modular systems often leverage large language or vision-language models for high-level planning, but still rely on predefined skill primitives or learned skills from real-world demonstrations for execution, thus reintroducing the data bottleneck.

The key innovation of NovaFlow within this landscape is its novel approach to task understanding: repurposing large-scale, pre-trained video generation models as a source of "commonsense task understanding and implicit physical knowledge." Unlike prior video-based manipulation methods that train inverse dynamics models or policies from generated videos but still require extensive *robot-specific* data for adaptation, NovaFlow extracts an **actionable 3D object flow** from these *internet-scale* video generation models. This object-centric, model-free representation is distinct from 6D pose-based methods (which assume rigidity) and other flow-based methods that still require robot data for training their flow generators or executors. By leveraging knowledge implicitly learned from vast, diverse internet videos, NovaFlow bypasses the need for robot-specific data collection for task understanding and achieves true zero-shot, demonstration-free, and embodiment-agnostic manipulation, pushing the frontier of robotic generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of NovaFlow is to enable robots to autonomously execute novel manipulation tasks in a **zero-shot, demonstration-free, and embodiment-agnostic** manner. This means a robot should be able to understand and perform a task described in natural language, even if it has never seen that specific task or object before, and without requiring any prior robot-specific demonstrations or fine-tuning.

The primary motivations driving this research are:

1. **Overcoming the Data Bottleneck in VLA Models:** Traditional end-to-end VLA models for robotics require immense amounts of robot-specific interaction data, which is expensive and time-consuming to collect. NovaFlow aims to circumvent this by leveraging readily available internet-scale video data through large pre-trained video generation models.
2. **Achieving Generalization across Tasks, Objects, and Embodiments:** Many existing robotic manipulation methods are task-specific, object-specific, or embodiment-dependent. NovaFlow seeks to develop a framework that can naturally transfer across different types of manipulation tasks (rigid, articulated, deformable objects) and various robotic platforms (e.g., a fixed-base arm and a mobile robot) without needing retraining.
3. **Decoupling Task Understanding from Low-Level Control:** By creating a modular system, NovaFlow aims to separate the high-level semantic reasoning and common-sense understanding (derived from video models) from the intricate details of robot control (handled by traditional robotics tools). This modularity is posited as key to achieving broad generalization and robustness.
4. **Leveraging Implicit Knowledge in Foundation Models:** The researchers hypothesize that large-scale video generation models, trained on diverse internet videos, encode a rich, generalizable understanding of object dynamics and plausible task execution. The goal is to distill this implicit knowledge into an actionable format for robots.
5. **Developing a Universal Intermediate Representation:** NovaFlow is motivated by the need for an intermediate representation that is abstract enough to capture diverse object motions but concrete enough to be directly translatable into robot actions. The proposed **3D actionable object flow** serves this purpose, offering a model-free and embodiment-agnostic description of desired object motion.

In essence, NovaFlow strives to unlock the potential of large generative models for robotics by providing a practical, data-efficient pathway to highly generalizable and autonomous manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NovaFlow is an object-centric and embodiment-agnostic framework composed of two core components: a **flow generator** and a **flow executor**. The entire pipeline operates in a demonstration-free and zero-shot manner, requiring no robot-specific data or training before task execution.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

Given an initial RGB-D image from the robot's perspective and a natural language instruction (with an optional goal image for precision tasks), NovaFlow first synthesizes a task-solving video. This video is then processed to distill a 3D actionable object flow, which subsequently guides the robot's actions.

#### 3.2. Flow Generator Pipeline

The flow generator's objective is to convert a high-level task description into a structured 3D object flow ( ∈ ℝ^(T × M × 3)) representing the desired trajectories of *M* object keypoints across *T* frames. This involves a sequence of five main steps:

1. **Video Generation:** A pre-trained video generation model (e.g., open-source Wan 2.1 or closed-source Veo) takes the initial image and language prompt to synthesize a video {Î_1..., Î_T}. For tasks requiring higher precision, an optional goal image can be provided, enabling First-Last-Frame-to-Video (FLF2V) generation. Prompt engineering, including prompt extension via LLMs, is used to enhance video quality and controllability.
2. **Monocular Depth Estimation and Calibration:** The generated 2D video is lifted to 3D by applying a monocular video depth estimation model (e.g., MegaSaM using MoGe) frame-by-frame, yielding estimated depth maps {Ô_1..., Ô_T}. A crucial step is **depth calibration**: since monocular depth is prone to scaling errors, the entire estimated depth sequence is anchored to the robot's initial ground-truth depth map D. A scaling factor is computed (median depth of Ô_1 vs. D) and applied to correct the metric scale, ensuring spatial accuracy.
3. **3D Point Tracking:** With the calibrated depth and generated video, a 3D point tracking model (e.g., TAPIP3D) tracks dense per-point 3D motion. Query points are uniformly sampled on the first frame, and the model outputs 3D trajectories for these points across all frames.
4. **Object Grounding and Rejection Sampling:** The dense 3D trajectories capture the entire scene's motion. To isolate the target object's motion, an object grounding pipeline (e.g., Grounded-SAM2, combining Grounding DINO and SAM2) is used to generate object masks throughout the video. These masks filter the dense trajectories to distill the final actionable 3D object flow (), representing consistently tracked keypoints on the object's surface. A critical step for robustness is **rejection sampling**: multiple video candidates are generated, their corresponding object flow images (back-projected flow onto the first frame) are created, and a VLM (e.g., Google Gemini) evaluates these flow images to select the most plausible one, effectively filtering out hallucinations or physically implausible motions from the video generation model.

#### 3.3. Flow Executor Pipeline

The flow executor translates the abstract 3D object flow into a sequence of executable robot actions. It is designed to handle different object types:

1. **Rigid Object Manipulation:** For rigid and articulated (treated as part-wise rigid) objects, the 3D flow of keypoints is used to estimate the object's rigid transformations (rotation R and translation t) across frames using the **Kabsch algorithm**. This algorithm finds the optimal rigid transformation that minimizes the sum of squared errors between corresponding keypoints. Assuming the object is firmly grasped, the object's 6D pose trajectory is converted into an end-effector trajectory by applying a grasp transformation, obtained from a grasp proposal model (e.g., GraspGen).
2. **Deformable Object Manipulation:** For deformable objects, their complex dynamics preclude rigid transformations. NovaFlow extends to this by using the 3D object flow  as a **dense tracking objective for model-based planning**. A particle-based dynamics model (e.g., PhysTwin) predicts the object's future state. The control problem is framed as a Model Predictive Control (MPC) task, minimizing a cumulative cost function based on the sum of squared Euclidean distances between the predicted particle states and the target flow points. This leverages explicit point-wise correspondences, offering potential advantages over correspondence-free metrics.
3. **Trajectory Optimization:** For both rigid and deformable manipulation, the derived end-effector or joint trajectories are refined using **trajectory optimization**. This non-linear least-squares problem (solved with, e.g., Levenberg-Marquardt via PyRoki and Jax) aims to find an optimal sequence of joint configurations that ensures smoothness, adheres to joint limits, and avoids collisions with the environment. Constraints are incorporated as cost terms or hard constraints (e.g., start/end configurations).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

NovaFlow's efficacy and generalizability were rigorously validated through real-world experiments on a variety of manipulation tasks and robot embodiments.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted using two distinct robotic platforms:
- A **table-top Franka arm** with a Robotiq-85 gripper for common manipulation tasks.
- A **Spot quadrupedal mobile robot** for mobile manipulation scenarios.
Tasks were categorized by object type:
- **Rigid (R):** "Hang the mug," "Inserting a block," "Placing a cup on a saucer," "Watering a plant."
- **Articulated (A):** "Opening a drawer," "Opening a lid."
- **Deformable (D):** "Straightening a rope."
Object placements were randomized after each trial to assess robustness. A single RealSense D455 depth camera was used for rigid/articulated tasks, while three synchronized cameras were used for deformable tasks (as required by the PhysTwin model).

#### 4.2. Performance Against Baselines

NovaFlow was compared against two groups of baselines:
1. **Demo-free, zero-shot baselines:**
 - **AVDC [\*]:** Adapted to NovaFlow's pipeline by applying its optical flow extraction directly to generated videos.
 - **VidBot:** Learns flow from large-scale human interaction datasets.
2. **Data-dependent baselines (requiring demonstrations):**
 - **Diffusion Policy (DP) [+]:** An imitation learning policy trained with 10 and 30 demonstrations per task.
 - **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) [+]:** Trained with 30 demonstrations to convert generated robot task-solving videos into actions.

### 5. Main Findings

- **Superior Zero-Shot Performance:** NovaFlow achieved the highest success rates across all tasks among zero-shot methods, significantly outperforming AVDC and VidBot.
- **Outperforms Data-Dependent Baselines:** Remarkably, NovaFlow also surpassed data-dependent baselines (DP and IDM) that were trained with 10–30 demonstrations. This highlights its ability to generalize effectively without any robot-specific data collection or task training.
- **Baseline Limitations:**
 - AVDC struggled with precision tasks and long-horizon placements due to its 2D optical flow lacking 3D awareness and long-term coherence.
 - VidBot excelled in affordance-centric interactions but failed on tasks requiring object-object relations and precise relative pose placement, indicating its training emphasized object affordances over multi-object constraints.
 - DP showed poor generalization from a few examples, especially when evaluation tasks were randomly sampled outside the training distribution.
 - IDM suffered from domain shift, as it was trained on real-world demos but had to interpret kinematically imperfect or inconsistent generated videos.

#### 5.1. Ablation Studies and Failure Analysis

- **Effect of Goal Image:** For millimeter-precision tasks like "block insertion," using a goal image (FLF2V) significantly improved NovaFlow's task success rate (80% vs. 40% for Wan2.1 without a goal image), primarily by increasing the "Video Success Rate" (valid actionable flow). The closed-source Veo model proved more robust, achieving high success even without a goal image.
- **Failure Modes:** A detailed analysis identified four primary failure categories:
 1. **Video Failure (2%):** Generative model produces physically implausible or inconsistent content (mitigated but not eliminated by VLM rejection).
 2. **Tracking Failure (2%):** Inaccuracies in 3D point tracking due to textureless surfaces, occlusions, or accumulated inconsistencies from video generation.
 3. **Grasp Failure (5%):** Robot fails to secure the object correctly (bad approach, missed grasp, slip).
 4. **Execution Failure (7%):** Errors during trajectory execution, such as collisions, joint limit violations, or inability to follow the planned path.
 - The analysis revealed that most failures occurred in the "last mile" – grasp and execution, suggesting that while flow estimation is robust, physical interaction remains the primary bottleneck, akin to the sim-to-real gap.

#### 5.2. Runtime Analysis

NovaFlow was deployed on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU. A complete flow generation pipeline using the Veo model took approximately 2 minutes end-to-end. The dominant time-consuming modules were video generation and 3D lifting, indicating areas for future optimization or hardware acceleration.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

NovaFlow represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in its push towards generalist robots capable of zero-shot task execution. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key achievements:

1. **Breakthrough in Zero-Shot Generalization:** By successfully demonstrating robust manipulation of rigid, articulated, and deformable objects across different embodiments (Franka arm and Spot robot) without any task-specific training or demonstrations, NovaFlow addresses a core challenge in robotics. This opens up possibilities for deploying robots in highly dynamic and unstructured environments where pre-training or extensive data collection is impractical or impossible.
2. **Efficient Leverage of Foundation Models:** NovaFlow effectively repurposes the implicit commonsense knowledge embedded in large-scale video generation models, turning them into powerful "task understanding" components for robotics. This strategy bypasses the severe data bottleneck faced by end-to-end VLA models, demonstrating a more data-efficient pathway to generalist AI in robotics.
3. **Robust and Flexible Modular Architecture:** The modular design, decoupling high-level task understanding (flow generator) from low-level robot control (flow executor) via an actionable 3D object flow, offers significant advantages. It allows for the independent improvement and replacement of individual components (e.g., using newer, faster video generation models or more robust perception modules), leading to a more adaptable and future-proof system.
4. **Novel Intermediate Representation:** The introduction and effective utilization of "actionable 3D object flow" as an embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation is a key conceptual contribution. It provides a more generalizable description of object dynamics compared to 6D poses and offers a well-conditioned objective for planning, especially for deformable objects. The VLM-based rejection sampling for flow validation further enhances the reliability of this representation.
5. **Superior Performance:** Outperforming both existing zero-shot methods and data-dependent imitation learning policies (trained on dozens of demonstrations) underscores NovaFlow's practical effectiveness and highlights the power of its approach. This suggests that leveraging vast internet data through generative models can yield better generalization than limited robot-specific datasets.

The potential impact of NovaFlow is far-reaching. It could accelerate the deployment of autonomous robots in diverse applications, from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and exploration, where robots need to adapt quickly to new tasks and environments. By reducing the reliance on costly robot data collection, it democratizes access to advanced robotic capabilities. Future work, focusing on integrating closed-loop feedback and dynamic replanning to address the identified bottlenecks in physical interaction (grasping and execution), could further enhance NovaFlow's robustness and push the boundaries towards truly autonomous and resilient robotic systems.
