# DemoHLM: From One Demonstration to Generalizable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and BeingBeyond developed DemoHLM, a framework enabling humanoid robots to acquire generalizable loco-manipulation skills from a single human demonstration in simulation. This approach achieves successful zero-shot transfer to a physical Unitree G1 robot across diverse tasks, demonstrating robust performance comparable to simulation.

## Problem

- Integrating stable locomotion and dexterous manipulation, known as loco-manipulation, remains challenging for humanoid robots in dynamic environments.
- Current methods often rely on extensive and costly real-world data collection or highly task-specific designs, limiting scalability and generalization.
- Policies learned often struggle to generalize to varied initial robot or object poses and achieve robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to physical robots.

## Method

- A hierarchical control architecture combines a robust low-level whole-body controller (WBC) with a high-level manipulation policy for efficient action space management.
- A novel simulation-based data generation pipeline synthesizes a large, diverse dataset from a single human teleoperated demonstration by transforming trajectories into object-centric and proprioception-centric forms.
- The high-level manipulation policy is trained using Behavior Cloning (BC) with advanced architectures like ACT or Diffusion Policy on this synthesized data, enabling generalization across varying initial conditions.

## Results

- The framework successfully achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, with policies trained solely in simulation performing robustly on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot across six diverse loco-manipulation tasks.
- Policies trained with more expressive Behavior Cloning architectures (ACT, Diffusion Policy) demonstrated higher success rates in simulation compared to MLP baselines, showing better capture of complex behaviors.
- Real-world experiments showed comparable success rates to simulation across tasks; for example, LiftBox and PressCube achieved 5/5 successful trials, and Handover 4/5, demonstrating strong generalization and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, generalizable loco-manipulation data can be efficiently generated in simulation from a single human demonstration by transforming trajectories into object-centric and proprioception-centric representations.
> - A hierarchical control structure effectively simplifies the learning problem by offloading complex whole-body dynamics to a robust low-level controller, allowing the high-level policy to focus on task-specific manipulation.
> - Advanced behavior cloning architectures, such as ACT and Diffusion Policy, are crucial for learning complex temporal dependencies in humanoid loco-manipulation from generated data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen remarkable advancements, particularly in whole-body control, enabling robots to perform expressive and dynamic movements such as dancing and martial arts. However, the integration of locomotion and manipulation – known as "loco-manipulation" – remains a significant challenge. This paper positions itself squarely within this underexplored area, seeking to bridge the gap between impressive whole-body control capabilities and the development of truly autonomous and generalizable loco-manipulation policies for real-world applications.

Prior research on humanoid control broadly falls into several categories:
- **Whole-Body Control and Teleoperation**: Much progress has been made in learning sophisticated whole-body controllers and enabling humanoid teleoperation. While these studies allow for impressive demonstrations, they often rely on human operators for real-time control or large, costly datasets collected through teleoperation, limiting true autonomy and scalability.
- **Simulation-Based Loco-Manipulation with Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Some works explore RL for loco-manipulation in simulation. However, these approaches often suffer from reliance on task-specific designs, such as predefined subtask decompositions or carefully engineered reward functions. This task-specific nature inherently restricts their scalability and generalization to novel scenarios or tasks.
- **Scalable Learning using Human-Object Interaction Datasets**: Another line of research leverages large human-object interaction datasets and RL for learning humanoid behaviors, often employing body models like SMPL. While offering scalability in data, these methods typically use specialized simulation parameters (e.g., exaggerated friction) that hinder direct sim-to-real transfer to physical humanoid robots, often failing to demonstrate success in real-world deployment.
- **Real-Robot Data Collection and Imitation Learning**: Learning autonomous policies from real-robot data, often via whole-body teleoperation and subsequent imitation learning, has shown promise in reproducing human-like behaviors. However, collecting such high-quality, diverse real-world datasets at scale for spatial generalization and multi-task learning remains prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- **Data Generation for Fixed-Base Manipulators**: A related area, exemplified by the MimicGen line of work, focuses on generating diverse demonstrations from a single seed demonstration for fixed-base robot arms. These methods automate the synthesis of varied trajectories under different initial object poses, significantly improving data efficiency for imitation learning.

DemoHLM directly addresses the limitations of these existing approaches. It tackles the challenge of generalizable humanoid loco-manipulation by integrating concepts from advanced whole-body control, imitation learning, and, most critically, pioneering scalable data generation techniques *specifically adapted for humanoid robots*. By extending the MimicGen paradigm to the complex domain of whole-body humanoid control, DemoHLM aims to overcome the bottlenecks of costly real-world data, task-specific designs, and poor sim-to-real transfer, thereby making a significant contribution to enabling more autonomous and versatile humanoid robot capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, DemoHLM, that enables generalizable loco-manipulation skills on a real humanoid robot, starting from merely a single human demonstration in simulation.

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:
- **Addressing the Loco-Manipulation Gap**: Despite advances in isolated whole-body control, humanoid loco-manipulation, which requires dynamic balance, coordinated movement, contact-rich interaction, and visual feedback, remains largely underexplored and difficult to achieve autonomously in human environments.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity and Cost**: Traditional approaches often depend on extensive and costly real-world data collection, or predefined task specifications, which severely limit the scalability, autonomy, and generalization capabilities of humanoid robots. There is a strong need for data-efficient learning paradigms.
- **Enhancing Generalization**: Existing methods frequently struggle with generalizing to varied initial conditions (e.g., different object or robot starting poses) or to a diverse suite of tasks without significant re-engineering or retraining. The goal is to learn policies that are robust to spatial variations.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**: While simulation offers a safe and scalable environment for learning, transferring policies effectively to physical robots remains a persistent challenge. The authors are motivated to develop a framework that guarantees robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- **Extending Data Generation Paradigms**: Inspired by the success of data generation methods like MimicGen for fixed-base manipulators, the authors are motivated to adapt and extend these concepts to the inherently more complex domain of humanoid loco-manipulation. This extension is non-trivial, as it requires the robot to coordinate its entire body to replay end-effector trajectories while maintaining balance, locomotion, and dynamic stability.

In essence, the research seeks to empower humanoid robots with versatile and adaptable loco-manipulation skills by creating an efficient, simulation-based learning framework that minimizes the need for burdensome data collection and maximizes policy generalization and real-world applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DemoHLM proposes a hierarchical control architecture coupled with a novel simulation-based data generation pipeline to achieve generalizable humanoid loco-manipulation.

**A. Hierarchical Control Architecture:**
The framework decomposes the control problem into two levels:
1. **Low-Level Whole-Body Controller (WBC)**:
 - **Foundation**: This controller is based on the Adaptive Motion Optimization (AMO) framework, known for its robustness and sim-to-real transfer capabilities.
 - **Inputs**: It takes a set of desired high-level motion parameters, including linear velocities (v_x, v_y), yaw rate (ω), desired torso configuration (height, roll, pitch, yaw), and desired upper-body joint positions (q_upper).
 - **Outputs**: The WBC translates these high-level commands into target joint positions for the entire robot, which are then tracked using PD control.
 - **Function**: It handles the complex dynamics of whole-body balance and locomotion, effectively reducing the dimensionality of the action space for the high-level policy. It operates at a high frequency (50 Hz) for fast stabilization and tracking.
 - **Active Vision**: A proportional controller manages a 2-DoF neck, steering the camera to keep the target object near the image center, mimicking human gaze stabilization.

2. **High-Level Manipulation Policy**:
 - **Objective**: To learn task-relevant manipulation behaviors by issuing commands to the low-level WBC.
 - **Observations**: The policy receives a state vector comprising joint positions and velocities (q_pos, q_vel), torso roll and pitch (r, p), and the estimated 6D pose of the target object in the camera frame (p_camera_obj).
 - **Actions**: It outputs the high-level motion commands directly to the whole-body controller.
 - **Learning**: Behavior Cloning (BC) is employed to train this policy on the large, diverse dataset generated in simulation. The authors evaluate different BC architectures, including a simple Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with action chunking, Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT), and Diffusion Policy, to assess their effectiveness. This policy operates at a lower frequency (10 Hz) for longer-horizon reasoning.

**B. Simulation-Based Data Generation Pipeline:**
This is the core innovation enabling data efficiency and generalization, building upon a single human demonstration.
1. **Single Demonstration Collection**: A human teleoperates the humanoid robot in simulation using a VR device (Apple Vision Pro, leveraging VisionProTeleop) to perform a task successfully. The robot's full trajectory, including joint positions, end-effector poses, and object poses, is recorded.
2. **Trajectory Segmentation**: The demonstration is divided into two phases based on the first contact time (t_c) with the object: a pre-contact segment (τ_o) and a post-contact (manipulation) segment (τ_p).
3. **Object-Centric and Proprioception-Centric Transformation**:
 - **Pre-Manipulation**: The pre-contact segment (τ_o) is converted into *object-centric* end-effector poses (T_eef_obj), meaning the end-effector poses are defined relative to the object's initial pose. This ensures that the robot's approach is consistent regardless of the object's initial position.
 - **Manipulation**: For the post-contact segment (τ_p), the poses are transformed into *proprioception-centric* end-effector poses (T_eef_pro), relative to the robot's own end-effector pose at the point of contact (t_c). This is crucial for tasks where end-effectors remain stationary relative to the object during manipulation (e.g., lifting) and allows the policy to rely on its own body state during complex interactions.
4. **Multi-Stage Data Generation in Simulation**: To create a diverse dataset from the transformed relative poses, the framework orchestrates new trajectories in simulation across varied initial robot and object poses:
 - **Locomotion Stage**: If the robot's initial position is far from the object, a PD controller generates velocity commands for the WBC to guide the robot to a suitable starting position near where the original demonstration began.
 - **Pre-Manipulation Stage**: The object-centric trajectory (τ_o) is used. To account for any slight positioning errors after locomotion, an interpolated trajectory is generated to smoothly transition from the robot's current end-effector pose to the initial target pose from the demonstration.
 - **Manipulation Stage**: The proprioception-centric trajectory (τ_p) is used to guide the manipulation action.
 - **Data Collection**: The integrated target trajectories from these three stages are fed into the low-level WBC. The WBC executes these commands, and the resulting robot states (proprioception, object pose from camera) and the high-level commands issued constitute a new synthetic successful trajectory for the dataset. This process is repeated hundreds to thousands of times with randomized initial robot and object poses to create a large, diverse training dataset.

**C. System Design and Real-World Deployment:**
- **Hardware**: A Unitree G1 EDU humanoid robot is used. It's augmented with a custom 2-DoF neck (with an Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D camera) and custom 3D-printed parallel-jaw grippers to enhance manipulation capabilities beyond the stock rubber hands.
- **Simulation Environment**: All simulated experiments are conducted in IsaacGym, known for its high-performance GPU-based physics simulation. Object assets are rescaled from PartNet-Mobility-v0.
- **Perception for Real World**: For real-world deployment, FoundationPose and its enhanced version, FoundationPose++, are used to provide real-time 6D object pose estimates from the RGB-D camera, ensuring consistency with simulated object pose representations.
- **Control Implementation**: PD control runs at 500 Hz on the Unitree SDK. I/O for sensors and motors is handled asynchronously to maintain the 50 Hz WBC operation.

This meticulous approach, from the hierarchical control to the ingenious data generation strategy and careful sim-to-real transfer considerations, forms the backbone of DemoHLM's ability to achieve robust and generalizable loco-manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both simulation and the real world provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness and generalizability of the DemoHLM framework.

**A. Simulation Experiments:**
1. **Impact of Synthetic Data Quantity**: The authors demonstrate a strong positive correlation between the amount of synthetic data generated by their pipeline and the policy's performance. As shown in Table 1, success rates for all ten loco-manipulation tasks consistently increase with larger datasets (from 100 to 5,000 trajectories). While the gains exhibit diminishing marginal returns, this finding underscores the high quality and effectiveness of the data generation pipeline.
2. **Behavior Cloning (BC) Architecture Performance**: Different BC architectures were evaluated. Table 2 clearly indicates that more expressive models, specifically ACT and Diffusion Policy, significantly outperform a simpler MLP-based baseline (even with action chunking). ACT and Diffusion Policy achieved comparable high success rates across tasks, suggesting that architectures capable of capturing temporal dependencies and complex action distributions are crucial for high-level humanoid control.
3. **Generalization to Initial State Variations**: The data generation pipeline was tested under varying initial state distributions (R1-R3, representing increasing randomness in robot and object poses). Table 3 shows that while data generation success rates modestly decrease as the initial state region broadens (e.g., Handover from 99.7% in R1 to 79.2% in R3), the pipeline still successfully produces a sufficient quantity of high-quality trajectories. This confirms DemoHLM's ability to generate data that encourages policies to generalize to diverse initial conditions, a critical aspect of robustness.

**B. Real-World Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
1. **Zero-Shot Transferability**: A central finding is the successful zero-shot transfer of policies trained exclusively in simulation to a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot. This validation is performed across six diverse loco-manipulation tasks.
2. **Comparable Performance**: As reported in Table 4, the policies achieve success rates in the real world that are notably comparable to their performance in simulation. For instance, LiftBox and PressCube achieved 5/5 successes, while PushCube and Handover achieved 4/5. Even more complex tasks like GraspCube, OpenCabinet, and EraseBoard showed promising real-world success rates (3/5 and 2/5 respectively), demonstrating robust performance despite inherent sim-to-real gaps.
3. **Robustness to Sim-to-Real Gaps**: The paper highlights that while the low-level whole-body controller might exhibit inconsistent tracking performance between simulation and reality, the closed-loop nature of the high-level manipulation policy continuously adjusts velocity commands. This adaptive behavior allows the robot to compensate for minor discrepancies and reach desired positions, maintaining consistent manipulation behaviors across environments. Figure 5 visually exemplifies this, comparing simulated and real-world executions of the LiftBox task at corresponding time steps.
4. **Diverse Task Suite Demonstrated**: The framework was successfully evaluated on a suite of ten challenging loco-manipulation tasks (Figure 3), ranging from simple object lifting and pushing to more complex actions like opening cabinets, erasing boards, pouring water, and object handovers, confirming its versatility.

Overall, the results robustly demonstrate that DemoHLM effectively addresses the challenges of data efficiency, generalization, and sim-to-real transfer for complex humanoid loco-manipulation tasks through its innovative data generation pipeline and hierarchical control strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DemoHLM presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in solving the long-standing challenge of generalizable loco-manipulation. Its contributions have several far-reaching implications:

- **Paradigm Shift in Data Collection**: The most significant impact lies in its ability to synthesize large, diverse, and high-quality training datasets from a *single human demonstration*. This fundamentally changes the data collection paradigm for humanoid robots, moving away from prohibitively expensive and time-consuming real-world teleoperation or tedious task-specific design. By making data generation scalable and efficient, DemoHLM drastically lowers the barrier for developing complex humanoid skills.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Robustness**: The framework enables learned policies to generalize effectively across varied initial conditions, including randomized robot and object poses. This inherent robustness is critical for deploying humanoids in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments where precise initial states cannot be guaranteed.
- **Accelerated Research and Development**: By providing a robust and data-efficient method for learning loco-manipulation, DemoHLM can significantly accelerate research in humanoid robotics. Researchers can quickly prototype and test new skills without extensive manual effort, fostering innovation in control, perception, and autonomy.
- **Broader Accessibility of Humanoid Capabilities**: The demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer allows complex skills to be developed and refined in simulation and then seamlessly deployed on physical robots. This makes advanced humanoid capabilities more accessible to a wider range of researchers and potentially paves the way for commercial applications.
- **Impact on Real-World Applications**: The ability of humanoids to perform versatile loco-manipulation tasks is crucial for their deployment in various sectors:
 - **Domestic Robotics**: Assisting with household chores, object retrieval, and general manipulation in human-centric spaces.
 - **Logistics and Manufacturing**: Handling diverse objects, operating tools, and navigating complex environments in warehouses or factories.
 - **Hazardous Environments**: Performing tasks in conditions unsafe for humans, such as disaster response or industrial inspection.
 - **Healthcare**: Assisting patients or performing tasks in clinical settings.
- **Foundation for Future Work**: The limitations acknowledged by the authors (reliance on simulation data, single RGB-D camera, reliance on 6D object pose estimators for *modeled* objects) serve as clear roadmaps for future research. This includes exploring hybrid training (sim-real data), integrating additional sensors (wrist cameras, tactile sensors), and developing purely visual sim-to-real approaches (e.g., end-to-end learning from RGB inputs) to handle unmodeled or novel objects. Addressing these could unlock even greater versatility for humanoid robots.

In conclusion, DemoHLM represents a pivotal step towards realizing truly autonomous and versatile humanoid robots. By elegantly combining hierarchical control with an innovative data generation pipeline, it provides a powerful and scalable framework for teaching humanoids complex loco-manipulation skills, with significant implications for both fundamental research and practical applications.
