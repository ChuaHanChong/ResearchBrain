# An Image is Worth 16x16 Words: Transformers for Image Recognition at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> Google Research's Vision Transformer (ViT) demonstrates that a pure Transformer architecture can achieve state-of-the-art performance in image classification by treating images as sequences of patches. When pre-trained on large datasets such as JFT-300M, ViT models surpass leading CNNs like BiT and EfficientNet across multiple benchmarks while requiring substantially fewer computational resources for pre-training.

## Problem

- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) overwhelmingly dominated image recognition, leveraging specific inductive biases that were believed indispensable for performance.
- Direct, "pure" applications of Transformer architectures to images were scarce and struggled with the quadratic computational complexity of global self-attention on high-resolution pixel data.
- The prevailing assumption in computer vision was that CNN-specific inductive biases (like locality and translation equivariance) were necessary for state-of-the-art image classification.

## Method

- The input image is reshaped into a sequence of ==flattened 2D patches==, each linearly projected into a constant latent vector size.
- A ==learnable classification token== and standard learnable 1D position embeddings are prepended and added, respectively, to the sequence of embedded patches.
- The combined sequence is fed into a ==standard Transformer encoder==, with a classification head attached to the output state of the classification token.

## Results

- ViT models pre-trained on JFT-300M surpassed state-of-the-art CNNs (e.g., BiT, EfficientNet) across various image recognition benchmarks, including **88.55%** on ImageNet and **77.63%** on VTAB.
- Pre-training ViT required substantially less computational resources (e.g., ViT-L/16 used **0.68k** TPUv3-core-days vs. BiT-L's **9.9k**) for comparable or better accuracy.
- ViT's performance scaled strongly with pre-training data size, outperforming CNNs only on large datasets, while underperforming on smaller ones, confirming that "large scale training trumps inductive bias."

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale pre-training data is crucial for ViT's performance, allowing it to overcome the lack of inherent image-specific inductive biases present in CNNs.
> - Vision Transformers achieve superior performance with significantly less pre-training compute compared to state-of-the-art CNNs.
> - The model implicitly learns spatial relationships between image patches, even with 1D position embeddings, demonstrating the power of self-attention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Before the advent of ViT, the landscape of computer vision, especially for tasks like image classification, was overwhelmingly dominated by Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Architectures like LeNet, AlexNet, VGG, ResNet, and EfficientNet had iteratively pushed performance boundaries, primarily leveraging the inductive biases inherent in convolutions: local receptive fields, translation equivariance, and hierarchical feature extraction. These properties were considered fundamental for efficient processing of visual data.

In contrast, Natural Language Processing (NLP) had undergone a paradigm shift with the introduction of the Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017). Transformers, based solely on self-attention mechanisms, revolutionized NLP by enabling highly parallelizable training, better capture of long-range dependencies, and unprecedented scalability when combined with large-scale pre-training on massive text corpora (e.g., BERT, GPT).

Inspired by NLP's success, researchers in computer vision had begun experimenting with attention mechanisms. However, most approaches either integrated attention *into* CNNs (e.g., augmenting feature maps, combining CNN backbones with attention for object detection) or attempted to replace certain CNN components while largely retaining the overall convolutional structure. Direct, "pure" applications of Transformers to images were scarce, and often struggled with the computational complexity of global self-attention on high-resolution pixel data (quadratic complexity with respect to the number of pixels). Approaches like Sparse Transformers or axial attention tried to approximate global attention to make it more tractable but often required complex engineering. Notably, Cordonnier et al. (2020) had previously explored using attention on image patches, but their work focused on small patch sizes and small-resolution images, and did not demonstrate competitiveness with state-of-the-art CNNs through large-scale pre-training.

ViT fundamentally altered this landscape. It demonstrated that the perceived necessity of CNN-specific inductive biases for high-performance visual recognition was not absolute. By treating images as sequences of patches, ViT showed that a standard Transformer encoder, applied with minimal modifications, could become a highly competitive, and even superior, vision model. This work bridged the gap between NLP and CV by proving the generalizability of the Transformer architecture beyond text, opening the door for a unified deep learning paradigm across different data modalities. It shifted the focus from meticulously designed vision-specific inductive biases to the power of large-scale data and general-purpose attention mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research was to demonstrate that a **pure Transformer architecture, applied directly to sequences of image patches, can perform exceptionally well on image classification tasks**. This challenged the prevailing belief that CNNs, with their inherent inductive biases for vision (locality, translation equivariance, 2D structure), were indispensable for state-of-the-art image recognition.

The core motivations driving this objective were:

- **Leveraging Transformer Scalability:** The unprecedented success of Transformers in NLP, particularly their ability to scale to models with hundreds of billions of parameters and benefit immensely from large-scale pre-training, served as a strong inspiration. The authors hypothesized that similar scalability and performance gains might be achievable in computer vision if the architecture could be adapted.
- **Simplifying Vision Architectures:** Existing attempts to incorporate attention into computer vision models often involved complex hybrid architectures or specialized attention patterns. The authors sought a simpler, more direct application of the standard Transformer to avoid such engineering complexities and leverage efficient, off-the-shelf NLP Transformer implementations.
- **Investigating the Role of Inductive Biases vs. Data Scale:** The authors acknowledge that Transformers inherently lack many of the image-specific inductive biases found in CNNs. A key motivation was to empirically test whether "large scale training trumps inductive bias" – that is, if sufficiently large datasets could enable a more general architecture like the Transformer to learn these patterns directly from data, thereby outperforming models with stronger, hand-crafted biases.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The paper also aimed to explore if a Transformer-based model could achieve competitive performance while requiring *fewer computational resources* for training compared to state-of-the-art CNNs, especially given the known efficiency of Transformer implementations on modern accelerators.

In essence, the paper sought to replicate the NLP Transformer revolution in computer vision by proving that images could be effectively treated as sequences, and that the Transformer's global attention mechanism, when paired with immense data, could learn robust visual representations without relying on convolutional priors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology lies in adapting the standard Transformer encoder, as proposed in "Attention Is All You Need," for image recognition with minimal modifications. This approach is termed the **Vision Transformer (ViT)**.

1. **Image Patching and Linear Embedding:**
 - Instead of processing individual pixels, an input image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ (Height, Width, Channels) is first reshaped into a sequence of flattened 2D patches.
 - Each patch has a resolution of $(P, P)$, resulting in $N = (HW)/P^2$ patches.
 - These patches are then flattened into 1D vectors of size $P^2 \cdot C$.
 - Each flattened patch vector is linearly projected to a constant latent vector size $D$ using a trainable linear projection layer. The outputs of this projection are called "patch embeddings."

2. **Class Token and Position Embeddings:**
 - Similar to BERT's `[CLS]` token for classification, a learnable "classification token" embedding (`x_class`) is prepended to the sequence of embedded patches. The state of this token at the output of the Transformer encoder serves as the image representation for classification.
 - To retain positional information lost during the flattening and sequencing of patches, standard learnable 1D position embeddings ($E_{pos}$) are added to the patch embeddings. The authors experimented with 2D-aware position embeddings but found no significant performance gains, highlighting that the model learns the 2D spatial relationships from 1D position embeddings implicitly.

3. **Transformer Encoder:**
 - The combined sequence of the classification token embedding and patch embeddings (with added position embeddings) is fed into a standard Transformer encoder.
 - The encoder consists of alternating layers of Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) and MLP blocks.
 - Layer Normalization (LN) is applied before every block, and residual connections are used after every block.
 - Each MLP block consists of two layers with a GELU non-linearity.

4. **Classification Head:**
 - For pre-training, a classification head (an MLP with one hidden layer) is attached to the output state of the `[CLS]` token.
 - For fine-tuning on downstream tasks, this pre-trained head is removed and replaced by a single, zero-initialized linear layer.

5. **Inductive Bias Discussion:**
 - The authors explicitly highlight that ViT has significantly fewer image-specific inductive biases compared to CNNs. Only the initial patch extraction and the 2D interpolation of position embeddings during fine-tuning (for higher resolution images) incorporate 2D structural information. All spatial relationships between patches are learned from scratch by the self-attention mechanism.

6. **Hybrid Architecture (Alternative):**
 - As an alternative, a "hybrid" model is proposed where the input sequence for the Transformer is formed from feature maps extracted by a conventional CNN backbone (e.g., ResNet). The linear projection is then applied to patches extracted from these CNN feature maps. This allows for an initial layer of convolutional processing to potentially provide some inductive bias.

7. **Training and Fine-tuning:**
 - **Pre-training:** Models are pre-trained on large datasets (ImageNet, ImageNet-21k, JFT-300M) using the Adam optimizer with a high weight decay, linear learning rate warmup and decay, and a large batch size (4096).
 - **Fine-tuning:** Models are fine-tuned on smaller downstream datasets using SGD with momentum. A crucial aspect is fine-tuning at a *higher resolution* than pre-training, for which the pre-trained 1D position embeddings are 2D-interpolated to match the new image resolution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling results demonstrating the effectiveness of ViT, especially when scaled.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance with Large-Scale Pre-training:**
 - When pre-trained on the massive JFT-300M dataset (303M images), ViT models (ViT-L/16 and ViT-H/14) consistently outperform state-of-the-art CNNs like BiT (ResNet152x4) and Noisy Student (EfficientNet-L2) across multiple image recognition benchmarks (ImageNet, ImageNet-ReaL, CIFAR-100, Oxford-IIIT Pets, Oxford Flowers-102, and the 19-task VTAB suite).
 - For example, ViT-H/14 achieved 88.55% on ImageNet, 94.55% on CIFAR-100, and 77.63% on VTAB, setting new benchmarks.
 - Even the ViT-L/16 model, pre-trained on the publicly available ImageNet-21k dataset (14M images), showed competitive performance, highlighting its potential even without access to proprietary datasets.

2. **Superior Computational Efficiency for Pre-training:**
 - A significant finding is that ViT models achieve superior performance while requiring *substantially fewer computational resources (TPUv3-core-days)* for pre-training compared to the leading CNNs. For instance, ViT-L/16 (JFT-300M) used 0.68k TPUv3-core-days, compared to BiT-L's 9.9k and Noisy Student's 12.3k, to achieve better or comparable accuracy. This implies a much better performance-compute trade-off.

3. **Crucial Role of Pre-training Data Scale:**
 - The paper explicitly investigates the impact of dataset size on ViT's performance. It shows that when pre-trained on smaller datasets (e.g., ImageNet, 1.3M images), ViT models (especially larger ones) *underperform* comparable ResNets.
 - However, as the pre-training dataset size increases (ImageNet-21k, JFT-300M), ViT's performance rapidly improves, eventually surpassing ResNets.
 - This confirms the central hypothesis: Transformers' lack of inherent inductive biases makes them less effective on smaller datasets where such biases are beneficial for generalization, but their capacity to learn directly from massive data sources allows them to excel and overcome this limitation at scale. "Large scale training trumps inductive bias."

4. **Scaling Study Insights:**
 - Controlled scaling experiments reveal that ViT models consistently dominate ResNets in terms of performance per computational budget. ViT often achieves the same performance with 2-4x less compute.
 - Hybrid models (ResNet backbone feeding into ViT) show slight advantages at smaller computational budgets but this gap diminishes for larger models, suggesting that the CNN front-end becomes less critical when the Transformer itself is sufficiently large and data-rich.
 - ViT models show no signs of performance saturation within the tested computational range, suggesting further performance gains with even larger models and more data.

5. **Interpretability and Internal Representations:**
 - Analysis of ViT's internal representations showed that the initial linear embedding filters resemble plausible basis functions for image patches.
 - Learned 1D position embeddings successfully encode 2D distance within the image, demonstrating that ViT implicitly learns the spatial topology without explicit 2D-aware embeddings.
 - Self-attention heads utilize their global integration capability: some heads attend broadly across the image even in early layers, akin to a large receptive field, while others maintain local attention. Attention distance generally increases with network depth, and the model attends to semantically relevant regions for classification.

6. **Initial Self-Supervision Exploration:**
 - Preliminary experiments with a masked patch prediction objective (similar to BERT's masked language modeling) showed promising results for self-supervised pre-training, leading to a 2% accuracy improvement on ImageNet over training from scratch for a small ViT model, though still lagging supervised pre-training. This hints at future potential.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper "An Image Is Worth 16x16 Words: Transformers for Image Recognition At Scale" marks a pivotal moment in computer vision research, holding significant implications and potential impact:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Computer Vision:** This work conclusively demonstrated that CNNs are not the only, nor necessarily the best, architecture for high-performance image recognition. It established the Vision Transformer as a viable and highly competitive alternative, fundamentally challenging the long-standing architectural paradigm in computer vision. This opened the floodgates for a wave of subsequent research exploring and improving upon ViT for various vision tasks.

2. **Unification of Deep Learning Architectures:** By showing the effectiveness of Transformers in both NLP and CV, the paper pushed towards a more unified view of deep learning architectures. This suggests that a single, versatile architecture (Transformer) could become a general-purpose backbone for diverse data modalities, simplifying research and development by reducing the need for domain-specific architectural innovations.

3. **Emphasis on Data Scaling:** The most profound insight is the crucial role of large-scale pre-training data. ViT's success, particularly its ability to overcome the lack of convolutional inductive biases, highlights that for sufficiently large models and datasets, the intrinsic capacity to learn patterns from data can outweigh the benefits of hand-crafted architectural priors. This has shifted research focus towards data curation, efficient large-scale training, and potentially finding ways to make "pure" architectures more data-efficient at smaller scales.

4. **Computational Efficiency for Training:** The finding that ViT models achieve better performance with significantly less pre-training compute (2-4x less than SOTA CNNs) is a major practical advantage. This makes high-performance visual representation learning more accessible and resource-efficient for organizations with large computational capabilities.

5. **New Research Avenues:** ViT has spurred extensive follow-up research in several directions:
 - **Downstream Tasks:** Adapting ViT for other computer vision tasks like object detection (as seen with DETR, Carion et al., 2020), semantic segmentation, and video understanding.
 - **Self-Supervised Learning:** The initial promising results with masked patch prediction have paved the way for more sophisticated self-supervised learning techniques for vision Transformers, aiming to close the gap with supervised pre-training and reduce reliance on labeled data.
 - **Architectural Variants and Efficiency:** Developing more efficient and performant ViT variants, addressing limitations like quadratic complexity on very high resolutions, or exploring hierarchical Transformers (e.g., Swin Transformer) that reintroduce some locality for efficiency.
 - **Data Efficiency for Smaller Scales:** Investigating methods to make ViT competitive on smaller datasets where current ViT models still underperform CNNs without extensive pre-training.

In conclusion, "An Image Is Worth 16x16 Words" is a seminal paper that fundamentally reshaped the trajectory of computer vision research. It demonstrated the unexpected power of a simple, generic architecture when scaled with vast amounts of data, thereby expanding the possibilities for building highly performant and generalized AI models across different domains.
