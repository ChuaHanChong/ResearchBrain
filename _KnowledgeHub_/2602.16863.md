# SimToolReal: An Object-Centric Policy for Zero-Shot Dexterous Tool Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SimToolReal from Cornell and Stanford University develops an object-centric policy for dexterous tool manipulation, enabling zero-shot transfer to novel tools and tasks in the real world. This approach, trained in simulation on procedurally generated primitives, significantly outperforms specialized baselines in task progress while eliminating the need for object-specific training or teleoperation.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation policies struggle with zero-shot generalization, often overfitting to specific objects or tasks.
- Dexterous tool use is complex, requiring stable grasping, in-hand reorientation, and robust contact, which are challenging to teach through human teleoperation or traditional sim-to-real RL.
- Current sim-to-real RL methods for dexterity demand substantial per-object simulation setup, accurate physics modeling, and laborious task-specific reward engineering.

## Method

- **SimToolReal** trains a single, general-purpose ==Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy== in simulation using a ==universal goal-reaching reward function== and vast ==procedural generation of tool-like primitives==.
- The ==object-centric policy== takes inputs like 6D tool pose and a coarse grasp bounding box, integrating interaction history via an ==LSTM== to implicitly infer unobserved properties.
- Real-world deployment is strictly ==zero-shot==, leveraging human video demonstrations for goal pose trajectories and vision foundation models (==SAM 3D==, ==FoundationPose==) for real-time object tracking.

## Results

- Achieved strong zero-shot generalization across **120 real-world rollouts** involving **24 tasks, 12 object instances, and 6 tool categories**, all unseen during training.
- Significantly outperformed Kinematic Retargeting and Fixed Grasp baselines on real-world brush sweeping tasks (**98.0% Task Progress vs. 0-10.8%**).
- Matched the performance of specialist RL policies in simulation zero-shot while maintaining high Task Progress across object and trajectory variations, unlike specialists which showed severe performance drops due to overfitting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing dexterous tool use as an object-centric problem of manipulating an object to arbitrary goal poses simplifies training and promotes generalization across diverse tools and tasks.
> - Training on a large variety of procedurally generated, abstract tool primitives in simulation effectively teaches fundamental dexterous skills (grasping, in-hand reorientation) that transfer to novel real-world tools.
> - Modern vision foundation models can robustly bridge the sim-to-real perception gap for complex dexterous tasks by providing real-time object pose and segmentation without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has made significant strides, yet general-purpose dexterous tool use remains a grand challenge. This work, SimToolReal, directly addresses a critical bottleneck in the broader research landscape: the lack of generalization in learning dexterous manipulation policies, particularly for tools.

Existing approaches typically fall into a few categories:
- **Imitation Learning (IL) from Teleoperation:** This paradigm is dominant for robot manipulation. However, as the paper highlights, teleoperation for multi-fingered dexterous hands is inherently challenging due to the human-to-robot correspondence gap (kinematics, actuation), limited haptic/tactile feedback, and the sheer complexity of coordinating many degrees of freedom. This makes collecting diverse, high-quality data for tool manipulation extremely difficult and often yields policies that are not robust to variations.
- **Functional Retargeting from Human Videos:** More recent methods attempt to learn from human videos, focusing on matching the *outcome* of the task (object motion) rather than precise hand kinematics. While these approaches have shown promise, they often require extensive, task-specific Reinforcement Learning (RL) training for each new object or task, limiting their scalability and generalizability. The resulting policies are usually tailored to a single demonstration.
- **Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Specific Skills:** Sim-to-real RL has enabled impressive dexterous behaviors (e.g., Rubik's Cube solving, in-hand reorientation). However, its success has largely been confined to specific, well-defined skills or objects. Extending these successes to diverse tools and tasks is hindered by the substantial per-object simulation setup, accurate object modeling, and laborious task-specific reward engineering required. This "per-task engineering" is a major impediment to achieving broad utility.

SimToolReal positions itself as a significant advancement by tackling the generalization problem head-on. Its core contribution is to move beyond task-specific or object-specific policies towards a *single, general-purpose RL policy* that can perform zero-shot dexterous tool manipulation on *novel* tools and tasks. This is achieved by:
- **Object-Centric View:** Framing tool use as manipulating an object through a sequence of goal poses, decoupling the policy from specific task semantics or detailed tool geometries.
- **Large-Scale Procedural Generation in Simulation:** Training on a vast diversity of procedurally generated tool-like primitives, rather than a few specific objects, to induce generalizable dexterous skills (grasping, in-hand reorientation). This is inspired by recent successes in unifying RL policies for locomotion across diverse embodiments.
- **Vision Foundation Models for Real-World Perception:** Leveraging modern foundation models (SAM 3D, FoundationPose) to bridge the sim-to-real visual gap by providing the necessary object-centric inputs (6D pose, coarse grasp bounding box) for real-world deployment without requiring detailed models or visual fine-tuning.

By demonstrating zero-shot transfer from a simulation trained on abstract primitives to a wide array of real-world tools and tasks, SimToolReal represents a crucial step towards truly generalist robotic manipulation policies. It seeks to break the cycle of costly, brittle, per-task development, aligning with the broader trend in AI and robotics towards more adaptable and versatile systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of SimToolReal is to enable a robot to perform **zero-shot dexterous tool manipulation on novel tools and tasks** in the real world, without requiring any object-specific modeling, task-specific training, or human teleoperation data for new scenarios.

This ambitious objective is motivated by several critical challenges and observations in the field of robot manipulation:

1. **Expanding Robot Capabilities:** Tools are fundamental to human civilization, allowing us to extend our physical capabilities. Equipping robots with the ability to effectively use tools would dramatically expand the range of tasks they can perform in diverse environments, from household chores to industrial applications.
2. **Complexity of Dexterous Tool Use:** Tool manipulation is inherently a challenging class of dexterity. It demands a suite of complex skills:
 - **Stable Grasping:** Often involving thin or irregularly shaped handles from flat surfaces.
 - **In-Hand Object Reorientation:** Manipulating the tool within the gripper to achieve functional poses (e.g., rotating a hammer to strike, aligning a brush for sweeping). This requires continuous contact regulation and fine motor control.
 - **Forceful Interactions:** Maintaining control during interactions with the environment (e.g., hammering, writing, sweeping), where external forces can easily dislodge the tool.
3. **Limitations of Current Learning Paradigms:**
 - **Teleoperation is Unsuitable for Dexterity:** As discussed, the inherent differences between human and robot hands, coupled with limited haptic feedback, make it extremely difficult for human operators to generate high-quality, precise demonstrations for complex dexterous tasks.
 - **Sim-to-Real RL Requires Extensive Engineering:** While successful for specific skills, current sim-to-real RL approaches demand substantial per-object simulation setup, accurate physics modeling, and laborious task-specific reward engineering. This makes scaling to a diverse range of tools and tasks impractical.
 - **Lack of Generalization:** Existing policies often overfit to the specific objects and tasks they were trained on, failing to generalize to novel instances or variations.

SimToolReal's **key insight** to overcome these limitations is to adopt an **object-centric lens**. Instead of defining a policy for "hammering" or "writing," the authors frame dexterous tool use as the universal goal of "manipulating an object to arbitrary goal poses." This abstraction simplifies the problem by:

- **Universal Training Objective:** A single goal-reaching reward function can be applied across all objects and tasks, significantly reducing the need for task-specific reward engineering.
- **Inducing Core Dexterous Skills:** By training a policy to manipulate diverse objects to random goal poses, the robot is forced to learn fundamental dexterous skills like stable grasping, in-hand reorientation, and robust contact maintenance. These skills are inherently required for most tool-use scenarios.
- **Enabling Compositionality:** Once trained, this general-purpose policy can be composed to perform complex tool-use tasks by simply providing it with a sequence of target object poses (e.g., extracted from a human demonstration video).

The objective is therefore to demonstrate that this object-centric, goal-conditioned training paradigm, combined with robust sim-to-real transfer mechanisms, can yield a single, highly generalizable RL policy capable of performing zero-shot dexterous tool manipulation across a wide variety of previously unseen tools and tasks in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimToolReal proposes a comprehensive sim-to-real reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to train a single, general-purpose, object-centric policy capable of zero-shot dexterous tool manipulation in the real world. The methodology can be broken down into three main components: (1) Training a General-Purpose Goal-Reaching Policy in Simulation, (2) Real-World Deployment, and (3) Benchmarking with DexToolBench.

#### 3.1. Training a General-Purpose Goal-Reaching Policy in Simulation

The core of SimToolReal's approach is to train an RL policy in a richly randomized simulation environment such that it learns universal dexterous manipulation skills.

- **Environment Setup:** The simulation environment utilizes IsaacGym for GPU-accelerated parallel simulation. Each episode begins with a randomly selected tool-like object placed on a table in a random pose, and the robot (Sharpa hand on KUKA iiwa arm) in a randomized joint configuration. The robot's primary task is to grasp the object and then manipulate it through a sequence of randomly sampled goal poses. Episodes terminate if the object is dropped, leaves the workspace, or a timeout occurs.
- **Reward Function:** A universal reward function is designed to induce goal-pose reaching and stable grasping. It comprises:
 - `r_smooth`: A regularization term penalizing joint velocities for smooth actions.
 - `r_grasp`: A shaping term that first encourages approaching the object, then lifting it from the table. This term becomes inactive once the object is successfully grasped.
 - `r_goal`: The dominant reward after grasping, driving the object towards the current goal pose. It includes a dense term for minimizing pose distance and a sparse bonus for successfully reaching a goal, which then triggers sampling of a new goal. Pose distance is calculated using 4 keypoints in the object's local frame, making the reward more sensitive to pitch/yaw (tool alignment) than roll.
- **Procedural Generation of Tools:** To foster generalization, the policy is trained on a vast diversity of procedurally generated tool-like primitives. Each primitive consists of a handle and a head, whose geometries (cuboids or capsules) and dimensions (length, width, height/diameter) are randomized within specified ranges, approximating everyday tools (markers, hammers, spatulas). Crucially, mass distribution is also randomized by assigning different densities to the handle and head, leading to varied centers of mass and rotational inertias. This ensures the policy learns to adapt to diverse physical properties.
- **Object-Centric Policy Inputs:** The policy is designed to operate with minimal object perception, ensuring feasibility for real-world deployment where detailed models are often unavailable. Inputs include:
 - Robot proprioception (joint positions, velocities, previous targets, palm pose, fingertip positions relative to palm).
 - Current 6D tool pose.
 - A coarse 3D grasp-region bounding box (center and extents in object frame), fixed for the episode. This bounding box defines the graspable region (e.g., hammer handle) and also scales the object keypoints used for observation.
 - Current 6D goal pose information (keypoint errors relative to the object).
 - An LSTM backbone is used to integrate interaction history, implicitly inferring unobserved physical properties.
- **RL Training Details:**
 - **SAPG (Split and Aggregate Policy Gradients):** A variant of PPO used to mitigate exploration bottlenecks in massively parallel simulations by maintaining a population of policies and fusing their gradients.
 - **Domain Randomization:** Extensive randomization is applied to bridge the sim-to-real gap, including observation delays, action latency, noise in object pose estimates, grasp bounding box perturbations, and external force/torque perturbations on the object.
 - **Asymmetric Critic:** The critic has access to privileged, noise-free, and instantaneous ground-truth simulation states, while the actor relies only on the minimal, randomized observations. This stabilizes training and improves value estimation.

#### 3.2. Real-World Deployment

The real-world deployment is strictly zero-shot, meaning no fine-tuning or object-specific training occurs after the policy is trained in simulation.

- **Human Video Processing:** For any novel tool-use task, a third-person RGB-D video of a human demonstration is recorded. This video is processed offline to extract:
 - A metric-scale 3D object mesh and a 3D grasp region bounding box using SAM 3D (modified to use captured depth for metric accuracy) and SAM 2 (for part segmentation into handle/head).
 - A 6D object pose trajectory using FoundationPose, which is then temporally downsampled and trimmed to remove static pre-grasp phases.
- **Inference-Time Object Tracking:** During real-time execution, FoundationPose continuously estimates the current 6D object pose from live RGB-D camera feeds. The RL policy then receives:
 - Robot proprioception.
 - The live-tracked current object pose.
 - The fixed grasp-region bounding box (derived from the human video).
 - The current goal pose from the pre-processed demonstration trajectory.
- **Sequential Goal Tracking:** The policy executes actions to reach the current goal. Once the object's pose is within a specified tolerance (epsilon) of the current goal, the system advances to the next goal pose in the sequence. The episode ends upon reaching the final goal or failure (e.g., object drop, tracking loss).

#### 3.3. DexToolBench: A New Benchmark

To evaluate the generalization capabilities, the authors introduce **DexToolBench**, a real-world benchmark for dexterous tool-use tasks.
- It comprises 24 daily tool-use tasks involving 12 unique object instances across 6 tool categories (Hammer, Marker, Eraser, Brush, Spatula, Screwdriver).
- Each task is defined by an RGB-D human video demonstration, which provides the goal pose trajectories for evaluation.
- The benchmark provides raw videos, processed data, simulation environments, and purchase links for reproducibility. Task Progress (percentage of successfully tracked goal poses) is the primary evaluation metric.

This comprehensive methodology, from procedural simulation training to perception-driven real-world deployment, aims to address the limitations of prior work by focusing on generalizable, object-centric skill learning and zero-shot transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SimToolReal presents a compelling set of experimental results demonstrating its ability to generalize dexterous tool manipulation. The findings validate the object-centric, zero-shot approach.

- **A. Zero-Shot Real-World Tool-Use Performance:**
 - SimToolReal achieved **strong zero-shot generalization** across 120 real-world rollouts spanning 24 tasks, 12 object instances, and 6 tool categories, all unseen during training.
 - **High Task Progress** was observed, particularly for tasks relying primarily on translational motion (e.g., eraser tasks achieved 100% progress).
 - Performance slightly degraded for **thinner tools** (e.g., markers, flat spatulas) due to less reliable grasps and pose tracking challenges, and **heavier tools** (e.g., mallet hammer) due to higher inertial forces during reorientation.
 - Tasks requiring significant **in-hand object rotation** (hammer, brush, spatula, screwdriver) were more challenging but still showed strong performance, demonstrating the policy's learned dexterity. The screwdriver tasks, requiring both reorientation and continuous spinning, were the most difficult category.
 - **Common Failure Modes:** The most frequent failure was **pose tracking loss (43.7%)**, often due to heavy occlusion from the robot hand, visual ambiguity (rotational symmetry), or low visual contrast. This highlights the current limitations of perception systems for complex dexterous tasks. Other failures included **object drops (34.5%)**, **incomplete in-hand rotation (18.2%)**, and **grasp failure (3.6%)**. The policy showed robust recovery behavior, often attempting to re-grasp dropped objects.

- **B. Comparisons to Retargeting and Fixed Grasp Baselines:**
 - SimToolReal significantly **outperformed Kinematic Retargeting and Fixed Grasp baselines** on real-world brush sweeping tasks, achieving 82.7% and 98.0% Task Progress compared to 0-10.8%.
 - **Kinematic Retargeting failed to grasp** the brush in both task variations, demonstrating its inability to reason about contact forces and maintain stable interactions, a known limitation of purely kinematic methods.
 - **Fixed Grasp succeeded only on the simpler task** variation that did not require in-hand rotation (Task Progress 61.0%). When in-hand rotation was necessary, the fixed grasp constraint caused the robot arm to **collide with the table** while attempting to track the goal trajectory, as the arm lacked the kinematic workspace to accommodate arbitrary tool rotations without hand assistance.
 - These results emphasize the critical role of the policy's ability to perform **dexterous in-hand reorientation** for complex tool use, a skill that SimToolReal explicitly induces.

- **C. Comparisons to Specialist Baseline (in Simulation):**
 - SimToolReal **matched the performance of specialist RL policies** (trained on a single object and task) on their respective training setups (Obj A / Traj A) **zero-shot**, achieving comparable high Task Progress.
 - However, specialist policies showed a **significant performance drop** when either the trajectory (Obj A / Traj B) or the object (Obj B / Traj A) was varied from their training conditions. This indicates severe overfitting to specific training data.
 - In contrast, SimToolReal maintained **high zero-shot Task Progress** across all object and trajectory variations, confirming its superior generalization capabilities.

- **D. Training Objective Predicts Generalization Performance:**
 - There was a **strong correlation** between the policy's training reward (for reaching random goal poses on procedurally generated primitives) and its zero-shot Task Progress on unseen DexToolBench trajectories in simulation.
 - As training progressed and the policy's performance on the general goal-reaching objective improved, its ability to generalize to specific tool-use tasks also consistently increased. This finding validates the core hypothesis that training on diverse primitives to random goal poses effectively teaches the foundational dexterous skills required for real-world tool use.

- **E. Ablations on RL Training:**
 - Both **SAPG (Split and Aggregate Policy Gradients)** and the **Asymmetric Critic** were found to be critical for maximizing training performance.
 - Replacing SAPG with standard PPO resulted in a significant drop in reward, confirming SAPG's role in promoting exploration diversity and scalability.
 - Removing the Asymmetric Critic (forcing the critic to rely on partial observations) also severely hindered learning, demonstrating the importance of privileged information for stable value estimation and effective guidance in partially observable tasks.

In summary, SimToolReal demonstrates a pioneering ability for zero-shot generalization in dexterous tool manipulation. It achieves this by learning foundational skills from procedurally generated objects in simulation and leveraging modern perception models for real-world deployment, outperforming specialized baselines and proving its generalist capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SimToolReal represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts on the field:

1. **Breaking the Generalization Barrier in Dexterous Manipulation:** The most critical impact of SimToolReal is its demonstration of **zero-shot generalization** for complex dexterous tool manipulation. This directly addresses one of the most persistent challenges in robotics: creating policies that are not brittle and can adapt to unseen objects and tasks. By shifting from task-specific or object-specific training to a universal, object-centric goal-reaching objective on procedurally generated primitives, SimToolReal opens a pathway towards truly generalist manipulation robots.

2. **Reduced Engineering Effort and Cost:** The current paradigm of sim-to-real RL often requires substantial per-object modeling, reward engineering, and task-specific fine-tuning. SimToolReal drastically reduces this burden by eliminating the need for any of these steps for new tools and tasks. This translates to **faster deployment, lower development costs, and increased accessibility** for researchers and practitioners, making advanced dexterous manipulation more practical outside of highly controlled laboratory settings.

3. **Unified Skill Acquisition:** The approach of inducing core dexterous skills (stable grasping, in-hand reorientation) through a generic goal-reaching objective is highly significant. Instead of learning individual skills in isolation, SimToolReal demonstrates that these capabilities emerge organically from a unified training process, suggesting a more efficient and robust way to build robot dexterity.

4. **Leveraging Foundation Models for Robotics:** The successful integration of modern vision foundation models (SAM 3D, FoundationPose) for real-world perception is a crucial enabler. This highlights a powerful trend where advancements in large-scale visual AI can be directly translated to robust robotic control, effectively bridging the sim-to-real observation gap without needing task-specific vision systems. This modularity makes the overall system more adaptable.

5. **Introduction of DexToolBench:** The creation of DexToolBench is a valuable contribution to the research community. By providing a standardized, challenging, and reproducible benchmark of real-world dexterous tool-use tasks (with corresponding human videos, processed data, and simulation environments), it will accelerate future research in generalist manipulation, allowing for rigorous comparison of novel approaches.

6. **Potential for Broader Applications:** While demonstrated on tool manipulation, the underlying principles of object-centric learning, procedural generation, and perception-driven zero-shot transfer are highly transferable. This methodology could pave the way for generalist policies in other complex manipulation domains, such as assembly, cooking, or even assistive robotics.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also point to avenues for future impact:
- **Functional Task Completion:** The policy focuses on trajectory following, not guaranteeing functional task completion, especially for high-force interactions. Future work could incorporate task-specific success criteria or environment feedback.
- **Environment-Blindness:** Conditioning solely on object pose goals makes the policy unaware of clutter, potentially leading to collisions. Integrating scene understanding or affordance reasoning would enhance robustness.
- **Rigid Tools and Fixed Goals:** The current approach assumes rigid tools and relies on a fixed, human-demonstrated goal sequence. Extending to non-rigid tools (e.g., cloth, wires) and dynamic goal replanning would expand its utility.
- **Perception Robustness:** While strong, the reliance on real-time pose tracking is a bottleneck, with occlusions and visual ambiguities leading to failures. Improvements in multi-view perception, temporal consistency, or active perception strategies could enhance system reliability.

Despite these limitations, SimToolReal marks a substantial step towards truly versatile, dexterous robots that can manipulate a wide range of tools in unstructured environments without extensive human intervention, moving closer to the vision of general-purpose embodied AI.
