# Learning the ARTS of Search for Automated Discovery

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Agentic Reasoning for Tree Search (ARTS), enhances automated scientific discovery by decoupling hypothesis quality from experimental execution and managing long-term search context. This approach achieved a 15.3% relative improvement in mean normalized scores across 22 benchmark tasks, with its test-time training variant enabling smaller models to match larger ones at 5x lower inference cost.

## Problem

- Existing score-based heuristic search algorithms conflate a hypothesis's inherent quality with its experimental execution quality, leading to premature abandonment of promising ideas.
- The accumulation of search history exceeds the context windows of large reasoning models, causing information loss through summarization or degraded reasoning over long contexts.
- Reasoning models guiding automated searches can suffer from diversity collapse and sensitivity to the exploration-exploitation tradeoff, often committing to suboptimal directions.

## Method

- **ARTS** introduces a modular agent design with a 'scientist' (LRM) for high-level reasoning and an 'executor' (coding agent) for code implementation, allowing the scientist to diagnose outcomes beyond just scores.
- The scientist leverages an `INSPECT` tool for detailed node analysis, baseline auditing, and uses verbalized sampling to promote diverse hypothesis exploration.
- ARTS* incorporates test-time training (TTT) via LoRA adapters, fine-tuning a smaller scientist model with the search tree history to encode experience into its weights and overcome context length limitations.

## Results

- ARTS achieved state-of-the-art performance, securing the best score on 16 out of 22 tasks and demonstrating a **15.3%** relative improvement in mean normalized score over leading prior methods.
- ARTS showed higher robustness with the best inter-quartile mean (**0.93**) and lower optimality gap, particularly on harder ML tasks where it sometimes surpassed human-best scores.
- Test-time training in ARTS* boosted a smaller Qwen3-4B scientist's mean normalized score by **40%** (from **0.72** to **1.01**), enabling it to match or exceed the performance of larger, more expensive models at approximately **5x** lower inference cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly distinguishing between hypothesis quality and execution quality enables the search agent to prevent premature abandonment of promising ideas, even after preliminary implementation failures.
> - Diverse hypothesis exploration, guided by verbalized sampling and deep reasoning, effectively covers a wider range of strategies and prevents diversity collapse.
> - Test-time training can distill extensive search experience into model weights, allowing smaller, more cost-effective models to achieve high performance on long-term search tasks by overcoming context window constraints.
