# Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> The Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) demonstrates that a simplified recursive architecture can tackle complex reasoning tasks with higher efficiency and generalization than larger models and its predecessor, the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM). For instance, TRM-MLP attained an 87.4% test accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme, a substantial increase from HRM's 55.0%, utilizing significantly fewer parameters.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with complex puzzle-solving tasks such as Sudoku, Maze pathfinding, and Abstract Reasoning Corpus (ARC-AGI), exhibiting error propagation and costly inference.
- The Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), a previous alternative, was overly complex, relied on questionable theoretical assumptions for gradient approximation, and its performance driver was unclear.
- There was a need for a simpler, more robust, and parameter-efficient model that could achieve better generalization on these challenging reasoning benchmarks.

## Method

- Developed the ==Tiny Recursive Model (TRM)==, utilizing a single 2-layer network for both latent reasoning and output refinement, replacing HRM's dual, more complex network structure.
- Implemented ==full backpropagation== through all recursive steps during deep supervision, abandoning the Implicit Function Theorem and 1-step gradient approximations used by HRM.
- Streamlined the ==Adaptive Computational Time (ACT)== mechanism by removing the "continue loss" component, which eliminated expensive extra forward passes during training.

## Results

- TRM-MLP achieved **87.4%** test accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme, marking a substantial increase from HRM's **55.0%**.
- TRM-Att demonstrated improved performance on Maze-Hard with **85.3%** accuracy (vs. HRM's **74.5%**) and on ARC-AGI-1 with **44.6%** accuracy (vs. HRM's **40.3%**).
- The model achieved exceptional parameter efficiency, with TRM-Att using only **7 million** parameters, approximately **4x** fewer than HRM's **27 million**, while consistently outperforming it.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Full backpropagation through the entire recursive process is crucial for performance, leading to a substantial increase in accuracy over HRM's 1-step gradient approximation.
> - A simplified architecture with a single 2-layer network significantly improves generalization and reduces overfitting, particularly on small datasets, by condensing the two 4-layer networks of its predecessor.
> - Reinterpreting latent features as a general reasoning feature ("z") and current embedded solution ("y") provides an optimal balance for iterative problem-solving.
