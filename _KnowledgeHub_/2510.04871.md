# Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> The Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) demonstrates that a simplified recursive architecture can tackle complex reasoning tasks with higher efficiency and generalization than larger models and its predecessor, the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM). For instance, TRM-MLP attained an 87.4% test accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme, a substantial increase from HRM's 55.0%, utilizing significantly fewer parameters.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with complex puzzle-solving tasks such as Sudoku, Maze pathfinding, and Abstract Reasoning Corpus (ARC-AGI), exhibiting error propagation and costly inference.
- The Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), a previous alternative, was overly complex, relied on questionable theoretical assumptions for gradient approximation, and its performance driver was unclear.
- There was a need for a simpler, more robust, and parameter-efficient model that could achieve better generalization on these challenging reasoning benchmarks.

## Method

- Developed the ==Tiny Recursive Model (TRM)==, utilizing a single 2-layer network for both latent reasoning and output refinement, replacing HRM's dual, more complex network structure.
- Implemented ==full backpropagation== through all recursive steps during deep supervision, abandoning the Implicit Function Theorem and 1-step gradient approximations used by HRM.
- Streamlined the ==Adaptive Computational Time (ACT)== mechanism by removing the "continue loss" component, which eliminated expensive extra forward passes during training.

## Results

- TRM-MLP achieved **87.4%** test accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme, marking a substantial increase from HRM's **55.0%**.
- TRM-Att demonstrated improved performance on Maze-Hard with **85.3%** accuracy (vs. HRM's **74.5%**) and on ARC-AGI-1 with **44.6%** accuracy (vs. HRM's **40.3%**).
- The model achieved exceptional parameter efficiency, with TRM-Att using only **7 million** parameters, approximately **4x** fewer than HRM's **27 million**, while consistently outperforming it.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Full backpropagation through the entire recursive process is crucial for performance, leading to a substantial increase in accuracy over HRM's 1-step gradient approximation.
> - A simplified architecture with a single 2-layer network significantly improves generalization and reduces overfitting, particularly on small datasets, by condensing the two 4-layer networks of its predecessor.
> - Reinterpreting latent features as a general reasoning feature ("z") and current embedded solution ("y") provides an optimal balance for iterative problem-solving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the current debate surrounding the capabilities and limitations of Large Language Models (LLMs) on complex reasoning tasks. While LLMs have achieved unprecedented success in various natural language understanding and generation benchmarks, they often struggle with "hard puzzle tasks" such as Sudoku, Maze pathfinding, and Abstract Reasoning Corpus (ARC-AGI). Their auto-regressive nature can lead to error propagation, and common improvements like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Test-Time Compute (TTC) are computationally expensive, require high-quality reasoning data, and can still be brittle.

Against this backdrop, the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), proposed by Wang et al. (2025), emerged as a notable alternative. HRM demonstrated superior performance on these specific puzzle tasks, beating LLMs with significantly fewer parameters (27M vs. billions or trillions) and much smaller training datasets (around 1000 examples). HRM's novelty lay in its combination of recursive hierarchical reasoning—using two small neural networks (fL and fH) recursing at different frequencies—and deep supervision.

However, this paper critically analyzes HRM, pointing out its reliance on complex biological arguments, questionable application of the Implicit Function Theorem (IFT) for gradient approximation, and overall architectural complexity. An independent analysis highlighted that deep supervision, rather than the hierarchical recursion itself, seemed to be the primary driver of HRM's performance gains.

"Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks" directly responds to these observations. It aims to distill the core effective mechanisms of recursive reasoning, simplify the architecture, and validate whether these simplifications can lead to even better performance and efficiency. It challenges the prevailing "bigger is better" paradigm that often guides LLM development, advocating for a "less is more" approach where architectural elegance and computational efficiency are prioritized for specific reasoning challenges. This work contributes to the growing body of research exploring alternatives to massive, general-purpose models for specialized cognitive tasks, aligning with trends towards more targeted, efficient, and potentially interpretable AI systems. It also implicitly touches upon the field of deep equilibrium models by discussing and then consciously moving away from fixed-point iteration assumptions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a recursive reasoning model that is significantly simpler, more robust, and achieves higher generalization performance than the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), particularly on challenging puzzle tasks. The author posits that HRM, despite its success, is unnecessarily complex, relying on intricate mathematical theorems and biological justifications that are not fully validated or applicable.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Improved Generalization:** To demonstrate that a simpler recursive architecture, the Tiny Recursive Model (TRM), can achieve substantially higher test accuracy on hard puzzle benchmarks (Sudoku, Maze, ARC-AGI) compared to HRM and various LLMs.
- **Architectural Simplification:** To move away from the complex dual-network, hierarchical design of HRM, proposing a single, tiny network that performs both latent reasoning and solution refinement. This includes reducing the number of layers and eliminating the need for separate low- and high-frequency networks.
- **Theoretical Clarity and Robustness:** To bypass the need for the Implicit Function Theorem (IFT) and its 1-step gradient approximation, which are deemed poorly applicable in HRM's context. TRM aims for a more straightforward and theoretically less contentious approach to backpropagation through recursion.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** To further reduce the parameter count and computational cost during both training and inference. This includes simplifying the Adaptive Computational Time (ACT) mechanism to eliminate redundant forward passes, making the training process more efficient.
- **Understanding Recursive Reasoning:** To provide a clearer, non-biological, and intuitive re-interpretation of the roles of different latent features in recursive reasoning, thereby demystifying its effectiveness and guiding future architectural choices.
- **Addressing Overfitting:** To design a model that is inherently more resistant to overfitting, especially when trained on small datasets, a common challenge for complex models.

In essence, the research is motivated by the belief that the core power of recursive reasoning can be harnessed more effectively through simplification, leading to better performance, efficiency, and understandability, thereby challenging the conventional wisdom that greater complexity or scale always yields superior results.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) is designed as a supervised learning model that iteratively refines its predictions through a recursive process. Unlike HRM, TRM adopts a "less is more" philosophy, streamlining its architecture and theoretical underpinnings.

The core methodology of TRM involves:

- **Single Tiny Network:** Instead of HRM's two 4-layer Transformer networks (fL and fH), TRM uses a single 2-layer network. This network is responsible for both updating the latent reasoning feature `z` and refining the output answer `y`. The distinction in tasks is achieved by controlling the inputs: `z = net(x, y, z)` for latent reasoning (incorporating input question `x`, current answer `y`, and current latent `z`), and `y = net(y, z)` for refining the answer (using current answer `y` and latent `z`). This simplification dramatically reduces the parameter count and, surprisingly, enhances generalization, attributed to reduced overfitting on small datasets.
- **Elimination of Fixed-Point Theorem and 1-step Gradient Approximation:** TRM completely abandons HRM's reliance on the Implicit Function Theorem and the 1-step gradient approximation. Instead, it performs `n` "latent reasoning" steps to update `z`, followed by one `y` update, defining this as a "full recursion process." During deep supervision, T-1 such full recursion processes are executed without backpropagation to improve `y` and `z`, followed by one full recursion process where gradients are tracked and backpropagated. This direct approach removes theoretical assumptions and empirically yields significant performance gains.
- **Simplified Latent Variable Interpretation:** TRM reinterprets HRM's zL and zH (low-level and high-level latent features) into `z` (a general latent reasoning feature, akin to a chain-of-thought) and `y` (the current embedded solution). This clarifies their roles and justifies why two, rather than one or multiple, such features are optimal for remembering the question, previous reasoning, and previous answer to iterate towards a solution.
- **Streamlined Adaptive Computational Time (ACT):** HRM's ACT mechanism, which used a Q-learning objective requiring an extra forward pass during training, is simplified. TRM removes the "continue loss" component, relying solely on a binary cross-entropy loss to learn a halting probability based on whether the correct solution has been reached. This eliminates the need for the expensive second forward pass during training without significantly impacting generalization.
- **Attention-Free MLP for Fixed, Small Contexts:** For tasks like Sudoku-Extreme with small, fixed context lengths, TRM replaces the self-attention layer with a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) applied across the sequence length, inspired by MLP-Mixer architectures. This further reduces parameters and improves performance for such specific tasks, although self-attention is retained for larger context problems like Maze-Hard and ARC-AGI.
- **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** To enhance training stability and prevent overfitting, especially on small datasets, EMA of the model weights is integrated into the training process. This technique helps in achieving higher generalization.
- **Hyperparameter Optimization for Recursions:** Extensive experiments were conducted to determine the optimal number of latent reasoning steps (`n`) within a recursion and the number of backpropagation-free supervision steps (`T`) for deep recursion. TRM found `T=3` and `n=6` (equivalent to 42 effective recursions) to be optimal on Sudoku-Extreme.
- **Training and Evaluation:** Models are trained using the AdamW optimizer with learning rate warm-up, stable-max loss, and heavy data augmentation (e.g., 1000 shufflings for Sudoku, dihedral transformations for Maze and ARC-AGI) to combat data scarcity. Evaluation is performed on Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Hard, ARC-AGI-1, and ARC-AGI-2, with comparisons against HRM and various LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that the Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) consistently and significantly outperforms HRM, while also showcasing superior parameter efficiency compared to both HRM and state-of-the-art LLMs on hard puzzle tasks.

- **Significant Generalization Improvement over HRM:**
 - **Sudoku-Extreme:** TRM-MLP achieved 87.4% test accuracy, a massive improvement over HRM's 55.0%.
 - **Maze-Hard:** TRM-Att obtained 85.3% accuracy, surpassing HRM's 74.5%.
 - **ARC-AGI-1:** TRM-Att reached 44.6% accuracy, higher than HRM's 40.3%.
 - **ARC-AGI-2:** TRM-Att achieved 7.8% accuracy, outperforming HRM's 5.0%.

- **Exceptional Parameter Efficiency:**
 - TRM-Att uses only 7 million parameters, which is approximately four times fewer than HRM's 27 million parameters.
 - TRM-MLP, when used, has even fewer parameters (5M for Sudoku).
 - Despite being orders of magnitude smaller (less than 0.01% of parameters), TRM-Att significantly outperforms most LLMs on ARC-AGI benchmarks (e.g., Deepseek R1, o3-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro). While Grok-4 variants show higher ARC-AGI scores, they are massive (1.7T parameters) and likely employ extensive test-time computation.

- **Ablation Study Insights (on Sudoku-Extreme):**
 - **Full Backpropagation vs. 1-step Gradient:** Replacing HRM's 1-step gradient approximation with full backpropagation through the entire recursion process (within a deep supervision step) led to a monumental accuracy increase from 56.5% to 87.4%. This highlights the critical flaw in HRM's theoretical justification for gradient approximation.
 - **Latent Feature Interpretation:** The proposed "y, z" two-feature model (87.4%) demonstrated superior performance compared to a single-z model (71.9%) or a multi-scale z model (77.6%), validating the intuitive re-interpretation of the latent variables.
 - **Single Network Efficacy:** Consolidating HRM's two networks into a single network not only reduced parameters by half but also improved generalization from 82.4% to 87.4%.
 - **"Less is More" in Layers:** Decreasing the network layers from four to two (while proportionally scaling recursions to maintain effective depth) surprisingly improved generalization from 79.5% to 87.4% and halved parameters again. This suggests 2 layers are optimal, likely due to mitigating overfitting on small datasets.
 - **Attention-Free MLP:** Replacing self-attention with an MLP for the Sudoku task (small, fixed context) boosted accuracy from 74.7% to 87.4%. However, this architecture was suboptimal for larger grids like Maze and ARC-AGI.
 - **Simplified ACT:** Removing the extra forward pass requirement from ACT for training did not significantly impact generalization (86.1% to 87.4%).
 - **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** Incorporating EMA improved stability and generalization, preventing performance collapse (79.9% to 87.4%).
 - **Optimal Recursion Depth:** Experiments showed `T=3, n=6` (42 effective recursions) to be optimal on Sudoku-Extreme, demonstrating the importance of tuning the recursive depth.

In summary, TRM successfully achieves its objectives, delivering significantly better performance and efficiency by simplifying the recursive reasoning architecture, adopting a more robust gradient approach, and making data-driven design choices informed by comprehensive ablation studies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Less is More" paradigm presented by the Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) carries significant implications across several dimensions of AI research and development:

- **Challenging the "Bigger is Better" Paradigm:** TRM fundamentally challenges the prevailing notion in deep learning that scaling up model parameters and training data indefinitely is the primary path to advanced AI capabilities. By outperforming LLMs with orders of magnitude fewer parameters and data on specific reasoning tasks, TRM demonstrates that specialized, efficient architectures can yield superior results in targeted domains. This could lead to a re-evaluation of scaling laws and encourage more focused architectural innovation for particular problem classes.
- **Enhanced Resource Efficiency:** The extreme parameter efficiency and reduced computational cost of TRM have profound practical implications. It offers a viable pathway to deploying sophisticated reasoning AI in resource-constrained environments, such as edge devices, embedded systems, or for applications where energy consumption is a critical factor. This contributes to more sustainable and accessible AI.
- **Improved Generalization on Scarce Data:** The findings that a "tiny" 2-layer network generalizes better than a 4-layer network, combined with the effectiveness of EMA, highlight TRM's robustness to overfitting on small datasets. This is crucial for real-world scenarios where large, curated datasets are unavailable or expensive to acquire, making advanced AI applicable to a broader range of niche problems.
- **Simplified Theoretical Foundations:** By discarding the complex and potentially misapplied theoretical justifications (like the Implicit Function Theorem) of its predecessor, HRM, TRM offers a more intuitive, transparent, and empirically validated framework for recursive reasoning. This simplification makes the model easier to understand, implement, debug, and further develop, potentially accelerating research in this area.
- **Advancing Abstract Reasoning Capabilities:** The strong performance of TRM on ARC-AGI benchmarks is particularly noteworthy. ARC-AGI is designed to test human-like fluid intelligence and abstract reasoning, areas where even advanced LLMs struggle. TRM's progress here suggests a step forward in developing AI systems that can grasp and generalize from very few examples, moving closer to human cognitive capabilities in abstract problem-solving.
- **Inspiration for Hybrid Architectures:** While currently a supervised learning model, TRM's success in recursive reasoning could inspire new hybrid architectures that integrate such efficient reasoning modules with generative capabilities. This could lead to AI systems that not only solve problems but also generate novel solutions or explanations in a robust and coherent manner.
- **Impact on Specialized AI Development:** The paper provides a strong case for developing specialized AI agents tailored to specific cognitive tasks rather than relying solely on monolithic, general-purpose models. This could lead to more robust, efficient, and reliable AI solutions for a variety of critical applications, from scientific discovery to complex logistics.

In conclusion, this research offers a compelling argument for the power of simplification and focused architectural design in AI. TRM represents a significant advancement in recursive reasoning, demonstrating that intelligent model design, rather than sheer scale, can be the key to unlocking superior performance and efficiency on challenging reasoning tasks.
