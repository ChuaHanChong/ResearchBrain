# Group Entropy-Controlled Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Laboratory developed Group Entropy-Controlled Policy Optimization (GEPO), an RL method that adaptively shapes advantage signals based on group-level entropy to manage the exploration-exploitation trade-off in heterogeneous multi-task LLM post-training. GEPO achieved the best average scores on Intern-S1-mini (54.2%) and Qwen3.5-9B (71.2%) across 13 benchmarks while maintaining stable training and differentiated exploration across tasks.

## Problem

- Existing RL methods for LLM post-training struggle with balancing exploration and exploitation across diverse, heterogeneous tasks that exhibit distinct characteristics and solution spaces.
- Global or token-level entropy control mechanisms fail to address task-specific exploration needs, leading to optimization imbalances when prompt groups have substantially different entropy levels.
- Normalized advantage signals (as used in GRPO) are prone to structural bias across groups with varying entropy, causing low-entropy tasks to dominate optimization while high-entropy tasks receive weaker, noisier gradients.

## Method

- Group Entropy-Controlled Policy Optimization (**GEPO**) extends ==GRPO== by introducing a ==group-level entropy estimator== to diagnose the policy's exploration state for each task.
- **GEPO** applies an ==entropy-conditioned asymmetric advantage shaping==: attenuating positive advantages in low-entropy groups (to prevent over-exploitation) and negative advantages in high-entropy groups (to preserve exploration).
- It employs ==adaptive, exponentially smoothed entropy thresholds==, dynamically computed from batch statistics, to ensure robustness across different base models and training stages without manual tuning.

## Results

- GEPO achieved superior overall performance on both Intern-S1-mini (**54.2%**) and Qwen3.5-9B (**71.2%**) across **13** diverse benchmarks, demonstrating balanced cross-task improvements compared to baselines.
- The method exhibited significantly enhanced training stability, showing smooth, monotonically improving validation performance and preventing catastrophic performance collapses observed in GRPO and other baselines on Qwen3.5-9B.
- GEPO maintained a stable and bounded policy entropy trajectory while preserving differentiated exploration levels across tasks, allowing tasks requiring more exploration to retain higher entropy and converged tasks to settle at lower entropy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Group entropy, estimated at the prompt group level, serves as an effective diagnostic signal for characterizing the policy's exploration state for specific tasks, revealing heterogeneity that global entropy misses.
> - There exists a structural bias in normalized advantages (like those in GRPO) induced by group entropy heterogeneity, where low-entropy groups generate stronger but potentially distorted gradients, while high-entropy groups produce weaker, noisier gradients.
> - Maintaining differentiated exploration levels across tasks, rather than homogenizing them, is crucial for achieving balanced performance improvements and stable training in heterogeneous multi-task LLM fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a paradigm for enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs) through post-training, particularly for improving alignment and reasoning capabilities across various tasks. A persistent challenge within RL is managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off. This challenge is amplified in the context of LLM post-training, which frequently involves optimization over diverse and heterogeneous task mixtures (e.g., instruction following, coding, mathematical reasoning). These tasks often exhibit distinct characteristics, solution diversity, and policy exploration uncertainty.

Existing RL methods attempt to address the exploration-exploitation balance through entropy-controlled mechanisms, operating either at a global policy level or a fine-grained token level. However, these approaches primarily utilize entropy as a general indicator to maintain stable RL training, without explicitly considering potential optimization imbalances that arise among heterogeneous tasks. For instance, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a prominent algorithm in LLM RL, normalizes rewards within each prompt group to generate relative advantages. This process implicitly assumes that the resulting advantage signals are comparable across different groups. The paper identifies a gap in existing research regarding whether this assumption holds true when prompt groups exhibit substantially different levels of entropy, and how such entropy heterogeneity influences joint multi-task optimization.

This work specifically addresses these unexplored aspects. It positions itself by delving into the interaction between group entropy and GRPO's optimization dynamics. By revealing a structural bias in normalized advantages induced by group entropy heterogeneity, the paper aims to contribute a novel mechanism for managing exploration-exploitation trade-offs that is tailored to the multi-task and heterogeneous nature of modern LLM post-training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an RL optimization method that effectively manages the exploration-exploitation trade-off in LLM post-training, particularly when dealing with mixtures of heterogeneous tasks. The authors identify a crucial limitation in current approaches: a global or token-level entropy regulation is often insufficient to address the distinct exploration needs of different tasks, which can induce varying entropy regimes under the same policy.

The motivation for this work stems from several detailed observations and theoretical propositions:

1. **Heterogeneity of Group Entropy:** The authors observe that group entropy, calculated from the response groups of each task, varies significantly based on task characteristics. For example, physics prompts and general knowledge prompts exhibit different entropy distributions, even under the same base policy. This indicates that a single, global entropy statistic cannot accurately represent the exploration states of all prompts in a multi-domain training mixture.
2. **Entropy-Dependent Bias in Normalized Advantages:** Empirical observations reveal that domains with different group entropy regimes also exhibit distinct normalized advantage distributions. Low-entropy domains tend to have more symmetric advantage distributions, while high-entropy domains (often correlated with lower group accuracy) display highly skewed distributions with numerous small negative advantages and rare, large positive outliers.
3. **Formal Characterization of Structural Bias:**
 - **Proposition 2.1 (Entropy-Dependent Distribution Distortion):** This proposition demonstrates that low policy entropy can lead to a larger deviation between the policy-weighted and a reference reward distribution. This implies that low-entropy prompts may be more sensitive to policy-induced reweighting of the response space, potentially distorting the perceived reward landscape.
 - **Proposition 2.2 (Structural Bias of Normalized Advantages):** This proposition formalizes that per-group centering and scaling (as in GRPO) do not ensure comparable advantage signals across groups with differing entropy levels. The potential discrepancy between policy-weighted and reference-normalized advantages remains dependent on entropy.
4. **Optimization Consequences:** The identified intra-group skewness and inter-group structural bias jointly create entropy-dependent optimization pressures:
 - **Within high-entropy groups:** The skewed advantage distribution results in "diffuse and noisy suppression" for incorrect responses and "inconsistent reinforcement" for rare correct responses. This can lead to unreliable learning signals and premature suppression of potentially valuable exploratory paths.
 - **Across groups:** The structural bias causes low-entropy groups, despite having more distorted advantages relative to the true reward landscape, to generate stronger and more consistent gradient signals. Conversely, high-entropy groups, whose advantages are closer to the reference frame, contribute weaker and noisier gradients. This leads to an imbalance where already converging low-entropy tasks dominate batch-level optimization, creating a self-reinforcing cycle that further widens the entropy gap and diminishes learning signals for high-entropy tasks.

These observations and theoretical analyses motivate the need for an entropy-conditioned intervention that can rebalance advantage signals across different entropy regimes, rather than treating all normalized advantage distributions uniformly. The core objective is to mitigate the optimization bias induced by entropy heterogeneity, leading to more stable and balanced learning outcomes across a diverse set of tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Group Entropy-Controlled Policy Optimization (GEPO), extends the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework by incorporating group-level entropy as a diagnostic signal for asymmetric advantage shaping.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO is an RL framework for LLM post-training that computes advantages directly from grouped samples, eliminating the need for a separate value function. For a given prompt $x$, GRPO samples $K$ responses, collects their rewards, and computes normalized advantages $A_i$ by centering rewards around their group mean and scaling by their standard deviation. The objective is to maximize a clipped surrogate loss, similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), using importance sampling ratios.

#### 3.2. Group Entropy Definition

GEPO introduces the concept of group entropy, $\mathcal{H}_g(x)$, as an estimator of the sequence-level entropy for each prompt group. This estimator is derived from Monte Carlo samples (the $K$ responses) and is normalized by the average response length to enhance comparability across groups with varying response lengths. Group entropy serves as a signal reflecting the current exploration state of the policy for a specific task: low values suggest over-exploitation, while high values indicate active exploration.

#### 3.3. Group Entropy-Controlled Policy Optimization (GEPO)

GEPO modifies the standard GRPO advantage calculation by applying a group entropy-based coefficient to shape advantages. The core mechanism is defined as follows:

- **Entropy-Conditioned Asymmetric Advantage Shaping:** For each advantage $A_i$ associated with a prompt $x$ and its group entropy $\mathcal{H}_g(x)$, a shaped advantage $\hat{A}_i$ is computed.
 - If $A_i > 0$ and the group entropy $\mathcal{H}_g(x)$ is below a lower adaptive threshold $\mathcal{H}^{(t)}_{\text{low}}$, the positive advantage is attenuated by a scaling coefficient $\alpha_{\text{low}}$. This aims to reduce over-exploitation in tasks where the policy is already confident and exploring minimally.
 - If $A_i < 0$ and the group entropy $\mathcal{H}_g(x)$ is above an upper adaptive threshold $\mathcal{H}^{(t)}_{\text{high}}$, the negative advantage is attenuated by a scaling coefficient $\alpha_{\text{high}}$. This preserves exploration in tasks where the policy is still actively exploring and negative signals might prematurely suppress diverse paths.
 - Otherwise, the advantage remains unchanged.

- **Asymmetric Scaling Coefficients:** An empirical constraint $\alpha_{\text{high}} < \alpha_{\text{low}}$ is imposed (e.g., 0.2 vs 0.5). This asymmetry is based on the observation that low-entropy regimes are more fragile; aggressively penalizing negative advantages in such scenarios could lead to "length collapse" (where the model shortens responses to reduce uncertainty). A gentler attenuation for positive advantages in low-entropy groups encourages exploration without triggering instability.

- **Adaptive Entropy Thresholds:** Recognizing the brittleness of fixed thresholds across diverse tasks, base models, and training stages, GEPO employs adaptive thresholds. At each training step $t$, the lower ($\mathcal{H}^{(t)}_{\text{low}}$) and upper ($\mathcal{H}^{(t)}_{\text{high}}$) thresholds are dynamically computed based on the batch mean ($\mu^{(t)}_H$) and standard deviation ($\sigma^{(t)}_H$) of group entropy, with scaling coefficients $\beta_{\text{low}}$ and $\beta_{\text{high}}$. These instantaneous thresholds are then smoothed using exponential moving averages (EMA) to ensure temporal stability and adapt to shifting exploration regimes throughout training. This approach makes GEPO distribution-aware and robust to different model characteristics without requiring manual tuning.

#### 3.4. Advantages of GEPO's Approach

The method is task-aware, providing fine-grained characterization at the prompt level. It is naturally compatible with GRPO's grouped sampling, incurring no additional sampling or computational overhead. By conditioning on group entropy, GEPO attenuates the structural bias identified in the preliminary analysis, promoting more balanced learning by mitigating over-exploitation in converged tasks and preserving exploration in nascent ones.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GEPO was conducted on two base models, Intern-S1-mini and Qwen3.5-9B, across thirteen diverse benchmarks spanning mathematical reasoning, scientific reasoning, code generation, instruction following, and multimodal understanding. Comparisons were made against GRPO, AEPO, Clip-Cov, and KL-Cov.

#### 4.1. Performance Across Multiple Benchmarks

- **Overall Superiority:** GEPO consistently demonstrated superior performance, achieving the best average scores on both Intern-S1-mini (54.2%) and Qwen3.5-9B (71.2%).
- **Balanced Improvements:** Unlike baselines which often showed strong gains on some tasks but limited or negative transfer on others (e.g., AEPO's strong performance on mathematics with limited gains elsewhere), GEPO delivered balanced cross-task improvements. On Intern-S1-mini, GEPO ranked first on 7 out of 13 benchmarks, showcasing gains in mathematical reasoning, physics, instruction following, and scientific understanding. On Qwen3.5-9B, GEPO surpassed or matched strong baselines, excelling particularly on IMO-Bench, CMPhysBench, MMMU-Pro, SFE, and MicroVQA.
- **Robustness to Model Characteristics:** GEPO's adaptive thresholds enabled successful transfer and strong performance on Qwen3.5-9B, a model with different entropy characteristics than Intern-S1-mini, without requiring model-specific tuning. This contrasted with AEPO, which showed significant degradation on Qwen3.5-9B, suggesting its uniform entropy control is less robust.

#### 4.2. Training Stability

- **Smooth Optimization:** GEPO exhibited smooth and monotonically improving validation performance throughout training on both base models and across all evaluated benchmarks. This was accompanied by notably lower variance in performance curves.
- **Mitigation of Instabilities:** In contrast, baseline methods frequently displayed instability. GRPO on Qwen3.5-9B experienced catastrophic performance collapse, with accuracy plummeting on multiple benchmarks. AEPO, while avoiding full collapse, showed persistent oscillations. Clip-Cov and KL-Cov improved stability over unconstrained GRPO but still exhibited task-dependent fluctuations and earlier plateaus, with KL-Cov also showing training collapse on Qwen3.5-9B after 100 training steps. GEPO's group entropy control with adaptive thresholds was attributed to preventing runaway entropy dynamics and maintaining stable learning.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics of Entropy

- **Stable Policy Entropy:** GEPO maintained a stable and bounded policy entropy trajectory throughout training, avoiding premature entropy collapse (which leads to mode-seeking) and runaway entropy growth (which leads to degenerate outputs), both observed in baselines.
- **Differentiated Task Entropy:** A key finding from the group entropy dynamics was that GEPO preserved differentiated exploration levels across tasks. Tasks requiring broader exploration maintained relatively higher group entropy, while those with more deterministic solution patterns converged to lower entropy levels. This contrasted with AEPO, which tended to homogenize entropy across tasks, effectively forcing a uniform exploration regime. This differentiated entropy profile in GEPO was highlighted as crucial for achieving balanced performance improvements across diverse tasks while maintaining stable training.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- An ablation study confirmed the necessity of all GEPO components. Removing either the low entropy control or high entropy control, or replacing the asymmetric advantage shaping with symmetrical shaping, led to performance degradation.
- High entropy control contributed most significantly, preventing unconstrained entropy growth and preserving exploitation on challenging reasoning tasks. Low entropy control was essential for preventing premature entropy collapse and maintaining exploration diversity.
- The asymmetric advantage shaping (with $\alpha_{\text{high}} < \alpha_{\text{low}}$) was shown to outperform symmetrical shaping, underscoring the importance of a gentler intervention in low-entropy regimes to avoid instability like length collapse.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents Group Entropy-Controlled Policy Optimization (GEPO) as an advancement in robust multi-task reinforcement learning for large language models, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Addressing Heterogeneity in Multi-task RL:** GEPO directly addresses a fundamental challenge in LLM post-training: the heterogeneous nature of multi-task learning and its impact on exploration-exploitation trade-offs. By recognizing and leveraging group entropy as a task-specific diagnostic signal, the method moves beyond global or token-level entropy controls, which are often insufficient for diverse task mixtures.
2. **Improved Performance and Generalization:** The consistent outperformance of GEPO across a wide range of benchmarks and two distinct base models (Intern-S1-mini and Qwen3.5-9B) demonstrates its effectiveness and generalizability. This suggests that GEPO can lead to more capable and broadly applicable LLMs.
3. **Enhanced Training Stability:** GEPO's ability to achieve smooth, monotonically improving validation performance with significantly reduced variance, while preventing catastrophic performance collapses observed in baselines, represents a substantial practical improvement. Training stability is a critical factor for the reliable development and deployment of LLMs, and GEPO offers a robust mechanism to achieve it.
4. **Novel Mechanism for Entropy Control:** The introduction of entropy-conditioned asymmetric advantage shaping at the group level, coupled with adaptive entropy thresholds, provides a novel and effective mechanism for regulating policy exploration. This approach intelligently moderates reinforcement in low-entropy groups to prevent over-exploitation and attenuates suppression in high-entropy groups to preserve exploration, respecting the diverse learning needs of different tasks.
5. **Efficiency and Practicality:** GEPO is a lightweight extension to existing group-based policy optimization methods like GRPO. It utilizes entropy estimated from existing grouped rollouts, incurring zero additional sampling cost or significant computational overhead. The adaptive nature of its entropy thresholds eliminates the need for manual hyperparameter tuning for specific models or training stages, enhancing its practicality for researchers and practitioners.
6. **Mechanistic Insight:** The paper provides empirical and theoretical insights into the structural bias induced by entropy heterogeneity in normalized advantages. Furthermore, its findings on differentiated task entropy dynamics – where GEPO maintains distinct exploration levels for different tasks rather than homogenizing them – offer valuable insights into effective multi-task learning strategies for LLMs. This understanding can inform future research into task-aware RL algorithms.

In conclusion, GEPO represents a step forward in making RL-based LLM fine-tuning more robust, efficient, and capable of balancing performance across heterogeneous tasks. Its contributions are likely to impact the development of more advanced and versatile large language models.
