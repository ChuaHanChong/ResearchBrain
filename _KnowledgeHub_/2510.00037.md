# On Robustness of Vision-Language-Action Model against Multi-Modal Perturbations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed RobustVLA, a fine-tuning framework that significantly enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models' resilience to multi-modal perturbations across action, observation, environment, and language inputs. It achieves absolute gains of 14.0% over vanilla π0 and operates 50.6 times faster than previous visual-robust methods, improving real-world robot task success.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, designed for general robot control, exhibit significant vulnerability to diverse real-world uncertainties in sensory input, actions, environment, and language instructions.
- Prior research on VLA robustness largely concentrated on visual perturbations, neglecting critical challenges posed by action, environment, and linguistic noise.
- Existing robustness enhancement methods are often computationally expensive, relying on large external models, which hinders their practical application in real-time robotic systems.

## Method

- The **RobustVLA** framework enhances VLA output robustness by optimizing against ==worst-case action noise== using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), effectively regularizing the action prediction.
- It improves VLA input robustness by adaptively selecting the most impactful input perturbations (observation, environment, language) for training via a UCB multi-armed bandit algorithm.
- RobustVLA integrates these output and input robustness mechanisms into a unified fine-tuning objective, primarily utilizing diffusion-based VLA backbones like π0, to achieve holistic multi-modal resilience.

## Results

- RobustVLA demonstrated an average robustness gain of **14.0%** over the vanilla π0 backbone and **12.6%** over the visual-robust BYOVLA across 17 diverse multi-modal perturbations on the LIBERO benchmark.
- In real-world robot experiments with limited data (25 demonstrations), RobustVLA outperformed π0 by **65.6%** in task success rate and maintained a **30%** advantage with more abundant data.
- The framework operates with comparable inference speed to the base π0 model, proving 50.6 times faster than the visual-robust BYOVLA, enabling efficient real-time deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A systematic evaluation revealed that action-related perturbations cause the most drastic performance degradation in VLAs, identifying it as the most fragile modality.
> - Existing visual-robust VLA methods offer minimal gains for visual corruptions and do not generalize to other perturbation modalities.
> - The diffusion-based π0 VLA model inherently exhibits superior robustness compared to autoregressive counterparts (OpenVLA, π0-FAST) under various uncertainties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm shift in robotics, serving as "robotic foundation models" that aim to achieve flexible, general, and dexterous manipulation by integrating visual and linguistic inputs with control actions. These models, often trained on vast, internet-scale robot datasets, are designed for cross-embodied, general-purpose control in real-world scenarios. Examples like RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0 have demonstrated impressive capabilities in embodied decision-making.

However, the practical deployment of VLAs hinges critically on their robustness to real-world uncertainties. The environments robots operate in are inherently noisy and unpredictable, facing perturbations across multiple modalities:
- **Observation**: Sensory noise, camera errors, lighting variations.
- **Action**: Sensorimotor noise, actuator wear, unexpected disturbances.
- **Environment**: External forces, distracting objects.
- **Language**: Synonymous instructions, ambiguous prompts.

Prior research has begun to address VLA robustness, but largely with significant limitations. VLATest (Wang et al., 2025) provided an initial evaluation framework, but primarily focused on visual perturbations. Methods like BYOVLA (Hancock et al., 2025) and GEVRM (Zhang et al., 2025) proposed enhancements, but were restricted to visual robustness and often relied on external Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) or Large Language Models (LLMs), introducing substantial computational overhead (e.g., BYOVLA is noted as 50.6x slower in inference). These existing works leave a critical gap: the lack of a comprehensive understanding and effective mitigation strategy for *multi-modal* perturbations, coupled with the need for computationally efficient solutions.

This paper directly addresses these lacunae by providing both a systematic multi-modal evaluation and a robust, efficient enhancement framework. It moves beyond the prevalent focus on visual robustness to investigate and improve VLA resilience across action, observation, environment, and language modalities. By identifying the most fragile modalities and proposing a method that is not only effective but also computationally lean, this work significantly advances the state-of-the-art in robust VLA design and brings these powerful models closer to reliable real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives of this research are twofold:
1. **Systematically Evaluate Multi-Modal Robustness of Mainstream VLAs**: The authors aim to empirically assess how well current, leading VLA models (such as OpenVLA, π0-FAST, and π0) withstand a diverse range of 17 perturbations spanning four critical modalities: action, observation, environment, and instruction. This evaluation seeks to uncover which modalities are most vulnerable and to identify the inherent robustness characteristics of different VLA architectures.
2. **Develop a Novel Multi-Modal Robustness Enhancement Framework (RobustVLA)**: Based on the insights gained from the evaluation, the primary objective is to propose and implement a fine-tuning framework that specifically targets and improves robustness against these multi-modal uncertainties in both VLA inputs and outputs, without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

The motivation for these objectives is deeply rooted in the current limitations of VLA models and the demands of real-world robotics:
- **Real-world Vulnerability**: VLAs are envisioned for broad deployment, yet their susceptibility to various common uncertainties (sensory noise, actuator errors, environmental shifts, language ambiguities) poses significant safety and reliability concerns. Without robust performance, their practical utility is severely limited.
- **Incomplete Robustness Research**: Existing efforts to enhance VLA robustness have primarily focused on visual perturbations. This narrow scope overlooks other crucial sources of failure, such as errors in executed actions or misinterpretations of instructions, which are equally critical in complex robotic tasks.
- **Computational Inefficiency of Existing Solutions**: The few available robustness solutions often rely on computationally expensive external large models (e.g., LLMs/LVLMs for visual interventions), rendering them impractical for real-time robotic control and deployment. There is a clear need for efficient, integrated robustness mechanisms.
- **Identifying Fragile Points**: By conducting a comprehensive evaluation, the authors aim to identify the "weakest links" in current VLA architectures (e.g., action modality being the most fragile), thereby guiding future research and development towards more impactful robustness strategies.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the critical need to bridge the gap between impressive VLA capabilities in controlled environments and their robust, reliable performance in the unpredictable, multi-modal reality of robotic operations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper is structured into two main phases: a comprehensive robustness evaluation and the development of the RobustVLA enhancement framework.

#### 3.1. VLA Robustness Evaluation

The first part of the study focuses on systematically evaluating the robustness of mainstream VLA models against a wide array of multi-modal perturbations.
- **Problem Formulation**: The decision process of VLAs is modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). Robustness is defined as the expected discounted return under uncertainties, where uncertainties (ω) can perturb language instructions (ψ), observations (o), actions (A), and environmental transitions (P).
- **Evaluated Algorithms**: Three representative VLA models are selected:
 - **OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2025)**: An autoregressive VLA model.
 - **π0-FAST (Pertsch et al., 2025)**: An autoregressive variant of π0.
 - **π0 (Black et al., 2024)**: A diffusion-based VLA model, known for using a rectified flow matching action head.
 Additionally, **BYOVLA (Hancock et al., 2025)**, an existing visual-robust VLA method, is used as a baseline for comparison, applying its visual enhancement procedure.
- **Evaluated Uncertainties**: A total of 17 distinct perturbation types are introduced across four modalities (visualized in Figure 2 of the paper):
 - **Action (5 types)**: Uniform Noise, Gaussian Noise, Action Bias, Random Flips, Sudden Spikes (simulating sensorimotor noise, actuator wear, unexpected disturbances).
 - **Observation (6 types)**: Gaussian Noise, Dead Pixel, Motion Blur, Color Jitter, Image Rotation, Image Shift (simulating sensory noise, camera errors).
 - **Environment (3 types)**: External Force (VLA output), Irrelevant Objects (VLA input), Lighting Variation (VLA input) (simulating external influences).
 - **Instruction (3 types)**: Lexical Transformation, Syntactic Transformation, Adversarial Prompts (simulating linguistic variability, ambiguity, and distraction).
- **Evaluation Process**: All evaluations are conducted using the **LIBERO benchmark suite (Liu et al., 2023)**, following its official settings to ensure comparability. Each uncertainty is applied at varying noise levels to measure the degradation in success rate.

#### 3.2. RobustVLA Framework

Building on the insights from the evaluation (specifically, that π0 is a robust backbone and action is the most fragile modality), the authors propose **RobustVLA**, a fine-tuning framework to enhance multi-modal robustness. The framework is primarily developed on the π0 backbone but designed to generalize to other VLAs.

#### 3.3. Robustness Against VLA Outputs

The approach for robustifying VLA outputs primarily addresses action uncertainties, which were identified as the most fragile.
- **Preliminaries: Conditional Flow Matching**: The method leverages π0's rectified flow matching action head, where a velocity field `v_θ` is learned to transform a simple Gaussian noise (A0) into a desired action from the dataset (A1) via a linear ODE path. The objective minimizes the squared difference between `v_θ` and the rectified flow `u`.
- **Worst-Case Action Noise**: To achieve output robustness, the framework aims to identify and train against the "worst-case" action noise (δ). This is defined as an ℓp-bounded noise that maximally increases the flow matching loss, effectively representing an action perturbation that steers the policy furthest from the expert behavior. This δ is computed iteratively using **Projected Gradient Descent (PGD)**.
- **Robust Optimization**: The training objective is augmented with this worst-case noise, following a **TRADES (Theoretically Principled Trade-off between Robustness and Accuracy)**-like objective. This ensures that the model not only matches the clean action distribution but also accounts for adversarially perturbed actions.
 - **Interpretation**: This robust optimization can be understood as flow matching against both clean and noisy action distributions, a form of label smoothing (discouraging overconfident decisions), and a penalization of outliers (amplifying mismatch for poorly fitted actions).
- **Generalization**: The approach is designed to generalize to other diffusion-based VLAs by adapting the perturbation generation to their specific flow matching objective, and to autoregressive VLAs by perturbing actions before binning to maximize cross-entropy loss within acceptable bins.

#### 3.4. Robustness Against VLA Inputs

This component focuses on enhancing robustness to noisy VLA inputs (observation, environment, instruction) by enforcing consistent actions under semantically invariant input variations.
- **Principle**: The core idea is that input perturbations that do not alter the underlying task semantics should not change the optimal action. Thus, the VLA policy should produce similar actions for original and semantically equivalent perturbed inputs.
- **Balancing Various Noises (Multi-Armed Bandit with UCB)**: To efficiently handle the diverse set of input perturbations, the problem of selecting which perturbation to train against is framed as a **multi-armed bandit problem**. The **Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) algorithm** is employed to adaptively select the most harmful perturbation at each training iteration.
 - **Reward Function**: The "reward" for selecting a perturbation ωᵢ is defined as the increase in the flow-matching loss induced by that perturbation, representing how much it deviates the model from the clean objective. This encourages training on perturbations that genuinely challenge the model.
- **Robust Training Objective**: The overall input robustness objective combines the clean flow matching loss with an additional term that maximizes the loss under the UCB-selected input perturbation. To further enhance local smoothness, an ℓp-bounded observation noise (η) is applied and maximized via PGD.
- **Generalization**: Similar to output robustness, this input robustness framework can be adapted to other diffusion-based VLAs by modifying the flow matching loss, and to autoregressive VLAs by applying perturbations to observations before mapping to action tokens.

#### 3.5. Overall RobustVLA Loss

The final **RobustVLA training objective** combines the original π0 loss, the input robustness term (L_in), and the output robustness term (L_out) with hyperparameters (λ_in, λ_out) to balance their contributions. This unified loss function enables holistic multi-modal robustness training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded significant findings in both the evaluation and enhancement phases.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Findings

The initial systematic evaluation of mainstream VLAs under 17 multi-modal perturbations led to three key insights:
1. **Action is the Most Fragile Modality**: Across varying noise levels, perturbations to the action modality (e.g., sensorimotor noise, actuator errors) caused the most drastic decline in VLA success rates. Even small action noise levels (e.g., 5%) could lead to complete task failure for π0, a phenomenon attributed to the cumulative and off-distribution nature of action errors in offline learning.
2. **Existing Visual-Robust VLAs Do Not Generalize to Other Modalities**: Methods like BYOVLA, designed to enhance visual robustness, showed only marginal improvements in visual corruptions (4.0% average gain) and no measurable improvement in non-visual modalities (+0.0% gain). This underscored the need for a truly multi-modal robustness approach.
3. **π0 Demonstrates Superior Intrinsic Robustness**: Among the evaluated backbones, the diffusion-based π0 significantly outperformed its autoregressive counterparts, OpenVLA and π0-FAST, by 27.9% and 5.1% respectively, suggesting an inherent advantage of diffusion-based action heads in handling uncertainties. This finding guided the choice of π0 as the primary backbone for developing RobustVLA.

#### 4.2. RobustVLA Performance on LIBERO

RobustVLA, developed based on these evaluation insights, demonstrated superior performance on the LIBERO benchmark:
- **Overall Robustness Gains**: RobustVLA achieved absolute gains of **12.6%** over the visual-robust baseline BYOVLA and **14.0%** over the vanilla π0 backbone across all 17 perturbations. It maintained competitive performance under clean (no perturbation) conditions (95.5% vs. 96.0% for π0), indicating that robustness was not achieved at the expense of clean accuracy.
- **Modality-Specific Improvements**: RobustVLA significantly improved robustness in the fragile action modality (+8.0% average success rate) and showed even larger gains in VLA input uncertainties (+17.2% average increase). It also generalized to unseen perturbations like external forces.
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - Removing input regularization (w/o in) or output regularization (w/o out) led to noticeable performance drops, demonstrating that both components contribute significantly to overall multi-modal robustness, with some cross-modal benefits observed.
 - A domain randomization (DR) variant performed poorly, often overfitting to simple perturbations.
 - RobustVLA without UCB (w/o UCB) showed a 7.3% drop in robustness, highlighting the critical role of the UCB algorithm in adaptively selecting the most harmful perturbations and preventing overfitting to a single noise source.
- **Consistency on OpenVLA Backbone**: RobustVLA's effectiveness was consistent across different VLA architectures, improving average robustness by **13.2%** over vanilla OpenVLA and **10.4%** over BYOVLA when applied to the OpenVLA backbone.

#### 4.3. Efficiency and Mixed Perturbations

Beyond robustness, RobustVLA also offered practical advantages:
- **Computational Efficiency**: RobustVLA achieved a per-episode inference time of approximately 11 seconds, comparable to the base π0 model. Crucially, it was **50.6 times faster** than BYOVLA, which relies on computationally expensive external LLMs for visual interventions. This makes RobustVLA a viable solution for real-time robotic deployment.
- **Robustness to Mixed Perturbations**: When subjected to a random combination of one input uncertainty and one output uncertainty, RobustVLA showed a **14.5%** higher robustness than π0 and **10.4%** higher robustness than BYOVLA, demonstrating its ability to handle complex, real-world uncertainty scenarios.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

Deployment on a Fairino FR5 robot with a Hitbot Z-EFG-C50 gripper under four types of real-world multi-modal perturbations (environmental, action, instruction, observation noise) showcased RobustVLA's strong practical utility:
- **Low-Data Performance**: With only 25 demonstration trajectories for fine-tuning, RobustVLA drastically outperformed π0 by **65.6%** success rate. Baselines failed due to overfitting to limited data and inability to cope with perturbations, leading to imprecise control, obscured observations, OOD environmental shifts, and language misinterpretations.
- **High-Data Performance**: Even with more abundant demonstrations (50 and 100 demos), RobustVLA maintained its advantage, achieving **30% higher success rate** than π0, which plateaued. This indicates that RobustVLA provides substantial robustness gains beyond what is achievable by simply increasing training data.
- **Failure Analysis**: Baselines often exhibited imprecise grasping, inability to identify objects clearly, failure to adapt to illumination changes (leading to OOD observations), and misinterpretation of instructions due to linguistic noise. RobustVLA, by contrast, remained reliable in most trials.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the development of reliable and deployable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics. Its contributions are substantial and have a wide-ranging potential impact:

1. **Systematic Multi-Modal Robustness Benchmarking**: The paper introduces the first comprehensive evaluation of VLA robustness across four critical modalities (action, observation, environment, language) and 17 distinct perturbation types. This establishes a much-needed benchmark for future research, highlighting the multi-faceted nature of robustness challenges in embodied AI. By identifying "action" as the most fragile modality and π0 as the most robust backbone, it provides crucial guidance for the VLA research community.

2. **Novel and Comprehensive Robustness Framework (RobustVLA)**: The proposed RobustVLA framework is a pioneering effort to simultaneously address robustness against perturbations in both VLA inputs and outputs. Its dual-pronged approach, leveraging worst-case action noise optimization (interpretable as label smoothing and outlier penalization) and adaptive input perturbation selection via UCB, is both theoretically grounded and empirically effective. This unified framework offers a holistic solution compared to fragmented, modality-specific existing methods.

3. **Enhanced Real-World Deployability**: RobustVLA's impressive performance gains on the LIBERO benchmark and, more critically, in real-world robot experiments (outperforming π0 by 65.6% with limited data and 30% with abundant data) directly addresses a major bottleneck in robotics: bridging the sim-to-real gap and ensuring reliable operation in unpredictable environments. Its ability to achieve strong robustness in low-data regimes is particularly impactful for real-world robotics where data collection is expensive and labor-intensive.

4. **Computational Efficiency**: The framework's ability to deliver robust performance without relying on computationally expensive external LLMs, making it 50.6 times faster than visual-robust BYOVLA, is a game-changer for practical, real-time robotic control. This efficiency ensures that robustness can be integrated into VLA models without sacrificing performance or increasing deployment costs.

5. **Improved Trustworthiness and Safety in Embodied AI**: By systematically understanding and mitigating vulnerabilities, this work directly contributes to making embodied AI systems more trustworthy, safe, and reliable. Robust VLAs are essential for applications in manufacturing, healthcare, exploration, and domestic assistance, where failures can have significant consequences. The detailed failure analysis of baselines under various perturbations further informs the development of safer robot behaviors.

In conclusion, this paper does not just incrementally improve VLA performance; it fundamentally shifts the perspective on VLA robustness from a largely visual problem to a multi-modal challenge requiring integrated solutions. RobustVLA provides a robust, efficient, and generalizable framework that propels VLA models closer to being truly capable and dependable agents in complex, unstructured real-world environments.
