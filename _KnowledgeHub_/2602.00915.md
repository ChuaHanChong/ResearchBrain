# UniMorphGrasp: Diffusion Model with Morphology-Awareness for Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Grasp Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> UniMorphGrasp presents a diffusion model that generates dexterous grasps by incorporating morphology-aware conditioning, enabling cross-embodiment generalization to diverse and even unseen robotic hand designs. The method achieved a 94.0% success rate and 0.535 rad diversity on the MultiDex dataset, outperforming baselines, and demonstrated robust zero-shot adaptation to novel hand morphologies and real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation requires adapting grasping capabilities across highly diverse robotic hand designs, which vary in kinematic structures, finger counts, and joint configurations.
- Existing dexterous grasp generation methods are often hand-specific, computationally expensive, or limited in generalizing to unseen hand structures, demanding extensive retraining or re-engineering for new hardware.
- There is a need for a universal, scalable solution that can generate stable and diverse grasps for any robotic hand morphology, including those not encountered during training.

## Method

- UniMorphGrasp developed a diffusion-based framework that integrates a Graphormer-based morphology encoder to represent the kinematic structure of diverse robotic hands as graph-structured features.
- It employs a canonical hand mapping to unify heterogeneous hand embodiments into a human-like pose format, along with a morphology-aware denoising model that uses cross-attention for conditioning on hand morphology and object geometry.
- A novel hierarchical, morphology-aware loss function (L_m) was introduced, which adaptively weights joint errors based on their kinematic influence, combined with standard reconstruction and physics-aware losses to ensure plausible grasps.

## Results

- Achieved an overall 94.0% success rate and 0.535 rad diversity on the MultiDex dataset, surpassing baselines by 4.9% in success rate and 11.2% in diversity, with competitive efficiency (0.47 seconds per grasp).
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets (Multi-GraspLLM and Objaverse), achieving 87.4% and 91.3% success rates respectively, and successfully adapted to novel hand morphologies (topological, geometrical, and embodiment variations) with limited performance degradation.
- Validated in real-world experiments using a UR5e arm with a Leap Hand, achieving a 91% success rate across 80 attempts on 8 representative YCB objects, confirming practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding and conditioning on the graph-structured kinematic morphology of robotic hands allows for effective zero-shot generalization to novel and unseen hand designs.
> - Mapping varied hand embodiments to a unified canonical representation provides a consistent input structure, enabling a single model to learn and generate grasps for diverse hardware.
> - The combination of a diffusion model's generative capacity with morphology-aware conditioning and a hierarchical loss function improves both the success rate and diversity of generated grasps across different robotic hands.
