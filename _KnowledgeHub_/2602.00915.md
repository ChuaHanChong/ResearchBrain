# UniMorphGrasp: Diffusion Model with Morphology-Awareness for Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Grasp Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> UniMorphGrasp presents a diffusion model that generates dexterous grasps by incorporating morphology-aware conditioning, enabling cross-embodiment generalization to diverse and even unseen robotic hand designs. The method achieved a 94.0% success rate and 0.535 rad diversity on the MultiDex dataset, outperforming baselines, and demonstrated robust zero-shot adaptation to novel hand morphologies and real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation requires adapting grasping capabilities across highly diverse robotic hand designs, which vary in kinematic structures, finger counts, and joint configurations.
- Existing dexterous grasp generation methods are often hand-specific, computationally expensive, or limited in generalizing to unseen hand structures, demanding extensive retraining or re-engineering for new hardware.
- There is a need for a universal, scalable solution that can generate stable and diverse grasps for any robotic hand morphology, including those not encountered during training.

## Method

- UniMorphGrasp developed a diffusion-based framework that integrates a Graphormer-based morphology encoder to represent the kinematic structure of diverse robotic hands as graph-structured features.
- It employs a canonical hand mapping to unify heterogeneous hand embodiments into a human-like pose format, along with a morphology-aware denoising model that uses cross-attention for conditioning on hand morphology and object geometry.
- A novel hierarchical, morphology-aware loss function (L_m) was introduced, which adaptively weights joint errors based on their kinematic influence, combined with standard reconstruction and physics-aware losses to ensure plausible grasps.

## Results

- Achieved an overall 94.0% success rate and 0.535 rad diversity on the MultiDex dataset, surpassing baselines by 4.9% in success rate and 11.2% in diversity, with competitive efficiency (0.47 seconds per grasp).
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets (Multi-GraspLLM and Objaverse), achieving 87.4% and 91.3% success rates respectively, and successfully adapted to novel hand morphologies (topological, geometrical, and embodiment variations) with limited performance degradation.
- Validated in real-world experiments using a UR5e arm with a Leap Hand, achieving a 91% success rate across 80 attempts on 8 representative YCB objects, confirming practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding and conditioning on the graph-structured kinematic morphology of robotic hands allows for effective zero-shot generalization to novel and unseen hand designs.
> - Mapping varied hand embodiments to a unified canonical representation provides a consistent input structure, enabling a single model to learn and generate grasps for diverse hardware.
> - The combination of a diffusion model's generative capacity with morphology-aware conditioning and a hierarchical loss function improves both the success rate and diversity of generated grasps across different robotic hands.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly dexterous grasping, faces a fundamental challenge in adapting grasping capabilities across diverse robotic hand designs. Robotic hands exhibit significant heterogeneity in their kinematic structures, including the number of fingers, joint configurations, and overall morphology. This heterogeneity necessitates universal solutions for cross-embodiment dexterous grasping, enabling robots to generate stable and diverse grasps irrespective of their specific hand design, ranging from multi-fingered human-like hands to specialized grippers.

Existing approaches to dexterous grasp generation can be broadly categorized, each with limitations concerning cross-embodiment generalization:
- **Hand-Specific Methods:** Early and recent data-driven methods, such as DexGraspNet and UniDexGrasp series, are often tailored to specific robotic hands (e.g., Shadow hand). These methods require substantial model re-design and retraining when confronted with a novel hand structure, rendering them inefficient for universal deployment.
- **Optimization-Based Methods:** Approaches like DFC and GenDexGrasp support multiple hand structures by incorporating physical constraints. However, their iterative nature often results in high computational costs, which can restrict real-time application in dynamic environments.
- **Representation Learning Methods:** Recent developments, including DexGrasp-Diffusion, DRO-Grasp, and the GeoMatch series, aim to enable cross-embodiment grasping by learning data-driven representations, often predicting intermediate contact points. While these methods allow for adaptation to different hand types, their generalization capability is typically limited to hand structures observed during the training phase. They tend to fail when presented with significantly different or unseen kinematic structures and may also exhibit limitations in generating diverse grasp poses due to their regression-based prediction mechanisms.
- **Diffusion Models in Grasping:** Diffusion models have emerged as a paradigm for dexterous grasp generation due to their capacity to model complex, multimodal distributions and stable training objectives. Prior work like Scene-Diffuser and UGG introduced diffusion models for grasp diversity and object generalization. DexGrasp-Diffusion extended this by incorporating hand *class tokens*, still implying a fixed, known set of hand types. DexGrasp-Anything further integrated physics-aware constraints to enhance grasp stability.

This paper positions UniMorphGrasp as a framework designed to overcome the core limitation of previous methods: the inability to generalize effectively to *unseen* hand morphologies. It aims to achieve true zero-shot generalization by explicitly integrating hand morphological information into a diffusion-based generative process, providing a unified and scalable solution for cross-embodiment dexterous grasping.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable framework for cross-embodiment dexterous grasp generation, capable of synthesizing stable and diverse grasps for robotic hands with heterogeneous kinematic structures, particularly demonstrating generalization to previously unseen hand morphologies.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:
- **Addressing Hardware Diversity:** As robots are deployed in increasingly varied and complex real-world scenarios, the necessity for reliable grasping capabilities across a spectrum of robotic hands becomes critical. The inherent diversity in robotic hand designs (e.g., number of fingers, joint types, link lengths) poses a significant challenge, as methods tailored to specific hardware lack scalability and universality.
- **Bridging the Generalization Gap:** Existing dexterous grasp generation methods either require extensive retraining for new hand designs (hand-specific methods) or exhibit limited generalization to hands not explicitly present in their training data (representation learning methods). Optimization-based methods, while more general, are often too slow for practical, real-time deployment. This creates a critical gap in the ability to deploy robotic grasping solutions broadly without prohibitive engineering effort.
- **Exploiting Hand Kinematic Structure:** The authors observe that dexterous robotic hands possess a structured kinematic organization, which can be naturally represented as graphs (joints as nodes, kinematic chains as edges). This graph-based representation offers a principled and unified way to describe diverse hand morphologies, providing a foundation for a generalizable learning approach. Explicitly incorporating this morphological information is hypothesized to enable a deeper understanding of hand capabilities and constraints, facilitating adaptation to novel designs.
- **Leveraging Diffusion Models for Diversity and Stability:** Diffusion models have demonstrated effectiveness in modeling complex data distributions and generating diverse outputs. Integrating these models into a morphology-aware framework can potentially combine the benefits of diverse grasp generation with the structural awareness needed for cross-embodiment generalization.
- **Creating a Unified Learning Space:** Learning across different hand embodiments is complicated by varying degrees of freedom and kinematic arrangements. A key motivation is to map these heterogeneous hand poses into a canonical, human-like representation. This canonical space would provide a common ground for the learning algorithm, simplifying the process of understanding and generating grasps for diverse hardware.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for a scalable, efficient, and truly generalizable solution to dexterous grasping that can accommodate the wide array of robotic hand designs encountered in real-world applications, moving beyond hand-specific or training-limited approaches. The specific goals include proposing a morphology-aware diffusion model, developing a novel evaluation methodology for zero-shot generalization, and achieving high-performance metrics on established benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniMorphGrasp proposes a diffusion-based framework that integrates morphology-aware conditioning for cross-embodiment dexterous grasp generation. The core idea is to encode the kinematic structure of diverse robotic hands into a unified representation and then use this representation to guide a diffusion model in synthesizing stable and diverse grasps.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The objective is to sample a batch of dexterous grasp poses (H) conditioned on an object point cloud (O) and the morphology (M) of a target hand. Each grasp pose is parameterized by a global translation (t), global rotation (R), and joint angles (θ). A diffusion model is employed to iteratively transform an isotropic Gaussian distribution into the desired conditional data distribution P(H|O, M).

#### 3.2. Canonical Hand Mapping

To address the varying degrees of freedom (DoFs) and kinematic structures across different robotic hands, all hand embodiments are mapped into a unified human-like canonical hand pose format (Hc). This canonical pose is parameterized as (t, R, θc, δ), where θc represents canonical joint angles and δ is a binary mask indicating active joints. For instance, the Shadow Hand's 24 DoFs are used as the canonical format. For hands with fewer DoFs (e.g., a 3-fingered Barrett hand), the corresponding joints in the canonical format that are absent in the physical hand (e.g., ring and pinky fingers, excess distal joints) are masked by setting their δ values to zero and their joint angles and morphological features to zero vectors. This provides a consistent input structure for the learning model.

#### 3.3. Morphology Encoder

A Graphormer, a type of Transformer designed for graph-structured data, is utilized as the morphology encoder. This encoder processes the hand's kinematic information to extract graph-structured features (M).
- **Joint Morphology Extraction:** For each of the Nc canonical joints, geometric and kinematic properties are extracted from the hand's URDF description. These include the child link's collision geometry (length, width, height), joint maximum and minimum limits, joint origin, and joint axis. These 11 dimensions constitute the joint morphology vector for each joint.
- **Morph Token Embedding:** These joint morphology vectors are embedded into a set of morph tokens (X).
- **Graphormer Processing:** The Graphormer processes these tokens through multiple self-attention layers. The attention mechanism is biased by:
 - **Graph Bias:** Learned graph-based attention biases derived from the hand's kinematic structure, specifically spatial adjacency and parent-child relationships, encoded via separate embedding layers.
 - **Mask Bias:** A bias derived from the active joint mask (δ), which informs the attention mechanism about active and inactive joints.
- **Output:** The morphology encoder outputs a set of morph representations (M) ∈ R^(Nc x D), where D is the feature dimension.

#### 3.4. Morphology-Aware Denoising Model

This is a key component of UniMorphGrasp, designed to generate grasp poses iteratively while being conditioned on hand morphology and object geometry.
- **Conditioning Inputs:** The model takes the morph representation (M) from the morphology encoder and object point cloud features (P). The point cloud features are extracted from the object point cloud (O) using a Point Transformer encoder.
- **Hand Feature Initialization:** An initial hand feature (hT) is created by embedding the noised canonical hand pose (Hc_T) and the active joint mask (δ).
- **Iterative Denoising:** The denoising process operates iteratively through a series of Transformer blocks, refining the hand feature from a noisy state (t=T) to the final denoised grasp (t=0).
 - **Timestep Conditioning:** At each timestep, the current hand feature (ht) is processed through a residual convolutional layer, conditioned on an embedded timestep representation.
 - **Cross-Attention Modules:** Two sequential cross-attention modules are applied:
 - **Morphology-aware Conditioning:** Cross-attention is performed between the current hand feature (as query) and the morph representation (M) (as key and value). This step enables the model to adapt its grasp generation based on the specific kinematic and geometric properties of the target hand.
 - **Object-aware Conditioning:** Following morphology conditioning, another cross-attention is applied between the morphology-conditioned hand feature (as query) and the object point cloud features (P) (as key and value). This integrates information about the object's geometry.
- **Output:** The refined feature is then passed through a feedforward layer. This iterative process ultimately yields a denoised hand feature (h0), which is decoded to generate the final canonical grasp pose (Hc0).

#### 3.5. Morphology-Aware Loss Function (L_m)

A novel loss function is introduced to account for the hierarchical structure of hand kinematics.
- **Adaptive Joint Weighting:** Different joints have varying influence on the overall hand pose (e.g., a proximal finger joint affects the entire finger chain more than a distal joint). The loss adaptively weights errors for each joint based on its position in the kinematic tree.
- **Weight Calculation:** A weight (w_i) is computed for each joint (i) based on the number of its descendant joints (c_i). Proximal joints with more descendants receive higher weights. A geometric mean (G) is used to normalize these weights.
- **Loss Formulation:** L_m combines L2 losses for global translation (t) and rotation (R), and a weighted L2 loss for canonical joint angles (θc), where the weights w_i are applied to the active joints.

#### 3.6. Total Training Loss

The total training objective (L) combines several components:
- **Reconstruction Loss (L_recon):** The standard noise reconstruction loss, typically the mean squared error between the sampled Gaussian noise and the noise predicted by the denoising network.
- **Morphology-Aware Loss (L_m):** The aforementioned hierarchical joint loss.
- **Physics-Aware Losses:** To ensure physically plausible and stable grasps, three auxiliary loss terms are incorporated, evaluated on hand mesh surface points:
 - **Surface Pulling Force (L_spf):** Encourages contact between the hand and object.
 - **External-Penetration Repulsion Force (L_erf):** Penalizes collisions between the hand and object.
 - **Self-Penetration Repulsion Force (L_srf):** Prevents self-intersections of hand links.
 - These physics losses are balanced using corresponding weights (α).

#### 3.7. Implementation Details and Datasets

The model is implemented in PyTorch and trained on an 80GB NVIDIA A100 GPU using the Adam optimizer. The training dataset is the MultiDex dataset. For evaluation, in-domain tests are conducted on MultiDex, while zero-shot cross-dataset evaluations are performed on Multi-GraspLLM and Objaverse to assess generalization to unseen objects and domains. The experiments involve three robotic hands: Barrett (3-fingered), Allegro (4-fingered), and Shadow (5-fingered).

#### 3.8. Baselines and Evaluation Metrics

UniMorphGrasp is compared against optimization-based methods (DFC, GenDexGrasp) and learning-based methods (GeoMatch, GeoMatch++, DRO-Grasp). Evaluation metrics include:
- **Success Rate:** Measured in the Isaac Gym simulator by applying external forces. A grasp is successful if object displacement remains below 2 cm.
- **Diversity:** Quantified as the standard deviation of joint configurations across successful grasps.
- **Efficiency:** Measured as the time taken to generate each grasp pose.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate UniMorphGrasp's performance in both in-domain and zero-shot cross-dataset evaluations, as well as its generalization to novel hand morphologies and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Existing Methods (In-Domain)

On the MultiDex dataset (Table 1), UniMorphGrasp (with L_m) achieved an overall success rate of **94.0%**, which represents an improvement of 4.9% over the best baseline (DRO-Grasp, 87.5%) and up to 35.0% over other baselines. It also demonstrated the highest diversity at **0.535 rad**, surpassing baselines by 11.2% to 113.4%. The model maintained computational efficiency with an average execution time of **0.47 seconds per grasp**, making it competitive with learning-based methods and significantly faster than optimization-based approaches. Notably, for the Shadow hand, UniMorphGrasp showed success rate improvements ranging from 12.7% to 41.5%. The inclusion of the morphology-aware loss function (L_m) further contributed to a 1.6% increase in overall success rate and a 1.5% increase in diversity.

#### 4.2. Cross-Dataset Zero-Shot Generalization

For generalization to unseen datasets (Table 2), UniMorphGrasp exhibited robust performance:
- **Multi-GraspLLM:** It achieved an 87.4% success rate and 0.547 rad diversity. In contrast, GenDexGrasp showed high diversity but a low success rate (38.3%), while DRO-Grasp had a higher success rate (78.4%) but limited diversity (0.455 rad).
- **Objaverse:** UniMorphGrasp achieved an average success rate of 91.3%, outperforming DRO-Grasp by 12.8%.
These results suggest that the model effectively generalizes to unseen domains without fine-tuning, maintaining performance even for hands with complex kinematic structures, such as the Shadow hand, where baselines exhibited notable performance degradation.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Morphology Encoding (Ablation Study)

Ablation studies (Table 3) confirmed the contribution of the morphology encoding mechanism:
- Incorporating morphology encoding resulted in a **7.3% improvement** in success rate (from 83.3% to 90.6%) and a 2.4% increase in diversity.
- Using a Graphormer instead of a basic Transformer in the morphology encoder provided an **additional 1.8% increase** in success rate (from 90.6% to 92.4%) and a 1.0% improvement in diversity.
- The computational overhead of morphology encoding was negligible (0.02 seconds increase in inference time).
Qualitative results (Figure 5, Appendix Figure 13) indicated that morphology encoding leads to more physically plausible and stable grasps.

#### 4.4. Generalization to Novel Hand Morphologies

The framework's zero-shot generalization capabilities to entirely novel hand structures were evaluated by introducing topological, geometrical, and embodiment variations to the Shadow hand (Table 4, Figure 5, Figure 6, Appendix Figure 14):
- **Topological Variations (Removed Fingers):** Performance degradation was limited (0.5%-16.1%). Removing the pinky finger resulted in the most notable performance drop (84.2% success rate), while removing middle or ring fingers had less impact.
- **Geometrical Variations (Altered Finger Lengths):** Lengthening fingers had a more significant negative impact (up to 6.3% drop) on success rate than shortening them, potentially due to increased kinematic redundancy or self-collisions.
- **Embodiment Variations (Replaced Fingers):** Creating hybrid hands by replacing Shadow Hand fingers with Allegro Hand fingers showed that replacing the thumb had a negligible impact (97.9% success rate), whereas altering other fingers (e.g., index and ring) led to a more distinct drop (90.6% success rate), likely due to kinematic mismatches.
These findings validate the model's ability to generalize to novel hand structures without requiring retraining for specific morphological changes.

#### 4.5. Real-World Validation

Real-world experiments using a UR5e arm equipped with a Leap Hand demonstrated the practical effectiveness of UniMorphGrasp (Table 5, Figure 7). The approach achieved an overall success rate of **91% (73/80 attempts)** across eight representative objects from the YCB dataset, confirming robust performance in physical environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents UniMorphGrasp, a method that addresses a critical challenge in robotic manipulation: the need for generalizable dexterous grasping across heterogeneous robotic hand morphologies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Zero-Shot Generalization:** The core contribution is the demonstration of robust zero-shot generalization to *unseen* hand structures, including topological, geometrical, and embodiment variations. This capability significantly advances the field beyond methods limited to hands observed during training, moving towards truly universal robotic grasping.
- **Increased Scalability and Practicality:** By reducing the need for extensive data collection, model retraining, or re-engineering for each new robotic hand design, UniMorphGrasp enhances the scalability and practicality of deploying dexterous manipulation systems. A single trained model can potentially be adapted to a wide array of existing and future robotic hands, making robot deployment more efficient and cost-effective.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The method exhibits high performance metrics, consistently surpassing existing cross-embodiment grasping methods in terms of grasp success rate and diversity on established benchmarks, while maintaining competitive computational efficiency. This provides a robust foundation for future research and applications.
- **Methodological Innovations:** The integration of graph-based hand kinematic information into a diffusion model, combined with a canonical hand pose representation and a morphology-aware hierarchical loss function, represents a novel methodological approach. These innovations allow the model to learn and exploit the underlying kinematic structure of hands more effectively.
- **Broadening Robotic Applications:** The ability to reliably generate stable and diverse grasps for various robotic hands, even those with novel designs, has potential applications across numerous domains, including warehouse logistics, manufacturing automation, household services, and disaster response. This can facilitate the wider adoption and more versatile deployment of general-purpose robots.
- **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitation concerning object-hand penetration, particularly for thin objects, suggests a clear avenue for future research. Incorporating human demonstration data or more sophisticated collision avoidance mechanisms within the diffusion framework could lead to even more physically plausible and robust grasp generation.

In conclusion, UniMorphGrasp represents a significant step forward in developing flexible and adaptive robotic grasping solutions. Its capacity for zero-shot generalization and strong performance in both simulation and real-world scenarios holds promise for advancing the capabilities and widespread utility of dexterous robotic manipulators.
