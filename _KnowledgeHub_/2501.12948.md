# DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepSeek-AI developed DeepSeek-R1, an LLM demonstrating that sophisticated reasoning capabilities can emerge through pure outcome-based reinforcement learning without reliance on human-annotated reasoning trajectories. The model achieved an AIME 2024 pass@1 accuracy of 77.9% with R1-Zero, and the multi-stage DeepSeek-R1 surpassed state-of-the-art models on numerous math, coding, and STEM benchmarks while exhibiting emergent self-reflection and dynamic strategy adaptation.

## Problem

- Existing LLM reasoning methods heavily rely on labor-intensive human-annotated data, leading to scalability issues and the introduction of human cognitive biases.
- Training models to imitate human reasoning pathways caps their performance and prevents exploration of potentially superior, non-human strategies.
- Pure RL-trained reasoning models exhibit poor readability and language mixing, limiting their practical usability despite strong underlying reasoning performance.

## Method

- Employs a pure ==outcome-based reinforcement learning== approach, **DeepSeek-R1-Zero**, directly on a powerful base LLM (DeepSeek-V3-Base) without initial supervised fine-tuning for reasoning tasks.
- Implements a ==multi-stage learning framework== (**DeepSeek-R1**) that combines initial ==SFT== for readability, continued RL with ==language consistency rewards==, and secondary SFT with both reasoning and non-reasoning data.
- Utilizes ==knowledge distillation== to transfer the advanced reasoning capabilities of DeepSeek-R1 to smaller, open-source models for broader accessibility.

## Results

- DeepSeek-R1-Zero, trained purely with RL, achieved significant performance gains, notably increasing AIME 2024 pass@1 accuracy from **15.6%** to **77.9%**, and exhibiting emergent self-reflection.
- The multi-stage DeepSeek-R1 model demonstrated superior performance across various challenging benchmarks, including **79.8%** on AIME 2024, **65.9%** on LiveCodeBench, and **84.0%** on MMLU-Pro, surpassing comparable state-of-the-art models.
- Knowledge distillation successfully transferred advanced reasoning abilities, allowing a **1.5B** parameter Qwen model to surpass non-reasoning baselines and some larger closed-source models in mathematical benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sophisticated reasoning capabilities can organically emerge in LLMs through outcome-based reinforcement learning, rather than requiring explicit human-annotated reasoning trajectories.
> - Reinforcement learning incentivizes emergent behaviors such as self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation, with models generating longer 'thinking chains' for harder problems.
> - Bypassing conventional supervised fine-tuning for reasoning tasks can allow LLMs to discover novel and potentially superior problem-solving strategies beyond human cognitive biases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs), which have demonstrated remarkable emergent abilities, including rudimentary reasoning. While early breakthroughs like GPT models (Brown et al., 2020; OpenAI, 2023) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022b; Kojima et al., 2022) significantly advanced reasoning capabilities, they largely relied on extensive human-annotated demonstrations or carefully crafted prompts. This reliance introduces several limitations: it hinders scalability, injects human cognitive biases, and constrains models to human-like thought processes, potentially preventing the discovery of more optimal, non-human reasoning pathways.

Conventionally, post-training of LLMs involves Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Ouyang et al., 2022; Christiano et al., 2017). SFT provides a strong baseline on curated examples, while RLHF aligns models with human preferences. However, this work critically examines the SFT stage's role in reasoning, proposing that it might inadvertently *impede* a model's ability to explore and develop effective reasoning strategies by forcing it to imitate human-provided (and often suboptimal) reasoning traces.

DeepSeek-R1 distinguishes itself by advocating for and demonstrating the power of *pure outcome-based reinforcement learning* applied directly to base LLMs, bypassing the conventional SFT phase for reasoning tasks. This approach aligns with a growing body of research exploring RL for reasoning enhancement, such as STaR (Singh et al., 2024) and process-based reward models (Lightman et al., 2024). However, DeepSeek-R1's unique contribution is its success in incentivizing complex reasoning without relying on human-labeled reasoning trajectories, pushing the boundaries of what can be achieved through self-evolution in an RL framework. Furthermore, the paper implicitly contributes to the "scaling inference-time compute" literature by showing that RL can incentivize LLMs to dynamically allocate computational resources (i.e., generate longer chains of thought) for harder problems, effectively scaling compute at test time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of current approaches to instilling reasoning capabilities in LLMs. The authors identify two major drawbacks:
1. **Dependence on Human Annotations:** Existing methods, particularly those leveraging Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting or supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on reasoning trajectories, are heavily reliant on human-annotated data. This dependency is not only labor-intensive and expensive, hindering scalability, but also introduces cognitive biases.
2. **Constraint on Reasoning Pathways:** By training models to replicate human thought processes, their performance is inherently capped by the quality and diversity of human-provided exemplars. This prevents LLMs from exploring potentially superior, non-human-like reasoning strategies.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of the DeepSeek-R1 project are:
- **To explore the potential of LLMs to develop advanced reasoning abilities through *self-evolution* within a reinforcement learning (RL) framework, with minimal reliance on human labeling.** This is the core hypothesis: that unrestricted RL training can better incentivize the emergence of novel and sophisticated reasoning capabilities.
- **To demonstrate that pure outcome-based RL can facilitate the emergent development of advanced reasoning patterns** such as self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation, without explicit human-labeled reasoning trajectories.
- **To achieve superior performance on verifiable tasks** like mathematics, coding competitions, and STEM fields, surpassing counterparts trained via conventional supervised learning on human demonstrations.
- **To develop a multi-stage learning framework (DeepSeek-R1)** that integrates these emergent reasoning capabilities with human preferences, addressing practical issues like readability and broader task performance (e.g., writing, open-domain Q&A) not covered by pure reasoning RL.
- **To enable broader access to powerful AI at a lower energy cost through knowledge distillation.** This involves systematically harnessing the emergent reasoning patterns of large-scale models to guide and enhance the capabilities of smaller models.

In essence, the research aims to unlock the intrinsic potential for complex reasoning within LLMs by providing the "right incentives" through RL, rather than prescribing "how to reason."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DeepSeek-R1 project employs a sophisticated multi-stage learning framework, built upon a powerful base model and leveraging an optimized reinforcement learning algorithm.

#### 3.1. Base Model and Core RL Algorithm

The foundation for DeepSeek-R1 is **DeepSeek-V3-Base** (DeepSeek-AI, 2024b), a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture with 671 billion total parameters and 37 billion activated per token. This large-scale base model provides the necessary capacity for emergent reasoning.
The core reinforcement learning algorithm utilized is **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** (Shao et al., 2024). GRPO is a more resource-efficient variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), simplifying training by:
- Foregoing a separate value model, thus reducing memory and computational overhead. It estimates advantages from a group of rewards for sampled outputs.
- Directly adding an unbiased estimator of KL divergence to the loss, rather than as a per-token dense reward, which might implicitly penalize response length and hinder the development of long chains of thought.
- Periodically updating the reference policy to the latest policy for training stability.

#### 3.2. DeepSeek-R1-Zero: Pure RL for Reasoning

This initial stage focuses solely on incentivizing reasoning capabilities without any prior Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- **No SFT Initialization:** A deliberate choice to avoid imposing human-defined reasoning patterns, allowing the model to explore novel strategies.
- **Reward Design:** Employs **rule-based rewards** that provide objective feedback without constraining the reasoning *process*:
 - **Accuracy Rewards:** Binary (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) based on the final prediction matching ground truth. For math problems, this involves answer format verification; for coding, compiler-based evaluation against test cases.
 - **Format Rewards:** Incentivize the model to encapsulate its reasoning process and final answer within specified tags (e.g., `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`), enhancing interpretability.
- **Training Setup:** Uses a simple prompt template requiring a reasoning process followed by an answer. It allows for very long response lengths (up to 65,536 tokens) and a high sampling temperature (1.0) to encourage exploration.

#### 3.3. DeepSeek-R1: Multi-Stage Learning Framework

This advanced model addresses the limitations of DeepSeek-R1-Zero (e.g., poor readability, language mixing, limited general performance) through a multi-stage pipeline:
1. **Cold Start SFT:** Initial SFT with a small dataset of human-aligned, conversational thinking process data. This aims to improve readability and prompt the model to adopt a first-person perspective in its reasoning.
2. **First RL Stage (Reasoning & Language Consistency):** Continues RL training on reasoning tasks, incorporating rule-based rewards. Crucially, a **language consistency reward** (proportion of target language words in CoT) is introduced to mitigate language mixing, even if it causes a slight performance degradation on reasoning benchmarks, prioritizing human preference.
3. **Rejection Sampling & Secondary SFT:** A large-scale SFT phase integrating:
 - **Reasoning data:** Curated from the first RL stage by rejection sampling successful reasoning trajectories from DeepSeek-R1-Zero, potentially enhanced by generative reward models (LLM-as-a-judge for correctness).
 - **Non-reasoning data:** From DeepSeek-V3 SFT data, covering tasks like writing, factual QA, and code engineering, to broaden the model's general capabilities.
4. **Second RL Stage (Preference Alignment & Refinement):** Further RL training using a combination of reward signals:
 - **Rule-based rewards:** For reasoning data (math, code, logic).
 - **Model-based rewards:** For general data, including:
 - **Helpful Reward Model:** Trained on DeepSeek-V3 preference pairs (arena-hard format), focusing on the final summary's utility.
 - **Safety Reward Model:** Trained point-wise on "safe" or "unsafe" responses, evaluating the entire output for potential risks.
 - This stage integrates diverse prompt distributions and carefully manages the training steps with preference rewards to avoid reward hacking.

#### 3.4. Knowledge Distillation

To democratize access to these advanced capabilities, DeepSeek-R1's generated outputs (an 800,000-sample dataset) are used to fine-tune smaller, open-source base models (e.g., Qwen, LLaMA) via SFT. This allows the transfer of reasoning prowess to more resource-efficient models without requiring additional RL for the student models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings across model capabilities, emergent behaviors, and practical implications:

#### 4.1. DeepSeek-R1-Zero: Pure RL Effectiveness

- **Dramatic Performance Improvement:** DeepSeek-R1-Zero, trained purely with RL and outcome-based rewards, shows a remarkable leap in reasoning performance. On the AIME 2024 benchmark, its pass@1 accuracy increased from an initial 15.6% to 77.9%, and further to 86.7% with self-consistency decoding, significantly surpassing the average human competitor.
- **Emergent Reasoning Patterns:** The model naturally learns to generate longer responses ("thinking time"), with average response length increasing steadily during training. This extended "thought" process is characterized by the emergent development of sophisticated reasoning strategies such as:
 - **Self-reflection:** The model frequently incorporates reflective words like "wait," "evaluate," "check," and "retry," with a 5- to 7-fold increase in their frequency during training.
 - **Verification:** It actively attempts to verify its steps and conclusions.
 - **Dynamic Strategy Adaptation:** Evidenced by "aha moments" (e.g., a sudden spike in "wait" usage), indicating a distinct shift in reasoning patterns where the model re-evaluates its approach.
- **Generalization to Diverse Tasks:** Beyond math, R1-Zero achieves strong performance in coding competitions and graduate-level STEM problems (biology, physics, chemistry), underscoring RL's effectiveness across verifiable reasoning domains.

#### 4.2. DeepSeek-R1: Multi-Stage Framework Success

- **Balanced Performance:** DeepSeek-R1 successfully inherits and refines the reasoning capabilities of R1-Zero while significantly improving in other areas. It achieves substantial gains in instruction-following (e.g., IF-Eval up to 83.3%, ArenaHard up to 92.3%) and general language generation (AlpacaEval 2.0 win-rate of 87.6%).
- **Superior Reasoning Benchmarks:** Compared to its predecessor, DeepSeek-V3, and other state-of-the-art models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, OpenAI-o1-mini), DeepSeek-R1 demonstrates superior performance across a wide range of challenging benchmarks:
 - **Mathematics:** Achieves 79.8% on AIME 2024, 97.3% on MATH-500, and 78.8% on CNMO 2024, often matching or exceeding OpenAI-o1-1217.
 - **Coding:** Achieves 65.9% on LiveCodeBench and 96.3 percentile on Codeforces, dominating these benchmarks.
 - **Knowledge & Reasoning:** Shows strong performance on MMLU-Pro (84.0%), GPQA Diamond (71.5%), and FRAMES (82.5%), particularly excelling in STEM categories.
- **Adaptive Compute Scaling:** DeepSeek-R1 dynamically adjusts the number of "thinking tokens" (CoT length) based on problem difficulty. It uses fewer than 7,000 tokens for simple math problems but over 18,000 for the most challenging ones, demonstrating efficient allocation of computational resources at test time.
- **Generalization to Unseen Competitions:** R1's performance on AIME 2025 and AMC 12 2024 (competitions held after its training cutoff) yielded a USAMO-qualifying score, indicating strong generalization capabilities beyond training data.

#### 4.3. DeepSeek-R1 Distillation

- **Effective Knowledge Transfer:** Distilled smaller models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B, Llama-8B, 70B) demonstrate impressive reasoning capabilities. The 1.5B parameter Qwen model, distilled from DeepSeek-R1, notably surpasses non-reasoning baselines and even some larger closed-source models in mathematical benchmarks.
- **Distillation vs. Direct RL on Smaller Models:** Experiments show that distilling from a powerful teacher model (DeepSeek-R1) into a smaller model (e.g., Qwen2.5-32B) yields significantly better performance than applying large-scale RL directly to the smaller base model (Qwen2.5-32B-Zero), highlighting the efficiency of distillation for democratizing AI.

#### 4.4. Ethics and Safety

- DeepSeek-R1, especially when augmented with its internal risk control system (which leverages DeepSeek-V3 for model-based risk review), achieves a moderate to superior safety level, comparable to GPT-4o (2024-05-13) on standard benchmarks and in-house safety taxonomies.
- The model demonstrates strong safety in multilingual contexts and in handling queries related to illegal/criminal behavior and moral/ethical issues.
- However, like other frontier models, it remains vulnerable to jailbreak attacks, underscoring the ongoing challenge in ensuring robust safety for highly capable LLMs, particularly for open-source releases without external protective systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DeepSeek-R1 research represents a significant advancement in the development of reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models, with profound implications across several dimensions:

- **Paradigm Shift in Reasoning Training:** The most significant impact is the demonstration that sophisticated reasoning can *emerge organically* through pure outcome-based reinforcement learning, without explicit human-annotated reasoning trajectories or an initial SFT phase focused on reasoning. This fundamentally challenges the conventional wisdom that high-quality human demonstrations are indispensable for teaching complex reasoning. It suggests that LLMs can discover novel, non-human-like, and potentially superior reasoning pathways, freeing them from human cognitive biases. This finding alone could accelerate AI research by shifting focus from labor-intensive data annotation to more scalable self-evolutionary processes.

- **Scalability and Efficiency for Advanced AI:** By proving that models can self-evolve reasoning capabilities, this work offers a highly scalable approach. The GRPO algorithm and the custom RL infrastructure further enhance the efficiency of this large-scale training, making it feasible to train and refine frontier models. This efficiency is critical for pushing the boundaries of AI, especially for tasks that are complex and verifiable but challenging for humans to explicitly annotate.

- **Emergence of Autonomous Learning:** The observation of emergent behaviors like self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation (the "aha moment") during training hints at a more autonomous and adaptive form of AI. This self-evolutionary capacity, driven by simple reward signals, points towards a future where AI systems can learn and improve complex cognitive skills with minimal human intervention, potentially leading to truly intelligent agents that iteratively optimize their problem-solving strategies.

- **Democratization of Advanced AI:** The success of knowledge distillation, where smaller, more resource-efficient models inherit DeepSeek-R1's powerful reasoning abilities, has immense potential for democratizing access to advanced AI. This enables powerful AI capabilities to be deployed in environments with limited computational resources, fostering innovation and application across a broader spectrum of users and industries, and reducing the energy footprint of advanced AI.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper clearly outlines limitations and future work, including improving structural output and tool use, enhancing token efficiency, addressing multilingual language mixing more robustly, and tackling "reward hacking" for less verifiable tasks. It explicitly calls for future research into developing robust reward models for complex, subjective tasks and leveraging external tools to enhance reasoning and validation. This provides a clear roadmap for the community, underscoring the iterative nature of AI development.

In conclusion, DeepSeek-R1's contribution is not merely another high-performing LLM but a significant conceptual leap in how we approach the training of reasoning-capable AI. By demonstrating the power of incentivized self-evolution through reinforcement learning, it opens new avenues for creating more autonomous, capable, and widely accessible intelligent systems.
