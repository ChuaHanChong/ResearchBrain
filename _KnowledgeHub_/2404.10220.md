# Closed-Loop Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation with GPT-4V

> [!summary] Summary
> COME-robot is a closed-loop robotic system that leverages GPT-4V for open-vocabulary mobile manipulation in real-world settings, achieving improved task success rates of 65% in mobile manipulation and 75% in tabletop tasks. It incorporates multi-level perception and a hierarchical failure recovery mechanism, leading to a 35% and 27.5% increase over state-of-the-art baselines in respective task categories.

## Problem

- Autonomous robots lack robust capabilities for Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM) in complex, unknown real-world environments based on natural language instructions.
- Current closed-loop replanning systems are limited by predefined symbolic logic and struggle with adaptability to unforeseen situations in open-ended scenarios.
- Applying Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to mobile manipulation beyond tabletop settings is challenging due to complex 3D environments, partial observability, and interdependencies between modules in long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- The **COME-robot** system integrates ==GPT-4V== with robust robotic primitive actions within a ==closed-loop architecture== for open-vocabulary mobile manipulation (OVMM) in real-world environments.
- A ==multi-level open-vocabulary perception and situated reasoning== module is employed for hierarchical environment exploration, understanding, and target object identification aligned with natural language instructions.
- An ==iterative closed-loop feedback and restoration== mechanism provides ==hierarchical failure recovery== by verifying task feasibility, monitoring execution, and diagnosing error causes across interdependent modules.

## Results

- COME-robot demonstrated an overall task success rate of **65%** in mobile manipulation (**35%** higher than baseline CaP*) and **75%** in tabletop tasks (**27.5%** higher than CaP*).
- The system achieved high step-wise success rates (SSR) of **101/122** for mobile manipulation and **123/140** for tabletop tasks, validating its error correction capabilities during execution.
- Its hierarchical failure recovery mechanism exhibited high efficacy, achieving a **17/23** recovery rate for grasp failures and **100%** recovery for missed object detections in specific perceptual error scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating LLM/VLM commonsense reasoning with interactive exploration and dynamic scene updates is vital for effective mobile manipulation in complex, unstructured environments.
> - Closed-loop replanning is crucial for long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks due to the increased likelihood of execution failures, enabling robust recovery and task completion.
> - GPT-4V can be effectively harnessed for complex physical tasks when combined with structured multi-level perception, situated reasoning, and hierarchical failure recovery mechanisms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a central challenge in robotics: developing autonomous robots capable of understanding, navigating, and manipulating objects in unknown real-world environments based on natural language instructions. This area is termed Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM).

Recent advancements in large foundation models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have significantly progressed machine perception and natural language understanding in an open-vocabulary, zero-shot manner. These models have enabled ambitious goals in robotics, such as generating code for robot primitive actions and elucidating failures based on textual feedback. However, their application in complex robotic tasks, especially mobile manipulation, faces significant hurdles.

Existing closed-loop replanning systems typically rely on predefined symbolic logic, which limits their adaptability to unseen situations in open-ended real-world scenarios. While LLMs and VLMs offer promising avenues for common-sense reasoning and instruction following, their applicability has largely been confined to tabletop settings. These environments often simplify challenges such as partial observability during environment exploration and understanding, as well as the interdependencies between different robotic modules (e.g., how navigation choices affect manipulation access). Current approaches often assume flawless low-level execution or employ simplistic re-attempt strategies for failures, neglecting the complex interactions that lead to errors in long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks. For instance, a naive combination of VLM-based high-level planners with low-level executors has been shown to lead to sequential errors.

This research positions COME-robot as a system designed to address these limitations. It extends the utility of VLMs, specifically GPT-4V, beyond tabletop settings to real-world mobile manipulation. The paper focuses on integrating open-ended reasoning and adaptive planning with robust robotic primitive actions, incorporating sophisticated perception for complex 3D environments and a multi-level failure recovery mechanism that considers interdependencies between modules. This situates COME-robot as an advancement in developing more robust and adaptable autonomous robotic systems for open-ended, real-world tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop autonomous robots capable of understanding, navigating, and manipulating objects in complex, unknown real-world environments by following natural language instructions. This overarching goal defines the task of Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM).

The authors identify two key challenges that motivate the design and development of COME-robot:

1. **Perceiving and Understanding Unstructured 3D Environments with Natural Language Alignment:** Robots require the ability to effectively structure and ground their understanding of complex, unstructured 3D environments. This understanding must then be aligned with natural language instructions to facilitate task-oriented planning and reasoning. The motivation stems from the limitation of existing methods that struggle to effectively interpret human instructions and identify target objects in diverse, real-world scenes, especially those with partial observability. This necessitates a system that can explore and comprehend the environment at various granularities, from identifying large furniture to discerning fine-grained object attributes.

2. **Robust Closed-Loop Replanning for Failure Recovery in Long-Horizon Tasks:** Successful execution of long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks demands robust systems that can effectively diagnose failures, propose corrections, and devise new plans based on continuous environmental feedback. Existing LLM/VLM-based approaches are often limited to tabletop scenarios, where environmental understanding is less complex and execution failures are often less pronounced or addressed through simple re-attempts. The motivation here is to overcome these limitations by developing a mechanism that not only detects failures but also traces their causes across different interdependent modules (e.g., navigation impacting manipulation feasibility) and efficiently recovers to ensure task completion, rather than simply restarting or failing.

To address these challenges, the authors propose COME-robot with two key innovations: (i) a multi-level open-vocabulary perception and situated reasoning module to enable effective exploration and target object identification using commonsense knowledge and situated information, and (ii) an iterative closed-loop feedback and restoration mechanism designed to verify task feasibility, monitor execution success, and trace failure causes for robust recovery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

COME-robot is designed as a closed-loop robotic system that integrates GPT-4V with robust robotic primitive actions to perform open-vocabulary mobile manipulation (OVMM) in real-world environments. The methodology is structured around two primary modules: Multi-level Open-Vocabulary Perception and Situated Reasoning, and Iterative Closed-Loop Feedback and Restoration.

#### 3.1. System Overview

Given a natural language task instruction, COME-robot utilizes GPT-4V for reasoning to generate a code-based plan. This plan is executed by the robot, and environmental feedback is continuously monitored. Through this feedback, the system performs closed-loop replanning, iteratively updating the plan or recovering from failures to ultimately achieve the given task.

#### 3.2. Multi-level Open-Vocabulary Perception and Situated Reasoning

This module is responsible for interpreting human instructions, exploring the 3D environment, organizing environmental information, and identifying target objects.

#### 3.2.1. Environment Explorer

The environment exploration is organized into three hierarchical levels to manage the complexity of large scenes:
- **Global-level perception:** Upon entering an unknown environment, the robot employs its mobile base with a frontier-based exploration strategy. It scans the environment to construct a global object map, which includes large furniture items. For each detected object, its category, location, merged point cloud, visual features, and language description are recorded.
- **Local-level perception:** After the global map is built, COME-robot leverages GPT-4V's commonsense reasoning to identify potential target regions relevant to the human task instructions (e.g., a cup is likely on a table). The robot then navigates to these identified regions and constructs a local object placement map of smaller objects located on larger receptacle objects. SAM-2 is employed for tracking object status, and local-level perception is re-executed during task if object positions change.
- **Object-level perception:** At this stage, COME-robot captures close-up images of potential target objects within a specific area. GPT-4V is then used to extract fine-grained object attributes, facilitating further reasoning and precise goal identification.

#### 3.2.2. Goal Reasoner

Following environmental exploration based on the natural language instruction, the Goal Reasoner identifies the specific target object. This process involves GPT-4V applying commonsense reasoning about object functionality and availability. For example, if multiple cups are observed, GPT-4V might infer that an empty cup is more suitable for a task requiring pouring water and then generate a function call to grasp that specific cup.

#### 3.3. Closed-Loop Feedback and Restoration

This module ensures task progress and completion by managing task planning and robot execution failures through a hierarchical recovery mechanism.

#### 3.3.1. Status Verifier

The status verifier has two components:
- **Feasibility verification:** This assesses whether the current task can proceed or if a rollback to a previous state is necessary. It uses perceptual feedback from the multi-level environment explorer and the VLM's dialogue context. An example is when no empty cup is found, prompting the robot to search another table.
- **Success verification:** This component evaluates the success of the robot's execution steps. It utilizes multi-modal feedback, including wrist camera images captured before and after an action, and feedback from the invoked action function calls. It also analyzes the specific cause of failure to determine the appropriate recovery level (e.g., plan modification or execution adjustment).

#### 3.3.2. Plan Restorer

To recover from unfeasible situations or various execution and perception failures, a hierarchical recovery mechanism is employed, mirroring the structure of the environment explorer:
- **Object-level recovery:** When a manipulation failure occurs (e.g., grasp failure), the robot first attempts recovery at the object level by re-attempting the action, possibly with an adjusted grasping pose. If the object has moved during failed attempts, the status verifier detects this via image comparison, leading to a rollback to a higher recovery level.
- **Local-level recovery:** If object-level recovery is insufficient (e.g., objects move during the task beyond simple re-attempt), the local-level mechanism is activated. It updates the local object map, reclassifies or relocates the target object, and then executes a new action.
- **Global-level recovery:** When the current plan becomes unfeasible or the robot cannot effectively interact with the target object from its current position (as verified by the status verifier), global-level recovery is initiated. The robot navigates to a new target position, searching for alternative furniture or a better vantage point to reattempt the task under improved conditions.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Prompt Design:** GPT-4V is prompted for both planning and failure recovery. The system prompt includes a task description, feedback handling instructions, details about the robot's hardware and available skills library, response guidelines (requiring JSON format with "reason" and "code" fields), and useful operational tips.
- **Primitive Actions as APIs:** A library of primitive actions is provided to COME-robot as APIs, categorized into:
 - **Perception APIs:** `explore_global()`, `explore_local()`, and `report_observation()`.
 - **Execution APIs:** `navigate()`, `grasp()`, and `place()`.
- **Real-robot Setup:** The physical robot system consists of a four-wheeled differential drive mobile base equipped with an RGB-D camera, Lidar, and an IMU unit. It also features a 7-DoF Kinova Gen3 robot arm fitted with a parallel gripper and an RGB-D camera on its wrist.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated COME-robot through comprehensive experiments in a real-world setting, comparing its performance against a state-of-the-art baseline.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Baseline:** The baseline chosen was Code as Policies (CaP), a method utilizing LLMs to generate code for complex robotic tasks. To provide a more robust comparison, CaP was augmented with the exploration and navigation API functions designed for COME-robot, resulting in a stronger baseline referred to as CaP*.
- **Task Design:** Eight challenging tasks were designed across two categories to evaluate various robotic capabilities:
 - **Mobile Manipulation Tasks (4 tasks, 5 trials each):** Conducted in a real-world room with diverse furniture.
 - **A1 MOVE TOY:** Basic mobile manipulation (move toy from table to bed).
 - **A2 TRANSFER ALL TOYS:** Object search and mobile manipulation (move scattered toys from different tables to sofa).
 - **A3 MOVE CUP AND TOY:** Sequential tasks and visual reasoning (find specific cup, place on plate, move toy to bed).
 - **A4 GATHER CUPS:** Efficient planning and object gathering (collect cups from multiple tables to a single, unspecified table).
 - **Tabletop Manipulation Tasks (4 tasks, 10 trials each):** Aligned with the CaP* setup for focused validation.
 - **B1 PLACE FRUIT:** Basic execution and instruction-following (put fruits onto a plate).
 - **B2 FRUIT AMONG CUPS:** Understanding spatial concepts (place fruit in the middle of cups).
 - **B3 PREPARE CUP:** Commonsense reasoning for object identification (pick clean, unused cup for pouring water).
 - **B4 TIDY TABLE:** Concept understanding and free-form instruction following (place objects into corresponding storage boxes).
 - Scene configurations were varied between trials for objects' types, arrangements, and quantities.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Success Rate (SR):** The proportion of tasks successfully completed.
 - **Step-wise Success Rate (SSR):** The number of successful execution API calls divided by the total number of execution API calls. This excludes perception APIs.
 - **Recovery Rate (RR):** The ratio of successful replanned executions to the total number of replanned executions, specifically to quantify COME-robot's failure recovery ability.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

COME-robot demonstrated consistent and statistically significant improvements across all experimental settings:
- **Overall Performance:**
 - **Mobile Manipulation:** COME-robot achieved an overall SR of 65% (13 out of 20 tasks), outperforming CaP* which achieved 30% (6 out of 20 tasks). This represents a 35% improvement. The SSR for COME-robot was 101/122 compared to CaP*'s 76/125.
 - **Tabletop Manipulation:** COME-robot achieved an SR of 75% (30 out of 40 tasks), significantly outperforming CaP* at 47.5% (19 out of 40 tasks), a 27.5% improvement. The SSR for COME-robot was 123/140 compared to CaP*'s 98/138.
- **Impact of Failure Recovery:** The higher SSR values for COME-robot indicate its ability to identify and correct errors during task execution through its replanning mechanism. This capability contributed directly to the higher overall success rates in both mobile and tabletop settings.

#### 4.3. Failure Analysis

The paper provides a detailed analysis of failure cases encountered by COME-robot, categorized by type, and highlights its recovery mechanisms:
- **Perceptual Failures:**
 - **Causes:** Primarily false positives (wrong detection) and missed detections during environment exploration.
 - **Recovery:** COME-robot used object-level perception for closer inspection and GPT-4V for visual feedback verification. For missed detections, rebuilding the local object scene graph led to 100% recovery. For wrong detections, while verification helped filter some errors, 6 instances of false detection persisted after verification, with 3 of these ultimately leading to task failure.
- **Execution Failures:**
 - **Causes:** Incorrect plans or invalid API calls generated by GPT-4V (e.g., attempting to place an object without grasping it, or using an object name instead of an object for navigation). Another primary cause was unsuccessful grasps, often due to the robot navigating to an impractical position that constrained its manipulation space (e.g., a table corner).
 - **Recovery:** The system verified generated plans and code, triggering exceptions that provided explicit feedback to GPT-4V, enabling it to rectify the plan. For grasp failures, the robot adjusted its grasping pose or, if necessary, navigated to a closer location to improve access. The recovery rate for grasp failures was 17/23, indicating a high success in overcoming these common issues.

#### 4.4. Discussions

- **Role of Commonsense Reasoning and Interactive Exploration:** The authors discuss that while LLMs provide crucial commonsense knowledge, interactive exploration and dynamic scene information updates are equally vital for mobile manipulation. For instance, in the "PREPARE CUP" task, understanding the state of multiple detected cups (e.g., "empty," "used") is necessary for identifying the most suitable target, which cannot be achieved by mere instruction interpretation without environmental interaction.
- **Importance of Closed-Loop Replanning:** Mobile manipulation tasks involve significantly longer action sequences compared to tabletop tasks, increasing the probability of execution failures. The closed-loop replanning mechanism in COME-robot enables the detection and recovery from these failures, thereby reducing overall task failure rates and validating its importance for complex, long-horizon robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces COME-robot, which represents an advancement in autonomous mobile manipulation by effectively integrating a large vision-language foundation model, GPT-4V, within a closed-loop system for real-world scenarios.

The significance of this work lies in several key contributions:
- **Enhanced Open-Ended Reasoning:** COME-robot demonstrates an improved capacity to interpret and act upon open-ended, natural language instructions in complex, unstructured environments. This is achieved through its multi-level perception and situated reasoning module, which grounds high-level human goals into actionable robotic plans by systematically exploring and understanding the 3D world.
- **Robustness through Closed-Loop Feedback:** The system's iterative closed-loop feedback and restoration mechanism is a critical development for robust robotic operation. It enables the robot to autonomously verify task feasibility, monitor execution success, and, crucially, diagnose and recover from various types of perception and execution failures by tracing their causes across different operational modules. This hierarchical recovery mechanism moves beyond simple re-attempts, addressing interdependencies between robot actions like navigation and manipulation.
- **Empirical Performance Improvement:** Through comprehensive real-robot experiments across 8 challenging mobile and tabletop manipulation tasks, COME-robot achieved significant improvements in task success rate (approximately 35% higher than state-of-the-art baselines). This quantitative evidence validates the effectiveness of its design in facilitating failure recovery, free-form instruction following, and long-horizon task planning.

The potential impact of COME-robot extends to making autonomous robots more reliable and versatile in real-world applications. By demonstrating how a powerful VLM like GPT-4V can be effectively harnessed for complex physical tasks, this research paves the way for:
- **More General-Purpose Robots:** Enabling robots to operate in dynamic, unknown environments with less human intervention, performing a wider array of tasks specified through natural language.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** Facilitating more intuitive and adaptable interaction paradigms where robots can reason about nuanced instructions and recover from unexpected situations encountered in everyday settings.
- **Further Research Directions:** The work inspires continued exploration into integrating advanced foundation models with robotic systems to enhance intelligence, autonomy, and resilience in complex, real-world scenarios, particularly focusing on how perception, reasoning, and action can be seamlessly interwoven.
