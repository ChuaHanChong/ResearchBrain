# Deep Whole-body Parkour

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a unified deep reinforcement learning framework, Deep Whole-body Parkour, allowing humanoid robots to execute dynamic, multi-contact parkour maneuvers by integrating depth perception with whole-body motion tracking. The system achieved robust adaptation to varying environmental conditions and initial robot positions, demonstrated through successful deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot performing skills such as vaulting and climbing.

## Problem

- Perceptive locomotion methods for legged robots were limited to pedal gaits, restricting whole-body multi-contact interactions with environments.
- General motion tracking approaches, while capable of reproducing complex whole-body skills, lacked environment awareness and could not adapt to real-world geometry or variations.
- A gap existed in enabling humanoids to perform dynamic, contact-rich maneuvers on unstructured terrain while perceiving and adapting to the environment's geometry.

## Method

- A deep reinforcement learning framework was developed that integrates exteroceptive depth perception directly into a whole-body motion tracking policy.
- A custom dataset of human parkour motions on physical obstacles was curated and retargeted to the robot, combined with high-fidelity digitized environments.
- Training utilized a massively parallel grouped ray-caster for efficient simulation and an adaptive sampling strategy with stuck detection for robust curriculum learning.

## Results

- Successfully deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating robust execution of complex maneuvers like 'kneel climb' and 'dive roll' in diverse real-world environments without external odometry.
- Policies trained with depth vision achieved a 100% success rate within an aggressive 1.2m x 1.2m out-of-distribution starting position range, significantly outperforming vision-less baselines.
- The system exhibited robustness to unseen distractors in simulation, maintaining high success rates even when novel objects were introduced into the scene.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating depth vision provides a crucial non-trivial benefit, enabling policies to correct for significant deviations in initial robot position and adapt to various environmental conditions.
> - A novel 'relative frame' reward design allows for global adaptation to obstacles while maintaining local skill fidelity, essential for complex, interaction-intensive motions.
> - Robust simulation-to-real-world transfer for depth perception can be achieved by intentionally adding Gaussian noise and patched artifacts to simulated images, mirroring real-world camera issues.
