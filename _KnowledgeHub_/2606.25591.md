# WOLF-VLA: Whole-Body Humanoid Optimal Locomotion Framework for Vision-Language-Action Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> WOLF-VLA is a framework that unifies optimal control (OC) motion synthesis with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) learning for whole-body humanoid locomotion. This approach generates a large-scale, dynamically consistent dataset from OC, which enables a VLA model to achieve high success rates and replicate optimal joint kinematics across diverse, complex locomotion tasks, showing robustness to environmental variations.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have limited applicability to whole-body, highly dynamic, and multi-contact humanoid locomotion tasks.
- A scarcity of large-scale, high-quality training data for humanoid VLA, as existing data collection methods often lack optimality or dynamic consistency.
- Difficulty in encoding optimality criteria (e.g., energy efficiency) and guaranteeing physical consistency and safety in learning-based policies for complex legged robots.

## Method

- Introduces **WOLF-VLA**, a unified framework combining ==optimal control (OC)== for motion synthesis with a VLA policy for learning humanoid locomotion.
- Generates the "WOLF-VLA-dataset," a large-scale collection of dynamically consistent and optimal whole-body humanoid trajectories across diverse tasks and randomized environments, using OC problem resolution.
- Trains a transformer-based VLA model, pre-trained with GR00T-N1.5-3B, using a flow-matching objective to learn locomotion policies from multi-modal inputs.

## Results

- The WOLF-VLA model achieved high success rates, with near-perfect performance (99-**100%**) for tasks like 'Walk Forward', and maintained low Range of Motion (ROM) errors across successful executions.
- The policy exhibited stable performance across varying task horizon lengths and demonstrated robustness to visual distractors in nominal environments.
- WOLF-VLA significantly outperformed baseline VLA methods (ACT and π0.5), which showed negligible success rates (**1.4%** and **0%** respectively), validating the approach's effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal Control (OC) is an effective method for generating large-scale, dynamically consistent, and optimal datasets essential for training VLA models for complex humanoid locomotion.
> - Ego-centric visual observations are a critical input for VLA models to understand environmental structure and achieve task success in humanoid locomotion.
> - VLA models, when trained on high-quality OC-derived data, can learn to generate robust and physically plausible whole-body humanoid locomotion policies, extending their capabilities beyond manipulation tasks.
