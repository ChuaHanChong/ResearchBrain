# WOLF-VLA: Whole-Body Humanoid Optimal Locomotion Framework for Vision-Language-Action Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> WOLF-VLA is a framework that unifies optimal control (OC) motion synthesis with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) learning for whole-body humanoid locomotion. This approach generates a large-scale, dynamically consistent dataset from OC, which enables a VLA model to achieve high success rates and replicate optimal joint kinematics across diverse, complex locomotion tasks, showing robustness to environmental variations.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have limited applicability to whole-body, highly dynamic, and multi-contact humanoid locomotion tasks.
- A scarcity of large-scale, high-quality training data for humanoid VLA, as existing data collection methods often lack optimality or dynamic consistency.
- Difficulty in encoding optimality criteria (e.g., energy efficiency) and guaranteeing physical consistency and safety in learning-based policies for complex legged robots.

## Method

- Introduces **WOLF-VLA**, a unified framework combining ==optimal control (OC)== for motion synthesis with a VLA policy for learning humanoid locomotion.
- Generates the "WOLF-VLA-dataset," a large-scale collection of dynamically consistent and optimal whole-body humanoid trajectories across diverse tasks and randomized environments, using OC problem resolution.
- Trains a transformer-based VLA model, pre-trained with GR00T-N1.5-3B, using a flow-matching objective to learn locomotion policies from multi-modal inputs.

## Results

- The WOLF-VLA model achieved high success rates, with near-perfect performance (99-**100%**) for tasks like 'Walk Forward', and maintained low Range of Motion (ROM) errors across successful executions.
- The policy exhibited stable performance across varying task horizon lengths and demonstrated robustness to visual distractors in nominal environments.
- WOLF-VLA significantly outperformed baseline VLA methods (ACT and π0.5), which showed negligible success rates (**1.4%** and **0%** respectively), validating the approach's effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal Control (OC) is an effective method for generating large-scale, dynamically consistent, and optimal datasets essential for training VLA models for complex humanoid locomotion.
> - Ego-centric visual observations are a critical input for VLA models to understand environmental structure and achieve task success in humanoid locomotion.
> - VLA models, when trained on high-quality OC-derived data, can learn to generate robust and physically plausible whole-body humanoid locomotion policies, extending their capabilities beyond manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot motion generation and control has historically relied on Optimal Control (OC) and Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods, particularly for complex legged robots such as humanoids and quadrupeds. This traditional approach focuses on achieving stable and reliable deployment by mathematically enforcing dynamic consistency, optimality criteria (e.g., energy efficiency, minimal joint motion), and safety guarantees.

In parallel, a distinct line of research has leveraged learning-based paradigms, including Reinforcement Learning (RL), Imitation Learning (IL), and deep learning. Recent advancements in large-scale machine learning, primarily from text and image domains, have led to the emergence of multi-modal machine learning models in robotics, specifically Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, supported by modern GPU computation and large-scale training strategies, have demonstrated capabilities in real-time robotic manipulation, exhibiting generalization and adaptability. Initial successes, such as those from the Physical Intelligence group, have paved the way for more interactive and adaptive robots that integrate multi-modal perception with versatile control policies. VLA models offer advantages such as heightened reactivity, adaptability, and the capacity for rich interaction in dynamic environments, and their model-free nature can facilitate transfer across robotic platforms.

Despite these advancements, existing VLA applications in robotics have been primarily demonstrated on relatively simple robotic platforms, often fixed-base manipulators or systems with low kinematic complexity. Their applicability to whole-body, highly dynamic, multi-contact systems like humanoid locomotion remains largely unexplored due to several limitations:
- **Data Scarcity:** Collecting large-scale, high-quality data for whole-body humanoid behaviors on physical robots is costly, time-consuming, and resource-intensive.
- **Lack of Dynamic Consistency and Optimality:** Data collected through teleoperation or motion capture often lacks the inherent optimality (e.g., energy efficiency, smooth motion) and dynamic consistency that classical model-based OC methods naturally enforce.
- **Safety Guarantees:** Model-free, data-driven methods do not inherently guarantee safety with respect to joint limits or interaction forces, a critical concern for highly dynamic legged robots. Additional control modules are typically required for sim-to-real transfer to ensure safety.
- **Absence of Standardized Benchmarks:** Existing multi-modal datasets for whole-body humanoid control often lack locomotion-rich demonstrations, comprehensive multi-modal annotations, and standardized benchmarks for diverse behaviors. While some synthetic datasets and benchmarks exist (e.g., LeVERB, Locomujoco, HumanoidBench), they may lack multi-modal alignment or focus on specific aspects rather than comprehensive VLA training.

This work fits into the landscape by directly addressing these identified gaps. It proposes a unified framework, WOLF-VLA, that bridges the gap between the rigorousness of optimal control for motion synthesis and the generalization capabilities of VLA models. By generating a large-scale dataset of dynamically consistent, optimal humanoid trajectories and using it to train a VLA model, the authors aim to establish a reproducible benchmark for whole-body humanoid locomotion within VLA, enabling scalable transfer of instruction-driven policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the limitations observed in applying Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to complex, whole-body humanoid locomotion. While VLA models have shown promise in robotic manipulation, their integration with humanoids faces significant hurdles. The authors identify several specific challenges:

- **Underexplored VLA for Whole-Body Humanoid Locomotion:** The application of VLA models to whole-body, contact-rich humanoid locomotion is a nascent area. Most prior VLA research focused on simpler robotic platforms or less dynamic tasks, leaving the potential for complex locomotion largely untapped.
- **Data Scarcity and Quality:** Training effective VLA models requires extensive, high-quality datasets. For humanoids, collecting real-world demonstration data through teleoperation or motion capture is expensive, time-consuming, and often results in motions that lack inherent optimality or dynamic consistency. Such data may not encode energy efficiency, minimal joint motion, or other crucial optimality criteria that classical model-based control methods naturally provide.
- **Absence of Dynamically Consistent and Optimal Demonstrations:** Learning-based pipelines typically rely on observed behaviors, which may not be physically optimal or dynamically consistent. This can lead to learned policies that are inefficient, unstable, or unsafe, especially for highly dynamic legged robots where joint limits, velocity limits, and contact forces must be precisely managed.
- **Difficulty in Encoding Optimality and Safety:** Incorporating optimality and safety guarantees directly into learning-based pipelines for complex systems remains a significant challenge. Traditional VLA training does not inherently provide the robustness and constraint satisfaction typically achieved by optimal control.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

- **Develop a Unified Framework (WOLF-VLA):** To integrate whole-body optimal control motion synthesis with large-scale multi-modal datasets, thereby enabling the training of VLA models capable of generating humanoid locomotion policies directly from natural-language instructions.
- **Create a Large-Scale, High-Quality Dataset (WOLF-VLA-dataset):** To construct a comprehensive dataset of dynamically feasible humanoid trajectories. This dataset is designed to be inherently optimal, smooth, feasible, and physically consistent, suitable for high-quality policy learning. It covers six locomotion-related task families (e.g., forward walking, stair climbing, side gait) with extensive parameterization of environmental variations, object properties, and visual distractors.
- **Establish a New Benchmark:** To provide a standardized and reproducible benchmark for whole-body humanoid locomotion within the VLA paradigm. This benchmark aims to facilitate the evaluation of challenging tasks and enable future research.
- **Train and Evaluate a VLA Model (WOLF-VLA-model):** To fine-tune and evaluate a VLA model baseline on the proposed benchmark, leveraging the collected joint trajectories, ego-centric visual observations, and natural language instructions. The objective is to demonstrate robust reasoning and competitive performance across various tasks and environmental settings.
- **Conduct Systematic Ablation Studies:** To systematically assess the impact of each modality (vision, language, proprioception, spatial cues) on the model's performance and quantify its robustness to injected perturbations, thereby understanding the stability and reliability of the learned policy.
- **Openly Release Resources:** To make the full dataset, model checkpoints, and benchmarking simulation suite openly available, fostering reproducible research and advancing the field of instruction-driven locomotion policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WOLF-VLA framework integrates optimal control for data generation with a transformer-based VLA model for policy learning. The methodology is structured around creating a high-quality dataset and subsequently training a VLA model on this data.

**A. Synthetic Dataset Creation (WOLF-VLA-dataset)**
To overcome data scarcity and quality issues, the authors developed a scalable demonstration generation pipeline based on Optimal Control (OC).
- **Motion Trajectory Generation:** Motion trajectories are generated by solving Optimal Control Problems (OCPs). This formulation inherently produces dynamically consistent behaviors by minimizing joint torques, rotations, and velocities, while ensuring smooth contact transitions and strict satisfaction of joint constraints. This approach yields physically consistent and optimal demonstrations.
- **Task Families:** The dataset covers six distinct locomotion skills:
 - Target-directed locomotion (forward walking).
 - Lateral locomotion (left and right side-walking).
 - Stair interaction (ascending a three-step staircase).
 - Compound stair behaviors (ascending and descending).
 - 180-degree turning motions.
 - Variable-height squatting.
- **Environmental Variations:** To ensure diversity and generalizability, the environment settings are randomized along three dimensions:
 - **Target Types:** Including boxes, cylinders, spheres, ground markers, single staircases (three steps), and double staircases (three steps up and down).
 - **Color Variants:** Six distinct colors applied to all target types.
 - **Spatial Configuration:** Systematic variation of target placements along X and Y axes, resulting in approximately 40x40 unique positions.
 - **Distractors:** Randomly placed non-target objects to increase visual complexity and diversity.
- **Data Collection:** Trajectories are executed in the MuJoCo physics simulator. Time-synchronized ego-centric visual observations (224x224 RGB frames captured at 33.33 Hz) are recorded. Concurrently, natural language (NL) instructions describing each task are automatically generated. Each dataset sample comprises RGB observations, the corresponding OC trajectory, and a task-level NL instruction. The dataset totals 277 hours of motion across 15,276 episodes.

**B. Action Skills Expressed as an OCP**
- **Robot Dynamics:** The robot dynamics are modeled as a multi-body system subject to contact constraints, using the Euler–Lagrange equations of motion. This formulation explicitly accounts for inertia, Coriolis, centrifugal, gravity effects, joint torques, and contact forces. Contacts are modeled as second-order kinematic constraints.
- **OCP Formulation:** Humanoid locomotion is formulated as a multi-phase OCP, where each phase corresponds to a specific contact configuration. The objective is to minimize a sum of cost functions over these phases, subject to the contact-constrained dynamics and admissible joint limits (position, velocity, torque).
- **OCP Solver:** The multi-phase OCP is solved using a multiple shooting formulation with Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP), specifically the Box-FDDP variant within the Crocoddyl framework, which leverages Pinocchio for efficient dynamics and derivatives and enforces torque limits.
- **Cost Function Components:** The cost function for each motion phase includes:
 - **CoM Cost:** To track a target Center of Mass (CoM) position.
 - **Feet Cost:** To track the target stepping foot position.
 - **Torque Minimization:** To ensure dynamic feasibility and energy efficiency.
 - **Posture Regularization:** To handle robot redundancy by regularizing joint configurations towards a default posture.
- **Validation:** Each generated motion undergoes manual validation to ensure success and minimize numerical errors, preserving dataset quality.

**C. Virtual Environment and Observation/Action Space**
- **Simulation Environment:** The Gymnasium simulation framework is used.
- **Robot Platform:** The RH5 humanoid robot model, featuring a free-flyer base and 25 actuated joints, is used for all demonstrations and evaluations.
- **Multi-modal Observation Space:**
 - **Proprioceptive State:** Includes the full humanoid kinematic configuration (32 joint rotations, 31 velocities, and floating base pose).
 - **Visual Information:** Ego-centric RGB images (224x224) from a virtual camera attached to the humanoid's head, with a 120-degree field of view.
 - **Natural Language Instruction:** A task-level instruction.
- **Action Space:** Defined as a delta joint rotation command (incremental updates to actuated joint rotations at each control step).

**D. Natural Language Instruction Generation**
- **Automated Generation:** Task instructions are automatically generated in parallel with OCP resolution, using metadata from each OCP formulation.
- **Structured Tags:** Instructions are augmented with spatial tags, such as `<DIST>` for target distance and `<HEIGHT>` for stepping height, to explicitly guide the Language Model (LLM) component during VLA training.

**E. VLA Policy Learning**
- **Model Architecture:** A transformer-based VLA model is trained, comprising:
 - **Visual Encoder (E_vis):** Maps frame sequences to spatio-temporal tokens.
 - **Language Encoder (E_lang):** Embeds the natural language task instruction.
 - **Action Decoder (D_act):** Auto-regressively predicts control actions.
- **Training Strategy:** Action prediction is formulated as next-token auto-regressive modeling over continuous action representations. The training leverages a flow-matching objective, where the model learns a conditional vector field that transports samples from a Gaussian prior towards the expert action data distribution, conditioned on observations. Specifically, a noisy interpolation `a_τ = τa + (1-τ)ϵ` is used, and the model `v_θ(a_τ|o)` is trained to predict the flow direction `a - ϵ`.
- **Inference:** During inference, action chunks are generated through K denoising steps using forward Euler integration, starting from an initial latent action sampled from a Gaussian prior.
- **Implementation Details:** The main model is initialized from the pretrained GR00T-N1.5-3B VLA policy. The LLM and vision backbone encoders are frozen, while the action diffusion model and projector layers are optimized. Training is performed on 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs for 200,000 gradient steps with an effective batch size of 128. A cosine decay learning-rate schedule with AdamW optimizer is employed. The dataset is formatted in LeRobot standard.

**F. Baselines and Ablation Settings**
- **Baselines:** The approach is compared against π_0.5 and ACT, trained with the same observation and action representation spaces.
- **Ablation Studies:** To assess modality contributions, configurations include:
 - **GROOT-N1:** Full model (vision, proprioception, NL with spatial cues).
 - **w.o spatial information:** Spatial tags removed from NL instructions.
 - **w.o language:** NL inputs removed.
 - **Instruction paraphrasing:** Using GPT-4o paraphrased instructions during evaluation.
 - **w.o vision:** Visual observations removed.

**G. Metrics and Evaluation Protocol**
- **Evaluation Protocol:** All configurations are evaluated with 20 rollouts per task under nominal and disturbed environments, across short (S), medium (M), and long (L) horizon lengths. Combinations of target objects and colors not seen in training are used in evaluation.
- **Success Rate:** Binary metric (task completion within horizon) for most tasks.
- **Soft Success Rate (SSR):** For stair-climbing tasks, it quantifies partial completion based on the number of reached intermediate stairs.
- **ROM Error Rate (∆ROM):** Measures the normalized difference between the mean actual and reference (OC-generated) Range of Motion (ROM) for hip, knee, and ankle joints, indicating motion fidelity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WOLF-VLA and its ablations, along with comparisons to baseline models, provided several key findings regarding whole-body humanoid locomotion:

**A. Dataset Statistics and Content**
The WOLF-VLA dataset comprises 277 hours of humanoid motion, consisting of 15,276 episodes across six task families. These tasks exhibit extensive parametric variations in environmental settings, object appearances, spatial configurations, and visual distractors, providing a rich multi-modal training resource.

**B. Motion Fidelity and Locomotion Quality**
- **Low ROM Errors:** The proposed WOLF-VLA model consistently exhibited low ∆ROM errors across successful executions (Table II). This indicates that the learned policy reproduces the joint trajectories from the Optimal Control (OC) demonstrations with high fidelity. This suggests that the model not only learns to complete tasks but also captures the underlying structured, efficient, and physically plausible locomotion patterns inherent in the OC-generated data.

**C. Long-Horizon Task Performance**
- **Stable Performance:** The model maintained stable performance even as horizon lengths increased (Short, Medium, Long). For instance, the "Walk Forward" (WF) task achieved near-perfect success rates (close to 100%) across all horizons, demonstrating the policy's reliability in sustaining locomotion over extended periods. More complex tasks, like stair climbing, while having lower overall success rates, remained solvable at longer horizons, indicating effective capture of temporal dependencies required for sustained control.

**D. Robustness to Visual Distractors**
- **Resilience:** The learned policy demonstrated robustness to visual distractors. For simpler tasks such as WF, performance remained largely unaffected. For more complex tasks like "Walk Around" (WA) or stair climbing, distractors introduced moderate decreases in success rates. However, the policy retained the ability to recover in a substantial portion of trials, suggesting generalization beyond the nominal training environments.

**E. Comparison with Baseline Methods**
- **Outperformance:** The WOLF-VLA model significantly outperformed the baseline policies, ACT and π_0.5, across all evaluated tasks (Table III). Both baselines achieved negligible success rates, indicating their limited applicability to complex whole-body humanoid locomotion in this benchmark. This suggests that the combination of the dynamically consistent, OC-generated dataset and the GR00T N1.5 backbone (even without specific humanoid pretraining) is effective for this domain.

**F. Role of Multimodal Inputs (Ablation Study)**
The ablation experiments provided insights into the contribution of each modality (Table IV):
- **Spatial Tags in Language:** Removing spatial tags (`<DIST>`, `<HEIGHT>`) from natural language instructions resulted in only a minor reduction in success rates. This suggests that spatial cues primarily provide contextual guidance rather than being strictly essential for task execution, with the visual and proprioceptive inputs contributing substantially to spatial understanding.
- **Language Input:** Removing language entirely led to a moderate decrease in performance for complex tasks. This indicates that language provides useful high-level task information and contributes to task grounding, but it is not the sole source of task definition for the policy.
- **Visual Observations:** Removing ego-centric visual observations resulted in a dramatic drop in performance across all tasks. Without vision, the policy struggled even with simple locomotion and failed entirely on tasks requiring environmental awareness (e.g., navigating around objects or climbing stairs). This highlights the critical and indispensable role of vision for interpreting scene structure, perceiving targets, and adapting locomotion behavior in varied environments. The results also indicate that ego-centric visual observations alone provide sufficient environmental context for long-horizon locomotion.

**G. Dataset Scalability and Transfer Potential**
The OC-based dataset generation pipeline offers scalability and reusability. New locomotion behaviors can be synthesized by modifying task objectives or environment configurations. Visual diversity can be enhanced through varied layouts or photorealistic rendering. The embodiment-agnostic design of the visual observations and natural language instructions suggests that the dataset can be reused to train policies for different humanoid robots by retargeting OC-generated trajectories to match the target platform's kinematic and dynamic constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of humanoid robotics and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) learning in several significant ways, with potential impacts on future development and deployment:

- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations in VLA for Humanoids:** The work directly addresses critical challenges in applying VLA models to whole-body, dynamic, and multi-contact humanoid locomotion. By tackling data scarcity, the absence of dynamically consistent demonstrations, and the difficulty of encoding optimality and safety, it provides a viable path for training robust humanoid control policies using VLA.
- **Establishment of a Reproducible Benchmark:** The introduction of WOLF-VLA as a unified framework, dataset, and benchmarking suite provides a standardized and reproducible platform. This benchmark, with its diverse tasks, environmental variations, and systematic ablation studies, offers a valuable resource for evaluating and comparing future VLA-based humanoid control methods. This will accelerate research in instruction-driven locomotion policies.
- **Novel Data Generation Methodology:** The use of Optimal Control Problems (OCP) for generating a large-scale dataset of humanoid locomotion trajectories is a key methodological contribution. This approach ensures that the training data is inherently optimal, dynamically consistent, smooth, and physically feasible. This contrasts with traditional data collection methods (teleoperation, MoCap) which often lack these qualities, thereby improving the foundation for learning high-quality behaviors. The scalability of OC-based generation allows for rapid expansion of the dataset's diversity.
- **Advancing VLA Capabilities for Complex Robotics:** The successful training of a VLA model that exhibits strong reasoning, robustness to initial condition variability, and competitive performance across challenging tasks demonstrates the potential of VLA for complex, whole-body control. It shows that by coupling rigorous motion synthesis with advanced learning architectures, humanoids can interpret natural language instructions and adapt their locomotion behavior based on visual cues.
- **Insights into Multimodal Input Importance:** The systematic ablation studies offer crucial insights into the contributions of different modalities (vision, language, proprioception). The finding that ego-centric visual observations are critical for environmental interpretation and adaptive locomotion, while language provides important but less direct guidance, helps inform the design of future VLA architectures for robotics.
- **Enabling Broader Robot Deployment:** By enhancing humanoids with the ability to perform complex locomotion tasks based on natural language commands and visual perception, this research contributes to making robots more interactive and adaptive. This capability could facilitate the deployment of humanoids not only in industrial settings but also in unstructured, everyday environments, leading to new applications in service robotics, assistance, and exploration.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework and findings lay a foundation for several future research directions, including expanding the dataset with more complex, long-horizon multi-task capabilities, integrating dynamic whole-body manipulation, and, eventually, enabling safe and reliable real-world deployment on physical humanoid robots. The embodiment-agnostic nature of the dataset further supports transfer learning across different humanoid platforms.
