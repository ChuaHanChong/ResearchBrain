# TTRL: Test-Time Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> TTRL is a framework enabling Large Language Models to self-improve through Reinforcement Learning on unlabeled test data, using self-generated rewards derived from majority voting. This approach substantially boosts performance on complex reasoning tasks, with one model achieving a 211% improvement on AIME 2024, by fostering a self-reinforcing learning loop.

## Problem

- Applying Reinforcement Learning to Large Language Models for reasoning tasks is bottlenecked by the high cost and scarcity of human-annotated ground-truth labels for reward estimation.
- Existing RL methods for LLMs heavily rely on supervised reward signals, making continuous self-improvement difficult on newly encountered or unlabeled test data.
- The inability to obtain explicit reward signals at inference time limits the ability of LLMs to adapt and enhance generalization to new distributions without human intervention.

## Method

- Introduces ==Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL)==, a framework that enables LLMs to update their parameters and enhance generalization at test time using RL.
- Generates reward signals without explicit ground-truth labels by using repeated sampling of model outputs and deriving a =='consensus output' via majority voting==.
- Calculates ==binary rule-based rewards== for individual sampled outputs based on their match with the majority-voted pseudo-label, which are then used to optimize the model's policy via an RL algorithm like GRPO.

## Results

- TTRL consistently improved performance across various LLMs and reasoning benchmarks; for instance, Qwen-2.5-Math-7B achieved an approximate **211%** improvement in pass@1 performance on AIME 2024 (from **12.9%** to **40.2%**).
- The method proved effective even on highly capable Large Reasoning Models, yielding approximately **10-point** improvements on models like Skywork-OR1-Math-7B and Qwen3-8B.
- TTRL demonstrated a sustainable self-evolution, consistently surpassing the initial majority accuracy (maj@n) of the model, and closely approached the performance of models trained with ground-truth labels (RL with leakage).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Repeated sampling combined with majority voting can reliably generate pseudo-rewards for test-time reinforcement learning, even without ground-truth labels.
> - Large Language Models can self-evolve by dynamically improving their own self-supervision signals, leading to a self-reinforcing learning loop that surpasses initial accuracy.
> - Test-time adaptation through Reinforcement Learning on unlabeled data yields generalizable improvements in reasoning capabilities, rather than just overfitting to specific test sets.
