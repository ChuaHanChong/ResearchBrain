# Prioritize the Process, Not Just the Outcome: Rewarding Latent Thought Trajectories Improves Reasoning in Looped Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Princeton University introduced RLTT (Reward Latent Thought Trajectories), a reinforcement learning framework designed to improve reasoning in Looped Language Models by distributing rewards across the entire internal thought trajectory. This method yielded performance gains of up to +16.6% on mathematical benchmarks and +18.7% on zero-shot non-mathematical tasks, while also promoting more token-efficient and robust reasoning.

## Problem

- Standard reinforcement learning (RL) objectives, such as GRPO, assign credit only to the final latent state in multi-step internal computation models like Looped Language Models (LoopLMs).
- This credit assignment bottleneck creates a mismatch with LoopLMs' inherent iterative reasoning process, limiting RL's effectiveness in enhancing their reasoning capabilities.
- Prior attempts to improve LoopLM performance using reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards reportedly failed due to this fundamental tension with multi-step internal computation.

## Method

- The authors introduced **RLTT** (==Reward Latent Thought Trajectories==), a reinforcement learning framework that distributes credit across the entire latent thought trajectory.
- **RLTT** modifies the ==policy gradient== formulation to incorporate a weighted sum of log-probabilities from all intermediate loops, using strategies like ==Exit PDF==, ==Progressive==, or ==Uniform weighting==.
- A ==KL-divergence regularization== term is added to the objective to maintain the general language abilities of the policy by comparing its terminal-loop distribution with a frozen reference policy.

## Results

- RLTT consistently and substantially outperformed GRPO across all mathematical reasoning benchmarks, achieving gains up to **+16.6%** on AIME24 and **+10.0%** on BeyondAIME for the **2.6B** model.
- The framework demonstrated positive zero-shot transfer to non-mathematical tasks, with notable gains including **+18.7%** on GPQA and **+3.5%** on MMLU-ST for the **2.6B** model.
- RLTT-trained policies converged to substantially shorter response lengths and exhibited a steeper reduction in terminal-loop next-token distribution entropy, indicating more efficient, confident, and stable latent reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distributing reward across the full latent thought trajectory provides a denser and more informative gradient signal, which is particularly beneficial for complex tasks with sparse final rewards.
> - Trajectory-level credit assignment encourages the model to converge to correct reasoning patterns earlier in its internal computation, leading to more efficient latent reasoning.
> - This approach intrinsically leads to shorter optimal decoding lengths and greater robustness under constrained inference budgets, as the model stabilizes correct solutions faster.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses an evolving area within Large Language Models (LLMs) focusing on latent reasoning. This paradigm emphasizes multi-step internal computations performed by the model prior to generating each output token, distinguishing it from conventional LLM architectures that execute a single forward pass per token. Latent reasoning allows for iterative refinement of internal representations, providing additional test-time computation without increasing the explicit output length, which is a key advantage over verbose methods like Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

Looped Language Models (LoopLMs), such as Ouro, are a specific architectural realization of latent reasoning, employing shared-weight transformer blocks recursively. These models have demonstrated strong reasoning performance by scaling latent computation, reducing the verbosity often associated with explicit reasoning approaches. Other related work includes deep recurrent-depth transformers (e.g., Huginn) and methods exploring continuous or compressed latent thought representations (e.g., Coconut, CODI, CCoT).

Despite the success of LoopLMs in improving reasoning, prior attempts to enhance their performance using reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) have reportedly failed. Standard reinforcement learning objectives, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), typically assign credit only to the final latent state before token emission. The authors identify this as a fundamental mismatch with the multi-step internal computation characteristic of LoopLMs, which perform reasoning over multiple internal refinement steps. This credit assignment bottleneck limits the effectiveness of reinforcement learning in improving LoopLM reasoning capabilities. This research aims to bridge this gap by proposing a reinforcement learning framework that aligns with the multi-step nature of LoopLM computation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reinforcement learning (RL) framework capable of improving the reasoning performance of Looped Language Models (LoopLMs) by addressing a identified credit assignment mismatch. The core motivation stems from the observation that while LoopLMs inherently perform multi-step latent reasoning, standard RL objectives like GRPO only assign credit to the final latent state. This approach implicitly treats the LoopLM's internal computation as a single-step decision process, creating a tension with the model's iterative refinement process.

Specifically, the key objectives are:
1. **Introduce a novel RL framework for LoopLMs:** The goal is to design a framework that resolves the credit assignment bottleneck in existing RL methods when applied to multi-step latent reasoning architectures.
2. **Distribute reward across the full latent reasoning trajectory:** Instead of rewarding only the final latent state, the framework should provide dense, trajectory-level credit assignment, allowing intermediate internal steps to receive meaningful gradients. This is intended to align the learning signal with the model's iterative computation.
3. **Avoid reliance on external verifiers:** The proposed framework should enable trajectory-level credit assignment directly within the LoopLM's internal computation, without requiring external models (like GPT-4.1 nano in some prior work) to decode and score intermediate latent states. This reduces computational and implementation overhead.
4. **Evaluate effectiveness on reasoning benchmarks:** The framework's ability to improve reasoning performance, particularly in challenging mathematical contexts, needs to be empirically demonstrated and compared against existing RL methods under matched conditions.
5. **Assess transferability to non-mathematical tasks:** Investigate whether the improvements in latent reasoning are domain-specific or represent a more general enhancement that generalizes to tasks beyond the training domain.
6. **Analyze underlying mechanisms:** Explore *why* the proposed method works, focusing on its effects on training dynamics (e.g., reward evolution, response length, entropy) and providing theoretical justification for its benefits, such as token efficiency and richer gradient signals.

The motivation is to unlock the full potential of RL for LoopLMs by providing a learning signal that appropriately reflects their internal, iterative reasoning process, thereby leading to more robust and efficient reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce **RLTT (Reward Latent Thought Trajectories)**, a reinforcement learning framework designed as a direct alternative to GRPO for LoopLMs. RLTT's core principle is to distribute credit across the entire latent thought trajectory rather than solely rewarding the final latent state.

#### 3.1. RLTT Policy Gradient Formulation

LoopLMs, during token generation, recursively apply shared-weight transformer blocks for a maximum of $T_{max}$ iterations. For each token $y_j$, intermediate "latent thought distributions" $P^{(t)}_{\theta}(y_j | x, y_{<j})$ are generated for $t = 1, \ldots, T_{max}-1$, alongside the terminal-loop "final thought distribution" $P^{(T_{max})}_{\theta}(y_j | x, y_{<j})$. Standard REINFORCE-style policy gradients (e.g., in GRPO) only use the terminal-loop distribution, $P^{(T_{max})}_{\theta}$, for gradient computation.

RLTT modifies this by incorporating a weighted sum of log-probabilities from all intermediate loops into the policy gradient:
$$ \nabla_{\theta} J_{RLTT PG}(\theta) = E_{x \sim D, \{y_i\}_{i=1}^g \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot|x)} \left[ \frac{1}{g} \sum_{i=1}^g \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{j=1}^{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{T_{max}} \omega_t \nabla_{\theta} \log P^{(t)}_{\theta}(y_{i,j} | x, y_{i,<j}) \hat{A}_i \right] $$
where $\omega_t \ge 0$ are loop weights satisfying $\sum_{t=1}^{T_{max}} \omega_t = 1$, and $\hat{A}_i$ is the advantage computed for rollout $y_i$. This formulation allows the gradient to be a direct function of the latent thought distribution from all loops, preventing the reward signal from exclusively back-propagating through the final thought distribution. This is intended to encourage faster convergence to high-advantage thinking patterns across the entire latent thought trajectory.

#### 3.2. Reference Policy KL Regularization

To maintain the general language abilities of the policy, a KL-divergence regularization term is added to the RLTT objective:
$$ J_{RLTT}(\theta) = J_{RLTT PG}(\theta) + \beta D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta} \| \pi_{ref}) $$
The KL-divergence is calculated between the terminal-loop distributions of the current policy $\pi_{\theta}$ and a frozen reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ (the policy before RLVR post-training).

#### 3.3. Loop Weighting Strategies

RLTT requires the selection of loop weights $\{\omega_t\}_{t=1}^{T_{max}}$. Three strategies are investigated:
- **Exit PDF:** $\omega_t = p_{exit}(t | x)$, where $p_{exit}(t | x)$ is the probability (from Ouro's learned exit head) that computation terminates at loop $t$. This uses the model's internal confidence to credit loops.
- **Progressive:** $\omega_t = \frac{t^{\alpha}}{\sum_{s=1}^{T_{max}} s^{\alpha}}$, with $\alpha \ge 0$. This scheme assigns higher weight to later loops, assuming later refinements are closer to the true next token distribution.
- **Uniform:** $\omega_t = \frac{1}{T_{max}}$. Every loop is considered an equally valid draft, encouraging early convergence to correct next token distributions.

#### 3.4. Computational Considerations

RLTT introduces minimal additional computational cost during the forward pass compared to GRPO, as per-loop logits are already generated. The main overhead is increased memory consumption, which scales linearly with $T_{max}$ due to the need to retain per-loop log-probabilities for gradient computation. This memory increase resulted in a reduction of the maximum number of tokens per GPU per optimization step in the experiments, compensated by additional mini-steps.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Base Architecture:** Ouro-1.4B/2.6B-Thinking, an open-source LoopLM, is used.
- **Comparison Baseline:** GRPO is used as the primary baseline, under strictly compute-matched training conditions (same MATH samples, rollout budgets, optimization settings, reward functions, and advantage normalization). Qwen3-1.7B/4B models trained with GRPO are also used as non-looped RL baselines.
- **Training Data:** Models are exclusively trained on samples from the MATH dataset.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** MATH-500, AIME24, AIME26, BeyondAIME, and GSM8K.
 - **Non-Mathematical Reasoning (Zero-shot transfer):** ARC-C, MMLU-ST, GPQA, and MBPP.
- **Decoding:** Deterministic decoding is primarily used for Pass@1 evaluation, with statistical significance analyzed using sampling-based evaluations (T=0.2, 10 seeds, paired t-tests).
- **Inference Budgets:** GRPO is given an expanded decode budget (e.g., 3072 tokens for math) compared to RLTT (e.g., 2048/512 for math), reflecting GRPO's tendency for longer responses. Robustness to varying decode budgets is also evaluated.
- **Weighting Strategy:** Exit-probability weighting is primarily used for RLTT, leveraging Ouro's halting signal. Sensitivity to other weighting strategies is also analyzed.
- **Theoretical Analysis:** A theoretical framework is provided to demonstrate that trajectory-level credit assignment can lead to shorter optimal decoding lengths.
- **Ancillary Analyses:** Gradient Signal-to-Noise Ratio (GSNR), Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), and loop-level performance analyses are conducted.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Training Dynamics

- **Reward Evolution:** RLTT consistently achieved higher reward than GRPO throughout MATH training, with the performance gap emerging early and widening over time. This is attributed to denser, trajectory-level credit assignment, which provides more informative updates by allowing intermediate latent states to receive direct gradients, thereby accelerating policy improvement.
- **Response Length Evolution:** RLTT-trained policies converged to substantially shorter responses than GRPO, despite the reward function solely depending on final answer correctness without an explicit brevity incentive. This reduction in response length is interpreted as an emergent consequence of RLTT inducing more efficient latent reasoning and faster internal convergence to correct patterns, reducing superfluous token-level exploration. This efficiency translated to a 10% reduction in training time for RLTT.
- **Entropy Evolution:** RLTT exhibited a steeper and more sustained reduction in the entropy of the terminal-loop next-token distribution compared to GRPO. This is interpreted as increased model confidence as correct reasoning trajectories stabilize, rather than entropy collapse. Pass@k analysis confirmed that RLTT maintains strong diversity and favorable scaling under increased sampling, indicating controlled confidence where probability mass is concentrated on correct solution manifolds without eliminating diversity.

#### 4.2. Math Benchmarks

- RLTT consistently and substantially outperformed GRPO across all mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, AIME24, AIME26, BeyondAIME, GSM8K).
 - On the 2.6B scale, RLTT achieved gains of +7.0% on MATH-500, +16.6% on AIME24, +10.0% on AIME26, and +10.0% on BeyondAIME.
 - On the 1.4B scale, gains included +3.8% on MATH-500, +10.0% on AIME24, +3.7% on AIME26, and +6.0% on BeyondAIME.
- Ouro-1.4B+RLTT achieved 46.0% mean benchmark accuracy, surpassing Qwen3-1.7B+GRPO (27.2%) and Qwen3-4B+GRPO (31.7%). Ouro-2.6B+RLTT further improved to 51.2%.
- Gains were particularly pronounced on challenging benchmarks like AIME24/26 and BeyondAIME, where GRPO often exhausted token budgets before reaching valid solutions. RLTT's improvement is attributed to enabling the model to arrive at correct solutions more efficiently, consistent with observed shorter responses during training.
- **Decode-Token Budget Robustness:** RLTT consistently outperformed GRPO across a wide range of decode lengths (1024 to 4096 tokens) on MATH-500, including regimes beyond its training horizon. This suggests that RLTT learns generalized reasoning policies rather than overfitting to specific decode lengths.
- **Loop-level Performance:** RLTT outperformed GRPO at every evaluated loop count (1 to 4 loops), with significant margins under 1-2 loop regimes. This indicates improved effectiveness of early latent reasoning iterations, contributing to robustness under tight loop and token budgets.
- **Statistical Significance:** Paired t-tests confirmed that RLTT's improvements over GRPO were statistically significant (p < 0.05) across 8/9 benchmarks at the 2.6B scale and 7/9 benchmarks at the 1.4B scale in sampling-based evaluations.
- **Sensitivity to Weighting Strategy:** Performance differences across uniform, progressive, and exit-probability weighting strategies were modest, suggesting that the core benefit of RLTT stems from exposing the full latent trajectory to the reward signal, rather than from fine-tuned credit allocation.

#### 4.3. Non-Math Benchmarks (Zero-shot Transfer)

- Despite being trained exclusively on mathematical tasks, RLTT demonstrated positive zero-shot transfer to non-mathematical reasoning benchmarks (ARC-C, MMLU-ST, GPQA, MBPP).
 - On the 2.6B scale, gains included +0.7% on ARC-C, +3.5% on MMLU-ST, +18.7% on GPQA, and +3.3% on MBPP.
 - On the 1.4B scale, gains included +1.2% on ARC-C, +2.7% on MMLU-ST, +12.1% on GPQA, and +5.1% on MBPP.
- Ouro-1.4B+RLTT achieved 64.8% mean non-math benchmark accuracy, outperforming Qwen3-1.7B+GRPO (51.1%) and Qwen3-4B+GRPO (58.7%). Ouro-2.6B+RLTT further improved to 71.8%.
- The most significant gain was on GPQA, a multi-hop factual reasoning task, where RLTT nearly doubled GRPO's performance. This suggests improved efficiency in stabilizing reasoning trajectories.
- Statistical significance analysis confirmed these improvements across all non-math benchmarks for both model scales. The observed gains indicate that RLTT modifies *how* the model reasons rather than merely *what* it learns to solve.

#### 4.4. Why RLTT Works (Theoretical and Gradient Analysis)

- **Token-Efficient Reasoning:** A theoretical analysis, based on assumptions of loop refinement decreasing uncertainty, diminishing returns of extra decoded tokens, and approximately linear total uncertainty cost, demonstrated that trajectory-level credit assignment can induce weakly shorter optimal decoding lengths. This theoretical result aligns with the empirical observation that RLTT-trained policies are more robust under constrained inference budgets because they achieve correct solutions more efficiently without relying on extended token-level reasoning.
- **Richer Gradient Signal:** Gradient Signal-to-Noise Ratio (GSNR) analysis showed statistically significant GSNR improvements for RLTT on challenging benchmarks like AIME24 and BeyondAIME. On these tasks, where rewards are sparse, RLTT's distribution of credit across the latent thought trajectory provides a denser and more informative gradient signal to shape earlier internal computation, improving learning dynamics. On easier tasks like GSM8K, GSNR decreased, interpreted as gradient saturation indicative of task mastery.
- **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA):** CKA analysis of latent representations across loops showed that RLTT encourages monotonic progression toward the final-loop representation, indicating stable latent refinement without premature collapse. In contrast, GRPO exhibited non-monotonic convergence, suggesting intermediate states might drift before late correction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces RLTT, a reinforcement learning framework that addresses a fundamental credit-assignment mismatch in Looped Language Models (LoopLMs). By distributing reward across the entire latent reasoning trajectory, RLTT provides a more aligned and dense supervisory signal for iterative internal computation, which has several significant implications:

1. **Improved Reasoning Performance:** RLTT demonstrates consistent and statistically significant improvements in mathematical reasoning, outperforming standard GRPO on challenging benchmarks. This suggests a more effective method for training LLMs designed for multi-step reasoning.
2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Robustness:** The framework leads to more token-efficient reasoning, with models producing shorter, yet accurate, responses. This efficiency translates to robustness under strict inference constraints and various decode budgets, which is critical for practical deployment scenarios where computational resources or latency are limited. The theoretical analysis provides a basis for understanding this increased efficiency.
3. **Generalizable Latent Reasoning:** The observed zero-shot transferability to non-mathematical reasoning tasks indicates that RLTT improves the underlying latent reasoning capabilities of LoopLMs, rather than merely optimizing for specific task heuristics. This suggests a broader impact on general problem-solving in LLMs.
4. **Richer Learning Signals:** By distributing gradients across the entire latent trajectory, RLTT provides a richer learning signal, particularly beneficial for complex tasks where terminal-only rewards are sparse. This can accelerate learning and policy improvement.
5. **Advancement in RL for LLMs:** The work contributes to the field of reinforcement learning for LLMs by proposing an approach tailored to architectures with iterative internal processes, moving beyond the traditional single-step decision process assumption.

While RLTT offers significant advantages, the authors acknowledge practical trade-offs. The increased memory footprint from retaining per-loop log-probabilities can limit per-GPU token packing, and the method is specialized for looped architectures. Furthermore, the current experiments use a fixed loop depth, which does not leverage Ouro’s adaptive early-exit mechanism. Future work aims to explore memory-efficient implementations and extensions for integrating adaptive halting, potentially combining the benefits of trajectory-level credit assignment with flexible compute allocation. The immediate societal impact is anticipated to be positive, contributing to more capable and efficient reasoning systems without foreseen negative consequences beyond those generally associated with advanced language models.
