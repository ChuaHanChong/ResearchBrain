# VLM3: Vision Language Models Are Native 3D Learners

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM³ introduces a framework enabling standard Vision Language Models (VLMs) to achieve proficiency in diverse and fine-grained 3D understanding tasks without relying on complex, task-specific designs. It demonstrates performance comparable to or exceeding specialized expert models across metric depth, object-level understanding, pixel correspondence, and camera pose estimation through focal length unification, text-based pixel referencing, and strategic data mixture.

## Problem

- Vision Language Models (VLMs) traditionally struggle with fine-grained 3D understanding, limiting their application in tasks requiring precise spatial awareness.
- Expert 3D vision models rely on complex, task-specific designs, including unique architectures, heavy data augmentations, and tailored loss functions for each task.
- Previous VLM-based approaches for 3D often required architectural modifications or were limited to coarse-grained object-level tasks, not matching expert model precision.

## Method

- VLM³ employs focal length unification, standardizing all input images to a 1000-pixel focal length to normalize camera intrinsics without extra VLM encoders.
- It utilizes text-based pixel/region reference with normalized coordinates [0, 2000) in text prompts, which enhances training and inference efficiency over visual prompting.
- The framework incorporates strategic data mixture and non-uniform weighting of diverse training datasets, prioritizing leveraging varied and large-scale data to avoid overfitting.

## Results

- VLM³-4B achieved a δ₁ accuracy of 0.904 for metric depth estimation, surpassing DepthLM-7B and setting new state-of-the-art on NuScenes and iBims1 datasets.
- The model outperformed SpatialRGPT-8B on object-level 3D understanding benchmarks using only text-based bounding box coordinates for object reference.
- VLM³-4B reduced the End-Point Error (EPE) for pixel correspondence by an order of magnitude and significantly improved camera pose estimation AUC@30° from **5%** to **94%**, matching expert models like DA3-Giant.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard VLMs possess an inherent capability for fine-grained 3D understanding, challenging the necessity for complex, task-specific architectural designs common in expert models.
> - A few fundamental design principles—focal length unification, text-based pixel reference, and effective data mixture and scaling—are sufficient for VLMs to master diverse 3D tasks.
> - Text-based pixel reference offers comparable accuracy to visual prompting while significantly improving training scalability and efficiency by allowing multiple queries per image.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual intelligence has seen advancements in unified models, particularly Vision Language Models (VLMs), which leverage prompting to address diverse vision tasks. While VLMs have demonstrated proficiency in semantic understanding, their application to fine-grained 3D understanding has traditionally been limited. This domain has largely remained the purview of expert vision models, which are characterized by complex, task-specific designs encompassing specialized architectures, extensive data augmentations, and tailored loss functions.

Previous efforts to integrate 3D understanding into VLMs have primarily focused on coarse-grained object-level tasks (e.g., Chen et al., 2024), which do not typically achieve the precision of expert models in fine-grained scenarios. Some VLM-based approaches for 3D understanding have also necessitated architectural modifications, such as the addition of extra encoders or modules (e.g., Cheng et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2026), which can compromise compatibility with standard VLM frameworks. More recently, DepthLM (Cai et al., 2025) demonstrated that standard VLMs could perform pixel-level metric depth estimation, albeit relying on visual prompting for pixel reference.

This work, VLM³, contributes to the evolving landscape by challenging the premise that complex, task-specific designs are prerequisites for effective 3D learning. It posits that VLMs possess inherent capabilities for 3D understanding, arguing that they are "native 3D learners." The research aims to bridge the gap between generalist VLMs and specialized 3D vision models by demonstrating that standard VLM architectures, with minimal inductive bias and simplified design principles, can achieve performance comparable to or exceeding expert models across a range of diverse and fine-grained 3D tasks. It extends beyond prior VLM 3D efforts by generalizing to multiple 3D tasks, simplifying the pixel reference mechanism, and emphasizing data mixture and scaling as primary drivers of performance, rather than complex architectural or loss function modifications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether standard Vision Language Models (VLMs), without the need for complex task-specific designs, can achieve performance comparable to expert vision models in diverse and fine-grained 3D understanding tasks beyond simple depth estimation.

This objective is driven by several motivations:
1. **Addressing VLM Limitations in 3D Understanding:** Existing VLMs, despite their advancements in semantic understanding, have shown limitations in grasping the intricacies of 3D space, particularly for fine-grained geometric tasks. This restricts their utility in applications requiring precise spatial awareness.
2. **Challenging the Necessity of Task-Specific Complexity:** Expert vision models for 3D tasks typically rely on intricate and specialized designs, including unique architectures, heavy data augmentations, and a variety of loss functions tailored to each specific task. The researchers hypothesize that much of this complexity might not be fundamentally necessary for effective 3D learning, especially within the context of powerful generalist models like VLMs.
3. **Promoting Model Unification and Generalization:** The broader goal of VLMs is to provide a unified framework for solving various vision tasks. Extending this unification to fine-grained 3D understanding without requiring task-specific architectural changes or extensive customization would significantly advance the development of truly generalist visual intelligence.
4. **Improving Efficiency and Scalability:** Complex, task-specific designs often lead to increased development effort, specialized hardware requirements, and reduced scalability. A simpler, more unified approach would inherently be more efficient to train and deploy, facilitating larger-scale studies and broader application.
5. **Re-evaluating Foundational Assumptions:** The work aims to critically re-evaluate foundational assumptions in 3D vision, such as the necessity of regression losses for metric tasks or the role of visual prompting for pixel localization. By demonstrating that simpler, text-based approaches can achieve high accuracy, the research seeks to inspire a reconsideration of design principles for future foundation models.

The study aims to demonstrate that a few fundamental design principles—namely focal length unification, text-based pixel reference, and effective data mixture and scaling—are sufficient for VLMs to become proficient 3D learners across a spectrum of tasks, including object-level understanding, metric depth estimation, pixel correspondence, and camera pose estimation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, VLM³, is a scalable framework designed to enable standard VLMs to master diverse 3D tasks with minimal architectural changes or complex task-specific designs. The approach leverages a standard VLM architecture (Qwen3-vl-4B as the base model) and relies on text-based Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). The core of VLM³ is built upon three key ingredients: focal length unification, text-based pixel reference, and strategic data mixture and scaling.

#### 3.1. Key Ingredients

1. **Focal Length Unification:**
 - **Rationale:** Camera ambiguity, arising from varying intrinsic camera parameters across datasets, poses a challenge for mixed-data training in 3D tasks.
 - **Implementation:** All input images are resized such that their focal length is standardized to 1000 pixels. This process effectively normalizes the camera intrinsics, eliminating the need for additional VLM encoders or modules to handle camera information.
 - **Handling Missing Data:** For images lacking camera intrinsic data (e.g., from unknown internet sources), a pre-trained single image calibration model (Tirado-Garín and Civera, 2025) is employed to estimate the intrinsics, enabling consistent focal length unification.

2. **Text-based Pixel/Region Reference:**
 - **Rationale:** Previous VLM approaches for pixel-level tasks, such as DepthLM, utilized visual prompting (rendering markers directly onto the image). This method presents scalability limitations, as training or inference on multiple pixels within a single image would necessitate duplicating the image for each query. Additionally, it is unsuitable for tasks where pixel references are required in the output. Prior work also suggested VLMs struggled with text-based pixel reference with arbitrary prompts.
 - **Implementation:** VLM³ normalizes the pixel coordinate space to a range of [0, 2000) for both horizontal and vertical axes. Pixel or object region references are then communicated to the VLM via text prompts using these normalized coordinates (e.g., "How far is the pixel at (x, y) from the camera? Both x and y are normalized to between [0, 2000).").
 - **Benefits:** This approach eliminates the need for visual markers and image duplication. It significantly enhances training and inference efficiency by allowing multiple questions related to different pixels/regions within the same image to be batched and processed concurrently. It also generalizes to tasks requiring pixel outputs, such as pixel correspondence. Empirical analysis indicated that this method achieved accuracy comparable to visual prompting.

3. **Data Mixture and Scaling:**
 - **Rationale:** The researchers hypothesize that once camera ambiguity and pixel reference issues are addressed, scaling up the training data is crucial, and complex task-specific designs become less necessary.
 - **Implementation:** VLM³ utilizes a diverse mixture of training datasets. A critical finding is the importance of non-uniform weighting for these datasets. Naive scaling with uniform weights, particularly with datasets of drastically different sizes, can lead to overfitting on smaller datasets and diminished overall performance.
 - **Weighting Strategy:** A baseline weighting strategy involves scaling dataset contributions proportional to their respective sizes. Further performance gains were achieved by manually adjusting weights, specifically reducing the influence of smaller datasets susceptible to overfitting. This strategy prioritizes leveraging diverse and large-scale data without resorting to heavy data augmentations or specialized losses common in expert models.

#### 3.2. Enablement of Diverse Tasks

To validate the generality of VLM³, the methodology was applied to four distinct 3D understanding tasks:

1. **Metric Depth Estimation:**
 - **Formulation:** Similar to DepthLM, depth estimation is framed as predicting the distance from a query pixel to the camera.
 - **Dataset:** An expanded mixture of 26M images, including 10M internal outdoor street view images, building on DepthLM's original 16M.
 - **Training:** Text-based pixel reference allows for packing 10 Question-Answer pairs (corresponding to 10 labeled pixels) per image sample, resulting in training on 32M samples (320M labeled pixels) using 32 GPUs for 3 days. Non-uniform dataset weighting was applied.

2. **Object-level 3D Understanding:**
 - **Datasets:** Training and evaluation were conducted on datasets similar to SpatialRGPT (Cheng et al., 2024), covering both qualitative and quantitative questions.
 - **Reference:** Instead of extra encoders for object region masks, VLM³ refers to objects using bounding box coordinates (xMin, yMin, xMax, yMax) within text prompts.
 - **Intrinsics:** Camera intrinsics for "in-the-wild" internet images were estimated using a pre-trained single image calibration model.
 - **Training:** 1M images, 32 GPUs for 3 hours.

3. **Pixel Correspondence Estimation:**
 - **Task:** Given a query pixel in a source image, identify its corresponding pixel in a target image.
 - **Datasets:** A mixture of approximately 10M image pairs. Dataset-size based weighting was used.
 - **Training:** 80M samples (10 QA per sample) on 64 GPUs for 7 days.
 - **Prompts:** Uses randomly generated LLM prompt templates, treating both query and output pixel locations as text (e.g., "What pixel in the second image corresponds to pixel (x1, y1) in the first image? Report the answer as (x2, y2)."). Focal length unification was not empirically required for this task but did not negatively impact performance.

4. **Camera Pose Estimation:**
 - **Task:** Given two input images, estimate the translation distance, translation direction, and rotation direction between the camera poses.
 - **Outputs:** Translation distance in meters, translation direction as a unit 3D vector, and rotation direction as yaw-pitch-roll numbers.
 - **Datasets:** Same training data and mixture weights as pixel correspondence.
 - **Training:** 10M samples on 32 GPUs for 4 days.
 - **Prompts:** Detailed, component-specific text prompts were used for each pose element, packed into a single sample during training and evaluation. This text-based approach to pose estimation, traditionally handled by multi-step optimization or complex regression losses in expert models, represents a departure from conventional methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence that Vision Language Models (VLMs), when configured with the VLM³ methodology, are capable of native 3D understanding across diverse tasks, matching or surpassing the performance of specialized expert vision models with significantly simpler designs.

#### 4.1. Performance Across Diverse 3D Tasks

- **Metric Depth Estimation:**
 - VLM³-4B demonstrated a substantial improvement over previous state-of-the-art VLMs for metric depth estimation. It increased the average δ₁ accuracy of DepthLM-7B from 0.84 to 0.904, utilizing a smaller model (4B vs. 7B parameters) and a simpler text-based pixel reference mechanism.
 - The model exhibited competitive accuracy compared to expert vision models such as MoGe-2 and UnidepthV2, achieving new state-of-the-art results on the NuScenes and iBims1 datasets.
 - Visualizations indicated that VLM³ generated high-quality point clouds for both indoor and outdoor scenes, notably avoiding "flying points" between objects, a common artifact in some expert vision models.

- **Object-level 3D Understanding:**
 - VLM³-4B surpassed the accuracy of SpatialRGPT-8B on the SpatialRGPT-Bench for both qualitative and quantitative 3D understanding tasks.
 - This achievement was notable because VLM³ accomplished it without requiring extra encoders for object region masks, as used by SpatialRGPT-8B, instead relying solely on text-based bounding box coordinates for object reference.

- **Pixel Correspondence Estimation:**
 - VLM³-4B reduced the End-Point Error (EPE) of baseline VLMs by an order of magnitude.
 - It demonstrated competitive performance against expert vision models, achieving lower EPE than DKM and RoMa. While it trailed UFM, the results suggest a strong capability for precise multi-view pixel matching in both indoor and outdoor environments.

- **Camera Pose Estimation:**
 - VLM³-4B significantly improved the AUC@30° metric of baseline VLMs from 5% to 94%.
 - Compared to expert vision models, VLM³ achieved accuracy comparable to DA3-Giant (94.0 vs. 94.7), and surpassed other mainstream models such as VGGT and MapAnything.
 - This task demonstrated that a standard VLM, using simple text-based next token prediction, could accurately estimate complex outputs like translation distance, translation direction, and rotation (yaw-pitch-roll), challenging the conventional reliance on regression losses or multi-step geometric optimization for pose estimation.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Key Ingredients and Design Choices

- **Text-based Pixel Reference Effectiveness:** Comparative experiments showed that text-based pixel reference, with normalized pixel space, achieved similar accuracy (0.853 δ₁) to visual prompting (0.849 δ₁) when trained on the same data and model. This validates the simplified and more scalable approach.

- **Importance of Data Mixtures:** The study revealed that naive uniform weighting of diverse datasets during large-scale training could lead to performance degradation (0.842 δ₁ for 32M samples + 10QA) compared to smaller-scale training with fewer labels (0.853 δ₁ for 8M samples + 1QA). Weighting datasets based on their size provided a significant improvement (0.884 δ₁), and further fine-tuned VLM³ weighting achieved the highest accuracy (0.904 δ₁). This highlights that strategic data mixture and weighting are more crucial than simply increasing raw data volume.

- **Sufficiency of Small VLMs:** The 4B model used in VLM³ achieved higher depth estimation accuracy (0.904 δ₁) than larger 8B (0.880 δ₁) or 32B (0.873 δ₁) models at the current training scale. Further scaling the 4B model's training to 64M samples (from 32M) resulted in a decrease in accuracy (0.880 δ₁), suggesting that even the 4B model can overfit the current dataset size with prolonged training. These findings indicate that for the current data scale (approx. 26M images), data scaling and optimization are more impactful than increasing model size, and that smaller VLMs are sufficient to achieve state-of-the-art performance.

- **Unnecessary Design Complexities:** The overall findings reinforce the argument that many elements considered foundational in expert 3D vision models—such as architectural modifications, heavy data augmentations, and complex regression losses—are not necessary conditions for effective 3D learning when leveraging the capabilities of standard VLMs through VLM³.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLM³ research offers significant implications for the development of generalist artificial intelligence and the field of 3D computer vision.

1. **A New Paradigm for 3D Learning:** VLM³ proposes and empirically validates a "simple and scalable 3D learning" paradigm. It demonstrates that standard Vision Language Models, without requiring complex, task-specific architectural designs, specialized data augmentations, or intricate loss functions, can achieve high accuracy across a broad spectrum of 3D tasks. This approach challenges the prevailing reliance on domain-specific engineering in 3D vision, suggesting a more unified and generalizable pathway.

2. **Rethinking Necessity in 3D Vision:** The findings compel a re-evaluation of what constitutes essential components for effective 3D learning. The work explicitly shows that focal length unification, text-based pixel/region reference, and strategic data mixture/scaling are highly effective. Conversely, it provides evidence that complex architectural modifications, larger models (beyond a certain scale), heavy data augmentations, and even fundamental regression losses are not strictly necessary, as text-based input/output can suffice even for highly precise metric tasks like camera pose estimation.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** By replacing visual prompting with text-based pixel reference, VLM³ significantly improves the efficiency and scalability of training. This allows for processing multiple queries per image without redundant data loading, making large-scale data utilization more feasible and cost-effective. The ability to leverage existing VLM architectures minimizes the development and computational overhead associated with creating specialized 3D models.

4. **Advancing Generalist AI:** The demonstration that a single VLM framework can seamlessly handle diverse 3D tasks—from fine-grained pixel-level depth and correspondence to object-level reasoning and complex multi-view pose estimation—represents a notable step towards truly generalist visual intelligence. This unification allows for a more holistic understanding of visual scenes, bridging semantic and geometric perception within a single model.

5. **Accessibility and Broader Application:** The success of VLM³ with a relatively small 4B parameter model (compared to much larger LLMs and VLMs) indicates that advanced 3D understanding might be achievable with more accessible computational resources. This could broaden the application of sophisticated 3D capabilities to a wider range of platforms and research initiatives.

6. **Future Research Directions:** This work is likely to motivate future research into simpler designs for foundation models, focusing on data curation strategies, effective prompting techniques, and the inherent representational power of VLMs. It opens avenues for exploring how minimal inductive biases can lead to robust and generalized capabilities across traditionally disparate vision tasks, potentially leading to more flexible and adaptive AI systems.
