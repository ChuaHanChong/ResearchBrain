# Physics-Based Dexterous Manipulations with Estimated Hand Poses and Residual Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Imperial College London and collaborators developed a physics-based residual reinforcement learning framework that enables dexterous manipulation in virtual environments using noisy bare-hand pose estimates from a depth sensor. The system refines imperfect user input to achieve physically plausible hand-object interactions, outperforming baselines in various tasks like 'door opening' (83% success) and 'give coin' (83.3% success) while maintaining visual similarity to the user's motion.

## Problem

- Hand Pose Estimators (HPEs) provide noisy and inaccurate 3D hand poses, especially at contact points, leading to interaction failures in physics-simulated environments.
- Traditional VR/AR dexterous manipulation relies on expensive, intrusive hardware (e.g., mocap gloves) or non-physical, animation-based solutions that lack realism and user intent.
- Lack of suitable training data with continuous, noisy estimated hand pose sequences corresponding to successful, physics-consistent manipulations.

## Method

- A ==residual reinforcement learning== agent is trained to apply small, corrective joint displacements to initial, imperfect ==Inverse Kinematics (IK)== mappings of estimated hand poses, ensuring physics-based interactions.
- A hybrid reward function combines task-oriented goals, an adversarial imitation learning component (==GAIL==) for human-like motion, and a 3D hand pose estimation reward to maintain visual fidelity to the user's input.
- A novel data generation scheme synthesizes training data by querying a large 3D hand pose dataset (**BigHand2.2M**) with virtual hand poses and viewpoints derived from mocap expert trajectories, then passing retrieved depth images through an HPE to simulate realistic noise.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved **83%** success for 'door opening' and **57.14%** for 'in-hand manipulation' with estimated poses, outperforming direct IK (**27.81%** and **20.30%** respectively) and other RL/IL baselines.
- In 'in-the-wild' reconstruction, it achieved **65.0%** task success and **33.15 mm** 3D hand pose error for 'pour juice', and **83.3%** success with **25.30 mm** error for 'give coin', significantly better than baselines.
- The residual policy demonstrated faster convergence, requiring **3.8M** samples for 'door opening' and **5.2M** for 'in-hand manipulation', compared to **7.9M** and **13.8M** samples for an RL-only baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Residual policy learning can effectively correct noisy user input for physics-based dexterous manipulation, leading to both task success and visually plausible motion without specialized hardware.
> - Combining task-specific rewards with imitation learning and a 3D hand pose fidelity reward is crucial for achieving high success rates while maintaining visual similarity to human intent, with residual learning converging faster than pure RL.
> - A synthetic data generation pipeline can effectively bridge the gap for training data, enabling the development of robust systems that operate with real-world Hand Pose Estimator noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation in virtual or augmented reality (VR/AR) environments, particularly using bare hands, constitutes a significant challenge within robotics, computer vision, and human-computer interaction. Traditional approaches often rely on expensive and intrusive motion capture (mocap) systems, such as data gloves or exoskeletons, to accurately track human hand movements and transfer them to virtual models. The goal of moving beyond such specialized hardware and enabling natural bare-hand interaction is a key driver for research in this area.

Hand Pose Estimators (HPEs), which infer 3D hand keypoint locations from images (e.g., depth or RGB), offer a less intrusive alternative. However, HPEs typically produce noisy and imperfect pose estimates. When these estimates are directly mapped to virtual hand models in physics-simulated environments, even minor inaccuracies in finger tips or contact points can lead to interaction failures. Previous attempts to address object interaction with HPEs include simple approaches like generating contact forces based on finger penetration into virtual objects. While effective for basic grasping, these methods often fail to produce physically realistic or dexterous manipulation, such as in-hand object repositioning. Commercial solutions, exemplified by systems like Leap Motion or Hololens, frequently bypass physics laws or rely on recognizing gestures to trigger pre-recorded animations, which can result in artificial or visually dissimilar motion compared to the user's intent.

Other research endeavors have explored physics-based pose estimation, where physics simulators are incorporated into optimization frameworks to refine hand poses or reconstruct hand-object interactions. However, these often focus on single-shot images or offline reconstruction rather than real-time, online correction of noisy user input.

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL) has demonstrated success in training agents to perform complex manipulation skills. However, these methods typically train autonomous agents based on task-specific rewards or expert demonstrations, without direct consideration of online, noisy user input. The learned motions, while effective for task completion, can sometimes appear unnatural or deviate significantly from human execution. Residual policy learning, a subset of RL, aims to improve an existing, suboptimal policy by learning a small corrective action. However, existing residual learning approaches commonly operate on top of a pre-trained policy rather than directly on noisy human user input.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these domains by proposing a system that leverages HPEs for bare-hand dexterous manipulation in physics-simulated environments. It directly addresses the limitations of HPE noise and the inadequacies of simple contact models by introducing a residual learning framework that corrects imperfect user input in real-time. By integrating a physics simulator, adversarial imitation learning, and a novel reward function that penalizes deviations from estimated hand poses, the authors aim to produce physically plausible and visually similar motions, thereby enabling complex dexterous interactions without specialized hardware. The proposed data generation scheme further addresses the practical challenge of acquiring suitable training data for this specific problem setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable dexterous manipulation of objects in virtual environments using only bare hand pose estimates derived from a depth sensor, without requiring expensive or intrusive motion capture hardware.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several challenges inherent in this problem domain:

- **Inherent Noise and Inaccuracies of Hand Pose Estimators (HPEs):** HPEs, while increasingly accurate, still produce estimates with inherent noise and errors, particularly at critical points like finger tips or contact locations. In a physics-simulated environment, which is governed by factors such as object weight and surface friction, even slight inaccuracies can lead to interaction failures. For example, a minor misestimation of finger position could prevent a successful grasp or cause an object to slip.
- **Lack of Force Feedback:** Unlike physical interaction, virtual environments typically lack haptic or force feedback, making it challenging for users to gauge the necessary forces or subtle movements required for dexterous tasks. The system needs to compensate for this absence by intelligently adjusting virtual hand poses to achieve stable interactions.
- **Limitations of Prior Contact Modeling Approaches:** Existing methods for simulating hand-object contact, such as generating forces based on the penetration of finger joints into virtual objects, are often too simplistic. While sufficient for basic grasping, they are inadequate for complex dexterous manipulations like in-hand object repositioning, where precise and dynamic force application is critical. These methods also suffer from direct dependency on noisy pose estimates.
- **Producing Natural and Visually Plausible Motion:** Many reinforcement learning (RL) or imitation learning (IL) approaches, while effective in training agents to perform tasks autonomously, can sometimes generate motions that appear unnatural or deviate significantly from typical human behavior. For VR/AR applications, maintaining visual resemblance between the user's input and the virtual hand's motion is crucial for a compelling and intuitive user experience.
- **Bridging the Domain Gap between Human and Virtual Hand Models:** There is an inherent kinematic and anatomical difference between a human hand and a typical anthropomorphic virtual hand model. The process of inverse kinematics or pose retargeting to map human hand poses to the virtual model is often ill-posed and further complicated by noisy input. The system needs to effectively bridge this gap to enable successful interaction.
- **Absence of Suitable Training Data:** To train a system that corrects noisy HPE input for successful physical interactions, a dataset of continuous, noisy estimated hand pose sequences corresponding to successful manipulation actions is required. Such data is difficult to collect naturally, as users tend to abort motions when interactions fail, leading to incomplete or non-representative sequences.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the proposed work include:
- Developing a model that can map noisy input hand poses to refined target virtual poses, enabling necessary contacts within a physics simulator.
- Implementing a residual reinforcement learning agent that operates on top of the user's estimated hand pose, applying minor but crucial joint displacements.
- Integrating a model-free hybrid RL+IL approach to learn these residual corrections, promoting both task accomplishment and human-like motion.
- Introducing a 3D hand pose estimation reward to guide the agent in maintaining visual similarity between the corrected virtual hand pose and the user's original estimated hand pose.
- Proposing a data generation scheme to synthesize realistic noisy hand pose sequences for training and evaluation by leveraging existing mocap and large-scale hand pose datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework aims to refine noisy user-provided hand pose estimates for dexterous manipulation in physics-simulated virtual environments. The core idea is to employ a "residual agent" that makes small, corrective adjustments to an initial, imperfect mapping of the user's hand pose.

**A. Inverse Kinematics (IK): From Human Hand Pose to Virtual Pose**
The initial step involves translating the user's estimated 3D hand pose, represented by 21 joint locations ($\mathbf{x}_t$), into a corresponding posture for the virtual hand model ($\mathbf{z}_t$). This is achieved by estimating the virtual hand's actuator parameters ($\mathbf{a}_t$, representing joint angles) using an inverse kinematics (IK) function, $\kappa(\mathbf{x}_t(\phi_t))$, where $\phi_t$ is the visual representation (e.g., image) from which $\mathbf{x}_t$ was estimated. The paper notes that this IK mapping is inherently ill-posed and susceptible to noise from the hand pose estimator.

**B. Residual Hand Agent**
To compensate for the imperfections in the IK mapping and the noise in the estimated hand pose, a residual controller is introduced. This controller generates a "residual action" ($\mathbf{f}_t$) that is subtracted from the initial IK output to produce the final action applied to the virtual hand model:
$\mathbf{a}_t = \kappa(\mathbf{x}_t(\phi_t)) - \mathbf{f}_t(\mathbf{s}_t, \kappa(\mathbf{x}_t(\phi_t)), \phi_t)$.
The residual action $\mathbf{f}_t$ is a function of the current simulation state ($\mathbf{s}_t$), the initial IK-mapped user input, and the visual features ($\phi_t$). A key constraint is that $\mathbf{f}_t$ is limited to a small, zero-centered interval to ensure that the final action does not deviate significantly from the user's intended input, thereby preserving visual similarity.

The learning of this residual policy is formulated as a reinforcement learning (RL) problem. A neural network, parameterized by $\theta$, acts as the policy $\pi_\theta(\mathbf{f}|\mathbf{s}, \kappa, \phi)$. The agent interacts with the MuJoCo physics simulator, observing the state, user input, and visual features. The policy is optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a policy gradient method known for its stability. The state $\mathbf{s}_t$ includes task-specific information such as relative positions between the hand and object, and the hand's velocity.

**1. Reward Function:**
The total reward function ($\mathbf{r}_t$) guiding the policy learning is a weighted sum of three components:
- **Task-oriented reward ($\omega_{task} \mathbf{r}_{task_t}$):** This reward is specific to each manipulation task (e.g., 'door opening', 'in-hand manipulation'). It incentivizes short-term goals like proximity to the object and long-term goals like completing the main task.
- **Imitation learning reward ($\omega_{il} \mathbf{r}_{il_t}$):** To encourage natural, human-like motion and prevent unnatural behaviors often seen in pure RL, an adversarial imitation learning (IL) reward is used, similar to Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL). This involves a discriminator $D_\psi$ that learns to distinguish between agent-generated actions and expert demonstration trajectories (obtained from a mocap dataset). The policy is then trained to "fool" the discriminator, producing actions that are similar to the expert. The objective is a min-max game between the policy and the discriminator.
- **3D hand pose estimation reward ($\omega_{pose} \mathbf{r}_{pose_t}$):** This reward term is crucial for ensuring that the virtual hand's posture ($\mathbf{z}_t$) remains visually similar to the user's input pose ($\overline{\mathbf{x}}_t$). If ground-truth hand poses are available during training, this reward is defined as the negative sum of squared Euclidean distances between the 3D positions of corresponding joints of the virtual hand and the ground-truth human hand. This helps to counteract potential divergence caused by other reward components.

**C. Data Generation Scheme**
A significant challenge for training this framework is the lack of existing datasets containing continuous sequences of noisy estimated hand poses that result in successful, physics-consistent manipulations. To address this, a novel data generation scheme is proposed:
1. **Leverage Mocap Data:** The scheme starts with a dataset of successful manipulation sequences recorded using a mocap glove (expert trajectories, $\tau$). These sequences provide ground-truth actions and corresponding virtual hand poses ($\mathbf{z}_t$).
2. **Virtual Sensor Readings:** For each step in the mocap sequence, virtual sensors and a virtual camera in the simulator record the virtual hand pose ($\mathbf{z}_t$) and the relative viewpoint ($\mathbf{v}_t$) between the camera and the hand's palm.
3. **Querying a 3D Hand Pose Dataset:** The normalized and aligned virtual hand pose ($\hat{\mathbf{z}}_t$) and viewpoint ($\mathbf{v}_t$) are used to query a large 3D hand pose dataset, BigHand2.2M. This dataset contains pairs of depth images and corresponding ground-truth 3D hand pose annotations, covering dense articulations and viewpoints.
4. **Retrieval and HPE Application:** The system retrieves the closest ground-truth hand pose ($\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t$) from BigHand2.2M that matches the virtual pose and viewpoint. The depth image associated with this retrieved ground-truth pose is then passed through a state-of-the-art 3D Hand Pose Estimator (HPE) to obtain a noisy "estimated hand pose" ($\mathbf{x}_t$) and visual features ($\phi_t$).
This pipeline generates sequences of estimated hand poses and visual features that, if perfectly translated to the virtual environment, would correspond to successful manipulations. This creates the necessary noisy input sequences for training the residual agent.

**D. Experiments and Applications**
The framework is evaluated across two main applications:
1. **Experiment A: Dexterous manipulations in a virtual space with estimated hand poses in mid-air:** This simulates a VR scenario where a user interacts with the environment using bare hands. It first tests with synthetic Gaussian noise added to expert mocap demonstrations to control noise levels, and then with real structured noise generated by an HPE from images synthesized by the data generation scheme.
2. **Experiment B: Physics-based hand-object sequence reconstruction in-the-wild:** This involves reconstructing real-world hand-object interactions captured in RGB-D videos (F-PHAB dataset) in a physics simulator, using estimated hand poses and initial object pose estimates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented a framework for dexterous manipulation and evaluated its performance across various experimental settings and baselines.

**A. Performing dexterous manipulations in a virtual space with estimated hand poses in mid-air (Experiment A)**

1. **Overcoming Random Noise on Demonstrations (Experiment A.1):**
 - The residual agent demonstrated the ability to recover meaningful motion even with significant synthetic noise (up to 0.20 radians standard deviation) added to expert demonstrations, provided it was trained with similar noise magnitudes. This indicates robustness to input perturbations.
 - The proposed method consistently outperformed various RL and IL baselines in terms of task success rate across different environments ('door opening', 'in-hand manipulation', 'tool use'). For example, in 'door opening', the proposed method achieved 83% success on test data, compared to 74% for the direct Inverse Kinematics (IK) baseline and 59% for an RL-only baseline.
 - A notable finding was the significantly faster convergence of the residual policy compared to other baselines. For instance, in 'door opening' and 'in-hand manipulation', the proposed policy converged after 3.8M and 5.2M samples respectively, whereas an RL baseline required 7.9M and 13.8M samples. This accelerated learning is attributed to the "help in exploration" provided by the user input as a starting point for the residual correction.
 - An ablation study confirmed that combining both the task-oriented reward and the imitation learning reward was crucial. Pure RL achieved 75.9% success (task-focused but potentially unnatural motion), while pure IL achieved 36.5% (natural motion but potentially less task-effective). The combined approach achieved task success while maintaining visually plausible, human-like motion.
 - For the 'object relocation' task, all baselines and the proposed method exhibited low success rates, suggesting that limitations of the underlying PPO algorithm or environmental complexity might propagate and hinder performance for this specific task.

2. **Overcoming Structured Hand Pose Estimation and Mapping Errors (Experiment A.2):**
 - This experiment utilized the proposed data generation scheme to create training data with realistic structured noise from an actual Hand Pose Estimator (HPE) and mapping functions.
 - The results indicated that a supervised IK baseline, trained on ground-truth pose-to-action pairs, was insufficient on its own, especially when dealing with estimated (noisy) poses (e.g., 27.81% success for 'door opening' with estimated poses, compared to 49.62% with ground-truth poses).
 - The proposed method demonstrated solid performance even when the initial IK output was suboptimal, particularly for complex tasks like 'in-hand manipulation' (57.14% success with estimated poses for the proposed method vs. 20.30% for IK alone). It also maintained strong performance when observing better quality inputs.
 - Baselines like 'RL-no user' could perform well (e.g., 71.42% for 'door opening' with estimated poses) but did so by operating independently of the user input, akin to triggering a pre-recorded sequence, thus not following the user's motion. The proposed method, in contrast, corrected the user input.
 - Qualitative results and analysis of generated contact forces (Fig. 3c) showed that the method effectively applied minor but crucial joint displacements to achieve successful contacts, particularly in contrast to methods that simply maximize contact, which often failed for dexterous tasks.

**B. Physics-based Hand-Object Sequence Reconstruction (Experiment B)**

- This experiment focused on reconstructing 'in-the-wild' hand-object interactions from RGB-D video sequences (F-PHAB dataset) in a physics simulator for tasks like 'pour juice' and 'give coin'.
- The proposed method outperformed the IK and 'closing hand' baselines across all metrics: task success, 3D hand pose error (E_pose), and average sequence length before instability (¯T).
 - For 'pour juice', the proposed method achieved 65.0% task success and an E_pose of 33.15 mm on test data, significantly better than the IK baseline (5.0% success, 33.22 mm E_pose) and the 'closing hand' baseline (38.0% success, 35.46 mm E_pose).
 - For 'give coin', the performance gap was even more pronounced, with the proposed method achieving 83.3% success and 25.30 mm E_pose on test data, while baselines showed very poor performance (e.g., IK: 0.0% success, 'closing hand': 28.57% success).
- The inclusion of the 3D hand pose estimation reward (r_pose) was found to be effective in encouraging the virtual pose to remain visually closer to the input visual pose, improving both pose accuracy and task success.
- A significant gap between training and test results was observed, particularly for the 'give coin' scenario. The authors attributed this to higher hand pose estimation errors in this experiment (average 14.54 mm joint error) and the small number of training sequences (leading to potential overfitting). This highlights the challenging nature of highly precise manipulation tasks with thin objects and the impact of data quality and quantity.

In summary, the main findings indicate that the proposed physics-based residual reinforcement learning framework, combined with a specialized data generation scheme and multi-component reward function, effectively addresses the challenges of noisy HPE input for dexterous manipulation. It achieves high task success rates while maintaining visual fidelity to user input and demonstrates faster learning convergence compared to alternative RL/IL approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers several significant advancements and holds considerable potential impact across various fields.

**Technological Advancement in Human-Computer Interaction:** The most immediate significance is the demonstration of bare-hand dexterous manipulation in virtual environments using only a commodity depth sensor and a hand pose estimator. This eliminates the need for expensive and often intrusive motion capture gloves, exoskeletons, or handheld controllers. This simplification of hardware requirements could democratize access to realistic interactive VR/AR experiences, making them more natural and accessible.

**Enhanced Realism and Plausibility in Virtual Interactions:** By integrating a physics simulator and learning to apply subtle, physically consistent corrections to noisy hand pose estimates, the system generates virtual hand-object interactions that are both realistic and physically plausible. Unlike commercial solutions that often ignore physics or rely on pre-recorded animations, this approach ensures that interactions respect virtual environment rules (e.g., object weight, friction). This leads to a more immersive and believable user experience in VR/AR.

**Robustness to Hand Pose Estimation Errors:** A critical contribution of this work is its ability to effectively handle the inherent noise and inaccuracies of state-of-the-art hand pose estimators. The residual learning approach specifically tackles the problem of minor but crucial errors at contact points, which can otherwise lead to complete interaction failures in physics-driven simulations. This robustness is vital for real-world deployment of vision-based interaction systems.

**Preservation of User Intent and Visual Fidelity:** The residual nature of the learning, combined with the 3D hand pose estimation reward and imitation learning component, ensures that the virtual hand's motion remains visually close to the user's input. This maintains a strong sense of direct control and agency for the user, preventing the artificial or disconnected feel that can arise when systems significantly deviate from the user's intended movements.

**Novelty in Reinforcement Learning for Human-in-the-Loop Systems:** The integration of residual reinforcement learning with online user input, alongside adversarial imitation learning, represents a novel application of these techniques. Unlike purely autonomous RL agents, this "shared autonomy" approach leverages human intent as a strong prior, accelerating learning and focusing corrections where needed. The data generation scheme is also a practical innovation for training such a system in the absence of ideal ground-truth data.

**Potential for Broader Applications:**
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality:** Directly applicable to improving user interaction in VR/AR games, training simulations, and collaborative design environments.
- **Teleoperation and Robotics:** The principles of correcting noisy human input for physically accurate manipulation could be transferred to teleoperating dexterous robot hands, allowing for more intuitive and robust control.
- **Motion Reconstruction and Animation:** The framework could be used to reconstruct and refine human hand-object interaction sequences from video, leading to more realistic animations for digital characters.
- **Assistive Technologies:** The idea of a residual agent assisting with fine motor tasks could be extended to assistive technologies where a user's movements are imprecise.

**Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines several promising avenues for future work, including making the entire framework end-to-end (propagating gradients from the simulator to the HPE), integrating 6D object pose estimators, and generating more sophisticated synthetic data to bridge the sim-to-real gap. These directions indicate the foundational nature of this work for advancing physics-based vision and interaction.
