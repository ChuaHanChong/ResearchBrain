# RoboForge: Physically Optimized Text-guided Whole-Body Locomotion for Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> The RoboForge framework unifies text-guided motion generation with whole-body locomotion control for humanoid robots, employing a Physical Plausibility Optimization (PP-Opt) module to ensure generated motions are physically executable. This approach iteratively refines both the motion generator and the control policy, demonstrating robust performance on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Generated human motions from text prompts often lack physical plausibility for humanoid robots, leading to instability such as foot skating, floating, or ground penetration.
- Conventional multi-stage pipelines for motion transfer (decode-retarget-track) introduce cumulative errors, especially at contact transitions, hindering robust robot control.
- The inherent disconnect between generative models focusing on visual plausibility and robot controllers demanding physical executability results in physically inconsistent reference motions.

## Method

- **RoboForge** introduces a unified, latent-driven framework that directly maps text prompts to robot actions, bypassing the traditional retargeting stage.
- A novel Physical Plausibility Optimization (PP-Opt) module acts as a bidirectional interface, refining both the motion generator and the whole-body tracker based on physics simulation rewards.
- PP-Opt iteratively enhances physical plausibility by penalizing artifacts like foot skating and ground penetration, feeding back curated, high-quality data to fine-tune the generative model.

## Results

- The PP-Opt module effectively eliminated ground penetration (from **0.042** to **0.000**) and substantially reduced floating (from **1.744** to **0.713**) in generated motions.
- The physics-optimized tracking policy achieved higher success rates and lower tracking errors, improving IsaacLab success rate from **0.94** to **0.96** and MuJoCo from **0.63** to **0.71**.
- The implicit latent-driven control interface demonstrated superior performance over explicit retargeting, reducing mean per-joint position error from **0.23** to **0.11** in IsaacLab and enabling robust locomotion on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating motion generation and physics-based control into a bidirectional optimization loop significantly enhances the physical plausibility of generated motions and the robustness of the tracking policy.
> - A latent-driven, retarget-free interface for robot control provides more consistent guidance and minimizes error accumulation compared to explicit retargeting methods.
> - Iterative refinement using physically plausible data curated from simulation can progressively improve both the generative model's output quality and the tracking policy's performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "RoboForge" addresses a challenge at the intersection of generative artificial intelligence and robotics: effectively translating human-like motions generated from text descriptions into physically executable actions for humanoid robots. Recent advancements in generative models, particularly diffusion-based approaches, have demonstrated increasing capability in synthesizing fluid, diverse, and semantically coherent human motions from natural language prompts. These models prioritize visual plausibility, enabling applications in animation and virtual environments.

However, a significant discrepancy exists between visually plausible animation and physically feasible robotic execution. Humanoid robots operate under strict physical laws, requiring motions to adhere to balance, contact dynamics, torque limits, and hardware capabilities. Motions that appear natural in an animation often exhibit physical inconsistencies when transferred to a robot, such as foot skating, ground penetration, and loss of contact, leading to instability.

Traditional pipelines for converting generated motions to robot control typically involve a "decode–retarget–track" sequence. This multi-stage process is prone to introducing and accumulating errors, particularly during crucial contact transitions, where small misalignments can lead to amplified physical inconsistencies and control failures (e.g., root drift or locomotion gait collapse). The retargeting step, which adapts human motion data to a robot's specific kinematics, is frequently identified as a bottleneck, compromising kinematic elegance for physical infeasibility.

Furthermore, the scalability of robotics learning is often constrained by the scarcity and high cost of real-robot data. While latent-space diffusion models offer an efficient means to compress high-dimensional motion capture (MoCap) data, the latent representations they generate are not inherently guaranteed to be physically grounded for robotic platforms. Existing approaches to improve physical consistency in motion generation include physics-guided generation (incorporating contact and dynamics priors), simulator iterative optimization (using physics feedback to steer behaviors), and post-generation refinement (correcting motions after generation). A key trend in the field is to "close the loop" by using simulation-based refinement not only to correct samples but also to iteratively improve the generative model's distribution.

"RoboForge" contributes to this landscape by proposing a unified, latent-driven framework that integrates motion generation and physics-based control into a bidirectional optimization loop. It directly addresses the "retargeting bottleneck" by replacing explicit retargeting with a latent-to-action interface and explicitly grounds motion generation in physical laws through a novel Physical Plausibility Optimization (PP-Opt) module. This approach positions the work as an advancement in achieving robust, deployable, text-guided humanoid intelligence by harmonizing the "imagination" of generative models with the demands of physical execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the existing gap between natural language descriptions of motion and the successful physical execution of these motions by humanoid robots. This overarching goal is driven by several specific challenges and motivations:

1. **Addressing Physical Infeasibility of Generated Motions**: Current generative models for text-to-motion synthesis prioritize visual plausibility, often at the expense of physical feasibility for robots. This leads to common non-physical artifacts such as foot skating (unrealistic sliding of feet on the ground), floating (feet lifting off the ground when contact is expected), and ground penetration (feet passing through the ground plane). When these motions are applied to a physical robot, they result in dynamic instability and control failures. The motivation is to ensure that generated motions are dynamically stable and adhere to the robot's physical constraints (balance, contact, hardware limits).

2. **Overcoming the Retargeting Bottleneck**: The conventional pipeline of decoding motion, retargeting it to a specific robot, and then tracking it introduces systematic misalignments and errors. These errors are particularly problematic during discrete contact transitions, where they can accumulate rapidly and lead to significant issues like root drift and collapse of locomotion gaits. A key motivation is to develop a "retarget-free" pipeline that minimizes such incremental errors and provides more stable guidance for the robot.

3. **Integrating Generation and Control**: Existing paradigms frequently treat motion generation and robotic control as separate, sequential steps. This fragmented approach often results in a disconnect, where the generative model operates without direct feedback on physical executability, and the controller is left to compensate for physically inconsistent reference motions. The researchers are motivated to establish a unified framework where generation and control are bidirectionally coupled and jointly optimized, making physical plausibility a shared objective.

4. **Leveraging Latent Representations for Robust Control**: While latent-space diffusion models efficiently compress high-dimensional motion data, the generated latent representations are not inherently physically grounded. The motivation is to use these compact, information-rich latent signals directly to condition downstream control policies, thereby simplifying the inference process and reducing errors associated with explicit trajectory reconstruction. However, this also necessitates grounding these latents in the robot's physically plausible space.

5. **Mitigating Data Scarcity through Self-Improvement**: Real-robot data is expensive and difficult to acquire at scale. The authors are motivated to design a system that can iteratively refine itself, using physically optimized simulation rollouts to create high-quality, physically plausible motion data. This curated data can then be used to fine-tune the generative model, establishing a self-improving cycle that reduces the reliance on large volumes of pre-existing, physically validated robot-specific data.

To address these motivations, "RoboForge" sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To develop a unified, latent-driven framework that directly maps text prompts to robot actions, bypassing the traditional retargeting stage.
- To introduce the Physical Plausibility Optimization (PP-Opt) module as a bidirectional interface to refine both the motion generator and the control policy, subject to physical constraints.
- To enable the motion generator to learn a more physically plausible latent distribution by feeding back reward-optimized simulation data.
- To train a controller capable of executing latent-conditioned behaviors with dynamic integrity.
- To demonstrate robust, text-guided whole-body locomotion on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1) and validate its performance against conventional methods in physics simulators.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "RoboForge" framework is an end-to-end solution designed to unify text-to-latent generation, physics-based optimization, and latent-driven tracking for humanoid robots. It consists of four key modules: Motion Generation, Physical Plausibility Optimization (PP-Opt), Training Pipeline, and Sim-to-Real Deployment.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The system receives proprioceptive observations `o_t` from the robot and a motion latent `l_t` from the motion generator at each control step `t`. A teacher policy, leveraging privileged information, proprioception, and reference motion data, outputs a control action `a_t` for simulation. The student diffusion policy is then distilled from the teacher's behavior using DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) for robust control during inference with deployable observations. The central questions are whether an implicit, latent-driven inference pipeline can replace explicit retargeting and whether physics-based optimization can systematically improve both the motion generator and the tracking policy.

#### 3.2. Motion Latent Diffusion-Based Human Motion Generation

This module is based on existing latent diffusion models (specifically Chen et al. 2023) and is trained on HumanML3D and KIT-ML datasets. Its purpose is to produce a motion latent representation `z` from a text condition `τ` that can be consumed by the downstream control policy.
1. **Latent Space Learning**: An encoder-decoder objective is used to learn an expressive and reconstructable latent space. The encoder compresses a motion sequence `x_1:T` into `z`, and the decoder reconstructs the motion to ensure semantic consistency.
2. **Conditional Diffusion Model**: With the encoder frozen, a Transformer-based denoising network is trained to learn the conditional distribution `p(z | τ)`. This involves a forward noising process in the latent space and a reverse generative process that predicts noise conditioned on the text `τ`.
At inference time, the model starts from Gaussian noise and iteratively denoises it to obtain a sampled motion latent `ˆz`, which embodies the semantics of the input text `τ`.

#### 3.3. Physical Plausibility Optimization Module (PP-Opt)

The PP-Opt module is the core innovation, establishing a bidirectional interface between the Motion Generator (MG) and the whole-body tracker. It uses physics simulation to enhance the physical plausibility of motions and iteratively refines both components.

- **Forward Optimization of the Tracker**:
 - PP-Opt optimizes the tracker as a control policy within a simulator. This policy architecture is shared with the teacher tracker used for DAgger distillation.
 - A sequence-level physical plausibility objective reward function `J(ξ)` is defined, which is the sum of per-frame objectives `J_t(ξ)` for an executed trajectory `ξ`.
 - `J_t(ξ)` penalizes three common non-physical artifacts:
 - **Foot Skating (`r_t^sk(ξ)`):** Penalizes foot displacement when a foot is expected to be in contact with the ground.
 - **Floating (`r_t^fl(ξ)`):** Penalizes instances where a foot is above the ground despite being expected to be in contact.
 - **Ground Penetration (`r_t^pen(ξ)`):** Penalizes cases where a foot falls below the ground plane.
 - Reinforcement learning is employed within a closed-loop physics simulation to optimize the whole-body tracker, conditioned on the MG's latent outputs. This process yields a tracker that executes motion intents with reduced non-physical behaviors.

- **Motion Quality Control and Backward Fine-tune**:
 - In the backward direction, PP-Opt fine-tunes the upstream motion generator using refined, high-quality motion data.
 - After the forward refinement by the tracker, motion quality control is applied to curate the fine-tuning dataset for MG.
 - For each motion clip, the average `E_mpjpe` (mean per-joint position error) between the motion before and after physical refinement is computed.
 - Motions with `E_mpjpe` below a fixed acceptance threshold `η = 0.5` are considered stable and retained. Motions exceeding this threshold or involving non-grounded behaviors (e.g., object interactions, swimming) are discarded to maintain a focus on flat-ground locomotion with robust contact.
 - The Motion Generator (MG) is then fine-tuned on this filtered, high-quality motion set. This biases MG's generative distribution toward physically executable regions, leading to the sampling of higher-quality latents in subsequent iterations and reducing the burden on the downstream tracker.
The bidirectional nature of PP-Opt creates a self-improving cycle, where the tracker's physical plausibility is enhanced, and the MG learns to encode motions within a physically grounded latent space.

#### 3.4. Training Pipeline

This module implements the latent-driven whole-body tracking control through a teacher-student distillation approach combined with DAgger. A teacher policy, leveraging privileged information and reference motions, guides the training of a student diffusion policy that relies only on deployable observations for robust control.

#### 3.5. Sim-to-Real Deployment

The trained policy is exported to the ONNX format for deployment. This allows for sim-to-sim transfer and evaluation in environments like MuJoCo, as well as real-robot execution on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot using the Unitree SDK.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the RoboForge framework through ablation studies and comparisons, focusing on the necessity of PP-Opt, its iterative refinement capabilities, and the benefits of the latent-driven interface. Experiments were conducted using the HumanML3D and KIT-ML datasets, filtered for flat-ground, non-interactive scenarios. Evaluations employed standard text-to-motion metrics (R-Precision, FID, Diversity), physics-based plausibility metrics (Penetrate, Float, Skate), and tracking performance metrics (Success Rate, E_mpjpe, E_mpkpe).

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Physics-Optimized Motion Generation (MG with/without PP-Opt)

The integration of PP-Opt with the Motion Generator (MLD) demonstrated improvements in both generation quality and physical plausibility, without significantly compromising diversity.
- **Generation Quality**: R-Precision Top-1 increased from 0.523 (MLD) to 0.531 (MLD&PP-Opt), indicating improved text-to-motion consistency. The Frechet Inception Distance (FID) decreased from 0.484 to 0.462, suggesting closer distributional alignment with real data. Diversity (Div) remained largely stable (9.734 to 9.682).
- **Physical Plausibility**: PP-Opt effectively suppressed contact-related artifacts. Penetration was reduced from 0.042 to 0.000, effectively eliminating ground penetration. Floating decreased substantially from 1.744 to 0.713. Skating showed a minor improvement from 0.064 to 0.061.
These results indicate that PP-Opt refines physically implausible motion details while maintaining the semantic integrity and overall distribution of the generated motions.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Physics-Optimized Tracking Policy (Tracker with/without PP-Opt training optimization)

Training the tracking policy using motions optimized by MLD+PP-Opt consistently led to higher success rates and lower tracking errors across both simulated environments.
- **IsaacLab**: Success rate improved from 0.94 to 0.96. Mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe) decreased from 0.14 to 0.11, and mean per-keypoint position error (E_mpkpe) decreased from 0.11 to 0.09.
- **MuJoCo**: Gains were more pronounced. Success rate increased from 0.63 to 0.71. E_mpjpe reduced from 0.26 to 0.21, and E_mpkpe reduced from 0.24 to 0.20.
This suggests that PP-Opt enhances the tracker's robustness by providing a physically consistent training distribution, leading to better accuracy and reduced sensitivity to contact mismatches across simulators.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Multi-Cycle PP-Opt Refinement on MG

Iterative application of the PP-Opt closed-loop refinement mechanism yielded cumulative improvements, although with diminishing returns after a few cycles.
- **One-Round**: Established initial improvements (FID 0.462, R-Precision Top-1 0.531, Float 0.713, Skate 0.061).
- **Two-Round**: FID further dropped to 0.456, Top-1 increased to 0.535, Float decreased to 0.709, and Skate to 0.059.
- **Three-Round**: FID reached 0.454, Top-1 0.537, Float 0.707, and Skate 0.058.
Penetration remained at 0.000 throughout the cycles, and diversity was maintained around 9.7. This demonstrates that the bidirectional loop successfully cleans the training data for the MG, which in turn generates better samples for subsequent PP-Opt cycles, leading to progressive gains.

#### 4.4. Evaluation of Latent-Driven Pipeline (Implicit vs. Explicit Interface)

Comparison between RoboForge's implicit latent-driven control interface and a conventional explicit pipeline (using GMR retargeting) showed the superior performance of the implicit approach.
- **IsaacLab**: The implicit interface achieved a success rate of 0.96, compared to 0.91 for the explicit method. Tracking errors were significantly lower with the implicit approach: E_mpjpe decreased from 0.23 to 0.11, and E_mpkpe decreased from 0.17 to 0.09.
- **MuJoCo**: The performance gap was wider in sim-to-sim transfer. Success rate improved from 0.62 (explicit) to 0.71 (implicit). E_mpjpe reduced from 0.26 to 0.21, and E_mpkpe from 0.31 to 0.20.
These results indicate that explicit retargeting introduces cumulative errors that destabilize the tracking process. The implicit latent-driven method, by eliminating this intermediate step, provides more consistent guidance to the policy under physical constraints.

In addition to simulations, the paper states that extensive experiments on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot validated the bidirectional optimization, demonstrating robust, text-guided whole-body locomotion in diverse scenarios, including martial arts kicks and javelin throws.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "RoboForge" framework represents a notable contribution to the fields of robot control and generative AI, with several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Unified and Retarget-Free Control Pipeline**: The framework unifies text-guided motion generation with whole-body locomotion control through a latent-driven and retarget-free approach. This directly addresses the limitations of conventional multi-stage pipelines that suffer from accumulating errors, particularly during the retargeting process. By removing this bottleneck, "RoboForge" offers a more direct and robust pathway from high-level linguistic commands to robot actions.

2. **Physics-Optimized Motion Generation**: The introduction of the Physical Plausibility Optimization (PP-Opt) module allows the generative model to produce motions that are not only visually appealing but also inherently physically executable. This is achieved by embedding physical constraints and rewards into a bidirectional optimization loop, iteratively refining the motion generator to produce a physically grounded latent space. This capability reduces common non-physical artifacts such as floating, skating, and penetration, which are critical for stable robot operation.

3. **Self-Improving System Architecture**: The bidirectional coupling between the motion generator and the controller, facilitated by PP-Opt, establishes a self-improving cycle. Simulation feedback informs the refinement of generated motions, and this curated high-quality data is then used to fine-tune the generative model. This iterative process allows the system to continuously enhance both the quality of generated motions and the robustness of the tracking policy, potentially reducing the need for extensive manual data curation or human intervention.

4. **Enhanced Robustness and Stability in Robot Execution**: The experimental results demonstrate consistent improvements in tracking errors and success rates across different physics simulators (IsaacLab and MuJoCo) and on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1). The implicit latent-driven approach consistently outperforms explicit retargeting methods, indicating a more stable and precise control mechanism for complex, contact-rich whole-body locomotion.

5. **Accelerating Humanoid Robot Capabilities**: By enabling robots to perform diverse, dynamic, and physically plausible actions directly from natural language prompts, "RoboForge" could accelerate the development of advanced humanoid robot skills. This has implications for various applications, including:
 - **Human-Robot Interaction**: More intuitive and natural interaction with robots that can interpret and execute complex commands.
 - **Robot Skill Learning**: Facilitating the learning of novel behaviors without extensive manual programming or costly real-world demonstrations.
 - **Embodied AI**: Advancing the development of AI agents that can physically interact with and navigate complex environments based on high-level instructions.

In summary, "RoboForge" contributes a principled and effective method for grounding the "imagination" of generative models in the physical reality of humanoid robots, paving a path towards more intelligent and capable robotic systems controllable via natural language.
