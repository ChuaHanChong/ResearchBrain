# RoboForge: Physically Optimized Text-guided Whole-Body Locomotion for Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> The RoboForge framework unifies text-guided motion generation with whole-body locomotion control for humanoid robots, employing a Physical Plausibility Optimization (PP-Opt) module to ensure generated motions are physically executable. This approach iteratively refines both the motion generator and the control policy, demonstrating robust performance on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Generated human motions from text prompts often lack physical plausibility for humanoid robots, leading to instability such as foot skating, floating, or ground penetration.
- Conventional multi-stage pipelines for motion transfer (decode-retarget-track) introduce cumulative errors, especially at contact transitions, hindering robust robot control.
- The inherent disconnect between generative models focusing on visual plausibility and robot controllers demanding physical executability results in physically inconsistent reference motions.

## Method

- **RoboForge** introduces a unified, latent-driven framework that directly maps text prompts to robot actions, bypassing the traditional retargeting stage.
- A novel Physical Plausibility Optimization (PP-Opt) module acts as a bidirectional interface, refining both the motion generator and the whole-body tracker based on physics simulation rewards.
- PP-Opt iteratively enhances physical plausibility by penalizing artifacts like foot skating and ground penetration, feeding back curated, high-quality data to fine-tune the generative model.

## Results

- The PP-Opt module effectively eliminated ground penetration (from **0.042** to **0.000**) and substantially reduced floating (from **1.744** to **0.713**) in generated motions.
- The physics-optimized tracking policy achieved higher success rates and lower tracking errors, improving IsaacLab success rate from **0.94** to **0.96** and MuJoCo from **0.63** to **0.71**.
- The implicit latent-driven control interface demonstrated superior performance over explicit retargeting, reducing mean per-joint position error from **0.23** to **0.11** in IsaacLab and enabling robust locomotion on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating motion generation and physics-based control into a bidirectional optimization loop significantly enhances the physical plausibility of generated motions and the robustness of the tracking policy.
> - A latent-driven, retarget-free interface for robot control provides more consistent guidance and minimizes error accumulation compared to explicit retargeting methods.
> - Iterative refinement using physically plausible data curated from simulation can progressively improve both the generative model's output quality and the tracking policy's performance.
