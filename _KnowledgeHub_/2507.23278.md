# UniLiP: Adapting CLIP for Unified Multimodal Understanding, Generation and Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> UniLIP adapts the CLIP model for unified multimodal understanding, generation, and editing, introducing a two-stage training process with self-distillation to infuse pixel-level detail into CLIP features without degrading its semantic comprehension. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance across image reconstruction, understanding benchmarks like MME-P, and generative tasks including image editing on the ImgEdit benchmark, often surpassing larger and more complex models.

## Problem

- CLIP's features, while excellent for semantic understanding, inherently lack the fine-grained pixel details required for high-fidelity image reconstruction.
- Existing unified models either sacrifice CLIP's understanding capabilities for reconstruction fidelity (e.g., quantization-based) or yield inconsistent, computationally expensive generative results (e.g., diffusion decoders on frozen CLIP).
- Fixed-length query embeddings often create information bottlenecks in generative models, leading to loss of crucial visual details necessary for robust and consistent image editing.

## Method

- A ==two-stage training scheme with self-distillation== is applied to finetune CLIP for image reconstruction, ensuring pixel-level detail injection while preserving original semantic understanding by preventing catastrophic forgetting.
- A novel ==dual-condition architecture== is introduced for image generation and editing, leveraging both multimodal hidden states from a large language model and learnable query embeddings as conditions for the diffusion transformer.
- The UniLIP framework is built upon InternVL3 (InternViT as CLIP encoder) and ==SANA (a linear diffusion transformer)== with a pixel decoder initialized from DC-AE, trained on extensive datasets for broad applicability.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art image reconstruction among CLIP-based tokenizers with significantly lower rFID (**0.79** at 256x256) and higher PSNR/SSIM compared to previous methods.
- Preserved and improved CLIP's multimodal understanding, with UniLIP-1B scoring **1499** on MME-P, surpassing the frozen CLIP's **1492**, demonstrating enhanced detail capture without semantic degradation.
- Set new state-of-the-art in image generation (GenEval **0.90**, WISE **0.63**) and image editing (ImgEdit **3.94**), often outperforming larger unified models like OmniGen2 and BAGEL with competitive inference speeds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A carefully designed two-stage training scheme with self-distillation effectively allows CLIP to acquire high-fidelity reconstruction capabilities while remarkably preserving, and in some cases even enhancing, its original understanding performance.
> - Infusing pixel-level details into CLIP's visual representations can surprisingly improve its ability to answer detail-oriented understanding queries, suggesting a richer, more versatile internal representation.
> - The dual-condition architecture successfully decouples contextual extraction and reasoning (MLLM) from image synthesis (DiT), ensuring lossless information propagation crucial for consistent and high-fidelity image editing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "UniLIP" is a direct response to a critical divergence in the field of multimodal AI: the traditional separation between models optimized for image understanding and those designed for image generation. Historically, these two tasks have relied on distinct architectural paradigms and objectives:

- **Image Understanding:** Models like CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) excel at aligning visual features with textual semantics, making them ideal for tasks such as image-text retrieval, zero-shot classification, and visual question answering. They achieve this by learning robust, high-level semantic representations.
- **Image Generation:** Generative models, such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Vector Quantized VAEs (VQVAEs), and more recently, diffusion models, focus on reconstructing images from latent representations or generating novel images conditioned on text. These models prioritize pixel-level detail and visual fidelity.

The success of Large Language Models (LLMs) has spurred a movement towards "unified modeling" in multimodal domains, aiming to create single models capable of both understanding and generation. Early attempts to achieve this unification by combining VQVAE or VAE with LLMs (e.g., Team, 2024; Zhou et al., 2024) often suffered from poor understanding performance because their encoders struggled to capture rich semantics.

More recent efforts have focused on building unified tokenizers based on CLIP, leveraging its strong semantic capabilities. However, these CLIP-based unified methods face a fundamental trade-off:
- **Quantization-based methods (e.g., VILA-U, Tokenflow, DualViTok):** These approaches discretize CLIP features for reconstruction. While they improve generation, they often lead to information loss and "semantic degradation," compromising CLIP's original understanding performance.
- **Frozen CLIP with Diffusion Decoders (e.g., Emu2, BLIP3-o):** These methods freeze the original CLIP encoder to preserve its understanding capabilities and then train a separate diffusion decoder to reconstruct images from CLIP features. However, as CLIP features inherently lose pixel-level details (being optimized for semantics), the diffusion decoder often struggles to produce "consistent reconstructions," leading to inaccuracies in generated or edited images. This design also adds computational cost and complexity.

UniLIP directly addresses this core challenge by proposing a novel framework that *adapts CLIP itself* to gain high-fidelity reconstruction abilities *without sacrificing its original comprehension performance*. It aims to establish CLIP's continuous features as the optimal choice for understanding tasks while also achieving highly competitive performance in generation and editing. By overcoming the limitations of previous unified approaches—namely, the trade-off between semantic preservation and reconstruction quality—UniLIP represents a significant step towards truly general-purpose multimodal foundation models that can seamlessly handle a wide array of visual-linguistic tasks within a single, efficient architecture. This positions UniLIP as a leader in bridging the gap between perception and generation, enhancing the versatility and efficiency of multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the UniLIP project is to develop a unified framework that extends the capabilities of CLIP to encompass multimodal understanding, generation, and editing, thereby addressing the limitations of existing approaches that struggle to balance these tasks.

The key motivations driving this research can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcoming the Understanding-Reconstruction Trade-off in CLIP-based Unified Models:** While CLIP is unparalleled in visual-language understanding due to its robust semantic representations, its features inherently lack the fine-grained pixel details necessary for high-fidelity image reconstruction. Previous attempts to adapt CLIP for unified tasks either:
 - Compromised its semantic understanding by discretizing features for reconstruction (leading to "semantic degradation").
 - Achieved inconsistent reconstructions by relying on external diffusion decoders to generate images from detail-deficient CLIP features (resulting in "inconsistent reconstructions").
 The primary motivation for UniLIP is to break this trade-off, enabling CLIP to perform both roles optimally within a single framework.

2. **Endowing CLIP with High-Fidelity Reconstruction Abilities:** The authors explicitly aim to develop a method that progressively instills pixel-level reconstruction capabilities into CLIP. This involves injecting fine-grained details into CLIP's representations without causing "catastrophic forgetting" of its original comprehension performance.

3. **Enhancing Reasoning and Consistency in Generation and Editing:** For unified models to be truly effective, they must not only understand and generate but also do so with high reasoning capability and consistency, especially in complex tasks like image editing. Existing query-based generation methods (e.g., MetaQuery) use fixed-length queries, which can lead to information loss and inconsistencies when adapting to editing tasks that require retaining rich details from reference images. A motivation is to address this bottleneck and enable precise, coherent editing.

4. **Establishing CLIP as a Unified Visual Encoder:** Ultimately, the researchers seek to solidify CLIP's position as the foundational choice for multimodal AI, not just for understanding, but as a "unified visual encoder" that can seamlessly bridge understanding with robust generation and editing tasks. This implies creating a model whose continuous features are versatile enough to serve diverse applications without needing separate, specialized encoders.

In essence, UniLIP is motivated by the desire to build a more capable, efficient, and truly unified multimodal foundation model based on the strengths of CLIP, resolving its inherent limitations in generative tasks while preserving its core competence in understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniLIP introduces a comprehensive methodology comprising a novel two-stage training scheme for adapting CLIP, a dual-condition architecture for generation and editing, and a three-stage overall training strategy.

#### 3.1. Finetuning CLIP for Image Reconstruction

The core of UniLIP's innovation lies in its ability to inject pixel-level details into CLIP features without degrading its semantic understanding. This is achieved through a **two-stage training scheme with self-distillation**:

- **Architecture:** UniLIP employs an autoencoder-like structure, pairing the CLIP encoder with a pixel decoder (D_pix). A projection layer (h_φ) aligns the feature dimensions between CLIP and D_pix. The process is defined as: `I_hat = D_pix(h_φ(CLIP(I)))`. For the CLIP encoder, UniLIP utilizes InternViT from InternVL3, and the pixel decoder is adapted from DC-AE.

- **Stage 1: Aligning the Pixel Decoder with CLIP:**
 - **Objective:** To enable the pixel decoder to learn reconstruction based on the *frozen* CLIP features. This stage helps locate and leverage the weak pixel-level cues already present in CLIP features.
 - **Process:** CLIP and its projection layers are frozen. Only the pixel decoder (D_pix) and projection h_φ are trained.
 - **Loss Function:** `L_stage1 = L_MSE + L_LPIPS` (Mean Squared Error for pixel-wise reconstruction and LPIPS for perceptual similarity).
 - **Initialization:** The pixel decoder is initialized with pre-trained weights, while the projection is randomly initialized.

- **Stage 2: Refining CLIP with Self-Distillation:**
 - **Objective:** To empower CLIP itself to capture more fine-grained details for improved reconstruction, while critically preventing "catastrophic forgetting" of its original understanding capabilities.
 - **Process:** CLIP, D_pix, and h_φ are all enabled for training.
 - **Self-Distillation:** A crucial self-distillation loss is introduced to constrain feature distribution changes. The original (frozen) CLIP acts as a teacher, guiding the fine-tuned CLIP features to remain close to their original distribution.
 - **Loss Function:** `L_stage2 = L_MSE + L_LPIPS + λ∥F_orig − F_ft∥^2_2`, where `F_orig` are features from the frozen CLIP teacher and `F_ft` are from the fine-tuned CLIP student.
 - **Parameter Constraint:** The learning rate for CLIP parameters is set to 0.1 times the global learning rate to further restrict updates and preserve original abilities. Adversarial training is also introduced after a certain number of steps to enhance reconstruction fidelity.

This two-stage approach effectively balances the trade-off, allowing CLIP to learn pixel details with minimal disruption to its semantic representations.

#### 3.2. Dual Conditions for Image Generation and Editing

UniLIP extends the MetaQuery framework for generation and editing, addressing the limitations of fixed-length query embeddings in capturing rich visual information for editing.

- **Problem Identification:** Fixed-length query embeddings, while useful for knowledge-augmented text-to-image generation by compressing MLLM reasoning, are insufficient for editing tasks. Editing requires retaining detailed information from one or more reference images, which fixed tokens cannot effectively encode, leading to information loss and inconsistencies.

- **Dual-Condition Architecture:** To resolve this, UniLIP proposes using *both* the **multimodal hidden states** of the MLLM and the **learnable query embeddings** as conditions for the diffusion transformer (DiT).
 - **Multimodal Hidden States:** Provide rich contextual details, especially vital for preserving pixel-level information from reference images in editing.
 - **Query Embeddings:** Leverage the MLLM's powerful reasoning abilities to implicitly define target image content, enhancing prompt alignment in generation.
 - **Mechanism:** These dual conditions are concatenated and passed through a connector to serve as cross-attention conditions for the DiT.

This architecture ensures "lossless information propagation" during task decoupling, enabling UniLIP to exploit its advantages in both high-quality generation and consistent editing.

#### 3.3. Overall Training Schemes

To construct a unified model capable of understanding, generation, and editing, UniLIP adopts a three-stage training strategy:

- **Stage 1: Connector Training:** The connector, which bridges the MLLM and the DiT, is trained to align MLLM outputs with the DiT's conditional feature space. The MLLM and DiT are frozen.
- **Stage 2: Unified Generation and Editing Training:** The connector and DiT are trained on large-scale generation and editing datasets. The MLLM remains frozen, eliminating the need for expensive understanding task training.
- **Stage 3: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** High-quality instruction-tuning data is used to enhance generation fidelity and prompt alignment for both tasks. Connector and DiT are optimized, MLLM remains frozen.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Model Variants:** UniLIP-1B (InternVL3-1B + SANA-0.6B) and UniLIP-3B (InternVL3-2B + SANA-1.6B).
- **Datasets:** BLIP3-o pretraining data (27M Qwen2.5-VL-7B recaptioned samples, 5M CC12M, 4M JourneyDB synthesized images) for generation; GPT-4o for instruction tuning; GPT-Image-Edit-1.5M for editing pretraining; ShareGPT-4o-Image for editing instruction tuning.
- **Training Parameters:** Specific batch sizes, learning rates, and resolutions are detailed for each stage (e.g., 4 A100 GPUs, batch size 64 for reconstruction, 512 for generation/editing). Adversarial training and classifier-free guidance are applied.

This comprehensive methodology allows UniLIP to adapt CLIP into a robust, unified multimodal foundation model, capable of excelling across diverse tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniLIP demonstrates significant advancements across multimodal understanding, generation, and editing, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art models, often with smaller parameter counts.

#### 4.1. Image Reconstruction Performance

- **Superior Fidelity:** UniLIP achieves state-of-the-art CLIP-based reconstruction performance. For example, at 448x448 resolution, UniLIP scores an rFID of 0.31 and a PSNR of 24.62, significantly surpassing Emu2 (rFID 3.27, PSNR 13.49).
- **Efficiency:** UniLIP maintains a higher downsampling ratio (32x) compared to other methods like Tokenflow (16x), indicating more efficient image representation while retaining quality.
- **Mechanism:** This superior performance validates the effectiveness of the two-stage training scheme and self-distillation, which allows UniLIP to capture pixel-level details without compromising the semantic integrity of CLIP features. Qualitative results clearly show UniLIP's ability to reconstruct images with high detail and consistency, avoiding distortions seen in quantization methods or inconsistencies from diffusion decoders.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Understanding

- **Preservation and Improvement:** A key finding is that UniLIP's reconstruction training not only preserves CLIP's original understanding capabilities but often *improves* them. For instance, UniLIP-1B achieves an MME-P score of 1499, slightly higher than the original InternVL3 (Frozen CLIP) at 1492.
- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) for Unified Models:** UniLIP achieves SOTA understanding performance among unified models of similar scales on various benchmarks including MME-P, MMBench, MMMU, MM-Vet, SEED, AI2D, and MMVP. It significantly outperforms previous CLIP-quantization methods (e.g., Tar, VILA-U, TokenFlow) despite being smaller. This suggests that incorporating reconstruction training can refine CLIP's feature extraction by enabling it to capture more subtle visual details relevant for complex comprehension tasks.

#### 4.3. Image Generation

- **Enhanced Prompt Alignment:** UniLIP demonstrates superior image generation capabilities, achieving SOTA scores on the GenEval (0.90) and WISE (0.63) benchmarks. GenEval assesses control over attributes like counting and position, while WISE evaluates understanding of complex semantics and world knowledge.
- **Outperforming Larger Models:** UniLIP (1B and 3B parameters) notably outperforms larger unified models such as BAGEL (7B) and Uniworld-V1 (12B) in generation. This highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of UniLIP's architecture and training.
- **Semantic Richness:** The strong performance is attributed to UniLIP's rich semantic features, which lead to better alignment with text prompts. Qualitative examples show UniLIP's ability to render complex positional relationships, clear faces, and artistic styles more accurately than competitors.

#### 4.4. Image Editing

- **High Fidelity and Consistency:** UniLIP achieves a highly competitive overall score of 3.94 on the ImgEdit benchmark, significantly surpassing models like OmniGen2 (3.44) and UniWorld-V1 (3.26).
- **Dual-Condition Architecture Effectiveness:** This success is attributed to UniLIP's ability to encode both pixel-level details and high-level semantics, combined with the dual-condition architecture that prevents information loss and maintains consistency in unedited regions. Qualitative results showcase UniLIP's precise edits and seamless integration of changes.

#### 4.5. Efficiency

- **Faster Inference:** UniLIP shows significantly faster image generation inference speed compared to other unified models, including autoregressive and multi-stage diffusion methods (e.g., 1.4s for UniLIP-1B vs. 8.5s for Janus-Pro-1B or 5.1s for BLIP3-o-4B). This is due to its streamlined generation process directly from its features.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies Confirming Design Choices

- **Reconstruction Training:** Ablations confirm that the two-stage training and self-distillation are critical for preserving understanding while enabling reconstruction. Directly finetuning CLIP for reconstruction leads to severe understanding degradation. The two-stage approach also ensures training stability.
- **Dual-Condition Architecture:** This architecture is crucial, as using only query embeddings performs poorly on editing (ImgEdit 3.38) and slightly worse on knowledge-driven generation (WISE 0.52) compared to the dual-condition approach (ImgEdit 3.81, WISE 0.56). This validates the need for both rich multimodal hidden states and MLLM reasoning.
- **Unified Encoder Superiority:** Ablations demonstrate that UniLIP serves as a more capable unified encoder than using plain CLIP or VAE for reference/target images, leading to better generation and editing quality.

In summary, UniLIP successfully addresses the limitations of previous CLIP-based unified models by achieving a remarkable balance between semantic understanding and pixel-level reconstruction. Its innovative training and architectural designs enable it to set new state-of-the-art benchmarks across diverse multimodal tasks with impressive efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniLIP represents a significant leap forward in the field of multimodal artificial intelligence, with profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Advancing True Multimodal Unification:** The most significant contribution of UniLIP is its successful realization of a truly unified framework that seamlessly integrates visual understanding, generation, and editing. By overcoming the long-standing trade-off between semantic comprehension and pixel-level detail reconstruction, UniLIP moves beyond modular pipelines or compromised unified models. This pushes the boundaries of what a single foundation model can achieve, paving the way for more holistic and general-purpose multimodal AI systems.

2. **Expanding CLIP's Role as a Foundation Model:** Historically, CLIP has been recognized as a powerful understanding model. UniLIP effectively expands CLIP's utility, transforming it into a versatile, reconstructable visual encoder suitable for both discriminative and generative tasks. This elevates CLIP's status as a core foundation model, demonstrating that its continuous features can serve as an optimal choice for a broader range of applications, reducing the need for separate, specialized encoders.

3. **Enhancing Multimodal Interaction and Control:** The superior instruction following and edit fidelity demonstrated by UniLIP have immediate practical implications. Its ability to generate images that closely align with complex text prompts and perform precise, consistent edits will greatly benefit content creation, design tools, and interactive AI systems. Users can expect more intuitive and powerful control over visual outputs through natural language.

4. **Efficiency and Scalability:** Achieving state-of-the-art performance with models of relatively smaller parameter counts (1B and 3B) compared to some larger competitors (e.g., BAGEL 7B, Uniworld-V1 12B) highlights UniLIP's efficiency. Furthermore, its faster inference speed makes it more viable for real-time applications and reduces computational costs associated with deploying complex multimodal AI. This efficiency is crucial for the broader adoption and scaling of such powerful models.

5. **Robustness and Consistency in Editing:** The dual-condition architecture is particularly impactful for image editing. By addressing the information bottleneck of fixed-length queries, UniLIP ensures that edits are not only semantically accurate but also visually consistent with unedited regions and reference images. This level of consistency is critical for professional content creation and avoids common artifacts or semantic drift seen in less robust editing models.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** UniLIP's success opens new avenues for research. It encourages further exploration into how foundational understanding models can be augmented with generative capabilities without compromise. Future work might focus on scaling UniLIP to even larger model sizes and higher resolutions to tackle current limitations like text rendering, or investigating its application in other multimodal domains beyond images and text, such as video or 3D.

In essence, UniLIP provides a compelling proof-of-concept for a new generation of unified multimodal models. By elegantly resolving fundamental architectural and training challenges, it lays a strong foundation for more capable, efficient, and versatile AI systems that can truly perceive, reason, and create across modalities.
