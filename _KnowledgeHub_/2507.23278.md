# UniLiP: Adapting CLIP for Unified Multimodal Understanding, Generation and Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> UniLIP adapts the CLIP model for unified multimodal understanding, generation, and editing, introducing a two-stage training process with self-distillation to infuse pixel-level detail into CLIP features without degrading its semantic comprehension. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance across image reconstruction, understanding benchmarks like MME-P, and generative tasks including image editing on the ImgEdit benchmark, often surpassing larger and more complex models.

## Problem

- CLIP's features, while excellent for semantic understanding, inherently lack the fine-grained pixel details required for high-fidelity image reconstruction.
- Existing unified models either sacrifice CLIP's understanding capabilities for reconstruction fidelity (e.g., quantization-based) or yield inconsistent, computationally expensive generative results (e.g., diffusion decoders on frozen CLIP).
- Fixed-length query embeddings often create information bottlenecks in generative models, leading to loss of crucial visual details necessary for robust and consistent image editing.

## Method

- A ==two-stage training scheme with self-distillation== is applied to finetune CLIP for image reconstruction, ensuring pixel-level detail injection while preserving original semantic understanding by preventing catastrophic forgetting.
- A novel ==dual-condition architecture== is introduced for image generation and editing, leveraging both multimodal hidden states from a large language model and learnable query embeddings as conditions for the diffusion transformer.
- The UniLIP framework is built upon InternVL3 (InternViT as CLIP encoder) and ==SANA (a linear diffusion transformer)== with a pixel decoder initialized from DC-AE, trained on extensive datasets for broad applicability.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art image reconstruction among CLIP-based tokenizers with significantly lower rFID (**0.79** at 256x256) and higher PSNR/SSIM compared to previous methods.
- Preserved and improved CLIP's multimodal understanding, with UniLIP-1B scoring **1499** on MME-P, surpassing the frozen CLIP's **1492**, demonstrating enhanced detail capture without semantic degradation.
- Set new state-of-the-art in image generation (GenEval **0.90**, WISE **0.63**) and image editing (ImgEdit **3.94**), often outperforming larger unified models like OmniGen2 and BAGEL with competitive inference speeds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A carefully designed two-stage training scheme with self-distillation effectively allows CLIP to acquire high-fidelity reconstruction capabilities while remarkably preserving, and in some cases even enhancing, its original understanding performance.
> - Infusing pixel-level details into CLIP's visual representations can surprisingly improve its ability to answer detail-oriented understanding queries, suggesting a richer, more versatile internal representation.
> - The dual-condition architecture successfully decouples contextual extraction and reasoning (MLLM) from image synthesis (DiT), ensuring lossless information propagation crucial for consistent and high-fidelity image editing.
