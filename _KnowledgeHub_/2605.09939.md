# Neural Distance-Guided Path Integral Control for Tractor-Trailer Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Davis and Zhejiang University developed a control framework for autonomous tractor-trailer navigation in cluttered agricultural settings. It integrates a neural encoder, which rapidly computes signed distances between the articulated vehicle and raw LiDAR points, with a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller. This system generated dynamically feasible, collision-free trajectories at approximately 30 Hz in simulation, achieving an MSE below 1e-5 for distance estimation.

## Problem

- Autonomous navigation for articulated tractor-trailer systems faces challenges due to complex multi-body dynamics, articulation constraints, and risks like jackknifing.
- Agricultural environments are cluttered with narrow passages, static and dynamic obstacles, requiring sophisticated real-time collision avoidance.
- Existing collision avoidance methods are often too slow for real-time exact geometric computation, rely on inaccurate vehicle geometry simplifications, or require costly precomputed maps that struggle in dynamic environments.

## Method

- A geometric neural encoder is developed to provide fast and accurate signed-distance estimates between the articulated vehicle (represented as convex polygons) and raw LiDAR point clouds.
- This encoder approximates the solution of a dual optimization problem for point-to-polygon distance, overcoming computational bottlenecks of exact methods.
- The learned distance estimates are integrated into a ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) control framework, which uses these distances in its cost function to enable perception-driven local planning and collision avoidance.

## Results

- The geometric neural encoders achieved high accuracy, with Mean Squared Error (MSE) dropping below 1e-5 for both dual variables and point-to-polygon distances on the test set.
- In simulation, the integrated MPPI controller successfully navigated the tractor-trailer through dense obstacle fields, consistently avoiding collisions and maintaining safe clearance.
- The system generated dynamically feasible trajectories and operated at approximately 30 Hz in simulation, demonstrating its potential for real-time application in complex agricultural settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Approximating the dual formulation of point-to-polygon distance with a neural encoder offers a computationally efficient way to obtain accurate real-time geometric distance information from raw sensor data for complex articulated bodies.
> - Combining a high-fidelity geometric distance estimation with a sampling-based control framework like MPPI allows for robust handling of nonlinear vehicle dynamics and complex, potentially discontinuous, collision avoidance costs.
> - Directly using raw sensor data for collision checking via a neural encoder enables map-free navigation, enhancing adaptability to dynamic and partially unknown environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation for articulated tractor-trailer systems is a critical area of research, particularly in agricultural settings where such vehicles are extensively used for tasks such as crop transport, implement towing, and maneuvering within complex environments like orchards, vineyards, and open farmlands. These systems present significant challenges due to their multi-body coupling, off-axle hitching, articulation constraints, and inherent nonlinear dynamics, which can lead to phenomena like jackknifing or degraded reversing performance. The complexity is further amplified in agricultural environments characterized by narrow passages, static obstacles (e.g., tree rows, storage bins), and dynamic elements (e.g., farm workers, other machinery). Ensuring real-time, accurate collision avoidance and dynamic feasibility is therefore essential for their safe and reliable autonomous operation.

Prior research in this field has explored various aspects of tractor-trailer control. Studies have addressed stability and controllability, including issues like jackknifing and reversing performance, in both agricultural and industrial contexts (David and Manivannan, 2014). Approaches such as optimal control for headland turning (Oksanen and Visala, 2004), hybrid control strategies (Altafini et al., 2002), model predictive control (MPC) for coordinated regulation (Backman et al., 2010), and safety-governed articulation control for reverse motion (Hejase et al., 2018) have been investigated for path tracking and control. More recently, reinforcement learning has been applied to derive robust control policies (Kang et al., 2024), and efforts have focused on minimizing swept paths during reversing maneuvers (Liu et al., 2019). However, a common limitation of these methods is their primary focus on path following and control within simplified geometric environments, often without explicitly addressing dense obstacle fields encountered in real-world scenarios.

Trajectory planning methods that integrate environmental obstacles have also seen advancements. Frameworks such as progressively constrained nonlinear programming (Li et al., 2019) and hierarchical optimization strategies (Li et al., 2021) have demonstrated safe navigation capabilities. Nevertheless, these approaches typically rely on accurate prior maps and exhibit high computational demands, which can limit their applicability in dynamic settings. More recent developments, like the trajectory deformation method by Xu et al. (2025), offer improved efficiency but require the maintenance of an Euclidean Signed Distance Field (ESDF). The computational cost of updating ESDFs in dynamic or partially occluded agricultural environments can be prohibitive.

A significant challenge in navigating articulated vehicles is the accurate and computationally efficient computation of distances between the complex vehicle body and surrounding obstacles. Traditional solutions frequently simplify vehicle geometry (e.g., using circles or inflated bounding boxes), leading to inaccurate distance estimates and potentially overly conservative or even infeasible navigation, especially in tightly constrained spaces. While safe-corridor based methods (Wei et al., 2025) provide safety, they are map-dependent and can be restrictive. Optimization-based Collision Avoidance (OBCA) (Zhang et al., 2020) offers exact polygon-to-polygon distance queries but is often too slow for real-time applications when many obstacles are present.

This work addresses the identified limitations by developing a map-free, accurate, and computationally efficient geometric distance model that operates directly on raw sensor data. It aims to bridge the gap between precise geometric reasoning for articulated bodies and real-time motion planning in dynamic, cluttered agricultural environments. By integrating a novel neural encoder for distance estimation with a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control framework, the research seeks to enable robust and safe navigation for tractor-trailer systems, directly utilizing raw sensor information for collision avoidance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the critical need for advanced autonomous navigation capabilities for tractor-trailer systems in agricultural operations. These systems are fundamental to modern agriculture but pose considerable challenges for autonomous control due to their inherent characteristics and the operational environment.

**Motivation:**

1. **Complexity of Tractor-Trailer Dynamics**: Articulated tractor-trailer vehicles exhibit complex nonlinear dynamics, including multi-body coupling, off-axle hitching, and articulation angle constraints. These factors make control and prediction more difficult compared to single-body vehicles, with risks such as jackknifing during reverse maneuvers.
2. **Challenging Agricultural Environments**: Agricultural settings are often cluttered, featuring narrow passages between crop rows or trees, static obstacles (e.g., machinery, structures, natural features), and dynamic elements (e.g., farm personnel, other moving vehicles). These environments demand sophisticated collision avoidance capabilities and responsive control.
3. **Limitations of Existing Collision Avoidance Methods**: Traditional methods for obstacle avoidance often rely on geometric simplifications of the vehicle or environment, which can lead to imprecise distance estimates, overly conservative trajectories, or even infeasible paths in constrained spaces. Methods that provide exact distance computations, like optimization-based approaches, are frequently too computationally intensive for real-time application with dense obstacle fields. Map-dependent approaches, such as those relying on precomputed distance fields, struggle in dynamic or partially unknown environments where map updates are costly or impractical.
4. **Need for Real-time, Map-free Geometric Reasoning**: To achieve truly autonomous and safe navigation in dynamic agricultural contexts, there is a clear requirement for a method that can rapidly and accurately compute minimum distances between the full articulated vehicle body and environmental obstacles directly from raw sensor data, without needing a pre-built map. This would enable real-time geometric reasoning, which is essential for predictive collision avoidance and smooth control.

### 3. Key Objectives

Based on these motivations, the research establishes three primary objectives:

1. **Develop a Geometric Neural Encoder**: The first objective is to design and implement a geometric neural encoder. This encoder is intended to provide fast and accurate signed-distance estimates between the complex, articulated tractor-trailer geometries and raw LiDAR point clouds. The aim is to enable real-time geometric reasoning for collision avoidance without requiring a pre-built map, thereby addressing the limitations of traditional geometric simplification or map-dependent approaches.
2. **Integrate Distance Estimates into a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) Framework**: The second objective involves integrating these learned distance estimates into an MPPI control framework. This integration aims to facilitate perception-driven local planning and collision avoidance for articulated agricultural vehicles. MPPI is chosen for its suitability in handling nonlinear dynamics and complex, potentially discontinuous, cost structures arising from distance-based penalties, which are characteristic of collision avoidance in cluttered environments.
3. **Evaluate Performance in Simulation**: The third objective is to evaluate the proposed pipeline in simulated environments. The goal is to demonstrate its effectiveness in generating dynamically feasible and safe trajectories for navigating tractor-trailer systems through cluttered and complex agricultural scenarios, thereby validating the combined efficacy of the neural encoder and the MPPI controller.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates a novel geometric neural encoder for real-time distance computation with a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control framework to achieve autonomous and safe navigation for tractor-trailer systems.

#### 4.1. Tractor-Trailer Kinematic Model

The tractor-trailer system is modeled using a simplified kinematic formulation. Given that agricultural machinery typically operates at low speeds, a no-slip kinematic bicycle model is adopted as a first-order approximation. This model represents the geometric coupling between the tractor and a single off-axle semi-trailer.

The system's state is defined by the tractor rear-axle position $(x, y)$, tractor heading $\theta$, and the articulation angle $\phi = \theta_1 - \theta$, where $\theta_1$ is the trailer heading. The continuous-time kinematic equations define the evolution of these states based on the tractor's longitudinal velocity $v$ and steering angle $\psi$. Key geometric parameters include the tractor wheelbase $L_0$, hitch offset $L_h$ from the tractor rear axle, and the distance $L_1$ from the hitch point to the trailer axle. The trailer's pose $(x_1, y_1, \theta_1)$ is derived from the tractor's pose and articulation angle. For control purposes, the state is augmented to include $v$ and $\psi$, and the control inputs become longitudinal acceleration $a$ and steering rate $\zeta$, representing the derivatives of $v$ and $\psi$, respectively.

#### 4.2. Geometric Neural Encoder for Efficient Distance Computation

To overcome the limitations of traditional collision checking methods, a geometric neural encoder is developed to provide fast and accurate minimum distance computations between the articulated vehicle body and raw LiDAR point clouds.

- **Vehicle Body Representation**: The tractor and trailer bodies are represented as collections of convex polygons, where each polygon $V_i$ is defined by its half-space representation: $V_i = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid G_i x \le h_i\}$. The polygons are transformed into the global frame based on the current system state.
- **Distance Formulation**: The minimum distance from a polygon $E_i$ to a set of LiDAR obstacle points $O_t = \{o_1^t, \dots, o_M^t\}$ is defined as the minimum of exact point-to-polygon distances $D(E_i^t, o_j^t)$ over all points $o_j^t$. The point-to-polygon distance is typically found by solving a quadratic program.
- **Dual Optimization and Neural Approximation**: Leveraging strong duality, the point-to-polygon distance computation can be reformulated as a dual optimization problem involving dual variables $\mu_i^t \in \mathbb{R}^{l_i}$ (identifying closest polygon edges) and $\lambda_i^t \in \mathbb{R}^2$ (encoding the distance vector direction). Solving this optimization for many LiDAR points is slow. Therefore, a neural encoder is trained to approximate the solution of a penalized version of this dual problem.
- **Neural Encoder Architecture**: The network architecture consists of fully connected layers with layer normalization and tanh/ReLU activations. It processes a batch of transformed obstacle points (expressed in the polygon's local frame) and outputs the estimated dual variable $\hat{\mu}_i^t$. The corresponding $\hat{\lambda}_i^t$ can then be analytically recovered from the equality constraint of the dual formulation. The encoder is polygon-specific, meaning a separate encoder is trained for each convex polygon composing the tractor and trailer.
- **Training Procedure**:
 - A dataset is generated for each polygon by sampling 100,000 random points.
 - For each sampled point, the ground-truth optimal dual variables $\mu_{i,j}^*$ are obtained by solving the dual optimization problem using CVXPY with the ECOS solver.
 - The neural encoder is trained in a supervised manner using an Adam optimizer. The loss function combines an L2 norm on the difference between predicted and ground-truth dual variables, and an L2 norm on the difference between predicted and ground-truth induced signed distances. This ensures accuracy in both the vector-valued dual variables and the scalar distance expression.

#### 4.3. Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) Control

MPPI is chosen as the control framework due to its ability to handle nonlinear dynamics, discontinuous cost functions, and its gradient-free nature, which is suitable for the complex collision penalties derived from raw distance queries.

- **Control Loop**: MPPI operates in a receding horizon fashion. At each time step, given the current state $s_t$ and a nominal control sequence $U_t = \{u_t, \dots, u_{t+N}\}$, where $N$ is the prediction horizon:
 1. **Perturbed Control Sequences**: $K$ perturbed control sequences $u_{\tau}^{(k)}$ are generated by adding Gaussian exploration noise $w_{\tau}^{(k)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_w)$ to the nominal controls.
 2. **Trajectory Rollouts**: Each perturbed control sequence is used to simulate a trajectory $s_{\tau}^{(k)}$ over the prediction horizon using the discretized tractor-trailer kinematic model. This ensures dynamic feasibility of the sampled trajectories.
 3. **Cost Evaluation**: For each simulated trajectory, a comprehensive cost function is evaluated, comprising several terms:
 - **Goal Tracking Cost**: Penalizes deviation from a desired goal state $s_{goal}$.
 - **Control Cost**: Penalizes the magnitude of acceleration and steering rate commands to limit control effort.
 - **Smoothness Cost**: Penalizes rapid changes in control commands to promote smoother motion.
 - **Articulation Cost**: Penalizes large articulation angles $\phi$ to prevent jackknifing.
 - **Obstacle Avoidance Cost**: This is the key integration point. At each predicted state $s_{\tau}^{(k)}$, the geometric neural encoder computes the minimum signed distance $d_{\tau}^{(k)}$ between the articulated tractor-trailer body and the LiDAR point cloud. This distance is then used in a smooth barrier-style potential function that sharply penalizes proximity to obstacles and transitions to a linear penalty upon penetration.
 - **Terminal Cost**: A cost applied at the end of the prediction horizon to bias the final state towards the goal.
 4. **Weighted Averaging**: Each rollout $k$ is assigned a weight $\gamma^{(k)}$ based on its total trajectory cost $J^{(k)}$, using an exponential function with a temperature parameter $\lambda$. These weights are then normalized $\bar{\gamma}^{(k)}$.
 5. **Control Update**: The nominal control sequence $U_t$ is updated as a weighted average of all sampled control sequences, biased towards lower-cost trajectories.
 6. **Execution**: The first control input of the updated nominal sequence is applied to the real tractor-trailer system, and the process repeats with a shifted horizon.

By combining the fast and accurate neural distance estimation with the sampling-based MPPI framework, the approach enables perception-driven local planning and collision avoidance that respects nonlinear articulated dynamics in cluttered and partially unknown environments.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the performance of the proposed neural distance-guided MPPI navigation framework within a simulated environment. The evaluation focuses on the accuracy and speed of the geometric neural encoder, as well as the overall navigation capabilities of the integrated system.

#### 5.1. Geometric Neural Encoder Performance

- **Vehicle Geometry**: The simulated tractor-trailer model consisted of three convex polygons: one for the tractor body and two for the trailer (a rectangular body and a triangular connector part). Specific dimensions were used: tractor 3.35 m × 1.48 m; trailer rectangular body 1.2 m × 3.6 m. Kinematic parameters were set as $L_0 = 1.9$ m (tractor wheelbase), $L_h = 0.5$ m (hitch offset), and $L_1 = 1.5$ m (distance from hitch point to trailer axle).
- **Training**: A separate geometric neural encoder was trained for each of these three convex polygons. The training process required approximately 2.2 hours on a machine equipped with an Intel i9-13900KF CPU and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. Each encoder was trained for 5,000 epochs using an 80/20 train-test split of the generated dataset.
- **Accuracy**: Post-training, the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted and ground-truth dual variables, and between the predicted and ground-truth point-to-polygon distances on the test set, was observed to drop below $1 \times 10^{-5}$. This indicates that the neural encoders achieved strong convergence and high accuracy in approximating the exact dual optimization solutions for distance computation. Once trained, these encoders were integrated into the MPPI controller for online use without requiring further optimization.

#### 5.2. MPPI Navigation Performance in Simulation

- **Simulation Setup**: A simulated 2D LiDAR sensor provided point clouds at each time step, serving as the input for the neural encoders. The robot’s state was assumed to be known, simulating output from a localization module.
- **MPPI Configuration**: The MPPI controller was configured with a prediction horizon of 50 steps and a time step of $\Delta t = 0.1$ s. It employed 1000 sampled trajectories per update to explore the control space. Gaussian exploration noise $\Sigma_w = \text{diag}(2, 2)$ was used, and the temperature parameter $\lambda$ was set to 1. The running cost function incorporated specific weights for each component: $W_g = \text{diag}(1, 1, 0.5, 0.5, 0, 0)$ for goal tracking, $W_u = \text{diag}(0.1, 0.1)$ for control effort, $W_{\Delta u} = \text{diag}(0.1, 0.1)$ for smoothness, $w_{\phi} = 1$ for articulation penalty, and $w_{obs} = 5$, $w_{coll} = 50$ for collision avoidance. A terminal cost weight $W_T = 10 W_g$ was applied. These parameters were empirically tuned. The controller was implemented in Python with GPU acceleration.
- **Navigation Task**: The tractor-trailer system was tasked with navigating from a starting pose ($s_{start} =^T$) to a goal pose ($s_{goal} = [40, 12, -0.5, 0, 0, 0]^T$) within a simulated environment characterized by a dense field of obstacles and narrow passages.
- **Performance Metrics**:
 - **Safety**: The simulation results demonstrated that the tractor-trailer consistently maintained safe clearance from all obstacles throughout the maneuver, successfully avoiding collisions. The system reached the specified goal configuration without any contact.
 - **Dynamic Feasibility**: Because all sampled rollouts within the MPPI framework were generated using the tractor-trailer kinematic model, the resulting optimal trajectories inherently adhered to the vehicle's dynamic constraints, ensuring dynamic feasibility.
 - **Real-time Operation**: The controller achieved an operating frequency of approximately 30 Hz in simulation. This performance indicates that the framework is computationally efficient enough for real-time application.
- **Visualization**: Visualizations (as depicted in Figure 3 of the paper) illustrate the sampling process of MPPI, showing the various candidate rollouts and the resulting optimal trajectory. These figures confirm the successful and collision-free arrival of the tractor-trailer at the goal, navigating through a complex and cluttered environment.

In summary, the simulation results collectively demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed neural distance-guided MPPI control framework. It successfully generated dynamically feasible and safe trajectories for an articulated tractor-trailer system in a cluttered and complex environment, leveraging accurate real-time distance estimates from raw LiDAR data.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact, particularly within the domain of agricultural robotics and autonomous vehicle navigation.

#### 6.1. Scientific Significance

- **Novel Geometric Reasoning for Articulated Vehicles**: The development of a duality-inspired geometric neural encoder provides a novel and efficient method for real-time, map-free geometric reasoning for complex articulated vehicle bodies. This approach circumvents the limitations of traditional methods that either oversimplify geometry or rely on computationally expensive precomputed distance fields, which are unsuitable for dynamic and partially unknown environments. By learning to approximate the solution of the dual optimization problem, the encoder directly translates raw LiDAR data into accurate distance estimates.
- **Effective Integration of Learning and Control**: The work demonstrates an effective integration of a machine learning component (neural encoder) with a sampling-based optimal control framework (MPPI). This combination allows the control system to directly incorporate perception-derived, high-fidelity geometric information into its cost evaluation, leading to more responsive and safer navigation in challenging scenarios. The choice of MPPI is particularly relevant for handling the nonlinear dynamics of articulated systems and the discontinuous nature of collision penalties.
- **Addressing Key Limitations**: The research directly addresses critical limitations in the field of autonomous articulated vehicle navigation, specifically concerning the trade-off between geometric accuracy, computational efficiency, and adaptability to dynamic environments. It provides a blueprint for how complex collision avoidance can be managed in real-time without relying on static maps or simplified vehicle models.

#### 6.2. Practical Implications and Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Safety in Agricultural Operations**: By enabling accurate and real-time collision avoidance for tractor-trailer systems, this framework can significantly enhance safety in agricultural operations. This includes preventing collisions with static obstacles (e.g., trees, structures), dynamic elements (e.g., other vehicles, farm workers), and the crop itself, reducing the risk of damage, injury, and economic loss.
- **Increased Autonomy and Efficiency**: The ability to navigate autonomously and safely in cluttered agricultural environments contributes directly to the advancement of fully autonomous farming. This can lead to increased operational efficiency, allowing for extended working hours, optimized routes, and potentially reduced labor costs. The real-time performance (30 Hz) of the controller is crucial for responsive operation in dynamic field conditions.
- **Versatility in Dynamic Environments**: The map-free nature of the geometric neural encoder makes the system highly adaptable to environments where maps are unavailable, imprecise, or subject to frequent changes (e.g., due to harvesting, planting, or weather conditions). This versatility is particularly valuable in agriculture, where environments are inherently dynamic.
- **Foundation for Future Agricultural Robotics**: This work provides a robust foundation for further developments in agricultural robotics, especially for complex multi-body systems. The methodology can be extended to other articulated vehicles or specialized implements, paving the way for more sophisticated autonomous agricultural machinery.

#### 6.3. Future Directions and Broader Impact

The paper also outlines several promising directions for future work, which further underscore its potential impact:

- **Generalization of Encoders**: Developing a universal or geometry-conditioned encoder that can generalize across different tractor-trailer configurations without requiring retraining for each polygon geometry would significantly improve the scalability and practical applicability of the approach.
- **Integration with Global Planning**: Combining the local MPPI planner with a high-level global planner would enhance robustness in highly constrained environments, addressing the potential for local minima entrapment.
- **Real-world Deployment**: Transitioning from simulation to real-world deployment is a crucial next step, which will necessitate robust sensor processing techniques (e.g., 3D LiDAR filtering for ground returns and overhanging obstacles, outlier rejection for noise) and extensive field validation.
- **Expanded Testing and Benchmarking**: Future work includes conducting expanded studies across diverse field layouts, extensive testing in challenging reverse-motion scenarios, evaluation under dynamic and moving obstacles, and quantitative comparisons with other state-of-the-art planners to establish a comprehensive performance benchmark.

Overall, this research represents a significant step towards enabling safe, efficient, and truly autonomous navigation for complex articulated vehicles in challenging real-world environments, with a direct and positive impact on the agricultural sector.
