# FairDICE: Fairness-Driven Offline Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> FairDICE introduces an offline Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning framework that directly optimizes nonlinear social welfare functions, like Nash social welfare, from fixed datasets. It achieves competitive or superior welfare performance on continuous control benchmarks while implicitly identifying optimal preference weights.

## Problem

- Existing Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) approaches predominantly use linear scalarization, which is insufficient for capturing complex, nonlinear fairness objectives such as Nash social welfare or max-min fairness.
- The absence of a unified framework for optimizing these nonlinear welfare criteria in the *offline* setting, where learning must occur from fixed datasets without active environment interaction.
- Offline Reinforcement Learning methods face the challenge of distribution shift, where learned policies may exploit unrepresented state-action regions, leading to unreliable value estimates.

## Method

- Formulates offline fair MORL as a ==convex optimization== problem, extending the dual of V-LP, to maximize the sum of strictly concave utility functions over objective returns.
- Integrates an f-divergence regularization term into the problem formulation to mitigate distribution shift, balancing welfare maximization with data distribution adherence.
- Derives a sample-based algorithm from the Lagrangian dual using importance weights, enabling efficient optimization of nonlinear welfare functions and policy extraction via direct derivation or weighted behavior cloning.

## Results

- Successfully learned policies in discrete environments (e.g., MO-Four-Room) that maximized target welfare (e.g., Nash social welfare) and drove balanced visitation, even when trained on data from unfair or suboptimal behavior policies.
- Achieved competitive or superior Nash Social Welfare (NSW) scores on five D4MORL continuous control benchmarks, outperforming baselines that required extensive and explicit searches over preference weights.
- Demonstrated scalability to higher-dimensional objective spaces (e.g., 3-objective MO-Hopper), efficiently identifying welfare-maximizing policies without explicit preference conditioning and producing solutions on or near the Pareto frontier.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - FairDICE establishes a theoretical equivalence between its regularized fair MORL formulation and regularized linear MORL, demonstrating it implicitly optimizes the preference weights that maximize the target welfare function.
> - The concavity of the chosen α-fairness objective (e.g., Nash social welfare) can inherently act as a regularizer, mitigating adverse effects of distribution shift by discouraging policies that exclusively maximize single returns.
> - By introducing slack variables and leveraging convex conjugate functions in the dual formulation, the framework enables practical, sample-based optimization of complex nonlinear welfare criteria in an offline setting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a specific challenge within the intersection of Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) and Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL):** Traditional MORL often employs linear scalarization, where a policy aims to maximize a weighted sum of returns from multiple objectives. While effective for certain preference structures, this approach is limited in capturing complex, fairness-oriented trade-offs, such as those prescribed by Nash social welfare or max-min fairness. These fairness objectives are inherently nonlinear and non-additive, leading to the development of "fair MORL" approaches. Existing fair MORL algorithms predominantly operate in online settings, where agents learn through active interaction with the environment.

**Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL):** Offline RL focuses on learning policies from a fixed dataset of previously collected interactions, without further active exploration. This paradigm is crucial for real-world applications where online interaction is costly, risky, or impractical (e.g., autonomous driving, healthcare). A primary challenge in offline RL is mitigating "distribution shift," where a learned policy's visitation distribution deviates significantly from the behavior policy that generated the data, leading to unreliable value estimates and suboptimal performance. Current offline MORL research primarily deals with linear scalarization, learning policies conditioned on explicit preference weights and often aiming to identify various points on the Pareto front. These methods typically require a comprehensive search over preference weights to find solutions for specific objectives.

**The Research Gap:** The paper identifies a gap where a unified framework for optimizing nonlinear welfare criteria (i.e., fair MORL) in the *offline* setting is largely unexplored. Existing fair MORL algorithms are online, and existing offline MORL algorithms primarily handle linear scalarization. This work aims to bridge this gap by developing an offline method capable of directly optimizing nonlinear fairness objectives while simultaneously addressing the distribution shift inherent in offline learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient offline Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) framework capable of directly optimizing nonlinear welfare functions. This framework, named FairDICE, is designed to enable learning of policies that promote fair outcomes across multiple conflicting objectives, using only a fixed dataset of previously collected interactions.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations of current MORL and offline RL approaches:

- **Inadequacy of Linear Scalarization for Fairness:** In many real-world sequential decision-making problems, balancing conflicting objectives necessitates considerations beyond a simple weighted sum. Fairness-oriented objectives, such as Nash social welfare or max-min fairness, involve nonlinear and non-additive trade-offs that linear scalarization cannot adequately capture. For example, linear scalarization might prioritize objectives that are easier to optimize, potentially leading to highly unequal outcomes, whereas fairness objectives aim to ensure a more equitable distribution of returns.
- **Challenges of Nonlinear Optimization in RL:** The nonlinearity introduced by welfare functions (e.g., the logarithm in Nash social welfare) breaks the standard Bellman recursion. This makes it difficult to define Temporal Difference (TD) targets and apply conventional value-based or policy-gradient methods, which are foundational in single-objective and linear MORL. Most existing fair MORL algorithms operate in online settings, which allow for iterative interaction and exploration to approximate these complex objectives.
- **Absence of Offline Fair MORL:** While offline RL has gained prominence for its ability to learn from fixed datasets, preventing costly or unsafe online exploration, research in offline MORL has predominantly focused on linear scalarization. These methods often require explicit preference weights during training or evaluation, or extensive searching across a range of weights to find a desirable policy. This approach is impractical for nonlinear welfare objectives, which inherently define a preference structure that is not directly expressible as a fixed linear weighting and aims to directly maximize welfare rather than recover a Pareto front.
- **Mitigating Distribution Shift:** Offline RL algorithms face the challenge of distribution shift, where a policy might attempt to exploit regions of the state-action space that are poorly represented in the static dataset, leading to inaccurate value estimations and poor real-world performance. Any offline fair MORL method must incorporate mechanisms to regularize policy learning against such shifts.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines three specific contributions:

1. **A regularized offline MORL formulation:** This formulation is designed to optimize nonlinear welfare objectives while simultaneously mitigating distributional shift, integrating fairness directly into the offline learning process.
2. **A theoretical connection:** The work establishes a theoretical link between the proposed regularized welfare optimization and regularized linear scalarization. This connection suggests that FairDICE implicitly optimizes the preference weights that would maximize welfare within a linear framework, providing a deeper understanding of its operational mechanism.
3. **FairDICE, a practical algorithm:** Based on the theoretical insights, a sample-based algorithm is developed. FairDICE extends the Distribution Correction Estimation (DICE-RL) framework to handle the nonlinearity of fair MORL, offering a practical solution for offline welfare optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of FairDICE involves formulating offline fair MORL as a convex optimization problem, leveraging the DICE-RL framework for regularization, and deriving a sample-based algorithm for practical implementation.

#### 3.1. Convex Formulation of Fair MORL

The paper first formulates Fair MORL as a convex optimization problem (P1) by expressing objective returns *J_i(π)* in terms of the stationary distribution *d(s,a)*. The objective is to maximize the sum of strictly concave utility functions *u_i(·)* applied to each expected objective return *Σ_i u_i(J_i(π))*, subject to the Bellman flow constraints that ensure *d(s,a)* is a valid stationary distribution. This formulation extends the dual of V-LP by incorporating a welfare function instead of a linear objective, drawing an analogy to a sequential version of the Eisenberg–Gale program for Nash social welfare.

#### 3.2. Reformulation for Sample-based Optimization

To enable sample-based optimization, the problem (P1) is reformulated into (P2) by introducing slack variables *k_i* for each objective return. This move brings the expectation outside the concave utility function, making it amenable to methods that rely on importance sampling. The Lagrangian dual of (P2) is then derived, which introduces Lagrange multipliers *μ_i* for the return constraints and *ν(s)* for the Bellman flow constraints. It is noted that, at optimality, *μ_i* acts as an implicit preference weight *u_i'(k_i*)*, penalizing larger returns for strictly concave *u_i(·)* and promoting balanced outcomes, consistent with fairness objectives like Nash social welfare (where *μ_i* would be the reciprocal of the *i*-th return). A key theoretical distinction is made: simply using these optimal *μ_i* as fixed weights in a linear MORL problem (P3) does not generally yield the same unique optimal policy as (P2), because (P3) might have multiple optimal solutions with different welfare outcomes.

#### 3.3. Regularized Welfare Optimization Framework (FairDICE)

To address the distribution shift challenge in offline RL, an *f*-divergence regularization term is introduced into (P2), leading to (P2-reg). This term penalizes deviations between the learned stationary distribution *d* and the empirical data distribution *d_D*, controlled by a hyperparameter *β*. The resulting objective balances welfare maximization with distributional shift regularization.
The Lagrangian dual of (P2-reg) is then formulated. The stationary distribution *d(s,a)* is reparameterized as *w(s,a)d_D(s,a)*, where *w(s,a)* are importance weights. By applying the Lagrangian dual and deriving closed-form solutions for *w* and *k_i* from first-order conditions, the final optimization problem is expressed as minimizing a loss function in terms of *ν* and *μ*. This loss involves the convex conjugate functions *f*^*(y)* and *u*^*_i(y)*. The use of slack variables *k_i* is crucial here, as directly applying the dual to (P1) would keep expected returns inside concave functions, complicating importance sampling.

#### 3.4. Policy Extraction and Scalability

After minimizing the dual loss, the optimal stationary distribution *d*(s,a) is obtained from *w*(s,a) *d_D*(s,a). For discrete domains, the optimal policy *π*(a|s) is directly derived as *d*(s,a) / *Σ_a d*(s,a)*. For continuous domains, a weighted behavior cloning approach is used to extract the policy by maximizing *E_((s,a)~d_D) [w*(s,a) log π_φ(a|s)]*. FairDICE introduces only one additional scalar parameter per objective, making it scalable with the number of objectives. The Soft-χ² divergence is used for *f*, whose conjugate functions have closed-form solutions, simplifying the optimization.

#### 3.5. Equivalence to Regularized Linear MORL

A theoretical proposition (Proposition 1) is presented, demonstrating that the regularized fair MORL problem (P2-reg) and a regularized linear MORL problem (P3-reg) become equivalent if (P3-reg) uses the optimal Lagrange multipliers *μ* from (P2-reg) as its preference weights. This equivalence implies that FairDICE implicitly optimizes the preference weights that correspond to welfare maximization, thus providing a principled way to find welfare-optimal policies without explicit preference specification or exhaustive search.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted in both discrete and continuous domains.
- **Discrete Domains:** Adapted versions of the Four-Room environment and Random MDPs were used. α-fairness was employed as the generalized social welfare function, allowing evaluation across utilitarian (α→0), Nash social welfare (α=1), and max-min fairness (α→∞). Performance was assessed using Utilitarian welfare, Jain’s Fairness Index, and Nash social welfare.
- **Continuous Domains:** The D4MORL benchmark (based on MuJoCo) was used, involving tasks with two or three objectives (e.g., speed, height, energy efficiency). Datasets were collected using "expert" and "amateur" behavioral policies. Baselines included preference-conditioned offline MORL algorithms (BC(P), MODT(P), MORvS(P)), which required extensive searching over preference weights to find policies maximizing Nash social welfare.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of FairDICE demonstrated its capability in optimizing nonlinear welfare objectives in offline MORL across various environments.

#### 4.1. Performance in Discrete Domains (MO-Four-Room and Random MOMDP)

- **MO-Four-Room:** FairDICE successfully learned offline MORL policies that maximized target welfare even from data collected by an unfair, suboptimal policy. When maximizing utilitarian welfare (α=0), FairDICE prioritized the nearest goal, achieving the highest sum of returns but exhibiting lower fairness. In contrast, when maximizing Nash social welfare (α=1), FairDICE encouraged balanced visitation to all goals, including in an extended 8-objective version of the environment (Figure 1), demonstrating its ability to drive fair behavior.
- **Effective Optimization of Trade-offs (Random MOMDP):** FairDICE balanced two distinct trade-offs: the choice of α for fairness among objectives and β for regularization against distributional shift. Increasing α generally improved Jain’s fairness index, while decreasing α enhanced utilitarian performance. Higher β values constrained learned policies closer to the data distribution, which was beneficial for stability but could limit objective pursuit. Lower β values allowed more aggressive pursuit of α-fairness, though excessively low β could lead to performance degradation due to distribution shift. A notable finding was that policies optimized for higher α values (more fairness-oriented) often sustained strong utilitarian performance, suggesting that the concavity of the α-fairness objective implicitly regularized against excessive return maximization, thus mitigating distribution shift effects.
- **Welfare Optimization via Implicit Preference Weight:** Experiments perturbing the optimal Lagrange multipliers (μ\*) derived from FairDICE-NSW in the Random MOMDP confirmed the theoretical connection to regularized linear MORL. The Nash Social Welfare (NSW) decreased as the preference weights applied to a FairDICE-fixed variant deviated from the optimal μ\*, indicating that FairDICE indeed identifies and implicitly optimizes the welfare-maximizing preference weights.

#### 4.2. Welfare Maximization in Continuous Domains (D4MORL Benchmark)

- **Competitive or Superior NSW Performance:** Across five two-objective D4MORL tasks (e.g., MO-Hopper, MO-Walker2d), FairDICE achieved competitive or superior Nash Social Welfare (NSW) scores compared to existing preference-conditioned baselines (BC(P), MODT(P), MORvS(P)). These baselines required an exhaustive search over 30 linearly spaced preference weights to find their best NSW performance, whereas FairDICE directly optimized the welfare objective (Figure 4).
- **Pareto Front Alignment:** Visualization of raw returns demonstrated that FairDICE's solutions consistently lay on or near the Pareto frontier formed by the preference-conditioned baselines (Figure 5). This indicates that FairDICE not only achieves strong fairness but also maintains efficiency in objective optimization.
- **Scalability to Higher Dimensions (MO-Hopper-3obj):** For the MO-Hopper-3obj task, which involves three objectives and thus a significantly larger preference weight space, FairDICE effectively identified a welfare-maximizing policy without requiring explicit preference conditioning. This capability is crucial where exhaustive weight searches become computationally prohibitive. The resulting policies also achieved raw returns close to or surpassing the Pareto frontier established by baselines (Figure 6), further confirming both efficiency and fairness.
- **Efficiency:** FairDICE’s approach required only one additional scalar parameter per objective, highlighting its efficiency and scalability, particularly beneficial in high-dimensional preference spaces where traditional methods struggle with weight exploration.
- **Robustness to Data Quality and Coverage (Appendix H):** FairDICE showed robustness when evaluated on both "expert" and "amateur" quality datasets within D4MORL. Furthermore, it maintained reliable performance even when trajectories with "balanced" preference weights were filtered out, indicating a degree of resilience to limited data coverage and biased data distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FairDICE represents a contribution to the field of Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning, particularly by addressing the previously underexplored problem of optimizing nonlinear welfare objectives within an offline setting. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Offline Fair MORL:** FairDICE is presented as the first offline MORL framework that directly optimizes nonlinear welfare objectives. This fills a notable gap in the literature, enabling the application of fairness-driven policy learning in real-world scenarios where online interaction is impractical or risky, thereby expanding the applicability of MORL.
- **Enhanced Real-World Applicability:** Many real-world problems require policies that are not only efficient but also fair, especially when dealing with diverse stakeholders or conflicting system goals (e.g., resource allocation, autonomous system behavior, healthcare decisions). By enabling the direct optimization of welfare functions from static data, FairDICE provides a tool for developing policies that explicitly consider and balance multiple objectives to achieve more equitable outcomes without costly online experimentation.
- **Theoretical Foundation and Insights:** The work establishes a theoretical connection between regularized fair MORL and regularized linear MORL. This connection demonstrates that FairDICE implicitly learns and optimizes the appropriate preference weights that maximize the target welfare function. This insight not only grounds the algorithm in established theory but also explains its effectiveness, providing a more principled approach than exhaustive search over explicit preference weights.
- **Practical Efficiency and Scalability:** FairDICE extends the DICE-RL framework into the multi-objective domain, providing a sample-based algorithm that efficiently handles the nonlinearity of welfare objectives. It introduces minimal overhead (one additional scalar parameter per objective), allowing it to scale effectively with an increasing number of objectives. This efficiency is particularly important in high-dimensional preference spaces where methods relying on explicit preference conditioning and exhaustive search become computationally prohibitive.
- **Robustness to Data Imperfections:** Empirical results suggest that FairDICE exhibits a degree of robustness to suboptimal data quality and limited data coverage in offline datasets. This characteristic is crucial for practical offline RL applications, where data is often noisy, incomplete, or collected suboptimally.
- **Broadening the Scope of Offline RL:** By integrating complex preference structures like fairness into the offline learning paradigm, FairDICE expands the capabilities of offline RL beyond simple return maximization, making it suitable for a wider range of ethical and societal considerations in AI system design.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations:
- FairDICE is designed for strictly concave scalarization functions. It does not generalize to all forms of nonlinear scalarization, specifically those that are non-convex, due to its reliance on the convexity properties of the Lagrangian dual.
- Like other offline RL methods, the final performance of FairDICE is still influenced by the choice of hyperparameters (especially the regularization coefficient β) and the overall quality and coverage of the offline dataset, even though concave scalarization can inherently mitigate some sensitivity to distribution shift.
