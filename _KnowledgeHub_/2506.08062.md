# FairDICE: Fairness-Driven Offline Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> FairDICE introduces an offline Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning framework that directly optimizes nonlinear social welfare functions, like Nash social welfare, from fixed datasets. It achieves competitive or superior welfare performance on continuous control benchmarks while implicitly identifying optimal preference weights.

## Problem

- Existing Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) approaches predominantly use linear scalarization, which is insufficient for capturing complex, nonlinear fairness objectives such as Nash social welfare or max-min fairness.
- The absence of a unified framework for optimizing these nonlinear welfare criteria in the *offline* setting, where learning must occur from fixed datasets without active environment interaction.
- Offline Reinforcement Learning methods face the challenge of distribution shift, where learned policies may exploit unrepresented state-action regions, leading to unreliable value estimates.

## Method

- Formulates offline fair MORL as a ==convex optimization== problem, extending the dual of V-LP, to maximize the sum of strictly concave utility functions over objective returns.
- Integrates an f-divergence regularization term into the problem formulation to mitigate distribution shift, balancing welfare maximization with data distribution adherence.
- Derives a sample-based algorithm from the Lagrangian dual using importance weights, enabling efficient optimization of nonlinear welfare functions and policy extraction via direct derivation or weighted behavior cloning.

## Results

- Successfully learned policies in discrete environments (e.g., MO-Four-Room) that maximized target welfare (e.g., Nash social welfare) and drove balanced visitation, even when trained on data from unfair or suboptimal behavior policies.
- Achieved competitive or superior Nash Social Welfare (NSW) scores on five D4MORL continuous control benchmarks, outperforming baselines that required extensive and explicit searches over preference weights.
- Demonstrated scalability to higher-dimensional objective spaces (e.g., 3-objective MO-Hopper), efficiently identifying welfare-maximizing policies without explicit preference conditioning and producing solutions on or near the Pareto frontier.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - FairDICE establishes a theoretical equivalence between its regularized fair MORL formulation and regularized linear MORL, demonstrating it implicitly optimizes the preference weights that maximize the target welfare function.
> - The concavity of the chosen α-fairness objective (e.g., Nash social welfare) can inherently act as a regularizer, mitigating adverse effects of distribution shift by discouraging policies that exclusively maximize single returns.
> - By introducing slack variables and leveraging convex conjugate functions in the dual formulation, the framework enables practical, sample-based optimization of complex nonlinear welfare criteria in an offline setting.
