# SkillMimic: Learning Basketball Interaction Skills from Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> SkillMimic presents a unified data-driven framework that enables physically simulated humanoids to learn diverse and precise human-object interaction skills, such as basketball maneuvers, directly from demonstrations. By introducing the Contact Graph and Contact Graph Reward, the framework effectively masters complex interactions and achieves continuous basketball scoring, outperforming prior methods.

## Problem

- Traditional methods for physics-based character animation and robot control require labor-intensive, manually designed, skill-specific reward functions for human-object interaction (HOI).
- Existing approaches lack a unified, data-driven framework for learning diverse and complex HOI skills, hindering generalization and scalability.
- Naive extensions of locomotion imitation methods to HOI often result in unstable and unnatural interactions due to insufficient handling of precise relative motion and contact dynamics.

## Method

- Introduces **SkillMimic**, a unified data-driven reinforcement learning framework that learns a single policy for diverse HOI skills from demonstrations without hand-crafted rewards.
- Developed the ==Contact Graph (CG)== and Contact Graph Reward (CGR) to explicitly model and reward precise physical contact during interactions, addressing a key challenge in HOI.
- Employs a hierarchical control scheme where a High-Level Controller reuses learned interaction skills to efficiently tackle complex, long-horizon tasks like continuous basketball scoring.

## Results

- SkillMimic successfully learned 8 diverse basketball skills (e.g., dribbling, shooting, pickup, layup) with a unified reward, outperforming adapted DeepMimic* and AMP* baselines by large margins (e.g., **86.7%** success rate for Pickup vs. **19.6%** for DeepMimic*).
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of the Contact Graph Reward, which eliminated "kinematic local optima" and led to precise, natural contacts, with reward multiplication also improving overall imitation accuracy.
- Demonstrated strong scalability, with skill generalization improving significantly from **0.5%** success (1 clip) to **85.6%** (131 clips) for out-of-distribution pickup tasks, and achieved continuous basketball scoring in simulation for the first time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified, data-driven reward structure, particularly using reward multiplication and a Contact Graph Reward, is crucial for balanced learning and achieving precise, natural human-object interactions.
> - Learning multiple interaction skills jointly within a single policy significantly improves individual skill robustness, generalization, and enables seamless zero-shot skill switching.
> - Leveraging pre-learned low-level interaction skills as priors through a hierarchical controller drastically simplifies reward design and accelerates training for complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of **Reinforcement Learning (RL), Human-Object Interaction (HOI), physics-based character animation, and robotics control.** The broader landscape has seen significant advancements in animating and controlling physically simulated characters, largely driven by deep RL and imitation learning.

Traditionally, the focus in physics-based character animation and robot control using RL has been on **locomotion skills**. Pioneering works like DeepMimic, AMP (Adversarial Motion Priors), and ASE (Adversarial Skill Embeddings) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in learning dynamic locomotion, such as walking, running, and acrobatics, by imitating human motion data. However, these methods primarily concentrate on the character's movement within an environment, often treating objects as static obstacles or simple targets. When interactions with objects are involved, existing methods typically require **labor-intensive, manually designed, skill-specific reward functions**. For example, previous works have learned skills like striking pillars, playing tennis, climbing ropes, or carrying boxes, but each required bespoke reward engineering for that particular interaction.

The critical gap that SkillMimic addresses is the **lack of a unified, data-driven framework for learning diverse and complex HOI skills** without the need for hand-crafted rewards for each new interaction. Basketball, as highlighted in the paper, is an "exemplary testbed" for HOI because it demands a wide array of complex, dynamic, and precise interactions with a ball (dribbling, shooting, passing, catching, retrieving). Designing separate rewards for each of these variations would be an "extraordinary engineering effort" and fundamentally limit the generalization and scalability of such systems.

Furthermore, naive extensions of locomotion imitation methods to HOI often lead to "unstable and unnatural results" because they do not adequately account for the unique challenges of HOI, such as unbalanced rewards, precise relative motion between human and object, and crucial contact dynamics. While some methods have explored learning HOI (e.g., using finite-state machines or policy network control for activities like skateboarding or basketball dribbling), they often lack the generalizability or require substantial manual intervention. Even interaction graph modeling, while useful, has been limited by focusing only on kinematic relationships, failing to ensure proper physical contact.

SkillMimic aims to fundamentally change this paradigm by proposing a **unified data-driven approach** that learns a single policy capable of mastering multiple interaction skills and facilitating smooth transitions between them, with performance improving as the HOI dataset grows. By introducing novel components like the **Contact Graph (CG) and Contact Graph Reward (CGR)**, the work directly tackles the challenge of precise physical contact imitation, a crucial aspect often overlooked or poorly handled by previous methods. The hierarchical solution for reusing learned interaction skills to tackle long-horizon, complex tasks like consecutive basketball scoring further extends the utility and impact of the learned low-level policies, pushing the boundaries of what physically simulated humanoids can achieve in interactive environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of SkillMimic is to enable physically simulated humanoids to **learn a wide variety of human-object interaction (HOI) skills in a simple, scalable, and generalizable manner**, moving beyond the limitations of traditional methods that rely on labor-intensive, skill-specific reward engineering.

The core motivations driving this research are:

1. **Overcoming the Reward Design Bottleneck:** Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) for HOI requires manually designed skill rewards that are difficult to create, do not generalize well across different interactions, and become prohibitively complex for diverse skill sets (e.g., in basketball). SkillMimic seeks to eliminate this need through a unified, data-driven approach.
2. **Achieving Unified Multi-Skill Learning:** Current methods typically learn isolated locomotion or simple interaction skills. The paper aims to train a *single policy* that can master *multiple, diverse interaction skills* (e.g., shooting, dribbling, retrieving a basketball) and facilitate smooth transitions between them, enhancing the agent's versatility.
3. **Improving Scalability and Generalization with Data:** Inspired by how humans learn through observation and imitation (e.g., basketball players watching game footage), SkillMimic aims for a system where the diversity and generalization of learned skills naturally improve as the size and variety of the HOI demonstration dataset increase.
4. **Enabling Complex, Long-Horizon Tasks:** While learning individual skills is important, the ultimate goal is to compose these skills to achieve challenging high-level tasks. The paper specifically targets "consecutive basketball scoring" as a benchmark, which requires sequencing and adapting multiple interaction skills.
5. **Addressing HOI-Specific Challenges:** Recognizing that simply extending locomotion imitation methods to HOI is insufficient, the research is motivated to explicitly tackle the unique aspects of HOI, such as maintaining precise relative motion between the human and object and ensuring accurate physical contact. This led to the development of the Contact Graph and Contact Graph Reward.
6. **Providing Public Datasets:** To facilitate further research in this underserved area, the authors aimed to collect and release two basketball HOI datasets, BallPlay-V and BallPlay-M, addressing the scarcity of high-quality data for dynamic human-object interactions.

In essence, SkillMimic is driven by the vision of creating highly capable, physically simulated agents that can acquire a rich repertoire of interaction skills autonomously from demonstrations, mirroring human learning, and then intelligently apply these skills to accomplish sophisticated tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SkillMimic proposes a **unified data-driven framework** that leverages reinforcement learning (RL) to imitate Human-Object Interaction (HOI) demonstrations. Its core methodology can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. The BallPlay Datasets

To address the scarcity of HOI data for basketball, the authors introduce two new datasets:
- **BallPlay-V:** Created using a monocular vision estimation pipeline to derive human SMPL-X parameters and object translations from RGB videos. It contains eight basic basketball skills.
- **BallPlay-M:** A more comprehensive and accurate dataset built using a multi-view optical motion capture system. It records approximately 35 minutes of diverse basketball interactions, including detailed body and finger movements, and ball trajectories, at 120 fps. This dataset is crucial for learning precise interaction skills.

#### 3.2. Defining Interaction Skills Using HOI Data

Interaction skills are defined as collections of HOI state transitions derived directly from captured HOI motion clips. If a simulated humanoid can manipulate objects such that its HOI state transitions closely resemble these reference clips, it is considered to have learned the skill. This data-driven definition eliminates the need for manual skill descriptions or reward engineering.

#### 3.3. Learning Interaction Skills by HOI Imitation (SkillMimic)

- **Training Pipeline:** The system trains an **Interaction Skill (IS) policy** using a standard RL setup (Proximal Policy Optimization - PPO with Isaac Gym for simulation).
 - For each training episode, a frame from a skill-labeled clip is randomly selected to initialize the humanoid and object states.
 - The IS policy receives the current HOI state ($s_t$) and a skill label ($c_j$) as input.
 - It predicts actions ($a_t$) (target joint rotations) which are executed by PD controllers in the physics simulator, producing the next state ($s_{t+1}$).
 - A **unified HOI imitation reward** measures the consistency between simulated and reference state transitions.
 - The environment resets when the humanoid falls or reaches maximum episode length.
- **HOI Observation ($s_t$):** The policy observes the humanoid's proprioception (root height, local body position, rotation, velocities), net contact forces at fingertips (crucial for sensing contact), and the object's local position, rotation, velocity, and angular velocity. Skill labels are one-hot encoded inputs.
- **IS Policy and Action:** The policy output is modeled as a Gaussian distribution, with the mean predicted by a three-layer MLP. Actions are then converted to joint torques via PD controllers.

#### 3.4. Unified HOI Imitation Reward

This is a cornerstone of SkillMimic, designed to be skill-agnostic and robust:
- The total reward ($r_t$) is a **multiplicative combination** of several sub-rewards to encourage balanced learning and prevent local optima:
 $r_t = r_b_t * r_o_t * r_{rel_t} * r_{reg_t} * r_{cg_t}$
- **Body Kinematics Imitation Reward ($r_b_t$):** Encourages alignment of the humanoid's body movements (joint positions, rotations, velocities) with the reference.
- **Object Kinematics Imitation Reward ($r_o_t$):** Ensures the object's movements (position, rotation, velocities) match the reference.
- **Relative Motion Reward ($r_{rel_t}$):** Crucial for interaction naturalness, this reward focuses on the relative positions and motions between key body parts and the object.
- **Adaptive Velocity Regularization ($r_{reg_t}$):** A term to suppress high-frequency jitters in the humanoid, especially when it should be stationary.
- **Contact Graph (CG) and Contact Graph Reward (CGR) ($r_{cg_t}$):** This is a key innovation.
 - **Contact Graph:** A simple, general method to model contact in interactions. It's a complete graph where nodes represent aggregated parts (e.g., "hands," "rest of body," "ball") and edges denote contact between these nodes. The node definitions are unified for a given scene.
 - **CGR:** Measures the element-wise absolute difference between the simulated and reference CG state (edge set values). This reward explicitly prioritizes physical contact learning, which is critical for precise interactions and prevents the policy from converging to "kinematic local optima" (e.g., using the head to stabilize the ball instead of hands).

#### 3.5. Reusing Interaction Skills for High-level Tasks

A **High-Level Controller (HLC)** is trained to compose the pre-learned interaction skills for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- The HLC takes the current HOI state ($s_t$) and additional task observations ($h_t$, e.g., basket position) as input.
- It outputs a discrete skill embedding ($c_t$) which is then fed into the pre-trained IS policy, effectively switching between learned skills.
- This hierarchical approach, by leveraging interaction skills as priors, significantly simplifies task reward design and accelerates training for tasks like throwing, heading, circling, and, most notably, continuous basketball scoring.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness and novelty of SkillMimic across various aspects:

1. **Superior Interaction Skill Learning:**
 - SkillMimic successfully masters a wide range of diverse basketball skills (e.g., various styles of dribbling, layup, shooting, robust ball pickup, rebound, pass, cross leg) using the *same configuration* and *unified reward*. Qualitative results in Figure 6 showcase this comprehensive skill acquisition.
 - **Comparison with Baselines:** SkillMimic significantly outperforms variants of traditional imitation learning methods (DeepMimic* and AMP*) adapted for HOI. For instance, in Table 2, SkillMimic achieves an 86.7% success rate for Pickup compared to 19.6% for DeepMimic* and 0.0% for AMP*. Similar large margins are observed for Dribble Forward, Layup, and Shot. AMP*-like adversarial rewards struggled with precision and suffered from mode collapse, making them ineffective for accurate interaction learning.

2. **Criticality of Contact Graph Reward (CGR) and Reward Multiplication:**
 - **Ablation Studies (Table 1, Figure 5):** Experiments on GRAB and BallPlay-V datasets clearly show that without CGR, the policy often converges to "kinematic local optima," where the humanoid uses incorrect or unnatural contacts (e.g., head, wrist, or body parts) to control the object or maintain balance, rather than the intended interactions. CGR effectively eliminates these issues, leading to precise and natural interactions.
 - The absence of reward multiplication (i.e., using additive rewards) leads to imbalanced learning, where the policy might prioritize body motion over critical interaction-related metrics (e.g., object position error, contact error), resulting in overall lower imitation accuracy.

3. **Scalability and Generalization with Data:**
 - Experiments on the pickup skill with varying data scales (1, 10, 40, and 131 clips from BallPlay-M) show a direct correlation between dataset size and generalization performance (Figure 7, Figure 8). The success rate for picking up balls placed randomly outside the training distribution increased dramatically from 0.5% (1 clip) to 85.6% (131 clips). This highlights SkillMimic's strong scalability and potential for learning robust and generalized skills from larger datasets without additional design for robustness.

4. **Robustness Against Physical Property Variations:**
 - Perturbation tests on ball radius, density, and restitution demonstrate that the learned skills (dribble forward, pickup) are robust to a wide range of changes in physical properties during inference (Table 5). While extreme deviations cause failure, the skills maintain high success rates within reasonable variations, indicating practical applicability.

5. **Effectiveness of Mixed Skill Training and Skill Switching:**
 - An ablation study (Table 6) shows that training multiple skills (layup, dribble left, dribble right, dribble forward) jointly in a single policy significantly improves both the success rates of individual skills and, more importantly, the ability to **switch between skills**. Mixed training enables cross-learning, enhancing generalization (e.g., improving the "dribble left" skill which otherwise fails due to inadequate state cycle in individual training data) and facilitating zero-shot skill switching.

6. **Successful Accomplishment of Complex High-Level Tasks:**
 - SkillMimic, by training a High-Level Controller (HLC) to reuse its learned interaction skills, achieves high success rates (ranging from 79.92% to 93.40%) on challenging long-horizon tasks like Heading, Circling, Throwing, and **continuous basketball Scoring** (Table 3, Figure 9b-d). This marks a significant achievement as it's "for the first time achieves continuous basketball scoring."
 - In contrast, baselines including PPO (learning from scratch), ASE (using locomotion priors), and ASE* (using body and object motion priors with adversarial rewards) largely failed to converge or showed negligible success rates on these complex tasks (Figure 13, Figure 14). This stark difference underscores the critical advantage of leveraging the robust interaction skill priors acquired through SkillMimic.
 - The HLC for the Scoring task required only 3 hours of training on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, showcasing impressive learning efficiency for complex tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SkillMimic represents a significant advancement in the fields of robotics, character animation, and reinforcement learning, particularly in the domain of Human-Object Interaction (HOI). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift in HOI Skill Learning:** The most profound impact is the introduction of a **unified, data-driven framework that eliminates the need for labor-intensive, skill-specific reward engineering** for diverse and complex interaction skills. This fundamentally changes how physically simulated agents can acquire HOI capabilities, moving from brittle, handcrafted solutions to a more scalable and generalizable learning approach.

2. **Scalability and Generalization:** The demonstrated ability of SkillMimic to improve skill diversity and generalization with increasing HOI dataset size is a crucial step towards robust and versatile agents. This opens avenues for collecting large, diverse datasets (like the BallPlay datasets introduced) to train highly capable humanoid policies, similar to how large-scale datasets have revolutionized other AI domains.

3. **Enabling Complex, Long-Horizon Tasks:** By providing a robust set of interaction skill priors, SkillMimic's hierarchical control scheme allows for the efficient learning and accomplishment of previously intractable long-horizon tasks, such as continuous basketball scoring. This capability is vital for developing intelligent agents that can operate effectively in dynamic, interactive environments. The ability to smoothly transition between diverse skills is a key enabler for complex behaviors.

4. **Advancements in Physical Interaction Fidelity:** The novel **Contact Graph (CG) and Contact Graph Reward (CGR)** are crucial methodological contributions. By explicitly prioritizing precise physical contact imitation, SkillMimic overcomes a major limitation of prior work that often resulted in unstable or unnatural interactions. This ensures higher fidelity and realism in simulated humanoids, which is critical for both animation and real-world robotics transfer.

5. **Resource for Future Research:** The introduction of the **BallPlay-V and BallPlay-M datasets** provides valuable public resources for the research community, filling a critical gap in high-quality HOI data, especially for dynamic and complex scenarios like basketball. These datasets will undoubtedly accelerate future research in HOI, physics-based character control, and embodied AI.

6. **Potential for Real-World Robotics:** While currently demonstrated in simulation, the framework lays strong groundwork for future applications in **real humanoid robotics**. The ability to learn robust, generalizable interaction skills from demonstrations could drastically simplify the programming and deployment of humanoid robots in complex real-world tasks, such as assisting in homes, logistics, or even performing sports. The listed affiliation with Unitree Robotics strongly hints at this real-world ambition, although significant challenges like sim-to-real transfer and HOI data retargeting remain.

7. **Broader Impact on Embodied AI:** SkillMimic contributes to the broader goal of creating more intelligent and capable embodied AI agents. By enabling humanoids to learn rich, human-like interaction skills, it moves closer to agents that can naturally perceive, interact with, and manipulate objects in human environments, fostering advancements in areas like human-robot collaboration, virtual reality avatars, and game AI.

In conclusion, SkillMimic pushes the boundaries of physically simulated character control by providing a scalable, data-driven solution for learning diverse and precise human-object interaction skills, paving the way for more intelligent and versatile embodied agents in both virtual and real-world settings.
