# SkillMimic: Learning Basketball Interaction Skills from Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> SkillMimic presents a unified data-driven framework that enables physically simulated humanoids to learn diverse and precise human-object interaction skills, such as basketball maneuvers, directly from demonstrations. By introducing the Contact Graph and Contact Graph Reward, the framework effectively masters complex interactions and achieves continuous basketball scoring, outperforming prior methods.

## Problem

- Traditional methods for physics-based character animation and robot control require labor-intensive, manually designed, skill-specific reward functions for human-object interaction (HOI).
- Existing approaches lack a unified, data-driven framework for learning diverse and complex HOI skills, hindering generalization and scalability.
- Naive extensions of locomotion imitation methods to HOI often result in unstable and unnatural interactions due to insufficient handling of precise relative motion and contact dynamics.

## Method

- Introduces **SkillMimic**, a unified data-driven reinforcement learning framework that learns a single policy for diverse HOI skills from demonstrations without hand-crafted rewards.
- Developed the ==Contact Graph (CG)== and Contact Graph Reward (CGR) to explicitly model and reward precise physical contact during interactions, addressing a key challenge in HOI.
- Employs a hierarchical control scheme where a High-Level Controller reuses learned interaction skills to efficiently tackle complex, long-horizon tasks like continuous basketball scoring.

## Results

- SkillMimic successfully learned 8 diverse basketball skills (e.g., dribbling, shooting, pickup, layup) with a unified reward, outperforming adapted DeepMimic* and AMP* baselines by large margins (e.g., **86.7%** success rate for Pickup vs. **19.6%** for DeepMimic*).
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of the Contact Graph Reward, which eliminated "kinematic local optima" and led to precise, natural contacts, with reward multiplication also improving overall imitation accuracy.
- Demonstrated strong scalability, with skill generalization improving significantly from **0.5%** success (1 clip) to **85.6%** (131 clips) for out-of-distribution pickup tasks, and achieved continuous basketball scoring in simulation for the first time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified, data-driven reward structure, particularly using reward multiplication and a Contact Graph Reward, is crucial for balanced learning and achieving precise, natural human-object interactions.
> - Learning multiple interaction skills jointly within a single policy significantly improves individual skill robustness, generalization, and enables seamless zero-shot skill switching.
> - Leveraging pre-learned low-level interaction skills as priors through a hierarchical controller drastically simplifies reward design and accelerates training for complex, long-horizon tasks.
