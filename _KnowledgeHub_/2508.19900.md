# Adaptive Scaling of Policy Constraints for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Science and Technology Beijing and the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) developed Adaptive Scaling of Policy Constraints (ASPC), a framework that automatically adjusts the policy constraint scale in offline reinforcement learning. This method results in policies with a 35% average improvement in normalized performance over a TD3+BC baseline across D4RL benchmarks, while eliminating the need for manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies often suffer from "distribution shift" or "extrapolation error" when querying unseen state-action pairs, leading to unreliable value estimates.
- Policy constraint methods, though effective, require extensive, task-specific manual tuning of a critical constraint scale, which is computationally expensive and impractical for diverse datasets.
- Existing adaptive constraint methods often provide limited performance gains compared to meticulously tuned baselines or lack mechanisms for globally adjusting the fundamental trade-off scale.

## Method

- **ASPC** introduces a ==second-order differentiable== framework that treats the policy constraint scale factor (α) as a learnable parameter, dynamically optimized through a bilevel optimization strategy.
- The framework employs an inner loop to update policy parameters and an outer loop with a three-component loss (L1,L2,L3L_1, L_2, L_3L1,L2,L3) to adjust α, balancing the RL objective and behavior cloning.
- The approach incorporates a robust critic network and an optimized α-update interval, and its adaptive mechanism is extended to other offline RL algorithms like IQL, CQL, Diffusion-QL, and FQL.

## Results

- ASPC achieved an average **35%** improvement in normalized performance over a robust TD3+BC baseline across 39 D4RL datasets, consistently outperforming other adaptive constraint methods and state-of-the-art algorithms that require per-dataset tuning.
- The framework demonstrated robust adaptability by automatically adjusting the constraint scale (α) based on dataset quality (smaller α for expert data, larger α for suboptimal data) and task characteristics, without manual intervention.
- ASPC successfully integrated into diverse offline RL algorithms (IQL, CQL, Diffusion-QL, FQL), consistently improving their performance on Gym-MuJoCo and OGBench benchmarks with minimal computational overhead compared to their backbones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The optimal scale for policy constraints is dynamic and highly dependent on the quality of the dataset and the specific task, requiring continuous adjustment during training.
> - A bilevel optimization approach with second-order gradients can effectively and automatically adapt this critical constraint scale, leading to more stable learning and higher policy performance across diverse settings.
> - The inclusion of specific regularization terms (L2L_2L2 for Q-value stability and L3L_3L3 for BC loss variation) is crucial, as they mutually constrain changes in policy and Q-values, enabling stable convergence across various domains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) is a subfield of RL that focuses on learning policies solely from pre-collected, fixed datasets, without any further interaction with the environment. This characteristic makes it suitable for real-world applications where environmental interactions are costly, risky, or impractical, such as autonomous driving, healthcare, and industrial control.

A fundamental challenge in offline RL is the "distribution shift" or "extrapolation error." This occurs when the learned policy attempts to query Q-values for state-action pairs that are outside the distribution of the offline dataset, leading to unreliable value estimates and potentially suboptimal or unstable policies.

Existing research has proposed several strategies to mitigate distribution shift. These include:
- **Policy Constraint Methods**: These explicitly or implicitly regularize the learned policy to stay close to the behavior policy that generated the dataset. Examples include TD3+BC and Implicit Q-Learning (IQL).
- **Uncertainty-Aware Approaches**: These methods penalize actions associated with high epistemic or aleatoric uncertainty to avoid unreliable regions of the state-action space.
- **Sequence Modeling Methods**: These reframe RL as a conditional trajectory modeling problem, often using transformer architectures.

Among these, policy constraint methods are widely adopted due to their direct approach to addressing extrapolation error. However, a crucial and often overlooked aspect within policy constraint methods is the *scaling* of these constraints. The constraint scale determines the balance between maximizing the RL objective (seeking higher returns) and adhering to the behavior cloning (BC) objective (staying close to the observed data).

Current approaches to managing this constraint scale broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Dataset-Specific Hyperparameter Tuning**: Methods in this category can achieve strong performance but require extensive, time-consuming, and often impractical hyperparameter tuning for each task or dataset of varying quality. When a single configuration is applied universally, their performance often degrades significantly.
2. **Adaptive Variants with Fixed Hyperparameters**: These methods attempt to adapt constraint strength, often locally (e.g., by reweighting samples or actions). While they reduce tuning costs, they may not address the global trade-off scale effectively, leading to limited performance gains compared to carefully tuned baselines.

This paper positions itself to bridge the gap between these two categories by proposing a method that *automatically and dynamically adjusts the global scale of policy constraints* during training. The goal is to achieve robust performance with a single hyperparameter configuration across diverse datasets, without requiring per-dataset tuning, while matching or exceeding the performance of meticulously tuned methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an adaptive mechanism for scaling policy constraints in offline reinforcement learning, enabling robust and high-performing policy learning across diverse tasks and datasets without the need for manual, per-dataset hyperparameter tuning.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges inherent in current offline RL methodologies:
1. **The Critical Role of Constraint Scale**: In policy constraint methods, the scale factor (e.g., $\alpha$ in TD3+BC) that balances the RL objective and the behavior cloning (BC) term is crucial. An inappropriate scale can lead to either aggressive policy updates that suffer from distribution shift and Q-value overestimation, or overly conservative updates that underperform by sticking too closely to suboptimal behavior data.
2. **Impracticality of Manual Tuning**: Optimal constraint scales vary significantly across different tasks and datasets, especially those with varying data quality (e.g., expert, medium, random). Achieving high performance with existing methods often requires meticulous, dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning, which is computationally expensive, time-consuming, and often infeasible in real-world scenarios where new datasets may continually arise or tuning resources are limited.
3. **Limitations of Existing Adaptive Methods**: While some adaptive approaches exist (e.g., trajectory- or sample-weighting methods, or those introducing auxiliary models), they often reweight actions locally or lack a mechanism for globally adapting the fundamental trade-off scale. Consequently, they may not achieve the same level of performance as methods tuned specifically for each dataset. They address tuning costs but leave a significant performance gap when a fixed configuration is applied across diverse domains.
4. **Need for Robustness and Generality**: There is a pressing need for offline RL algorithms that can deliver reliable and effective policies with a single, generalizable hyperparameter configuration. Such algorithms would be more practical and widely applicable, moving offline RL closer to deployment in complex, real-world systems.

To address these issues, the authors propose a method that dynamically adjusts the constraint scale, balancing exploration (via the RL objective) with adherence to the behavior policy (via the BC objective). This dynamic adjustment aims to discover a more stable learning path and achieve adaptability across a wide range of tasks and dataset qualities. The intention is to enable a single hyperparameter configuration to perform comparably to, or better than, finely tuned methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Adaptive Scaling of Policy Constraints (ASPC), is a second-order differentiable framework designed to automatically adjust the scale factor (denoted as $\alpha$) of policy constraints during training. ASPC builds upon the TD3+BC algorithm but introduces a learnable $\alpha$ and a bilevel optimization strategy.

#### 3.1. Core Framework: Adaptive Scaling of Policy Constraints

ASPC treats the scale factor $\alpha$ as a learnable parameter that balances the RL objective ($L_{RL}$) and the BC objective ($L_{BC}$) within a combined loss function. The core mechanism is a meta-learning approach involving inner and outer optimization loops.

- **Inner Update**: This loop optimizes the policy parameters ($\theta$) for a given value of $\alpha$. The inner objective is defined as:
 $L_{inner}(\theta; \alpha) = E_{(s,a)\sim D}[-\lambda(\alpha) Q(s, \pi_\theta(s)) + \|\pi_\theta(s) - a\|^2]$
 where $\lambda(\alpha) = \alpha / E_{s\sim D}[|Q(s, \pi_\theta(s))|]$ normalizes the RL term. A standard gradient descent step is applied to update $\theta$ to $\tilde{\theta}(\alpha)$.

- **Outer Update**: This loop is responsible for dynamically adjusting $\alpha$. The outer loss, $L_{outer}(\tilde{\theta}(\alpha))$, consists of three components:
 1. **$L_1$**: This term mirrors the TD3+BC objective, steering $\alpha$ towards a balance between Q-value maximization and BC. It combines a normalized Q-value term and the BC loss for the updated policy $\tilde{\theta}$.
 $L_1 = -\alpha \frac{E_{s\sim D}[Q(s, \pi_{\tilde{\theta}}(s))]}{E_{s\sim D}[|Q(s, \pi_{\tilde{\theta}}(s))|]} + E_{(s,a)\sim D}[\|\pi_{\tilde{\theta}}(s) - a\|^2]$
 2. **$L_2$**: This term penalizes large squared changes in the expected Q-value of the policy after an inner update. It helps prevent abrupt increases in Q-values, which can indicate instability or overestimation.
 $L_2 = (E_{s\sim D}[Q(s, \pi_{\tilde{\theta}}(s))] - E_{s\sim D}[Q(s, \pi_\theta(s))])^2$
 3. **$L_3$**: This term constrains large shifts in the behavior cloning loss. Its specific form is derived from theoretical analysis to ensure single-step performance guarantees. Intuitively, it combines factors related to the rate of Q-value change (from $L_2$ detached), the upper bound of the BC loss, and the variation in BC loss across iterations. It helps suppress distributional shift when Q-value fluctuations are high or BC deviation is significant.
 $L_3 = (L_2.detach) \frac{\sup_{(s,a)\in D}\|\pi_\theta(s) - a\|^2}{\sup_{(s,a)\in D}\|\pi_{\tilde{\theta}}(s) - a\|^2 - \|\pi_\theta(s) - a\|^2}$
 Note: The `.detach` indicates that gradients are stopped for $L_2$ within $L_3$'s calculation.

The outer objective is $L_{outer}(\tilde{\theta}(\alpha)) = L_1 + L_2 + L_3$. The parameter $\alpha$ is updated using a gradient descent step based on the second-order derivative of $L_{outer}$ with respect to $\alpha$, treating $\tilde{\theta}(\alpha)$ as an implicit function of $\alpha$.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical insights into ASPC's behavior:
- **Proposition 4.2 (Mutual constraints between $\Delta L_{BC}$ and $(\Delta Q)^2$)**: This proposition demonstrates that the change in BC loss and the squared change in Q-values are inherently coupled. This explains why, in some tasks, only one of the regularization terms ($L_2$ or $L_3$) might suffice, while others require both for stability.
- **Proposition 4.3 (Single-step performance lower bound)**: This establishes a lower bound for the policy performance improvement ($J(\pi_{t+1}) - J(\pi_t)$) that depends on the change in Q-values ($\Delta Q$) and a function ($\Phi$) of the BC-loss variation and upper bound. This proposition directly motivates the design of $L_3$.
- **Theorem 4.4 (Single-step performance condition for ASPC)**: An idealized ASPC update that satisfies $\Delta Q \geq \Phi$ leads to non-decreasing policy performance. The outer objective of ASPC is designed as a smooth relaxation to guide updates towards this stable regime.
- **Theorem 4.5 (Performance gap to optimal)**: This theorem shows that ASPC progressively reduces the suboptimality gap to the optimal policy, with the remaining gap at convergence controlled by the mismatch between the behavior and optimal policies ($\Psi(\epsilon_\beta)$).

#### 3.3. Implementation on TD3+BC

ASPC is implemented as a modification of the TD3+BC algorithm. The main changes are:
- **Robust Critic Network**: The original TD3+BC critic (two hidden layers) is extended to three hidden layers, with LayerNormalization applied after each layer. This modification aims to mitigate Q-value overestimation and improve stability, which is particularly relevant when $\alpha$ is dynamically adjusted.
- **Learnable Scale Factor ($\alpha$)**: The fixed $\alpha$ in TD3+BC is replaced by a learnable parameter.
- **Alpha Update Interval ($k_\alpha$)**: To manage the computational cost of second-order gradients, the $\alpha$-update interval ($k_\alpha$) is set to be substantially longer than the actor-update interval ($k_\pi$). This minimizes the added computational overhead while maintaining performance.

#### 3.4. Extension to Other Offline RL Algorithms

The paper demonstrates the generality of ASPC by integrating its adaptive policy constraint mechanism into other offline RL algorithms that feature a similar trade-off parameter: IQL, CQL, Diffusion-QL, and FQL. For each, the algorithm's specific trade-off hyperparameter is made learnable and updated via a bi-level second-order procedure, similar to how $\alpha$ is optimized in the TD3+BC context.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings that support the effectiveness, adaptability, and generality of the ASPC framework.

#### 4.1. Comparative Performance on Benchmark

- **Overall Performance**: ASPC, using a single hyperparameter configuration, outperforms other adaptive constraint methods (wPC, A2PR) and state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms that rely on per-dataset tuning (IQL, ReBRAC) on the D4RL benchmark (39 datasets across MuJoCo, AntMaze, Maze2d, and Adroit domains).
- **Average Improvement**: ASPC achieves an average 35% improvement in normalized performance over the baseline (TD3+BC with robust critic).
- **Domain-Specific Performance**: ASPC attains the best average performance on MuJoCo and Maze2d domains and demonstrates competitive results on AntMaze and Adroit.
- **Robustness**: Performance profiles show ASPC consistently above baselines across almost all thresholds, and a radar chart indicates a larger, more balanced performance polygon, suggesting strong and stable performance without per-dataset tuning.

#### 4.2. Adaptability of the Scale Factor ($\alpha$)

- **Dataset Quality Adaptability (Figure 3)**: The learning curves of $\alpha$ on HalfCheetah, Hopper, and Walker2d tasks show that ASPC automatically adjusts $\alpha$ based on dataset quality. Higher-quality datasets (e.g., expert, medium-expert) lead to smaller $\alpha$ values, increasing the emphasis on behavior cloning (BC). Conversely, lower-quality datasets (e.g., random, medium-replay) result in larger $\alpha$ values, shifting emphasis towards the reinforcement learning (RL) objective.
- **Task Adaptability (Figure 4)**: Across heterogeneous tasks (e.g., Adroit vs. AntMaze/Maze2d), $\alpha$ converges to different values. Tasks with narrow expert data distributions (e.g., Adroit tasks like door, pen, hammer, relocate) favor BC with $\alpha$ settling near $10^{-1}$. Tasks with suboptimal trajectories (e.g., AntMaze, Maze2d) drive $\alpha$ above 10, prioritizing RL. This demonstrates task-aware scaling without manual intervention.
- **Training Adaptability (Figures 3 & 4)**: A common dynamic observed is that $\alpha$ initially drops or rises slightly, indicating a greater reliance on BC during the early, unstable learning phases. As training progresses and the critic stabilizes, $\alpha$ gradually increases, allowing RL to exert more control. This stage-wise adjustment contributes to stable convergence.

#### 4.3. Necessity of Dynamic Scale Factor Adjustment

- **Performance Comparison (Table 2)**: Experiments comparing fixed $\alpha$ (naive, fixed to converged, linear interpolation) against dynamic $\alpha$ (ASPC) confirm that dynamic adjustment is crucial. While fixing $\alpha$ to its converged value (found by ASPC) or linearly interpolating to it yields improvements over a naive fixed $\alpha$, ASPC (dynamic $\alpha$) still significantly outperforms these variants. This indicates that simply finding a good fixed $\alpha$ is insufficient; adapting the scale throughout training is essential for optimal performance.

#### 4.4. Runtime Analysis

- **Minimal Overhead (Figure 5a)**: ASPC introduces only minimal additional computational overhead compared to its TD3+BC backbone, despite using second-order gradients.
- **Efficiency with Update Interval (Figure 5b)**: Increasing the $\alpha$-update interval ($k_\alpha$) reduces runtime while maintaining strong performance. For instance, with $k_\alpha=30$, runtime is nearly identical to TD3+BC, yet performance remains high. This suggests ASPC efficiently captures optimization directions even with less frequent updates.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Robust Critic (RC) (Figure 6a)**: The redesigned critic network (three hidden layers with LayerNorm) is essential for stability. Without RC, Q-values become unstable and prone to overestimation, leading to catastrophic algorithm failure for both ASPC and wPC.
- **Outer Loss Function Components (Figure 6b)**:
 - Using only $L_1$ results in the poorest performance.
 - Adding $L_2$ alone improves performance on MuJoCo and Adroit to the level of the full ASPC.
 - Adding $L_3$ alone significantly boosts performance on AntMaze.
 - For Maze2d, neither $L_2$ nor $L_3$ alone suffices; both are required for optimal performance.
 - This supports Proposition 4.2, indicating that $L_2$ and $L_3$ can implicitly constrain each other in some domains, while in others, explicit enforcement of both is necessary.
- **L3 Formulation (Appendix E.2, Table 13)**: The inclusion of the detached $L_2$ term is essential for performance gains on Maze2d and AntMaze. Replacing supremum operators with dataset expectations in $L_3$ yields nearly identical performance to the original formulation, suggesting that strict worst-case bounds are not strictly necessary in practice.

#### 4.6. Extending ASPC to Other Offline RL Methods

- **Broad Applicability (Table 3)**: Integrating ASPC's adaptive constraint mechanism into IQL, CQL, and Diffusion-QL consistently improves their performance on Gym-MuJoCo datasets.
- **Varied Gains**: The magnitude of improvement varies across algorithms, with IQL showing the smallest gains (possibly due to its implicit Q-learning structure), CQL benefiting more from direct conservatism adjustment, and Diffusion-QL also seeing further improvements despite its already strong baseline performance.
- **OGBench Performance (Table 4)**: ASPC, integrated into FQL, also shows consistent improvements on the OGBench (goal-conditioned RL) benchmark, further demonstrating its broad generality beyond D4RL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, with the introduction of Adaptive Scaling of Policy Constraints (ASPC), addresses a significant practical and methodological challenge in offline reinforcement learning: the manual tuning of policy constraint hyperparameters. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Practicality and Reduced Overhead**: ASPC eliminates the need for time-consuming and resource-intensive per-dataset hyperparameter tuning. By dynamically adjusting the constraint scale, it enables offline RL algorithms to achieve robust performance with a single, generalizable hyperparameter configuration. This reduction in operational complexity makes offline RL more accessible and practical for real-world applications where datasets are diverse, constantly changing, or tuning is prohibitively expensive.

2. **Improved Performance and Stability**: ASPC consistently outperforms existing adaptive constraint methods and state-of-the-art algorithms that rely on meticulous tuning. Its ability to dynamically balance the RL objective with behavior cloning leads to more stable learning paths and higher ultimate policy performance across a wide range of tasks and dataset qualities. The theoretical analysis provides a foundation for its stability by offering performance improvement guarantees.

3. **Generality and Broad Applicability**: The framework is demonstrated to be effective not only as a standalone improvement to TD3+BC but also when integrated into other diverse offline RL algorithms like IQL, CQL, Diffusion-QL, and FQL. This suggests that the principle of adaptive constraint scaling is a broadly applicable concept that can enhance various offline RL methodologies.

4. **Deeper Understanding of Constraint Dynamics**: The theoretical analysis, particularly concerning the mutual constraints between changes in BC loss and Q-values, offers valuable insights into the underlying dynamics of policy constraint methods. The ablation studies further illuminate the specific roles of different regularization components in ensuring stable and effective learning across different domains.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: ASPC provides a strong foundation for future research in adaptive offline RL. The observation that simple integrations yield smaller gains than with TD3+BC suggests avenues for developing method-specific adaptive mechanisms tailored to the unique training dynamics of various algorithms. This could lead to more specialized and powerful adaptive solutions.

6. **Potential for Real-World Deployment**: By increasing the robustness and reducing the tuning burden, ASPC moves offline RL closer to deployment in safety-critical and economically sensitive domains such as autonomous driving, personalized healthcare, and industrial control, where interaction with the environment is limited and reliable policies are paramount.

In conclusion, ASPC represents a methodological advancement that significantly improves the robustness, efficiency, and practicality of offline reinforcement learning by automating a critical aspect of algorithm configuration.
