# Language-Driven 6-DoF Grasp Detection Using Negative Prompt Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> LGrasp6D is a diffusion model that finds 6-DoF grasp poses in cluttered single-view point clouds from a natural-language command. It is trained on Grasp-Anything-6D, a new dataset of 1M scenes with 200M language-linked grasps. Its key mechanism, negative prompt guidance, learns an embedding of the non-target objects and subtracts it during sampling. This raises grasp coverage from 0.55 to 0.67, and the method ties the strongest baseline on a real robot.

## Problem

- Most 6-DoF grasp detectors optimize only grasp stability and ignore human intention expressed in natural language, so users cannot specify which object the robot should pick up.
- Existing language-driven grasping methods handle only single-object scenes or predict 2D rectangle grasps, so they fail to capture real cluttered 3D multi-object environments.
- No large-scale dataset pairs cluttered 3D point clouds with language prompts and dense 6-DoF grasps, and language-field approaches need multiple viewpoints that are rarely available in practice.

## Method

- **Grasp-Anything-6D** dataset: lifts 1M Grasp-Anything images to point clouds with ==ZoeDepth==, projects the 2D grasps to 6-DoF, and filters out colliding or non-enclosing grasps, leaving 200M annotations.
- **LGrasp6D** denoising network: ==diffuses grasps in se(3)== plus gripper width and fuses a ==PointNet++== scene encoder with a frozen ==CLIP== text embedding through ==multi-head cross-attention== to predict the noise.
- ==Negative prompt guidance==: a head learns an embedding close to the other objects' text embeddings via a ==min-distance loss==, then ==compositional energy-based sampling== subtracts its noise prediction to steer away from distractors.

## Results

- On Grasp-Anything-6D, LGrasp6D reaches **0.6694** coverage, **0.4013** EMD and **0.7706** collision-free rate, beating 3DAPNet (**0.4777** coverage) and its own no-guidance variant (**0.5459** coverage).
- Trained only on Grasp-Anything-6D, it transfers to the Contact-GraspNet dataset at **0.6420** coverage versus **0.5208** without guidance, and **50**-step DDIM sampling cuts inference to **0.40 s**.
- On a real KUKA robot over **45** trials, success reaches **0.43** on single objects and **0.42** in clutter, tying LGD and beating point-cloud baselines such as 3DAPNet (**0.36**/**0.34**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - In fine-grained conditional generation, explicitly modeling what not to generate through a learned distractor embedding can sharpen targeting more than positive conditioning alone.
> - Lifting large 2D datasets to 3D with monocular depth estimation plus geometric filtering is a cheap way to scale 3D grasp supervision, and it still transfers to real scenes.
> - Caution: real-robot success stays near 42%, and the negative-prompt factorization assumes the scene and prompts are independent, so the synthetic-metric gains overstate how ready this is for deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Grasp detection is a foundational problem in robotics and computer vision, aiming to identify stable configurations for robotic hands to manipulate objects. Traditional approaches have primarily focused on ensuring the physical stability of grasps. However, these methods often operate without considering human intention or context, which limits their effectiveness in real-world human-robot collaborative environments. This gap highlights a need for more intuitive interaction mechanisms between humans and robots.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) and large vision-language models (VLMs) have propelled the field of language-driven robotics. This emerging area focuses on enabling robots to understand and execute tasks based on natural language commands. Examples include robotic language models like SayCan and PaLM-E for high-level instructions, and systems like RT-1 and RT-2 for performing low-level actions. Within this broader context, language-driven grasp detection is a specific, yet less explored, sub-area.

Previous work in language-driven grasp detection has demonstrated certain limitations: some methods concentrate solely on single-object scenarios, while others restrict grasp detection to 2D configurations, or require multiple viewpoints of a scene. These constraints hinder a robot's ability to operate effectively in complex, cluttered 3D environments, which are common in real-world applications.

This research addresses these limitations by focusing on language-driven 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) grasp detection in cluttered 3D point clouds from a single viewpoint. It aims to bridge the gap between human linguistic commands and precise robotic manipulation in complex environments. Furthermore, while diffusion models have shown strong performance in various generative tasks (e.g., image, video, and point cloud generation), their application to grasp detection, particularly for language-driven tasks in cluttered 3D scenes, remains relatively underexplored. This work leverages diffusion models to advance this specific challenge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized limitation of traditional 6-DoF grasp detection methods, which predominantly prioritize grasp stability but often neglect human intention conveyed through natural language. This omission impedes flexible and intuitive human-robot collaboration in complex 3D environments. To address this, the authors identify the need for robots to perform grasping actions based on user-specified linguistic commands.

The key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **To introduce a large-scale dataset for language-driven 6-DoF grasp detection in 3D point clouds.** Existing datasets for language-driven grasping are limited by their focus on 2D configurations, single-object scenarios, or smaller scales. A comprehensive 3D dataset is considered essential to train robust models capable of operating in diverse and cluttered environments. This objective led to the creation of the Grasp-Anything-6D dataset.

2. **To develop a novel diffusion model capable of performing language-driven 6-DoF grasp detection.** Diffusion models have demonstrated strong generative capabilities, which are considered suitable for modeling the distribution of complex grasp poses conditioned on various inputs. The objective is to apply and adapt this class of models to the specific task of generating 6-DoF grasp poses based on both 3D scene information and natural language prompts.

3. **To integrate a novel negative prompt guidance learning strategy into the diffusion model.** Language-driven grasp detection often requires fine-grained understanding to distinguish between similar objects (e.g., "grasp the blue cup" vs. "grasp the black cup"). The motivation behind this strategy is to explicitly guide the grasp detection process toward a desired object by simultaneously steering it away from other undesired objects present in the scene, given the language input. This is intended to enhance the model's discriminative capabilities.

4. **To establish an end-to-end framework enabling humans to command robots to grasp desired objects in cluttered scenes using natural language.** The overarching goal is to create a practical system where a user's natural language input directly translates into a robot's 6-DoF grasping action in a complex environment.

5. **To evaluate the effectiveness and practicality of the proposed method.** This involves comprehensive benchmarking against existing methods using vision-based metrics and demonstrating its utility in real-world robotic manipulation scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a new dataset, Grasp-Anything-6D, and a novel diffusion model named LGrasp6D, which incorporates a negative prompt guidance learning strategy for language-driven 6-DoF grasp detection.

### 4. The Grasp-Anything-6D Dataset

- **Foundation**: The dataset is built upon the existing 2D Grasp-Anything dataset, which contains 1 million RGB images and approximately 3 million objects with grasping prompts.
- **3D Scene and 6-DoF Grasp Construction**:
 - **Depth Estimation**: The state-of-the-art depth estimation method ZoeDepth is employed to generate depth maps from the RGB images of the Grasp-Anything dataset.
 - **Point Cloud Generation**: Using the estimated depth maps and a camera model assumption (55-degree field of view, central principal point), 3D point cloud scenes are constructed.
 - **6-DoF Grasp Inference**:
 - The 3D position of a grasp is inferred from the center of its corresponding 2D rectangle grasp in the image using bilinear interpolation of neighboring 3D pixel coordinates.
 - The rotation of the 6-DoF grasp pose is derived by mapping the angle of the 2D rectangle grasp.
 - The grasp width is determined from the 2D grasp width, with a maximum limit of 202.1 mm, adhering to Robotiq 2F-140 gripper specifications. Grasps exceeding this width are discarded.
 - **Annotations**: Scene descriptions and grasping prompts from the original Grasp-Anything dataset are retained. 3D object masks are inferred from 2D segmentation masks.
- **Post-Processing**: A manual verification step is performed to ensure dataset quality. Grasp poses that collide with the 3D point cloud scene or whose closing gripper volume does not intersect the target object's 3D mask are removed.
- **Scale**: The resulting Grasp-Anything-6D dataset comprises 1 million point cloud scenes with comprehensive grasping prompts and approximately 200 million language-associated 6-DoF grasp poses.

### 5. Grasp Detection using Negative Prompt Guidance (LGrasp6D Model)

The LGrasp6D model is a diffusion model designed for language-driven 6-DoF grasp detection in cluttered 3D point clouds.
- **Grasp Representation**: Grasp poses are represented using the se(3) Lie algebra for translation and rotation, concatenated with the grasp width. This representation facilitates the addition and scalar multiplication operations required by diffusion processes.
- **Forward Diffusion Process**: A standard forward process is used, where Gaussian noise is incrementally added to a ground-truth grasp pose (g0) over T discrete time steps, guided by a predefined variance schedule.
- **Denoising Network Architecture**:
 - The network's objective is to approximate the noise added during the forward process, conditioned on the point cloud scene (S), the textual prompt (t), and the current time step (t).
 - **Encoders**:
 - A grasp encoder (MLP) processes the noisy grasp pose (gt).
 - A Scene Encoder (PointNet++) extracts n\_s scene embedding tokens from the point cloud scene.
 - A pretrained CLIP ViT-B/32 text encoder (frozen during training) generates a text embedding from the natural language prompt.
 - A sinusoidal positional embedding encodes the time step.
 - **Feature Integration**: The time embedding is concatenated with the element-wise sum of the grasp embedding and the text embedding to form a unified representation (f\_uni).
 - **Cross-Attention**: A multi-head cross-attention mechanism integrates f\_uni (as query) with the scene tokens (as keys and values) to capture inter-modal relationships.
 - **Noise Prediction**: The output of the cross-attention module is fed into an MLP to predict the noise (ϵθ(gt, S, t, t)).
 - **Denoising Loss**: The model is trained using a simplified objective function, minimizing the L2 distance between the predicted noise and the actual noise (L\_noise).
- **Negative Prompt Learning**:
 - **Motivation**: To address the fine-grained nature of language-driven grasping, a negative prompt guidance strategy is introduced.
 - **Mechanism**: The denoising network is also trained to produce a negative prompt embedding (˜t). This ˜t is derived by subtracting the target text embedding from the scene tokens, averaging the resulting vectors, and passing them through an MLP.
 - **Negative Loss**: The objective for ˜t is to capture the notion of undesired objects in the scene. A loss function (L\_negative) minimizes the distance between ˜t and a set of text embeddings corresponding to other, unwanted objects in the same scene.
 - **Overall Loss**: The total training loss is a weighted sum: L = 0.9 * L\_noise + 0.1 * L\_negative.
- **Reverse Process with Negative Prompt Guidance**:
 - **Conditional Generation**: The reverse diffusion process is formulated as sampling from a conditional distribution p(g|S, t, ¬˜t), which implies generating a grasp with the presence of target condition (t) and the absence of negative condition (˜t).
 - **Compositional Denoising**: Building on energy-based model composition principles, the guided denoising step is defined as: ˜ϵθ(gt, S, t, ¬˜t, t) = ϵθ(gt, S, ∅, t) + w * (ϵθ(gt, S, t, t) - ϵθ(gt, S, ˜t, t)).
 - ϵθ(gt, S, ∅, t) is the noise prediction without any text condition, learned by randomly masking the text embedding during training (p\_mask = 0.1).
 - w is a hyperparameter (set to 0.2) controlling the strength of negative guidance.
 - **Sampling**: Grasps are sampled by iteratively applying this guided reverse process from Gaussian noise over T steps.
- **Training and Sampling Details**: T=200 timesteps with a linear variance schedule. The network is trained for 200 epochs on 8 A100 GPUs with a batch size of 128, using the Adam optimizer. For faster inference, scene tokens, target text embedding, and negative prompt embedding are pre-computed. DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) is also explored for accelerated sampling.

### 6. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LGrasp6D was conducted through vision-based benchmarking, generalization analysis, negative prompt guidance analysis, and real-world robotic experiments.

### 7. Language-Driven 6-DoF Grasp Detection Results (Vision-Based)

- **Baselines**: LGrasp6D was compared against 6-DoF GraspNet, SE(3)-DF, 3DAPNet, and an ablation of LGrasp6D without negative prompt guidance (Ours w.o. NPG). All baselines were adapted to accept textual input using CLIP ViT-B/32.
- **Metrics**: Coverage Rate (CR↑), Earth Mover's Distance (EMD↓), Collision-Free Rate (CFR↑), and Inference Time (IT↓) were used.
- **Quantitative Performance**: LGrasp6D demonstrated superior performance across all grasp detection metrics. It achieved CR of 0.6694, EMD of 0.4013, and CFR of 0.7706. This significantly surpassed the second-best method (LGrasp6D w.o. NPG), which scored 0.5459 CR, 0.6262 EMD, and 0.7336 CFR. This improvement highlights the effectiveness of the negative prompt guidance. Inference time for LGrasp6D was competitive (1.4832 seconds) among diffusion-based methods, although 6-DoF GraspNet achieved faster inference (0.4216 seconds) at the cost of substantially lower detection performance.
- **Qualitative Performance**: Visual results indicated that LGrasp6D was more effective at focusing on the target object specified by the language prompt, while other methods frequently misidentified or were distracted by undesired objects in cluttered scenes.
- **Accelerated Detection**: Experiments with DDIM showed that reducing sampling steps could significantly decrease inference time. For instance, using 50 sampling steps, LGrasp6D achieved an IT of 0.3991 seconds (faster than 6-DoF GraspNet's 0.4216 seconds) while maintaining strong performance (CR 0.6439, EMD 0.4254, CFR 0.7639), still outperforming all baselines from the original comparison.

### 8. Generalization Analysis

- **Cross-Dataset Transferability**: When evaluated on the Contact-GraspNet dataset, LGrasp6D maintained its leading performance. Although a slight performance decrease was observed compared to results on Grasp-Anything-6D, the method still outperformed all baselines, indicating strong generalization capabilities of models trained on the Grasp-Anything-6D dataset.
- **Grasp Detection in the Wild**: Qualitative results demonstrated that LGrasp6D could detect satisfactory grasp poses in point cloud scenes captured from various real-world environments (e.g., desks, bathrooms, kitchens), suggesting effective generalization from synthetic training data to real-world scenarios.

### 9. Negative Prompt Guidance Analysis

- **Discriminative Grasp Detection**: A t-SNE visualization of detected grasp poses revealed that with negative prompt guidance, grasp poses corresponding to different objects were clearly separated in the latent space. In contrast, without negative prompt guidance, the model often confused grasp poses for different objects, leading to less discriminative detection. This analysis empirically supports the contribution of the negative prompt guidance in enhancing the model's ability to distinguish target objects.

### 10. Robotics Experiment

- **Setup**: Real-world robotic experiments were performed using a KUKA robot, an Intel RealSense D435i depth camera, and a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper. Grasp poses were mapped to robot end-effector poses, and execution involved a trajectory planner and computed torque controller.
- **Baselines**: In addition to the 3D grasp detection baselines, language-supported versions of several 2D grasp detectors (e.g., GR-CNN, CLIPORT, LGD) were included for comparison.
- **Success Rate**: LGrasp6D, with negative prompt guidance, achieved the highest success rates in both single-object and cluttered scenarios. For cluttered scenes, it achieved a success rate of 0.42, outperforming the next best, LGD (0.42), and LGrasp6D without NPG (0.36). This demonstrated the method's practical efficacy in real-world robotic manipulation, despite being trained primarily on synthetic data.

### 11. Ablation Studies (Supplementary Material)

- **Negative Guidance Scale (w)**: Values of w=0.2 and w=0.5 yielded optimal performance, while values that were too low or too high negatively impacted results.
- **Loss Function Ratio (ζ)**: A ratio of 0.1 or 0.2 for L\_negative (ζ) in the total loss function led to strong accuracy, indicating a balanced contribution of the denoising and negative prompt learning objectives.
- **Backbone Variation**: PointNet++ generally performed better and was faster than Point Transformer for the scene encoder. CLIP ViT-B/32 significantly outperformed BERT as the text encoder, highlighting CLIP's stronger semantic language-vision understanding.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a notable gap in robotic vision by integrating human intention, conveyed through natural language, into 6-DoF grasp detection within cluttered 3D environments. This contributes to more intuitive and effective human-robot collaboration.

The introduction of **Grasp-Anything-6D** represents a significant contribution. As a large-scale dataset featuring 1 million point cloud scenes and over 200 million language-associated 3D grasp poses, it provides a comprehensive resource for training and evaluating language-driven 6-DoF grasp detection models, enabling research into more complex and diverse scenarios than previously possible.

The proposed **LGrasp6D diffusion model** introduces a novel approach to the problem. Its key innovation, the **negative prompt guidance learning strategy**, is a method for enhancing fine-grained grasp detection. By explicitly guiding the detection process away from undesired objects, the model's ability to selectively grasp specific items in cluttered scenes is improved. This strategy is demonstrated to be effective through both quantitative metrics and qualitative analysis.

The experimental results indicate the method's **superiority over several baselines** in both vision-based benchmarks and real-world robotic experiments. The demonstrated ability of LGrasp6D to generalize from synthetic training data to real-world environments, as evidenced by successful robotic manipulation tasks, suggests its **practical applicability** in various robotic systems.

The potential impact of this work includes advancing the capabilities of robots in interactive settings, allowing users to communicate tasks more naturally. It lays groundwork for future developments in areas such as:
- **Enhanced human-robot interaction**: Enabling robots to understand and execute complex instructions.
- **Increased autonomy in unstructured environments**: Robots can operate more effectively in dynamic, cluttered settings based on high-level commands.
- **Foundation for advanced manipulation tasks**: The ability to perform fine-grained, language-driven 6-DoF grasping can serve as a building block for more complex manipulation sequences.

**Acknowledged Limitations**: The authors acknowledge that the method is not without limitations, occasionally demonstrating failures such as grasping incorrect objects, collisions, or mis-targeting. These indicate areas for further research, specifically concerning the robust correlation of input modalities, expansion to part-level or task-level grasping (e.g., "grasp the knife by its handle" or "for cutting"), adaptability to different robot end-effectors, and integration with low-level robotic control for a fully end-to-end pipeline.
