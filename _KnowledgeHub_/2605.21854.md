# CrossVLA: Cross-Paradigm Post-Training and Inference Optimization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CrossVLA investigates post-training and inference optimization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for continuous-action flow-matching architectures via a tractable surrogate log-probability. The framework demonstrates that DoRA adapters improve autoregressive VLA performance by an average of +10.4 percentage points on LIBERO benchmarks and reveals that traditional KV-cache inference acceleration methods are ineffective for flow-matching VLAs.

## Problem

- Continuous-action flow-matching VLA models lacked a tractable log-probability formulation, preventing the application of DPO for preference alignment.
- The optimal parameter-efficient adapter (LoRA vs. DoRA) for VLA DPO had not been empirically compared across different VLA architectures and tasks.
- Inference acceleration techniques like KV-caching, effective for autoregressive VLAs, had not been evaluated for their transferability or benefit to flow-matching VLA architectures.

## Method

- A tractable surrogate log-probability was defined for flow-matching VLA models, enabling DPO by approximating log-likelihood with negative mean squared error (MSE) of predicted and target velocities.
- Parameter-efficient adapters, ==LoRA== and DoRA, were empirically compared for VLA DPO across OpenVLA (autoregressive) and \u03c00.5 (flow-matching) models on the LIBERO 4-suite benchmark.
- A detailed latency analysis was performed on flow-matching VLA inference, and two KV-cache based acceleration strategies were tested for their effectiveness.

## Results

- DoRA + DPO improved OpenVLA SFT performance by a mean of +**10.4** percentage points across LIBERO suites, achieving a +20.0 pp gain on the Object suite (from **56%** to **76.0%**).
- The surrogate log-probability enabled stable DPO training for \u03c00.5, with monotonic loss decrease and positive chosen/rejected margin growth, even on saturated tasks.
- KV-cache strategies for flow-matching VLAs resulted in negative outcomes, including a **30%** slowdown and **20%** success rate drop for chunk-level caching, and complete failure (**0%** success) for token-level prefix caching.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DPO can be successfully applied to continuous-action flow-matching VLAs using a defined surrogate log-probability, unifying post-training across VLA paradigms.
> - DoRA adapters offer stable and often superior performance compared to LoRA for VLA DPO, particularly on tasks requiring magnitude refinement rather than broad directional shifts.
> - Inference acceleration strategies targeting visual token caching (like KV-cache) are not effective for flow-matching VLAs, where the denoise loop is the primary computational bottleneck.
