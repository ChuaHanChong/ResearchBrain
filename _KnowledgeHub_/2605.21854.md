# CrossVLA: Cross-Paradigm Post-Training and Inference Optimization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CrossVLA investigates post-training and inference optimization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for continuous-action flow-matching architectures via a tractable surrogate log-probability. The framework demonstrates that DoRA adapters improve autoregressive VLA performance by an average of +10.4 percentage points on LIBERO benchmarks and reveals that traditional KV-cache inference acceleration methods are ineffective for flow-matching VLAs.

## Problem

- Continuous-action flow-matching VLA models lacked a tractable log-probability formulation, preventing the application of DPO for preference alignment.
- The optimal parameter-efficient adapter (LoRA vs. DoRA) for VLA DPO had not been empirically compared across different VLA architectures and tasks.
- Inference acceleration techniques like KV-caching, effective for autoregressive VLAs, had not been evaluated for their transferability or benefit to flow-matching VLA architectures.

## Method

- A tractable surrogate log-probability was defined for flow-matching VLA models, enabling DPO by approximating log-likelihood with negative mean squared error (MSE) of predicted and target velocities.
- Parameter-efficient adapters, ==LoRA== and DoRA, were empirically compared for VLA DPO across OpenVLA (autoregressive) and π0.5 (flow-matching) models on the LIBERO 4-suite benchmark.
- A detailed latency analysis was performed on flow-matching VLA inference, and two KV-cache based acceleration strategies were tested for their effectiveness.

## Results

- DoRA + DPO improved OpenVLA SFT performance by a mean of +**10.4** percentage points across LIBERO suites, achieving a +20.0 pp gain on the Object suite (from **56%** to **76.0%**).
- The surrogate log-probability enabled stable DPO training for π0.5, with monotonic loss decrease and positive chosen/rejected margin growth, even on saturated tasks.
- KV-cache strategies for flow-matching VLAs resulted in negative outcomes, including a **30%** slowdown and **20%** success rate drop for chunk-level caching, and complete failure (**0%** success) for token-level prefix caching.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DPO can be successfully applied to continuous-action flow-matching VLAs using a defined surrogate log-probability, unifying post-training across VLA paradigms.
> - DoRA adapters offer stable and often superior performance compared to LoRA for VLA DPO, particularly on tasks requiring magnitude refinement rather than broad directional shifts.
> - Inference acceleration strategies targeting visual token caching (like KV-cache) are not effective for flow-matching VLAs, where the denoise loop is the primary computational bottleneck.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a growing area within robotics and artificial intelligence, aiming to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and execute complex manipulation tasks based on visual observations. The field has seen the emergence of distinct architectural paradigms. Prominently, these include autoregressive models that discretize actions into tokens and predict them sequentially (e.g., OpenVLA), and flow-matching models that generate continuous action chunks via probability-flow ODE integration (e.g., π0.5).

Despite advancements in VLA model development and supervised fine-tuning (SFT), post-training techniques, analogous to reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) in large language models (LLMs), remain less explored for VLAs. Existing work on preference alignment for VLAs has largely focused on autoregressive architectures, where the calculation of log-probabilities required by DPO is straightforward. Flow-matching models, however, present a challenge due to the lack of a closed-form chunk-level log-probability, making standard DPO formulation difficult without computationally intensive ODE integration. This paper addresses this gap by seeking to extend DPO to flow-matching backbones.

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), are widely used to efficiently adapt large models. A newer variant, DoRA (Weight-Decomposed Low-Rank Adaptation), has demonstrated modest gains in LLM instruction tuning, but its applicability and comparative performance in the context of VLA post-training, particularly for diverse robotic manipulation tasks, had not been empirically studied. This work contributes to understanding the interaction between PEFT layer choice and VLA architecture/task families.

Furthermore, inference acceleration techniques, such as caching mechanisms for Key-Value (K/V) pairs, have been developed for autoregressive VLAs (e.g., VLA-Cache) to improve speed by reusing static visual tokens. Whether these strategies effectively transfer to flow-matching VLAs, which possess different computational profiles, is an open question that this research investigates.

Finally, the paper contributes to the area of self-supervised pretraining for robot representations. While various methods exist for learning visual encoders for robotics (e.g., R3M, SiamMAE, MV-MWM), this work introduces a multi-view + temporal contrastive pretraining framework tailored to generate features compatible with existing VLA vision encoders like SigLIP, aiming to provide a beneficial downstream initialization.

This research, therefore, positions itself at the intersection of VLA model architectures, advanced post-training, efficient fine-tuning, and inference optimization, aiming to generalize and optimize practices across the evolving landscape of robotic learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are structured around three core empirical questions, with an additional contribution concerning representation learning:

1. **Enabling DPO on Flow-Matching VLAs:**
 - **Objective:** To determine if Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) can be effectively applied to flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as π0.5, through the development of a tractable surrogate log-probability estimator.
 - **Motivation:** DPO has become a standard and effective post-training technique for aligning language models with preferences. However, its application to flow-matching VLAs is hindered by the absence of a closed-form log-probability for continuous action chunks. Standard methods for calculating this would involve computationally prohibitive probability-flow Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) integration and Jacobian costs. The motivation is to bridge this methodological gap, allowing the benefits of preference alignment to extend to a critical architectural paradigm within VLAs.

2. **Evaluating Parameter-Efficient Adapters (LoRA vs. DoRA) for VLA DPO:**
 - **Objective:** To conduct a head-to-head empirical comparison of LoRA and DoRA as parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) layers for VLA DPO, assessing their interaction with backbone architectures and specific task families within robotics.
 - **Motivation:** LoRA is a widely adopted PEFT technique. DoRA, a more recent advancement, decomposes weight updates into magnitude and direction components, which has shown benefits in LLM instruction tuning. The motivation is to investigate whether this magnitude-direction decoupling offers advantages in the context of VLA fine-tuning, particularly given that VLA adaptation on benchmarks like LIBERO often involves narrow-distribution adjustments rather than large semantic transfers. Understanding which adapter is more effective and why, especially across different task complexities, can inform optimal PEFT strategies for robot learning.

3. **Assessing Inference-Time Acceleration Transferability to Flow-Matching VLAs:**
 - **Objective:** To empirically evaluate whether inference-time acceleration techniques designed for autoregressive VLAs, specifically K/V-cache reuse strategies (e.g., VLA-Cache), can be successfully transferred to and benefit flow-matching VLA models.
 - **Motivation:** Efficient inference is crucial for deploying VLA models in real-world robotic systems. Techniques like VLA-Cache have demonstrated speedups for autoregressive VLAs by reusing cached visual tokens. However, the computational profile of flow-matching VLAs, which heavily relies on multi-step denoise loops, differs significantly from autoregressive models. The motivation is to analyze the latency breakdown of flow-matching VLAs and test if existing caching approaches are effective, or if the primary computational bottlenecks require alternative acceleration strategies. This aims to provide foundational understanding for future VLA inference optimization efforts.

4. **Developing Multi-View + Temporal Contrastive Pretraining:**
 - **Objective:** To develop and pretrain a multi-view and temporal projection head on a large dataset of robot interaction frames to learn robust visual representations.
 - **Motivation:** High-quality visual representations are fundamental for VLA model performance. By pretraining a lightweight projection head on multi-view and temporal data from LIBERO, the aim is to create a readily deployable module that can enhance the feature extraction capabilities of VLA vision encoders, potentially serving as an improved initialization for downstream fine-tuning tasks and leading to better task retrieval capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology to address its objectives, encompassing novel algorithm development, empirical comparisons, and architectural analysis.

**Cross-Paradigm VLA Interface:**
To enable unified experimentation across diverse VLA architectures, the authors define a minimal `VLABase` protocol. This interface abstracts architectural specifics, exposing core functionalities required for preference alignment, such as `policy_logp` (log-probability of an action chunk), `policy_logp_with_ref` (log-probabilities for current and reference policies), `policy_sample` (sampling multiple action sequences), `encode_obs` (processing observations), and `sample_actions` (sampling actions for environment interaction). This abstraction allows the same DPO pipeline to be applied to both autoregressive (OpenVLA) and flow-matching (π0.5) backbones.

**Surrogate Log-Probability for Flow-Matching VLAs:**
For flow-matching models like π0.5, a direct computation of `log p_theta(chunk|obs)` is intractable due to the need for probability-flow ODE integration. The authors propose a surrogate log-probability, `log_p_theta(x_1|obs)`, defined as the negative mean squared error (MSE) of the conditional flow-matching loss. Specifically, for a chunk `x_1` and noise `x_0`, the target velocity `v_target` is `x_1 - x_0`, and the model predicts `v_theta(x_t, t, obs)`. The surrogate is `-(1/T_eval) * Sum_t ||v_theta(x_t, t, obs) - v_target||^2`. The prefactor relating MSE to log-likelihood in diffusion models is absorbed into the DPO temperature `beta`. This surrogate is deterministic given the observation, chunk, and noise seed, satisfying DPO requirements. Four stratified `t`-samples are used for evaluation (`T_eval=4`).

**Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) with DoRA:**
The study investigates DoRA (Weight-Decomposed Low-Rank Adaptation) as an alternative to LoRA. DoRA enhances LoRA by decomposing the adapted weight `W_eff` into a magnitude component (`m`) and a direction component derived from the LoRA-adapted weight. This approach is hypothesized to be beneficial for VLA fine-tuning, which often involves narrow-distribution adaptations where preserving the direction of pretrained vision-language grounding while adjusting magnitude could be advantageous. The `_DoRALinear` implementation materializes `W_eff` once per forward pass. The memory cost increase compared to LoRA is quantified.

**DPO Loss Application:**
The standard DPO objective is utilized, where `L_DPO(theta) = -E log sigma(beta * Delta_theta(c+, c-))`. Here, `c+` and `c-` denote chosen and rejected action chunks, respectively. The `Delta_theta` term incorporates the difference between the log-probability ratios of the current policy and a frozen reference policy for chosen and rejected chunks. The surrogate log-probability is employed for flow-matching models. Hyperparameters for DPO training include `beta=0.1`, learning rate `5e-5`, batch size `1`, `500` maximum steps, and `100` warmup steps.

**Multi-View + Temporal Contrastive Pretraining:**
A contrastive pretraining framework is developed for learning robust visual representations. A small projection head (Linear-GELU-Linear, 1152-d to 128-d) is trained on top of a *frozen* SigLIP-so400m vision encoder, ensuring compatibility with OpenVLA's existing visual processing pipeline. The training objective is a dual-stream InfoNCE loss, combining `L_mva` (symmetric InfoNCE between agent-view and wrist-view embeddings at the same timestep) and `L_tc` (InfoNCE between agent-view embeddings at `t` and `t+delta`, with `delta=5`). The model is trained on 6000 LIBERO frames for 10 epochs using AdamW optimizer.

**Experimental Setup and Evaluation:**
Experiments are conducted on NVIDIA H20-class GPUs. Two primary VLA backbones are used: OpenVLA-7B (autoregressive) and π0.5 (flow-matching), utilizing publicly available checkpoints. Evaluation is performed on the LIBERO benchmark, comprising four suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long-horizon). Each evaluation cell involves 10 tasks per suite with 5 trials per task, totaling 50 trials per seed. The simulation environment uses MuJoCo with EGL rendering. DPO training data consists of approximately 200 (chosen, rejected) chunk pairs per suite, generated via SFT rollout sampling with action noise.

**Inference Optimization Study:**
For the inference optimization objective, a latency breakdown of π0.5's `sample_actions` function is performed. This involves profiling the time spent in image preprocessing, prefix embedding, and the multi-step denoise loop. Subsequently, two caching strategies are benchmarked on LIBERO Spatial tasks:
1. **Chunk-level Cache:** Naively reuses entire 10-step action chunks if the current visual signature is highly similar (cosine similarity ≥ 0.95) to a cached observation.
2. **Token-level Prefix Cache (VLA-Cache style):** Modifies `sample_actions` to reuse prefix K/V values across environment timesteps when visual signatures are similar, while still re-running the action expert (suffix) at each step.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several empirical findings across the defined objectives.

### 5. Cross-Paradigm Validation of DPO on Flow-Matching VLAs

The proposed surrogate flow-matching log-probability enabled stable and non-degenerate DPO training on the π0.5 flow-matching VLA model. When applied with LoRA-r32, this DPO protocol demonstrated a mean success rate improvement of +1.15 percentage points (pp) across the LIBERO 4-suite compared to the `openpi` paper's SFT baseline for π0.5. Specifically, gains were observed on Long-horizon (+1.6 pp), Goal (+2.0 pp), and Spatial (+1.2 pp). Performance on the Object suite was within the margin of 50-trial noise (-0.2 pp). These results indicate that the surrogate log-probability functions as a calibrated preference signal, allowing DPO to improve performance where SFT leaves room for improvement and maintain performance where SFT is already strong.

### 6. Effectiveness of DoRA for VLA DPO on Autoregressive VLAs

The empirical study of DoRA on OpenVLA, when combined with DPO, revealed a mean improvement of +10.4 pp over the OpenVLA SFT baseline across the LIBERO 4-suite. This finding is based on 600 trials across 3 random seeds. Notable per-suite gains include:
- **Object:** +20.0 pp (from 56% to 76.0%). This suite exhibited perfect seed stability for DoRA, achieving 38/50 successes on each of the 3 seeds, which is highlighted as an unusually consistent signal.
- **Long-horizon:** +11.0 pp (from 53% to 64.0%).
- **Goal:** +8.0 pp (from 70% to 78.0%).
- **Spatial:** +2.7 pp (from 72% to 74.7%).
Compared to LoRA, DoRA generally matched or marginally surpassed performance in multiseed-comparable cells and demonstrated tighter per-seed spread, suggesting a practical advantage of magnitude/direction decoupling for narrow-distribution adaptations. The largest gains were observed in suites where the SFT baseline underperformed the most (Object, Long-horizon), consistent with DoRA’s ability to refine existing skills while preserving pretrained knowledge.

### 7. Inference-Time Performance and Limitations of Caching for Flow-Matching VLAs

A latency anatomy of π0.5's `sample_actions` function revealed that the multi-step denoise loop (action expert) constitutes the dominant computational cost, accounting for 78.6% of the per-call latency (~220 ms out of ~280 ms). The prefix forward, which is the target of K/V caching strategies like VLA-Cache, represents only 21.4% of the cost. This analysis indicates a hard acceleration ceiling of approximately 21% for strategies solely targeting the prefix forward.
Empirical evaluation of caching strategies further supported this:
- **Chunk-level Cache:** While achieving an 82.1% chunk reuse rate, this strategy resulted in a 20% degradation in success rate (from 100% to 80%) and a 30% increase in wall-clock time, attributed to rollout drift and cache check overhead.
- **Token-level Prefix Cache (VLA-Cache style):** This approach also led to significant degradation in success rate (down to 0-86% depending on similarity threshold), with minimal to no speedup. The reuse of stale prefix K/V values was found to negatively impact the action expert's attention.
These findings collectively indicate that K/V-cache strategies, effective for autoregressive VLAs, do not transfer effectively to flow-matching architectures due to their distinct computational bottlenecks. The results suggest that acceleration efforts for flow-matching VLAs should prioritize reducing the cost of the denoise loop.

### 8. Multi-View + Temporal Contrastive Pretraining

The developed multi-view + temporal contrastive pretraining framework successfully converged on 6000 LIBERO frames within 10 epochs. The total InfoNCE loss decreased from 2.418 to 0.366, recovering 92.5% from the random baseline. The trained projection head achieved high k-NN retrieval performance, with 99.5% recall@1 for same-task retrieval across all 6000 LIBERO frames (representing a 36x improvement over random). The Object suite exhibited perfect 100% recall@1, aligning with DoRA's strong performance on Object tasks, indicating a clear visual signal for this suite. A 2.6 MB `proj_head.pt` checkpoint is publicly released.

### 9. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and advancement of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in several key areas:

**1. Unifying Post-Training for VLA Architectures:** The development of a tractable surrogate log-probability for flow-matching VLAs enables the application of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to both autoregressive and continuous-action flow-matching VLA models. This is a significant step towards a unified post-training methodology across diverse VLA paradigms. It suggests that a robust, LLM-proven preference alignment technique can be broadly adopted in robotics, potentially leading to more sophisticated and human-aligned robot behaviors without the need for architecture-specific reward modeling.

**2. Optimizing VLA Fine-Tuning Efficiency and Performance:** The empirical demonstration of DoRA's superior performance and remarkable seed stability over LoRA for OpenVLA fine-tuning, particularly on tasks requiring magnitude refinement, provides practical guidance for the VLA community. DoRA's ability to achieve substantial performance gains (mean +10.4 pp) with reduced variance suggests a more robust and effective parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy for certain types of robotic tasks. This can lead to more reliable model development, reduced computational costs, and higher-performing robot policies.

**3. Directing Inference Optimization Efforts for Flow-Matching VLAs:** The detailed latency analysis and the structural negative results regarding K/V-cache transferability to flow-matching VLAs are critical. By clearly identifying the denoise loop as the dominant computational bottleneck (78.6% of latency), the research redirects future inference acceleration efforts towards methods like consistency model distillation, which directly target this cost. This insight can prevent misallocated resources on less impactful caching strategies and accelerate the development of truly efficient flow-matching VLA inference, making these models more suitable for real-time robotic control.

**4. Enhancing VLA Representation Learning:** The multi-view + temporal contrastive pretraining framework, yielding a lightweight yet highly effective projection head (99.5% k-NN recall@1), provides a valuable resource for initializing VLA vision encoders. This can improve the quality of visual representations, potentially leading to more robust and generalized VLA models, and offers a strong foundation for future research into embodied representation learning.

**5. Promoting Open Science and Reproducibility:** The commitment to open-sourcing all code, checkpoints, training logs, and reproduction scripts facilitates transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative research. This allows other researchers to directly build upon these findings, validate the results, and accelerate innovation in the VLA domain.

In summary, this work provides both methodological advancements and empirical insights that streamline VLA development by generalizing post-training techniques, optimizing fine-tuning strategies, and guiding inference acceleration, thereby contributing to the creation of more capable and deployable robot learning systems.
