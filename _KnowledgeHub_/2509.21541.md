# ControlHair: Physically-based Video Diffusion for Controllable Dynamic Hair Rendering

> [!summary] Summary
> CONTROLHAIR introduces a hybrid framework that integrates a physics simulator with a conditional video diffusion model to render photorealistic videos with controllable dynamic hair. The method provides fine-grained control over hair dynamics, achieving superior adherence to physical parameters and demonstrating up to 47% better PSNR and 29% better SSIM in control-signal-to-video reconstruction compared to baseline models.

## Problem

- Existing video diffusion models lack fine-grained control over specific physical attributes of hair dynamics, which are influenced by numerous entangled intrinsic and external parameters.
- Training conditional video diffusion models for physics-based control is difficult due to the challenge of acquiring large datasets with accurate, precisely annotated ground-truth physics values.
- The entanglement of diverse physics parameters (e.g., mass, stiffness, wind) makes it hard for models to disentangle and learn their individual contributions from visual cues alone.

## Method

- A three-stage pipeline that first uses a ==physics simulator== to translate user-defined physics parameters (e.g., wind, gravity, hair stiffness) and human motion into a sequence of 3D hair geometries.
- The 3D hair geometries are then projected into 2D per-frame control signals, specifically hair strand maps and sparse human poses, combined with camera trajectory information.
- A conditional ==video diffusion model==, fine-tuned from UniAnimate-==DiT== with ==LoRA==, generates photorealistic videos by leveraging a reference image and these 2D geometric control signals, effectively decoupling physics reasoning from visual rendering.

## Results

- Qualitative comparisons show CONTROLHAIR generates realistic hair motion consistent with strong wind effects and precise head movements, outperforming text-conditioned (WAN2.1) and general pose-conditioned (UNIANIMATE-DIT) baselines.
- Quantitative evaluation on a control-signal-to-video reconstruction task demonstrated CONTROLHAIR achieved the best performance with an average PSNR of **17.15**, SSIM of **0.668**, and LPIPS of **0.370**, representing improvements of up to 47% in PSNR and 29% in SSIM over WAN2.1.
- The framework enables various practical use cases, including dynamic hair try-on, bullet-time hair effects by controlling camera trajectories, and cinemagraphic effects where only hair animates while the subject remains static.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the complex physics reasoning to a specialized simulator and using its output (3D geometry) as a common control signal for the video diffusion model simplifies the overall task.
> - Converting 3D simulated dynamics into 2D geometric control signals like hair strand maps and human poses simplifies the training data annotation process, as these can be extracted from real-world videos more easily than raw physics parameters.
> - Leveraging a pre-trained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, fine-tuned with LoRA, allows the model to efficiently learn to render photorealistic hair dynamics while maintaining strong generative capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses the intersection of two challenging areas within computer graphics and artificial intelligence: human hair simulation and rendering, and video diffusion models.

Human hair simulation and rendering have long been recognized as computationally intensive problems in computer graphics. The complexity arises from the need to model tens of thousands of interacting strands, diverse material properties, and intricate light-hair interactions. Traditional approaches, such as mass-spring chains, rigid multi-body chains, discrete elastic rods, or material point methods, often require complex modeling and significant computational resources to achieve realistic dynamics and rendering. While physically-based and data-driven rendering methods exist, they often struggle with either complexity or photorealism.

Concurrently, video diffusion models have demonstrated substantial progress in generating high-quality videos, with some models rivaling professional productions. Beyond basic text- and image-conditioned generation, there has been a growing interest in incorporating fine-grained and multi-modal controls to enable more practical applications. Examples include conditioning on human pose, lighting, speech, or music to guide video generation. While these advancements have expanded the applicability of video diffusion, existing methods generally lack the capacity for precise control over the physical dynamics of complex structures like human hair.

CONTROLHAIR positions itself to bridge this gap by integrating physics-based simulation with conditional video diffusion. Previous work in controllable video diffusion has explored various modalities but has not specifically investigated the control of dynamic hair structures based on physical parameters. While some studies have used simulators to guide dynamics generation (e.g., PhysAnimator for 2D mesh deformation in cartoons), CONTROLHAIR extends this concept to the intricate, 3D dynamics of human hair within a photorealistic video generation context. By doing so, it introduces a novel hybrid framework that leverages the strengths of both physics simulators (for accurate dynamics) and video diffusion models (for photorealistic rendering and temporal consistency), addressing a previously unexplored area of fine-grained, physics-informed control in generative video.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of CONTROLHAIR is to enable controllable dynamic hair rendering through a physics-informed video diffusion framework. This involves generating photorealistic videos where hair dynamics are precisely dictated by user-defined physical parameters.

The motivation for this work stems from two main challenges encountered when attempting to apply existing video diffusion models to dynamic hair rendering:

1. **Lack of Fine-Grained Control over Hair Dynamics:** Current video diffusion models, despite their ability to generate high-quality videos, do not offer fine-grained control over specific physical attributes of hair dynamics. Hair behavior is influenced by a multitude of parameters, including intrinsic hair properties (e.g., mass, stiffness, damping, thickness) and external forces (e.g., wind, gravity, friction, human motion). Integrating these diverse and often scene-specific conditions directly into a standard video diffusion model presents a significant hurdle. There is no established universal encoding mechanism capable of accommodating such a wide array of physical inputs. Moreover, the effects of different physics parameters can be highly entangled; for instance, reduced damping, weaker gravity, or stronger wind can all lead to visually similar outcomes, making it difficult for a model to disentangle and learn their individual contributions effectively.

2. **Difficulty in Training Data Annotation:** Training conditional video diffusion models to understand and respond to physics parameters would necessitate a large dataset of videos meticulously annotated with ground-truth physics values. Parameters such as hair stiffness, wind strength, or specific wind directions are challenging to infer accurately from real-world videos. This difficulty in obtaining precise annotations for underlying physical factors makes it impractical to directly train a diffusion model on physics parameters. Furthermore, different physical modeling approaches or tasks may require distinct sets of parameters, making it challenging to design a single, flexible diffusion model architecture that can handle arbitrary parameter sets without requiring extensive redesign and retraining for each scenario.

To overcome these challenges, CONTROLHAIR was motivated by the idea of decoupling the complex physics reasoning from the video generation process. The researchers proposed a hybrid approach that integrates a physics simulator upfront. This design allows the simulator to handle the intricate dynamics calculations, encoding the physical parameters into a more manageable geometric representation. This geometric representation then serves as a standardized control signal for the video diffusion model, which can then focus on generating photorealistic visual content based on this derived geometry. This cascaded design not only makes the framework general to various physics inputs but also simplifies the training data annotation process, as only per-frame geometry (which can be extracted from 2D projections) is required for supervision, rather than hard-to-infer physics parameters.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CONTROLHAIR employs a three-stage pipeline to achieve controllable dynamic hair rendering, integrating a physics simulator with a conditional video diffusion model.

**Stage 1: Simulator-based Physics Encoding**
The first stage focuses on translating user-defined physics parameters into a sequence of per-frame 3D hair geometries. This approach addresses the complexities of entangled physics parameters and the need for a flexible system that can accommodate diverse physics models.
- **Inputs:** The system takes a reference image as input, from which an initial 3D hair model is estimated using a method like DiffLocks. Alongside this, users specify intrinsic hair properties (e.g., mass, stiffness, damping) and external forces (e.g., wind strength and direction, gravity, and human motion).
- **Process:** A physics simulator (specifically, Blender's particle system) is used. It takes the estimated 3D hair model, intrinsic hair properties, and external forces as input. If human motion is involved, a digital human character with attached hair strands is driven by the desired motions within the simulator. The simulator then performs time-step simulations to generate a sequence of per-frame 3D hair geometries (H_1:T).
- **Rationale:** This cascaded design offloads the computationally intensive and complex task of physics-based dynamics generation to a specialized simulator. This simplifies the subsequent video diffusion model's task, allowing it to concentrate on photorealistic rendering. Crucially, it unifies diverse physics parameters into a common, geometric representation, making the diffusion model agnostic to the specific physics settings or simulator used. This design enhances flexibility, as different simulators or physics conditions can be swapped without redesigning downstream modules. While 3D hair estimation from a single image can introduce errors, the diffusion model often corrects these inconsistencies by leveraging the reference image during generation.

**Stage 2: Per-frame Control Signals Extraction**
The second stage converts the simulated 3D hair geometry into 2D per-frame control signals that are directly usable by the video diffusion model, while also incorporating camera trajectory information.
- **Inputs:** The per-frame 3D hair geometry (H_1:T) from Stage 1, a human model sequence (M_1:T) derived from the human motion poses, and a user-defined camera trajectory (T_cam).
- **Process (Two steps):**
 1. **Trajectory-aware 3D-to-2D Projection:** For each frame `t` in the sequence, the 3D hair geometry (H_t) and the 3D human model (M_t) are projected onto a 2D image plane. This projection is performed according to the camera pose (T_cam,t) specified by the desired camera trajectory, yielding a sequence of projected images (I_proj,1:T). This step explicitly encodes camera movement and viewpoint changes.
 2. **Geometric Feature Extraction:** From each 2D projected image (I_proj,t), specific geometric features are extracted to serve as control signals:
 - **Hair Strand Maps:** These maps, which capture hair orientation and structure, are extracted using a pre-trained U-Net from HairStep.
 - **Sparse 2D Human Poses:** These are estimated using DWPose, providing control over human body motion.
 - A binary hair mask (B_hair), estimated by a hair segmentation network, is used to combine these two features into a single RGB control image (C_t) for each frame. The hair mask ensures that hair dynamics are controlled by strand maps, while other body parts are controlled by human poses.
- **Rationale:** This design ensures that the control signals (1) reflect the desired camera trajectory, enabling explicit camera pose control, and (2) depend solely on geometric information, which is directly available from the physics simulation. This methodology simplifies the process of creating training data, as these 2D control signals can be extracted from real-world videos more readily than inferring complex 3D geometry or underlying physics parameters.

**Stage 3: Conditioned Video Diffusion for Hair Rendering**
The final stage utilizes a conditional video diffusion model to generate the photorealistic video, integrating the reference image and the extracted per-frame control signals.
- **Inputs:** A reference image (I_ref) (for appearance consistency) and the sequence of per-frame control signals (C_1:T) from Stage 2.
- **Model Architecture:** CONTROLHAIR builds upon the Wan 2.1 architecture, specifically its Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone, encoder, and decoder.
 - The reference image (I_ref) is encoded using the Wan 2.1 encoder (E_I).
 - The per-frame control signals (C_1:T) are encoded using a dedicated 3D convolutional network (E_c3d).
 - Additionally, geometric features (similar to C_t) are extracted from the reference image and encoded via a 2D convolutional network (E_c2d). This helps bridge the modality gap between the static reference image and the dynamic control signals.
 - All these encoded representations are then fed into the Wan 2.1 DiT backbone, which processes them to generate the output video.
- **Training Data:** To train this model, a curated dataset of 10K high-quality real-world videos containing human hair is collected. For each video, control signals (strand maps and human poses) are extracted from every frame, and one frame is sampled as the reference image. This approach avoids the difficulty of annotating videos with physics parameters.
- **Training Process:** The model is fine-tuned from a pre-trained UniAnimate-DiT model to leverage existing priors. During training, the Wan encoder and decoder are kept fixed. The newly introduced convolutional networks (E_c3d, E_c2d) are fully fine-tuned. The core DiT backbone is adapted using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) with a rank of 128. This strategy efficiently updates the model by adding low-rank components to weight matrices, reducing computational cost and preventing overfitting while retaining the strong generative capabilities of the pre-trained model.
- **Output:** The model generates an RGB video (V_1:T) with a resolution of 81x832x480 (T×H×W), where hair dynamics and human poses are faithfully controlled according to the input conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CONTROLHAIR encompassed qualitative and quantitative comparisons against baseline models, as well as demonstrations of its applicability in various use cases.

**Qualitative Comparison:**
CONTROLHAIR was qualitatively compared against two intuitive baselines: text-conditioned generation using WAN2.1 and pose-conditioned generation using UNIANIMATE-DIT.
- **Strong Wind Effect:** In a scenario requiring strong wind to blow hair in a specific direction, CONTROLHAIR demonstrated superior performance. Both WAN2.1 and UNIANIMATE-DIT, despite receiving detailed text prompts describing strong wind effects, largely failed to induce noticeable hair motion, indicating the limitations of text-based control for fine-grained physical dynamics. CONTROLHAIR, leveraging its physics-simulator-derived control signals, successfully generated realistic hair motion consistent with the specified wind conditions.
- **Human Head Motion:** When controlling a user's head motion (e.g., rotating left, then right, then center), WAN2.1 failed to execute the full sequence described in the text prompt. UNIANIMATE-DIT successfully controlled the head pose due to its conditioning on human poses but did not exhibit controllable hair dynamics, relying instead on its diffusion prior. CONTROLHAIR achieved accurate control over both the specified head motion and the corresponding hair dynamics, following the precise signals generated by its physics simulator. These results indicate that CONTROLHAIR provides a more granular and reliable control over physics-driven hair dynamics compared to existing text- or general pose-conditioned methods.

**Quantitative Comparison:**
To quantitatively assess the model's ability to leverage and follow control signals, a control-signal-to-video reconstruction task was performed. Videos generated by Google VEO 2 were treated as ground truth, and control signals were extracted from them to reconstruct the original videos.
- **Methodology:** For WAN2.1, text prompts from VEO 2 plus manually annotated human motion (in text) were used. For UNIANIMATE-DIT, VEO 2 text prompts and per-frame human poses (extracted with DWPose) were used. For CONTROLHAIR, per-frame human poses and hair strand maps (extracted using Equation 6) served as control signals. The goal was to measure how accurately each model could reconstruct the ground-truth video based on its respective control signals.
- **Metrics:** PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS were used to compare generated and ground-truth videos, averaged across all frames and videos.
- **Results:** CONTROLHAIR achieved the best quantitative performance, with average PSNR of 17.15, SSIM of 0.668, and LPIPS of 0.370. This represented improvements of up to 47% in PSNR, 29% in SSIM, and 26% in LPIPS compared to WAN2.1, and also outperformed UNIANIMATE-DIT. These metrics indicate that CONTROLHAIR exhibits higher controllability over video generation and effectively utilizes the specific hair-dynamics control signals provided.

**Use Cases:**
CONTROLHAIR demonstrated its practical utility across three distinct applications:
- **Dynamic Hair Try-On:** By cascading CONTROLHAIR after an existing static hair try-on system (HairFusion), the framework extended static hairstyle previews to dynamic scenarios, showing how a hairstyle would look with head rotations or wind effects.
- **Bullet-Time Hair Effects:** Leveraging camera trajectory control, CONTROLHAIR enabled the "bullet-time" effect. At a specific moment of simulated hair dynamics, the physics simulator was frozen, and the scene was rendered from multiple consecutive viewpoints, allowing users to observe the hair from different perspectives while its geometry remained fixed.
- **Cinemagraphic Effects:** The model successfully generated cinemagraphic videos where only the hair was animated (e.g., by wind), while the human subject remained static, and the video looped. This showcased CONTROLHAIR's ability to produce specific, sometimes unconventional, physical effects that are difficult to create or capture in real-world videos without complex post-processing.

**Discussion on Errors:**
The paper also discussed potential limitations:
- **Imprecise 3D Hair Strand Estimation:** Single-image 3D hair reconstruction is an under-constrained problem, leading to potential inaccuracies in the initial 3D hair model. However, the video diffusion model often mitigates these errors by leveraging the visual cues from the reference image, leading to a perceived correction of hairstyle inconsistencies in the final video.
- **Physics Simulator Performance:** The realism of the generated hair dynamics is dependent on the fidelity of the chosen physics simulator. Imperfect simulators may yield less realistic dynamic behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CONTROLHAIR introduces a significant advancement in controllable video generation, specifically addressing the complex domain of dynamic human hair. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Physics-Informed Video Diffusion:** CONTROLHAIR is presented as the first framework to integrate a physics simulator directly into a video diffusion model for controllable dynamic hair rendering. This establishes a novel paradigm for embedding physical conditions into generative video models, moving beyond purely data-driven or text-based control.

- **Enhanced Control and Realism:** The framework enables unprecedented fine-grained and flexible control over hair dynamics, encompassing intrinsic hair properties, external forces, human motion, and camera trajectories. This level of control allows for the generation of physically consistent and realistic hair movements in videos, which was previously a challenge for general video diffusion models. The qualitative and quantitative results demonstrate superior adherence to control signals compared to text- or pose-conditioned baselines.

- **Decoupled and Flexible Design:** The cascaded, three-stage pipeline effectively decouples the computationally intensive and complex task of physics reasoning from the visual generation process. This design allows the diffusion model to specialize in photorealistic rendering based on simulated geometry, while the physics simulator handles the dynamics. This modularity makes the system highly flexible; different physics models or simulators can be integrated without requiring a complete redesign or retraining of the downstream video diffusion components.

- **Simplified Data Annotation:** A key practical impact is the simplification of training data annotation. By converting complex 3D physics parameters into 2D geometric control signals (strand maps and human poses), the framework avoids the arduous task of inferring precise physics parameters from real-world videos. These 2D control signals are more easily extractable from existing video datasets, facilitating the training of robust models.

- **Broad Applicability and New Creative Possibilities:** The demonstrated use cases—dynamic hairstyle try-on, bullet-time hair effects, and cinemagraphic animations—highlight the broad applicability and creative potential of CONTROLHAIR. It can significantly impact industries such as film, gaming, virtual reality/augmented reality, and digital human creation by providing tools for generating complex, physically accurate hair animations with relative ease. The ability to create rare or unconventional physical effects, such as zero-gravity hair or extreme wind scenarios, further expands creative horizons.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** CONTROLHAIR opens new avenues for research in several directions. These include exploring improved 3D hair reconstruction from single images (e.g., using multi-view priors), integrating more advanced or learned physics simulators for enhanced realism, and extending the hybrid physics-informed diffusion approach to other deformable objects or complex physical phenomena in video generation. The release of code and models, as indicated by the authors, would further facilitate community-wide exploration and development in this area.

In conclusion, CONTROLHAIR represents a significant step towards more controllable and physically grounded generative video models, addressing a specific and complex aspect of human hair dynamics with a practical and effective hybrid framework.
