# ESI-Bench: Towards Embodied Spatial Intelligence that Closes the Perception-Action Loop

> [!summary] Summary
> The ESI-BENCH benchmark introduces a new paradigm for evaluating embodied spatial intelligence, requiring AI agents to actively interact with a simulated environment to acquire information and resolve perceptual ambiguities. Evaluations show that current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) achieve significant accuracy gains through active exploration but exhibit 'action blindness' and a 'metacognitive gap' compared to human performance.

## Problem

- Existing spatial intelligence benchmarks primarily rely on passive observation, assuming all necessary information is pre-given.
- Real-world spatial understanding often requires uncovering hidden physical properties and latent scene states that cannot be resolved from static, pre-provided visual data.
- Current AI models, including MLLMs, lack frameworks to evaluate their ability to actively acquire information by interacting with an environment.

## Method

- ESI-BENCH, a new benchmark built on the OmniGibson simulator, features 3,081 tasks across 10 categories requiring active interaction.
- Agents must employ high-level discrete actions (locomotion, perception, manipulation) to resolve spatial ambiguities and uncover hidden information within the environment.
- The benchmark's tasks are grounded in cognitive science, assessing abilities like object representation, layout, number representation, and agent-driven actions, specifically when passive sensing is insufficient.

## Results

- Active exploration yielded substantial accuracy improvements for models; for instance, Gemini 3.1's performance on View Hallucination increased from **39.9%** (Passive Single-View) to **68.1%**.
- Providing optimal ground-truth views significantly boosted MLLM accuracy (e.g., GPT-5 on Rigid Containment improved from **42.5%** to **95.0%**), indicating action selection, not perception, is a primary bottleneck.
- Humans dramatically outperformed MLLMs in active tasks (e.g., **88.3%** vs. **64.2%** for Physical Contact), attributed to more diverse evidence gathering and belief revision strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active exploration by agents leads to significant accuracy gains over passive observation, demonstrating emergent spatial strategies for resolving ambiguities.
> - Current MLLMs exhibit 'action blindness,' where the inability to select informative actions is a greater limitation than their perceptual capacity for many tasks.
> - A substantial 'metacognitive gap' separates human and MLLM performance in active settings, with humans showing greater epistemic caution and belief revision.
