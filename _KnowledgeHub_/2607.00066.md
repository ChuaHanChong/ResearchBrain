# Learning Expert Strategy for Autonomous Robotic Endovascular Intervention via Decoupled Procedural Execution

> [!summary] Summary
> A new autonomous robotic system for endovascular interventions, developed by researchers at Tongji University and collaborators, decouples high-level strategic navigation from low-level motion execution using a hybrid reinforcement learning and model predictive control framework. This approach demonstrated a 29.3% reduction in procedural steps and achieved zero vessel-boundary violations in physical phantom experiments, enhancing both efficiency and safety in navigation.

## Problem

- Endovascular interventions require precise guidewire manipulation in complex anatomies, leading to steep learning curves, prolonged procedures, and radiation exposure for clinicians.
- Existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods for autonomous navigation are prone to suboptimal decisions (redundant maneuvers) and lack explicit safety constraint enforcement.
- Purely model-based control struggles to accurately capture the highly non-linear, anatomy-dependent behavior of flexible guidewires, especially with incomplete sensing.

## Method

- An integrated architecture that decouples strategic decision-making (==Reinforcement Learning== policy) from procedural execution (==Nonlinear Model Predictive Control== refinement).
- A visual perception pipeline (==YOLOv5==, ==ResNet50==) extracts guidewire tip position and anatomical features from 2D fluoroscopic images to inform the strategic policy.
- The strategic policy, trained with ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== in simulation, generates high-level navigation intents, which an ==NMPC== layer refines to enforce real-time kinematic, curvature, and vessel-boundary safety constraints.

## Results

- The integrated framework achieved an aggregated success rate of over **96%** in simulations and **90%** in in-vitro validation, demonstrating enhanced robustness across multiple anatomical geometries.
- It reduced average procedural steps by **29.3%** compared to baseline RL policies and decreased lateral trajectory width by **11.4%** in simulations, indicating improved efficiency and consistency.
- Physical phantom experiments recorded **zero** vessel-boundary violations, confirming the NMPC layer's ability to rigorously maintain procedural safety in a real-world setting despite sim-to-real gaps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level strategic planning from low-level constrained execution effectively replicates expert clinical decision-making, allowing RL to focus on global tactics while NMPC handles safety.
> - An optimal balance (λ_MPC = 0.1) between learned strategy and model-based constraint enforcement is crucial, as an overly dominant optimization layer can suppress the learned strategic policy.
> - Hybrid learning-optimization frameworks can significantly improve trajectory consistency and predictability compared to pure RL, which is vital for high-stakes medical applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Endovascular interventions are a critical class of minimally invasive procedures for treating cardiovascular and neurovascular diseases. These procedures demand precise manipulation of flexible guidewires and catheters within complex and tortuous vascular anatomies, often guided by 2D fluoroscopic imagery. Manual operation necessitates extensive clinical expertise, involves a steep learning curve, can lead to prolonged procedure times, and exposes both patients and clinicians to sustained radiation. Furthermore, variations in operator skill can introduce inconsistencies in procedural outcomes, highlighting a clinical need for systems that can standardize performance.

Robot-assisted autonomous endovascular navigation has emerged as a strategy to address these challenges, aiming to reduce operator workload and standardize procedural quality. Within this context, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown potential for acquiring guidewire navigation policies directly from simulated environments, adapting to complex geometries and non-linear device dynamics. However, existing pure RL-based methods present limitations in clinical viability. They can be prone to suboptimal decisions, resulting in redundant maneuvers and excessive navigation steps. More critically, RL methods typically lack explicit mechanisms for enforcing safety constraints, which is essential for guaranteeing compliant device behavior in the uncertain contact conditions prevalent in real endovascular procedures, thus posing potential risks.

Conversely, optimization-based frameworks, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC), offer robust mechanisms for ensuring technical compliance and motion consistency by embedding operational constraints within an online optimization process. Nevertheless, purely model-based approaches can struggle to accurately capture the anatomy-dependent and highly non-linear behavior of flexible guidewires, particularly under conditions of incomplete sensing or unmodeled dynamics.

Recent advancements in autonomous systems have explored hybrid architectures that combine learning-based decision-making with optimization-based constraint regulation. These integrated paradigms have demonstrated gains in robustness in demanding domains like agile flight and autonomous driving. This paper contributes to the research landscape by adapting and applying such a hybrid integration strategy specifically to autonomous endovascular interventions. It seeks to overcome the limitations of pure RL while leveraging the strengths of optimization-based control to achieve both strategic adaptability and rigorous safety compliance in a high-stakes medical domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties and existing limitations in endovascular interventions, both manual and autonomously assisted.

**Clinical Challenges Driving Motivation:**
Endovascular procedures are critical for treating severe vascular diseases, yet they are characterized by several significant challenges. Operators must perform precise manipulations of flexible guidewires and catheters through complex, tortuous vascular structures while interpreting real-time 2D fluoroscopic images. This necessitates anticipating guidewire deformation and applying delicate control to avoid vessel wall contact. These demands result in a steep learning curve for clinicians, prolonged procedure times, and significant radiation exposure. Moreover, the variability in operator skill contributes to inconsistencies in procedural outcomes, indicating a clear need for intelligent robotic systems that can standardize interventional performance and mitigate these risks.

**Limitations of Current Autonomous Approaches:**
While robot-assisted endovascular navigation and, particularly, Reinforcement Learning (RL) have shown promise for automating these procedures, they present specific drawbacks that motivate the current work:
- **Suboptimal Efficiency:** Pure learning-based policies are often susceptible to locally optimal decisions, which can manifest as redundant maneuvers and an excessive number of navigation steps. This reduces procedural efficiency.
- **Lack of Safety Guarantees:** A critical limitation is the absence of explicit constraint enforcement in many RL methods. This makes it difficult to guarantee compliant device behavior, especially under uncertain contact conditions, leading to potentially unpredictable or risky outcomes such as vessel wall damage.
- **Incomplete System Understanding:** Model-based control methods, while effective for constraint enforcement, struggle with the highly non-linear and anatomy-dependent behavior of flexible guidewires, particularly when sensing is incomplete or dynamics are unmodeled.

**Overarching Objectives:**
Given these challenges, the key objective of this work is to develop a learning-based expert strategy that enhances procedural consistency, predictability, and safety in autonomous robotic endovascular interventions. Specifically, the authors aim to:
1. **Decouple Strategic Decision-Making from Procedural Execution:** Replicate the expert clinical decision-making process by explicitly separating high-level strategic navigation intent from low-level, expert-informed execution. This allows the RL policy to focus on global planning without being burdened by explicit constraint satisfaction.
2. **Ensure Explicit Constraint Satisfaction and Safety:** Develop an execution module that strictly adheres to expert operational norms, real-time kinematic limits, and vessel safety constraints, thereby providing safety guarantees that pure RL methods often lack.
3. **Improve Operational Efficiency:** Reduce redundant movements and navigation steps to enhance the efficiency of endovascular procedures.
4. **Enhance Procedural Standardization:** Achieve superior trajectory consistency and predictability, aligning autonomous behavior with established clinical norms.
5. **Validate in Realistic Settings:** Demonstrate the framework's effectiveness across high-fidelity 3D simulations and on a real-world robotic platform to confirm its potential for practical application.

By addressing the trade-off between strategic adaptability and rigorous safety requirements, the research seeks to advance the clinical viability of autonomous robotic endovascular systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates a learning-based strategic policy with an optimization-based motion refinement module, designed to mirror the multi-stage decision-making process of expert interventionalists.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The task is framed as a sequential decision-making problem within a continuous 3D vascular environment, modeled as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP). The objective is to navigate a guidewire from an initial state to a target anatomical location, minimizing cumulative procedural costs while strictly adhering to operational constraints.
- **State Definition (x_t):** Observable projected guidewire tip configuration in the 2D imaging plane, including tip position (x, y), velocity (v_x, v_y), and orientation (θ).
- **Control Input (u_t):** Actuation applied at the proximal end by the robotic manipulator, comprising insertion velocity (v_insert) and rotational velocity (ω).
- **Constraints (S_bound):** Include maintaining vessel wall clearance, adhering to curvature limits (κ_max) to prevent device deformation, and tracking the vascular centerline.

**B. Integrated Strategy-Execution Architecture**
The system employs a dual-stage architecture consisting of three primary modules:
1. **Visual Perception Pipeline:** Extracts observations from fluoroscopic imagery.
2. **Strategic Navigation Policy:** A Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent that generates high-level navigation intents.
3. **Predictive Motion Refinement Module:** An optimization-based controller that ensures constraint adherence during execution.

**C. Visual Perception and State Estimation**
Real-time state estimation relies on a vision processing pipeline that interprets fluoroscopic feedback:
- **Guidewire Tip Localization:** A YOLOv5 object detection model identifies the guidewire tip position (p_tip) from bounding box coordinates. A temporal consistency check is used to mitigate detection failures.
- **Visual Feature Extraction:** A ResNet50 Convolutional Neural Network processes standardized input images to extract a 2048-dimensional feature vector (f_img), implicitly encoding spatial relationships and anatomical context.
- **Composite Observation (o_t):** The input to the strategic policy combines p_tip, f_img, the previous control action (u_t-1), and the goal position (p_target).

**D. Strategic Navigation Policy**
The strategic navigation behavior is learned using the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, an off-policy RL algorithm that maximizes an entropy-regularized objective, suitable for continuous action spaces.
- **Network Architecture:** Policy and twin Q-function networks are implemented as Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) integrated with a shared Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) embedder. The LSTM captures temporal dependencies in the observation stream, generating an embedded state for the policy network.
- **Training Environment:** Training is conducted in a simulated 3D vascular environment, specifically modeling complex aortic arch anatomy.
- **Reward Function (R):** Structured to balance task completion, efficiency, and general path awareness:
 - `r_target`: Sparse positive reward upon reaching the goal.
 - `r_path`: Rewards reduction in geodesic distance along the centerline.
 - `r_step`: Constant penalty to encourage rapid completion.
 - `r_boundary`: Negative cost for violating a minimum safe distance from the vessel wall. Crucially, this is a behavioral bias during training, not a strict enforcement. The design explicitly avoids enforcing strict boundary constraints within the RL reward, allowing the policy to focus on high-level navigation tactics.
- **Output:** The policy network π_ϕ(u_t|o_t) generates a reference control action, or navigation intent (u_RL).

**E. Predictive Motion Refinement**
The reference intents (u_RL) from the strategic policy are refined by a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) layer to ensure technical compliance and kinematic feasibility.
- **Dynamics Model:** A discrete-time kinematic model of guidewire evolution (position, velocity, orientation) is used to predict future trajectories over a finite horizon (H). A smoothing factor (α) models the inertial response of the physical device.
- **Optimization Formulation:** At each control step, the NMPC solves a constrained optimization problem to find an optimal sequence of control inputs (u*_0:H-1). The objective function minimizes the deviation from a target-directed reference trajectory (x_ref,k) and the learned strategic intent (u_RL).
 - **Constraints:**
 - System dynamics: x_k+1 = f(x_k, u_k).
 - Actuator limits: u_min ≤ u_k ≤ u_max.
 - Curvature limits: |ω_k| / (|v_insert,k| + ϵ) ≤ κ_max.
 - Vessel boundary compliance: d_C(x_k) ≤ d_max (ensuring clearance from vessel wall).
- **Implementation:** The optimization problem is solved using the CasADi framework with the IPOPT solver, employing a receding horizon strategy where only the first optimal action (u*_0) is executed.

This decoupled approach allows the RL policy to learn adaptable strategies while the MPC module rigorously enforces safety and kinematic constraints in real-time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed MPC-constrained RL framework was evaluated through comprehensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments.

**A. Simulation Evaluation**
Experiments were conducted using a high-fidelity 3D vascular simulation platform (Sim4EndoR), modeling patient-specific 3D vascular models and realistic guidewire dynamics. Each experiment involved 50 navigation episodes with consistent initial conditions.

1. **Ablation Study on Refinement Weighting Factor (λ_MPC):**
 - The study varied λ_MPC (weight for the policy intent vs. constraint refinement) from 0.0 to 1.0.
 - For λ_MPC values between 0.0 and 0.5, the navigation success rate consistently exceeded 85%, indicating that moderate refinement did not compromise task completion.
 - However, when λ_MPC surpassed 0.5, performance significantly deteriorated, with success rates dropping from 70% to 40%. This suggests that an overly dominant optimization layer can suppress the learned strategic policy, reducing the system to a local tracking controller without adequate global guidance.
 - An intermediate value of **λ_MPC = 0.1** was identified as optimal, yielding the highest success rate and overall navigation efficiency. This value was used for subsequent comparisons.

2. **Trajectory Consistency Analysis:**
 - The framework’s ability to generate repeatable and consistent trajectories was assessed by comparing it against a baseline pure RL policy under stochastic rollouts from identical start and goal configurations.
 - Qualitative analysis of 3D trajectories and probability density heatmaps demonstrated that the integrated framework produced a noticeably more concentrated distribution of guidewire tip positions, in contrast to the diffuse patterns of the baseline RL policy (Figure 3b-g).
 - Quantitative metrics confirmed these observations:
 - The lateral trajectory width in the x–y plane (σ_w,xy) decreased by **11.4%**.
 - The spatial spread along the primary navigation axis (σ_x) was reduced by **13.0%**.
 - These reductions indicate that the framework yields more consistent and predictable trajectories, which is crucial for standardization in medical procedures.

3. **Comparative Study on Navigation Performance Across Multiple Anatomical Geometries:**
 - The proposed framework was compared against pure RL policies (SAC and DDPG) in three distinct anatomical geometries: Brachiocephalic, Carotid, and Subclavian arteries.
 - **Success Rate:** The integrated framework achieved an aggregated success rate of **over 96%**, demonstrating enhanced robustness and stability compared to baseline RL policies (e.g., 98% for Brachiocephalic vs. 92% for SAC and 86% for DDPG).
 - **Procedural Steps:** The average procedural steps were reduced by **29.3%** compared to baseline RL policies across the three geometries. This difference was statistically significant (p < 0.003), indicating substantial improvement in operational efficiency.
 - **Path Ratio and Reward:** The framework achieved higher path ratios and rewards, suggesting closer adherence to the vessel centerline and generation of safer and smoother trajectories. For instance, in the Brachiocephalic artery, the path ratio was 0.984 ± 0.008 for the proposed method, compared to 0.957 ± 0.075 for SAC.
 - These results collectively indicate that the decoupled strategy not only increases task success rates but also enhances procedural efficiency and reliability.

**B. In vitro Validation on Vascular Phantoms**
The trained policy was deployed on the ALL-VAS™ robotic system using a silicone vascular phantom to assess real-world performance.
- **Setup:** A high-resolution camera provided real-time feedback for YOLOv5-based tip, catheter, and sheath detection, and ResNet50 for visual feature extraction. The CasADi-based MPC controller enforced vessel-boundary clearance, curvature limits, and velocity bounds.
- **Performance:** Across 20 navigation trials, the framework achieved a **90.0% success rate** (18 out of 20 trials).
- **Efficiency:** The average navigation time was 4.2 ± 0.8 minutes, with 156 ± 32 control steps per trial.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** A moderate performance drop from simulation (98%) to the physical phantom (90%) was observed. This difference is attributed to the expected sim-to-real gap, including unmodeled physical factors such as non-linear friction, mechanical hysteresis, and visual occlusions inherent in real systems.
- **Safety:** Critically, **zero vessel-boundary violations** were recorded across all physical experiments. The MPC layer successfully intervened during potential buckling or near-boundary events, demonstrating its effectiveness in maintaining procedural safety in a real-world setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a learning expert strategy for autonomous robotic endovascular intervention that holds significant implications for the field, primarily by addressing the critical balance between operational efficiency, precision, and safety.

**Addressing Core Challenges:**
The work effectively confronts the trade-off inherent in autonomous endovascular navigation: the need for strategic adaptability to complex anatomies, coupled with the non-negotiable requirement for precise constraint satisfaction within confined and tortuous vascular spaces.

**Enhanced Safety and Predictability:**
By implementing an optimization-based refinement layer, the framework successfully filters high-level policy commands through explicit kinematic and boundary constraints. This addresses a key limitation of purely learning-based methods, which often struggle with strict boundary adherence and can exhibit unstable, oscillatory behaviors near bifurcations. The result is superior trajectory consistency and motion smoothness, providing a robust mechanism to maintain predictable device behavior within delicate anatomical spaces. The observation of zero vessel-boundary violations in physical experiments underscores the system's ability to ensure safety in real-world scenarios.

**Improved Efficiency and Standardization:**
The framework demonstrated a substantial reduction in operational steps (29.3% in simulation) and a decrease in trajectory variance (13% in simulation). These improvements translate directly to enhanced operative efficiency and minimized device-vessel interaction, which can lead to shorter procedure times and reduced risk of complications. The increased consistency in trajectories also suggests greater procedural standardization, aligning autonomous behavior with established clinical norms.

**Clinical Alignment and Acceptability:**
The architecture is designed to mirror the multi-stage decision-making logic of expert interventionalists, who separate global procedural planning from the nuanced, fine-grained manipulation of instruments. This expert-informed design enhances the intuitive understanding and potential clinical acceptance of autonomous systems by replicating familiar workflow paradigms.

**Modularity and Versatility:**
The decoupled nature of the framework—separating strategic intent acquisition from constraint enforcement—offers significant versatility. Optimization constraints can be adjusted for diverse device configurations or patient-specific anatomies without necessitating extensive policy retraining. This modularity establishes a scalable foundation for future autonomous interventional robotics, enabling adaptation to evolving clinical needs.

**Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:**
While a performance drop was observed during the transition from simulation to a physical phantom, this is a common challenge in robotics. However, the maintained zero-violation rate in physical trials highlights the optimization layer's capacity to mitigate policy-level deviations and preserve procedural safety despite real-world uncertainties such as unmodeled friction, hysteresis, and visual occlusions.

**Future Impact:**
This research represents a step towards clinically viable autonomous endovascular intervention. The potential impact includes:
- **Reduced Operator Workload and Radiation Exposure:** Automation can significantly decrease the physical and cognitive burden on clinicians and limit radiation exposure for both medical staff and patients.
- **Improved Patient Outcomes:** Enhanced precision, safety, and consistency can lead to more predictable and favorable patient outcomes.
- **Democratization of Expertise:** Standardized autonomous procedures could potentially make high-quality interventions more accessible.

Future research integrating high-fidelity biomechanical models, including vessel wall compliance and complex guidewire deformation, will further narrow the sim-to-real gap. Subsequent in vivo studies are necessary to evaluate the framework's clinical efficacy and robustness under dynamic physiological conditions, paving the way for eventual clinical translation.
