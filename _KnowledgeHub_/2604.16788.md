# LongBench: Evaluating Robotic Manipulation Policies on Real-World Long-Horizon Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> LongBench is a real-world benchmark of 1,000+ episodes across 10 long-horizon manipulation tasks, splitting difficulty into Context-Independent (fully observable, execution-focused) and Context-Dependent (ambiguity-driven, memory-focused) regimes with stage-wise scoring. Evaluating six policies shows no single model handles both regimes well: pi-0 dominates execution but collapses under ambiguity, while memory-based models trade robustness for weak context retention.

## Problem

- Prior long-horizon benchmarks are mostly simulation-based, abstracting away real contact dynamics, perceptual noise, and physical execution variability that dominate real-world failure.
- Existing real-world suites like FurnitureBench report only aggregate success rates, conflating distinct failure sources such as execution instability, dynamic response limits, and ambiguity resolution.
- No prior benchmark separates long-horizon difficulty into structural capability types or ambiguity patterns, leaving it unclear whether a policy fails from unstable execution or from broken context retention.

## Method

- ==Task taxonomy== separating **Context-Independent** tasks (four structural properties: Phase Dependence, Iterative Progress, Error Accumulation, Temporal Windows) from **Context-Dependent** tasks (four ambiguity patterns: Completion, Count, Subtask-Branch, Cross-Episode).
- A tabletop real-world platform using a 6-DoF ARX-R5 arm, dual top-down and wrist RGB cameras, and leader-follower teleoperation to collect 1,000 synchronized demonstrations across 10 tasks.
- A ==stage-wise scoring== protocol decomposing each task into atomic sub-steps for partial-credit evaluation, aggregated at task, mechanism (capability/ambiguity), and regime levels, plus a centralized leaderboard for reproducible remote submission. Together this forms **LongBench**.

## Results

- Across five Context-Independent tasks pi-0 averages **86.3%** versus **32.7-51.2%** for the other five policies, but all models except pi-0 collapse on temporal-window tasks (**0.0-13.3%** versus pi-0's **73.3%**).
- Across five Context-Dependent tasks scores drop sharply and unevenly: MemoryVLA leads on count ambiguity at **55.0%** yet Subtask-Branch ambiguity stays near-zero for most models, only reaching **30.0%** with memory.
- Cross-regime comparison shows pi-0's Context-Dependent score falls to **37.3%** (largest drop from its Context-Independent **86.3%**) while MemoryVLA shows the smallest gap, **49.1%** versus **45.3%**, without leading overall.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Long-horizon robustness is not one axis: strong fully-observable execution and strong context retention trade off, so no evaluated architecture excels at both simultaneously.
> - Explicit memory helps narrow ambiguity types like counting but does not generalize across ambiguity patterns, sometimes underperforming memoryless models like SmolVLA on completion and cross-episode tasks.
> - Failure analysis shows memory-based policies often commit to wrong contextual judgments early rather than merely running out of retained history, implicating faulty binding and retrieval over window length.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is a fundamental area of embodied intelligence, with a key challenge being the reliable execution of tasks over extended time horizons in dynamic physical environments. Recent advancements in vision–language–action (VLA) and vision–action (VA) policies have demonstrated improved performance on short-horizon manipulation skills. However, achieving robust long-horizon manipulation in real-world settings remains difficult due to the accumulation of errors, perceptual noise, and the intricate interdependence of sub-tasks.

Existing benchmarks for long-horizon manipulation can be broadly categorized into simulation-based and real-world datasets. Simulation-based benchmarks, such as RLBench, CALVIN, LIBERO, VLABench, RoboCasa, and RoboCerebra, offer scalable evaluation in controlled environments, allowing for studies of multi-step manipulation, language-conditioned control, and compositional task structures. These platforms have expanded the temporal depth and semantic diversity of tasks. However, a limitation of simulation is its abstraction of persistent physical execution dynamics and simplification of perceptual uncertainty. Consequently, simulation-based benchmarks often fail to disentangle distinct sources of temporal failure that are prevalent in real-world execution, such as cumulative control drift, recovery robustness, or delayed ambiguity resolution.

Real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks, exemplified by efforts like FurnitureBench, have focused on standardized evaluation and infrastructure for multi-step tasks under consistent hardware conditions. While these benchmarks provide realistic environments and complex multi-stage behaviors, they typically focus on reproducibility and aggregate task success rates. This aggregate view treats long-horizon manipulation as a single difficulty axis and does not provide mechanisms to differentiate between various failure sources, such as execution instability, dynamic response limitations, or context-dependent reasoning challenges.

The broader landscape of VLA models has also seen progress, with policies like π₀ leveraging flow-based action representations and models such as SmolVLA and OpenVLA-OFT exploring efficient fine-tuning. To address long-horizon dependencies, some policies, including MemoryVLA and CronusVLA, have incorporated explicit temporal modeling mechanisms like memory augmentation or multi-frame architectures. Despite these advancements, it has remained unclear how different policy designs perform under distinct sources of long-horizon difficulty, especially in real-world settings where factors like execution robustness and contextual ambiguity interact.

LongBench addresses this gap by introducing a real-world benchmark specifically designed for "mechanism-aware" evaluation of long-horizon robotic manipulation. It distinguishes itself by explicitly decomposing long-horizon difficulty into complementary regimes (Context-Independent and Context-Dependent) and organizing tasks into capability- and ambiguity-specific subsets. This approach aims to move beyond aggregate success metrics to diagnose the underlying causes of policy failures in realistic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce LongBench, a real-world benchmark for evaluating robotic manipulation policies on long-horizon tasks, with a particular focus on enabling mechanism-aware diagnosis of policy performance.

The motivation for developing LongBench stems from several limitations identified in the existing research landscape:
- **Insufficient Real-World Evaluation for Long-Horizon Tasks:** A significant portion of current long-horizon benchmarks are simulation-based. While valuable for scalable research, simulations often abstract away the complexities of real-world physics, such as noise, varied contact dynamics, and intricate perception errors. These real-world challenges can critically impact policy performance over extended durations and are not adequately captured by simulated environments.
- **Lack of Diagnostic Granularity in Existing Benchmarks:** Prior long-horizon benchmarks typically treat the problem as a monolithic challenge, often relying solely on aggregate success rates to measure performance. This approach, while providing an overall score, fails to differentiate between the diverse reasons for policy failure. For instance, a policy might fail due to execution instability, inability to react to dynamic changes, or a failure in context-dependent reasoning. Existing aggregate metrics do not allow researchers to disentangle these distinct sources of temporal difficulty.
- **Need to Decompose Long-Horizon Challenges:** The paper argues that long-horizon tasks do not share a single underlying challenge. Some tasks primarily demand robust, sustained execution under fully observable conditions, while others require policies to interpret ambiguous observations by recalling historical context. A benchmark that conflates these distinct difficulties limits the ability to precisely diagnose policy strengths and weaknesses.
- **Understanding Policy Limitations:** By providing a diagnostic framework, the authors aim to reveal the structural limitations of current state-of-the-art policies. This includes understanding whether memory-based methods consistently improve performance across various forms of contextual dependency and identifying which specific aspects of execution robustness (e.g., error accumulation, temporal windows) remain challenging.
- **Fostering Reproducible and Comparable Research:** The creation of a standardized real-world evaluation platform, including testing APIs and environment replication protocols, is motivated by the need to ensure reproducibility and comparability of results across different research groups, a challenge often encountered in real-world robotics research.

In summary, LongBench is motivated by the need for a real-world, diagnostic benchmark that explicitly decomposes long-horizon manipulation into its constituent challenges, enabling a more fine-grained understanding of policy failures beyond simple success rates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LongBench is designed based on three core principles: real-world long-horizon evaluation, explicit separation of execution and context, and mechanism-aware task decomposition.

### 4. Task Taxonomy

The benchmark organizes long-horizon tasks into two complementary regimes to differentiate sources of temporal difficulty:

- **Context-Independent Long-Horizon Tasks (5 tasks):** In this regime, all decision-relevant information is present in the current observation (visual input, robot state, language instruction). The primary challenge is maintaining stable and feasible execution over extended horizons, dealing with error accumulation, phase transitions, and time-critical interactions. These tasks are characterized by four structural properties:
 - **Phase Dependence (PD):** Tasks with strict preconditions between stages, where early mistakes can invalidate later steps.
 - **Iterative Progress (IP):** Sequences of similar subgoals requiring progress tracking.
 - **Error Accumulation (EA):** Small errors compounding over time, leading to degradation.
 - **Temporal Windows (TW):** Actions requiring execution within limited time frames.
 - Examples: "waste sorting", "thread rope", "pull drawer", "stack block", "dynamic grasping".

- **Context-Dependent Long-Horizon Tasks (5 tasks):** In this regime, the current observation is insufficient for correct action determination due to state-aliasing. Policies must retain and utilize historical context to resolve ambiguity. These tasks are characterized by four ambiguity patterns:
 - **Completion Ambiguity (CP):** Current observation is indistinguishable from a valid terminal state, leading to premature termination or incomplete execution.
 - **Count Ambiguity (CT):** Current observation does not indicate how many times a repeated action has been executed.
 - **Subtask-Branch Ambiguity (SB):** Visually similar observations correspond to different subtask branches requiring incompatible actions.
 - **Cross-Episode Ambiguity (CE):** Similar observations recur across episodes with different task requirements, demanding retrieval of much earlier context.
 - Examples: "repeat placement", "swap blocks", "wipe plate", "hide block", "vase sticks".

### 5. Platform and Data Collection

LongBench utilizes a tabletop manipulation platform featuring a 6-DoF ARX-R5 robotic arm. Visual observations are captured by two RGB cameras: a fixed top-down camera for a global view and a wrist-mounted camera for close-range perception, both at 320x240 resolution and 20 Hz. Robot actions and states are logged at the same frequency. The dataset comprises over 1,000 real-world episodes collected via leader–follower teleoperation, ensuring consistent observation and control setups between data collection and policy evaluation.

### 6. Evaluated Policies

Six state-of-the-art policies were evaluated, covering both single-frame and multi-frame architectures:
- **Single-frame policies (no explicit temporal history):** π₀, OpenVLA-OFT, SmolVLA, Diffusion Policy (DP).
- **Multi-frame policies (explicitly incorporate history):** MemoryVLA, CronusVLA.
All policies were trained on the same LongBench demonstrations.

### 7. Evaluation Protocol

Policies are evaluated under a unified observation–action interface, receiving synchronized top and wrist RGB observations and robot proprioceptive state. They predict 16-step action chunks in an open-loop manner, operating in the end-effector delta pose and gripper action control space at 20 Hz, under consistent scene randomization.

### 8. Evaluation Metric

A stage-wise scoring scheme is employed instead of a binary success metric. Each task is decomposed into *N* atomic sub-steps, and each completed sub-step contributes (100/N) points. The episode score is calculated as `score = (#completed / N) × 100`.
Performance aggregation occurs at three levels:
- **Task-level:** Mean episode score over 10 distinct initializations for each task.
- **Mechanism-level:** Average task scores over all tasks labeled with a specific capability (PD, IP, EA, TW) for Context-Independent tasks, or ambiguity pattern (CP, CT, SB, CE) for Context-Dependent tasks.
- **Regime-level:** Average task scores within the Context-Independent and Context-Dependent regimes, respectively.

### 9. Benchmark Access and Reproducibility

To ensure practical usability and fair comparisons, LongBench offers a centralized evaluation platform for policy submissions and a public leaderboard. Additionally, environment replication protocols, including a detailed object list and setup manual, are provided to support local development and ablation studies.

### 10. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation using LongBench revealed several non-uniform patterns in long-horizon robotic manipulation performance, highlighting that it is not governed by a single factor.

### 11. Results on Context-Independent Long-Horizon Tasks

These tasks, which are fully observable, primarily test execution robustness over extended rollouts.
- **Performance Gaps:** Significant performance differences were observed among models. π₀ consistently outperformed all other methods across all subsets, suggesting its robust execution capabilities in fully observable settings.
- **Temporal-Window Weakness:** All models except π₀ exhibited a sharp drop in performance on Temporal-Window (TW) tasks, indicating a critical weakness in handling time-critical interactions. While other execution-oriented capabilities (Phase Dependence, Iterative Progress, Error Accumulation) showed partial competence, responsiveness under strict time constraints remained a challenge.
- **Error Accumulation Variability:** Tasks involving Error Accumulation (EA) showed larger performance variance across methods, pointing to a general difficulty in maintaining robustness under long-horizon error propagation, even for π₀ which achieved high average scores.
- **Implication:** Full observability alone does not guarantee robust performance. Current policies exhibit systematic weaknesses on specific capability subsets, particularly temporal-window tasks, suggesting uneven robustness across Context-Independent challenges.

### 12. Results on Context-Dependent Long-Horizon Tasks

These tasks require policies to leverage historical context to resolve ambiguity.
- **Heterogeneous Contextual Difficulty:** Performance varied drastically across different ambiguity types. While moderate success was observed in Count Ambiguity (CT), performance was near-complete failure for Subtask-Branch (SB) ambiguity for several models. This indicates that contextual tasks do not share a unified difficulty axis.
- **Limited Generalization of Memory:** Memory-conditioned models (MemoryVLA, CronusVLA) showed gains on Count Ambiguity (CT), outperforming some baselines. However, these improvements were not consistent across all ambiguity types. In Completion (CP) and Cross-Episode (CE) tasks, memory-based models did not consistently outperform strong frame-based policies (e.g., SmolVLA).
- **Persistent Challenge in Subtask-Branch Ambiguity:** Subtask-Branch (SB) ambiguity remained challenging for all methods, with several models achieving near-zero performance. This suggests that merely having access to history is insufficient; effective utilization, binding, and retrieval of context for complex branching logic remains an unsolved problem.
- **Implication:** Access to historical information does not consistently translate to improved performance across all forms of context-dependent tasks. Policies struggle with diverse contextual dependencies, indicating that context-dependent behavior is not solely a memory problem but involves various forms of reasoning.

### 13. Cross-Regime Analysis: Context-Independent vs. Context-Dependent Tasks

This analysis compared policy performance across fully observable and ambiguity-driven settings.
- **Performance Degradation:** All policies exhibited a sharp performance difference between the two regimes. Models strong in Context-Independent tasks (e.g., π₀ with 86.3%) experienced significant degradation in Context-Dependent tasks (37.3%).
- **Distinct Cross-Regime Profiles:** There was an observed trade-off: policies that performed strongly in fully observable settings tended to degrade more under contextual ambiguity, while models with relatively stable performance under ambiguity did not necessarily achieve strong execution performance.
- **Memory vs. Overall Performance:** Explicit memory in models like MemoryVLA resulted in a smaller performance gap between regimes (49.1% vs. 45.3%) but did not lead to overall superior performance compared to all other methods. SmolVLA, a frame-based policy, achieved competitive Context-Dependent performance, sometimes outperforming history-conditioned models.
- **Implication:** Achieving robust performance across both execution-oriented and context-dependent tasks simultaneously is a significant challenge for current models. Robustness cannot be captured by a single performance axis, but requires addressing both execution reliability and context-dependent decision-making.

### 14. Significance and Potential Impact

LongBench represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for long-horizon tasks, due to several key aspects:

- **Addresses a Critical Gap in Evaluation:** By focusing on real-world, mechanism-aware evaluation, LongBench moves beyond the limitations of simulation-based benchmarks and aggregate success metrics. It provides a more realistic and nuanced understanding of policy performance in complex physical environments, where noise, contact dynamics, and perceptual uncertainty are prevalent.
- **Enables Fine-Grained Diagnostic Analysis:** The benchmark's structured decomposition into Context-Independent and Context-Dependent tasks, further categorized by capability- and ambiguity-specific subsets, allows researchers to diagnose *why* policies fail, rather than just *if* they fail. This diagnostic lens is crucial for identifying specific bottlenecks and guiding more targeted policy development.
- **Highlights Structural Limitations of Current Policies:** The empirical study of six state-of-the-art policies reveals specific weaknesses. For example, the consistent failure of most policies on temporal-window tasks in Context-Independent settings highlights a need for improved real-time responsiveness and robustness under dynamic constraints. Similarly, the varied performance across Context-Dependent ambiguity types and the limited generalization of memory-based methods indicate that context utilization is a multifaceted problem beyond simple history retention.
- **Informs Future Policy Development:** The findings suggest that a unified solution for long-horizon manipulation, addressing both execution robustness and complex contextual reasoning simultaneously, is still lacking. This encourages the development of more sophisticated architectures that can robustly handle accumulating errors, time-critical interactions, and diverse forms of ambiguity. It challenges the assumption that explicit memory automatically translates to robust contextual understanding across all scenarios.
- **Fosters Reproducible and Comparable Research:** By providing a standardized real-world evaluation platform, including detailed environment replication protocols and a centralized leaderboard, LongBench promotes reproducibility and fair comparison of different robotic manipulation policies. This infrastructure lowers the barrier for researchers to contribute to and validate advancements in real-world long-horizon robotics.
- **Foundation for Further Research:** LongBench serves as a valuable framework for studying the intricate interplay between perception, control, and temporal reasoning in real-world robotic systems. It can facilitate research into novel architectural designs, learning algorithms, and representation choices that aim to overcome the identified challenges in long-horizon manipulation.

In conclusion, LongBench is poised to significantly impact the trajectory of research in long-horizon robotic manipulation by providing a diagnostic tool and a robust real-world benchmark that exposes the intricate and diverse challenges inherent in such tasks, thereby accelerating the development of more capable and reliable robotic agents.
