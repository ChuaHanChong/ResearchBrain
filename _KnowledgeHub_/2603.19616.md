# UniPR: Unified Object-level Real-to-Sim Perception and Reconstruction from a Single Stereo Pair

> [!summary] Summary
> UniPR is the first end-to-end object-level real-to-sim framework: from one stereo image pair it detects and reconstructs every object in a scene in a single forward pass. Stereo cross-attention over a triplane-view feature volume feeds DETR-style object queries, and a Pose-Aware Shape Representation jointly encodes pose and geometry, giving metric-scale meshes with up to 100x faster full-scene generation and 3x better shape-proportion accuracy.

## Problem

- Modular real-to-sim pipelines chain detection, segmentation, shape reconstruction and pose estimation, accumulating error because each stage sees only partial, locally cropped context.
- Category-level methods need hand-defined canonical spaces, so most handle under six categories and break down under large intra-class shape variation.
- Monocular image-to-3D generators suffer scale ambiguity, distort true object proportions, and reconstruct only one object per inference, making multi-object scenes slow.

## Method

- **Pose-Aware Shape Representation** (PASR) encodes pose and geometry jointly in observation space via a ==spherical-voxel-space shape VAE==, removing canonical spaces and rotation-induced scale ambiguity.
- ==Triplane-view encoder== initializes UV/UD/VD planes in camera UVD space and lifts DINOv2 stereo features through ==stereo cross-attention== plus ==cross-plane self-attention== over N layers.
- ==DETR-style transformer decoder== turns object queries into embeddings, with 3D MLP for position and scale, shape MLP for the ==KL-regularized== latent, ==Hungarian matching==, and CLIP for labels.

## Results

- On **50** LVS6D objects, Chamfer Distance drops to **0.0083** versus **0.0644** for Hunyuan3D 2.1, F-Score rises to **0.883**, shape-proportion error falls to **0.109** from **0.320**.
- Full-scene inference takes **0.63s** versus **43.08s** for Trellis and **370.78s** for Hunyuan3D, a roughly **100x** speed-up on **five**-object scenes without external masks or poses.
- Ablations: removing PASR raises hard-subset Chamfer Distance from **1.224** to **12.363**; monocular input drops AP from **0.752** to **0.270**; spherical voxels lift hard AP **0.677** to **0.752**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting shape directly in observation space instead of a canonical frame lets category-level reconstruction scale to hundreds of vocabularies without hand-defined orientations.
> - Metric geometry, not generative fidelity, drives real-to-sim usefulness: stereo constraints preserve true proportions that image-conditioned 3D generators systematically distort.
> - Parallel query-based scene processing beats per-object cascades on both speed and occlusion robustness, since global image context survives instead of being cropped away.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate perception and 3D reconstruction of real-world objects are fundamental requirements for various robotic applications, including manipulation and scene understanding. The output of such systems must possess not only visual fidelity but also physical accuracy in geometry and position to enable reliable interaction in both real and simulated environments (real-to-sim transfer).

Current methodologies for object-level real-to-sim reconstruction can be broadly categorized into several types, each with limitations.
- **Modular Pipelines:** Many existing approaches decompose the perception and reconstruction process into a series of isolated submodules, such as 2D detection, segmentation, shape reconstruction, and pose estimation. This modular design often leads to several issues:
 - **Error Propagation:** Errors accumulate through sequential stages, as inaccuracies from one module impact subsequent ones.
 - **Computational Inefficiency:** Each stage operates on partial or locally refined information, often requiring individual processing for each detected object, which can be slow for complex scenes.
 - **Inconsistent Shape Proportion:** Information loss between modules can result in reconstructed objects lacking true physical proportions.
 - **Dependency on Upstream Modules:** Many methods require predefined object bounding boxes and segmentation masks, limiting their autonomy.
- **Instance-Level Methods:** These methods typically rely on accurate CAD models or multi-view images (e.g., NeRF-based reconstruction) to reconstruct objects and predict their poses. A significant limitation is their constrained generalization ability to novel or unseen objects, as they require object-specific priors.
- **Category-Level Approaches:** These frameworks aim to reconstruct object shapes within predefined canonical spaces while simultaneously estimating relative poses. However, their reliance on category-specific shape priors inherently limits practical scalability, often handling only a small number of categories due to the challenges in defining universal canonical spaces and accommodating large intra-class variations.
- **Large-Scale 3D Generative Models (Image-to-3D):** Recent advancements in generative models have shown notable zero-shot capabilities and high visual fidelity in producing 3D assets from images. However, these methods often struggle to preserve accurate shape proportions because they generate object meshes primarily from visual inputs lacking metric information. Furthermore, most of these models are designed to process only a single object at a time, rendering them inefficient for complex scenes containing multiple objects.
- **Monocular vs. Stereo Vision:** Monocular approaches inherently suffer from scale ambiguity, making it challenging to obtain the metric-accurate reconstructions crucial for robotic manipulation. In contrast, stereo vision provides the geometric constraints necessary to resolve this ambiguity, thus enabling the preservation of object shape proportions and metric accuracy.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a unified, end-to-end framework that integrates perception and reconstruction directly from raw stereo inputs. The aim is to overcome error propagation, improve computational efficiency, ensure physically accurate object proportions, and scale to a large vocabulary of objects without relying on predefined canonical spaces, thus advancing real-to-sim transfer for robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current object-level perception and reconstruction pipelines in achieving scalable physical accuracy and computational efficiency for real-to-sim transfer in robotics. The paper identifies a critical gap in existing approaches that are either modular, restricted by object-specific or category-specific priors, or inherently suffer from scale ambiguity and inefficiency.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a Unified End-to-End Framework:** The central objective is to integrate all perception and reconstruction tasks—namely, object detection, segmentation, shape reconstruction, and pose estimation—into a single, unified pipeline. This aims to eliminate intermediate modules, thereby preventing error accumulation and improving overall computational efficiency. The framework processes raw sensor inputs (stereo images) directly to generate physically accurate 3D reconstructions.

2. **Achieve Physically Accurate 3D Reconstructions with True Proportions:** A significant goal is to ensure that reconstructed objects maintain their true physical proportions and accurate spatial relationships. This metric accuracy is essential for reliable robotic manipulation and simulation interaction, which existing monocular or generative models often fail to provide due to scale ambiguity.

3. **Resolve Scale Ambiguity using Geometric Constraints:** The paper aims to leverage stereo vision to provide the necessary geometric constraints. This is critical for obtaining metric-accurate reconstructions, addressing a fundamental limitation of monocular approaches that cannot determine the true scale of objects without additional information.

4. **Enable Parallel Processing of Multiple Objects:** To improve efficiency in complex, multi-object scenes, an objective is to design a system capable of processing all objects in a scene concurrently within a single forward pass. This contrasts with traditional sequential pipelines that handle objects individually.

5. **Introduce Pose-Aware Shape Representation (PASR):** A core objective is to propose a novel shape representation that jointly encodes object pose and geometry directly in the observation space. This design is intended to remove the dependence on predefined canonical spaces, allowing for greater scalability across diverse object categories and tightly coupling pose estimation with shape reconstruction, thus removing error-prone decoupling.

6. **Construct a Large-Scale Stereo Dataset (LVS6D):** To facilitate large-scale research and robust evaluation of the proposed framework, an objective is to create a comprehensive stereo dataset with a diverse and extensive vocabulary of objects. This dataset is designed to support scalable perception-reconstruction tasks without relying on canonical definitions.

7. **Demonstrate Robust Generalization and Practical Applicability:** The research aims to validate the framework's ability to generalize to real-world scenes and unseen object categories, and to confirm its direct applicability in robotic manipulation tasks requiring precise physical alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces UniPR, a single-forward network designed to infer object position, size, and pose-aware 3D shape directly from stereo images. The framework is structured to integrate multiple perception and reconstruction tasks into an end-to-end pipeline.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

UniPR takes a stereo image pair as input. It first extracts 2D stereo features using a DINOv2 backbone. These features are then aggregated into a global spatial framework via a Triplane Encoder. An Object Embedding Decoder, based on a transformer architecture, generates instance-specific object embeddings. These embeddings are subsequently used by specialized prediction heads to infer object properties such as 3D position, physical scale, and a pose-aware shape representation. Finally, a pre-trained shape decoder reconstructs the object's 3D shape.

#### 3.2. Pose-Aware Shape VAE (PASR)

A core component of UniPR is the Pose-Aware Shape VAE, which aims to represent object shapes in a rotation-invariant and scale-consistent manner.
- **Motivation for Spherical Voxel Space:** Traditional cubic voxel spaces can suffer from scale ambiguities when objects are rotated, as a normalized object might extend beyond the cube's boundary or require re-normalization that alters its perceived scale. To address this, PASR utilizes a spherical voxel space, normalizing objects within a unit sphere, ensuring they remain within bounds regardless of rotation and preventing scale ambiguities.
- **Encoder (ε_VAE):** Given an object's surface point cloud, positional embeddings are first applied to generate surface embeddings. Self-attention layers process these into point latent embeddings. An object embedding is initialized, and cross-attention layers learn a mapping from the point distribution to a compact object-level representation. This process involves predicting the mean (μ) and variance (σ²) for a Gaussian distribution, with KL regularization applied to project embeddings into a lower-dimensional latent space.
- **Decoder (D_VAE):** The decoder samples an object latent embedding from the learned Gaussian distribution. It then uses cross-attention to transform this sampled embedding into surface embeddings. Further self-attention blocks enhance these point embeddings. Finally, positional embeddings generate query embeddings, and another cross-attention mechanism maps the surface distribution to these query embeddings. An MLP projects the query point embeddings into occupancy values to reconstruct the 3D object shape. The pre-trained VAE decoder is integrated into UniPR as the shape decoder.

#### 3.3. Triplane Encoder

This module aggregates stereo features into a global coordinate system.
- **Triplane-View (TPV) Representation:** UniPR initializes a TPV feature representation consisting of three planes: T_UV, T_UD, and T_VD. This UVD space is designed for object detection in camera space, facilitating accurate representation and matching.
- **Stereo Feature Aggregation:** For each voxel grid point in the UVD space, corresponding pixels in the left and right stereo images are back-projected. Stereo cross-attention is then used to lift and update the TPV features by aggregating features extracted from the stereo images (e.g., via DINOv2).
- **Information Fusion:** Self-attention mechanisms are applied to fuse information across the three TPV planes, enhancing the overall TPV representation. This combination of self-attention and stereo cross-attention is iterated through multiple encoder layers.

#### 3.4. Object Embedding Decoder

This module generates object embeddings that encapsulate all relevant information (position, scale, 3D shape).
- **Transformer Decoder:** It adopts a standard transformer decoder structure (similar to DETR), comprising self-attention (for object query interaction), cross-attention (to integrate stereo-aware TPV features), and a feed-forward network (to update object queries).
- **Prediction Heads:**
 - **3D MLP:** Regresses the object's 3D position (x, y, z) and scale.
 - **Shape MLP:** Generates the shape embedding distribution (μ, σ²) for the object, adhering to a KL-regularized Gaussian distribution as established by the PASR VAE.
- **Shape Reconstruction:** The predicted shape embedding distribution is fed into the pre-trained shape decoder to calculate the occupancy of spherical query points, thus yielding the object's pose-aware 3D point cloud.
- **Category Classification:** Instead of a traditional classification head, UniPR utilizes the CLIP model to determine the object category from a preset list, based on the 2D bounding box projected from the 3D location. An object confidence score is regressed to identify valid objects.

#### 3.5. Training Loss

- **Pose-Aware Shape VAE Training:**
 - `L_recon`: Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss for occupancy prediction.
 - `L_kl-reg`: KL regularization loss for the encoded shape distribution against a standard Gaussian distribution.
- **UniPR Detection Pipeline Training:**
 - The Hungarian algorithm is employed for one-to-one matching between ground truth and predicted objects.
 - `L_position` and `L_scale`: L1 loss functions are used for supervising object position and scale.
 - `L_shape`: KL regularization (`L_kl`) quantifies the distance between the ground truth and predicted shape embedding distributions.
 - The total detection loss is `L_detection = L_position + L_scale + λ_shape × L_shape`, where `λ_shape` is a hyperparameter balancing the shape loss contribution.

#### 3.6. LVS6D Dataset

To support the research, a new dataset, LVS6D (Large-Vocabulary Stereo 6D), was created.
- **Composition:** It integrates 3D models from OmniObject3D and Google Scanned Objects, encompassing 192 categories and over 6,300 diverse everyday objects.
- **Data Generation:** Approximately 0.4 million stereo images for training and 1,000 for testing were generated using BlenderProc with over 500 HDRI backgrounds. The stereo camera setup features a 13 cm baseline, with images at 1920x1200 resolution.
- **Annotations:** For each stereo image, object masks, category labels, 3D position, scale, rotation, and corresponding 3D shapes (textured meshes) are provided.
- **Subsets:** The test categories are divided into Easy, Medium, and Hard subsets based on object complexity and intra-class variation to facilitate comprehensive evaluation.
- **Real-World Data:** Real-scene images captured with the same stereo camera configuration are included to evaluate generalization performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented in the paper demonstrate UniPR's performance across 3D reconstruction, object detection, pose estimation, and real-world generalization.

#### 4.1. 3D Reconstruction Results

- **Quantitative Comparison with Generative Models (Table 1):** UniPR was compared against state-of-the-art image-to-3D generative models like Trellis and Hunyuan2.1.
 - UniPR achieved a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.0083, significantly lower than Trellis (0.1096) and Hunyuan2.1 (0.0644), indicating superior geometric fidelity.
 - The F-Score for UniPR was 0.883, higher than Trellis (0.334) and Hunyuan2.1 (0.553).
 - Crucially, UniPR exhibited a Shape Proportion Error (SPE) of 0.109, outperforming Trellis (0.475) and Hunyuan2.1 (0.320), demonstrating its ability to preserve true physical proportions.
 - Even when considering successful cases (CD@Top10), UniPR maintained superior performance.
- **Efficiency (Table 1):** UniPR processed entire scenes (e.g., 5 objects) in 0.63 seconds, achieving up to a 100x speed-up compared to sequential baselines like Trellis (43.08 s) and Hunyuan2.1 (370.78 s) for full-scene reconstruction.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 4):** UniPR consistently produced reconstructions that accurately preserved shape proportions, handled unseen objects, and demonstrated robustness under occlusion, attributed to its stereo vision and full-image context processing.

#### 4.2. Comparison on LVS6D Dataset

- **Quantitative Comparison with Coders (Table 2):** UniPR was evaluated against Coders, a stereo-based category-level 6D object pose estimation and shape reconstruction method, on the newly introduced LVS6D dataset.
 - UniPR consistently surpassed Coders across all metrics (Average Precision (AP), Average Position Error (APE), Average Chamfer Distance (ACD)) and dataset subsets (Easy, Medium, Hard).
 - On the challenging Hard subset, UniPR achieved an AP of 0.752, compared to Coders' 0.070, indicating a significant improvement in overall 3D detection.
 - APE for UniPR (1.248) was lower than Coders (2.230), signifying better localization accuracy.
 - ACD for UniPR (1.224) was substantially lower than Coders (10.146), highlighting superior shape reconstruction quality.
 - The integration of PASR into Coders ("Coders with PASR") showed improved performance over the original Coders, validating the benefits of the pose-aware design in mitigating ambiguities from predefined canonical spaces.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 5):** UniPR's rotation predictions were noted to be significantly improved over Coders, primarily because the PASR design effectively eliminated rotational ambiguities for objects with similar geometric structures.

#### 4.3. Comparison on Public Stereo Datasets (TOD, SS3D)

- **Quantitative Results (Table 3):** UniPR demonstrated strong performance on public datasets like TOD and SS3D, outperforming most other methods across various metrics (e.g., 3D IoU, threshold-based accuracy for rotation and translation).
 - On SS3D, UniPR achieved a 3D_50 of 89.3, significantly higher than Coders' 61.7.
 - For the TOD dataset, UniPR also showed competitive results, maintaining high accuracy in 3D IoU and pose metrics.
- **Part-Aware Refinement (Supplementary Table 9):** An additional experiment on the TOD dataset for "mug-like" objects showed that incorporating a part-aware refinement stage (similar to baselines) further enhanced UniPR's performance, resolving topological complexities and surpassing Coders for specific categories.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Pose-Aware Shape Representation (PASR) (Table 4):** Experiments confirmed the criticality of PASR. Removing PASR led to a nearly 10x increase in ACD on challenging subsets, indicating that conventional canonical representations failed to establish robust priors for geometrically diverse objects.
- **Stereo vs. Monocular (Table 4):** The stereo configuration significantly outperformed its monocular counterpart. This improvement was attributed to stereo's ability to recover accurate depth information, which is a limitation for monocular inputs, especially for objects with diverse scales or complex arrangements.
- **Spherical vs. Cubic Voxel Space (Table 5):** The spherical voxel space was found to significantly enhance training stability and performance, particularly on the hard subset of LVS6D, by maintaining rotational consistency and preventing scale dependencies.
- **KL-based Supervision (Supplementary Table 7):** This supervision was found to be important for effective utilization of the pre-trained VAE model, contributing to higher AP and lower ACD.
- **Fairness Comparison on Input Modalities (Supplementary Table 8):** Further experiments confirmed that UniPR's performance gains primarily originate from architectural innovations (PASR and Triplane integration), rather than solely from the stereo input modality. UniPR's monocular variant still outperformed other monocular baselines, and its full stereo model significantly exceeded stereo-extended baselines in shape proportion accuracy.

#### 4.5. Real-World Generalization and Robotic Applications

- **Qualitative Results (Figure 12):** UniPR demonstrated robust generalization capabilities when applied to real-world inputs, showing accurate reconstructions.
- **Robotic Grasping (Supplementary Video):** Real-robot experiments confirmed the metric accuracy of UniPR's predicted object positions and scales. By directly using these predictions, a robot successfully performed grasping tasks on various objects, validating the practical reliability of the method for robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniPR framework represents a notable advancement in object-level perception and reconstruction for real-to-sim transfer, addressing several long-standing limitations in the field.

- **Paradigm Shift to End-to-End Processing:** By integrating detection, segmentation, shape reconstruction, and pose estimation into a single, end-to-end pipeline from stereo inputs, UniPR establishes a unified paradigm. This eliminates error propagation inherent in sequential modular systems and streamlines the entire process. This approach could influence future research directions by promoting integrated system designs over fragmented pipelines.

- **Enhanced Efficiency for Multi-Object Scenes:** The ability to process an entire scene with multiple objects in a single forward pass, achieving up to a 100x speed-up, is a significant contribution. This improved computational efficiency is crucial for real-time robotic applications, enabling faster decision-making and interaction in dynamic environments.

- **Superior Physical Accuracy and Scale Consistency:** The leverage of stereo vision to resolve scale ambiguity, combined with the Pose-Aware Shape Representation (PASR), allows UniPR to reconstruct objects with true physical proportions and metric-accurate poses. This level of physical accuracy is vital for realistic simulation environments and precise robotic manipulation, where the interaction between real and virtual objects must be meticulously aligned.

- **Scalability and Generalization to Diverse Objects:** The PASR design eliminates the need for predefined, category-specific canonical spaces, a major bottleneck for previous category-level methods. This enables UniPR to scale seamlessly to a large vocabulary of diverse object categories and generalize effectively to unseen objects and complex geometries. This capability expands the applicability of such systems beyond controlled environments to more unstructured real-world scenarios.

- **New Dataset for Research:** The introduction of LVS6D, a large-scale stereo dataset comprising 192 categories and over 6,300 objects, provides a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset can serve as a benchmark for developing and evaluating future large-vocabulary real-to-sim perception and reconstruction methods.

- **Direct Applicability in Robotics:** The demonstrated robustness in real-world scenarios and successful validation through real-robot grasping experiments highlight UniPR's immediate practical utility. Its ability to provide physically accurate 3D geometry and pose is directly beneficial for tasks such as robotic grasping, assembly, and scene understanding in industrial and service robotics.

- **Foundation for Future Development:** While the current work primarily focuses on shape reconstruction, the underlying representation (VecSet) and end-to-end architecture offer a foundation for integrating additional functionalities, such as external texture generation models. The identified limitation in monocular settings also paves the way for future research into incorporating large-scale depth modules to expand its applicability to simpler camera setups.
