# Effective Reasoning Chains Reduce Intrinsic Dimensionality

> [!summary] Summary
> Effective reasoning chains reduce the intrinsic dimensionality of a task, making it more compressible and easier for a model to learn and generalize from, as evidenced by a 0.93 Spearman rank correlation with generalization performance for a 4B parameter model. This framework offers a quantitative explanation for Chain-of-Thought reasoning benefits, rooted in information theory.

## Problem

- The precise mechanisms by which Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning strategies improve large language model (LLM) generalization are poorly understood, relying on qualitative or conflicting quantitative explanations.
- There is a lack of a principled, robust, and generalizable metric to characterize *why* and *when* specific reasoning strategies are effective.
- Existing metrics like trajectory length or perplexity offer inconsistent or incomplete insights into CoT efficacy, hindering practical guidance for LLM development and data annotation.

## Method

- The authors introduce ==intrinsic dimensionality== (ID) as a quantifiable measure of a task's learnability, defined by the minimum number of model parameters required to achieve a specified performance threshold.
- Intrinsic dimensionality is measured by fine-tuning **Gemma-3** 1B and 4B base models with ==Low-Rank Adaptation== (**LoRA**) using various ranks and adapted layers, sweeping parameter counts.
- They systematically evaluate 14 diverse reasoning strategies, generated by powerful teacher models, on the GSM8K dataset and out-of-distribution stress tests to correlate ID with generalization performance.

## Results

- Intrinsic dimensionality strongly predicts generalization performance, showing a 0.93 inverse Spearman rank correlation with overall test performance for the Gemma-3 4B model, and 0.75 for the 1B model.
- This metric consistently outperformed baseline proxies; trajectory length showed weak positive correlation (0.31 for 4B), and sequence KL divergence exhibited weak or negative correlation (-0.17 for 4B).
- The 'Executed PoT' (Program-of-Thought with actual program execution) strategy consistently exhibited the lowest intrinsic dimensionality and achieved the strongest out-of-distribution generalization across both model sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective reasoning strategies reduce the intrinsic dimensionality of a task, making it more compressible and thus easier for a language model to learn and generalize from, even if the explicit output length increases.
> - Intrinsic dimensionality provides a robust, quantitative, and theoretically grounded explanation for CoT effectiveness, outperforming commonly used proxy metrics like trajectory length and sequence KL divergence.
> - The inherent learnability and compressibility of a reasoning task, as captured by ID, is a strong predictor of generalization performance, offering a new perspective on optimizing LLM reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research is situated within the field of Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, specifically focusing on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and its variants. CoT prompting, which involves generating intermediate textual rationales before a final answer, has been shown to improve LLM performance on complex reasoning tasks. A wide array of CoT strategies exists, including code-based solutions, decomposition approaches, and iterative reasoning with verification. While these strategies empirically yield varying performance, the underlying mechanisms by which they facilitate generalization remain incompletely understood.

Existing explanations for CoT effectiveness often suffer from limitations. Qualitative hypotheses, such as the importance of "structure" or "relevance" in reasoning chains, lack quantifiable metrics, thus limiting their predictive capacity and theoretical grounding. Quantitative measures, like reasoning trajectory length, have yielded conflicting evidence, with some studies suggesting gains from longer chains and others reporting performance degradation with excessive length. Furthermore, current approaches like process reward models often rely on subjective specifications of desirable properties, lacking a principled, objective measure of effectiveness.

This work addresses this gap by introducing "intrinsic dimensionality" as a quantitative measure to characterize the effectiveness of reasoning chains. Drawing on information-theoretic perspectives, particularly the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, which posits an inverse relationship between the capacity required to represent a solution and its expected generalization, the paper leverages the concept of intrinsic dimensionality. Prior research has typically used intrinsic dimensionality to analyze how different models vary in their parameter efficiency for a fixed task. This paper, however, inverts this setup by fixing the model and instead varying the training data through different reasoning strategies. By doing so, it aims to establish a consistent, quantifiable link between reasoning strategies and generalization performance, thereby offering a new analytical tool for understanding and optimizing LLM reasoning processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify and validate a quantitative measure for characterizing the effectiveness of reasoning chains, thereby providing a more principled understanding of when and why different reasoning strategies facilitate language model generalization.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations in the current understanding and evaluation of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning:
1. **Lack of Quantifiable Explanations:** While CoT and its variants have empirically demonstrated substantial performance improvements on complex reasoning tasks, the precise mechanisms driving these gains are not well understood. Existing hypotheses about the importance of "structure" or "relevance" are often qualitative and lack a consistent, quantifiable link to generalization. This limits the ability to predict which reasoning strategies will be most effective or to develop theoretically grounded explanations.
2. **Conflicting Quantitative Evidence:** Some quantitative measures, such as the length of reasoning trajectories, have yielded conflicting findings in prior work. Some studies suggest longer chains correlate with better performance, while others indicate that shorter chains can be more effective or that excessive length can degrade performance. This inconsistency highlights the need for a more robust and universally applicable metric.
3. **Subjectivity in Current Metrics:** Approaches like process reward models or correctness-based classifiers often require subjective specifications of desirable properties of reasoning chains. This subjectivity can hinder the development of generalizable methods for annotating data, aligning models, or designing effective regularizers.
4. **Information-Theoretic Foundation:** The authors are motivated by the long-standing literature linking information-theoretic concepts, such as the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, to the generalization capabilities of neural networks. The notion of intrinsic dimensionality, which quantifies the effective number of parameters needed to fit a given task, offers a potential avenue to apply these insights.
5. **Hypothesis on Compressibility:** The central hypothesis is that effective reasoning strategies do not merely add "test-time computation" or "structural guidance," but rather render the underlying input-output mapping more compressible. By effectively bridging the logical gap between input and answer, an effective reasoning strategy should reduce the degrees of freedom required for a model to learn the task, thereby resulting in lower intrinsic dimensionality. This contrasts with an intuitive expectation that generating longer reasoning chains might *increase* task complexity.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to provide a reliable, quantitative predictor of reasoning strategy effectiveness, which could have practical implications for data annotation, model alignment, and training optimization, moving beyond ad-hoc heuristics towards a principled framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs intrinsic dimensionality as a quantitative metric to evaluate the effectiveness of various reasoning strategies. The core approach involves fixing the model architecture while systematically varying the task formulation through different reasoning strategies.

#### 3.1. Intrinsic Dimensionality Measurement

- **Definition:** Intrinsic dimensionality ($d_{int}$) is defined as the minimum number of trainable parameters required for a model to reach a specified performance threshold on a given task. It represents the effective number of parameters needed to fit a task objective within a lower-dimensional subspace of the model's full parameter space.
- **Mathematical Formulation:** Model parameters $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^D$ are expressed as $\theta = \theta_0 + P(\theta_d)$, where $\theta_0$ are pretrained parameters, $D$ is the total parameter count, $\theta_d \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is a lower-dimensional parameter vector with $d \le D$, and $P: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^D$ is a projection operator.
- **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):** Instead of global random projections, which can be suboptimal for large models, the study adopts LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation). LoRA constrains weight updates of specific matrices in the transformer architecture to low-rank subspaces. For a pretrained weight matrix $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, the update is $W = W_0 + BA$, where $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r}$ and $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n}$ are learned low-rank matrices with rank $r \le \min(m, n)$. $W_0$ remains frozen during training. The total number of trainable parameters for LoRA is given by $2 \times L_{LoRA} \times d_{model} \times r$, where $L_{LoRA}$ is the number of adapted weight matrices and $d_{model}$ is the model's hidden dimension.
- **Measuring $d_{int}$:** $d_{int}$ is calculated as $min\{d : A(d) \ge \tau\}$, where $A(d)$ is the training accuracy achieved with $d$ trainable parameters, and $\tau$ is a common performance threshold. This involves a sweep of $k$ LoRA configurations, varying both the rank ($r$) and the specific weight matrices ($L_{LoRA}$) to which LoRA is applied. Configurations are chosen to distribute parameter counts uniformly in log scale. The minimum parameter count achieving accuracy $\ge \tau$ is recorded.
- **Threshold Selection ($\tau$):** A crucial aspect is setting a common threshold $\tau$ across different reasoning strategies, as their maximum attainable performance can vary. Instead of a percentage of individual strategy's max performance, $\tau$ is set as the maximum training accuracy achieved by *any* strategy at full capacity after one epoch of training. This aims to isolate learnability of the reasoning structure, avoiding overfitting. Robustness to threshold choice is also evaluated.

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:**
 - **GSM8K:** The training split of the GSM8K dataset (grade-school math word problems) is used for fine-tuning.
 - **Evaluation:** Models are evaluated on the GSM8K in-domain test split and several out-of-distribution (OOD) stress tests: GSM-Symbolic (3 variants, testing robustness to phrasing), GSM-IC (irrelevant sentences), and GSM-Hard (complex arithmetic). Overall performance is the geometric mean across all six splits.
 - **Reasoning Gym:** For generalization to diverse reasoning domains, tasks from Reasoning Gym (Stojanovski et al., 2025) are used, categorized into algorithmic and cognitive reasoning, with ID, hard OOD, and held-out OOD splits.
- **Reasoning Strategies:** Fourteen diverse reasoning strategies are evaluated, varying in length, structure, and generation method. Examples include:
 - **Baselines:** No CoT, No CoT with extra tokens.
 - **Natural Language CoT:** Very Short CoT, Short CoT, Short CoT with n Distractors (n=2, 4, 8), Gemma 27B CoT, Gemini CoT.
 - **Structured/Code-based:** Executed PoT (with actual program execution), Simulated PoT (internal code simulation), Plan and Solve (decomposition).
 - **Advanced:** Critical CoT, High Review Ratio CoT.
 - All strategies (except Gemini CoT) are generated by prompting an instruction-tuned Gemma-3 27B and filtering for correctness.
- **Student Models:** Gemma-3 base models (Gemma Team et al., 2025) of two sizes are used: 1B and 4B parameters.
- **LoRA Training Hyperparameters:** Consistent hyperparameters are used: AdamW optimizer, batch size 8. 1B model trained for 8,000 steps (LR $10^{-3}$), 4B model for 6,000 steps (LR $10^{-4}$). LoRA sweeps involve $k=20$ (1B) or $k=30$ (4B) configurations, uniformly distributed in log space from rank 1 (query/value projections) to full rank (all attention/MLP layers).
- **Baseline Metrics:** To compare against, the study also computes:
 - **Trajectory Length:** Average token length of CoTs.
 - **Token Perplexity:** Average token-level perplexity of CoT training data under the pretrained student model.
 - **Sequence KL Divergence:** Average sequence-level negative log-likelihood, representing the cost to encode training data.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Spearman rank correlation is used to assess the predictive power of each metric (intrinsic dimensionality, length, perplexity, KL divergence) with respect to the overall generalization performance (geometric mean of ID and OOD accuracies).

#### 3.3. Additional Analyses

- **Robustness to Threshold Selection:** The study examines how Spearman correlations change with different $\tau$ values (70%, 80%, 90% of epoch 1 train accuracy, and 90% of validation accuracy).
- **Effect of Training Data Correctness:** Mixed training sets (50% correct, 50% incorrect reasoning chains) are constructed for select strategies to analyze the impact on intrinsic dimensionality and generalization.
- **Generalization to Diverse Reasoning Domains:** Intrinsic dimensionality is assessed on Reasoning Gym tasks, covering algorithmic and cognitive reasoning categories, to determine if findings extend beyond math word problems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the relationship between intrinsic dimensionality (ID) of reasoning strategies and their impact on language model generalization.

#### 4.1. Intrinsic Dimensionality as a Predictor of Reasoning Effectiveness

- **Strong Correlation with Generalization:** On the GSM8K dataset, intrinsic dimensionality consistently exhibited a strong inverse correlation with overall generalization performance (geometric mean of in-distribution and out-of-distribution accuracies). For the Gemma-3 4B model, the Spearman rank correlation was 0.93. For the smaller Gemma-3 1B model, the correlation remained strong at 0.75, despite lower absolute performance, indicating the metric's consistency across model scales.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** Intrinsic dimensionality demonstrated substantially stronger predictive power compared to other baseline metrics. Trajectory length showed weak correlations (0.31 for 4B, 0.24 for 1B), consistent with prior conflicting evidence on its utility. Sequence KL Divergence also exhibited weak or negative correlations (-0.17 for 4B, -0.18 for 1B). Token perplexity, while showing a notable correlation (0.82 for 4B, 0.63 for 1B), was still lower than intrinsic dimensionality. This suggests that while likelihood under the base model is related, intrinsic dimensionality captures a more fundamental aspect of learnability.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Threshold Selection

- The findings regarding intrinsic dimensionality's predictive power were robust across various choices of the performance threshold ($\tau$) used for its computation. Spearman correlations remained consistently high (ranging from 0.72 to 0.94 for both 1B and 4B models) when using 70%, 80%, or 90% of epoch 1 training accuracy, or 90% of validation accuracy. This indicates that the relative ordering of reasoning strategies by their intrinsic dimensionality is stable and not an artifact of specific threshold hyperparameter tuning. The use of epoch 1 training accuracy for threshold determination also offers a practical advantage, reducing computational overhead by not requiring validation sets.

#### 4.3. Performance of Specific Reasoning Strategies

- **Executed PoT:** This strategy consistently achieved the lowest intrinsic dimensionality and the strongest out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization performance across both Gemma-3 1B and 4B models on GSM8K. For the 4B model, Executed PoT required only 1.49M parameters to reach the training threshold, while achieving 43.40% OOD accuracy. This suggests that code-based reasoning with execution provides a highly compressible and robust representation of the task.
- **Ineffective Strategies:** Strategies like "No CoT," "No CoT with extra tokens," and "Short CoT with distractors" exhibited considerably higher intrinsic dimensionality and lower generalization performance. This indicates that unstructured, noisy, or less logically coherent reasoning chains demand significantly more model capacity to learn.

#### 4.4. Model Size and Compressibility

- **Efficient Compression by Larger Models:** For effective reasoning strategies, the larger 4B model exhibited lower intrinsic dimensionality (relative to the task complexity and its absolute performance) compared to the 1B model. This suggests that larger models, despite their overparameterization, are more effective at compressing effective reasoning tasks into lower-dimensional representations.
- **Higher ID for Ineffective Strategies in Larger Models:** Conversely, for less effective or noisy reasoning strategies (e.g., "Very Short CoT" or those with distractors), the 4B model often showed disproportionately higher intrinsic dimensionality than the 1B model. This indicates that when the reasoning structure is poor or contains noise, larger models require substantially more parameters to fit these less compressible patterns, while smaller models might default to simpler, less parameter-intensive memorization strategies.

#### 4.5. Effect of Training Data Correctness

- Mixing incorrect reasoning chains (50% incorrect) into the training data significantly increased intrinsic dimensionality and decreased generalization performance for both "Short CoT" and "Gemma 27B CoT" strategies. For "Short CoT," ID increased over 500-fold with a 12.0 point drop in accuracy, while for "Gemma 27B CoT," ID increased approximately 125-fold with a 12.8 point drop. This supports the notion that correct and consistent reasoning provides a coherent, compressible mapping, whereas contradictory signals demand more capacity.

#### 4.6. Generalization to Diverse Reasoning Domains (Reasoning Gym)

- Intrinsic dimensionality's predictive power extended to tasks in Reasoning Gym across both algorithmic and cognitive reasoning categories.
 - For **algorithmic tasks**, Executed PoT again achieved the lowest ID (0.74M parameters) and the best overall performance (73.21%), mirroring the GSM8K results.
 - For **cognitive tasks**, "No CoT" achieved the lowest ID (0.40M parameters) and the best overall performance (49.82%). This finding aligns with prior work suggesting that CoT provides limited or no benefit for certain knowledge-intensive or cognitive tasks, where direct answers are more efficient.
- Across both categories, intrinsic dimensionality consistently showed strong correlations (1.00 for both algorithmic and cognitive tasks in Table 4) with generalization performance, while KL divergence and length were less consistent predictors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the understanding and practical application of reasoning in Large Language Models.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **Principled Explanation for CoT Efficacy:** The study establishes a quantitative, theoretically grounded explanation for why Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning can improve generalization. It posits that effective reasoning chains reduce the intrinsic dimensionality of the task, thereby making the underlying input-output mapping more compressible. This finding grounds the effectiveness of CoT in information theory, specifically aligning with the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, which suggests that simpler, more compressed models generalize better.
- **Quantitative Metric for CoT Analysis:** Intrinsic dimensionality provides a quantifiable metric that outperforms heuristic-based measures (like chain length) and other distribution-based metrics (like KL divergence and token perplexity) in predicting generalization performance. This offers a more robust analytical tool for dissecting the mechanisms of reasoning.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact

- **Guidance for Reasoning Data Annotation:** The findings can inform how reasoning data should be collected or annotated. Strategies that yield lower intrinsic dimensionality for a given task are likely to be more effective for model training. This could lead to more efficient data curation processes, focusing on generating reasoning chains that simplify the learning objective.
- **Improved Model Alignment and Training Optimization:** Understanding which reasoning strategies lead to lower intrinsic dimensionality can guide the alignment of reasoning capabilities in LLMs. It suggests that training models on more compressible reasoning structures could lead to more efficient learning and better generalization, potentially informing the design of better regularizers or fine-tuning approaches.
- **Diagnostic Tool:** Intrinsic dimensionality can serve as a diagnostic tool for evaluating reasoning strategies. Researchers and practitioners can use this metric to assess the "learnability" of different CoT variants without necessarily relying solely on costly downstream test-time evaluations across various OOD datasets. This is particularly valuable given the observation that intrinsic dimensionality correlations remain strong even with thresholds derived solely from training accuracy after one epoch.
- **Understanding Model Scale and Task Complexity:** The results demonstrate how model size interacts with reasoning effectiveness. Larger models are shown to be better compressors of *effective* reasoning tasks but require disproportionately more capacity for *ineffective* or noisy reasoning, offering insights into model scaling laws in the context of reasoning.
- **Extensibility to Diverse Domains:** The observed consistency of intrinsic dimensionality's predictive power across mathematical and diverse algorithmic/cognitive reasoning tasks suggests its broad applicability, potentially extending its utility beyond specific problem types.

#### 5.3. Future Directions and Limitations

- While the method provides a robust analytical tool, the direct estimation of intrinsic dimensionality via LoRA sweeps can be computationally expensive. Future work could focus on developing more computationally tractable alternatives or proxies for identifying effective reasoning chains that still leverage the insights gained from this research.
- The study focuses on fine-tuning models given reasoning trajectories; future work could investigate its applicability in other post-training settings, such as reinforcement learning from human feedback.
- The paper notes that intrinsic dimensionality captures coherence across the *complete set* of trajectories, which could be an important distinction from token-level perplexity. Further investigation into this aspect could yield additional insights.
