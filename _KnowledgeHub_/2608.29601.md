# $\mathcal{N}_0$-Foundation: Towards the Age of Tactile Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> N0-Foundation is a tactile-centric stack for contact-rich robot manipulation: durable camera-based tactile fingers plus a handheld TacUMI collector, NeoData with over 30,000 hours and 1.4M synchronized visuo-tactile episodes across six embodiments and 450+ tasks, NeoForce which maps any sensor's raw gel image into dense three-axis force fields, and paired NeoReal and NeoSim benchmarks. Force conditioning lifts average real success from 26.5% to 32.5%.

## Problem

- Large manipulation corpora are vision-dominated, yet contact transitions, local force, friction and incipient slip stay weakly observable from external or wrist cameras.
- Tactile hardware is fragmented: optical gel, capacitive and fiber sensors emit device-specific formats, so demonstrations collected on one device cannot be pooled or reused on another.
- Existing visuo-tactile corpora sit at hundreds to tens of thousands of demonstrations, use one collection mode or one platform, and rarely document per-episode quality control.

## Method

- Tactile infrastructure: layered glass-elastomer-camera fingers plus N0-TacUMI, a handheld gripper with fisheye camera, infrared 6-DoF tracker and magnetic aperture encoder for robot-free collection.
- NeoData and its open 5,000-hour OpenNeoData subset merge teleoperation across five arms with TacUMI demonstrations under one action and observation schema, with automatic and human quality control.
- NeoForce learns a hardware-agnostic dense three-axis force field as the interchange format, trained by masked force reconstruction with contact-mask loss, teacher-student latent prediction and visual-tactile contrastive alignment.

## Results

- On NeoReal, conditioning the action expert on NeoForce features raises average success from 26.5% to 32.5% and progressive score from 38.1% to 47.5% over the vision-only backbone.
- Every tactile variant beats vision-only, and force-field conditioning outperforms raw tactile image conditioning (30.0%) and simple concatenation (27.5%), unlocking previously zero-success insertion tasks.
- On NeoSim's 12 tasks over 100 rollouts, the best policy averages 45.8%, dual-arm scores collapse (InternVLA-A1 23.8% to 1.0%), and one world action model scores 0.0%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policies gain from the physical contact state, not the sensor's appearance; a calibrated force field is a portable interface across heterogeneous tactile hardware.
> - Where tactile signal enters matters: feeding contact directly to the action-generating module beats concatenating it into the observation stream.
> - Sim and real rankings diverge sharply, so a policy strong on real contact-rich tasks can score zero under simulated contact dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence has increasingly focused on learning generalizable manipulation policies from large-scale demonstration data. Recent advancements in vision-language-action (VLA) models and world action models have demonstrated that scaling diverse multimodal demonstrations improves the generalization and robustness of visuomotor policies. Existing real-robot datasets range from single-embodiment teleoperated corpora to cross-embodiment collections. Portable collection systems, such as the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI), have further enabled scalable demonstration collection beyond fixed robot workcells, inspiring the development of embodiment-agnostic interfaces. Simultaneously, large-scale egocentric videos provide behavioral and semantic priors for embodied learning by observing human-object interactions.

Despite these developments, two significant limitations persist in the broader research landscape. First, most large-scale embodied datasets primarily rely on visual observations. However, vision alone is often insufficient for contact-rich manipulation tasks, which demand sensitivity to local force, friction, incipient slip, and contact transitions. These unobservable factors are critical for tasks such as deformable-object manipulation, precise insertion, delicate force control, and sustained surface interaction. Second, the tactile modality remains fragmented due to the heterogeneity of hardware-specific signal formats generated by various sensors (e.g., camera-based visuo-tactile sensors, capacitive arrays, fiber-based sensors). This fragmentation hinders the pooling of tactile demonstrations across different devices, complicating policy training and preventing tactile datasets and models from benefiting from scale.

N$_{0}$-Foundation addresses these limitations by establishing a tactile-centric foundation for embodied manipulation. It introduces an integrated paradigm that connects scalable tactile data infrastructure, large-scale multimodal data acquisition, unified tactile representation learning, and standardized policy evaluation. By focusing on integrating tactile perception, this work aims to extend the capabilities of embodied policies beyond what vision-only systems can achieve in fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation tasks. It contrasts with existing large visual corpora by adding dense tactile observations at a comparable scale and with existing tactile datasets by offering a larger episode volume, greater embodiment diversity, and a blend of real-robot and UMI demonstrations, all while striving for a unified tactile encoding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a comprehensive, tactile-centric foundation for embodied manipulation, thereby enabling policies to acquire fine-grained contact-rich manipulation capabilities that extend beyond the limitations of vision-only systems. This overarching goal is driven by two main motivations:

1. **Addressing the Insufficiency of Vision-Only Observations for Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Current large-scale embodied datasets are predominantly visual. While visual data has advanced general robot learning, it often proves inadequate for tasks requiring nuanced physical interaction. Critical information such as local force distribution, friction conditions, the onset of slip, and subtle contact transitions are often difficult or impossible to infer accurately from visual input alone. This limitation impacts a broad spectrum of manipulation challenges, including the handling of deformable objects, tasks demanding precise assembly or insertion, operations requiring delicate force control, and scenarios involving sustained interaction with surfaces. The motivation is to provide robots with a sensory modality that directly captures these essential physical contact states.

2. **Overcoming the Fragmentation and Heterogeneity of the Tactile Modality:** The diverse nature of existing tactile sensing hardware presents a significant barrier to scaling tactile intelligence. Different sensor designs (e.g., camera-based optical gels, capacitive arrays, piezoresistive skins) produce distinct signal formats, units, resolutions, and sensing geometries. This heterogeneity prevents tactile data collected on one device from being readily reused or pooled with data from another, thereby impeding the development of large-scale tactile datasets and generalizable tactile models. A key motivation is to develop a unified, hardware-agnostic representation that can abstract away device-specific appearances, allowing for direct comparison and joint training over pooled tactile data.

To achieve these objectives, N$_{0}$-Foundation is built upon four technical pillars, each representing a specific objective:

- **Develop a Scalable Tactile Infrastructure:** To engineer the underlying hardware and systems necessary for efficient, large-scale tactile data collection and practical deployment. This includes robust vision-based tactile sensors, a tactile-enabled Universal Manipulation Interface (N$_{0}$-TacUMI), and a synchronized visual-tactile data collection system compatible with multiple robot embodiments and N$_{0}$-TacUMI demonstrations.
- **Construct a Large-Scale, Cross-Embodiment Visuo-Tactile Dataset (NeoData):** To leverage the developed infrastructure to acquire a comprehensive dataset of synchronized visual and tactile demonstrations, capturing critical contact information across diverse tasks and robot platforms. A further objective is to release a substantial open-source subset to facilitate broader research.
- **Propose a Unified, Force-Based Tactile Representation (NeoForce):** To formulate a hardware-agnostic tactile representation based on dense three-axis force fields. This representation aims to convert heterogeneous tactile signals into a common physical space, enabling transferable tactile representations for downstream embodied policies.
- **Establish a Standardized Evaluation Protocol:** To create a comprehensive benchmark, combining real-world (NeoReal) and simulated (NeoSim) suites, for systematically evaluating tactile-enabled embodied models and assessing the effectiveness of tactile integration strategies in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

Collectively, these objectives aim to provide the foundational tools—hardware, data, representation, and evaluation—necessary to scale tactile perception in embodied AI and advance robot capabilities in fine-grained physical interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The N$_{0}$-Foundation paradigm integrates several components: a tactile sensing infrastructure, a large-scale multimodal dataset, a unified tactile representation learning model, and a comprehensive evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. Tactile Infrastructure

The underlying tactile infrastructure is designed for scalable data collection and deployment. It includes:
- **Vision-Based Tactile Sensor:** The sensor is a layered assembly comprising a wear-resistant glass panel, an elastic sensing layer with a textured pattern, an embedded RGB camera, and a control board. When contact occurs, the sensing layer deforms, and the camera captures the texture displacement as a tactile image (T$_t$). The objective is to recover the underlying force field (F$_t$) from T$_t$ by inverting the combined transduction and imaging processes.
- **N$_{0}$-TacUMI:** This is a human-operated handheld parallel gripper for robot-free data collection, adhering to the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) philosophy. It integrates two tactile sensors, a 160$^\circ$ fisheye camera for wrist view, an infrared tracker for 6-DoF pose, and a magnetic encoder for gripper aperture. It records synchronized wrist images, left and right tactile images, gripper aperture, and relative end-effector motion. Actions are represented as relative end-effector pose and gripper width, making them compatible with robot embodiments.
- **Synchronized Data Collection System:** This system supports data acquisition from both multiple robot embodiments (Franka Research 3, Piper, ARX X5, UR5e, Flexiv Rizon 4s) and N$_{0}$-TacUMI demonstrations. All six embodiments share aligned tactile sensing, visual observations, and action conventions to facilitate data integration.

#### 3.2. NeoData Dataset Construction

NeoData is a large-scale visuo-tactile robot learning dataset with over 30,000 hours of synchronized visual and tactile demonstrations across six embodiments and 450+ tasks, comprising 1.4 million episodes and billions of paired RGB and tactile frames.
- **Data Sources:** It combines real-robot teleoperation (physically grounded executions) and N$_{0}$-TacUMI demonstrations (scalable human-operated trajectories).
- **OpenNeoData:** A 5,000-hour open-source subset is released to facilitate research.
- **Data Format:** Episodes store synchronized sequences of observations and actions.
 - **Robot Observations:** Include external third-person RGB image (I$_{ext}$), wrist RGB image (I$_{wrist}$), and left/right tactile images (T$^l_t$, T$^r_t$) at 640x360 resolution.
 - **Robot Actions:** Comprise commanded Cartesian end-effector motion ($\Delta$x$_{tcp}$) and target joint configuration (q$_{t+1}$), with rotations in continuous 6D representation.
 - **N$_{0}$-TacUMI Observations:** Include fisheye wrist image (I$_{fisheye}$) and left/right tactile images.
 - **N$_{0}$-TacUMI Actions:** Relative end-effector motion ($\Delta$x$_{t\rightarrow t+k}$) and target gripper aperture (g$_{t+k}$).
- **Data Curation and Preprocessing:** A five-stage pipeline ensures data quality:
 1. **Data Completeness:** Verifies presence of all required streams.
 2. **Motion Quality:** Screens for trajectory smoothness, removing abrupt tracking pulses or lost segments.
 3. **Episode Quality:** Filters episodes with abnormal durations or incomplete tasks based on statistical ranges.
 4. **Video Quality:** Validates visual streams for corruption, blur, dropped frames, and abnormal illumination, aligning asynchronous modalities.
 5. **Transfer Feasibility:** For N$_{0}$-TacUMI data, it checks inverse kinematics solvability and adherence to joint/workspace limits on target robots.
- **Data Annotation:** A semi-automatic hierarchical annotation pipeline assigns a four-level hierarchy to each episode: task (L3), subtask (L2), action (L1), and atomic segment (L0). A Vision-Language Model (VLM) generates time-independent task templates (verified by human experts). Each episode is then segmented by fusing action-based (kinematics, gripper events, tactile signals) and visual event boundaries (unsupervised Generic Event Boundary Detection). The VLM labels these fixed intervals according to the verified template, ensuring temporal accuracy.

#### 3.3. NeoForce: Unified Force-Based Tactile Representation

NeoForce learns a temporally structured visuo-tactile representation from force fields.
- **Motivation and Principles:** Addresses tactile fragmentation by grounding the representation in physical force rather than device appearance. It aims for:
 1. **Physical Grounding:** Encoding the intrinsic physical effect of contact.
 2. **Cross-Sensor Unification:** Creating a common space for different tactile devices.
 3. **Spatially Rich Semantics:** Preserving contact patch location, shape, extent, and intensity distribution.
- **Definition:** Tactile observations are described as dense three-axis force fields (F$_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$) over the sensing surface, with two tangential (f$_x$, f$_y$) and one normal (f$_z$) component per spatial location (u,v).
- **Implementation of Force Field Conversion:** A model (GoogLeNet backbone) learns a dense mapping g$_\theta$(T$_t$) from raw tactile images to estimated force fields. This converter is trained on a mixture of:
 - **Real Force Calibration Data:** Produced by a robot arm pressing an indenter against the tactile surface with a 6-axis force/torque sensor providing reference load under quasi-static conditions, covering various depths and tilt angles.
 - **Tactile Simulation Augmentation:** Simulated visuo-tactile data from Abaqus finite element solver, using relative contact position, material, and geometric parameters to obtain force and displacement fields. The displacement field deforms a particle texture, generating image pairs (I$_{ori}$, I$_{def}$) and pixel-aligned force fields (F) for diverse indenter shapes.
- **NeoForce Model Architecture:** Receives synchronized chunks of RGB observations and tactile force fields. Each modality is embedded into patch tokens and fused by a shared transformer backbone (ViT-B from DINOv2). It is jointly supervised by:
 - **Reconstruction Loss (L$_{recons}$):** Predicts force fields and contact masks using Huber norm for force and L$_{mask}$ for segmentation.
 - **Latent Prediction Loss (L$_{pred}$):** Predicts teacher latent outputs (from an exponential moving average teacher model) for visual, tactile, cross-modal (aligning modalities by forcing each to be recoverable from the other), and masked patch latents.
 - **Contrastive Alignment Term (L$_{con}$):** Between visual and tactile embeddings.

#### 3.4. Standardized Evaluation Protocol

A comprehensive benchmark is proposed for systematic evaluation of tactile embodied models.
- **NeoReal:** Comprises 10 real-world contact-rich tasks (e.g., Cardboard Box Folding, Bag Packing, Socket Plugging, Towel Folding) executed on physical robots equipped with tactile fingers. Tasks cover deformable-object shaping, force-guided mating, delicate grasping, and bimanual folding. It uses a standardized initial state distribution, reset protocol, binary success criterion, and a progressive score for partial completion.
- **NeoSim:** Contains 12 simulated contact-rich tasks (4 single-arm, 8 dual-arm) built on the UniVTAC/TacEx simulation platform. It performs high-precision tactile data collection in simulation and renders per-contact force fields in the same format as NeoForce, allowing for reproducible, large-scale evaluation of force-based tactile inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from evaluations of the NeoForce tactile representation and tactile-aware policies on the NeoReal and NeoSim benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Evaluations on Tactile Representation (NeoForce)

**Finding 1: Latent prediction enhances force estimation and representation space quality.**
An ablation study of the NeoForce training objective on force field reconstruction demonstrated that adding latent prediction to a reconstruction-only objective improves the quantitative accuracy of force estimation. The reconstruction-only objective achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.070 and a Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of 0.095, with a contact region overlap (mIoU) of 0.968. Introducing latent prediction reduced the MAE to 0.066 and the RMSE to 0.089, while the mIoU remained stable at 0.966. This improvement is primarily concentrated in the magnitude of the reconstructed force rather than contact localization. The latent prediction objective, particularly through masked patch-level and cross-modal latent prediction, compels the model to infer contact at unobserved locations, leveraging surrounding contact area, neighboring frames, and visual context. This process refines the representation by enabling it to convey contact intensity distribution across space and time more precisely, with a large-scale visual prior contributing to the estimation.

**Finding 2: Force grounding yields a tactile representation consistent across diverse tasks.**
Visualizations of NeoForce's reconstructed force fields across various contact behaviors (press, twist, grasp, lift, hold) indicated that the representation consistently captures the properties of interaction, including force intensity, contact patch geometry, and spatial extent. For instance, press, twist, and grasp resulted in localized peaks, while lift and hold produced broader, sustained distributions. The reconstructed fields accurately tracked the fine-grained intensity of the ground truth over a wide dynamic range. This consistency is attributed to the choice of force as the representation space, which describes the physical effect of contact independently of the specific sensor or behavior. This approach enables a stable carrier of tactile interaction, generalizing over various manipulation behaviors.

#### 4.2. Evaluations on Tactile-aware Manipulation Tasks

##### 4.2.1. NeoReal (Real-World Benchmark)

The NeoReal benchmark, consisting of 10 real-world contact-rich tasks, revealed the following:
- **Policy Performance:** Tasks on NeoReal are challenging under real contact dynamics. Among the evaluated policies (π$_{0.5}$, LingBot-VA, Fast-WAM), π$_{0.5}$ achieved the highest average success rate (26.5%) and progressive score (38.1%). It demonstrated relative strength in tasks requiring sustained contact and force guidance, such as Board Wiping, Cup Stacking, and Socket Plugging. LingBot-VA and Fast-WAM trailed, solving only a subset of tasks.
- **Progressive Score Insights:** The progressive score, which credits partial completion of task milestones, consistently exceeded the binary success rate for all policies. The gap was particularly wide in contact-rich tasks (e.g., Socket Plugging: 60% success vs. 73.5% progressive; Bag Packing: 20% success vs. 43.0% progressive). This indicates that policies often achieved intermediate goals like grasping and alignment even when failing to complete the entire task. The results highlight that policies relying primarily on visual observations and proprioceptive state are limited in fine and precise manipulation due to a lack of direct contact state information for perception and real-time action correction.
- **Impact of Tactile Integration Strategy:** An analysis using π$_{0.5}$ as a backbone compared different tactile integration strategies: no tactile input, raw tactile image concatenation, raw tactile image conditioning for the action expert, and NeoForce force representation conditioning for the action expert.
 - All tactile variants surpassed the vision-only policy, confirming the benefit of contact information for contact-rich tasks.
 - Conditioning the action expert with raw tactile images was more effective than simple concatenation.
 - The NeoForce force representation further improved performance over raw tactile images, increasing the average progressive score from 38.1% (no tactile) to 47.5%. This indicates that representing contact as a compact, physically interpretable force field is more effective for policies than using device-specific raw tactile images. The physical contact representation is more beneficial than the device-specific appearance.

##### 4.2.2. NeoSim (Simulated Benchmark)

The NeoSim benchmark, comprising 12 contact-rich simulated tasks, provided reproducible evaluation:
- **Policy Performance:** π$_{0.5}$ led with a mean success rate of 45.8%, followed by LingBot-VA at 32.1%. Other policies like Xiaomi-Robotics-0 and StarVLA-α achieved approximately 23% success, while Fast-WAM failed on all tasks despite its performance on NeoReal, suggesting potential sim-to-real transfer challenges for its specific competence.
- **Single-Arm vs. Dual-Arm Tasks:** Dual-arm tasks proved consistently more difficult than single-arm tasks for all policies. Most policies experienced a significant performance drop, with tasks demanding sustained mutual contact (e.g., Insert Screw, Place Gears, Cup Handover) remaining largely unsolved (best score 18% on Place Gears, 25% on Cup Handover).
- **Bimanual Contact Bottleneck:** The results exposed contact-rich bimanual manipulation as a major bottleneck for current policies that primarily rely on visual observations. These observations provide limited information about grasp security or stable contact maintenance. The findings reinforce the necessity of tactile signals to make grasp security and contact stability directly observable, which is crucial for bimanual manipulation challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

N$_{0}$-Foundation represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied intelligence and robotic manipulation by comprehensively addressing critical limitations in current approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Gap in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The primary impact is to move beyond the limitations of vision-only systems in tasks requiring nuanced physical interaction. By integrating tactile sensing, N$_{0}$-Foundation enables policies to perceive and respond to local forces, friction, and incipient slip, which are essential for deformable-object manipulation, precise assembly, and delicate force control. This directly translates to robots that can perform a wider array of complex, real-world tasks with greater dexterity and robustness.

- **Scalable and Unified Tactile Data:** The creation of NeoData, a large-scale, cross-embodiment visuo-tactile dataset, is a foundational step. At over 30,000 hours of interaction, it offers an unprecedented scale of synchronized multimodal data. The dual collection strategy, combining real-robot teleoperation with the scalable N$_{0}$-TacUMI demonstrations, ensures both physical grounding and broad coverage. The release of OpenNeoData further democratizes access to this crucial resource, accelerating research and development across the tactile AI community.

- **Hardware-Agnostic Tactile Representation:** The NeoForce model, which unifies heterogeneous tactile signals into a dense three-axis force field representation, is a pivotal technical contribution. This hardware-agnostic approach overcomes the fragmentation inherent in diverse tactile sensor designs. By providing a transferable physical interface, NeoForce enables the pooling of data from different sensors and facilitates the development of generalizable tactile policies, a major step towards universal tactile perception.

- **Standardized Evaluation for Tactile AI:** The introduction of the NeoReal and NeoSim benchmark suites provides critical tools for systematic evaluation. NeoReal offers a real-world testing ground for assessing physical performance, while NeoSim provides a reproducible environment for large-scale analysis in simulation. The inclusion of a progressive score offers finer-grained insights into policy competence beyond binary success, fostering a more detailed understanding of robot learning progress in contact-rich scenarios. These benchmarks are essential for tracking advancements and comparing different tactile-aware models.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** N$_{0}$-Foundation provides an integrated ecosystem—hardware, data, representation, and evaluation—that serves as a robust platform for future work. It encourages researchers to explore advanced tactile-enabled embodied policies, refine tactile representation learning, and investigate novel ways to bridge the sim-to-real gap for contact dynamics. The paper explicitly identifies extending the unified representation to dexterous hands and non-camera-based transducers, and scaling policy learning to the full NeoData corpus, as natural next steps, indicating its role as an ongoing catalyst for innovation.

In summary, N$_{0}$-Foundation establishes a comprehensive resource that directly tackles long-standing challenges in robot manipulation. Its integrated approach holds the potential to significantly advance the development of more capable, intelligent, and versatile robots that can interact with their physical environment with human-like finesse and sensitivity.
