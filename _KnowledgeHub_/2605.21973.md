# Foresee-to-Ground: From Predictive Temporal Perception to Evidence-Driven Reasoning for Video Temporal Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> The Foresee-to-Ground (F2G) framework reformulates video temporal grounding into an "Identify-then-Measure" problem, moving beyond direct timestamp regression by using explicit, citable event evidence. This method enhances prediction stability and accuracy, particularly at stricter IoU thresholds, and reduces variance across various Video-LLM backbones.

## Problem

- Current Video-LLM based temporal grounding often uses direct timestamp regression, leading to numerically brittle predictions and inconsistent temporal boundaries due to the architectural mismatch between discrete LLM tokens and continuous time.
- Existing black-box regression approaches for temporal grounding lack verifiability and interpretability, making it difficult to ascertain the basis for specific temporal predictions.
- Direct timestamp generation typically results in high variance and instability in predictions, especially under repeated inferences from the same input.

## Method

- Reformulate Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) as a verifiable "Identify-then-Measure" problem, separating the identification of an event from the precise measurement of its temporal boundaries.
- Implement a three-stage training curriculum: Predictive Temporal Perception for learning boundary-sensitive features, Proposal Warm-up for constructing a compact evidence pool of candidate segments, and Evidence-Driven Identify-then-Measure Fine-tuning.
- Enable the Video-LLM to explicitly cite one discrete event hypothesis from the evidence pool and then generate precise metric intervals and answers conditioned on the selected evidence, ensuring verifiability.

## Results

- Achieved improved grounding accuracy across benchmarks, with F2G surpassing conventional fine-tuning on Charades-STA (R@0.7 of 25.7 vs 21.6) and ActivityNet-Captions (R@0.7 of 28.4 vs 21.7), showing more pronounced gains at stricter IoU thresholds.
- Demonstrated robust transferability across different Video-LLM backbones (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B), consistently improving VTG performance on various datasets.
- Significantly reduced prediction variance and instability during repeated inferences, concentrating the IoU distribution closer to zero deviation and preserving the general video understanding capabilities of the underlying Video-LLM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling event identification from precise boundary measurement, akin to human cognitive processes, leads to more stable and verifiable temporal grounding predictions.
> - Learning dynamics-aware, boundary-sensitive temporal representations through predictive pretraining is crucial for constructing a high-quality and discriminative evidence pool of candidate event segments.
> - Explicitly supervising the LLM to commit to a discrete identifier from an evidence pool before generating precise timestamps is critical for significantly improving grounding accuracy and reducing prediction stochasticity.
