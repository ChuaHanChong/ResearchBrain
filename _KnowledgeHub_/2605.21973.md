# Foresee-to-Ground: From Predictive Temporal Perception to Evidence-Driven Reasoning for Video Temporal Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> The Foresee-to-Ground (F2G) framework reformulates video temporal grounding into an "Identify-then-Measure" problem, moving beyond direct timestamp regression by using explicit, citable event evidence. This method enhances prediction stability and accuracy, particularly at stricter IoU thresholds, and reduces variance across various Video-LLM backbones.

## Problem

- Current Video-LLM based temporal grounding often uses direct timestamp regression, leading to numerically brittle predictions and inconsistent temporal boundaries due to the architectural mismatch between discrete LLM tokens and continuous time.
- Existing black-box regression approaches for temporal grounding lack verifiability and interpretability, making it difficult to ascertain the basis for specific temporal predictions.
- Direct timestamp generation typically results in high variance and instability in predictions, especially under repeated inferences from the same input.

## Method

- Reformulate Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) as a verifiable "Identify-then-Measure" problem, separating the identification of an event from the precise measurement of its temporal boundaries.
- Implement a three-stage training curriculum: Predictive Temporal Perception for learning boundary-sensitive features, Proposal Warm-up for constructing a compact evidence pool of candidate segments, and Evidence-Driven Identify-then-Measure Fine-tuning.
- Enable the Video-LLM to explicitly cite one discrete event hypothesis from the evidence pool and then generate precise metric intervals and answers conditioned on the selected evidence, ensuring verifiability.

## Results

- Achieved improved grounding accuracy across benchmarks, with F2G surpassing conventional fine-tuning on Charades-STA (R@0.7 of 25.7 vs 21.6) and ActivityNet-Captions (R@0.7 of 28.4 vs 21.7), showing more pronounced gains at stricter IoU thresholds.
- Demonstrated robust transferability across different Video-LLM backbones (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B), consistently improving VTG performance on various datasets.
- Significantly reduced prediction variance and instability during repeated inferences, concentrating the IoU distribution closer to zero deviation and preserving the general video understanding capabilities of the underlying Video-LLM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling event identification from precise boundary measurement, akin to human cognitive processes, leads to more stable and verifiable temporal grounding predictions.
> - Learning dynamics-aware, boundary-sensitive temporal representations through predictive pretraining is crucial for constructing a high-quality and discriminative evidence pool of candidate event segments.
> - Explicitly supervising the LLM to commit to a discrete identifier from an evidence pool before generating precise timestamps is critical for significantly improving grounding accuracy and reducing prediction stochasticity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) is a fundamental task in long-form video understanding, aiming to align open-vocabulary natural language queries with specific temporal segments within an untrimmed video. Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have led to the development of Video-LLMs, which are increasingly adopted as backbones for VTG due to their open-vocabulary generalization and unified instruction-following capabilities.

However, the prevailing paradigm in Video-LLM-based VTG often involves treating temporal grounding as a direct timestamp regression problem. This approach typically maps flattened visual tokens from a video stream to continuous time coordinates within the LLM's discrete representation space. This architectural mismatch can lead to several challenges, including numerically brittle predictions, inconsistent temporal boundaries, and high variance in results.

Existing methods attempt to mitigate these issues through two main strategies:
1. **Timestamp tokenization and injection:** These methods discretize time into bins or tokens, or inject explicit temporal cues (e.g., timestamps, visual indices) into the LLM input. While these approaches improve the time-to-text interface, the underlying model still performs a direct mapping from a visual stream to time.
2. **Segment-level structural bias:** This category includes techniques like event-structured decoding, segment-level fine-tuning, and coarse-to-fine multi-pass prompting. These methods introduce some structural understanding, but intermediate segment hypotheses may not always be explicit, directly supervised, or attributable, potentially increasing inference costs.

This work addresses the limitations of direct timestamp regression by proposing a framework that reformulates VTG into a verifiable "Identify-then-Measure" problem. This formulation is inspired by human cognitive processes, where an event is first identified, and then its precise boundaries are refined. The research aims to move beyond black-box regression towards a structured grounding process that uses explicit evidence selection to guide boundary refinement, thereby enhancing the stability and verifiability of temporal grounding predictions in Video-LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
- Develop a framework, Foresee-to-Ground (F2G), that reformulates Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) as a verifiable "Identify-then-Measure" problem, moving away from direct timestamp regression.
- Integrate Predictive Temporal Perception to learn boundary-sensitive temporal representations and construct a video-wide evidence pool of candidate event segments.
- Implement Evidence-Driven Reasoning to enable a Video-LLM to identify the most relevant event hypothesis from the evidence pool and subsequently measure precise temporal boundaries based on the selected evidence.
- Enhance the stability and accuracy of temporal grounding predictions in Video-LLMs, particularly at stricter Intersection over Union (IoU) thresholds, and reduce prediction variance across repeated inferences.
- Demonstrate the transferability of the proposed approach across various Video-LLM backbones and its ability to preserve general video understanding capabilities.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the observed limitations of current Video-LLM approaches to VTG. Existing methods typically treat VTG as a direct timestamp regression task, generating numerical timestamps from an unstructured visual-token stream. This approach has several drawbacks:
- **Architectural Misalignment:** LLMs operate in a discrete token space, which is fundamentally misaligned with the task of generating continuous metric time coordinates. This can lead to numerically brittle predictions and inconsistent temporal boundaries.
- **Lack of Verifiability:** The black-box nature of direct regression makes it difficult to ascertain the basis for a particular temporal prediction, hindering verifiability and interpretability.
- **Cognitive Incongruence:** Human temporal grounding does not involve direct mapping of a continuous video stream to metric time. Instead, humans typically identify an event segment first and then refine its precise temporal extent. This cognitive process is not well-reflected in current Video-LLM architectures for VTG.
- **Instability and High Variance:** Direct timestamp generation often results in high variance and instability, particularly under repeated inference, due to the unconstrained nature of predicting continuous values from discrete representations.

By addressing these motivations, F2G aims to create a more robust, accurate, and verifiable framework for video temporal grounding that better aligns with human-like reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Foresee-to-Ground (F2G) reformulates VTG as a verifiable structured prediction problem, conceptualized as an "Identify-then-Measure" process. This involves a factorization where an event index `z` is first identified from a pool of candidates, and then the answer and precise timestamps are generated conditioned on that identified event. The framework comprises a three-stage training curriculum: Predictive Temporal Perception pretraining, Proposal Warm-up, and Evidence-Driven Identify-then-Measure Fine-tuning.

**Overall Framework:**
The problem is factored as:
$p(A, T, z | V, Q, S_K(V)) = p(z | V, Q, S_K(V)) \cdot p(A, T | z, V, Q, S_K(V))$
where `V` is the video, `Q` is the query, `T = (t_st, t_ed)` is the metric interval, `A` is the answer, and `z ∈ {1..., K}` indexes a candidate segment from a compact, video-wide evidence pool `S_K(V)`.

**Stage 1: Predictive Temporal Perception: Dynamics-Driven Event Discovery**
This stage pretrains a temporal module (`f_θ`) to generate boundary-sensitive representations suitable for event discovery in untrimmed videos.
- **Input:** Video `V` processed by a vision encoder and projector to obtain spatially pooled temporal sequence `X ∈ R^(N×D)`.
- **Objective:** Learn dynamics-aware features by predicting global temporal dynamics from local views.
 - Multiple views (global `X_g` and local `X_l^(v)`) are constructed from `X`.
 - A shared temporal module `f_θ` processes these views to produce latents `U_g` and `U_l^(v)`.
 - A lightweight predictor `g_ϕ` maps each local-view latent `U_l^(v)` (augmented with a view embedding `e_v`) to the global target latent `sg(U_g)` (stop-gradient). This forms the predictive loss `L_pred`.
- **Regularization:** A sliced isotropic Gaussian regularizer (SIGReg), `L_SIG`, is applied to stabilize the latent geometry and improve optimization stability by encouraging projected latent samples to match a standard normal distribution.
- **Overall Stage-1 Objective:** `L_S1 = (1 − λ)L_pred + λL_SIG`.
After Stage-1, `g_ϕ` is discarded, and `f_θ` generates `U = f_θ(X)` for subsequent stages.

**Stage 2: Proposal Warm-up and Evidence Pool Construction**
This stage trains a lightweight proposal head (`h_ψ`) to extract a compact, ranked set of candidate event segments from the boundary-sensitive temporal features `U`.
- **Proposal Head:** `h_ψ` consists of a multi-head self-attention stack followed by an MLP for regression of normalized segment `(˜τ_st, ˜τ_ed)` and an objectness score `˜c`.
- **Top-K Selection:** From the dense predictions `˜S_N(V)`, the Top-K candidates are selected based on their objectness scores, forming `˜S_K(V)`.
- **Supervision:** `h_ψ` is warmed up using a query-agnostic loss `L_S2 = L_reg + ηL_score`, where `L_reg` is a standard regression loss against ground-truth intervals and `L_score` uses IoU-derived targets for objectness.
- **Span Evidence Encoder (SEE):** For each Top-K candidate interval `T_k`, the relevant temporal features `U_k` are cropped. A Q-Former-style SEE, with `M` learnable query tokens, aggregates `U_k` into fixed-length evidence tokens `P_k`.
- **Evidence Pool:** The final evidence pool `S_K(V)` is constructed, containing `K` units, each comprising a discrete citable ID `⟨Span_k⟩`, a coarse metric interval `T_k`, and the segment-local visual evidence `P_k`.

**Stage 3: Identify-then-Measure: Evidence-Driven Reasoning**
In this stage, the Video-LLM performs verifiable grounding by enforcing the Identify-then-Measure routine.
- **LLM Input:** The LLM receives the instruction, video tokens, and the serialized evidence pool `S_K(V)` (including span IDs, coarse intervals, and `P_k`).
- **Identification:** The LLM is supervised to explicitly cite exactly one identifier token `⟨Span_z⟩` from the pool, effectively selecting an event hypothesis.
- **Measurement:** Conditioned on the cited evidence `(T_z, P_z)`, the LLM then generates the final precise metric interval `T` and answer `A` within the same structured response.
- **Fine-tuning:** The LLM reasoning stack is fine-tuned using LoRA. The Span Evidence Encoder (SEE) is trained simultaneously.
- **Supervision:** Given a ground-truth interval `T*`, the citation target `z*` is assigned to the best-overlapping candidate in the pool (`argmax_k IoU(T_k, T*)`).
- **Overall Stage-3 Objective:** `L_S3 = L_LM + αL_id + βL_time`, where `L_LM` is the sequence cross-entropy, `L_id` supervises the cited identifier token, and `L_time` supervises the emitted numeric timestamp tokens. To ensure continued evidence quality, `f_θ` and `h_ψ` are also trained with a small learning rate using an additional lightweight proposal loss `L_reg` (`L_S3+`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of F2G was conducted on standard VTG benchmarks, including Charades-STA and ActivityNet Captions for moment retrieval, and QVHighlights for highlight detection. Experiments used Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct as the base model, with results on Charades-STA and QVHighlights primarily representing zero-shot transfer due to training data composition.

**Comparison with Prior VTG Methods:**
F2G (+F2G-FT) demonstrated improvements in grounding accuracy compared to both prior state-of-the-art Video-LLM VTG methods and a backbone-controlled Qwen3-VL-8B baseline fine-tuned conventionally (+FT).
- On Charades-STA (zero-shot), F2G achieved R@0.7 of 25.7 and mIoU of 47.2, surpassing Qwen3-VL-8B (+FT) which scored 21.6 R@0.7 and 42.9 mIoU.
- On ActivityNet-Captions, F2G achieved 28.4 R@0.7 and 45.7 mIoU, compared to 21.7 R@0.7 and 40.8 mIoU for Qwen3-VL-8B (+FT).
- On QVHighlights (zero-shot), F2G improved mAP to 29.7 and HIT@1 to 45.6, while Qwen3-VL-8B (+FT) achieved 24.6 mAP and 36.8 HIT@1.
The gains were more pronounced at stricter IoU thresholds (e.g., R@0.7), suggesting improved boundary sharpness from the evidence-conditioned Identify-then-Measure decoding.

**Effectiveness across Video-LLMs:**
F2G exhibited robust transferability across different Video-LLM backbones. Applying the +F2G-FT recipe to LLaVA-NeXT-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and Qwen3-VL-8B consistently improved VTG performance on both Charades-STA and ActivityNet-Captions. For example, on LLaVA-NeXT-7B, +F2G-FT improved R@0.7 from 7.9 to 12.5 on Charades-STA and from 6.5 to 12.4 on ActivityNet-Captions.

**Generalization to TimeLens-Bench:**
F2G was evaluated on the fine-grained VTG protocol of TimeLens-Bench by replacing Stage-3 instruction data with TimeLens-100K. F2G consistently improved over the Qwen3-VL-8B baseline and remained competitive with TimeLens-specialized models, even without RL-based post-training.

**Qualitative Results:**
Case studies illustrated F2G's Identify-then-Measure behavior. For stable visual content or rapid scene changes where baselines (Qwen3-VL +FT, TimeChat, VTimeLLM) showed drift, truncation, or over-extension, F2G was observed to first cite an appropriate evidence interval and then measure boundaries closer to the ground truth, improving temporal precision.

**Stability and Robustness:**
Evaluation of repeated inference on ActivityNet-Captions indicated that F2G reduced the stochasticity of direct timestamp decoding. F2G shifted the mean IoU distribution rightward and concentrated significantly more mass near |∆IoU| = 0, demonstrating higher accuracy with lower variance. Further analysis revealed F2G substantially reduced "stochastic-collapse" cases (where identical inputs yield success or failure depending on sampling noise), with remaining errors dominated by "consistent-miss" cases.

**Efficiency Overhead:**
F2G introduced a modest overhead. The serialization of Top-K candidates added approximately 100-200 tokens to the LLM context, which is minor compared to the video-token stream. The additional perception components (temporal module, proposal head, and SEE) contributed about 0.5B extra parameters to an 8B backbone and increased end-to-end inference latency by less than 5%.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Predictive Temporal Perception (Stage-1/2):** Removing SIGReg or skipping Stage-1 predictive pretraining consistently degraded performance, particularly at strict IoU thresholds (R@0.7), suggesting that dynamics-aware pretraining improved boundary fidelity. Replacing Stage-1 with an action-only Stage-2 partially recovered gains but did not fully substitute the transition cues learned in Stage-1.
- **Identify-then-Measure Fine-tuning (Stage-3):**
 - Removing the explicit ID citation (w/o Identify) resulted in a substantial performance drop, indicating that committing to a discrete candidate segment is crucial for stable and precise boundary generation.
 - Removing visual evidence tokens (`P_k`) while keeping candidate intervals (Interval-only evidence) further reduced accuracy, suggesting `P_k` provides discriminative segment content beyond coarse temporal priors.
 - Ablations on evidence budget (Top-K candidates, SEE queries) showed that reducing these parameters lowered strict-threshold accuracy, while increasing SEE beyond the default (4 queries) yielded diminishing returns.

**Mechanism Analysis:**
- PCA visualization of temporal representations showed that the prediction-trained temporal module reshaped the temporal trajectory into more coherent clusters consistent with event phases, facilitating span enumeration.
- Stage-wise diagnostics (Retrieve@K, Cited, Refined IoU) indicated that the proposal pool provided a strong coverage-oriented hypothesis space. The "Cited" performance closely tracked the best achievable "Retrieve@K," and "Refined" further improved over "Cited," demonstrating the effectiveness of supervised citation and evidence-conditioned measurement.
- Citation-gap analysis (difference between best achievable IoU in pool and IoU of cited span) showed that for most queries (87.8% on ActivityNet-Captions, 93.6% on Charades-STA), the model's citation was near-optimal given the pool. This implies that remaining errors are more often due to insufficient candidate-set coverage rather than mis-selection.

**Preserving General Video Understanding:**
F2G was evaluated on VideoMME, a benchmark for general video question answering, and matched the performance of the Qwen3-VL-8B baseline. This indicated that VTG-oriented fine-tuning with F2G did not compromise the backbone's general video understanding capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Foresee-to-Ground (F2G) framework represents a methodological contribution to video temporal grounding by re-conceptualizing the task as an "Identify-then-Measure" problem. Its significance lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Reliability and Verifiability:** By decoupling event identification from precise boundary measurement and introducing explicit, citable evidence units, F2G stabilizes grounding predictions and makes them verifiable. This departs from previous black-box regression approaches and offers a more transparent and auditable mechanism for understanding how temporal predictions are derived.
- **Improved Accuracy and Stability:** Experimental results demonstrate consistent improvements in grounding accuracy, particularly at stricter IoU thresholds, and a reduction in prediction variance across repeated inferences. This indicates a more robust system for fine-grained temporal localization.
- **Generalizability across Video-LLMs:** The framework is shown to transfer robustly across different Video-LLM backbones (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL), positioning it as a broadly applicable adaptation recipe rather than a backbone-specific modification.
- **Preservation of General Capabilities:** F2G achieves these VTG-specific improvements without degrading the general video understanding capabilities of the underlying Video-LLM, as evidenced by its performance on benchmarks like VideoMME.
- **Modular Design:** The three-stage training pipeline (Predictive Temporal Perception, Proposal Warm-up, Evidence-Driven Reasoning) allows for distinct optimization of perception and reasoning components, fostering a synergistic interaction. The boundary-sensitive temporal features and coverage-oriented evidence pool improve the quality of event hypotheses, while the supervised citation and refinement steps enhance LLM decision-making.

**Potential Impact:**
The principles introduced by F2G could have implications for various applications requiring reliable and precise video understanding:
- **Auditable Video Search and Navigation:** Improved grounding accuracy and verifiability could facilitate more precise video search results, allowing users to quickly locate specific moments based on natural language queries and understand the underlying evidence for the temporal boundary.
- **Educational Content Analysis:** In educational videos, F2G could enable fine-grained navigation to key instructional segments, supporting more effective learning and content review.
- **Assistive Video Analysis and Media Review:** For tasks like content moderation, surveillance, or professional video editing, F2G could provide a more reliable tool for identifying and localizing events, potentially streamlining human-in-the-loop review processes.
- **Advancement of Long-Form Video Agents:** By offering a generalizable paradigm for structured temporal discovery and semantic reasoning, F2G contributes to the development of more capable and reliable AI agents for complex, long-form video analysis tasks.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The current F2G implementation focuses on standard visual VTG, where queries are typically mapped to a single primary temporal interval. Future work could extend the framework to:
- Handle complex queries involving multi-event reasoning, compositional temporal constraints, or multiple target spans. This might require hierarchical evidence selection or multi-span Identify-then-Measure capabilities.
- Integrate spatio-temporal grounding, where evidence units include not only temporal intervals but also object-level or region-level spatial support.
- Incorporate other modalities such as speech, sound events, and environmental audio to enable audio-visual multimodal grounding.

The core principle of making intermediate evidence explicit, citable, and verifiable is suggested as a guiding direction for these extensions. The authors also note that real-world deployment, especially in privacy-sensitive contexts, should be accompanied by appropriate data licenses, privacy safeguards, and human oversight.
