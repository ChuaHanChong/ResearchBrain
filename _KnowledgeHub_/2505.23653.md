# How do Transformers Learn Implicit Reasoning?

> [!summary] Summary
> Ye et al. from Tsinghua University, Peking University, and Siemens AG investigate how Transformers acquire implicit multi-hop reasoning by training them from scratch in a controlled symbolic environment. They identify a three-stage generalization trajectory, showing that successful reasoning correlates with the consistent cosine-space clustering of intermediate entity representations, while challenging the assumption that functional internal states must be explicitly decodable.

## Problem

- Understanding the internal processes by which Large Language Models (LLMs) acquire and perform implicit multi-hop reasoning.
- Distinguishing genuine internal reasoning within LLMs from mere memorization or superficial shortcut learning.
- Addressing the limitations of existing research, which either lack control in pre-trained LLMs or sufficient mechanistic granularity in symbolic datasets.

## Method

- Training decoder-only Transformers from scratch in a highly controlled symbolic environment, extended with fine-grained query-level control and targeted omissions.
- Developing novel diagnostic tools: =='cross-query semantic patching'== to causally identify semantically transferable intermediate representations, and a =='cosine-based representational lens'== to analyze the structural consistency of internal representations.
- Systematically tracking model performance across distinct training phases and various ==in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD)== query types, including 2-hop and 3-hop reasoning.

## Results

- Models achieve robust generalization for first-hop OOD queries (Test-OI), which is explained by OOD-derived representations aligning with ID-based clusters, but consistently fail for second-hop OOD queries (Test-IO, Test-OO) due to a lack of query-level compositional supervision.
- Cross-query semantic patching successfully localized transferable intermediate entity representations primarily at the `r₁` token in middle layers of the Transformer, with patching success rates increasing as reasoning capabilities emerge.
- Intermediate representations corresponding to the same entity coalesce into tight clusters in cosine space (high ID Cohesion) during in-distribution generalization, and OOD-derived representations align with these clusters (high OOD Alignment) during cross-distribution generalization, indicating a representational unification mechanism for generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit reasoning emerges through a consistent three-phase generalization trajectory: memorization, in-distribution generalization ('grokking'), and a newly identified cross-distribution generalization.
> - Successful reasoning, particularly generalization, is strongly correlated with the geometric regularity and consistent clustering of intermediate entity representations in cosine similarity space.
> - The explicit decodability of intermediate hidden states into tokens does not reliably indicate their functional role in reasoning; representations can be decodable even before reasoning capabilities emerge.
