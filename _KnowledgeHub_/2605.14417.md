# Before the Body Moves: Learning Anticipatory Joint Intent for Language-Conditioned Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The DAJI framework enables humanoid robots to follow natural language instructions by learning an anticipatory, dynamics-aligned joint-intent representation. This approach achieves a 94.42% rollout success rate and superior text-motion alignment on HumanML3D-style benchmarks, while also improving smoothness and stability in long-horizon streaming tasks.

## Problem

- Existing language-conditioned humanoid control systems often generate high-level kinematic references or abstract latent codes that lack explicit information about robot dynamics, contact forces, and balance requirements.
- Many robot control representations lack anticipatory information, failing to prepare for complex maneuvers requiring advance support shifts or momentum regulation, leading to less stable and natural motions.
- A need for an intermediate representation that is both executable by a closed-loop controller and explicitly aware of future physical transitions, especially for continuous streaming deployment.

## Method

- **DAJI** proposes a hierarchical framework utilizing a compact ==64D anticipatory joint-intent latent== that encodes future physical transitions and is directly executable by a closed-loop controller.
- The ==DAJI-Act module== (physical execution) is a low-level policy trained through ==student-driven in-loop distillation== from a ==future-aware privileged teacher==, decoding intents into high-frequency joint-position actions.
- The ==DAJI-Flow module== (semantic generation) is a ==language-conditioned flow-matching Diffusion Transformer== that generates streaming sequences of joint-intent latents, using ==scheduled self-conditioning== to mitigate exposure bias.

## Results

- DAJI-Act demonstrated the viability of the joint-intent latent as a real-time execution interface, achieving an **80.8%** rollout success rate with an average latency of **4.71 ms** (CPU).
- On HumanML3D-style benchmarks, DAJI achieved a **94.42%** rollout success rate and superior text-motion alignment, with the lowest **MM-D (1.093)** and highest **R@1 (0.549)** among non-ground truth methods.
- In streaming instruction following on BABEL, DAJI outperformed baselines with improved subsequence quality (**R@3 0.443**) and better boundary continuity (lower **Transition FID 0.236**, lower **Peak Jerk 0.115**) during instruction switches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An anticipatory, dynamics-aligned joint-intent latent effectively bridges the gap between high-level language commands and low-level robot control, enabling proactive physical preparation for movements.
> - Training the execution policy via in-loop distillation from a future-aware privileged teacher is critical for ensuring the learned intent space is inherently executable and aligned with complex humanoid dynamics and recovery behaviors.
> - Scheduled self-conditioning is crucial for robust autoregressive generation of intent sequences, mitigating exposure bias in streaming scenarios and ensuring long-term coherence and stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of language-conditioned control for humanoid robots, specifically focusing on generating streaming, whole-body motion. The broader landscape has seen progress in using natural language as an intuitive interface for robots, moving beyond teleoperation to allow non-expert users to specify complex behaviors.

Current approaches generally fall into two categories:
1. **Kinematic Reference Generation:** Many systems generate kinematic references, such as desired poses or trajectories, which a low-level tracker then attempts to execute. Examples include methods like Harmon, FRoM-W1, TextOp, and ECHO. While modular and effective for demonstrations, a key limitation identified is the reactive nature of the tracking policy. When the generated reference is inconsistent with the robot's current dynamic state—concerning contact timing, root momentum, support transitions, or balance recovery—the tracker must reactively compromise either tracking accuracy or physical stability. The generated reference remains an "external target" rather than an intrinsically executable command.
2. **Latent or Action-Based Policies:** Other approaches use latent spaces or directly predict actions, aiming for a tighter coupling between language, motion descriptors, and policies. Methods like LangWBC, RoboGhost, RoboForge, SENTINEL, UniAct, and MotionStreamer fall into this category. However, the paper posits that these latents are often treated as semantic skill codes, compact motion descriptors, or direct action-conditioning variables. They do not necessarily encode explicit preparatory behaviors for future physical transitions, making them potentially "myopic" in streaming deployment where each executed segment affects the robot's subsequent feasible state.

The identified gap is the lack of an intermediate representation that is both directly executable by a closed-loop controller *and* anticipatory of future physical state changes. This is particularly crucial for streaming contexts where continuous, stable, and coherent long-horizon motion is required, and successive generated segments are dynamically interdependent. This paper argues that the interface should mimic human motor control's anticipatory postural adjustments (APA), preparing the robot for upcoming balance shifts, contact changes, and task progression well in advance of the overt motion. DAJI proposes to fill this gap by introducing an anticipatory, dynamics-aligned joint-intent interface.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a hierarchical framework, DAJI (Dynamics-Aligned Joint Intent), that enables streaming language-conditioned whole-body control for humanoid robots. This framework aims to generate robot behavior that is semantically aligned with natural language instructions, physically executable, and extendable over long horizons.

The motivation for DAJI stems from several specific limitations observed in existing language-conditioned humanoid control systems:

1. **Mismatch between Language and Physical Execution:** Current systems often generate kinematic references that a low-level tracker must reactively repair. This creates a fundamental disconnect where the generated motion might be semantically plausible but dynamically inconsistent with the robot's real-time physical state (e.g., balance, contact forces, momentum). The authors are motivated to create a control representation that is *executable now* while also *anticipatory of future physical transitions*.
2. **Lack of Anticipatory Behavior:** Complex dynamic maneuvers, such as stepping, turning, or recovering balance, require preparatory actions—support shifts, angular momentum regulation, and postural adjustments—that must occur *before* the main motion is visibly executed. Existing latent or action policies often treat latents as skill codes or direct action variables without explicitly encoding these anticipatory requirements. Inspired by anticipatory postural adjustment (APA) in human motor control, a key motivation is to design an interface that explicitly incorporates this "future-aware" quality, encoding coarse future tendencies across contacts, balance shifts, and task progression.
3. **Need for a Unified, Dynamics-Aligned Interface:** The research seeks to define an intermediate representation, termed "anticipatory motor intent," that can serve as a robust interface between high-level language understanding and low-level closed-loop control. This interface must be compact, capable of guiding the robot under live proprioception, and inherently understand the dynamics of the humanoid. This addresses the problem of fragmented control where semantic planning is decoupled from the physical realities of execution.
4. **Enabling Long-Horizon Streaming Control:** In streaming deployment, each generated motion segment alters the robot's state, impacting subsequent feasible actions. Myopic generation, where segments are treated independently, can lead to instability and accumulation of errors over long horizons. A core motivation is to develop a system that can autoregressively generate coherent, stable, and physically aligned motion chunks over extended periods, adapting to new instructions while maintaining physical continuity.
5. **Robustness through In-Loop Training:** Existing imitation learning approaches often suffer from a train-test mismatch, where policies trained on teacher-generated data encounter different state distributions during their own rollouts. The motivation is to train the deployable policy *under its own closed-loop state distribution* through student-driven rollouts, thereby shaping the latent space around actual humanoid dynamics and recovery behaviors rather than solely relying on offline reconstruction.

In summary, the key objective is to establish an "anticipatory joint-intent" as the central interface for language-conditioned humanoid control, thereby improving physical executability, temporal coherence, and long-horizon stability in streaming scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DAJI is a hierarchical framework designed for streaming language-conditioned humanoid control, employing a novel "joint-intent" latent as the interface between semantic generation and closed-loop control. The framework comprises two main modules, DAJI-Act and DAJI-Flow, trained sequentially.

#### 3.1. Learning DAJI-Act: Executable Joint-Intent Policy

DAJI-Act is the low-level component responsible for decoding the learned joint-intent latents into high-frequency robot actions while leveraging live proprioception. It focuses on learning an executable and anticipatory latent space through interaction with a physics simulator.

1. **Future-Aware Teacher Training:**
 - A privileged teacher policy (𝜋_tea) is initially trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This teacher is designed to be "future-aware" by observing not only the current robot proprioception (𝐨_prop_𝑡) and privileged simulator-only information (𝐨_priv_𝑡) but crucially, also multi-horizon *future* reference information (𝐨_ref_𝑡). This future context includes target frames, allowing the teacher to learn control behaviors that anticipate upcoming transitions, support shifts, and balance demands.
 - The teacher is optimized against whole-body tracking and robustness rewards, ensuring it can generate stable and compliant motions.

2. **Student-Driven In-Loop Distillation for DAJI-Act:**
 - DAJI-Act, the student policy, is then trained to imitate the frozen, future-aware teacher. This distillation process is performed "in-loop," meaning DAJI-Act drives the simulator using its own generated actions, and the teacher provides target actions (𝐚_tea_𝑡) based on the states visited by the student. This mitigates exposure bias by training DAJI-Act under the state distribution it will encounter during deployment.
 - DAJI-Act consists of three main parts:
 - **Proprioceptive Encoder (𝐸_prop):** An MLP that encodes real-time deployable proprioceptive observations (𝐨_prop_𝑡).
 - **Stochastic Intent Encoder (𝐸_ref):** A training-only MLP that maps the multi-horizon reference information (𝐨_ref_𝑡) into a stochastic joint-intent distribution 𝑞_𝜓(𝐳_𝑡|𝐨_ref_𝑡) = 𝒩(𝝁_𝑡, diag(𝝈²_𝑡)). The joint-intent latent 𝐳_𝑡 is sampled from this distribution. This encoder compresses future-aware information into a compact, executable latent.
 - **Diffusion Action Head (𝒟_𝜙):** A lightweight denoiser, implemented as an MLP with adaptive layer normalization, that predicts the robot's joint-position actions. It takes a noised version of the teacher's action (𝐱_𝜏) and a control context 𝐜_𝑡 = [𝐸_prop(𝐨_prop_𝑡); 𝐳_𝑡] (concatenation of proprioceptive features and the sampled latent) to predict the clean teacher action.
 - **DAJI-Act Objective (ℒ_Act):** The training objective combines a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, minimizing the difference between the diffusion model's predicted action and the teacher's action (𝐚_tea_𝑡) on student-visited states, with a KL regularization term. The KL term encourages the stochastic intent distribution to stay close to a standard Gaussian, promoting a compact and smooth latent space suitable for generation.
 - **Post-Distillation:** After DAJI-Act training, the training-only intent encoder (𝐸_ref) is used offline to encode all reference motions from the dataset into sequences of joint-intent latents. These "text–intent trajectory pairs" serve as the training data for DAJI-Flow. At deployment, DAJI-Act only uses its proprioceptive encoder and diffusion action head, receiving generated latents from DAJI-Flow and real-time proprioception.

#### 3.2. Learning DAJI-Flow: Streaming Joint-Intent Generation

DAJI-Flow is the high-level module responsible for autoregressively generating sequences of joint-intent latents from natural language instructions.

1. **Architecture:** DAJI-Flow is a language-conditioned flow-matching DiT (Diffusion Transformer).
2. **Inputs and Conditioning:**
 - **Language Instruction (ℒ):** Processed by a frozen language encoder (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) to extract text features (𝐇_text). This encoding happens once per instruction or instruction switch.
 - **Recent Intent History:** A temporal encoder processes a compact encoding of the robot's recent executed joint-intent latent history to generate observation features (𝐇_obs).
 - **Combined Condition (𝐂):** The text features and observation features are concatenated to form the conditioning sequence for the flow-matching model (𝐂 = [𝐇_text; 𝐇_obs]).
3. **Flow-Matching Generation:** DAJI-Flow predicts the next clean latent chunk (𝐗_0 ∈ ℝ^(𝐻×𝑑_𝑧)), where 𝐻 is the chunk length (15 frames or 0.3 seconds). It is trained using a flow-matching objective (ℒ_Flow), which learns a velocity field 𝐯_𝜃 to transform a noisy sample (𝝐) into a target sample (𝐗_0) along a continuous path (𝐗_𝑠).
4. **Scheduled Self-Conditioning:** To address exposure bias inherent in autoregressive generation (where training occurs on ground-truth history but deployment relies on previously generated history), DAJI-Flow is trained with scheduled self-conditioning.
 - During training, the model initially conditions on ground-truth latent history (teacher-forced).
 - Over a specified curriculum, the training gradually incorporates previously *generated* (but detached) latent chunks into the history buffer for conditioning subsequent chunks. This exposes the generator to its own output distribution, improving robustness during long-horizon rollouts.
5. **Inference:** During deployment, DAJI-Flow iteratively predicts future intent chunks. The ODE for flow-matching is integrated using a few Euler steps to obtain a chunk, which is then appended to the latent history for the next prediction.

#### 3.3. Training and Deployment Overview

- **Training Stages:**
 1. **Stage 1:** Train the future-aware privileged teacher with PPO.
 2. **Stage 2:** Train DAJI-Act through student-driven in-loop distillation. Then, use the trained intent encoder offline to convert reference motions into latent trajectories for DAJI-Flow.
 3. **Stage 3:** Train DAJI-Flow using flow matching and scheduled self-conditioning on the constructed text–latent trajectory pairs, with the language encoder frozen.
- **Deployment:** DAJI-Flow generates low-frequency intent chunks from a language instruction and recent intent history. These chunks are then passed to DAJI-Act, which decodes each joint-intent latent (𝐳_𝑡) in real-time into high-frequency joint-position actions, conditioned on live proprioception. The two modules run asynchronously.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate DAJI across three axes: the viability of the latent as an execution interface, the impact of its anticipatory temporal structure, and the overall performance in single-instruction and streaming language-conditioned humanoid control.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Physical Hardware Deployment:** Qualitative deployments on physical humanoid hardware (Figure 4) demonstrate DAJI's capability to produce executable motions, including during streaming instruction switches and single-instruction generation.
- **Simulation Rollouts:** Visualizations in simulation (Figure 3) show that DAJI decodes generated joint-intent latents into continuous, whole-body humanoid motions, including dynamic and articulated behaviors.

#### 4.2. Execution Interface Validation (Table 1)

- The learned joint-intent latent is functional as a real-time control interface.
- The DAJI-Act policy (64D MLP variant) achieves a rollout success rate of 80.8% and maintains good local articulation accuracy and closed-loop stability, operating in real-time on a CPU with a latency of 4.71 ms.
- Reducing the latent dimensionality to 16D significantly degrades performance (49.5% success), indicating that the latent's efficacy is not a trivial consequence of a compact representation.
- Using a Transformer architecture for DAJI-Act (64D) increases latency (7.48 ms) without improving execution quality, supporting the MLP-based controller design.

#### 4.3. Main Benchmarks

- **HumanML3D-Style Robot Motion Generation (Table 2):**
 - DAJI demonstrates strong performance in generating semantically aligned robot motions with high executability. It achieves a rollout success rate of 94.42%, which is the highest among all non-ground truth (GT) methods.
 - It also outperforms other language-conditioned humanoid control methods across key text-motion metrics, including the lowest MM-D (1.093), highest R@1 (0.549), R@2 (0.711), R@3 (0.796), and MultiModality (0.847), and the lowest FID (0.147) among non-GT baselines. These metrics indicate superior language-motion alignment and generation quality.
 - The results are presented with a conservative comparison, as reference-based baselines utilize the same privileged teacher tracker as GT (Sim), while DAJI-Act is specific to DAJI's joint-intent latents. This suggests the improvements stem from DAJI's generation-control interface.

- **Streaming Instruction Following on BABEL (Table 3):**
 - DAJI's advantages are more pronounced in long-horizon streaming scenarios, where anticipatory intent is crucial for coherent instruction switches.
 - Compared to TextOp, the closest compatible streaming baseline, DAJI shows improvements in both subsequence quality (e.g., R@3 of 0.443 vs. 0.287, FID of 0.152 vs. 0.538) and transition continuity around instruction boundaries (e.g., Transition FID of 0.236 vs. 0.594, significantly lower Peak Jerk (PJ) of 0.115 vs. 0.231, and Mean Jerk (MJ) of 0.054 vs. 0.156). This indicates smoother and more stable transitions.

#### 4.4. Ablations and Design Analysis

- **Representation-level Latent Temporal Design (Table 4):**
 - The inclusion of future reference frames in latent construction is critical for anticipatory behavior. A "No-Future" latent results in significantly lower future-prediction ability (Probe@40 drops from 0.393 to 0.047) and drastically reduced long-horizon closed-loop success (60s success rate drops from 87% to 10%).
 - "Short-Future" performs better but still lags "Full DAJI" in coherence and long-horizon stability.
- **Generation-level Latent Temporal Design (Table 5):**
 - Consistent with the representation-level findings, "Full DAJI" (which includes short-horizon future offsets) provides the best balance of semantic alignment, generative predictability, and closed-loop executability for language-conditioned generation. Overly dense or distant future targets (e.g., "Dense-Future," "Long-Future") do not consistently improve or even degrade some generation metrics.
- **Generator Training Design (Table 6):**
 - Scheduled self-conditioning in DAJI-Flow improves language alignment (higher R@3) and contributes to a modest but consistent increase in rollout success rate, by reducing the train-test mismatch during autoregressive generation.
- **Text Encoder Ablation (Table 8):**
 - The choice of the frozen text encoder impacts performance. Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct yields the best overall results in terms of semantic alignment (MM-D, R-precision), FID, and rollout success, indicating its effectiveness in encoding language instructions for intent generation.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Table 18):**
 - The framework exhibits stability across reasonable ranges for self-conditioning depth and latent-history length, with K=8 chunks for both offering optimal performance tradeoffs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DAJI framework introduces a novel paradigm for language-conditioned humanoid control by establishing an "anticipatory joint-intent interface" as the central connection between high-level semantic generation and low-level closed-loop physical execution. This approach addresses several fundamental challenges in robotic motion generation and control.

**Key Contributions and Advancements:**

- **Anticipatory Control:** By incorporating multi-horizon future reference information into the learned joint-intent latent, DAJI moves beyond reactive control. This anticipatory capability, inspired by human motor control, enables the humanoid to prepare for future contact changes, balance shifts, and support transfers *before* the visible motion manifests, which is crucial for dynamic and complex behaviors.
- **Dynamics-Aligned Executability:** The use of student-driven in-loop distillation trains the DAJI-Act policy to decode intents into stable actions under its own rollout distribution. This ensures that the learned joint-intent is inherently "executable" and aligned with the robot's physical dynamics, rather than merely being a kinematic target that requires reactive repair.
- **Improved Long-Horizon Coherence and Stability:** In streaming scenarios, DAJI demonstrates superior performance in maintaining temporal coherence and stability during instruction following, particularly at instruction-switching boundaries. This is a significant advancement over methods that may generate locally plausible motions but struggle with long-term consistency and smooth transitions.
- **Hierarchical and Modular Framework:** DAJI effectively decouples semantic generation (DAJI-Flow, generating low-frequency intents) from high-frequency feedback control (DAJI-Act, decoding intents with live proprioception). This modularity allows for specialized optimization of each component while maintaining a unified, shared, and anticipatory representation.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The framework achieves competitive results on established benchmarks like HumanML3D and BABEL, showing improvements in language-motion alignment, motion quality, and, critically, rollout executability on a humanoid robot.

**Potential Impact:**

- **More Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** By making humanoid control more amenable to natural language commands, DAJI could significantly lower the barrier for non-expert users to program or guide complex robot behaviors. This could facilitate applications in assistive robotics, entertainment, and general-purpose service robots.
- **Robust Embodied AI:** The focus on anticipatory and dynamics-aligned control contributes to developing more robust and versatile embodied AI systems. Robots equipped with such capabilities would be better prepared to operate autonomously in dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Advanced Animation and Virtual Characters:** The principles of learning anticipatory, executable motion intents could also be applied to physics-based character animation, enabling more realistic and responsive virtual agents.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** DAJI's success in learning a dynamics-aligned, anticipatory interface provides a foundation for future work exploring richer context grounding (e.g., object interaction), multi-modal inputs, and interactive human-robot co-adaptation.

While the quantitative evaluations primarily occurred in simulation, with qualitative demonstrations on hardware, the results suggest a viable pathway toward more capable and intuitive language-controlled humanoids. Addressing the sim-to-real gap, improving dataset diversity, and integrating explicit safety constraints remain important future considerations for real-world deployment.
