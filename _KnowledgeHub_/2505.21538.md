# Caption This, Reason That: VLMs Caught in the Middle

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from McGill University, Mila, and Microsoft Research developed the Perception-Attention-Memory (PAM) and Composite Visual Reasoning (CVR) datasets to assess Vision-Language Models (VLMs) from a cognitive science perspective. The study determined that the main limitation in VLM reasoning, particularly for spatial tasks, is the integration of visual information into the reasoning process, which can be improved by an 18.31% average increase in location-based task accuracy using vision-text decoupling through self-captioning.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate persistent weaknesses in specific visual decision-making and reasoning tasks, such as spatial reasoning and multi-object understanding.
- Existing VLM benchmarks primarily assess high-level task performance and often fail to diagnose the root causes of failures by neglecting low-level cognitive abilities like Perception, Attention, and Memory.
- The underlying mechanisms of VLM struggles with complex visual reasoning are not well-understood, hindering the development of more robust and human-like artificial intelligence.

## Method

- Introduced the ==Perception-Attention-Memory (PAM) dataset== to systematically evaluate VLMs on isolated cognitive abilities, including fine-grained perception, selective attention, and information maintenance in memory.
- Developed the ==Composite Visual Reasoning (CVR) dataset==, consisting of procedurally generated, complex tasks that combine various cognitive abilities, to assess integrated reasoning.
- Implemented a ==vision-text decoupling paradigm==, using ground-truth or self-generated captions, to diagnose whether VLM reasoning bottlenecks stem from visual encoding or the integration of visual information into the language model component.

## Results

- GPT-4o demonstrated the highest performance across PAM and CVR tasks, often nearing human levels on category-based tasks, but showed notable gaps in spatial attention and memory tasks.
- Vision-text decoupling via self-captioning led to substantial performance gains, with an average **18.31%** improvement on location-based tasks, by effectively providing accurate visual information in a textual format to the LLM component.
- Targeted LoRA fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-VL-7B improved core cognitive abilities, especially for location-based PAM tasks, but these improvements translated to only modest gains on out-of-distribution benchmarks like MMBench and VQAv2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs, including top-performing proprietary models like GPT-4o, exhibit a pervasive and significant weakness in integrating spatial object information into their reasoning processes.
> - The primary bottleneck for VLM spatial reasoning lies in inadequate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training or visual-to-textual integration, as models can often describe visual information accurately but fail to leverage it during direct visual reasoning.
> - Self-captioning significantly mitigates spatial reasoning deficits, indicating that providing textual representations of visual content can unlock the underlying language model's reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as powerful AI systems, integrating the impressive reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) with visual understanding components like Vision Transformers (ViTs). These models have demonstrated remarkable progress in a wide array of vision-language tasks, including visual question answering, image captioning, and general scene description. Benchmarks such as InfoVQA, VQAv2, MMMU, MMBench, and MME are commonly used to assess VLM performance across diverse domains, from simple object recognition to complex scientific reasoning and mathematical problem-solving.

Despite their rapid advancements and high performance on many benchmarks, VLMs are known to exhibit persistent weaknesses in specific visual decision-making and reasoning tasks. Previous research has highlighted their struggles with multi-object reasoning, counting, visual binding, and particularly spatial reasoning. While existing benchmarks offer broad evaluations, many tend to focus on high-level task performance, often using abstract stimuli or specialized domains. Critically, these evaluations frequently "deviate from human cognitive science," where intelligence is deconstructed into core underlying faculties: Perception, Attention, and Memory. This paper posits that by not rigorously evaluating these low-level cognitive abilities and their interrelations, existing benchmarks fail to pinpoint the root causes of VLM failures in higher-level reasoning.

This work directly addresses this gap by adopting a cognitive science perspective. It aims to dissect VLM intelligence along these fundamental cognitive axes, moving beyond surface-level task performance to diagnose specific "cognitive" mechanisms that might be failing. By using procedurally generated, controlled visual stimuli (ShapeNet objects in controlled environments) that are more aligned with cognitive experiments, the authors aim to avoid issues of data representation biases often found in abstract stimuli. This approach allows for a systematic and granular understanding of *why* VLMs struggle with certain visual reasoning tasks, thereby complementing existing benchmarks and contributing to the development of more human-like AI. The paper positions itself as providing a more comprehensive cognitive evaluation, particularly focusing on the integration of these core abilities in complex reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically analyze the cognitive strengths and weaknesses of state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by drawing methodologies from cognitive science. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives:

1. **Systematic Cognitive Profiling:** To develop and utilize the Perception-Attention-Memory (PAM) and Composite Visual Reasoning (CVR) datasets for a systematic analysis of VLMs' cognitive profiles. This involves evaluating models across core cognitive axes: Perception (fine-grained sensory encoding), Attention (selecting goal-relevant inputs), and Memory (maintaining information despite distraction).
2. **Identification of Specific Weaknesses:** To pinpoint specific cognitive deficits in VLMs, particularly those related to spatial understanding (localization) and selective attention, which are critical for visual reasoning.
3. **Investigation of Reasoning Bottlenecks:** To explore the underlying causes of observed failures, specifically whether limitations stem from visual encoding, language-based reasoning, or, crucially, the integration of visual information into the reasoning process. This is pursued through a novel vision-text decoupling paradigm.
4. **Demonstration of Improvement Strategies:** To evaluate whether targeted fine-tuning on diverse cognitive reasoning tasks can enhance core cognitive abilities in VLMs, and whether these improvements generalize to broader visual reasoning performance on established benchmarks.
5. **Validation of Evaluation Framework:** To validate the effectiveness of the PAM and CVR evaluation datasets by demonstrating strong correlations between VLM performance on these tasks and their performance on widely recognized benchmarks like MMMU-Pro and VQAv2.

The **motivation** for this research stems from the recognized gap between the impressive general capabilities of VLMs and their persistent struggles in specific visual reasoning tasks. Traditional VLM evaluations often assess high-level reasoning without dissecting the underlying cognitive faculties. By adopting a cognitive science lens, the authors aim to move beyond merely observing *what* VLMs fail at, to understanding *which* "cognitive" mechanisms—Perception, Attention, or Memory—are at fault, and *how* these deficits manifest in complex reasoning tasks. This deeper understanding is crucial for developing more robust and human-like artificial intelligence. The paper explicitly aims to reveal key bottlenecks in simultaneous perception and reasoning and propose effective solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach involving specially designed datasets, evaluation of diverse VLM architectures, and targeted intervention strategies.

#### 3.1. Data Source and Generation

All cognitive tasks and fine-tuning data are generated using the `iWISDM` task environment. `iWISDM` enables the procedural generation of an effectively limitless number of vision-language decision-making tasks. This ensures a controlled environment for testing specific cognitive abilities and allows for scalability.
- **Objects and Scenes:** Tasks utilize 3D-rendered everyday objects from the `ShapeNet` dataset. There are 8 object categories (e.g., benches, boats, cars) with 8 unique objects per category. Objects are placed in one of four possible locations: top left, top right, bottom left, and bottom right. Backgrounds are uniformly colored. This controlled setting allows for precise measurement of low-level cognitive abilities, minimizing biases from naturalistic image complexity or underrepresentation in training data.

#### 3.2. Cognitive Tasks for Perception, Attention, & Memory (PAM)

The `PAM` dataset is manually designed to isolate core cognitive abilities:
- **Perception (Perc):** Assesses immediate access to visual object properties (e.g., category, spatial location) from one or more object frames.
- **Attention (Att):** Evaluates the ability to select task-relevant objects from distractors within individual frames. This includes:
 - **Spatial Attention:** Target specified by location (e.g., "top-right").
 - **Feature Attention:** Target specified by category (e.g., "chair").
- **Memory (Mem):** Measures the ability to retain and recall visual object information across irrelevant inputs (blank or distractor frames).
Each cognitive axis includes two task variants:
- **Report (R):** Requires reporting an object property.
- **Compare (C):** Requires comparing a property between two objects.
The property type is either **Location (Loc)** or **Category (Cat)**, leading to tasks like "Perc-Cat-R" (Perception Category Report) or "Mem-Loc-C" (Memory Location Compare).

#### 3.3. Composite Visual Reasoning (CVR) Tasks

The `CVR` dataset consists of complex, composite reasoning problems generated randomly using `iWISDM`'s AutoTask framework. These tasks involve various combinations of Perception, Attention, and Memory abilities. They are categorized into three levels of complexity: Low (L), Medium (M), and High (H).

#### 3.4. Vision-Language Models Evaluated

Seven state-of-the-art VLMs were tested:
- **Open-source:** InternVL2.5-8B, LLaVa-OneVision-7B, MiniCPM-V-2.6-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B (and its larger 72B variant).
- **Proprietary:** GPT-4o-Mini, GPT-4o.
Models were chosen to represent a range of sizes and capabilities. Proprietary models were evaluated via official APIs, while open-source models were hosted using `llama-factory` or Hugging Face Transformers.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Protocol

- **Prompting:** All models received a nearly identical task prompt template, which described the task, listed possible answers, included trial instructions and images, and suggested Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
- **Answer Extraction:** VLM responses were passed to a Qwen2.5-72B LLM, prompted to extract the final answer from a predefined list, for accuracy calculation.
- **Human Baselines:** Eight human subjects performed all 22 task types (5 trials each) to provide a comparative baseline for performance and response times.

#### 3.6. Vision-Text Decoupling Analysis

To investigate reasoning bottlenecks, a vision-text decoupling paradigm was employed on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (and later validated on GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL-72B) using prompt modifications:
- **PC (Pre-captioned):** Images in the prompt were replaced with ground-truth captions containing object category and location. This tests the LLM's reasoning ability *given* perfect visual information.
- **SC (Self-captioning):** Models were first instructed to generate their own captions (category and location) for images in separate conversations. These self-captions then replaced the images in the task prompt. This assesses the model's ability to reason *over its own visual interpretation*.
- **SC-I (Self-captioning-Interleaved):** Self-captions were interleaved between their corresponding images in the task prompt, allowing the model to process both visual and textual information simultaneously.

#### 3.7. Targeted Fine-tuning

LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) fine-tuning was performed on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (and Qwen2.5-VL-32B) using separate sets of randomly generated CVR tasks (1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 trials).
- **Architecture:** The ViT vision encoder was kept frozen, while the vision projector and the core LLM were unfrozen for adaptation.
- **Objective:** Supervised fine-tuning to output the correct final answer, implicitly encouraging reasoning without explicit CoT.
- **Generalization Assessment:** Fine-tuned models were benchmarked on MMBench, MMMU-Pro, and VQAv2 to assess out-of-distribution performance. Dropout was used to mitigate overfitting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings regarding the cognitive profiles, limitations, and potential for improvement in state-of-the-art VLMs.

#### 4.1. Cognitive Evaluation of VLMs with PAM Dataset

- **Overall Performance:** GPT-4o consistently demonstrated the best performance, often nearing human levels, particularly on category-based tasks. GPT-4o-Mini followed, matching human levels on category tasks but showing larger gaps in other areas.
- **Spatial Reasoning Deficit:** A pervasive and significant weakness across most VLMs, especially open-source models and GPT-4o-Mini, was observed in tasks requiring spatial object information (localization). This contrasted with human performance, where category-based tasks often required longer response times.
- **Perception:** VLMs showed better performance in object categorization than in object localization. Granular biases were found, with models sometimes performing better on "report" tasks and sometimes on "compare" tasks, varying by model and property type.
- **Attention:** GPT-4o showed its first major performance gap with humans in attention tasks, especially spatial attention. All open-source models performed substantially below human levels, indicating difficulty in selecting task-relevant objects amidst distractors.
- **Memory:** Most models performed similarly to their perception scores, suggesting robustness to irrelevant frames. However, location-based memory tasks remained a significant challenge for even GPT-4o, highlighting a persistent spatial reasoning issue. GPT-4o also struggled more with memory comparison tasks containing irrelevant objects.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of VLMs on Composite Visual Reasoning (CVR)

- **Performance Trends:** GPT-4o remained the strongest, even outperforming humans on most CVR tasks. Open-source models exhibited pronounced performance drops as task complexity increased.
- **Compounding Deficits:** The spatial reasoning deficits observed in PAM tasks were amplified in the more demanding CVR setting, leading to larger performance gaps for location-based CVR tasks compared to category-based ones.
- **GPT-4o Anomaly:** GPT-4o surprisingly performed better on medium-complexity CVR tasks than low-complexity ones, potentially suggesting a bias towards certain logical operations (e.g., if-then-else) excluded from low-complexity task generation.

#### 4.3. Relationship Between PAM & CVR

- **Strong Correlation:** All three core cognitive axes (Perception, Attention, Memory) showed strong positive correlations with CVR task performance. Memory performance exhibited the highest correlation (R²=0.94), likely due to the prevalence of memory-intensive comparison subtasks in CVR.
- **Benchmark Validation:** VLM accuracies on PAM and CVR tasks were significantly correlated with their performance on established benchmarks like MMMU-Pro and VQAv2, validating the diagnostic power of the newly introduced datasets.

#### 4.4. What It Takes for a VLM to Reason (Decoupling & Fine-tuning)

- **Vision-Text Decoupling via Captioning:**
 - **Pre-captioning (PC):** Replacing images with ground-truth captions led to the largest and most consistent performance gains across PAM and CVR tasks. This indicates that the underlying LLM components are capable of semantic interpretation and reasoning if given accurate visual information in textual form.
 - **Self-captioning (SC):** Using model-generated captions for images also resulted in significant improvements, comparable to PC on many tasks. This suggests VLMs *can* extract sufficient visual information (e.g., object locations and categories) into text but struggle with directly integrating visual tokens into the reasoning process.
 - **Spatial Reasoning Bottleneck:** The largest performance gains from SC were observed in location-based tasks (average 18.31% improvement) compared to category tasks (3.54%). This stark difference strongly suggests that a primary bottleneck in VLM spatial reasoning lies in inadequate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training or visual-to-textual integration, rather than purely visual perception or linguistic reasoning. The model can describe locations but fails to use this information when reasoning directly from images.
 - **Generalizability:** The effectiveness of the SC method extended to GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL-72B, confirming the CoT diagnosis for larger and proprietary models.
 - **Self-captioning-Interleaved (SC-I):** Interleaving self-captions with original images showed smaller improvements than SC, and even decreased performance on category-only PAM tasks. This suggests that the simultaneous presence of images might introduce attention capacity limitations or interference issues during reasoning, aligning with previous findings on VLM spatial reasoning.
- **Fine-tuning VLMs:**
 - **Improvements in Core Abilities:** Targeted LoRA fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-VL-7B using random CVR tasks led to substantial gains in core cognitive abilities, particularly for location-based PAM tasks, with performance increasing with more training data.
 - **Generalization to Benchmarks:** While promising, these improvements translated to only modest gains on MMBench and VQAv2, and surprisingly, slight decreases on MMMU-Pro for smaller fine-tuning datasets. This indicates that direct fine-tuning on `iWISDM` tasks improves specific cognitive skills but does not always generalize broadly to highly out-of-distribution, complex benchmarks, suggesting challenges in transfer learning or potential overfitting even with dropout.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a significant contribution to the field of Vision-Language Models by offering a detailed, cognitive science-inspired framework for understanding their capabilities and limitations. Its impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Advancing VLM Evaluation:** The introduction of the `Perception-Attention-Memory (PAM)` and `Composite Visual Reasoning (CVR)` datasets marks a crucial step towards more rigorous and diagnostically meaningful evaluations of VLMs. By breaking down visual intelligence into core cognitive faculties, this work moves beyond high-level task performance to pinpoint the specific mechanisms underlying VLM failures, thereby complementing and enhancing existing benchmarks. The strong correlation between PAM/CVR performance and established benchmarks like MMMU-Pro and VQAv2 validates the utility of these new diagnostic tools.

2. **Identifying Key Bottlenecks:** The paper definitively identifies the **integration of visual features into the reasoning process** as a primary bottleneck for VLMs, especially for spatial reasoning. This is a critical insight, suggesting that the issue is often not the VLM's ability to *perceive* information (as demonstrated by self-captioning success) nor its *language reasoning* capabilities, but rather its capacity to effectively *utilize* visual information within its Chain-of-Thought (CoT) process. This shifts the focus from purely improving visual encoders or LLMs to enhancing the multimodal alignment and reasoning integration mechanisms.

3. **Proposing Effective Solutions:** The research demonstrates that a simple, yet powerful, **vision-text decoupling method (self-captioning)** can significantly mitigate these bottlenecks, particularly for challenging location-based reasoning tasks. This provides a practical and generalizable strategy for boosting VLM performance, even for leading proprietary models like GPT-4o. The findings highlight the strong need for improved CoT abilities, suggesting that future VLM development could benefit from training regimes that explicitly encourage the conversion of visual perceptions into structured textual representations for more robust reasoning.

4. **Implications for VLM Architecture and Training:** The results from the SC-I method, showing diminished performance when images are present alongside captions, hint at potential **attention capacity limitations or interference issues** in multimodal processing. This suggests that simply augmenting input with more information (both visual and textual) might not always be beneficial and could inform architectural designs that better manage multimodal information flow and attention allocation. Furthermore, the demonstrated improvements from targeted fine-tuning, especially for location-based tasks, indicate that specific cognitive skills can be enhanced, albeit with challenges in broad generalization to out-of-distribution tasks. This opens avenues for more sophisticated, cognitively informed fine-tuning approaches, such as reinforcement learning with CoT-based rewards.

In essence, this work not only provides a deeper, more granular understanding of VLM cognitive deficiencies but also offers actionable insights and methods for designing and training more capable and human-like multimodal AI systems. It underscores the value of interdisciplinary research, bridging cognitive science with artificial intelligence to unravel the complexities of machine intelligence.
