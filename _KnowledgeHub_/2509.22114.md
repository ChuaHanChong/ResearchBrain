# SK2Decompile: LLM-based Two-Phase Binary Decompilation from Skeleton to Skin

> [!summary] Summary
> SK2 Decompile, developed by a research team including Hanzhuo Tan and Yuqun Zhang, introduces a two-phase Large Language Model-based binary decompiler that overcomes the traditional trade-off between functional correctness and code readability. It achieves approximately 70% re-executability on HumanEval and 60% on MBPP, while also significantly improving structural and semantic readability compared to existing LLM-based decompilers.

## Problem

- Traditional decompilers (e.g., Ghidra, IDA Pro) generate low-level, obfuscated pseudocode lacking meaningful identifiers, which hinders human comprehension and further analysis.
- Existing LLM-based decompilers often struggle with functional correctness, frequently failing to produce re-compilable and executable code, limiting their practical utility.
- The complexity of simultaneously inferring intricate control-flow, data structures, and meaningful identifiers in a single decompilation process leads to inherent trade-offs between output correctness and readability.

## Method

- A two-phase LLM-based approach decomposes binary decompilation into '==Structure Recovery==' (reconstructing the program skeleton with generic identifiers) and 'Identifier Naming' (assigning meaningful names to the recovered structure).
- A novel Intermediate Representation (IR) is introduced as 'obfuscated source code,' which retains structural and functional information while abstracting identifier semantics, acting as a bridge between low-level pseudocode and high-level source.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) is applied to both phases: Structure Recovery uses compiler feedback for functional correctness rewards, and Identifier Naming uses semantic similarity rewards for generating human-centric, meaningful names.

## Results

- SK2 Decompile achieves unprecedented re-executability rates for LLM-based decompilers, reaching approximately **70%** on HumanEval and **60%** on MBPP.
- It surpasses commercial state-of-the-art GPT-5-mini by an average of **21.6%** on HumanEval and **26.3%** on MBPP in re-executability.
- The method demonstrates superior structural readability (e.g., **18.4%** R2I gain over Idioms on ExeBench) and high-quality identifier naming (4.24/5 on HumanEval via GPT-Judge), indicating both functionally correct and human-readable output.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the complex decompilation task into separate structural recovery and identifier naming phases enables LLMs to achieve significantly higher functional correctness and readability.
> - An intermediate representation designed as 'obfuscated source code' effectively preserves program structure while abstracting identifier specifics, serving as an optimal information bottleneck for multi-phase translation.
> - Phase-specific reinforcement learning rewards, such as compiler feedback for structural integrity and semantic similarity for identifier quality, are crucial for fine-tuning LLMs to meet distinct, high-level objectives in code generation.
