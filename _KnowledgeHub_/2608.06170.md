# Prior-SG: Task and Prior Driven Region Segmentation for Scene Graphs in Arbitrarily-Structured Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Prior-SG, a framework from ETH Zurich and RAI Institute, addresses hierarchical 3D scene graph generation by aligning noisy sensory observations with LLM-synthesized task-conditioned structural priors. It achieves state-of-the-art semantic region segmentation, with an F1 score of 68.0% and mIoU of 61.8% on HM3D Navigation, and demonstrates zero-shot ontological flexibility in arbitrarily-structured environments.

## Problem

- Existing 3D scene graph methods rely on rigid, hard-coded geometric assumptions (e.g., wall-separated rooms), limiting their applicability to specific, structured environments and failing in open-plan spaces.
- Frameworks based purely on bottom-up visual clustering are susceptible to sensory noise and semantic smearing, requiring dense, proximal observations that hinder efficient operation in large-scale environments.
- A gap exists in integrating noisy sensory observations with probabilistic structural expectations and adapting spatial partitioning to specific task requirements, moving beyond fixed spatial ontologies.

## Method

- **Prior-SG** frames scene graph generation as a probabilistic alignment problem, inferring high-level functional semantics by reconciling observed physical information (==Instance Graph==) against a task-conditioned structural prior (==Prior Graph==).
- A Large Language Model (LLM) dynamically synthesizes a Prior Graph, encoding expected region semantics, object containment, and topological structure based on a high-level task and environment definition.
- Global region inference is performed via ==graph cuts on a Markov Random Field==, minimizing an energy function that fuses heterogeneous modalities (visual, geometric, object, environment priors) with topological regularization.

## Results

- Prior-SG achieved state-of-the-art robustness in semantic region segmentation, consistently outperforming baselines (e.g., Clio, HOV-SG) across diverse datasets, with **68.0%** F1 score and **61.8%** mIoU on HM3D Navigation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic contribution of all expert components; the topological prior improved regularization, the object expert provided discriminative signals, and the geometric expert enhanced boundary delineation in open-plan architecture.
- The framework demonstrated zero-shot ontological flexibility, dynamically restructuring spatial and semantic partitioning of identical environments based on varied task prompts (e.g., 'real estate listing' vs. 'hospitality persona').

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scene graph generation can be effectively framed as a Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) inference problem, allowing for a principled integration of noisy, multi-modal sensory data with abstract, task-conditioned structural priors.
> - Leveraging LLMs to dynamically synthesize a 'Prior Graph' enables zero-shot ontological flexibility, allowing the system to adapt its spatial understanding and region definitions to diverse natural language task prompts.
> - A multi-scale feature pyramid and confidence-weighted log-linear pooling strategy for experts effectively integrate heterogeneous information sources, resolving local perceptual ambiguities and enabling robust semantic anchoring from partial observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human perception of environments is characterized by hierarchical abstraction, moving from low-level geometric primitives to structured compositions of objects, regions, and environments. Endowing autonomous robotic systems with comparable spatial intelligence necessitates the use of hierarchical data structures, such as 3D scene graphs. These graphs facilitate spatial reasoning, provide scalability due to inherent sparsity, and enable integration with Large Language Models (LLMs) for open-ended reasoning and interaction.

Existing approaches to 3D scene graph extraction, particularly for higher-level semantics like regions, frequently exhibit limitations. Many methods rely on rigid, hard-coded geometric assumptions (e.g., wall-separated rooms or strict street topologies), which restricts their applicability to specific, structured environments. This geometric rigidity leads to failures in open-plan or arbitrarily-structured spaces. Additionally, frameworks based purely on bottom-up visual clustering are susceptible to sensory noise and semantic smearing, and they require dense, proximal observations, which hinders efficient operation in large-scale environments where semantic understanding of distant or partially observed regions is necessary for tasks like navigation.

The current research landscape presents a dichotomy: either 3D graphs are constructed with heavy reliance on geometric heuristics and raw visual clustering, or abstract knowledge is extracted from language models for planning without dense physical grounding. A gap exists in integrating noisy sensory observations with probabilistic structural expectations and adapting spatial partitioning to specific task requirements.

This work addresses this gap by proposing a framework that harmonizes high-level semantic expectations with sensory observations. It conceptualizes scene graph generation not merely as a reconstruction task bound by fixed spatial ontologies, but as a probabilistic alignment problem. This approach draws inspiration from human spatial reasoning, which incorporates task-driven semantic priors to interpret surroundings, moving beyond local geometry or isolated visual signals. By framing scene graph generation as a probabilistic alignment, the proposed method aims to unify open-vocabulary semantic perception and probabilistic reasoning, enabling the resolution of local perceptual ambiguities through global structural context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust framework for generating hierarchical 3D scene graphs that can accurately delineate semantic regions in arbitrarily-structured environments, including those without clear physical boundaries. This is motivated by the limitations of existing scene graph extraction methods that struggle in open-plan settings or with sparse observations, primarily due to their reliance on restrictive geometric heuristics or purely bottom-up visual clustering.

The key motivations and objectives can be detailed as follows:

1. **Overcome Geometric Rigidity:** Traditional methods often assume environments are structured into discrete, wall-separated rooms or fixed topologies (e.g., street lanes). This work aims to develop a system that does not depend on such assumptions, enabling it to operate effectively in open-plan offices, large public spaces, or complex outdoor environments where functional zones might not have physical dividers.
2. **Mitigate Sensory Noise and Semantic Smearing:** Purely bottom-up visual clustering approaches can suffer from inconsistencies caused by noisy sensor data or by "semantic smearing," where global image features incorrectly influence distant parts of the 3D map. An objective is to create a feature fusion strategy that robustly extracts scale-aware, open-vocabulary descriptors, thereby maintaining semantic integrity across varying scales and observation conditions.
3. **Enable Task-Driven Spatial Partitioning:** Humans interpret their surroundings with respect to their current tasks. The work is motivated by the desire to allow autonomous systems to adapt their spatial understanding based on a given high-level task. This involves developing a mechanism for generating dynamic, task-relevant "prior" expectations of the environment's structure, rather than relying on fixed ontologies.
4. **Formalize Scene Graph Generation as Probabilistic Alignment:** A core objective is to cast the problem of scene graph generation, specifically region segmentation, as a probabilistic alignment problem. This involves defining a Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimate to reconcile incoming sensor data (aggregated into an "Instance Graph") with an LLM-synthesized "Prior Graph" that encodes structural and semantic expectations. This probabilistic framework aims to provide a principled way to resolve ambiguities and integrate heterogeneous information sources.
5. **Achieve Zero-Shot Ontological Flexibility:** By dynamically synthesizing the Prior Graph using an LLM, the system aims to demonstrate "zero-shot ontological flexibility," allowing it to entirely restructure its spatial partitioning (i.e., define new region categories and relationships) based on natural language task prompts, without requiring re-training or manual re-configuration.
6. **Integrate Heterogeneous Information:** The work seeks to fuse diverse expert modalities—visual, geometric, and discrete object detections—alongside topological priors within a unified global inference framework. This integration, achieved through a Markov Random Field (MRF) optimized via graph cuts, is intended to leverage the strengths of each modality to produce more accurate and consistent region segmentations.

In essence, the motivation is to advance robotic spatial intelligence beyond purely metric or flat semantic maps towards a more cognitively aligned, hierarchical, and adaptable understanding of the environment, crucial for complex robotic tasks in diverse, real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Prior-SG framework conceptualizes scene graph generation as a probabilistic alignment problem, specifically inferring high-level functional semantics by aligning observed physical information against a task-conditioned structural prior. The system operates in three main phases: Prior Graph Generation, Instance Graph Construction, and Global Region Inference.

#### 3.1. Prior Graph Generation (G$_{P}$)

A Large Language Model (LLM) is utilized to dynamically synthesize a "Prior Graph" (G$_{P}$), which acts as a discrete structural prior encoding expected region semantics, object containment, and topological structure based on a high-level task ($T$) and environment definition ($E_{0}$).

- **Top-Down State Space Truncation:** The LLM is prompted to enumerate probable functional zones, generating a prior region set ($R_{P}$). For each region, it populates a prior object set ($O_{P}$) with task-relevant or discriminative objects. A single prototypical place node ($p_r \in P_{P}$) is instantiated per region to provide a unimodal spatial constraint. This process bounds the state space to task-relevant vocabulary.
- **Intrinsic Node Attributes:** Each observable node in the prior graph (objects and prototypical places) is assigned intrinsic attributes.
 - **Vision-aligned appearance descriptors ($d_{vis}$):** Text labels of nodes are processed through a CLIP text encoder, using standard observation phrases (e.g., "A picture of a [label]") to project abstract linguistic concepts into a shared vision-language embedding space.
 - **Geometric descriptors ($d_{geo}$):** For place nodes, the LLM defines expected local geometry (e.g., planarity, inclination) which are parsed into parametric mathematical parameters (e.g., angles, variances). These serve as soft statistical priors.
- **Probabilistic Structural Edges:** The edge set ($E_{P}$) encodes relational probability distributions as penalty terms for the graph cut solver.
 - **Containment Priors:** Hierarchical edges represent the conditional likelihood $P(o | r, E_0)$, the probability of an object $o$ existing within a region $r$. This forms a probabilistic signature for each region. Global region existence prior $P(r | E_0)$ is also estimated.
 - **Topological Priors:** Adjacency edges encode the transition likelihood $P(r_i \leftrightarrow r_j | E_0)$, representing logically plausible spatial layouts to resolve local ambiguities.

#### 3.2. Instance Graph Construction (G$_{I}$)

The raw RGB-D sensor stream ($Z$) is incrementally aggregated into an "Instance Graph" (G$_{I}$), forming a persistent state representation for observed physical elements. The construction of its observable layers (objects $O_{I}$ and places $P_{I}$) is guided by the Prior Graph.

- **Physical Backbone Instantiation:**
 - **Objects ($O_{I}$):** An open-set object detector (GroundingDINO) is explicitly constrained by the prior object set ($O_{P}$) to detect only task-relevant objects, avoiding irrelevant information. Detections are lifted to 3D and associated temporally.
 - **Places ($P_{I}$):** Continuous free space is represented using established spatial mapping techniques, specifically adapting Hydra's approach for topological place graph construction. Each place node represents a spatial volume with a 3D centroid.
- **Multi-Scale Feature Pyramid:** To characterize the visual texture of unoccupied 3D space, a multi-scale feature pyramid is introduced.
 - Each place is modeled as a 3D Gaussian volume with a radius ($r_Q$) derived from the Prior Graph, defining the expected physical size of semantic context.
 - For each camera observation, a place's 3D Gaussian is projected into the image plane to define a target query state ($s_p = (u_p, \sigma_p)$), comprising a 2D pixel coordinate and a scale.
 - A static Feature Pyramid is constructed from the input image, composed of layers with uniformly sized, overlapping crops ($S_l \times S_l$). Each crop has a fixed pixel center and scale ($s_{i,l} = (u_{i,l}, \sigma_l)$).
 - Crops observing minute physical fragments (area < $A_{min}$) are discarded using a depth map to estimate 3D area. Remaining crops are resized and encoded using a CLIP encoder, forming a repository of state-feature pairs.
- **Semantic Integration Kernel:** An integration kernel approximates the optimal feature for a place query ($s_p$) by mapping it against the feature pyramid.
 - **Spatial Relevance ($w_{spa}$):** Modeled as a 2D Gaussian response between query and crop states.
 - **Scale Trust Factor ($w_{sca}$):** Penalizes pyramid layers where candidate crop scale deviates from the prior-expected scale using a band-pass filter.
 - The effective integration weight ($W$) is the product of spatial affinity and scale trust. This aggregates discrete latent embeddings ($e_{i,l}$) to produce $e_p$ (isolated visual observation) and a scalar trust value $T(p)$ (quality of scale-matched observation).
- **Temporal Aggregation and Geometric Descriptors:**
 - **Vision-aligned appearance descriptor ($\hat{d}_{vis}$):** Place nodes accumulate a history of single-frame feature vectors and confidence scores. For places, a trust-weighted average is used to favor high-quality scale matches. For objects, a centrality-based average filters out transient occlusions/misdetections.
 - **Geometric descriptors ($\hat{d}_{geo}$):** Local structural properties are extracted by analyzing the topological neighborhood of a place node within the Instance Graph. The sample covariance matrix of 3D centroids yields parametric measures (e.g., local inclination angle $\theta_p$), which are evaluated against the LLM's geometric templates.

#### 3.3. Global Inference via Graph Cuts

The final phase aims to instantiate the region set ($R_{I}$) by finding the optimal discrete labeling field ($L: P_{I} \rightarrow R_{P}$) that assigns a region category to each place node. This is formulated as a Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) inference problem, which is transformed into a Markov Random Field (MRF) energy minimization problem: $E(L) = \sum_{p \in P_I} \psi_U(p, L(p)) + \sum_{(u,v) \in E_{P_I}} \psi_P(L(u), L(v))$.

- **Unary Potentials ($\psi_{U}$):** Measures the cost of assigning place $p$ to region $r \in R_{P}$ based on local observations. It fuses heterogeneous modalities (visual, geometric, object, environment) using a Confidence-Weighted Log-Linear Pool (Product of Experts). Each expert's contribution is weighted by a global hyperparameter ($\lambda_k$) and a dynamic, instance-specific observation confidence ($c_k(p)$), allowing experts to be smoothly ignored if data is insufficient.
 - **Visual Expert ($P_{vis}$):** Computes cosine similarity between place's observed descriptor ($\hat{d}_{vis}(p)$) and prior region descriptors ($d_{vis}(r)$). A "Null" score handles open-set ambiguity, preventing forced alignment of irrelevant clutter.
 - **Geometric Expert ($P_{geo}$):** Evaluates observed geometric descriptor ($\hat{d}_{geo}(p)$) against LLM's parametric templates (mean $\mu_r$, variance $\sigma^2_r$) using a von Mises distribution. Confidence ($c_{geo}(p)$) is based on the size and anisotropy of the topological neighborhood.
 - **Object Expert ($P_{obj}$):** Grounds observed objects to LLM's vocabulary via cosine similarity. Uses the LLM's Containment Prior $P(r | c)$ to compute object-to-region marginal probability $P(r | o)$. This evidence is transferred to the place layer via a proximity-discounted max-pooling operation over nearby objects. Confidence ($c_{obj}(p)$) is based on the total valid probability mass in the place's proximity.
 - **Environment Prior Expert ($P_{env}$):** Injects the global structural prior $P(r | E_0)$ from the LLM, favoring regions naturally expected to dominate the environment.
- **Topological Regularization ($\psi_{P}$):** Acts as a Potts-like model, penalizing transitions between adjacent places ($u,v \in E_{P_I}$) if they are assigned to different regions ($L(u) \neq L(v)$). The penalty is modulated by distance and the topological transition log-likelihood from the Prior Graph ($P_{u \leftrightarrow v}$). The MRF energy field is minimized using the $\alpha$-$\beta$-swap algorithm, which accommodates non-metric costs from complex LLM-generated topological rules.
- **Instantiating the Instance Layers:** Upon convergence, the optimal labeling field $L^*$ assigns a region label to each place node. Region nodes in $R_{I}$ are formally instantiated by extracting Connected Components (CC) from vertex-induced subgraphs for each region label. Hierarchical edges connect regions to their constituent places and objects, forming the complete scene representation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Prior-SG was conducted across diverse simulated and real-world datasets, including residential layouts (Habitat-Matterport 3D), varied semantic domains (TartanGround), large-scale indoor/outdoor environments (CODa), and open-plan architecture (Train Station). The findings support four primary claims:

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Robustness

Prior-SG demonstrated significant improvements in semantic region segmentation compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- **Quantitative Performance:** Prior-SG consistently outperformed competing methods (Clio and HOV-SG) across all evaluated datasets in both Semantic F1 score (F1$_{S}$) and Semantic mean Intersection over Union (mIoU$_{S}$). For instance, in HM3D Navigation, Prior-SG achieved 68.0% F1$_{S}$ and 61.8% mIoU$_{S}$, significantly higher than HOV-SG (37.8% F1$_{S}$, 46.2% mIoU$_{S}$) and Clio (35.7% F1$_{S}$, 23.4% mIoU$_{S}$).
- **Robustness to Sensor Motion:** The framework exhibited consistent semantic performance across different robot traversal types (e.g., "Navigation" vs. "Scan" trajectories in HM3D), unlike baselines that showed drops in performance with less exhaustive exploration. This indicates that the prior-guided MAP formulation and multi-scale feature pyramid effectively anchor semantics from partial observations.
- **Performance in Large-Scale and Open-Plan Environments:** Prior-SG drastically outperformed Clio in large, unbounded settings like TartanGround, CODa, and the Train Station. For example, in CODa, Prior-SG achieved 43.8% semantic mIoU compared to Clio's 14.4%. This highlights its ability to maintain accurate semantic understanding at scale, where purely visual clustering struggled with boundary precision and semantic assignment.
- **Geometric Performance:** While focusing on semantic interpretation, Prior-SG remained competitive with geometric pipelines like HOV-SG in geometric metrics for classic indoor environments, demonstrating strong performance in delineating physical boundaries (e.g., 65.9% geometric mIoU in HM3D Navigation, comparable to HOV-SG's 65.1%).

#### 4.2. Expert Synergy

Ablation studies quantified the contribution of each heterogeneous signal within the log-linear pool:
- **Topological Prior (E) and Object Expert (O):** The topological prior consistently improved performance across all datasets when combined with the visual expert (V+E), validating its role in regularizing visual embeddings and preventing improbable region classifications. The object expert provided strong discriminative signals in constrained indoor environments (HM3D), further enhancing performance (V+E+O configuration achieved highest F1$_{S}$ and mIoU$_{S}$ for HM3D). In large-scale environments (TartanGround, CODa), where object contexts are less defining, the V+E configuration achieved optimal performance.
- **Geometric Expert (G):** The geometric expert, while sometimes introducing minor degradation in F1$_{S}$ due to noise at observation boundaries in sparse navigation trajectories, proved critical for anchoring semantic labels to physical structural transitions in open-plan architectures. In the Train Station dataset, adding the geometric expert (V+E+G) increased semantic mIoU from 50.8% (V+E) to 57.9%, demonstrating its value in providing crisp boundaries based on elevation changes.

#### 4.3. Predictive Incremental Construction and Open-Plan Robustness

Qualitative results in the Train Station environment demonstrated the system's ability for long-range predictive inference and robust boundary delineation in open-plan architecture:
- **Accurate Prediction:** Prior-SG accurately segmented immediate surroundings while predicting distant regions (e.g., detecting a staircase 18m away from the platform at t=0.0s) and predicting upcoming concourse regions before full observation.
- **Impact of Multi-Scale Feature Pyramid:** Ablating the feature pyramid (relying only on global image features) led to failures in detecting distant features due to foreground domination and introduced severe semantic smearing, overestimating region sizes.
- **Role of Geometric Expert:** Disabling the geometric expert resulted in boundary bleed and overestimation of region footprints (e.g., staircase size at the concourse transition), confirming its role as a critical structural filter for tightening physical boundaries.

#### 4.4. Task-Conditioned Flexibility

The framework demonstrated zero-shot ontological flexibility:
- By dynamically generating the prior graph from high-level task prompts (e.g., "real estate listing," "hospitality persona," "facility maintenance persona"), Prior-SG restructured its spatial and semantic partitioning of an identical physical environment.
- For example, a "real estate listing" task recovered standard architectural room definitions, while a "hospitality" task merged living and dining rooms into a guest service area, and a "facility maintenance" task isolated plumbing/gas zones. This illustrates the system's capacity to dynamically redefine regions based on operational requirements.

#### 4.5. System Sensitivity and Computational Trade-Offs

- **Feature Pyramid Resolution:** A "Medium" configuration (224x224 pixels minimum crop resolution) for the feature pyramid achieved the highest F1$_{S}$ score while balancing inference time, avoiding upsampling latency and bounding dynamic crop count compared to "Fine" (112x112) or "Coarse" (448x448) resolutions.
- **CLIP Foundation Model Capacity:** While ViT-H/14 was used as the default, ViT-L/14 offered a reasonable compromise between latency and performance for real-time deployment, both architectures providing high-quality segmentation. ViT-B/16 was deemed unsuitable due to inconsistent spatial reasoning.
- **Spatial Query Radius (r$_{Q}$):** An optimal spatial query radius of approximately 0.75m was identified, balancing local detail with regional stability. Radii too small led to susceptibility to sparse noise, while radii too large resulted in semantic smearing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Prior-SG represents a methodological shift in hierarchical 3D scene graph generation by framing it as a probabilistic alignment problem rather than purely a reconstruction task. This approach, which integrates task-conditioned structural priors from Large Language Models with bottom-up sensory observations, offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalizability:** By moving beyond rigid geometric heuristics and purely local visual clustering, Prior-SG demonstrates improved robustness and accuracy in arbitrarily-structured environments, including large-scale open-plan spaces and those lacking clear physical divisions. This increases the applicability of autonomous systems in diverse real-world settings that previously challenged conventional scene understanding methods.
2. **Cognitively Aligned Spatial Reasoning:** The framework's inspiration from human spatial cognition, where task-driven priors guide interpretation, allows robots to develop a more contextual and relevant understanding of their surroundings. This bridge between low-level perception and high-level cognitive abstraction could lead to more intelligent and adaptable robotic behaviors.
3. **Zero-Shot Ontological Flexibility:** A key contribution is the demonstration of zero-shot ontological flexibility, where the system can dynamically restructure its spatial partitioning based on natural language task prompts. This enables robots to adapt their environmental understanding to specific missions or user requirements without costly re-training or manual re-configuration, significantly enhancing their versatility in human-robot interaction and task planning.
4. **Integration of Heterogeneous Information:** The successful fusion of diverse sensory modalities (visual, geometric, object detections) with abstract topological priors within a principled probabilistic framework provides a robust mechanism for resolving perceptual ambiguities. This multi-modal integration allows the system to leverage complementary information sources, leading to more accurate and reliable scene graph generation.
5. **Efficient and Predictive Perception:** The multi-scale feature fusion strategy, coupled with the ability to leverage priors, enables the system to predict distant functional boundaries and maintain crisp region delineations even from partial or sparse observations. This predictive capability is crucial for efficient navigation and planning in large, unknown environments.
6. **Foundation for Advanced Robotic Tasks:** Hierarchical 3D scene graphs are a foundational representation for a wide array of advanced robotic tasks, including long-horizon planning, natural language interaction, and complex manipulation. Prior-SG's ability to generate more accurate, flexible, and task-relevant scene graphs could significantly impact these downstream applications, making robots more capable in complex environments.

Future work could focus on transitioning the global graph cut optimization to an incremental inference scheme for strict real-time deployment, introducing additional hierarchical abstraction layers for city-scale missions, and evolving the static prior into a dynamic, learning entity where local semantic cues could update the LLM's internal model, enabling structural extrapolation and more informed exploration planning.
