# EaDex: A Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Manipulation Framework from Low-Cost Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> The EaDex framework enables cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation from low-cost RGB-D demonstrations by combining a novel data acquisition pipeline with a contact-reward-based dynamic demonstration annealing mechanism. It achieved an average 36.5% success rate across nine cross-embodiment tasks using low-cost data and improved task success by 55.3% compared to a fixed-weight baseline.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation with high-degree-of-freedom robotic hands poses significant challenges for reinforcement learning due to vast exploration spaces and high computational costs.
- Imitation learning requires high-quality, expensive demonstration data from specialized equipment like teleoperation setups or high-fidelity motion capture systems.
- The high cost of data acquisition and/or policy training creates a bottleneck for scalable and accessible dexterous manipulation research and application.

## Method

- A low-cost data acquisition pipeline using a single RGB-D camera to capture bimanual human demonstrations, followed by MANO model fitting and ==motion retargeting== to various robotic hands.
- A hybrid ==reinforcement learning== approach that combines task-specific rewards with imitation, behavior cloning, and contact-based rewards.
- A contact-reward-based dynamic demonstration annealing mechanism that adaptively adjusts the weights of demonstration-related rewards, reducing reliance on potentially noisy data as stable contact behaviors are learned.

## Results

- EaDex achieved an average success rate of **36.5%** across nine cross-embodiment manipulation tasks using custom low-cost RGB-D demonstration data.
- The dynamic demonstration annealing mechanism improved average task success rates by **55.3%** (from **23.5%** to **36.5%**) compared to a baseline without annealing, demonstrating its effectiveness in handling noisy data.
- The dynamic annealing mechanism also enhanced performance on high-quality datasets, improving ADD-AUC3 success rates for tasks like Ketchup from **9.0%** to **57.91%** and Waffleiron from **9.1%** to **23.01%** on the ARCTIC dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Coarse-grained human demonstrations, even with lower precision, can provide sufficient initial contact priors to effectively guide dexterous manipulation policy learning.
> - Adaptively decaying demonstration reward weights based on the policy's progress in establishing stable contact with the object mitigates the impact of noisy data and prevents overfitting.
> - An end-to-end framework combining low-cost data acquisition with dynamic reward scheduling allows for efficient and robust cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation.
