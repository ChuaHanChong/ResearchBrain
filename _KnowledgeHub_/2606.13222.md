# Proprioceptive-visual correspondence enables self-other distinction in humanoid robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Eastern Institute of Technology, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Peking University developed a self-supervised framework enabling humanoid robots to acquire self-other distinction and build a kinematics-free 3D self-model from proprioceptive-visual data in multi-agent scenes. This approach achieved over 99% accuracy in self-distinction and allowed the robot to successfully perform physical tasks such as target reaching and collision-aware motion planning.

## Problem

- Robots need to distinguish their own bodies from others for social intelligence (e.g., imitation, coordination) in multi-agent environments.
- Current self-modeling approaches often assume single-agent settings, creating a circular dependency where self-distinction is needed to build a self-model, but a self-model is needed for distinction.
- Robots lack the ability to learn self-other distinction and self-modeling from raw sensorimotor experience without predefined identity labels, kinematic models, or geometric descriptions.

## Method

- A two-stage, self-supervised framework leverages ==proprioceptive-visual correspondence== in multi-agent scenarios, operating solely on proprioceptive states and visual observations.
- A self-other distinction module identifies the robot's body among multiple agents by aligning proprioceptive state features with visual instance features via a two-level, attention-guided contrastive optimization.
- A kinematics-free implicit 3D self-modeling module then uses the robustly identified self-masks as pseudo-ground truth supervision to learn a differentiable representation of the robot's body's 3D occupancy and visibility.

## Results

- Achieved **99.69%** self-other distinction accuracy in real-world human-robot scenes and **99.50%** in simulated scenes with morphologically identical humanoid distractors, significantly outperforming VLM baselines.
- The learned 3D self-model produced coherent and accurate 3D occupancy predictions, with fidelity closely matching an oracle model trained with actual ground truth masks.
- Enabled the robot to perform downstream physical interaction tasks, including **88.0%** success in target reaching (mean distance **51.3 mm**), **71.4%** success in collision-aware motion planning, and human-to-robot motion retargeting with a mean per-keypoint error of **36.1 mm**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprioceptive-visual correspondence provides a sufficient self-supervised signal for robots to achieve robust self-other distinction and self-modeling in multi-agent settings, even with visually identical distractors.
> - Initial self-other distinction can break the circular dependency with self-modeling, providing the necessary pseudo-ground truth labels for learning a detailed 3D self-model.
> - Current advanced vision-language models do not effectively solve this cross-modal correspondence problem, highlighting that this task requires grounding in sensorimotor experience rather than purely semantic understanding.
