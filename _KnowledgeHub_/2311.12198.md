# PhysGaussian: Physics-Integrated 3D Gaussians for Generative Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysGaussian introduces a unified framework where 3D Gaussian kernels serve as both rendering primitives and discrete elements for physical simulation, enabling the generation of photo-realistic and physically plausible dynamics across diverse material types. This approach eliminates intermediate geometric representations, achieving a "what you see is what you simulate" paradigm for dynamic 3D content.

## Problem

- Existing novel view synthesis methods, like NeRFs and 3D Gaussian Splatting, struggle to generate physically accurate novel dynamics, often relying on coarse proxy meshes for deformation.
- Traditional physics-based content generation pipelines are multi-stage, involving separate geometry construction, meshing for simulation, and rendering, which introduces discrepancies between simulated geometry and final visualization.
- Prior dynamic scene representations lacked a comprehensive physics engine to predict complex material behaviors beyond simple deformations or data-driven movements.

## Method

- Extends 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== by endowing each Gaussian kernel with physical attributes and evolving them using continuum mechanics principles.
- Employs a custom ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) implementation, where Gaussians act as Lagrangian particles, to simulate various material behaviors (elasticity, plasticity, fracture, granular, viscoplastic).
- Integrates the local rotation derived from the deformation gradient to correctly evolve spherical harmonic orientations for photorealistic rendering of rotating objects, and introduces an optional internal filling step for robust volumetric simulations.

## Results

- Successfully simulated a wide range of material dynamics including elastic solids, plastic metals, fracture, granular materials, and non-Newtonian viscoplastic substances, with accompanying photorealistic sequences.
- Achieved higher PSNR scores compared to state-of-the-art NeRF deformation methods (e.g., NeRF-Editing, Deforming-NeRF, PAC-NeRF) on synthetic lattice deformation benchmarks, maintaining fine local details.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of full Gaussian kernel kinematics and spherical harmonics rotation for achieving visual consistency and preventing artifacts, while internal filling enabled realistic volumetric object behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying the geometric representation for both rendering and physical simulation (3D Gaussians) eliminates discrepancies and streamlines the content generation pipeline.
> - Leveraging continuum mechanics and MPM allows 3D Gaussians to inherently capture complex material properties and dynamic interactions, providing physically plausible motion.
> - Applying an incremental update to Gaussian covariances and rotating spherical harmonics based on local deformation gradients ensures high-fidelity visual consistency under large deformations and rotations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

PhysGaussian emerges at the intersection of two rapidly advancing fields: novel view synthesis, particularly Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS), and physics-based simulation for content generation.

Recent years have seen remarkable progress in 3D graphics and vision with NeRFs, which can generate photo-realistic novel views from sparse input images. The advent of 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) further revolutionized this by offering explicit, unstructured scene representations that significantly accelerate both training and rendering, enabling real-time performance. However, a significant limitation of most NeRF and GS-based methods has been their capacity to generate *novel dynamics*, especially those governed by physically accurate principles. While efforts exist to deform NeRFs for static shape editing or reconstruct dynamic scenes from videos, these often rely on embedding visual geometry into coarse proxy meshes (like tetrahedra or cage meshes), leading to discrepancies between the rendered visual representation and the simulated geometry. Such approaches also typically lack a comprehensive physics engine for predicting complex material behaviors beyond simple deformations.

Simultaneously, traditional physics-based visual content generation pipelines are multi-stage and tedious: geometry construction, meshing for simulation, physics simulation, and finally rendering. This fragmented pipeline often introduces "discrepancies between simulation and final visualization," where the simulated geometry is distinct from the rendered output.

PhysGaussian directly addresses this critical gap by proposing a unified framework where the same 3D Gaussian kernels serve as *both* the rendering primitives and the discrete elements for physical simulation. This "what you see is what you simulate (WS²)" philosophy distinguishes it from prior works by eliminating the need for intermediate meshing, explicit geometry embedding, or separate representations for graphics and physics. It extends dynamic NeRF/GS by endowing the explicit 3D Gaussians with physically meaningful attributes and evolving them through continuum mechanics, rather than relying solely on data-driven approaches or rigid transformations of existing kernels. This integration allows for generative dynamics that are both photo-realistic and physically plausible, applicable across a wide range of material types, a capability largely absent in previous dynamic scene representation methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of PhysGaussian is to **seamlessly integrate physically grounded Newtonian dynamics within 3D Gaussian Splatting to achieve high-quality novel motion synthesis.** This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Unifying Simulation and Rendering Representations:** The core motivation is to bridge the gap between physical simulation and visual rendering. Traditional pipelines, even those involving NeRFs, often use distinct geometric representations for simulation and rendering, leading to potential inconsistencies and resolution mismatches. PhysGaussian aims to overcome this by advocating for a *unified representation*—the 3D Gaussian kernels themselves—for both tasks, embodying the "what you see is what you simulate (WS²)" principle.

2. **Generating Physically Plausible Generative Dynamics:** While data-driven methods can synthesize movements, they often lack physical realism, inertia effects, and the ability to extrapolate to novel interactions. The paper seeks to imbue 3D Gaussians with physical attributes (kinematic deformation, mechanical stress, elasticity, plasticity) governed by continuum mechanics, enabling the generation of realistic and diverse dynamics for various materials.

3. **Eliminating Intermediate Geometry Representations:** A significant inefficiency in existing pipelines is the necessity for explicit meshing (e.g., triangles, tetrahedrons, cage meshes) to prepare geometry for physical simulation. This adds complexity and potential for error. PhysGaussian aims to eradicate this requirement by treating Gaussians directly as discrete representations of the continuum, simplifying the content generation workflow.

4. **Achieving Versatility Across Material Types:** The authors are motivated to demonstrate a robust framework capable of simulating a broad spectrum of material behaviors, including elastic solids, plastic metals, non-Newtonian viscoplastic fluids (like foam or gel), and granular materials (like sand). This versatility is crucial for broad applicability in content creation.

5. **Leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting's Efficiency:** Building upon the speed advantages of 3D GS for real-time rendering, the method aims to integrate an efficient simulation method (MPM) to enable not only photo-realistic output but also potentially real-time physics-driven dynamics for simpler scenes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysGaussian introduces a novel, unified simulation-rendering pipeline. Its methodology revolves around extending the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework with principles from continuum mechanics and the Material Point Method (MPM).

1. **3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) as Base Representation:** The process begins by reconstructing a static 3D GS representation of a scene from input images, comprising a set of unstructured 3D Gaussian kernels, each defined by its center, opacity, covariance matrix, and spherical harmonic coefficients. These Gaussians are treated as the discrete spatial representation of the continuous material to be simulated.

2. **Continuum Mechanics Integration:** The core idea is to model the deformation of these Gaussian kernels based on continuum mechanics. A continuous deformation map `x = ϕ(X, t)` (mapping material space to world space) and its deformation gradient `F = ∇ₓϕ(X, t)` are used to describe local transformations. By assuming particles undergo local affine transformations, the deformation of a Gaussian kernel `Gₚ(X)` in material space results in a world-space Gaussian `Gₚ(x, t)` with an updated center `xₚ(t) = ϕ(Xₚ, t)` and covariance `aₚ(t) = Fₚ(t)AₚFₚ(t)ᵀ`. This is crucial as it ensures the deformed representation remains Gaussian, thus compatible with the efficient GS rendering pipeline.

3. **Material Point Method (MPM) for Dynamics:** To solve the governing equations of continuum mechanics (conservation of mass and momentum), PhysGaussian employs a custom MPM implementation. MPM is a hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian method that discretizes the continuum with Lagrangian particles (the Gaussian kernels) while performing momentum conservation calculations on an Eulerian grid. The particles (Gaussians) track properties like position, velocity, and deformation gradient, which are then transferred to a background grid for stress and force calculations, followed by transfer back to particles for updates. This method inherently supports topology changes, frictional interactions, and a wide range of material models.

4. **Evolving Orientations of Spherical Harmonics (SH):** For photorealistic rendering of rotating objects, the spherical harmonic bases, which encode view-dependent color, must also rotate with the object. PhysGaussian addresses this by leveraging the local rotation matrix `Rₚ` derived from the polar decomposition of the deformation gradient `Fₚ`. This `Rₚ` is used to apply an inverse rotation to the view direction, effectively rotating the spherical harmonics and maintaining consistent appearance from novel viewpoints.

5. **Incremental Evolution of Gaussians:** An alternative to using the total deformation gradient `F` is proposed, where the world-space covariance matrix `a` is updated incrementally based on the rate form of kinematics (`ȧ = (∇v)a + a(∇v)ᵀ`). This formulation simplifies updates and allows for material models that do not solely rely on `F` as the strain measure. Similarly, the rotation matrix for spherical harmonics can be incrementally updated.

6. **Internal Filling:** Standard 3D Gaussian reconstructions often focus on surface appearance, leaving object interiors hollow. This leads to unrealistic volumetric behaviors in simulations. PhysGaussian introduces an optional "internal filling" step, where additional particles are generated within the object's reconstructed opacity field (derived from Gaussian opacities). These internal particles inherit properties from their nearest Gaussian kernels, enabling more robust and physically accurate volumetric simulations.

7. **Anisotropy Regularizer:** To prevent "over-skinny" Gaussian kernels, which can lead to visual artifacts under large deformations, an optional anisotropy loss term is introduced during the initial 3D Gaussian reconstruction. This regularizer constrains the ratio between the major and minor axis lengths of the Gaussians, enhancing the fidelity of the representation under dynamic conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PhysGaussian demonstrates exceptional capabilities across various benchmarks and material types, showcasing its versatility and the effectiveness of its unified simulation-rendering approach.

1. **Material Versatility:** The method successfully simulates a wide array of physical dynamics:
- **Elastic entities (e.g., fox, vasedeck):** Objects deform and return to their original shape.
- **Plastic metals (e.g., plane, can):** Objects undergo permanent deformation following von-Mises plasticity.
- **Fracture (e.g., toast, bread):** Large deformations cause objects to break apart, naturally handled by MPM.
- **Granular materials (e.g., ruins, wolf):** Sand-like behavior is captured using Drucker-Prager plasticity, accounting for frictional effects.
- **Non-Newtonian viscoplastic substances (e.g., jam, cake):** Materials like foam or gel are simulated with Herschel-Bulkley plasticity, exhibiting shear-dependent flow.
- **Collision (e.g., sofa suite):** Interactions between objects are automatically managed by the grid-based time integration of MPM.
 The accompanying figures visually confirm these diverse behaviors, displaying initial states and deformed sequences with photo-realistic quality.

2. **Quantitative and Qualitative Benchmarking (Lattice Deformations):**
- **Comparison with Baselines:** PhysGaussian was benchmarked against state-of-the-art NeRF deformation methods (NeRF-Editing, Deforming-NeRF, PAC-NeRF) using synthetic lattice deformation datasets (bending and twisting wolf, stool, plant). Quantitatively, PhysGaussian consistently achieved higher PSNR scores across all test cases, indicating superior rendering quality after deformation (e.g., Wolf bend: Ours 26.96 vs. NeRF-Editing 26.74, Deforming-NeRF 21.65, PAC-NeRF 26.91).
- **Qualitative Superiority:** Visual comparisons highlight that PhysGaussian maintains high-fidelity rendering quality and fine local details, unlike methods that rely on coarse proxy meshes or smooth interpolations, which can lead to blurry outputs or inaccurate deformation.

3. **Ablation Studies:** These studies validated the necessity of key components of the PhysGaussian framework:
- **Fixed Covariance:** Only translating Gaussians, without deforming their shapes, led to severe visual artifacts as surfaces were not properly covered after deformation.
- **Rigid Covariance:** Applying only rigid transformations to Gaussians (as in some prior works) still resulted in artifacts compared to the full affine deformation.
- **Fixed Harmonics:** Not rotating the spherical harmonics orientations led to inconsistent appearances when objects rotated.
 The ablation results (both qualitative in Fig. 5 and quantitative in Table 1) confirmed that all proposed enhancements—full Gaussian kernel kinematics and spherical harmonics rotation—are essential for achieving the best performance and visual consistency.

4. **Additional Qualitative Studies:**
- **Internal Filling:** Crucially, internal filling was shown to enable realistic volumetric object behaviors. Objects without internal particles collapsed unnaturally under gravity, resembling hollow shells, while filled objects responded appropriately to different material parameters (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio), demonstrating nuanced control over dynamics.
- **Volume Conservation:** Unlike geometry-based editing methods (e.g., surface ARAP deformation in NeRF-Editing), PhysGaussian inherently captures and maintains the volume of deformed objects, leading to more physically accurate and realistic dynamics during stretching or compression.
- **Anisotropy Regularizer:** The optional anisotropy constraint effectively mitigated "burr-like" visual artifacts caused by excessively slender Gaussian kernels during large deformations, enhancing the fidelity of the dynamic representation.

5. **Performance:** While full scenes might be computationally intensive, the paper highlights that some cases with simple dynamics (e.g., plane, toast, jam) can achieve real-time performance (25-36 FPS) on an RTX 3090 GPU, demonstrating the efficiency of the combined GS rendering and optimized MPM simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysGaussian represents a significant advancement in the field of generative dynamics for 3D content, with broad implications and potential impact:

1. **Unified and Seamless Pipeline:** The most significant contribution is the creation of a unified simulation-rendering pipeline. By using 3D Gaussian kernels as the sole discrete representation for both physics and graphics, PhysGaussian eliminates the need for problematic intermediate steps like meshing or geometry embedding. This "what you see is what you simulate" paradigm simplifies the workflow, reduces discrepancies, and potentially makes the process more robust and accessible.

2. **Photo-Realistic and Physically Plausible Dynamics:** The integration of continuum mechanics and MPM directly into 3D Gaussians enables the generation of novel motions that are not only photo-realistic (due to 3D GS's rendering quality) but also physically accurate, accounting for real-world material properties and inertia. This moves beyond purely data-driven or geometrically-constrained deformations, offering a higher degree of realism.

3. **Versatility Across Diverse Materials:** The demonstrated ability to simulate a wide range of materials—from elastic solids and plastic metals to granular and viscoplastic substances—showcases the method's robustness and flexibility. This makes it a powerful tool for creating diverse visual content, from mundane object deformations to complex material interactions.

4. **Potential for Content Creation and Editing:** PhysGaussian has immense potential for creative industries, including film, gaming, and virtual reality. It could significantly simplify the creation of dynamic assets with realistic physical behaviors. The benchmarking results against editing methods also suggest its utility in realistically editing existing static 3D Gaussian scenes.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions. Its limitations, such as the manual setting of material parameters and the lack of shadow evolution, point towards avenues for developing differentiable MPM simulators for automatic parameter learning from videos, integrating geometry-aware 3DGS reconstructions, and extending to more complex materials like liquids. The mention of potential integration with Large Language Models (LLMs) suggests future advancements towards more intuitive and high-level user control over dynamic content generation.

In conclusion, PhysGaussian pushes the boundaries of generative dynamics by synergistically combining explicit neural scene representations with robust physical simulation, offering a more coherent, efficient, and realistic approach to creating dynamic 3D content.
