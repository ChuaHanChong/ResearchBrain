# Temporal Difference Calibration in Sequential Tasks: Application to Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Technion - Israel Institute of Technology developed a framework for sequential uncertainty calibration in episodic tasks, linking a novel sequential Brier score to reinforcement learning value functions. Their Temporal-Difference Q-based Calibration (TDQC) method improved calibration and failure detection for Vision-Language-Action models, reducing sequential Brier scores and increasing ROC-AUC, and further enhanced task success rates by 13% through test-time action search.

## Problem

- Lack of robust uncertainty quantification (calibration) for sequential decision-making tasks, especially in critical applications like robotics where models often operate as black boxes.
- Delayed ground truth for task success or failure, which is only available at the end of an episode, making traditional single-step calibration methods unsuitable.
- The confidence in a decision at any given step in a sequential task is interdependent with the full sequence of past and future decisions.

## Method

- Formalized sequential calibration within a ==POMDP== framework, introducing a ==sequential Brier score== to evaluate confidence over time.
- Established a theoretical equivalence between minimizing the sequential Brier score for binary outcomes and learning a policy's ==Q-function== in reinforcement learning.
- Developed **TDQC** (Temporal-Difference Q-based Calibration), training a neural network as a Q-function via ==temporal difference loss== to predict episodic success, applicable to both white-box and black-box VLA models.

## Results

- TDQC methods consistently achieved lower sequential Brier scores and higher ROC-AUC for failure detection across various VLA models (OpenVLA, UniVLA, π0, π0-FAST) on benchmarks including LIBERO and real-robot data.
- Black-box RNN-TDQC, which uses only action probabilities or logits, demonstrated competitive or superior Brier scores on all LIBERO benchmarks compared to static baselines and other methods.
- Applying the calibrated Q-function for test-time action search increased OpenVLA's success rate by **13%** on unseen tasks from the LIBERO-10 benchmark, showing the practical utility for policy improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing the proposed sequential Brier score implicitly encourages both accuracy and calibration in predictions of future task success across an episode.
> - A fundamental theoretical link connects the problem of optimal sequential calibration to reinforcement learning's value function estimation, allowing the integration of robust TD learning algorithms for calibration.
> - Black-box predictors, utilizing only VLA action probabilities and calibrated with TDQC, can extract useful signals for long-term task success prediction, performing competitively with white-box methods.
