# Temporal Difference Calibration in Sequential Tasks: Application to Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Technion - Israel Institute of Technology developed a framework for sequential uncertainty calibration in episodic tasks, linking a novel sequential Brier score to reinforcement learning value functions. Their Temporal-Difference Q-based Calibration (TDQC) method improved calibration and failure detection for Vision-Language-Action models, reducing sequential Brier scores and increasing ROC-AUC, and further enhanced task success rates by 13% through test-time action search.

## Problem

- Lack of robust uncertainty quantification (calibration) for sequential decision-making tasks, especially in critical applications like robotics where models often operate as black boxes.
- Delayed ground truth for task success or failure, which is only available at the end of an episode, making traditional single-step calibration methods unsuitable.
- The confidence in a decision at any given step in a sequential task is interdependent with the full sequence of past and future decisions.

## Method

- Formalized sequential calibration within a ==POMDP== framework, introducing a ==sequential Brier score== to evaluate confidence over time.
- Established a theoretical equivalence between minimizing the sequential Brier score for binary outcomes and learning a policy's ==Q-function== in reinforcement learning.
- Developed **TDQC** (Temporal-Difference Q-based Calibration), training a neural network as a Q-function via ==temporal difference loss== to predict episodic success, applicable to both white-box and black-box VLA models.

## Results

- TDQC methods consistently achieved lower sequential Brier scores and higher ROC-AUC for failure detection across various VLA models (OpenVLA, UniVLA, π0, π0-FAST) on benchmarks including LIBERO and real-robot data.
- Black-box RNN-TDQC, which uses only action probabilities or logits, demonstrated competitive or superior Brier scores on all LIBERO benchmarks compared to static baselines and other methods.
- Applying the calibrated Q-function for test-time action search increased OpenVLA's success rate by **13%** on unseen tasks from the LIBERO-10 benchmark, showing the practical utility for policy improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing the proposed sequential Brier score implicitly encourages both accuracy and calibration in predictions of future task success across an episode.
> - A fundamental theoretical link connects the problem of optimal sequential calibration to reinforcement learning's value function estimation, allowing the integration of robust TD learning algorithms for calibration.
> - Black-box predictors, utilizing only VLA action probabilities and calibrated with TDQC, can extract useful signals for long-term task success prediction, performing competitively with white-box methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern neural network-based AI systems, particularly in critical applications like robotics, frequently operate as "black boxes." This characteristic necessitates the development of methods for reliable uncertainty quantification, referred to as calibration. Calibration ensures that a model's stated confidence aligns with the empirical probability of its predictions being correct. While calibration has been extensively investigated in single-step decision problems, such as image classification or regression, its application to sequential tasks presents unique challenges.

In sequential decision-making problems, like robot manipulation, the ground-truth outcome (e.g., task success or failure) is often delayed until the completion of a sequence of actions. Furthermore, the confidence in a current decision may be interdependent with past and future decisions within the sequence. Existing calibration techniques for single-step problems are not directly applicable to this dynamic setting.

Recent advancements in robotics have seen the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, π0, and π0-FAST. These models translate visual observations and language instructions into low-level robot actions. While powerful, current VLAs demonstrate varying success rates, especially on tasks not seen during training, which highlights the need for robust failure detection and confidence calibration. Previous work in this area, such as Zollo & Zemel (2025), explored action calibration and early action confidence. Gu et al. (2025) proposed SAFE, a method for cross-task failure detection in VLAs that uses internal features and conformal prediction. However, these approaches do not explicitly formulate sequential calibration within a unified framework, nor do they fully exploit the temporal nature of the problem.

This research connects sequential calibration to the field of reinforcement learning (RL). In RL, the prediction of terminal success corresponds to value estimation, and Temporal Difference (TD) methods are standard algorithms for learning value functions. The paper explores a novel link between the Brier score, a popular measure of accuracy and calibration, and RL value functions. This connection allows the integration of established RL algorithmic insights, such as TD learning, into the domain of sequential calibration. Concurrent work by van der Laan & Kallus (2025) has also explored connections between value learning and calibration in the context of offline RL, indicating a growing recognition of this interdisciplinary link.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work arises from the challenges of implementing reliable uncertainty quantification in sequential tasks, particularly within complex systems like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics. The authors identify two primary difficulties in such settings: (i) the ground-truth label for task success or failure is typically delayed until the end of an episode, and (ii) the confidence in a decision at any given step depends on the full sequence of past and future decisions. Traditional calibration methods, designed for single-step, immediate feedback scenarios, are insufficient to address these sequential complexities.

Against this background, the key objectives of this research are multi-faceted:

1. **Formalize Sequential Calibration:** To develop a formal framework for sequential calibration that explicitly accounts for delayed ground truth and the temporal interdependence of decisions within an episodic task. This involves defining how "task-success confidence" can be generated throughout an episode, even when true success is only ascertainable at its conclusion.

2. **Introduce and Leverage a Sequential Brier Score:** To propose a sequential extension of the Brier score, a standard metric for assessing both accuracy and calibration in probabilistic predictions. The motivation is to demonstrate that minimizing this sequential Brier score leads to a predictor that is inherently both well-calibrated and accurate over time.

3. **Bridge Calibration and Reinforcement Learning:** To establish a fundamental theoretical connection between the problem of minimizing the sequential Brier score for binary outcomes and the problem of learning a policy's value function (specifically, the Q-function) in a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). This connection is motivated by the desire to integrate the robust algorithmic machinery of reinforcement learning, particularly Temporal-Difference (TD) learning, into the domain of sequential calibration. TD methods are known for their ability to estimate future rewards by bootstrapping from future predictions, offering a favorable bias-variance tradeoff.

4. **Develop a Novel TD-based Calibration Method (TDQC):** To design and implement "Temporal-Difference Q-based Calibration" (TDQC) as a principled mechanism for improving uncertainty calibration over time, drawing directly from the established principles of TD value estimation in RL.

5. **Empirically Validate TDQC on VLA Models:** To apply the TDQC method to state-of-the-art VLA models for robotic control. This includes evaluating its performance relative to existing failure detection and calibration techniques on diverse benchmarks, including simulated and real-robot data. The motivation is to demonstrate the practical efficacy of TDQC in enhancing the reliability of VLA predictions.

6. **Assess Black-Box Calibration with Action Probabilities:** To investigate whether VLA models' single-step action probabilities, when calibrated using TDQC, can yield competitive uncertainty estimates for long-term task success. This objective is motivated by prior findings that suggested the insufficiency of raw action probabilities for task success prediction and aims to explore the potential of black-box calibration, which is critical for proprietary models.

7. **Explore Downstream Applications:** To illustrate the practical utility of a calibrated success predictor by demonstrating its application in scenarios such as early stopping of failed trajectories using conformal prediction and guiding action selection during test-time search to improve policy success rates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper addresses sequential calibration by formulating it within a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) framework and drawing a connection to reinforcement learning value estimation.

**Problem Formulation:**
The work defines success in an episodic task based on the cumulative reward `R(h_T)` of a trajectory `h_T` exceeding a predefined threshold `c`, resulting in a binary outcome `Y(h_T) ∈ {0, 1}`. To quantify confidence in sequential settings, the authors introduce the `sequential Brier score (BS_seq(f, t))`. This metric evaluates a success prediction function `f(h_t)` at any time step `t` against the final task outcome `Y(h_T)`. Analogous to the standard Brier score, the sequential Brier score can be decomposed into terms related to sequential calibration (discrepancy between predicted and true conditional success probabilities) and accuracy (informativeness of the prediction). Minimizing this score implicitly encourages both properties.

A key distinction is made between "black box" and "white box" success predictors. Black box predictors derive confidence solely from a model's observable outputs, such as action probabilities (`f(h_t) = f(π(h_0)..., π(h_t))`). White box predictors, conversely, require access to internal model representations, like hidden states or feature activations. The paper emphasizes the practical importance of black box predictors for interfacing with proprietary foundation models.

**Connection to Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
A foundational theoretical contribution is Theorem 5.1, which establishes that the predictor `f_hat(h_t)` that minimizes the sequential Brier score for future rewards `R(h_T)` is equivalent to the expected future rewards, which is the sum of observed rewards up to `t-1` and the Q-function `Q_π_t(h_t, a_t)`. For the binary success outcome `Y(h_T)`, this implies that learning the Q-function of a policy `π` is mathematically equivalent to learning a calibrated predictor of eventual success. This formal link bridges uncertainty calibration and reinforcement learning.

**Temporal-Difference Q-based Calibration (TDQC) Method:**
Leveraging the connection to value estimation, the paper proposes Temporal-Difference Q-based Calibration (TDQC). This method trains a parametric function `f_θ` (typically a neural network) to approximate the Q-function. The training data `D_cal` consists of `N` trajectories labeled with `y_i = Y(h_i_T)`. Instead of standard Monte Carlo (MC) regression against the final outcome `y_i` at each step, TDQC utilizes a Temporal Difference (TD) loss. For `t < T`, the loss is `(f_θ(h_i_t) - f_θ(h_i_{t+1}))^2`, bootstrapping the prediction from the next time step. For the terminal step `t=T`, the loss is `(f_θ(h_i_T) - y_i)^2`. A target network `θ⁻` (periodically updated from `θ`) is used for `f_θ⁻(h_i_{t+1})` to enhance training stability, a common practice in deep RL.

**Extracting History (h_t):**
- **Black-box:** For token-based VLA architectures (e.g., OpenVLA, UniVLA, π0-FAST), `h_t` is constructed from token probabilities or logits over the action vocabulary.
- **White-box:** For architectures that allow it, `h_t` is composed of internal representations, such as hidden states from intermediate layers, following the approach of SAFE (Gu et al., 2025).

**Downstream Applications:**
1. **Early Stopping with Conformal Prediction (CP):** The learned `f_θ` is used as a confidence score for future success. For safety-critical scenarios, `1 - f_θ` serves as a failure predictor. Conformal prediction is employed to calculate time-varying upper thresholds `δ_t`. If `1 - f_θ(h_t)` exceeds `δ_t`, the trajectory is stopped early, with a statistical guarantee (`1 - α` probability) that a successful rollout will not be prematurely halted.
2. **Q-Value Guided Action Search:** At test time, if the predicted Q-value `f_θ(x_t, a_t)` indicates low confidence (below a threshold `T̄`), the method samples multiple candidate actions from the policy. For each candidate, a one-step look-ahead simulation is performed, and the resulting state is evaluated by `f_θ`. The action leading to the highest predicted Q-value is then selected. This aims to improve policy success rates by steering away from low-confidence paths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to address three primary questions: whether TD loss improves calibration and failure detection, if black-box calibration can yield competitive results, and if calibrated models can enhance success rates via test-time action search.

1. **TD Loss Improves Calibration and Failure Detection Results:**
 - **Calibration:** The sequential Brier score (Table 1, Figure 1) was used to measure calibration and accuracy. TDQC-based methods, including both white-box (SAFE-RNN-TDQC, SAFE-MLP-TDQC) and black-box (RNN-TDQC) variants, consistently achieved lower sequential Brier scores across all evaluated VLA models (OpenVLA, UniVLA, π0, π0-FAST) and benchmarks (LIBERO, WidowX, Franka real-robot). This indicates superior calibration and more accurate success probability estimates compared to non-TDQC baselines (e.g., SAFE-RNN, SAFE-MLP BCE) and static predictors. The performance improvement of TDQC methods became more pronounced as the episode progressed (Figure 1).
 - **Failure Detection:** ROC-AUC was used to evaluate failure detection (Table 2). TDQC predictors either matched or surpassed the best baselines, achieving state-of-the-art performance on unseen tasks in OpenVLA LIBERO, π0 LIBERO, UniVLA LIBERO, and the real-robot π0-FAST Franka benchmark.
 - **Correlation:** A strong negative Spearman correlation (ρ = -0.686) was observed between lower sequential Brier scores and higher ROC-AUC values (Figure 3), suggesting that better-calibrated success probabilities are beneficial for effective failure detection.

2. **Black-Box Based Calibration Yields Competitive Results:**
 - The study found that black-box RNN-TDQC predictors, which only utilize action probabilities or logits as input, achieved competitive or superior Brier scores on all LIBERO benchmarks when trained with TDQC (Table 1). This performance was notably better than static baselines using similar action probability inputs and competitive with white-box methods that access internal VLA features. This finding contrasts with earlier research that suggested VLA action probabilities alone were insufficient for robust success prediction. The application of the TDQC technique enabled useful signal extraction from these black-box outputs.

3. **Calibrated Model Improves Success Rate Using Test-Time Guided Action Search:**
 - An application of the calibrated model demonstrated its utility in guiding action selection at test time to improve policy success rates. On three unseen tasks from the LIBERO-10 benchmark with OpenVLA, using the learned Q-function (`f_θ`) for action search significantly increased the success rate (Figure 4).
 - The RNN-TDQC method, guiding action selection, achieved an average success rate of 55%, representing a 13% improvement over the baseline OpenVLA policy. While a BCE-trained variant also improved performance, it peaked lower at 50%.
 - A thresholded TDQC approach (`TDQC - Thresh 0.35`) showed a slight performance drop compared to the unconstrained TDQC but reduced computational overhead by nearly half, indicating a viable trade-off between performance and compute efficiency. A near-linear relationship was observed between additional computational steps and the resulting success rate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the fields of machine learning, robotics, and reinforcement learning by introducing a novel framework and methodology for uncertainty calibration in sequential decision-making tasks. The work carries several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Foundational Framework for Sequential Calibration:** The paper provides the first formal formulation for calibration in sequential tasks, bridging a conceptual gap in the existing literature that predominantly focuses on single-step predictions. By explicitly addressing delayed feedback and inter-dependent confidence over time, this framework offers a robust theoretical basis for understanding and quantifying uncertainty in dynamic environments.

2. **Theoretical Link Between Calibration and RL:** The establishment of a fundamental connection between minimizing a sequential Brier score and learning an RL value function (Q-function) is a significant theoretical insight. This link allows researchers to leverage decades of algorithmic advancements in reinforcement learning, particularly Temporal Difference methods, to solve problems in uncertainty calibration. This interdisciplinary bridge opens new avenues for transferring knowledge and techniques between these two fields.

3. **Algorithmic Innovation with TDQC:** The proposed Temporal-Difference Q-based Calibration (TDQC) method is a direct practical outcome of the theoretical connection. Its empirical superiority in achieving lower Brier scores and higher ROC-AUC on various VLA benchmarks demonstrates that TD learning principles are effective in improving calibration and failure detection performance in sequential contexts.

4. **Enhanced Reliability for VLA Models:** By significantly improving the calibration and failure detection capabilities of Vision-Language-Action models, this work has a direct positive impact on the safety and reliability of robotic systems. Well-calibrated VLA models can more accurately assess their likelihood of success, enabling proactive safety mechanisms, such as early stopping, to prevent potential failures or damages in real-world deployments.

5. **Empowering Black-Box Model Calibration:** The demonstration that TDQC can achieve competitive results using only a VLA model's black-box action probabilities is particularly impactful. This capability is crucial for interacting with proprietary foundation models, where access to internal features is often restricted. It expands the applicability of robust calibration techniques to a wider range of commercial and research-oriented black-box AI systems.

6. **Improved Policy Performance through Guided Search:** The application of the calibrated Q-function to guide test-time action search, resulting in a notable increase in VLA policy success rates, indicates a promising avenue for enhancing robotic policy performance. This approach suggests a paradigm where uncertainty quantification not only informs safety but also actively contributes to better decision-making by allocating computational resources for search in challenging situations.

**Potential Future Impacts:**
- **Resource-Efficient AI:** The idea of using calibrated predictors to selectively apply more computational effort (e.g., search) only for "difficult" decisions could lead to more resource-efficient and intelligent AI systems.
- **Broader Application to Sequential Generative Models:** The framework could be extended to other sequential generative AI models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs), to improve their uncertainty quantification and detect phenomena like hallucinations, by treating text generation as a sequential task.
- **Robust Real-World Robotics:** Future integration with advanced world models could allow the guided action search to operate effectively in real-world scenarios, further boosting the autonomy and reliability of robots.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges limitations such as restricted generalization of predictors across different environments or embodiments, primary focus on binary episodic success, and the current dependency of guided action selection on simulator access or a learned forward model. Addressing these limitations in future research could further amplify the impact of this work, especially for broader real-world deployment.
