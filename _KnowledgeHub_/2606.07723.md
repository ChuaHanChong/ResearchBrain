# VoLo: A Physical Orchestrator for Open-Vocabulary Long-Horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VoLoAgent is a VLM-driven physical orchestrator that treats a VLA/WAM, perception models (SAM3, Molmo2), and grasp/place primitives as interruptible tools in one closed loop, planning, monitoring, and recovering mid-rollout. Paired with the 126-task RoboVoLo benchmark, it beats standalone VLAs, code-as-policy, and TAMP baselines on open-vocabulary long-horizon manipulation, and triples real-robot success over a bare VLA.

## Problem

- End-to-end VLA/WAM models execute precise but largely open-loop action chunks, lacking robust planning, monitoring, and perception for multi-object long-horizon scenes.
- Code-as-policy and TAMP methods support explicit reasoning but rely on fixed toolsets and control APIs, largely overlooking real-time monitoring and failure recovery.
- Existing hierarchical VLM-planner-plus-VLA-executor systems hard-wire the control flow, treating the VLA as a fixed executor rather than an interruptible capability among tools.

## Method

- ==Physical Orchestration== formalism: a monitor-halt-redirect control law requiring the agent to detect divergence, halt in-flight actions, and redirect while the physical world keeps moving.
- **VoLoAgent**: a VLM agent routing among VLA/WAM rollouts, perception tools, and grasp/place primitives via asynchronous execution, fast/slow dual-context memory, and safety-aware idling.
- ==RoboVoLo benchmark==: 126 tasks across common sense, memory, complex references, and world knowledge suites, built on RoboLab/Isaac Lab with 501 new assets for diagnosing task- and failure-level performance.

## Results

- VoLoAgent (Full) beats the strongest baseline per suite by **+38.9%** on Common Sense, **+30.2%** on Complex References, **+14.3%** on Memory, and **+13.1%** on Robolab-Vague.
- On real Franka robot trials (168 rollouts), VoLoAgent achieves **42.9%** success versus **14.3%** for a standalone pi-0.5 VLA, a **3x** improvement.
- VoLoAgent recovers from **54%** (38/70) of failure episodes versus **13%** (11/86) for pi-0.5, with **5x** more failure-free episodes (20 vs 4 of 90).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating a visuomotor policy as an interruptible tool rather than a fixed executor lets a VLM orchestrator recover from perception errors mid-rollout instead of failing outright.
> - Completion-monitoring errors dominate VLM failure modes across all backends (>67% of events), pointing to monitoring accuracy as the key bottleneck, not planning.
> - Orchestration gains transfer across VLA backbones (2-6x multiplier) and VLM choices, but degrade sharply with weaker open-weight VLMs, showing the orchestrator's reasoning quality is load-bearing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is progressing towards agents capable of understanding and executing complex, open-ended instructions in diverse, multi-object environments. This requires capabilities that extend beyond simple pick-and-place tasks, encompassing high-level reasoning, adaptive execution, and robust failure recovery.

Current approaches address parts of these challenges:
- **End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAMs)** excel at precise, scaled manipulation by mapping observations and instructions directly to actions. However, these models typically operate in an open-loop manner for action chunks, limiting their ability to perform robust planning, real-time monitoring, and adaptive, tool-based recovery during execution in long-horizon tasks. They often struggle with open-vocabulary grounding and multi-object scenes.
- **LLM/VLM-driven code-as-policy methods** leverage large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) for explicit reasoning, generating code that interfaces with perception and classical control primitives. While these methods offer explicit reasoning, they are often constrained by fixed toolsets and control APIs, and typically do not adequately address real-time monitoring and dynamic failure recovery in complex interaction scenarios.
- **Hierarchical systems** integrate VLM planners with VLA executors. In these architectures, the VLM generates high-level plans, which are then executed by a VLA. However, the control flow between the VLM and VLA is frequently hard-wired, treating the VLA as a fixed executor. This architecture often lacks the flexibility to adaptively compose VLAs with other perception, action, monitoring, and recovery tools, particularly by interrupting VLA rollouts mid-execution.

Existing benchmarks for robot manipulation predominantly focus on short-horizon skills, simplified scenes, or limited open-vocabulary reasoning. This limits the comprehensive evaluation of long-horizon state tracking, complex reference resolution, world knowledge application, and adaptive failure recovery mechanisms.

This research aims to bridge these gaps by introducing "physical orchestration," a closed-loop agent design that integrates VLA/WAMs as interruptible tools within a flexible VLM-managed system. Concurrently, it provides a high-fidelity benchmark specifically designed to evaluate these advanced, long-horizon capabilities across a wider range of reasoning and recovery challenges. The work positions itself as an advancement towards more generalist robotic agents that can reason and act effectively in dynamic, unconstrained physical environments, addressing limitations in robust planning, real-time monitoring, and adaptive failure recovery that characterize prior approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this work are driven by the complexities of real-world robotic manipulation, which often involves open-vocabulary instructions and long-horizon tasks in multi-object scenes. The core motivation stems from the recognition that existing robotic manipulation systems only partially address these demands.

The key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Addressing Open-Vocabulary Long-Horizon Manipulation:** Real-world tasks frequently require robots to interpret flexible natural language instructions (e.g., "put every item on the table into the bowl, except the red block") and execute multi-step plans. This demands robust capabilities for language understanding (including negative references, spatial reasoning), planning over sequences of actions, and managing state changes in dynamic scenes over an extended period. Existing systems, particularly end-to-end VLAs, often lack the high-level reasoning and planning necessary for such tasks.

2. **Developing Adaptive Planning, Execution, Monitoring, and Recovery:** A robot operating in a physical environment must not only plan actions but also continuously monitor their execution, detect divergences from the plan (e.g., picking the wrong object, collisions), and adaptively recover from failures. Unlike virtual agents, physical robots operate in a world that does not pause for reasoning, necessitating real-time decision-making. Current hierarchical systems often hard-wire the control flow between planner and executor, limiting dynamic adaptation and recovery.

3. **Introducing "Physical Orchestration":** A central motivation is to conceptualize and implement "physical orchestration." This paradigm emphasizes that a physical agent must decide *when* to act, advance, or stop, actively monitoring the world for deviations and halting in-flight actions if necessary. This contrasts with virtual AI agents that can pause their environment during deliberation. The requirements of physical orchestration include:
 - **Monitoring for divergence:** Continuously comparing perceived reality with the expected state of progress.
 - **Halting in-flight actions:** Rapidly stopping an ongoing action upon detecting divergence.
 - **Redirecting with corrective actions:** Choosing appropriate recovery strategies such as replanning, reissuing actions, or switching to different tools.

4. **Unifying Heterogeneous Robot Capabilities as Interruptible Tools:** The work aims to overcome the limitations of systems that treat VLAs as fixed, monolithic executors. Instead, a key objective is to treat VLA/WAMs as one interruptible capability among many, alongside perception models and classical action primitives (like grasping and placing). This allows a central VLM to flexibly compose and steer these diverse tools mid-rollout, leveraging their complementary strengths to handle complex scenarios and recover from failures.

5. **Creating a High-Fidelity Benchmark for Long-Horizon Open-Vocabulary Manipulation:** To rigorously evaluate the proposed agent architecture and diagnose its capabilities and limitations, there is a need for a benchmark that:
 - Focuses on *long-horizon* tasks requiring multiple steps and state tracking.
 - Emphasizes *open-vocabulary* reasoning, including common sense, complex linguistic references, memory, and world knowledge.
 - Features *high-fidelity* simulation with realistic physics and diverse objects to enable effective sim-to-real transfer.
 - Provides both task-level success metrics and detailed *failure-mode diagnostics* to understand underlying issues. Existing benchmarks are noted as lacking these combined attributes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research introduces VoLoAgent, a VLM-managed physical orchestrator, and RoboVoLo, a benchmark for its evaluation.

#### 3.1. VoLoAgent: A Physical Orchestrator

VoLoAgent is designed as a single VLM agent that operates in a closed loop to plan, monitor, and adaptively route among various robotic tools, incorporating a concept termed "physical orchestration."

##### 3.1.1. Principles of Physical Orchestration

The design of VoLoAgent is guided by three principles to address the challenges of operating in a dynamic physical world:
- **P1) Asynchronous Tools:** Robot motion (e.g., VLA rollouts) runs independently of the agent's reasoning. This allows the VLM to interleave monitoring with execution rather than blocking, enabling rapid interruption.
- **P2) Fast and Slow Memory:** The agent maintains a short "monitor context" (current observation, active subgoal, recent decisions) for frequent, near-motion-timescale checks (0.2 Hz). A fuller "deliberation context" (task memory, scene history, tool catalog) is consulted only at less frequent planning or recovery points.
- **P3) Safety-Aware Idling:** When the VLM needs to reason or recover mid-task, the robot is idled, typically by holding it still for a fixed-base arm, to ensure safety.

##### 3.1.2. VoLoAgent System Architecture

The system comprises a VLM as the central orchestrator and three families of callable tools:

- **VLM (Claude Opus 4.6):** Acts as the agent, performing planning, monitoring, and recovery decisions. It interacts with the environment through the available tools.
- **VLA/WAM (e.g., π0.5, DreamZero):** Serves as an interruptible visuomotor tool for continuous, dexterity-focused manipulation. It receives observations and instructions and outputs action chunks.
- **Perception Tools (GroundingDINO, SAM2, SAM3, Molmo2):** Provide open-vocabulary object detection and segmentation capabilities, crucial for grounding natural language references in the scene.
- **Action Primitives (GraspGen, IK, Depth-Projected Placement):** Offer geometry-grounded motion skills like `grasp(target)` and `place(destination)`. These combine perception (for object localization), GraspGen (for 6-DoF grasp poses), and Inverse Kinematics (IK) for motion planning.

##### 3.1.3. Operational Phases

The VoLoAgent operates through a continuous loop with three main phases:

1. **Initial Execution:**
 - Given a user instruction and initial scene, the VLM decomposes the task into a sequence of atomic subgoals, stored in external memory.
 - The agent then initiates the first tool call, often a VLA rollout due to its continuous visuomotor control capabilities, and begins concurrent monitoring.

2. **Monitoring & Routing:**
 - At each monitoring step, the VLM assesses the latest observation and memory (P2).
 - It decides on one of three actions: `continue` (keep the current tool running), `next_subgoal` (current subgoal complete, advance to the next), or `recovery` (failure detected, intervention needed).
 - This decision process is adaptive, with no fixed split between planner and executor.

3. **Recovery:**
 - If `recovery` is chosen, the active tool (e.g., a VLA rollout) is idled (P3).
 - The VLM enters a deeper "deliberation context" to choose a corrective action:
 - `continue`: Resume the rollout if the detected "failure" was a false positive.
 - `replan`: Re-issue the remaining subgoal decomposition from the current state.
 - `rewrite`: Re-prompt the VLA with a new subgoal instruction.
 - `grasp / place`: Execute a perception-grounded action primitive on a specific target or destination.

#### 3.2. RoboVoLo Benchmark

RoboVoLo is a high-fidelity simulation benchmark designed to evaluate open-vocabulary, long-horizon manipulation, addressing limitations of existing benchmarks. It is built on RoboLab (NVIDIA Isaac Lab with PhysX).

##### 3.2.1. Task Taxonomy

The benchmark consists of 126 tasks categorized into four reasoning suites and 15 task categories:

1. **Common Sense (32 tasks):** Evaluates the robot's ability to infer implicit goals from scene context and functional roles of objects. Categories include: Infer, Kit, Recover, Sort.
2. **Memory (30 tasks):** Assesses the policy's capacity to track and maintain information about earlier scene states across multiple actions. Categories include: Order, Recall, Swap.
3. **Complex References (32 tasks):** Tests fine-grained language understanding for spatial, ordinal, relational, size-based, or negative references. Categories include: Spatial, Counting, Negation, Size+Sort.
4. **World Knowledge (32 tasks):** Requires the application of external knowledge beyond immediate scene geometry, spanning domains like arithmetic, chemistry, recycling, and visual art. Categories include: Math, Art, Chem, Recycle.

##### 3.2.2. Simulation Environment and Assets

- **Simulator:** RoboLab, running on NVIDIA Isaac Lab (Isaac Sim with PhysX physics engine), provides a high-fidelity simulation environment.
- **Assets:** The benchmark expands RoboLab’s existing asset library with 501 new objects, including 247 household items and 254 task-specific assets (e.g., periodic-table element cubes, geometric art objects, wooden math cubes). All assets incorporate collision geometry and realistic physics materials to support contact-rich interactions.
- **Robot & Cameras:** A 7-DoF Franka Research 3 arm with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, consistent with the DROID setup. Observations are derived from external ZED 2i and wrist ZED mini cameras, and optionally a front-mounted egocentric ZED 2i for the orchestrator.

##### 3.2.3. Evaluation Protocol

Each task is evaluated over three fixed-seed trials, ensuring consistent initial states for comparative analysis across different systems. Task success is determined by objective termination predicates (e.g., object in container, final pose tolerance).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates VoLoAgent's performance in open-vocabulary long-horizon manipulation across simulation and real-robot settings, along with detailed failure analysis and component ablations.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Dominant Performance:** VoLoAgent (Full) achieved the highest overall success rate across all RoboVoLo benchmark suites, consistently outperforming standalone action models (e.g., π0.5, DreamZero), code-as-policy systems (CaP-X), and TAMP-style baselines (TiPToP). The full system showed significant statistical superiority (p < 0.05) over most baselines, with a notable exception against its own VoLoAgent (Only VLA) ablation (p = 0.0598).
- **Quantitative Gains:** VoLoAgent (Full) improved success rates by substantial margins compared to the strongest baseline in most suites: +38.9% on Common Sense, +30.2% on Complex References, +14.3% on Memory, and +13.1% on the Robolab-Vague benchmark.
- **Competitive World Knowledge:** The World Knowledge suite was the only area where a TAMP baseline was competitive, with VoLoAgent showing a smaller gain of +2.1%, indicating TAMP's strength in symbolic planning for such tasks.
- **Ablation Comparison:** The full VoLoAgent system also surpassed its own strongest ablation (VoLoAgent (Only VLA)) by 1.9% to 10.4% across suites, highlighting the cumulative benefit of its full toolset and orchestration capabilities.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Task Execution

- **Adaptive Tool Use:** Qualitative examples illustrated VoLoAgent's ability to adapt. For instance, in a "Complex References – Spatial" task, while π0.5 struggled with open-vocabulary constraints, and VoLoAgent (No VLA) using only primitives timed out on contact-rich picks, VoLoAgent (Full) successfully combined VLA dexterity with a grasp tool for recovery. When the VLA initially picked a wrong object, the orchestrator-managed grasp tool repositioned the gripper on the correct target, allowing the VLA to complete the task.
- **Memory and Reasoning:** In a "Memory – Recall" task, standalone π0.5 failed to track and recall prior states, while VoLoAgent (Full) successfully unstacked blocks and then picked the correct ones based on their original positions, demonstrating effective state tracking and recovery.

#### 4.3. Failure Mode Analysis

The analysis identified both world-level execution failures and VLM-level reasoning errors.

- **World Failures:**
 - **Reduced Failures:** VoLoAgent exhibited a higher number of failure-free episodes (20) compared to π0.5 (4) out of 90 episodes.
 - **Improved Recovery:** In episodes where failures did occur, VoLoAgent recovered from 54% (38/70) of world failures (e.g., wrong-object pick (WOP), wrong-target placement (WTP), or robot getting stuck), significantly higher than π0.5's 13% (11/86) recovery rate. This indicates that VoLoAgent not only achieves higher direct success but also possesses superior failure recovery mechanisms.

- **VLM Failures:**
 - **Dominant Completion Monitoring Errors:** Completion-monitor errors were the most frequent VLM failure type across all evaluated VLM backends (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Qwen3-VL-8B), accounting for over 67% of total VLM-failure events. These errors involved the VLM misjudging whether a subgoal was complete or incomplete based on visual evidence.
 - **Significant Failure Monitoring Errors:** Missed failure detection (Failure-monitor errors) was another substantial class of VLM failures for all backends except Claude Opus 4.6.
 - **Rare Planning and Tool-Use Errors:** Planning errors and tool-use mismatches (e.g., hallucinating target names) were relatively rare across VLM backends.
 - **VLM Capability Impact:** The performance of the VLM backend significantly affected the VLM failure rate, with stronger VLMs like Claude Opus 4.6 showing substantially fewer total VLM-failure events compared to weaker models like Qwen3-VL-8B.

#### 4.4. Component Ablations

Ablation studies systematically varied components across system design, perception tools, VLM models, and VLA backbones.

- **System Variants:** VoLoAgent (Full) scored 41.80% overall, significantly outperforming a pure VLA (π0.5, 12.57%), a VLM with only primitives (VoLoAgent (No VLA), 17.76%), and a VLM verbally steering a VLA without other tools (VoLoAgent (Only VLA), 34.97%). This highlights the synergistic effect of the full orchestration.
- **Perception Robustness:** The system demonstrated robustness to different perception tool choices (e.g., GDino+SAM2/Molmo2 vs. SAM3/VLM-point) and camera views (exterior vs. egocentric), achieving substantial gains in all configurations, with most ablations being statistically non-significant compared to the full system.
- **VLM Model Impact:** The choice of orchestrating VLM significantly impacted performance. Frontier VLMs like Claude Opus 4.6 yielded 19-29% gains over VLA-only baselines. Weaker VLM models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B) resulted in marginal gains, aligning with their higher VLM-failure rates.
- **VLA Backbone Multiplier:** The orchestrator consistently improved the performance of every VLA backbone it was integrated with, multiplying their success rates by 2–6 times overall.

#### 4.5. Real Robot Validation

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** VoLoAgent was successfully deployed on a real Franka FR3 robot across 14 representative RoboVoLo tasks.
- **Significant Real-World Improvement:** The full VoLoAgent achieved 42.9% success rate, a 3x improvement over the standalone π0.5 (14.3%). This validates the applicability of the agent loop design in physical environments.
- **Qualitative Recoveries:** Real-world examples demonstrated the agent's ability to monitor and recover from physical failures such as wrong-object picks and incorrect placements, mirroring simulation results.
- **Ablation Nuance:** Intermediate VoLoAgent variants (No VLA, Only VLA) achieved comparable real-robot success rates (45.2% and 40.5%), with overlapping confidence intervals. Further extensive real-robot studies would be required for statistically significant comparisons between these ablations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents VoLoAgent, a framework for physical orchestration in robotic manipulation, and RoboVoLo, a high-fidelity benchmark, making several contributions to the field.

The introduction of **VoLoAgent** represents an advancement in designing robotic agents capable of open-vocabulary long-horizon manipulation. By conceptualizing "physical orchestration," the work addresses the critical distinction between virtual and physical agents, where real-time monitoring, adaptive intervention, and dynamic recovery are paramount. The agent's ability to unify and orchestrate heterogeneous tools—including VLA/WAMs, perception models, and classical action primitives—as interruptible components within a single VLM-managed closed loop enables a more flexible and robust approach to complex tasks. This modular design allows the system to leverage the strengths of different tools (e.g., VLA for dexterity, perception for grounding, primitives for precise actions) and adaptively switch between them for effective execution and recovery. The experimental results, particularly the substantial performance gains over existing baselines and its own ablations, confirm the efficacy of this orchestration paradigm.

The development of **RoboVoLo**, a 126-task benchmark, addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of advanced robotic capabilities. Its focus on common sense, memory, complex references, and world knowledge, coupled with high-fidelity simulation and detailed failure diagnostics, provides a comprehensive platform for rigorous assessment. This benchmark allows researchers to analyze task-level success and pinpoint specific failure modes, facilitating targeted improvements in future agent designs. Its design, independent of the proposed system, ensures impartiality and broad applicability for evaluating a range of open-vocabulary long-horizon manipulation agents.

The **significance** of this work lies in:
- **Advancing Generalist Robots:** VoLoAgent pushes towards more general-purpose robots that can handle diverse, unconstrained instructions and environments, moving beyond specialized, narrow tasks.
- **Robustness through Adaptive Recovery:** The explicit focus on monitoring and adaptive recovery from failures, demonstrated through improved recovery rates, enhances the reliability of robotic systems in unpredictable real-world scenarios.
- **Unified Agent Architectures:** The integration of different AI and robotic paradigms (VLMs, VLAs, classical control, perception) into a single, orchestrating agent offers a promising direction for future robot control systems.
- **Benchmarking Complex Reasoning:** RoboVoLo provides the necessary tools to rigorously evaluate not just low-level dexterity but also high-level cognitive abilities like symbolic reasoning, memory, and language understanding in an embodied context.

The **potential impact** of this research includes:
- **Enabling More Capable Robotic Applications:** The improved robustness and flexibility could translate into more practical applications for robots in logistics, manufacturing, domestic assistance, and hazardous environments, where adaptive problem-solving and error recovery are essential.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The detailed failure analysis, particularly the identification of completion monitoring as a dominant VLM failure mode, offers clear directions for future research in VLM design and real-time monitoring mechanisms. The call for faster local monitors to complement cloud VLMs points to critical areas for latency reduction.
- **Standardizing Evaluation:** RoboVoLo could become a standard benchmark for evaluating complex robotic manipulation, fostering comparative research and accelerating progress in developing intelligent robotic agents.
- **Foundational for Multi-Modal Control:** The framework's embodiment-agnostic nature suggests its potential extension to more complex robot morphologies like bimanual or dexterous-hand manipulators, or mobile embodiments, although such extensions would require further specialized tool implementations.

While the current demonstration is on a single-arm manipulator with safety-aware idling simplified to halting, these identified limitations also outline clear pathways for future work, indicating the foundational nature of this research.
