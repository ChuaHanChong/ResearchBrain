# CodeDance: A Dynamic Tool-integrated MLLM for Executable Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> CodeDance is an MLLM framework that employs a dynamic code-integrated reasoning loop for complex visual tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance on counting (91.2% on CountBench) and chart QA (87.5%). The system also demonstrates emergent behaviors like cross-domain tool transfer and novel tool composition during reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from information bottlenecks due to static, text-only reasoning, limiting active interaction with visual inputs.
- Advanced proprietary systems capable of 'thinking with images' are opaque 'black boxes,' hindering transparency, reproducibility, and systematic study.
- Open-source visual reasoning tools often rely on rigid, schema-based integration, leading to inflexible workflows and non-adaptive tool invocation (overuse or underuse).

## Method

- CodeDance introduces a =="think-execute-feedback" reasoning loop== where the MLLM interleaves natural language thoughts with executable Python code snippets for visual-symbolic operations.
- A two-stage training approach involves ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== on a curated 34k trajectory dataset, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.
- A novel composite reward function, ==R_BAT (Reward for Balanced Adaptive Tool-call)==, is implemented during RL to dynamically balance tool exploration for complex tasks with efficiency for simpler ones, mitigating both overuse and underuse.

## Results

- CodeDance-7B achieves **91.2%** accuracy on CountBench and **87.5%** on Chart QA, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-72B and GPT-4o on specific visual reasoning tasks.
- It demonstrates improved math reasoning performance, with a **18.4%** accuracy gain on MathVision and an **11.9%** gain on WeMath compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model.
- Ablation studies validate the effectiveness of the R_BAT reward design, confirming its role in achieving higher accuracy and controlled tool usage across benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL training fosters emergent behaviors, including cross-domain tool transfer (e.g., bounding boxes for counting) and novel composition of atomic operations (e.g., pointing, cropping, and zooming for validation).
> - The R_BAT reward effectively prevents tool overuse and underuse, achieving a superior balance between exploration and efficiency, leading to higher accuracy and controlled tool usage.
> - Turn-level rewards prevent policy entropy collapse by penalizing flawed intermediate steps, sustaining exploration during training and improving overall generalization for longer reasoning horizons.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated progress in visual perception and reasoning, often leveraging Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanisms to decompose complex problems. However, traditional CoT in visual tasks typically relies on static contexts, which restricts models from dynamically interacting with visual inputs or incorporating new observations during iterative reasoning. This limitation can create an information bottleneck that hinders multi-round focusing and validation.

Proprietary systems, such as OpenAI's o3, have integrated active information seeking through multiple tool invocations, enabling iterative reasoning over visual inputs and exhibiting advanced perception and analysis capabilities. Despite this progress, several research gaps remain in the open-source domain. Current open-source approaches largely extend CoT through text-only templates, which often fail to incorporate new visual observations or refine intermediate steps against visual evidence. Furthermore, proprietary systems like o3 lack transparency, making their internal mechanisms, reasoning processes, and outputs difficult to study or reproduce. Most existing open-source systems that incorporate visual reasoning are restricted to predefined visual workflows or rigid, schema-based pipelines (e.g., predicting bounding box coordinates for cropping), which limits their flexibility and transferability across diverse tools and tasks. Additionally, many current methods do not adaptively determine when tools should be invoked, leading to either underuse (failing to leverage visual interactions when needed) or overuse (incurring unnecessary steps and complexity).

CodeDance positions itself within this landscape by introducing an open and verifiable framework that uses executable code as a general solver for visual reasoning. It aims to overcome the inflexibility of fixed-schema calls and the limitations of text-only reasoning by enabling MLLMs to dynamically define, compose, and execute code for orchestrating multiple tools, computing intermediate results, and rendering visual artifacts. The work also addresses the challenge of adaptive tool invocation through a reward mechanism designed to balance exploration and efficiency, thereby mitigating tool overuse and underuse. This approach seeks to provide a scalable and transferable mechanism for executable visual reasoning, offering a transparent alternative to proprietary systems and a more flexible paradigm than existing open-source methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce CodeDance, a multimodal reasoning framework that leverages executable code as a unified medium for visual reasoning. This framework aims to enable MLLMs to perform "thinking with images" by dynamically integrating and orchestrating diverse visual and symbolic operations.

Specific objectives and the motivations behind them include:

- **Enabling Flexible and Interpretable Visual Reasoning with Executable Code**: The authors motivate this by highlighting the limitations of current schema-driven approaches that use fixed operation templates. CodeDance seeks to allow MLLMs to define, compose, and execute a variety of visual–symbolic operations, thereby producing intermediate artifacts (e.g., cropped regions, plots, annotations) and final answers within a unified, verifiable reasoning process. This approach is intended to enhance interpretability by making the reasoning steps transparent and self-checkable.
- **Developing a Foundation for Atomic Visual Capabilities**: To achieve sophisticated reasoning, the model requires fundamental skills. The authors motivate the curation of a high-quality trajectory dataset for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to teach atomic capabilities such as counting, spatial grounding, and image annotation. This initial training is designed to establish an iterative exploration-reflection reasoning process.
- **Implementing Adaptive and Balanced Tool Calling**: A central challenge identified is the trade-off between exploration and efficiency in tool use, where naive policies often either overuse tools (leading to unnecessary steps) or underuse them (failing to leverage visual interactions). The motivation is to design a difficulty-adaptive tool-reward mechanism that adjusts incentives based on task complexity. This mechanism is intended to encourage longer operation chains for challenging problems while discouraging redundant calls for simpler ones, thereby aligning learning with the structure of multimodal tasks and resulting in more adaptive and transparent reasoning.
- **Investigating Emergent Behaviors**: The authors are motivated to empirically observe and study whether the framework exhibits behaviors beyond direct atomic supervision during Reinforcement Learning (RL) training. This includes potential emergent capabilities such as composing operations (e.g., localization followed by counting), generating novel procedural code for visual analysis, and cross-task transfer without specific fine-tuning. Such observations would suggest a general and scalable mechanism for executable visual reasoning.
- **Outperforming Existing Models**: A key motivation is to demonstrate that CodeDance can consistently outperform both schema-driven and text-only baselines, as well as advanced closed models like GPT-4o and larger open-source models, across various reasoning benchmarks. This objective serves to validate the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed code-based, difficulty-adaptive reasoning paradigm.

Collectively, these objectives are motivated by the need for an open, verifiable, flexible, and adaptive framework that can address the limitations of current MLLMs in complex visual reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CodeDance operates on a "think-execute-feedback" reasoning unit, allowing an MLLM to dynamically interact with visual inputs through executable code.

**Overall Framework (Think-Execute-Feedback)**
Given a multimodal user query (text and image), the MLLM, acting as a policy model ($\pi$), produces rollouts that interleave natural-language reasoning with executable code snippets (e.g., cropping, plotting). This code is executed within a dedicated sandbox. The resulting visual evidence (e.g., a cropped image region) and textual feedback are concatenated with the ongoing reasoning trace and fed back into the MLLM to refine its next thinking step or formulate a final answer. Each reasoning turn is defined as a `(state, action, next_state)` tuple, where the state `s_t` comprises the original query `x`, the accumulated reasoning trace `o_t`, and interpreter feedback `ϵ_t`. Actions `a_t` include tool calls (code snippets) or a terminal answer. This iterative process continues until an answer is produced or a maximum turn budget is reached, making each step an executable and verifiable reasoning unit.

**Policy Optimization via Verifiable Evidence**
The framework employs Reinforcement Learning (RL) for policy optimization, specifically building upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). While GRPO typically assigns uniform advantage across a trajectory, CodeDance extends the reward design to include sequence-level and turn-level components to better address multi-turn tool reasoning. The composite reward `r(τ)` for a trajectory `τ` integrates outcome and tool-related signals:
`r(τ) = R_acc(τ) + R_format(τ) + R_BAT(τ)`
where `R_acc` is for final-answer correctness, `R_format` enforces output format compliance, and `R_BAT` is the Reward for Balanced Adaptive Tool Calling.

**R_BAT (Reward for Balanced Adaptive Tool Calling)**
`R_BAT` is designed to advocate adaptive tool calling based on task difficulty and decomposes into:

- **Sequence-level adaptive reward (R_seq)**: This component prevents degenerate behaviors like tool spamming on easy problems. It conditions tool incentives on the group-level accuracy (`μ_acc`) of multiple rollouts for the same task. If `μ_acc` is high (easy task), further tool invocations are discouraged. If `μ_acc` is low (hard task), additional exploration via tools is encouraged. The formula for `R_seq` incorporates a scaling factor `d` that depends on `μ_acc`, `γ`, and `δ` (hyperparameters) and the ratio of successful to total tool calls (`N_succ(τ) / N_total(τ)`). This mechanism adaptively modulates the reward for tool use.
- **Turn-level execution reward (R_turn)**: This provides dense, immediate feedback by penalizing failed code executions. For each turn `m`, `r_turn,m = -0.5` if code execution fails, and `0` otherwise. These penalties are accumulated into a discounted return `R_m_turn` using a discount factor `β`, which helps to discourage credit assignment to incorrect intermediate reasoning steps and mitigate entropy collapse during training.

The final advantage for policy updates combines a sequence-level advantage (`A_seq`) derived from `R_acc`, `R_format`, and `R_seq`, with a turn-level advantage (`A_turn`) from `R_turn`.

**Data Engineering**
To bootstrap the MLLM, a 34K high-quality dataset is curated for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). This dataset features reasoning trajectories that include code execution results as visual evidence and incorporates adaptive tool invocation based on question difficulty.
The data curation pipeline involves:
1. **Weak-to-strong filtering**: Public resources (e.g., SA1B, GEOqa plus, MMK12) are automatically filtered and stratified by difficulty using Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Easier questions are encouraged to be answered directly, while harder questions necessitate code-based tool integration.
2. **Multi-turn atomic supervision**: Hard cases are decomposed into trajectories covering three categories: fundamental image transforms (crop, resize), mathematical computation (measurement, algebra, aggregation), and open-ended visual editing (drawing, annotation).
Each trajectory is further cross-validated for correctness by a stronger MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B). The query, code, and response are embedded within the training data format.

**Implementation Details**
CodeDance is built upon Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the base MLLM. SWIFT is used for SFT, and VeRL is used for RL. Specific hyperparameters are set for `γ`, `δ`, and `β`. Evaluations are conducted using VLMEvalKit with a maximum of 10 inference turns and 6 training turns.

**Code Sandbox Implementation**
All Python code generated by the model is executed in an isolated, internal sandboxed environment. This environment assigns a dedicated working directory and namespace to each execution instance, ensuring no interference between concurrent rollouts. Key safety features include disabling risky APIs, reverting to the last successful state upon failure, enforcing a 15-second wall-clock time limit per call, and capturing structured stdout/stderr logs and graphical outputs (from libraries like matplotlib, PIL) as feedback for the MLLM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents CodeDance's performance across various benchmarks, alongside observations regarding emergent behaviors during RL training and ablation studies on the reward design and scaling.

**Performance on Visual Reasoning Tasks**
CodeDance demonstrates consistent performance across diverse visual reasoning benchmarks. On counting tasks (CountBench, PixmoCount), visual search tasks (V\* Bench, HR-Bench 4K/8K), and general understanding tasks (ChartQA, CharXiv), CodeDance-7B consistently surpasses all Qwen2.5-VL 7B baselines. Notably, it achieves state-of-the-art performance on Counting and ChartQA, outperforming both the baseline models by significant margins and exceeding the performance of larger open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-32B) and even advanced closed models like GPT-4o on several metrics (Table 1). These results suggest that delegating fine-grained visual analysis to code-based tools can extend beyond the raw perceptual capacity of the base model.

**Performance on Math Reasoning Tasks**
In mathematical reasoning, CodeDance shows consistent improvements over open-source baselines (Table 2). For example, it improves accuracy on MathVision from 25.0% to 29.6% (+18.4%) and on WeMath from 35.4% to 39.6% (+11.9%), while maintaining competitive results on other benchmarks like MathVista and MathVerse. The authors attribute these gains to the executable code's ability to support precise symbolic manipulation and stepwise calculations, offering higher accuracy and reliability compared to relying solely on internal approximations.

**Emergent Behaviors during RL**
During RL training, CodeDance empirically exhibits behaviors that extend beyond the atomic supervision provided during SFT, indicating potential for scalability and generalization:

- **Cross-domain tool transfer**: Visual operations trained for specific tasks (e.g., bounding-box for chart tasks) are repurposed for different contexts, such as using bounding boxes to localize and count objects in new counting tasks (Figure 3A). This suggests that visual operations can function as transferable reasoning primitives.
- **Novel tool composition of learnt capabilities**: Despite SFT data restricting tasks to single predefined tools, the model develops the ability to compose multiple atomic operations for complex tasks. An example includes combining pointing operations (to check position correctness) with cropping and zooming (for fine-grained detail inspection) to validate an object's color (Figure 3B).
- **Incentivizing emergence of novel unseen capabilities**: The model demonstrates a potential to generate tool codes not explicitly defined in the SFT data, often leveraging its pretraining knowledge. For instance, when counting headsets, the model attempts to write Python code using OpenCV functions (e.g., `cv2.rectangle`) to overlay a grid for better visualization (Figure 3C). This suggests the model can adapt pretrained capabilities to support reasoning.

**Ablation Studies**
- **Validation of reward design**: Comparisons between different reward designs (Table 3) indicate that the full R_BAT (Reward for Balanced Adaptive Tool Calling) mechanism yields the best overall accuracy and execution success rate (0.99). Outcome-level GRPO rewards lead to tool underutilization, while a DeepEyes-style reward (positive for every successful tool execution) leads to tool overuse without consistent accuracy gains. Removing the turn-level reward from R_BAT significantly degrades the execution success rate. This supports the effectiveness of R_BAT in balancing efficiency with necessary exploration and preventing reward hacking.
- **Validation on scaling up compute budget**: Scaling studies across four dimensions (Figure 4) consistently show benefits:
 - **SFT dataset size**: Enlarging the SFT dataset from 5K to 34K yields steady accuracy gains across various benchmarks.
 - **Model capacity**: Increasing model capacity from 3B to 7B substantially boosts reasoning performance, with CodeDance-3B outperforming a stronger 7B baseline on certain tasks.
 - **RL training steps**: Extending RL training up to 240 steps further improves accuracy without overfitting.
 - **Inference turn budget**: Allowing more inference turns (up to 10) improves reasoning performance, suggesting the learned policy generalizes to longer reasoning horizons.
- **Empirical study on inference entropy and accuracy**: The presence of the turn-level reward in R_BAT delays policy entropy collapse during training and translates into consistently higher validation accuracy (Figure 5). This indicates that local, turn-level feedback improves global generalization by preventing premature convergence and sustaining exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CodeDance introduces a multimodal reasoning framework that leverages executable code as a unified and generalizable medium for visual reasoning. This approach represents an advancement over existing MLLMs by offering an open, verifiable, and flexible mechanism for tool integration.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Enhanced Transparency and Verifiability**: By enabling models to generate and execute code, CodeDance produces interpretable reasoning traces and intermediate visual artifacts. This transparency is crucial for applications requiring accountability and auditability, such as scientific analysis, engineering, and education. Users can inspect the exact steps taken by the model, including code execution results, to understand and validate its reasoning process.
- **Flexible and Adaptive Tool Use**: Unlike rigid, schema-based approaches or models prone to tool underuse/overuse, CodeDance's difficulty-adaptive reward mechanism promotes a balanced and context-aware approach to tool invocation. This enables the model to strategically employ tools when necessary for complex tasks while avoiding redundant calls for simpler ones, leading to more efficient and effective problem-solving.
- **Demonstrated Superior Performance**: CodeDance consistently outperforms open-source baselines and, on several benchmarks, surpasses larger open-source models and advanced closed models like GPT-4o. This suggests that the code-based, tool-integrated paradigm can yield substantial gains in diverse visual and mathematical reasoning tasks, particularly those requiring fine-grained analysis or precise symbolic manipulation.
- **Observation of Emergent Capabilities**: The empirical observation of emergent behaviors during RL training—including cross-domain tool transfer, novel tool composition, and the generation of unseen tool codes—highlights the scalability and generality of the framework. These emergent properties, arising without explicit atomic supervision, suggest a path towards more autonomous and versatile multimodal agents that can adapt and innovate beyond their initial training.
- **Practicality and Scalability**: The framework's design, favoring a single dense MLLM over complex agent pipelines, offers practical advantages in terms of lower latency, reduced compute cost, improved robustness, and unified optimization. The demonstrated scalability with increased dataset size, model capacity, and training steps further positions CodeDance as a foundation for future progress in multimodal and agentic AI.

Potential impacts include accelerating scientific discovery by providing AI assistants capable of verifiable visual analysis, improving educational tools with transparent reasoning explanations, and developing more robust and interpretable AI systems for real-world applications. However, the authors also acknowledge potential risks associated with code-generating models, such as misuse for unsafe automation or manipulation via generated visual artifacts, recommending safeguards like safety filters and responsible deployment practices.
