# CodeDance: A Dynamic Tool-integrated MLLM for Executable Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> CodeDance is an MLLM framework that employs a dynamic code-integrated reasoning loop for complex visual tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance on counting (91.2% on CountBench) and chart QA (87.5%). The system also demonstrates emergent behaviors like cross-domain tool transfer and novel tool composition during reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from information bottlenecks due to static, text-only reasoning, limiting active interaction with visual inputs.
- Advanced proprietary systems capable of 'thinking with images' are opaque 'black boxes,' hindering transparency, reproducibility, and systematic study.
- Open-source visual reasoning tools often rely on rigid, schema-based integration, leading to inflexible workflows and non-adaptive tool invocation (overuse or underuse).

## Method

- CodeDance introduces a =="think-execute-feedback" reasoning loop== where the MLLM interleaves natural language thoughts with executable Python code snippets for visual-symbolic operations.
- A two-stage training approach involves ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== on a curated 34k trajectory dataset, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.
- A novel composite reward function, ==R_BAT (Reward for Balanced Adaptive Tool-call)==, is implemented during RL to dynamically balance tool exploration for complex tasks with efficiency for simpler ones, mitigating both overuse and underuse.

## Results

- CodeDance-7B achieves **91.2%** accuracy on CountBench and **87.5%** on Chart QA, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-72B and GPT-4o on specific visual reasoning tasks.
- It demonstrates improved math reasoning performance, with a **18.4%** accuracy gain on MathVision and an **11.9%** gain on WeMath compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model.
- Ablation studies validate the effectiveness of the R_BAT reward design, confirming its role in achieving higher accuracy and controlled tool usage across benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL training fosters emergent behaviors, including cross-domain tool transfer (e.g., bounding boxes for counting) and novel composition of atomic operations (e.g., pointing, cropping, and zooming for validation).
> - The R_BAT reward effectively prevents tool overuse and underuse, achieving a superior balance between exploration and efficiency, leading to higher accuracy and controlled tool usage.
> - Turn-level rewards prevent policy entropy collapse by penalizing flawed intermediate steps, sustaining exploration during training and improving overall generalization for longer reasoning horizons.
