# Temporal Straightening for Latent Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced "temporal straightening," a geometric regularization technique that encourages straighter trajectories in latent space, improving representations for model-based reinforcement learning. This method, inspired by the perceptual straightening hypothesis, significantly enhances gradient-based planning performance by creating a latent space where Euclidean distances more accurately reflect true environmental distances, leading to substantial gains in goal-reaching success rates.

## Problem

- Planning in learned latent spaces often involves highly non-convex objectives, hindering efficient gradient-based optimization and forcing reliance on computationally expensive search methods.
- Euclidean distances in existing curved latent spaces can inaccurately represent true geodesic distances, leading to suboptimal action sequences from planners.
- Current representation learning methods, such as reconstruction or frozen pre-trained features, may not effectively optimize the latent space geometry for efficient temporal dynamics and gradient-based planning.

## Method

- A novel =="temporal straightening"== geometric regularization term is proposed, which explicitly minimizes the angle between consecutive latent velocity vectors.
- A world model, comprising a sensory encoder (e.g., ==DINOv2== projector or ResNet) and a ==Vision Transformer== predictor, is trained jointly with a prediction loss and this straightening loss.
- For visual features retaining spatial structure, a learnable pooling head aggregates patch-level representations into a global vector before applying the straightening penalty, balancing local detail with global straightness.

## Results

- Temporal straightening consistently improved goal-reaching success rates by **20–60%** for open-loop planning and **20–30%** for Model Predictive Control with gradient-based optimizers across various 2D environments.
- The method substantially reduced the performance gap between gradient descent and computationally more intensive search-based planning algorithms like CEM, sometimes outperforming unstraightened CEM baselines.
- Learned representations exhibited reduced curvature, and their latent Euclidean distances closely approximated ground-truth environmental geodesic distances, even in environments with non-visual dynamics like Teleported-PointMaze.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly promoting "straighter" latent trajectories (by penalizing curvature) significantly improves the conditioning of the planning objective's Hessian, enabling more efficient gradient-based optimization.
> - Regularizing the latent space geometry creates a more faithful distance metric where Euclidean distances between latent states accurately reflect the true geodesic distances in the environment.
> - The "perceptual straightening hypothesis" from human vision offers a valuable inspiration for designing latent spaces that facilitate efficient planning in artificial intelligence systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of model-based reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on latent world models for planning. Latent world models compress high-dimensional observations (e.g., images) into compact latent representations, learn predictive dynamics in this reduced space, and then use these learned dynamics for action optimization through imaginary rollouts. This approach offers advantages in efficiency and generalization compared to planning directly in pixel or state space.

However, a key challenge in this area is that optimization in learned latent spaces often remains difficult. The induced planning objectives can be highly non-convex, making gradient-based optimizers prone to local minima. Consequently, many successful methods, such as Dreamer and TD-MPC2, rely on search-based techniques like the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) or Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), which are computationally intensive. Additionally, the commonly used Euclidean distance as a goal cost metric can be misleading if the latent space geometry does not accurately reflect the geodesic (path-dependent) distance between states. This discrepancy arises particularly when feasible latent trajectories are highly curved.

Existing approaches to learning representations for world models include:
- **Reconstruction-based objectives**: These aim to reconstruct the original high-dimensional observations from their latent forms. The paper notes that these methods can overemphasize low-level visual details irrelevant to planning.
- **Pretrained visual encoders**: Approaches like DINO-WM leverage powerful, general-purpose pretrained visual encoders (e.g., DINOv2) to provide rich semantic features. While effective, these features are not intrinsically optimized for the specific dynamics of a given environment, potentially leading to challenging planning objectives.
- **Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)**: This paradigm focuses on learning representations by predicting future or masked latent states, rather than reconstructing pixels. JEPAs aim to capture predictable structure without retaining unpredictable low-level details, showing promise for predictive modeling and planning. The current work builds upon the JEPA framework.
- **Temporal Contrastive Learning**: This method seeks to align embeddings based on temporal proximity, encouraging nearby frames to have similar embeddings and distant ones to be dissimilar. However, its effectiveness can depend on careful selection of positive and negative samples, and it may not accurately reflect geodesic distances in all cases, particularly with suboptimal training trajectories.

This research distinguishes itself by drawing inspiration from the "perceptual straightening hypothesis" in human vision, which suggests that biological visual systems transform complex natural video inputs into straighter internal representations. Prior work has explored implicit straightening in pretrained visual encoders or used it as an objective for robust video models, but its impact on world modeling and planning had not been extensively studied. This paper introduces an explicit regularization method to encourage temporal straightening of latent trajectories specifically for the purpose of facilitating gradient-based latent planning.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the quality of representations learned by latent world models to facilitate more effective and stable gradient-based planning. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations of current approaches to latent planning:

1. **Challenging Optimization Landscape**: Planning in learned latent spaces often involves optimizing a highly non-convex objective function. This non-convexity poses difficulties for gradient-based optimization algorithms, leading them to get stuck in local minima or exhibit slow convergence. This issue necessitates the use of computationally intensive search-based methods (e.g., CEM) which introduce significant compute burden and latency. The authors aim to create a latent space where the planning objective is better conditioned, making gradient-based methods more reliable and efficient.

2. **Misleading Euclidean Distances**: In existing latent spaces, the Euclidean distance between latent states may not accurately reflect the true "geodesic" distance or the minimum number of actions required to transition between those states. This is particularly problematic when latent trajectories corresponding to feasible transitions are highly curved. When Euclidean distance is used as a cost function for goal-reaching tasks, it can provide inaccurate gradients, guiding the planner away from optimal paths. The research seeks to regularize the latent space such that Euclidean distances more faithfully approximate geodesic distances along feasible trajectories.

3. **Suboptimal Representation for Planning**: While general-purpose visual encoders provide strong semantic features, they are not specifically tailored to capture the dynamics of an environment or to filter out information irrelevant to planning. Such extraneous information can hinder dynamics learning and downstream action optimization. The motivation is to learn representations that are:
 - **Sufficient for dynamics prediction**: They should capture all necessary information to accurately predict future states given actions.
 - **Concise**: They should exclude task-irrelevant low-level details or noise.
 - **Regularized for planning**: Their geometry should actively support efficient planning, especially gradient-based methods.

Inspired by the perceptual straightening hypothesis, the authors propose that by encouraging latent trajectories to be "straighter," the Euclidean distance becomes a better proxy for geodesic distance, and the planning objective becomes better conditioned. This would enable the full potential of differentiable latent world models for efficient, gradient-based action optimization, circumventing the need for computationally expensive search methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed "temporal straightening," involves jointly learning an encoder and a predictor within a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) world model, while imposing regularization on the curvature of latent trajectories during training.

#### 3.1. World Model Architecture

The world model comprises three main components:
- **Sensory Encoder (E_s_ϕ)**: This component maps raw high-dimensional observations (o_t) into compact latent representations (z_t ∈ R^d). For visual observations, the encoder can be a pre-trained backbone (like DINOv2) followed by a lightweight projector, or a ResNet trained from scratch.
- **Action Encoder (E_a_ψ)**: This maps actions (a_t ∈ R^n_a) into latent action embeddings (R^d_a).
- **Predictor (f_θ)**: This models transitions in the latent space. It takes a history of K past latent states and their corresponding action embeddings to predict the next latent state (ˆz_t). A Vision Transformer (ViT) is used as the predictor, often conditioning on both visual and proprioceptive states.

#### 3.2. Straightening Latent Trajectories

The core of the method is a geometric regularization that encourages "straight" latent trajectories.
- Given three consecutive latent representations (z_t, z_{t+1}, z_{t+2}), approximate latent velocity vectors are defined as v_t = z_{t+1} - z_t and v_{t+1} = z_{t+2} - z_{t+1}.
- Curvature is penalized by minimizing the angle between these consecutive velocity vectors, or equivalently, by maximizing their cosine similarity (C = (v_t · v_{t+1}) / (||v_t||_2 · ||v_{t+1}||_2)).
- For spatial features (where latent representations have spatial dimensions), the cosine similarity can be computed per-patch and averaged, on flattened features, on average-pooled features, or using a learnable aggregation head to derive a global feature before computing similarity. The learnable aggregation head is chosen for main experiments.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

The world model parameters (ϕ, ψ, θ) are trained jointly using a total objective (L_total) that combines a prediction loss and a straightening loss:
- **Prediction Objective (L_pred)**: A Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is used between the predicted latent state (ˆz_{t+1}) and the target latent state (z_{t+1}). A stop-gradient (sg) operation is applied to the target (sg(z_{t+1})) to prevent representational collapse and stabilize training, similar to practices in self-supervised learning.
 L_pred = ||ˆz_{t+1} - sg(z_{t+1})||^2_2
- **Straightening Objective (L_curv)**: This objective directly minimizes the negative cosine similarity of consecutive latent velocity vectors:
 L_curv = 1 - C
- **Overall Objective**: The total training objective is a weighted sum:
 L_total = L_pred + λL_curv, where λ is a hyperparameter controlling the strength of straightening.

#### 3.4. Planning with Straightened Dynamics (Theoretical Analysis)

The paper provides a theoretical analysis to explain how straightening improves gradient-based planning.
- **Linear Latent Dynamics Assumption**: For analysis, a simplified linear latent dynamics model (z_{t+1} = Az_t + Ba_t) is considered.
- **ε-Straight Transition**: A transition is defined as ε-straight if ||A - I||_2 ≤ ε, meaning the dynamics matrix A is close to the identity matrix, implying that the state evolves nearly linearly, modified by control input.
- **Conditioning of Planning Hessian**: For goal-reaching tasks minimizing terminal MSE (L(a) = ||z_K - z_g||_2^2), the planning Hessian (∇^2_a L(a)) is shown to be related to the finite-horizon controllability Gramian (W_K). The effective condition number of the Hessian (κ_eff(H)) is bounded by factors related to the condition numbers of A and B, and grows with the horizon K.
- **Impact of Straightening**: If the transition is ε-straight (i.e., A is close to I), the bound on κ_eff(H) becomes significantly smaller (e.g., κ_eff(H) ≤ κ(B)^2 * ( (1+ε)/(1-ε) )^(2(K-1)) ). A smaller condition number implies that the loss landscape is less non-convex, facilitating faster convergence for gradient descent optimizers.
- **Cosine Similarity as Proxy**: The cosine similarity (L_curv) is presented as a practical proxy to encourage A to be close to I along visited velocity directions, especially under assumptions of constant velocity magnitudes and smooth actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of temporal straightening was evaluated across a suite of goal-reaching tasks, including Wall, PointMaze (UMaze and Medium-Maze), and PushT environments. An additional custom environment, Teleported-PointMaze, was introduced to test the model's ability to learn non-intuitive dynamics.

#### 4.1. Analysis of Embedding Space Quality

The authors first investigated the properties of the learned embedding spaces before and after applying temporal straightening.
- **Reduced Curvature**:
 - Pretrained DINOv2 features exhibit high curvature, as visualized by PCA plots and indicated by low cosine similarities of velocity vectors (Figure 5, Figure 2).
 - Training a JEPA world model, even without explicit straightening, induces some degree of "implicit straightening," improving the linearity of trajectories. This is attributed to the JEPA objective favoring easily predictable temporal evolutions.
 - Explicit curvature regularization (L_curv) consistently and significantly further reduces the curvature of latent trajectories.
 - Training a ResNet encoder from scratch generally yields lower curvatures compared to using a frozen DINOv2 backbone with a projector, suggesting greater flexibility for adapting the geometry.
 - Applying straightening to features aggregated by a learnable head was found to be more effective than per-patch or flattened feature straightening, especially for complex motions like in PushT.
- **Faithful Distance Representation**:
 - Euclidean distances in pretrained DINOv2 feature spaces often do not accurately reflect geodesic distances (Figure 6).
 - After temporal straightening, the Euclidean distance in the latent space becomes a more faithful proxy for the geodesic distance, closely aligning with ground-truth shortest path distances computed via A-star. This implies that the model learns to approximate the minimum number of steps to transition between states, even when trained on suboptimal trajectories.
 - Spatial features and aggregated global features capture different aspects of distance: spatial features provide local, fine-grained distance variations, while global features offer smoother, more coherent long-range signals.
- **Sufficient Information Preservation**:
 - Decoders trained to reconstruct images from the learned latents demonstrated high-fidelity reconstruction even from aggressively compressed features (e.g., 14x14x8 dimensions). This indicates that the straightened representations retain sufficient task-relevant information for planning.

#### 4.2. Planning Performance

The core evaluation involved comparing planning success rates using gradient-based planners in both open-loop and Model Predictive Control (MPC) settings.
- **Significant Improvement in Success Rates**: Temporal straightening consistently led to substantial improvements in goal-reaching success rates across all tested environments and model configurations (Table 1).
 - Open-loop planning success rates improved by 20–60%.
 - MPC success rates improved by 20–30%.
 - For instance, in UMaze, open-loop success increased from 44% to 94% with a projector-based encoder, and from 14.67% to 64.67% with a ResNet from scratch.
- **Efficiency of Gradient Descent (GD) Planner**: Straightening enabled GD planners to achieve high success rates rapidly, sometimes reaching 100% success in Wall and UMaze environments within a few MPC steps (Figure 8). This suggests that the improved latent space geometry makes GD a more competitive and efficient alternative to search-based methods.
- **GD vs. CEM Comparison**: While CEM generally achieved higher absolute success rates than GD, straightening significantly reduced the performance gap between the two. This makes GD a more viable option, offering a better trade-off between success rate and computational latency (Table 5, Figure 10).
- **Effect of Feature Dimensions**: Aggressively reducing the channel dimension of DINOv2 features (e.g., from 384 to 8) did not degrade performance, and sometimes improved it, provided spatial structure was preserved. Conversely, excessively high dimensions could hinder planning. Collapsing patch features into a single global vector negatively impacted performance, as it made precise dynamics prediction more difficult.
- **Comparison with Other Regularizations**: Alternative temporal regularizations such as smoothness or temporal contrastive objectives did not yield comparable improvements in planning performance (Figure 12).
- **Long-Horizon Planning**: For longer planning horizons (50 steps), success rates naturally decreased but straightening consistently outperformed baselines. The study also found that combining local spatial goal costs with global aggregated feature costs could further improve long-horizon MPC (Table 2).
- **Teleported-PointMaze**: In this environment with non-intuitive teleportation dynamics, the straightened model demonstrated the ability to plan paths that exploited the teleportation mechanism (Figure 27), rather than simply relying on visual similarity. This indicates that the straightening objective helps the model capture the true dynamics of the environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of latent planning within model-based reinforcement learning by demonstrating the effectiveness of explicitly regularizing the geometry of learned latent representations.

#### 5.1. Core Contributions and Mechanisms

The central finding is that temporal straightening, achieved by penalizing the curvature of latent trajectories during world model training, substantially improves gradient-based planning. This improvement stems from two key mechanisms:
1. **Better-Conditioned Planning Objectives**: By encouraging latent trajectories to be straighter, the non-convexity of the planning objective in the latent space is reduced. The theoretical analysis provides a foundation for this, showing that ε-straight dynamics lead to better-conditioned planning Hessians, which translates to more stable and faster convergence for gradient-based optimization.
2. **Faithful Distance Metrics**: The straightened latent space results in Euclidean distances that more accurately reflect the true geodesic distances between states. This property ensures that the planning cost function provides more reliable gradients, guiding the agent towards optimal goal-reaching paths.

#### 5.2. Practical Implications

- **Enhanced Gradient-Based Planning**: The work mitigates a major challenge in latent planning by making gradient-based optimizers significantly more effective and stable. This could reduce the reliance on computationally expensive search-based methods (like CEM), offering a more efficient alternative for real-world applications where low latency is critical.
- **Improved Representation Learning**: It highlights that representation quality for planning is not solely about semantic richness or reconstruction fidelity, but also critically depends on the geometry of the latent space and its alignment with environmental dynamics. This suggests a valuable direction for designing future representation learning objectives tailored for control tasks.
- **Broader Applicability**: The method is shown to be compatible with both pre-trained visual encoders and models trained from scratch, offering flexibility in implementation.

#### 5.3. Future Directions and Potential Impact

The findings open several avenues for future research:
- **Asymmetric and Irreversible Dynamics**: Investigating whether the benefits extend to environments with asymmetric or irreversible dynamics, potentially requiring non-Euclidean planning costs (e.g., quasimetrics).
- **Decoupled Representation Spaces**: Exploring architectures where the world model learns dynamics in one latent space, while planning objectives are optimized in a different, possibly projected, geometry-aware space.
- **More Complex Environments**: Extending the evaluation to 3D environments, robotic manipulation with more complex dynamics, or real-world data.

From a broader societal perspective, improved latent planning capabilities could have significant beneficial applications in areas such as robotics (e.g., more robust and efficient robot manipulation), autonomous systems (e.g., self-driving cars navigating complex scenarios), and potentially scientific discovery (e.g., designing experiments). However, the authors also acknowledge the importance of considering safety implications when deploying such advanced systems in real-world settings, advocating for continued research into safe and responsible deployment.
