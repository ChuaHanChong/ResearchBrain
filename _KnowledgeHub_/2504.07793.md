# Revisiting Likelihood-Based Out-of-Distribution Detection by Modeling Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Linköping University developed a method to re-evaluate likelihood-based out-of-distribution (OOD) detection by applying score-based diffusion models to learned representation spaces from pre-trained encoders. This approach demonstrates that these methods can achieve state-of-the-art performance, with the class-conditional variant reaching an average AUROC of 96.35% and FPR95 of 17.06% on the ImageNet benchmark, challenging the previous perception of their ineffectiveness.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks exhibit unpredictable behavior on out-of-distribution (OOD) data, posing reliability and safety concerns in real-world applications.
- Traditional explicit likelihood-based generative models often failed at OOD detection in raw image space, assigning higher likelihoods to OOD data, leading the community to prioritize post-hoc methods.
- Many current state-of-the-art OOD detection methods rely on supervised training and labeled in-distribution data, limiting their applicability in label-scarce domains or with self-supervised foundation models.

## Method

- Introduces the **Representation Diffusion Model (RDM)**, which estimates data density using a ==score-based diffusion model== trained on learned representations from pre-trained image encoders, rather than raw pixel data.
- Leverages the precise ==likelihood computation== capabilities of score-based diffusion models via the ==probability flow ODE== formulation.
- Develops a class-conditional variant, **ConRDM**, which utilizes in-distribution labels to guide the diffusion model, further enhancing OOD detection performance by modeling class-specific likelihoods.

## Results

- The Representation Diffusion Model (RDM) achieved competitive label-free OOD detection, with an average AUROC of **93.16%** and FPR95 of **32.17%** on the ImageNet benchmark when paired with a DINOv2 encoder.
- ConRDM, the class-conditional variant, surpassed all benchmarked methods with supervised ViT encoders, achieving an average AUROC of **96.35%** and FPR95 of **17.06%** on the ImageNet OOD benchmark.
- The method demonstrated computational efficiency, with training taking approximately **12 minutes** on an RTX4090 GPU and likelihood estimation achieving a throughput of **1500 representations/sec**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The historical failures of likelihood-based OOD detection were not due to an inherent flaw in the likelihood principle but rather from applying it in raw pixel space, where low-level features obscure semantic shifts.
> - Likelihood functions effectively as an OOD score when applied to semantically clustered representation spaces generated by modern pre-trained encoders.
> - More accurate likelihood estimation in the representation space, as provided by score-based diffusion models, directly correlates with improved OOD detection performance.
