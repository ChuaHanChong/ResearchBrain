# CoMo: Learning Continuous Latent Motion from Internet Videos for Scalable Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> CoMo introduces a self-supervised framework for learning continuous latent motion from internet videos, employing early temporal difference and temporal contrastive learning to overcome 'shortcut learning' without discrete quantization. This approach provides effective pseudo action labels, boosting robot policy success rates on benchmarks like LIBERO by 4.2 percentage points and demonstrating robust performance in real-world fine manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robot learning suffers from data scarcity, low diversity, and high heterogeneity, limiting generalization compared to vision and language models.
- Existing latent motion learning methods from videos often rely on discrete (VQ-VAE) representations, which lead to information loss and limit generalization to continuous, fine-grained real-world motion patterns.
- Directly learning continuous latent motion from action-less videos is prone to 'shortcut learning,' where the model encodes excessive static background information from future frames instead of focusing on relevant foreground dynamics.

## Method

- **CoMo** employs an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) with an early Temporal Difference (Td) mechanism that explicitly enhances dynamic motion cues by performing element-wise subtraction of future and current frame features.
- Temporal ==Contrastive Learning== (Tcl) is introduced to structure continuous latent motion representations, ensuring they focus on meaningful foregrounds and mitigate the encoding of action-irrelevant static backgrounds.
- The learned continuous latent motion representations serve as effective pseudo action labels, enabling joint training of a unified generative robot policy with both real robot data and large-scale action-less internet videos.

## Results

- CoMo improved average success rates on the LIBERO benchmark by **4.2** percentage points (from **75.9%** to **80.1%**) over discrete baselines, demonstrating the effectiveness of its pseudo action labeling.
- CoMo achieved the lowest Action Prediction MSE and a significantly lower Past-to-Current and Future-to-Current Similarity (S-PCFC) of 0.5496 compared to a naive continuous baseline's 0.9268, indicating better motion fidelity and reduced background noise.
- The framework demonstrated generalizability and superior performance across various simulated robot embodiments (single-arm, dual-arm, humanoid) and in real-world Franka robot tasks requiring fine manipulation, such as opening a drawer and inserting bread.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The synergy between early temporal differencing (Td) and temporal contrastive learning (Tcl) is essential for robustly learning continuous, action-relevant latent motion from action-less videos while effectively preventing 'shortcut learning.'
> - Achieving high robot policy success rates correlates with a balanced trade-off in latent motion representation, capturing relevant information (low Action Prediction MSE) while suppressing action-irrelevant background noise (low Past-to-Current and Future-to-Current Similarity - S-PCFC).
> - Continuous latent motion, when properly regularized by Td and Tcl, allows for scalable dimensionality without introducing excessive noise, leading to improved downstream policy performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning often encounters limitations due to data scarcity, low diversity, and high heterogeneity in comparison to the vast datasets available for vision and language models. A current research paradigm for scaling robot learning involves extracting latent motion models from extensive video data. These methods commonly employ an inverse dynamics encoder-forward dynamics decoder (IDM-FDM) architecture, coupled with a self-supervised reconstruction objective and a VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder) objective, to generate pseudo action labels from unlabeled video data.

Previous works have predominantly utilized discrete latent motion representations, often with a small codebook size (e.g., 8 or 16), to address the "shortcut learning" problem. Shortcut learning occurs when models, in an attempt to easily reconstruct future frames, capture excessive static background information rather than focusing on the dynamic foreground motion relevant to actions. The quantization into a small discrete codebook is intended to abstract away fine-grained details and force the model to represent only rough motion trends.

However, this reliance on discrete representations presents several limitations. Real-world motion is inherently continuous and encompasses complex, fine-grained dynamics. Discrete codebooks inevitably lead to information loss, restricting the model's ability to capture subtle movements and generalize to novel motion patterns. Furthermore, an inherent distributional gap exists between discrete latent motion and the continuous nature of robot actions, which complicates the joint learning of a unified policy. Research in visual generation and robot learning has suggested that continuous representations can lead to improved performance.

This paper positions itself by addressing the challenge of learning continuous latent motion directly from action-less videos, without relying on vector quantization. It aims to mitigate the shortcut learning problem typically associated with continuous representations, thereby enabling more precise and scalable motion capture. By doing so, CoMo seeks to bridge the gap between video-derived motion and continuous robot actions, facilitating a more direct and unified approach to policy learning that leverages large-scale internet video data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of CoMo is to learn precise, continuous latent motion representations from internet-scale videos in an unsupervised manner. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Overcome Shortcut Learning in Continuous Latent Motion**: Directly learning continuous latent motion from videos is susceptible to "shortcut learning," where the model prioritizes reconstructing static background information rather than extracting action-relevant foreground motion. Existing discrete methods address this via vector quantization, but CoMo aims to achieve this for continuous representations. The motivation is to develop mechanisms that increase the difficulty for the inverse dynamics encoder to exploit static backgrounds, thereby ensuring it focuses on genuine motion cues.

2. **Capture Fine-Grained and Complex Dynamics**: Discrete latent motion, with its limited codebook size, leads to information loss and struggles to represent the nuanced, continuous nature of real-world movements. A key motivation is to enable the model to capture more fine-grained and informative inter-frame changes, which is crucial for complex robotic manipulation tasks requiring precision.

3. **Facilitate Unified Policy Learning**: There is an inherent mismatch between discrete latent motion and the continuous control signals of robots. Learning continuous latent motion aims to provide better consistency with continuous robot actions, which directly simplifies the joint learning of a unified policy. This removes the need for complex, multi-stage pretraining and finetuning procedures or explicit two-stage pipelines previously employed to reconcile these different data distributions.

4. **Enhance Scalability and Generalization for Robot Learning**: By developing a method that can effectively learn continuous latent motion from a vast array of action-less internet videos, the research aims to provide a scalable paradigm for robot learning. This involves generating high-quality pseudo action labels for diverse, unlabeled video data, enabling robots to learn from a much richer and larger data source than typically available through robot-specific demonstrations. A related motivation is to ensure the learned representations generalize well to unseen videos and different robotic tasks and embodiments.

5. **Develop Robust Evaluation Metrics**: The paper also aims to introduce more direct, stable, and cost-effective quantitative analysis tools to evaluate learned motion representations independently of downstream policy performance. This is motivated by the observation that policy success rates can be susceptible to external factors, and a more direct measure of motion encoding capacity and background noise is needed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CoMo framework is built upon an inverse dynamics model (IDM) and a forward dynamics model (FDM) architecture. It introduces two main mechanisms: an early temporal difference (Td) strategy and a temporal contrastive learning (Tcl) scheme, designed to learn continuous, fine-grained latent motion from videos without vector quantization.

#### 3.1. Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) with Early Temporal Difference (Td) Mechanism

The IDM's primary role is to extract precise, background-irrelevant continuous motion information.
- **Feature Extraction**: Given a current frame $O_t$ and a future frame $O_{t+n}$, a shared MAE-pretrained ViT (Vision Transformer) extracts token-level features, $F_t$ and $F_{t+n}$, respectively.
- **Temporal Difference**: To explicitly enhance dynamic motion cues, an element-wise subtraction operation is performed between $F_t$ and $F_{t+n}$ to obtain token-level temporal feature differences, $D_t$.
- **Shortcut Learning Mitigation**: Crucially, the direct encoding of the future frame features $F_{t+n}$ as an input to the motion encoder is removed. Instead, only the current frame features $F_t$ and the temporal feature differences $D_t$ are concatenated, forming the combined representation $[F_t, D_t]$. This design choice aims to increase the difficulty for the model to engage in shortcut learning by reducing its ability to simply reconstruct static backgrounds from the future frame.
- **Motion Q-former**: Following the approach of Moto-GPT, a set of learnable query embeddings are concatenated with the $[F_t, D_t]$ representation. These are then processed through standard multi-layer Transformer blocks (termed Motion Q-former). The query features from the Transformer's output constitute the motion representation $Z_{t,t+n}$.

#### 3.2. Latent Motion Learning with Temporal Contrastive Learning (Tcl) Scheme

While the early Td mechanism enhances motion cues and increases the difficulty of shortcut learning, the sparse feature differences alone might still contain irrelevant information. To address this and further structure the latent motion to focus on meaningful foregrounds, CoMo incorporates a temporal contrastive learning mechanism.
- **Pair Construction**:
 - **Positive Pairs**: A frame $O_{t+n+\delta}$ is sampled near $O_{t+n}$, where $\delta$ is a small temporal offset (practically set to range from $-n/5$ to $n/5$). The motion representations $Z_{t,t+n+\delta}$ and $Z_{t,t+n}$ are treated as a positive pair. This encourages similarity between motion representations that are temporally close.
 - **Negative Pairs**: Motion representations with reversed temporal direction between the current and future frames are used as negative pairs. Specifically, $Z_{t+n,t}$ is generated by passing $O_{t+n}$ as the "current" frame and $O_t$ as the "future" frame to the IDM. The pairs $(Z_{t,t+n+\delta}, Z_{t+n,t})$ and $(Z_{t,t+n}, Z_{t+n,t})$ are considered negative. This encourages dissimilarity for motions perceived in opposite temporal directions.
- **Loss Function**: The InfoNCE loss is applied to maximize the agreement between positive pairs and disagreement with negative pairs.
- **Synergistic Effect**: The Td and Tcl mechanisms are designed to work synergistically. Td provides strong motion cues and makes shortcut learning difficult. Tcl then refines these cues, ensuring the continuous motion representations focus on foreground regions and capture action-relevant temporal patterns ("how" the motion occurs), rather than just foreground object identity ("what" and "where"). This collaborative approach allows CoMo to learn continuous motion without needing strict vector quantization constraints.

#### 3.3. Forward Dynamics Model (FDM)

The FDM is responsible for reconstructing the future frame $O_{t+n}$ given the current frame $O_t$ and the latent motion representation $Z_{t,t+n}$ from the IDM.
- **Reconstruction Process**: A low-level patch embedding of $O_t$ is first obtained. This is then combined with a pooled version of the motion representation $Z_{t,t+n}$. Several Transformer layers process these combined features. Finally, convolutional layers and a pixel shuffling operation are used to reconstruct the predicted future frame $\hat{O}_{t+n}$.
- **Training Objective**: The overall CoMo framework is trained by minimizing a weighted combination of the InfoNCE loss ($L_{tcl}$), a reconstruction loss (e.g., L1 or L2) for pixel-level accuracy of the FDM, and a perceptual loss to ensure perceptual fidelity of the predicted frames.

#### 3.4. Joint Unified Policy Learning

After CoMo is trained to extract latent motion, it is used to augment action-less video data for joint policy learning with robot demonstration data.
- **Pseudo Action Label Generation**: For a given action-less video dataset $D_V$, the trained CoMo IDM extracts continuous latent motion embeddings $z_t$ for each time step $t$. This transforms each video trajectory into an augmented trajectory $[(o_0, z_0), \dots, (o_T, z_T)]$.
- **Unified Generative Policy**: Since both robot actions ($a_t$) and CoMo's latent motions ($z_t$) are continuous, they can be jointly used for imitation learning within a unified generative policy. The combined dataset $D_R \cup D_V$ (robot data and augmented video data) is used for training.
- **Architecture**: This flexible co-training strategy typically involves allocating separate, lightweight heads within the unified policy model for handling robot actions and video latent motions.
- **Policy Architectures Explored**: The paper applies this joint learning approach to both diffusion-based and auto-regressive policy architectures.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics

To provide a direct and stable evaluation of learned motion representations, CoMo proposes two metrics:
- **Action Prediction MSE (MSE)**: An MLP is trained to regress ground-truth robot actions from the offline-extracted latent motion embeddings. The resulting Mean Squared Error (MSE) assesses the latent motion's capacity to encode action-relevant information.
- **Past-to-Current and Future-to-Current Similarity (S-PCFC)**: This metric measures the cosine similarity between $z(O_{t-n}, O_t)$ (motion from past to current) and $z(O_{t+n}, O_t)$ (motion from future to current). It is computed on motion-centric robot datasets to minimize background variations. A lower S-PCFC indicates better motion fidelity and less action-irrelevant background noise, as high-dimensional latent motion can introduce redundancy. The paper posits that the combination of low MSE and low S-PCFC correlates with higher downstream policy success rates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments to evaluate CoMo. Key findings include:

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of CoMo in Generating Pseudo Action Labels (Q1)

- **Simulation (LIBERO)**: CoMo, trained on large-scale out-of-domain internet videos (SAM-V, EgoVid, Droid), demonstrated zero-shot cross-domain transfer capability to simulated robotic scenarios. Incorporating video data with CoMo pseudo labels into a diffusion-based policy increased the average success rate on LIBERO from 70.4% (without videos) to 80.1%.
- **Simulation (CALVIN)**: For an auto-regressive policy on CALVIN, CoMo joint learning improved performance from 1.878 (w/o motion) to 3.070, demonstrating its utility for long-horizon tasks.
- **Conclusion**: CoMo effectively generates pseudo action labels for action-less video data, leading to enhanced policy performance due to richer data sources and supervision.

#### 4.2. Individual and Synergistic Contributions of Td and Tcl (Q2, Q3)

- **Ablation Studies on LIBERO (Table 1)**:
 - **Naive Continuous Baseline ('Con.')**: Simply removing vector quantization resulted in severe shortcut learning, indicated by high MSE (5.6743) and high S-PCFC (0.9268). Its average success rate was 75.2%.
 - **Temporal Difference ('Con.+Td')**: Adding Td reduced MSE to 1.6256 and slightly reduced S-PCFC (to 0.9245-0.9124 per task type), improving the success rate to 76.9%. This showed Td’s role in enhancing motion cues and increasing the difficulty of shortcut learning.
 - **Temporal Contrastive Learning ('Con.+Tcl')**: Adding Tcl alone also reduced MSE to 1.5209 and significantly reduced S-PCFC to 0.8887, resulting in an average success rate of 77.9%. This highlighted Tcl's role in structuring representations and focusing on foreground.
 - **CoMo ('Con.+Td+Tcl')**: The combination of Td and Tcl achieved the best performance: lowest MSE (1.2588), lowest S-PCFC among continuous methods (0.5496), and highest average success rate (80.1%). This indicates a synergistic effect where both mechanisms collaboratively mitigate shortcut learning and refine motion representations.
- **Qualitative Visualization (Figure 2, 6)**: Visualizations confirmed that the naive continuous baseline's future frame predictions contained static background noise from prompt videos. Td mitigated this, and Tcl further refined the latent motion to accurately capture fine-grained foreground movements, resulting in more precise future frame predictions.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Discrete Latent Motion and Other Methods (Q3)

- **Discrete vs. Continuous**: CoMo (continuous) consistently outperformed the discrete latent motion baseline ('Dis.', 75.9% S.R.) by a notable margin (80.1% S.R.) on LIBERO. While discrete motion achieved the lowest S-PCFC (1.0000, likely due to forced abstraction), its MSE was higher, and success rate was lower, especially in fine-grained tasks. This suggests continuous representations, when properly learned, avoid information loss and offer better policy performance.
- **Comparison (Table 3)**: CoMo achieved the highest average success rate (80.1%) on LIBERO compared to ATM (65.7%), GR2-like (73.8%), GR00T-like (75.9%), and Dynamo (73.7%). This positions CoMo's latent motion as a more effective predictive signal for improving robot policies.

#### 4.4. Scalability of Latent Motion Dimension (Q4)

- **Figure 3 and Table 2 ('x4')**: When the latent motion dimension was increased with only Td, MSE initially decreased but S-PCFC increased, indicating the introduction of action-irrelevant noise. In contrast, CoMo (Td + Tcl) maintained a consistently low S-PCFC and a decreasing MSE as the dimension increased, demonstrating its ability to scale the latent motion dimension without adverse effects. Using higher-dimensional latent motion (e.g., 512 vs 128) for CoMo further improved CALVIN policy success rates (3.247 vs 3.070). This scalability is crucial for complex tasks.

#### 4.5. Efficacy of MSE and S-PCFC as Evaluation Metrics (Q5)

- **Correlation with Policy Success (Table 1)**: The paper observed that reducing either MSE or S-PCFC alone did not guarantee better policy performance. Optimal policy success rates were achieved when both metrics were relatively low, indicating a balanced trade-off between encoding action-relevant information and suppressing noise. For all continuous approaches, the average rankings of MSE, S-PCFC, and policy success rate were consistent (Con. < Con.+Td < Con.+Tcl < CoMo). This suggests these metrics are reliable indicators of downstream policy performance.

#### 4.6. Generalizability Across Complex Embodiments (Q6)

- **Dual-arm and Humanoid Robots (Table 4)**: The proposed Td and Tcl mechanisms demonstrated effectiveness in reducing Action Prediction MSE for more complex embodiments, including dual-arm robots (14-dimensional absolute action space) and humanoid robots with dexterous hands (128-dimensional absolute action space). This indicates CoMo's generalizability beyond single-arm, relative action space settings.

#### 4.7. Real-World Experiments (Table 5)

- CoMo improved policy success rates in several real-world Franka robot tasks, such as "Open Drawer" (from 45.0% to 60.0%) and "Insert Bread" (from 25.0% to 35.0%), compared to discrete and naive continuous baselines. This advantage was particularly evident in low-tolerance tasks requiring fine manipulation, validating CoMo's ability to extract effective pseudo-action labels from human hand videos for real-world control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CoMo framework offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robot learning:

1. **Advancement in Unsupervised Latent Motion Learning**: CoMo addresses a core challenge in robot learning by enabling the unsupervised learning of continuous, fine-grained, and information-rich latent motion representations directly from internet videos. This overcomes the limitations of discrete representations, such as information loss and inability to capture complex dynamics, while mitigating the "shortcut learning" problem inherent in continuous approaches.

2. **Enhanced Scalability for Robot Learning**: By providing a robust method for generating effective pseudo action labels from vast, action-less internet video datasets, CoMo offers a scalable paradigm for robot learning. This allows robotic systems to leverage a much larger and more diverse data source than traditional robot-specific demonstrations, which are often scarce and costly to collect.

3. **Facilitation of Unified Policy Learning**: The continuous nature of CoMo's latent motion representation inherently aligns with continuous robot actions. This consistency simplifies the joint learning of unified policies, eliminating the need for complex multi-stage pretraining, fine-tuning procedures, or action discretization that have been bottlenecks in previous approaches. This streamlines the development of generalist robot policies.

4. **Improved Policy Performance**: The demonstrated superior performance of CoMo-guided policies in both simulated benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN, dual-arm, humanoid) and real-world manipulation tasks suggests that more precise and continuous latent motion representations lead to more capable and robust robot control, especially for fine-grained operations.

5. **Introduction of Robust Evaluation Tools**: The proposed MSE and S-PCFC metrics provide a more direct, stable, and cost-effective method for quantitatively analyzing and evaluating latent motion representations. These metrics can guide future research and development in this area by offering a clear correlation with downstream policy success rates.

6. **Broad Generalizability**: CoMo's effectiveness across various robot embodiments (single-arm, dual-arm, humanoid) and diverse action spaces (relative, absolute, high-dimensional) indicates its broad applicability and potential for widespread adoption in different robotic platforms and manipulation scenarios.

Overall, CoMo contributes to the development of more scalable, data-efficient, and capable robot learning systems by providing a method to effectively translate the richness of internet video data into actionable, continuous motion understanding for robotic agents. Future work could explore scaling CoMo to multi-frame and multi-view modeling to address complex real-world challenges like occlusions and further enhance motion extraction accuracy, and constructing more complex benchmarks for fine-grained manipulation.
