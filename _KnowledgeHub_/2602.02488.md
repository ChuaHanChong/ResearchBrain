# RLAnything: Forge Environment, Policy, and Reward Model in Completely Dynamic RL System

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced RLAnything, a framework that establishes a completely dynamic reinforcement learning system, enabling the environment, policy, and reward model to continuously and jointly optimize each other. This integrated approach amplifies learning signals, leading to significant performance gains across diverse LLM agent tasks, including up to an 18.7% accuracy increase on Alf World and a 9.1% increase on OSWorld.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) struggles with reward sparsity in long-trajectory, multi-turn tasks, providing insufficient supervision for effective policy learning.
- Training generative reward models for nuanced step-wise feedback is bottlenecked by the expensive and unscalable manual collection of high-quality, task-specific supervision data.
- Existing RL systems lack integrated, closed-loop mechanisms for jointly adapting environment difficulty, optimizing policy, and improving reward models, hindering overall system strength and generalization.

## Method

- RLAnything employs an =='integrated feedback' mechanism== for policy training, combining sparse final outcome rewards with rich, step-wise signals generated by the reward model.
- The reward model is optimized using =='consistency feedback'==, where its step-level evaluations are trained to align with the combined overall quality of a step, guided by theoretical guarantees.
- Environment adaptation is driven by =='critic feedback'==, where a language model modifies task difficulty based on the policy's rollout accuracy and error patterns summarized from the reward model's negative step-wise evaluations.

## Results

- RLAnything achieved substantial performance gains, with Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking's accuracy on OSWorld increasing by **9.1%** and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's accuracy on Alf World increasing by **18.7%**.
- The jointly optimized reward model demonstrated improved 'process accuracy' and 'outcome accuracy,' with its automatically generated step-wise signals outperforming external human-labeled outcome rewards for policy training.
- The framework enhanced agentic coding performance across methods like MPSC and AlphaCodium, improving code pass rates and the quality of generated unit tests, while policy models exhibited longer chain-of-thought and more stable reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The co-optimization of environment, policy, and reward model within a closed-loop system leads to a self-reinforcing learning cycle, consistently improving each component and overall system performance.
> - Integrated rewards, which combine sparse outcome signals with nuanced step-wise feedback, are critical for effective policy training in complex, long-trajectory tasks.
> - Automated, critic-guided environment adaptation significantly enhances active learning, providing targeted task perturbations that balance difficulty and improve both policy exploration and reward model training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "RLAnything" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: large language models (LLMs), reinforcement learning (RL), and agentic AI. The broader research landscape is characterized by efforts to imbue LLMs with more robust reasoning, decision-making, and interactive capabilities, moving them from sophisticated text generators to effective autonomous agents.

Historically, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has proven effective for improving the reasoning capabilities of LLMs in tasks like mathematical problem-solving or code generation, where a clear, verifiable outcome signal is available. However, a significant challenge arises when LLMs are tasked with complex, real-world applications that involve iterative interactions over long trajectories, such as controlling a computer's graphical user interface (GUI) or navigating text-based games. In these multi-turn scenarios, binary outcome rewards become sparse, providing insufficient supervision for effective learning.

To address reward sparsity, generative reward models have emerged as a promising direction, providing nuanced, step-wise signals. However, training these models traditionally requires collecting high-quality, task-specific supervision, which is often expensive and unscalable. Concurrently, the quality and adaptability of the environment itself are recognized as crucial for scaling RL. Research has shown that aligning task difficulty with an agent's current capabilities (curriculum learning) and increasing task diversity can significantly improve training dynamics and generalization. Yet, existing systems for environment adaptation or reward modeling often operate in isolation or lack the integrated step-wise signals necessary for long-horizon interactive tasks.

"RLAnything" directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel, unified framework. It aims to bridge the gap between traditional RLVR and the demands of complex agentic scenarios by integrating policy learning, reward model optimization, and environment adaptation into a single, closed-loop system. This holistic approach distinguishes it from prior work that might focus on only one or two of these components. By making the entire RL system dynamic and self-improving, RLAnything seeks to amplify learning signals and create more robust, generalizable LLM agents capable of active learning from their own experiences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a dynamic reinforcement learning framework, named RLAnything, that can jointly and continuously optimize the environment, policy model, and reward model in a closed-loop system. The ultimate goal is to amplify learning signals and strengthen the overall RL system, enabling it to handle diverse and complex LLM or agentic scenarios more effectively.

The motivations driving this objective are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Reward Sparsity in Long-Trajectory Tasks:** Current RLVR methods, while effective for single-turn or short-horizon tasks, struggle with real-world applications involving many interaction steps. In such scenarios, a binary final outcome provides a very sparse learning signal, making it difficult for the policy to learn effective strategies. There is a clear need for richer, more granular feedback.
- **Automating High-Quality Step-wise Supervision:** Generative reward models can provide valuable step-wise feedback, outperforming simpler scalar models. However, the manual collection of high-quality, task-specific supervision data for training these reward models is a significant bottleneck, limiting their scalability and applicability. The authors seek a more automated and scalable method for generating reliable step-wise supervision.
- **Enhancing Learning Dynamics through Environment Quality:** The quality of the training environment, specifically the alignment of task difficulty with the agent's current capabilities, is critical for efficient and effective RL. Existing methods for adapting task difficulty often lack the precision or active learning mechanisms needed to truly benefit both the policy and the reward model simultaneously, especially in dynamic interactive settings.
- **Creating a Synergistic Learning System:** The core question guiding this work is whether a system can be designed where the environment, policy, and reward model constantly provide feedback to each other, leading to a self-reinforcing learning cycle. This closed-loop optimization aims to ensure that improvements in one component immediately benefit the others, leading to a stronger and more adaptive overall system.

By tackling these challenges concurrently within a unified framework, RLAnything aims to unlock greater potential for LLM-based agents to learn and operate autonomously in complex, dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLAnything proposes a tightly coupled, closed-loop optimization framework that dynamically adjusts the policy, reward model, and environment. The methodology can be broken down into three core components:

#### 3.1. Integration Feedback for Policy Training

The policy model ($\pi_\theta$) is trained using an "integrated feedback" mechanism. This combines two types of reward signals for each step ($\tau_i$) within a trajectory ($\tau$):
- **Outcome Reward ($O_\tau$):** A verifiable binary signal (e.g., -1 for failure, +1 for success) obtained at the end of a trajectory.
- **Step-wise Signals ($S_{\tau_i, j}$):** Generated by the reward model, indicating progress or error at each individual step. The reward model provides $m$ independent evaluations for each step.

The combined reward for a step $i$ ($R_{\tau_i}$) is defined as: $R_{\tau_i} = O_\tau + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{m} S_{\tau_i, j} / m$. By default, $\lambda=1$, balancing the influence of outcome and step-wise signals. This integrated reward is then used to compute advantages, which standardize rewards across trajectories at the same step index, facilitating policy updates.

#### 3.2. Consistency Feedback for Reward Model Optimization

The reward model ($r_\phi$) itself is jointly optimized with the policy using a "consistency feedback" mechanism. During policy optimization, the generated policy trajectories also serve as training data for the reward model. For each of its $j$-th evaluations ($S_{\tau_i, j}$) of a step $\tau_i$, the reward model receives a supervision signal ($R_{S_{\tau_i, j}}$) defined as: $R_{S_{\tau_i, j}} = R_{\tau_i} \cdot S_{\tau_i, j}$.
Intuitively, this objective incentivizes the reward model's step-level evaluations ($S_{\tau_i, j}$) to align with the overall quality of that step ($R_{\tau_i}$), which is a combination of its own step-wise assessment and the final outcome. The paper provides theoretical insights (Theorem 1 and Theorem 2) showing that optimizing this objective improves the reward model's accuracy in predicting future outcomes. This enhanced reward model then provides stronger and more informative learning signals back to the policy.

#### 3.3. Environment Adaptation via Critic Feedback

A key innovation of RLAnything is its dynamic environment adaptation, motivated by theoretical findings that balancing task difficulty is crucial for optimizing both the policy and the reward model.
- **Difficulty Estimation:** Task difficulty is estimated using the policy's rollout accuracy on a given task.
- **Adaptive Mechanism:** When the policy's accuracy on a task falls outside predefined thresholds ($\alpha_{low}$ and $\alpha_{high}$), a language model is prompted to modify the task to make it either easier or harder.
- **Critic Feedback Guidance:** Crucially, these modifications are not arbitrary. They are guided by "critic feedback" summarized from the reward model's evaluative traces ($r_{\tau_i, j}$). Specifically, the system summarizes error patterns from steps where the reward model assigned a negative score ($S_{\tau_i, j}=-1$), capturing the policy's likely failure modes. This diagnostic information is then fed to the LM to provide concrete guidance on how to perturb the task (e.g., adding hints, removing ambiguities, changing object rarity or number of instances in the task description).
- **Quality Control:** To ensure the validity and effectiveness of the new tasks ($\text{q}'$), a quality control mechanism is in place. For harder tasks, $\text{q}'$ is accepted only if its accuracy is between $\alpha_{low}$ and the original task's accuracy ($\text{acc}(\text{q})$). For easier tasks, $\text{q}'$ is accepted only if its accuracy is between $\text{acc}(\text{q})$ and $\alpha_{high}$. This prevents the generation of trivial or unsolvable tasks.

The entire process is summarized in Algorithm 1, outlining an iterative loop of sampling, policy update, reward model update, and environment adaptation. The framework's generality is demonstrated across diverse domains: computer-use agents (OSWorld), text-based interactive games (Alf World), and coding LLMs (LiveCodeBench, CodeContests, LiveBench), utilizing various Qwen series LLMs for the policy, reward, and task adaptation models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of RLAnything demonstrates its effectiveness across a range of LLM and agentic tasks. The key findings and results are:

- **Consistent System-wide Improvement:** A central finding is that each dynamic component, when progressively added, consistently improves the overall system performance. The "Policy + Reward + Env" configuration (RLAnything) yields the strongest optimization and highest convergence accuracy for both the policy and reward models, outperforming setups where only the policy or both policy and reward model are dynamic (Table 1, Figure 4).
- **Significant Performance Gains:** RLAnything achieved substantial gains in practical applications:
 - Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking's accuracy on OSWorld increased by 9.1%.
 - Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's accuracy on Alf World increased by 18.7%.
 - Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's accuracy on LiveBench (coding) increased by 11.9%.
 - Notably, improvements were also observed on out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks, highlighting enhanced generalization capabilities (Table 1).
- **Stronger Reward Model:** The joint optimization process successfully improves the reward model. Both its "process accuracy" (ability to evaluate step-wise correctness) and "outcome accuracy" (ability to predict final outcomes based on step-wise rewards) significantly improve. Environment adaptation further boosts these metrics, validating the theoretical premise that balanced task difficulty aids reward model training (Table 1).
- **Effective Active Learning through Environment Adaptation:** The automated environment adaptation mechanism, guided by critic feedback from the reward model, successfully enables active learning. Examples illustrate how summarized error patterns are used to modify tasks—e.g., adding targeted hints to make difficult GUI tasks easier, or changing object rarity in text games to make easy tasks harder. This targeted adaptation promotes more effective policy exploration and learning (Figure 3, Figure 7a).
- **Necessity of Integrated Rewards:** For complex, long-trajectory tasks, integrated rewards (combining sparse outcome signals with nuanced step-wise feedback) are crucial. The paper shows that training with integrated rewards significantly outperforms using only outcome-based rewards, which are too sparse for sufficient exploration and effective policy training (Figure 6a).
- **Optimized Reward Model Outperforms Human Labels:** A remarkable finding is that policy training using *only* step-wise signals from the optimized reward model, without any external, human-labeled outcome rewards (e.g., from evaluator scripts), can surpass training with verifiable outcome rewards. This demonstrates the robustness and high quality of the automatically learned step-wise supervision, paving the way for large-scale, self-evolving agents that require less human effort for environment scaling and reward definition (Figure 6a).
- **Broad Applicability, Including Coding Tasks:** The framework's applicability extends beyond interactive agent settings to single-turn RLVR-style coding tasks. Joint training of coding policy and unit-test generation (as a form of reward modeling) along with environment adaptation improves both code pass rates and the quality/detecting ability of generated unit tests (Table 1).
- **Enhanced Agentic Coding Performance:** When integrated into existing agentic coding methods (MPSC, AlphaCodium, S*, Best of N), the optimized policy and unit tester from RLAnything consistently lead to significant improvements in coding performance (Figure 8, right).
- **Improved Reasoning Patterns:** Post-optimization, policy models exhibit increased chain-of-thought length and more stable, efficient reasoning during training (Figure 7b).
- **Dynamics of Task Acceptance:** The number of newly accepted tasks grows approximately linearly with training steps, and their difficulty stabilizes at a moderate level, indicating effective and controlled environment scaling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLAnything framework represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model agents and reinforcement learning. Its contributions and potential impact are profound:

- **Pioneering a Fully Dynamic, Closed-Loop RL System:** RLAnything is among the first to propose and empirically validate a truly dynamic, closed-loop system where the environment, policy, and reward model continuously adapt and optimize each other. This holistic approach transcends limitations of previous methods that often focused on isolated components, providing a more robust and self-improving learning paradigm.
- **Overcoming Key Challenges in LLM Agents:** The framework directly addresses critical obstacles hindering the development of capable LLM agents, namely reward sparsity in long-horizon tasks, the high cost of human-labeled supervision for reward models, and the need for adaptive training environments. By providing integrated feedback, automated reward model optimization, and intelligent environment adaptation, RLAnything offers practical solutions to these long-standing problems.
- **Enabling Scalable and Autonomous Agent Development:** The most impactful finding is the demonstration that optimized, automatically generated reward model signals can outperform human-labeled outcomes in policy training. This suggests a powerful pathway towards reducing reliance on expensive human supervision, allowing for much larger-scale environment generation and agent evolution. This could unlock a new era of highly autonomous, self-improving agents capable of mastering complex real-world environments without constant human intervention.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent improvements observed across diverse tasks—from visual GUI interactions to text-based games and abstract coding problems—and particularly on out-of-distribution tasks, highlight that RLAnything fosters stronger generalization and more robust LLM agents.
- **Theoretical Grounding for Adaptive Environments:** The theoretical motivation for environment adaptation benefiting both policy and reward models provides a deeper understanding of why curriculum learning is effective, extending its application to more complex, coupled systems.
- **Broad Applicability:** The framework's success across interactive multi-turn scenarios and single-turn code generation tasks underscores its versatility and potential for impact across a wide spectrum of AI applications.
- **Foundation for Future AI Research:** RLAnything lays a robust foundation for future research into generalist AI agents. By demonstrating how environment, policy, and reward can co-evolve, it opens doors to exploring even more sophisticated self-curating and self-improving AI systems, potentially leading to agents that can autonomously discover and master new tasks in highly dynamic and open-ended environments.

In essence, RLAnything signifies a pivotal step towards more intelligent, adaptable, and self-sufficient LLM-powered agents, accelerating the progression towards truly general artificial intelligence.
