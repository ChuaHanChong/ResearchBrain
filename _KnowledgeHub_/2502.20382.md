# Physics-Driven Data Generation for Contact-Rich Manipulation via Trajectory Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A physics-driven pipeline combines VR-based human demonstrations, kinematic retargeting, and trajectory optimization to generate large, dynamically feasible datasets for contact-rich robotic manipulation. This method, developed by researchers at MIT, enables cross-embodiment generalization and improves learned policy success rates on hardware up to 74% in zero-shot deployment compared to 26% for policies trained on raw demonstrations.

## Problem

- High cost and difficulty of collecting diverse, high-quality data for contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks impede the development of generalist robot policies.
- Existing data generation methods, such as kinematic replay or purely model-based planning, often lack dynamic feasibility or struggle with global exploration for complex contact sequences.
- Transferring task knowledge across different robot embodiments requires extensive re-collection of data for each specific hardware configuration.

## Method

- A novel ==VR-based interface==, utilizing Apple Vision Pro and the ==Drake simulator==, facilitates low-cost, embodiment-flexible human-hand demonstration collection.
- ==Kinematic motion retargeting== adapts human demonstrations to specific robot embodiments by solving a non-convex optimization problem to ensure collision-free joint configurations.
- ==Demonstration-guided trajectory optimization== dynamically refines these kinematically feasible trajectories, enabling ==domain randomization== across physical parameters to generate diverse, dynamically consistent datasets.

## Results

- Trajectory optimization significantly enhanced dynamic feasibility, increasing success rates of generated trajectories to **72-82%** on various robot embodiments even under physical perturbations, compared to very low rates for kinematic retargeting alone.
- Policies trained on the generated datasets achieved substantially higher success rates in simulation (e.g., **81%** for Allegro Hand vs **21%** baseline) and exhibited smoother, more robust behavior capable of error recovery.
- Zero-shot deployment on physical iiwa arms demonstrated a **70-74%** success rate for a cube flipping task, a notable increase from the **26%** achieved by policies trained on only the original human demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically feasible trajectories, ensured by physics-driven trajectory optimization, are essential for successful execution of contact-rich manipulation tasks, as purely kinematic approaches often fail.
> - Human demonstrations provide crucial global guidance, which, when combined with the local refinement capabilities of model-based trajectory optimization, overcomes limitations of each method in isolation.
> - Task knowledge can be effectively transferred across vastly different robot embodiments and from human hands via VR demonstrations, reducing the need for hardware-specific data collection.
