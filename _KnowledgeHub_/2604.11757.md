# StarVLA-$α$: Reducing Complexity in Vision-Language-Action Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> StarVLA-α presents a streamlined Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that achieves competitive performance across diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks by leveraging a strong pre-trained VLM backbone and a minimal action head. This simplified design matched or surpassed existing complex methods, for instance, yielding up to 98.8% success on LIBERO and demonstrating a 33.6% success rate on the real-world RoboChallenge benchmark, significantly outperforming prior baselines.

## Problem

- The Vision-Language-Action (VLA) research landscape is highly fragmented, characterized by a wide array of architectures, training data, robot embodiments, and benchmark-specific engineering, complicating performance comparisons.
- A lack of methodological consensus in VLA development makes it difficult to ascertain which specific components contribute to performance gains.
- The entanglement of modeling innovations with various data and engineering choices obscures fundamental insights into effective VLA system design.

## Method

- Introduce **StarVLA**-α, a simplified VLA baseline utilizing a powerful ==pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone== and a lightweight ==Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) action head==.
- Employ a ==minimal data processing pipeline== and a clean architecture to reduce experimental confounders and enable systematic analysis of VLA design choices.
- Integrate diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks into a ==unified pipeline==, allowing a ==single generalist model== and training recipe to operate across multiple embodiments and tasks without customization.

## Results

- StarVLA-α achieved competitive or superior performance on several VLA benchmarks, including **98.8%** average success on LIBERO, **76.0%** on SimplerEnv Google VM, and a **33.6%** success rate on the real-world RoboChallenge.
- Continuous action prediction consistently surpassed discrete action methods, while the simplest MLP-based action head performed **comparably** to more complex continuous action heads.
- A single generalist model, trained jointly across all benchmarks, demonstrated robust capabilities across diverse tasks and embodiments, with effective action space management through simple padding, improving RoboCasa-GR1 by **4.8%** over robot-specific solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone, coupled with a simple action head, can serve as the primary driver of performance in VLA systems, challenging the necessity of overly complex architectures.
> - Continuous action prediction consistently outperforms discrete action modeling strategies across various benchmarks.
> - Action-specific pretraining and extensive data engineering techniques offer conditional and often diminishing returns, especially when a strong VLM and sufficient task-specific data are available.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotics aimed at developing general-purpose agents beyond task-specific policies. The field has advanced through integrating large foundation models, scaling robotic datasets, and leveraging general multimodal supervision, leading to improved policy transferability and task coverage across various benchmarks. Open-source initiatives have also contributed to accelerating experimentation in this domain.

Despite this progress, the VLA landscape is characterized by significant fragmentation and complexity. Existing approaches exhibit substantial variations in architectures, training data, embodiment configurations, and benchmark-specific engineering. This heterogeneity makes it challenging to ascertain which specific components are responsible for observed performance gains, as improvements are often confounded by dataset choices, preprocessing pipelines, and specialized engineering. Unlike Vision-Language Models (VLMs), where training practices have shown convergence towards standardized methodologies, VLA research lacks a clear methodological consensus. This absence of consensus creates difficulties in interpreting empirical comparisons and guiding future research directions.

The current work addresses this issue by proposing a simplified VLA baseline, StarVLA-α, designed to reduce experimental confounders and enable a more systematic analysis of design choices. It aims to establish a clearer methodological understanding by minimizing architectural and pipeline complexity, thereby providing a controlled environment for re-evaluating commonly adopted VLA practices.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to reduce the complexity and fragmentation within the Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model landscape. This involves identifying and demystifying the essential components that drive performance in VLA systems, rather than introducing further architectural innovations. The authors propose StarVLA-α as a simple, transparent, and robust baseline to achieve these objectives.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges prevalent in current VLA research:

- **Fragmented Design and Lack of Consensus:** The rapid development of VLA systems has led to a diverse array of model architectures, pre-training data, embodiment configurations, and benchmark-specific fine-tuning strategies. This fragmentation complicates empirical comparisons and makes it difficult to attribute performance improvements definitively to core modeling innovations versus experimental variations or specific engineering. The field lacks a standardized recipe for VLA training, in contrast to the emerging consensus in VLM research.
- **Heterogeneous Data and Embodiments:** Pre-training data and robot embodiments vary significantly across studies, leading to incompatible interfaces, action spaces, and normalization schemes. The diversity of robotic platforms—from single-arm manipulators to dual-arm systems and humanoid robots, coupled with differences in camera viewpoints and end-effectors—further entangles modeling choices with embodiment-specific preprocessing requirements.
- **Varied Evaluation Practices:** Evaluation practices in VLA research often involve benchmark-specific hyperparameter tuning, dataset splits, and action chunking strategies. This tailored approach can lead to strong in-benchmark results that do not necessarily translate to robustness or generalization under broader distribution shifts.
- **Unclear Drivers of Performance:** It remains unclear which components truly contribute to performance gains in VLA systems. The entanglement of modeling innovations with data choices and engineering obscures fundamental insights into effective VLA design.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of StarVLA-α are:

- **Minimize Complexity:** Deliberately reduce architectural and pipeline complexity by employing a pre-trained VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL) without robot-specific pre-training or elaborate action engineering. This simplification aims to minimize experimental confounders.
- **Enable Systematic Analysis:** Create a controlled environment to systematically re-evaluate widely adopted VLA design choices, including action modeling strategies, robot-specific pretraining, and interface engineering, under comparable conditions.
- **Establish a Strong Baseline:** Demonstrate that a streamlined VLM design, coupled with minimal data processing and a lightweight action head, can achieve competitive or leading performance across diverse benchmarks and embodiments.
- **Promote Methodological Clarity:** Foster consensus on VLA training practices by providing a reproducible and easily extensible framework that can serve as a solid starting point for future research.
- **Assess Generalization Capabilities:** Evaluate the robustness of a single, generalist model trained jointly across multiple benchmarks, without task-specific adaptation, to understand its capacity for cross-task and cross-embodiment generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

StarVLA-α is designed based on a "minimal-sufficiency hypothesis," positing that a strong VLM combined with a lightweight action head can capture most of the benefits often attributed to more complex VLA designs. The framework emphasizes clean architecture and minimal data processing to reduce experimental variables.

#### 3.1. Framework Design: StarVLA-α

- **Minimal Data Processing:** A single, minimal data pipeline is used across all environments to promote generalization. The model directly accepts raw RGB images and language instructions without benchmark-specific engineering. Actions are normalized using only the training split (zero mean, unit variance). Evaluation strictly follows each benchmark's official protocol. This approach is intended to ensure direct applicability to new robot embodiments without additional adaptation.
- **Clean Architecture:** The architecture pairs a Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone with a lightweight action head for continuous action prediction. The VLM backbone is instantiated with Qwen3-VL (from the Qwen family of models) due to its open-source nature, community support, and native processing of both vision and language inputs. A simple Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) action head is attached to read the hidden state of a designated action token and regress a chunk of continuous actions. This modular design facilitates swapping backbones or action heads.
- **Unified Benchmark Integration:** The framework integrates a diverse suite of manipulation benchmarks—LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboTwin 2.0, and RoboCasa-GR1—into a unified pipeline without benchmark-specific design. Strict adherence to original data and evaluation protocols is maintained, with minimal processing and consistent action representation. Thin adapters standardize observation formats, action interfaces, and evaluation entry points, allowing the same model and training recipe to run across all benchmarks without customization. This setup also supports training a single model jointly across all benchmarks for generalist evaluation.

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluation is conducted on LIBERO (compositional multi-task), SimplerEnv (simulated real-world policies), RoboTwin 2.0 (dual-arm coordination), and RoboCasa-GR1 (humanoid tabletop manipulation). Supplementary evaluations include LIBERO-Plus (robustness), RoboChallenge (real-world), and real-world OOD experiments.
- **Baselines:** StarVLA-α is compared against representative VLA methods: FAST, OpenVLA-OFT, π0, π0.5, and GR00T-N1.6. These prior methods are typically trained separately on each benchmark with custom data processing.
- **Training Protocols:**
 - **Specialist Training:** StarVLA-α is trained independently on each benchmark’s training set using the unified minimal data pipeline.
 - **Generalist Training:** All benchmarks' data are merged into a single training set, and a single model is trained. For unified settings, actions from different robots are padded with zeros to a maximum dimension of 32, requiring no per-task engineering.
- **Optimization:** A learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁵ for the VLM backbone and 1 × 10⁻⁴ for the action head is used, with a cosine learning rate schedule. Models are trained for a maximum of 100k steps with a per-GPU batch size of 16.
- **Computation Resources:** The training setup scales with dataset size, with specific GPU allocations (e.g., 8 A100s for LIBERO, 64 A100s for all benchmarks jointly).

#### 3.3. Rethinking Common Practices (Ablation Studies)

The study systematically analyzes three common design choices:

- **Action Head Design:** Four designs are evaluated with the same VLM backbone:
 1. **StarVLA-α-FAST:** Discrete action prediction via autoregressive FAST tokenizer (similar to π0-FAST).
 2. **StarVLA-α:** Continuous action regression with a lightweight MLP head (following OpenVLA-OFT).
 3. **StarVLA-α-GR00T:** Dual-system architecture where VLM provides high-level reasoning and a flow-matching module performs action execution (following GR00T N1.5).
 4. **StarVLA-α-π:** Diffusion-style continuous action prediction using a flow-matching expert (analogous to π0).
- **Action-Specific Pretraining:** The necessity of pretraining with large-scale action data is investigated using the StarVLA-α architecture. Four settings are compared on RoboCasa-GR1 and RoboTwin:
 1. No additional pretraining (VLM-based, fine-tuned directly).
 2. Pretraining with OXE dataset (+OXE).
 3. Pretraining with InternData-A1 (+InternData-A1).
 4. Pretraining with RoboTwin randomized data (+RoboTwin-Rand).
- **Data Engineering:** Four common data engineering techniques are examined:
 1. **Proprioception:** Adding robot joint states as input.
 2. **History frames:** Stacking previous two frames for temporal context.
 3. **Delta action:** Predicting relative changes from current joint position.
 4. **Relative action:** Predicting actions in a reference coordinate frame.
 These are evaluated across LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0, and RoboCasa-GR1 under varying data scales.

#### 3.4. All-in-one Generalist Evaluation

A single model is trained jointly on all datasets (LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa-GR1) and evaluated directly across them without fine-tuning. Differences in action dimensions are handled by padding action spaces to a uniform 32 dimensions. Ablations on model initialization, model size (2B, 4B, 8B Qwen3-VL), and batch size (64 to 1024) are conducted in this generalist setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive evaluation of StarVLA-α, both as a specialist model and a generalist model, alongside ablations on common VLA design choices.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance of StarVLA-α (Table 1)

- **Competitive Performance:** StarVLA-α (Specialist) achieved an average success rate of 98.8% on LIBERO, 64.6% on WidowX, 76.0% on Google VM in SimplerEnv, and up to 53.8% on RoboCasa-GR1 (random*). These results indicate performance levels competitive with or exceeding prior VLA methods across these benchmarks.
- **Generalist Model Efficacy:** The StarVLA-α (Generalist) model, trained on merged data from all benchmarks, demonstrated competitive per-benchmark performance, with notable improvements on challenging benchmarks like RoboCasa-GR1 (57.3% on random*), relative to specialist models.

#### 4.2. Impact of Action Head Design (Table 2)

- **Continuous vs. Discrete Actions:** Continuous action prediction (StarVLA-α, StarVLA-α-GR00T, StarVLA-α-π) consistently outperformed discrete action prediction (StarVLA-α-FAST) across nearly all benchmarks. For example, StarVLA-α achieved 76.0% on SimplerEnv Google VM, compared to StarVLA-α-FAST's 60.1%.
- **Limited Impact of Complex Continuous Heads:** Among the continuous-action variants, the three action heads (MLP, GR00T-style flow matching, π-style diffusion) achieved comparable performance. The simplest MLP-based design (StarVLA-α) performed similarly to or better than more complex continuous action heads across various tasks and embodiments. This suggests that when a powerful VLM is used, architectural complexity in the action head may not be critical.

#### 4.3. Effect of Action-Specific Pretraining (Table 3)

- **Conditional Benefits:** Additional action-specific pretraining did not consistently improve performance. While pretraining with in-domain or partially aligned data (e.g., InternData-A1, RoboTwin-Rand) provided modest improvements in low-data regimes on RoboTwin (e.g., 50.3% to 63.6% for InternData-A1 on RoboTwin Clean 50x50), it often reduced performance on other benchmarks (e.g., RoboCasa-GR1 results decreased from 53.8% to 35.4% with InternData-A1).
- **Potential for Degradation:** Out-of-domain pretraining data, such as OXE, could degrade results (e.g., RoboCasa-GR1 performance dropped from 53.8% to 27.8% with OXE). This indicates that a strong VLM backbone provides a robust foundation, and further pretraining benefits are context-dependent.

#### 4.4. Necessity of Data Engineering (Table 4)

- **Context-Dependent Gains:** Data engineering techniques (proprioception, history frames, delta action, relative action) provided modest improvements primarily when task-specific data was limited. For instance, proprioception increased RoboCasa-GR1 success from 9.8% to 12.5% at 24x10 demonstrations.
- **Diminishing Returns with Sufficient Data:** As the amount of task-specific data increased, the impact of these techniques became less pronounced, often performing similarly to the baseline without data engineering. This suggests that a strong VLM, combined with sufficient task-specific data, can mitigate the need for extensive data engineering.

#### 4.5. All-in-one Generalist Evaluation Insights (Table 5, Table 6, Figure 5, Tables 9, 10, 11)

- **Unified Action Parameterization:** A simple padding strategy for actions across multiple embodiments achieved performance competitive with or superior to complex, robot-specific solutions like multi-action heads or RDT actions, particularly on Google Robot VM (+2.9%) and RoboCasa-GR1 (+4.8%). This suggests VLMs can learn to manage diverse action spaces with minimal architectural intervention.
- **Model Initialization Importance (Table 9):** Strong VLM initialization (e.g., Qwen3-VL) was critical for generalist performance, substantially outperforming random initialization and older VLM versions (e.g., Florence-2, Qwen2.5-VL). Better pretrained multimodal representations directly correlated with stronger cross-benchmark generalization.
- **Model Size Influence (Table 10):** Scaling model size from 2B to 4B (Qwen3-VL) yielded noticeable performance gains, especially on challenging benchmarks like SimplerEnv WidowX (+18.1%) and RoboCasa-GR1 (+6.6%). However, the improvement from 4B to 8B was marginal (within 1%), indicating diminishing returns beyond a moderate capacity. A 4B parameter scale appeared sufficient for the current training scales.
- **Batch Size Impact (Table 11):** Larger batch sizes consistently improved performance across all benchmarks in the generalist setting. Significant gains were observed, for example, on RoboCasa-GR1 (40.0% at 64 batch size to 59.2% at 1024). This highlights the importance of diverse gradient signals for stable optimization and stronger cross-benchmark generalization.

#### 4.6. Real-World Experiments

- **RoboChallenge Performance (Table 7):** StarVLA-α's generalist model achieved an average success rate of 33.6% and a progress score of 54.5% on the RoboChallenge benchmark with the ARX5 robot. This significantly surpassed comparative methods like π0.5 (12.7% SR, 27.6 score) and π0 (3.6% SR, 14.7 score), demonstrating effectiveness in real-world settings across multiple robot platforms (full results in supplementary).
- **Real-World OOD Generalization (Table 12):** In out-of-distribution (OOD) experiments on a Franka Research 3 robot, StarVLA-α maintained robust performance. The average success rate dropped from 85.3% in in-domain (ID) settings to 76.3% in OOD settings across tasks like waste sorting, pick-colored-egg, and egg-carton placement. This indicates that the policy learns transferable visuomotor capabilities rather than memorizing training distributions.

#### 4.7. Robustness Evaluation on LIBERO-Plus (Table 17)

- **Robust Transfer:** Both specialist and generalist StarVLA-α models demonstrated robust transfer from standard LIBERO to LIBERO-Plus under various perturbations (camera, robot, language, lighting, background, noise, layout). The total average success rates (77.8% for Specialist, 79.7% for Generalist) surpassed prior baselines, despite training only on standard LIBERO data without specific robustness augmentations. This reinforces the notion that a strong VLM backbone provides robust multimodal representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of StarVLA-α and its accompanying systematic analysis carries several significant implications for the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:

- **Reduction of Complexity:** The work demonstrates that a simple and streamlined VLA design, utilizing a strong pre-trained VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL) with a lightweight MLP action head and minimal data processing, can achieve performance competitive with or superior to more architecturally complex and heavily engineered systems. This finding challenges the prevailing trend of increasing complexity in VLA models, suggesting that many elaborate techniques may not be strictly necessary for high performance or generalization.
- **Methodological Clarity and Reproducibility:** By deliberately minimizing architectural and pipeline complexity, StarVLA-α provides a controlled baseline that reduces experimental confounders. This clarity facilitates more accurate attribution of performance gains, distinguishing between core modeling innovations and experimental variations or engineering tricks. The release of the codebase further promotes reproducibility and provides a standardized foundation for future research.
- **Validation of VLM Power:** The results underscore the critical role of a strong VLM backbone as the primary driver of performance in VLA systems. The study shows that robust multimodal representations from a capable VLM can effectively handle diverse robotic tasks and embodiments, often diminishing the impact of specialized action heads, extensive data engineering, or even additional action-specific pretraining.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The findings offer practical guidance for researchers by highlighting key factors that *do* matter (e.g., continuous action prediction, strong VLM initialization, larger batch sizes for generalist training) and those that *may be less critical* (e.g., overly complex continuous action heads, certain data engineering techniques when sufficient data is available, out-of-domain action pretraining). This can help researchers focus efforts on genuinely impactful areas.
- **Advancement Towards Generalist Robots:** The successful demonstration of a single generalist model performing robustly across diverse benchmarks (LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboTwin, RoboCasa) and real-world tasks (RoboChallenge, OOD experiments) without task-specific adaptation is a step towards truly general-purpose robotic agents. The simple action padding strategy for multi-embodiment control further simplifies the path to unified robot learning.
- **Implications for Resource Allocation:** The finding that a 4B parameter model size is often sufficient, with diminishing returns beyond that scale for current training scenarios, has implications for computational resource allocation in VLA research. Similarly, the emphasis on batch size over model scale for generalist training suggests a strategic focus for optimization efforts.

Overall, StarVLA-α serves as a robust benchmark and a methodological framework that advocates for simplicity and rigor in VLA research. It has the potential to guide the community toward more standardized practices, accelerate the development of truly generalist robot policies, and improve the interpretability of research findings in embodied AI.
