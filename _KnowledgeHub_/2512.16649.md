# JustRL: Scaling a 1.5B LLM with a Simple RL Recipe

> [!summary] Summary
> JustRL introduces a simplified reinforcement learning framework for enhancing 1.5B parameter language models in mathematical reasoning tasks. It achieves state-of-the-art or competitive accuracy on nine benchmarks while utilizing up to 2.4 times less computational resources and demonstrating greater training stability compared to more complex methods.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipelines for smaller Large Language Models (LLMs) on reasoning tasks have become excessively complex, obscuring the efficacy of individual techniques.
- Existing distillation methods for smaller LLMs are inherently limited by the performance ceiling of their teacher models, restricting further skill acquisition.
- A proliferation of intricate 'tricks' aimed at stabilizing RL for smaller LLMs often addresses symptoms rather than fundamental issues, leading to unclear and inefficient practices.

## Method

- JustRL employs a minimalist approach, utilizing the ==GRPO algorithm== with a lightweight ==binary rule-based verifier== for rewards.
- It deliberately avoids complex multi-stage training, dynamic hyperparameter schedules, explicit length penalties, and advanced data filtering.
- The framework applies a single, fixed set of hyperparameters and a simple prompt across different 1.5B LLM backbones, with =='clip higher'== being the only adopted 'trick' for stability.

## Results

- JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B achieved an average accuracy of **54.87%** across nine math benchmarks, surpassing ProRL-V2's **53.08%** while using half the computational resources.
- On the OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B backbone, JustRL achieved **64.32%** average accuracy, marginally outperforming QuestA's **63.81%** with **2.4x** less compute.
- Ablation studies revealed that adding an explicit 'Overlong Penalty' or a 'Robust Verifier' to the simple recipe degraded performance, with AIME 2024 accuracy dropping from **55%** to **50%** and then to **45%**, respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A deliberately simple RL framework can achieve state-of-the-art performance on reasoning tasks for smaller LLMs, challenging the necessity of intricate multi-stage training and dynamic controls.
> - Many commonly adopted 'tricks' in RL for LLMs, such as explicit length penalties or robust verifiers, can be counterproductive, leading to performance degradation and collapsed exploration.
> - Stable training dynamics, including consistent policy entropy and monotonic reward improvement, can be maintained with minimal intervention, allowing models to naturally optimize response length.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "JustRL" directly addresses a critical and evolving aspect of the LLM landscape: the application of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to enhance model capabilities, particularly for smaller, more lightweight models (SLMs). Historically, large models like OpenAI’s o1 and DeepSeek-R1 have demonstrated the power of RLVR for complex tasks such as mathematics and coding. However, for SLMs, the dominant strategy has been distillation—supervised fine-tuning on outputs from larger, more capable teacher models. Distillation is efficient and stable, providing immediate performance gains, as seen with models like Qwen3 and DeepSeek-R1-Distill.

The authors identify a fundamental limitation of distillation: its performance is inherently capped by the teacher model's capabilities. Once the teacher model's performance plateaus or updates become infrequent, further gains for the distilled student model become challenging. This is where RL steps in, offering a pathway for models to learn beyond their teacher's explicit knowledge by exploring and refining their outputs based on external feedback (rewards).

However, the field of RL for SLMs has concurrently developed a reputation for instability and complexity. Recent research, as summarized in Table 1 of the paper, shows a "proliferation of methods" attempting to stabilize and improve RL training for small models. These include multi-stage training pipelines, dynamic hyperparameter schedules, adaptive temperature controls, explicit response length penalties, and various data curation and filtering strategies. The paper cites examples like STILL, DeepScaleR, FastCuRL, ProRL, BroRL, QuestA, POLARIS, and e3, all of which employ multiple sophisticated techniques. This trend has created a research environment where it's increasingly difficult to ascertain which components truly contribute to performance and which merely compensate for instabilities introduced by other complex practices. The authors, echoing the sentiment of Antoine de Saint-Exupéry, question whether "this complexity is necessary."

JustRL directly challenges this prevailing trend by demonstrating that a significantly simpler RL setup can achieve state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance. It suggests that many of the "standard tricks" and complex interventions might be addressing symptoms rather than fundamental problems, or even degrading performance by stifling beneficial learning dynamics. By providing a "simple, validated baseline," JustRL aims to re-evaluate the necessity of such complexity and guide the field towards a more parsimonious, empirically driven approach to RL for LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "JustRL" paper is to critically examine the necessity of the increasing complexity in reinforcement learning (RL) pipelines for smaller large language models (LLMs) specialized in mathematical reasoning. The authors pose a direct and fundamental question: "Is this complexity necessary?"

This question is motivated by several observations:
- **Proliferation of Techniques:** The past year has seen a rapid accumulation of sophisticated techniques (multi-stage training, dynamic hyperparameters, length penalties, curriculum learning, etc.) in RL for SLMs, making it difficult to discern their individual or combined efficacy.
- **Unclear Contribution:** When various works combine different subsets of these techniques and report varying results, it becomes challenging to isolate what truly drives performance gains.
- **Addressing Symptoms vs. Causes:** Many complex techniques are introduced to address training instabilities like reward collapse, entropy drift, and length explosion. However, these techniques are often applied on top of already complex baselines, making it unclear whether they provide genuine benefits or merely compensate for issues introduced by prior complexity. The authors suggest that "accumulated ‘best practices’ may be fighting each other rather than the fundamental challenges of RL."
- **Limitations of Distillation:** While distillation is effective for SLMs, its performance is bounded by the teacher model. RL offers a path for further improvement, but its current perception as unstable and difficult hinders its widespread adoption for SLMs.

Against this backdrop, the authors aim to demonstrate that stable, competitive training can be achieved with a deliberately minimal approach. Their motivation is not to argue against all techniques, but to provide evidence that simpler baselines deserve more attention and to establish what is "fundamentally sufficient" before layering on additional complexity. They seek to offer a practical, simple recipe that can enhance model performance even when distillation limits are reached, challenging the assumption that sophistication is a prerequisite for success in this domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the JustRL methodology is its deliberate embrace of simplicity, aiming to achieve competitive performance by stripping away the complex techniques that have become commonplace in RL for LLMs. The authors refer to this as a "minimal approach" or a "simple RL recipe."

**Core Algorithm:**
The paper utilizes the **GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization)** algorithm, which is a variant of policy gradient methods known for its stability. The specific implementation used is from `veRL` [Sheng et al., 2025].

**Reward Mechanism:**
Rewards are binary (success/failure outcomes) and are provided by a lightweight rule-based verifier from **DAPO** [Yu et al., 2025]. Crucially, this verifier avoids computationally expensive symbolic math libraries like SymPy. The authors augment this rule-based verifier with a lightweight model-based verifier, **CompassVerifier-3B** [Liu et al., 2025c], during evaluation to address potential false negatives.

**What JustRL Keeps Simple (i.e., what it *avoids*):**
The distinguishing feature of JustRL is its conscious omission of many "standard tricks" prevalent in current RL for LLMs:
- **Single-stage training:** Unlike multi-stage approaches (e.g., DeepScaleR, FastCuRL, ProRL) that progressively lengthen context or switch curricula, JustRL trains continuously from start to finish in a single stage.
- **Fixed hyperparameters:** It eschews dynamic adjustments such as adaptive temperature scheduling, dynamic batch size adjustments, or mid-training reference model resets. The hyperparameters, detailed in Table 2, remain constant throughout training.
- **Standard data:** Training is performed on the **DAPO-Math-17k** dataset [Yu et al., 2025] without any offline difficulty filtering or online dynamic sampling strategies.
- **Basic prompting:** A simple, untuned suffix prompt ("Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}.") is used.
- **Implicit Length Control:** Instead of explicit length penalty terms (like those used in ProRL-V2 or DAPO), JustRL simply sets a maximum context length of 16K tokens.

**The One Technique Used (Considered a Baseline):**
The only established practice they employ is **"clip higher,"** a technique described as "well-established for stability in long-horizon RL training." The authors consider this as part of a fundamental baseline rather than an added complexity.

**Training Models and Resources:**
The simple recipe is applied to two popular 1.5B reasoning models:
1. **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B**
2. **OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B**

Each model was trained using 32 A800-80GB GPUs for approximately 15 days. A key demonstration of robustness is that *the same fixed hyperparameters are used for both models without any per-model tuning*.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
Performance is evaluated across nine challenging mathematical reasoning tasks, including AIME 2024/2025, AMC 2023, MATH-500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench, HMMT Feb 2025, CMIMC 2025, and BRUMO 2025. Evaluation reports Pass@1 accuracy, averaged over N sampled responses (N=4 for MATH-500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench; N=32 for others), using a temperature of 0.7, top-p 0.9, and allowing up to 32K tokens for generation.

**Ablation Studies:**
To further validate their claims, the authors conduct two ablation studies on JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B:
1. Adding an explicit **"Overlong Penalty"** term for the last 4k tokens (as used in DAPO).
2. Adding both the "Overlong Penalty" and a **"Robust Verifier"** from DeepScaleR to reduce false negatives. These ablations are designed to test whether commonly adopted "tricks" truly improve performance when added to a stable, simple baseline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "JustRL" paper presents compelling findings that underscore the efficacy of a minimalist RL approach for scaling 1.5B LLMs on mathematical reasoning tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance and Computational Efficiency on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B Backbone (Weaker Base)

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B achieved an average accuracy of 54.87% across nine mathematical benchmarks. This directly outperformed more complex approaches like ProRL-V2 (53.08%), which employed a nine-stage training pipeline with dynamic hyperparameters. JustRL also led on six out of nine individual benchmarks.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A significant finding is that JustRL achieved these results using approximately **2 times less compute** than ProRL-V2. For instance, JustRL-DeepSeek required an estimated token budget of 1.4x10^8k, compared to ProRL-V2's 2.8x10^8k. Moreover, it used 4.9 times less compute than BroRL, which dramatically increased rollouts. This demonstrates that simplicity does not come at a computational cost; rather, it often leads to greater efficiency.
- **Training Stability:** The training curve for JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B (Figure 1a) showcased smooth and monotonic improvement over 4,380 steps, without the oscillations, plateaus, or sudden drops that typically necessitate complex interventions in other methods.

#### 4.2. Performance and Computational Efficiency on OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B Backbone (Stronger Base)

- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The *identical simple recipe* (without any hyperparameter adjustments) was applied to the OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B backbone. JustRL-Nemotron-1.5B achieved an average accuracy of 64.32%, slightly outperforming QuestA (63.81%), a method known for its innovative curriculum learning and question augmentation. JustRL-Nemotron led on five of the nine benchmarks.
- **Reduced Compute with Comparable Performance:** Despite achieving slightly better average performance, JustRL-Nemotron utilized **2.4 times less compute** than QuestA (1.1x10^8k vs. 2.6x10^8k token budget), which required complex curriculum design and augmented data.
- **Consistent Training Stability:** The training curve for JustRL-Nemotron-1.5B (Figure 1b) also exhibited smooth, stable progress, reinforcing the robustness of the simple recipe across different foundation models.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics Analysis (Figure 2)

A detailed analysis of training dynamics for JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B revealed:
- **Stable Policy Entropy:** Entropy remained stable, oscillating naturally between 1.0 and 1.6, with no systematic drift or collapse, suggesting effective exploration was maintained by the "clip higher" technique.
- **Monotonic Mean Reward:** The mean training reward showed a consistent, upward trend from around -0.6 to +0.4, indicating continuous learning without prolonged plateaus or drops requiring intervention.
- **Natural Response Length Convergence:** Without any explicit length penalties, the model naturally compressed its responses from an initial average of ~8,000 tokens to a stable range of 4,000-5,000 tokens by step 1,000, and maintained this. This organic compression is contrasted with explicit penalties that might force premature convergence or "gaming" by the model.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Figure 3)

The ablation studies yielded counter-intuitive but crucial results:
- **Degradation from Overlong Penalty:** Adding an explicit length penalty term (as used in DAPO) to the base recipe *degraded* performance, causing it to plateau at 50% AIME 2024 accuracy compared to the baseline's 55%. Analysis showed this penalty collapsed exploration, driving policy entropy down to 0.5-0.6, hindering learning.
- **Further Degradation from Robust Verifier:** Further adding a more sophisticated "robust verifier" (from DeepScaleR) alongside the overlong penalty led to even worse performance, plateauing at 45% AIME 2024. The authors suggest this might be due to a loss of nuanced learning signals from a stricter verifier or a reduced incentive for precise internal computations.

These ablations critically demonstrate that "standard tricks" are not universally beneficial and can actively harm performance and learning dynamics when applied to a stable, simple baseline. They highlight the delicate balance achieved by JustRL's minimalist design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "JustRL" paper carries significant implications for the future direction of research in applying reinforcement learning to large language models, particularly in the context of scaling smaller models.

**Challenging the Status Quo:** The most profound impact of JustRL is its direct challenge to the prevailing assumption that complexity is a prerequisite for stability and high performance in RL for LLMs. By demonstrating state-of-the-art results with a "simple RL recipe," the authors effectively debunk the notion that multi-stage pipelines, dynamic hyperparameters, and various "tricks" are universally necessary. This can lead to a paradigm shift, encouraging researchers to re-evaluate the true contributions of increasingly complex methodologies.

**Advocacy for Simplicity and Robust Baselines:** The paper makes a compelling methodological argument: "start simple, scale up, and only add complexity when a simple, robust baseline demonstrably fails." This call to action advocates for establishing transparent, reproducible, and robust baselines before layering on additional techniques. Such a shift could lead to more efficient research, clearer understanding of what drives performance, and a reduction in computational waste. If simpler methods are "sufficient more often than current practice assumes," this suggests a more accessible path for researchers with limited compute resources.

**Redefining "Best Practices":** The negative results from the ablation studies are particularly impactful. They show that "standard tricks" like explicit length penalties and robust verifiers, which are often adopted with the assumption of improvement, can actually degrade performance and collapse exploration in a stable, simple setup. This highlights the delicate interplay between different design choices and warns against blindly adopting techniques without thorough empirical validation within a given context. It suggests that some complexities might be solving problems they themselves or other complexities create, rather than fundamental RL challenges.

**Increased Accessibility and Reproducibility:** By releasing their models and code, the authors provide the community with a well-validated, simple baseline. This not only encourages replication but also lowers the barrier to entry for researchers to experiment with RL for LLMs, as they no longer need to navigate a bewildering array of intricate techniques. This contribution can accelerate research by providing a common, robust starting point.

**Potential for Broader Impact:** While the results are currently limited to mathematical reasoning tasks and 1.5B parameter models, the fundamental insights—that stability and high performance can arise from simplicity—could generalize to other domains (e.g., coding, general question answering) and model scales. If this holds true, it could streamline the development of RL-enhanced LLMs across the board, making advanced capabilities more widely attainable.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:** The authors are transparent about the limitations of their work, which include:
- **Domain Specificity:** Results are currently limited to mathematical reasoning tasks at the 1.5B parameter scale. Generalization to other domains (coding, general Q&A) and model sizes (larger or smaller) remains unexplored.
- **Component Isolation:** While demonstrating overall simplicity, the paper does not definitively isolate which specific components (hyperparameters, verifier design, training data) are most critical to its success.
- **Compute Budget:** Although more efficient than complex methods, the compute budget (32 A800-80GB GPUs for 15 days) may still be prohibitive for resource-constrained researchers.
- **Training Horizon:** The study does not explore whether the approach maintains advantages over even longer training horizons or if additional techniques might become necessary at extreme scales.

Despite these limitations, "JustRL" presents a robust argument for reassessing the role of complexity in RL for LLMs, proposing a methodological shift towards simplicity and empirical validation that could significantly influence future research and development in the field.
