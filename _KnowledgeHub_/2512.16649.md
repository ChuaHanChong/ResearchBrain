# JustRL: Scaling a 1.5B LLM with a Simple RL Recipe

> [!summary] Summary
> JustRL introduces a simplified reinforcement learning framework for enhancing 1.5B parameter language models in mathematical reasoning tasks. It achieves state-of-the-art or competitive accuracy on nine benchmarks while utilizing up to 2.4 times less computational resources and demonstrating greater training stability compared to more complex methods.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipelines for smaller Large Language Models (LLMs) on reasoning tasks have become excessively complex, obscuring the efficacy of individual techniques.
- Existing distillation methods for smaller LLMs are inherently limited by the performance ceiling of their teacher models, restricting further skill acquisition.
- A proliferation of intricate 'tricks' aimed at stabilizing RL for smaller LLMs often addresses symptoms rather than fundamental issues, leading to unclear and inefficient practices.

## Method

- JustRL employs a minimalist approach, utilizing the ==GRPO algorithm== with a lightweight ==binary rule-based verifier== for rewards.
- It deliberately avoids complex multi-stage training, dynamic hyperparameter schedules, explicit length penalties, and advanced data filtering.
- The framework applies a single, fixed set of hyperparameters and a simple prompt across different 1.5B LLM backbones, with =='clip higher'== being the only adopted 'trick' for stability.

## Results

- JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B achieved an average accuracy of **54.87%** across nine math benchmarks, surpassing ProRL-V2's **53.08%** while using half the computational resources.
- On the OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B backbone, JustRL achieved **64.32%** average accuracy, marginally outperforming QuestA's **63.81%** with **2.4x** less compute.
- Ablation studies revealed that adding an explicit 'Overlong Penalty' or a 'Robust Verifier' to the simple recipe degraded performance, with AIME 2024 accuracy dropping from **55%** to **50%** and then to **45%**, respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A deliberately simple RL framework can achieve state-of-the-art performance on reasoning tasks for smaller LLMs, challenging the necessity of intricate multi-stage training and dynamic controls.
> - Many commonly adopted 'tricks' in RL for LLMs, such as explicit length penalties or robust verifiers, can be counterproductive, leading to performance degradation and collapsed exploration.
> - Stable training dynamics, including consistent policy entropy and monotonic reward improvement, can be maintained with minimal intervention, allowing models to naturally optimize response length.
