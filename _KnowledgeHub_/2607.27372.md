# Explorative Modeling: Unlocking a Third Pretraining Axis and End-to-End Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Explorative Modeling introduces a paradigm where generative models explore multiple candidate generations during training, optimizing against the best match to address multimodality and enable end-to-end generation. This method establishes "generative expressivity" as a new pretraining axis, leading to notable efficiency gains—for instance, achieving comparable image generation performance with 6.2 times less data—and enabling end-to-end robotics control with up to 256 times fewer inference steps than existing multi-step diffusion models.

## Problem

- Generative models typically lack the end-to-end training common in other deep learning fields, creating a mismatch between their training and multi-step inference procedures.
- Existing scalable reconstructive models factor the generation process into many small, sequential steps to handle multimodal distributions, which introduces exposure bias and compounding errors.
- Current scaling efforts for generative models focus on increasing parameters and data volume, but overlook "generative expressivity," a fixed capacity of the training objective to capture distinct data modes, which limits performance.

## Method

- **Explorative Modeling (XM)** factors the *training loop* by having the model explore K candidate generations/targets and training only on the best match, thus allowing predictions to commit to specific data modes.
- Two variants are proposed: ==Forward XM== (mass-covering, generating K candidates for one target) and ==Reverse XM== (mode-seeking, comparing one generation to K targets), with mathematical formulations detailing their optimization objectives.
- XM enables scalable handling of multimodal distributions, conceptualized as a form of scalable latent variable modeling or coupling resolution, which directly enhances ==generative expressivity==.

## Results

- Exploration significantly improved efficiency across domains, enabling models to achieve baseline performance with up to **6.2×** less data and **4.1×** fewer FLOPs in image generation, and demonstrating enhanced parameter efficiency.
- Performance consistently improved monotonically with increased exploration (K) across various modalities (image, video, language), with these gains intensifying at larger model and data scales.
- End-to-end Explorative Models matched or surpassed multi-step diffusion baselines in robotics control tasks (Behavior Cloning, Goal-Conditioned World Modeling) while using substantially fewer inference steps (e.g., **1** network forward pass vs. **100**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative expressivity, the ability of a training objective to capture distinct data modes, is a critical and scalable third pretraining axis that directly benefits from increased exploration.
> - Factoring the training loop through exploration can effectively substitute for factoring the generation procedure, enabling models to become more end-to-end and reducing reliance on multi-step generation.
> - Increased computational investment in exploration during training can lead to improved generalization performance and reduced overfitting, demonstrating a compute-generalization tradeoff.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deep learning paradigm, significantly influenced by advancements like AlexNet, has progressively shifted towards end-to-end training, demonstrating that learning an entire task directly from data often surpasses approaches that decompose a problem into hand-designed stages. This trend has been observed across various fields, including image classification, object detection, and image segmentation. A key advantage of end-to-end models is their consistent inference and training procedures, which mitigates distribution shifts and exposure bias that can degrade performance and generalization.

However, generative modeling has largely remained an exception to this end-to-end trend. Despite the capabilities of modern generative models, such as autoregressive and diffusion models, they are typically not trained in a manner that directly mirrors their inference process. These models often factor the generation procedure into numerous smaller, sequential steps during training (e.g., predicting one token or denoising step at a time). This factorization is adopted to handle multimodal distributions by ensuring that each individual step's target is nearly unimodal, thereby preventing a reconstruction loss from blurring distinct modes into an average. This practice, however, introduces a fundamental mismatch between training and inference, where models trained on single-step predictions are unrolled over hundreds or thousands of steps during generation. This mismatch can lead to compounded errors and exposure bias, where the model encounters inputs during inference that deviate from its training distribution.

Current progress in scaling generative models has primarily focused on two axes: increasing model parameters (size/FLOPs), which enhances representational capacity, and expanding the volume of training data, which improves learning. This paper argues for the existence of a third, overlooked scaling capacity termed "generative expressivity," defined as the number of distinct modes a generative model's training objective allows it to capture. Unlike parameters and data, generative expressivity is determined by the training objective itself and remains fixed regardless of increases in model size or data. When generative expressivity is insufficient, models may fail to capture all modes in the data, leading to averaged or blurred generations.

This work introduces Explorative Modeling as a novel paradigm that attempts to reconcile generative modeling with the end-to-end training principle observed in other deep learning domains. By proposing to factor the *training loop* rather than the *generation procedure*, this approach seeks to enhance generative expressivity and enable truly end-to-end reconstructive generative modeling, thereby aligning it more closely with the broader trajectory of deep learning advancements. This approach builds on the theoretical framework of "Mode Forcing," which posits that modern generative modeling centers on designing objectives whose loss minimizers capture modes rather than averaging them.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a perceived discrepancy in the deep learning landscape: while end-to-end training has become a standard for many tasks, generative modeling remains an exception. The core problem identified is that existing scalable reconstructive generative models, such as autoregressive and diffusion models, are not trained end-to-end. This means their sampling mechanism during inference differs significantly from their training procedure. For instance, a diffusion model trained to predict a single denoising step is unrolled over hundreds of such steps during inference, creating a train-inference mismatch. This disparity leads to issues such as exposure bias, where errors compound as generated inputs drift off the training distribution.

The authors posit that this limitation arises because generative modeling inherently deals with multimodal probability distributions, where a single input can correspond to multiple valid outputs. To handle this multimodality at scale, current models factor the generation procedure into numerous smaller steps. Each step is designed to have a nearly unimodal target, preventing the reconstruction loss from blurring distinct modes into an average. However, this very factorization is what precludes end-to-end training.

Against this background, the key objectives of the research are multifaceted:

1. **Introduce Explorative Modeling as a new paradigm:** The central objective is to propose and define Explorative Modeling (XM) as an alternative method for handling multimodal distributions. Instead of decomposing the generation process, XM aims to factor the *training loop* by exploring multiple candidate matches between model generations and data. This allows predictions to commit to specific modes rather than blurring them, all while enabling end-to-end generation.
2. **Establish exploration as a third pretraining axis:** A significant motivation is the argument that generative expressivity—the number of distinct modes a training objective permits a model to capture—is a critical, yet currently fixed, capacity. Existing models primarily scale parameters and data. This work aims to demonstrate that exploration can act as a new scaling axis that directly increases generative expressivity, thereby relieving a bottleneck that cannot be addressed by scaling parameters or data alone. The objective is to show that scaling exploration monotonically improves performance and efficiency across diverse domains and that its benefits grow with increasing model and data scale.
3. **Investigate the compute-generalization tradeoff:** The research seeks to determine if increased computational investment in exploration during training can directly lead to improved generalization performance by reducing overfitting, particularly on fixed datasets.
4. **Demonstrate substitutability of factorization axes:** An objective is to empirically show that factoring the training loop (via exploration) can substitute for factoring the generation procedure. This would imply that as exploration increases, the optimal generative model design would become more end-to-end, reducing reliance on multi-step generation.
5. **Enable end-to-end reconstructive generative modeling:** Ultimately, the research aims to showcase XMs as a standalone paradigm capable of performing end-to-end reconstructive generative modeling, matching or exceeding the performance of existing multi-step generative models on complex tasks, but with significantly fewer inference steps.

In essence, the motivation is to address a fundamental limitation in scalable generative modeling by proposing a new way to handle multimodality, thereby extending the benefits of end-to-end training to this domain and introducing a new dimension for scaling model capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology introduced is **Explorative Modeling (XM)**, a paradigm that factors the training loop to handle multimodal distributions, contrasting with existing models that factor the generation procedure. The central idea is that at each training step, the model "explores" K possible matches between its generations and the target data, and then trains solely on the best match. This process aims to enable predictions to commit to distinct modes rather than converging to a blurred average.

#### 3.1. Underlying Intuition

XMs are conceptualized in several ways:
- **Scalable Latent Variable Models:** XM can be viewed as conditioning the generator on a latent variable (e.g., input noise) that resolves the one-to-many mapping inherent in multimodal data. Instead of learning an encoder for latent inference (as in VAEs, which can face scaling challenges), XM recovers this pairing through exploration and selection.
- **Scalable Coupling Resolution:** It offers a method to find a suitable coupling between inputs and targets. Unlike optimal transport methods that compute couplings based purely on geometry within a minibatch (which can be biased), XM searches for a coupling aligned with the model’s current samples, allowing co-adaptation during training.
- **Generative Modeling via Search:** This framework reinterprets generative modeling as a search problem to find the optimal latent or coupling that best explains the data, leveraging the computational scaling properties of search.
- **Spreading Predictions Across Modes:** Geometrically, exploration encourages the model to spread its K guesses to cover different clusters (modes) of data, thereby capturing more modes as K increases.
- **Implicit Energy Minimization:** The process can be seen as minimizing an implicit energy function over pairings of generations with data, where searching for the lowest-loss match corresponds to finding the best-coupled samples.

#### 3.2. Two Variants of Explorative Modeling

The paper describes two directions for exploration, differing in what is held fixed and what is searched:

1. **Forward XM (Algorithm 1):**
 - **Mechanism:** For a given data sample `x`, the model generates `K` candidate samples ($\hat{y}_1..., \hat{y}_K$). The objective is to minimize the loss $J(\hat{y}_i, x)$ for the candidate $\hat{y}_i$ that is closest to `x`. Only this "best" generation receives gradients.
 - **Properties:** This approach is described as "mass-covering," prioritizing recall by ensuring every datapoint pulls in its nearest generation.
 - **Computational Cost:** Each of the K candidates requires a separate generation (forward pass), making it more computationally intensive during training for larger K.

2. **Reverse XM (Algorithm 2):**
 - **Mechanism:** The model generates a single sample $\hat{y}$. This sample is then compared against `K` randomly drawn data targets ($x_1..., x_K$). The objective is to minimize the loss $J(\hat{y}, x_i)$ for the data target $x_i$ that is closest to $\hat{y}$.
 - **Properties:** This variant is "mode-seeking," focusing on precision by pulling each generation onto the data manifold. It is computationally cheaper because it only requires one generation per training step, regardless of K.
 - **Limitation:** On its own, Reverse XM can suffer from mode collapse as it lacks pressure to cover every mode. This can be mitigated by combining it with Forward XM or adding an entropy bonus.

#### 3.3. Mathematical Foundations

The paper provides a theoretical analysis of what these variants optimize in their smooth, large-K forms (where the hard minimum is replaced by a soft average of kernel scores).
- **Forward XM:** Minimizes KL($p^* || p_\theta$) + H($p^*$), where $p_\theta$ is the blurred model distribution and $p^*$ is the data distribution. This formulation corresponds to maximum likelihood estimation of the data under a mixture of K generated candidates. K directly scales how many modes this mixture can capture (generative expressivity).
- **Reverse XM:** Minimizes KL($g_\theta || p^*_\sigma$) + H($g_\theta$), where $g_\theta$ is the distribution of model generations and $p^*_\sigma$ is the blurred data distribution. This targets the reverse KL, which is mode-seeking and prone to collapse if the model's entropy H($g_\theta$) is not counteracted (e.g., by an explicit entropy bonus or coverage constraint).

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Batching:** For Forward XM, K candidate generations can be processed in parallel by expanding the batch dimension, improving computational efficiency.
- **Gradients and Memory:** Gradients are only passed through the best candidate. This can be implemented in a memory-saving mode (re-forwarding the best candidate) or a FLOP-efficient mode (forwarding all with gradients and backpropagating only the lowest loss).
- **Exploration FLOP Cost:** For transformers, each additional explored candidate in Forward XM adds roughly 1/3 of a standard training step's FLOPs. Reverse XM's additional costs are negligible.
- **Candidate Generation:** For continuous models, different input noise draws are used for each candidate. For Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs), K discrete latent embeddings are learned and sampled.
- **Reverse XM Implementation:** Can leverage dataloaders (loading K datapoints per condition) or vector databases for large-scale data searching.

#### 3.5. Experimental Approach

The research evaluates Explorative Modeling in two main capacities:

1. **As a New Scaling Axis (Hybrid XMs):** Exploration is integrated into existing generative models.
 - **Models Tested:** Diffusion/Flow models and Jumpy generative models (which generalize Diffusion/Flow by varying generation steps).
 - **Domains:** Continuous (ImageNet 256x256 for image generation, Something-Something V2 for video generation) and discrete (masked diffusion language modeling - MDLM).
 - **Metrics:** FDr$_6$ and FID for images, FVD for video, Perplexity-Entropy frontier for language.
 - **Setup:** Baselines (equivalent to XM-1, no exploration) are compared against XM-K variants (K > 1), without explicit XM-specific hyperparameter tuning for the baselines.
 - **Scaling Analysis:** Performance gains are measured across varying model sizes, data scales, and training compute.

2. **As a Standalone End-to-End Generative Modeling Approach (End-to-End XMs):**
 - **Tasks:** Robotics control tasks including Behavior Cloning (compared to Diffusion Policy) and Goal-Conditioned World Modeling (compared to Diffuser).
 - **Setup:** End-to-end XMs (using Forward XM with K=10) are trained with minimal hyperparameter tuning, sometimes adding recurrent blocks.
 - **Key Comparison:** Inference compute (Network Forward Passes, NFE) against strong diffusion baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings, indicating the utility of Explorative Modeling (XM) both as an enhancement for existing generative models and as a standalone end-to-end generation paradigm.

#### 4.1. Exploration as a New Scaling Axis for Existing Generative Models

- **Improved Efficiency Across Domains:**
 - Adding exploration to the RAE (Representation Autoencoder) image generation recipe demonstrated significant efficiency gains. Exploration enabled models to achieve the baseline's performance with 6.2 times less data and 4.1 times fewer FLOPs.
 - For an optimally tuned SiT (Scalable Interpolant Transformer) baseline, exploration led to a 2.5 times reduction in data needed for equivalent performance and up to a 52% improvement in FLOP efficiency.
 - Exploration improved parameter efficiency; a "Large" model with 5 modes explored outperformed an "XLarge" model (with 47% more parameters) that did not use exploration.
- **Monotonic Performance Improvement:** Increasing the amount of exploration (K) consistently improved performance. This trend was observed across various modalities and metrics:
 - Image generation (FID for XDiffusion and XJumpy models).
 - Video generation (FVD for XDiffusion and XJumpy models).
 - Discrete data generation (Perplexity-Entropy frontier for Masked Diffusion Language Models (XMDLMs)).
- **Enhanced Generalization:** In video generation experiments, increasing exploration reduced overfitting on a fixed dataset, resulting in a better absolute minimum FVD (30.0 with XM-12 compared to 37.5 without exploration). This suggests a compute-generalization tradeoff, where additional training compute spent on exploration can improve generalization.
- **Impact on State-of-the-Art Recipes:** Exploration significantly improved state-of-the-art image generation recipes. An XRAE (Explorative RAE) model achieved a near-state-of-the-art 1.43 FID on ImageNet 256x256 without guidance and demonstrated improved FDr$_6$ scores, converging almost 300 times faster than the standard SiT recipe.
- **Gains Increase with Scale:** The benefits of exploration were observed to intensify with increasing scale. Performance gains from exploring 5 modes (XM-5) rose from 7% to 36% as data scaled and from 13% to 23% as model size scaled. The FLOP-optimal amount of exploration was found to increase as models trained for longer, indicating that generative expressivity becomes an increasing bottleneck at larger scales.
- **Beneficial to More End-to-End Models:** Jumpy generative models, which offer a spectrum of end-to-end behavior by varying the number of generation steps, exhibited greater performance improvements with exploration compared to Diffusion/Flow models. As exploration increased, the optimal number of jumps for XJumpy models decreased, suggesting that factoring the training loop can substitute for factoring the generation procedure, allowing models to become more end-to-end.

#### 4.2. End-to-End Explorative Models

- **Reduced Inference Compute on Control Tasks:** Standalone end-to-end XMs demonstrated competitive performance against multi-step diffusion baselines in robotics control tasks, but with substantially reduced inference computation.
 - **Behavior Cloning:** An Explorative Policy matched or surpassed Diffusion Policy's success rates on Robomimic tasks using a single network forward pass (NFE) at inference, compared to Diffusion Policy's 100 NFE.
 - **Goal-Conditioned World Modeling:** An Explorative World Model matched Diffuser's average performance on Maze2D tasks while utilizing 16-256 times fewer NFE (averaging 80 times less). This highlights that XMs' computational cost is primarily shifted to training rather than inference.

#### 4.3. Confirmation of Mode Forcing Theory

The experimental results are presented as empirical validations of predictions derived from the "Mode Forcing" theory. These include the idea that existing models lack sufficient generative expressivity, that this deficit becomes more pronounced at scale, that factoring training can substitute for factoring generation, and that exploration enables end-to-end generation.

#### 4.4. Implications of Surplus Generative Expressivity

The research also noted that exploration can be beneficial even when generative expressivity is not a severe bottleneck, such as in weakly multimodal distributions (e.g., video generation). This suggests that surplus generative expressivity, similar to overparametrization, may ease optimization and improve generalization by allowing models to find simpler explanations for the data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces Explorative Modeling as a novel paradigm that has several implications for the field of generative AI, addressing fundamental challenges and opening new avenues for research and application.

**Fundamental Shift in Generative Modeling:** The introduction of Explorative Modeling proposes a fundamental re-evaluation of how generative models handle multimodal distributions. By factoring the training loop rather than the generation procedure, it offers a distinct approach that aligns generative modeling with the broader deep learning trend of end-to-end training, which has driven significant successes in other domains. This challenges the long-held necessity of multi-step, factored generation processes in reconstructive models.

**Unlocking a Third Pretraining Axis:** The research establishes generative expressivity, scaled through exploration, as a critical third pretraining axis alongside parameters and data. This insight is significant because it identifies a bottleneck that previous scaling strategies could not address. By demonstrating that gains from exploration increase with model and data scale, the work suggests that future large-scale generative models will increasingly benefit from incorporating this axis, potentially leading to more capable and efficient foundation models.

**Enhanced Efficiency and Performance:** The empirical results indicate substantial improvements in FLOP, sample, and parameter efficiency. Achieving baseline performance with significantly less data and compute, and notably faster convergence, implies that Explorative Modeling can accelerate the development and training of high-performance generative models. This has practical implications for researchers and organizations facing resource constraints or aiming to push the boundaries of model scale and performance. The lift of state-of-the-art image generation recipes to near-SOTA unguided FID further underscores its practical utility.

**Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstration of a compute-generalization tradeoff, where increased training compute for exploration leads to better generalization by reducing overfitting, is a notable finding. As data becomes an increasingly precious resource in large-scale training, strategies that improve generalization from fixed datasets are highly valuable. Furthermore, by enabling more end-to-end models, exploration indirectly reduces exposure bias, leading to more robust models.

**Enabling Scalable End-to-End Generation:** One of the most significant impacts is the realization of scalable end-to-end reconstructive generative modeling. By matching the performance of multi-step diffusion models on control tasks with substantially fewer inference steps (up to 256x less), Explorative Models offer a pathway to generative systems that are highly efficient at inference time. This dramatically reduces the computational cost and latency for generating outputs, which is critical for real-time applications such as robotics, interactive content generation, and embedded AI systems.

**Theoretical Grounding and Predictive Power:** The consistent validation of predictions derived from the "Mode Forcing" theory lends strong theoretical grounding to Explorative Modeling. This theoretical framework provides a deeper understanding of why existing models struggle with generative expressivity and offers a guiding principle for designing future generative architectures and training objectives. This predictive power can accelerate informed research and development in the field.

**Future Research Directions:** The paper outlines several promising avenues for future work, including applying exploration to other generative model families (e.g., autoregressive LLMs, Energy-Based Transformers), expanding end-to-end XM applications (e.g., inpainting, super-resolution, feature-based world models), refining the core exploration mechanisms (e.g., gradient-based search, dynamic K selection, advanced Reverse XM scaling with vector databases), and integrating exploration beyond pretraining (e.g., in reinforcement learning fine-tuning). These directions suggest a rich research agenda stimulated by this work.

In conclusion, Explorative Modeling represents a substantial contribution to generative AI by fundamentally rethinking how multimodal distributions are handled during training. Its establishment as a new scaling axis and its capacity to enable efficient end-to-end generation are poised to influence the design, training, and application of future generative models.
