# Look Carefully: Adaptive Visual Reinforcements in Multimodal Large Language Models for Hallucination Mitigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Science and Technology of China and Nanyang Technological University developed the Adaptive vIsual Reinforcement (AIR) framework to mitigate hallucinations in multimodal large language models. The training-free, inference-time method selectively reinforces salient visual information using prototype-based token reduction and Optimal Transport-guided patch selection, achieving reduced hallucination rates across various MLLMs while maintaining general performance.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently generate content that deviates from or contradicts visual input (hallucination), hindering their reliability for real-world applications.
- Existing hallucination mitigation strategies are often computationally intensive, require costly human annotations (training-time), or can compromise stability/efficiency (inference-time).
- Current vision-enhancement methods for MLLMs indiscriminately inject all visual tokens, including irrelevant background information, which can distract the model and exacerbate hallucinations.

## Method

- Introduced ==Adaptive vIsual Reinforcement (AIR)==, a training-free, inference-time framework that selectively amplifies critical visual evidence and suppresses redundancy within MLLMs' feed-forward networks.
- Employed ==prototype-based token reduction==, which condenses the numerous visual tokens into a compact subset by selecting the Top-Q most distinctive tokens based on their Euclidean distance to a global prototype.
- Utilized ==Entropically Regularized Optimal Transport (OT)== to quantify the fine-grained semantic alignment between the model's hidden states and cropped image patches, selecting and reinforcing only the most relevant visual patches.

## Results

- AIR consistently reduced hallucination rates across LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen-VL-Chat, and GLM-4V-9B on the CHAIR (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B CHAIR_S reduced from **22.0** to **18.4**) and POPE benchmarks, demonstrating improved robustness without degrading general reasoning.
- The framework operates with only a marginal increase in average latency (e.g., **2.07s** for AIR vs. **1.68s** for vanilla LLaVA-1.5-7B) and GPU memory, making it practical for real-world deployment.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both prototype-based token reduction and OT-guided patch reinforcement contribute to hallucination mitigation, with optimal performance achieved when applying reinforcement in mid-to-deep Transformer layers (e.g., layers 24-32).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Indiscriminate re-injection of all visual tokens, including irrelevant noise, can be detrimental to MLLM performance by distracting the model from critical visual cues and potentially increasing hallucination.
> - Salient visual patches consistently exhibit higher semantic alignment with MLLM decoder hidden states than irrelevant background regions, suggesting that selective reinforcement of these areas is more effective.
> - Optimal Transport offers a more sensitive and principled measure for assessing the fine-grained semantic alignment between multimodal representations (hidden states and visual patches) compared to simpler distance metrics.
