# Grounding Everything in Tokens for Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GETok enhances Multimodal Large Language Models' 2D spatial reasoning by introducing a lexical spatial representation with learnable grid and offset tokens for native object grounding. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance in referring expression segmentation and demonstrates improvements in complex real-world driving tasks.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs struggle with precise 2D spatial grounding due to the loss of fine-grained spatial information during image tokenization.
- Current spatial representations (text-based, patch-based, 1D bin-based) are inefficient, ambiguous, lack unified handling for various formats, and often require architectural modifications.
- MLLMs typically make one-shot spatial predictions without an inherent mechanism for iterative self-correction, limiting precision in complex scenarios.

## Method

- GETok augments MLLM vocabulary with learnable ==Grid Tokens== for coarse spatial anchoring and ==Offset Tokens== for fine-grained, iterative refinement and self-correction, directly integrating into autoregressive architectures.
- A novel ==greedy mask-to-token conversion algorithm== and an offset-aware dataset construction method are used for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) without altering the MLLM architecture.
- A self-improving Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework employs ==two-stage GRPO optimization== with ==geometry-aware reward functions== to leverage GETok's structured 2D lattice for stable policy learning and efficient exploration.

## Results

- GETok-RL achieved state-of-the-art performance in Referring Expression Segmentation, showing a **+4.5%** gain over GETok-SFT on ReasonSeg and RefCOCO datasets, with offset tokens improving performance by **+1.0%** (SFT) and **+1.5%** (RL).
- In a real-world driving case study, GETok outperformed traditional visual prompts, improving traffic sign color recognition by **+12.24%** and achieving a **+10%** F1-score gain for curved lane polyline detection over coordinate-based methods.
- A **32x32** grid with offset tokens achieved comparable or superior performance to a **64x64** grid while significantly reducing token length and vocabulary overhead, demonstrating the efficiency of the offset mechanism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GETok's 2D grid tokens natively encode spatial topology, outperforming 1D text coordinates and bin tokens by enabling smoother, topology-aware attention maps and reducing format brittleness.
> - Offset tokens provide fine-grained precision and iterative self-correction with minimal vocabulary overhead, achieving performance comparable to higher-resolution grids at a significantly reduced computational cost.
> - The structured 2D lattice of GETok creates a low-entropy action space, making it highly amenable to GRPO, leading to accelerated convergence and consistently higher reward levels in RL for spatial reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated advancements in vision understanding and reasoning, predominantly relying on autoregressive Transformer architectures. However, a notable limitation persists in their ability to accurately ground objects within the 2D image space. This issue arises because the tokenization of input images, analogous to text tokenization, often results in a loss of spatial information, which hinders precise spatial localization and reasoning.

Existing efforts to address this challenge have explored several approaches:
- **Text-based coordinate representations:** Methods like Qwen-VL describe object locations using text, but these often struggle to preserve spatial topology, leading to syntactic overhead and tokenization bias.
- **Image-patch based visual tokens:** Approaches such as ClawMachine project image patches into visual tokens via linear projection. A limitation of these methods is the fixed patch size determined by the image encoder, which can entangle texture with geometry and potentially confuse texture-similar objects at different spatial locations.
- **Bin-based methods:** Techniques like Pix2Seq discretize coordinates into one-dimensional bins to describe bounding boxes. While these introduce learnable spatial tokens, they do not adequately capture explicit 2D structural relationships. This 1D indexing can also lead to unstable reward landscapes in reinforcement learning (RL) schemes like GRPO, where small index changes can result in disproportionately large reward fluctuations.
- **Specialized region modules or external decoders:** Some methods introduce additional architectural components (e.g., GPT4RoI, LISA) or trigger external segmenters (e.g., Seg-Zero) to handle complex shape representations or mask-based referring. While these can improve alignment or complex shape representation, they often increase architectural complexity and may require task-specific modifications. Kosmos-2 also uses 2D spatial tokens but is limited to basic bounding box grounding.

This work positions itself within this landscape by identifying the core challenge as establishing a reliable mapping between discrete sequential tokens and continuous 2D space. It proposes a lexical spatial representation, GETok, that integrates learnable, discrete tokens into MLLMs, aiming to provide a general-purpose, unified spatial lexicon that overcomes the limitations of prior tokenization and architectural approaches. GETok's objective is to enable native 2D space reasoning and precise object grounding without modifying the underlying autoregressive Transformer architecture, offering a solution that is both architecturally simple and capable of iterative refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the capacity of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for precise 2D spatial reasoning and object grounding within images. The authors aim to achieve this by developing a novel spatial representation that seamlessly integrates into existing MLLM architectures.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current MLLMs:
- **Loss of Spatial Information:** The autoregressive Transformer architecture, foundational to MLLMs, necessitates the tokenization of input images. This process frequently results in a substantial loss of fine-grained spatial information, impeding the models' ability to perform accurate 2D spatial localization.
- **Inefficiencies of Existing Grounding Methods:**
 - **Text-based coordinates:** While simple, these methods struggle with maintaining spatial topology and introduce considerable syntactic overhead and potential tokenization biases.
 - **Image-patch-based methods:** These approaches are often constrained by fixed patch sizes and coupling with visual encoders, which can lead to ambiguity when distinguishing texture-similar objects at different locations.
 - **Bin-based methods:** Quantizing 2D coordinates into 1D bins does not adequately capture the smooth, continuous nature of 2D space. This discontinuity can destabilize reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks, where minor changes in 1D indices can lead to significant and unpredictable changes in 2D spatial outcomes and rewards.
- **Lack of Unified Representation:** Current MLLMs often require task-specific modules or specialized output heads to handle diverse spatial grounding tasks, such as identifying points, bounding boxes, or segmentation masks. There is a need for a unified representation that can accommodate these varied formats within a standard autoregressive framework.
- **Absence of Self-Correction:** Existing methods typically perform one-shot spatial predictions. If an initial prediction is inaccurate, the model generally lacks an inherent mechanism for iterative self-correction or refinement, leading to unaddressed localization errors.
- **Suboptimal RL Action Space:** For reinforcement learning, unstructured or discontinuous spatial representations can create high-entropy action spaces with unstable reward landscapes, hindering efficient policy optimization.

To address these motivations, the authors propose GETok, which aims to:
1. **Establish a Reliable Mapping:** Create a robust and learnable mapping between discrete sequential tokens and continuous 2D space.
2. **Provide a Unified Spatial Lexicon:** Augment MLLMs with a specialized vocabulary of grid and offset tokens that can universally represent various spatial entities (points, bounding boxes, polylines, masks) without architectural modifications.
3. **Enable Iterative Refinement:** Implement a mechanism for coarse-to-fine localization refinement using offset tokens, allowing the model to correct initial grounding errors and improve precision through a self-improving chain.
4. **Facilitate Geometry-Aware Policy Optimization:** Construct a geometrically grounded action space that supports stable reward landscapes and efficient exploration, particularly beneficial for reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GETok (Grounding Everything in Tokens) method integrates a specialized vocabulary of learnable tokens into MLLMs to enhance 2D spatial reasoning. It comprises two primary token types: Grid tokens for initial coarse localization and Offset tokens for precise, iterative refinement. The approach is instantiated under both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and self-improving reinforcement learning (RL) settings.

#### 3.1. GETok Architecture

- **Grid Tokens (`<grid_i,j>`):** These tokens discretize the image plane into an `n × n` uniform grid, with each cell assigned a unique, learnable token. This establishes a structured spatial topology, associating spatial anchors with local image regions. While providing native 2D spatial awareness, increasing resolution quadratically expands the vocabulary.
- **Offset Tokens (`<OFF_δu,δv>`, `<DELETE>`):** These tokens address the vocabulary bottleneck of grid tokens by enabling fine-grained spatial refinement. They represent discrete displacement vectors (e.g., `δu, δv ∈ {-1, 0, 1}`), allowing adjustments around a grid token's location, and include a `<DELETE>` token to reject erroneous proposals. This mechanism allows for increased effective precision (e.g., `32x32` grid with offsets can achieve `64x64` effective precision) with minimal vocabulary expansion. The use of the `<DELETE>` token also facilitates progressive, iterative localization refinement.
- **Unified Vocabulary:** The complete vocabulary `V = V_LLM ∪ T_grid ∪ T_offset` treats these spatial tokens as precise spatial pronouns, integrating them directly into the MLLM's autoregressive framework without architectural modification.

#### 3.2. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The SFT approach focuses on constructing training data that leverages the GETok vocabulary.
- **Greedy Mask-to-Token Conversion:** To convert dense masks into discrete grid points (which is challenging for complex, multiply-connected regions), a greedy algorithm is employed.
 1. An image and `n^2` grid points are fed into the Segment Anything Model (SAM) to generate `K` candidate masks.
 2. Given a ground-truth mask (`M_gt`), the algorithm identifies a minimal set of grid points whose union of corresponding SAM-generated masks approximates `M_gt` above a specified Intersection-over-Union (IoU) threshold (`τ`).
 3. This is framed as an optimization problem (minimizing `∥π∥_0` subject to IoU constraint) and solved efficiently by iteratively adding the mask proposal that yields the highest IoU improvement with `M_union`.
 4. A mask is thus represented as an unordered sequence of matched grid tokens, e.g., `<seg><grid_i1,j1>...<grid_in,jn></seg>`. This process is training-free.
- **Offset-Aware Dataset Construction:** To train offset tokens for localization refinement, a systematic approach categorizes grid points based on their spatial relation to mask boundaries using morphological operations:
 1. **Inside:** Points stably within the mask, mapped to `<OFF_0,0>`.
 2. **Ring:** Points near the boundary, potentially requiring non-zero offsets.
 3. **Far:** Distant negative points, mapped to `<DELETE>`.
 4. **Hard-Delete:** Challenging edge cases, also mapped to `<DELETE>`.
 Training pairs are sampled with a bias towards 'Inside' and 'Ring' regions, where offset corrections are most valuable. This curated dataset focuses on boundary-proximal scenarios to foster effective refinement.

#### 3.3. Self-Improving Reinforcement Learning (RL)

GETok's structured 2D lattice provides a geometrically grounded action space, making it suitable for RL. The framework uses GRPO and incorporates a multi-turn refinement process for self-correction.
- **Cold Start Initialization:** The pipeline begins with an SFT-trained model to provide a foundation for generating spatially grounded GETok responses.
- **Two-Stage GRPO Training:**
 1. **Grid Token Generation (Stage 1):** Focuses on generating initial grid tokens.
 - **Format Reward:** Encourages structured output with `<think>` tags for reasoning and `<answer>` tags for spatial predictions (`<box>`, `<seg>` tokens).
 - **Non-repeat Reward:** Penalizes sentence-level repetition.
 - **Mask Reward:** Promotes high IoU between generated and ground-truth masks (using SAM for mask generation from predicted points/boxes).
 - **Box Reward:** Encourages high box IoU and small L1 corner distances.
 - **Semantic-Critical Points Reward:** Evaluates segmentation quality by combining hit ratio (points inside `M_gt`) and spatial distribution, balancing point density to avoid sparse or excessive predictions.
 2. **Offset Token Refinement (Stage 2):** Introduces multi-turn dialogues for precision improvement. This stage explicitly encourages meaningful geometric updates to avoid collapses where the model predicts no offsets.
 - **Format Reward:** Enforces a minimal schema for per-instance `<offset>` tokens (containing `<box>` and `<seg>` serializations).
 - **Point Refinement Reward:** Assigns a ternary score (-1, 0, +1) per point based on movement relative to `M_gt` (leaving, entering/staying, valid deletion). A deletion is valid if `<DELETE>` is predicted and no point in the 3x3 neighborhood is inside `M_gt`.
 - **Box Refinement Reward:** Rewards positive IoU gain for refined bounding boxes over initial predictions.
 - **Mask IoU Gain Reward:** Favors larger IoU improvements by normalizing the gain by the maximum possible improvement.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B (7B parameters)
- **SFT Training:** Uses `ms swift` framework with LoRA (rank=64), batch size of 16, learning rate of `1 × 10^-6`. Trained on public corpora including image reasoning, referring grounding, and segmentation data.
- **RL Training:** Uses GRPO algorithm via `easy-r1` framework, initialized from a cold-start SFT model. Stage 1 (grid token) uses a 9K dataset (LISA++, RefSeg samples) for 200 steps. Stage 2 (offset token) focuses on refinement.
- **Hardware:** 8x80GB GPUs with DeepSpeed engine.
- **Parameters:** Grid size of 32, offset size of 64.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluated on Referring Expression Comprehension (REC), Referring Expression Segmentation (RES), Reasoning Segmentation, Referring Captioning, Generalized Referring Expression Segmentation (gRES), Lane Polyline Detection, Object Pointing, and a proprietary driving case study.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various visual referring tasks demonstrate GETok's performance in both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) settings.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Referring Expression Segmentation (RES):**
 - **GETok-SFT** achieved competitive performance against specialized methods while maintaining architectural simplicity. It attained an average score of 68.2% (cIoU) across ReasonSeg and RefCOCO (+/g) datasets, showing improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (65.7%) and LISA (64.2%).
 - **GETok-RL** demonstrated superior performance, reaching a state-of-the-art average score of 72.7%. This represents a +4.5% gain over its SFT counterpart, indicating that the structured action space of GETok is conducive to stable policy optimization and efficient exploration within RL paradigms.
 - The **offset mechanism** consistently contributed to performance gains: +1.0% in SFT and +1.5% in RL over grid-only configurations, highlighting its importance for mask generation precision.
 - Qualitative results showed that GETok, when combined with SAM, retains SAM's generalization capabilities, producing high-quality masks even for small objects and complex shapes, with adaptive corrections via offset tokens.

- **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC):**
 - **GETok-SFT** achieved solid performance with an Acc@0.5 of 88.2% (average across RefCOCO datasets), showing a +1.6% improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (86.6%).
 - Under the stricter **Acc@0.8 metric**, GETok-SFT improved to 72.8%, showcasing significant gains in spatial reasoning, particularly for smaller objects.
 - **GETok-RL** further enhanced performance, reaching an average Acc@0.5 of 90.3% and Acc@0.8 of 76.1%. The gains from RL were more pronounced in complex reasoning tasks, suggesting that iterative refinement and chain-of-thought reasoning benefit most from this approach.

#### 4.2. Additional Benchmarks (from Supplementary Materials)

- **Referring Captioning:** GETok-SFT achieved competitive or superior results on refCOCOg and Visual Genome datasets compared to models using specialized region feature extractors. For instance, on refCOCOg, it achieved a METEOR score of 16.9 and CIDEr of 110.5, outperforming several baselines. This suggests its effectiveness in region-aware comprehension, especially in scenarios with overlapping objects where traditional bounding boxes might be insufficient.
- **Generalized Referring Expression Segmentation (gRES):** GETok supports multi-instance expressions and achieved competitive cIoU scores on the gRefCOCO dataset (67.8% for SFT, 69.0% for RL), indicating its capability for simultaneous referencing of multiple objects.
- **Object Pointing:** GETok-SFT showed competitive performance on HumanRef and RefCOCOg datasets (70.7% on HumanRef, 84.1% on RefCOCOg val), especially in dense object scenarios. Its single spatial token representation for coordinates helps reduce formatting errors common in longer text-based coordinate sequences.

#### 4.3. Grid Resolution

- The `32x32` grid configuration achieved performance comparable to `64x64` (e.g., REC Acc@0.8 of 70.9% vs. 71.2%), but with significantly lower average token length per mask (8.7 vs. 14.6).
- Offset tokens demonstrated efficiency, enabling `32x32` with offsets to outperform the `64x64` configuration (REC Acc@0.8 of 72.6% vs. 71.2%) with only 10 additional tokens, compared to introducing 3072 new grid tokens for a `64x64` grid over `32x32`.

#### 4.4. Real-World Driving Case Study

- Using a proprietary driving dataset with polylines (lanes), static obstacles (bounding boxes), and traffic signs (key points), GETok consistently outperformed traditional visual prompts.
- It achieved a +12.24% increase in traffic sign color recognition and a +7.95% increase in static obstacle classification, demonstrating superior region-referencing in complex driving scenarios.
- For lane detection, GETok's transformation of continuous coordinate regression into discrete point selection resulted in a +3% increase in precision, a +18% increase in recall, and a +10% increase in F1-score compared to coordinate-based methods, showcasing particular strength with curved lanes.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (from Supplementary Materials)

- **Point Representation Formats:** Compared to text coordinates and 1D bin tokens, grid tokens showed a clear advantage in REC tasks, attributed to better preservation of spatial topology, reduced format brittleness, and a more direct correlation between token-level changes and geometric errors. Visualizations confirmed smoother, topology-aware activations with grid tokens.
- **GRPO Effectiveness:** GETok's structured representation led to accelerated convergence and higher mean rewards in GRPO optimization compared to text coordinates, validating its geometrically grounded action space.
- **Mask Representation with Sparse Geometry:** The greedy mask-to-token conversion algorithm proved effective for dense masks. This training-free decoding approach offers flexibility, allowing for seamless updates to the underlying mask decoder (e.g., from SAM to SAM2) without retraining the entire MLLM, unlike methods that rely on task-specific decoders.
- **Image Preprocessing:** Simple image resizing performed comparably to dynamic resolution and outperformed padding or center cropping in REC tasks, which could introduce artifacts or remove relevant context.
- **Reward Function Design:** Ablations on reward components confirmed the necessity of all designed rewards (format, non-repeat, mask, box, semantic-critical points for grid token generation; format, point refinement, box refinement, mask IoU gain for offset token refinement) for robust performance and preventing undesirable model behaviors.
- **Reasoning vs. No Reasoning for Offset Refinement:** The `<think>` preamble, while beneficial in initial reasoning, provided negligible performance gain during the offset refinement stage, suggesting that point-level adjustments do not substantially benefit from additional verbal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of GETok represents an advancement in the capability of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform precise 2D spatial reasoning and object grounding. This work addresses a fundamental limitation in current MLLMs, where the autoregressive Transformer architecture and traditional image tokenization often lead to a loss of spatial information and difficulties in accurate 2D localization.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
1. **Unified Spatial Representation:** GETok offers a novel lexical spatial representation that directly embeds a vocabulary of learnable tokens into MLLMs. This enables accurate reasoning over native 2D spatial space for various tasks (points, bounding boxes, polylines, and masks) without requiring architectural modifications or task-specific modules, thereby simplifying the MLLM architecture while enhancing its versatility.
2. **Iterative Localization Refinement:** The integration of offset tokens facilitates a coarse-to-fine correction mechanism. This scheme enables iterative recovery from initial grounding errors, allowing the model to refine its spatial predictions. This self-correction capability is a significant advancement over one-shot prediction methods, which often leave initial errors unaddressed, and is particularly crucial for tasks requiring high precision like mask generation for small or complex objects.
3. **Geometry-Aware Policy Optimization:** By creating a geometrically grounded and low-entropy action space through its grid and offset token hierarchy, GETok significantly improves the efficiency and stability of policy optimization in reinforcement learning frameworks. This structural advantage contributes to faster convergence and higher rewards in RL settings, broadening the applicability and effectiveness of RL for spatial reasoning tasks in MLLMs.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced MLLM Capabilities:** GETok can expand the utility of MLLMs in applications requiring fine-grained spatial interaction and reasoning, such as sophisticated visual assistants, advanced robotics perception, and augmented reality systems, where precise object localization and manipulation are critical.
- **Improved Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** The demonstrated superior performance across a wide array of referring tasks, including segmentation, comprehension, captioning, object pointing, and lane detection, suggests that GETok could become a foundational component for general-purpose visual grounding in MLLMs.
- **Real-World Application Potential:** The successful application in complex real-world scenarios, such as the autonomous driving case study, indicates that GETok is robust enough for high-stakes environments where accurate spatial understanding of dynamic and static objects is essential for safety and operational efficiency.
- **Research Direction for Spatial AI:** This work opens new avenues for research in optimizing spatial tokenization strategies and further integrating reinforcement learning with structured spatial representations to tackle more complex multimodal tasks. It proposes a path towards MLLMs that can more naturally and precisely interact with the 2D visual world.
- **Architectural Simplicity:** The ability to achieve these advancements without modifying the underlying autoregressive architecture of MLLMs implies that GETok can be readily adopted and integrated into existing and future MLLM frameworks, potentially accelerating development in multimodal AI.
