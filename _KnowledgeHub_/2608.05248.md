# WorldClaw: Agentic 3D Open-World Generation at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> Tencent Hunyuan introduces WorldClaw, an agentic framework for generating large-scale, explicit, and editable 3D open-worlds from open-ended text prompts. The system produces globally coherent terrains with rich local content, composed of independent textured meshes suitable for free-viewpoint exploration and downstream editing.

## Problem

- Generating large-scale 3D worlds requires simultaneously maintaining global spatial coherence and rich, diverse local detail.
- Existing methods struggle to produce explicit, editable 3D assets that are suitable for downstream content production workflows.
- Previous MLLM agents often lack precise 3D spatial control, leading to inconsistencies in object placement and scene structure.

## Method

- **WorldClaw** employs a ==coarse-to-fine, global-to-regional agentic framework== with three stages: Intent Analysis & Planning, Global Terrain Generation, and Regional Object Generation & Placement.
- A ==semantic-layout-guided procedural terrain generator== creates controllable landforms and asset scattering based on region-aware plans.
- An ==iterative agentic refinement loop==, connected to a 3D renderer, continually improves terrain and object quality, corrects scales and poses, and resolves object-terrain contact issues.

## Results

- The system successfully generates diverse 3D open-worlds with varied landforms and continuous global terrain, like tropical islands, river canyons, and mountain valleys, maintaining spatial coherence.
- WorldClaw produces rich, region-specific local content (e.g., settlements, vegetation, vehicles) with plausible object scales, orientations, and object-terrain contacts.
- Qualitative comparisons show WorldClaw surpasses baselines in geometric expressiveness, spatial organization, content richness, prompt alignment, and the provision of explicit, editable 3D assets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Globally coherent worlds can be effectively generated by establishing shared global constraints and progressively realizing local content and relationships.
> - Orchestrating specialized agents that communicate via structured intermediate representations enables the decomposition and management of complex 3D generation tasks.
> - Combining large language models for high-level planning with image generation, 3D reconstruction, and render-guided refinement provides fine-grained control and maintains geometric fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of large-scale, freely explorable 3D worlds from open-ended text input represents a long-standing challenge in generative content creation, with applications across video games, film production, virtual reality, embodied intelligence, and robot simulation. Such generated worlds require coherent geometry, appearance, and semantics across a continuous space, along with properties like navigability, structural consistency, and editability.

Existing methods for 3D scene generation can be categorized into four main paradigms:
- **Procedural Content Generation (PCG):** These methods, exemplified by systems like Infinigen, rely on predefined rules and executable programs to build terrain, vegetation, and structures. While offering strong controllability and scalability, their expressiveness is often limited by the pre-coded rules.
- **Image/Video-Lifting Methods:** Approaches such as Marble leverage image or video generators to synthesize visual observations, subsequently lifting them into 3D representations (e.g., meshes, 3D Gaussians) via depth estimation or multi-view reconstruction. These methods can produce rich content but frequently struggle with global consistency, geometric fidelity, and computational efficiency for large scenes. Novel viewpoints may also exhibit blurring or incoherence.
- **Native 3D Diffusion Methods:** These models learn scene distributions directly over 3D voxels, distance fields, or Gaussians, aiming for improved geometric fidelity and cross-view consistency (e.g., XCube, LT3SD). While efficient for high-quality geometry and textures, their content diversity is often constrained by the scarcity of large-scale 3D scene datasets.
- **Multi-Modal LLM Agents (MLLM-Agents):** These leverage large language models for intent understanding, spatial planning, tool orchestration, and render-based refinement (e.g., SceneSmith, SAGE). While proficient in global planning and semantic-level task execution, they may lack precise 3D spatial control, leading to overcorrection or inadequate spatial awareness when adjusting object properties.

WorldClaw positions itself within the MLLM-Agent paradigm but introduces a coarse-to-fine, global-to-regional agentic framework designed to address the joint challenge of maintaining global spatial coherence, rich local content, and explicit, editable instance-level assets. It seeks to mitigate the limitations of previous MLLM-Agent systems by enhancing precise 3D spatial control and structured generation, while also attempting to overcome the content diversity constraints of native 3D diffusion and the consistency issues of visual-prior-based methods. The work emphasizes an "explorable and editable" output, bridging the gap between generative models and practical 3D content-production workflows.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a system, WorldClaw, capable of generating large-scale, freely explorable 3D worlds from open-ended text prompts. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges inherent in 3D content creation:

1. **Joint Maintenance of Global Coherence and Local Detail:** Generating expansive 3D worlds requires a system to simultaneously ensure overall spatial consistency and plausible transitions between different regions (global coherence) while also populating specific areas with rich, diverse, and high-quality content (local detail).
2. **Explicit, Editable, and Reusable Assets:** Unlike static images or videos, generated 3D worlds for applications like gaming or simulation must be composed of explicit 3D assets (e.g., textured meshes) that are suitable for downstream editing, reuse, and integration into existing production pipelines. This demands an instance-level representation rather than a monolithic scene.
3. **Structural Consistency and Navigability:** The generated environments need to be physically plausible, supporting free-viewpoint exploration and interaction. This implies coherent geometry, consistent appearance, and structurally sound landforms and objects that users can "walk" through.
4. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Methods:**
 - **Procedural methods** often lack the expressive capacity for diverse content beyond their hard-coded rules.
 - **Image/Video-lifting methods** frequently suffer from global inconsistency and geometric inaccuracies due to limited viewpoints or computational demands for large scenes.
 - **Native 3D diffusion models** are constrained by the scarcity of diverse 3D datasets, limiting their content generation range.
 - **MLLM agents**, while strong in high-level planning, have shown limitations in fine-grained 3D spatial control and precision.

WorldClaw's design is guided by the insight that a globally coherent world does not need to be generated everywhere at once. Instead, shared constraints (scene semantics, spatial organization, terrain foundation) can be established globally, with local objects and relationships progressively realized where needed. This coarse-to-fine, global-to-regional principle forms the core motivation.

Specifically, the paper lists three main contributions:
- Proposing a semantic-layout-guided procedural terrain generator that creates controllable, region-aware landforms across spatial scales.
- Translating global scene and local terrain constraints into executable regional plans to achieve diverse, spatially coherent object layouts with fine-grained regional control.
- Introducing an agentic refinement loop that iteratively improves terrain and object quality, corrects object scale and pose, and resolves object-terrain contact issues.

These objectives collectively aim to produce explicit 3D worlds that are not only visually compelling but also functionally robust for exploration, interaction, simulation, and iterative content creation workflows.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldClaw employs a coarse-to-fine, global-to-regional agentic framework structured into three sequential stages, orchestrated by specialized agents that communicate through structured intermediate representations. The overall process transforms a user prompt `q` into a final 3D world `S` composed of a global terrain `T` and a set of regional object instances `O`.

#### 3.1. Intent Analysis and Planning (Section 2.1)

This initial stage converts an open-ended text prompt `q` into a structured scene specification `P = (R, C_terrain, C_object)`.
- **Intent Analysis Agent:** Extracts explicit constraints from `q`, such as scene type, theme, visual style, key regions, objects, spatial relationships, and user preferences. This agent summarizes existing information without introducing new content.
- **Scene Planning Agent:** Resolves ambiguities and completes unspecified information according to a predefined schema, ensuring consistency for downstream modules. `R` describes major regions and their relationships; `C_terrain` details terrain types, landform characteristics, materials, and associated assets for each region; `C_object` specifies object categories, appearance, densities, and relationships. Global properties like scene theme and visual style are maintained as shared attributes across these specifications.

#### 3.2. Global Terrain Generation (Section 2.2)

This module transforms the structured scene specification `P` into a coherent global terrain representation `T`, progressively building the terrain through explicit intermediate representations.

- **Terrain Planning:** A terrain planning agent refines `P` into `P_terrain = (p_layout, p_asset, p_material, θ_terrain)`.
 - `p_layout`: Specifies region categories, relative positions, adjacencies, and approximate coverage.
 - `p_asset`: Describes terrain-associated assets, regional affinities, and target densities.
 - `p_material`: Defines surface types, visual styles, and texture requirements.
 - `θ_terrain`: Contains numerical parameters like world scale, base elevations, noise frequencies, geomorphic operators, and blending widths.
 - This stage may invoke a search tool for external knowledge or generate a scene concept image `I_concept` for visual conditioning.

- **Terrain Asset Generation:** A terrain asset generation agent, conditioned by `P_terrain` and `I_concept`, produces `A_terrain = (I_layout, I_asset, O_asset, M_terrain)`.
 - **Scene Layout Map (I_layout):** A 2D image generated by GPT-Image-2, using distinct colors to encode predefined terrain categories and convert textual descriptions into a unified spatial partition.
 - **Terrain Asset Prototypes (O_asset):** A set of reusable 3D assets (e.g., rocks, vegetation) generated from representative images `I_asset` (also by GPT-Image-2) using Hunyuan3D's image-to-3D capability.
 - **Terrain Materials (M_terrain):** Constructed using generative texture synthesis (e.g., albedo, normal maps for complex surfaces) and procedural material generation (Blender material nodes for tileable, adjustable surfaces).

- **Terrain Generation & Refinement:**
 - **Initial Height-Field Generation:** The agent parses `I_layout`, extracts region masks, and generates a composite height field `H(x)` using Equation (6), which combines region-specific base elevations, noise components, and geomorphic operators. Regional weights are used for material assignment and blending.
 - **Global Terrain Asset Scattering:** Reusable 3D asset prototypes from `O_asset` are scattered across the terrain based on layout masks, regional affinities, and local surface conditions (elevation, slope, normals) to ensure plausible distribution and adaptation.
 - **Terrain Refinement:** A terrain refinement agent (powered by BlenderMCP) iteratively renders the scene from predefined viewpoints, inspects geometry, materials, asset scattering, and rendering configuration. It performs localized corrections to parameters like terrain blending, material scales, and asset transforms, repeating until issues are resolved or an iteration budget is met.

#### 3.3. Regional Object Generation and Placement (Section 2.3)

This module populates selected regions with fine-grained instance-level content while preserving the global terrain structure.

- **Regional Planning:** The regional planning agent examines `P` and `T`, prioritizes regions with uninstantiated object requirements, selects a subset `R+` for development, and refines scene-level constraints into `P_regional`, which comprises tuples `P_r` for each selected region. Each `P_r` defines the region's functional role (`ϕ_r`), object categories, counts, and densities (`C_object_r`), object-object and object-terrain relationships (`p_spatial_r`), and appearance requirements (`p_appearance_r`).

- **Object Generation & Placement:** For each selected region `r`, this stage produces a set of objects `O_r = {M_i, U_i, T_i_place}` (geometry, appearance, placement transformation).
 - **Region Composition:** The agent renders the local terrain as `I_terrain_r` using camera parameters `κ_r`. It then converts `P_r` into a regional prompt and generates a `I_comp_r` (region composition image) conditioned on `I_terrain_r`, `P_r`, and optionally `I_concept` using a generative image model `G_image`. This `I_comp_r` serves as a 2D layout prior.
 - **Object Generation:** Text-guided SAM3 extracts individual 2D object instances from `I_comp_r`. For each instance `i`, an object-centric image `I_i` and mask `S_i` are created. SAM3D then reconstructs a 3D mesh `M_i`, appearance `U_i`, and a local-to-object-camera transformation `T_i_l2c`. Image-space scale calibration (Equation 11) is performed to correct the reconstructed scale within the object-camera coordinate system.
 - **Object Placement:** 3D placement (`T_i_place`) is recovered by matching corresponding rays from the object reconstruction camera and the regional terrain camera. An initial 3D scale `s_i` is calculated (Equation 12) based on focal lengths and depths. The complete transformation (Equation 13) maps an object reference point `P_o` to a target terrain anchor `P_t`. Further refinement adjusts anchor depth and isotropic scale to optimize object-terrain contact ratio.

- **Scene Refinement:** A scene refinement agent (connected to Blender via MCP) iteratively processes objects and terrain.
 - **Object Refinement:** Evaluates object pose, mesh quality, and scale against semantic and regional context. Inconsistent objects are corrected by updating placement transforms. Low-quality SAM3D reconstructions can be further refined by Hunyuan3D, which uses the coarse mesh as a structural constraint and the object-centric image for shape and appearance cues.
 - **Terrain Refinement:** Examines support-surface quality and object-terrain collisions (e.g., floating, penetration). Defects are corrected through object-terrain co-deformation (vertical repositioning, partial embedding, local terrain displacement, flattening, or smoothing). Modifications are localized to preserve global landform.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WorldClaw demonstrates the capability to generate diverse, large-scale 3D open-worlds from open-ended text prompts, characterized by global spatial coherence, rich local content, and editable instance-level assets.

**Qualitative Results:**
The paper presents various generated worlds, including a tropical pirate island, a river canyon with tribal settlements, a desert battlefield, and a snow-covered mountain valley with futuristic facilities. Additional examples feature a medieval-style village, a volcanic demon lair, and an island with Japanese-style towns.
- **Diverse World Structures:** The system successfully generates varied landform compositions, such as irregular coastlines, continuous rivers with elevation changes, layered rocky terrains, and enclosed mountain valleys, consistently anchoring these with a coherent global terrain foundation. This indicates an ability to create distinct yet spatially connected regions.
- **Rich Local Content and Coherent Placement:** Local views showcase region-specific content, including settlements, vegetation, docks, defensive structures, and vehicles. Object distributions are adapted to the terrain and regional function. Objects generally maintain plausible scales, orientations, and object-terrain contacts across both level and sloped surfaces.
- **Explicit and Editable Representation:** The renderings (instance, depth, normal) confirm that the generated scenes are composed of independent, manageable textured meshes for both global terrain and regional objects, supporting free-viewpoint exploration and downstream editing.

**Qualitative Comparison with Baselines (SynCity, Marble, MajutsuCity, WorldGen, GPT 5.6 Sol):**

- **Terrain and Region Organization:** WorldClaw demonstrates superior geometric expressiveness and spatial organization compared to baselines. While SynCity had weaker long-range organization and Marble lacked explicit region-level organization, WorldClaw's semantic-layout-guided approach produces continuous global terrain with pronounced elevation variations and semantically distinct yet connected regions. MajutsuCity and GPT-5.6 Sol yielded more regular or simpler ground geometry, and WorldGen displayed relatively flat terrain.
- **Content Richness and Prompt Alignment:** WorldClaw produced richer content variation and closer semantic alignment with prompts. Unlike SynCity's coarse or repeated structures, Marble's limited object categories, or GPT-5.6 Sol's simplified geometry, WorldClaw selectively populates regions with diverse elements (settlements, vegetation, animals, vehicles), aligning well with their intended regional functions. MajutsuCity, while good at instance layout, was more city-centric and less aligned with varied natural settings.
- **Free-Viewpoint Appearance and Scene Representation:** WorldClaw maintains geometric stability and exposes independently controllable object assets. SynCity's block-wise generation could lead to local inconsistencies, and Marble's quality degraded over longer distances, exhibiting incomplete or distorted geometry. While WorldGen provided explicit textured meshes and stable local structures, and GPT-5.6 Sol also produced editable objects, their visual fidelity and aesthetic quality in walk views were lower due to simplified geometry and basic materials. WorldClaw's combination of explicit global terrain and independently reconstructed object meshes enables high fidelity in both large-scale structure and local content, with instance-level editability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WorldClaw represents a notable advancement in the field of 3D content generation, specifically for open-world environments. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable agentic, coarse-to-fine framework capable of generating large-scale, explicit, and editable 3D worlds from natural language descriptions.

**Key Impacts:**

1. **Enabling Scalable and Coherent World Creation:** The global-to-regional generation principle allows for the creation of expansive 3D worlds that maintain overall spatial coherence and logical regional organization, addressing a fundamental challenge where previous methods often sacrificed either global consistency or local detail.
2. **Facilitating Content Production Workflows:** By generating scenes as independently editable textured meshes with explicit terrain placements, WorldClaw directly supports conventional 3D content production workflows in industries such as video games, film, and virtual reality. This allows for free-viewpoint rendering, asset reuse, and subsequent manual or automated modifications.
3. **Advancing Embodied AI and Simulation:** The creation of structurally consistent and navigable environments is crucial for training and testing embodied AI agents and robotic simulations. WorldClaw's ability to produce plausible and interactive 3D spaces can accelerate research in these areas by providing diverse and controllable simulation environments.
4. **Improving User Accessibility:** By enabling open-ended text prompts to drive complex 3D world generation, WorldClaw lowers the barrier for content creators, allowing individuals without extensive 3D modeling expertise to conceptualize and generate detailed virtual environments.
5. **Blueprint for Future Generative Systems:** The agentic, modular architecture, which combines large language models, image generation, 3D reconstruction, and render-guided refinement, offers a robust framework for future generative visual intelligence systems. It highlights the potential for decomposing complex creative tasks into manageable, verifiable stages.

**Future Directions and Broader Vision:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, such as reliance on underlying foundation models, stability risks in LLM-generated code, and computational efficiency. However, these also point to avenues for future impact. Integrating code-native 3D modeling and deeper collaboration with production-oriented game engines like Unreal Engine could lead to even more efficient, scalable, and practically applicable systems. The ultimate vision is a future where 3D content creation moves beyond manual asset preparation and complex technical processes, focusing instead solely on the creator's intent and imagination, with agents handling the underlying technical execution through executable code. This could fundamentally transform how virtual worlds are conceived, designed, and brought to life.
