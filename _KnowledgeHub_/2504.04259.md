# ORCA: An Open-Source, Reliable, Cost-Effective, Anthropomorphic Robotic Hand for Uninterrupted Dexterous Task Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ORCA hand from ETH Zurich introduces an open-source, anthropomorphic, tendon-driven robotic hand designed for high reliability and cost-effectiveness. It enables long-duration dexterous task learning by integrating features like "poppable" joints and an auto-calibration mechanism, achieving performance comparable to direct-driven hands and supporting zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous robotic hands are either prohibitively expensive and proprietary, or inexpensive but lack sufficient dexterity and reliability for advanced research.
- Hardware fragility and complex maintenance of anthropomorphic hands often bottleneck continuous, data-intensive machine learning training (e.g., Reinforcement Learning).
- Direct-driven hands suffer from bulkiness, restricted form factors, and high inertia, hindering agile and human-like manipulation.

## Method

- Developed **ORCA**, an open-source, ==tendon-driven==, anthropomorphic robotic hand using 3D-printable components for a material cost under 2,000 CHF.
- Integrated novel =="poppable" pin joints== that dislocate instead of breaking, and a user-friendly ==ratchet spool system== for quick tendon re-tensioning.
- Implemented an ==auto-calibration mechanism== to ensure accurate and consistent ==motor-to-joint mapping==, crucial for precise control without expensive ==proprioceptive sensors==.

## Results

- Achieved a material cost under **2,000 CHF** and an assembly time of less than **eight hours**.
- Demonstrated exceptional durability, completing over **10,000** continuous operation cycles (~**20 hours**) in IL experiments and **2,250** grasping cycles over **2.5 hours** without hardware failure.
- Enabled zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for an in-hand ball reorientation task and executed a continuous pick-and-place task for over **7 hours** via imitation learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A combination of simple, compliant mechanical design (like poppable joints) and tendon-driven actuation can yield high dexterity and durability at a low cost.
> - Automated calibration of motor-to-joint relationships, especially with linear actuation, is critical for achieving consistent control and enabling successful sim-to-real transfer in tendon-driven systems.
> - Designing for extreme reliability and ease of maintenance directly addresses a major bottleneck in robot learning, allowing for uninterrupted, long-duration real-world experiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of dexterous robotic hands that can mimic the versatility and agility of the human hand has been a central and enduring challenge in robotics for decades. While industrial grippers are efficient for specific tasks, their limited versatility restricts their use in environments designed for human interaction and with human tools. This has driven extensive research into anthropomorphic robotic hands capable of complex manipulation.

The existing landscape of dexterous robotic hands can be broadly categorized:
- **High-End, Proprietary Hands:** Examples like the Shadow Hand demonstrate impressive capabilities in dexterous manipulation. However, their prohibitive cost (over 100,000 CHF), complex maintenance requirements, and proprietary, highly integrated designs severely limit their accessibility for broader research and practical applications.
- **Open-Source, Low-Cost Tendon-Driven Hands:** Designs such as the InMoov hand and DexHand offer affordability and openness. Yet, they often suffer from limitations in dexterity, challenging assembly processes, or lack demonstrated real-world applicability in autonomous manipulation tasks.
- **Direct-Driven Hands:** The Allegro Hand is a notable example, priced around 15,000 CHF. The open-source LEAP hand offers a more cost-effective alternative (under 2,000 CHF) and boasts rapid assembly and high reliability. However, direct-driven hands generally face inherent limitations such as bulkiness, restricted form factors, and increased inertia due to motors embedded within the fingers, which hinders the agile, quick, and forceful movements characteristic of human hands. They struggle to match the softness, form factor, and agility essential for human-like manipulation.

The "ORCA" hand directly addresses a critical gap in this landscape. There is a pressing need for a robotic hand that combines:
1. **Anthropomorphism and Dexterity:** To effectively interact with human-designed objects and leverage vast datasets of human-hand interactions.
2. **Reliability and Durability:** Crucial for continuous, long-duration machine learning training (Reinforcement Learning, Imitation Learning) where hardware bottlenecks, not just software, often limit progress.
3. **Accessibility:** Achieved through open-source design, low material cost, and ease of assembly and maintenance.

By presenting an open-source, tendon-driven, anthropomorphic hand that is not only cost-effective and easy to assemble but also robust enough for prolonged, uninterrupted operation in various machine learning paradigms, ORCA positions itself as a significant enabler for advancing dexterous manipulation research. It aims to democratize access to high-performance robotic hardware, fostering innovation across the robotics community by providing a reliable platform for developing and testing advanced control algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the ORCA project is to overcome the existing hardware bottlenecks in dexterous manipulation by developing a robotic hand that is accessible, reliable, and capable of human-like versatility. The authors posit that while significant research has focused on software for dexterous manipulation, hardware limitations – particularly the cost, complexity, and fragility of existing anthropomorphic hands – often impede progress.

The core motivations and specific design objectives for the ORCA hand are:

- **Bridging the Accessibility Gap:** To provide the research community with a reliable and dexterous robotic hand that is both affordable (material cost below 2,000 CHF) and easy to get started with (assembly in less than eight hours). This lowers the barrier to entry for institutions and researchers who cannot afford expensive proprietary systems.
- **Enabling Uninterrupted Task Learning:** Machine learning techniques like Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL) require extensive, continuous operation in real-world environments. Existing hands often struggle with durability, requiring frequent maintenance or repairs. ORCA aims to be robust and reliable enough to withstand tens of hours of continuous operation without hardware failure, facilitating robust policy training.
- **Achieving Human-like Dexterity and Agility:** The hand is designed to be anthropomorphic, closely mimicking the human hand's form factor and 17 degrees of freedom (DoF). This allows for natural interaction with human tools and objects and enables the use of large human-hand interaction datasets, making learned policies more transferable to human-centric tasks. The tendon-driven actuation is specifically chosen to achieve low finger inertia and agility, akin to human movement.
- **Simplifying Design and Maintenance:** A key motivation was to reduce the complexity inherent in anthropomorphic hands. This translated into design features such as 3D-printable components, "poppable" joints that dislocate rather than break, and a user-friendly ratchet spool system for quick tendon re-tensioning.
- **Ensuring Accurate and Consistent Control:** For successful sim-to-real transfer in RL and consistent performance in IL, precise control of joint movements is paramount. The authors aimed to achieve this through design choices like routing tendons through the center of rotation and implementing an auto-calibration mechanism to minimize joint position errors and increase repeatability without relying on costly proprioceptive sensors.
- **Integrating Essential Sensing:** To enhance manipulation capabilities, the hand was designed with fully integrated tactile sensors on the fingertips, providing valuable feedback for grasping and interaction, even if initially implemented with binary interpretation for simplicity.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** A fundamental motivation is to release the design files, source code, and documentation as open-source. This commitment to transparency and community sharing encourages further development, customization, and broader adoption of the ORCA platform.

In essence, ORCA is motivated by the desire to democratize advanced robotic manipulation by providing a practical, robust, and high-performing hardware solution that addresses the practical needs of current and future robotics research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ORCA hand's development is characterized by a strategic combination of thoughtful mechanical design, cost-effective manufacturing, and integrated system-level solutions, all geared towards achieving its core objectives of reliability, dexterity, and accessibility.

**Design Philosophy and Actuation:**
The hand adopts a **tendon-driven actuation system**, utilizing fishing lines (Nylon fibers) for its 17 DoF (five fingers with an opposable thumb and an actuated wrist). This choice is motivated by the desire for a smaller form factor and lower finger inertia, closely mimicking the agility of human hands, in contrast to bulkier direct-driven designs. The main challenge of tendon-driven systems (friction, wear, slack) is addressed through specific mitigation strategies:
- **Friction Reduction:** Avoiding direct tendon contact with 3D-printed Polylactic Acid (PLA) by deflecting tendons around smooth metal pins and rods, and using Teflon tubes for non-linear routing.
- **Slack Management:** A user-friendly **ratchet spool mechanism** allows for quick manual re-tensioning of tendons without disassembly, enhancing longevity and precision.
- **Auto-calibration:** Tendon routing through the center of rotation (CoR) for each joint ensures approximately decoupled and linear actuation, which is foundational for the hand's self-calibration system.

**Key Mechanical Design Features:**
- **Poppable Pin Joints:** A novel joint design where bearings are placed in circular arc-shaped grooves. This allows the joints to "pop" out and dislocate under excessive radial or axial loads, preventing breakage of 3D-printed components. This combines the robustness of ligament-based rolling contact joints with the stability and kinematic simplicity of traditional pinhole joints, while also simplifying assembly.
- **Anthropomorphic Finger and Palm Design:** The hand mimics human anatomy, sized similarly to an average human hand. Fingers 2-5 (index to pinky) have three actuated joints: Proximal Interphalangeal (PIP), Metacarpophalangeal (MCP), and Abduction (ABD). The Distal Interphalangeal (DIP) joints are intentionally fixed to simplify design, eliminate slack build-up, improve accuracy, and create space for tactile sensors, as DIP joints rarely move independently in the human hand. The thumb features four DoF (Interphalangeal (IP), MCP, ABD, and Carpometacarpal (CMC)) and is positioned for opposition.
- **Integrated Wrist Joint:** A single rotational DoF (flexion/extension) is included in the wrist to allow the palm to orient parallel to surfaces, significantly improving grasping performance. Unlike fingers, the wrist uses a **belt drive (GT2 timing belt)** for increased load capacity and negligible slack build-up.
- **3D-Printable Structure:** The entire hand is designed for 3D printing, contributing significantly to its low cost and ease of part replacement.

**Sensing and Control:**
- **Integrated Tactile Sensing:** Force Sensing Resistors (FSRs) (RP-C7.6-ST) are mounted on PLA backplates on each fingertip and covered by silicone skin. These provide binary tactile feedback. Wiring is routed internally through PTFE tubing for protection and a clean aesthetic. A small microcontroller (Arduino Nano Every) handles sensor readout.
- **Self-Calibration for Accurate Control (Algorithm 1):** This crucial feature addresses the challenge of consistent joint control without expensive proprioceptive sensors. By leveraging the linear actuation enabled by tendons routed through the CoR, the hand automates the estimation of motor-to-joint mappings. The process involves:
 1. Determining the absolute Range of Motion (ROM) for each joint from CAD models.
 2. Programmatically moving each joint to its mechanical limits and recording the corresponding motor positions.
 3. Calculating a linear motor-to-joint transmission ratio ($\rho_j$) from the motor travel and known joint ROM.
 This method largely eliminates errors from physical modeling and manual measurements, ensuring consistent and accurate control.

**Experimental Validation and Benchmarking:**
The authors conducted a comprehensive set of experiments to validate ORCA's performance:
- **Reliability and Robustness:** Continuous operation tests involving thousands of grasping cycles and wrist movements over several hours, monitoring motor current and temperature. Payload capacity was measured for different grasps.
- **Accuracy and Latency:** An AprilTag-based system, synchronized with commanded actions, was used to measure ground-truth joint angles on the index finger. ORCA's performance was benchmarked against the direct-driven LEAP hand for sine wave inputs at different frequencies (0.2 Hz and 0.5 Hz).
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Demonstrating zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for an in-hand ball reorientation task. An A2C architecture trained 4096 ORCA models in parallel in IsaacGymEnvs with domain randomization.
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** A continuous pick-and-place task was performed over several hours using a robotic diffusion transformer. Demonstrations were collected via ROKOKO motion capture gloves, retargeted to the robot's state space. Ablation studies on image pre-processing (raw RGB, masked, mixed) were conducted.
- **Teleoperation:** Qualitative demonstrations of the hand's dexterity in a variety of complex manipulation tasks (e.g., writing, using tools, pouring liquid, stacking objects).
- **Tactile Sensing Evaluation:** Absolute threshold measurements for FSR sensors were performed, and their long-term degradation was observed during extended reliability tests.

This multi-faceted approach, encompassing design, manufacturing, sensing, control, and rigorous experimental validation, systematically demonstrates the ORCA hand's capabilities and addresses its targeted objectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ORCA hand successfully demonstrates its capabilities across all key objectives, validating its design philosophy and technical innovations.

**Cost and Assembly:**
- The hand is built for a material cost of **less than 2,000 CHF**.
- It can be fully assembled by a single person, even without prior experience, in **less than eight hours**. This significantly reduces the barrier to entry for research groups.

**Reliability and Durability:**
- In continuous operation tests, the ORCA hand successfully completed **2,250 grasping cycles** and 550+ wrist cycles over **2.5 hours** without any hardware failure, motor shutdown, or excessive tendon slack. The experiment was voluntarily concluded.
- Further, it withstood **more than 10,000 continuous operation cycles (equivalent to approximately 20 hours)** during imitation learning experiments without hardware failure, demonstrating exceptional robustness for long-duration task learning.
- The **poppable joints** proved effective in preventing component breakage.
- Payload capacity tests showed the hand can hold up to **10.5 kg (103 N)** using all four fingers and **2 kg (19.6 N)** with only the index finger, showcasing its strength despite its compliant nature.

**Accuracy and Latency:**
- The **auto-calibration mechanism** effectively accounts for tendon changes and allows ORCA's finger joints to accurately follow commanded sine wave inputs.
- The ORCA hand achieved **accuracy and latency comparable to the direct-driven LEAP hand**, despite its tendon-driven design.
- Joint rotations were observed to be **smoother** than those of the LEAP hand, which exhibited more jerks.
- Both hands showed average latencies of **less than 0.2 seconds**, largely attributed to software processes. Regular re-tensioning was noted as important to mitigate latency caused by tendon slack.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
- A robust policy for **in-hand ball reorientation** was successfully deployed on the physical ORCA hand in a **zero-shot sim-to-real transfer** scenario, after only one hour of training in simulation with domain randomization. This highlights ORCA's low joint errors and consistent behavior, which are critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap.

**Imitation Learning (IL):**
- The hand demonstrated **long-duration autonomous task execution** in a continuous pick-and-place task, running for **7 hours and 17 minutes (approximately 2,000 grasping cycles)** with minimal human intervention. This achievement underscores both the hardware's reliability and the robustness of the trained IL policy.
- Ablation studies for the pick-and-place task revealed that policies trained with **masked images** (segmenting the cube's color) achieved the highest success rates (e.g., 86.6% total success rate compared to 50% for raw RGB).

**Teleoperation - Dexterous Manipulation:**
- Qualitative evaluations through teleoperation using motion capture gloves successfully demonstrated ORCA's **high dexterity and versatility** in performing complex, human-centric tasks. These included stacking cubes, grasping various objects (plush toy, tennis ball), twisting a jar cap, spinning a fidget toy, writing "Hello" with a pen, picking up paper, and pouring water between cups.

**Integrated Tactile Sensing:**
- The FSR sensors on the fingertips could register forces as low as **0.05 N** with high accuracy over 10 cycles.
- However, the tactile sensing system exhibited limitations in long-term reliability:
 - **Silicone skin degradation** was observed in two fingertips after approximately 2,000 to 4,000 grasp cycles, leading to an increased absolute threshold (e.g., 6.38 N on the pinkie finger).
 - The thin copper wires connecting the sensors to external electronics **snapped** in three fingertips after approximately 4,500 to 7,000 grasp cycles, concentrated around the MCP and ABD joints. These issues are identified as areas for future improvement.

In summary, the ORCA hand largely meets its design goals, providing a highly capable, reliable, and accessible platform, though acknowledging specific areas for refinement, particularly in long-term tactile sensor robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ORCA hand represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, primarily due to its unique combination of accessibility, reliability, and human-like dexterity. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

**1. Democratizing Dexterous Manipulation Research:**
The most immediate and impactful contribution of ORCA is its open-source nature, low material cost (under 2,000 CHF), and ease of assembly (under 8 hours). This drastically lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, universities, and individual innovators worldwide who previously could not afford or maintain expensive proprietary dexterous hands. By making advanced manipulation hardware widely accessible, ORCA can significantly accelerate innovation, foster new research directions, and diversify the pool of contributors in dexterous manipulation robotics.

**2. Enabling Robust and Scalable Machine Learning for Robotics:**
A critical bottleneck in applying data-hungry machine learning techniques like Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL) to real-world robots has been the fragility and high maintenance requirements of robotic hardware. ORCA's demonstrated durability—withstanding over 10,000 continuous operation cycles—is a game-changer. It enables researchers to conduct long-duration, uninterrupted training experiments in the real world, reducing the need for constant human intervention and hardware repairs. This reliability is crucial for developing truly robust and generalizable robotic policies.

**3. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:**
The ORCA hand's high accuracy and its innovative auto-calibration mechanism directly address a major challenge in robotics: transferring policies learned in simulation to the real world (sim-to-real). By providing consistent and accurate motor-to-joint mappings, ORCA facilitates zero-shot sim-to-real deployment, as demonstrated with the in-hand ball reorientation task. This capability can dramatically reduce the time and resources required to deploy learned behaviors on physical robots.

**4. Advancing Human-Robot Interaction and Collaboration:**
The anthropomorphic design of the ORCA hand, closely mimicking human hand proportions and kinematics, is vital for developing robots that can seamlessly interact with human-designed environments and tools. Furthermore, it allows researchers to leverage vast datasets of human hand interactions, potentially leading to more intuitive and effective human-robot collaboration. Its compliance and ability to pop joints instead of breaking also contribute to safer interactions in shared spaces.

**5. Serving as a Platform for Future Innovation:**
ORCA is not just a product; it's a foundation. Its open-source design encourages further development, customization, and integration of new technologies. The identified limitations, such as the need for more robust tactile sensing and autonomous re-tensioning, provide clear pathways for future research within the community. This continuous improvement, driven by a global community, can lead to even more capable and autonomous dexterous manipulation systems.

**6. Educational Impact:**
Given its accessibility and well-documented open-source resources, ORCA has immense potential as an educational tool. It can provide students with invaluable hands-on experience in building, controlling, and programming advanced robotic manipulators, fostering the next generation of robotics engineers and researchers.

In conclusion, the ORCA hand offers a practical, high-performance, and accessible solution to many long-standing challenges in dexterous manipulation robotics. Its design ethos prioritizes reliability and cost-effectiveness without sacrificing dexterity, positioning it as a pivotal platform for accelerating research and development in robot learning, human-robot interaction, and real-world autonomous tasks.
