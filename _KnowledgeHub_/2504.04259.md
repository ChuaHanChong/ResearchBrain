# ORCA: An Open-Source, Reliable, Cost-Effective, Anthropomorphic Robotic Hand for Uninterrupted Dexterous Task Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ORCA hand from ETH Zurich introduces an open-source, anthropomorphic, tendon-driven robotic hand designed for high reliability and cost-effectiveness. It enables long-duration dexterous task learning by integrating features like "poppable" joints and an auto-calibration mechanism, achieving performance comparable to direct-driven hands and supporting zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous robotic hands are either prohibitively expensive and proprietary, or inexpensive but lack sufficient dexterity and reliability for advanced research.
- Hardware fragility and complex maintenance of anthropomorphic hands often bottleneck continuous, data-intensive machine learning training (e.g., Reinforcement Learning).
- Direct-driven hands suffer from bulkiness, restricted form factors, and high inertia, hindering agile and human-like manipulation.

## Method

- Developed **ORCA**, an open-source, ==tendon-driven==, anthropomorphic robotic hand using 3D-printable components for a material cost under 2,000 CHF.
- Integrated novel =="poppable" pin joints== that dislocate instead of breaking, and a user-friendly ==ratchet spool system== for quick tendon re-tensioning.
- Implemented an ==auto-calibration mechanism== to ensure accurate and consistent ==motor-to-joint mapping==, crucial for precise control without expensive ==proprioceptive sensors==.

## Results

- Achieved a material cost under **2,000 CHF** and an assembly time of less than **eight hours**.
- Demonstrated exceptional durability, completing over **10,000** continuous operation cycles (~**20 hours**) in IL experiments and **2,250** grasping cycles over **2.5 hours** without hardware failure.
- Enabled zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for an in-hand ball reorientation task and executed a continuous pick-and-place task for over **7 hours** via imitation learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A combination of simple, compliant mechanical design (like poppable joints) and tendon-driven actuation can yield high dexterity and durability at a low cost.
> - Automated calibration of motor-to-joint relationships, especially with linear actuation, is critical for achieving consistent control and enabling successful sim-to-real transfer in tendon-driven systems.
> - Designing for extreme reliability and ease of maintenance directly addresses a major bottleneck in robot learning, allowing for uninterrupted, long-duration real-world experiments.
