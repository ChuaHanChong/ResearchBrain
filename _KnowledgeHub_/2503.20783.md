# Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sea AI Lab and partner universities critically analyze R1-Zero-like training for large language models, revealing inherent biases in the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm and pre-existing self-reflection behaviors in base models. The study introduces an unbiased optimization method, Dr. GRPO, and a minimalist recipe that achieves 43.3% accuracy on AIME 2024 with a 7B model, setting a new state-of-the-art efficiently.

## Problem

- The mechanisms behind the reported success of R1-Zero training, including the emergence of 'Aha moments' and reasoning improvements without supervised fine-tuning (SFT), were not fully understood.
- The influence of diverse base model characteristics (e.g., pretraining data, template responsiveness) on subsequent reinforcement learning (RL) performance lacked systematic investigation.
- Potential optimization biases within the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, particularly concerning response length and training efficiency, remained unexamined.

## Method

- Conducted a comprehensive analysis of various open-source base models to assess their inherent reasoning capabilities, template dependencies, and pre-existing self-reflection behaviors.
- Performed theoretical derivations and empirical experiments to identify and correct ==optimization biases in GRPO==, leading to the development of =='Dr. GRPO'== as an unbiased alternative.
- Developed and validated a =='minimalist R1-Zero recipe'== by studying the interaction of templates, question sets, and domain-specific pretraining to create an efficient and reproducible training approach.

## Results

- Dr. GRPO effectively mitigates the length bias in GRPO, preventing uncontrolled growth of response length during training and reducing the length of incorrect responses during evaluation without sacrificing accuracy.
- The developed minimalist R1-Zero recipe, using RL-tuned Qwen2.5-Math-7B with Dr. GRPO, achieved **43.3%** accuracy on AIME 2024, establishing a new state-of-the-art for a **7B** model.
- The study demonstrated that even smaller, out-of-distribution question sets can effectively induce strong reasoning ability through RL when paired with suitable base models and templates, suggesting RL reinforces existing capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-reflection ('Aha moment') is largely a pre-existing characteristic of many base models, including DeepSeek-V3-Base, rather than a solely emergent property of pure RL training.
> - Standard GRPO exhibits a response-level length bias that disproportionately penalizes shorter correct responses and artificially increases the length of incorrect responses, a bias also found in many open-source PPO implementations.
> - Specific base models, like Qwen2.5-Math, demonstrate peak reasoning performance without explicit prompt templates, suggesting their pretraining implicitly creates SFT-like capabilities that impact RL.
> - Domain-specific continual pretraining significantly elevates the performance ceiling for subsequent RL, enhancing the reasoning capabilities of even inherently weaker base models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is strategically positioned at the forefront of large language model (LLM) post-training and alignment research, specifically within the paradigm of **Reinforcement Learning from Human/AI Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF)**. It directly builds upon and critically examines the "R1-Zero-like training" approach introduced by DeepSeek-R1-Zero (Guo et al., 2025). The DeepSeek-R1-Zero paper proposed a revolutionary pipeline: applying reinforcement learning directly to base LLMs to enhance reasoning capabilities *without* an intermediate supervised fine-tuning (SFT) step. This approach challenged conventional wisdom, which typically dictates SFT as a prerequisite for RL alignment.

The broader landscape is characterized by intense efforts to improve LLM reasoning, reduce hallucination, and enhance alignment with human intentions. RL-based methods have shown immense promise in these areas. DeepSeek-R1-Zero's claim of "RL scaling phenomenon" and the emergence of "Aha moment" (self-reflection) through pure RL captured significant attention, prompting numerous attempts at replication and further understanding across the community (e.g., Pan et al., 2025; Zeng et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b; Hu et al., 2025).

This paper contributes by providing a rigorous, evidence-based analysis that aims to demystify some of these phenomena. It questions whether certain observed behaviors, like increased response length and self-reflection, are truly emergent properties of RL or are influenced by pre-existing base model characteristics and specific optimization biases. By offering a "critical perspective," the authors aim to move beyond mere replication to a deeper scientific understanding, thereby informing the design of more effective and efficient LLM post-training methodologies. It seeks to standardize and simplify the process with a "minimalist R1-Zero recipe," making advanced RL techniques more accessible and reproducible for the wider research community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **critically examine and understand "R1-Zero-like training"** by dissecting its two fundamental components: the **base models** and the **Reinforcement Learning (RL) process** itself.

The motivations driving this investigation are multi-faceted:

- **Demystifying DeepSeek-R1-Zero's Success:** DeepSeek-R1-Zero demonstrated a groundbreaking method for enhancing LLM reasoning without SFT, but the underlying mechanisms and reproducibility across different base models were not fully understood. The authors aimed to shed light on why it works and what factors contribute to its observed scaling phenomena and "Aha moment."
- **Understanding Base Model Influence:** A key motivation was to investigate how the intrinsic characteristics of various pre-trained base models (e.g., DeepSeek-V3-Base, Qwen2.5 family, Llama-3.1)—such as their pretraining data, inherent reasoning capabilities, and responsiveness to templates—influence the subsequent RL performance and the manifestation of emergent behaviors. Specifically, the peculiar behavior of Qwen2.5 models performing best *without* templates sparked curiosity about potential pretraining biases.
- **Identifying and Correcting RL Optimization Biases:** The authors suspected that the widely used Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, central to R1-Zero-like training, might contain inherent optimization biases that could unintentionally shape model behavior, such as artificially inflating response length. A crucial motivation was to identify these biases, understand their impact, and propose a corrective, unbiased optimization method.
- **Developing an Efficient and Reproducible Recipe:** Ultimately, the researchers were motivated to distill their findings into a "minimalist R1-Zero recipe" that is not only effective but also more token-efficient and easier to reproduce. This aims to lower the barrier to entry for researchers wanting to implement similar high-performing RL strategies for LLMs.
- **Challenging Assumptions:** The paper explicitly sets out to challenge prevailing interpretations regarding the emergence of "Aha moments" and the consistent increase in response length during R1-Zero training, suggesting that these might be pre-existing or artifactual rather than purely emergent from RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic, two-pronged approach, focusing on the characteristics of base models and the intricacies of the RL optimization algorithm.

#### 3.1. Analysis on Base Models

The methodology for analyzing base models involved a comprehensive evaluation across a diverse range of open-source models: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, DeepSeek-Math-7B, and DeepSeek-V3-Base-685B (the foundation for the original DeepSeek-R1-Zero).
- **Question Set:** 500 questions sampled from the MATH (Hendrycks et al., 2021) training set were used for analysis.
- **Template Impact:** The models were tested with three different prompt templates: the R1 template, the Qwen-Math template, and a "No template" condition. GPT-4o-mini was used as a judge to assess whether responses were in an "answering format" or "sentence-completion pattern."
- **Exploration Ability:** Pass@8 accuracy at various sampling temperatures was used to gauge a base model's capacity to explore rewarding trajectories, a prerequisite for effective RL.
- **Self-Reflection ("Aha Moment") Detection:** This was a critical component. The authors used a hybrid detection method:
 - **Keyword-based detection:** A curated list of self-reflection keywords (e.g., "recheck," "rethink," "double-check") was used.
 - **LLM-based detection:** GPT-4o-mini was employed as a judge to identify both explicit and implicit self-reflection behaviors, helping to filter false positives from keyword-based methods and capture more nuanced reflections.
 - **Cross-validation:** The results from both methods were cross-validated to enhance robustness.
- **Benchmark Evaluation:** Reasoning abilities were quantitatively assessed on five standard benchmarks: AIME 2024, AMC, MATH500, Minerva Math, and OlympiadBench, using greedy decoding and a 3000-token limit.

#### 3.2. Analysis on Reinforcement Learning

The methodology for analyzing the RL component focused on identifying and rectifying biases in the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.
- **Theoretical Derivations:** The authors provided mathematical derivations to show how standard PPO objective functions (Eq. 2) relate to GRPO (Eq. 3), explicitly highlighting the `1/|o_i|` (response-level length bias) and `1/std(R)` (question-level difficulty bias) terms in GRPO that deviate from the unbiased policy gradient. They also pointed out that this length bias is prevalent in many open-source PPO implementations.
- **Dr. GRPO Introduction:** Based on the derivations, they proposed "Dr. GRPO" by simply removing these two biasing terms from the GRPO objective, effectively recovering the PPO objective with Monte Carlo return and an unbiased baseline.
- **Experimental Validation of Dr. GRPO:**
 - **Framework:** All RL experiments were implemented using their open-source LLM RL framework, Oat (Liu et al., 2025a).
 - **Base Model & Template:** Qwen2.5-1.5B with the R1 template was used for online RL-tuning.
 - **Reward Function:** A minimalistic rule-based verifier (Math-Verify) awarded 1 for correct final answers and 0 otherwise.
 - **Metrics:** Comparisons between vanilla GRPO and Dr. GRPO tracked training dynamics (reward, output length) and evaluation results (overall benchmark score, correct/incorrect output length). Ablation studies were also performed on the bias terms.
- **Template and Question Set Interaction Study:**
 - **Objective:** To understand how different templates (R1, Qwen-Math, No template) and varying question set coverages (ORZ 57k, MATH 12k, GSM 8k, ASDiv 2k – ranging from diverse/large to simple/small) affect RL training dynamics and final performance, starting from Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B.
- **Domain-Specific Pretraining Impact:**
 - **Models:** Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.2-3B-FineMath (continual pretrained on math data), and Llama-3.2-3B-NuminaQA (further pretrained on concatenated QA texts).
 - **Goal:** Investigate if domain-specific pretraining can raise the "RL ceiling" for inherently weaker math reasoners.

All experiments were conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, leveraging actor-learner collocation for efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several critical findings, challenging conventional understandings and providing practical insights for R1-Zero-like training:

#### 4.1. Insights from Base Model Analysis

- **Template Dependency:** Most base models (Llama, DeepSeek) require appropriate prompt templates (e.g., R1 template) to elicit question-answering capabilities. However, a highly counter-intuitive finding was that **Qwen2.5-Math base models achieve peak reasoning performance when *no template* is applied**, demonstrating an immediate ~60% improvement over traditional 4-shot prompting. This led to the hypothesis that Qwen2.5 models might be pretrained on concatenated question-answer texts, making them inherently SFT-like without explicit prompting.
- **Pre-existing "Aha Moment":** The study revealed that "Aha moment" (self-reflection behaviors) is **already present in nearly all base models, including DeepSeek-V3-Base**, prior to any RL training. This contradicts the narrative that self-reflection is a solely emergent skill from pure RL. Furthermore, for DeepSeek-R1-Zero, self-reflection behaviors were not consistently correlated with higher accuracy during inference.
- **Exploratory Base Policies:** All tested base models were found to be sufficiently "exploratory" (i.e., could sample correct trajectories at certain temperatures), indicating they possess the necessary foundation for RL to improve upon.

#### 4.2. Insights from RL Optimization (GRPO vs. Dr. GRPO)

- **GRPO's Optimization Bias:** The analysis rigorously demonstrated that the original GRPO algorithm contains two biases:
 - **Response-level length bias:** The `1/|o_i|` term penalizes shorter correct responses less and longer incorrect responses less, leading the policy to prefer brevity for correct answers and, more problematically, **artificially increase the length of incorrect responses** during training.
 - **Question-level difficulty bias:** The `1/std(R)` term assigns higher weights to questions with low reward standard deviation (too easy or too hard), introducing an unintended difficulty bias.
- **Prevalence of Length Bias:** The length bias was surprisingly identified in many popular open-source PPO implementations for LLM post-training (e.g., trl, OpenRLHF, verl), stemming from a misalignment between objective formulation and loss implementation.
- **Dr. GRPO's Effectiveness:** The proposed **Dr. GRPO** (by removing `1/|o_i|` and `std(R)`) successfully mitigates these biases. It prevents the uncontrolled growth of response length during training and significantly **reduces the length of incorrect responses** during evaluation, thereby enhancing token efficiency without sacrificing reasoning performance. It essentially corrects the "overthinking" behavior for incorrect outputs.

#### 4.3. Template and Question Set Interaction in RL

- **Model-Template Mismatch:** When there's a significant mismatch between the base model and the template (e.g., R1 template with Qwen2.5-Math), RL largely *reconstructs* the reasoning capability.
- **Efficiency of Reinforcement:** For models like Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B that already possess strong underlying math capabilities, even a small and "out-of-distribution" (o.o.d.) question set (like GSM-8K, which is simpler) can effectively induce strong reasoning ability when paired with a suitable template (Qwen-Math template). This suggests RL might be more about reinforcing useful existing behaviors than infusing new knowledge.

#### 4.4. Impact of Domain-Specific Pretraining

- **Raising the RL Ceiling:** While RL can improve even vanilla "weak" base models (e.g., Llama-3.2-3B), math-specific continual pretraining (e.g., Llama-3.2-3B-FineMath, Llama-3.2-3B-NuminaQA) significantly **boosts the ceiling of subsequent RL performance**, enabling much stronger reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.5. State-of-the-Art Achievement

- Leveraging these insights, the authors present a minimalist R1-Zero recipe: RL-tuning Qwen2.5-Math-7B using Dr. GRPO on MATH level 3-5 questions with the Qwen-Math template. This recipe achieved **43.3% accuracy on AIME 2024 with a 7B base model**, establishing a new state-of-the-art and demonstrating high efficiency (27 hours on 8x A100 GPUs).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper holds substantial significance for the field of LLM research and development, particularly concerning reinforcement learning for alignment and reasoning:

- **Refined Understanding of R1-Zero-like Training:** By critically dissecting the base model characteristics and RL optimization, the work provides a much clearer and nuanced understanding of how R1-Zero-like training actually works. It moves beyond superficial observations to reveal underlying mechanisms, challenging common interpretations about the "emergence" of traits like self-reflection and long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) behaviors, which are often mistakenly attributed solely to the RL phase.
- **Improved Efficiency and Reproducibility:** The identification and correction of optimization biases in GRPO (leading to Dr. GRPO) significantly improve token efficiency, preventing models from generating excessively long and incorrect responses. This translates to reduced computational costs and faster training. The "minimalist R1-Zero recipe" offers a practical, high-performance, and resource-efficient approach, which will be invaluable for researchers and developers seeking to reproduce or build upon this paradigm.
- **Guidance for Base Model Selection and Pretraining:** The findings underscore the critical importance of base model characteristics and pretraining strategies. The discovery of Qwen2.5 models' unique template-free performance suggests that *pretraining data and methodology can implicitly create SFT-like capabilities*, altering the starting point for RL. The demonstration that domain-specific pretraining significantly raises the RL ceiling provides clear guidance for developing more capable foundation models optimized for specific downstream RL tasks.
- **Advancing LLM Alignment Research:** The paper contributes to the broader goal of making LLM alignment (via RL) more principled and less heuristic. By pinpointing specific biases and offering concrete solutions, it pushes the field towards more robust and theoretically sound RL algorithms for LLMs.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The open-sourcing of models and the Oat framework is a significant contribution to the community. It fosters transparency, enables direct verification of their findings, and accelerates future research by providing readily available tools and baselines.
- **Rethinking RL's Role:** The paper implicitly suggests a re-evaluation of RL's primary role in LLM post-training. Instead of solely generating entirely new, emergent behaviors, RL's power might lie more in efficiently *reinforcing desired behaviors* that are already nascent in the base model and effectively *aligning* the model's output distribution to a specific reward signal, while pruning inefficient or undesirable generation patterns.

In essence, this research provides both a scientific demystification of a cutting-edge LLM training paradigm and a practical, efficient recipe for its application, poised to impact how LLMs are understood, trained, and aligned in the future.
