# NORA: A Small Open-Sourced Generalist Vision Language Action Model for Embodied Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> A 3B-parameter Vision-Language-Action model combines Qwen-2.5-VL-3B with FAST+ tokenization to enable real-time robotic control on consumer GPUs, achieving comparable or better performance than larger models across simulation and real-world robot manipulation tasks while reducing computational requirements by 57%.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models (7B+ parameters) are too computationally intensive for real-time robotics applications
- Current approaches struggle with visual encoding and action generation for precise manipulation tasks

## Method

- Leverage ==Qwen-2.5-VL-3B=='s visual-semantic capabilities with augmented ==action tokens==
- Implement **FAST+** tokenization for efficient action sequence generation
- Optimize training with ==FlashAttention== and ==bf16 precision==

## Results

- Outperforms baseline models on out-of-domain object grasping tasks
- Achieves comparable performance to OpenVLA on LIBERO benchmark tasks
- Runs effectively on consumer GPUs with **57%** lower computational overhead
- Demonstrates robust generalization across diverse manipulation scenarios

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action chunking improves performance in simulation but creates challenges in real-world settings
> - Visual encoding quality significantly impacts manipulation success rates
> - Model size can be reduced while maintaining task performance through targeted architecture choices

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work directly addresses several key trends and challenges in the field of Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics:

- **Generalist Robot Learning:** The paper contributes to the growing trend of developing generalist robot policies that can perform a variety of tasks across different environments and robot embodiments. This is in contrast to traditional reinforcement learning approaches that are often task-specific and lack generalization.
- **Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs):** It builds upon the recent advancements in VLAs, which leverage multimodal inputs (vision and language) to generate robot actions. The paper acknowledges the success of existing VLA models but focuses on addressing their limitations.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A major focus of the paper is addressing the high computational cost associated with large-scale VLA models (7B+ parameters). The development of a smaller, more efficient model (3B parameters) addresses the need for models that can be deployed in real-time robotic environments with limited computational resources.
- **Open-Source and Reproducibility:** By releasing the NORA framework, including model checkpoints, training strategy, and evaluation protocols, the authors contribute to the open-source movement in AI research and promote reproducibility. This is crucial for fostering collaboration and accelerating progress in the field.
- **Bridging the Gap between Simulation and Real World:** The paper emphasizes evaluation in both simulated and real-world environments. This is crucial for ensuring that models trained in simulation can effectively transfer to real-world robotic tasks, which often involve complexities and uncertainties not present in simulation.

The paper positions itself within the research landscape by:

- Acknowledging the limitations of current large-scale VLAs, particularly their computational overhead.
- Leveraging existing pre-trained models (Qwen-2.5-VL-3B) to accelerate development and improve performance.
- Employing techniques like FAST+ tokenization to optimize action sequence generation.
- Conducting comprehensive experiments to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives and motivation behind the NORA project are:

- **Reduce Computational Overhead:** The primary motivation is to develop a VLA model that is computationally efficient and can be deployed on consumer-grade GPUs, making it more practical for real-time robotic applications.
- **Maintain Strong Task Performance:** While reducing model size, the authors aim to maintain or even improve task performance compared to existing larger VLA models.
- **Improve Visual Encoding:** The paper highlights the limitations of visual encoding in existing VLAs as a source of failure, particularly in tasks like object grasping. NORA aims to improve visual reasoning and action grounding by leveraging the visual-semantic understanding capabilities of the Qwen-2.5-VL-3B multimodal model.
- **Enhance Action Sequence Generation:** The use of the FAST+ tokenizer aims to improve the efficiency and robustness of action sequence generation.
- **Facilitate Research and Reproducibility:** By open-sourcing the NORA framework, the authors aim to promote further research in scalable VLA models for robotics and ensure that their results can be easily reproduced and extended by other researchers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology and approach employed in the NORA project can be summarized as follows:

- **Model Architecture:**
 - NORA is a 3B-parameter VLA model based on the Qwen-2.5-VL-3B multimodal model.
 - The architecture leverages the pre-trained vision and language capabilities of the backbone model.
 - The vocabulary is augmented with 2048 additional tokens for the FAST+ tokenizer.
- **Action Tokenization:**
 - Continuous action spaces are discretized using a quantile-based binning approach.
 - The FAST+ tokenizer is used to compress action tokens and improve efficiency.
- **Training Data:**
 - NORA is trained on the Open X-Embodiment dataset, which comprises a large collection of real-world robot demonstrations.
 - Data augmentation techniques are used to improve generalization.
- **Training Procedure:**
 - The model is trained using a standard language modeling objective of next-token prediction loss.
 - FlashAttention and bf16 precision are used to enhance training efficiency and reduce memory footprint.
- **Evaluation:**
 - NORA is evaluated in both simulated (LIBERO benchmark) and real-world environments (WidowX robot platform).
 - Performance is assessed on a variety of tasks, including object grasping, spatial reasoning, and multi-object manipulation.
 - The model is compared to several baseline methods, including OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, TraceVLA, and RT-1.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the NORA project are:

- **Outperforms Existing VLAs in Real-World Environments:** NORA outperforms existing large-scale VLA models in real-world robot manipulation tasks, demonstrating better task performance with reduced computational overhead.
- **Strong Performance in OOD Object Grasping:** NORA achieves impressive success rates in out-of-domain (OOD) object grasping tasks, indicating its ability to generalize to novel objects and environments.
- **Comparable Performance in Simulated Environment:** NORA demonstrates comparable performance to OpenVLA in spatial, object, and goal-related tasks in the LIBERO simulation benchmark. Action chunking significantly improves long-horizon task performance.
- **Action Chunking Benefits Simulation but not Real-World:** Action chunking improves task performance in the simulation environment, but it is found to be less effective in the real-world WidowX robot platform, leading to issues with affordance point estimation and difficulties in grasping smaller objects.
- **Robustness Across Diverse Tasks:** The experimental results demonstrate that NORA exhibits robust performance across a variety of task settings, showcasing superior generalization capabilities compared to the baseline models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NORA project has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Democratization of Robotic AI:** By creating a smaller, more efficient VLA model that can be run on consumer-grade GPUs, NORA makes robotic AI more accessible to a wider range of researchers and developers.
- **Real-Time Robotic Applications:** The reduced computational overhead of NORA makes it a more practical solution for real-time robotic applications, such as autonomous navigation, manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The paper demonstrates that NORA exhibits strong generalization capabilities and robustness across a variety of task settings and environments.
- **Advancement of VLA Research:** The open-sourcing of the NORA framework will facilitate further research in scalable VLA models for robotics, accelerating progress in the field.
- **Potential for Commercial Applications:** The technology developed in this project could be applied to a variety of commercial applications, such as warehouse automation, manufacturing, and healthcare.
- **Insights into Action Chunking:** The contrasting results of action chunking in simulation versus the real world provide valuable insights into the challenges of transferring models from simulation to the real world and the importance of considering control frequency and robot dynamics.

In conclusion, the NORA project represents a significant contribution to the field of VLA models for robotics. By addressing the limitations of existing large-scale models and promoting open-source research, NORA has the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robots in a variety of real-world applications.
