# Generative AI Act II: Test Time Scaling Drives Cognition Engineering

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, SII, and the Generative AI Research Lab delineate "Cognition Engineering" as the new paradigm for generative AI's "Act II," emphasizing test-time scaling methods to advance Large Language Models beyond knowledge retrieval towards deep reasoning. The work introduces a framework for understanding and implementing techniques such as parallel sampling, tree search, multi-turn correction, and long Chain-of-Thought, demonstrating their role in cultivating AI's cognitive abilities, particularly when enhanced by reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Existing Large Language Models (LLMs) from the "Act I" era exhibited limitations in deep reasoning, struggling with complex, multi-step problems and novel concepts.
- These models primarily functioned as knowledge retrieval systems, lacking the capacity for human-like depth of thought or generating truly novel insights.
- Traditional scaling methods (increasing parameters and training data) were insufficient to bridge this observed "wisdom gap" in AI capabilities.

## Method

- Introduces =="Cognition Engineering"== as a new systematic methodology to develop and optimize AI's thinking capabilities, moving models from the knowledge level to the wisdom level.
- Proposes =="test-time scaling"== as the core mechanism, categorizing and detailing methods like ==Parallel Sampling, Tree Search, Multi-turn Correction, and Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT)==.
- Explores how training strategies, especially Reinforcement Learning (RL) with verifiable rewards and Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) on high-quality CoT data, elicit and enhance these advanced cognitive behaviors.

## Results

- Test-time scaling methods, particularly RL-driven Long Chain-of-Thought, achieved human-competitive performance in complex domains, exemplified by DeepSeek-R1's **79.8** score on AIME and OpenAI's o1 series excelling on coding benchmarks.
- Parallel sampling consistently increased `Pass@N` metrics, while tree search and multi-turn correction (with reliable external feedback) significantly enhanced deliberate planning and iterative response refinement.
- The "RL scaling phenomenon" demonstrated that online Reinforcement Learning with carefully designed rewards enabled LLMs to learn and master advanced, complex long-CoT behaviors, often surpassing outcomes achieved through supervised learning alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative AI is transitioning into "Act II," driven by "test-time scaling" which enables Large Language Models to move from superficial knowledge retrieval to deep reasoning and thought construction.
> - Online Reinforcement Learning (RL) with verifiable, rule-based rewards can unlock significant, autonomous reasoning capabilities in LLMs, demonstrating an "RL scaling phenomenon" for complex long Chain-of-Thought behaviors.
> - Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods, incorporating patterns like reflection and backtracking, facilitate the most human-like cognitive behaviors and achieve the highest performance for complex problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself as a critical demarcation point in the evolution of generative AI, effectively drawing a line between what it terms "Act I" (2020-2023) and "Act II" (2024-present).

"Act I" is characterized by the remarkable success of Large Language Models (LLMs) achieved primarily through massive scaling of parameters and training data, exemplified by models like GPT-4 and Llama 3. This era saw the rise of "prompt engineering" as the primary interface for human-AI interaction, enabling knowledge retrieval and dialogue-level communication. However, the authors argue that Act I models harbored fundamental limitations: knowledge latency, shallow reasoning, and constrained cognitive processes. They functioned primarily as "knowledge-retrieval systems" from latent space, lacking true "deep thinking."

This paper declares the advent of "Act II," a new paradigm driven by "test-time scaling" techniques. In Act II, models transition from mere knowledge repositories to "thought-construction engines," aiming for a "mind-level connection with AI through language-based thoughts." This shift moves beyond superficial interactions to unlock deeper cognitive abilities, with recent models like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1 serving as early examples.

By systematically defining "cognition engineering" as the methodology for this new era and providing a comprehensive breakdown of test-time scaling techniques, the paper serves as a foundational roadmap. It synthesizes a disparate set of recent advancements under a unifying conceptual framework, aiming to guide researchers, practitioners, educators, and even investors in navigating and contributing to this evolving landscape. In essence, it aims to clarify, consolidate, and accelerate the transition from an AI primarily focused on information recall to one capable of advanced reasoning and problem-solving, thereby redefining the pursuit of artificial general intelligence (AGI).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from the inherent limitations observed in the first generation of LLMs (Act I). Despite their unprecedented scale and impressive conversational abilities, these models exhibited a "wisdom gap" when confronted with tasks requiring genuine cognitive depth. The authors highlight three specific shortcomings:
- **Knowledge Latency:** Models are primarily adept at retrieving high-frequency information present in their training data, struggling with novel or emerging concepts.
- **Shallow Reasoning:** They perform basic logical inferences but falter on complex, multi-step reasoning problems that demand deeper analysis.
- **Limited Thought Processes:** They lack human-like depth of thought, particularly when facing open-ended questions or generating truly novel insights.

The authors contend that merely accumulating more data or parameters (the scaling paradigm of Act I) is insufficient to bridge this gap and achieve human-like intelligence. Instead, AI systems must develop the capacity for "deep thinking and reasoning."

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:
1. **Define Cognition Engineering:** To formally introduce and clarify "cognition engineering" as the systematic development and optimization of AI's thinking capabilities, moving models from the "knowledge" level to the "wisdom" level within the DIKW (Data-Information-Knowledge-Wisdom) pyramid.
2. **Explain the "Why Now" Factor:** To articulate the technical breakthroughs—a robust knowledge foundation, mature test-time scaling techniques, and advanced self-training methodologies—that make the pursuit of cognition engineering feasible and critical at this juncture.
3. **Democratize Advanced Techniques:** To systematically break down and explain various test-time scaling methods through comprehensive tutorials and optimized implementations, making these advanced approaches accessible to a broader community of researchers and practitioners.
4. **Provide a Research Framework:** To offer a structured framework for understanding generative AI's "second act," outlining current progress, identifying profound implications, and pinpointing future research directions across diverse application domains.
5. **Catalyze AI's Evolution:** To inspire and enable every practitioner to participate in this paradigm shift, fostering the development of AI systems capable of complex reasoning, creative problem-solving, and eventually, a symbiotic human-AI cognitive partnership.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to push AI beyond being sophisticated pattern matchers and information retrievers towards becoming true cognitive partners.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper adopts a comprehensive, multi-faceted approach, combining conceptual definition, technical review, empirical synthesis, and practical guidance.

**Conceptual Foundation:**
The authors introduce "Cognition Engineering" as the central concept of "Act II," defining it as a systematic methodology for constructing and optimizing AI systems' ability to ascend from knowledge to wisdom on the DIKW pyramid. This involves deliberate cultivation of deep cognitive processes through both human cognitive pattern distillation and AI-driven discovery. They contrast this with Act I's "behavior imitation" (knowledge-driven prompt engineering), proposing a shift to "thought imitation" (thought-driven cognition engineering).

**Theoretical Underpinnings:**
The paper posits a "Three Scaling Phases" model for intelligence development:
1. **Pre-training Scaling:** Forms isolated "knowledge islands."
2. **Post-training Scaling:** Densifies these islands with learned connections.
3. **Test-time Scaling:** Enables dynamic "cognitive pathway formation," bridging distant concepts through extended computation at inference, facilitating multi-hop reasoning. This is the paper's core focus.

The emergence of cognition engineering "now" is attributed to three "Pillars":
1. **Knowledge Foundation:** Massive, high-quality pre-training data provides the "raw materials."
2. **Test-time Scaling Foundation:** Innovations like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree Search, and Self-Correction provide a "cognitive workspace" for deeper inference.
3. **Self-Training Foundation:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) with verifiable rewards and iterative self-training allow AI to discover novel cognitive patterns and internalize complex reasoning.

**Technical Breakdown of Test-Time Scaling Methods:**
The core of the methodology lies in a systematic review and categorization of four primary test-time scaling methods:
1. **Parallel Sampling:** Generating multiple candidate responses and selecting the best using scoring functions (Best-of-N, ORM, PRM) or majority voting (Self-Consistency). The paper discusses its scaling laws (Pass@N vs. practical metrics) and strategies for improving efficiency (query-aware sampling, early stopping, model size-sample number tradeoffs, improved verifiers, inference-aware SFT).
2. **Tree Search:** Framing problems as searches over tree structures. Components include defining the search space (token, step, solution level), designing value functions (self-evaluation, trained models, likelihood, self-consistency, rollouts), and employing search algorithms (BFS, DFS, MCTS). Scaling laws (breadth, depth, rollouts) and efficiency improvements (algorithm selection, reducing value function overhead, adaptive expansion, node redundancy reduction) are analyzed.
3. **Multi-turn Correction:** Iteratively refining responses through an initial generator, a feedback model (self-feedback, external tools/oracles/trained models), and a refinement model. The effectiveness of external vs. intrinsic feedback, scaling laws (revision steps), and efficiency (high-quality feedback, improved LLM refinement ability) are discussed.
4. **Long CoT:** Extending traditional CoT to incorporate advanced cognitive patterns like reflection, backtracking, verification, and divergent/internal thinking. The paper analyzes scaling laws (token count vs. performance), "overthinking" issues, and efficiency strategies (prompting for conciseness, finetuning on compressed responses, query-aware compression, model merging, intermediate state compression, latent space reasoning).

**Training Strategies for Test-Time Scaling:**
The paper also details how these inference-time capabilities are elicited or enhanced through training:
1. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Discusses various algorithms (REINFORCE, PPO, GRPO, REINFORCE++), reward function design (rule-based vs. model-based, outcome vs. process), policy model selection, training data construction, and multi-stage training (cold start, iterative lengthening, curriculum sampling).
2. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Focuses on eliciting long CoT abilities using long CoT data. Discusses data sources (synthesis, distillation), quality, quantity, training methods, and base model characteristics.
3. **Iterative Self-reinforced Learning (ISRL):** A self-improvement cycle using trajectories from test-time scaling for offline updates.

**Application and Infrastructure Review:**
The paper surveys the application of these methods across diverse domains (Mathematics, Code, Multimodality, Agent, Embodied AI, Safety, RAG, Evaluation), highlighting successes and identifying domain-specific future directions. It also includes a brief overview of the necessary infrastructure for RL and MCTS.

**Practical Implementation:**
A tutorial section is included, providing step-by-step guidance and code analysis for setting up RL training using the veRL framework, aiming to bridge theory with practice.

Overall, the approach is highly structured, aiming to provide both a high-level conceptual understanding and granular technical details for the emerging field of cognition engineering.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a synthesis of recent advancements, arguing for a new phase in generative AI. Its main findings and presented results are as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift to "Act II":** The core finding is the identification and conceptualization of a shift from "Act I" (knowledge-driven, prompt engineering) to "Act II" (thought-driven, cognition engineering). This transition is driven by "test-time scaling," enabling LLMs to move from knowledge retrieval to thought construction and deep reasoning.

2. **Effectiveness of Test-Time Scaling Methods:**
 - **Parallel Sampling:** Demonstrated to consistently increase `Pass@N` (probability of finding at least one correct answer) with increased sampling. However, practical metrics (e.g., `Maj@1`, `BoN`) often saturate due to the inherent limitations of imperfect verifiers and the amplification of false positives. Efficiency gains are achieved through adaptive sampling, early stopping, and advanced response selection mechanisms.
 - **Tree Search:** Enhances deliberate planning and problem-solving. Performance scales with search breadth and depth (e.g., more MCTS rollouts or larger beam sizes). The reliability of value functions (e.g., Process Reward Models, PRMs) is crucial for effective scaling. Efficiency can be improved by selecting algorithms suited to the task, reducing value function overhead, and pruning redundant nodes.
 - **Multi-turn Correction:** Shows significant performance improvements in tasks where reliable *external* feedback (e.g., code interpreters, ground truth oracles) is available. However, intrinsic self-correction (models critiquing themselves without external help) often struggles and can even degrade performance, unless the model is specifically fine-tuned for this ability. Performance scales with the number of revision steps, eventually saturating.
 - **Long CoT:** Exhibits the most human-like cognitive behaviors (reflection, backtracking, verification, divergent thinking, internal thinking) and generally achieves the highest ceiling performance, particularly for complex problems. Performance positively correlates with token count/reasoning length, often showing a log-linear relationship. Challenges include "overthinking" (generating unnecessary tokens for simple tasks), which can be mitigated by strategies like query-aware compression and targeted RL training.

3. **"RL Scaling Phenomenon" (Aha Moment):** A key finding is that online Reinforcement Learning (RL) with well-designed, rule-based (verifiable) rewards, particularly in domains like mathematics and code, can unlock significant reasoning capabilities in LLMs. This "RL scaling phenomenon" enables models to autonomously learn and master complex long-CoT behaviors like self-reflection and self-correction, often surpassing what's achievable through supervised learning alone. Larger models tend to exhibit higher token efficiency in this context.

4. **Role of Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** SFT on high-quality long-CoT data can effectively elicit advanced reasoning behaviors, offering a more data-efficient and simpler alternative to RL. The quality (challenging queries, filtered responses) and structural coherence of the training data are more important than sheer quantity. However, SFT-trained models might struggle to surpass their "teacher" models or generalize true cognitive abilities beyond memorized patterns, especially for smaller base models.

5. **Limitations of Iterative Self-Reinforced Learning (ISRL):** While promising for self-improvement by leveraging self-generated data, ISRL approaches often show performance plateauing or even decline after a few iterations. This contrasts with the monotonic improvements seen in online RL scaling, attributed to differences in on-policy vs. off-policy sampling and the effective use of negative gradients in RL.

6. **Broad Application Across Domains:** The paper highlights the successful application of test-time scaling across numerous domains:
 - **Mathematics:** DeepSeek-R1 achieved human-competitive performance on AIME (79.8 score) through RL-driven long CoT.
 - **Code:** Models like OpenAI's o1-series excelled on benchmarks like SWE-bench and competitive programming platforms, often via RL with execution-based verification.
 - **Multimodality:** Post-DeepSeek-R1 research increasingly uses RL to enhance multimodal reasoning depth and breadth.
 - **Agents:** ReAct and Reflexion paradigms leverage CoT and multi-turn correction to improve planning and decision-making.
 - **Safety:** Parallel sampling aids hallucination detection; tree search enables constrained, safe generation; and long CoT/multi-turn correction promotes deliberate alignment.
 - **RAG:** Agentic workflows (IterDRAG) and RL-trained searchers enhance multi-hop reasoning over external documents.
 - **Evaluation:** LLM-as-a-Judge frameworks benefit from fine-grained evaluation, multi-agent systems, and MCTS for systematic assessment.

In summary, the paper's findings establish test-time scaling as a powerful, albeit computationally intensive, mechanism for advancing AI cognitive capabilities, with RL being a particularly potent driver for unlocking complex, human-like thought processes in LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper "Generative AI Act II: Test Time Scaling Drives Cognition Engineering" carries profound significance and potential impact, positioning itself as a landmark conceptualization for the next phase of AI development.

**1. A Definitive Paradigm Shift:** The most significant contribution is the formal declaration and definition of "Cognition Engineering" as the successor to "Prompt Engineering." By framing the evolution of generative AI into "Act II," the authors provide a clear conceptual framework that shifts the field's focus from mere knowledge retrieval and behavior imitation to the deliberate cultivation of deep thinking, complex reasoning, and creative problem-solving. This re-orientation could redefine research agendas, funding priorities, and industry benchmarks for AI systems.

**2. Democratizing Advanced AI Capabilities:** The paper’s explicit goal of "democratizing access" to test-time scaling techniques through tutorials and optimized implementations is highly impactful. By breaking down complex methodologies (like RL scaling and sophisticated tree searches) into understandable and reproducible components, it lowers the barrier to entry for a wider range of researchers and practitioners. This could accelerate innovation, foster diverse applications, and prevent these advanced capabilities from being concentrated among a few large labs.

**3. The Emergence of Cognition Data Engineering:** The proposal of "Cognition Data Engineering" is a visionary concept with long-term implications. It redefines what constitutes valuable training data, moving beyond static knowledge to dynamic "thought processes" (human cognition projections, AI-generated cognition, and human-AI co-creation). This new data paradigm could become as strategically critical as large computing resources, fostering novel data collection and synthesis techniques that directly feed into the development of more intelligent AI.

**4. Redefining Human-AI Collaboration:** The paper envisions a "Human-AI Cognitive Partnership" characterized by bidirectional cognitive exchange, cognitive amplification, and new interaction paradigms. This moves beyond AI as a mere tool or assistant, proposing a symbiotic relationship where AI can expand human memory and exploration breadth, while humans provide creative leaps and value alignment. This could revolutionize scientific discovery, complex problem-solving, and creative endeavors by overcoming inherent human cognitive limitations through complementary AI capabilities.

**5. Accelerated Scientific Discovery:** The application of cognition engineering promises to dramatically accelerate scientific research. By enabling AI to systematically map knowledge gaps, generate diverse hypotheses, detect anomalies, and integrate cross-disciplinary findings, AI systems can act as powerful scientific partners. This could lead to unprecedented rates of knowledge creation and breakthroughs in fields from medicine to climate science, tackling global challenges at an accelerated pace.

**6. Shaping Future AI Architectures and Training:** The paper highlights critical future directions, including the need for new architectural paradigms beyond Transformers (e.g., Mamba) to handle long contexts more efficiently. It also emphasizes the importance of pretraining on "latent thoughts" and further developing RL scaling, which could fundamentally change how foundational models are designed and trained, moving towards intrinsically more cognitive architectures.

**7. Advancing AI Safety and Evaluation:** The insights into how test-time scaling can enhance AI safety (e.g., for hallucination detection, robust generation) and evaluation (LLM-as-a-Judge with deeper reasoning) are crucial. As AI systems become more capable, ensuring their safety and developing robust, cognitively aligned evaluation metrics becomes paramount. This work provides pathways to address these challenges.

In conclusion, "Generative AI Act II: Test Time Scaling Drives Cognition Engineering" is more than a technical survey; it's a manifesto for the next era of AI. Its framework, technical insights, and forward-looking vision have the potential to profoundly influence the direction of AI research, its applications, and its integration into human society, driving a fundamental shift towards truly cognitive artificial intelligence.
