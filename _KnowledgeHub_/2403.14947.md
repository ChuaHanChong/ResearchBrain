# GPT-Connect: Interaction between Text-Driven Human Motion Generator and 3D Scenes in a Training-free Manner

> [!summary] Summary
> GPT-Connect generates scene-aware, text-driven 3D human motion without any training. A Scene-GPT channel describes the 3D scene to ChatGPT as object types plus bounding boxes, and step-by-step prompting elicits a partial skeleton sequence; a GPT-Generator channel uses that skeleton as gradient guidance on the clean-motion prediction of an off-the-shelf motion diffusion model, beating supervised baselines on HUMANISE.

## Problem

- Most text-driven motion generators synthesize motion in a blank background, ignoring the 3D scenes in which people actually move and interact.
- Supervised scene-aware generators need motion sequences collected and annotated across many diverse 3D scenes, which is costly and troublesome to obtain.
- Existing datasets are narrow: PROX has only 12 indoor scenes, HUMANISE covers 4 actions, limiting generalization of training-based methods to arbitrary scenes.

## Method

- ==Scene-GPT== channel renders the scene from 16 camera poses, tags objects with ==Recognize Anything==, segments instances with ==OpenIns3D==, and feeds ChatGPT object types plus 3D bounding boxes.
- Step-by-step instruction splits skeleton generation into four sub-tasks (locate target object, plan trajectory endpoints, reason initial orientation, decide frame count), yielding a masked partial skeleton sequence.
- ==GPT-Generator== channel aligns the diffusion model's clean-motion prediction, not the noisy step output, toward the skeleton via an ==L2 gradient guidance==, deactivating alignment once the gap ratio falls below a threshold.

## Results

- On the full HUMANISE test set, training-free GPT-Connect beats the supervised baseline on quality (**2.83** vs **2.57**), action (**3.67** vs **3.59**), body-to-goal distance (**0.87** vs **1.01 m**), and contact (**0.80** vs **0.73**).
- On the walking subset it reaches **3.04** quality, **4.01** action, and **0.96 m** body-to-goal distance, versus **1.37 m** for the supervised method and **1.44-1.50 m** for two alternative architectures.
- Ablations show both guidance modifications matter: removing both drops quality to **2.22** and distance to **1.27 m**; dropping the clean-prediction alignment gives **2.29**; dropping selective deactivation gives **2.75**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A language model can act as a cross-modal connector when the input is recast into a format it saw in pretraining, such as layout-style object bounding boxes.
> - Guiding diffusion through the predicted clean sample rather than the current noisy state keeps guidance and data in the same distribution, avoiding trajectory disruption.
> - Noisy language-model guidance should be applied partially: deactivating alignment once the gap shrinks lets the pretrained generative prior repair unrealistic details.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of text-driven human motion generation, a domain with applications in areas such as game development, film production, and virtual reality. Historically, much of the research in this area, including methods leveraging diffusion models, has focused on generating human motion sequences against a blank background. Examples include methods like T2M, TEMOS, MotionCLIP, and the motion diffusion model (MDM) by Tevet et al.

A recognized extension of this research involves generating human motions that not only correspond to a given text prompt but also properly interact with a specific 3D scene. This "scene-aware" motion generation task presents additional challenges. Previous approaches to scene-aware motion generation have primarily relied on supervised training mechanisms or reinforcement learning. For instance, Wang et al. proposed training a specific scene-aware text-driven motion generator using supervised learning, which conditions the generation process on both the 3D scene and the text prompt. Other works have explored reinforcement learning for this purpose.

The current paper identifies a key limitation of these existing scene-aware methods: their reliance on large-scale and diverse datasets of motions specifically annotated within various 3D scenes. The authors argue that collecting and annotating such datasets is difficult and often results in datasets with limited diversity, thereby restricting the generalizability and applicability of supervised training-based models.

GPT-Connect aims to address this data dependency by introducing a novel, training-free approach to scene-aware text-driven motion generation. It seeks to integrate off-the-shelf blank-background motion generators with 3D scene information without requiring additional supervised training. This approach leverages large language models (LLMs), specifically ChatGPT, as a connector, thereby differentiating itself from prior work that developed custom, scene-conditioned generative models. This places GPT-Connect at the intersection of generative AI, 3D computer vision, and the emerging capabilities of large language models for complex reasoning and multimodal integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to achieve scene-aware text-driven human motion generation. This means generating human motion sequences that are not only based on a given text prompt but also interact plausibly and appropriately with a specified 3D scene.

The primary motivation for pursuing a novel approach to this task stems from the identified shortcomings of existing methods, particularly those relying on supervised training:

- **Troublesome Data Collection and Annotation:** Current supervised learning methods for scene-aware motion generation require extensive datasets. These datasets must contain a large number of ground-truth motion sequences that are diverse in terms of text prompts and, crucially, accurately interact with a wide variety of 3D scenes. The authors emphasize that collecting and annotating such data is problematic and time-consuming.
- **Limited Dataset Diversity:** Existing datasets suitable for this task, such as PROX and HUMANISE, are often limited in their diversity. For example, PROX includes only 12 indoor 3D scenes, while HUMANISE primarily focuses on indoor environments and a restricted set of actions (e.g., walk, sit, stand up, lie). This limited diversity hinders the ability of supervised models to generalize to arbitrary 3D scenes and a broader range of text prompts.
- **Restricted Generalizability and Applicability:** The scarcity and limited diversity of training data for scene-aware tasks directly impact the generalizability and applicability of models trained using supervised mechanisms. Such models may struggle to produce plausible human motions in unseen scenes or for novel text descriptions.

To address these limitations, the authors investigate an alternative, "training-free" paradigm. Their specific motivations for this alternative approach include:

- **Bypassing Supervised Training:** The core motivation is to eliminate the need for laborious supervised training, which is particularly challenging for scene-aware motion generation.
- **Leveraging Existing Models:** The authors seek to utilize the capabilities of existing, highly effective blank-background human motion generators (specifically, diffusion models) rather than developing new ones from scratch. This leverages prior research investments.
- **Exploring ChatGPT as a Connector:** The observation that ChatGPT models possess extensive implicit common-sense knowledge, acquired from vast training corpora, motivates its exploration as a potential "connector." The hypothesis is that ChatGPT could bridge the gap between a 3D scene and a motion generator, guiding the latter to produce scene-aware motions without explicit scene-motion paired training.

However, the authors also identify specific challenges in using ChatGPT for this role:

- **3D Scene Interpretation for ChatGPT:** 3D scene data is not inherently in a format "friendly" to language models like ChatGPT. Interpreting a 3D scene in a way that allows ChatGPT to effectively leverage its implicit knowledge for understanding the scene and interaction possibilities is a non-trivial challenge.
- **Guidance Mechanism for Motion Diffusion Models:** Even if ChatGPT could interpret a 3D scene, successfully translating its understanding into actionable guidance for an off-the-shelf motion diffusion model, which is typically conditioned only on text, is a further challenge.

The GPT-Connect framework is designed to directly address these motivations and challenges by enabling a novel, training-free method for scene-aware text-driven human motion generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GPT-Connect framework proposes a training-free approach to generate scene-aware text-driven human motion. It leverages an off-the-shelf blank-background motion diffusion model (MDM) and ChatGPT, employing ChatGPT as an intermediary to connect the 3D scene with the motion generator. The framework consists of two main channels: the Scene-GPT Channel and the GPT-Generator Channel. The common data format used to transmit "useful information" between these channels is a partial skeleton sequence, denoted as s[m_s].

#### 3.1. Scene-GPT Channel

This channel is responsible for interpreting the 3D scene for ChatGPT and then prompting ChatGPT to generate the partial skeleton sequence (s[m_s]) based on both the scene interpretation and the text prompt.

- **Understanding the 3D Scene (S_3D):**
 - The primary aspect of the 3D scene relevant for motion interaction is its layout.
 - Inspired by how LLMs process web page layouts (e.g., CSS scripts with object types and bounding boxes), the authors propose describing S_3D as a collection of object types and their 3D bounding boxes.
 - This process involves three steps:
 1. **Recognition:** To identify objects in a 3D scene, the framework first renders *M* photos of the scene from different camera poses. An off-the-shelf open-world recognition model, Recognize Anything (RAM), is then used to list objects in each photo. A unified set of these lists forms the potential objects in S_3D.
 2. **Bounding Boxes Derivation:** Once object types are identified, an off-the-shelf 3D open-vocabulary instance segmenter, OpenIns3D, is employed to derive the type and 3D bounding box for each object instance in S_3D. Each 3D bounding box is represented by six values (x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max, z_min, z_max).
 3. **Passing to ChatGPT:** This collection of object types and their 3D bounding box coordinates is then formatted into a textual description that ChatGPT can understand.

- **Deriving s[m_s] from ChatGPT:**
 - The framework leverages ChatGPT's implicit knowledge of human skeleton structure and behaviors, acquired from its vast training corpus which includes descriptions of human motions and programming data (numbers, coordinates).
 - To facilitate ChatGPT's generation of a plausible partial skeleton sequence (s[m_s]), a "step-by-step guidance mechanism" is used in the prompt. This mechanism, inspired by chain-of-thought prompting, breaks down the complex task into four simpler sub-tasks:
 1. Locate the target object mentioned in the text prompt using the provided bounding boxes.
 2. Reason and plan the motion trajectory, including the starting and ending points.
 3. Determine the reasonable initial orientation of the person.
 4. Determine the number of frames in the motion based on the text prompt and trajectory.
 - ChatGPT's output, s[m_s], is a partial skeleton sequence (s ∈ R^(N×J×3)) with a binary activation mask (m_s ∈ {0, 1}^(N×J)) indicating which joint coordinates are active (outputted by ChatGPT). This sequence roughly implies how the motion should interact with S_3D.

#### 3.2. GPT-Generator Channel

This channel takes the partial skeleton sequence (s[m_s]) from ChatGPT and the original text prompt (t) to guide the off-the-shelf blank-background Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) to generate a scene-aware motion sequence. The MDM's reverse diffusion process is modified to incorporate s[m_s] as an additional conditioning factor.

- **Addressing Challenges in Conditioning MDM:** A naive approach of directly aligning the intermediate noisy motion (x_k-1) at each step of the reverse process towards s[m_s] would be suboptimal. This is because x_k-1 exists in a distribution (X_k-1) that is different from the clean motion distribution (X_0) where s[m_s] resides. Directly aligning x_k-1 would pull it away from its desired distribution, disrupting the diffusion process. Also, s[m_s] might contain inaccuracies due to ChatGPT's nature.
- **Two Modifications to the Guidance Strategy:**
 1. **Modification 1: Aligning ˆx_k_0 instead of x_k-1:**
 - Instead of aligning the noisy intermediate step (x_k-1), the framework aligns ˆx_k_0, which is the MDM's prediction of the *final clean motion* at step *k*. This ensures consistency as both ˆx_k_0 and s[m_s] are conceptually in the "clean" motion space (X_0).
 - A gap function, g_k(s[m_s], ˆx_k_0), is defined as the L2 distance between s[m_s] and the skeleton projection of ˆx_k_0 (f_m-s(ˆx_k_0)[m_s]).
 - The aligned clean motion prediction (ex_k_0) is then derived by adjusting ˆx_k_0 with the gradient of this gap: ex_k_0 = ˆx_k_0 - λ∇_x_k g_k(s[m_s], ˆx_k_0), where λ is a hyperparameter.
 - The next noisy step (x_k-1) is then calculated using ex_k_0, indirectly conditioning it on s[m_s] without disrupting its inherent noisy distribution.
 2. **Modification 2: Selectively Deactivating Alignment:**
 - To mitigate the impact of potential inaccuracies in ChatGPT's s[m_s] prediction and to preserve the realism of the MDM's generative capabilities, the alignment process is selectively deactivated.
 - Alignment (using ex_k_0) is only performed when the current gap g_k is significantly large relative to the initial gap g_K (i.e., g_k / g_K > ξ, where ξ is a hyperparameter).
 - If the gap is already small (g_k / g_K ≤ ξ), the MDM generates x_k-1 directly from its own prediction ˆx_k_0, relying solely on its internal motion knowledge.

- **Output Clipping:** The MDM typically generates motion sequences of a fixed length. To ensure the final output (x_0) aligns with the relevant segment of s[m_s] (which implies the interaction's duration), the generated motion x_0 is clipped to a sub-sequence x_c_0 based on the first (n_start) and last (n_end) active frames in the mask m_s. This results in the final scene-aware motion output.

#### 3.3. Overall Inference Process

The entire process is automated: given S_3D and t, they are first passed to the Scene-GPT channel to obtain s[m_s]. Then, s[m_s] and t are fed into the GPT-Generator channel to produce the final scene-aware motion sequence x_c_0. The framework operates without requiring any additional training steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GPT-Connect included quantitative assessments on the HUMANISE dataset and qualitative comparisons across various 3D scenes.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **On the Whole Testing Set of HUMANISE:**
 - GPT-Connect was compared against an existing training-based method by Wang et al..
 - GPT-Connect achieved superior performance across all evaluated metrics:
 - **Quality score:** 2.83 (Ours) vs. 2.57 (Wang et al.) (Higher is better)
 - **Action score:** 3.67 (Ours) vs. 3.59 (Wang et al.) (Higher is better)
 - **Body-to-goal distance:** 0.87 (Ours) vs. 1.01 (Wang et al.) (Lower is better)
 - **Non-collision score:** 1.00 (Ours) vs. 1.00 (Wang et al.) (Higher is better)
 - **Contact score:** 0.80 (Ours) vs. 0.73 (Wang et al.) (Higher is better)
 - These results suggest the effectiveness of the training-free framework in generating high-quality, scene-aware motions.

- **On the "Walking" Subset of HUMANISE:**
 - Further comparisons were made on a specific subset of the HUMANISE dataset focusing on "walking" actions, against Wang et al. and two baselines (Baseline A and B) from that work, which used different motion generator architectures.
 - GPT-Connect consistently outperformed all existing training-based methods on this subset as well:
 - **Quality score:** 3.04 (Ours) vs. 2.91 (Wang et al.), 2.88 (Baseline A), 2.80 (Baseline B)
 - **Action score:** 4.01 (Ours) vs. 3.88 (Wang et al.), 3.80 (Baseline A), 3.75 (Baseline B)
 - **Body-to-goal distance:** 0.96 (Ours) vs. 1.37 (Wang et al.), 1.50 (Baseline A), 1.44 (Baseline B)
 - **Non-collision score:** 1.00 (Ours) vs. 1.00 (Wang et al.)
 - **Contact score:** 0.76 (Ours) vs. 0.69 (Wang et al.)
 - These results further support the superiority and robustness of the GPT-Connect framework.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- The framework demonstrated the ability to generate plausible human motion sequences across a variety of 3D scenes, including both indoor scenes from HUMANISE and external outdoor scenes.
- The generated motions consistently exhibited appropriate interactions with the specified 3D environments, as shown in the provided visual examples (Figure 3).
- The qualitative results were described as showing improved interaction with 3D scenes compared to the method by Wang et al., further highlighting the generalizability and efficacy of GPT-Connect.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Guidance Strategy in the GPT-Generator Channel:**
 - To evaluate the efficacy of the two proposed modifications to the MDM guidance strategy (aligning ˆx_k_0 and selective deactivation), three variants were tested:
 1. **w/o both modifications:** Direct alignment of x_k-1 to s[m_s] in every step.
 2. **w/o modification 1:** Alignment between x_k-1 and s[m_s] in active steps (without selective deactivation).
 3. **w/o modification 2:** Alignment between ˆx_k_0 and s[m_s] in every step (without selective deactivation).
 - GPT-Connect, incorporating both modifications, outperformed all three variants across all metrics. For example, its Quality score was 2.83, compared to 2.22 (w/o both), 2.29 (w/o mod 1), and 2.75 (w/o mod 2). Similar trends were observed for other metrics.
 - This demonstrates that both aligning the clean motion prediction (ˆx_k_0) and selectively deactivating alignment are effective components of the guidance strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GPT-Connect framework introduces a novel approach to scene-aware text-driven human motion generation, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Pioneering Training-Free Approach:** This work is presented as the first to achieve scene-aware text-driven human motion generation in a completely training-free manner. This paradigm shift significantly reduces the resource demands associated with developing such models.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** By eliminating the need for large-scale, meticulously collected and annotated datasets of human motions interacting with 3D scenes, GPT-Connect bypasses a major bottleneck in this research area. This makes it more practical to generate contextually relevant motions for diverse and arbitrary 3D environments.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Flexibility:** The framework demonstrates strong generalizability across various indoor and outdoor 3D scenes and different text prompts. Its ability to leverage existing off-the-shelf models (ChatGPT and a blank-background motion diffusion model) without further training indicates a high degree of flexibility and adaptability to new scenarios.
- **Integration of Large Language Models for 3D Tasks:** The research highlights a novel application of large language models like ChatGPT in guiding complex 3D generative tasks. It demonstrates that LLMs, with their vast common-sense knowledge, can be effectively employed as intelligent connectors or reasoning engines for problems beyond traditional natural language processing, bridging symbolic reasoning with geometric understanding.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** The ability to generate realistic and contextually appropriate human motions on demand and without specific training data for each new scene has implications across various fields:
 - **Game Development and Virtual Reality:** Developers could generate more dynamic and interactive non-player character (NPC) behaviors or user avatars that realistically engage with virtual environments, reducing manual animation efforts.
 - **Film and Animation:** Automating the generation of background character motions or prototyping scene interactions could streamline production pipelines.
 - **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** The framework could contribute to developing robots capable of more human-like and contextually aware movements and interactions within complex environments.
 - **Architecture and Design Simulation:** Simulating human movement patterns within proposed architectural designs could inform ergonomic and spatial planning.

In summary, GPT-Connect represents a notable advancement in human motion generation by offering a practical, efficient, and highly generalizable method that leverages the synergistic capabilities of large language models and established generative models, fundamentally altering the approach to creating scene-aware interactive animations.
