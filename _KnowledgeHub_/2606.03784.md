# Revisiting Embodied Chain-of-Thought for Generalizable Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Xiaomi Robotics developed ERVLA, a vision-language-action model that leverages a large-scale embodied Chain-of-Thought corpus and a novel training strategy to integrate linguistic reasoning for generalizable robot manipulation. ERVLA achieved state-of-the-art success rates of 86.9% on LIBERO-Plus and 53.2% on VLABench, demonstrating improved zero-shot generalization and robustness in real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action (VLA) models often struggle with complex, out-of-distribution robot manipulation tasks requiring deliberate reasoning before execution.
- Existing Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) methods face challenges in identifying optimal reasoning forms and integration mechanisms with action policies, often suffering from autoregressive instability.
- The scalability of embodied CoT data and its impact on improving robot action generation has not been systematically verified at a large scale.

## Method

- Constructed a large-scale embodied ==Chain-of-Thought== (CoT) corpus (over 226M samples) with hierarchical linguistic annotations for task understanding, planning, spatial grounding, and action-oriented motion.
- Designed **ERVLA**, a mixture-of-transformers architecture integrating a Vision-Language Reasoning Module (VLM) with an auxiliary action-regression loss and a ==Diffusion== ==Transformer== (DiT) for continuous action generation, conditioned via "knowledge truncation."
- Implemented a CoT-dropout training strategy that leverages reasoning supervision during training but enables direct action prediction during inference, reducing autoregressive instability and mitigating "CoT contamination."

## Results

- ERVLA achieved state-of-the-art performance, with an **86.9%** average success rate on LIBERO-Plus and **53.2%** on VLABench, demonstrating improved zero-shot generalization and robustness.
- The study showed that embodied CoT scales effectively when supervision is integrated to shape action-relevant representations, leading to steady performance gains with increasing pre-training data.
- Real-world robot experiments confirmed ERVLA's superior robustness, especially in tasks involving semantic ambiguity and long-horizon requirements, surpassing existing competitive baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-related Chain-of-Thought (CoT) signals, such as movement descriptions and end-effector trajectories, prove more beneficial for action learning than isolated high-level reasoning.
> - Explicit, autoregressive CoT approaches do not scale reliably with increased data, often degrading performance due to compounding errors.
> - "CoT contamination," arising from noisy labels in large-scale auto-annotated data, can hinder embodied control, but it can be mitigated through reasoning dropout strategies.
