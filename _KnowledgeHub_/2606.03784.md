# Revisiting Embodied Chain-of-Thought for Generalizable Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Xiaomi Robotics developed ERVLA, a vision-language-action model that leverages a large-scale embodied Chain-of-Thought corpus and a novel training strategy to integrate linguistic reasoning for generalizable robot manipulation. ERVLA achieved state-of-the-art success rates of 86.9% on LIBERO-Plus and 53.2% on VLABench, demonstrating improved zero-shot generalization and robustness in real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action (VLA) models often struggle with complex, out-of-distribution robot manipulation tasks requiring deliberate reasoning before execution.
- Existing Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) methods face challenges in identifying optimal reasoning forms and integration mechanisms with action policies, often suffering from autoregressive instability.
- The scalability of embodied CoT data and its impact on improving robot action generation has not been systematically verified at a large scale.

## Method

- Constructed a large-scale embodied ==Chain-of-Thought== (CoT) corpus (over 226M samples) with hierarchical linguistic annotations for task understanding, planning, spatial grounding, and action-oriented motion.
- Designed **ERVLA**, a mixture-of-transformers architecture integrating a Vision-Language Reasoning Module (VLM) with an auxiliary action-regression loss and a ==Diffusion== ==Transformer== (DiT) for continuous action generation, conditioned via "knowledge truncation."
- Implemented a CoT-dropout training strategy that leverages reasoning supervision during training but enables direct action prediction during inference, reducing autoregressive instability and mitigating "CoT contamination."

## Results

- ERVLA achieved state-of-the-art performance, with an **86.9%** average success rate on LIBERO-Plus and **53.2%** on VLABench, demonstrating improved zero-shot generalization and robustness.
- The study showed that embodied CoT scales effectively when supervision is integrated to shape action-relevant representations, leading to steady performance gains with increasing pre-training data.
- Real-world robot experiments confirmed ERVLA's superior robustness, especially in tasks involving semantic ambiguity and long-horizon requirements, surpassing existing competitive baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-related Chain-of-Thought (CoT) signals, such as movement descriptions and end-effector trajectories, prove more beneficial for action learning than isolated high-level reasoning.
> - Explicit, autoregressive CoT approaches do not scale reliably with increased data, often degrading performance due to compounding errors.
> - "CoT contamination," arising from noisy labels in large-scale auto-annotated data, can hinder embodied control, but it can be mitigated through reasoning dropout strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models represent a paradigm for generalizable robot control, leveraging visual and semantic priors from pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs). These models aim to ground linguistic instructions in visual observations and translate them into robot actions, offering a unified interface for open-vocabulary and long-horizon manipulation tasks. Despite advances in perception and semantic coverage, VLA models often encounter difficulties in complex, out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios that necessitate deliberate reasoning prior to execution.

This challenge has motivated the development of "reason-before-act" policies, particularly Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) methods. ECoT involves supervising explicit reasoning traces to enhance robustness and generalization in robotic tasks. The field has explored various reasoning modalities, architectural designs for reasoning-action coupling, and specialized training strategies, including scene understanding, subtask decomposition, spatial grounding, end-effector trajectory prediction, and future-frame prediction. However, a systematic understanding of what constitutes effective ECoT, how reasoning should interact with the policy, and whether ECoT can scale with larger datasets remains underexplored. Previous ECoT formulations that model reasoning as an autoregressive prefix to action generation have shown limitations, including increased latency and compounding errors during inference. More recent works have attempted to decouple reasoning from action, either by distilling reasoning into latent plans, conditioning action heads on selected reasoning states, or using reasoning data during training without explicit ECoT at inference. This paper directly addresses these gaps by systematically investigating the effective forms, integration mechanisms, and scalability of embodied CoT at an extensive scale.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to revisit embodied Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for robotic control at a large scale to gain a systematic understanding of its effective forms and optimal integration methods. The authors aim to bridge linguistic reasoning with robotic control by analyzing how semantic abstractions learned by VLMs can be translated into intermediate representations that are genuinely beneficial for action generation.

The motivation for this work is driven by three main unresolved questions within the field of VLA models:

1. **Identifying Effective Forms of Embodied Reasoning:** Existing ECoT methods employ diverse forms of reasoning, but these are often tightly coupled with specific architectures and training objectives. This makes it challenging to determine which specific forms of embodied reasoning—such as scene understanding, subtask decomposition, spatial grounding, or action-level descriptions—contribute most effectively to action generation. The research seeks to establish a principled understanding of these signals.

2. **Determining Optimal Reasoning-Policy Interaction:** A significant question revolves around how reasoning should interact with the action policy. Early ECoT designs, which model reasoning as an autoregressive prefix to action generation, have demonstrated limitations. These include increased latency and the propagation of compounding errors during inference due to long reasoning traces. The authors explore whether reasoning should manifest as a visible trace, a latent variable, or primarily as a training signal that reshapes the policy's internal representations to avoid these issues.

3. **Assessing the Scalability of Embodied CoT:** The scalability of embodied CoT data, and its impact on generalizable action generation, is largely unverified. Publicly available reasoning-enhanced robotic datasets are scarce and costly to annotate, particularly given the need for dense labeling across multiple perception tasks and a large volume of images. The paper aims to conduct a large-scale study to determine if scaling reasoning supervision leads to stronger action generation capabilities.

To address these motivations, the authors propose ERVLA, a vision-language-action (VLA) model designed to effectively leverage linguistic reasoning for generalizable robot manipulation. ERVLA incorporates a novel training strategy, CoT-dropout, to utilize rich reasoning traces during training while predicting actions directly, without explicit CoT, during inference. This approach aims to circumvent autoregressive instability and enable reliable scaling with increasing pre-training data. Furthermore, the work identifies and seeks to mitigate a phenomenon termed "CoT contamination," where noisy or inconsistent labels in large-scale auto-annotated CoT data can hinder adaptation to embodied control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves three main stages: constructing a large-scale embodied CoT corpus, designing the ERVLA architecture for integrating reasoning with action generation, and developing a specific training recipe that includes reasoning dropout and knowledge truncation.

#### 3.1. Embodied CoT Data Construction

The authors curated a large-scale embodied CoT corpus, focusing on linguistic CoT, as the foundation for ERVLA.
- **Hierarchical CoT Schema:** A structured CoT format was developed, decomposing robot manipulation into hierarchical categories:
 - **Task Understanding:** High-level semantic intent.
 - **Planning:** Subtask decomposition and task progress.
 - **Spatial Grounding:** Alignment of language with visual entities (e.g., objects, gripper location).
 - **Action-Oriented Motion Description:** Concrete linguistic guidance for executable motion.
- **Auto-labeling Pipeline:** An automated pipeline was established to generate these annotations, extending prior single-view reasoning to multi-view settings.
 - **Trajectory Segmentation and Episode-Level Planning:** Raw trajectories were segmented based on end-effector motion and gripper dynamics. Qwen3.5-397B was used to generate episode-level task goals, plans, subtask sequences, and reasoning based on instructions and keyframes, ensuring temporal consistency.
 - **Action-Oriented Reasoning from Future Motion:** For each frame, short-horizon action chunks were summarized into compact language descriptions (`movement_description`) indicating translation, rotation, and gripper state changes, verbalizing only significant movements to avoid noise.
 - **Geometric Gripper Projection:** For static external cameras, `Acting.gripper` annotations were generated via geometric projection of the end-effector's tool center point into image coordinates, providing precise, view-indexed pixel locations. This avoids unreliable detector-based localization for grippers.
 - **Future Point-Trajectory Annotation:** `Acting.point_trajectory` was constructed by projecting a sequence of future gripper positions into the image plane, providing a spatially grounded motion trend while skipping duplicate points for stability.
 - **Object Grounding:** LLMDet was used to produce bounding box annotations for task-relevant objects. A compact, episode-level vocabulary was derived from instructions and plans to query the detector. Sparse grounding annotation (on keyframes or at fixed intervals) was adopted to mitigate noise from dense, frame-independent detections.
 - **Multi-view Correspondence and Bimanual Formatting:** Spatial CoT fields (object grounding, gripper projection, point trajectories) were stored as view-indexed multi-view correspondences for cameras (base, front, wrist), allowing the model to learn view-consistent reasoning. For bimanual data, arm identity was preserved in action-oriented fields.
- **Corpus Size:** The resulting corpus comprises 978,743 trajectories, 226.3M samples, and 2592.5 hours of data, curated from datasets including Bridge, Fractal, Droid, MolmoAct, and AgiBot.

#### 3.2. ERVLA Architecture

ERVLA employs a mixture-of-transformers architecture designed for integrating reasoning pre-training with end-to-end action generation.
- **Vision-Language Reasoning Module (VLM):** Instantiated with Qwen3-VL-4B, this module processes multi-view visual observations, text instructions, optional CoT, robot state, and trainable control-query tokens (action-query tokens, score-query token). It outputs hidden states and per-layer key-value (KV) caches.
- **Auxiliary Action-Regression Loss:** During training, the VLM is supervised with an auxiliary action-regression loss through query tokens. This guides the VLM backbone to produce textual reasoning and understand action-relevant semantics by predicting *N* candidate action chunks and their scores. This auxiliary supervision aims to stabilize training, accelerate convergence, and align high-level reasoning with low-level action generation in the VLM's representation space.
- **Diffusion-based Action Module (DiT):** A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) generates continuous robot actions. It is conditioned on the robot state and the KV cache from the VLM, allowing direct access to the VLM's semantic representations.
- **Knowledge Truncation:** A key component, knowledge truncation, ensures that the DiT conditions only on the *semantic-prefix* KV cache from the VLM. This means the DiT accesses memory from the initial instruction and CoT processing, but *excludes* the appended state and action-query tokens. This prevents the DiT from exploiting synthetic query shortcuts and reduces interference from training-specific token patterns, providing cleaner semantic memory.

#### 3.3. Training Recipe

The ERVLA model is trained using a comprehensive objective function: L = λ_vlm L_vlm + λ_flow L_flow + λ_choice L_choice + λ_score L_score.
- **End-to-End Trainability:** The entire reasoning-action system is fully end-to-end trainable, allowing the flow-matching loss from the DiT action head to back-propagate into the VLM backbone.
- **Loss Components:**
 - `L_vlm`: Token-level CoT cross-entropy loss for next-token prediction over embodied CoT traces, shaping the VLM's representation space.
 - `L_flow`: Rectified flow loss for continuous action generation by the DiT.
 - `L_choice`: Mean absolute error loss for supervising the best of *N* candidate action chunks predicted by the auxiliary choice policy branch.
 - `L_score`: Mean squared error loss for predicting candidate-wise errors of the action chunks.
- **CoT-dropout Strategy:** During training, reasoning supervision is randomly dropped out with a probability *p_cot* (e.g., 0.5). Each sample is processed as either `/cot` (with explicit reasoning) or `/no_cot` (without explicit reasoning), signaled by a toggle. This strategy serves multiple purposes:
 - Allows the model to generate actions without CoT during inference, bypassing autoregressive instability.
 - Reduces reliance on visible reasoning, encouraging internalization into VLM states and the cache-conditioned action interface.
 - Mitigates the "CoT contamination" problem by regularizing the influence of noisy CoT supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness, scalability, and integration of embodied CoT, supported by experiments on simulation benchmarks and real-robot platforms.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of Embodied CoT Signals

- **Action-related vs. High-level CoT:** Experiments indicated that action-related CoT signals, such as `Movement` descriptions and `Point trajectory` (end-effector image-space trajectories), provided more direct benefits to action learning. Isolated high-level fields like `Goal`, `Planning`, `Subtask`, and `Reasoning` alone yielded marginal or even negative gains.
- **Coupling High-level with Action-level:** High-level understanding became beneficial when coupled with concrete action guidance. For example, `Movement+Reasoning` showed improved performance, and combining `Subtask+Movement+Point trajectory` yielded stronger gains, suggesting that effective embodied CoT links semantic understanding to executable motion.
- **CoT Contamination and Mitigation:** The study identified "CoT contamination," where noisy labels (e.g., unstable bounding box coordinates or drifting gripper positions) in large-scale auto-labeled CoT annotations, especially during pre-training, could degrade performance. Unreliable coordinate fields like `Gripper` and `Bounding box` were particularly detrimental without specific mitigation. Reasoning dropout substantially reduced this negative impact, allowing the model to leverage useful action-oriented supervision while regularizing unstable grounding fields.

#### 4.2. Scalability of Embodied CoT

- **Autoregressive Limitations:** Explicit CoT did not scale reliably as an autoregressive action prefix. Simply adding more CoT data to an autoregressive `CoT+action-token` model (like FAST) led to performance degradation across various VLABench tracks. This was attributed to the fragility of autoregressive decoding, where reasoning noise and length variability directly affected action prediction, leading to compounding errors.
- **ERVLA's Scaling Behavior:** In contrast, ERVLA, with its choice policy branch, knowledge truncation, and flow-matching action generation, demonstrated steady improvement as the amount of embodied CoT pre-training data increased across both LIBERO-Plus and VLABench. This indicated that CoT scales effectively when its supervision is integrated for synergistic reasoning-action representation learning rather than being treated as a brittle autoregressive target.

#### 4.3. VLM-to-VLA Transfer and ERVLA Design Choices

- **Improved VLM-to-VLA Alignment:** Embodied CoT facilitated a more reliable transfer of capabilities from stronger VLM backbones to stronger VLA action policies. Without CoT, the correlation between VLM strength and downstream performance was weak. With explicit embodied CoT, stronger VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL series) consistently achieved better results, suggesting CoT acts as an interface that converts VLM semantic priors into action-relevant representations.
- **ERVLA Achieves State-of-the-Art:** ERVLA achieved state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks:
 - **LIBERO-Plus:** 86.9% average success rate, surpassing previous competitive baselines (e.g., π0.5 at 85.5%). It reached 100% success on the background and lighting variations of its Spatial track, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization.
 - **VLABench:** 53.2% average success rate, exhibiting superior performance in out-of-distribution settings that demand semantic understanding and instruction following.
- **Contributions of ERVLA Components:** The choice policy and knowledge truncation components of ERVLA contributed to its performance gains. Removing the choice policy branch weakened action-aware representation shaping, while allowing the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to access the full VLM cache (without knowledge truncation) led to shortcut learning and performance drops on both benchmarks. This highlights that the choice policy injects action-level discrimination into the VLM, and knowledge truncation provides the DiT with cleaner semantic memory.

#### 4.4. Real-World Robot Experiments

- **Superior Real-World Robustness:** ERVLA outperformed competitive state-of-the-art baselines (ECoT, WorldVLA, UniVLA, π0.5) in real-robot experiments. This was particularly evident in tasks involving semantic ambiguity and long-horizon requirements, where ERVLA showed stronger robustness.
- **Performance on Challenging Tiers:** While ERVLA and π0.5 performed similarly on "Basic" tasks, ERVLA exhibited clearer gains on "Semantic" and "Long-horizon" tiers. This indicates that ERVLA's approach of internalizing reasoning as action-relevant representations, rather than relying on explicit verbalization, is effective for real-world generalization under semantic and temporal shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the field of generalizable robot manipulation, particularly in advancing the understanding and application of embodied Chain-of-Thought (CoT).

- **Reframing Embodied CoT:** The work proposes a fundamental shift in how embodied CoT should be understood: not merely as an explicit verbal output format, but as a crucial interface that shapes action-relevant representations within a robot policy. This perspective emphasizes that the utility of reasoning lies in its reliable, action-relevant integration into the policy's internal states, rather than its explicit verbosity or length.

- **Establishment of a Large-Scale CoT Corpus:** The curation of the largest embodied CoT corpus to date (978,743 trajectories, 226.3M samples, 2592.5 hours) provides an invaluable resource. This dataset, which includes multi-view and bimanual settings, establishes a foundation for future large-scale research into reasoning-aware robot learning, enabling more comprehensive studies on how semantic, spatial, planning, and action-level reasoning influence model representations.

- **Novel Architectural and Training Contributions:** ERVLA's design, which integrates explicit linguistic CoT supervision, reasoning dropout, VLM-level action shaping (via an auxiliary choice policy), and semantically conditioned continuous control (via a Diffusion Transformer with knowledge truncation), offers a robust end-to-end trainable framework. This approach effectively addresses known limitations of autoregressive reasoning (e.g., latency, compounding errors) and mitigates the "CoT contamination" issue arising from noisy large-scale labels.

- **Advancements in Generalizable Robot Manipulation:** By achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging simulation benchmarks (LIBERO-Plus, VLABench) and demonstrating superior performance in real-robot experiments (especially for semantic ambiguity and long-horizon tasks), ERVLA demonstrates improved zero-shot generalization and robustness. This indicates progress toward more capable and adaptable robot policies that can operate effectively in complex, out-of-distribution environments.

- **Guidance for Future Research:** The findings provide clear insights for future work, highlighting the importance of reliable grounding supervision, the potential for adaptive test-time reasoning strategies, and the need for architectures that genuinely bridge perception and action through internalized reasoning. The work suggests that future robots might not need to "talk through every motion," but their actions should embody the structure of reasoning, even when explicit verbalization is absent. This has implications for developing more intelligent and less verbose robotic agents.
