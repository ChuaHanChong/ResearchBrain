# Beyond Euclidean Clipping: Overcoming Exploration Collapse in LLM RL via Riemannian Isometric Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and ByteDance Seed identified a geometric inconsistency in PPO-Clip, a widely used reinforcement learning algorithm, that causes exploration collapse in large language models (LLMs). Their proposed Riemannian Isometric Policy Optimization (RIPO) algorithm, which corrects this flaw with a dynamic clipping mechanism, consistently improved LLM mathematical reasoning performance by up to 37.2% and demonstrated superior stability and exploration across various reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- PPO-Clip-based reinforcement learning algorithms for LLMs suffer from "exploration collapse," where the policy quickly concentrates on a narrow set of high-probability actions, neglecting rare but potentially vital actions.
- This limitation hinders LLMs' ability to find effective solutions in vast state-action spaces, particularly in complex, long-horizon reasoning tasks.
- Prior heuristic modifications to PPO-Clip offer symptomatic fixes but do not address the fundamental geometric cause of the algorithm's failure.

## Method

- The paper re-evaluates ==PPO-Clip==, attributing its exploration collapse to an implicit ==Euclidean distance== measure for policy discrepancy, which is inconsistent with the intrinsic ==Riemannian geometry== of policy space.
- It proposes the ==Riemannian Isometric Clip (RIC)== mechanism, which dynamically adjusts clipping bounds based on action probabilities to ensure all state-action updates move within the same geometric distance (==KL divergence== budget) on the ==Riemannian manifold==.
- RIC is integrated into the **Riemannian Isometric Policy Optimization (RIPO)** algorithm, adapting a ==group-relative policy optimization== objective suitable for LLM reinforcement learning.

## Results

- RIPO achieved superior performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, with average improvements of up to **37.2%** on Qwen3-1.7B-Base and **35.1%** on Qwen3-8B-Base over GRPO.
- Training dynamics showed RIPO exhibited faster performance improvement, stable optimization without collapse, balanced policy entropy, and fluctuation-free gradient norms, indicating improved stability and token-efficiency.
- The RIC mechanism successfully transferred to the standard PPO objective, outperforming PPO-Clip and its variants on GSM8k, and demonstrated consistent gains on long-horizon coding and multi-hop search tasks, proving its broad applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - PPO-Clip's fixed clipping boundary, when interpreted geometrically, leads to overly conservative updates for low-probability actions and aggressive updates for high-probability actions, systematically suppressing exploration.
> - By deriving a dynamic, probability-dependent clipping threshold, RIC ensures that policy updates are isometric (equal geometric distance) on the Riemannian manifold, balancing exploration and exploitation more effectively.
> - RIC induces statistical homoscedasticity, leading to a principled bias-variance trade-off by reducing the variance contribution from clipped samples in a density-independent manner.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become a prominent methodology for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). This includes applications in areas such as mathematical reasoning and long-horizon decision-making. A common RL algorithm employed in this context is Proximal Policy Optimization with Clipping (PPO-Clip), known for its stability and effectiveness.

However, existing PPO-Clip-based RL algorithms for LLMs face a challenge known as "exploration collapse." This phenomenon involves the policy rapidly concentrating on a narrow set of high-probability actions, resulting in rare but potentially vital actions remaining unexplored. This limitation is particularly pronounced in long-horizon reasoning tasks, where the vast state-action space necessitates extensive exploration to identify effective solutions.

Previous research efforts have primarily focused on heuristic modifications to PPO-Clip to mitigate exploration collapse or gradient instability. Examples include:
- **DAPO (Yu et al., 2025):** Introduces a decoupled clipping boundary (Clip-Higher) to encourage more exploration.
- **DCPO (Yang et al., 2025b):** Dynamically adapts clipping thresholds.
- **GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025) and GMPO (Zhao et al., 2025):** Address gradient variance through sequential or geometric-mean ratio clipping.
- **CISPO (Chen et al., 2025), GPPO (Su et al., 2025), and SAPO (Gao et al., 2025):** Propose methods to maintain gradients of clipped tokens.

While these variants have demonstrated empirical improvements, the paper argues that they offer symptomatic fixes without identifying or addressing the fundamental cause of PPO-Clip's limitation. This work positions itself by proposing a theoretical re-evaluation of PPO-Clip's mechanism, attributing its failure to a geometric inconsistency, and subsequently developing an algorithm grounded in this re-interpretation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify and resolve a fundamental limitation within PPO-Clip-based reinforcement learning algorithms when applied to Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically the phenomenon of exploration collapse.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
1. **Prevalence of Exploration Collapse:** PPO-Clip, despite its widespread adoption, leads to policies that quickly converge on a limited set of actions. This hinders the discovery of alternative or less probable but potentially optimal action sequences, particularly in complex, long-horizon reasoning tasks where the solution space is vast. Such premature convergence (exploration collapse) prevents LLMs from reaching their full reasoning potential.
2. **Limitations of Existing Heuristics:** Prior attempts to address exploration collapse have largely involved heuristic adjustments to the clipping mechanism (e.g., modifying clipping bounds, adapting thresholds). While these methods sometimes yield empirical gains, the paper suggests they lack a foundational theoretical understanding of why PPO-Clip fails. This limits their generalizability and long-term effectiveness, as they do not tackle the root cause.
3. **Identification of a Geometric Mismatch:** The core motivation for this paper is the hypothesis that PPO-Clip's failure originates from a "geometric mismatch." Specifically, PPO-Clip implicitly quantifies policy discrepancy using a Euclidean metric, which treats equal deviations in the importance ratio as equal policy changes. However, the intrinsic geometry of the statistical manifold of policies, as governed by Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence (the basis for Trust Region Policy Optimization), is Riemannian and non-uniform.
4. **Consequences of the Mismatch:** This geometric inconsistency results in pathological update behavior:
 - **Overly conservative updates in low-probability regions:** For rare actions (low probability), a fixed Euclidean clipping boundary translates to a very small change in KL divergence (geometric distance). This means the policy makes negligible updates to explore new or less frequent actions, even if they are valuable.
 - **Aggressive updates in high-probability regions:** For frequent actions (high probability), the same fixed Euclidean clipping boundary corresponds to a much larger change in KL divergence. This allows already dominant actions to become even more so, further suppressing exploration.
 - This asymmetry systematically under-updates rare but informative actions, leading to premature policy concentration and exploration collapse.

Given this identified fundamental flaw, the key objective is to propose a theoretically grounded RL algorithm that corrects this geometric inconsistency. The algorithm should ensure policy updates are isometric on the Riemannian manifold, thereby balancing exploration and exploitation more effectively and leading to more stable policy optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper involves identifying a theoretical flaw in PPO-Clip, deriving a geometrically consistent policy update mechanism, and integrating it into an RL framework for LLMs, followed by extensive empirical validation.

#### 3.1. Identification of the Geometric Flaw of PPO-Clip

The paper first re-examines the mechanism of PPO-Clip, which constrains the importance ratio `r(θ) = π_θ(a|s) / π_θ_old(a|s)` within an interval `(1 - ϵ, 1 + ϵ)`. This is mathematically equivalent to `|r(θ) - 1| < ϵ`, implying an implicit Euclidean distance measure `d_clip(π_θ_old, π_θ) = (r(θ) - 1)^2`. Under this Euclidean view, any two updates with the same ratio deviation are treated identically in terms of "trust level."

The paper then contrasts this with the intrinsic geometry of policy discrepancy. Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) utilizes KL divergence, `D_KL(π_θ_old(·|s)||π_θ(·|s))`, which, through a second-order Taylor expansion and mapping from parameter space to policy space, can be approximated as:
`D_KL(π_θ_old(·|s)||π_θ(·|s)) ≈ (1/2) Σ_a π_θ_old(a|s)(r_s,a(θ) - 1)^2`.
This derivation shows that the geometric distance between policies on the Riemannian manifold, `d_geom(π_θ_old, π_θ) ∝ π_θ_old * (r(θ) - 1)^2`, depends on the underlying probability `π_θ_old`. This implies that distances in high-probability regions are geometrically "larger" for the same ratio deviation than in low-probability regions. The paper argues that PPO-Clip's fixed `ϵ` ignores this `π_θ_old` dependency, leading to a mismatch. Specifically, a fixed ratio deviation allows large KL updates for high-probability actions and negligible KL updates for low-probability actions, causing exploration collapse.

#### 3.2. Riemannian Isometric Clip (RIC)

To address the geometric mismatch, the paper proposes Riemannian Isometric Clip (RIC). The core idea is to ensure that all state-action updates move within the same *geometric distance* (consume the same KL divergence budget) on the Riemannian manifold, rather than having the same Euclidean ratio deviation.
This is achieved by setting the geometric distance for each update to be bounded by a constant `δ`:
`(1/2) π_θ_old(a|s)(r_s,a(θ) - 1)^2 ≤ δ`
Solving for `r_s,a(θ)`, this yields a dynamic, distribution-dependent clipping threshold:
`|r_s,a(θ) - 1| ≤ ϵ_s,a(π_θ_old) = √(2δ / π_θ_old(a|s))`
This `ϵ_s,a` actively adjusts the clipping boundary: for low-probability actions (`π_θ_old(a|s)` is small), `ϵ_s,a` becomes larger, allowing for more substantial updates. Conversely, for high-probability actions, `ϵ_s,a` becomes smaller, constraining aggressive updates. This mechanism aims to balance exploration and exploitation by allocating isometric trust regions.

#### 3.3. Geometric Isometry and Homoscedasticity

The paper further analyzes RIC from the perspective of the bias-variance trade-off in off-policy RL. The variance of importance sampling `V[r(x)A(x)] ≈ E[r(x)^2 A(x)^2]` is often dominated by terms where `π_θ_old(x)` is small, leading to `v(x) = π_θ_old(x) r(x)^2 = π_θ(x)^2 / π_θ_old(x)` becoming arbitrarily large. While PPO-Clip reduces variance by truncating large `r(x)`, it introduces bias by discarding these samples.
RIC, by contrast, adopts `r(x) ≤ 1 + √(δ/π_θ_old(x))`. When `r(x)` is near this boundary, its variance contribution `v(x)` becomes approximately `O(δ)`, which is density-independent and constant-order. This implies that RIC induces statistical homoscedasticity, leading to a principled bias-variance trade-off: reduced variance without severe bias compared to PPO-Clip.

#### 3.4. Riemannian Isometric Policy Optimization (RIPO)

The complete RIPO algorithm integrates the RIC mechanism into an LLM RL framework. It adapts the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective, which is designed for LLM RL tasks and uses a group-relative advantage estimator. The RIPO objective is defined as:
`J_RIPO(θ) = E[min(r_i,t(θ) * Â_i,t, clip(r_i,t(θ), 1−ϵ_i,t(π_θ_old), 1+ϵ_i,t(π_θ_old)) * Â_i,t)]`
where `ϵ_i,t(π_θ_old)` is the dynamic clipping boundary from RIC. RIPO also incorporates token-level policy gradient loss aggregation, similar to DAPO, to manage gradient influence across different trajectory lengths.

#### 3.5. Experimental Validation

The paper conducts extensive experiments:
- **Models:** Four LLMs of varying scales and architectures (Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-1.7B-Base, Qwen3-4B-Base, Qwen3-8B-Base).
- **Baselines:** Six representative RL algorithms (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO, GMPO, DCPO). Later, GPPO and Clip-Cov are included for comparative analysis of clipping motivations.
- **Training Data:** DAPO-Math-17k dataset (17,917 questions).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Seven competition-level mathematical reasoning datasets (AMC23, AIME24, AIME25, HMMT25, BRUMO25, CMIMC25, SMT25), using the Avg@8 metric.
- **Hyperparameters:** A dual clipping range (0.5 to 10) for importance ratios is applied to all methods, and KL penalty is removed, consistent with prior works. The `δ` for RIPO is set to 0.05 by default.
- **Additional Studies:** Ablation studies on `δ` values (symmetric and decoupled), comparison with other clipping motivations (GPPO, Clip-Cov), transferability of RIC to the standard PPO objective on GSM8k, and Pass@k analysis for capacity boundaries on challenging benchmarks. Generalization to coding and search tasks is also evaluated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across various experiments:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance in Mathematical Reasoning (Table 1)

RIPO consistently demonstrated superior average performance across all four LLM architectures and seven competition-level mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- On Qwen3-1.7B-Base, RIPO achieved an average improvement of 37.2% over GRPO.
- For Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, the average improvement was 34.4% over GRPO.
- On Qwen3-4B-Base, RIPO showed a 17.1% average improvement over GRPO.
- For the largest model, Qwen3-8B-Base, RIPO achieved a 35.1% average improvement over GRPO.
- RIPO generally outperformed other advanced clipping methods (DAPO, GSPO, GMPO, DCPO) across the majority of benchmarks, with particularly strong gains on challenging datasets like AIME24 and BRUMO25. The performance gains were more pronounced on larger models.

#### 4.2. Training Dynamics and Analysis (Figure 1)

Analysis of training dynamics using Qwen3-8B-Base revealed several characteristics:
- **Performance Improvement:** RIPO exhibited faster performance improvement on AIME24, achieving the performance of GRPO at 200 steps within approximately 40 steps, indicating higher token-efficiency.
- **Optimization Stability:** RIPO's evaluation curve showed a smooth increase with minimal oscillation and did not exhibit training collapse, suggesting stable optimization.
- **Policy Entropy:** While GRPO showed rapid entropy collapse, indicating deterministic policy outputs, and DAPO exhibited uncontrolled entropy growth, RIPO maintained policy entropy within a moderate, stable range. This behavior indicates a balance between exploration and exploitation.
- **Gradient Norm:** RIPO maintained an almost fluctuation-free gradient norm throughout training, contrasting with the pronounced oscillations and spikes observed in other RL algorithms, suggesting more stable and lower-variance gradients.
- **Clipped Tokens Proportion:** RIPO displayed a balanced clipping ratio, intermediate between methods that rarely clipped (DCPO, GMPO) and those that clipped much more frequently (GSPO, DAPO), suggesting a well-calibrated trust region.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on δ (Table 2, Figure 2)

An ablation study on the `δ` parameter for RIPO (Qwen3-8B-Base on AIME24) demonstrated:
- **Robustness:** RIPO maintained stable performance across a range of symmetric `δ` values (e.g., 0.02 to 0.08).
- **Importance of Symmetry:** Performance significantly degraded when `δ_high` was set substantially larger than `δ_low` (asymmetric clipping). Training dynamics for asymmetric settings showed sudden reward degradation and entropy explosion, suggesting that disproportionate constraints on increasing versus decreasing action probabilities can destabilize training and lead to excessive exploration followed by collapse.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Other Clipping Motivations (Table 3)

Comparing RIPO with GPPO (gradient-preserving clipping) and Clip-Cov (high-covariance token clipping) on Qwen3-8B-Base, RIPO consistently outperformed both across all tested benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, HMMT25, BRUMO25, SMT25), indicating superior stability and effectiveness over these alternative clipping strategies.

#### 4.5. Transfer to PPO Objective (Table 4, Figure 3)

When the Riemannian Isometric Clip (RIC) mechanism was applied to a standard PPO objective (with a learned value function and GAE) across different Qwen2.5-Instruct models (0.5B to 14B) on the GSM8k math dataset:
- RIPO-Clip consistently outperformed PPO-Clip, DAPO-Clip, and DCPO-Clip in terms of Avg@1 metric across all model scales, demonstrating the generality of RIC beyond GRPO-style objectives.
- Training dynamics showed that PPO with RIPO-Clip maintained higher entropy levels compared to PPO-Clip, which rapidly decayed to near zero, again indicating better exploration. PPO with RIPO-Clip also exhibited smoother gradient norms, implying more stable optimization.

#### 4.6. Capacity Boundaries (Table 5)

Pass@k analysis (up to k=128) on AIME-25 and HMMT-25 with Qwen3-8B-Base indicated:
- The base model's performance plateaued early (around k=16).
- RIPO consistently outperformed other RL algorithms and demonstrated continuous scaling of performance up to k=128 (e.g., 60.0% on AIME-25 and 45.3% on HMMT-25 at k=128), suggesting that RIPO mitigates exploration collapse and enables LLMs to effectively utilize higher numbers of samples to break through inherent reasoning capacity limitations.

#### 4.7. Generalization to Coding and Search Tasks (Table 6)

RIPO also demonstrated consistent and significant performance gains over GRPO on long-horizon coding (Codeforces, CodeContest, TACO, APPS) and multi-hop search tasks (TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, WikiMultiHopQA) using Qwen3-8B-Base. This indicates the broad applicability and universal effectiveness of the method across different types of reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the field of reinforcement learning for large language models through both theoretical and practical advancements.

**Theoretical Significance:**
The paper identifies a fundamental geometric mismatch within PPO-Clip, a widely used RL algorithm. By demonstrating that PPO-Clip implicitly employs a Euclidean metric for policy discrepancy, which is inconsistent with the intrinsic Riemannian geometry induced by KL divergence, it provides a deeper theoretical understanding of the root cause of exploration collapse. This re-interpretation shifts the focus from heuristic adjustments to a principled geometric correction. The derivation of Riemannian Isometric Clip (RIC) from first principles, ensuring isometric policy updates on the Riemannian manifold, represents a theoretical contribution to policy optimization. Furthermore, the connection drawn between geometric isometry and statistical homoscedasticity provides insights into the bias-variance trade-off in off-policy RL.

**Algorithmic and Practical Impact:**
The proposed Riemannian Isometric Policy Optimization (RIPO) algorithm offers a theoretically grounded solution to the persistent problem of exploration collapse in LLM RL.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** RIPO has demonstrated consistent and substantial improvements in the reasoning capabilities of various LLMs across diverse and challenging competition-level mathematical benchmarks. This directly translates to LLMs being able to tackle more complex, long-horizon problems effectively.
- **Improved Stability and Efficiency:** The observed stable training dynamics, balanced policy entropy, and fluctuation-free gradient norms indicate that RIPO promotes more stable and efficient optimization processes. This reduces the brittleness often associated with RL training, making it more reliable for developing high-performing LLMs.
- **Mitigation of Exploration Collapse:** The Pass@k analysis shows that RIPO enables LLMs to continuously scale performance with increased sample counts, effectively breaking through what appeared to be intrinsic capacity limitations. This suggests a significant step towards truly overcoming exploration collapse in complex decision-making scenarios.
- **Generalizability:** The demonstrated effectiveness of RIC when transferred to the standard PPO objective and its strong performance on coding and multi-hop search tasks suggest that the geometric principle is broadly applicable beyond GRPO-style algorithms and specific reasoning domains. This broadens the potential utility of the approach across various LLM applications requiring robust exploration.

In conclusion, this research establishes a principled framework for designing more effective and stable reinforcement learning algorithms for large language models by addressing a core geometric inconsistency. Its findings offer valuable theoretical insights into policy optimization and provide practical solutions for enhancing the reasoning and exploratory capabilities of LLMs in complex tasks.
