# Scaling Mixture-of-Experts Video Pretraining for Embodied Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> LingBot-Video introduces an open-source Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) video foundation model tailored for embodied intelligence, enhancing physical coherence and instruction following in video generation. It integrates an MoE architecture for computational efficiency, a robot-augmented data corpus for physical grounding, and a multi-dimensional reward system for physically rational training. The model achieved an average score of 0.620 on the RBench benchmark and 40.4 on Physics-IQ Verified, outperforming other open-source models in generating physically plausible and task-oriented video content.

## Problem

- Existing video generative models are primarily optimized for perceptual quality rather than properties critical for physical interaction, such as physical correctness, controllability, or long-horizon state consistency.
- Current dense video transformer architectures incur high inference costs and face scalability challenges, limiting their use in complex, real-time embodied scenarios.
- Internet-scale video datasets often lack explicit robot embodiment priors or precise interaction dynamics, hindering the model's ability to understand and simulate physical environments.
- Traditional training objectives primarily focus on aesthetic quality and text-video correspondence, without incorporating explicit reward signals for physical feasibility or task completion.

## Method

- Developed **LingBot-Video**, a ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)==-based pretraining paradigm incorporating a sparse ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== framework to improve computational efficiency and scalability.
- Constructed a ==robot-augmented pretraining corpus==, leveraging a data profiling engine and a ==World-Knowledge Topological Graph==, to imbue the model with an intrinsic understanding of actions and embodied dynamics.
- Implemented a ==multi-dimensional reward system== for post-training, optimizing for physical rationality, motion coherence, human-motion consistency, and task completion, guided by six specialized ==reward models== and ==GRPO==.
- Designed a ==cascaded refiner model== to enhance lower-resolution video outputs to higher resolutions while maintaining global semantics and refining fine details.

## Results

- LingBot-Video achieved the highest performance in internal benchmarks for the embodied domain on TI2V tasks and ranked **second** for T2V, demonstrating strong intrinsic physical priors.
- The model scored **0.620** on RBench (robot-centric interactions) and **40.4** on Physics-IQ Verified (physical phenomena prediction), surpassing all other evaluated open-source models.
- User studies showed LingBot-Video received higher 'Good' rates than 'Bad' rates against most open-source baselines in T2V and TI2V generation, exhibiting a consistent advantage in TI2V tasks.
- The Action-to-Video (A2V) post-trained model demonstrated improved adherence to physical laws and more accurate action following on EgoDex Eval and DreamDojo-HV Eval datasets, even with novel objects and actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures to Diffusion Transformers for video generation offers an effective approach to achieve high modeling capacity and inference efficiency for embodied AI tasks.
> - A dedicated, robot-augmented data corpus, combined with dense structured captioning and a progressive data curriculum, is crucial for grounding video models in physically plausible interaction dynamics.
> - Reinforcement learning with multi-aspect reward functions, including metrics for physical plausibility and task completion, significantly improves a video model's ability to generate physically rational and controllable content.
> - LingBot-Video's architecture and training methodologies enable superior generation of robot-centric interactions and prediction of physical phenomena compared to existing open-source baselines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video generative models, primarily diffusion-based and autoregressive architectures, have demonstrated capabilities in content creation, synthesizing temporally coherent and photorealistic sequences. This capability has led to their exploration as implicit simulators of the physical world, finding applications in domains such as robotics, autonomous driving, and interactive environments. In this context, video models can function as predictive world models, supporting tasks like planning, policy learning, and imagination-based control.

Despite these advancements, a gap exists between the design principles of current video generation models and the specific requirements of embodied intelligence. Existing video foundation models are typically optimized for perceptual quality—including realism, aesthetics, and text alignment—rather than for properties critical to physical interaction, such as physical correctness, controllability, contact stability, rigid-body dynamics, or long-horizon state consistency under intervention. The internet-scale video datasets commonly used for training these models offer visual diversity but often lack explicit fidelity to the constraints of embodied interaction.

Current approaches to integrating video generation with embodied intelligence generally encounter limitations in three areas:
1. **Architecture**: Most diffusion-based video transformers employ dense computation, which activates all parameters uniformly. This results in high inference costs and scalability challenges, particularly for long or high-resolution video sequences. While sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have shown efficiency gains in large language models, their adoption in video generation has been limited.
2. **Data**: Training corpora frequently consist predominantly of internet videos. These datasets often lack robot embodiment priors or precise interaction dynamics, which can hinder the model's grounding in physical simulation.
3. **Training Objectives**: Current alignment strategies often prioritize aesthetic quality and text-video correspondence. They generally do not incorporate explicit reward signals for physical feasibility, task completion, or long-horizon consistency.

This work positions itself to address these identified limitations by proposing a video pretraining paradigm specifically designed for embodied intelligence, aiming to bridge the functional gap between digital video generation and physical actuation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop LingBot-Video, a Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based video pretraining paradigm tailored for embodied intelligence. This overarching goal is driven by the motivation to overcome the limitations of existing video generative models when applied to active embodied reasoning and intelligence tasks.

The key objectives are articulated through three integrated components:

1. **Architectural Efficiency and Scalability through Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)**:
 - **Motivation**: Traditional dense architectures in video transformers suffer from prohibitive inference costs and limited scalability as model capacity increases. This bottleneck hinders the application of large video models in complex, real-time embodied scenarios.
 - **Objective**: To incorporate and scale a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework into the video generation architecture. This aims to achieve an improved trade-off between modeling capacity and inference efficiency by enabling sparse, conditional computation, thereby allowing the model to scale its parameter count without a proportional increase in active computational cost per token.

2. **Enhanced Data Grounding with Robot-Augmented Corpus**:
 - **Motivation**: Internet-scale video datasets, while vast, frequently lack the specific priors and precise interaction dynamics essential for training models that understand and simulate embodied agents. This leads to models that may generate visually plausible content but lack physical correctness or an intrinsic understanding of actions.
 - **Objective**: To construct a dedicated data profiling engine and a robot-augmented pretraining corpus. This corpus integrates standard internet videos with extensive robot-oriented footage, including manipulation, navigation, and egocentric perspectives. The goal is to imbue the base model with an intrinsic understanding of actions, world dynamics, and embodiment-specific behaviors.

3. **Physically Rational and Task-Oriented Training Objectives**:
 - **Motivation**: Existing training objectives primarily focus on perceptual quality metrics such as aesthetics, prompt-following, and motion consistency. These criteria do not explicitly enforce physical realism, task completion, or adherence to physical interaction constraints, which are crucial for embodied applications.
 - **Objective**: To develop a multi-dimensional reward system that extends beyond conventional aesthetic and text-alignment objectives. This system incorporates explicit signals for physical rationality and task-oriented success, guiding the model to learn dynamics and interactions that are consistent with embodied environments.

By addressing these architectural, data, and training challenges, the work aims to enable the creation of a physically grounded and computationally efficient video foundation model suitable for embodied intelligence applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LingBot-Video framework is structured around a DiT-based video pretraining paradigm that integrates a novel architecture, a tailored data pipeline, and progressive training strategies, supported by a specialized infrastructure.

#### 3.1. Architecture Design

LingBot-Video employs a cascaded design comprising a base generator and a refiner.
- **Task-Unified Single-Stream Diffusion Transformer**: The core of the base generator is a single-stream Diffusion Transformer. It processes compact visual latents (encoded by Wan2.1-VAE) and multimodal conditions (extracted by Qwen3-VL-4B).
 - **Unified Input Formulation**: All training samples (Text-to-Image, Text-to-Video, Image-to-Video) are represented as a single token sequence by concatenating visual latent patches and condition tokens. Image targets are treated as single-frame videos.
 - **Multi-Modal 3D RoPE**: A 3D Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) mechanism places condition and visual tokens in non-overlapping temporal coordinate ranges, preserving spatial locality and temporal order within the single stream.
 - **QK-Norm and AdaLN-Single Modulation**: Per-head RMSNorm is applied to queries and keys for attention stability, and an AdaLN-single design provides efficient timestep modulation.
- **Scaling with Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)**:
 - The dense Feed-Forward Network (FFN) in each transformer block is replaced with a token-choice sparse MoE layer. This design includes shared experts for general priors and routed experts for specialized features.
 - **Routing**: A sigmoid router determines token-expert affinities, and a group-limited routing strategy controls communication costs.
 - **Load Balancing**: An online bias correction mechanism dynamically adjusts expert biases during training to balance load without an explicit auxiliary loss. Additionally, a sequence-wise auxiliary loss encourages balanced expert usage within individual video sequences.
 - **Recipe Exploration**: Experiments indicated that a configuration with 128 total routed experts, routing 8 experts per token (13B total parameters, 1.4B active), offered a beneficial trade-off between performance, latency, and memory. Fine-grained specialization showed advantages over coarse routing.
 - **Scaling Experiments**: MoE models demonstrated performance advantages over dense models with comparable active parameters. They also exhibited cross-compute dominance, outperforming dense models with approximately twice their active parameter scale. Scaling studies up to 120B total parameters showed predictable scaling behaviors. Inference efficiency benchmarks indicated near-parity with active-parameter equivalent dense models and significant speedups (up to 3.18x) against larger dense models at long sequence lengths.
- **Cascaded Refiner**: A dedicated second-stage model refines lower-resolution base generator outputs (e.g., 480p) to higher resolutions (e.g., 1080p). It learns a conditional rectified flow from synthetically degraded low-resolution inputs toward clean high-resolution targets, maintaining global semantics while enhancing fine details.

#### 3.2. Data Curation and Management

An integrated data infrastructure was developed to manage heterogeneous image and video data.
- **Data Profiling Engine**: Annotates each sample with structural (resolution, duration), semantic (objects, actions, styles), motion (camera, subject, tracked), camera (cinematic attributes), and quality (aesthetic, synthetic likelihood, artifacts) attributes using VLMs and specialized models.
- **World-Knowledge Topological Graph**: Organizes semantic concepts and actions into hierarchical trees. This graph facilitates distribution-aware sampling, allowing for up-weighting of rare or difficult embodied concepts and guiding targeted data acquisition.
- **Dense Structured Captioning**: Annotates all training data with detailed, JSON-formatted captions for images, videos, VLA (robot manipulation) videos, and egocentric videos. These captions include comprehensive descriptions, camera tags, world knowledge, and timestamped actions for dynamic elements.
- **Caption Rewriter**: A two-stage LLM-based pipeline transforms brief user prompts into the detailed structured JSON format used during training, bridging the gap between user input and model conditioning.
- **Data Curriculum**: A five-stage progressive data introduction strategy aligns with the pre-training schedule, gradually increasing resolution and complexity of data sources, with a focused rebalancing stage for embodied intelligence data.

#### 3.3. Training Regimen

The training process involves progressive pre-training, post-training with reinforcement learning, and an action-to-video adaptation.
- **Progressive Pre-Training (Five Stages)**:
 - **Stage 1: Image-Only Prior Acquisition**: Establishes text-visual semantic alignment and single-frame visual priors using 192p images (T=1 videos).
 - **Stage 2: Low-Resolution Temporal Learning**: Introduces 192p video data for Text-to-Video (T2V) generation, focusing on temporal dynamics.
 - **Stage 3: Multi-Task Conditioning**: Broadens video tasks to include Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V) generation, teaching the model to leverage visual conditions.
 - **Stage 4: Weighted Distribution Harmonization**: Utilizes a weighted sampler at 480p to balance data quality and source, prioritizing high-value embodied sources.
 - **Stage 5: High-Resolution Refinement**: Trains the cascaded refiner on a small, high-quality 1080p video subset.
- **Post-Training**:
 - **Reinforcement Learning with Multi-Aspect Rewards**: Employs six specialized reward models to optimize for: Vision Quality, Text-Video Alignment (action-centric VQA), Dynamic Degree (VLM-assessed motion intensity), Motion Coherence (natural playback speed), Human-Motion Consistency (structural and temporal correctness of human actions), and Physical Plausibility (motion causality, object permanence, material-kinematic realism, task completion). These are aggregated into a single advantage.
 - **On-Policy GRPO Training**: Uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with group-shared single-step exploration, Coefficients-Preserving Sampling (CPS) for stochastic transitions, and timestep-balanced gradient reweighting. Multi-reward advantages are normalized independently.
 - **Negative-Aware Finetuning with Real-World Videos**: Adapts the DiffusionNFT framework, using real videos as positive samples and model-generated videos as negative samples to mitigate reward hacking and bypass trajectory-level log-probability computation.
- **Action-to-Video (A2V) Post-Training (LingBot-Video-A2V)**: Adapts the pre-trained model into an action-conditioned world model. This involves recaptioning data to describe only the initial state, representing raw robot actions as relative actions, and injecting these action embeddings as residual signals into transformer blocks.
- **Distillation**: The model is distilled into a few-step generator using an improved Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD2) framework to enhance inference efficiency.

#### 3.4. Infrastructure

The project developed co-designed pre-training, post-training, and serving infrastructures to support large-scale video model development.
- **Pre-Training Infrastructure**: Includes a token-budgeted data pipeline with dynamic length-aware scheduling, composable multi-dimensional parallelism (DP, FSDP, SP, EP), activation checkpointing, compile-first graph capture (torch.compile with Inductor), and asynchronous monitoring.
- **Post-Training Infrastructure**: Features a diffusion-native RL system that decouples rollout, reward evaluation, and training, handles gigabyte-scale intermediate states, and achieves high MFU.
- **Serving Infrastructure**: Provides a Diffusers-compatible model package and an SGLang Diffusion runtime with optimized CUDA kernels. It supports text-to-image, text-to-video, and text-image-to-video generation, with fidelity-first and speed-first execution modes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LingBot-Video involved internal benchmarks, public benchmarks, user studies, and specific tests for its action-to-video capabilities.

#### 4.1. Internal Benchmark

LingBot-Video was evaluated on an internal benchmark covering "General Quality" (Motion Quality, Prompt Following, Visual Consistency, Aesthetic Quality) and "Embodied Domain" (Human Interaction, Physical Simulation, Robotics, Egocentric Perspective, Navigation) for both Text-to-Video (T2V) and Text-and-Image-to-Video (TI2V) tasks.
- **TI2V Task**: LingBot-Video achieved the highest performance among open-source competitors in both general quality and embodied domain scores. This indicates its capability in simulating precise physical trajectories, such as robotic arm manipulation and obstacle avoidance.
- **T2V Task**: The model ranked second in general quality but demonstrated superior performance on the embodied domain score compared to competitive baselines like NVIDIA Cosmos 3. This result suggests that LingBot-Video possesses intrinsic physical priors relevant for embodied AI, even without an initial image condition.

#### 4.2. Public Benchmarks

- **RBench**: This benchmark assesses robot-centric interactions. LingBot-Video attained an average score of 0.620, surpassing all other evaluated open-source models (e.g., Cosmos3 Super 0.581, Wan 2.2 A14B 0.507, HunyuanVideo 1.5 0.460, LongCat-Video 0.437). This result supports the model's embodied generation capabilities, particularly in scenarios requiring physical coherence and instruction following.
- **Physics-IQ Verified**: This benchmark evaluates the prediction of real-world physical phenomena. In the Image-to-Video (I2V) setting, LingBot-Video achieved a score of 40.4, ranking first among the evaluated open-source models. It marginally exceeded Cosmos 3 (39.5) and showed a more substantial lead over Hunyuan Video 1.5 (33.4) and Wan 2.2 A14B (32.2). This indicates robust predictive consistency for physical processes.

#### 4.3. User Study

A Good-Same-Bad (GSB) human evaluation was conducted against several open-source and commercial video generation models across 400 prompts for T2V and TI2V tasks.
- **T2V Generation**: LingBot-Video received a higher "Good" rate than "Bad" rate when compared against several open-source baselines, including Wan 2.2 5B, LongCat-Video, Wan 2.2 A14B, and LTX-2.3. Its performance was competitive with stronger open-source models such as HunyuanVideo 1.5 and Cosmos 3.
- **TI2V Generation**: LingBot-Video demonstrated a more consistent advantage, showing higher "Good" rates than "Bad" rates against all evaluated open-source baselines, including significant margins over Wan 2.2 5B and notable gains over Cosmos 3, LTX-2.3, Wan 2.2 A14B, LongCat-Video, and HunyuanVideo 1.5. While performing strongly among open-source models, it was observed to trail some commercial models.

#### 4.4. Action-to-Video Post-Training

LingBot-Video-A2V was evaluated on EgoDex Eval and DreamDojo-HV Eval datasets, which feature novel objects and actions not present in its training data. The post-trained model exhibited improved adherence to physical laws and more accurate action following when compared to DreamDojo. For example, it preserved object properties and followed hand poses relative to objects more consistently.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces LingBot-Video as an open-source Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) video foundation model developed specifically for embodied intelligence. Its significance lies in several aspects:

- **Architectural Innovation**: The successful scaling of an MoE-based Diffusion Transformer from inception demonstrates a viable approach for achieving a balance between high modeling capacity and inference efficiency. This addresses a key limitation in applying large video generative models to computationally demanding embodied AI tasks.
- **Data-Centric Approach**: The development of a comprehensive data profiling engine, a World-Knowledge Topological Graph, and dense structured captioning, coupled with a robot-augmented corpus and a progressive data curriculum, indicates an effective strategy for grounding video models in physical and embodied interaction dynamics.
- **Physically Grounded Training**: The multi-dimensional reward system for post-training, which incorporates physical plausibility, motion coherence, human-motion consistency, and task completion metrics, represents an advancement beyond purely aesthetic or text-alignment objectives in video generation. This approach aims to yield models that produce physically rational and controllable video content.

The potential impact of LingBot-Video for the robotics and embodied AI communities is outlined in three primary roles:

1. **Data Engine**: The model's capacity to synthesize high-fidelity, low-cost training data at scale could mitigate data scarcity, a persistent challenge in robotics research and development. This offers a method for generating diverse and controlled synthetic environments for robot learning.
2. **Policy Evaluator**: By serving as a visual simulator, LingBot-Video could enable the evaluation of robot policies in simulated environments. This can facilitate safety-critical testing and refinement of robot behaviors without the inherent risks and resource demands of real-world deployment.
3. **Action Planner**: The model's ability to predict future visual trajectories conditioned on current states and planned action sequences positions it as a tool for assisting robots in real-time decision-making and planning. This could enhance autonomous capabilities by allowing robots to "imagine" and evaluate the outcomes of their actions.

By open-sourcing LingBot-Video, the authors aim to facilitate collaborative research and development, potentially contributing to the advancement of physical engines and the capabilities of future robotic systems.
