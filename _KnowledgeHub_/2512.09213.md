# MPC for momentum counter-balanced and zero-impulse contact with a free-spinning satellite

> [!summary] Summary
> A nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework was developed for a servicer satellite to achieve zero-impulse contact with a free-spinning target satellite. The framework explicitly manages momentum coupling and operational constraints, achieving 0% constraint violations and higher mission success rates in simulations compared to a PID baseline.

## Problem

- Performing on-orbit servicing (OOS) tasks like satellite capture or repair requires making contact with non-cooperative, often free-spinning, target satellites.
- Free-flying space robots exhibit momentum coupling, where manipulation arm movements exert reaction torques on the servicer's base, complicating stable control.
- Prior control methods for OOS frequently lack explicit enforcement of actuation and state constraints, risking system failures and unsafe operations.

## Method

- A nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (**MPC**) framework is developed, dividing the contact maneuver into two phases: spin synchronization and zero-impulse contact.
- The servicer satellite is modeled as a momentum-coupled multibody system, explicitly accounting for interaction dynamics between its base, reaction wheels, and 3-DoF robotic arm.
- The MPC framework formulates optimal control problems that intrinsically incorporate and enforce actuation and state constraints.

## Results

- The MPC controller achieved **0%** constraint violations across all successful simulations, significantly outperforming the PID baseline which experienced up to **26.41%** violations.
- MPC consistently showed lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for servicer angular velocity and end-effector states, and higher mission success rates (up to **92%**) compared to PID (down to **10%** under noise).
- While effective, MPC required substantially higher average computation times (**0.49**-**1.0** seconds) per control cycle compared to PID (**0.018**-**0.019** seconds).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating momentum-coupled dynamics and operational constraints within an MPC framework is crucial for safe and reliable contact operations in free-flying space robotics.
> - Nonlinear MPC intrinsically enforces actuation and state constraints, preventing violations that traditional controllers often miss and enhancing operational safety.
> - While offering superior precision and robustness, the computational demands of MPC remain a practical challenge for real-time deployment on resource-constrained satellite platforms.
