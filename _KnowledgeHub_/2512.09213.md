# MPC for momentum counter-balanced and zero-impulse contact with a free-spinning satellite

> [!summary] Summary
> A nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework was developed for a servicer satellite to achieve zero-impulse contact with a free-spinning target satellite. The framework explicitly manages momentum coupling and operational constraints, achieving 0% constraint violations and higher mission success rates in simulations compared to a PID baseline.

## Problem

- Performing on-orbit servicing (OOS) tasks like satellite capture or repair requires making contact with non-cooperative, often free-spinning, target satellites.
- Free-flying space robots exhibit momentum coupling, where manipulation arm movements exert reaction torques on the servicer's base, complicating stable control.
- Prior control methods for OOS frequently lack explicit enforcement of actuation and state constraints, risking system failures and unsafe operations.

## Method

- A nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (**MPC**) framework is developed, dividing the contact maneuver into two phases: spin synchronization and zero-impulse contact.
- The servicer satellite is modeled as a momentum-coupled multibody system, explicitly accounting for interaction dynamics between its base, reaction wheels, and 3-DoF robotic arm.
- The MPC framework formulates optimal control problems that intrinsically incorporate and enforce actuation and state constraints.

## Results

- The MPC controller achieved **0%** constraint violations across all successful simulations, significantly outperforming the PID baseline which experienced up to **26.41%** violations.
- MPC consistently showed lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for servicer angular velocity and end-effector states, and higher mission success rates (up to **92%**) compared to PID (down to **10%** under noise).
- While effective, MPC required substantially higher average computation times (**0.49**-**1.0** seconds) per control cycle compared to PID (**0.018**-**0.019** seconds).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating momentum-coupled dynamics and operational constraints within an MPC framework is crucial for safe and reliable contact operations in free-flying space robotics.
> - Nonlinear MPC intrinsically enforces actuation and state constraints, preventing violations that traditional controllers often miss and enhancing operational safety.
> - While offering superior precision and robustness, the computational demands of MPC remain a practical challenge for real-time deployment on resource-constrained satellite platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the domain of on-orbit servicing (OOS), which involves the observation or alteration of satellites after initial launch by servicer satellites. OOS tasks, such as refueling, attitude stabilization, debris removal, and in-orbit assembly, necessitate making contact with orbital objects that frequently lack inherent coordination capabilities.

While similar robotic manipulation tasks are routinely performed on Earth using optimal control and well-characterized kinematic models, their execution in space is more complex. This is primarily due to the free-flying, momentum-coupled multibody nature of space robotic systems. In contrast to ground-based manipulators with anchored bases, OOS manipulation arms operate from a freely floating base, introducing intricate rotational dynamics. A significant challenge in achieving zero-impulse contact is the momentum coupling between the servicer satellite's manipulation module and its moment-generation module; movements of the robotic arm exert reaction torques on the base, influencing the servicer's overall orientation. The reaction wheel (RW) cluster within the moment-generation module must counteract these torques to maintain the servicer satellite's stability.

Existing control approaches for OOS, including PID and Lyapunov-based controllers, as well as various optimal control methods for tasks like detumbling, have been developed. However, these prior approaches frequently encounter limitations regarding the explicit enforcement of actuation and state constraints. Such constraints are crucial for operational safety and preventing system failures, but their omission can lead to critical issues for actuation modules. Model Predictive Control (MPC) has demonstrated utility in various real-time control applications, including robotic manipulation, automotive systems, and aerospace guidance, due to its capability to handle constraints and optimize performance over a finite prediction horizon. This research leverages MPC to specifically address the constraint handling and momentum management issues inherent in OOS, thereby aiming to improve the safety and reliability of contact operations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework that enables a servicer satellite to achieve zero-impulse contact with a free-spinning target satellite. This maneuver requires coordinated control of two distinct modules within the servicer satellite: a moment generation module (comprising a reaction wheel cluster and a cuboid base) and a manipulation module (a robotic arm).

The key motivations and more specific objectives driving this work are:

- **Addressing Constraint Violations**: Prior control approaches for on-orbit servicing (OOS) operations, such as satellite capture or detumbling, often do not explicitly account for actuation and state constraints. This can lead to the servicer satellite exceeding its physical limits, potentially resulting in system failures or unsafe operations. The research aims to develop a controller that intrinsically incorporates and enforces these operational constraints.
- **Managing Momentum Coupling**: The dynamics of free-flying, multi-body space robots are characterized by strong momentum coupling between the manipulation arm and the servicer satellite's base. Movement of the robotic arm generates reaction torques that affect the attitude and angular velocity of the servicer satellite. The moment-generation module must counteract these effects to maintain stability. The objective is to explicitly model and control these cross-coupling dynamics within the control framework.
- **Achieving Spin and Orientation Synchronization**: Before contact, the servicer satellite must precisely match the target satellite's spin rate and orientation. This phase requires the servicer's moment-generation module to execute a controlled maneuver to align itself with the free-spinning target.
- **Facilitating Zero-Impulse Contact**: Following synchronization, the manipulation module must extend its end-effector to a designated contact point on the target satellite with minimal relative velocity at the moment of contact, i.e., zero-impulse contact. This requires precise trajectory tracking and control of the manipulator's joints while maintaining the servicer's synchronized attitude.
- **Enhancing Robustness**: OOS operations occur in an environment with inherent uncertainties, including imperfect knowledge of system parameters and the presence of observation and actuation noise. The motivation is to develop a control strategy that demonstrates robustness to these uncertainties.

In essence, the research seeks to provide a control solution that enhances the safety, precision, and reliability of complex OOS contact maneuvers by explicitly integrating system dynamics, constraints, and optimization within a predictive control framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves developing a nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework applied to a servicer satellite model composed of two distinct, momentum-coupled modules.

**System Modeling:**
The servicer satellite is modeled as a free-flying multibody system consisting of a cuboid base (moment-generation module) and a 3-DoF manipulation arm (manipulation module).
- **Moment-generation module**: This module, considered the primary actuation system for attitude control, comprises a cuboid base equipped with a 3-DoF reaction wheel (RW) cluster. Each RW is modeled as aligning with one of the principal axes of the base, enabling independent torques.
- **Manipulation module**: This module, tasked with achieving contact, is a 3-DoF manipulation arm with three revolute joints and an end-effector. Its configuration is defined by joint angles ($\boldsymbol{\theta}$) and angular velocities ($\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$). Forward kinematics, derived using the Denavit-Hartenberg convention, link joint angles to the end-effector's position ($\mathbf{p}_{ee}$) and velocity ($\mathbf{v}_{ee}$) within the servicer's body-fixed frame.
- **Relative Dynamics**: Quaternions ($\mathbf{q}_{rel}$) are used to represent the servicer satellite's relative orientation to the target satellite, offering a singularity-free representation. The relative angular velocity ($\boldsymbol{\omega}_{rel}$) is defined as the difference between the servicer's angular velocity and the target's angular velocity transformed into the servicer's frame.
- **Momentum-Coupled Dynamics**: A key aspect of the modeling is the explicit derivation of the servicer satellite's total system dynamics, accounting for the momentum coupling between the cuboid base, the RW cluster, and the manipulation arm. This is achieved using the law of momentum conservation and the Euler-Lagrange equation. The total kinetic energy of the system is expressed as a quadratic function of generalized velocities. The full dynamics are then represented in a generalized inertia matrix form relating accelerations of the base, arm, and RWs to applied torques and Coriolis/centrifugal forces. To reduce complexity and enable MPC implementation, the dynamics are further reduced by implicitly accounting for linear displacement and explicitly solving for RW cluster acceleration, resulting in a control-affine, nonlinear system that couples the base's angular acceleration ($\ddot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_B$) and the arm's joint accelerations ($\ddot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$) to the RW and arm torques.

**MPC Framework:**
The overall maneuver is decomposed into two consecutive phases, each controlled by a distinct MPC formulation:
1. **Phase A: Spin Synchronization MPC**:
 - **Objective**: Align the servicer satellite's angular velocity ($\boldsymbol{\omega}_B$) and relative orientation quaternion ($\mathbf{q}_{rel}$) with the target satellite's spin. This is formulated as an optimal control problem (OCP) that minimizes a weighted sum of relative orientation error, angular velocity error, and RW torque expenditure over a finite horizon.
 - **Assumptions**: During this phase, the manipulation arm is assumed inactive and its joints are locked.
 - **Constraints**: The OCP includes quaternion kinematics, relative angular velocity dynamics, servicer angular velocity dynamics (simplified due to inactive arm), actuation limits for RW torques, state limits for servicer angular velocity, and terminal constraints to ensure convergence to the desired synchronized state.
2. **Phase B: Zero-Impulse Contact MPC**:
 - **Objective**: Guide the manipulation arm's end-effector to a designated contact point on the target satellite while maintaining the spin synchronization achieved in Phase A. The OCP minimizes a weighted sum of RW torque, manipulator joint torque, joint angle and velocity errors (relative to a reference trajectory), and relative orientation and angular velocity errors.
 - **Assumptions**: The manipulation arm is active, and its joints are unlocked. A fifth-order polynomial spline is used to generate smooth reference trajectories for the manipulator's joint angles and velocities.
 - **Constraints**: This OCP builds upon Phase A's constraints by incorporating the full momentum-coupled dynamics (linking base and arm accelerations to both RW and arm torques), actuation limits for manipulator joint torques, state limits for joint angles and velocities, and terminal constraints for joint states in addition to attitude and angular velocity.

**Implementation and Evaluation:**
- **Solver**: The nonlinear MPC problems are solved using `acados`, an open-source framework designed for fast embedded optimal control. `acados` uses Gauss-Newton for Hessian approximation, merit-function backtracking for globalization, SQP for the nonlinear solver with Implicit Runge-Kutta (IRK) integration for dynamics discretization, and HPIPM for the QP solver.
- **Comparative Analysis**: The performance of the MPC controller is evaluated through numerical Monte Carlo (MC) simulations and compared against a PID controller baseline, which is expanded from prior work. The PID controller's gains are tuned using the Ziegler-Nichols method.
- **Case Studies**: Three distinct case studies are performed:
 - **Case Study A**: Nominal setting with time-invariant terminal joint angles and velocities.
 - **Case Study B**: Time-varying terminal joint angles and velocities to simulate updated contact points.
 - **Case Study C**: Performance evaluation under observation and actuation noise, modeled as state-dependent and control-dependent Gaussian noise, respectively, and parameter uncertainties (10% Gaussian uncertainty on physical parameters).
- **Performance Metrics**: Key metrics include Constraint Violation (CV), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for various states (relative quaternion, servicer angular velocity, end-effector position/velocity, joint angle/velocity), Average Computation Time ($\bar{t}_{comp}$), and mission success rate (tracking state divergence and convergence criteria).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation involved comparing the proposed nonlinear MPC controller against a PID baseline across three distinct case studies through 50 randomized Monte Carlo (MC) trials.

**Constraint Violation (CV):**
- The MPC controller demonstrated a notable advantage by achieving 0% constraint violations across all successful simulation runs in all three case studies. This indicates its effectiveness in respecting operational safety limits.
- In contrast, the PID controller exhibited constraint violations, with rates of 0.4% in Case A (nominal), 8.03% in Case B (time-varying trajectory), and 26.41% in Case C (noise). This suggests that the PID approach did not consistently maintain operation within specified constraints.

**Tracking Error (RMSE):**
- **Relative Orientation Quaternion (𝒒 rel,RMSE):** MPC achieved lower errors in Case A (0.0087 vs. 0.031) and Case B (0.016 vs. 0.077). However, in Case C (noise), PID demonstrated a lower error (0.269 vs. 0.81), indicating a specific area for potential improvement in MPC's noisy orientation tracking.
- **Servicer Angular Velocity (𝝎 𝑩,RMSE):** MPC consistently outperformed PID, achieving significantly lower errors across all cases: 0.00084 vs. 0.0073 in Case A, 0.0033 vs. 0.018 in Case B, and 0.054 vs. 0.058 in Case C.
- **End-Effector Position (𝒑 𝒆𝒆,RMSE):** MPC showed lower errors in all cases: 0.28 vs. 0.33 in Case A, 0.38 vs. 0.41 in Case B, and 0.30 vs. 0.95 in Case C.
- **End-Effector Velocity (𝒗 𝒆𝒆,RMSE):** MPC also exhibited lower errors in all scenarios: 0.0096 vs. 0.012 in Case A, 0.07 vs. 0.14 in Case B, and 0.02 vs. 0.41 in Case C.
- For the manipulation module, the MPC controller generally demonstrated superior tracking capabilities for both joint angles and velocities, particularly when tracking time-varying trajectories (Case B) and under noisy conditions (Case C).

**Mission Success Rate:**
- MPC exhibited higher mission reliability. Its success rates were 86% in Case A, 92% in Case B, and 60% in Case C. Failures for MPC were primarily attributed to OCP solver failures.
- The PID controller had lower success rates: 64% in Case A, 80% in Case B, and 10% in Case C. PID failures were predominantly due to state-divergence issues, signifying a lack of robustness and control stability under various conditions, especially noise.

**Average Computation Time ( ¯ 𝑡comp):**
- A notable difference was in computational demand. The PID controller consistently required substantially less computation time per control cycle (approximately 0.018-0.019 seconds).
- The MPC controller required significantly more computation time (1.0 second in Case A, 0.72 seconds in Case B, and 0.49 seconds in Case C), posing a challenge for real-time onboard implementation.

**Torque Expenditure:**
- Under noisy conditions (Case C), the MPC controller generally expended less torque than the PID controller, suggesting a potentially more efficient or smoother control output in disturbed environments.

In summary, the MPC controller generally provided superior performance in terms of tracking accuracy for most states, successfully enforced operational constraints, and demonstrated higher mission success rates and greater robustness to uncertainties and noise. Its main disadvantage was a higher computational load compared to the PID baseline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of on-orbit robotics by developing and evaluating a nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for complex on-orbit servicing (OOS) maneuvers. The primary significance of this work lies in its explicit integration of momentum-coupled dynamics and operational constraints, which are critical for safe and reliable contact operations with free-spinning satellites.

The **significance** of the findings includes:

- **Enhanced Operational Safety**: The demonstrated ability of the MPC controller to achieve 0% constraint violations across various challenging scenarios represents a fundamental advancement over prior control approaches. This capability is paramount for OOS, where exceeding actuation limits or state bounds can lead to mission failure or damage to orbital assets.
- **Improved Control Precision and Robustness**: The MPC framework showed superior performance in tracking accuracy (RMSE) for most critical states, including servicer angular velocity and end-effector position and velocity. Its higher mission success rates and robustness against dynamics uncertainty and external noise (observation and actuation noise) indicate a more reliable control solution for autonomous space robotics.
- **Explicit Handling of Coupled Dynamics**: The work's explicit modeling of cross-coupling dynamics between the servicer's moment-generation and manipulation modules allows for integrated control, which is essential for free-flying systems where manipulator movements directly affect the base's attitude. This contrasts with simpler control architectures that might treat these effects as disturbances.
- **Foundation for Complex OOS Tasks**: By providing a robust and constraint-aware control method for zero-impulse contact and spin synchronization, this research lays a stronger foundation for the autonomous execution of advanced OOS tasks such as refueling, repair, debris removal, and in-orbit assembly.

The **potential impact** of this work is substantial for the future of space exploration and satellite maintenance:

- **Increased Feasibility of OOS Missions**: The robust and safe control capabilities offered by MPC can increase confidence in planning and executing complex OOS missions, potentially extending the lifespan of valuable satellites and enabling new types of in-orbit operations.
- **Reduced Risk of Space Debris**: More reliable contact and detumbling procedures for non-cooperative satellites can contribute to debris mitigation efforts, reducing the risk of collisions in increasingly congested orbital environments.
- **Advancements in Autonomous Space Robotics**: The framework contributes to the development of more autonomous and intelligent space robots, reducing the need for direct human intervention and expanding the scope of feasible robotic operations in space.

Despite its advantages, the research identifies a key limitation: the higher computational demands of MPC compared to traditional PID controllers. This increased complexity presents challenges for onboard implementation on resource-constrained satellite platforms. Therefore, future work is suggested to focus on:

- **Computational Tractability**: Investigating decomposition strategies or more efficient solution methods to reduce the real-time computational requirements of the MPC framework.
- **Hardware Validation**: Conducting experimental hardware validation to assess the real-time feasibility and performance of the MPC controller in a physical, rather than purely simulated, environment.
- **Further Refinement**: Addressing specific performance gaps, such as the observed higher relative orientation quaternion error under noisy conditions for MPC compared to PID in one case study.

Overall, this research represents a step towards enhancing the safety, precision, and autonomy of space robotics, with implications for a wide range of OOS applications.
