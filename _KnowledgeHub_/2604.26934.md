# World2VLM: Distilling World Model Imagination into VLMs for Dynamic Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The World2VLM framework enables Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to internalize dynamic spatial reasoning by distilling spatial imagination from generative world models during training. This approach equips VLMs with the ability to understand how scenes transform under egocentric motion, achieving average performance gains of over 15 percentage points on various spatial reasoning benchmarks compared to a base VLM.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) excel at static visual comprehension but struggle with dynamic spatial reasoning, such as understanding scene evolution under egocentric motion or predicting action consequences.
- Existing approaches, like static spatial supervision or inference-time coupling with external world models, are either insufficient for dynamic reasoning or introduce substantial computational overhead during deployment.
- VLMs lack an intrinsic capability to 'mentally simulate' viewpoint shifts and multi-step spatial transformations, often relying on pattern matching rather than inferring underlying spatial dynamics.

## Method

- **World2VLM** distills ==spatial imagination== from a generative world model into a VLM during training — treating the world model as a ==training-time teacher== that synthesizes motion-conditioned view transitions.
- A ==bidirectional task suite== supervises both ==inverse spatial reasoning== (inferring actions from view changes) and ==forward spatial reasoning== (predicting view changes from actions).
- A two-stage post-training recipe combines ==supervised distillation== with ==GRPO== for accurate, structured spatial outputs.

## Results

- **World2VLM-GRPO** outperformed the base VLM across four spatial reasoning benchmarks with **+15.44 to +15.98 pp** average gains.
- Substantially surpassed a MindJourney-style test-time world-model-coupled baseline while eliminating inference-time image generation overhead.
- Ablations confirm bidirectional distillation benefits; mixing real and simulated scene data improves average performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative world models can effectively serve as training-time teachers to instill dynamic spatial reasoning capabilities into VLMs, enabling internal imagination rather than relying on external inference-time calls.
> - A bidirectional training approach, encompassing both inverse (motion inference) and forward (consequence prediction) spatial tasks, is crucial for developing a robust VLM representation that supports both explaining and predicting scene dynamics.
> - The combination of supervised distillation with a subsequent reinforcement learning refinement stage (GRPO) significantly enhances the VLM's ability to produce precise and structured spatial reasoning outputs, including numerical values and ordered sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated proficiency in tasks involving static visual comprehension, such as visual question answering and image captioning. However, a recognized limitation of current VLMs is their capacity for dynamic spatial reasoning. This involves understanding how scenes evolve under egocentric motion, predicting action consequences, or mentally simulating unseen viewpoints. This limitation is particularly evident in scenarios requiring perspective shifts, multi-step transformations, and action-conditioned reasoning, where VLMs may rely on pattern matching rather than inferring underlying spatial dynamics.

Existing approaches to address this challenge typically fall into two categories. The first involves scaling spatial supervision through synthetic data, simulations, and expanded benchmarks. While these efforts contribute to data availability, they often provide static supervision signals and do not explicitly model how observations change in response to actions. The second approach integrates VLMs with external world models or imagination-based reasoning procedures during inference. This can enhance dynamic spatial reasoning but introduces significant computational overhead at deployment and does not inherently modify the VLM's internal capabilities.

This research introduces World2VLM as an alternative, proposing that a generative world model can function as a training-time teacher to distill spatial imagination directly into a VLM. This approach contrasts with inference-time coupling by internalizing dynamic reasoning capabilities into the VLM's parameters, aiming to mitigate the computational costs associated with external model calls during deployment while enhancing the VLM's intrinsic spatial understanding. The work contributes to the development of more capable and efficient multimodal models for embodied AI, navigation, and robotic manipulation, where dynamic spatial reasoning is a fundamental requirement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the dynamic spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by enabling them to internalize how scenes change under egocentric motion.

The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current VLMs:
- **Struggle with Dynamic Spatial Reasoning:** While VLMs excel at understanding static images, they generally lack the ability to imagine or predict how a scene will transform when the camera (or an embodied agent) moves. This "mental simulation" deficiency prevents them from effectively reasoning about perspective shifts, action consequences, or multi-step spatial transformations.
- **Inadequacy of Current Solutions:**
 - **Static Supervision:** Existing methods that scale static spatial supervision (e.g., using synthetic 3D data) often fail to explicitly model action-conditioned state transitions. This means VLMs trained on such data might learn object recognition or static spatial relations but not the dynamics of how these relations change with movement.
 - **Inference-Time Coupling with World Models:** Coupling VLMs with world models during inference, as seen in some prior works, can improve dynamic reasoning by generating imagined views. However, this approach introduces substantial computational overhead during deployment, as the VLM needs to repeatedly query an external generative model. Furthermore, it leaves the core VLM largely unchanged, treating the world model as an external tool rather than integrating its capabilities.
- **Need for Internalized Imagination:** The authors propose that for VLMs to genuinely perform dynamic spatial reasoning efficiently, they must internalize the understanding of motion-conditioned state transitions. This leads to the central question motivating the work: "Can a world model serve as a training-time teacher that distills spatial imagination into a vision-language model?"

World2VLM aims to address these issues by:
- **Distilling World Model Knowledge:** Leveraging generative world models, which are adept at synthesizing view transitions based on actions, to create structured supervision data.
- **Enabling Efficient Dynamic Reasoning:** Training VLMs with this specialized supervision to directly internalize dynamic spatial priors, thereby eliminating the need for expensive inference-time world model calls.
- **Developing a Bidirectional Reasoning Framework:** Designing a task suite that trains the VLM in both *inverse spatial reasoning* (inferring actions from observed view changes) and *forward spatial reasoning* (predicting view changes from given actions), fostering a comprehensive understanding of motion and its consequences.
- **Improving Robustness and Accuracy:** Through a two-stage training recipe involving supervised distillation and reinforcement learning refinement, the framework seeks to make the VLM's dynamic spatial outputs more accurate, numerically precise, and robust.

By achieving these objectives, World2VLM seeks to equip VLMs with an intrinsic capability for dynamic spatial reasoning, making them more suitable for real-world embodied applications that demand an understanding of how environments evolve with interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

World2VLM is a training framework designed to distill spatial imagination from a generative world model into a vision-language model (VLM). The approach involves treating the world model as a training-time teacher to synthesize view transitions under egocentric motion, convert these transitions into structured reasoning tasks, and then train a VLM to internalize these spatial regularities. The method comprises three main components: world-model-guided transition construction, a bidirectional task suite, and a two-stage post-training recipe.

#### 3.1. World-Model-Guided Transition Construction

This component generates motion-conditioned source-target view pairs with explicit action semantics.
- **Trajectory-Conditioned View Synthesis:** The process begins with an anchor RGB observation ($s_t$) and a sampled egocentric camera motion ($a_t$). Egocentric actions include translations (forward, backward, shift-left, shift-right), rotations (turn-left, turn-right), and short multi-step compositions. A view-consistent world model then synthesizes a future observation ($s_{WM_{t+1}}$) aligned with the specified action. The study utilizes two types of world models: Stable Virtual Camera (SVC), a diffusion-based novel-view synthesis model specializing in camera-conditioned view generation, and HY-WorldPlay, an action-conditioned interactive video world model.
- **Max-Displacement Pairing:** To maximize the geometric information and challenge the VLM with non-trivial spatial transformations, training pairs are constructed using the anchor frame and the farthest valid synthesized endpoint in the trajectory, rather than adjacent views.
- **Spatial Anchoring:** For tasks requiring object-level reasoning, synthesized transitions are augmented with object correspondences. A detector-tracker identifies and tracks objects across views. Bounding boxes ($B_t, B_{t+\Delta}$) are normalized to integer coordinates and serialized into the text prompt rather than rendered on the image. This provides object-level anchors connecting motion to object displacement, visibility changes, and cross-view identity, intended to prevent shortcut learning based solely on language priors.

#### 3.2. Task Suite for Spatial Internalization

The generated view transitions are converted into a set of complementary spatial reasoning tasks. This suite aims to provide supervision for both motion inference and action-conditioned consequence prediction. The tasks are categorized as follows:
- **Motion Reasoning Tasks (Inverse):**
 - **Motion Distance Estimation:** Inferring translational distance from two views.
 - **Motion Orientation Estimation:** Inferring turning direction and magnitude from two views.
 - **Multi-step Motion Prediction:** Reconstructing an ordered sequence of actions that connects two views.
 - **Object-Guided Motion Reasoning / Viewpoint Transformation Inference:** Inferring camera action sequences based on cross-view object correspondences.
- **Object Grounding and Consistency Tasks (Forward/Verification):**
 - **Action Verification:** Verifying if a candidate motion description matches an image pair.
 - **Post-Action Object Bounding Box Prediction:** Predicting an object's bounding box after a specified action.
 - **Post-Action Object Visibility Detection:** Determining if an object remains visible after an action.
 - **Cross-View Object Consistency Judgment:** Judging if two boxes in different views refer to the same physical object under a given action.

This bidirectional design is intended to force the VLM to bind actions, viewpoint changes, and object-level geometry into a unified spatial representation, supporting both explanation (inverse tasks) and prediction (forward tasks). The training dataset, built from ScanNet and MulSeT images, comprises approximately 100K samples for supervised fine-tuning.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Post-Training Recipe

The VLM is post-trained using a two-stage approach:
- **Stage I: Supervised Distillation (SFT) with Bidirectional Spatial Supervision:**
 - The VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) undergoes supervised fine-tuning using the constructed dataset.
 - Inverse tasks train the model to infer motion from a pair of observations (P($a_t$ | $s_t$, $s_{t+1}$)).
 - Forward tasks train the model to predict spatial consequences given an initial observation and an action (P(y | $s_t$, $a_t$)).
 - A joint supervised objective ($L_{SFT} = \lambda_{inv}L_{inv} + \lambda_{fwd}L_{fwd}$) is used. This bidirectional training is central to distilling world-model imagination, as it imposes complementary constraints on the same action-conditioned transition. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is used for parameter-efficient adaptation, keeping the visual tower frozen.
- **Stage II: Task-Aware GRPO Refinement:**
 - Following SFT, the model is further refined using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on a smaller, balanced subset of 1K examples.
 - The GRPO stage aims to sharpen structured spatial outputs. Responses are rewarded based on a task-aware function $r(\hat{y}, y^\star)$ that considers:
 - $r_{fmt}$: Valid structured output format.
 - $r_{sem}$: Semantic correctness of the answer.
 - $r_{num}$: Numeric accuracy (e.g., for distances or angles).
 - $r_{geo}$: Localization quality for bounding box predictions.
 - $r_{ord}$: Sequence consistency for multi-step motion reasoning.
 - Penalties are applied for overly long or malformed responses. GRPO optimizes the policy by maximizing expected rewards while regularizing against divergence from the SFT policy. This stage calibrates decision boundaries for precise outputs like numerical values and ordered action sequences.

After training, the World2VLM model performs dynamic spatial reasoning directly during inference, without requiring external world model calls or image generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of World2VLM was conducted on four spatial reasoning benchmarks: SAT-Real, SAT-Synthesized, VSI-Bench, and MindCube, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the base VLM.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Consistent Improvements:** World2VLM-GRPO consistently delivered improvements over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model across all four benchmarks. With SVC as the teacher, World2VLM-GRPO achieved an average gain of +15.44 percentage points, and with HY-WorldPlay, an average gain of +15.98 percentage points.
- **Outperforming Test-Time Coupling:** World2VLM variants (both SFT and GRPO stages) substantially outperformed a MindJourney-style test-time world-model-coupled baseline. The test-time baseline showed a modest average gain of +2.02 percentage points but also performance degradation on SAT-Real (-13.34 points). World2VLM achieved higher accuracy while eliminating the computational overhead of inference-time image generation.
- **Benefits of GRPO:** The GRPO refinement stage consistently improved performance over the SFT-only checkpoints, demonstrating its role in sharpening structured spatial outputs. For instance, World2VLM-GRPO (SVC) improved from 46.75% to 52.07% average accuracy over World2VLM-SFT (SVC).
- **Teacher Model Efficacy:** The framework was effective with both SVC and HY-WorldPlay as teacher world models. SVC showed stronger performance on benchmarks sensitive to camera-conditioned viewpoint consistency (SAT-Real, VSI-Bench), while HY-WorldPlay was more effective for synthetic dynamics and mental simulation (SAT-Synthesized, MindCube), suggesting the framework's adaptability across different world-model priors.

#### 4.2. Data Scaling Effects

- Performance on the benchmarks improved rapidly in the low-data regime (0-20K samples) of world-model-generated SFT data, showing diminishing returns as data quantity increased up to 100K samples. This indicates that world-model-generated transition supervision provides scalable benefits, particularly for tasks requiring mental propagation of scene changes.

#### 4.3. SAT-Real Task Breakdown

- Analysis on SAT-Real revealed that World2VLM's gains were concentrated on dynamic categories. World2VLM-SFT (SVC) already improved egocentric movement (+21.74), object movement (+21.74), goal aim (+29.41), and action consequence (+29.73), though perspective taking initially showed a slight decrease.
- The GRPO stage further broadened these improvements, notably lifting perspective taking from below to above the base model performance (+9.09). This indicates that GRPO contributed to better-calibrated structured reasoning in addition to transition-based supervision.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Bidirectional Distillation:** Training with both inverse and forward spatial reasoning tasks yielded the strongest overall results. Forward-only distillation was stronger on predicting action consequences (e.g., SAT-Synthesized), while inverse-only distillation was stronger on inferring motion from view changes (e.g., SAT-Real). Their combination was complementary, encouraging a spatial representation that supports both explanation and prediction.
- **Effect of Training Source Composition:** Mixing real-scene data (ScanNet) and simulated-scene data (MulSeT) for training resulted in better average performance than using either source exclusively. Real scenes provided exposure to clutter and appearance variation, while simulated scenes offered cleaner, controllable transitions, suggesting complementary benefits for robustness across real and synthetic evaluation settings.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis of GRPO Improvements

- GRPO primarily refined the model's output layer knowledge. It improved numeric precision (e.g., depth estimation, size estimation), enhanced discrete decision consistency (e.g., object counting, directional judgments), and regularized response formatting to align with task-specific schemas, demonstrating its role in sharpening knowledge expression.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The World2VLM framework represents a notable contribution to the field of vision-language models and embodied AI by proposing a novel paradigm for endowing VLMs with dynamic spatial reasoning capabilities.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in World Model Utilization

Traditionally, generative world models have been considered tools for inference-time imagination or planning. World2VLM shifts this perspective by demonstrating that world models can serve as effective *training-time teachers*. This re-framing opens up new avenues for leveraging the advanced generative capabilities of world models to enhance the foundational reasoning abilities of VLMs.

#### 5.2. Enhanced VLM Capabilities for Dynamic Reasoning

The core significance lies in enabling VLMs to internalize an understanding of how scenes change under egocentric actions. This capability is crucial for tasks requiring:
- **Mental Simulation:** The ability to "imagine" future views or states after an action, without needing to actually generate those images at test time.
- **Action-Conditioned Prediction:** Accurately predicting the consequences of an agent's actions on objects, their visibility, and spatial relationships.
- **Egocentric Motion Understanding:** Comprehending and explaining observed view transitions in terms of camera movements.

#### 5.3. Improved Efficiency and Scalability

By distilling spatial imagination into the VLM's parameters during post-training, World2VLM eliminates the need for expensive, computationally intensive inference-time calls to generative world models. This results in a more efficient deployment model, as the VLM can perform dynamic spatial reasoning with standard, faster inference. Furthermore, the use of world models for generating synthetic supervision data offers a scalable method to acquire diverse and complex motion-conditioned transitions, bypassing the challenges and costs of collecting large-scale human-annotated dynamic spatial reasoning datasets.

#### 5.4. Robustness and Generalization

The bidirectional task suite and two-stage training recipe (SFT then GRPO) contribute to a more robust internalization of spatial priors. Training on both inverse (explanation) and forward (prediction) reasoning tasks, combined with data from both real and simulated scenes, enhances the model's ability to generalize across different settings and types of spatial queries. The GRPO stage further refines the precision and format of the VLM's spatial outputs, improving their practical utility.

#### 5.5. Potential Impact on Embodied AI and Robotics

The enhanced dynamic spatial reasoning capabilities of World2VLM have significant implications for embodied AI agents and robotics. Agents equipped with such VLMs could more effectively:
- **Navigate Complex Environments:** Better understand how their movements affect their perception of the surroundings.
- **Plan Multi-step Actions:** Predict the spatial outcomes of sequences of actions, leading to more informed decision-making.
- **Manipulate Objects:** Reason about how object positions and orientations change with agent and object interactions.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** Understand natural language commands that involve dynamic spatial concepts (e.g., "move forward until you see the object on your left").

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The work also highlights areas for future research, such as developing more explicit teacher reliability modeling to mitigate the propagation of world model generation errors, task-aware teacher selection (e.g., using different world models for different types of spatial tasks), and deeper integration of world model priors into VLM architectures beyond fine-tuning.

In summary, World2VLM provides a compelling demonstration that treating world models as training-time teachers can effectively and efficiently equip VLMs with an internalized capacity for dynamic spatial reasoning, representing a step towards more capable and autonomous embodied AI systems.
