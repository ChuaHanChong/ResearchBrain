# Internalizing World Models via Self-Play Finetuning for Agentic RL

> [!summary] Summary
> A collaborative effort from City University of Hong Kong, National University of Singapore, AI2, and other institutions presents SPA, a reinforcement learning framework that instills an internal world model in Large Language Model (LLM) agents via self-play supervised finetuning. This approach substantially boosts agent performance and generalization in out-of-distribution environments, for instance, raising the Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct's Sokoban success rate (Pass@1) from 25.6% to 59.8%.

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) agents exhibit severe performance degradation and poor generalization in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments due to a lack of understanding of environmental rules.
- Vanilla Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning for LLM agents often leads to narrow, exploitative solution paths (improved Pass@1) but systematically degrades their ability to find diverse solutions or generalize (Pass@k) in OOD settings.
- Current LLM agents tend to rely on memorized trajectories or superficial patterns rather than internalizing robust, explicit world models of environmental dynamics.

## Method

- Introduces **SPA** (Self Play Agent), a two-stage RL framework that first utilizes ==Self-Play Supervised Finetuning (SFT)== for world model cold-start, followed by ==PPO== for policy refinement.
- Decomposes world modeling into ==state estimation== (converting raw observations into structured natural language) and ==transition modeling== (predicting next states given current states and actions).
- Employs a ==self-play data generation== process where the LLM explores and predicts states, but ground-truth environment states replace predictions during SFT to ensure learning from factual environmental feedback.

## Results

- SPA consistently outperforms all baselines, including vanilla RL and VAGEN, across various LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-0.5B to 3B) and OOD environments (Sokoban, FrozenLake, Sudoku).
- Demonstrates enhanced generalization by improving both Pass@1 (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct's Sokoban success rate from **25.6%** to **59.8%**) and Pass@k (e.g., Sokoban Pass@8 from **34.0%** to **69.5%**) in OOD scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirm the vital role of explicit transition modeling supervision, the necessity of ground-truth state information during SFT, and the importance of a prior-aware policy for generating effective self-play trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internalizing an explicit world model via self-play SFT prior to RL training is crucial for LLM agents to achieve robust generalization and improved performance in out-of-distribution environments.
> - The decomposition of world modeling into state representation and transition dynamics, combined with the use of ground-truth data during SFT, provides a stable and effective mechanism for grounding LLMs in environmental rules.
> - Pre-training the world model through SFT avoids multi-objective interference during the RL phase that can hinder learning in online, reward-based world modeling approaches like VAGEN.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and rapidly evolving area at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically focusing on improving the robustness and generalization capabilities of LLM agents in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments.

The broader research landscape for LLM agents has seen rapid advancements, with LLMs being adopted as agents capable of complex tasks such as planning, tool use, and web navigation. These agents typically operate using frameworks like ReAct-style scaffolds or general RL pipelines, employing a "think-then-act" paradigm where they generate actions based on current observations and internal reasoning.

However, a significant limitation of current LLM agents is their performance degradation in OOD scenarios. These are environments that differ substantially from the data distribution encountered during their pre-training, often characterized by complex, dynamic, stochastic, and task-specific rules (e.g., grid worlds like Sokoban, robotic control, web automation). The paper empirically diagnoses this problem, showing that while vanilla RL might marginally improve an agent's success on a single, exploitative path (Pass@1), it often causes a marked *decline* in the ability to find diverse successful solutions or generalize (Pass@k), indicating a fundamental lack of understanding of environmental dynamics. This suggests that current LLM agents tend to rely on memorized trajectories or superficial patterns rather than internalizing the underlying rules of the environment.

This work draws inspiration from classical model-based reinforcement learning, where explicit world models (composed of state representations and transition dynamics) have long been established as crucial for enhanced planning, stronger credit assignment, and improved sample efficiency. The core gap this paper aims to fill is how to effectively and scalably equip LLM agents with such internal world models. Previous attempts in the LLM agent space have largely focused on prompting strategies or online learning methods that integrate world modeling via reward shaping (e.g., VAGEN, Wang et al., 2025a).

This paper positions itself as a novel approach to bridge this gap. Instead of real-time, reward-driven world model learning, it proposes a two-stage framework: a "cold-start" phase where a world model is explicitly learned through self-play supervised finetuning (SFT) *before* the main RL policy optimization. This distinguishes it from online world-modeling methods, arguing that pre-learning the dynamics avoids multi-objective interference during the RL phase, leading to more stable and effective learning.

Therefore, this work contributes to the evolving field of LLM agents by providing a principled and empirically effective method for grounding LLMs in environmental dynamics, thereby improving their generalization and performance in challenging OOD interactive tasks. It represents a significant step towards creating more robust and genuinely "agentic" LLMs that can reason about and adapt to unfamiliar environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observed fragility of Large Language Model (LLM) agents when deployed in environments that are "out-of-distribution" (OOD) relative to their pre-training data. The authors empirically demonstrate a critical failure mode: while vanilla Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning might lead to a marginal increase in the success rate of the agent's highest-probability trajectory (Pass@1), it often causes a systematic *degradation* in the success rate across multiple sampled trajectories (Pass@k). This divergence indicates that LLM agents, under standard RL, develop narrow, exploitative solution paths without acquiring the broader world knowledge necessary for robust generalization in unfamiliar OOD environments. They "get better at one path" but fail to build a comprehensive understanding of how their actions transform the environment.

Against this backdrop, the paper poses a fundamental research question: **"How can we achieve effective and efficient agentic reinforcement learning in OOD environments?"**

The key objectives and motivations driving the SPA framework are:

1. **Address OOD Performance Degradation:** The primary motivation is to overcome the severe performance drop and generalization issues of LLM agents in complex, dynamic, and rule-governed OOD environments (e.g., Sokoban, FrozenLake, Sudoku), where their pre-trained knowledge is insufficient.

2. **Internalize a World Model:** The core hypothesis is that equipping LLM agents with an *internal world model*—an understanding of the environment's underlying rules, current state, and how actions transform it—can better align their reasoning with environmental dynamics. This internal model would allow agents to simulate future states and ground their reasoning in the environment's rules, rather than relying on memorized trajectories or brittle patterns.

3. **Decompose World Modeling:** To make the complex task of internalizing a world model tractable for LLMs, the authors aim to decompose it into two manageable components:
 - **State Representation:** Learning to convert raw, unfamiliar observations into structured, informative, and familiar natural language descriptions of the world state. This is motivated by the observation that LLMs struggle to parse symbolic text strings of states (e.g., grid layouts) and that providing more abstract, natural language descriptions (e.g., coordinates of key elements) significantly reduces perplexity and reasoning complexity.
 - **Transition Modeling:** Learning to predict the next state given the current state and an action. This component aims to imbue the LLM with an understanding of the environment's dynamics, allowing it to foresee the consequences of its actions.

4. **Develop an Effective and Scalable Learning Framework:** The authors aim to introduce a simple, yet effective, reinforcement learning framework that can scalably inject world model knowledge into LLM agents. They specifically propose and motivate *self-play supervised finetuning (SFT)* as a superior vehicle for this knowledge injection, contrasting it with online RL methods that rely on reward shaping. The motivation here is that self-play SFT enables robust exploration of the state and action space, building a strong, reusable world model prior *before* exploitation-focused RL, thereby improving Pass@k.

5. **Improve Both Exploitation (Pass@1) and Generalization (Pass@k):** The ultimate objective is to develop an agent that not only improves its ability to find a single successful path (Pass@1) but, crucially, also enhances its capacity to explore diverse correct solutions and generalize across various scenarios (Pass@k), addressing the systematic divergence observed in vanilla RL.

In essence, the research is motivated by the empirical shortcomings of LLM agents in OOD settings and aims to introduce a foundational mechanism—an internalized world model acquired through a specific self-play SFT process—to make these agents more robust, generalizable, and genuinely intelligent in interacting with dynamic, unfamiliar environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces **SPA (Self Play Agent)**, a novel reinforcement learning framework designed to equip Large Language Model (LLM) agents with an internal world model, thereby enhancing their performance and generalization in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments. The methodology is structured around a two-stage process: a cold-start via Self-Play Supervised Finetuning (SFT) for world model internalization, followed by Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for policy refinement.

#### 3.1. World Model Cold-Start via Self-Play Finetuning (SFT)

The central idea is to strengthen the LLM agent's grounding by explicitly modeling the world prior to extensive policy learning. This stage decomposes world modeling into two interdependent components: state representation and transition dynamics.

**a. Optimizing State Estimation:**
- **Problem:** LLM agents often struggle to process raw, symbolic environment states (e.g., a grid of characters) because these representations are OOD with respect to their pre-training data and make spatial relationships hard to parse. This leads to high perplexity (PPL) for state descriptions, indicating difficulty in understanding.
- **Approach:** The authors propose enriching the raw symbolic state (`s'_t`) with a more structured, informative, and natural language description (`b_t`). For text-based grid games like Sokoban, `b_t` comprises human-readable coordinates of key game components (player, boxes, targets). The combined representation becomes `s_t = Concat(s'_t, b_t)`.
- **Impact:** This approach significantly reduces the perplexity of state representations for OOD environments and, even in isolation, yields modest improvements in RL performance (Pass@1 and Pass@k) compared to vanilla RL. This confirms that better state representations facilitate grounding and reasoning.

**b. Injecting Transition Modeling via Self-Play Fine-Tuning:**
- **Goal:** To teach the LLM agent the environment's dynamics—how actions transform one state into the next—using the interpretable state representations.
- **Self-Play Data Generation:**
 - The base LLM model is used to freely explore and interact with the environment.
 - During interaction, the model is prompted to explicitly articulate its *current state belief* (`s_t`) and *predict the next state* (`s_{t+1}`) within its `<think>` block, alongside its chosen action (`a_t`). The format is: `<think><observation>ˆs_t</observation><prediction>ˆs_{t+1}</prediction></think><answer>ˆa_t</answer>`.
 - **Crucially**, the model's generated `ˆs_t` and `ˆs_{t+1}` are *replaced* with the *ground-truth* states (`s_t` and `s_{t+1}`) obtained from the environment after executing `ˆa_t`. This ensures the model learns from factual environmental feedback, not its own potentially hallucinated beliefs.
 - This process generates trajectories of `(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})` triples, augmented with the explicit state and next-state descriptions.
- **World Modeling Training (Supervised Finetuning - SFT):**
 - A masked token-level cross-entropy loss (`L_W(θ)`) is applied to these ground-truth-augmented trajectories. The loss specifically targets the tokens within the `<think>` and `<answer>` spans, enabling the model to learn to generate effective next states and actions. Tokens corresponding to the environment's raw observations are masked out from the loss calculation.
 - **Outcome:** This SFT stage trains the LLM to internalize the environment's dynamics, effectively building a robust world model that predicts how actions lead to state transitions. The resulting SFT-tuned model serves as a strong initialization ("cold start") for the subsequent RL phase.

#### 3.2. RL Training

- **Initialization:** The policy model is initialized with the checkpoint obtained from the self-play SFT stage, which now incorporates the internalized world model.
- **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) is used for reinforcement learning.
- **State Representation:** During RL training, the optimized state representation (`s_t` from Section 2.1) is used to encode world knowledge, maintaining consistency with the world model's understanding.
- **Objective:** The PPO loss (`J_PPO(θ)`) focuses on optimizing the policy to maximize task-specific rewards, with a mask applied to ensure only answer tokens contribute to the loss. This stage refines the agent's ability to act strategically based on its learned world model.

This two-stage approach leverages the strengths of supervised learning for robust world model acquisition (using ground-truth data) and reinforcement learning for policy optimization, aiming to overcome the OOD challenges faced by vanilla LLM agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SPA significantly improves the performance and generalization of LLM agents in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments. The key findings are:

1. **Superior Performance Across Models and Environments:** SPA consistently outperforms all baselines, including vanilla RL, State Estimation RL, and VAGEN, across various LLM sizes (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B, LLaMA3.2-1B-Instruct) and OOD environments (Sokoban, FrozenLake, Sudoku).
 - For example, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct's Sokoban success rate (Pass@1) jumped from 25.6% (vanilla RL) to 59.8% with SPA, and FrozenLake success rate from 22.1% to 70.9%. This often allowed smaller models trained with SPA to even outperform larger, state-of-the-art models like GPT-OSS-20B on some metrics.

2. **Enhanced Generalization (Pass@k):** Unlike vanilla RL, which showed a degradation of Pass@k in OOD environments, SPA consistently improves both Pass@1 and Pass@k. This indicates that SPA agents acquire a broader understanding of environmental dynamics, enabling them to find diverse successful solutions, not just single memorized paths. For instance, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct's Sokoban Pass@8 improved from 34.0% (vanilla RL) to 69.5% with SPA.

3. **Advantage over Reward-Based World Modeling (VAGEN):** SPA significantly outperforms VAGEN, a method that performs online world modeling via reward shaping. The authors hypothesize this is because SPA's pre-training of the world model via SFT avoids "multi-objective interference" during RL training, leading to a more stable and effective learning process.

4. **Crucial Role of Transition Modeling:** Ablation studies confirm that explicit transition (world modeling) supervision during SFT is vital. When the SFT loss on current and next states is masked out, no performance improvement is observed, demonstrating that learning environmental dynamics is key to SPA's success.

5. **Necessity of Ground-Truth States:** Using only the model's self-belief states (without ground-truth supervision) during SFT degrades RL performance, underscoring that accurate, ground-truth world modeling is essential. Furthermore, supplying randomized (incorrect) coordinates in the state representation during RL training causes performance to collapse, highlighting the indispensable role of accurate spatial grounding and credit assignment provided by explicit coordinates.

6. **Importance of Initial Policy for Exploration:** World-modeling trajectories generated by a random-action policy yield substantially worse downstream RL performance compared to those generated by a prior-aware policy. This suggests that even a weak, but goal-directed, policy for self-play data collection is crucial for effective world model training.

7. **Impact of Data Quality and Extended SFT:**
 - Filtering out SFT samples that deviate from the required output format improves training stability and yields better Pass@k and Pass@1, emphasizing the importance of strict format adherence for learning accurate state and transition structures.
 - Extending the SFT training duration (e.g., from 1 to 5 epochs) consistently improves subsequent RL performance (higher Pass@1, Pass@k, shorter trajectories), indicating that a stronger, more refined world-model prior leads to better and more efficient RL.

8. **Exploration-then-Exploitation Dynamics:** Unlike prior single-turn RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in the math domain, SPA's multi-turn RL training exhibits a distinct trajectory: Pass@k consistently rises in early training phases (exploration) and only decreases during later exploitation phases. This signifies a fundamental shift in how LLM agents learn in interactive environments.

9. **Easy-to-Hard Transfer Gains:** World models learned in simpler environments (e.g., 4x4 FrozenLake) can be successfully transferred to accelerate RL training in more complex variants (e.g., 6x6 FrozenLake). This demonstrates that learned world dynamics serve as a reusable prior that generalizes to increased task complexity within the same environment family.

10. **Generalization Limits:** While SPA shows strong cross-complexity generalization within the same game (e.g., simple to complex Sokoban), it fails to generalize across games with fundamentally different dynamics (e.g., Sokoban to FrozenLake), highlighting a current limitation.

In summary, the results strongly support the effectiveness of the SPA framework, demonstrating that internalizing a world model via self-play SFT with ground-truth data, combined with optimized state representations, is a powerful approach for developing robust and generalizable LLM agents in challenging OOD scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "INTERNALIZING WORLD MODELS VIA SELF-PLAY FINETUNING FOR AGENTIC RL" carries significant implications for the development of more capable and robust Large Language Model (LLM) agents, with potential impacts across several domains.

1. **Addressing a Core Limitation of LLM Agents:** The primary significance lies in directly tackling a fundamental limitation of current LLM agents: their poor performance and generalization in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments. By demonstrating a method to enable LLMs to internalize a "world model" – an understanding of environmental dynamics and state transitions – this work moves LLM agents beyond brittle reliance on pre-trained distributions or memorized trajectories. This is crucial for their deployment in real-world interactive scenarios, which are inherently dynamic, stochastic, and often unfamiliar.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The observed simultaneous improvement in both Pass@1 (exploitative success) and Pass@k (exploratory success and generalization) is a major breakthrough. It signifies that SPA agents develop a broader, more robust understanding of the environment, allowing them to explore diverse solution paths and adapt to varied situations, rather than merely optimizing for a single, narrow solution. This directly contributes to building more reliable and resilient AI agents.

3. **Bridging Classical and LLM-based RL:** The paper effectively bridges insights from classical model-based reinforcement learning with the LLM paradigm. It demonstrates that the long-established benefits of explicit world models—such as stronger credit assignment, more effective planning, and improved sample efficiency—can be successfully adapted and applied to language-based agents. This offers a principled direction for future research in integrating powerful model-based reasoning into LLMs.

4. **A Reproducible Backbone for Model-Based LLM Agents:** SPA provides a "minimal and reproducible backbone" for training model-based LLM agents. Its two-stage approach (SFT-based world model pre-training followed by PPO) offers a clear, stable, and effective methodology that can serve as a foundation for further research. This structured approach contrasts with more ad-hoc prompting strategies or complex online learning schemes that may be less stable.

5. **Improved Sample Efficiency and Training Stability:** The "cold start" initialization from a strong world model learned via SFT likely contributes to improved sample efficiency during the subsequent RL phase. By internalizing core environmental dynamics beforehand, the RL agent can focus on policy optimization with a more accurate prior, leading to faster learning and potentially reducing the vast data requirements typically associated with deep RL. The avoidance of multi-objective interference, as argued in the paper, also contributes to greater training stability.

6. **Enabling Easy-to-Hard Transfer Learning:** The finding that world models learned in simpler settings can accelerate RL training in more complex variants within the same environment family is highly impactful. This suggests a pathway for progressive learning, where agents can build foundational understandings in easier tasks and then efficiently transfer this knowledge to tackle more challenging problems, making the training of complex agents more feasible.

7. **Potential for Broader Applications:** The ability of LLM agents to robustly operate in OOD environments opens doors for their application in a wider array of real-world interactive domains:
 - **Robotics:** Where real-world dynamics are complex, partially observable, and constantly changing.
 - **Complex Game Playing:** Beyond simple text games, to more intricate strategic environments.
 - **Web Automation:** For tasks requiring flexible navigation and interaction with diverse and dynamic web interfaces.
 - **Scientific Discovery:** Where agents might need to interact with novel experimental setups or simulate complex systems.

In conclusion, SPA represents a significant step forward in the quest to create truly intelligent and adaptable LLM agents. By equipping them with internal world models, this research moves beyond mere pattern matching and toward a more profound understanding of how actions influence states, thereby laying the groundwork for more autonomous, generalizable, and robust AI systems.
