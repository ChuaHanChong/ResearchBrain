# ACQUIRED: A Dataset for Answering Counterfactual Questions In Real-Life Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> The ACQUIRED dataset provides a benchmark for evaluating multimodal counterfactual reasoning in diverse, real-life video scenarios, featuring 3.7K videos and over 11K question-answer pairs across physical, social, and temporal dimensions. Benchmarking reveals a substantial performance gap, with human accuracy surpassing state-of-the-art models by over 13% in both MCQ and pairwise true/false settings.

## Problem

- Existing counterfactual reasoning datasets for AI are limited, relying on synthetic videos or narrow real-world domains like traffic accidents.
- Current benchmarks often lack diversity in event types, viewpoints (first-person vs. third-person), and comprehensive coverage of commonsense reasoning dimensions (physical, social, temporal).
- AI systems struggle with understanding causality, learning from hypothetical scenarios, and making informed predictions based on 'what-if' questions, which is crucial for advanced intelligence.

## Method

- Developed **ACQUIRED**, a video question answering dataset that pairs real-life videos (from Oops! and Ego4D) with natural language ==counterfactual== questions and carefully crafted answer pairs.
- Employed a four-step ==model-in-the-loop crowdsourcing== approach, incentivizing annotators to create challenging questions by rewarding those that 'fooled' a continuously finetuned text-only QA model.
- Designed questions to cover three key ==commonsense reasoning== dimensions (physical, social, temporal) and ensured diverse event types and viewpoints (first-person and third-person).

## Results

- The ACQUIRED dataset contains **3.7K** real-life videos and over **11K** question-answer pairs, exhibiting a high diversity of event types and balanced representation across physical, social, and temporal dimensions for third-person videos.
- Human performance reached **92.59%** accuracy in the Multiple Choice Question (MCQ) setting and **77.78%** in the challenging pairwise True/False setting, establishing a robust upper bound for AI systems.
- The best-performing fine-tuned models achieved **73.40%** MCQ accuracy (UnifiedQA-3B) and **71.53%** MCQ accuracy (VL-Adapter, multimodal), demonstrating a substantial performance gap of over **13%** compared to human capabilities, with large language models performing even lower.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The creation of ACQUIRED provides the first comprehensive benchmark for multimodal counterfactual reasoning on diverse real-life video content, incorporating varied viewpoints and covering physical, social, and temporal commonsense dimensions.
> - A significant performance disparity exists between human capabilities and state-of-the-art AI models, with humans outperforming models by over 13% in counterfactual reasoning tasks on the ACQUIRED dataset.
> - Current multimodal models show only marginal improvements over strong text-only baselines and struggle with temporal reasoning, indicating that effectively leveraging visual information for deep counterfactual inference remains an open challenge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a specific area within multimodal artificial intelligence: counterfactual reasoning, particularly in the context of real-life video events. Multimodal counterfactual reasoning refers to an AI system's ability to infer what might have happened if certain conditions were different from what actually occurred, based on both visual and linguistic inputs. This capacity is considered crucial for advanced AI, as it enables models to understand causality, learn from hypothetical scenarios and failures, and make informed predictions.

The broader research landscape includes Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets, which typically involve answering questions about images or videos using natural language. Early VQA datasets like VQA (Antol et al., 2015), CLEVR (Johnson et al., 2017), and GQA (Hudson and Manning, 2019) focused on general image-based reasoning or synthetic environments. Subsequently, video question answering datasets such as VLEP (Lei et al., 2020), MovieQA (Tapaswi et al., 2016), and NExT-QA (Xiao et al., 2021) emerged, expanding the scope to dynamic visual content and various reasoning skills.

Within this landscape, the sub-field of counterfactual reasoning datasets for AI is less developed. Existing benchmarks that target counterfactual abilities exhibit limitations. CLEVRER (Yi et al., 2020), for instance, utilizes synthetically generated videos in controlled environments, which limits its applicability to real-world scenarios due to a domain gap. TrafficQA (Xu et al., 2021) addresses real-world events but focuses exclusively on traffic accidents, lacking diversity across other types of everyday occurrences. These limitations mean that existing datasets may not reliably benchmark a model's generalization ability across diverse real-world events or comprehensive reasoning dimensions.

The ACQUIRED dataset aims to fill this gap by providing a benchmark for evaluating counterfactual reasoning in diverse, real-life video contexts. It seeks to move beyond synthetic data and single-domain constraints, offering a more comprehensive assessment tool for the counterfactual reasoning capabilities of multimodal AI models. By introducing a dataset that spans various event types, viewpoints, and reasoning dimensions, this work contributes to the development of AI systems with a more nuanced understanding of causality and hypothetical situations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and introduce ACQUIRED, a novel video question answering dataset specifically designed for evaluating the multimodal counterfactual reasoning abilities of AI models in diverse real-life video scenarios.

The motivation behind this endeavor stems from several key observations regarding the current state of AI research and existing benchmark limitations:

- **Importance of Counterfactual Reasoning for AI:** The authors emphasize that counterfactual reasoning is a vital cognitive ability for human intelligence, enabling understanding of causality, learning from past experiences, and predicting outcomes of hypothetical circumstances. For AI systems, developing this capability is considered a significant and challenging area of research, crucial for enhancing their understanding of causal relationships and assessing the impact of interventions or changes in conditions.
- **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:**
 - **Lack of Real-world Diversity:** Current counterfactual reasoning datasets are primarily limited in scope. For example, CLEVRER utilizes synthetic environments, which restricts its effectiveness in evaluating models' performance in realistic contexts.
 - **Narrow Event Focus:** Datasets like TrafficQA, while addressing real-world scenarios, concentrate on specific event types (e.g., traffic collisions). This narrow focus hinders the reliable benchmarking of a model's generalization ability across the wide spectrum of real-life events.
 - **Incomplete Reasoning Dimensions:** Existing datasets often do not comprehensively cover multiple dimensions of commonsense reasoning that are integral to human counterfactual thought.
- **Need for a Comprehensive Benchmark:** To address these deficiencies, the authors identify a critical need for a benchmark that can:
 - Evaluate models' counterfactual reasoning on a wide array of real-world events.
 - Incorporate diverse visual perspectives, including both first-person (egocentric) and third-person (exocentric) viewpoints, to ensure broader real-world applicability.
 - Span multiple distinct dimensions of commonsense reasoning—specifically physical, social, and temporal—to offer a comprehensive evaluation of counterfactual abilities across various aspects of human experience.
 - Provide challenging questions that require deep understanding beyond superficial patterns, evidenced by the use of carefully curated correct and distractor answer pairs.

By creating ACQUIRED, the researchers aim to provide a robust and diverse benchmark that highlights the current performance gap between AI models and human capabilities in counterfactual reasoning. This, in turn, is intended to inspire and guide future research efforts toward developing more sophisticated multimodal AI models capable of advanced counterfactual inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of the ACQUIRED dataset involved a structured approach to problem definition, data sourcing, annotation, and quality control.

**Problem Definition:**
The dataset defines counterfactual reasoning as a Video Question Answering (VQA) task. Each data point consists of a video, a natural language question posing a "what-if" scenario, and a pair of answers: one correct response and one carefully designed distractor. Both answers are individually judgeable, allowing the dataset to be used in either a binary True/False (T/F) prediction setting or a two-choice Multiple Choice Question (MCQ) setting. The distractors are crafted to be minimally contrastive to the correct answer, requiring models to possess a nuanced understanding of the visual content and counterfactual implications rather than relying on surface-level heuristics.

**Commonsense Dimensions:**
The dataset focuses on three dimensions of commonsense reasoning, inspired by the Theory of Core Knowledge (Spelke and Kinzler, 2007), to systematically evaluate diverse aspects of counterfactual abilities:
- **Physical:** Concerns knowledge about objects, their properties (e.g., shape, size, functionality), motion, and location within events.
- **Social:** Relates to human social behaviors, including personality traits, emotions, intentions, and social interactions. For Ego4D videos, where social interactions are less common, this dimension was not required for annotation.
- **Temporal:** Involves aspects of events and activities concerning their ordering, duration, and frequency or speed of motions.
Annotators were instructed to label each question with the primary commonsense dimension it addressed, even if multiple dimensions might be tangentially involved.

**Video Resources and Sampling:**
The videos for ACQUIRED were sourced from two existing datasets to ensure real-world diversity and different viewpoints:
- **Oops! (Epstein et al., 2020):** Provides third-person viewpoint videos, often featuring "failing moments" of intended actions, which are naturally conducive to generating counterfactual "what-if" questions.
- **Ego4D (Grauman et al., 2022):** Offers first-person (egocentric) viewpoint videos of humans performing daily activities. This source contributes a task-knowledge layer and broadens the types of events.

To ensure a balanced and diverse distribution of event types, a specific sampling strategy was employed:
1. NLP tools, such as Semantic Role Labeling (SRL), were used to extract key verbs (representing events) from the video descriptions (narrations).
2. Videos were grouped based on these extracted event types.
3. During annotation, event groups were sampled with a probability inversely proportional to their current representation in the collected data, aiming to encourage a more uniform distribution of key events.

**Data Collection Workflow (Amazon Mechanical Turk - MTurk):**
The dataset was collected using a four-step model-in-the-loop crowdsourcing protocol via Amazon Mechanical Turk (MTurk):
1. **Qualification:** Workers underwent a questionnaire to assess their understanding of counterfactuality, video relevancy, commonsense dimensions, and the criteria for desirable QA pairs.
2. **Adversarial Game (Model-in-the-Loop):** Qualified workers used an interactive interface to create QA pairs. A pre-trained text-only QA model was deployed in the loop, providing immediate feedback. Workers received bonus monetary rewards if their created QA pairs successfully "fooled" this model (i.e., the model answered incorrectly). This mechanism was intended to incentivize the creation of more challenging and less predictable samples.
3. **Data Quality Validation:** Internal research team members conducted a secondary quality validation of the created samples. They provided customized tips and feedback to workers for improvement, aiming to address common mistakes and align annotations with guidelines.
4. **Iterative Model Finetuning:** The deployed QA model was iteratively finetuned on the validated samples after each batch of annotations. This continuous improvement of the model was designed to keep it challenging for annotators, further encouraging the creation of difficult and diverse samples.

**Quality Validation Analysis:**
Rigorous quality checks were implemented. Internal members performed manual cross-validation of annotations, especially during the initial ramping-up phase. Detailed guidelines were developed from this validation process. The agreement among internal validators, as measured by a Kappa score, was 0.85 for commonsense dimension agreement and an overlapping ratio of 0.91 for video relevancy. The data drop-rates (samples rejected by validators) decreased significantly over the initial batches, from 28% in Batch-1 to 2.6% in Batch-5, indicating an improvement in overall data quality and worker adherence to guidelines.

**Benchmarking Models:**
The dataset was benchmarked against various state-of-the-art models:
- **Language-Only Models:** DeBERTa-v3, UnifiedQA (base, large, 3B), ChatGPT, and GPT-4. These models were evaluated without video input (except for "Desc.-GPT" versions which used video descriptions) to gauge intrinsic dataset biases and establish a textual baseline.
- **Multimodal (Vision-Language) Models:** VIOLET, VALOR, and VL-Adapter (using CLIP-ViT-B/16 and UnifiedQA-Large as encoders). These models process both video and language inputs.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Data Splits:** The dataset was officially split into training, development, and test sets with a 45-5-50% ratio. Crucially, no unique video appeared in more than one split, and videos from Oops! and Ego4D were evenly distributed across splits.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Model performance was measured by simple accuracy for both T/F and MCQ settings. Additionally, a "pairwise accuracy" metric was used for the T/F setting, where a model was considered correct only if it accurately predicted the true/false label for *both* the correct answer and its corresponding distractor answer. This metric aims to assess more robust counterfactual reasoning. Performance was also analyzed based on commonsense dimensions and video viewpoints.
- **Training Details:** Models were trained on multiple Nvidia A100 GPUs. Hyperparameters were manually tuned, and checkpoints were selected based on validation performance.
- **Human Performance:** A human performance baseline was established by randomly sampling and annotating 500 videos from the dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ACQUIRED dataset comprises 3.7K annotated videos and over 11K question-answer pairs, serving as a benchmark for multimodal counterfactual reasoning.

**Dataset Statistics:**
- **Overall:** The dataset includes 2,664 unique videos from Oops! (third-person view) with 7,853 QA-pairs, and 1,038 unique videos from Ego4D (first-person view) with 2,695 QA-pairs.
- **Content Diversity:** Analysis of key verbs and nouns indicated a high diversity of event types and entities involved in the questions and answers. For Oops! videos, physical, social, and temporal dimensions were almost equally represented (34%, 33%, 33% respectively). For Ego4D videos, the questions were predominantly physical (77%) and temporal (23%), with no social dimension questions, consistent with the nature of the first-person daily activity videos.
- **Linguistic Complexity:** Questions had an average length of 11.5 tokens (Oops!) and 11.1 tokens (Ego4D). Answers averaged 8.2 tokens (Oops!) and 9.4 tokens (Ego4D).
- **Video Characteristics:** Oops! videos averaged 10.7 seconds duration, while Ego4D videos averaged 13.3 seconds.
- **Challenging Samples:** The model-in-the-loop collection process achieved average model "fooling rates" of 57.69% for Oops! videos and 51.43% for Ego4D videos, suggesting that annotators successfully created challenging examples that current models could not easily answer.

**Benchmarking Performance (Table 6 Summary):**
- **Human-Model Performance Gap:** Human performance demonstrated a substantial gap over all tested models. In the MCQ setting, humans achieved an accuracy of 92.59%, compared to the best model's (UnifiedQA-3B) 73.40%. For the T/F (pairwise) setting, human performance was 77.78%, whereas the best model (UnifiedQA-3B) achieved 54.91%. This significant difference (>13%) indicates that multimodal counterfactual reasoning remains a challenging open problem for AI.
- **Multimodal vs. Text-Only Models:**
 - The best-performing multimodal model, VL-Adapter, achieved an overall MCQ accuracy of 71.53%, slightly outperforming its text-only counterpart, UnifiedQA-large (70.38%). Similarly, in the T/F setting, VL-Adapter achieved 68.75% compared to UnifiedQA-large's 69.59% (though UnifiedQA-large slightly edged it out here, likely due to its larger parameter count).
 - This marginal improvement suggests that while visual context is beneficial, current multimodal models may not be effectively leveraging video information for robust counterfactual reasoning.
 - UnifiedQA-3B, a larger text-only model, generally achieved the highest overall accuracy in both T/F (70.49%) and MCQ (73.40%) settings among fine-tuned models, possibly due to its greater parameter capacity. However, its pairwise accuracy (54.91%) was only modest, indicating a potential reliance on superficial patterns rather than deep reasoning.
- **Large Language Models (LLMs):**
 - ChatGPT and GPT-4, even when provided with video descriptions ("Desc.-ChatGPT" and "Desc.-GPT-4"), performed notably lower than the fine-tuned models. For instance, Desc.-GPT-4 achieved 60.80% in MCQ, significantly below UnifiedQA-3B's 73.40%.
 - This finding suggests that the counterfactual questions in ACQUIRED require specific visual understanding and reasoning capabilities beyond the general knowledge encoded in LLMs or the information conveyable through brief descriptions. The questions necessitate a close examination of visual details.
- **T/F vs. MCQ Performance:** Models generally performed better in the MCQ setting compared to the T/F setting. This is attributed to the MCQ format allowing models to compare two options and select the more plausible one, potentially reducing the difficulty compared to making an absolute true/false judgment without such explicit comparison.
- **Pairwise Accuracy:** The pairwise accuracy, which requires correct judgment for both the positive and counterfactual scenarios, was consistently lower for all models compared to single T/F accuracy. This metric effectively demonstrated that models struggle with the subtle distinctions required for true counterfactual understanding.
- **Commonsense Dimension Breakdown:**
 - Models generally performed better on physical and social dimensions compared to the temporal dimension. The physical dimension often showed the highest performance across models.
 - This trend implies that current models may have limitations in accurately capturing temporal commonsense, potentially due to biases in pre-training data or inherent difficulty in temporal reasoning from video.
- **Viewpoint Analysis:**
 - VL-Adapter, the best-performing multimodal model, showed good generalization to first-person viewpoints (72.63% T/F, 75.34% MCQ for 1st-person) despite primarily being pre-trained on third-person view videos.
 - However, the authors noted that constructing diverse and subtle counterfactual questions for Ego4D (first-person) videos might have been more challenging for annotators compared to Oops! (third-person) videos, as Ego4D videos did not always explicitly contain failed actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ACQUIRED dataset and its accompanying benchmarking results carry several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence:

- **Establishment of a Comprehensive Benchmark:** ACQUIRED represents a novel contribution as the first video question answering dataset specifically dedicated to comprehensively evaluating multimodal counterfactual reasoning. It distinguishes itself by incorporating real-life video content, spanning diverse event types, including both first-person and third-person viewpoints, and covering three critical commonsense reasoning dimensions (physical, social, and temporal). This comprehensive scope addresses limitations of prior datasets that were either synthetic or restricted to narrow domains.
- **Highlighting an Open Challenge:** The consistent and substantial performance gap (exceeding 13% in accuracy) between state-of-the-art AI models and human performance on ACQUIRED clearly demonstrates that multimodal counterfactual reasoning remains a significant open research challenge. This gap is particularly evident in the "pairwise accuracy" metric, which demands a more robust understanding of both actual and hypothetical outcomes.
- **Driving Future Research Directions:** The dataset is expected to spur advancements in several key areas of AI research:
 - **Development of Advanced Multimodal Models:** The observed limitations of current models, particularly their struggles with effectively leveraging visual context and temporal reasoning, will encourage the design and development of more sophisticated video-language models capable of deeper counterfactual inference.
 - **Assistive AI and Failure Analysis:** The dataset's focus on "what-if" scenarios, especially those related to potential failures, can inspire research into assistive AI systems. Such systems could potentially analyze observed events to estimate causes of failures, propose remedial actions, and provide proactive guidance, with applications in augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) for training or real-time assistance.
 - **Enhanced Visual Event Understanding:** To perform well on ACQUIRED, models must achieve a more nuanced and granular understanding of visual events, including causal relationships, object properties, human intentions, and temporal dynamics. This will push the boundaries of visual event understanding and reasoning.
- **Ethical Considerations and Responsible AI:** The research acknowledges and addresses ethical considerations related to data collection, including worker privacy, fair compensation, and efforts to mitigate potential biases in annotations, particularly within the social domain. The focus on counterfactual reasoning, which inherently involves hypothetical scenarios, is not anticipated to produce harmful outputs towards vulnerable populations, contributing to the development of more robust and reliable AI systems.

In summary, ACQUIRED provides a critical resource for advancing multimodal AI by offering a challenging and diverse benchmark for counterfactual reasoning, thereby guiding the development of more intelligent and causally aware AI systems.
