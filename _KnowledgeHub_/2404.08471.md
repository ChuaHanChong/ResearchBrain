# Revisiting Feature Prediction for Learning Visual Representations from Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Meta's FAIR division, Inria, and NYU developed Video Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (V-JEPA), a self-supervised method for video representation learning that predicts latent features of masked video regions instead of raw pixels. V-JEPA demonstrates superior performance in frozen evaluation protocols, achieves state-of-the-art results on the Something-Something-v2 dataset, and offers improved label efficiency and computational advantages over pixel reconstruction approaches.

## Problem

- Traditional self-supervised video learning methods, often based on pixel reconstruction, incur high computational costs and tend to focus on low-level visual details, limiting semantic understanding and representation transferability.
- There was a need to evaluate the effectiveness of feature prediction as a standalone objective for unsupervised learning from video using modern transformer architectures and masked modeling.
- Developing AI systems that can learn from vast amounts of unlabeled video data is challenging due to the expense and impracticality of manual annotation.

## Method

- Video Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (**V-JEPA**) employs an Encoder (==Vision Transformer==) to extract latent feature representations from video clips and a Predictor (transformer-based) to map representations of unmasked regions to predict masked regions.
- The core idea is to predict higher-level latent feature representations of masked video sections rather than raw pixel values, allowing the model to focus on meaningful semantic content.
- A "multi-block" masking strategy, combining short-range (15%) and long-range (70%) masks, is applied to encourage the model to learn both local details and global semantic structures within videos.

## Results

- V-JEPA consistently outperforms pixel prediction approaches in frozen evaluation protocols, demonstrating improved transferability of learned representations.
- The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Something-Something-v2 dataset, which requires fine-grained temporal understanding.
- V-JEPA exhibits superior label efficiency, maintaining higher performance than pixel-reconstruction models with reduced amounts of labeled data, and is competitive in full fine-tuning while requiring shorter training schedules.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feature prediction naturally encourages models to focus on semantically meaningful content and higher-level concepts, rather than irrelevant visual details that pixel reconstruction methods might prioritize.
> - Operating in feature space significantly reduces computational requirements, enabling faster training times and lower memory consumption compared to pixel-level reconstruction.
> - The use of a stop-gradient operation and an exponential moving average (EMA) for the target encoder is crucial for preventing representation collapse during self-supervised training.
