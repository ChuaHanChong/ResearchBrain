# MeshMimic: Geometry-Aware Humanoid Motion Learning through 3D Scene Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> MeshMimic is a framework that trains humanoid robots from monocular video by reconstructing both high-fidelity human motion and 3D scene geometry. This method enables robust, terrain-aware control policies, showing reductions in motion tracking errors by 15.9-25.5% and improvements in scene reconstruction accuracy by 18.7% compared to previous video-based techniques, leading to higher success rates in real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional Motion Capture (MoCap) data for humanoid training is expensive, confined to controlled environments, and lacks the necessary geometric context of the surrounding physical environment.
- Existing methods decouple human motion from 3D scene geometry, leading to physical inconsistencies like foot slippage or mesh penetration when robots interact with complex, non-flat terrains.
- Prior video-based motion imitation approaches often rely on coarse scene modeling and do not offer the fine-grained contact optimization required for robust terrain interaction.

## Method

- A "Real-to-Sim" pipeline extracts human motion and 3D environmental geometry from monocular video using state-of-the-art vision models like SAM3D-Body and 
A
	3. This initial data is then refined through a joint optimization.
- A comprehensive optimization procedure ensures kinematic consistency by addressing noise, enforcing metric-scale alignment, minimizing human-scene penetration via TSDF volumes, and applying contact and trajectory smoothness losses.
- The refined human motion is adapted to the humanoid robot morphology using a terrain-aware MeshRetargeting method with TSDF-based collision correction, providing robust reference motions for an asymmetric PPO reinforcement learning pipeline.

## Results

- MeshMimic reduced World-frame Aligned Mean Per Joint Position Error (WA-MPJPE) by 15.9% (from 112.13 to 94.32 mm) and World-frame MPJPE by 25.5% (from 696.62 to 518.98 mm) compared to VideoMimic for human motion reconstruction.
- Scene geometry reconstruction accuracy improved, with the Chamfer Distance decreasing by 18.7% (from 0.75 to 0.61) compared to VideoMimic, demonstrating better environmental modeling.
- The framework enabled humanoid robots to achieve higher real-world success rates on challenging terrain-interaction tasks (e.g., various jumps, climbs, and vaults) by providing superior quality, geometry-aware references for policy training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, simultaneous reconstruction of both human motion and explicit 3D terrain geometry from monocular video is crucial for successful reinforcement learning and robust real-world robot deployment.
> - Integrating accurate 3D scene geometry into the motion optimization and retargeting processes enables physically plausible interactions, effectively preventing common artifacts like foot interpenetration and improving contact reasoning.
> - Leveraging advanced 3D vision foundation models significantly enhances the fidelity and robustness of extracted human-scene interactions, overcoming limitations of traditional video-based motion imitation.
