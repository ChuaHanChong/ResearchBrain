# MeshMimic: Geometry-Aware Humanoid Motion Learning through 3D Scene Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> MeshMimic is a framework that trains humanoid robots from monocular video by reconstructing both high-fidelity human motion and 3D scene geometry. This method enables robust, terrain-aware control policies, showing reductions in motion tracking errors by 15.9-25.5% and improvements in scene reconstruction accuracy by 18.7% compared to previous video-based techniques, leading to higher success rates in real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional Motion Capture (MoCap) data for humanoid training is expensive, confined to controlled environments, and lacks the necessary geometric context of the surrounding physical environment.
- Existing methods decouple human motion from 3D scene geometry, leading to physical inconsistencies like foot slippage or mesh penetration when robots interact with complex, non-flat terrains.
- Prior video-based motion imitation approaches often rely on coarse scene modeling and do not offer the fine-grained contact optimization required for robust terrain interaction.

## Method

- A "Real-to-Sim" pipeline extracts human motion and 3D environmental geometry from monocular video using state-of-the-art vision models like SAM3D-Body and 
A
	3. This initial data is then refined through a joint optimization.
- A comprehensive optimization procedure ensures kinematic consistency by addressing noise, enforcing metric-scale alignment, minimizing human-scene penetration via TSDF volumes, and applying contact and trajectory smoothness losses.
- The refined human motion is adapted to the humanoid robot morphology using a terrain-aware MeshRetargeting method with TSDF-based collision correction, providing robust reference motions for an asymmetric PPO reinforcement learning pipeline.

## Results

- MeshMimic reduced World-frame Aligned Mean Per Joint Position Error (WA-MPJPE) by 15.9% (from 112.13 to 94.32 mm) and World-frame MPJPE by 25.5% (from 696.62 to 518.98 mm) compared to VideoMimic for human motion reconstruction.
- Scene geometry reconstruction accuracy improved, with the Chamfer Distance decreasing by 18.7% (from 0.75 to 0.61) compared to VideoMimic, demonstrating better environmental modeling.
- The framework enabled humanoid robots to achieve higher real-world success rates on challenging terrain-interaction tasks (e.g., various jumps, climbs, and vaults) by providing superior quality, geometry-aware references for policy training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, simultaneous reconstruction of both human motion and explicit 3D terrain geometry from monocular video is crucial for successful reinforcement learning and robust real-world robot deployment.
> - Integrating accurate 3D scene geometry into the motion optimization and retargeting processes enables physically plausible interactions, effectively preventing common artifacts like foot interpenetration and improving contact reasoning.
> - Leveraging advanced 3D vision foundation models significantly enhances the fidelity and robustness of extracted human-scene interactions, overcoming limitations of traditional video-based motion imitation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

MeshMimic addresses several critical challenges within the rapidly advancing fields of humanoid robotics and embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on motion imitation and control.

Traditionally, humanoid motion control, especially for complex, human-like behaviors, has relied heavily on deep reinforcement learning (RL) but suffered from the impracticality of manual motion design. This led to a strong dependence on **Motion Capture (MoCap) data**. However, MoCap, while high-fidelity, is expensive, restricted to controlled laboratory environments, and crucially, often **lacks the necessary geometric context of the surrounding physical environment**. This fundamental decoupling of human motion from its interaction with terrain causes significant physical inconsistencies—such as foot slippage, contact misalignment, or mesh penetration—when robots attempt to navigate complex, non-flat terrains.

Recent research has begun to explore **video-based motion imitation** as an alternative to MoCap. Works like VideoMimic (Allshire et al., 2025) have shown promise in using video data, but they frequently rely on coarse scene modeling and do not offer the fine-grained contact optimization required for robust terrain interaction. Other approaches, such as OmniRetarget (Yang et al., 2025), introduce "Interaction Meshes" for object manipulation but are limited to simple geometric primitives and struggle to generalize to irregular, large-scale terrains.

The broader landscape of **3D spatial modeling and environment reconstruction** has witnessed transformative advancements, with techniques like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) (Mildenhall et al., 2021) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) (Kerbl et al., 2023) dramatically improving the quality of 3D scene reconstruction. Concurrently, **3D-aware foundation models** such as SAM3D (SAM 3D Team et al., 2025) have made significant strides in robust instance-level segmentation and 3D generation. MeshMimic leverages these cutting-edge computer vision developments to form a unified framework that recovers both high-quality human motion and the associated 3D environmental geometry directly from raw video.

Furthermore, in **humanoid motion retargeting**, while many learning-based methods (e.g., PHC, OmniH2O, Spider) have excelled at mapping human kinematics to robots, they often prioritize the agent's internal state or assume a simplified flat ground. MeshMimic critically addresses this **lack of terrain-aware retargeting** by explicitly incorporating high-resolution reconstructed meshes of the terrain into the retargeting process, ensuring contact-invariance on irregular surfaces.

Finally, regarding **humanoid motion tracking and whole-body control (WBC)**, existing frameworks (e.g., DeepMimic, ASE, ExBody, OmniH2O, BeyondMimic, VideoMimic, Sonic, Kungfubot, UniTracker) are predominantly "scene-agnostic," operating under the assumption of a uniform flat ground. This limits their ability to proactively adjust gait or contact points for complex obstacles inherent in human motion. MeshMimic distinguishes itself by integrating high-fidelity 3D scene reconstruction directly into the motion-tracking loop, grounding the WBC in the reconstructed environment geometry for physically consistent, terrain-aware motion imitation.

In summary, MeshMimic pioneers a crucial step forward by directly bridging state-of-the-art 3D vision with embodied intelligence, resolving the long-standing issue of decoupling motion and scene context in humanoid learning. It offers a scalable, low-cost alternative to traditional MoCap, enabling humanoid robots to learn robust, terrain-aware skills from readily available video data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MeshMimic stems from the inherent limitations of current humanoid motion learning paradigms, particularly their reliance on expensive and context-agnostic Motion Capture (MoCap) data. The authors identify two major shortcomings:

1. **High Cost and Restricted Environments of MoCap:** MoCap data is prohibitively expensive to acquire and confined to controlled laboratory settings. This significantly limits the diversity and realism of human behaviors that can be used for robot training, hindering the development of robots capable of operating in unstructured, real-world environments.
2. **Decoupling of Motion and Scene:** Traditional MoCap and many video-based imitation learning methods fail to capture the critical geometric context of the surrounding environment. This decoupling leads to physical inconsistencies—such as "foot skating," contact misalignment, or mesh penetration—when humanoid robots attempt to execute terrain-aware tasks, making robust and realistic interaction with complex environments challenging.

To address these motivations, the **key objectives** of the MeshMimic framework are:

- **Enable direct learning from monocular video:** To bypass the need for expensive MoCap systems, the framework aims to extract both human motion and environmental geometry directly from ordinary consumer-grade monocular videos. This significantly lowers the cost of data acquisition and increases scalability.
- **Achieve coupled "motion-terrain" interactions:** A central goal is to unify human motion with the 3D geometry of the terrain and objects. This ensures that the robot learns not just the movement, but also how humans physically interact with and leverage their environment, providing "physically-grounded" references.
- **Reconstruct high-quality 3D scenes and human trajectories:** By leveraging state-of-the-art 3D vision models, the system aims to precisely segment and reconstruct accurate human trajectories and underlying 3D environmental meshes from unposed video sequences.
- **Extract physically plausible motion from noisy visual data:** Visual reconstructions often contain noise. An objective is to introduce optimization algorithms, specifically kinematic consistency, to refine these noisy visual estimations into high-quality, physically plausible reference motions suitable for robot control.
- **Develop a contact-invariant retargeting method:** To bridge the morphological gap between humans and robots, an objective is to create a novel retargeting mechanism that effectively transfers human-environment interaction features, such as stepping on uneven rocks, while preserving essential contact constraints and avoiding penetrations.
- **Facilitate robust, dynamic performance on challenging terrains:** The ultimate goal is to enable humanoid robots to learn and perform highly dynamic, terrain-aware skills consistently and robustly across diverse and irregular environments, such as those involving stepping, vaulting, or climbing.
- **Pave a scalable path for autonomous humanoid evolution:** By providing a low-cost, video-centric pipeline, MeshMimic aims to offer a scalable solution for training complex physical interactions, fostering the autonomous evolution of humanoid robots in unstructured, real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MeshMimic introduces a comprehensive **Real-to-Sim-to-Real pipeline** designed to learn robust, terrain-aware humanoid control from monocular video.

#### 3.1. Real-to-Sim Process (Data Generation and Simulation Environment Setup)

This phase focuses on extracting high-fidelity human-scene interactions from raw video and preparing them for reinforcement learning.

- **Preprocessing (Section 3.1):**
 - **Scene Reconstruction:** The pipeline utilizes $\pi_3$ (Wang et al., 2025) to reconstruct the 3D scene, yielding per-frame depth maps, camera poses, and intrinsics. Critically, unlike methods that produce dense meshes or simple planar primitives, MeshMimic approximates the scene using planar polygonal primitives. This approach effectively suppresses noise in dynamic reconstructions and provides a faithful yet robust scene description.
 - **Human Reconstruction:** For the human actor, ViTDet (Li et al., 2022) is used for detection, SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2024) for identity association across frames, and SAM3D-Body (SAM 3D Team et al., 2025) to reconstruct per-frame human body geometry (SMPL-X model) and motion (local pose parameters, body shape, 3D joints, orientation, translation).
 - **Initial Challenge:** Both scene and human reconstructions are initially unscaled and expressed in camera coordinates, lacking metric consistency and world alignment.

- **Human-Scene Reconstruction (Section 3.2):** To address the initial inconsistencies and noise, a joint optimization approach is employed.
 - **Depth-Edge-Guided Contact Prediction:** To obtain reliable human-scene contact points, especially from noisy internet videos prone to jitter and blur, the method leverages stable visual cues: depth edges from monocular depth and silhouette boundaries from human segmentation. A "contact band" is defined as silhouette-boundary pixels not covered by dilated depth discontinuities, from which candidate scene contacts are selected. This is more robust than learning-based contact prediction in such conditions.
 - **Metric-Scale Human-Scene Alignment:** A joint optimization is performed to align the human trajectory and scene scale. The SMPL-X pose and shape are kept fixed, while per-frame global translations and a single global scene scale ($\alpha$) are optimized. An alignment loss $L_{align}$ is used, combining a 2D joint reprojection error ($L_{J2d}$) and a symmetric Chamfer distance ($L_d$) between camera-facing SMPL-X vertices and the metric-scale human point set.
 - **Kinematic Consistency Optimization:** To ensure physical plausibility and counteract artifacts like interpenetration or hovering, a comprehensive total loss $L_{total}$ is minimized:
 - **Contact Loss ($L_c$):** Anchors predicted human contact vertices to the estimated (scaled) scene contact locations, reducing hovering.
 - **Penetration Loss ($L_p$):** Penalizes human mesh intersection with a TSDF (Truncated Signed Distance Function) volume constructed from the background point cloud. A slack parameter $\tau$ allows for small tolerances.
 - **Trajectory Smoothness Loss ($L_{sm}$):** Regularizes the global translation trajectory to mitigate frame-to-frame jitter and drift, penalizing high velocity and acceleration changes.
 - **Foot-Snapping Loss ($L_{fs}$):** Explicitly encourages foot joints to lie on the scene surface when they are near it, preventing hovering ("foot snap" artifacts).

- **MeshRetargeting (Section 3.3):** The refined human motion is then adapted to the humanoid robot morphology while preserving crucial interactions with the 3D environment.
 - The method builds upon OmniRetarget by constructing an "interaction mesh" from human/robot anatomical keypoints and sampled object/terrain points.
 - Crucially, it samples *additional* terrain points in the vicinity of the human, alongside global terrain points, to preserve global geometric proportions while improving local alignment accuracy.
 - An SQP-style optimizer solves for the robot configuration per frame, incorporating hard constraints for collision avoidance, joint/velocity limits, and stance-foot anchoring.
 - **TSDF-Based Collision Correction:** A lightweight, iterative correction is applied to the robot's global translation. By querying the TSDF of the terrain at robot vertices, any penetration ($d(v) < 0$) is detected. An offset is calculated along the average SDF gradient direction to push the robot out of penetration, ensuring collision-free global translation.

#### 3.2. Sim-to-Real Process (Policy Training and Deployment)

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Pipeline:**
 - The high-fidelity human-scene reconstruction provides such strong implicit supervision that a minimal BeyondMimic-style reward formulation (generic tracking terms and standard regularization) is sufficient, reducing the need for elaborate reward engineering.
 - An **asymmetric PPO** (Proximal Policy Optimization) setup is used in IsaacLab (Mittal et al., 2025). The actor uses proprioceptive and reference features, while the critic has access to privileged scene information. A global torso position signal is added to the observations during training, obtained from optical MoCap during real-robot deployment.
 - **Training Strategy:** To improve efficiency, a generic whole-body motion tracker is pre-trained on $\sim$50 hours of non-interactive human motion data. This policy is then fine-tuned on the scene-interactive motion references generated by MeshMimic.
 - **Architecture:** Both actor and critic are lightweight 4-layer MLPs, using 5-step observation histories and a 5-step future motion horizon.
- **Deployment:** The trained policy is deployed onboard a Unitree G1 robot (running on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin at 50 Hz) within the *exact same real-world scenes* used for video capture, leveraging the preserved terrain geometry.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MeshMimic's experimental evaluation demonstrates its superior performance in both motion and scene reconstruction, and its effectiveness in real-world robot deployment across challenging, terrain-aware tasks.

#### 4.1. Reconstruction Comparison (Section 4.1)

- **Evaluation Setup:** The framework was benchmarked against established methods (WHAM, TRAM, VideoMimic) using a subset of the SLOPER4D dataset (Dai et al., 2023), focusing on activities like running, walking, and stair ascent/descent.
- **Metrics:** Performance was assessed using:
 - **World-frame Mean Per Joint Position Error (W-MPJPE):** Measures global consistency over long horizons.
 - **World-frame Aligned MPJPE (WA-MPJPE):** Measures local trajectory accuracy.
 - **Chamfer Distance:** Quantifies the accuracy of scene geometry reconstruction between the predicted and LiDAR point clouds.
- **Results:** MeshMimic consistently achieved the best overall performance:
 - **Human Motion:** It significantly reduced WA-MPJPE from 112.13 (VideoMimic) to **94.32** (a 15.9% reduction) and W-MPJPE from 696.62 (VideoMimic) to **518.98** (a 25.5% reduction). This indicates improved local fidelity and substantially better global trajectory stability compared to state-of-the-art video-based methods.
 - **Scene Geometry:** Chamfer Distance decreased from 0.75 (VideoMimic) to **0.61** (an 18.7% reduction). The improvement was even more pronounced compared to TRAM (10.66 vs 0.61), highlighting the benefit of MeshMimic's explicit environment modeling.
- **Conclusion:** The results validate that MeshMimic's reconstruction pipeline delivers higher-quality human-scene inputs, which are crucial for subsequent contact reasoning and physics-based humanoid learning.

#### 4.2. Real2Sim2Real Comparison (Section 4.3)

- **Scene Setup:** Eight diverse scene-interaction tasks were used, including various jumps, climbs, and vaults over boxes of different heights and complexities.
- **Baselines:** Primarily compared against VideoMimic (Allshire et al., 2025) due to its similar monocular real2sim2real pipeline.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Mean Training Reward:** Measured in IsaacLab after 40k PPO iterations.
 - **Real-World Success Rate (SR):** Computed over 10 trials per scene, with success indicating completion without intervention.

- **Impact of Motion and Terrain Reconstruction Quality:**
 - **MMM+MMT (MeshMimic motion + MeshMimic terrain):** Achieved the highest simulation rewards and real-world success rates across almost all tasks. Failures were primarily in highly dynamic or long-horizon multi-contact scenes (e.g., SV1, CB1, JCD1).
 - **VMM+MMT (VideoMimic motion + MeshMimic terrain):** Showed significantly lower rewards. VideoMimic's motion exhibited frequent foot-in-air artifacts, interpenetrations, and frame-to-frame drift, making policy tracking difficult, especially in contact-intensive scenarios. Several tasks failed sim2sim validation, preventing real-world deployment.
 - **VMM+VMT (VideoMimic motion + VideoMimic terrain):** Performed the worst. Inaccurate terrain geometry from VideoMimic (e.g., floating obstacles, hollow surfaces, irregular topologies) led to severe sim2sim failures and prevented deployment for most tasks, except for "Walk1" where terrain interaction was minimal.
 - **Overall Conclusion:** High-quality reconstruction of *both* human motion and terrain geometry is paramount for successful reinforcement learning and robust real-world deployment, significantly reducing model mismatch and enabling reliable contact reasoning in complex scenarios.

- **Effect of Global Torso Position as an Observation:**
 - **Benefits for Long-Horizon Tasks:** Including global torso position in the observation space significantly improved success rates for long-horizon traversal behaviors (e.g., +20% on JB2, +20% on CB1, +30% on JCD1). This external cue helps mitigate global pose drift and enhances foot placement accuracy over meters-long movements and multi-contact interactions.
 - **Degradation for Short, Dynamic Tasks:** Conversely, short-duration yet highly dynamic motions (e.g., SV1, SV2, CB2) showed degraded performance (-20% to -30% SR). This was attributed to noise from intermittent marker occlusions during optical motion capture (used for this cue in real deployment) and the tasks' higher reliance on local agility and precise contact timing rather than global pose.
 - **Conclusion:** Global observability is beneficial for path-dependent and long-horizon tasks, but for short, high-acceleration motions, purely proprioceptive policies might be preferable when external perception noise is significant.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MeshMimic represents a significant leap forward in humanoid robotics, offering solutions that directly address long-standing limitations in data acquisition and environmental interaction. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Democratization of Data Collection and Scalability:** By entirely eliminating the need for expensive motion capture (MoCap) systems, markers, or specialized sensing hardware, MeshMimic democratizes data collection for humanoid control. It enables the use of ordinary consumer-grade monocular videos, making complex humanoid motion learning accessible and scalable. This drastically lowers the cost barrier for research and development, allowing a broader range of institutions and individuals to contribute to the field and gather "in-the-wild" data from arbitrary outdoor scenarios.

2. **Enhanced Physical Realism and Robustness on Complex Terrains:** MeshMimic fundamentally solves the critical problem of decoupling human motion from environmental geometry. By reconstructing and optimizing both human motion and scene geometry simultaneously, it provides physically consistent, geometry-aware reference motions. This leads to humanoid robots that can perform robustly and realistically on diverse, challenging, and irregular terrains, avoiding common issues like foot skating, mesh penetration, or misaligned contacts that plague scene-agnostic controllers. This is crucial for real-world deployment in unstructured environments.

3. **Bridge Between 3D Vision and Embodied AI:** The framework effectively integrates state-of-the-art advancements in 3D computer vision (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting, 3D-aware foundation models like SAM3D) with reinforcement learning for embodied intelligence. This tight coupling allows perception to directly inform and constrain control, leading to a more holistic understanding of human-environment interaction for robots. This interdisciplinary approach is likely to inspire further research at this intersection.

4. **Foundation for Advanced Humanoid Autonomy:** MeshMimic lays a robust foundation for the autonomous evolution of humanoid robots. By enabling learning from diverse human demonstrations in varied environments, it accelerates the acquisition of complex motor skills. The ability to perceive and interact with terrains in a unified manner is a prerequisite for advanced capabilities such as navigation, manipulation, and agile locomotion in unknown and dynamic real-world settings.

5. **Simplified RL Reward Engineering:** The high quality of the extracted kinematic reference motions and watertight scene geometry significantly reduces the burden on manual reward shaping in reinforcement learning. This simplifies the development process for robot controllers, allowing researchers to focus more on core learning algorithms rather than tedious reward engineering.

Looking ahead, MeshMimic opens exciting avenues for future work, including pushing towards vision-based generalized parkour over diverse, previously unseen terrains, and developing fully closed-loop systems that tightly integrate perception, planning, and control for long-horizon autonomous navigation. The demonstrated ability to transfer complex human skills to robots using readily available video data truly pushes the frontier of practical, intelligent humanoid robotics.
