# CLARE: Continual Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models via Autonomous Adapter Routing and Expansion

> [!summary] Summary
> CLARE is a framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that enables continuous acquisition of new robotic skills while preventing catastrophic forgetting of previous knowledge. It achieves superior overall performance (up to 75.11% AUC) and near-zero negative backward transfer without requiring past data or explicit task identifiers, increasing model parameters by only 1.7-2.3% per task.

## Problem

- Real-world robotic deployment requires continual learning for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to adapt to new tasks without forgetting previously acquired skills.
- Existing continual learning methods for robotics often suffer from catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning VLA models on sequential tasks.
- Many current solutions are impractical due to high storage requirements for experience replay or the need for explicit task identifiers during autonomous robot deployment.

## Method

- A modularized adapter framework injects lightweight, low-rank adapter modules into the VLA's feedforward network layers, training only new adapters for new tasks while freezing existing parameters.
- An autonomous, autoencoder-based routing mechanism dynamically selects the most relevant adapter during inference by minimizing reconstruction error on input features, removing the need for explicit task identifiers.
- A dynamic expansion strategy determines when to add new adapters based on the novelty of new task features, ensuring parameter-efficient growth and promoting skill reuse.

## Results

- CLARE achieved the highest overall performance (AUC), reaching **66.71%** with DiT-EncDec and **75.11%** with DiT-Dec, outperforming the best exemplar-based baseline by up to **15%**.
- Catastrophic forgetting was effectively eliminated, demonstrated by near-zero Negative Backward Transfer (NBT) values ranging from **-0.80%** to **1.85%** across different backbones.
- The framework maintained high parameter efficiency (**1.7%**-**2.3%** increase per task), and expanding adapters exclusively in the encoder yielded 30-**40%** higher absolute AUC and Forward Transfer than expanding the decoder or both.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Freezing the base VLA and previously learned adapters while training new, task-specific adapters effectively prevents catastrophic forgetting, and dynamic expansion of model capacity driven by the novelty of new task features leads to efficient, sub-linear parameter growth while enabling knowledge transfer and reuse.
> - Autonomous routing via autoencoder-based discriminators allows dynamic adapter selection during inference without explicit task identifiers, making the system viable for open-world robotics.
> - The encoder component of VLA policies is the primary location for storing task-specific knowledge in continual learning settings, outperforming decoder-only or combined expansion strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the field of robotics, specifically the long-term adaptability of robots in dynamic, real-world environments. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have enabled robots to perform complex manipulation tasks by integrating perception, language understanding, and action generation. These VLAs, often pre-trained on large-scale internet data and robot demonstrations, exhibit some generalization capabilities. However, they typically require fine-tuning on task-specific data to achieve high performance on unseen tasks. This fine-tuning paradigm is problematic in continual learning (CL) settings, where new tasks emerge sequentially, and models must acquire new skills without forgetting previously learned ones. This issue is known as catastrophic forgetting.

Existing continual learning methods commonly fall into several categories:
- **Experience Replay (ER) based methods:** These store a subset of past data (exemplars) and replay them during training on new tasks to mitigate forgetting. While effective, ER can be computationally and memory intensive, and may face constraints regarding data storage or privacy in real-world, lifelong learning scenarios.
- **Regularization-based methods:** These constrain parameter updates for weights deemed important to past tasks to preserve existing knowledge. Methods like EWC or PackNet can struggle with long task sequences due to fixed initial parameter sets.
- **Architectural methods:** These involve injecting new parameters or modules for novel tasks, thereby allocating new capacity rather than overwriting shared representations. This approach often uses techniques like low-rank adaptation (LoRA) for memory efficiency. A limitation of many architectural methods is their reliance on oracle task identifiers during deployment, which are frequently unavailable in autonomous, open-world robotics. Furthermore, their application in robotics has largely been confined to multitask learning where all tasks are known in advance, rather than true sequential continual learning.

The paper aims to bridge the gap between the capabilities of VLAs and the requirements of continual learning in robotics by proposing an exemplar-free and task-identifier-free architectural method. It specifically focuses on addressing the limitations of existing CL approaches in robotics, such as the need for storing previous data, poor scalability with long task sequences, and the reliance on task identifiers for deployment. This work builds upon the foundation of modular and expandable architectures, adapting them for autonomous operation and efficient knowledge acquisition in VLA-powered robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a general, parameter-efficient framework that enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to continually learn new tasks without experiencing catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired knowledge. This framework, named CLARE (Continual Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models via Autonomous Adapter Routing and Expansion), aims to address several critical limitations inherent in current approaches to VLA deployment in real-world, non-stationary environments.

The motivation for CLARE stems from the following challenges:

- **Catastrophic Forgetting in VLAs:** Robots operating in dynamic environments, such as homes or warehouses, must continually adapt to new tasks and conditions. While pre-trained VLAs provide broad priors for complex manipulation, fine-tuning them on new task-specific data often leads to catastrophic forgetting. This phenomenon causes significant degradation in performance on previously learned tasks because shared parameters are updated without regard for prior representations. A key objective is to enable VLAs to acquire new skills while explicitly preserving old ones.

- **Limitations of Exemplar-Based Continual Learning:** Experience replay (ER) methods can mitigate forgetting by storing and replaying past data. However, this approach has practical drawbacks for lifelong robotics. Storing large volumes of past demonstration data may be infeasible due to memory constraints, privacy concerns, or computational overhead. The selection of representative samples for replay also presents a challenge. Therefore, a primary motivation is to develop an exemplar-free continual learning method, removing the reliance on storing previous data.

- **Requirement for Task Identifiers in Modular Architectures:** Modular and expandable architectures, which add new capacity for each task, represent a promising direction for continual learning. However, many of these methods require an "oracle" task identifier during deployment to select the appropriate module or skill. In autonomous robot operation in open-world settings, such explicit task labels are generally unavailable. A crucial objective is to enable autonomous task-agnostic inference, where the robot itself can determine which learned knowledge is most relevant to the current situation without external task cues.

- **Inefficient Parameter Growth:** While adding new capacity is necessary, an uncontrolled expansion of model parameters can lead to excessive memory consumption and computational overhead. The framework aims for parameter-efficient growth, adding new parameters only where necessary to maintain a balance between model plasticity (ability to learn new tasks) and stability (retention of old knowledge).

- **Scalability to Long Task Sequences:** Existing regularization-based methods often struggle with long sequences of tasks, as they are constrained by a fixed set of initial parameters. Architectural methods that involve merging parameters can also become complex. The research seeks a framework that can scale robustly to a continuous stream of new tasks over an extended operational period.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Enable VLA models to continually learn new tasks without catastrophic forgetting.
2. Achieve this without requiring the storage of previous task data (exemplar-free).
3. Allow for autonomous deployment without needing explicit task identifiers.
4. Ensure parameter-efficient model expansion.
5. Demonstrate robust performance and scalability across long sequences of diverse robotic manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CLARE is an exemplar-free, parameter-efficient, and task-identifier-agnostic framework for continual learning in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. It integrates lightweight modular adapters, an autonomous routing mechanism, and a dynamic expansion strategy.

**A. Base Policy**
The core policy learns using **flow matching**, a generative modeling technique that trains a vector field to transport samples from a simple base distribution (e.g., Gaussian noise) to the target action distribution. The policy takes as input an observation (camera images, proprioceptive state, language command) and generates action chunks, which are sequences of future actions. The training objective is a standard conditional flow matching loss, minimizing the squared difference between the learned vector field and the true vector field that transports action-chunk samples. For inference, new action chunks are generated by Euler integration of the learned vector field. The method is designed to be architecture-agnostic. The base VLA utilized is a decoder-only diffusion transformer (DiT-Dec) architecture with six transformer layers and Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) conditioning. Pre-trained DINOv2 and CLIP encoders are used for vision and text, respectively, and are frozen during continual learning.

**B. Modularized Adapters**
CLARE addresses catastrophic forgetting and parameter efficiency by incorporating lightweight modular adapters into selected VLA modules. Inspired by Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) approaches, these adapters serve as specialized sub-networks.
1. **Selection of Expandable Layers:** A set of $n_e$ "expandable modules" (E) is defined for continual learning. These are typically mid-layer feedforward network modules in transformer-based architectures, chosen because prior work indicates they store factual associations and high-level knowledge. In the DiT-Dec architecture, adapters can be added to the linear projection layer of the encoder and the transformer layers in the decoder.
2. **Adapter Structure:** Each adapter is a lightweight encoder-decoder structure with ReLU activation functions. For an input feature $x_ℓ$ of an expandable layer $ℓ$, the adapter computes an output $A_i^ℓ(x_ℓ) = W_{ℓ,i}^{\text{up}} \text{ReLU}(W_{ℓ,i}^{\text{down}} x_ℓ)$, where $W^{\text{down}}$ and $W^{\text{up}}$ are low-rank matrices ($r \ll d_ℓ$), ensuring parameter efficiency.
3. **Training and Inference:** Only newly added adapters are trained on data for a new task, while the rest of the model (including the pre-trained base VLA and previously learned adapters) remains frozen. During inference, a routing mechanism selects one adapter $A_∗^ℓ$ per layer, and its output is added to the output of the original pre-trained module $M_{\text{pre}}^ℓ(x_ℓ)$ as $M_ℓ(x_ℓ) = M_{\text{pre}}^ℓ(x_ℓ) + A_∗^ℓ(x_ℓ)$. This parallel side-branch injection preserves the network structure.

**C. Autonomous Routing**
To enable task-agnostic deployment, CLARE employs an expandable and lightweight routing mechanism that selects the most relevant adapter for each layer based on the current observation, without requiring explicit task labels.
1. **Discriminator Modules:** Each expandable layer $ℓ$ is paired with an expanding set of autoencoder-based discriminators $D_ℓ = \{D_1^ℓ, D_2^ℓ, \ldots\}$. A new discriminator $D_n^ℓ$ is attached at each stage $n$. All discriminators receive the same input features $x_ℓ$.
2. **Linking Discriminators to Adapters:** Each discriminator $D_j^ℓ$ is linked to a corresponding adapter $A_i^ℓ = B_ℓ(D_j^ℓ)$ through a surjective mapping $B_ℓ: D_ℓ \to A_ℓ$.
3. **Reconstruction Error for Selection:** Discriminators are trained to reconstruct their input features. The loss function $L_{\text{recon}}(D_n^ℓ) = E_{x_ℓ \sim D_n} [\|x_ℓ - D_n^ℓ(x_ℓ)\|^2]$ ensures that a discriminator trained on data from task $T_n$ will have a lower reconstruction error for features belonging to that task.
4. **Inference-time Routing:** During deployment, for each layer, the discriminator with the smallest reconstruction error for the current input feature $x_ℓ$ is identified. The adapter linked to this "winning" discriminator is then activated. Specifically, $A_∗^ℓ(x_ℓ) = B_ℓ(D_{j_∗}^ℓ)$, where $j_∗ = \text{arg min}_{j \in \{1, \ldots, n\}} e_j^ℓ(x_ℓ)$.
5. **Two-Stage Training:** To ensure stable discriminator training, a two-stage process is used: first, new adapters are trained with the flow-matching loss; then, all parameters except the new discriminators are frozen, and discriminators are trained with the reconstruction loss.

**D. Dynamic Expansion**
CLARE prevents excessive parameter growth while capturing new task-specific knowledge through a dynamic expansion strategy.
1. **Novelty Detection with Z-Scores:** To decide whether a layer $ℓ$ requires a new adapter for task $T_n$, the features of $T_n$ ($x_ℓ$) are compared against all previously learned tasks. For each existing discriminator $D_j^ℓ$, a normalized z-score $z_j^ℓ = \frac{1}{|D_n|} \sum_{x_ℓ \in D_n} \frac{e_j^ℓ(x_ℓ) - \mu_j^ℓ}{\sigma_j^ℓ}$ is computed, where $\mu_j^ℓ$ and $\sigma_j^ℓ$ are the running mean and standard deviation of $D_j^ℓ$'s reconstruction loss.
2. **Expansion Criteria:** If all existing discriminators $D_1^ℓ, \ldots, D_{n-1}^ℓ$ yield a z-score greater than a predefined threshold $\gamma$, it indicates that the features of the new task $T_n$ are out-of-distribution with respect to all previously learned tasks for that layer. In this case, a new adapter $A_{k_ℓ}^ℓ$ and a new discriminator $D_n^ℓ$ are added to layer $ℓ$, and $D_n^ℓ$ is linked to $A_{k_ℓ}^ℓ$.
3. **Auxiliary Discriminators for Feature Stability:** If at least one discriminator's z-score is below $\gamma$, the layer is not expanded with a new adapter. However, a new auxiliary discriminator $D_n^ℓ$ is still attached. This auxiliary discriminator is linked to the existing adapter whose corresponding discriminator had the smallest reconstruction error on the new task data, i.e., $B_ℓ(D_n^ℓ) = B_ℓ(D_{j_∗}^ℓ)$, where $j_∗ = \text{arg min}_{j \in \{1, \ldots, n-1\}} E_{x_ℓ \sim D_n} [e_j^ℓ(x_ℓ)]$. This ensures stable routing and prevents distribution shifts for subsequent layers.
4. **Forced Expansion for Novelty:** If no layer is deemed to require expansion based on the z-score criterion, an adapter is still added to the shallowest layer ($ℓ_1 \in E$) to ensure new task peculiarities are captured. At the first stage ($n=1$), all expandable layers are expanded by default. This strategy ensures that CLARE adds a small, task-dependent number of parameters without compromising performance on previous tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CLARE involved extensive experiments in both simulation using the LIBERO benchmark and real-world settings with an FR3 manipulator. The performance was assessed using Area under the success rate curve (AUC), Forward Transfer (FWT), and Negative Backward Transfer (NBT).

**A. Simulation Results (LIBERO Benchmark)**

1. **Impact of Expandable Layers (Q1):** An ablation study on LIBERO-Long with $\gamma=0$ (always expand) showed that expanding the encoder part of the model (e.g., linear projection layers or encoder transformer layers) yielded superior performance compared to expanding only the decoder. Expanding both encoder and decoder performed similarly to expanding only the encoder. This indicates that the observation-conditioning modules are crucial for storing new knowledge during continual learning. Based on these findings, subsequent experiments primarily focused on expanding these encoder-side modules.

2. **Continual Learning Performance and Forgetting (Q2):**
 - **Baseline Comparison:** CLARE was compared against seven baselines: SeqFFT, SeqLoRA, PackNet, ER, LOTUS, DMPEL, and MLR across LIBERO-Long, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Spatial suites.
 - CLARE consistently achieved the highest overall performance (AUC). For instance, on LIBERO-Long, CLARE achieved an AUC of 75.1%, outperforming the best baseline, ER (60.5%), by approximately 14 percentage points. Similar trends were observed on LIBERO-Goal (CLARE: 89.3%, ER: 76.0%) and LIBERO-Spatial (CLARE: 87.4%, ER: 77.6%).
 - CLARE demonstrated comparable FWT to full fine-tuning methods (SeqFFT, ER), indicating effective new task acquisition.
 - Crucially, CLARE exhibited approximately zero NBT (LIBERO-Long: 1.9%, LIBERO-Goal: 0.3%, LIBERO-Spatial: 0.9%), signifying robust prevention of catastrophic forgetting without exemplar data or task identifiers. In contrast, ER, despite using exemplars, showed NBT values ranging from 20.9% to 25.1%. SeqFFT and SeqLoRA showed very high NBT (e.g., ~70-90%), indicating significant forgetting.
 - **Long-term Scalability (LIBERO-40):** On a sequence of 40 tasks from concatenated LIBERO suites, CLARE maintained robust performance (AUC = 83.6%, FWT = 82.9%, NBT = -1.0%). ER, in contrast, showed significant performance degradation and forgetting (NBT = 23.2%) for several early tasks.

3. **Autonomous Dynamic Expansion Strategy (Q3):**
 - **Impact of Expansion Threshold ($\gamma$):** Varying the $\gamma$ threshold on LIBERO-Long demonstrated the trade-off between parameter growth and performance. Increasing $\gamma$ from 0 to 20 reduced the number of added adapters from 60 to 16, resulting in a slight decrease in AUC and FWT. However, AUC remained higher than ER, and NBT stayed near zero, indicating that forgetting was avoided even with fewer added parameters. This suggests that the dynamic expansion effectively reuses relevant skills and adds new capacity only when substantial novelty is detected.
 - **Parameter Growth:** The dynamic expansion strategy resulted in a parameter count increase of approximately 2% per task in the experiments.

**B. Real-World Results (FR3 Manipulator)**

1. **Continual Learning on Diverse Tasks:** CLARE was evaluated on five real-world manipulation tasks involving diverse objects, grasp configurations, dynamic interactions, and multi-stage strategies (e.g., "Put a bowl on a plate," "Close a drawer," "Put a Lego block into the drawer and close it"). Adapters were added to linear projection layers and AdaLN modules of the transformer decoder layers.
2. **Performance and Forgetting (Table IV & V):** CLARE achieved an AUC of 63.3%, which was 12 percentage points higher than ER (51.1%). It demonstrated a near-zero NBT of -2.9%, effectively preventing catastrophic forgetting in real-world scenarios. SeqFFT and SeqLoRA showed high FWT (68.0%, 64.0%) but significant forgetting (NBT of 80.0%, 76.9%). Per-task success rates (Table V) confirmed CLARE's ability to retain performance on earlier tasks while learning new ones, outperforming ER in task retention.
3. **Computational Overhead (Q4):**
 - **Inference Time:** The inference-time overhead relative to the base policy was measured to be below 3 ms, indicating minimal impact on real-time operation.
 - **GPU VRAM and Disk Usage:** GPU VRAM utilization increased by approximately 2% per task. Additional disk usage for adapters and discriminators was significantly lower than explicitly storing datasets for ER.

4. **Router Robustness:** Experiments demonstrated the robustness of the autoencoder-based routing mechanism. When the language command was changed mid-execution (e.g., from "put the Lego block into the drawer" to "close the drawer"), the router immediately switched to activate the correct adapter, allowing the robot to adapt its behavior accordingly. This functionality was consistent across 10 rollouts and validated in continuous multi-task scenarios without external task identifiers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLARE framework introduces a robust approach to continual learning for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, addressing several fundamental limitations that hinder the long-term deployment of robots in dynamic, real-world environments. Its significance lies in enabling robots to continually acquire new skills without necessitating the storage of previous task data or relying on explicit task identifiers during operation.

The key aspects of its potential impact include:

- **Enabling Long-Term Robot Autonomy:** By effectively mitigating catastrophic forgetting in an exemplar-free manner, CLARE allows robots to operate for extended periods, continually learning new tasks and adapting to changing conditions without the need for periodic retraining or memory-intensive data management. This moves towards truly lifelong learning capabilities for robotic systems in homes, hospitals, or warehouses.

- **Reduced Operational Cost and Complexity:** The exemplar-free nature of CLARE eliminates the need for maintaining and accessing large replay buffers, which can reduce computational overhead, memory requirements, and data privacy concerns associated with storing sensitive real-world demonstration data. This simplifies the deployment and maintenance of robotic systems in diverse operational settings.

- **Enhanced Adaptability to Novelty:** The autonomous adapter routing and dynamic expansion strategy allow the model to selectively inject new capacity only when features of a new task deviate substantially from previously learned ones. This capability enables robots to efficiently adapt to novel situations while leveraging and preserving established knowledge, ensuring both plasticity and stability. The low parameter growth (approximately 2% per task) contributes to memory-efficient scalability.

- **Autonomous Task Switching:** The autoencoder-based routing mechanism facilitates autonomous inference, dynamically activating the most relevant adapters based solely on current observations, without requiring manual task labels. This is critical for open-world scenarios where tasks are not explicitly provided. The demonstrated ability of the router to switch capabilities dynamically during execution based on changes in language commands highlights its potential for reactive and context-aware robot behavior.

- **Generality and Applicability:** While evaluated on a compact VLA architecture, the modular design of CLARE is stated to be architecture-agnostic. This suggests its potential applicability to larger and more complex VLA models, broadening its impact across the developing landscape of generalist robot policies.

The findings, particularly the superior performance against even exemplar-based methods and robust real-world validation, suggest that CLARE represents a step towards more adaptable, autonomous, and continuously learning robotic systems.
