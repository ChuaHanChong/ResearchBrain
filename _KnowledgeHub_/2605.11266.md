# PG-3DGS: Optimizing 3D Gaussian Splatting to Satisfy Physics Objectives

> [!summary] Summary
> PG-3DGS couples 3D Gaussian Splatting with a differentiable Eulerian fluid-solid simulator through a soft occupancy mask derived from the Gaussian parameters, so physics objectives and image losses jointly shape geometry. Optimizing pouring and lift objectives yields teapots with connected cavities and airplanes that gain altitude, while SSIM stays near baseline and 3D-printed planes measure higher benchtop lift.

## Problem

- Gaussian Splatting and NeRF optimize appearance across views only, so reconstructions look correct externally while hiding sealed interiors, disconnected spouts, or aerodynamically useless wings.
- Differentiable fluid solvers enable inverse design but represent geometry as static meshes or voxel grids, offering no gradient path into continuous Gaussian primitives.
- Existing scene-representation plus physics couplings only run forward dynamics or infer material fields; they are not differentiable with respect to the underlying geometry parameters.

## Method

- ==Gaussian-to-grid soft solid mask==: a probabilistic union of per-Gaussian occupancy contributions gives a smooth field on the simulation grid, differentiable in means, covariances, and opacities.
- Differentiable ==Eulerian fluid solver== with ==Brinkman penalization== damps velocity proportionally to the soft mask, plus rigid-body coupling that recovers net force and torque from discrete momentum exchange.
- Joint ==Gibbs-posterior== objective sums visual ==L1== plus ==SSIM== loss, a trajectory functional physics loss, and regularization, with a staged schedule enabling physics gradients only after visual warm-up.

## Results

- Benchtop lift tests of 3D-printed aircraft under identical blower airflow: Cessna rose **13.7** to **20.5** grams, B-2 Spirit **9.5** to **13.8**, paper plane **-13.4** to **0.3**.
- Weight sweep over **8** physics scalings spanning orders of magnitude, **40** runs, keeps SSIM roughly constant until about **0.1**, after which physics gains saturate and SSIM mildly degrades.
- In simulation only the physics-guided teapots pour; cutaways show a connected cavity and open spout, while SDF, NeRF, and 3DGS baselines trap fluid inside.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image formation is insensitive to interior structure and connectivity, so physics gradients mostly select among visually equivalent geometries instead of trading appearance away for function.
> - Binary voxelization destroys gradients at solid boundaries; a soft occupancy field is the general device for backpropagating simulator objectives into any explicit geometry parameterization.
> - Optimized shapes satisfy the stated objective, not reality: misspecified functionals, coarse grids, and mask sharpness bound how far improvements transfer beyond the simulator.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D generative modeling has advanced significantly, moving from 2D image generation to the creation of 3D structures. Recent developments, particularly in neural and explicit radiance field methods like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have enabled high-fidelity 3D reconstruction and novel-view rendering from multi-view image supervision. A common principle in these methods is inverse graphics, where differentiable rendering allows gradients from image-space objectives to optimize 3D representations to match observed images. This approach has also been extended to text- and image-conditioned 3D generation using score distillation techniques.

However, a limitation in this landscape is that purely visual optimization often yields geometries that are photometrically accurate but lack physical functionality. For instance, an object might render correctly from all angles but possess a sealed interior or insufficient structural integrity to perform a physical task. This gap highlights a need for 3D generation methods that can account for physical behavior beyond mere appearance.

In parallel, differentiable physics frameworks have emerged, allowing gradients to propagate through partial differential equation (PDE) solvers. These frameworks facilitate the optimization of simulation parameters and control policies. Particle-based methods (e.g., differentiable material point methods) and grid-based methods (e.g., DiffTaichi, ΦFlow) have demonstrated success in learning and inverse design for fluid and deformable body dynamics. A challenge in this area is integrating these physics simulations with continuous, explicit 3D representations. Some prior work has explored coupling neural scene representations with physics, such as unifying 3D Gaussian Splatting with Position-Based Dynamics for forward simulation. However, these efforts have often not been fully differentiable with respect to the underlying geometric parameters for inverse design, or have focused on inferring material properties rather than directly optimizing geometry for specific physical functions.

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) represent another related direction, incorporating physical laws as soft constraints during training to improve data efficiency and discourage physically implausible predictions. While effective, PINNs can face optimization challenges due to potential imbalances between PDE residuals and data terms.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these fields, addressing the problem of generating physically functional 3D structures from 2D observations. It extends the capabilities of 3D Gaussian Splatting by integrating differentiable physics optimization, aiming to produce geometries that not only align with visual inputs but also exhibit desired physical behaviors. The work introduces a differentiable coupling mechanism between an explicit Gaussian representation and grid-based physics, allowing gradients from physical objectives to guide geometric optimization. This approach aims to move beyond appearance-only reconstruction towards end-to-end generation of 3D structures that are both realistic in appearance and correct in function, providing a mechanism for physics-objective-driven 3D generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recognized limitation in current 3D generative modeling: visual accuracy does not inherently imply physical functionality. While advanced techniques like 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) can reconstruct scenes with high visual fidelity from multi-view images, the resulting geometries often fail to exhibit expected physical behaviors when subjected to real-world or simulated interactions.

The paper highlights two specific examples to illustrate this core problem:
1. **Teapot Example**: An appearance-only reconstruction of a teapot might render flawlessly from all external viewpoints. However, the internal geometry could be sealed (lacking a functional cavity) or have a disconnected spout, preventing liquid from being poured. Such an object, despite its visual plausibility, would be physically non-functional.
2. **Airplane Example**: Similarly, an appearance-optimized airplane model might perfectly match its visual silhouette and texture. Yet, its wing geometry could be suboptimal, failing to generate sufficient aerodynamic lift during a physical test or simulation, leading to unstable or non-sustained flight.

These types of failures are significant because reconstructed 3D objects are increasingly utilized beyond mere rendering. Applications in simulation-based planning, interactive scene understanding, robotics, and the design of usable artifacts (including virtual reality environments) demand objects that behave realistically and fulfill their intended physical functions. A system unable to correlate an object's appearance with its physical behavior cannot meet these requirements.

Given this motivation, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **To develop a framework capable of generating 3D structures that are functional in the physical world.** This involves ensuring that the reconstructed geometry not only looks correct but also performs intended physical tasks.
2. **To integrate differentiable physics optimization with 3D Gaussian Splatting.** This involves augmenting the 3DGS representation with a mechanism to incorporate physical objectives into the shape optimization process.
3. **To enable joint optimization of visual appearance and physical functionality.** The goal is to allow physical objectives to guide geometric adjustments alongside conventional visual reconstruction losses, producing photometrically accurate and physically functional geometries.
4. **To establish a differentiable coupling between explicit Gaussian representations and grid-based physics simulators.** This is a core technical objective, addressing the challenge of smoothly converting continuous Gaussian geometry into a format suitable for grid-based physics simulations, while ensuring gradients can flow back to update Gaussian parameters.
5. **To demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach on specific tasks.** The paper aims to empirically validate that the proposed method can generate objects such as teapots that can pour and airplanes that can generate lift, without compromising visual quality, and potentially showing superior physical performance compared to appearance-only baselines.

In essence, the research aims to resolve geometric ambiguities that are unconstrained by image formation alone by leveraging the principles of physics, thereby elevating 3D content generation from purely visual fidelity to functional realism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Physics-Guided 3D Gaussian Splatting (PG-3DGS), is a framework for generating 3D structures that are both visually accurate and physically functional. It operates by performing a joint optimization of a 3D Gaussian scene representation, incorporating gradients from both visual rendering and differentiable physics simulations.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as minimizing a composite objective function, $J(\theta)$, which combines three terms: a visual loss ($L_{vis}$), a physical loss ($L_{phys}$), and an optional regularization loss ($L_{reg}$). The geometric parameters of the 3D Gaussian representation are denoted by $\theta$. This objective aims to find parameters $\theta$ that maximize the posterior probability of $\theta$ given visual observations (images $I$) and a desired physical objective ($\Phi$).

#### 3.2. 3D Gaussian Scene Representation

The scene geometry is represented by a collection of $N$ anisotropic 3D Gaussians, consistent with the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) formulation. Each Gaussian $i$ is parameterized by:
- $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Its mean (center position).
- $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: A positive-definite covariance matrix, encoding spatial extent and orientation. This is parameterized using a rotation-scale decomposition ($\Sigma_i = R(q_i) \text{diag}(s_i^2) R(q_i)^\top$) to ensure positive definiteness, where $q_i$ is a unit quaternion for orientation and $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3_+$ are scale parameters.
- $\alpha_i \in$: An opacity value.
- $c_i$: View-dependent appearance coefficients, modeled using a truncated spherical harmonics (SH) expansion to capture non-Lambertian effects.

#### 3.3. Visual Supervision

The visual loss, $L_{vis}(\theta)$, quantifies the discrepancy between rendered images of the current Gaussian representation and a set of input calibrated multi-view images.
- **Differentiable Rendering**: The 3DGS renderer projects 3D Gaussians onto the image plane. This involves a first-order approximation where each 3D Gaussian is mapped to a 2D Gaussian in screen space (with mean $\mu^{(2D)}_i$ and covariance $\Sigma^{(2D)}_i$) using the Jacobian of the camera projection.
- **Compositing**: Rendered images are formed by sorting 2D Gaussians by depth and compositing their screen-space contributions using front-to-back alpha blending. The rendered color at a pixel depends on the Gaussian's color, opacity, and accumulated transmittance from closer Gaussians.
- **Loss Function**: A combination of $\ell_1$ loss and SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure) is used to compare rendered images with ground truth. The entire rendering process is differentiable, allowing gradients to propagate back to all Gaussian parameters.

#### 3.4. Physics Supervision

The physical loss, $L_{phys}(\theta)$, is defined as a differentiable function $\Phi$ of the simulated trajectory $\tau(\theta)$, which is produced by a differentiable fluid-solid simulator. This term encourages the reconstructed geometry to exhibit task-specific physical behavior.

##### 3.4.1. Gaussian-to-Grid Coupling via a Soft Solid Mask

A critical component is bridging the continuous Gaussian representation with grid-based simulators.
- **Gaussian Occupancy Field**: Each Gaussian $i$ induces a soft, continuous occupancy contribution $\hat{\alpha}_i(x)$ at any spatial location $x$, based on its mean, covariance, and opacity.
- **Probabilistic Union**: Individual Gaussian contributions are aggregated into a global soft solid mask $\chi(x; \theta) \in$ using a probabilistic union: $\chi(x; \theta) = 1 - \prod_{i=1}^N (1 - \hat{\alpha}_i(x))$. This mask varies smoothly with Gaussian parameters, making it differentiable.
- **Efficient Evaluation**: To manage computational cost, a tiled evaluation strategy is employed (detailed in Appendix A), which exploits the local support of Gaussians.
- **Differentiability**: The soft mask avoids discontinuities inherent in binary voxelizations, ensuring that downstream quantities (e.g., fluid-solid coupling forces) remain differentiable with respect to $\theta$.

##### 3.4.2. Fluid Simulation

The simulator $S(\chi)$ models an incompressible Newtonian fluid interacting with a rigid solid on an Eulerian grid using the Navier-Stokes equations, augmented with a Brinkman penalization term defined by the soft solid mask $\chi(x; \theta)$.
- **Why Soft Boundaries**: Binary solid indicators in grid-based solvers lead to discontinuous changes and ill-defined gradients. The soft mask $\chi(x; \theta)$ continuously interpolates between fluid and solid regions, providing better-conditioned gradients.
- **Discrete State Update**: A standard projection method is used, splitting the update into stages: advection, external forces, Brinkman penalization, pressure projection, and a final velocity update.
- **Brinkman Penalization**: A damping force density, $f_{Br}(x) = \lambda \chi(x) (u_{solid}(x) - u(x))$, is introduced to relax fluid velocity toward the prescribed solid velocity $u_{solid}$ within solid regions. A semi-implicit update ensures stability for large $\lambda$.

##### 3.4.3. Rigid-Body Simulation Coupling Moving Solids

For tasks involving moving objects (e.g., airplanes), the simulator extends to rigid-body dynamics.
- **Rigid-Body State**: Position ($x_t$), linear velocity ($v_t$), orientation ($q_t$, unit quaternion), and angular momentum ($L_t$) are tracked.
- **Solid Velocity Field**: The velocity of the solid at any point $x$ is defined as $u_{solid}(x) = v_t + \omega_t \times (x - x_t)$, where $\omega_t$ is angular velocity.
- **Hydrodynamic Forces and Torques**: These are computed from the discrete momentum exchange induced by the Brinkman step, adhering to Newton's third law. The forces and torques are the negative volume sums of the fluid force density.
- **Time Integration**: Translational motion is updated using a first-order symplectic Euler step. Rotational motion uses an implicit Euler update for angular momentum, solving a 3x3 linear system for angular velocity to ensure numerical stability under strong fluid-solid coupling. Static solids are a special case where velocities and angular momenta are fixed at zero.

#### 3.5. Joint Visual-Physical Optimization

The overall objective $J(\theta) = L_{vis}(\theta) + L_{phys}(\theta) + L_{reg}(\theta)$ is minimized using first-order gradient methods.
- **Gradient Decomposition**: The gradient $\nabla_\theta J(\theta)$ is the sum of gradients from each loss term. $\nabla_\theta L_{vis}$ is obtained via backpropagation through the differentiable renderer, and $\nabla_\theta L_{phys}$ via backpropagation through the unrolled differentiable simulator.
- **Balancing Objectives**: To address potential scale differences and avoid early clashes between visual and physics gradients, a staged optimization procedure is used: an initial phase optimizes only $L_{vis}$ to establish a visually plausible reconstruction, followed by a joint phase where $L_{phys}$ is also enabled. A tradeoff parameter for the losses is also employed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence that PG-3DGS can generate 3D structures that maintain visual quality while significantly improving physical functionality.

#### 4.1. Physics-Guided Optimization Results

- **General Performance**: Across various tasks, PG-3DGS reconstructed objects appeared visually comparable to appearance-only baselines but demonstrated substantially enhanced physical behavior.

- **Pouring Task (Teapots)**:
 - **Baseline Limitations**: Appearance-only methods (3DGS, VolSDF, NeRF) produced visually plausible teapot exteriors but frequently resulted in sealed internal cavities or disconnected spouts. In simulations, this caused fluid to be trapped or unable to exit the teapot (Figures 3, 5, 6). NeRF also occasionally produced spurious mass, distorting dynamics.
 - **PG-3DGS Performance**: PG-3DGS consistently recovered coherent hollow interiors with connected spouts, allowing fluid to flow successfully out during simulation (Figures 3, 5, 6).
 - **Visual Quality Preservation**: The integration of physics objectives did not significantly degrade visual quality. Rendered images from PG-3DGS were visually indistinguishable from those of baselines (Figure 4).
 - **Training Dynamics**: Analysis of training progression (Figure 7) showed that the physics gradient actively reshaped the interior geometry and spout alignment over iterations, a behavior not achievable with appearance losses alone.

- **Aerodynamic Lift Task (Airplanes)**:
 - **Baseline Limitations**: Airplanes reconstructed using appearance-only baselines (3DGS) consistently lost altitude during simulated flight due to insufficient lift (Figure 9).
 - **PG-3DGS Performance**: PG-3DGS generated geometries capable of producing sufficient lift to sustain flight, resulting in stable or ascending trajectories over the simulation horizon (Figure 9).
 - **Training Dynamics**: Plotting the z-position of a paper plane over optimization steps demonstrated a consistent shift from downward to upward trajectories as training progressed (Figure 8), indicating effective optimization of lift generation.

#### 4.2. Benchtop Physical Lift Validation

To assess real-world applicability, benchtop physical lift tests were conducted using 3D-printed aircraft.
- **Setup**: Three distinct aircraft shapes (Cessna, B-2 Spirit, paper plane) were 3D-printed based on PG-3DGS and 3DGS baseline models. They were subjected to identical airflow conditions from a blower, and upward lift force was measured using a scale.
- **Results**: Across all three aircraft shapes, PG-3DGS-generated models consistently produced higher scale-measured lift than the appearance-only baseline (Table 2, Figure 10). For example, the Cessna model from PG-3DGS yielded 20.5 grams of lift compared to 13.7 grams for the baseline, an improvement of 6.8 grams. The paper plane saw the largest improvement, shifting from -13.4 grams (downward force) for the baseline to 0.3 grams (slight upward force) for PG-3DGS.
- **Significance**: These physical tests suggest that the simulation-driven geometric improvements translate to tangible real-world effects, capturing genuine aerodynamic properties despite potential simulation-reality mismatches.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study investigated the effect of physics-loss strength (controlled by the weight $\lambda$) on visual quality (SSIM) and physical objective satisfaction (average plane height in simulation).
- **Results**: Figure 11 illustrates that increasing $\lambda$ from zero led to a substantial improvement in the physics objective, while SSIM remained largely constant over a broad range of weights (up to $\lambda \approx 0.1$). Only at very high $\lambda$ values did the physics objective show diminishing returns and a mild degradation in SSIM.
- **Interpretation**: This indicates that physics supervision primarily resolves geometric ambiguities that are not constrained by image formation, rather than requiring a significant trade-off with visual appearance. The optimization selects physically functional geometries from a set of visually plausible solutions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a contribution by addressing the functional limitations of appearance-only 3D structure generation. The core significance lies in its introduction of a unified framework, Physics-Guided 3D Gaussian Splatting (PG-3DGS), which enables the creation of 3D assets that are not only photometrically accurate but also physically functional.

**Key Contributions and Their Significance:**

- **Bridging Appearance and Functionality:** The paper demonstrates that appearance alone is an insufficient signal for 3D reconstruction when objects are intended for physical interaction or simulation. PG-3DGS resolves ambiguities inherent in visual-only reconstruction by incorporating differentiable physics, leading to geometries that perform correctly in physical contexts.
- **Differentiable Coupling of Explicit Representations and Physics:** A notable technical achievement is the differentiable coupling between continuous 3D Gaussian representations and grid-based physics simulators via a soft occupancy mask. This smooth interface allows gradients from physical objectives to directly inform and update the Gaussian parameters (means, covariances, opacities), which is crucial for inverse design tasks. This mechanism is presented as reusable and adaptable to various physical objectives and simulators.
- **Empirical Validation of Functional Improvements:** Through tasks like liquid pouring and aerodynamic lift, the research provides concrete examples where PG-3DGS significantly improves physical functionality (e.g., successful pouring, sustained flight) while maintaining visual fidelity comparable to state-of-the-art appearance-only methods.
- **Real-World Applicability (Benchtop Tests):** The benchtop physical lift tests using 3D-printed aircraft offer important validation, suggesting that the simulation-driven geometric optimizations translate into measurable improvements in real-world physical performance. This supports the premise that incorporating physics during reconstruction can enhance downstream utility beyond the training simulator.
- **Efficient Guidance Mechanism:** The ablation study highlights that physics guidance can be introduced without a substantial visual penalty, especially when applied in a staged manner (initial visual-only optimization followed by joint optimization). This suggests that physics gradients primarily guide the optimization within a broad basin of visually acceptable solutions towards those that are also functional, akin to Bayesian posterior inference where physical evidence disambiguates under-determined geometry.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Generating physically functional objects is critical for training and deploying robots that interact with their environment, enabling more realistic simulations for planning and control.
- **Interactive Virtual Environments:** Creating objects that behave realistically allows for more immersive and believable virtual reality and gaming experiences.
- **Data-Driven Design and Engineering:** The framework could facilitate inverse design tasks, where desired physical behaviors directly inform the generative process of 3D shapes, potentially accelerating design cycles for functional artifacts.
- **Computer Vision and Graphics:** This work extends the capabilities of 3D reconstruction beyond passive rendering, pushing towards more comprehensive scene understanding that includes physical properties.
- **Scientific Simulation:** By connecting explicit geometric representations with differentiable simulators, PG-3DGS could serve as a foundation for optimizing complex physical systems.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The paper acknowledges several limitations, including the reliance on simulator fidelity and boundary treatment (soft mask trade-offs), grid resolution constraints for fine features, the task-conditional nature of optimization (geometry changes are specific to the defined objective), and the computational cost and hyperparameter tuning associated with differentiable simulation. These limitations suggest future research avenues such as:
- Developing higher-fidelity or multi-resolution physics solvers.
- Improving immersed-boundary treatments.
- Implementing automatic or adaptive balancing strategies for visual and physics loss terms.
- Extending the Gaussian-to-physics interface to incorporate more complex material properties (e.g., compliance, permeability).
- Integrating closed-loop learning with real-world measurements to calibrate simulators or correct biases.

Overall, PG-3DGS represents a step towards generating 3D content that is inherently useful and interactive, moving beyond purely aesthetic rendering to encompass a deeper understanding of physical behavior.
