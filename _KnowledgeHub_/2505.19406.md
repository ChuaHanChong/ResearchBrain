# Unveiling the Compositional Ability Gap in Vision-Language Reasoning Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong and Tencent Hunyuan Research systematically investigated and addressed the compositional ability gap in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) using ComPABench, a novel diagnostic benchmark. Their proposed RL-Ground training strategy, which incorporates visual captioning and fine-grained progress rewards, improved multimodal compositional task performance to 52.8% for a 7B model and demonstrated robust out-of-distribution generalization.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle to truly compose different skills to solve complex, multi-step problems requiring both visual perception and reasoning.
- There is a fundamental limitation in current VLMs regarding cross-modal composition (transferring text-learned reasoning to visual inputs) and cross-task integration (combining independently learned visual reasoning skills).
- Current VLM training paradigms often fail to ensure compositional abilities generalize to novel, out-of-distribution task objectives, indicating a lack of true understanding.

## Method

- Development of **ComPABench**, a ==diagnostic benchmark== with precisely controlled synthetic tasks (geometric, spatial, and compositional) in both pure-text and multimodal formats to systematically evaluate compositional abilities.
- Introduction of RL-Ground, a reinforcement learning approach that integrates 'Caption-Before-Thinking' to promote visual-to-text alignment and 'Fine-Grained Progress Reward' to provide dense supervision at intermediate reasoning steps.
- Comparison of RL-Ground against Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches using Qwen2.5-VL models to assess their impact on compositional reasoning.

## Results

- A dramatic cross-modal performance gap was observed: models trained purely on text achieved near-perfect accuracy (**99.8%**), but performance dropped catastrophically (to **4.2%**) when tested on corresponding multimodal tasks.
- The proposed RL-Ground method improved multimodal compositional task performance to **52.8%** for a **7B** model, significantly outperforming standard SFT (as low as **0.6%**) and other RL methods (**31.2%**).
- RL-Ground consistently maintained high performance across out-of-distribution conditions, demonstrating robust generalization to tasks with altered objectives, unlike SFT which showed brittleness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial 'modality gap' exists, indicating that reasoning abilities acquired from text-only training do not automatically transfer to visual inputs in current Vision-Language Models.
> - Standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) can severely impair compositional abilities through catastrophic forgetting, despite improving performance on individual tasks.
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically with fine-grained progress rewards and explicit visual grounding (e.g., captioning before thinking), demonstrates effectiveness in improving multimodal compositional reasoning and out-of-distribution generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown remarkable progress in understanding and reasoning about visual content. However, a critical question remains: can these models truly compose different skills to solve complex, multi-step problems that require integrating visual perception with reasoning abilities? A recent study by researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong and Tencent Hunyuan Research systematically investigates this "compositional ability gap" in current VLMs.

The research addresses a fundamental limitation: while VLMs excel at individual tasks, they struggle to combine learned skills when faced with novel, complex scenarios that require both visual understanding and multi-step reasoning. This work introduces a systematic framework to diagnose and address these compositional limitations.

### 2. The Compositional Challenge in Vision-Language Models

Compositional reasoning represents the ability to combine known skills or concepts to solve new, more complex problems. In the context of VLMs, this involves several dimensions:

**Cross-Modal Composition**: Can reasoning abilities learned from text-only training transfer to visual inputs? For example, if a model learns geometric calculations from textual descriptions, can it apply the same logic to shapes presented in images?

**Cross-Task Integration**: Can independently learned visual reasoning skills be combined? If a model learns spatial reasoning (finding nearest objects) and geometric reasoning (calculating areas) separately, can it solve composite tasks requiring both skills?

**Out-of-Distribution Generalization**: Can compositional abilities adapt to novel task objectives? This tests whether models truly understand underlying principles rather than memorizing specific patterns.

The challenge is particularly acute for VLMs because they must seamlessly integrate visual perception with linguistic reasoning, a process that current training paradigms may not adequately address.

### 3. Methodology and ComPABench

To systematically investigate compositional abilities, the researchers developed **ComPABench**, a diagnostic benchmark designed with precise control over reasoning requirements and modality variations.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design

ComPABench consists of carefully constructed tasks with both pure-text and multimodal variants:

1. **Geometric Reasoning (Shape Area Task)**: Models compute total areas of shapes, either from textual descriptions or images with dimension labels
2. **Spatial Reasoning (Grid Position Task)**: Models identify grid positions of shapes closest to targets using Manhattan distance
3. **Compositional Task**: Combines both skills, requiring models to find the nearest shape to a target and calculate the combined area

#### 3.2. Data Characteristics

- **Synthetic Control**: Uses synthetic images (up to 1000x1000 pixels) to ensure precise ground truth and eliminate ambiguity
- **Structured Output**: Responses include reasoning traces in `<think>` blocks and final answers in `<answer>` blocks
- **Scale**: 4,000 training samples and 500 evaluation samples per individual task type
- **OOD Variants**: Modified objectives (e.g., finding farthest instead of nearest shapes) test generalization

### 4. Key Research Questions

The study is organized around three critical research questions:

**RQ1: Cross-Modal Compositional Ability**
Can reasoning abilities acquired through pure-text training compose with visual recognition to solve multimodal reasoning tasks?

**RQ2: Cross-Task Compositional Ability**
Can independently learned visual reasoning skills be integrated to tackle composite tasks requiring both capabilities?

**RQ3: Compositional OOD Generalization**
Can compositional ability generalize to out-of-distribution variants with altered task objectives?

### 5. Training Paradigms and RL-Ground

The research compares three post-training strategies using Qwen2.5-VL models:

#### 5.1. Standard Approaches

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Traditional approach minimizing negative log-likelihood of target sequences
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Using Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) with outcome-based rewards
3. **SFT-initialized RL**: Hybrid approach starting RL training from SFT checkpoints

#### 5.2. Proposed Solution: RL-Ground

The researchers propose **RL-Ground**, which addresses observed limitations through two key components:

**Caption-Before-Thinking**: Forces models to generate natural language descriptions of visual content before reasoning, promoting visual-to-text alignment.

**Fine-Grained Progress Reward**: Provides dense supervision at intermediate reasoning steps rather than only final answers.

The reward structure is illustrated in the following equation:

```
Total Reward = Progress Reward + Format Reward + Accuracy Reward
```

Where Progress Reward evaluates intermediate steps (e.g., correct area calculations), Format Reward ensures proper structure, and Accuracy Reward validates final answers.

### 6. Experimental Results

#### 6.1. Cross-Modal Performance Gap (RQ1)

The experiments reveal a dramatic performance gap between text-only and multimodal reasoning:

- Models trained only on pure-text achieve near-perfect accuracy (99.8% on grid position tasks)
- When tested on corresponding multimodal tasks, performance drops catastrophically (to 4.2%, a >94% drop)
- This demonstrates that textual reasoning skills do not automatically transfer to visual inputs

#### 6.2. Cross-Task Composition Challenges (RQ2)

The study reveals several critical findings about compositional reasoning:

**SFT Catastrophic Forgetting**: Standard SFT severely impairs compositional abilities, dropping accuracy from ~46-49% to as low as 0.6% for compositional tasks.

**RL Benefits**: RL training substantially improves compositional reasoning in pure-text settings (93% for 3B model) but struggles in multimodal contexts (31.2% for 7B model).

**RL-Ground Effectiveness**: The proposed method achieves significant improvements in multimodal compositional tasks (52.8% for 7B model).

#### 6.3. Out-of-Distribution Generalization (RQ3)

The OOD evaluation reveals:

- **SFT Brittleness**: Standard SFT shows inconsistent and often poor generalization to modified task objectives
- **RL Robustness**: RL-trained models generalize well to individual OOD tasks but struggle with compositional OOD scenarios
- **RL-Ground Consistency**: The proposed method maintains high performance across all OOD conditions, demonstrating robust understanding of underlying reasoning principles

### 7. Implications and Future Directions

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of multimodal AI:

#### 7.1. Key Insights

1. **The Modality Gap**: There exists a fundamental disconnect between textual and visual reasoning in current VLMs, requiring explicit bridging mechanisms.

2. **SFT Limitations**: Standard supervised fine-tuning can actually harm compositional abilities through catastrophic forgetting, despite improving individual task performance.

3. **RL Promise and Challenges**: While RL shows promise for compositional reasoning, it requires careful design to work effectively in multimodal settings.

#### 7.2. Practical Applications

The findings have immediate implications for developing more robust VLMs across various domains:

- **Educational AI**: Creating tutors that can reason about visual diagrams and textual explanations
- **Scientific Analysis**: Developing AI assistants for interpreting complex figures and data visualizations
- **Safety-Critical Systems**: Improving reliability in autonomous systems requiring integrated visual-linguistic reasoning

#### 7.3. Future Research Directions

The work opens several avenues for future investigation:

1. **Architectural Innovations**: Designing VLM architectures that explicitly promote cross-modal skill composition
2. **Training Objectives**: Developing reward structures that better capture compositional reasoning requirements
3. **Evaluation Frameworks**: Creating more comprehensive benchmarks for assessing compositional abilities in real-world scenarios

The systematic identification of the "compositional ability gap" and the proposed RL-Ground solution provide a foundation for building more versatile and robust multimodal reasoning systems. As VLMs continue to evolve, addressing these compositional challenges will be crucial for achieving human-level understanding and reasoning across modalities.
