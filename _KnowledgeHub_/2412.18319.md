# Mulberry: Empowering MLLM with o1-like Reasoning and Reflection via Collective Monte Carlo Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Collective Monte Carlo Tree Search (CoMCTS) to empower Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with step-by-step reasoning and reflection. The method generates a high-quality, tree-structured multimodal reasoning dataset (Mulberry-260k), leading to Mulberry models that demonstrate enhanced reasoning capabilities and improved performance on complex multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with complex problems requiring multi-step reasoning, often failing to generate explicit intermediate steps and relying on direct prediction.
- Traditional Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) methods face challenges when applied to MLLMs due to low search effectiveness (getting stuck in low-quality reasoning nodes) and poor search efficiency (requiring many iterations for computationally intensive MLLMs).
- MLLMs lack inherent reflective capabilities, making it difficult for them to recognize and correct their own erroneous reasoning steps during long reasoning chains.

## Method

- Introduces ==Collective Monte Carlo Tree Search (CoMCTS)==, a novel method that enables a group of MLLMs to collaboratively search for effective reasoning paths by jointly expanding candidate nodes and collectively evaluating and pruning erroneous ones.
- Develops a mechanism for ==reflective reasoning== by identifying 'negative sibling nodes' from the CoMCTS tree and constructing reflective reasoning paths to teach the MLLM to learn from its mistakes.
- Constructs ==Mulberry-260k==, a large-scale multimodal reasoning dataset with explicit, tree-structured reasoning nodes, and trains a series of 'Mulberry' MLLMs using ==Collective Supervised Fine-Tuning (CoSFT)== on this dataset, including an objective for reflective reasoning.

## Results

- CoMCTS achieved an **80.2%** search success rate with only **12.7** average search iterations, outperforming traditional MCTS (**63.8%** S.S.R., 42.1 A.S.I.) and other variants like Omega-MCTS (**66.2%** S.S.R., 24.3 A.S.I.).
- Mulberry models trained on the CoMCTS-generated Mulberry-260k dataset showed average performance improvements of **+4.2%** (Mulberry-7B) to **+11.0%** (Mulberry-8B) over their base models across 8 diverse multimodal benchmarks.
- Mulberry consistently outperformed other dedicated reasoning models (e.g., LLaVA-Reasoner-8B) on mathematical and multi-disciplinary benchmarks, and competed with state-of-the-art closed-source models like GPT-4o and Claude-3.5 Sonnet.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Collective Monte Carlo Tree Search (CoMCTS) significantly enhances search effectiveness and efficiency for MLLMs by leveraging multiple models to jointly expand reasoning paths and collectively identify and prune erroneous steps.
> - Training MLLMs on data generated by CoMCTS, which includes explicit step-by-step reasoning and reflective paths, leads to improved reasoning capabilities and allows models to self-correct during complex tasks.
> - High-quality, tree-structured multimodal reasoning datasets, like Mulberry-260k, are crucial for advancing MLLM intelligence beyond direct prediction to more robust, explainable, and human-like reasoning processes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), advanced reasoning techniques, and self-improvement mechanisms for AI. The broader landscape is characterized by a rapid evolution of MLLMs, which have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding and generating content across modalities (e.g., image and text). However, a critical limitation persists: while MLLMs excel at straightforward tasks, they often struggle with complex problems that demand in-depth, multi-step reasoning.

Current MLLMs typically operate in a "direct prediction" mode, providing concise final answers without explicitly detailing the intermediate reasoning steps. This approach, as the paper points out with a quote from Richard Feynman ("What I cannot create, I do not understand"), suggests a lack of true understanding. If an MLLM cannot *create* the intermediate steps, its "understanding" of a complex problem is superficial. This is where the paper's contribution becomes vital.

The work draws inspiration from recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly methods like OpenAI o1, which have successfully applied "tree search" techniques (akin to AlphaGo's Monte Carlo Tree Search, MCTS) to enable LLMs to reason by building a tree of intermediate thoughts and exploring effective reasoning paths. The intuitive idea of directly applying these MCTS methods to MLLMs, however, faces significant challenges:
1. **Search Effectiveness:** Traditional MCTS relies on self-bootstrapping, but MLLMs, without explicit intermediate reasoning training, tend to get "trapped in homogeneous low-quality nodes" within their own reasoning space, leading to low success rates.
2. **Search Efficiency:** MCTS typically explores one node at a time, making it computationally inefficient for resource-intensive MLLMs, requiring massive iterations.

"Mulberry" addresses these challenges by introducing "collective learning" into MCTS for MLLMs, termed Collective Monte Carlo Tree Search (CoMCTS). This positions the work as a significant step forward in developing MLLMs that can perform "o1-like" step-by-step reasoning and reflection. It pushes the boundary of MLLM capabilities from mere pattern recognition and direct prediction to more robust, explainable, and human-like reasoning processes, potentially bridging the gap between current MLLMs and truly intelligent problem-solving agents. The paper specifically claims to be the first to explore collective learning with MCTS for MLLMs, highlighting its novelty.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop an MLLM that understands and solves questions by learning to create each intermediate step of the reasoning involved until the final answer.** This goal is rooted in the belief, echoing Richard Feynman's dictum, that true understanding of complex problems necessitates the ability to articulate and construct the underlying reasoning process.

The motivations driving this objective are multifaceted:
- **Addressing MLLM Reasoning Limitations:** While MLLMs have shown impressive progress in various multimodal tasks, they consistently struggle with complex problems requiring deep, multi-step reasoning. Current models often fail to produce accurate answers for such tasks because they largely rely on "direct prediction" rather than generating explicit, well-defined intermediate reasoning steps. This limitation is a significant barrier to their broader adoption in critical applications.
- **Enhancing Understanding and Explainability:** By learning to generate step-by-step reasoning, MLLMs can move beyond black-box output, providing transparent and verifiable problem-solving processes. This not only improves their performance but also enhances user trust and allows for better error diagnosis.
- **Overcoming MCTS Applicability Challenges for MLLMs:** Traditional MCTS, successful in domains like game playing and recently in LLMs (e.g., OpenAI o1), is not directly applicable or efficient for MLLMs. The authors specifically identify two core challenges:
 - **Low Search Effectiveness:** MLLMs, due to their training without explicit reasoning steps, tend to generate unhelpful or repetitive intermediate thoughts when self-bootstrapping, leading to poor exploration of the reasoning space.
 - **Poor Search Efficiency:** The computational intensity of MLLMs makes traditional MCTS's single-step expansion per iteration prohibitively slow.
- **Enabling Reflective Capabilities:** Beyond just reasoning, the authors aim to instill "reflection" in MLLMs. This means enabling the model to recognize and correct its own erroneous reasoning steps, dynamically calibrating its trajectory towards a correct solution during long reasoning chains.
- **Creating Valuable Resources:** A key motivation is to contribute back to the research community by constructing a high-quality, tree-based multimodal reasoning dataset (Mulberry-260k) and a series of powerful MLLMs (Mulberry) capable of step-by-step reasoning and reflection.

In essence, the paper aims to fundamentally advance MLLM intelligence by equipping them with robust, efficient, and transparent reasoning and self-correction capabilities, moving them closer to human-like problem-solving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the "Mulberry" research lies in **Collective Monte Carlo Tree Search (CoMCTS)**, a novel learning-to-reason method designed specifically for MLLMs, and its application in dataset construction and model training.

#### 3.1. Collective Monte Carlo Tree Search (CoMCTS)

CoMCTS introduces the concept of *collective learning* into the traditional Monte Carlo Tree Search framework. Instead of a single MLLM self-bootstrapping, CoMCTS leverages knowledge from a *group* of multiple MLLMs ({π1, π2..., πK}) to collaboratively search for effective reasoning paths. The process is iterative, with each iteration comprising four key operations:

1. **Expansion:**
 - **Goal:** Generate diverse and complementary candidate subsequent reasoning nodes.
 - **Mechanism:** From the current leaf node, *each* MLLM in the collective group generates a potential reasoning path in parallel, extending towards a terminal node. This contrasts with traditional MCTS which typically expands only one path or a few single-step nodes.
 - **Benefit:** This joint expansion allows CoMCTS to build a unified reasoning tree encompassing diverse perspectives, preventing the search from getting stuck in low-quality or homogeneous reasoning spaces of a single model. It also significantly boosts search efficiency by generating multiple potential next steps simultaneously.

2. **Simulation and Error Positioning:**
 - **Goal:** Evaluate the potential value of the newly expanded candidate nodes and prune erroneous ones.
 - **Mechanism:** The collective group of MLLMs jointly evaluates each candidate reasoning node (s_ji). This evaluation function (R(s_ji)) aggregates judgments from all models. Nodes with evaluation scores below a certain threshold (t) are identified as erroneous and pruned along with their child nodes.
 - **Benefit:** Collective knowledge is crucial here as it's often easier for other models to spot errors made by one specific model. This mechanism enhances search effectiveness by filtering out unproductive paths early, reducing the exploration of flawed trajectories, and enabling "skipping" of multiple intermediate steps to find the last correct step as the next starting point.

3. **Backpropagation:**
 - **Goal:** Update the statistics of the reasoning nodes in the tree based on the simulation results.
 - **Mechanism:** A bottom-up update propagates from the leaf nodes (newly expanded and simulated) back to the root. Each node's visit count (N) and node value (V) are updated to reflect the quality of the paths passing through them. The node value incorporates the rewards (evaluations) of its child nodes from the simulation phase.

4. **Selection:**
 - **Goal:** Choose the most promising leaf reasoning node to be the starting point for the next search iteration.
 - **Mechanism:** The selection is guided by the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) value, which balances exploitation (choosing nodes with high current value) and exploration (choosing less-visited nodes). The node with the highest UCB is selected as the new current node.

These four operations are repeated until a correct reasoning path is found or a predefined maximum number of iterations is reached. The output of CoMCTS is a multimodal learning-to-reason data triplet: {Q, Y, S}, where Q is the question, Y is the identified effective reasoning path, and S is the constructed reasoning tree.

#### 3.2. CoMCTS for Reflective Reasoning

To enable the model to learn from mistakes, CoMCTS is extended to generate "reflective reasoning paths":
- **Identifying Negative Sibling Nodes:** From the constructed reasoning tree (S), the algorithm identifies "negative sibling nodes" for nodes on the effective reasoning path (Y). A negative sibling node (s_neg) for a node (s) is a node at the same hierarchical level under the same parent that has a significantly lower UCB value than (s), indicating it's a less promising or erroneous path.
- **Constructing Reflective Reasoning Path:** A reflective trajectory is formed by concatenating a sampled negative sibling node (s_neg), a reflection prompt (e.g., "The previous reasoning step is wrong and let’s rethink it again."), and the corresponding positive node (s). This trajectory is then integrated into the effective reasoning path Y to form a reflective reasoning path (Y_reflect). This process creates a quadruplet {Q, Y, Y_reflect, S}.

#### 3.3. Data Construction (Mulberry-260k) and Model Training (Mulberry)

- **Mulberry-260k Dataset:** Using the CoMCTS algorithm, the authors searched effective and reflective reasoning paths for 260,000 raw multimodal input questions. These questions span diverse domains including mathematics, figure understanding, science, and medical image understanding. The resulting dataset, Mulberry-260k, provides a "tree of rich, explicit and well-defined reasoning nodes for each question," serving as a valuable resource for training MLLMs in step-by-step reasoning and reflection. The dataset analysis shows flexible step counts, adapting to task complexity.
- **Mulberry Model Training:** A series of MLLMs, collectively named "Mulberry," are trained using **Collective Supervised Fine-Tuning (CoSFT)** on the Mulberry-260k dataset.
 - **L_CoSFT:** Standard SFT objective to maximize the log probability of the effective reasoning path (Y) given the question (Q).
 - **L_CoSFT-Re:** An additional SFT objective specifically for reflective reasoning, maximizing the log probability of the reflective reasoning path (Y_reflect) given the question (Q). This loss term allows the model to leverage negative information from the CoMCTS search process by learning to calibrate from erroneous nodes.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a novel collective tree search with a dual-objective fine-tuning strategy on a meticulously constructed dataset, enables the Mulberry models to achieve advanced reasoning and reflection capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in the paper demonstrate the significant superiority of the proposed CoMCTS method and the resulting Mulberry models across various benchmarks.

#### 4.1. CoMCTS Superiority in Search Effectiveness and Efficiency

- **Comparison with Other Tree Search Methods:** CoMCTS was benchmarked against traditional MCTS, ReST-MCTS, Omega-MCTS, and direct prediction (GPT-4o without tree search) on search success rate (S.S.R.) and average search iteration (A.S.I.).
 - GPT-4o (direct prediction): 58.2% S.S.R.
 - Traditional MCTS: 63.8% S.S.R., 42.1 A.S.I.
 - Omega-MCTS: 66.2% S.S.R., 24.3 A.S.I.
 - Iter-MCTS: 66.2% S.S.R., 24.3 A.S.I. (Note: The paper's Table 1 and Figure 1 (a) show Omega-MCTS and Iter-MCTS with slightly different numbers. The text indicates Iter-MCTS as 66.2% and 24.3 A.S.I., which is the one mentioned again in the Discussion section with Omega-MCTS having slightly higher S.S.R. but similar A.S.I. The image in Figure 1(a) shows Omega-MCTS at 65.6% and 36.3 A.S.I., while Iter-MCTS is 66.2% and 24.3 A.S.I. The Table 4 in the Discussion section clarifies these as ReST-MCTS and Omega-MCTS, showing 65.6% and 36.3 for ReST-MCTS, and 66.2% and 24.3 for Omega-MCTS). For consistency with the discussion section, I'll use those.
 - **CoMCTS:** Achieved **80.2% S.S.R.** with only **12.7 A.S.I.**
- **Key Insight:** CoMCTS demonstrated remarkable gains, increasing S.S.R. by over 20 percentage points compared to direct prediction and significantly reducing search iterations. This superiority is attributed to its joint expansion mechanism, which diversifies the search space, and the collective error positioning that efficiently prunes bad paths.
- **Ablation on Collective Learning:** The study showed that progressively adding more models to the collective group (starting from single GPT-4o, then adding Qwen2-VL-7B, LLaMA-3.2-11B, and Qwen2-VL-72B) consistently improved the search success rate, validating the effectiveness of collective knowledge in reasoning tree search.

#### 4.2. Mulberry Model Performance

Mulberry models, trained on the CoMCTS-searched Mulberry-260k dataset, exhibited superior performance across a suite of 8 diverse and challenging benchmarks, including MathVista, MMStar, MMMU, ChartQA, DynaMath, HallBench, MM-Math, and MME.

- **Comparison with Baselines (Involved in CoMCTS):**
 - **Mulberry-7B:** Showed a +4.2% average improvement over its base model, Qwen2-VL-7B.
 - **Mulberry-11B:** Achieved a +7.5% average improvement over its base model, LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct.
 - This validates the effectiveness of the reasoning paths searched by CoMCTS in improving the capabilities of the models that contributed to the search.

- **Generalization to Uninvolved Models:**
 - **Mulberry-2B:** Enhanced Qwen2-VL-2B with a +5.4% average gain.
 - **Mulberry-8B:** Improved LLaVA-NeXT-8B with a substantial +11.0% average gain.
 - This demonstrates the strong generalization capability of the Mulberry-260k dataset, proving its value even for models not part of the initial collective search process.

- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art Reasoning-Response Models:**
 - Mulberry consistently outperformed other dedicated reasoning models like LLaVA-Reasoner-8B, Insight-V-8B, and LLaVA-COT-11B on mathematical (MathVista) and multi-disciplinary (MMMU) benchmarks. For instance, Mulberry-LLaVA-8B surpassed LLaVA-Reasoner-8B by +5.7% on MathVista. This highlights the advantage of CoMCTS-generated, rich, explicit, and flexible reasoning nodes.

- **Comparison with General State-of-the-Art MLLMs:**
 - Mulberry, across its various sizes (2B, 7B, 8B, 11B), generally outperformed most open-source MLLMs and achieved competitive results against powerful closed-source models like GPT-4o and Claude-3.5 Sonnet. This underscores its "outstanding abilities in step-by-step reasoning and reflection."

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Reflection Data

- Incorporating reflection data during training (via L_CoSFT-Re) led to a 0.8% performance improvement on MathVista (from 50.9% to 51.7%). This small but consistent gain validates the complementary contribution of learning from reflective reasoning paths.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Reasoning Data Distribution (Mulberry-260k)

- The dataset exhibited flexible reasoning step distributions: an average of 7.5 steps overall, 6.8 steps for simpler chart-related tasks, and 8.9 steps for complex mathematical/logical tasks. This indicates that CoMCTS is capable of generating reasoning trajectories with appropriate lengths based on problem complexity, allowing the trained Mulberry models to "think less and faster" for simple questions and "think more and slower" for complex ones.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Comparison

- Qualitative examples show that baseline MLLMs (LLaVA-NeXT-8B, Qwen2-VL-7B) often generate short, incorrect predictions. In contrast, Mulberry models produce rich, explicit, and well-defined reasoning steps, leading to correct answers through a comprehensive understanding of the problem.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that CoMCTS is a highly effective and efficient method for generating high-quality reasoning data, and that training MLLMs on this data significantly boosts their step-by-step reasoning and reflection capabilities, making them competitive with and often superior to existing state-of-the-art models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Mulberry" research makes several significant contributions that could profoundly impact the development and application of Multimodal Large Language Models:

1. **Pioneering Collective Learning for MLLM Reasoning:** To the best of the authors' knowledge, this is the first work to introduce and successfully apply the concept of collective learning to Monte Carlo Tree Search for MLLMs. This novel approach overcomes fundamental limitations of traditional MCTS when applied to MLLMs, addressing issues of search effectiveness (avoiding homogeneous low-quality nodes) and efficiency (parallel expansion, multi-step error positioning). This opens up a new paradigm for self-improvement and data generation in complex AI systems.

2. **Advancing MLLM Reasoning Capabilities (o1-like):** By enabling MLLMs to perform "o1-like" step-by-step reasoning and reflection, Mulberry moves beyond simple direct prediction. This capability is crucial for tackling complex, real-world problems that require in-depth understanding, logical deduction, and error correction. This brings MLLMs closer to exhibiting more human-like cognitive processes, making their outputs more reliable and trustworthy.

3. **Creation of a Valuable Public Dataset (Mulberry-260k):** The release of Mulberry-260k, a large-scale multimodal dataset with explicit, well-defined, tree-structured reasoning nodes, is a substantial contribution to the research community. Such high-quality, meticulously curated datasets are vital for training advanced reasoning models, and Mulberry-260k serves as a benchmark and a valuable resource for future research in step-by-step reasoning and reflection for MLLMs. Its dynamic step distribution based on problem complexity further enhances its utility.

4. **Development of Robust MLLMs (Mulberry Series):** The trained Mulberry models demonstrate empirically superior performance on various challenging benchmarks compared to many state-of-the-art open-source and even some competitive closed-source MLLMs. This validates the effectiveness of the CoMCTS-generated data and the collective supervised fine-tuning approach. These models represent a new class of MLLMs with enhanced reasoning capabilities that can serve as strong baselines for future research.

5. **Enhanced Explainability and Trustworthiness:** By generating explicit reasoning steps, Mulberry models offer greater transparency into their decision-making processes. This inherent explainability is critical for deploying AI in sensitive domains (e.g., healthcare, finance, legal), fostering greater trust and enabling easier debugging and auditing of AI systems.

6. **Broader Impact on AI Research:** The principles of CoMCTS—leveraging collective intelligence and structured search to generate high-quality data for training complex models—could be applicable beyond MLLMs, inspiring similar approaches in other challenging AI domains where generating explicit reasoning traces or complex task solutions is difficult. It offers new insights into how large foundational models can bootstrap and refine their own problem-solving skills.

In conclusion, "Mulberry" is a significant step towards more intelligent, robust, and explainable MLLMs. By effectively integrating collective learning into Monte Carlo Tree Search, the authors have provided a powerful framework, a valuable dataset, and demonstrably capable models that push the boundaries of multimodal reasoning.
