# SpatialReward: Verifiable Spatial Reward Modeling for Fine-Grained Spatial Consistency in Text-to-Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialReward introduces a verifiable spatial reward model for text-to-image generation that enhances fine-grained spatial consistency. This method combines prompt decomposition, expert-driven visual grounding, and VLM-based chain-of-thought reasoning, leading to improved adherence to complex spatial relations and object attributes.

## Problem

- Text-to-image (T2I) models often generate images with fine-grained spatial inconsistencies, such as incorrect object positions, orientations, or relationships, despite overall visual plausibility.
- Existing reward models for T2I optimization focus on global semantics and coarse visual quality, overlooking intricate spatial details and consequently rewarding spatially inaccurate images.
- Current evaluation benchmarks lack the granularity to comprehensively assess complex spatial relationships, object orientation, 3D positioning, and accurate rendered text placement in T2I outputs.

## Method

- A ==Prompt Decomposer== module (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL) translates free-form prompts into structured constraints to guide fine-grained spatial verification.
- Utilizes specialized expert detectors (e.g., open-set object detection, OCR, color/orientation models) to provide objective and verifiable sub-rewards for individual object attributes and text content/localization.
- Integrates verified visual grounding into a Vision-Language Model-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning process to infer complex spatial relations and provide robust reward estimation, reducing hallucination.

## Results

- SpatialReward improved 'Overall' scores on GenEval (e.g., SD3.5-M from **0.67** to **0.95**) and significantly on the new SpatRelBench (e.g., SD3.5-M from **0.23** to **0.42**), demonstrating enhanced spatial consistency across various dimensions.
- Human studies confirmed SpatialReward's high alignment with human judgments on spatial correctness, achieving Spearman's ρ of **0.63** and Pearson's r of **0.61**, surpassing other reward models.
- Ablation studies revealed that expert detection contributed most significantly to accuracy improvements, underscoring the importance of verifiable, domain-specific visual grounding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining structured prompt decomposition with verifiable expert detection and VLM-based CoT reasoning creates a robust framework for fine-grained spatial reward modeling in T2I generation.
> - Specialized expert detectors are crucial for objective and quantitative verification of object attributes and text properties, significantly improving accuracy over general-purpose VLM assessments alone.
> - Incorporating exclusion constraints and introducing a comprehensive benchmark like SpatRelBench are essential for evaluating and enhancing models' ability to handle complex spatial relationships effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image (T2I) generation has witnessed rapid advancements, particularly with diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion and FLUX. Reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, especially those based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have become instrumental in optimizing these generative models to produce images that align more closely with human preferences and textual prompts. A critical component in these RL-based approaches is the reward model (RM), which provides feedback on visual quality and semantic alignment to guide policy-gradient optimization.

Existing reward models generally fall into two categories:
- **Structured evaluation methods**, such as Geneval, rely on fixed-format prompts and predefined object detectors. While precise within their narrow scope, they demonstrate limited generalization to complex, compositional, and free-form prompts encountered in open-ended generation tasks.
- **Holistic evaluation methods**, often based on vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIPScore, ImageReward, or UnifiedReward, can process arbitrary prompts and capture global semantics. However, these methods frequently overlook fine-grained spatial relationships, leading to generated images that appear visually plausible but contain inaccuracies in object positioning or attribute binding. This limitation results in models rewarding scenes that are spatially incorrect.

This research addresses a notable gap in the T2I landscape: the absence of reward models capable of robustly and verifiably evaluating fine-grained spatial consistency. Current T2I models struggle with preserving precise spatial relationships among objects, object orientation, 3D positioning, and accurate placement of rendered text. Furthermore, existing evaluation benchmarks, while progressing beyond generic metrics like FID and IS, still tend to oversimplify complex spatial relations. Benchmarks like GenEval and T2I-CompBench are object-centric but often lack the granularity to assess nuanced inter-object relations or textual placement comprehensively.

SpatialReward positions itself at the intersection of RL-based T2I optimization and detailed spatial understanding. It aims to bridge the gap between global semantic alignment and fine-grained spatial fidelity by developing a verifiable, spatially-aware reward model. By combining prompt decomposition, expert-driven visual grounding, and VLM-based chain-of-thought reasoning, this work seeks to integrate the precision of structured methods with the flexibility of holistic approaches. Concurrently, the introduction of SpatRelBench provides a more challenging and discriminative benchmark specifically designed to assess these overlooked aspects of spatial consistency, thereby contributing to both model development and evaluation methodologies in T2I generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the SpatialReward research are driven by identified deficiencies in current text-to-image (T2I) generation models and their evaluation mechanisms.

**Core Problem:** Current T2I models, despite significant advancements in visual quality and global semantic alignment, frequently produce images with fine-grained spatial inconsistencies. Objects may be present and recognizable, but their positions, orientations, or relationships with other objects within the scene are often inaccurate relative to the prompt. This issue degrades the realism of generated images and compromises faithful adherence to detailed prompt semantics.

**Specific Motivations and Objectives:**

1. **Addressing Fine-Grained Spatial Inconsistencies:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing reward models (RMs) for RL-based T2I optimization primarily focus on global semantics and coarse visual quality. They neglect intricate spatial relationships, leading to situations where generated images appear generally plausible but are spatially incorrect (e.g., an object described as "left of" another might appear "right of" it).
 - **Objective:** To develop a verifiable reward model, SpatialReward, explicitly designed to evaluate and enforce fine-grained spatial layouts in generated images.

2. **Overcoming Prompt-Side Rigidity of Structured Evaluation:**
 - **Motivation:** Structured evaluation methods (e.g., Geneval) are limited by their reliance on fixed-format prompts and predefined object detectors. They cannot generalize effectively to the complex, compositional, and free-form natural language prompts commonly used in open-ended T2I generation tasks.
 - **Objective:** To enable SpatialReward to process arbitrary free-form prompts by introducing a "Prompt Decomposer" component. This component extracts core entities, attributes, and spatial metadata, normalizing the input for subsequent fine-grained evaluation.

3. **Mitigating Vision-Side Overlooking by Holistic Evaluation:**
 - **Motivation:** Holistic evaluation methods (e.g., CLIPScore, VLM-based RMs) can handle diverse prompts and capture global semantics. However, without dedicated fine-grained spatial verification, they fail to detect positional errors and often reward images that are visually coherent but spatially inaccurate. Current state-of-the-art VLMs also struggle with compositional prompts, attribute binding, counting, and complex logical reasoning in a verifiable manner.
 - **Objective:** To incorporate a collaborative verification mechanism drawing inspiration from rule-based, verifiable rewards. This involves using specialized, expert detectors (for objects, attributes, and text) to provide accurate, factual, and verifiable visual grounding of object positions and attributes, thereby reducing hallucinations and increasing the objectivity of the reward signal.

4. **Enabling Complex Spatial Relationship Assessment via Reasoning:**
 - **Motivation:** Assessing complex spatial relations (e.g., "above" vs. "on," 3D arrangements) requires more than simple geometric checks or isolated attribute verification. A sequence of reasoning steps is needed to link detected facts to relative positions and overall layouts.
 - **Objective:** To integrate the verified visual grounding into a chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning process within a vision-language model. This explicit reasoning aims to provide robust reward estimation for complex layouts and offer greater flexibility than conventional rule-based approaches.

5. **Establishing a Comprehensive Spatial Evaluation Benchmark:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing spatial benchmarks are often limited in their scope, typically focusing on basic positional or color attributes. They frequently ignore critical dimensions such as object orientation, multi-object 3D positioning, complex spatial arrangements, and the accurate placement of rendered text.
 - **Objective:** To introduce SpatRelBench, a new benchmark designed to cover a richer set of spatial relationships, thereby providing a more thorough and discriminative tool for evaluating the spatial consistency capabilities of T2I models.

In summary, the overarching motivation is to advance the spatial fidelity of T2I models by developing a novel, verifiable reward mechanism and a comprehensive evaluation benchmark that explicitly account for fine-grained spatial consistency, ultimately leading to more accurate, controllable, and human-aligned image generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SpatialReward framework operates through a multi-stage pipeline designed to provide verifiable, fine-grained spatial rewards for text-to-image generation, integrated into an RL training loop. Additionally, the paper introduces a new benchmark, SpatRelBench, for comprehensive spatial evaluation.

#### 3.1. SpatialReward Model Pipeline

SpatialReward consists of three main stages: Prompt Decomposition, Fine-grained Verifiable Rewards using expert detectors, and Spatial Chain-of-Thought Reasoning.

**Stage 1: Prompt Decomposition**
- **Purpose:** To transform diverse free-form text prompts into a structured set of constraints that can be precisely verified in the generated image. This step addresses the "prompt-side rigidity" of previous structured evaluation methods.
- **Mechanism:** A `Prompt Decomposer (D)` is employed, which is a fine-tuned `Qwen2.5-VL-7B` model. This model takes a free-form prompt `P` as input and outputs a structured constraint set `C = D(P) = (tag, C_inc, C_exc)`.
 - `tag`: Denotes the primary evaluation category (e.g., counting, orientation, spatial relation).
 - `C_inc`: Contains "inclusion constraints" specifying desired object categories, quantities, attributes (e.g., color, orientation, depth order), spatial relations, and textual inscriptions.
 - `C_exc`: Contains "exclusion constraints" specifying undesirable elements or relationships.
- **Training:** The `Prompt Decomposer` was fine-tuned on a dataset of approximately 100,000 multi-object metadata instances. These instances explicitly defined attributes, counts, spatial relations, and associated text for subjects. `GPT-4o` was used to generate diverse natural language prompts from this metadata, creating (prompt, metadata) pairs for supervised training.

**Stage 2: Fine-grained Verifiable Rewards**
- **Purpose:** To obtain objective and quantitatively accurate sub-rewards for individual object attributes and text content/localization by leveraging specialized, domain-specific expert detectors. This step addresses the "vision-side overlooking" of holistic VLM-based methods.
- **Mechanism:** The structured constraints from the Prompt Decomposer are fed into a set of expert detectors to verify properties on the generated image `I`.
 - **Object Attribute and Presence Reward:**
 - **Object Detection:** For each object category in an inclusion constraint `c`, an open-set object detector (e.g., YOLO-World, Grounding DINO) is applied to `I` to yield candidate bounding boxes `D_c`. A confidence threshold `τ_det` is used to filter `B_c`, and its cardinality `ˆN_c` is used.
 - **Presence Reward:** `R_presence(c) = 1` if `ˆN_c > 0`, `0` otherwise.
 - **Count Reward:** `R_count(c) = exp(-|ˆN_c - N*c|)`, where `N*c` is the target count.
 - **Color Reward:** `R_color(c) = sim_color(C_det, C*)`. `C_det` (detected color) is obtained via a CLIP-based classifier evaluating cropped object regions against prompt templates (object class + candidate color). `sim_color` measures similarity to the target color `C*`.
 - **Orientation Reward:** `R_ori(c) = 1` if `|θ_det - θ*| ≤ δ_θ`, `0` otherwise. `θ_det` is from an orientation-sensitive model, `θ*` is the target, `δ_θ` is tolerance.
 - **Depth Reward:** `R_depth(c) = exp(-|d_rank - d*rank|)`. `d_rank` is inferred from monocular depth estimation, `d*rank` is the target depth ordering.
 - **Text Content and Localization Reward:**
 - **OCR:** For prompts requiring text rendering, a global OCR model (e.g., PaddleOCR) extracts detected text strings `T'_j` and their bounding boxes `B'_j`.
 - **Reward Calculation:** `R_text(T*, B_obj) = max_j [sim(T*, T'_j) * IoA(B'_j, B_obj)]`. `sim` measures lexical similarity, and `IoA` (Intersection over Area) quantifies spatial containment of the text box `B'_j` within the target object's bounding box `B_obj`.

**Stage 3: Spatial Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning**
- **Purpose:** To infer complex spatial relations between multiple entities, which are challenging for simple rule-based geometric checks, by integrating verified attributes with higher-level reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** A `Qwen2.5-VL` model serves as the `Chain-of-Thought (CoT)` reasoning backbone (`F_vlm`).
 - **Input Construction:** A CoT prompt `P_CoT` is constructed comprising: (1) the target spatial relation `r` from the `Prompt Decomposer`, (2) the detected bounding boxes `B_A` and `B_B` for the involved entities, and (3) all attribute rewards (`R_pres`, `R_ori`, `R_depth`, `R_text`) for both entities from Stage 2.
 - **Reasoning Process:** The VLM is guided to reason step-by-step, interpreting attribute rewards, performing geometric analysis, and inferring whether the relation `r` holds. This explicit grounding with verifiable signals helps reduce hallucination.
 - **Reward Output:** The VLM's output, including a reasoning trace and a final score, is parsed by `P_score` to yield `R_spatial`.
 - **Aggregation:** The total spatial reward `R_total` is computed as the sum of `R_spatial(c)` for all inclusion constraints `c ∈ C_inc`, minus the sum of `R_spatial(c)` for all exclusion constraints `c ∈ C_exc`. This penalizes undesired relations.

#### 3.2. SpatRelBench Benchmark

- **Purpose:** To provide a comprehensive and fine-grained benchmark for evaluating spatial consistency in T2I models, addressing limitations of existing benchmarks.
- **Dimensions:** SpatRelBench covers five primary evaluation dimensions:
 1. **Complex Spatial Relations:** Relative positioning and arrangement of multiple objects.
 2. **Object Orientation:** Whether objects face the correct direction.
 3. **Three-Dimensional Relations:** Depth ordering and 3D layout consistency.
 4. **Text Position Accuracy:** Correct localization of rendered text relative to associated objects (using OCR).
 5. **Text Counting Consistency:** Matching the quantity of rendered text across multiple objects to prompt specifications.
- **Data Construction:**
 - **Categories:** Extends object categories beyond COCO-80 to include ImageNet-1k and Objects365 classes for broader coverage.
 - **Prompt Generation:** Prompts are generated using `Gemini-2.5-Pro` and then manually validated to ensure diversity and correctness.
 - **Size:** Approximately 2,000 annotated entries are currently released.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Domain-specific expert models are employed to score each dimension. For each sub-task, a binary decision (correct/incorrect) is recorded, and the overall accuracy is calculated.

#### 3.3. Training Configuration

- **RL Framework:** Flow-GRPO, an RL approach built upon GRPO.
- **Base T2I Models:** Stable Diffusion 3.5 (SD3.5-M) and FLUX1-dev.
- **Hyperparameters:** Sampling timestep T=10 (training), T=40 (evaluation); group size G=24; noise level a=0.7; image resolution 512x512.
- **Parameter-Efficient Tuning:** LoRA (rank r=32, scaling factor α=64).
- **Regularization:** KL regularization coefficient β=0.04.
- **Hardware:** 16 NVIDIA L20 GPUs.
- **Baseline Reward Models:** For comparison, SpatialReward is evaluated against TextOCR, PickScore, Qwen2.5-VL, ImageReward, and UnifiedReward, all trained under identical conditions on 100k spatial-relation prompts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate SpatialReward and SpatRelBench yielded several findings across quantitative, qualitative, human alignment, and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison on Spatial-Consistency Benchmarks

Evaluation on the standard GenEval benchmark and the newly introduced SpatRelBench demonstrated improvements with SpatialReward.
- **GenEval (80-Obj):** Integrating SpatialReward into the SD3.5-M backbone resulted in an improvement in the "Overall" score from 0.67 to 0.95. For FLUX1-dev, the "Overall" score increased from 0.76 to 0.97. These improvements were observed across various metrics, including single-object (S-Obj), two-object (T-Obj), counting (Cnt), color attributes (Attr-C), and object positions (Pos).
- **SpatRelBench (1k-Obj):** On this more challenging benchmark designed for fine-grained spatial reasoning, SpatialReward showed notable gains. With SD3.5-M, the "Overall" score increased from 0.23 to 0.42. For FLUX1-dev, the "Overall" score rose from 0.28 to 0.46. Significant improvements were observed in dimensions such as Text Position Accuracy (P-Text), Text Counting Consistency (C-Text), Complex Spatial Relations (Cpx), Orientation (Ori), and 3D Spatial Relations (3DRel). For instance, SD3.5-M with SpatialReward improved 3DRel from 0.36 to 0.55 and Ori from 0.07 to 0.26.
- **Consistency:** The improvements were consistent across both Stable Diffusion and FLUX base models, indicating the robustness and generalizability of SpatialReward in enforcing spatial coherence and semantic fidelity. SpatRelBench effectively revealed performance gaps not evident under broader evaluation protocols.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on General-Purpose Metrics

To assess the broader impact beyond spatial consistency, SpatialReward was evaluated using general-purpose metrics:
- **Overall Fidelity:** Wise and DPG scores were used. For SD3.5-M, Wise improved from 0.45 to 0.46, and DPG from 83.96 to 84.08. For FLUX1-dev, Wise improved from 0.50 to 0.52, and DPG from 83.84 to 84.19.
- **Visual Appeal:** Aesthetic scores were measured. SD3.5-M with SpatialReward showed a slight decrease (5.39 to 5.23), while FLUX1-dev showed a slight increase (6.13 to 6.15).
- **Prompt-Image Alignment:** PickScore was utilized. SD3.5-M improved from 22.34 to 22.52, and FLUX1-dev improved from 22.45 to 23.22.
These results suggest that SpatialReward optimization generally maintains or improves overall visual quality and semantic alignment while enhancing spatial reasoning, without significant perceptual loss.

#### 4.3. Human Alignment on Spatial Consistency

A human study involving 500 prompt-image pairs from SpatRelBench, labeled by annotators for spatial correctness, was conducted to compare SpatialReward with other reward models:
- **Correlation:** SpatialReward demonstrated the highest correlation with human judgments. Spearman's ρ was 0.63, and Pearson's r was 0.61, surpassing other models (e.g., UnifiedReward: ρ=0.51, r=0.49; CLIPScore: ρ=0.42, r=0.40).
- **Accuracy:** At a fixed threshold of τ=0.8, SpatialReward achieved the highest classification accuracy of 0.79, outperforming VisionReward (0.74), UnifiedReward (0.72), ImageReward (0.70), and CLIPScore (0.68).
These findings indicate that SpatialReward's explicit modeling of fine-grained spatial relations produces assessments more consistent with human perception, positioning it as a reliable metric for complex spatial tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study assessed the contribution of SpatialReward's key components: Exclusion Constraints, Expert Detection, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning.
- **Effect of Exclusion Constraints:** Removing exclusion constraints (penalizing undesired configurations) led to a reduction in accuracy across all benchmarks (e.g., GenEval dropped from 95.2 to 90.5, SpatRelBench from 37.1 to 25.9). This suggests that penalizing negative examples contributes to robustness and prevents reward hacking.
- **Effect of Expert Detection:** Omitting the expert detection stage, which provides fine-grained verification of object presence, attributes, and rendered text, resulted in the most significant accuracy decline (e.g., GenEval from 95.2 to 70.3, SpatRelBench from 37.1 to 21.6). This substantiates the hypothesis that verifiable, domain-specific detectors are crucial for objective spatial reasoning, surpassing the capabilities of general-purpose VLMs alone.
- **Effect of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** Eliminating the CoT reasoning process, which integrates verified attributes with geometric analysis for complex spatial inferences, caused moderate accuracy reductions (e.g., GenEval from 95.2 to 94.2, SpatRelBench from 37.1 to 27.9). Qualitative examples illustrated CoT's role in distinguishing nuanced relations like "above" from "inside" or resolving complex multi-object arrangements that rule-based heuristics cannot handle effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on SpatialReward and SpatRelBench carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of text-to-image (T2I) generation and broader multimodal AI.

**Enhanced T2I Generation Fidelity:**
SpatialReward directly addresses a major limitation in current T2I models: their struggle with fine-grained spatial consistency. By integrating verifiable reward signals from expert detectors and leveraging chain-of-thought reasoning, the proposed method allows T2I models to generate images that not only achieve high visual quality and global semantic alignment but also precisely adhere to complex spatial relations specified in prompts. This capability leads to more accurate, controllable, and instruction-following image synthesis, which is critical for applications requiring precise visual narratives.

**Improved Evaluation Benchmarking:**
The introduction of SpatRelBench fills a critical gap in T2I evaluation. Existing benchmarks often lack the granularity to assess nuanced spatial relationships, including object orientation, 3D positioning, and the accurate placement of rendered text. SpatRelBench offers a comprehensive and challenging evaluation protocol that can effectively discriminate between models based on their spatial reasoning capabilities. This benchmark will serve as a valuable tool for researchers to identify weaknesses, track progress, and drive the development of more spatially intelligent T2I models.

**Advancing Verifiable AI Systems:**
This work demonstrates a practical approach to building verifiable reward models. By combining the strengths of structured, rule-based verification (via expert detectors) with the flexibility and reasoning capabilities of vision-language models (via CoT), SpatialReward offers a framework for grounded and interpretable assessment. This hybrid approach contributes to the growing field of verifiable AI, where objective evidence rather than solely probabilistic or black-box judgments supports model decisions, fostering greater trustworthiness in generative systems.

**Guidance for Reinforcement Learning in Generative Models:**
The consistent improvements observed when incorporating SpatialReward into RL training for T2I models (Stable Diffusion and FLUX) highlight the potential of fine-grained, verifiable objectives for optimizing complex generative processes. This suggests that carefully designed, specific reward signals can effectively guide model policies toward desired characteristics that are difficult to achieve with general-purpose preference models. This could inspire further exploration into specialized reward engineering for various aspects of generative AI, such as style transfer, compositionality, or temporal coherence in video generation.

**Broader Applications in Multimodal AI:**
The methodology of prompt decomposition, expert-based grounding, and CoT reasoning is not confined to T2I generation. These principles could be adapted to other multimodal tasks requiring fine-grained understanding and generation, such as controllable video generation, scene graph generation, visual question answering with spatial reasoning, or even robotics where precise spatial understanding is paramount. The general framework for breaking down complex instructions into verifiable facts and then employing reasoning holds potential for numerous applications demanding robust multimodal intelligence.

**Enhanced Human-AI Alignment:**
The finding that SpatialReward aligns more closely with human judgments of spatial consistency is significant. This indicates that models trained with SpatialReward are likely to produce outputs that better match human expectations and intentions regarding spatial arrangements, leading to a more intuitive and satisfying user experience. This alignment is crucial for the wider adoption and impact of T2I technologies in creative, design, and educational domains.

In conclusion, SpatialReward and SpatRelBench represent a methodological advancement in enforcing fine-grained spatial consistency in T2I generation. The work underscores the importance of verifiable and spatially-aware reward models for building more accurate, controllable, and human-aligned generative AI, paving the way for future research into reliable and interpretable optimization objectives across diverse multimodal tasks.
