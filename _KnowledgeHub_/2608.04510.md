# GUARD: Grounding Uncertainty and Ablation-Based Risk Detection for Diffusion-Based VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> GUARD introduces a test-time failure detection method for diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies by measuring how functionally grounded generated actions are in salient visual and language evidence. Developed by Bosch Research India, the system achieved an average unseen-task ROC-AUC of 88.84%, outperforming existing runtime monitors by 5.73 percentage points, with minimal computational overhead.

## Problem

- VLA policies frequently produce unreliable actions in novel environments or out-of-distribution conditions without explicit indications of potential failure.
- Existing runtime monitors often rely on embedding-space novelty or action consistency, which do not directly assess if generated actions are functionally grounded in the specific multimodal evidence.
- Developing task-specific failure detectors is impractical for generalist VLAs due to the vast number of potential unseen tasks and the infeasibility of collecting labeled success/failure rollouts.

## Method

- **GUARD** identifies influential visual and language tokens in the VLM's key-value (KV) cache using a ==saliency backward pass== based on the generated action.
- It constructs ==counterfactual KV caches== by ablating (replacing with modality-specific means) the most salient visual and language entries, then compares the action head's denoising response from the original and ablated caches.
- A diagnostic stream, including sensitivity, modality bias, attention entropy, and grounding efficiency, is generated, calibrated online with episode-specific baselines, and processed by a lightweight temporal classifier using ==functional conformal prediction== for alarm thresholds.

## Results

- GUARD achieved an average unseen-task ROC-AUC of **88.84%**, outperforming the strongest competing runtime monitor (FIPER) by **5.73 percentage points**.
- It demonstrated strong balanced accuracy and earlier detection times on various benchmarks, raising alarms after approximately one-fifth to three-fifths of the rollout duration.
- The method incurred a modest computational overhead (**1.14x** for Pi0 and **1.27x** for SmolVLA), making it suitable for real-time deployment without significant performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A reliable action sequence maintains a strong functional dependence on salient multimodal evidence, while weak dependence, even with plausible actions, indicates a potential grounding failure.
> - Probing how the action generation mechanism responds to the removal of its most influential visual and language evidence provides a robust and transferable failure signal.
> - Focused intervention by ablating a small percentage (e.g., top 10%) of the most salient KV-cache entries is critical for effective failure detection, outperforming random or least-salient ablations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, facilitated by large-scale robot datasets and vision-language pretraining, have advanced robot capabilities to interpret natural language instructions and perform diverse manipulation tasks across various embodiments. Architectures such as RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and Octo have demonstrated the potential of Transformer-based policies, often integrating pretrained vision-language backbones with generative action heads, notably those based on diffusion or flow models (e.g., Diffusion Policy, Pi0, SmolVLA, Alpamayo-R1). These generative models offer expressive mechanisms for continuous, multimodal robot behavior.

Despite these advancements, the deployment of VLAs faces vulnerabilities. Policies can exhibit unreliable actions in novel environments, under varying object appearances, due to compounding control errors, or in task conditions inadequately represented in training data. Such situations may lead to the execution of incorrect actions without any explicit indication of unreliability. This necessitates timely failure detection to enable intervention strategies, such as halting execution, activating fallback mechanisms, or requesting human assistance, before errors become irrecoverable.

Failure detection for generalist VLAs presents unique challenges. An unfamiliar observation or instruction does not inherently signify a failure, as the policy might successfully generalize to situations outside its training distribution. Developing task-specific detectors is impractical for generalist policies due to the vast number of potential unseen tasks, making the collection of labeled success and failure rollouts infeasible.

Existing runtime monitors address aspects of this problem through various methods:
- **Embedding-space novelty or likelihood scores:** These assess whether test-time observations or actions deviate from training distributions.
- **Temporal action consistency and progress monitoring:** These methods track the coherence and progression of actions during execution.
- **Observation novelty combined with sampled action uncertainty:** This approach considers both unfamiliar inputs and the variability in generated actions.
- **Supervised probes over internal VLA features:** These train auxiliary models to predict failure using the internal representations of the VLA.
- **Epistemic uncertainty estimation in flow-based VLAs:** This involves measuring disagreement among ensembled velocity fields, typically requiring multiple policy models.

While these approaches provide evidence regarding distribution shift, representation state, action variability, or model disagreement, they often do not directly assess whether the generated action remains functionally grounded in the specific visual and language evidence defining the current task. GUARD aims to address this gap by focusing on the functional coupling between action generation and salient multimodal evidence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce GUARD, a test-time failure detection method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies that utilize diffusion-based action heads. The core aim is to measure how effectively generated actions are *grounded* in the visual and language evidence presented by the task, without requiring modifications to the pretrained VLA policy itself.

The central hypothesis guiding GUARD's development is that a reliable action sequence should maintain a strong functional dependency on the salient multimodal evidence that defines what the robot is supposed to perceive and execute. Conversely, if the diffusion action head becomes insensitive to critical visual or language tokens, the resulting action may appear plausible or "confident" but lack genuine grounding in the task context. This weak coupling is hypothesized to be a robust indicator of potential failure.

This hypothesis is motivated by qualitative observations, as illustrated in examples where saliency patterns differ between successful and failed rollouts. For instance, in a successful task requiring object placement, visual and language saliency initially concentrates on the first target object and then shifts appropriately to the second target. In a failed rollout, while initial saliency might be correct, it may fail to shift to the subsequent target, indicating a breakdown in grounding despite the action sequence continuing. Such instances suggest that current actions might not be adequately supported by the evidence most relevant to the task, even if the policy produces seemingly smooth movements.

To operationalize this hypothesis, GUARD aims to:
1. **Estimate token influence:** Identify which token-indexed entries within the final Vision-Language Model (VLM) key-value (KV) cache exert the most influence on the generated action. This KV cache preserves token positions and integrates multimodal information, making it suitable for token-level attribution and intervention.
2. **Construct counterfactuals:** Create modified KV caches by ablating (replacing with modality-specific means) the most salient visual and language entries.
3. **Compare denoising responses:** Evaluate the action head's denoising response using both the original and these counterfactual caches from the same noisy action state. The difference in denoising responses is intended to isolate the policy's dependence on the removed evidence.
4. **Derive diagnostics:** Based on these comparisons, generate a compact diagnostic stream, including:
 - **Sensitivity:** Quantifies the change in the denoising response under ablation.
 - **Attention entropy:** Measures the distribution of influence across tokens.
 - **Modality bias:** Compares the policy's reliance on visual versus language evidence.
 - **Grounding efficiency:** Relates sensitivity to attention entropy.
5. **Online calibration and classification:** Calibrate these diagnostics online using an episode-specific baseline and process them with a lightweight temporal classifier. Functional conformal prediction is employed to derive time-dependent alarm thresholds, allowing for user-controlled false-alarm tolerance.

The overarching motivation is to develop a transferable failure signal that is effective across various policies, tasks, embodiments, and domains by directly probing the functional dependence of action generation on its multimodal conditioning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GUARD is a test-time failure detection framework designed for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies with diffusion-based action heads. The methodology consists of three main stages: VLA rollout with saliency estimation and counterfactual probing, generation of a diagnostic stream, and online temporal classification with conformal prediction.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

At each control step *t*, the robot perceives an observation *o_t* comprising camera inputs (*I_t*), a language instruction (*ℓ_t*), and the robot state (*s_t*). A pretrained VLA policy *π_θ* predicts an action chunk *A_t* (a sequence of *H* actions of dimension *d_a*). The VLM component of the policy encodes the multimodal observation into a final Key-Value (KV) cache, *Z_t* = [*z_1*..., *z_N*], where each *z_i* = (*k_i*, *v_i*) is a token-indexed entry. This KV cache serves as the conditioning context for the diffusion action head, which generates the action chunk through iterative denoising.

#### 3.2. Saliency over Token-Indexed KV-Cache

To identify influential KV-cache entries, GUARD performs a saliency backward pass. An action-level scalar objective, Φ(*A_t*) = ||vec(*A_t*)||^2, which measures the squared L2-norm of the vectorized action chunk, is defined. The saliency *g_i* for each token position *i* is computed as the squared L2-norm of the gradient of Φ(*A_t*) with respect to *z_i* (∂Φ(*A_t*)/∂*z_i*|^2). This score quantifies the local influence of each token's KV-cache entry on the generated action.

To ensure both visual and language modalities are considered, salient positions are selected independently within their respective token index sets (*M_vis*, *M_lang*). Specifically, *S_m* comprises the top *ρ_m* fraction of entries with the highest saliency scores within modality *m* (visual or language), where *ρ_vis* and *ρ_lang* are modality-specific saliency fractions. The combined set of selected salient positions is *S* = *S_vis* ∪ *S_lang*.

#### 3.3. Counterfactual Mean-Ablation Probes

After identifying salient positions, GUARD constructs counterfactual caches to probe the policy's dependence on this evidence. For each modality *m*, a modality mean representation, *¯z_m*, is computed as the average of all KV-cache entries within that modality. This mean replacement strategy ensures that the ablated representations remain on a similar scale to the original features while removing token-specific information.

Three ablated conditioning states are generated:
- *Z^-vis_t*: Salient visual positions in *Z_t* are replaced by *¯z_vis*.
- *Z^-lang_t*: Salient language positions in *Z_t* are replaced by *¯z_lang*.
- *Z^-both_t*: Salient positions from both visual and language modalities are replaced by their respective means.

All these original and ablated cache variants are evaluated from the *same noisy action state* (*˜n_K-1*) in a batched single-step denoising probe. This ensures that any observed differences in responses are attributable solely to the ablated conditioning.

Based on these responses, two primary diagnostics are computed:
- **Sensitivity (S_t)**: Defined as ||*r^+_t* - *r^-both_t*||^2 / (||*r^+_t*||^2 + *ε*). It measures the normalized change in the denoising response when salient visual and language evidence is jointly ablated. A low *S_t* indicates that the action is weakly affected by the identified evidence, signaling a potential grounding failure.
- **Modality Bias (B_t)**: Calculated as ||*r^+_t* - *r^-vis_t*||^2 / (||*r^+_t* - *r^-lang_t*||^2 + *ε*). This ratio indicates whether visual or language ablation induces a greater change in the denoising response, providing insight into the dominant modality influencing the action.

#### 3.4. Entropy-Normalized Grounding and Online Calibration

Complementing sensitivity, GUARD incorporates an attention-based diagnostic.
- **Attention Entropy (E_t)**: This measures the entropy of the final-layer cross-attention probabilities from action queries to conditioning tokens, averaged over attention heads. It quantifies how broadly influence is distributed across tokens, serving as a concentration statistic.
- **Grounding Efficiency (G_t)**: Defined as *S_t* / (*E_t* + *ε*). High *G_t* signifies that the policy is sensitive to salient evidence while maintaining relatively concentrated attention, indicating efficient grounding.

To account for variations in *S_t* across different policies, tasks, and episodes, an episode-level baseline, *C*, is calibrated during an initial window. This baseline is the average of entropy-adjusted sensitivity values (*S_u* / (*E_u* + *ε*)^γ) within the calibration window. An **adaptive threshold (θ_t)** is then set as *μ* * C * (*E_t* + *ε*)^γ, where *μ* is a safety margin. A **low-sensitivity event (LSE)**, *b_t*, is recorded when *S_t* falls below this adaptive threshold. A calibration status *c_t* indicates if calibration is ongoing.

#### 3.5. Final GUARD Diagnostic Stream

At each action-chunk generation step, GUARD produces a seven-dimensional diagnostic vector: *x_t* = [*S_t*, *B_t*, *E_t*, *G_t*, *θ_t*, *c_t*, *b_t*].

#### 3.6. Window-Based Online Failure Classifier

To mitigate noise in single-step diagnostics, a lightweight temporal classifier (e.g., GRU, LSTM, or Transformer) is trained on short, fixed-length windows of these diagnostic vectors. The classifier is trained with a "from_first_lse" target, meaning it predicts failure if the timestep is at or after the first detected LSE in a failed episode. At inference time, the classifier processes the most recent diagnostic window, yielding timestep probabilities (*p_t*), which are then aggregated into a window-mean score (*q_mean*).

**Functional Conformal Prediction** is employed to calibrate time-dependent alarm thresholds (*τ_α(w)*) from classifier score trajectories obtained from successful seen-task calibration rollouts. This method provides distribution-free thresholding, enabling a user-controlled false-alarm tolerance.

#### 3.7. Efficiency and Deployability

The computational overhead introduced by GUARD is minimal, involving one saliency backward pass and one batched single-step counterfactual probe. This results in a 1.14x overhead for Pi0 and 1.27x for SmolVLA, with the temporal classifier's cost being negligible, making GUARD suitable for real-time deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GUARD was conducted across five policy–benchmark settings, including Pi0, SmolVLA, and Alpamayo-1.5 on LIBERO, SimplerEnv, MetaWorld, and PhysicalAI-AV datasets. The evaluation used task-held-out splits to assess generalization to unseen tasks and compared GUARD against several baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Failure–Success Separability (ROC-AUC)

- **Overall Performance:** GUARD achieved an average unseen-task ROC-AUC of 88.84%, outperforming the strongest competing runtime monitor (FIPER) by 5.73 percentage points and the learned VLA-feature detector (SAFE) by 12.62 points. It ranked first on four of the five unseen settings and second on the remaining MetaWorld setting.
- **Seen-Task Performance:** GUARD maintained strong performance on seen tasks, remaining within 0.19 points of the best seen-task average (89.99% for GUARD vs. 90.18% for Cosine). The seen-unseen performance gap for GUARD was also significantly smaller compared to learned-feature and nearest-neighbor baselines.
- **Generalization:** The performance gains were consistent across different VLA architectures (Pi0, SmolVLA), diverse embodiments (LIBERO, SimplerEnv, MetaWorld), and a distinct domain (PhysicalAI-AV for autonomous driving).
- **Comparison to Baselines:** Embedding-space detectors (Euclidean, Cosine) performed well on seen tasks but experienced substantial performance drops on held-out tasks. SAFE showed improved robustness on unseen tasks compared to fixed-distance scores. FIPER was the strongest competitor among runtime monitors, particularly on MetaWorld. STAC/STAC-Single showed less consistent performance, indicating that action-chunk instability alone is not a universally robust failure signal.
- **Attribution of Robustness:** The robustness of GUARD is attributed to its direct measurement of whether the action head's generation process remains functionally dependent on salient multimodal evidence, distinguishing it from methods that primarily rely on embedding distances or action variability.

#### 4.2. Online Failure Detection (Balanced Accuracy and Detection Time)

- **Balanced Accuracy:** GUARD demonstrated strong balanced-accuracy performance in three of the five settings (Pi0 Libero, SmolVLA Libero, and SmolVLA MetaWorld), indicating effective differentiation between successes and failures while balancing true positives and true negatives. It was consistently competitive across all settings, even when policy architecture, embodiment, and domain varied.
- **Detection Time:** GUARD raised alarms earlier in Pi0 Libero, SmolVLA Libero, and MetaWorld, detecting failures after approximately one-fifth to three-fifths of the rollout duration. For instance, on Pi0 Libero, it provided warnings about ten percentage points earlier than FIPER while achieving higher balanced accuracy. On MetaWorld, it detected failures at approximately 20% progress with the best accuracy. FIPER exhibited slightly earlier detection times in SimplerEnv and PhysicalAI-AV, but GUARD remained a close competitor in terms of accuracy in those settings.

#### 4.3. Importance of Saliency-based Ablation

- **Selection Mode:** The default configuration, which ablates the top 10% most salient entries within each modality, achieved the highest average performance for both seen and unseen tasks. Ablating the most salient entries significantly outperformed random and least-salient selection, improving unseen-task ROC-AUC by 8.10 percentage points over random and 9.84 points over least-salient ablation. This finding suggests that a focused intervention on action-relevant evidence is crucial for the detector's effectiveness.
- **Ablation Percentage:** Increasing the ablation percentage from 10% to 30% or 50% generally reduced performance, particularly on unseen tasks. The 10% configuration provided the most reliable balance, indicating that ablating too much of the cache can obscure the diagnostic signal by corrupting excessive task context.

#### 4.4. Efficiency and Deployability

- GUARD incurred a modest computational overhead: 1.14x for Pi0 and 1.27x for SmolVLA per action-generation step. This overhead results from one saliency backward pass and one batched single-step counterfactual probe. The lightweight temporal classifier contributes negligibly to the cost. This indicates that GUARD is computationally efficient and suitable for deployment in real-world systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GUARD represents a shift in the approach to VLA failure detection, moving beyond measures of input novelty, representation distance, or action variability to focus on the concept of *functional grounding*. The research demonstrates that probing how an action generation mechanism responds when its most influential visual and language evidence is removed provides a robust and transferable failure signal.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to offer an informative signal across a wide range of tasks, policies, embodiments, and domains. This generalizability is a critical aspect for the deployment of generalist robot policies, which frequently encounter unseen conditions. By providing timely and reliable online warnings through temporal aggregation and functional conformal prediction, GUARD enhances the safety and trustworthiness of autonomous systems.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robot Safety and Reliability:** By enabling early detection of grounding failures, GUARD facilitates proactive intervention strategies such as stopping execution, invoking fallback behaviors, or requesting human assistance. This capability can prevent costly errors, mitigate risks in human-robot interaction, and improve overall system reliability in real-world applications.
- **Improved Generalization for Robot Learning:** The method addresses a key limitation of existing failure detectors, which often struggle with out-of-distribution or unseen tasks. GUARD's performance across diverse settings suggests a more fundamental understanding of policy failure, applicable even when task semantics change.
- **Actionable Diagnostics:** The diagnostic stream (sensitivity, attention entropy, modality bias, grounding efficiency) provides not just a binary failure signal but also richer information about the nature of the grounding breakdown, potentially aiding in debugging and policy improvement.
- **Practical Deployability:** The minimal computational overhead makes GUARD viable for integration into existing VLA pipelines without necessitating significant hardware upgrades or compromising real-time performance.

**Limitations Identified by the Authors:**
- **Applicability to Diffusion-Based VLAs:** GUARD is currently designed for VLAs with diffusion-based action heads and does not directly apply to autoregressive VLAs. Extending it to other generative action models is a future research direction.
- **VRAM Usage for Saliency:** The saliency backward pass requires retaining intermediate activations of the action head, which increases peak VRAM consumption compared to standard policy inference. Future work could explore memory-efficient gradient computation or selective probing to reduce this cost.

Despite these limitations, GUARD offers a promising framework for making advanced VLA policies more robust and deployable, laying foundational work for grounding-based failure detection in complex embodied AI systems.
