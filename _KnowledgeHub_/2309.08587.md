# Compositional Foundation Models for Hierarchical Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> HiP (Compositional Foundation Models for Hierarchical Planning) introduces a robotic planning system that integrates distinct pre-trained foundation models for language, visual planning, and action control. It employs an iterative refinement mechanism to ensure consistency across these modalities, enabling robots to solve complex, long-horizon tasks in novel environments by leveraging broad pre-trained knowledge and reducing the need for extensive paired robotics data.

## Problem

- Developing AI agents, particularly robots, capable of performing long-horizon decision-making tasks in novel environments that necessitate hierarchical reasoning.
- The inherent scarcity and high cost associated with collecting large, diverse, and perfectly paired multi-modal (visual, language, action) datasets for robotic learning.
- Limitations of existing monolithic foundation models or heavily fine-tuned large language models (LLMs) in robotics due to data expense and difficulties in scaling.

## Method

- A three-tier hierarchical planning system that factorizes decision-making into abstract task planning, geometric visual planning, and low-level action execution.
- Leverages separately trained, modality-specific foundation models: LLMs for abstract subgoal generation, video diffusion models for physically plausible visual trajectory generation, and inverse dynamics models for inferring low-level actions.
- Implements iterative refinement mechanisms, including learned classifiers, to ensure consistency and physical plausibility across the different hierarchical levels and disparate foundation models.

## Results

- HiP significantly outperformed all baselines, including goal-conditioned policies and other planners, on various long-horizon table-top manipulation tasks, and demonstrated strong combinatorial generalization to unseen task variations.
- Pre-training the video diffusion model on Ego4D and initializing the inverse dynamics model with VC-1 weights led to higher success rates and drastically reduced data requirements for fine-tuning (e.g., **10x** less data for inverse dynamics).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the iterative refinement mechanisms, particularly the visual grounding of LLM-generated subgoals, are critical for HiP's performance and robust planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A compositional approach, integrating independently trained expert foundation models, offers a scalable and data-efficient alternative to monolithic models for complex robotic tasks.
> - Iterative refinement between hierarchical planning levels is crucial for maintaining consistency and feasibility across abstract goals, visual plans, and executable actions, preventing 'hallucinations' or unexecutable plans.
> - Pre-training modality-specific models on broad internet-scale data significantly reduces the reliance on expensive, task-specific robotics datasets, enhancing generalization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a central challenge in artificial intelligence, particularly in robotics: enabling agents to perform **long-horizon tasks in novel environments** that require complex **hierarchical reasoning**. This problem is critical for moving beyond narrowly defined, short-term robotic operations towards more general-purpose, intelligent agents.

The authors situate their work within the burgeoning paradigm of "foundation models," which have revolutionized fields like Natural Language Processing (NLP) and computer vision. The core idea of foundation models is that a single, large model, pre-trained on vast amounts of data, can be adapted to a wide range of downstream tasks with minimal fine-tuning.

The research landscape for robotics and foundation models currently presents several approaches and challenges:
1. **Monolithic Foundation Models for Robotics:** Some prior works (e.g., RT-1, Gato) attempt to build a single, large neural network by collecting extensive paired visual, language, and action data. HiP argues that this approach is *expensive and difficult to scale* due to the scarcity of such rich, multi-modal robotics data compared to readily available internet text or image data.
2. **Fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) for Embodied Tasks:** Other approaches (e.g., PaLM-E) fine-tune LLMs with visual and language inputs for specific robot demonstrations. HiP points out that this still relies on expensive, paired vision-language robotics data, and often involves *closed-source LLMs* (like GPT-3.5/4 or PaLM), making fine-tuning impossible for many researchers.
3. **Compositional Approaches:** Several works explore combining different pre-trained models. *SayCan* uses an LLM as a high-level planner to sequence *skill-based value functions*. *Flamingo* combines vision and language models through joint fine-tuning on paired datasets. *Socratic models* integrate zero-shot multimodal reasoning using language or iterative consensus. HiP builds on this compositional idea but distinguishes itself by proposing a system that leverages *separately trained expert foundation models* (language, vision, action) and focuses on a robust *iterative refinement mechanism* to ensure consistency across these models by maximizing the product of their learned distributions.
4. **Diffusion Models for Planning:** The paper also connects to a growing body of work using diffusion models for continuous-space planning (e.g., Diffuser, UniPi). HiP explicitly states that it builds on UniPi by integrating it with LLMs to construct longer-horizon continuous video plans.

HiP's core contribution is its *compositional* approach, which offers a scalable alternative to monolithic or heavily fine-tuned models. By assembling pre-trained expert models for different modalities (language, vision, action) and introducing an iterative refinement process, HiP aims to reduce the need for costly, paired multi-modal robotics data, thereby addressing a major bottleneck in developing general-purpose robotic agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop an AI agent capable of **solving novel and long-horizon decision-making tasks in unfamiliar environments** that necessitate **hierarchical reasoning**. This means going beyond simple, single-step commands to executing complex sequences of actions guided by abstract goals.

The motivations driving this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Addressing the Data Scarcity Problem in Robotics:** The primary motivation stems from the high cost and difficulty of collecting large, diverse, and perfectly paired multi-modal (visual, language, action) datasets for robotics. Unlike the abundance of internet-scale text and images, rich robotics demonstration data is scarce. HiP seeks a scalable alternative that minimizes reliance on such expensive datasets.
2. **Leveraging Existing Expert Foundation Models:** Rather than building a single, monolithic foundation model for decision-making from scratch, the authors aim to capitalize on the recent successes of individually trained, powerful foundation models in specific modalities:
- **Large Language Models (LLMs):** Excel at abstract, symbolic reasoning and plan generation.
- **Video Diffusion Models:** Proficient in generating high-quality, physically plausible visual trajectories.
- **Inverse Dynamics Models:** Capable of inferring low-level actions from visual observations.
 The motivation is to compose these specialized "experts" to collectively solve complex robotic tasks.
3. **Ensuring Hierarchical Consistency:** A critical motivation is to maintain consistency across the different levels of hierarchical reasoning. An abstract plan must be physically plausible at the visual level and executable at the control level. Naively chaining independently generated outputs can lead to inconsistencies and planning failures. The authors are motivated to develop a mechanism that enforces this self-consistency.
4. **Enabling Generalization to Novel Tasks:** The goal is not just to solve seen tasks but to generalize to novel environments and combinatorial variations of tasks (e.g., new object arrangements, new subtask combinations). A compositional approach, leveraging broad prior knowledge from internet data, is hypothesized to facilitate this generalization.
5. **Efficiency and Accessibility:** The proposed approach aims to be computationally efficient by avoiding large model fine-tuning and by not requiring access to model weights (working with API access). This makes it more practical and accessible for broader research and application.

In essence, the authors are motivated to build a more scalable, data-efficient, and generalizable robotic planning system by intelligently composing and iteratively refining outputs from existing, modality-specific foundation models, thereby overcoming the limitations of current monolithic or heavily fine-tuned approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HiP (Compositional Foundation Models for Hierarchical Planning) is designed as a three-tier hierarchical planning system that leverages distinct, pre-trained foundation models for different levels of abstraction. A crucial element of its design is an **iterative refinement mechanism** to ensure consistency between these disparate models.

The decision-making process is factorized into three primary levels:

1. **Task Planning via Large Language Models (LLM):**
- **Objective:** Decompose a high-level language goal (`g`) into a sequence of abstract language subgoals (`w_i`).
- **Model:** A pre-trained Large Language Model (specifically GPT3.5-turbo) is used. It leverages its vast internet-trained knowledge to understand task semantics and generate logical subgoal sequences.
- **Approach:** The LLM is prompted with examples of domain-specific goal-subgoal sequences to adapt it to the task domain.
- **Consistency with Visual Planning (Iterative Refinement):** A key challenge is that an LLM-generated subgoal might be semantically plausible but visually infeasible given the current environment. To address this, HiP uses a **learned multi-class classifier** (`f_phi`) to visually ground the LLM.
 - The LLM first generates multiple candidate subgoals.
 - The classifier `f_phi` (parameterized as an MLP on top of a ResNet-18 vision encoder and a Flan-T5-Base language encoder) takes the current observation (`x_i,1`), the overall goal (`g`), and the set of candidate subgoals. It then selects the *most visually plausible* subgoal by implicitly estimating the likelihood of the observation given each subgoal. This acts as feedback to guide the LLM's output towards executable plans without requiring computationally expensive video generation for each candidate.

2. **Visual Planning with Video Generation:**
- **Objective:** Generate a physically plausible observation trajectory (`τ_x`) for a given language subgoal (`w_i`) and current observation (`x_i,1`). This forms a "geometric" or "visual" plan.
- **Model:** A **latent space video diffusion model** (based on PVDM) is employed. Diffusion models are powerful generative models known for synthesizing high-quality images and videos.
- **Approach:** The video diffusion model is pre-trained on a large-scale text-to-video dataset (Ego4D, which provides rich priors for physically plausible motions) and then fine-tuned on task-specific video datasets. The model generates videos conditioned on the subgoal (encoded by Flan-T5-Base) and the initial observation.
- **Consistency with Action Planning (Iterative Refinement):** A visually plausible video plan might still be difficult or impossible to execute with low-level actions. To ensure action feasibility, HiP integrates feedback from the action planning module during the video diffusion's denoising process.
 - A **binary classifier** (`g_psi`) is trained to distinguish between observation trajectories that lead to high-likelihood action trajectories (from the `D_video` dataset) and infeasible ones (created by shuffling frames).
 - During video generation, the gradients from `g_psi` (which operates on the video's latent representation) are used to bias the denoising process. This "classifier-guided diffusion" steers the generated video trajectory towards sequences that are more easily executable by the action model, maximizing the joint likelihood of the video and the subsequent actions.

3. **Action Planning with Inverse Dynamics:**
- **Objective:** Infer a sequence of low-level actions (`τ_a`) from the generated observation trajectory (`τ_x`). This handles the visual-motor control.
- **Model:** An **inverse dynamics model** (`p_psi`) parameterized by a vision transformer (ViT-B).
- **Approach:** The model is initialized with weights from VC-1 (a vision model pre-trained on egocentric internet images with a masked-autoencoding objective), providing strong visual priors. It is then fine-tuned on a task-specific dataset (`D_inv`) consisting of paired observation and robot state trajectories. The model predicts robot states (joint angles, gripper state) from consecutive observation pairs (`x_i,t`, `x_i,t+1`), and actions are derived from these state differences.

The entire system operates in a loop: the LLM proposes a subgoal (refined by `f_phi`), the video diffusion model generates a visual plan (refined by `g_psi`), and the inverse dynamics model executes actions. After executing actions for one subgoal, the process repeats for the next. This tightly integrated, iteratively refined hierarchical structure is the core of HiP's methodology.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate HiP's efficacy and adaptability across three distinct long-horizon table-top manipulation tasks: `paint-block`, `object-arrange`, and `kitchen-tasks`.

1. **Superior Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks:** HiP significantly outperforms all baselines, including goal-conditioned policies (Transformer BC, Gato), action planners (Trajectory Transformer, Action Diffuser), and a video planner (UniPi), even when these baselines are provided with *oracle subgoals*. This highlights the importance of HiP's hierarchical structure, the task planning component (outperforming UniPi), and the visual planning component (outperforming action planners). It also surpasses SayCan, particularly noting SayCan's limitations in domains without pre-defined primitives (like `kitchen-tasks`).

2. **Strong Combinatorial Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** Crucially, HiP's performance remains robust when evaluated on *unseen* long-horizon tasks, which feature novel combinations of object colors, categories, or subtasks. This demonstrates its ability to generalize compositionally, a vital attribute for real-world robotics.

3. **Significant Benefits of Pre-training:**
- **Video Diffusion Model:** Pre-training the video diffusion model on the Ego4D dataset consistently leads to higher success rates and lower Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) scores (indicating higher quality, more realistic video generation). This benefit becomes even more pronounced as the amount of task-specific training data decreases, underscoring the value of broad internet-scale pre-training for visual priors.
- **Inverse Dynamics Model:** Initializing the inverse dynamics model with VC-1 weights (pre-trained on egocentric images) drastically reduces the data requirements for fine-tuning. For `paint-block` and `object-arrange`, it achieves the performance of a randomly initialized model trained on 10K trajectories with just 1K trajectories. Similar gains are observed in `kitchen-tasks`. This finding is crucial for data-efficient robot learning.

4. **Critical Role of Iterative Refinement:**
- **Task Plan Refinement (LLM grounding):** Ablation studies show that the visual grounding of the LLM via the learned classifier (`f_phi`) is *critical* for HiP's performance. Without it, the LLM generates subgoals inconsistent with the robot's visual observations, leading to planning failures.
- **Visual Plan Refinement (Action feasibility):** While task plan refinement is more impactful, visual plan refinement (biasing video generation with action feasibility from `g_psi`) also contributes positively to HiP's performance, albeit by a smaller margin.

5. **Learned Classifier Outperforms Frozen VLM for LLM Grounding:** Comparing the learned classifier (`f_phi`) against a frozen pre-trained Vision-Language Model (MiniGPT-4) for visual grounding of the LLM reveals that while VLMs can perform comparably in simpler domains (`paint-block`), the learned classifier significantly outperforms them in more visually complex environments (`object-arrange`). This is attributed to VLM's limitations in recognizing uncommon objects and occasional "hallucinations" of object presence in novel scenarios.

6. **Subgoal Granularity Matters (Appendix E.3):** Experiments indicate that there is an optimal level of subgoal granularity. "Standard" or "more granular" subgoals (involving a pick-and-place operation, or just a pick/place) yield similar success rates. However, "less granular" subgoals (two pick-and-place operations) or very coarse goals (entire trajectory from LLM like UniPi) degrade performance, likely because the visual planner struggles with longer, more complex visual generation or too short steps.

7. **Video Diffusion for Visual Planning is Key (Appendix E.4):** Replacing the video diffusion model with a recurrent state space model (RSSM) as the visual planner (`HiP (RSSM)`) results in reduced performance, especially in visually complex domains. This highlights the advantages of diffusion models for generating high-quality, diverse visual plans.

8. **Computational Runtime (Appendix F):** The majority of HiP's planning time is consumed by the visual planning component.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Compositional Foundation Models for Hierarchical Planning" offers several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of robotics and artificial intelligence:

1. **Paradigm Shift Towards Compositional AI:** The paper champions a **compositional approach** to building general-purpose intelligent agents, moving away from the "one model to rule them all" philosophy. By integrating multiple, independently trained expert foundation models (for language, vision, and action), HiP demonstrates a scalable and data-efficient method to tackle complex, long-horizon tasks. This strategy is particularly powerful in robotics, where collecting truly "foundation-scale" paired multi-modal data is currently intractable.

2. **Addressing the Data Bottleneck in Robot Learning:** One of the most critical challenges in robot learning is the prohibitive cost and effort of acquiring large, diverse, and labeled datasets. HiP mitigates this by leveraging vast, readily available internet data for pre-training modality-specific models (LLMs, video diffusion, inverse dynamics via egocentric vision) and then using minimal task-specific data for fine-tuning. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for developing capable robotic systems.

3. **Robust Hierarchical Planning with Consistency:** The introduction of **iterative refinement mechanisms** for ensuring consistency across abstract linguistic plans, geometric visual plans, and low-level action execution is a crucial innovation. This principled approach prevents "hallucinations" or infeasible plans that can arise from naively chaining outputs of independent models, thereby making the planning process more robust and reliable for real-world deployment.

4. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** By pre-training on broad, diverse datasets and integrating knowledge from different modalities, HiP demonstrates strong **combinatorial generalization** to novel task compositions and unseen environments. This ability to handle variations in objects, arrangements, and subtasks is essential for developing robots that can adapt to the unpredictability of human environments.

5. **Blueprint for Future Embodied AI Systems:** HiP provides a clear architectural blueprint for developing future embodied AI systems. The authors explicitly state their belief that this approach will be substantially cheaper to train and will ultimately result in more capable and general-purpose decision-making systems. This work paves the way for integrating even larger and more capable future foundation models in vision and action, and potentially expanding to other modalities like touch and sound.

6. **Advancing Diffusion Models for Planning:** By demonstrating the effectiveness of video diffusion models for visual planning and refining their outputs for action feasibility, the paper further validates the utility of generative models like diffusion in control and decision-making contexts.

In conclusion, HiP represents a significant step forward in making long-horizon robotic planning more practical and generalizable. Its compositional architecture, emphasis on data efficiency through leveraging pre-trained expert models, and robust consistency mechanisms offer a compelling direction for the future of embodied artificial intelligence.
