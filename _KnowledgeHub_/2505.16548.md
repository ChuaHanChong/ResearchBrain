# Incremental Sequence Classification with Temporal Consistency

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UiPath and UCL developed Temporal-Consistency (TC-λ) loss for incremental sequence classification, allowing models to make accurate predictions as sequences unfold. This method, inspired by temporal-difference learning, enhanced predictive accuracy across various text tasks and significantly improved efficiency for large language model generation verification by enabling early assessments.

## Problem

- Current sequence classification methods typically predict only at sequence completion or focus on determining the earliest reliable prediction time, not on maximizing accuracy across all unfolding prefixes.
- Delaying predictions until full sequences are observed incurs significant real-world costs and computational inefficiencies, particularly in large language model (LLM) generation and verification.
- Existing LLM verification techniques using direct cross-entropy often provide noisy training signals for early sequence prefixes, hindering efficient early assessment.

## Method

- A novel loss function, ==Temporal-Consistency== (TC-λ), is introduced, extending temporal-difference learning principles to multiclass classification with cross-entropy loss.
- The TC-λ loss enforces a temporal-consistency condition, requiring predictions at any prefix to align with the expected predictions from subsequent prefixes, thus regularizing predictions across timesteps.
- A hyperparameter "lambda" 	0	1	 controls the trade-off between aligning with immediate future predictions and relying on the final observed class label, paralleling TD(lambda) algorithms.

## Results

- The TC-λ method consistently achieved superior predictive accuracy compared to baseline methods across various text classification datasets, with notable gains for early sequence prefixes.
- It demonstrated significant computational efficiency, with a smaller OPT-**125M** model trained with TC-λ matching the performance of much larger OPT-**1.3B** models trained conventionally.
- For LLM verification on GSM8K, TC-λ verifiers enabled earlier and more accurate correctness predictions, leading to a 23-**33%** reduction in generated tokens for "boosted best-of-N" decoding while maintaining answer accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incremental sequence classifiers should naturally exhibit temporal consistency, meaning predictions at a given time step should align with expected predictions from the next step.
> - Enforcing this temporal consistency during training via a TD-inspired loss improves data efficiency and robustness, yielding better predictions, especially for shorter, less informative prefixes.
> - The proposed method can enable smaller models to achieve performance on par with much larger, conventionally trained models, indicating significant computational and data efficiency benefits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the problem of incremental sequence classification, a variant of the fundamental machine learning task of classifying sequences. In this incremental setting, predictions are required at various points as a sequence unfolds, rather than solely at its completion. This contrasts with traditional sequence classification, which typically focuses on a single prediction for the entire sequence, and also with "early classification" methods that aim to determine when sufficient information has been observed to make a reliable prediction. The current work focuses on maximizing accuracy across all prefixes, irrespective of the decision-making timing.

The core contribution draws inspiration from temporal-difference (TD) learning, a key methodology in reinforcement learning (RL) used for estimating value functions. While classical TD learning primarily deals with scalar values and squared error losses, this work extends the underlying principle of temporal consistency to the multiclass classification setting using cross-entropy loss. This positions it in relation to distributional RL, which also considers distributions of returns, but typically not in a classification context. Prior applications of TD-like ideas have been seen in survival analysis and early event prediction, but this paper specifically tailors the approach for classification.

A significant application area for incremental classifiers is in verifying large language model (LLM) generations. Existing LLM verifiers, often framed as binary classification, predominantly use direct cross-entropy (DCE) loss against the final observed outcome, sometimes with soft targets derived solely from observed outcomes. A notable exception includes work that frames verification as a regression problem using TD learning with a squared loss. This paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing a TD-style approach specifically adapted for classification outcomes, thereby contributing a novel methodology to the growing field of LLM verification and efficient LLM deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective method for incremental sequence classification. This involves training a predictive model that can accurately classify any prefix of a sequence, providing updated predictions as new elements are revealed. The model is specifically designed to maximize classification accuracy across all timesteps (prefixes), rather than just focusing on the final sequence prediction or deciding *when* to classify.

The motivation for this incremental classification capability stems from several practical considerations:

1. **Cost of Waiting for Full Sequences**: In many real-world domains, delaying a prediction until the entire sequence is observed incurs significant costs. Examples include:
 - **Healthcare**: Early diagnosis or intervention can be critical, where waiting for all diagnostic information might lead to worse patient outcomes or missed opportunities.
 - **Finance**: Timely market analysis or fraud detection can provide a competitive advantage or prevent losses, where the cost might be opportunity or financial.
 - **User Experience**: In interactive applications, providing real-time feedback or predictions as a user types or interacts with a system can enhance the experience.

2. **Computational Efficiency in LLM Applications**: The deployment of large language models often involves generating extensive text sequences. This generation process can be computationally intensive and costly. If an incremental classifier (or "verifier") can accurately assess the correctness or quality of an LLM generation after only a few initial tokens, it allows for early termination of unpromising generations. This enables a more favorable trade-off between the accuracy of LLM outputs and the associated computational resources required for generation.

The core intellectual motivation for the proposed method arises from the observation that a calibrated predictive model for incremental sequence classification should inherently satisfy a "temporal-consistency" condition. This condition, expressed as $p(y | s_t) = E_{p(s_{t+1}|s_t)}[p(y | s_{t+1})]$, states that the class distribution at any given timestep $t$ should equal the expected class distribution at the subsequent timestep $t+1$, where the expectation is taken over the possible next elements $s_{t+1}$. This identity, which parallels the Bellman equation in reinforcement learning, suggests a structural property that predictions across timesteps should adhere to. The researchers hypothesize that leveraging this temporal-consistency property during training can lead to more robust and data-efficient classifiers, particularly for early prefixes where the direct signal from the final label might be noisy or sparse.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology developed in this paper focuses on leveraging a temporal-consistency condition to create a novel loss function for training incremental sequence classifiers.

**Problem Setup and Notation:**
The problem is framed as classifying a sequence $x = (x_1, \dots, x_T)$ into one of $K$ classes. For theoretical development, the sequence is conceptualized as a series of Markov states $s = (s_1, \dots, s_T)$, where $s_t$ represents the prefix $x_{\le t}$. The goal is to learn a probabilistic classifier $p_\theta(y | s_t)$ that approximates the ground-truth distribution $p(y | s_t)$, for all $t \le T$.

**Direct Cross-Entropy (DCE) Baseline:**
A standard approach is to minimize the direct cross-entropy loss, $\ell_{\text{DCE}}(\theta; s, y) = - \sum_{t=1}^T \log p_\theta(y | s_t)$. This loss penalizes deviations of predictions from the observed one-hot class label $\delta_y$ across all prefixes $s_t$. This method is common for training token-level verifiers in LLM applications. The paper identifies a potential drawback: for early states ($t \ll T$), the final label $y$ provides a noisy training signal, as predictions $p_\theta(y | s_t)$ must account for uncertainty in the future sequence elements and the label given the final state.

**Temporal-Consistency (TC) Loss:**
Inspired by the temporal-consistency condition $p(y | s_t) = E_{p(s_{t+1}|s_t)}[p(y | s_{t+1})]$, the authors propose a novel loss function, $\ell_{\text{TC}}(\theta; \theta', s, y)$. This loss consists of two main components:
1. A cross-entropy term for the final state $s_T$: $H[\delta_y || p_\theta(\cdot | s_T)]$.
2. A sum of cross-entropy terms for intermediate states ($t < T$): $\sum_{t=1}^{T-1} H[p_{\theta'}(\cdot | s_{t+1}) || p_\theta(\cdot | s_t)]$.
 This second component penalizes temporal inconsistency by making predictions at state $s_t$ align with the predictive distribution from the *next* state $s_{t+1}$, as estimated by a reference model $p_{\theta'}$.

The training procedure for $\ell_{\text{TC}}$ involves an iterative optimization similar to the iterative evaluation of value functions in TD learning: $\theta^{(i+1)} \leftarrow \arg \min_\theta \sum_n \ell_{\text{TC}}(\theta; \theta^{(i)}, s_n, y_n)$.

**Generalized Temporal-Consistency (TC-λ) Loss:**
The authors further generalize this to $\ell_{\text{TC-λ}}(\theta; \theta', s, y)$, which uses a weighted average of future predictive distributions as targets. The target $z_t$ for $p_\theta(\cdot | s_t)$ is defined as $z_t = \lambda^{T-t}\delta_y + (1-\lambda)\sum_{k=1}^{T-t} \lambda^{k-1} p_{\theta'}(\cdot | s_{t+k})$. Here, $\lambda \in$ is a hyperparameter that controls the influence of distant states:
- $\lambda = 0$ recovers the original $\ell_{\text{TC}}$ loss, where the target for $s_t$ is primarily derived from $p_{\theta'}(\cdot | s_{t+1})$.
- $\lambda = 1$ recovers the $\ell_{\text{DCE}}$ loss, where the target for all $s_t$ is solely the final observed label $\delta_y$.
This parallels the TD(λ) family of algorithms in RL. The implementation uses stochastic gradient updates, and the loss is averaged over all prefixes of each sequence.

**Connections to Temporal-Difference (TD) Learning:**
The approach is explicitly inspired by TD learning, particularly Sutton's concept of assigning credit based on "differences between temporally successive predictions." The probabilistic classifier is viewed as a state-value function for categorical outcomes, and the temporal-consistency condition is a form of the Bellman equation. The primary distinction is the use of categorical outcomes and cross-entropy loss, as opposed to scalar outcomes and squared loss typical in classical TD learning.

**Theoretical Analysis (Tabular Setting):**
The paper provides a theoretical analysis in a tractable tabular setting, modeling multiclass sequence classification as finding absorption probabilities in a Markov chain with transient and absorbing states.
- **Proposition 1**: Demonstrates that iterative refinement of absorption probabilities ($P_{i+1} = Q P_i + R$) converges to the true probabilities $P^\star$.
- **Proposition 2**: Establishes that the iterative optimization of the TC loss (Eq. 6) is equivalent to iterating on empirically estimated transition matrices ($\hat{Q}, \hat{R}$) from the dataset. This implies that the TC estimator is guaranteed to converge and is consistent under mild assumptions.
- **Proposition 3**: Shows statistical benefits. For a specific Markov chain example, the indirect estimator (equivalent to TC) can be $W$ times more data-efficient than the direct estimator (equivalent to DCE) for a state in the first layer, where $W$ is the number of states in a layer. This efficiency gain is attributed to "data pooling" and regularization through the temporal-consistency property.

**Empirical Evaluation Setup:**
- **Model Architecture**: Decoder-only transformers (OPT family for text classification, Qwen2.5 for LLM verification) are used due to their natural incremental processing capabilities. A classification head (softmax(Ah$_t$ + b)) is appended.
- **Training**: Fine-tuning pre-trained language models, optimizing both the classification head and transformer parameters.
- **Baselines**: Compared against a range of methods including "Most frequent," GPT-4o, "Filtering" (generative), "Last token" (only final prediction), "Specialist" (separate model per prefix length), and squared-loss variants of direct estimation (Direct $\ell^2$ loss) and TD learning (LSTD(λ)).
- **Metrics**: Predictive accuracy, Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL), and Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (ROC AUC).
- **Compute**: Experiments conducted on NVIDIA H100 GPUs, with careful hyperparameter tuning and reporting of results averaged over multiple random seeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical evaluations across text classification benchmarks and an LLM verification task, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed temporal-consistency (TC-λ) method.

#### 4.1. Text Classification Datasets

The method was evaluated on four diverse text classification datasets: IMDB (sentiment), OHSUMED (medical topic), NEWSGROUPS (newsgroup topic), and AG-NEWS (news topic).

- **Superior Predictive Accuracy (Table 1, Figure 6)**: TC-λ consistently outperformed the Direct Cross-Entropy (DCE) baseline and most other competing approaches across all datasets and prefix lengths (4 tokens, 16 tokens, and full sequences). In most cases, TC-λ achieved the highest or second-highest accuracy. The gains were particularly noticeable for shorter prefixes, where the training signal from the final label is typically noisier.
- **Improved Full-Sequence Accuracy**: Surprisingly, optimizing for temporal consistency also led to improvements in full-sequence classification accuracy compared to DCE and the "Last token" method (which only trains on the final token). This suggests that including prefix predictions in the loss, especially with temporal consistency, acts as a beneficial auxiliary signal.
- **Cross-Entropy vs. Squared Loss**: Models trained with cross-entropy loss (DCE, TC-λ) generally performed better than their squared-loss counterparts (Direct $\ell^2$ loss, LSTD(λ)), especially on datasets with more classes (OHSUMED, NEWSGROUPS). While ROC AUC could be similar for binary/three-way tasks, cross-entropy models produced better-calibrated predictive probabilities (lower NLL, Figure 6).
- **Model Scaling Efficiency (Figure 2)**: On the OHSUMED dataset, TC-λ trained on a smaller OPT-125M model achieved ROC AUC performance comparable to or exceeding DCE trained on much larger models (e.g., OPT-1.3B) for early prefixes (4, 8, 16 tokens). This indicates substantial data and computational efficiency gains, potentially equivalent to a 10x increase in model size for DCE to match TC-λ's performance.
- **Optimal λ Parameter (Figure 3, left)**: The hyperparameter $\lambda$ plays a critical role. The DCE setting ($\lambda=1$) was never optimal. The best $\lambda$ value, which determines the effective lookahead, varied by prefix length and dataset, generally falling between 0.8 and 0.98, corresponding to an effective lookahead of 5-50 tokens.
- **Demonstrated Temporal Consistency (Figure 3, right)**: TC-λ models exhibited significantly lower KL divergence between successive predictive distributions compared to DCE models. This empirically confirms that the TC-λ loss effectively enforces the temporal-consistency property, leading to more stable predictions across timesteps.

#### 4.2. Language Model Verification on GSM8K

The method was applied to verifying LLM generations for grade-school math problems using Qwen2.5-0.5B.

- **Early Correctness Prediction (Figure 5, left)**: TC-λ verifiers significantly outperformed DCE in distinguishing correct from incorrect LLM generations, particularly after observing only a few tokens. For instance, with just 8 tokens, TC-λ achieved nearly 70% accuracy in this binary classification task (ROC AUC).
- **Improved Predictive Calibration (Figure 8)**: Similar to text classification, while ROC AUC was comparable between cross-entropy and squared-loss variants on this binary task, cross-entropy-trained models achieved substantially lower (better) negative log-likelihood, indicating more accurate and calibrated predictive probabilities.
- **Enhanced Test-Time Scaling (Figure 5, center & right)**: The improved early prediction capabilities of TC-λ verifiers enabled a more efficient "boosted best-of-N" decoding strategy. By sampling 10 tokens from 2N generations, ranking them with the TC-λ verifier, and only completing the top N, the approach achieved a 23%-33% reduction in the average number of generated tokens for a given level of answer accuracy, compared to a vanilla best-of-N strategy. This demonstrates practical benefits for reducing computational costs in LLM applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces a novel and theoretically grounded approach to incremental sequence classification that demonstrates significant practical utility.

**Methodological Advancement**: The paper presents a novel loss function, TC-λ, which is inspired by temporal-difference learning from reinforcement learning. This framework extends the concept of temporal consistency to the multiclass classification setting with cross-entropy loss, moving beyond the scalar value and squared loss typically seen in TD learning. The theoretical analysis in the tabular setting provides guarantees of convergence and consistency, along with insights into its data-efficiency benefits.

**Improved Performance and Efficiency**: The empirical results consistently demonstrate that models trained with TC-λ achieve higher predictive accuracy across various text classification benchmarks and for LLM generation verification. These improvements are particularly pronounced for predictions based on short prefixes, which is crucial for early decision-making. The efficiency gains are notable; for instance, a smaller model trained with TC-λ can match or exceed the performance of a much larger model trained with conventional direct cross-entropy (DCE), suggesting a substantial reduction in compute or data requirements. The method adds negligible computational overhead during both training and inference.

**Broad Applicability**: The proposed approach is architecture-agnostic, making it applicable to any sequence classification problem beyond the transformer-based text models used in the experiments. This opens avenues for its use in diverse fields.

**Impact on LLM Applications**: A key potential impact is on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of large language models. The ability of TC-λ verifiers to accurately assess the correctness or quality of LLM generations early in their production cycle enables techniques like "boosted best-of-N" decoding. This can lead to significant reductions in computational costs for LLM inference while maintaining or even improving the accuracy of the final output. This contributes to making advanced LLM capabilities more accessible and sustainable.

**Potential for Future Research**: The work suggests several promising directions. The application of TC-λ to multimodal sequence classification (e.g., robotics, games) is a natural next step. Furthermore, its principles could be adapted to multi-token prediction within LLMs, which is an active area of research for improving pre-training and accelerating inference. While current evaluations focus on models up to 1.3 billion parameters, assessing its effectiveness on even larger models is also identified as important future work.

In summary, the TC-λ method offers a robust, efficient, and theoretically informed approach to incremental sequence classification, with clear benefits for predictive accuracy and resource utilization, particularly in the context of emerging LLM applications.
