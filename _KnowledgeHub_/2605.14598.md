# DSSP: Diffusion State Space Policy with Full-History Encoding

> [!summary] Summary
> DSSP is a diffusion policy leveraging State Space Models (SSMs) for efficient full-history encoding and action denoising in robot manipulation, enabling robots to resolve temporal ambiguities in complex, long-horizon tasks. The policy achieved an average 62.30% success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 and a 133.3% relative improvement over baseline DP3 in real-world memory-dependent tasks, while using a smaller model footprint.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation policies often rely on short-context observations, struggling with temporal ambiguity in long-horizon, multi-stage tasks where visually similar states require history-dependent actions.
- This short-context limitation leads to failures such as ignoring completed subgoals, undoing previous actions, or getting trapped in repetitive loops due to temporal aliasing.
- Existing approaches to incorporate longer historical context are computationally expensive (e.g., attention-based models) or risk losing task-critical information through overly aggressive compression.

## Method

- A Diffusion State Space Policy (DSSP) is proposed, utilizing Mamba State Space Models (SSMs) for efficient causal history encoding and as the diffusion backbone for action generation.
- A dynamics-aware auxiliary loss is introduced to train the history encoder, ensuring the learned compact context representation is predictive of future state evolution.
- The diffusion denoising model employs hierarchical prefix conditioning, combining long-term historical context with recent local observations, and uses timestep-decoupled Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) for stable conditioning.

## Results

- DSSP achieved an average success rate of 62.30% across 50 bimanual RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, surpassing the DP3 baseline's 55.24% while using a model with 44.3M parameters compared to DP3's 264.4M.
- For full-history encoding, the Mamba encoder reduced inference latency by 45.4% and peak GPU memory by 59.3% compared to a Transformer encoder, demonstrating significant efficiency gains.
- In real-world memory-dependent manipulation tasks, DSSP significantly outperformed DP3, achieving an average success rate of 70% compared to DP3's 30%, with a substantial improvement on the "Morse Tapping" task from 15% to 85%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State Space Models, particularly Mamba, efficiently process streaming long-horizon histories by adaptively preserving task-relevant events and filtering redundant observations with linear-time complexity.
> - Training the history encoder with a dynamics-aware auxiliary objective forces the learned context to retain action-relevant information predictive of future states, effectively resolving observation ambiguities.
> - A hierarchical conditioning strategy, combining compact long-term history with immediate local observations, offers a stable and informative signal for diffusion-based action generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot manipulation in complex, unstructured environments necessitates policies capable of reasoning over multi-step, long-horizon tasks, often relying on high-dimensional sensory inputs. Imitation learning (IL) has emerged as a prominent paradigm for acquiring such skills from expert demonstrations. Within IL, diffusion policies have demonstrated capabilities in modeling complex and multi-modal action distributions, with advancements through expressive denoising backbones, multi-modal conditioning, and improved action generation.

A common limitation in most existing robot policies, including many diffusion-based approaches, is their reliance on short-context conditioning, typically predicting actions based solely on the current observation or a limited window of recent observations. This short-context approach presents a bottleneck in long-horizon tasks characterized by temporal ambiguities. In such scenarios, visually similar observations may correspond to different stages of task progress (e.g., an object having already been moved, a container filled, or a specific object placed in a buffer). Without access to the complete interaction history, policies may fail to disambiguate these temporally aliased states, potentially leading to repetitive actions, ignored subgoals, or reversal of task progress.

Prior attempts to incorporate temporal context have included extending the observation horizon or feeding longer observation histories into policies. However, these methods often incur increased memory and inference costs and can introduce redundant visual inputs or spurious correlations. Attention-based and multi-frame Vision-Language-Action (VLA) methods offer more flexible access to past observations but still involve substantial computational overhead as the context horizon grows. To mitigate this overhead, researchers have explored compact memory forms, such as keyframes, visual traces, point tracks, or recurrent latent states. A challenge with these compact representations is ensuring they preserve task-relevant information while discarding irrelevant details, as they may inadvertently discard progress-critical events or focus on non-essential visual changes.

State Space Models (SSMs) have recently gained attention for sequence modeling due due to their ability to maintain an evolving latent state over time, allowing for efficient processing of long sequences. Modern selective SSMs, such as Mamba, enhance this by enabling input-dependent state updates, which allows the model to adaptively select which information to retain or discard. This property makes SSMs potentially suitable for efficiently processing long observation histories in manipulation tasks. The presented work builds upon the integration of SSMs into diffusion-based robot policies and the concept of history-aware policy learning to address the challenges of long-horizon manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robot manipulation policy that can effectively and efficiently utilize a full history of observations to resolve temporal ambiguities in long-horizon tasks. The authors identify the limitations of existing short-context policies, which struggle with visually aliased states where the correct action depends on past events not discernible from the current observation alone.

The motivation for DSSP stems from several specific challenges:
1. **Resolving History-Dependent Ambiguities:** In multi-stage tasks, current observations can be visually identical despite representing different stages of task completion (e.g., a container empty vs. filled, an object moved vs. yet to be moved). Policies conditioned only on short observation windows often fail to track task progress accurately, leading to errors like repeating completed subgoals or undoing previous actions. DSSP aims to overcome this by incorporating a comprehensive historical context.
2. **Efficient Full-History Conditioning:** Directly feeding long observation histories to policies incurs high computational costs in terms of memory and inference time. Existing methods often suffer from redundancy or inefficient scaling with increased history length. A key objective is to achieve *efficient* full-history conditioning without prohibitive computational overhead.
3. **Preserving Task-Relevant Information:** While compact memory forms exist, they do not inherently guarantee that progress-critical events are retained or that nuisance changes are ignored. DSSP aims to learn a compact context representation that actively preserves information relevant to future state evolution and decision-making.
4. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance with Smaller Models:** The authors aim to demonstrate that their approach, by effectively leveraging full-history information and employing an efficient architecture, can achieve competitive or superior performance compared to existing methods, potentially with a reduced model size.

To achieve these objectives, DSSP proposes leveraging State Space Models (SSMs) for continuous sequence modeling to compress the entire observation stream into a compact context representation. This context is further refined using a dynamics-aware auxiliary objective to ensure its predictive power for future states, and it is hierarchically fused with recent observations to condition a diffusion-based action generation model, which also utilizes an SSM backbone for architectural consistency and efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DSSP (Diffusion State Space Policy) is designed as a full-history conditioned diffusion policy for long-horizon manipulation, integrating a State Space Model (SSM) backbone for both history encoding and action denoising, alongside a dual-level conditioning mechanism.

**Problem Formulation**
The work frames long-horizon 3D manipulation as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), where the agent receives observations ($o_t$) generated from an unobserved true state ($s_t$). Due to the partial observability, $o_t$ is insufficient to infer $s_t$, necessitating the use of interaction history ($h_t = (o_0, a_0, \ldots, a_{t-1}, o_t)$). A core challenge is "observation aliasing," where distinct histories ($h^1_t, h^2_t$) yield identical current observations ($o^1_t = o^2_t$) but require different expert action distributions ($P_E(a_t | h^1_t) \neq P_E(a_t | h^2_t)$). DSSP aims to learn a history-dependent policy $\pi_\theta(a_t | h_t)$ to resolve this ambiguity.

**Long-Horizon Context Learning**
To address the need for historical context in resolving task-level ambiguities while mitigating computational redundancy, DSSP introduces a learnable history encoder. This encoder compresses the full history into a compact, task-relevant context representation.

- **Causal History Encoding:** Raw multi-modal observations ($o_t$), comprising visual inputs (e.g., point clouds) and robot proprioceptive states, are first projected into a state representation ($z_t = E_{obs}(o_t)$) using parallel visual and proprioceptive encoders. A causal history encoder $G_\psi$ then processes the sequence of these step-wise state representations $z_{1:t}$ into a temporally-integrated context representation $\tilde{z}_{1:t}$. The SSM architecture, specifically Mamba, is chosen for the history encoder $G_\psi$. Mamba's recurrent formulation maintains a compact hidden state, enabling linear-time aggregation of long histories without re-encoding the entire past. Its input-dependent selective updates allow it to filter redundant observations and preserve sparse, task-relevant events. The context representation $c_t$ is defined as the final output token of the encoded sequence ($\tilde{z}_t$).
- **Dynamics-Aware Context Learning:** To ensure the context representation $c_t$ encodes information relevant to future decisions, an auxiliary training objective is introduced. A lightweight dynamics predictor $g_\phi$ is trained to predict the next state representation ($\hat{z}_{t+1}$) given the current context $c_t$ and the executed action $a_t$. This prediction is supervised using a cosine similarity loss ($L_{dyn}$), encouraging $c_t$ to retain action-relevant historical information that is predictive of future state evolution.

**Hierarchical Context-Conditioned Denoising**
The learned history representation is integrated into the policy through a hierarchical conditioning mechanism. This mechanism combines the high-level context with recent local observations to address the needs for both long-term task progress information and fine-grained local control cues.

- **Hierarchical Prefix Conditioning:** A condition sequence $C_t$ is constructed by concatenating the learned context representation $c_t$ with the most recent $N$ state tokens ($z_{t-N+1}, \ldots, z_t$). This $C_t$ is prepended as a prefix to the noisy future action trajectory ($x_\tau$) that the diffusion model aims to denoise. This ordering allows the long-term context ($c_t$) to contextualize recent states before information propagates to the action tokens.
- **Causal Action Denoising:** The denoising model $f_\theta$ is instantiated with a Mamba backbone, processing the concatenated sequence $[C_t, x_\tau]$. The Mamba's recurrent selective state updates naturally align with this left-to-right conditioning flow, supporting efficient iterative diffusion sampling.
- **Timestep-Decoupled Action Denoising:** To maintain a stable conditioning signal, the diffusion timestep $\tau$ (representing the noise level) is decoupled from the prefix condition. The timestep embedding is injected through Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) only into the action tokens, leaving $C_t$ as a stable representation of history and local observations throughout the denoising process.

**Training**
The overall training objective combines the diffusion reconstruction loss ($L_{diff}$) for action generation with the dynamics-aware auxiliary loss ($L_{dyn}$) for context learning. The combined loss is $L(\theta, \psi, \phi) = L_{diff}(\theta, \psi) + \lambda L_{dyn}(\psi, \phi)$, where $\lambda$ balances the two objectives. Training uses trajectory-wise batch construction to ensure causal history encoding.

**Theoretical Analysis**
The paper presents two propositions justifying the benefits of history conditioning:
- **Proposition 4.1:** The minimum achievable diffusion-based imitation loss for a history-conditioned policy is always less than or equal to that of a reactive policy ($min_\theta L_{diff}(\pi_\theta; h_t) \leq min_\theta L_{diff}(\pi_\theta; o_t)$). This is derived from the law of total variance, where history contains the current observation.
- **Proposition 4.2:** In the presence of observation aliasing, history conditioning strictly reduces the minimum achievable imitation loss compared to a reactive policy ($min_\theta L_{diff}(\pi_\theta; h_t) < min_\theta L_{diff}(\pi_\theta; o_t)$) whenever there is conditional mutual information between actions and history given the current observation ($I_E(a_t; h_t | o_t) > 0$). This implies history resolves state ambiguity by capturing mutual information inaccessible to a reactive policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DSSP encompassed diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks, employing task success rate as the primary metric.

**Simulation Experiments**
- **RoboTwin 2.0:** DSSP was evaluated on 50 bimanual tasks, categorized by horizon length. It achieved an average success rate of 62.30%, demonstrating a 12.8% relative improvement over DP3 (55.24%). The largest improvement was observed on long-horizon tasks, where DSSP achieved 64.06% compared to DP3's 52.76%, a 21.4% relative gain. Notably, DSSP achieved this with a model size of 44.3M parameters, significantly smaller than other baselines (e.g., DP3 at 264.4M, π0 at 3.3B).
- **Adroit and MetaWorld:** On 3 Adroit tasks and 34 MetaWorld tasks (shorter-horizon), DSSP achieved an overall average success rate of 80.1±2.6%, outperforming all compared methods, including MP1 (78.9±2.1%) and FlowPolicy (71.6±3.5%).
- **Analysis of History Encoding:**
 - **Temporal Backbones and History Length:** A comparison on six long-horizon tasks showed Mamba's effectiveness in scaling with increasing context. While a Transformer encoder achieved 68.00% success with a history length of 10, its performance stagnated with longer histories (66.00% for full history). Mamba, in contrast, achieved its peak success rate of 71.33% with full history, an 8.1% relative improvement over the full-history Transformer.
 - **Efficiency:** Full-history Mamba encoding required 1.97 ms and 238.5 MB peak GPU memory, a 45.4% reduction in latency and 59.3% reduction in peak memory compared to the full-history Transformer (3.61 ms, 586.2 MB). This efficiency is attributed to Mamba's linear-time state-space formulation and compact hidden-state cache during inference.
 - **Robustness to Perturbations:** Under Gaussian perturbations to recent state tokens during inference, DSSP with full history maintained substantially higher success rates (e.g., 37.00% at σ=0.10) compared to DP3 (15.83%) and a DSSP variant without full history (23.33%). This suggests that the learned context token integrates earlier information, preventing over-reliance on unreliable recent observations.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study on the six history-sensitive RoboTwin tasks confirmed the contribution of each component:
 - Removing the history encoder led to the largest performance drop.
 - Replacing the Mamba action denoising backbone with a Transformer-based one resulted in lower performance than the full model.
 - Removing timestep-decoupled conditioning decreased performance to 66.67%.
 - Removing recent-state conditioning or the dynamics-aware loss also degraded performance. The full model achieved 71.33% success, a 21.56% relative improvement over DP3 (58.67%).

**Real-World Experiments**
DSSP was evaluated on three real-world tabletop tasks requiring long-horizon memory or progress tracking: Put Bottles, Object Swap, and Morse Tapping. DSSP significantly outperformed DP3, increasing the average success rate from 30% to 70% (a 133.3% relative improvement). Specifically, on Morse Tapping, DSSP improved from 15% to 85% success rate. This demonstrates the effectiveness of full-history context for real-world memory-dependent manipulation, where decisions often rely on previous interactions. Failure analysis indicated that for tasks like Morse Tapping, DSSP effectively tracks progress even when visual observations are aliased, while DP3 struggled with incorrect termination or redundant actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces DSSP, a Diffusion State Space Policy with Full-History Encoding, presenting a novel approach to addressing the challenges of long-horizon robot manipulation in partially observable environments.

The significance of this work lies in several key contributions:
- **Efficient Full-History Conditioning:** DSSP demonstrates that explicit full-history encoding can be achieved efficiently by leveraging State Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba. This addresses a critical limitation of previous methods that either sacrificed historical context for efficiency or incurred high computational costs for longer contexts. The empirical results show substantial reductions in latency and GPU memory overhead compared to Transformer-based approaches for full-history encoding.
- **Dynamics-Aware Context Learning:** The introduction of a dynamics-aware auxiliary objective for the history encoder is significant. By encouraging the context representation to be predictive of future state evolution, DSSP ensures that the compressed history retains information critical for decision-making, rather than merely summarizing past observations. This makes the learned context more informative and actionable.
- **Hierarchical and Timestep-Decoupled Conditioning:** The proposed hierarchical conditioning mechanism, which fuses a high-level context token with recent local state observations, provides a structured way to combine long-term task progress information with fine-grained control cues. The timestep-decoupled injection of diffusion noise information further stabilizes the conditioning process, allowing the policy to effectively manage both task-level and denoising-specific signals.
- **Architectural Unification and Efficiency:** By instantiating both the history encoder and the diffusion denoising backbone with SSMs (Mamba), the policy maintains architectural consistency and benefits from the efficiency of Mamba's linear-time sequence modeling. This contributes to the overall compact model size and improved inference efficiency, making the approach more practical for deployment.
- **Improved Performance in History-Sensitive Tasks:** Extensive experiments across complex simulation benchmarks (RoboTwin, MetaWorld, Adroit) and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that DSSP achieves state-of-the-art performance, particularly on tasks requiring long-horizon reasoning and disambiguation of temporally aliased states. The substantial gains in success rates, especially on long-horizon and memory-dependent tasks, underscore the practical impact of full-history conditioning.

The potential impact of DSSP extends to broader applications of robot learning, particularly in scenarios demanding robust decision-making over extended time horizons, such as multi-stage assembly, complex object rearrangement, or human-robot collaboration. By enabling robots to effectively leverage their full interaction history, this work contributes to developing more intelligent, adaptable, and robust robotic agents capable of operating in dynamic and unstructured environments.

However, the authors acknowledge limitations, including that DSSP does not directly eliminate low-level perception or control failures (e.g., inaccurate grasping) and that real-world evaluation was confined to three tabletop tasks with a fixed-camera point-cloud setup. Future work could explore extending DSSP to more diverse embodiments, viewpoints, deformable objects, and dynamic environments to further broaden its applicability.
