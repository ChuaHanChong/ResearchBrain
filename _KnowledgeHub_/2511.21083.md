# Dual-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Adaptive and Cost-Aware Visual-Inertial Odometry

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-agent reinforcement learning framework adaptively manages computational resources and fuses sensor data for Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO). This framework achieves an average throughput of 39 Frames Per Second (FPS), which is 1.77 times faster than DPVO, with 45.2% less VRAM usage while maintaining competitive accuracy in ego-motion estimation.

## Problem

- Traditional VIO systems face a persistent trade-off between accuracy (achieved by optimization-based methods like VIBA) and computational efficiency (offered by filter-based approaches).
- Existing VIO pipelines incur redundant computational costs by processing visual frames for feature extraction and tracking even when they are later discarded by keyframe selection heuristics.
- Loosely coupled VIO schemes often rely on fixed-gain filters, lacking adaptability to varying motion and sensor conditions, and IMU predictions are susceptible to bias drift, impacting overall system accuracy.

## Method

- A dual-agent reinforcement learning (RL) architecture is introduced, comprising a '==Select Agent==' for computational scheduling and a 'Fusion Agent' for adaptive sensor data blending.
- The IMU-only 'Select Agent' proactively decides whether to run the full Visual Odometry (VO) pipeline based on high-frequency IMU signals, completely skipping visual processing for redundant frames.
- A 'Fusion Agent' learns to dynamically weigh high-rate IMU state propagation against sparse, high-accuracy VO updates, utilizing an uncertainty-aware reward function and supported by a dedicated IMU Bias Estimator network.

## Results

- The framework achieved an average throughput of 39 FPS on the EuRoC MAV dataset, 1.77 times faster than DPVO, while consuming only **4.37 GB** of VRAM, a **45.2%** reduction compared to DROID-VO, with competitive ATE of **0.092m**.
- The Select Agent reduced CPU Visual-Inertial Bundle Adjustment (VIBA) time by up to 9.5 times compared to ORB-SLAM3, effectively managing computational load by avoiding feature extraction during scheduling.
- The RL-trained Fusion Agent yielded the lowest ATE (**0.112m**) on EuRoC MH 04 compared to heuristic and EKF fusion baselines and improved DROID-VO's ATE from **0.399m** to **0.237m**, demonstrating its generalization capabilities and robustness to visual degradations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling VIO into an IMU-only scheduling agent and an adaptive fusion agent allows for substantial computational savings without fully abandoning the accuracy benefits of traditional optimization-based methods.
> - Proactive, IMU-only scheduling decisions can significantly reduce computational load by avoiding feature extraction and tracking on redundant frames before visual processing even begins, leading to higher throughput.
> - Reinforcement learning can effectively learn context-dependent fusion policies that adaptively balance IMU predictions and visual observations, enhancing accuracy and robustness to motion and sensor degradations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate, robust, and real-time ego-motion estimation is a foundational requirement for autonomous systems, encompassing applications in mobile robotics, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), self-driving cars, and augmented reality (AR). Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO), which integrates visual and inertial sensor data, has emerged as a dominant paradigm for achieving high-frequency and metrically scaled pose estimates, particularly in environments where Global Positioning System (GPS) signals are unavailable.

Historically, VIO backend estimators have been categorized into two main paradigms. Filter-based approaches, such as the Multi-State Constraint Kalman Filter (MSCKF) and ROVIO, are known for their computational efficiency, making them suitable for real-time deployment. However, their accuracy can be suboptimal due to linearization errors and the accumulation of noise. Conversely, optimization-based methods, exemplified by VINS-Mono, ORB-SLAM3, and DM-VIO, achieve superior accuracy through non-linear Visual-Inertial Bundle Adjustment (VIBA). This precision, however, comes at a substantial computational cost, presenting a significant challenge for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.

The advent of deep learning (DL) has introduced new approaches to VIO. End-to-end DL models, such as VINet and SelfVIO, attempt to directly regress ego-motion from raw sensor inputs. While novel, these methods often exhibit limitations in precision and generalization compared to established classical frameworks. A hybrid paradigm has also emerged, integrating learned components into traditional pipelines. These hybrid approaches aim to enhance robustness through techniques like improved feature matching or learned backend optimization components. However, many still rely on or are constrained by the underlying computational burden of VIBA. Consequently, the fundamental trade-off between accuracy and efficiency remains a practical impediment for real-time VIO on platforms with limited computational resources.

This work addresses this persistent accuracy-efficiency trade-off by proposing a strategy that does not seek to entirely replace VIBA but rather to reduce the frequency and computational intensity of its invocation. It frames two critical design choices in VIO—when to process visual data and how to weight visual information against inertial predictions—as sequential decision problems. By employing lightweight reinforcement learning (RL) agents for these decisions, the research differentiates itself from existing DL-based methods that primarily focus on improving accuracy or adapting fusion at the feature/latent representation level, often still processing every visual frame.

Furthermore, within the emerging field of RL applications in odometry, prior work has explored using RL agents to replace internal heuristics within Visual Odometry (VO) pipelines, such as keyframe selection, often conditioned on already-extracted visual features. This paper extends beyond this by introducing an IMU-only scheduler that makes a priori decisions about whether to run the *entire* VO pipeline, thereby achieving greater computational savings. It also addresses the full visual–inertial fusion problem with a dedicated RL agent, which learns to adaptively fuse high-frequency IMU predictions with sparse VO updates using uncertainty-aware state representations. This dual-agent RL architecture thus represents an advancement in developing cost-aware and adaptive VIO systems suitable for environments with strict computational budgets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the accuracy-efficiency trade-off in Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) by mitigating the computational bottleneck associated with Visual-Inertial Bundle Adjustment (VIBA). Rather than attempting to eliminate VIBA, the work aims to reduce its invocation frequency and computational burden through intelligent, adaptive decision-making.

This overarching goal is driven by three specific motivations and corresponding design objectives:

1. **Reduce Reliance on Tightly-Coupled VIBA:** Traditional tightly-coupled VIO systems, while accurate, are computationally expensive due to the monolithic nature of VIBA, making them challenging for deployment on resource-constrained platforms. Loosely coupled schemes, often relying on fixed-gain filters, offer efficiency but lack adaptability to varying motion and sensor conditions. The motivation here is to move beyond fixed filters by introducing a learned fusion policy. By casting sensor fusion as a sequential decision problem, the researchers aim to leverage reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize a long-horizon, cost-aware policy for dynamically blending visual and inertial information. This approach seeks to retain the accuracy benefits associated with optimization while gaining the adaptability and efficiency of a learned system.

2. **Avoid Redundant Visual Odometry (VO) Computations:** A significant portion of VIO's computational cost comes from processing visual frames, including feature extraction, tracking, and bundle adjustment. Existing keyframe selection strategies typically require some level of image processing before a decision on frame utility can be made, incurring a computational overhead even for discarded frames. The motivation is to proactively avoid this wasted computation. To achieve this, a "Select Agent" is designed to make *a priori* scheduling decisions based purely on high-frequency Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) signals. This allows the system to completely bypass the entire costly visual pipeline for frames deemed redundant or uninformative, thereby achieving substantial computational savings before any visual processing begins.

3. **Ensure High-Fidelity IMU State Predictions:** The effectiveness of a decoupled, RL-based VIO system, particularly one that relies on IMU-only decisions and IMU propagation, is highly dependent on the accuracy of inertial measurements. IMU integration is known to be sensitive to sensor biases, which can lead to significant drift over time. The motivation is to establish a robust and reliable foundation for the RL agents. To this end, the research introduces a dedicated IMU Bias Estimator network. This estimator is designed to provide pre-compensated inertial measurements, thereby improving the quality of IMU predictions and ensuring a more dependable basis for both the Select Agent's scheduling decisions and the Fusion Agent's state propagation.

By addressing these motivations through a dual-agent RL architecture, the research aims to develop a VIO framework that achieves a more favorable accuracy–efficiency–memory trade-off than existing methods, making it more practical for real-world autonomous applications on resource-limited hardware.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed VIO framework is structured around a decoupled, dual-agent reinforcement learning (RL) architecture designed to manage computational cost and adaptively fuse sensor data. The system comprises four primary interacting modules: IMU Preprocess, Select Agent, Visual Odometry (VO) Module, and Fusion Agent.

#### 3.1. IMU Bias Estimator (IMU Preprocess Module)

A high-fidelity IMU prediction is considered fundamental. Instead of a general signal denoiser, a bias estimator is trained to model dominant, slowly-varying bias patterns.
- **Architecture:** Two lightweight encoder networks: $f_{g}^{bias}$ for the gyroscope and $f_{a}^{bias}$ for the accelerometer. Each network takes a window of raw, noisy sensor data and calibrated intrinsic parameters (noise density, bias stability).
- **Output:** $f_{g}^{bias}$ outputs a 3-axis gyro bias estimate ($\hat{b}_{g}$), and $f_{a}^{bias}$ outputs an accelerometer bias estimate ($\hat{b}_{a}$).
- **Pre-integration:** At inference, the pre-trained networks correct raw IMU measurements. These corrected measurements are then fed into a standard IMU pre-integration process.
- **Final Output:** The module provides relative motion ($\Delta p, \Delta q, \Delta v$) and estimated biases ($\hat{b}$) for each IMU interval, which are used by the RL agents.

#### 3.2. RL-based Computational Scheduling (Select Agent)

VO scheduling is formulated as a sequential decision problem under uncertainty, where running the VO pipeline improves accuracy but increases computation, and skipping it saves resources at the risk of drift.
- **Formulation:** A Markov Decision Process (MDP) is used to learn a long-horizon, cost-aware observation policy via RL.
- **State Space ($S_{sel}$):** A compact IMU-only state $s_{sel_t} = \{\Delta p_t, \Delta q_t, \Delta v_t, \Delta t_{vo_t}\}$ is observed by the agent, summarizing pose/velocity drift and time since the last VO update. This allows decisions without visual feature computation.
- **Action Space ($A_{sel}$):** A binary action $a_{sel_t} \in \{0 (\text{Skip VO}), 1 (\text{Run VO})\}$ determines whether to activate the VO pipeline. This enables complete omission of the visual encoder for skipped frames.
- **Reward Function ($R_{sel}$):** Combines a terminal Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE)-based objective with a dense per-step shaping term. The terminal reward is $R_{episode} = \frac{A}{\text{ATE} + \epsilon} - B \cdot N_f$, where $N_f$ is the number of VO calls, encouraging low error with few invocations. A small penalty based on instantaneous pose error is added at each step for training stability.
- **Policy Network:** Parameterized by a small multi-layer perceptron (MLP).
- **Training:** The agent is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within a log-driven, closed-loop simulator.

#### 3.3. Visual Odometry Module

This module is activated only when the Select Agent outputs $a_{sel_t} = 1$. Its architecture is inspired by Deep Patch Visual Odometry (DPVO).
- **Feature Encoder:** A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) extracts dense feature maps from the input image.
- **Correction Predictor:** Takes feature maps and current state (poses, patch depths). It reprojects patch centers into the current frame, computes visual similarity, and feeds this with context features into a recurrent network. The output consists of 2D correction targets and confidence weights.
- **Map Points and Poses:** Patches and poses form the variables for optimization.
- **Bundle Adjustment:** A differentiable layer minimizes an objective function defined by the correction targets, solving for optimal pose and depth updates. The final optimized pose serves as a high-accuracy observation for the fusion policy.

#### 3.4. RL-based Adaptive Fusion (Fusion Agent)

This module fuses high-rate IMU state propagation with sparse, high-accuracy VO updates, while resolving monocular scale ambiguity.
- **System Initialization:**
 - The world frame is aligned with the initial IMU body frame.
 - Metric scale ($s$) and initial body-frame velocities are estimated using a linear initialization method (adapted from VINS-Mono) over a sliding window of keyframes, relating IMU pre-integrations and VO-derived relative translations.
 - An over-determined linear system is solved via least squares to obtain a single global metric scale $s$. All VO poses are subsequently scaled and aligned.
- **Adaptive Fusion Policy:** Models the fusion process as an MDP, learning a long-term policy for dynamically balancing IMU propagation and visual updates based on evolving uncertainty and motion dynamics.
 - **MLP1 (VO Velocity Estimator):** A supervised network estimates metric velocity from scaled VO poses and IMU pre-integration.
 - **State Propagation:** A deterministic module propagates the last fused state using IMU pre-integration and the estimated velocity.
 - **MLP2 (Adaptive Fusion Policy):** An RL agent that learns to weigh the IMU prediction against the VO observation. It outputs per-axis fusion weights $w \in^7$ (for position, velocity, and quaternion orientation). A convex per-axis blend is used, where the IMU weight is $1-w$. If VO is skipped, $w$ defaults to zero.
- **Reward Function:** $r_k = -\|p_k - p_{gt}\|^2_2 - \lambda \text{Tr}(\Sigma_k)$. The first term reduces trajectory error. The second term, a trace penalty on a low-cost approximation of fused-state uncertainty ($\Sigma_k$), discourages overconfident yet inaccurate updates.
- **Training Protocol:** A two-stage scheme: MLP1 is trained under supervision. MLP2 is initialized with a supervised fusion loss before PPO fine-tuning on real-world trajectories (EuRoC, TUM-VI) in a Gym-style replay environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed dual-agent RL-based VIO framework across accuracy, efficiency, and resource consumption metrics, including ablation studies and robustness tests.

#### 4.1. Accuracy and Computational Analysis

- **Comparison with Classical CPU-based VIO:** On the EuRoC MAV and TUM-VI datasets, the proposed method's accuracy surpassed filter-based approaches (e.g., MSCKF, ROVIO). When compared against optimization-based methods, it achieved an accuracy level comparable to systems like DM-VIO, demonstrating robust and effective performance. While it did not match the precision of ORB-SLAM3, the results indicated a controlled trade-off where accuracy remained competitive while mitigating the VIBA bottleneck.
- **CPU-Side Breakdown:** A detailed analysis of CPU-side processing times per keyframe on EuRoC showed a substantial reduction in BA/VIBA computation time. The proposed method reduced CPU BA/VIBA time from 121.09 ms (ORB-SLAM3) and 26.49 ms (DM-VIO) to 12.77 ms. This indicates a structural reduction in the load imposed by backend optimization.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Resource Comparison

- **Comparison with GPU-based VIO/VO Systems:** On the EuRoC MAV dataset, the framework demonstrated a favorable accuracy–throughput–memory trade-off among GPU-based VIO/VO methods.
 - **Throughput:** Achieved an average throughput of 39 Frames Per Second (FPS), which is 1.77 times faster than DPVO (22 FPS). This efficiency was attributed to the Select Agent's ability to avoid redundant computations by selectively activating the VO pipeline.
 - **Accuracy:** Attained the best average ATE (0.092m) compared to DPVO (0.106m), iSLAM (0.529m), and DROID-VO (0.188m).
 - **VRAM Usage:** Consumed only 4.37 GB of VRAM, representing a 45.2% reduction compared to DROID-VO (8.63 GB), and also lower than DPVO (4.92 GB) and iSLAM (6.47 GB).

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **IMU Bias Estimator:**
 - Tests on 5-second trajectory segments showed that the full bias estimation model (gyro + accelerometer) yielded the lowest ATE for both pure IMU strapdown and the final framework, confirming its importance for high-fidelity predictions.
 - A "Fixed Bias" model strategy resulted in lower final framework ATE compared to a "Random Noise" model, validating the design choice.
- **RL-based Computational Scheduling (Select Agent):**
 - The PPO-trained Select Agent converged stably over 1 million timesteps, with consistent improvement in mean episodic reward.
 - **Policy vs. Fixed/Heuristic/RL-gating (KF):** When comparing accuracy at similar average skip ratios (50%, 75%, 87.5%), the IMU-only prior scheduling maintained a flatter degradation curve under aggressive skipping compared to heuristic gating and remained competitive with RL-gating (KF) (which uses IMU+visual inputs). This indicated that the learned policy effectively captured motion-dependent uncertainty for scheduling without visual features.
 - **IMU-only vs. RL-gating (KF) Efficiency:** At a 50% average skip target, the IMU-only policy yielded substantially higher FPS (39 FPS vs. 21 FPS for RL-gating (KF)) because it avoided feature extraction during scheduling, with only a marginal increase in ATE (< 3%).
- **RL-based Adaptive Fusion (Fusion Agent):**
 - On EuRoC MH 04, the RL-trained Fusion Agent consistently yielded the lowest ATE (0.112m) compared to a Heuristic Fusion baseline (0.143m) and an EKF Fusion baseline (0.127m). This suggested that PPO fine-tuning learned context-dependent fusion weights that adapted to motion and VO confidence.
 - The learned policy also demonstrated generalization by substantially reducing the ATE of DROID-VO when applied as its fusion component (from 0.399m to 0.237m), indicating its independence from architecture-specific features.

#### 4.4. Robustness to Visual Degradations

The system's robustness to visual quality deterioration was assessed by synthetically corrupting the EuRoC MH 04 sequence with blurred and noise-corrupted images.
- Under 5% and 10% image corruption, the proposed method's ATE increased but consistently maintained lower absolute errors (e.g., 0.153m at 10% blur) compared to DPVO (0.192m at 10% blur). This indicated that the learned Select and Fusion agents contributed to the system's ability to remain accurate even when a fraction of visual observations became unreliable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The presented dual-agent reinforcement learning framework for Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) addresses a long-standing challenge in the field: the fundamental trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Mitigation of VIBA Bottleneck:** The work introduces a novel strategy to reduce the computational burden of Visual-Inertial Bundle Adjustment (VIBA) by intelligently scheduling its invocation and adaptively fusing its outputs. This approach represents a departure from efforts to completely replace VIBA, instead focusing on making it more resource-efficient and adaptable without sacrificing its core accuracy benefits.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** By introducing an IMU-only Select Agent, the framework demonstrates the capability to pre-emptively skip the entire visual odometry (VO) pipeline for redundant frames. This "shift-left" strategy, making decisions solely on high-frequency inertial data, leads to substantial computational savings, translating into significantly higher throughput (e.g., 1.77× faster than DPVO) and reduced backend processing cost (9.5× reduction compared to ORB-SLAM3's average backend cost).
- **Adaptive and Robust Sensor Fusion:** The composite Fusion Agent, with its learned, context-dependent fusion policy, dynamically balances the contributions of IMU propagation and visual updates. This adaptability, guided by an uncertainty-aware reward function, contributes to competitive accuracy and improved robustness under visual degradations (e.g., blur, noise), which are common challenges in real-world scenarios.
- **Resource-Constrained Deployment Feasibility:** The combined benefits of high throughput, reduced computational load, and lower GPU VRAM consumption (45.2% reduction compared to DROID-VO) make the proposed framework a promising candidate for deployment on resource-constrained platforms such as edge devices, mobile robots, UAVs, and augmented reality (AR) systems. This addresses a critical need for real-time applications where computational budgets are often tight.
- **Advancement in RL for Odometry:** The dual-agent architecture, featuring an IMU-only scheduler and an RL-based VIO fusion mechanism, represents a notable step in applying reinforcement learning to low-level control and decision-making within odometry pipelines. It expands the scope of RL in this domain beyond internal VO heuristics to encompass holistic, cost-aware system management.
- **Generalizability of Learned Policies:** The demonstration that the Fusion Agent's learned policy can generalize across different VO backends (e.g., improving DROID-VO's performance) suggests that the learned decision-making rules are robust to variations in underlying visual processing, potentially easing integration into diverse VIO ecosystems.

In conclusion, this research provides a practical and effective solution for developing more flexible, cost-aware, and performant VIO systems. Its impact lies in enabling more widespread and robust deployment of ego-motion estimation capabilities in real-world autonomous applications where computational resources are a primary consideration.
