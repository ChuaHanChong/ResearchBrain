# Self-Supervised Learning of Structured Dynamics from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fundamental AI Lab, UTN, and University of Oxford introduce a Structured Dynamics Model (SDM) that recovers disentangled camera and object motion representations from frozen pretrained image features. This model, trained with weak self-supervision, achieves performance competitive with strongly supervised 3D models on various motion understanding tasks and improves action recognition by 9.6 percentage points over a prior self-supervised approach.

## Problem

- Video data inherently entangles camera motion and independent object motion, complicating the learning of distinct motion representations.
- Existing self-supervised video models often capture aggregate temporal change without explicitly decomposing the underlying causes of motion.
- Many successful 3D perception systems rely on substantial explicit 3D annotations or pseudo-ground truth, limiting scalability and generalization with weaker supervision.

## Method

- The **Structured Dynamics Model (SDM)** is proposed, operating on frozen visual features extracted from a pretrained image backbone (e.g., ==DINOv2==).
- SDM employs a ==two-stage compensation process== to predict future features, decomposing temporal change into ==primary (dominant)== and ==residual (localized)== motion components.
- Training utilizes ==self-supervised feature prediction== combined with ==weak scene-level supervision== (indicating static camera or static scene in synthetic data) to guide the decomposition of motion.

## Results

- SDM consistently outperformed direct frozen-backbone descriptors and the self-supervised DeltaTok baseline on **five of seven** ProbeMotion tasks, including a **9.6 percentage point** improvement in SSv2-110k action classification accuracy.
- The model achieved performance competitive with or superior to larger, strongly supervised 3D representation models (e.g., VGGT-1B, Pi3X) on various motion tasks, particularly for object motion prediction (e.g., **0.13 MSE** improvement on Kubric object motion).
- Learned primary and residual tokens demonstrated specialization: primary tokens captured dominant motion (e.g., camera motion or object-centric actions), while residual tokens explained remaining localized dynamics, with the primary token achieving **23.1%** accuracy on SSv2-110k.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured motion representations, disentangling camera and object dynamics, can be effectively learned from frozen, pretrained image features using appropriate modeling and weak supervision.
> - A two-stage recurrent token-based architecture (primary and residual) provides an effective inductive bias for separating dominant global motion from localized, object-centric changes.
> - Integrating weak scene-level labels with unlabeled real-world videos during training significantly enhances the specialization of motion tokens and improves generalization to real-world benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the field of computer vision, specifically at the intersection of self-supervised learning for image and video understanding, and 3D perception. Recent advancements in self-supervised image backbones, such as DINOv2, MAE, and SigLIP, have demonstrated the capacity to extract rich semantic and geometric structures from large-scale image datasets. These pretrained representations serve as foundational components for various 3D perception and reconstruction systems. However, many successful approaches in this domain, including VGGT and Pi3X, often necessitate substantial supervision during adaptation, which may involve 3D annotations, pseudo-ground truth derived from Structure-from-Motion pipelines, or other explicit geometric labels. This reliance on dense supervision can limit scalability and leaves open the question of how much scene dynamics can be recovered from generic pretrained visual features with minimal or no explicit geometric labels.

Video data naturally contains temporal information that could potentially inform motion understanding. However, directly modeling frame-to-frame changes in video presents a challenge because it inherently entangles two primary sources of dynamics: camera motion and object motion. Existing self-supervised prediction objectives in video learning often model aggregate temporal change without explicitly decomposing these underlying causes. While latent action or world models summarize scene change with a single latent token, this approach can blend dominant global changes with more localized, object-centric dynamics. Consequently, the explicit decomposition of camera and object motion in representation learning, particularly with weaker forms of supervision, remains an area that has been underexplored.

This work investigates whether the information already present in frozen features of a pretrained image backbone is sufficient to support an explicit structuring of scene dynamics. Instead of training an entire video model from scratch or adapting existing models with dense supervision, the paper focuses on remodeling these static image features into structured motion representations. This approach aims to bridge the gap between powerful static image features and interpretable video dynamics by introducing a lightweight model trained with a combination of self-supervised learning and weak, scene-level supervision, thereby offering an alternative to methods requiring extensive 3D-specific annotations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain whether structured motion representations, capable of disentangling camera and object motion, can be effectively recovered from the frozen features of a pretrained image vision transformer. This objective is motivated by the following challenges and gaps in current representation learning for video understanding:

1. **Entangled Dynamics in Video:** Frame-to-frame changes in video simultaneously reflect both camera motion and independent object motion. This entanglement complicates the learning of robust motion representations that can differentiate meaningful object dynamics from variations induced by camera movement.
2. **Underexplored Decomposition:** While crucial for learning interpretable motion representations, the explicit decomposition of camera and object dynamics has remained largely unaddressed in mainstream representation learning approaches. Many existing latent action or world models condense all scene changes into a single latent token, thereby mixing dominant global motion with localized dynamics.
3. **Reliance on Dense Supervision:** Despite the development of strong self-supervised image backbones, many successful 3D perception and reconstruction systems still rely on substantial supervision (e.g., 3D annotations, pseudo-ground truth from Structure-from-Motion, or other geometric labels) during their adaptation to video data. This dependence limits scalability and raises the question of whether scene dynamics can be recovered with weaker forms of supervision.
4. **Repurposing Pretrained Image Backbones:** The research questions whether the rich semantic and geometric structure already captured by frozen pretrained image backbones contains sufficient information to support an explicit structuring of scene dynamics, rather than requiring end-to-end training of complex video models.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper are:
- To investigate the feasibility of extracting structured motion representations from frozen pretrained image features.
- To propose the **Structured Dynamics Model (SDM)**, an architecture designed to operate on top of self-supervised image backbones. SDM aims to learn structured motion representations through future-feature prediction, explicitly separating dominant temporal change (primary motion) from residual dynamics.
- To combine self-supervised learning on real-world video data with weak scene-level supervision on synthetic data (specifically, indicating whether the camera or scene is static) to guide the decomposition of motion.
- To develop **ProbeMotion**, a new evaluation suite encompassing synthetic and real videos with camera motion, object motion, and combined dynamics, for assessing the quality and interpretability of the learned structured motion representations.
- To evaluate SDM against various baselines, including direct frozen-backbone descriptors, other self-supervised video models, and strongly supervised 3D representations, to determine its competitive performance and the effectiveness of its structured approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology involves learning structured motion representations by predicting how frozen visual features change over time. The proposed **Structured Dynamics Model (SDM)** operates on top of a frozen pretrained image backbone, aiming to decompose temporal change into primary and residual motion components.

#### 3.1. Frozen Visual Features

The process begins by extracting features from each video frame using a frozen vision transformer (E), specifically DINOv2-B/14 with registers. A spatial feature map $f_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times D}$ is obtained for each frame $x_t$. Following previous work, intermediate features from all 12 blocks of the backbone are concatenated channel-wise and then projected via a two-layer MLP to a dimension of $D=768$. SDM then operates in this frozen feature space.

#### 3.2. Structured Dynamics Model (SDM)

SDM processes consecutive feature maps $(f_{t-1}, f_t)$ to predict $f_t$ through a two-stage compensation process:

- **Primary Motion Stage:**
 - **Primary Motion Extraction ($\phi_p$):** A recurrent motion token $p_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is updated by a 4-block transformer decoder ($\phi_p$). This decoder attends to the current feature pair $(f_{t-1}, f_t)$ and incorporates the previous primary token $p_{t-1}$ (where $p_0$ is learnable). This recurrence accumulates motion information over time, with each update focusing on adjacent frames.
 - **Primary Motion Compensation ($\psi_p$):** A shallow 2-block decoder ($\psi_p$) utilizes $p_t$ to compensate the source feature map $f_{t-1}$, generating an intermediate prediction $f_t^{>}$. This output is designed to explain the dominant part of the transition from $f_{t-1}$ to $f_t$.

- **Residual Motion Stage:**
 - **Residual Motion Extraction ($\phi_r$):** A second recurrent token $r_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is extracted by a 4-block transformer decoder ($\phi_r$). This stage conditions $r_t$ on the mismatch between the primary-compensated feature map $f_t^{>}$ and the target $f_t$, updating the previous residual token $r_{t-1}$ (where $r_0$ is learnable). This encourages $r_t$ to specifically represent dynamics not explained by the primary component.
 - **Residual Motion Compensation ($\psi_r$):** Another shallow 2-block decoder ($\psi_r$) refines $f_t^{>}$ into the final prediction $f_t^{>>}$ using $r_t$.

The pair $(p_t, r_t)$ constitutes the structured motion representation. The model extends naturally to longer clips by carrying $p_{t-1}$ and $r_{t-1}$ forward, accumulating temporal context.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

SDM is trained via feature prediction in the frozen encoder space using an MSE loss. To encourage the desired primary/residual semantic decomposition, weak scene-level annotations are utilized:

- **Unlabeled or Dynamic Videos:** For videos without specific labels (or with both camera and scene dynamics), only the final prediction is supervised: $L = L_{MSE}(f_t^{>>}, f_t)$.
- **Static Scenes (Camera Motion Only):** For samples where only the camera moves (scene is static), the residual stage is bypassed, and the primary compensation is directly supervised: $L = L_{MSE}(f_t^{>}, f_t)$.
- **Static Cameras (Object Motion Only):** If the camera is static, the primary stage is regularized to leave the source representation unchanged, while the residual stage captures scene dynamics: $L = L_{MSE}(f_t^{>>}, f_t) + \lambda_{reg}L_{MSE}(f_t^{>}, f_{t-1})$.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Motion Extractors ($\phi_p, \phi_r$):** 4-block transformer decoders with 4 learnable registers. They employ 3D RoPE for spatial and temporal positions.
- **Predictors ($\psi_p, \psi_r$):** 2-block decoders cross-attending to motion tokens, using 2D RoPE for spatial positions.
- **Training:** A mixture of Kubric (synthetic), SSv2, and DL3DV (real) videos. Kubric provides the weak scene-level labels for 1/4 static-scene and 1/8 static-camera samples in each mini-batch. 5 frames are sampled at 2 fps, resized to 224x224. Trained for 200k iterations with AdamW.

#### 3.5. Datasets

- **Training:**
 - **Kubric:** 180k synthetic sequences generated with controlled camera/object motion and weak scene-level labels (static scene/camera).
 - **SSv2:** Approximately 170k clips, unlabeled.
 - **DL3DV:** Approximately 4k clips, unlabeled.
- **Evaluation (ProbeMotion):** A suite of 7 benchmarks for linear probing:
 - **Kubric:** Synthetic camera and object motion.
 - **DL3DV, CameraBench:** Real-world camera motion.
 - **Static DAVIS2017, Static YouTubeVOS:** Real-world 2D object motion under approximately static cameras (filtered using VGGT-estimated camera motion and mask-centroid displacement).
 - **SSv2-110k:** Motion-heavy action semantics.

#### 3.6. Evaluation Protocol

Linear probes are trained on frozen SDM motion tokens (p or r).
- **Tasks:** Regression for continuous motion targets (e.g., 6-DoF camera pose, 3D object translation, 2D object displacement) and classification for discrete labels (e.g., camera-motion class, action class).
- **Metrics:** Normalized MSE for regression, top-1 accuracy for classification.
- **Token Selection:** $p$ is probed for camera motion (Kubric, DL3DV, CameraBench), object motion in static-camera scenes (static YouTubeVOS), and action recognition (SSv2-110k). $r$ is probed for Kubric object motion in dynamic-camera scenes.
- **Probe Training:** Single linear layer trained with SGD over 100 epochs, using multiple learning rates and motion-magnitude/class balancing.

#### 3.7. Baselines

- **Direct Frozen-Backbone Descriptors:** Derived from DINOv2 CLS token or average-pooled patch features (concatenation or feature differences, multi-frame variants).
- **Self-supervised DeltaTok:** A model that represents frame-to-frame change with a single delta token.
- **Strongly Supervised 3D/Geometry Models:** VGGT-1B, Depth Anything 3 (DA3), and Pi3X, which are larger models trained with explicit geometric supervision (camera pose, depth, point clouds). Descriptors derived from average-pooled feature maps or camera/register tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SDM on the ProbeMotion benchmark suite yielded several findings regarding its performance and the characteristics of its learned representations.

#### 4.1. Performance Against Baselines

- **Outperforms Direct Frozen-Backbone Descriptors:** SDM consistently improved over baselines using global CLS tokens or average-pooled features from DINOv2. On 3D motion probes (Kubric, DL3DV), SDM showed substantial gains. For instance, on Kubric camera motion, SDM achieved an MSE of 0.16 compared to 0.96 for AVG-pool and 1.16 for CLS. This suggested that structured future-feature prediction provided benefits beyond strong frozen features alone. The only exception was static DAVIS2017 2D object-displacement regression, where AVG-pool performed marginally better. The improvements persisted even when direct baselines were given access to multi-layer DINOv2 features, indicating that SDM's gains were not merely due to feature aggregation.
- **Outperforms DeltaTok:** Despite being trained with significantly less compute and at lower input resolution (200k iterations, 224x224 input vs. 1.6M iterations, 512x512 input), SDM outperformed DeltaTok on five of seven ProbeMotion tasks. Notable gains were observed on Kubric object motion (0.16 lower MSE) and SSv2-110k action classification (9.6 percentage points higher accuracy). DeltaTok showed better performance on static DAVIS2017 and CameraBench. This indicated that structured latent representations could offer robust performance across a range of motion and action tasks.
- **Competitive with Supervised 3D Representations:** Despite using weaker supervision (synthetic scene-level labels and unlabeled real data) and fewer parameters (215M vs. ~1B), SDM achieved performance comparable to or surpassing strongly supervised 3D and geometry models. SDM approached supervised-model performance on DL3DV and performed similarly on CameraBench. Furthermore, SDM achieved the best performance on two out of three object-motion probes, with a 0.13 MSE improvement on Kubric object motion and 0.04 MSE improvement on static YouTubeVOS compared to the strongest supervised baseline. On static DAVIS2017, SDM was on par with DA3. These results suggested that SDM could extract motion-relevant information not fully captured by supervised descriptors, even without explicit geometric supervision.
- **Primary Tokens for Object-Centric Action Prediction:** On SSv2-110k, SDM's primary token demonstrated an accuracy of 23.1%, which was an 8.2 and 20.8 percentage point improvement over CLS and AVG-pool baselines, respectively. It also exceeded DeltaTok by 9.6 percentage points and was 3.3 percentage points higher than the best supervised descriptor (Pi3X average-feature concatenation). This indicated the generalization capability of SDM's primary token to object-centric action semantics.

#### 4.2. Analysis of SDM

- **Temporal Context Utilization:** SDM demonstrated the ability to benefit from longer temporal context, particularly on temporally coherent benchmarks. Increasing context from T=2 to T=4 reduced MSE for Kubric camera motion (0.26 to 0.16) and object motion (0.22 to 0.19). SSv2-110k accuracy monotonically increased from 16.7% at T=2 to 23.1% at T=7, surpassing the training range of T=5. This suggested that SDM effectively integrated motion information over time, outperforming multi-frame baselines.
- **Structured Motion Specialization:** The learned primary (p) and residual (r) tokens exhibited specialization according to dominant motion regimes. On dynamic-camera datasets, $p$ was significantly better for camera-motion prediction, while $r$ performed better for Kubric object motion, capturing dynamics not explained by the primary component (0.19 vs 0.21 MSE for object motion, 0.28 vs 0.16 MSE for camera motion). Under static cameras, where object motion was dominant, $p$ performed best on static YouTubeVOS. Similarly, on SSv2-110k, $p$ was optimal for action recognition. Concatenating $p$ and $r$ tokens often yielded similar performance to the best individual token, implying that task-relevant information was primarily encoded in one token, though Kubric object motion showed some benefit from concatenation (0.14 MSE vs 0.19 for $r$).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Weak Anchors and Sequential Extraction:** Removing weak static-scene or static-camera supervision weakened token specialization, indicating their importance in guiding the primary/residual structure. Joint extraction of $p$ and $r$ also reduced specialization.
 - **Intermediate Features and Real Data:** Using intermediate encoder features (from all 12 blocks) improved performance across all benchmarks compared to using only final-layer features (e.g., Kubric camera error from 0.50 to 0.16). Incorporating unlabeled real video data during training significantly improved generalization to real-world benchmarks.
 - **Loss Function and Regularization:** MSE loss generally improved Kubric object-motion probing compared to L1 loss. The static-camera regularization weight ($\lambda_{reg}$) allowed for balancing the trade-off between Kubric object-motion performance and SSv2-110k action recognition.
 - **Backbone Agnosticism:** SDM demonstrated applicability across diverse image encoders, including MAE, SigLIP, DINOv3, and DINOv2, with DINOv2/3-based backbones generally yielding the best overall performance.
- **Qualitative Evaluation:**
 - **Stage-wise Compensation:** Visualizations showed that the primary stage effectively corrected dominant global changes (e.g., camera-induced movement), while the residual stage refined predictions by reducing localized errors around independently moving objects.
 - **Motion Extrapolation:** Repeatedly applying the last observed motion token led to plausible short-horizon feature extrapolations, suggesting stable capture of local temporal dynamics. However, predictions drifted over longer horizons without new observations.
 - **Latent Motion Swapping:** Transferring motion tokens from a source clip to a target clip with a different object induced consistent short-horizon feature-space motion, indicating that the learned tokens could parameterize local and generalizable transformations across videos.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented by Knobel et al. introduces the Structured Dynamics Model (SDM) and makes several contributions that have implications for video understanding, representation learning, and the utilization of pretrained models.

The primary significance of this work lies in demonstrating that structured motion representations can be extracted from frozen, pretrained image backbones using a combination of self-supervised learning and weak scene-level supervision. This approach challenges the traditional paradigm of end-to-end video model training or reliance on dense 3D annotations for motion understanding.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** By explicitly decomposing video dynamics into primary and residual motion components, SDM facilitates more interpretable temporal abstractions. The specialization of these tokens (e.g., primary for camera motion or dominant object motion, residual for remaining object-centric dynamics) offers insights into the underlying causes of temporal change, mirroring the effectiveness of coarse-to-fine refinement strategies found in other computational vision tasks.
- **Efficient Video Understanding:** SDM provides a method to derive robust video dynamics representations from powerful static image features without the computational cost and data requirements of full video pretraining. This makes it more efficient to adapt existing image models for video tasks, potentially reducing the need for extensive video-specific datasets and computational resources for initial training.
- **Reduced Supervision Requirements:** The reliance on weak scene-level supervision (e.g., knowledge of whether the camera or scene is static) for guiding the decomposition, combined with unlabeled real-world videos, represents a significant reduction in annotation burden compared to methods requiring explicit 3D geometry, depth maps, or camera poses. This could accelerate the development of motion understanding systems in scenarios where dense labels are scarce or expensive.
- **Improved Generalization:** The ability of SDM's tokens to generalize across different objects and types of motion, as demonstrated by the motion extrapolation and latent motion swapping experiments, suggests that the model learns generalizable motion patterns rather than merely memorizing specific dynamics.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The findings suggest that existing image models already encode substantial information relevant to video dynamics. This could inspire further research into leveraging and remodeling frozen features for other video-related tasks, such as video generation, prediction, or robotic control, particularly in low-data regimes. The effectiveness of residual compensation as an inductive bias for temporal dynamics may also find applications in other domains.
- **Development of Evaluation Benchmarks:** The introduction of ProbeMotion, a diverse evaluation suite, provides a standardized platform for assessing structured motion representations, contributing to the rigorous evaluation of future models in this domain.

In summary, this research advances the field by showing that interpretable, structured video dynamics can emerge from less data-intensive and computationally lighter approaches, by effectively repurposing and building upon the strong foundations of pretrained image models.
