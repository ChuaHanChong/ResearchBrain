# EgoEngine: From Egocentric Human Videos to High-Fidelity Dexterous Robot Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoEngine transforms egocentric human manipulation videos into synchronized, high-fidelity robot observation-action pairs, allowing for zero-shot visuomotor dexterous policy learning on real robots. The framework generates executable robot trajectories and realistic visual observations, achieving comparable task success rates to real-robot demonstrations with improved efficiency in data generation.

## Problem

- Collecting large volumes of high-quality, diverse demonstration data for dexterous robotic manipulation policies is expensive and time-consuming using traditional teleoperation.
- Abundant egocentric human videos, while rich in manipulation behaviors, suffer from visual differences between human and robot embodiments.
- Human motions from videos are often physically infeasible or sub-optimal for robots due to kinematic and dynamic discrepancies, posing an "action gap".

## Method

- **EgoEngine** reconstructs an object-centric ==digital twin== from egocentric human videos, including object pose tracking and scene geometry.
- A visual generation branch removes human elements and synthesizes robot observations by rendering a robot into the inpainted scene.
- An action generation branch retargets human hand motions to the robot, then refines these trajectories using a physics simulator with an adaptive multi-solver (Replay, MPC, RL) optimization strategy.

## Results

- EgoEngine generates robot observations with improved realism and closer feature-space alignment to real-robot data compared to previous methods.
- The action generation branch produces executable and task-aligned robot trajectories, achieving strong success rates (e.g., **83%** on TACO, **90%** on Aria datasets).
- Visuomotor policies trained exclusively on EgoEngine's synthetic data demonstrate non-trivial zero-shot performance on real robots, outperforming real-robot teleoperation on some tasks (e.g., **60%** vs. **25%** on Hammer).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bridging both the visual embodiment mismatch and the action feasibility gap simultaneously is essential for enabling dexterous robot learning from human egocentric videos.
> - Refining retargeted human motions through object-centric trajectory optimization in a physics simulator yields physically executable robot actions.
> - An adaptive multi-solver approach, progressively engaging Replay, MPC, and RL based on task difficulty, efficiently generates high-fidelity action trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning, particularly for dexterous manipulation, requires large volumes of diverse and high-quality demonstration data to train effective policies. Historically, collecting such data on real robots has been a costly and time-consuming process, often relying on complex teleoperation systems that are difficult to scale. This constraint has limited the diversity and robustness of learned dexterous policies.

In contrast, egocentric human videos, captured from wearable devices like smart glasses, are abundant and naturally contain a rich variety of contact-rich dexterous manipulation behaviors across diverse scenes and objects. These videos represent a potentially scalable and cost-effective source of data for robot learning. However, directly leveraging human videos for robot training presents two primary challenges, often referred to as "gaps":

1. **The Visual Gap:** The appearance of human arms and hands differs substantially from robot manipulators, and human body parts often occlude the scene, making direct visual imitation challenging for a robot.
2. **The Action Gap:** Differences in morphology, kinematics, actuation, and contact dynamics between humans and robots mean that human motions, even if accurately retargeted, are often physically infeasible or sub-optimal for a robot to execute. Furthermore, human videos typically provide observable proprioceptive trajectories rather than explicit robot-executable action commands.

Prior research has explored various approaches to bridge these gaps. In human-to-robot action generation, methods range from teleoperation and direct motion retargeting (which often face feasibility issues) to more advanced structured motion planning or Real2Sim2Real approaches that refine trajectories using simulation-based optimization (e.g., Model Predictive Control or Reinforcement Learning). For human-to-robot video generation, techniques have included lightweight visual gap reduction (e.g., masking) and more sophisticated inpainting, rendering, and blending pipelines to replace human elements with robot counterparts. Learning from human videos has involved pretraining or co-training strategies, although many still rely on a foundational set of real robot demonstrations. More recent efforts have explored zero-shot policy learning from human videos, but these have often been demonstrated on simpler manipulation tasks or robots with fewer degrees of freedom.

EgoEngine positions itself within this landscape by presenting a unified framework designed to simultaneously address both the visual and action gaps. Its contribution lies in transforming egocentric human videos directly into high-fidelity, robot-executable observation-action pairs, thereby enabling zero-shot visuomotor dexterous policy learning without any reliance on real-robot teleoperation data. This approach aims to unlock the scalability of human video data for complex dexterous robot manipulation, overcoming limitations of prior methods that either address only one gap or still require significant robot-specific data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable framework, named EgoEngine, for converting egocentric human manipulation videos into high-fidelity robot demonstration data. This generated data is specifically designed to be suitable for training dexterous robot policies, ideally enabling zero-shot execution on real robots.

The motivation for EgoEngine stems from several challenges prevalent in modern robot learning:

- **High Cost of Robot Data Collection:** Training robust robotic policies, particularly for dexterous manipulation involving complex, contact-rich interactions, necessitates large and diverse datasets. Collecting such data on real robots through teleoperation is inherently expensive, time-consuming, and resource-intensive due to hardware availability, the complexity of high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) control interfaces, and the need for skilled operators. This cost severely limits the scale and diversity of robot demonstration datasets.
- **Untapped Potential of Human Videos:** Egocentric human videos represent an abundant and naturally rich source of diverse manipulation behaviors. These videos capture a wide range of human interactions with objects and environments, offering a low-cost, scalable alternative for observing task execution. Leveraging this readily available data could significantly alleviate the data scarcity problem for robot learning.
- **Inherent Discrepancies between Human and Robot Domains:** Despite the wealth of information in human videos, their direct application to robot learning is hindered by two fundamental domain gaps:
 - **Visual Embodiment Mismatch:** The appearance and kinematics of human hands and arms differ considerably from robotic manipulators. Human body parts often occlude crucial parts of the scene or task object, making direct visual imitation difficult for a robot. This necessitates transforming the visual perspective from human-centric to robot-centric.
 - **Action Feasibility and Control Mismatch:** Human motion trajectories are not directly executable by robots due to differences in morphology, joint limits, actuation capabilities, and contact dynamics. A simple retargeting of human poses to robot poses often results in physically infeasible or unstable robot actions. Furthermore, human demonstrations implicitly show proprioceptive states, not explicit action commands (e.g., joint torques or Cartesian velocities) required for robot control, creating a "proprio-to-action" gap.
- **Limitations of Existing Transfer Methods:** Previous works attempting human-to-robot transfer either focus predominantly on reducing the visual gap (often insufficient for robust action learning), use human videos primarily for pretraining or co-training (still relying on robot demonstrations for final policy learning), or are restricted to simpler manipulation tasks. A method capable of generating complete, high-fidelity robot observation-action pairs from human videos, enabling zero-shot learning for complex dexterous tasks, remained an open challenge.

To address these motivations, EgoEngine sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

1. **Generate High-Fidelity Robot Observation Videos:** Given an egocentric human RGB video, EgoEngine aims to produce a corresponding robot observation video where the human demonstrator is replaced by a robot, while meticulously preserving the original scene context, object states, and temporal alignment of the interaction.
2. **Produce Task-Aligned, Executable Robot Action Trajectories:** Simultaneously, EgoEngine seeks to generate a physically feasible and task-aligned robot action trajectory. This trajectory must accurately replicate the demonstrated object manipulation from the human video while adhering to the kinematic and dynamic constraints of the target robot.
3. **Enable Zero-Shot Visuomotor Policy Learning:** By creating synchronized pairs of generated robot observations and executable robot actions, the ultimate objective is to form a synthetic dataset that can be used to train visuomotor policies capable of performing dexterous manipulation tasks on a real robot in a zero-shot manner, i.e., without any prior real-robot demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoEngine operates as a two-branch data engine designed to convert egocentric human videos into synchronized robot-executable demonstrations. The process begins with the construction of an object-centric digital twin from the input human video, followed by parallel visual and action generation pipelines.

#### 3.1. Human Video to Simulation (Digital Twin Reconstruction)

For each input human video, an object-centric digital twin is reconstructed to provide a common grounding space for subsequent branches.
- **Input Data:** Egocentric RGB frames (e.g., from Aria Gen2 glasses) and synchronized 3D human hand poses (21 keypoints).
- **Depth Estimation:** Absolute depth maps are estimated from RGB frames using FoundationStereo.
- **Masking:** SAM2 is employed to generate two types of masks: human arm-hand masks (for demonstrator removal) and task-object masks (tracked from a first-frame prompt).
- **Object Pose Tracking:** Given the RGB-D frames, tracked object masks, and object mesh, FoundationPose estimates a temporally consistent 6D object trajectory {T^t_o}.
- **Camera Calibration:** For the Aria dataset, AprilTag-based calibration aligns the Aria Gen2 coordinate system to the robot base frame. For the TACO dataset, a heuristic offset from object positions defines a pseudo robot base.
- **Output:** The digital twin consists of camera geometry, depth, masks, human hand poses, object mesh, and the tracked 6D object trajectory.

#### 3.2. Action Generation Branch

This branch focuses on converting human motion into executable robot actions.

- **4.2.1. Human-Centric Retargeting:**
 - The first step involves an initial mapping of human hand motion to the robot's embodiment.
 - Using human fingertip positions and orientations, along with wrist orientation from the video, an inverse kinematics (IK) problem is solved with MINK.
 - The IK objective minimizes L2 losses to align robot fingertips and wrist with human poses, subject to robot joint limits and self-collision constraints.
 - This generates a reference robot trajectory (τ_ref), serving as an initial motion prior.

- **4.2.2. Object-Centric Trajectory Optimization:**
 - The retargeted trajectory from MINK is often kinematically and dynamically infeasible for the robot. Therefore, this step refines the trajectory in a physics simulator using an object-centric objective.
 - **Objective Function:** The primary objective is to reproduce the demonstrated 6D object motion (T^t_o) extracted from the human video. An object pose tracking error (e_t) is defined, combining Euclidean distance for translation and geodesic distance for rotation between the simulated object pose (T̂^t_o) and the target object pose (T^t_o).
 - **Reward Structure:** Within a valid regime (e_t ≤ C, where C is a threshold), the object-centric reward (r^obj_t = C - e_t) is non-negative, encouraging lower tracking error. Rollouts terminate early if e_t exceeds C. Auxiliary reward terms include regularization for contact, action smoothness, and human-mimetic pose matching (Appendix C.2).
 - **Solver Modes:** EgoEngine utilizes three progressively capable (and costly) solver modes:
 - **Replay:** Directly executes the retargeted reference trajectory.
 - **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** Performs local trajectory optimization by searching over short-horizon action samples around the reference trajectory.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** A hand residual policy (trained with PPO) predicts a corrective action (δa_t) added to a base command (a^base_t), providing stronger refinement for difficult segments.
 - **MCTS-style Adaptive Mode Switching:** To optimize efficiency, EgoEngine decomposes the long-horizon trajectory into temporal chunks. It adaptively selects the cheapest feasible solver for each chunk:
 - Starts with Replay. If it meets feasibility criteria for the current and next chunk, it's used.
 - If Replay fails, escalates to MPC.
 - If MPC fails, escalates to RL.
 - A two-chunk lookahead window is used to avoid local minima, but only the current chunk is executed. This prevents unnecessary use of expensive solvers.
 - **Domain Randomization:** For real-robot tasks, domain randomization is applied during optimization to improve robustness to sim-to-real discrepancies, calibration errors, and noisy object trajectories (Appendix C.2).

#### 3.3. Visual Generation Branch

This branch transforms the egocentric human video into an egocentric robot observation video.

- **Human Removal:** The human arm-hand regions (identified by SAM2 masks) are masked out from the raw egocentric frame (I^h_t). Inpaint-Anything v2 is then used to fill these masked regions, recovering the occluded scene and object content. This results in a demonstrator-free frame (Ī_t).
- **Occlusion-aware Blending:**
 - Using the robot trajectory from the action branch, the robot is rendered from the egocentric viewpoint to obtain (R_t).
 - An occlusion-aware mask (M̃^r_t) is computed via two-pass differential rendering: comparing a scene render with a fully transparent robot to one with an opaque robot. This implicitly removes occluded robot pixels.
 - The final generated robot observation (õ^r_t) is created by blending the rendered robot (R_t) with the demonstrator-free scene (Ī_t) using the occlusion-aware mask.

#### 3.4. Policy Distillation

- After both branches complete, each human video is converted into a synchronized robot demonstration consisting of generated robot observations (õ) and executable actions (ã).
- These pairs are aggregated into a synthetic robot dataset (D̃_robot).
- A visuomotor policy (π_θ) is then trained on this dataset using HPT, with an ℓ2 action regression loss. The policy uses a ResNet-18 visual backbone, a proprioceptive stem, a token fusion transformer encoder, and a flow-matching transformer decoder for action prediction (Appendix D).
- This training distills the generated observation-action pairs into a closed-loop controller capable of mapping robot observations to actions for zero-shot execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EgoEngine was structured around three key areas: visual fidelity, action fidelity, and downstream policy distillation on real robots.

#### 4.1. Visual Fidelity of Generated Robot Observations

- **Qualitative Assessment:** Visual comparisons (Figure 3, Appendix B.1) indicated that EgoEngine produces robot observations that are physically consistent and closely align with real robot observations. Compared to baselines (EgoMimic, VACE, Phantom), EgoEngine showed improved realism, especially around robot-object contact and occlusion synthesis.
- **Quantitative Feature-Space Alignment:** Features extracted from generated videos were compared to real robot observations using three pretrained encoders (ResNet18, VGG16, DINOv2). Fréchet Distance (FD) analysis (Table 1, Figure 4) showed that EgoEngine's features were closer to the real-robot feature distribution than the original human videos and most baselines. EgoEngine reduced FD across encoders, closely matching or outperforming other methods.

#### 4.2. Action Fidelity of Generated Robot Trajectories

- **Executability and Task Alignment:**
 - Direct execution of retargeted human motion (Mink/Replay) yielded low success rates (SR = 0.17 on TACO, 0.10 on Aria) due to embodiment mismatch and contact dynamics.
 - Local trajectory optimization (Spider/MPC) improved performance on some tasks (SR = 0.25 on TACO, 0.20 on Aria) but remained insufficient for grasp-centric stages.
 - Full RL refinement (H2S2R/RL) achieved strong performance (SR = 0.83 on TACO, 0.90 on Aria), demonstrating the need for robust refinement.
 - EgoEngine, using its adaptive mode switching, achieved comparable performance to full RL (SR = 0.83 on TACO, 0.90 on Aria), indicating its ability to generate executable and task-aligned trajectories across diverse tasks (Table 2).
- **Efficiency of Adaptive Mode Switching:**
 - EgoEngine significantly reduced the average simulation steps per successful trajectory timestep (generation cost) compared to full RL. On TACO, it reduced cost from 73,675 to 34,842 steps, and on Aria, from 20,237 to 16,560 steps.
 - This efficiency gain stems from assigning solver capacity only where needed: easier chunks are handled by Replay or MPC, while difficult contact-rich segments fall back to RL (Figure 6).
 - For Aria tasks, EgoEngine improved generation efficiency by 22% (Figure 5). This advantage was more pronounced in longer-horizon, complex tasks like those in TACO.
- **Motion Smoothness:** EgoEngine-generated action trajectories exhibited higher smoothness (lower SPARC score, indicating smoother motion) on average compared to real-robot teleoperation demonstrations, especially for tasks like Mustard and Drawer (Table E.1). This was attributed to starting from naturally smoother human motions and applying explicit smoothness regularization during optimization.

#### 4.3. Downstream Policy Distillation

- **Zero-Shot Real-Robot Policy Learning:** Policies trained solely on EgoEngine-generated demonstrations demonstrated non-trivial zero-shot performance on real robot tasks (Table 3).
 - Directly retargeted human videos or Phantom (visual editing only) resulted in near-zero success rates, emphasizing the criticality of action refinement for dexterous policy learning.
 - EgoEngine achieved success rates competitive with or exceeding real-robot teleoperation demonstrations on 2 out of 4 Aria tasks (e.g., 0.60 vs. 0.25 on Hammer, 0.70 vs. 0.70 on Flower). On Hammer, EgoEngine's policies learned a more stable grasp strategy (Figure 7b).
- **Behavioral Strengths and Failure Modes:** Learned policies from EgoEngine data could recognize task states, recover from intermediate failures, and adapt to object pose variations (Figure 7a, 7b). Primary failure modes included unstable pinch grasps and mistimed contact, suggesting areas for further refinement in contact modeling and reward engineering (Figure 7c, 7d).
- **Ablation Study (Table 4):**
 - Removing the action branch resulted in a substantial performance drop (0.03 SR for human videos, 0.05 SR with visual branch only), indicating that executable action generation is the primary contributor to downstream policy performance.
 - Removing the visual branch resulted in a smaller performance drop (0.43 SR), suggesting that policies can tolerate moderate visual embodiment mismatch if actions are executable.

#### 4.4. Scalability and Motion Pattern Robustness

- **Sample Efficiency:** Comparisons showed that EgoEngine-generated data provided a sample-efficient source of supervision, allowing policies to achieve positive success with small numbers of demonstrations and scale effectively in the low-data regime, reducing dependence on large-scale real-robot teleoperation (Figure E.1).
- **Complex Task Transfer:** EgoEngine successfully generated robot actions for complex manipulation tasks, such as knife cutting (Figure E.2), which are challenging to collect via visual teleoperation due to high-dimensional control and precision requirements. This highlights an advantage in transferring intricate human tool-use behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoEngine represents a methodological advancement in robot learning by providing a framework that converts egocentric human videos into robot-executable demonstrations. This work addresses several critical challenges in the field, with potential implications for the scalability and accessibility of robotic skill acquisition.

**Addressing the Data Bottleneck:** The primary significance of EgoEngine is its potential to mitigate the data scarcity issue in dexterous robot manipulation. By automatically generating high-fidelity robot observation-action pairs from readily available human videos, the framework offers a scalable alternative to costly and time-consuming real-robot data collection via teleoperation. This could significantly increase the volume and diversity of training data available for robot policies.

**Bridging Fundamental Gaps:** EgoEngine's integrated approach to simultaneously addressing both the visual gap (human vs. robot appearance) and the action gap (human vs. robot executability and dynamics) is a key contribution. Previous methods often focused on one aspect, limiting their applicability. By grounding both visual synthesis and action optimization in an object-centric digital twin, EgoEngine provides a more comprehensive solution for human-to-robot skill transfer.

**Enabling Zero-Shot Dexterous Policy Learning:** The demonstration of zero-shot visuomotor dexterous policy learning on a real humanoid robot, solely from EgoEngine-generated data and without any real-robot demonstrations, marks a notable achievement. This suggests a pathway for robots to acquire complex, contact-rich manipulation skills directly from human observation, substantially reducing the dependency on robot-specific teleoperation expertise and hardware.

**Improved Efficiency in Trajectory Optimization:** The adaptive MCTS-style mode-switching strategy for action generation contributes to efficiency. By selectively deploying computationally intensive solvers (like RL) only when necessary, EgoEngine optimizes the quality-efficiency trade-off. This makes the generation process more practical for long-horizon and complex tasks, where full RL refinement across entire trajectories would be prohibitive.

#### 5.1. Potential for Broader Application and Democratization

- **Foundation Models in Robotics:** EgoEngine's ability to generate large-scale, diverse robot datasets from human sources could be instrumental in training larger, more generalizable foundation models for robotics, leading to more capable and adaptable robot systems.
- **Accessibility of Robot Programming:** By enabling learning from common human videos, EgoEngine could lower the technical barrier for developing and deploying advanced robot skills, potentially democratizing access to robotic capabilities for a wider range of users and applications, including domestic robots, industrial automation, and assistive robotics.
- **Complex Skill Transfer:** The demonstrated ability to transfer complex tool-use behaviors, which are particularly challenging for direct teleoperation, highlights EgoEngine's potential for acquiring intricate and nuanced manipulation skills.

#### 5.2. Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions

Despite its contributions, EgoEngine has identified limitations that indicate areas for future research:
- **Quality of Generated Data:** The visual branch currently relies on blending-based synthesis, which may not achieve full photorealism. Action generation can still be affected by contact modeling errors and the sim-to-real gap, limiting the fidelity and robustness of the generated actions.
- **Scalability of Digital Twin Reconstruction:** While EgoEngine scales with human video collection, the process of building the digital twin remains a bottleneck. Challenges include obtaining high-quality object assets, estimating object states under severe occlusion, and handling deformable objects. Further automation and robustness in this stage are required.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Simulation-based trajectory optimization, while improved by adaptive mode switching, can still be slow at very large scales, even with parallelization. Future work could explore incorporating pretrained models to accelerate this process.

In summary, EgoEngine offers a viable pathway to unlock human egocentric videos as a scalable data source for dexterous robot learning, demonstrating capabilities for zero-shot policy transfer that address long-standing challenges in the field. Its continued development could lead to more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.
