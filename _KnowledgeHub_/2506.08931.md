# CLONE: Closed-Loop Whole-Body Humanoid Teleoperation for Long-Horizon Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> CLONE introduces a closed-loop system for whole-body humanoid teleoperation that achieves precise control for long-horizon tasks, virtually eliminating positional drift using only head and hand tracking. The system demonstrated a mean tracking error of 5.1 cm over 8.9 meters on a Unitree G1 robot, effectively enabling robust and coordinated execution of diverse dynamic skills.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid teleoperation systems often decouple upper and lower body control, limiting natural whole-body coordination and expressiveness.
- Open-loop teleoperation systems suffer from accumulated positional drift over time, degrading accuracy for long-horizon tasks.
- Learning-based methods frequently prioritize stability, resulting in conservative motions and a lack of robustness for diverse, dynamic skills, especially those requiring precise hand orientation.

## Method

- A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) policy is developed, allowing different experts to specialize in distinct motion patterns to achieve unified whole-body control for diverse skills.
- A closed-loop error correction mechanism, integrating LiDAR odometry with operator position tracking, continuously corrects the robot's global pose to prevent positional drift.
- A specialized training dataset, **CLONE**D, augments existing MoCap data with custom sequences and critical hand orientation information to support robust learning of dexterous whole-body movements.

## Results

- Achieved a mean global position tracking error of **5.1** cm over **8.9** meters in real-world straight-path experiments, effectively eliminating positional drift.
- Demonstrated successful real-time whole-body teleoperation on a Unitree G1 humanoid across diverse skills, including waving, squatting, and dynamic jumping, with high motion fidelity.
- Simulation results showed CLONE's MoE architecture and CLONED dataset outperformed ablated baselines, exhibiting superior accuracy in key body position, joint velocity, and hand orientation tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Mixture-of-Experts architecture effectively handles the conflicting control objectives of diverse whole-body movements within a single policy, allowing for adaptive skill specialization.
> - Integrating real-time global position feedback from LiDAR odometry directly into the control policy is crucial for eliminating accumulated positional drift in long-horizon teleoperation tasks.
> - Curating a purpose-built dataset that includes specific kinematic configurations and comprehensive hand orientation data is essential for training highly dexterous and robust humanoid control policies.
