# CLONE: Closed-Loop Whole-Body Humanoid Teleoperation for Long-Horizon Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> CLONE introduces a closed-loop system for whole-body humanoid teleoperation that achieves precise control for long-horizon tasks, virtually eliminating positional drift using only head and hand tracking. The system demonstrated a mean tracking error of 5.1 cm over 8.9 meters on a Unitree G1 robot, effectively enabling robust and coordinated execution of diverse dynamic skills.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid teleoperation systems often decouple upper and lower body control, limiting natural whole-body coordination and expressiveness.
- Open-loop teleoperation systems suffer from accumulated positional drift over time, degrading accuracy for long-horizon tasks.
- Learning-based methods frequently prioritize stability, resulting in conservative motions and a lack of robustness for diverse, dynamic skills, especially those requiring precise hand orientation.

## Method

- A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) policy is developed, allowing different experts to specialize in distinct motion patterns to achieve unified whole-body control for diverse skills.
- A closed-loop error correction mechanism, integrating LiDAR odometry with operator position tracking, continuously corrects the robot's global pose to prevent positional drift.
- A specialized training dataset, **CLONE**D, augments existing MoCap data with custom sequences and critical hand orientation information to support robust learning of dexterous whole-body movements.

## Results

- Achieved a mean global position tracking error of **5.1** cm over **8.9** meters in real-world straight-path experiments, effectively eliminating positional drift.
- Demonstrated successful real-time whole-body teleoperation on a Unitree G1 humanoid across diverse skills, including waving, squatting, and dynamic jumping, with high motion fidelity.
- Simulation results showed CLONE's MoE architecture and CLONED dataset outperformed ablated baselines, exhibiting superior accuracy in key body position, joint velocity, and hand orientation tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Mixture-of-Experts architecture effectively handles the conflicting control objectives of diverse whole-body movements within a single policy, allowing for adaptive skill specialization.
> - Integrating real-time global position feedback from LiDAR odometry directly into the control policy is crucial for eliminating accumulated positional drift in long-horizon teleoperation tasks.
> - Curating a purpose-built dataset that includes specific kinematic configurations and comprehensive hand orientation data is essential for training highly dexterous and robust humanoid control policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is rapidly advancing, with a long-term vision of deploying human-like robots in environments designed for humans, performing complex tasks from household chores to disaster response. A critical component in realizing this vision is effective teleoperation, allowing human operators to intuitively control humanoid robots and leverage human intelligence and adaptability in complex, unstructured scenarios.

However, the broader research landscape in humanoid teleoperation faces several persistent challenges that CLONE aims to address:

- **Decoupled Body Control:** Many existing teleoperation systems, in an effort to maintain stability, traditionally decouple the control of the upper and lower body. This approach sacrifices natural human-like coordination, limiting the robot's ability to perform integrated actions such as reaching for an object while walking or dynamically adjusting posture during manipulation. This significantly restricts the expressiveness and utility of humanoids in real-world scenarios that demand fluid, whole-body movements.
- **Accumulated Positional Drift (Open-Loop Limitation):** A fundamental drawback of most current teleoperation systems is their open-loop nature. Without real-time feedback on the robot's actual position in the environment, small tracking errors accumulate over time. This leads to significant positional drift, gradually degrading the operator's spatial awareness and control precision, especially during long-horizon tasks requiring sustained accuracy. This drift can culminate in task failure, particularly for precise manipulation.
- **Limited Expressiveness and Robustness of Learning-Based Methods:** While learning-based approaches using motion capture data have shown promise, they often prioritize stability, leading to conservative motions confined to the training data distribution. Furthermore, many methods overlook critical factors like hand orientation, which is vital for dexterous manipulation. Monolithic MLP-based architectures, commonly used in these approaches, struggle to handle the diverse and often conflicting control objectives inherent in different motion types (e.g., dynamic walking versus precise crouching).
- **Dataset Deficiencies:** Existing large-scale motion capture (MoCap) datasets, such as AMASS, were primarily designed for animation and graphics. They often lack the specific kinematic configurations, dynamic transitions, and comprehensive hand orientation data crucial for training robust, generalizable humanoid control policies for real-world robotic applications.

CLONE directly confronts these challenges by introducing a system that enables unprecedented whole-body coordination and virtually eliminates positional drift for long-horizon tasks. It pushes the boundaries by demonstrating high-fidelity teleoperation with minimal operator input, making it highly practical. This work represents a significant step forward in making humanoid robots more capable and versatile in complex, real-world human-scene interactions, building upon and extending previous efforts in learning-based humanoid control and teleoperation by the same research community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of CLONE is to overcome the critical limitations of current humanoid teleoperation systems, thereby enabling precise, coordinated whole-body control of humanoid robots over extended durations while maintaining accurate global positioning. The motivation stems from the significant gap between the current capabilities of humanoid teleoperation and the potential for humanoids to replicate complex human-scene interactions in diverse environments.

The key objectives can be summarized as follows:

1. **Achieve Coordinated Whole-Body Teleoperation:** The system aims to enable natural and fluid coordination across the entire humanoid body, breaking away from the decoupled upper- and lower-body control that limits expressiveness in many existing systems. This includes dynamic movements that integrate locomotion and manipulation, such as squatting to pick up objects while navigating.
2. **Eliminate Positional Drift in Long-Horizon Tasks:** A central goal is to solve the fundamental problem of accumulated tracking errors. CLONE seeks to develop a closed-loop system that provides real-time position feedback to prevent drift, ensuring sustained spatial accuracy for the robot relative to the operator and the environment over extended operation periods.
3. **Support Diverse Motion Skills with a Unified Policy:** The research aims to develop a robust control policy capable of generating a wide range of motion patterns (e.g., walking, crouching, reaching, jumping) from a single, unified architecture, addressing the limitations of monolithic policies that struggle with conflicting objectives.
4. **Enable Practical Deployment with Minimal Input:** A significant objective is to achieve high-fidelity teleoperation using only minimal and readily available input from a commercial Mixed Reality (MR) headset (specifically head and hand tracking), simplifying the setup and making the system practical for real-world applications.
5. **Curate a Comprehensive Training Dataset:** To support robust learning of diverse and dexterous whole-body movements, the project aims to create an augmented dataset that addresses the deficiencies of existing motion capture data, particularly concerning specific kinematic configurations and crucial hand orientation information.

In essence, the researchers are motivated by the vision of humanoids effortlessly performing complex, multi-stage tasks in human environments. They identify that the current "capability gap" in teleoperation—marked by limited coordination and unbounded drift—is a major impediment to this vision. CLONE seeks to bridge this gap by integrating advanced learning architectures with real-time feedback mechanisms, thereby establishing a new benchmark for practical and capable humanoid teleoperation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CLONE framework is a sophisticated closed-loop whole-body teleoperation system that integrates learning-based coordination with real-time feedback correction. It operates on minimal input, specifically the 3D head position and 6D wrist poses from a commercial Mixed Reality (MR) headset (e.g., Apple Vision Pro). The methodology is structured around three core components: an Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) policy learning framework, a closed-loop error correction mechanism, and a meticulously curated training dataset.

#### 3.1. Policy Learning with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)

CLONE employs a teacher-student training strategy, building upon the OmniH2O framework.
- **Teacher Policy Training:** An Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) serves as the teacher policy, trained with privileged information (full robot state, velocities, angular velocities of all links, and environmental context). This teacher learns to output target joint positions for PD control, processing comprehensive state and task observations (reference motion and tracking errors).
- **Student Policy Distillation (MoE Architecture):** The student policy, designed for real-world deployment, operates with limited, real-world observable information (IMU data for joint positions/velocities, root angular velocity, gravity vector; and LiDAR-derived head/wrist positions, target poses). The key innovation here is the **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)** architecture.
 - The MoE consists of multiple layers, each containing several independent "experts" (feed-forward MLPs).
 - A "router" dynamically determines activation weights for these experts based on the input, selecting the top-k experts whose outputs are then combined via a weighted sum to produce the layer's output.
 - This design allows different experts to specialize in distinct motion patterns (e.g., one expert for walking, another for crouching), effectively handling the diversity of whole-body movements within a single policy.
 - To prevent mode collapse where only a few experts dominate, a balancing loss is introduced, encouraging more uniform activation across all experts.

#### 3.2. Closed-Loop Error Correction

This component addresses the critical issue of positional drift inherent in open-loop systems.
- **LiDAR Odometry:** CLONE integrates FAST-LIO2, a robust algorithm that tightly couples IMU and LiDAR data, to provide real-time, accurate global position estimates for the humanoid robot.
- **Operator Position Tracking:** The MR headset similarly tracks the operator's global position using its internal odometry.
- **Feedback Mechanism:** The student teleoperation policy directly receives the *difference* between the humanoid's and the operator's global positions. This positional error is incorporated into the task observations.
- **Drift Mitigation:** A carefully designed reward function incentivizes the policy to minimize this positional difference, ensuring that the robot continuously corrects its global pose to match the operator's, thereby preventing the accumulation of errors over extended periods. This mechanism operates at 50 Hz, with LiDAR data updated at 10 Hz.

#### 3.3. Reward Design and Domain Randomization

The training builds upon established reward terms and domain randomization techniques from OmniH2O, with several critical enhancements:
- **Velocity-Dependent SDE Noise Model:** To make the policy robust to real-world LiDAR odometry errors, a stochastic differential equation (SDE) noise model is applied to the head position during training. This noise scales proportionally to movement speed, mimicking real-world sensor limitations.
- **Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP):** Since the system only receives upper-body references (head and wrists), AMP is used to regularize lower-body movements. This encourages the policy to generate natural, stable, and dynamically plausible locomotion without explicit lower-body guidance.
- **Comprehensive Reward Terms:** A detailed set of reward functions guides the policy, including penalties for torque, joint limits, acceleration, velocity, action rates, stumbling, slippage, and alignment rewards for various body parts (DoF position, velocity, body position/rotation, hand rotation).
- **Extensive Domain Randomization:** Various environmental and robot parameters are randomized during training (e.g., friction, CoM offset, link mass, PD gains, torque noise, control/global step delays, random pushes, diverse terrain types) to enhance the policy's robustness and generalization capabilities in unstructured environments.

#### 3.4. The CLONED Dataset

A crucial aspect of CLONE is its specialized training dataset, CLONED, designed to support robust whole-body teleoperation:
- **Augmented AMASS:** A curated subset of 149 AMASS sequences is used, focusing on diverse upper- and lower-body movements, enhanced through motion editing to increase compositional diversity.
- **Custom MoCap Sequences:** 14 additional sequences are captured using an IMU-based Xsens MoCap system. These fill gaps in existing datasets, emphasizing continuous transitions and varied upper-body poses critical for manipulation tasks.
- **Hand Orientation Augmentation:** Crucially, hand orientation data is systematically augmented using procedurally generated 6D wrist targets, smoothed via Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP). This ensures that the policy learns coherent and natural hand motions, vital for dexterous teleoperation.

The integration of these components—a specialized MoE architecture for diverse skills, a robust closed-loop error correction system for global accuracy, and a comprehensive, purpose-built dataset—forms the foundation of CLONE's ability to achieve high-fidelity, long-horizon humanoid teleoperation with minimal input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides comprehensive validation of CLONE's capabilities through both real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid and extensive simulations. The findings consistently demonstrate significant improvements in whole-body motion fidelity and precise position tracking for long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.1. Real-World Experiments (Unitree G1 Humanoid)

- **Global Position Tracking Accuracy:**
 - **Straight-path tracking:** CLONE achieved a remarkably low mean tracking error of **5.1 cm** over distances up to 8.9 meters, with a maximum deviation of 12.0 cm. Statistical analysis confirmed no significant performance degradation across different tested distances (p-values > 0.05), indicating effective and sustained error mitigation by the closed-loop system.
 - **Curved-path tracking:** For a 10-meter trajectory involving two 90-degree turns, CLONE maintained a mean translational error of **20 cm** (maximum 27 cm) and a mean rotational drift of just **2 degrees**. These results underscore the system's ability to sustain high precision even over complex paths characteristic of real-world navigation.
 - **Significance:** These figures represent a substantial advancement, demonstrating that CLONE effectively addresses the long-standing problem of accumulated positional drift in humanoid teleoperation.

- **Whole-Body Motion Tracking Fidelity:**
 - CLONE successfully enabled real-time teleoperation across a diverse range of whole-body skills, including waving, squatting, standing up from squatted positions, and dynamic movements like jumping (Fig. 5).
 - The robot accurately reproduced complex operator movements, showcasing high whole-body motion fidelity and precise coordination, even for challenging dynamic skills requiring intricate balance control and force application.

- **Long-Horizon Mixed Navigation:**
 - Extended teleoperation sessions involving complex sequences were performed, where the operator traversed a path over 15 meters incorporating various locomotion patterns (forward walking, turning, side-stepping, returning to origin).
 - The robot consistently tracked the operator's movements throughout, returning to its starting position with minimal drift. This validates CLONE's robustness and reliability for sustained operations combining diverse locomotion and whole-body control, a critical capability for practical humanoid applications.

#### 4.2. Simulation Experiments

- **Motion Tracking Evaluation (CLONED Dataset):**
 - CLONE was quantitatively compared against two ablated baselines: CLONE$^{\dagger}$ (MLP student policy, similar to OmniH2O) and CLONE$^{*}$ (MoE policy trained on OmniH2O data instead of CLONED).
 - CLONE demonstrated superior performance across all key metrics: lower mean per-keybody position error (E$_{mpkpe}$), root-relative mean per-keybody position error (E$_{r-mpkpe}$), average joint velocity error (E$_{vel}$), and hand orientation tracking error (E$_{hand-rot}$).
 - This confirms that both the MoE architecture and the specialized CLONED dataset contribute significantly to accurate reference motion tracking and overall performance.
 - Qualitative comparisons further showed CLONE's robustness in complex motions like "crouch" or "squat to pick up an object," where CLONE$^{*}$ failed despite a larger training dataset, highlighting the importance of tailored data and architecture.

- **Tracking Diverse Stances:**
 - The system's robustness across varying postures, from standing (1.2m head height) to deep squatting (0.6m head height), was evaluated.
 - While CLONE sometimes showed slightly higher absolute position errors (E$_{mpkpe}$) at lower heights compared to baselines, it consistently outperformed them in local motion metrics (E$_{r-mpkpe}$, E$_{vel}$, E$_{hand-rot}$). This indicates that CLONE prioritizes faithful reproduction of the *reference motion itself*, especially for challenging postures, over strict global positioning, which is often more critical for complex interactions.
 - All methods naturally exhibited increased tracking errors at lower heights, affirming the inherent difficulty of teleoperating robots in deep squatting postures.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Analysis of history length revealed an optimal performance with 25 timesteps of history.
 - Architectural ablations confirmed that a configuration of three MoE layers, each with four experts, yielded optimal performance, balancing model complexity with tracking accuracy. Increasing expert count (N=8) showed redundancy for the current data.
 - The MoE architecture consistently outperformed a monolithic MLP baseline, underscoring its effectiveness in handling diverse motion patterns.

- **Expert Activation Analysis:**
 - Visualization of expert activation weights across nine distinct motion types (squat, crouchwalk, turn, punch, jump, stepside, forward, backward, stand) revealed clear specialization. For example, experts 1 and 2 predominantly activated during standing motions, while experts 3 and 4 were more active during squatting. Dynamic motions like jumping activated multiple experts, indicating collaborative processing. This analysis empirically validates the MoE's ability to adaptively learn and coordinate diverse skills.

In summary, the results firmly establish CLONE as a highly capable and robust teleoperation system, delivering superior whole-body coordination and effectively resolving the positional drift problem in long-horizon tasks, all while requiring minimal input.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLONE framework represents a significant leap forward in the field of humanoid robotics and teleoperation, addressing long-standing challenges that have hindered the practical deployment of humanoids in complex, unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact can be delineated as follows:

1. **Establishes a New Benchmark for Whole-Body Teleoperation:** By demonstrating robust whole-body coordination over long trajectories with minimal positional drift, CLONE sets a new standard for humanoid teleoperation fidelity. The achievement of a mean tracking error of 5.1 cm over 8.9 meters, coupled with precise execution of diverse and dynamic skills, signifies a critical advancement towards truly capable humanoids.

2. **Solves Fundamental Limitations:**
 - **Overcomes Positional Drift:** The closed-loop error correction mechanism, leveraging LiDAR odometry and operator tracking, fundamentally solves the problem of accumulated drift that plagues open-loop systems. This enables long-horizon tasks requiring sustained precision, which was previously unattainable.
 - **Achieves Natural Whole-Body Coordination:** The MoE-based policy learning effectively resolves the issue of decoupled upper- and lower-body control. It allows for natural, fluid synergies across the entire robot, enabling complex coordinated movements like "picking up objects from the ground" that integrate locomotion and manipulation seamlessly.

3. **Enhances Practicality and Accessibility:** By relying solely on head and hand tracking from a single commercial MR headset (like Apple Vision Pro), CLONE significantly simplifies the hardware requirements and calibration complexity compared to systems requiring full-body motion capture or intricate haptic devices. This ease of setup makes the system highly practical for real-world deployment in a wide array of applications.

4. **Methodological Contributions:**
 - **Novel MoE Framework:** The introduction of the first MoE-based framework for whole-body teleoperation provides a powerful and adaptive architecture for learning diverse motion skills within a unified policy. This approach can be generalized to other complex robotic control problems.
 - **Closed-Loop System for Drift Correction:** The elegant integration of LiDAR odometry for real-time pose correction offers a robust solution to a pervasive problem in legged robot control and teleoperation.
 - **CLONED Dataset:** The creation and augmentation of the CLONED dataset, specifically tailored for dexterous whole-body motions and proper hand orientation coverage, provide a valuable resource for future research and training of advanced humanoid controllers.

5. **Broadens Application Scope:** The capabilities demonstrated by CLONE open new possibilities for practical humanoid applications. These could include:
 - **Hazardous Environments:** Enabling human operators to perform complex tasks in dangerous or inaccessible locations (e.g., disaster relief, nuclear facility maintenance).
 - **Logistics and Manufacturing:** Allowing humanoids to handle diverse objects and navigate complex layouts in warehouses or factories with human-like dexterity.
 - **Healthcare and Assistance:** Facilitating personal assistance or medical support in home or clinical settings where precise manipulation and mobility are required.
 - **Exploration:** Enhancing human interaction with robots in space or underwater exploration.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as reduced performance in highly dynamic movements (e.g., jumping) and the trade-off between minimal input and fine-grained stability control, the work fundamentally shifts the paradigm for humanoid teleoperation. CLONE's ability to enable precise, coordinated, and long-horizon tasks with simple input will likely accelerate the development and adoption of humanoids, paving the way for more intelligent and versatile robots capable of operating effectively alongside humans in everyday environments. Future work building on CLONE could explore multimodal sensing and expanded datasets to further enhance dynamic capabilities, solidifying the framework's foundational impact.
