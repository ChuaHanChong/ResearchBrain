# Exploiting Both Domain-specific and Invariant Knowledge via a Win-win Transformer for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beijing Institute of Technology, Tsinghua University, and Inceptio Tech developed WinTR, a Transformer-based framework for unsupervised domain adaptation that simultaneously leverages domain-specific and domain-invariant knowledge. This method achieved state-of-the-art results on VisDA-2017 (90.1%), Office-Home (77.2%), and DomainNet (46.8%) benchmarks, outperforming previous Transformer and CNN-based approaches.

## Problem

- Existing Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) methods struggle to simultaneously leverage domain-specific information and transfer domain-invariant knowledge without compromise.
- Traditional UDA often relies on a shared classifier that biases towards the source domain, leading to sub-optimal performance on the target domain.
- Many UDA approaches built on CNNs face limitations in transferability due to strong inductive biases, motivating the exploration of Vision Transformers.

## Method

- Introduces ==WinTR==, a Vision Transformer-based framework employing two distinct ==classification tokens ([src] and [tgt])== with a domain-wise mask to learn separate source-oriented and target-oriented representations.
- Utilizes two separate ==domain-specific classifiers== (g_s and g_t) for the source and target domains, trained with ground truth and pseudo-labels respectively.
- Implements ==bi-directional cross-domain knowledge transfer== through single-sided contrastive feature alignment with stop-gradient regularization, and refines target pseudo-labels using source-oriented target features.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art average accuracy on VisDA-2017 with **90.1%** (WinTR-B), surpassing CDTrans-B (**88.4%**) and CNN-based FixBi (**87.2%**).
- Obtained **77.2%** average accuracy on the Office-Home dataset (WinTR-S), outperforming CDTrans-S (**74.7%**) and other strong baselines.
- Reached **46.8%** average accuracy on the challenging DomainNet dataset (WinTR-B), a substantial improvement over CDTrans-B (**37.0%**) and the DeiT-B backbone (**34.6%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision Transformers exhibit superior inherent transferability compared to CNNs, making them a strong backbone for UDA tasks.
> - Explicitly disentangling and exploiting both domain-specific and domain-invariant knowledge via dedicated tokens and classifiers within a Transformer architecture leads to a synergistic "win-win" outcome in UDA.
> - Source-oriented target features, supervised by clean source labels, retain finer structural relationships that are beneficial for refining noisy target pseudo-labels.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)** is a critical sub-area of transfer learning that addresses the challenge of deploying deep learning models in environments where the data distribution differs from the training data (domain shift). Traditional deep neural networks, despite their success, struggle to generalize to new domains without extensive re-labeling, which is often prohibitively expensive. UDA aims to transfer knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain.

Historically, most UDA methods, particularly those built on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) like ResNet, have adopted a paradigm centered on learning **domain-invariant representations**. This is typically achieved through techniques such as:
1. **Statistical alignment**: Minimizing divergence metrics like Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) between source and target feature distributions.
2. **Adversarial learning**: Using a domain discriminator to encourage the feature extractor to produce features indistinguishable by domain.
3. **Pseudo-labeling**: Generating pseudo-labels for target samples and using them for self-training.

A common characteristic of these approaches is the use of a **shared classifier** across both domains. While effective to a certain extent, this paradigm faces a fundamental dilemma: forcing a unified representation and classifier can lead to a loss of **domain-specific information** that might still be relevant to the task within each domain. Moreover, the source classifier inevitably biases towards its domain, hindering optimal performance on the target domain. Some prior works have tried to balance this, but still within the shared-classifier framework.

Recently, **Vision Transformers (ViTs)** have emerged as powerful alternatives to CNNs in computer vision tasks. They inherently process images as sequences of patches, relying on self-attention mechanisms rather than localized convolutions. An important observation, highlighted in this paper (Fig. 1), is that Transformers tend to learn **more transferable representations** due to their weaker inductive biases (e.g., locality, spatial invariance) compared to CNNs. This makes them particularly promising for UDA, where cross-domain generalization is key. Early efforts in Transformer-based UDA, such as TransDA and CDTrans, have begun exploring this potential, often by adapting attention mechanisms or introducing cross-attention.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these two trends, addressing the limitations of conventional UDA paradigms by leveraging the unique capabilities of Vision Transformers. It challenges the widely accepted notion of solely pursuing domain-invariant features and a shared classifier, proposing a "win-win" approach that explicitly accommodates and exploits both domain-specific and domain-invariant knowledge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the proposed WinTR framework are rooted in overcoming the inherent limitations of conventional UDA approaches and leveraging the unique advantages of Vision Transformers:

1. **Address the "dilemma" in UDA**: The paper identifies a core problem in existing UDA methods: they struggle to simultaneously explore task-relevant knowledge within each domain (domain-specific information) and transfer knowledge across domains (domain-invariant information) without one compromising the other. Most methods prioritize domain invariance, often at the cost of losing valuable domain-specific details or biasing the shared classifier towards the source. The authors aim to develop a "win-win" solution where both types of knowledge are effectively exploited.

2. **Exploit the transferability of Vision Transformers**: The authors empirically observe and highlight that Vision Transformers (e.g., DeiT) generate more transferable representations than CNNs (e.g., ResNet) of comparable size and performance on ImageNet (Fig. 1). This intrinsic property of Transformers motivates their adoption as the backbone architecture for UDA, as it provides a better starting point for knowledge transfer.

3. **Leverage Transformer's flexibility with classification tokens**: A key insight is the flexibility offered by learnable classification tokens in Transformers. Unlike CNNs which typically output a single feature vector, Transformers can incorporate multiple classification tokens, each potentially learning different mappings. This provides an architectural means to naturally disentangle and process domain-specific information.

4. **Develop separate, yet mutually beneficial, classifiers**: Instead of a single shared classifier that often struggles to fit both source and target distributions perfectly, the authors propose training two distinct, domain-specific classifiers. This allows each classifier to optimize for its respective domain's characteristics, mitigating the classifier-level competition seen in traditional UDA.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the belief that a well-designed Transformer-based UDA framework can move beyond the trade-off between domain-specific and domain-invariant knowledge, achieving superior adaptation by fostering a synergistic relationship between them.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Win-Win TRansformer (WinTR)** framework is designed to simultaneously exploit domain-specific and cross-domain knowledge. It builds upon the DeiT (Data-efficient Image Transformer) architecture and introduces several novel components:

- **Two Classification Tokens for Domain-Oriented Representations**:
 - Unlike standard Transformers that use one `[CLS]` token, WinTR introduces two distinct learnable classification tokens: `[src]` and `[tgt]`.
 - These tokens are appended to the input image patches, forming an input sequence `X = [x_[src]; X_p; x_[tgt]]`.
 - The goal is for `[src]` to primarily learn source-oriented representations (`f_s`) and `[tgt]` to learn target-oriented representations (`f_t`).
 - **Domain-wise Token Mask**: To ensure these two tokens learn distinct domain-specific mappings and prevent them from interfering with each other during self-attention, a domain-wise token mask is applied. This mask sets specific attention scores (between `[src]` and `[tgt]`) to negative infinity, effectively making them ignore each other during feature aggregation.

- **Two Domain-Specific Classifiers**:
 - Instead of a single shared classifier, WinTR employs two separate linear classifiers: `g_s` for the source domain and `g_t` for the target domain.
 - `g_s` operates on the source-oriented representation `f_s` (derived from `[src]`) and is trained using the ground truth labels of the source data (`L_s`). This allows `g_s` to fully explore source-specific discriminative knowledge.
 - `g_t` operates on the target-oriented representation `f_t` (derived from `[tgt]`) and is trained using pseudo-labels for the unlabeled target data (`L_t`). This classifier is tailored to the target domain's characteristics.

- **Bi-direction Cross-domain Knowledge Transfer Mechanisms**:
 - **Target Pseudo-Label Refinement incorporating Source Structural Information**: Pseudo-labels for the target domain are inherently noisy. WinTR refines these labels using **source-oriented target features** (`f_s_tj`). The crucial insight here is that `f_s_tj`, being supervised by clean source labels, maintains better structural relationships (i.e., class clusters).
 - The refinement process uses a weighted K-means clustering approach (similar to SHOT). Class centers (`c_k`) are computed based on the *source-oriented target features* (`f_s_tj`) and their predicted probabilities from `g_s`. Target pseudo-labels (`˜y_tj`) are then assigned by finding the nearest class center in this feature space. This mechanism injects source-domain structural knowledge into the target pseudo-label generation.
 - **Single-sided Feature Alignment with Stop-Gradient Regularization**: To facilitate knowledge transfer without compromising the integrity of each domain's specific representation, WinTR employs contrastive learning with a unique stop-gradient mechanism in both directions:
 - **Source-to-Target Alignment (L_con_s)**: The source-oriented target features (`f_s_tj`) are aligned towards the source-oriented source features (`f_s_si`). Critically, a `stop-gradient` operation is applied to `f_s_si`. This means `f_s_tj` is encouraged to move closer to `f_s_si`, but `f_s_si` itself remains stable and unaffected by target data, preserving its source-specific information. This transfers target domain knowledge to the source-oriented representations without diluting the source's discriminative power.
 - **Target-to-Source Alignment (L_con_t)**: Similarly, the target-oriented source features (`f_t_si`) are aligned towards the target-oriented target features (`f_t_tj`). A `stop-gradient` is applied to `f_t_tj`, ensuring that `f_t_tj` remains stable while `f_t_si` adapts to target domain characteristics. This transfers source discriminative information to the target-oriented representations.
 - Supervised Contrastive Learning is chosen for this alignment, using (pseudo) label information to attract same-class samples and repel different-class samples.

- **Overall Objective Function**: The total loss is a weighted sum of the source classification loss (`L_s`), target pseudo-label classification loss (`L_t`), and the two contrastive alignment losses (`L_con_s`, `L_con_t`): `L = L_s + L_t + λ(L_con_s + L_con_t)`.

The training procedure involves an initial stage of training a source model for preliminary pseudo-labels, followed by iterative refinement of pseudo-labels and joint optimization of the entire WinTR framework with all four loss components.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The WinTR framework demonstrates significant advancements in unsupervised domain adaptation, particularly leveraging the strengths of Vision Transformers. The key findings are:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance**: WinTR achieves new state-of-the-art results on three widely recognized UDA benchmark datasets:
 - **VisDA-2017 (Synthetic to Real)**: WinTR-B (using DeiT-Base backbone) achieves 90.1% average accuracy, surpassing previous bests including other Transformer-based methods like CDTrans-B (88.4%) and CNN-based methods like FixBi (87.2%) with a ResNet-101 backbone. WinTR-S also significantly outperforms its DeiT-S backbone by a large margin (88.8% vs. 66.7%).
 - **Office-Home**: WinTR-S outperforms all baseline methods, including advanced CNN-based approaches like ATDOC-NA (72.2%) and other Transformer-based methods like CDTrans-S (74.7%), achieving an average accuracy of 77.2%.
 - **DomainNet**: This challenging dataset with 345 categories and six domains showcases WinTR's robustness. WinTR-S achieves an impressive 41.8% average accuracy, significantly higher than CDTrans-S (32.2%) and SCDA (28.4%). WinTR-B pushes this further to 46.8%, representing a substantial gain over its DeiT-B backbone (34.6%) and CDTrans-B (37.0%). These results particularly highlight the method's effectiveness on tasks with larger domain gaps and more classes.

2. **Superiority of Vision Transformers in UDA**: The experiments confirm the initial hypothesis that Vision Transformers (DeiT) inherently possess higher transferability than CNNs (ResNet). Even without specific UDA algorithms, DeiT-S and DeiT-B baselines show notably better performance on target domains compared to their ResNet counterparts (e.g., DeiT-S > ResNet-101 on VisDA-2017). This emphasizes the importance of the backbone architecture for UDA.

3. **Effectiveness of Domain-Specific Knowledge Exploration**:
 - **Divergent Token Representations**: Analysis of cosine similarity between `f_s_tj` and `f_t_tj` shows that WinTR encourages more divergent representations for target samples compared to methods with combined losses or single classifiers. This indicates that the two tokens indeed learn different, domain-oriented perspectives.
 - **Importance of Token Mask**: Ablation studies reveal that removing the domain-wise token mask (WinTR-S w/o Mask M) leads to a performance drop (from 77.2% to 76.3% on Office-Home), confirming its role in preventing mutual interference and preserving domain-specific information.

4. **Critical Role of Bi-direction Cross-domain Knowledge Transfer**:
 - **Impact of Contrastive Losses**: Removing both contrastive losses (`L_con_s + L_con_t`) results in a significant performance decrease (from 77.2% to 74.6% on Office-Home). This underscores the importance of explicit feature alignment for cross-domain knowledge transfer.
 - **Improved Generalization**: Experiments show that applying the contrastive losses significantly improves the generalization performance of classifiers when tested on data from the *other* domain (e.g., `g_s` on target data or `g_t` on source data). This validates that the alignment mechanisms learn more robust and generalized feature embeddings.
 - **Source-Oriented Representation for Label Refinement**: A crucial finding is that refining target pseudo-labels using **source-oriented target features** (`f_s_tj`) yields better results than using target-oriented features (`f_t_tj`) (e.g., 77.2% vs. 76.2% on Office-Home average). This supports the hypothesis that source-oriented features, being supervised by clean labels, retain finer structural information valuable for pseudo-labeling.

5. **Robustness to Hyperparameters**: The method shows stable performance across a range of hyperparameters for the trade-off `λ` and temperature `τ`, indicating its practical applicability and less sensitivity to tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WinTR framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Unsupervised Domain Adaptation, with several profound implications:

1. **New Paradigm for UDA with Transformers**: The work fundamentally challenges the long-standing UDA paradigm of learning a single domain-invariant representation and sharing one classifier. By demonstrating the effectiveness of explicitly learning **both domain-specific and domain-invariant knowledge simultaneously** via dedicated tokens and classifiers, WinTR opens a new research avenue for UDA. This "win-win" approach overcomes the limitations of previous methods that faced a trade-off between preserving distinct domain characteristics and enabling cross-domain transfer.

2. **Highlighting the Power of Vision Transformers for Transfer Learning**: This paper strongly reinforces the notion that Vision Transformers are inherently well-suited for transfer learning tasks like UDA. Their weaker inductive biases and the flexibility of their architecture (especially classification tokens) allow for more adaptive and generalizable feature learning than traditional CNNs. This will likely encourage more researchers to explore Transformer-based solutions for various transfer learning problems beyond UDA.

3. **Innovative Architectural and Algorithmic Designs**:
 - The use of **multiple classification tokens with a domain-wise mask** is an elegant architectural solution for disentangling domain-specific representations within a single model. This could inspire similar designs for other multi-domain or multi-task learning scenarios.
 - The **single-sided feature alignment with stop-gradient** in the contrastive learning framework is a clever mechanism for achieving knowledge transfer without corrupting the integrity of the "anchor" domain's representation. This balance is crucial for the "win-win" outcome and offers a principled way to guide feature learning.
 - The insight that **source-oriented features are better for target pseudo-label refinement** (due to preserving finer structural relationships) is a valuable contribution to pseudo-labeling strategies in UDA.

4. **Strong Empirical Validation**: The consistent state-of-the-art results across diverse and challenging UDA benchmarks (VisDA-2017, Office-Home, DomainNet) provide strong empirical evidence for the efficacy and robustness of the WinTR framework. Its performance on DomainNet, in particular, suggests its potential for real-world applications with complex domain shifts.

5. **Future Research Directions**: WinTR's success will likely stimulate further research into:
 - More sophisticated mechanisms for distinguishing and combining domain-specific and invariant knowledge within Transformer architectures.
 - Exploring other forms of domain-wise masking or attention mechanisms.
 - Applying the "win-win" principle to other transfer learning settings (e.g., multi-source DA, partial DA, open-set DA) or even other modalities.
 - Understanding the theoretical underpinnings of why Vision Transformers exhibit superior transferability and how their components can be best exploited for adaptation.

In summary, this work not only delivers superior empirical results but also provides a fresh perspective on how to tackle the fundamental challenges in UDA by strategically leveraging the architectural advantages of Vision Transformers, thereby laying groundwork for more effective and versatile transfer learning solutions.
