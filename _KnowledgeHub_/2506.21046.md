# Boosting Generative Adversarial Transferability with Self-supervised Vision Transformer Features

> [!summary] Summary
> The Beijing Institute of Technology research presents dSVA, a generative adversarial attack leveraging self-supervised Vision Transformer (ViT) features for enhanced black-box transferability. This approach, which exploits ViT facet-level features, self-attention saliency maps, and jointly disrupts both global structural and local textural representations, achieves superior performance, outperforming state-of-the-art attacks by an average of 13.70% and robust defenses by 32.98% against diverse models.

## Problem

- Generating highly transferable adversarial examples for black-box attacks, where the adversary has no access to the target model's internal parameters.
- Existing black-box adversarial attacks predominantly relied on features from supervised Convolutional Neural Networks (ConvNets).
- The rich, semantically meaningful representations learned by self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViTs) remained largely unexplored for boosting adversarial transferability.

## Method

- Developed ==dSVA==, a generative adversarial attack that exploits specific ==facet-level features== within self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViTs).
- Utilized ==self-attention saliency maps== from ViTs as 'dense semantic guides' to focus adversarial perturbations on semantically important image regions.
- Jointly optimized the attack by disrupting complementary features from both ==Contrastive Learning (DINO)== and ==Masked Image Modeling (MAE)== self-supervised ViT models.

## Results

- The dSVA attack achieved state-of-the-art black-box transferability, outperforming existing generative and gradient-based attacks on ImageNet.
- dSVA (Joint) improved attack success rates by an average of **13.70%** over single-model variants and by **32.98%** against adversarially trained defense models.
- Facet-level exploitation (keys for DINO, queries for MAE) and self-attention saliency maps in the penultimate layer (l=10) were found to be most effective for maximizing transferability across diverse target architectures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exploiting granular 'facet-level' features (queries and keys) within ViT attention blocks yields more generalizable adversarial perturbations than aggregated layer outputs.
> - Self-attention saliency maps from self-supervised ViTs provide effective semantic guidance for regularizing attacks, focusing on perceptually important regions.
> - Features learned by Contrastive Learning (global structure) and Masked Image Modeling (local texture) are complementary, and jointly disrupting both leads to superior adversarial transferability.
