# Deep SPI: Safe Policy Improvement via World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces DEEP SPI, a framework and algorithm for safe policy improvement in online deep reinforcement learning environments that utilize world models and representation learning. It provides theoretical guarantees for monotonic policy improvement and convergence, while empirically demonstrating competitive performance on the ALE-57 benchmark.

## Problem

- Classical Safe Policy Improvement (SPI) methods are constrained to offline, tabular Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and require extensive state-action coverage, making them unsuitable for continuous or high-dimensional deep reinforcement learning.
- World models in online reinforcement learning often exhibit unreliable predictions in infrequently visited 'out-of-trajectory' (OOT) regions of the state space, potentially leading to unsafe policy updates.
- The simultaneous updating of policies and their underlying representations can lead to 'confounding policy updates,' where poor representations restrict agents to suboptimal behaviors or evolving policies hinder representation correction.

## Method

- A theoretical framework extending **SPI** to online, high-dimensional settings was developed, incorporating a novel neighborhood operator that constrains policy updates based on importance ratios.
- Local reward and transition losses were introduced and bounded by Lipschitz continuous world models to quantify model accuracy and ensure policy safety within the framework.
- DeepSPI, an on-policy algorithm, was designed by integrating these theoretical insights with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), modifying the utility function to include weighted auxiliary loss terms for model inaccuracies and representation learning.

## Results

- DeepSPI matched or exceeded the performance of baseline algorithms, including PPO and DeepMDPs, across aggregate human normalized scores on the ALE-57 benchmark.
- The algorithm achieved significantly lower transition losses compared to DeepMDPs, indicating improved world model accuracy in predicting future states.
- In a toy maze environment designed to highlight the confounding policy update problem, DeepSPI learned distinct latent representations for critical states, enabling it to learn the optimal policy where PPO failed to differentiate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A novel neighborhood operator for constraining policy updates based on extremal importance ratios guarantees monotonic policy improvement and convergence to the optimal value function in finite state-action spaces.
> - Local reward and transition losses can effectively bound the discrepancy between a policy's performance in the true environment and a learned world model, addressing the out-of-trajectory problem by ensuring model reliability within defined policy deviation limits.
> - Minimizing these local losses implicitly enforces representation quality, ensuring that the value function maintains 'almost' Lipschitz continuity in the latent space, thereby mitigating confounding policy updates by preserving distinctiveness for states with similar values.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents learn to interact within complex environments through iterative trial and error. For high-dimensional domains, contemporary RL methods frequently employ function approximation, necessitating representation learning to construct latent spaces where states with similar behavioral characteristics are positioned in proximity. This approach facilitates the estimation of policies and value functions. Complementary to this is model learning, involving the training of a predictive environmental model. Such models can be utilized for planning, generating simulated experience, or enhancing value estimates.

In online RL, where an agent continuously updates its policy during interaction, preventing detrimental errors is critical. This setting presents two primary challenges:
1. **Out-of-Trajectory (OOT) World Models:** World models may exhibit unreliable predictions in regions of the state space that are infrequently visited during training, potentially leading to unsafe policy updates if the agent explores these areas.
2. **Confounding Policy Updates:** Simultaneous adjustments to both the policy and its underlying representation can lead to suboptimal outcomes. Poor representations can restrict an agent to inferior behaviors, while the evolving policy might hinder corrective updates to the representation.

Safe Policy Improvement (SPI) aims to mitigate these risks by ensuring that updated policies do not substantially degrade performance compared to their predecessors. However, classical SPI methods primarily offer theoretical guarantees in offline, tabular Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and depend on extensive state-action coverage. These characteristics render them unsuitable for continuous or high-dimensional state spaces prevalent in modern deep RL.

This research addresses the limitations of classical SPI by integrating it with representation learning and world models within general online settings. It positions itself relative to existing literature by:
- **Regularized Policy Improvements:** Building on techniques like TRPO and PPO, which stabilize policy optimization through regularization, this work extends this concept to the joint training of world models and representations. It constrains policy updates within a defined neighborhood while managing model quality via transition and reward losses.
- **SPI Methodologies:** While drawing inspiration from SPI, this work fundamentally differs by focusing on online RL with high-dimensional inputs, where global constraints are impractical. It introduces local, on-policy losses, contrasting with the offline, tabular assumptions of prior SPI methods (e.g., those relying on robust MDP formulations or fixed datasets without representation learning).
- **Representation Learning and Model-Based RL:** This study considers auxiliary transition and reward prediction losses, common in model-based approaches like DeepMDP and Dreamer, as critical for representation learning. It establishes a direct link between the quality of the learned representation and model accuracy to enable safe policy improvement, conceptually connecting to state abstraction and bisimulation in MDPs.

The paper aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical robustness of SPI and the practical demands of deep, online reinforcement learning in complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to extend the principles of Safe Policy Improvement (SPI) to complex, high-dimensional online reinforcement learning environments that leverage both world models and representation learning. This involves addressing limitations inherent in applying traditional SPI methods to such settings and mitigating specific challenges that arise from the joint optimization of policies, models, and representations.

The motivation for this work is rooted in several detailed considerations:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Theory and Practice:** Classical SPI theory provides strong guarantees against policy degradation but is largely confined to offline and tabular MDPs, requiring exhaustive state-action coverage. Modern deep RL, however, operates in continuous or high-dimensional state spaces and often involves online interaction. A key motivation is to develop a theoretical framework and practical algorithms that enable SPI in these more general and realistic deep RL settings.

2. **Addressing Out-of-Trajectory (OOT) World Model Issues:** When an agent updates its policy using a learned world model, the model's predictions can be unreliable in regions of the state space that were rarely, or never, visited by the behavioral policy. This "hallucination" can lead to policies that appear optimal in the model but perform poorly in the true environment, resulting in unsafe updates. The research aims to develop mechanisms that ensure model accuracy and policy safety even when a new policy explores regions not extensively covered by prior experience.

3. **Mitigating Confounding Policy Update Challenges:** In online deep RL, the policy and its underlying state representation are often updated simultaneously. A change in policy can alter the distribution of visited states, potentially invalidating a previously adequate representation. Conversely, a poor representation can constrain policy learning. This co-dependence can lead to a "confounding update" where performance degrades because the representation fails to adequately differentiate states that become important under a new policy. The research seeks to establish conditions that ensure the representation remains suitable for policy updates, preventing such performance degradation.

4. **Enabling Principled Online Policy Improvement:** The motivation extends to designing a principled approach for online policy improvement that moves beyond heuristic regularization techniques. By grounding policy updates in a rigorous theoretical framework that incorporates model quality and representation learning, the aim is to achieve monotonic policy improvement and convergence with explicit guarantees.

5. **Integrating Model Learning and Representation Quality:** The work is motivated by the potential benefits of tightly coupling model learning with representation quality. By showing that accurate models inherently encourage representations where behaviorally similar states are grouped, the research aims to establish a framework where model accuracy directly contributes to the utility of the latent space for policy learning.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Introduce a novel neighborhood operator for constraining policy updates, ensuring monotonic improvement and convergence.
- Combine this operator with principled model losses to bound the discrepancy between policy performance in the world model and the true environment, thereby enabling safe policy improvement in complex MDPs.
- Demonstrate that this framework implicitly enforces representation quality by ensuring that states with similar values remain close in the learned latent space.
- Develop a practical, on-policy algorithm, DeepSPI, that integrates these theoretical insights with existing policy optimization methods like PPO, demonstrating strong empirical performance while maintaining theoretical guarantees.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved the development of a theoretical framework for safe policy improvement in online, high-dimensional settings, followed by the design and evaluation of a practical algorithm.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework

The theoretical approach centers on three interconnected components: neighborhood-restricted policy updates, model-quality bounds through local losses, and representation quality guarantees.

- **Neighborhood Operator for Policy Updates:**
 - A novel neighborhood operator, $N_C(\pi)$, is introduced to define a "trust region" around the current behavioral policy $\pi_b$. This operator constrains policy updates $\pi'$ such that their extremal importance ratios (IR), $D_{inf\_IR}(\pi_b, \pi')$ and $D_{sup\_IR}(\pi_b, \pi')$, remain within the range $[2-C, C]$ for a constant $1 < C < 2$. The support of the new policy must also match that of the base policy.
 - **Theorem 1** demonstrates that restricting policy updates to this neighborhood, when coupled with an objective to maximize the expected advantage, results in a policy improvement scheme that guarantees monotonic improvement of the value function and convergence to the optimal value function, assuming finite state-action spaces and full-support policies. This is shown by connecting the update rule to the framework of mirror learning.

- **Model-Quality Bounds via Local Losses:**
 - To address high-dimensional state spaces and avoid the intractability of global error bounds, the framework utilizes local error measures for the world model. These are formalized as a reward loss ($L_B_R$) and a transition loss ($L_B_P$).
 - The reward loss quantifies the expected absolute difference between the true reward and the model's predicted reward, averaged over experiences from a buffer $B$.
 - The transition loss employs the Wasserstein distance to measure the discrepancy between the pushforward measure of the true next-state distribution (via the encoder $\phi$) and the model's predicted next-state distribution in the latent space. This generalizes various transition objectives found in literature and simplifies to total variation distance for discrete latent spaces.
 - The world model is assumed to have Lipschitz continuous latent reward ($K_{\pi_R}$) and transition ($K_{\pi_P}$) functions. These Lipschitz constants ensure that neighboring latent states exhibit similar dynamics, providing a structured latent space. Techniques like gradient penalties or bisimulation-distance enforcement are noted as ways to control these bounds.
 - **Theorem 2** establishes that if a new policy's supremum importance ratio relative to the behavioral policy remains below $1/\gamma$ (where $\gamma$ is the discount factor), the gap between the policy's return in the true environment and its return in the world model can be bounded by the local reward and transition losses calculated with respect to the behavioral policy's stationary distribution. This theorem quantifies the world model's accuracy under the learned representation.
 - **Theorem 3 (Deep Safe Policy Improvement)** builds on Theorem 2 by showing that the improvement of a policy in the true environment can be guaranteed relative to its improvement in the world model, minus a cumulative modeling error ($\zeta$). This error term is dependent on the average episode length, the local losses, and the policy's deviation from the behavioral policy, directly addressing the OOT issue. A PAC variant of this theorem is also provided for discrete latent spaces.

- **Representation Quality Guarantees:**
 - **Theorem 4 (Deep SPI for representation learning)** addresses the confounding policy update problem. It demonstrates that by minimizing the local reward and transition losses, the probability increases that the learned representation will maintain "almost" Lipschitz continuity for the value function. This implies that states with similar values will remain close in the latent space, preventing distinct states from collapsing into identical representations that could lead to suboptimal policy decisions.

#### 3.2. Algorithmic Approach: DeepSPI

Based on the theoretical framework, the authors propose DeepSPI, a principled on-policy algorithm:
- **PPO Integration:** DeepSPI leverages the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) objective, which uses a clipping mechanism to regularize policy updates, conceptually aligning with the neighborhood operator. The clipping coefficient $\epsilon$ in PPO is directly related to the neighborhood constant $C$.
- **Utility Function Modification:** Instead of using the standard advantage function $A_{\pi_n}(s, a)$, DeepSPI incorporates the local reward and transition losses directly into a modified utility function $U_{\pi_n}(s, a, s')$. This utility function includes weighted auxiliary loss terms ($\alpha_R \cdot \ell_R$ and $\alpha_P \cdot \ell_P$) to penalize model inaccuracies. This modification ensures that policy updates not only maximize advantage but also minimize model errors, thereby simultaneously updating the policy and the representation while staying within the theoretical neighborhood.
- **On-Policy Training:** DeepSPI operates in an on-policy setting, collecting data from vectorized environments to ensure broad state-space coverage. It simultaneously updates the world model, encoder, and policy.
- **DreamSPI Variant:** A variant, DreamSPI, is introduced to explore pure model-based planning. In DreamSPI, the world model and representation are learned by DeepSPI, and a separate PPO agent updates its policy by planning within imagined trajectories generated by this learned world model.

#### 3.3. Experimental Design

- **Environment:** The Arcade Learning Environment (ALE) with 57 Atari games, using four stacked frames as observations. Stochasticity was added via sticky actions and random initializations.
- **Baselines:** PPO (cleanRL implementation) and DeepMDPs (Gelada et al., 2019) integrated into the PPO framework for fair comparison.
- **Architectural Details:** Latent space derived from convolutional layers. DeepSPI utilized a mixture of multivariate normal distributions for transitions, while DreamSPI used categorical distributions. Lipschitz networks (norm-constrained GroupSort architectures) were employed in DeepSPI to enforce Lipschitz continuity of reward and transition functions.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Human normalized scores (mean, median, interquartile mean, optimality gap) were used to assess performance, alongside an analysis of transition and reward losses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several theoretical findings and empirical results demonstrating the effectiveness of DeepSPI.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Monotonic Improvement and Convergence (Theorem 1):** The proposed neighborhood operator, which constrains policy updates based on importance ratios, was proven to ensure monotonic improvement of the policy's value function and convergence to the optimal value function in finite state-action spaces. This result establishes a theoretical foundation for principled policy learning within the defined trust region.
- **World Model Quality Guarantees (Theorem 2):** The framework demonstrated that local reward and transition losses effectively quantify the accuracy of the world model relative to the true environment. When policy updates are restricted to a well-defined neighborhood (specifically, with a supremum importance ratio below $1/\gamma$), the difference in policy return between the true environment and the world model can be bounded by these local losses. This provides a measurable link between model accuracy and real-world performance.
- **Deep Safe Policy Improvement (Theorem 3):** This theorem provides a core SPI guarantee for deep reinforcement learning. It shows that policy improvements made within the learned world model translate to improvements in the true environment, albeit with a bounded cumulative modeling error ($\zeta$). This directly addresses the "out-of-trajectory" (OOT) problem by ensuring that even when a new policy explores less-visited regions, the model's predictions remain sufficiently reliable within the specified policy deviation limits. A PAC variant for discrete latent spaces was also presented, incorporating statistical confidence bounds.
- **Representation Learning Guarantees (Theorem 4):** The research established that minimizing the local losses promotes a useful learned representation. Specifically, it demonstrated that with a high probability, the value function in the latent space maintains "almost" Lipschitz continuity. This means that states with similar values remain close in the learned latent space, preventing the "confounding policy update" problem where distinct states are erroneously merged, leading to suboptimal or unsafe behaviors.

#### 4.2. Empirical Results

- **Performance on ALE-57 Benchmark:** On the stochastic Atari Arcade Learning Environment (ALE-57), DeepSPI exhibited strong empirical performance. It matched or exceeded the performance of baseline algorithms, including PPO and DeepMDPs, across aggregate metrics such as mean, median, and interquartile mean (IQM) normalized scores (Figures 5 and 6). This indicated that the theoretical guarantees did not necessarily lead to a compromise in performance, which is often a trade-off in safety-focused RL methods.
- **Sample Efficiency:** DeepSPI demonstrated competitive sample efficiency when compared to PPO and DeepMDPs on the ALE-57 suite (Figure 6).
- **World Model Accuracy Assessment:** DeepSPI consistently achieved lower transition losses ($L_P$) during training compared to DeepMDPs (Figure 7). Statistical analysis, including a paired bootstrap with a 95% confidence interval and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test, confirmed that this difference was statistically significant, indicating that DeepSPI learns more accurate transition functions. Reward losses were observed to be comparable between the two methods. The stability of losses was attributed to DeepSPI's on-policy approach, which uses fresh interaction data.
- **Illustrative Example: Confounding Policy Update:** A toy maze environment designed to highlight the confounding policy update problem demonstrated DeepSPI's representation learning capabilities. PPO failed to distinguish between two critical states (star cells), leading to a collapsed latent representation and a suboptimal policy. In contrast, DeepSPI, guided by the representation quality guarantees of Theorem 4, learned distinct representations for these states, enabling it to learn the optimal policy and achieve a higher return (Figure 4).
- **DreamSPI as Model-Based Planning Proof-of-Concept:** A naive variant, DreamSPI, which uses DeepSPI to learn world models and representations for subsequent policy updates via imagined trajectories, showed learning progress and coherent behaviors in some environments (Figure 8). While its aggregate median score remained below the baselines, this result suggests the feasibility of pure deep SPI model-based planning, acknowledging the stricter data requirements of such on-policy model updates.

In summary, the main findings include both theoretical guarantees for safe policy improvement and representation quality in online deep RL, and empirical evidence that the proposed DeepSPI algorithm achieves competitive performance on challenging benchmarks while upholding these guarantees.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by extending the theoretical framework of Safe Policy Improvement (SPI) to complex, high-dimensional online settings that incorporate world models and representation learning. This represents a significant step towards developing more robust and reliable deep RL systems.

The potential impacts and significance of this work include:

1. **Advancement of SPI Theory:** The paper provides a theoretical foundation for SPI in general online settings, moving beyond the traditional limitations of offline, tabular MDPs. This expands the applicability of SPI guarantees to modern deep RL architectures and complex environments.
2. **Mitigation of Critical Deep RL Challenges:** By directly addressing the "out-of-trajectory" (OOT) world model problem and "confounding policy updates," the research offers principled solutions to common failure modes in model-based and representation learning in online RL. This could lead to more stable and predictable learning processes.
3. **Development of Principled Algorithms:** DeepSPI is a practical algorithm that integrates theoretical guarantees with existing robust policy optimization techniques (PPO). Its competitive performance on the ALE benchmark, coupled with explicit theoretical controls, suggests that safety and performance are not necessarily mutually exclusive in deep RL.
4. **Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness:** The explicit guarantees on monotonic policy improvement, convergence, and representation quality can contribute to building more reliable and trustworthy RL agents, particularly important for applications in safety-critical domains.
5. **Improved Representation Learning:** The work highlights the importance of representation quality for safe policy improvement by linking model accuracy and local losses to the Lipschitz continuity of value functions in the latent space. This provides a mechanism for learning representations that are inherently more suitable for value-based and policy-based methods.
6. **Foundation for Advanced Safety and Formal Methods:** A principled world model, backed by theoretical guarantees, can serve as a foundation for integrating formal methods into safe reinforcement learning. This could enable future work in areas such as reactive synthesis (generating policies that meet formal specifications) or shielding (preventing agents from executing unsafe actions).
7. **Future of Model-Based RL:** The preliminary success of DreamSPI, despite its strict data requirements, suggests the potential for making pure model-based planning in deep SPI more practical. Further research in this direction could lead to highly sample-efficient and theoretically sound model-based RL agents.
8. **Computational Efficiency Considerations:** The on-policy and parallelizable nature of DeepSPI's data collection strategy can maintain reasonable wall-clock times, making it a feasible option for large-scale applications where off-policy methods might incur significant computational overhead.

In conclusion, this research not only advances the theoretical understanding of safe policy improvement in deep online RL but also provides a practical algorithm that demonstrates its utility, laying groundwork for more robust, reliable, and potentially verifiable AI systems.
