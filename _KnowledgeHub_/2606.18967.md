# EfficientRollout: System-Aware Self-Speculative Decoding for RL Rollouts

> [!summary] Summary
> EfficientRollout introduces a system-aware self-speculative decoding framework to accelerate on-policy reinforcement learning rollouts for large language models. It integrates a weight-quantized drafter derived from the target model with a regime-aware toggle policy and an adaptive draft-length policy, reducing rollout-generation latency by up to 19.6% and end-to-end training-step latency by up to 12.7% without compromising training quality.

## Problem

- Rollout generation, driven by autoregressive sampling, is a primary latency bottleneck in LLM reinforcement learning post-training, accounting for nearly 70% of total latency due to long responses and shrinking batch sizes.
- Existing speculative decoding (SD) methods struggle in RL due to the continuously evolving target policy, which quickly renders static drafters ineffective, and potential slowdowns if applied indiscriminately in compute-bound phases.
- Prior SD approaches for RL, including history-based and learned auxiliary drafters, suffer from low block efficiency, require warm-up periods, or introduce complex pretraining and online adaptation overheads.

## Method

- A ==weight-quantized drafter==, derived directly from the current target policy using ==4-bit quantization==, automatically synchronizes with the evolving RL policy, eliminating external drafter training and reducing dense projection costs, as part of the overall **EfficientRollout** framework.
- A ==regime-aware SD toggle policy==, calibrated via a ==roofline model==, activates ==speculative decoding== only in memory-bound phases (small batch sizes) where it provides a speedup, preventing slowdowns in early, compute-bound stages.
- An ==adaptive draft-length policy== dynamically adjusts the number of speculated tokens based on measured ==block efficiency==, leveraging the improving drafter quality as the RL policy sharpens to maximize acceleration over training steps.

## Results

- EfficientRollout reduced rollout-generation latency by up to **19.6%** and overall end-to-end training-step latency by up to **12.7%** across Qwen2.5-{**7B**,**14B**} and Llama3.1-**8B** models.
- The framework achieved consistently high per-token acceptance rates (**95.8–98.2%**) and block efficiency (**5.4–8.6**), outperforming history-based and learned auxiliary drafting baselines in speedup and robustness.
- Training dynamics, including reward trajectories and validation accuracy, were preserved, confirming the lossless nature of the acceleration and demonstrating no degradation of final model quality compared to autoregressive decoding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - In memory-bound rollout-tail phases, dense projection computations dominate single-token decoding latency, making weight quantization an effective strategy to accelerate the drafter model.
> - The effectiveness of speculative decoding is highly dependent on the computational regime (compute-bound vs. memory-bound), necessitating a system-aware toggle policy to ensure consistent speedups.
> - The inherent evolution of the target policy during RL training can lead to improved agreement with its quantized self-drafter, allowing for dynamic adjustment of draft length to exploit increasing block efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the post-training paradigm for large language models (LLMs) using reinforcement learning (RL). RL, particularly on-policy RL, has become a method for enhancing LLM capabilities such as reasoning and agency. However, a significant bottleneck in the RL pipeline is rollout generation. This process involves autoregressive (AR) sampling, which decodes responses token by token, leading to high latency, especially for the increasingly long responses generated by post-trained models. The problem is exacerbated by "long-tail" generations, where a few extended responses determine the overall completion time, leaving computational resources underutilized as batch sizes shrink.

Speculative decoding (SD) is a known technique for accelerating LLM inference. It operates by using a smaller, faster "drafter" model to propose multiple tokens, which are then verified in parallel by the larger "target" model, preserving the target model's output distribution. While effective for fixed LLM serving, applying SD directly to RL rollouts introduces specific complexities. The target policy in RL continuously evolves, making any static drafter quickly become mismatched. Additionally, rollout decoding transitions from compute-bound regimes (large initial batch sizes) to memory-bound regimes (small, shrinking batch sizes during the "tail" phase), meaning parallel verification benefits vary.

Prior attempts to adapt SD for RL rollouts fall into two main categories:
1. **History-based drafting:** These methods reuse past rollouts as draft proposals. While easy to implement and without requiring a parameterized drafter, they often yield limited "block efficiency" (number of accepted tokens per SD iteration) due to sparse coverage of future trajectories. They also may require warm-up periods to collect rollout history and sometimes employ lossy heuristics.
2. **Learned auxiliary drafting:** These approaches train a separate, lightweight drafter model. They aim for higher block efficiency but typically necessitate dedicated drafter pretraining and continuous online adaptation to keep pace with the evolving RL policy, adding system complexity and overhead.

EfficientRollout positions itself as a system-aware self-speculative decoding framework designed to overcome these limitations. It seeks to integrate SD into RL rollouts by maintaining drafter effectiveness with an evolving policy and adapting SD use to varying hardware utilization regimes, all without the need for extensive drafter pretraining or complex online adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly reduce the latency of rollout generation in on-policy reinforcement learning for large language models, thereby accelerating the overall RL post-training process.

This objective is motivated by several detailed observations and challenges:

- **Dominant Latency Bottleneck:** Rollout generation constitutes a substantial portion, often nearly 70%, of the total latency in RL post-training. This is primarily because RL tends to induce longer outputs, which, when decoded autoregressively, are processed token by token, leading to sequential, memory-bandwidth-bound operations. Other stages of the RL loop, such as policy updates and log-probability computation, are more parallelizable.
- **Long-Tail Completion Dynamics:** Within a rollout generation phase, a small number of long-tailed responses often dictate the total completion time. As shorter responses finish, the active batch size decreases, leading to underutilized compute resources. This shrinking-batch dynamic creates specific regimes where SD could be beneficial, but also highlights periods (large initial batches) where it might be counterproductive.
- **Evolving Target Policy:** Unlike fixed LLM serving, the target policy in RL continuously changes throughout training. This renders static drafters rapidly "stale," leading to a reduction in block efficiency—the average number of target-distributed tokens produced per draft-verify iteration. Existing learned auxiliary drafters require continuous online adaptation to track this evolution, introducing system complexity and computational overhead.
- **System-Aware Deployment of SD:** The effectiveness of SD is contingent on both algorithmic efficiency (high block efficiency) and system-level factors (avoiding compute-bound regimes where parallel verification cannot exploit underutilized compute). Naive application of SD in early, large-batch, compute-bound phases of RL rollouts can result in slowdowns rather than speedups.
- **Limitations of Prior SD Methods for RL:**
 - **History-based methods:** While simple, they generally suffer from low block efficiency because past trajectories are typically poor predictors of future ones, especially in evolving RL environments. They also often require a "warm-up" period to collect history and may use lossy heuristics to achieve meaningful reuse.
 - **Learned auxiliary methods:** These can achieve higher block efficiency but typically demand separate drafter pretraining, which may not align with the specific RL rollout distribution, and complex online adaptation to track policy evolution. This adds significant training overhead and system management complexity.

In summary, the motivation is to develop a robust, efficient, and practical SD framework for RL rollouts that effectively handles the evolving nature of the target policy and the dynamic computational regimes of rollout generation, without introducing substantial overhead or compromising training quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EfficientRollout is a system-aware self-speculative decoding framework for on-policy RL rollouts, integrating three coordinated components: a target-induced quantized drafter, a regime-aware SD toggle policy, and an adaptive draft-length policy.

#### 3.1. Weight-Quantized Drafter for Self-Speculative Decoding

- **Self-Speculative Decoding Rationale:** The core idea is to derive the drafter directly from the current target model. This "self-SD" approach ensures that the drafter automatically remains synchronized with the evolving RL policy, eliminating the need for separate drafter pretraining, warm-up periods, or complex online adaptation processes seen in auxiliary drafter methods.
- **Rollout-Tail Latency Decomposition:** Analysis of single-token decoding latency in rollout-tail phases (small batch sizes, memory-bound) revealed that dense projections (QKVO, FFN, LM-head computations) account for approximately 90% of the total latency. Attention operations contribute a significantly smaller fraction.
- **Weight-Quantized Drafting:** To address the dominant dense projection cost, EfficientRollout induces a weight-quantized drafter. Specifically, it applies lightweight Round-To-Nearest (RTN) 4-bit quantization to the FFN and QKVO projection layers of the current target policy at the beginning of each training step. This reduces the weight-loading cost, lowering the drafter-to-target latency ratio. 4-bit weights (W4) were chosen as a practical trade-off between reduced latency and preserved acceptance quality.
- **Comparison to Alternatives:** Sparse-attention drafting, another self-SD approach, primarily targets attention time, which is a less dominant factor in the identified memory-bound rollout-tail regimes. Layer skipping, while an option, often provides limited practical speedup and is challenging to integrate with static inference optimizations in modern serving engines.
- **KV-Cache Sharing:** The quantized drafter shares the target model's KV-cache storage, avoiding additional memory overhead, which is important for long responses in RL rollouts. During SD iterations, the drafter writes provisional KV states, which are overwritten or discarded by the target model during parallel verification and rejection sampling. This ensures that every new SD iteration starts from KV states generated by the full-precision target model, maintaining target distribution preservation.

#### 3.2. Regime-Aware SD Toggle Policy via Roofline Modeling

- **Motivation:** SD is not universally beneficial. In early rollout phases with large active batches, compute resources can be saturated, leaving little underutilized compute for parallel verification. In such compute-bound regimes, SD can be slower than AR decoding.
- **Toggle Mechanism:** A system-aware SD toggle policy activates SD only when beneficial, using a calibrated roofline model to predict the SD-over-AR speedup for the current decoding state (batch size B, sequence length S). The roofline model estimates memory-side (weight and KV-cache traffic) and compute-side (FLOPs for dense projections and attention) costs for target, drafter, and verification operations.
- **Predicted Speedup Formula:** The policy uses these cost estimates to calculate a predicted speedup, activating SD if it exceeds a safety margin (e.g., 1 + ϵ, where ϵ ≥ 0.05).
- **Calibration:** The parameters of the roofline model (e.g., effective memory bandwidth, KV-cache traffic scaling, residual batch-dependent overheads) are calibrated once per model-hardware pair through a pre-hoc profiling sweep, making them reusable across RL runs.
- **Monotone Toggle Policy:** Once activated, SD remains enabled for the remainder of the rollout because the active batch size monotonically decreases, progressively moving into SD-beneficial memory-bound tail regimes.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Draft-Length Policy

- **Evolving Acceptance Behavior:** Empirical observations indicate that the weight-quantized drafter's effectiveness (block efficiency τ) tends to improve over training steps. This is attributed to RL sharpening the target policy distribution, making top-token decisions less sensitive to quantization-induced perturbations and increasing agreement between the target and its quantized copy.
- **Adaptive Draft Length (γ):** To exploit this improving drafter quality, EfficientRollout dynamically adjusts the draft length γ. It maintains an ordered set of candidate draft lengths Γ. The next draft length γ_{t+1} is chosen based on the measured block efficiency τ_t, serving as a proxy for current drafter quality.
- **Update Mechanism:** The draft length is increased if τ_t consistently (over a "patience window" P steps) approaches the current draft-length ceiling, and decreased if acceptance is too low to amortize draft computation. This adaptive approach removes the need to manually select a fixed γ through post-hoc sweeps.

#### 3.4. EfficientRollout Pipeline (Algorithm 1 Summary)

The complete EfficientRollout pipeline operates within the RL training loop. At each training timestep:
1. The drafter is refreshed by quantizing the current target model.
2. During rollout generation, an `sd_on` flag controls whether AR or SD is used.
3. The `π_SD` toggle policy is continuously evaluated. If SD is off and `π_SD` predicts benefit, SD is turned on. Once on, it remains on.
4. Decoding proceeds either autoregressively or with SD using the current draft length γ_t.
5. After rollout generation, step-level block efficiency τ_t is measured.
6. The adaptive draft-length policy updates γ_t to γ_{t+1} based on τ_t and a patience window.
7. Standard post-rollout optimization (log-probability computation and policy update) then occurs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EfficientRollout was conducted across Qwen2.5-{7B,14B} and Llama3.1-8B models on SimpleRL math datasets, comparing it against an accelerated AR baseline, history-based drafting, learned auxiliary drafting, and always-on quantized self-SD.

#### 4.1. End-to-End RL Training Acceleration

- **Overall Latency Reduction:** EfficientRollout achieved reductions in rollout-generation latency by up to 19.6% and in end-to-end training-step latency by up to 12.7%. These gains were observed across all evaluated models.
- **Modest Overhead:** The per-step quantization overhead for drafter refresh was modest (1.3–2.6 s), representing a small fraction of the latency savings achieved in rollout generation.

#### 4.2. Comparison to Baselines

- **Accelerated AR Baseline (veRL (AR)):** EfficientRollout consistently reduced rollout and step times compared to this baseline.
- **History-Based Drafting:** This baseline, despite low preparation overhead (1.1 s), did not reduce rollout generation latency. It often increased it (e.g., +4.9% for Qwen2.5-7B, +3.7% for Llama3.1-8B), as prefix reuse rates were low (4.4–50.2%) and verification overhead was high (10.7–19.5 s per step), failing to amortize the costs.
- **Learned Auxiliary Drafting:** This method yielded model-dependent and limited gains. While reducing Qwen2.5-14B step latency by 3.3%, it increased Llama3.1-8B step latency by 25.6%. This variability was attributed to insufficient block efficiency (54.3–60.3% per-token acceptance rate with average block efficiency τ ~2.0–2.2) from generally pretrained drafters when applied to long, high-temperature RL rollouts, failing to offset SD overheads.
- **Quantized Self-SD (Always-On):** This configuration, using the same quantized drafter as EfficientRollout but without the SD toggle and adaptive draft length, showed gains on some models (e.g., -8.2% rollout gen for Qwen2.5-7B, -5.9% for Llama3.1-8B) but experienced slowdowns on others (e.g., +6.7% for Qwen2.5-14B). This highlights the importance of the regime-aware toggle policy for robust speedups.

#### 4.3. Acceptance Rate and Block Efficiency

- EfficientRollout consistently achieved high per-token acceptance rates (α between 95.8% and 98.2%) and large block efficiency (τ between 5.4 and 8.6, with corresponding average draft lengths ¯γ between 5.0 and 8.2). This indicates strong alignment between the target model and its quantized self-drafter, which the adaptive draft length policy exploited by increasing the draft budget.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics and Quality Preservation

- EfficientRollout demonstrated similar reward trajectories and validation accuracy compared to the veRL (AR) baseline across all evaluated models. This indicates that the acceleration is lossless, preserving the target policy distribution and ensuring no degradation of final model quality or training stability.

#### 4.5. Impact of Regime-Aware SD Toggle Policy

- Validation experiments confirmed that the calibrated roofline model accurately distinguished between SD-beneficial and SD-harmful (B, S) regimes. By activating SD only in favorable regimes (typically after 6–11% of decoding steps), the policy prevented slowdowns during early, compute-bound, large-batch phases, leading to consistently larger rollout-generation latency reductions compared to an always-on SD approach.

#### 4.6. Impact of Adaptive Draft-Length Policy

- The adaptive γ policy adjusted the draft length over training steps, leading to an overall 19.6% reduction in rollout-generation latency on Qwen2.5-7B, compared to 13.5% (fixed γ=5) and 11.8% (fixed γ=11). This demonstrated the policy's ability to leverage smaller draft lengths early in training and larger, more efficient drafts later as drafter quality improved (policy sharpened and block efficiency increased).

#### 4.7. Challenges of Auxiliary Drafters

- Further analysis using the NeMo RL SD stack and DAPO-Math-17K dataset showed that various public EAGLE3 drafters achieved substantially lower block efficiency (τ = 1.2–2.4) compared to target-induced quantized drafters (τ = 3.6–3.9 under γ=3). This suggested that generally pretrained auxiliary drafters often lack alignment with long, high-temperature RL rollout distributions and require specialized training pipelines for effective performance, which adds significant practical barriers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EfficientRollout presents a system-aware approach to speculative decoding for RL rollouts that addresses critical latency bottlenecks in LLM post-training. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

- **Accelerated RL Post-Training:** By substantially reducing rollout generation latency (up to 19.6%) and overall training step time (up to 12.7%), EfficientRollout can enable faster iteration cycles in RL research and development. This allows researchers and practitioners to conduct more experiments, explore larger models, or deploy RL-tuned LLMs more rapidly.
- **Practical and Low-Overhead Solution:** The framework offers a practical solution by deriving the drafter directly from the target model through quantization. This eliminates the need for external drafter pretraining, warm-up periods, or complex continuous online adaptation, which are common challenges with learned auxiliary drafters. The per-step overhead of drafter quantization is minimal compared to the achieved speedups.
- **Robustness and Quality Preservation:** EfficientRollout's lossless nature ensures that the target model's output distribution is preserved, maintaining training dynamics and preventing degradation of the final model's quality, as evidenced by consistent reward and validation accuracy trajectories. The regime-aware toggle policy also ensures robust speedups by preventing SD from being activated in detrimental computational regimes.
- **System-Aware Design Philosophy:** The work highlights the importance of incorporating system-level characteristics, such as compute-bound vs. memory-bound regimes and shrinking batch sizes, into algorithmic design for effective acceleration. This provides a blueprint for developing more efficient LLM training and inference systems.
- **Generalizability:** The approach, based on inducing a drafter from the target model and adapting to observed system behavior, is conceptually generalizable across different LLM architectures and RL tasks, provided the quantization strategy remains effective.

Future directions for this work include exploring tree verification for potentially higher block efficiency, investigating less-lossy quantization methods for earlier training stages or models with inherently lower RTN acceptance, and considering sparse-attention drafting in scenarios where KV-cache loading becomes dominant (e.g., very long contexts or dense attention mechanisms). Overall, EfficientRollout contributes a method for accelerating a critical component of LLM post-training while maintaining practicality and quality.
