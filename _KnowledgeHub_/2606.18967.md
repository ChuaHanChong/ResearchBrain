# EfficientRollout: System-Aware Self-Speculative Decoding for RL Rollouts

> [!summary] Summary
> EfficientRollout introduces a system-aware self-speculative decoding framework to accelerate on-policy reinforcement learning rollouts for large language models. It integrates a weight-quantized drafter derived from the target model with a regime-aware toggle policy and an adaptive draft-length policy, reducing rollout-generation latency by up to 19.6% and end-to-end training-step latency by up to 12.7% without compromising training quality.

## Problem

- Rollout generation, driven by autoregressive sampling, is a primary latency bottleneck in LLM reinforcement learning post-training, accounting for nearly 70% of total latency due to long responses and shrinking batch sizes.
- Existing speculative decoding (SD) methods struggle in RL due to the continuously evolving target policy, which quickly renders static drafters ineffective, and potential slowdowns if applied indiscriminately in compute-bound phases.
- Prior SD approaches for RL, including history-based and learned auxiliary drafters, suffer from low block efficiency, require warm-up periods, or introduce complex pretraining and online adaptation overheads.

## Method

- A weight-quantized drafter, derived directly from the current target policy using 4-bit quantization, automatically synchronizes with the evolving RL policy, eliminating external drafter training and reducing dense projection costs.
- A regime-aware SD toggle policy, calibrated via a roofline model, activates speculative decoding only in memory-bound phases (small batch sizes) where it provides a speedup, preventing slowdowns in early, compute-bound stages.
- An adaptive draft-length policy dynamically adjusts the number of speculated tokens based on measured block efficiency, leveraging the improving drafter quality as the RL policy sharpens to maximize acceleration over training steps.

## Results

- EfficientRollout reduced rollout-generation latency by up to 19.6% and overall end-to-end training-step latency by up to 12.7% across Qwen2.5-{7B,14B} and Llama3.1-8B models.
- The framework achieved consistently high per-token acceptance rates (95.8–98.2%) and block efficiency (5.4–8.6), outperforming history-based and learned auxiliary drafting baselines in speedup and robustness.
- Training dynamics, including reward trajectories and validation accuracy, were preserved, confirming the lossless nature of the acceleration and demonstrating no degradation of final model quality compared to autoregressive decoding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - In memory-bound rollout-tail phases, dense projection computations dominate single-token decoding latency, making weight quantization an effective strategy to accelerate the drafter model.
> - The effectiveness of speculative decoding is highly dependent on the computational regime (compute-bound vs. memory-bound), necessitating a system-aware toggle policy to ensure consistent speedups.
> - The inherent evolution of the target policy during RL training can lead to improved agreement with its quantized self-drafter, allowing for dynamic adjustment of draft length to exploit increasing block efficiency.
