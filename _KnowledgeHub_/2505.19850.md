# DISCOVER: Automated Curricula for Sparse-Reward Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new curriculum learning method, DISCOVER, addresses sparse-reward reinforcement learning by strategically selecting intermediate goals that balance achievability, novelty, and relevance to the final task. This approach from ETH Zürich and Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems consistently outperforms existing goal-conditioned RL techniques and scales to higher-dimensional environments.

## Problem

- Efficient exploration in sparse-reward, long-horizon tasks is challenging due to infrequent feedback and intractable random exploration in high-dimensional spaces.
- Existing goal selection strategies often prioritize either exploration or exploitation, lacking a principled way to integrate target relevance alongside achievability and novelty.
- Many theoretically-backed exploration methods for multi-goal RL suffer from dependencies on the total volume of the goal space, limiting scalability.

## Method

- **DISCOVER** proposes a ==goal selection== strategy that quantifies goal utility as a sum of Achievability, Novelty, and Relevance to the target goal.
- An ensemble of critic networks estimates value and uncertainty for these three principles, guiding the selection of intermediate goals from previously achieved states.
- The method incorporates an online adaptation strategy for a key parameter, adjusting the balance between achievability/novelty and relevance to maintain a target goal achievement rate.

## Results

- DISCOVER consistently outperforms state-of-the-art goal selection strategies on complex `antmaze` and `arm` tasks, particularly in challenging configurations.
- The method scales effectively to 6-dimensional `pointmazes`, achieving success rates where undirected baselines fail entirely beyond 3 dimensions.
- Visualizations confirm that DISCOVER's agents direct their goal selection efforts towards the target region after an initial exploration phase, contrasting with undirected approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating target relevance into goal selection provides a crucial "sense of direction" for exploration, enabling agents to focus on skills applicable to the ultimate objective.
> - The approach is theoretically grounded, connecting to UCB sampling and offering a bound on episodes needed to reach the target that is independent of the overall goal space volume.
> - Online parameter adaptation allows the method to automatically tune its exploration strategy based on learning progress, optimizing for efficient skill acquisition.
