# Industry 6.0: New Generation of Industry driven by Generative AI and Swarm of Heterogeneous Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Skoltech's Intelligent Robotics Laboratory introduced "Industry 6.0," a fully autonomous production system that translates natural language descriptions into physical products using generative AI and a heterogeneous robot swarm. This prototype system reduced overall production time by 4.44 times compared to human experts, achieving a 47-fold speedup in the blueprinting stage.

## Problem

- Current industrial paradigms, even Industry 5.0, still require significant human involvement in product design and manufacturing.
- The need for an entirely automated system capable of translating high-level natural language requests into tangible products without human intervention.
- Achieving enhanced efficiency, adaptability, and higher levels of automation in manufacturing beyond existing human-robot collaborative models.

## Method

- A fully autonomous **"Industry 6.0"** production pipeline was developed, integrating ==generative AI== (e.g., OpenAI models via ==LangChain==) to orchestrate design, manufacturing, logistics, and assembly.
- The system generates 3D-printable components and assembly files from natural language inputs, automates 3D printing, and uses drones as "flying conveyors" for inter-stage logistics.
- Product assembly is managed by an ==LLM-based supervisor== assigning tasks to a swarm of ==heterogeneous robots== (e.g., UR manipulators) using predefined API functions and object data.

## Results

- Top-performing LLMs (OpenAI's o1-preview and GPT-4o) achieved perfect scores in mechanism analysis and executable code generation, and **8/10** for functional STL production.
- The autonomous system completed manufacturing in **119.10 minutes** on average, reducing total production time by approximately **4.44 times** compared to human experts (**528.64 minutes**).
- The design (blueprinting) stage was completed in **0.50 minutes** by the system, demonstrating a **47-fold** speed-up compared to human CAD operators (**23.50 minutes**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leading generative AI models can effectively interpret natural language product descriptions to generate accurate 3D printable designs and orchestrate complex multi-robot manufacturing workflows.
> - A fully autonomous "human-out-of-the-loop" manufacturing system can achieve substantial time reductions, particularly in product design and the initial manufacturing stages.
> - The integration of heterogeneous robot swarms with generative AI and drone-based logistics enables a flexible and adaptable production environment with dynamic resource allocation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the evolving fields of cognitive robotics, generative artificial intelligence (AI), and autonomous manufacturing. It builds upon advancements in integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) with robotic systems, which have enabled progress in reasoning and real-world interaction for various robot types.

The paper references existing works such as Google DeepMind’s PaLM-E, RT-2, and RT-X for advanced robotic manipulator interaction; CognitiveDog for quadruped robot mobility and decision-making; and integrations of generative AI in humanoid robots like Tesla Optimus, Agility Robotics, and OpenAI. Mobile robot advancements, and bimanual manipulation systems like Bi-VLA are also noted for their ability to translate natural language commands into actions.

Beyond robot control, the work incorporates the capability of LLMs to generate 3D objects from natural language descriptions, citing examples like Point-E, G3PT systems, and Diffusion-SDF. The application of LLMs to manage complex multi-agent environments, as demonstrated by FlockGPT, is also a foundational element. Further, the integration of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for enhanced robotic autonomy and adaptability, such as CognitiveOS, is acknowledged.

The authors position their work as an evolution from previous industrial paradigms. Industry 5.0 is characterized by intelligent human-robot collaborations, while this paper proposes "Industry 6.0," a stage fully driven by generative AI and heterogeneous robot swarms, aiming to remove human involvement from the design and manufacturing process entirely. This concept addresses the need for advanced coordination in heterogeneous multi-robot systems, and leverages real-time adjustments and decision-making capabilities offered by generative AI in manufacturing. The research aligns with studies exploring LLMs for autonomous industrial robotics to enhance decision-making and task execution, and methods for generating robot behavior trees using LLMs, such as ‘LLM-BRAIn’.

Overall, the paper situates itself at the intersection of AI-driven design, multi-robot coordination, and autonomous production, pushing towards a higher level of automation beyond current human-robot collaborative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and demonstrate the concept of "Industry 6.0" through the development of a fully automated production system. This system aims to autonomously manage the entire product design and manufacturing process, starting from a user-provided natural language description and culminating in a physical product, without direct human intervention in these stages.

The motivation for this work stems from several factors:

- **Advancements in Generative AI and Cognitive Robotics:** The rapid progress in generative AI has enabled new capabilities in cognitive robotics, allowing robots to perform complex reasoning and interact with the real world more effectively. This creates an opportunity to extend AI's role beyond individual robot tasks to orchestrate entire production workflows.
- **Limitations of Current Industrial Paradigms:** While Industry 5.0 focuses on intelligent human-robot collaboration, the authors identify a potential next stage where human involvement in design and manufacturing is minimized or eliminated. This suggests a desire to overcome the inherent limitations or inefficiencies that may still exist when humans are part of the direct production loop.
- **Need for Full Autonomy:** The paper articulates a vision for future industries that are "fully driven by Generative AI and a swarm of heterogeneous robots." This implies a motivation to achieve higher levels of automation, adaptability, and efficiency in manufacturing by leveraging autonomous decision-making and dynamic resource allocation.
- **Automated Product Design and Manufacturing Chain:** A specific motivation is to automate critical aspects of production, including product blueprint design, component manufacturing, logistics, and assembly. This involves creating a seamless pipeline from a high-level user request to a tangible product.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Adaptability:** By orchestrating a heterogeneous swarm of robots, each equipped with individual AI capabilities through LLM integration, the system aims to achieve flexible and efficient production. This includes adaptable production lines through flying conveyors and real-time decision-making to optimize task allocation and completion in complex manufacturing setups.

In essence, the research is motivated by the potential of advanced AI and robotics to create an entirely autonomous, human-out-of-the-loop manufacturing ecosystem that can interpret natural language, design, produce, and assemble complex products.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Industry 6.0 system is designed as a fully autonomous production pipeline, translating a user's natural language command into a physical product through four main stages: assembly blueprint generation, part production, flying conveyor-based logistics, and product assembly.

**A. System Architecture and Generative AI Integration:**
The system architecture (Fig. 2) outlines the flow, with generative AI as a central orchestrator. OpenAI API services are primarily utilized for the AI component, with local LLM inference via a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach also considered. LangChain is employed to integrate LLMs into the system, enabling controlled agentic workflows and tool access. The AI accesses a list of available tools and resources as context, using relevant information as contextual input for API-based models and the RAG approach for local models.

**B. Assembly Blueprint Generation Stage:**
This initial stage converts a natural language product description into 3D-printable components and a comprehensive assembly file. The process involves two sub-stages:
1. **User Input Analysis:** The LLM enriches the user's input by describing the mechanism assembly, its operating principle, individual parts, constituent primitives, and necessary connections. A list of 2D primitives (e.g., balk, circle, rectangle) is provided to the LLM as context.
2. **Code Generation for Mechanism Signed Distance Function (SDF):** The refined product specification is then used by the LLM to develop Python code for generating the SDF mechanism. An open-source Python library forms the basis of the SDF implementation, augmented by a custom library of mechanical primitives (gears, beams, rings) and expert rules/examples for 2D mechanisms provided to the LLM context.
The SDF mechanism representation is subsequently processed into 3D STL assembly files. To facilitate assembly, a base with pins is automatically generated, and components are arranged on a printing platform. Currently, the design supports single-layer assembly with a pin-and-hole dynamic connection. A JSON assembly file is also created, detailing component positions, print bed layout, connection points, and grasping elements for robots.

**C. Part Production Stage:**
Upon STL file generation, the 3D models are converted into G-code for an Ultimaker 3D printer. Support structures and raft layers are intentionally excluded to streamline automated post-processing. A specialized magnetic printing surface with a custom holder is used, allowing autonomous insertion and removal by a robotic manipulator. AruCo markers on the surface aid in precise post-print localization of parts relative to the JSON file data. After printing, a Universal Robot UR10 robotic arm transfers the printing surface with components onto a drone positioned on an adjacent landing pad (Fig. 4).

**D. Flying Conveyor Stage:**
This stage utilizes drones for component transport between production points. The prototype features an 8-inch custom drone equipped with a SpeedyBee F405 flight controller (ArduPilot firmware, MAVROS interface) and an OrangePi 5B onboard computer for control and state processing. A Vicon Tracking system (14 infrared cameras) provides precise localization, with PID control used for drone navigation. The Robot Operating System (ROS) framework manages software packages. The drone employs a magnetic mechanism to securely transport the 3D printer surface. Generative AI is intended to analyze the production line state and generate transportation plans, though in the current work, this is simplified to a single part production and assembly cell.

**E. Product Assembling Stage:**
Two Universal Robots UR3 manipulators, each fitted with a Robotiq 2F-83 gripper, perform the assembly. The workflow is orchestrated by LLMs using LangGraph (Fig. 5). An LLM-based supervisor assigns tasks to robots based on their capabilities, generating a list of subtasks for each. These subtasks trigger predefined API functions for robot motion control and gripper operation, utilizing object names and positions from the JSON assembly file generated earlier. Robot 1 typically handles component transfer to and from the drone, while Robot 2 detaches parts from the printing surface and performs the assembly.

**F. Experiments:**
Two experiments were conducted:
1. **LLM Generation Comparison:** Evaluated nine LLMs (commercial: OpenAI o1-preview, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Google Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3 Haiku; open-source: Hermes 3 Llama 3.1 405B, Reflection Llama 3.1 70B, Gemma 2 9B) across ten mechanisms of varying complexity. The assessment used a binary scoring system for three categories: Mechanism Analysis (detailed description, part decomposition, connections), Executable Code (Python SDF code generation), and Mechanism STL (functional final assembly STL).
2. **User Study:** Ten participants with mechanical design and 3D printing backgrounds (aged 23-34) were tasked with manufacturing a gripper mechanism. They designed using preferred CAD tools, 3D printed parts, delivered them to an assembly area, and assembled the product. The time taken for each stage and the total production time were recorded for human subjects and compared against the autonomous system's performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. LLM Generation Comparison (Table I):**
The evaluation of LLMs across different generation steps yielded the following results:
- **Top Performers:** OpenAI’s o1-preview and GPT-4o models exhibited the highest performance, achieving perfect scores (10/10) in both the "Analysis" and "Executable Code" categories and the highest score (8/10) in "Mechanism STL" production.
- **Comparable Performance:** Claude 3.5 Sonnet showed comparable results, with scores of 10/10 in "Analysis," 9/10 in "Executable Code," and 8/10 in "Mechanism STL."
- **Varying Performance:** Other models displayed lower scores, particularly in the "Executable Code" and "Mechanism STL" categories. For example, Google Gemini 1.5 Pro scored 9, 8, and 5 respectively, while smaller or open-source models like Claude 3 Haiku and Gemma 2 9B received scores as low as 4/10 in "Executable Code" and 1/10 to 2/10 in "Mechanism STL."
- **Complexity Impact:** All models demonstrated a decline in performance as the complexity of the task increased, with the "Mechanism STL" category identified as the most challenging.
The results indicated that leading models such as GPT-4o are suitable for the task of mechanism assembly generation due to their capability in producing accurate mechanism representations.

**B. User Study (Fig. 6):**
The user study compared the production efficiency of the autonomous system against expert human participants in manufacturing a gripper mechanism:
- **Overall Production Time:** The automated system completed the production process in an average of 119.10 minutes. Human participants averaged 528.64 minutes. This represents a reduction in production time by a factor of approximately 4.44 for the autonomous system.
- **Design Stage:** The system completed the design (blueprinting) stage in 0.50 minutes, whereas human CAD operators averaged 23.50 minutes. This indicates a speed-up factor of 47 for the automated system in this specific stage.
- **3D Printing Stage:** The automated system completed the 3D printing stage in 117.00 minutes. Human participants averaged 504.57 minutes for this stage. This corresponds to a speed-up factor of approximately 4.3 for the system.
- **Component Delivery:** Human participants completed component delivery in 0.46 minutes, slightly faster than the system's 0.50 minutes.
- **Assembly Stage:** Human participants assembled the components in 0.11 minutes, which was approximately 10 times faster than the system's 1.10 minutes.
The study concluded that the autonomous system provided substantial time reductions in the design and 3D printing stages, largely attributed to LLM-driven shape generation optimized for printing. Human performance was marginally faster in component delivery and notably faster in the manual assembly of the specific gripper mechanism. The authors anticipate that with increased part complexity and greater distances between manufacturing and assembly areas, the system's advantages in delivery and assembly could increase.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces the "Industry 6.0" concept, defining it as a fully autonomous production system capable of managing the entire design-to-manufacturing process based solely on natural language descriptions. The paper presents a prototype that integrates generative AI with a heterogeneous swarm of robots, orchestrating blueprint creation, part manufacturing, logistics, and assembly.

The system's demonstrated capability to produce multi-component products autonomously, following user commands, marks a step towards advanced manufacturing automation. The evaluation of LLMs indicates that current state-of-the-art models can effectively generate product descriptions and convert them into valid, printable 3D models.

The user study results highlight the potential for efficiency gains, particularly in the initial stages of product development and manufacturing. The system's ability to reduce average production time to 119.10 minutes compared to 528.64 minutes for human experts, with a 47-fold speed-up in the blueprinting stage alone, suggests that fully autonomous systems could significantly accelerate product realization.

The proposed "human-out-of-the-loop" model for manufacturing could have several impacts:
- **Enhanced Productivity:** Automating the entire chain from design to assembly has the potential to reduce production cycles and increase throughput.
- **On-Demand Personalization:** The ability to generate designs from natural language descriptions could facilitate the creation of highly personalized products on demand, opening new avenues for customized manufacturing.
- **Adaptable Production Systems:** The use of generative AI for decision-making and a flying conveyor system for logistics suggests a more flexible and adaptable manufacturing environment, capable of dynamically adjusting to production needs and layouts.
- **Reduced Human Workload in Design and Manufacturing:** By removing humans from direct involvement in these stages, the system could free up human resources for other tasks and potentially operate in environments unsuitable for human presence.

Future implications of this technology, as suggested by the authors, include the development of autonomous production systems for personalized products and systems capable of analyzing market trends to optimize production for demand or profitability. This work represents an advancement towards realizing highly autonomous, intelligent manufacturing ecosystems.
