# Geometry of Uncertainty: Learning Metric Spaces for Multimodal State Estimation in RL

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for multimodal state estimation in Reinforcement Learning, METRICMM, models uncertainty geometrically within a learned latent metric space, correlating distances with environmental temporal dynamics. This approach yields enhanced robustness against diverse and unseen sensor corruptions across various tasks, improving RL agent performance without needing explicit noise augmentation during training.

## Problem

- Robustly estimating environment states from high-dimensional, multimodal, and noisy observations is challenging, especially when noise characteristics are unknown or unpredictable.
- Traditional probabilistic state estimation methods rely on explicit noise distribution assumptions (e.g., Gaussian), limiting their generalization to real-world, non-Gaussian, or unseen noise.
- Deep learning-based state representations often lack intrinsic mechanisms to quantify observation uncertainty, and effective multimodal fusion typically requires prior knowledge of individual sensor noise.

## Method

- METRICMM learns a ==latent metric space== where distances between latent states correlate with the minimum number of actions required to transition between corresponding environmental states.
- Uncertainty is interpreted geometrically in this latent space, bypassing the need for explicit probabilistic noise modeling or training with explicit noise augmentation.
- An adaptive sensor fusion mechanism, based on inverse distance weighting, combines information from multiple modalities and latent dynamics predictions without requiring prior knowledge of sensor noise characteristics.

## Results

- METRICMM demonstrated superior and consistent robustness to various unseen sensor corruptions (e.g., Gaussian, Salt-and-Pepper, Failure, Hallucination) across MuJoCo and Fetch environments, maintaining performance even under high-severity noise.
- The method exhibited a 'flatter degradation' in return and 'graceful decay versus collapse' compared to baselines under increasing noise, notably on high-degree-of-freedom tasks and when multiple modalities were simultaneously corrupted.
- RL agents trained with METRICMM achieved enhanced performance and stability without the need for explicit noise augmentation during training, offering a practical advantage over baselines that often require noise-augmented data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Uncertainty in state estimation can be effectively modeled geometrically by leveraging temporal dynamics within a learned latent metric space, removing the reliance on explicit probabilistic noise assumptions.
> - An inverse distance weighting scheme allows for adaptive multimodal sensor fusion, where the contribution of each sensor is weighted inversely to its consistency with the learned dynamics prediction, enhancing robustness to unknown corruptions.
> - Learning a structured latent representation through contrastive temporal distance, latent transition, and multimodal invariance losses enables robust state estimation and improved RL performance without needing noisy training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Estimating the environmental state from high-dimensional, multimodal, and noisy observations is a foundational challenge in Reinforcement Learning (RL). The broader research landscape addresses this challenge through various paradigms, each with inherent limitations that the current work attempts to mitigate.

Traditional approaches to state estimation in the presence of uncertainty often rely on probabilistic models, such as Bayesian filtering techniques (e.g., Kalman filters, Monte Carlo methods). While these methods can provide optimal state estimates, they typically necessitate explicit assumptions regarding noise distributions (e.g., Gaussian noise) and require knowledge of the functional form of state distributions and observation models. These requirements often limit their generalization to arbitrary or unseen noise conditions and pose difficulties in robustly handling multimodal observations without complex, often hand-designed, fusion mechanisms or generative models.

Within deep learning, methods for state representation learning have primarily focused on extracting compact, low-dimensional, and often deterministic representations from high-dimensional observations. Techniques like variational autoencoders (VAEs) and contrastive learning methods (e.g., CURL, CORAL) have shown promise in learning effective representations. However, many of these approaches either lose the inherent temporal structure of the state space or rely on training under explicitly noisy conditions, which assumes prior knowledge of noise characteristics and can impede learning efficiency. Some advancements merge deep learning with Bayesian filtering, such as models that use discriminative encoders or attention mechanisms (like α-MDF) to approximate Kalman gain, yet they often still restrict state estimates to known probabilistic forms and may struggle with multimodality.

More recently, metric learning has gained traction in RL. Prior work in this area has leveraged learned metric or quasimetric spaces to approximate temporal distances between states, primarily to facilitate policy learning, planning (e.g., model predictive control, reward shaping), exploration, or learning from suboptimal demonstrations. These methods use the geometric structure to improve specific aspects of the RL problem.

The present work, METRICMM, positions itself within this landscape by addressing a distinct gap: it exploits metric spaces not primarily for policy learning or planning, but specifically for *uncertainty estimation* in multimodal state representation. A key distinction is its ability to interpret uncertainty geometrically, thereby circumventing the need for explicit probabilistic noise modeling or training with explicit noise augmentation. This approach aims to provide robust state estimation that generalizes to *unseen* and *arbitrary* sensor corruptions, an aspect where many existing probabilistic and deep learning methods face limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central problem addressed by this research is the robust estimation of an environment's state from high-dimensional, multimodal, and potentially noisy sensory observations, particularly when the nature of the noise is unknown or varies unpredictably. This is a critical challenge in real-world reinforcement learning (RL) applications, such as robotics, where sensor data is inherently unreliable due to external disturbances or sensor failures.

The authors are motivated by several limitations of existing state estimation methods:
1. **Dependence on Explicit Noise Assumptions:** Traditional probabilistic approaches, like Bayesian filters, require explicit modeling of noise distributions (e.g., Gaussian noise). This limits their generalization capabilities because real-world noise is often non-Gaussian, complex, and unknown a priori. Adapting these models to new noise conditions typically requires re-engineering or retraining.
2. **Lack of Intrinsic Uncertainty Representation in Deep Learning:** While deep learning has enabled the extraction of powerful low-dimensional representations from high-dimensional sensory inputs, many deep RL methods produce deterministic state representations. These representations often lack an inherent mechanism to quantify or account for observation uncertainty, making them brittle in the face of unexpected sensor noise or failures. Some methods attempt to incorporate uncertainty through variational inference, but these often impose known forms on the latent distributions and still require explicit noise examples during training.
3. **Challenges in Multimodal Sensor Fusion:** When multiple sensors are available, they can compensate for one another, leading to more precise state estimates. However, fusing multimodal data robustly, especially when individual modalities are differentially affected by noise, requires sophisticated mechanisms. Traditional fusion often demands prior knowledge of each modality's noise characteristics or complex designs to weigh their contributions adaptively.
4. **Need for Noise Augmentation during Training:** Many robust representation learning methods achieve resilience to noise by training on datasets that have been explicitly augmented with various noise types. This process is computationally expensive, requires foreknowledge of potential noise patterns, and may still not generalize to entirely novel corruptions.

To overcome these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:
- To propose a novel method, METRICMM, that learns a structured latent representation for multimodal observations. This representation is designed such that distances within the latent space directly correlate with the minimum number of actions required to transition between the corresponding environmental states.
- To introduce a geometric interpretation of uncertainty, thereby eliminating the need for explicit probabilistic modeling of noise distributions. This reframes the problem of uncertainty from a statistical inference task to a distance-based reasoning problem within a learned metric space.
- To develop an adaptive sensor fusion mechanism based on inverse distance weighting. This mechanism integrates information from multiple sensor modalities and the latent dynamics prediction without requiring prior knowledge of the individual sensor noise distributions.
- To empirically demonstrate that the proposed approach leads to improved robustness to various forms of sensor noise, including types unseen during training, and yields superior state estimation compared to baseline methods.
- To show that RL agents trained with this learned representation achieve enhanced performance, removing the necessity for explicit noise augmentation during the training phase.

Overall, the motivation is to provide a principled, scalable, and practical solution for robust state estimation in RL environments characterized by high-dimensional, multimodal, and unreliable sensory input, without the restrictive assumptions or extensive data requirements of prior methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Metric Learning for Multimodal State Estimation (METRICMM), introduces a novel approach to robust state estimation by learning a structured latent metric space. This space geometrically interprets uncertainty, enabling adaptive sensor fusion without explicit probabilistic noise modeling.

**Core Concept: Latent Metric Space**
METRICMM's central idea is to learn a latent space, denoted as `Z ⊆ R^m`, which forms a metric space `M = (Z, ||·||_2)` under the Euclidean norm. An ideal injective mapping `φ: S → Z` is assumed, where `S` is the true underlying Markovian state space. The crucial property of this latent space is that distances between latent states `z ∈ Z` are designed to correlate with the minimum number of actions required to transition between their corresponding real environment states `s ∈ S`. This concept aligns with temporal distances in the environment dynamics.

An approximate latent transition model, `φ_T: Z × A → Z`, is learned to predict the next latent state given the current latent state and action. A key **Transition Error Assumption** is made: errors in the transition model's prediction are generally local. Specifically, the true latent state `z_t` is assumed to lie within a small `ϵ`-radius ball `B(φ_T(z_{t-1}, a_{t-1}), ϵ)` centered at the predicted latent state `φ_T(z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})`. This implies that prediction errors primarily occur within a neighborhood of states that are dynamically close.

Each of the `N` sensor modalities `O_i` is mapped to this shared latent space `Z` by a deterministic encoder `φ_i: O_i → Z`. The objective is to learn these mappings such that the resulting latent representations are aligned across modalities and conform to the defined metric space properties.

**Sensor Fusion via Inverse Distance Weighting**
To robustly combine information from multiple sensors and the latent dynamics model, METRICMM employs an adaptive sensor fusion mechanism based on inverse distance weighting. This mechanism is motivated by two intuitions:
1. An observation's latent encoding `φ_i(o_i_t)` is likely accurate if it is close to the latent transition prediction `φ_T(z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})`.
2. Conversely, a distant observation encoding is likely corrupted or uninformative.

Therefore, the contribution of each modality `i` is weighted inversely to its Euclidean distance from the predicted latent state `ˆz_t = φ_T(z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})`. The final estimated latent state `z_t` at time `t` is computed as a weighted average:
`z_t = (Σ_i (1 / (||z_i_t - ˆz_t||_2 + δ)))^-1 * Σ_i (z_i_t / (||z_i_t - ˆz_t||_2 + δ))`
where `z_i_t = φ_i(o_i_t)` is the latent encoding of observation `o_i_t`, and `δ = 10^-5` is a small constant to prevent division by zero. This adaptive weighting scheme allows the model to prioritize more consistent and reliable sensor inputs without requiring prior knowledge of noise characteristics.

**Learning the Latent Representation (Loss Functions)**
METRICMM's latent representation is learned through a combination of three loss functions, designed to enforce the desired metric structure and consistency. Importantly, training does not necessitate noisy observations. During training, `¯z_t` is defined as the average of the sensor encodings at time `t` (i.e., `(1/N) Σ_i φ_i(o_i_t)`), serving as a proxy for the uncorrupted state estimate.

1. **Contrastive Temporal Distance Loss (`L_+` and `L_-`):** This loss encourages temporal consistency by ensuring that successive states are close in the latent space, while randomly sampled states are pushed further apart.
 - `L_+ = E[(||¯z_{t+1} - ¯z_t||_2 - 1)^2]`: Penalizes deviations from a desired unit distance between consecutive states, ensuring local temporal proximity.
 - `L_- = E[-log(||¯z_r - ¯z_t||_2)]`: Maximizes the distance between a current state `¯z_t` and a randomly sampled state `¯z_r`. This term prevents the latent representation from collapsing to a single point and promotes a spread-out, meaningful geometry that preserves non-adjacent temporal distances.

2. **Latent Transition Loss (`L_T`):** This loss enforces that the learned latent transition model `φ_T` accurately predicts the subsequent latent state.
 - `L_T = E[(φ_T(¯z_t, a_t) - ¯z_{t+1})^2]`

3. **Multimodal Invariance Loss (`L_inv`):** This loss aligns the representations learned by individual sensor encoders, ensuring that all modalities are mapped into the same shared metric space.
 - `L_inv = E[(φ_i(o_i_t) - φ_j(o_j_t))^2]` for all pairs of distinct modalities `i, j`.

**Integration with Reinforcement Learning**
The overall loss function for METRICMM is a linear combination of these components:
`L = L_T + λ_1 L_+ + λ_2 L_- + λ_3 L_inv`
where `λ_1, λ_2, λ_3` are scalar weighting coefficients (set to 1 in experiments). The learned latent representation `z_t` is then fed as input to a standard reinforcement learning algorithm (e.g., Soft Actor-Critic, SAC). The entire system, including the representation module and the RL policy, is optimized end-to-end. This approach implicitly handles observation uncertainty through the geometric structure of the latent space, bypassing the need for explicit noise modeling or data augmentation during RL training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical validation of METRICMM was conducted on a range of multimodal RL tasks, demonstrating its robustness to sensor noise and its ability to enhance RL agent performance.

**Experimental Setup:**
The evaluation spanned two distinct suites:
- **MuJoCo environments:** Hopper-v5, HalfCheetah-v5, Ant-v5, Walker2d-v5, and Humanoid-v5, using synchronized RGB and depth image streams (84x84 resolution, 3 stacked frames).
- **Fetch manipulation tasks:** FetchPickAndPlace-v4 and FetchSlide-v4, utilizing RGB, depth images (128x128 resolution, 3 stacked frames), and point clouds.
For all experiments, a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agent was trained end-to-end with the representation module. Performance was measured by mean return ± standard deviation over 5 seeds, with testing conducted over 50 trajectories per seed.

**Corruption Protocol:**
To assess robustness, seven families of perturbations were applied *at test time* (unseen during training) with varying probabilities: Gaussian, Salt-and-Pepper, Patches, Puzzle, Texture, Failure (observation set to 0), and Hallucination (observation replaced by another in-distribution observation from a different trajectory).
- **MuJoCo:** Corruptions were applied to one modality at a time to isolate sensor contributions.
- **Fetch:** Corruptions were applied to one or two of the three modalities simultaneously, testing resilience under severe conditions.

**Baselines:**
METRICMM was compared against six representative fusion/representation methods, with matched encoder capacities and tuning budgets:
1. **Linear Combination (LinearComb):** A learned linear map combining per-modality latent features.
2. **Concatenation (ConCat):** Feature concatenation followed by a shared projection.
3. **CURL (Laskin et al., 2020):** Contrastive learning on augmented views.
4. **GMC (Poklukar et al., 2022):** Cross-modal contrastive alignment.
5. **α-MDF (Liu et al., 2023):** An attention-weighted differentiable Bayesian filter.
6. **CORAL (Becker et al., 2023):** Joint latent space via reconstruction and temporal contrastive alignment.

**Main Findings:**

- **Robustness to Single-Modality Corruption (MuJoCo Suite):** METRICMM consistently demonstrated the most robust performance, exhibiting the "flattest degradation" in return as perturbation probability increased across various tasks and corruption types (Figure 3, Appendix Figures 8-9). It preserved a substantial fraction of clean performance even at high noise severities. In contrast, simple fusion baselines (LinearComb, ConCat) degraded steeply, and even contrastive methods like CURL and GMC, while competitive at low noise, showed sharp performance drops under higher corruption frequencies. This suggests that METRICMM's geometric interpretation of uncertainty and adaptive fusion is more effective against diverse and unseen noise. The performance gap widened on high-degree-of-freedom tasks (Humanoid-v5, HalfCheetah-v5), indicating the increasing importance of principled cross-modal consistency for complex control.

- **Robustness under Multi-Modality Corruption (Fetch Suite):** Under severe conditions where two of the three modalities were simultaneously corrupted (Tables 1 and 2), METRICMM maintained substantially higher returns compared to all baselines. For instance, on Fetch Slide with failure noise, METRICMM sustained positive rewards even at very high corruption severities, while other baselines collapsed much earlier. This "graceful decay versus collapse" behavior highlights METRICMM's ability to effectively prioritize and re-weight the remaining reliable modality.

- **Training with Noisy Observations is Not Required:** Experiments showed that training baselines with explicitly injected noise, while potentially inducing invariances, generally slowed exploration and lowered asymptotic returns (Figure 2). Severe noise significantly delayed the onset of learning. METRICMM achieves its robustness without requiring any noisy training data, offering a practical advantage by simplifying the training pipeline and avoiding the need for prior knowledge of corruption families.

- **Effective Utilization of All Modalities:** An analysis of performance when only a single modality was corrupted at high probability (Table 3) revealed that many baselines exhibited minimal degradation when point clouds (the most informative but complex modality) were corrupted. This indicated an over-reliance on simpler RGB/depth information and under-utilization of 3D structure. In contrast, METRICMM showed a more uniform sensitivity profile across modalities, suggesting it effectively distributes credit and meaningfully integrates information from each sensor.

- **Robustness to Temporally Dependent Noise:** METRICMM was the most reliable method when sensor failures persisted for multiple consecutive frames (e.g., 3 or 10 frames), a common characteristic of real-world sensor malfunctions (Figure 4).

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies on a one-dimensional pendulum environment investigated the contribution of METRICMM's loss components (Figure 5). Removing the multimodal invariance loss (`L_inv`) or the metric loss pair (`L_+` and `L_-`) led to a loss of noise robustness, even though the SAC agent could still learn a policy. This confirms that both the alignment between modalities and the learned temporal metric structure are crucial for the observed resilience to noise.

**Limitations:**
- **Symmetric Metric Assumption:** The use of a Euclidean norm in the latent space restricts it to symmetric distances, which is an approximation for inherently asymmetric temporal distances (quasimetrics) that arise in RL due to irreversibility or constraints. While this limits theoretical fidelity, practical performance was observed to be acceptable.
- **Deterministic Dynamics Assumption:** The current formulation assumes deterministic environment dynamics. In stochastic environments, the model embeds all possible next states at a unit distance and estimates the barycenter of the resulting distribution, which might require higher latent space dimensionality and reduced expressivity with a deterministic transition module. While METRICMM demonstrated robustness in moderately stochastic settings (Figure 6), fully stochastic dynamics may necessitate a different notion of distance or a probabilistic transition model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "GEOMETRY OF UNCERTAINTY: LEARNING METRIC SPACES FOR MULTIMODAL STATE ESTIMATION IN RL" introduces a novel and significant approach to robust state estimation in reinforcement learning. Its primary contribution lies in offering a geometric interpretation of uncertainty within a learned metric space, thereby circumventing the limitations of traditional probabilistic modeling that require explicit noise assumptions.

The significance of this work stems from several key aspects:
- **Novel Uncertainty Modeling:** By correlating latent space distances with temporal distances (minimum actions for transition), the method provides an intrinsic, dynamics-aware measure of uncertainty. This allows for principled state estimation without explicitly modeling noise distributions, which is a departure from conventional Bayesian filtering or variational approaches.
- **Adaptive Multimodal Fusion:** The inverse distance weighting mechanism for sensor fusion is adaptive and knowledge-free regarding specific noise profiles. This allows for robust integration of diverse sensor modalities, enabling the system to dynamically prioritize more reliable inputs, a crucial capability for complex, real-world robotic systems with varied sensor characteristics.
- **Robustness to Unseen and Diverse Corruptions:** The empirical results demonstrate that METRICMM exhibits superior and consistent robustness against a wide array of sensor corruptions, including those unseen during training, and even under conditions where a majority of sensor modalities are degraded. This generalization capability is a substantial advantage over methods that rely on explicit noise augmentation during training.
- **Simplified Training Regimen:** The ability to achieve robust state estimation without requiring explicit noise augmentation during training simplifies the development process, reduces computational burden, and alleviates the need for extensive, domain-specific knowledge of potential noise types.
- **Enhanced RL Agent Performance:** The robust and informative state representations learned by METRICMM translate directly into enhanced performance for RL agents across various locomotion and manipulation tasks, underscoring its practical utility in sequential decision-making.

The potential impact of this research is substantial, particularly for applications in real-world robotics and autonomous systems. In these domains, sensor data is often high-dimensional, multimodal, and susceptible to unpredictable noise, faults, or failures. By providing a scalable and principled solution for robust state estimation, METRICMM could enable:
- More resilient and reliable autonomous agents that can operate effectively in unstructured and dynamic environments.
- Simplified deployment of RL agents, as the laborious process of noise characterization and data augmentation may be reduced or eliminated.
- Advancements in safe and trustworthy AI systems, where understanding and mitigating uncertainty in perception is paramount.

Future directions, such as adapting the method to non-stationary environments, evaluating its robustness against adversarial attacks, and deploying it on real-world robotic platforms, suggest continued relevance and broader applicability of this geometric approach to uncertainty in perception for control.
