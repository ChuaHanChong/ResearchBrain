# Geometry of Uncertainty: Learning Metric Spaces for Multimodal State Estimation in RL

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for multimodal state estimation in Reinforcement Learning, METRICMM, models uncertainty geometrically within a learned latent metric space, correlating distances with environmental temporal dynamics. This approach yields enhanced robustness against diverse and unseen sensor corruptions across various tasks, improving RL agent performance without needing explicit noise augmentation during training.

## Problem

- Robustly estimating environment states from high-dimensional, multimodal, and noisy observations is challenging, especially when noise characteristics are unknown or unpredictable.
- Traditional probabilistic state estimation methods rely on explicit noise distribution assumptions (e.g., Gaussian), limiting their generalization to real-world, non-Gaussian, or unseen noise.
- Deep learning-based state representations often lack intrinsic mechanisms to quantify observation uncertainty, and effective multimodal fusion typically requires prior knowledge of individual sensor noise.

## Method

- METRICMM learns a ==latent metric space== where distances between latent states correlate with the minimum number of actions required to transition between corresponding environmental states.
- Uncertainty is interpreted geometrically in this latent space, bypassing the need for explicit probabilistic noise modeling or training with explicit noise augmentation.
- An adaptive sensor fusion mechanism, based on inverse distance weighting, combines information from multiple modalities and latent dynamics predictions without requiring prior knowledge of sensor noise characteristics.

## Results

- METRICMM demonstrated superior and consistent robustness to various unseen sensor corruptions (e.g., Gaussian, Salt-and-Pepper, Failure, Hallucination) across MuJoCo and Fetch environments, maintaining performance even under high-severity noise.
- The method exhibited a 'flatter degradation' in return and 'graceful decay versus collapse' compared to baselines under increasing noise, notably on high-degree-of-freedom tasks and when multiple modalities were simultaneously corrupted.
- RL agents trained with METRICMM achieved enhanced performance and stability without the need for explicit noise augmentation during training, offering a practical advantage over baselines that often require noise-augmented data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Uncertainty in state estimation can be effectively modeled geometrically by leveraging temporal dynamics within a learned latent metric space, removing the reliance on explicit probabilistic noise assumptions.
> - An inverse distance weighting scheme allows for adaptive multimodal sensor fusion, where the contribution of each sensor is weighted inversely to its consistency with the learned dynamics prediction, enhancing robustness to unknown corruptions.
> - Learning a structured latent representation through contrastive temporal distance, latent transition, and multimodal invariance losses enables robust state estimation and improved RL performance without needing noisy training data.
