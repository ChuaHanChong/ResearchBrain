# TeleOpBench: A Simulator-Centric Benchmark for Dual-Arm Dexterous Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and collaborating institutions developed TeleOpBench, a simulator-centric benchmark for dual-arm dexterous teleoperation, which unifies evaluation across 30 tasks and four distinct interfaces. The benchmark demonstrates a strong correlation between simulated and real-world performance, enabling reliable assessment of diverse teleoperation systems.

## Problem

- The absence of a standardized benchmark for evaluating and comparing diverse dual-arm dexterous teleoperation systems.
- Difficulty in objectively assessing and reproducing results across various hardware platforms and task environments.
- High cost and inherent uncertainties of real-world robotics experiments hinder systematic progress in teleoperation research.

## Method

![[2505.12748-method.png]]
*TeleOpBench overview: four operator interfaces (VR, RGB camera, IMU MoCap, exoskeleton) mapped to upper-body and dexterous-hand joint angles, evaluated in both simulation and the real world.*

- Introduces **TeleOpBench**, a simulator-centric benchmark in ==NVIDIA Isaac Sim== featuring 30 high-fidelity bimanual manipulation tasks of varying complexity.
- Integrates ==four representative teleoperation modalities== (inertial MoCap, VR, arm-hand exoskeletons, monocular vision tracking) into a unified, modular framework.
- Validates the benchmark's fidelity through comparative real-world experiments on physical robot platforms, demonstrating ==correlation between simulated and hardware performance==.

## Results

- Xsens MoCap-based teleoperation consistently achieved the **highest success rates** and **fastest completion times** across a suite of **10 tasks** in both simulation and real-world environments.
- Vision-based teleoperation generally exhibited the longest completion times and lowest success rates, particularly failing on complex bimanual tasks (**0% success** on 'ball bimanual' and 'pen brushpot').
- A **strong positive correlation** was observed between performance metrics (completion time and success rate) in simulated and physical tasks, validating TeleOpBench's utility as a predictive benchmarking tool.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simulation-based benchmarks can reliably predict real-world performance for complex dual-arm dexterous teleoperation, reducing the need for extensive physical testing.
> - Different teleoperation modalities exhibit distinct performance profiles: high-fidelity MoCap systems offer superior precision and speed, while vision-based systems face challenges with occlusion and accuracy.
> - Standardizing evaluation environments is critical for fostering fair comparisons and accelerating innovation in advanced robot learning and human-robot interaction.
