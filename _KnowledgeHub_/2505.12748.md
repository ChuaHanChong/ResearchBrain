# TeleOpBench: A Simulator-Centric Benchmark for Dual-Arm Dexterous Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Laboratory and collaborators introduce TeleOpBench, a simulator-centric benchmark for dual-arm dexterous teleoperation built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim. The platform standardizes evaluation across three commercial humanoid robots (Unitree H1-2, Fourier GR1-T2, Unitree G1) and four representative teleop modalities (motion capture, VR, exoskeleton, vision-only), with strong simulator–real-world performance correlation validating the benchmark's fidelity.

## Problem

- Evaluating dual-arm dexterous teleoperation systems is hampered by tight coupling of hardware, control software, and task environments, making cross-method comparison impossible.
- Existing studies use custom setups with non-standardized robots and tasks, preventing fair benchmarking of teleop interface modalities.
- The lack of a shared evaluation platform slows progress in collecting demonstration data for downstream robot learning (imitation, behavior cloning).

## Method

- A simulator-centric benchmark built on **NVIDIA Isaac Sim** that fixes robot morphology and task environments, eliminating hardware/scene variability across teleop methods.
- A multi-tiered suite of **30 bimanual dexterous-manipulation tasks** hierarchically organized by complexity, from simple pick-and-place to long-horizon routines, each with explicit success criteria.
- Four representative teleoperation pipelines implemented under a unified modular interface: ==vision-based== (three-module body-parameter + limb + hand pipeline), ==MoCap-based== (Xsens MVN + Manus Metagloves with joint-specific rescaling), ==VR-based== (Apple VisionPro, OpenXR coordinate system), and ==exoskeleton-based== for high-precision control.
- Three commercial humanoid platforms — ==Unitree H1-2==, ==Fourier GR1-T2==, ==Unitree G1== — enable hardware-evaluation generalization.

## Results

- The four teleop modalities show distinct strengths: vision-based is simple but limited by **low frame rates** and occlusion; VR offers good grasping but is occlusion-sensitive; exoskeleton provides smooth control on most tasks; **Xsens MoCap** excels in smoothness and precision but is the most expensive.
- Strong **positive correlation** between simulator and real-hardware completion-time curves validates the simulator's fidelity for cross-method comparison.
- A user study with **4 participants** confirmed usability rankings consistent with the quantitative metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A simulator-centric benchmark is the right way to compare teleoperation interface modalities — it fixes morphology and tasks while exposing the interface as the variable of interest.
> - Strong sim-to-real correlation in completion times means TeleOpBench can serve as a low-cost surrogate for expensive real-robot teleop studies.
> - The 30-task hierarchy and multi-embodiment evaluation make TeleOpBench a candidate anchor benchmark for bimanual dexterous humanoid policy evaluation, beyond just teleop.
