# CanViT: Toward Active-Vision Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CanViT introduces the first task- and policy-agnostic Active-Vision Foundation Model, integrating a recurrent Vision Transformer with a scene-wide memory to process visual scenes from sequences of localized glimpses. The model achieved 38.5% mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation from a single glimpse, outperforming previous active vision models while using 19.5x fewer FLOPs, and demonstrated strong generalization across various policies and resolutions.

## Problem

- Existing active computer vision models struggle with scalability, generality, and often fail to match the accuracy and rich representations of passive vision models, especially for dense prediction tasks.
- Most active vision systems lack a high-capacity, persistent memory capable of integrating information from sequential, localized glimpses to build a comprehensive, scene-wide spatio-semantic understanding.
- Developing general-purpose active vision models is complicated by the entanglement of perceptual learning ("how to see") and action selection policies ("where to look"), making pretraining complex and task-specific.

## Method

- Introduces **CanViT**, a ==recurrent Vision Transformer== with a dual-stream architecture comprising a localized glimpse-processing backbone and a persistent, scene-wide "canvas" memory.
- Employs an ==asymmetric Canvas Attention mechanism== for efficient, bidirectional interaction between the backbone and canvas, which significantly reduces computational cost by restricting QKVO projections to the backbone-side tokens.
- Develops a policy-agnostic, ==passive-to-active dense latent distillation== pretraining scheme that reconstructs latent representations from a frozen ==DINOv3== teacher across randomized glimpse rollouts and sequence lengths.

## Results

- CanViT-B achieved **38.5%** mIoU on ADE**20K** semantic segmentation from a single 128x128 glimpse, surpassing the previous active-vision state-of-the-art (AME) by a large margin (**27.6%** mIoU) with **19.5x** fewer inference FLOPs (15.86 GFLOPs vs. 309 GFLOPs).
- The model demonstrated strong generalization to various unseen viewing policies (e.g., Coarse-to-Fine (C2F)) and significantly longer temporal horizons (up to T=21 glimpses) than those encountered during pretraining, improving ADE**20K** mIoU to **45.9%** with C2F.
- CanViT-B attained **81.2%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k with the C2F policy and 21 glimpses, performing competitively with other active vision models without requiring end-to-end Reinforcement Learning or task-specific fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-performing perceptual and memory capabilities for active vision ("how to see") can be effectively learned independently of complex action selection policies ("where to look").
> - A coherent, scene-wide spatio-semantic understanding can be built and continuously refined by iteratively integrating information from localized, variable-scale glimpses into a persistent memory.
> - Asymmetric cross-attention efficiently enables recurrent interaction between ephemeral glimpse features and persistent canvas memory, allowing for scalable processing in active vision models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) have achieved notable success in various computer vision tasks, predominantly through passive vision encoders that process individual frames independently. This approach contrasts with human visual perception, which is inherently active and sequential, involving strategic gaze shifts, integration of information over time in working memory, and top-down recurrent feedback.

Active Computer Vision (ACV) models, drawing inspiration from human vision, aim to emulate this sequential, localized glimpse-based processing for computational efficiency and biological plausibility. However, existing ACV models have historically struggled to match the accuracy, efficiency, flexibility, and representational richness of their passive counterparts. This disparity is particularly evident in dense prediction tasks, where the few active vision models capable of such tasks significantly underperform passive models on standard benchmarks like ADE20K segmentation.

Prior ACV research has often prioritized the "action selection" aspect—determining where and at what zoom level to look next—rather than developing robust mechanisms for "instantaneous vision" (making sense of what is seen at a given moment) and "memory" (updating a persistent understanding of the scene). Concurrently, powerful passive-vision foundation models, such as DINOv3, excel at general-purpose instantaneous vision but lack the architectural components necessary for localized glimpses or memory retention across time.

This work addresses the gap between advanced passive vision models and the limitations of current active vision models. It proposes to build a task- and policy-agnostic Active-Vision Foundation Model (AVFM) that focuses on the interplay between instantaneous vision and memory. By decoupling "how to see" from "where to look," the authors aim to simplify active-vision pretraining by removing the complexities often associated with Reinforcement Learning (RL) for policy optimization. The objective is to create a vision model capable of understanding scene structure from arbitrary sequences of glimpses, with representations that transfer effectively across diverse tasks and viewing policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a scalable, general-purpose architecture and pretraining pipeline for active computer vision, specifically an Active-Vision Foundation Model (AVFM), to address the limitations of existing active vision approaches. The motivation stems from several key observations and goals:

- **Bridging the Performance Gap:** Current deep active vision models, despite their theoretical advantages in efficiency and biological plausibility, significantly lag behind passive vision models in accuracy and representational richness, particularly for dense prediction tasks. The researchers aim to narrow this gap.
- **Developing General-Purpose Active Vision:** The goal is to move beyond task-specific or policy-dependent active vision models. The authors seek to create a foundation model that can learn robust visual representations from sequential glimpses, applicable across a wide range of downstream tasks (e.g., classification, segmentation) and adaptable to various sensory policies without requiring retraining.
- **Decoupling Perception and Action:** Traditional active vision often integrates action selection (where to look next) with visual processing, frequently relying on complex Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipelines. This work aims to disentangle "how to see in an active-vision setting" (i.e., instantaneous vision and memory) from "where to look." This policy-agnostic approach simplifies the pretraining process by eliminating the need for RL, making the development of active vision models more accessible and efficient.
- **Efficiently Integrating Glimpses into a Persistent Memory:** A core motivation is to develop an architecture that can effectively accumulate information from sequential, localized, and variable-scale glimpses into a coherent, scene-wide latent representation. This persistent "working memory" should allow for continuous refinement of scene understanding and provide contextually informed feedback for subsequent visual processing, mimicking aspects of human visual working memory.
- **Enabling Dense Prediction in Active Vision:** Most existing active vision models are not designed for dense prediction tasks, which require scene-wide, spatially rich outputs. A key objective is to build an architecture that inherently supports dense prediction by maintaining a spatially grounded memory, capable of direct decoding into pixel-level outputs.
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The proposed model aims for high computational efficiency, particularly for sequential inference, and scalability to high output resolutions, where the advantages of active vision are most pronounced. This involves designing components that avoid quadratic computational costs with increasing memory capacity.

In summary, the motivation is to advance active computer vision by creating a general-purpose, high-performing, and computationally efficient active-vision foundation model through a novel architecture and a simplified, policy-agnostic pretraining paradigm, ultimately making active vision a more viable and competitive paradigm in computer vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CanViT framework introduces a recurrent architecture centered around a latent scene-wide representation called the canvas, pre-trained using a novel distillation scheme.

#### 3.1. CanViT Architecture

CanViT employs a dual-stream structure: a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone for processing localized glimpses and a persistent scene-wide memory referred to as the canvas. These two streams interact bidirectionally through a novel mechanism named Canvas Attention.

- **Glimpse Stream:**
 - Ingests fixed-resolution 128x128 pixel glimpses, which are crops extracted from a scene at a given viewpoint (x, y, s, representing center coordinates and scale/zoom level).
 - Each glimpse is patchified into 16x16 pixel patches.
 - Processed through the ViT backbone alongside ephemeral register tokens, a recurrent CLS token, and a Viewpoint Encoding (VPE) token.
 - Patch tokens are not directly forwarded across time due to variable spatial alignment across glimpses. Instead, relevant information is transferred via the canvas.
- **Canvas Stream:**
 - Functions as a fully persistent, scene-wide spatio-semantic memory, conceptualized as a cognitive map.
 - Comprises a few non-spatial canvas registers and a large HxW spatial grid of D_can-dimensional canvas patch tokens that tile the scene coordinate space.
 - The spatial grid is broadcasted from a single learnable initial patch at the start of each rollout, allowing the canvas resolution to be dynamically set at inference time.
 - Each canvas patch corresponds to a fixed scene region, enabling direct token-wise decoding for dense predictions.
 - Crucially, canvas tokens evolve exclusively through Canvas Attention Write operations, without Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) or self-attention layers. This design choice is critical for efficiency and scalability.
- **Scene-Relative Rotary Position Embeddings (SR-RoPE):**
 - 2D RoPEs are computed from the centers of both glimpse patches and canvas patches within the scene's normalized [-1, +1] coordinate system.
 - This consistent use of scene-relative coordinates establishes a shared reference frame, binding the retinotopic glimpse stream (viewpoint-dependent) and the spatiotopic canvas stream (fixed to scene).
 - RoPE also enables generalization across different patch grid sizes.
- **Canvas Attention:**
 - An asymmetric cross-attention mechanism designed for efficient interaction between the smaller set of glimpse tokens and the larger set of canvas tokens.
 - Alternates between "Read" and "Write" operations within the model's depth (and implicitly across time). For CanViT-B, this occurs with a stride of 2 ViT blocks.
 - **Read Operation:** Glimpse tokens query the canvas, conditioning the backbone's processing on existing canvas information. The output is added to the glimpse stream.
 - **Write Operation:** Canvas tokens query the glimpse, updating the canvas with new information. The output is added to the canvas stream.
 - **Asymmetric Projections:** QKVO (Query, Key, Value, Output) projections are applied only to the glimpse-side tokens. Canvas-side tokens only undergo LayerNormalization, RoPE, and element-wise residual addition. This significantly reduces FLOPs, especially when the canvas has many tokens, making high-resolution canvases feasible.
- **Viewpoint Encoding (VPE) Token:**
 - A dedicated glimpse-side token encoding the viewpoint triplet (x, y, s) using a scale-invariant parameterization (x/s, y/s, log s). This triplet is lifted to a higher dimension via Random Fourier Features (RFF).
 - Intended to facilitate future end-to-end policy learning, allowing policy decisions to be decoded from a rich representation of the current viewpoint.

#### 3.2. Policy-agnostic Passive-to-Active Dense Latent Distillation

CanViT is pre-trained using a label-free, policy-agnostic dense latent distillation scheme that teaches the model to understand and integrate visual information across glimpse sequences.

- **Pretext Task: Scene Reconstruction:** The model is trained to reconstruct the overall scene from a sequence of glimpses, encouraging it to extrapolate to unseen regions and details and build a semantically rich "mental image."
- **Teacher Model:** A frozen DINOv3 ViT-B model acts as a high-resolution scene-wide spatio-semantic teacher. It provides dense patch features and a global CLS token, representing an idealized scene understanding for CanViT to match. These teacher features are precomputed to avoid repeated expensive forward passes during training.
- **Decoding:** CanViT's updated canvas patches and CLS token are linearly projected into the DINOv3 latent space.
- **Loss Function:** The loss combines patch-level and CLS-level L2 reconstruction errors, averaged across space and time. Per-position z-score standardization is applied to the DINOv3 reconstruction targets.
- **Policy Agnosticism through Rollout Randomization:**
 - **Dual Rollouts:** For each scene, two independent rollouts are performed, and their losses are averaged.
 - **R-IID (Random-then-IID):** Samples random viewpoints (center and scale) at all timesteps, including the initial one, promoting robustness to arbitrary starting points.
 - **F-IID (Full-then-IID):** Always starts with a full-scene (0,0,1) viewpoint, followed by random viewpoints. This ensures the model observes the entire scene at least once. Using one F-IID and one R-IID rollout accelerates convergence.
 - **Viewpoint Sampling:** For random viewpoints, a scale `s` is sampled such that `p(s) ∝ (1 − s)`, favoring small, localized glimpses. Centers are sampled uniformly within the valid region for the chosen scale.
 - **Rollout Length Randomization:** Truncated Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) is applied over chunks of K=2 glimpses. Rollouts are stopped with a 50% probability at each chunk boundary, leading to a geometric distribution of rollout lengths with an average of T=4 glimpses. This ensures robustness to varying sequence lengths while managing VRAM.

#### 3.3. Pretraining and Evaluation Details

- CanViT-B was pretrained from random initialization on 13.2 million ImageNet-21k scenes, sampling approximately 1 billion glimpses, requiring 166 hours on a single H100 GPU.
- A canvas resolution of 32x32 patches was used during pretraining, with 128x128 pixel glimpses.
- Evaluation involved ADE20K semantic segmentation (using frozen features and linear probes) and ImageNet-1k classification (using frozen features with linear probes, and full fine-tuning).
- Multiple inference-time viewing policies were tested, including Coarse-to-Fine (C2F), Fine-to-Coarse (F2C), Entropy-Guided C2F (EG-C2F), and Repeated Full-Scene (RFS), alongside the R-IID and F-IID training policies. The model was also evaluated at varying canvas resolutions (8x8, 16x16, 32x32, 64x64) to assess generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CanViT-B demonstrated advancements in active computer vision, particularly in performance, efficiency, and generalization.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - **ADE20K Semantic Segmentation:** CanViT-B achieved 45.9% mIoU, significantly outperforming previous active vision models (e.g., AME at 27.6% mIoU and AdaGlimpse at 25.7% mIoU).
 - **ImageNet-1k Classification:** CanViT-B attained 84.5% top-1 accuracy after fine-tuning, surpassing the prior active vision state of the art (e.g., AdaptiveNN at 82.2% and Saccader at 75.0%). With frozen weights, it achieved 81.1% top-1 accuracy.
- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - On ADE20K segmentation, CanViT-B reached 38.5% mIoU with a single low-resolution glimpse, requiring approximately 20 times fewer FLOPs than AME (which achieved 27.6% mIoU).
 - This single-glimpse performance also exceeded that of its FLOP- or input-matched DINOv3 teacher, indicating effective feature learning even in resource-constrained scenarios.
 - The model exhibited an attractive accuracy-efficiency frontier on ADE20K at low FLOP budgets, suggesting practical applicability.
- **Generalization Capabilities:**
 - **Across Policies:** CanViT-B demonstrated robust generalization across various viewing policies (R-IID, F-IID, C2F, F2C, EG-C2F, RFS) and temporal horizons, even with frozen weights. The inference-time-only Coarse-to-Fine (C2F) policy notably outperformed the random policies in both peak accuracy and efficiency on ADE20K. The Entropy-Guided C2F (EG-C2F) policy further improved efficiency on ADE20K by prioritizing uncertain regions.
 - **Across Temporal Horizons:** On ADE20K, accuracy consistently improved up to T=21 glimpses, well beyond the average T≈4 used during pretraining. On ImageNet-1k, accuracy improvements plateaued more quickly, around T=9.
 - **Across Canvas Resolutions:** Despite being pretrained with a 32x32 canvas, evaluating CanViT-B with a finer 64x64 canvas at inference time consistently boosted ADE20K accuracy. This advantage was particularly pronounced for small ground-truth segmentation masks, suggesting improved handling of fine details.
- **Importance of Context and Recurrence:**
 - The Coarse-to-Fine (C2F) policy achieved higher peak accuracies than the Fine-to-Coarse (F2C) policy, despite both eventually providing the same set of viewpoints. This indicates that receiving broad contextual information before fine details is beneficial for building a robust scene understanding.
 - Under the Repeated Full-Scene (RFS) policy, accuracy initially improved purely from recurrent processing, even with a constant input, highlighting the value of iterative canvas refinement.
- **Input-Output Dissociation (Passive Vision Context):** CanViT's dual-stream design allows for decoupling glimpse (input) resolution from canvas (output) resolution. In a single-timestep (passive) setting, a frozen CanViT-B processing a low-resolution full-scene glimpse could outperform an input-matched DINOv3 teacher when utilizing a finer canvas for segmentation.
- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - Reducing canvas embedding dimension significantly impacted reconstruction quality.
 - Frequent and bidirectional Canvas Attention (Read/Write operations) proved beneficial, underscoring the importance of information flow between the glimpse and canvas streams.
 - Dense (patch-level) supervision was found to contribute positively to CLS-level reconstruction, suggesting a synergistic relationship between the two objectives.
 - The inclusion of F-IID rollouts (starting with a full-scene glimpse) during pretraining was crucial for effective learning and convergence.
 - Even minimal Backpropagation Through Time (K=2 glimpses) contributed meaningfully to learning.
 - The Viewpoint Encoding (VPE) token showed a minor impact on the current reconstruction task, consistent with its intended role as an architectural affordance for future policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of CanViT marks a significant step in the development of active computer vision, particularly in applying the foundation model paradigm to this domain. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Active Computer Vision:** CanViT substantially narrows the performance gap between passive and active computer vision models. By achieving new state-of-the-art results on both dense prediction (ADE20K segmentation) and classification (ImageNet-1k) benchmarks, it demonstrates that active vision models can now compete with, and in some efficiency-focused scenarios, surpass passive models.
- **Pioneering Active-Vision Foundation Models (AVFMs):** CanViT represents the first task- and policy-agnostic AVFM. This general-purpose capability means the model can be utilized "as-is" across diverse downstream tasks and viewing policies without extensive re-training, a characteristic central to the foundation model concept. This reduces the development overhead for new active vision applications.
- **Simplified Pretraining Paradigm:** The proposed policy-agnostic passive-to-active dense latent distillation scheme offers a more straightforward pretraining approach compared to complex Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipelines typically employed in active vision. This simplification, coupled with highly informative learning signals from a robust teacher (DINOv3), enables efficient and effective learning of active visual processing.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** CanViT's architecture, particularly the Canvas Attention mechanism with asymmetric projections and the decoupling of thinking (backbone) from memory (canvas), facilitates fast sequential inference and scalability to high output resolutions. This efficiency is crucial for deploying active vision in resource-constrained environments or applications requiring real-time processing.
- **Improved Scene Understanding and Memory:** The canvas, acting as a persistent, scene-wide spatio-semantic memory, allows the model to incrementally build and refine its understanding of a scene across sequential glimpses. This mechanism enables contextually informed processing and supports high-fidelity dense predictions, addressing a long-standing limitation of many active vision models.
- **Generalization Across Diverse Operating Conditions:** The model's ability to generalize to longer rollouts, larger scenes, and novel inference-time policies (e.g., Coarse-to-Fine, Entropy-Guided) without retraining demonstrates its robustness and adaptability, making it suitable for a wider range of real-world scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - **Dynamic Environments:** CanViT's constant-memory recurrent design and efficiency make it a promising candidate for adaptation to real-time video processing and embodied active perception, where dynamic scenes and continuous interaction are paramount.
 - **Goal-Directed Active Vision:** While policy-agnostic currently, the architecture is well-suited for integration with RL-based policy learning for goal-directed tasks like visual search, where optimal viewpoint selection is critical.
 - **Broader Task Applicability:** The model is expected to be directly applicable to other dense prediction tasks such as monocular depth estimation, leveraging its rich spatial representations.
 - **Self-Distillation:** Future work could explore active-to-active self-distillation, removing the reliance on a passive teacher and potentially scaling to even higher resolutions and video data.

**Potential Broader Impact:** The capabilities demonstrated by CanViT could underpin advancements in fields requiring efficient and adaptive visual intelligence, such as robotics, autonomous systems, medical imaging analysis, and environmental monitoring. However, as with many general-purpose computer vision models, its potential application in surveillance and security technologies warrants careful consideration of ethical implications and responsible development practices.
