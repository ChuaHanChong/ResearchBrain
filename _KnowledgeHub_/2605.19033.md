# RLFTSim: Realistic and Controllable Multi-Agent Traffic Simulation via Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> RLFTSim introduces a reinforcement learning framework that fine-tunes pre-trained multi-agent traffic simulators to enhance realism and instill controllable behaviors. The method achieves state-of-the-art realism with a WOSAC RMM of 0.7867 and effectively distills diverse goal-conditioned maneuvers without relying on human feedback.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent traffic simulators, particularly rule-based and open-loop learning-based models, suffer from a 'sim-to-real gap' and accumulate errors in closed-loop deployment, leading to unrealistic behaviors.
- The WOSAC Realism Meta-Metric (RMM) is too sparse and population-based to serve as an effective, sample-efficient reward signal for direct reinforcement learning.
- A lack of robust methods for distilling controllable, goal-conditioned behaviors into multi-agent simulators while maintaining high realism limits their utility for targeted scenario testing in autonomous driving development.

## Method

- **RLFTSim** fine-tunes a pre-trained ==imitation learning== model using reinforcement learning, employing a novel Meta-metric Leave-One-Out (MLOO) reward signal to provide dense, low-variance feedback for realism alignment.
- The framework incorporates Goal-Conditioned Fine-Tuning (GCFT) which integrates goal information via positional encoding or agent token concatenation, enabling the generation of specific maneuvers.
- Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) is utilized to address reward sparsity in goal-reaching tasks, augmenting training data with achieved goals to improve sample efficiency.

## Results

- RLFTSim achieved state-of-the-art realism on the WOSAC private test split with an RMM of **0.7867**, surpassing its SMART-tiny base model (**0.7824**) and other fine-tuning methods.
- The MLOO reward significantly outperformed other reward formulations (e.g., minADE, RLOO) in realism alignment, exhibiting empirically confirmed quadratic variance reduction and greater stability for RL training.
- Goal-Conditioned Fine-Tuning variants improved goal passing rates and enabled the generation of diverse, novel maneuvers (e.g., U-turns, specific turns) while largely preserving realism, with the positional encoding indication for goals maintaining higher realism (**0.7819** RMM with soft goals) compared to direct concatenation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The MLOO reward mechanism provides an unbiased gradient estimator and achieves quadratic variance reduction (O(1/N^2)), significantly improving the stability and sample efficiency of RL training for realism alignment.
> - Distilling controllability into learned traffic simulators is achievable by combining realism-aligned RL fine-tuning with goal-conditioned observations and a mixed reward signal, allowing agents to perform diverse, targeted maneuvers.
> - The framework is model-agnostic, demonstrating its ability to enhance realism and controllability across different base simulator architectures, including discrete-token and continuous-action models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving (AV) development and verification heavily rely on simulation to test and validate AV systems in various scenarios, including rare or hazardous events, thereby reducing the costs and risks associated with real-world testing. Historically, microscopic multi-agent traffic simulation has utilized rule-based models, such as the Intelligent Driver Model. However, these models often exhibit a significant "sim-to-real gap" due to their pre-defined, non-reactive behaviors, limiting their realism in dynamic, closed-loop environments.

More recently, learning-based simulation models have emerged, leveraging large datasets like the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) and transformer architectures to generate reactive multi-agent traffic rollouts. These models are typically trained in an open-loop setting using imitation learning objectives, which predict future agent motions based on observed histories. A recognized limitation of open-loop training is its susceptibility to distribution shifts and error accumulation during closed-loop deployment, leading to unrealistic or causally confused agent behaviors.

To address these challenges, research has explored closed-loop training or post-training methods, including reinforcement learning (RL). Prior RL applications in this domain have faced difficulties such as the absence of explicit, human-like driving reward signals, reliance on costly human feedback for alignment, or sample inefficiency in offline methods.

Additionally, a significant area of development in learned simulators is controllability – the ability to steer simulations to generate specific, targeted scenarios rather than unconstrained traffic patterns. This often involves goal-conditioning agent policies to enable diverse behaviors without compromising realism. Current methods range from target-driven prediction to goal-based planning, but effectively distilling controllable behaviors into multi-agent simulators while maintaining realism remains an active research area. This work contributes to bridging the realism gap in learning-based multi-agent traffic simulation and enhancing its controllability, building upon existing state-of-the-art imitation learning models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the realism of multi-agent traffic simulation models by aligning simulator rollouts with real-world data distributions. A secondary objective is to provide a method for distilling goal-conditioned controllability into scenario generation.

The motivation for these objectives is rooted in several challenges within autonomous driving development:

- **Cost and Feasibility of AV Verification:** Verifying the safety aptitude of an AV requires an immense amount of driving data (e.g., millions of kilometers without fatal accidents). Real-world testing is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, making high-fidelity simulation an essential tool for practical AV development and safety validation, especially for rare accident scenarios.
- **Limitations of Existing Simulation Approaches:**
 - **Rule-based simulators:** Suffer from a "sim-to-real gap" as their agents are not reactive to dynamic environmental changes, leading to unrealistic behaviors in closed-loop settings.
 - **Learning-based, open-loop imitation models:** While offering improved realism over rule-based systems, models trained solely via open-loop imitation learning (e.g., SMART) accumulate errors during closed-loop deployment. This causes distribution shifts and the "causal confusion problem," where agents diverge from realistic trajectories and interact unnaturally.
- **Need for Closed-Loop Alignment:** To overcome the limitations of open-loop training, a closed-loop training or post-training approach is necessary to ensure simulated behaviors adhere to traffic rules and physical constraints, thereby making the simulation outputs more aligned with real-world driving.
- **Challenge of Reward Design for Realism:** The Waymo Open Simulation Challenge (WOSAC) provides a comprehensive Realism Meta-Metric (RMM) for evaluating simulator realism. However, RMM is a sparse, population-based signal (computed over groups of 32 rollouts), which makes it sample-inefficient and problematic for direct use as a reward signal in reinforcement learning. Alternative metrics like Average Distance Error (ADE) are also deemed unsuitable as they fail to provide robust corrective feedback when agents deviate from ground-truth trajectories in stochastic environments.
- **Demand for Controllable Simulations:** Beyond generating generally realistic traffic, AV developers require the ability to control specific aspects of the simulation, such as directing individual agents to perform particular maneuvers or achieve specific goals. This "controllability" is crucial for targeted scenario testing and validating AV responses to desired or challenging situations, moving beyond merely reproducing observed behaviors.
- **Scalability of Alignment Methods:** Existing closed-loop fine-tuning techniques, including those based on human preferences, often face scalability limitations due to the high cost of human annotation or sample inefficiency.

To address these points, the authors propose RLFTSim, a reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning framework that aims to enhance realism and enable controllability efficiently and without relying on explicit human feedback for reward signals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RLFTSim framework is designed to fine-tune a pre-trained imitation learning model using reinforcement learning (RL). It operates in two main modes: goal-free simulation for pure realism alignment and goal-conditioned simulation for achieving controllability while maintaining realism.

#### 3.1. Low-Variance and Dense Reward Signal with Meta-metric Leave-One-Out (MLOO)

The core of RLFTSim's realism alignment is the Meta-metric Leave-One-Out (MLOO) reward signal, which addresses the challenges of using the Waymo Open Simulation Challenge (WOSAC) Realism Meta-Metric (RMM) directly in RL.

- **Challenges with RMM and ADE:** The RMM, while comprehensive, is a sparse, population-based metric computed over a batch of 32 rollouts. This sparsity leads to sample inefficiency in RL training. The Average Distance Error (ADE), an alternative, is considered ineffective for providing corrective supervision once agents diverge from a ground-truth trajectory due to the inherent stochasticity of simulation.
- **MLOO Formulation:** RMM\_MLOO\_i is defined for an individual rollout 'i' within a batch of 'N' rollouts. It calculates the difference between the RMM computed excluding rollout 'j' (RMM\_-j) and the RMM computed excluding rollout 'i' (RMM\_-i), averaged over all 'j'. This formulation provides a dense, per-rollout reward that indicates an individual rollout's relative contribution to the overall realism of the batch.
 - *RMM\_MLOO\_i = (1/N) \* Σ_{j=1 to N} (RMM\_-j - RMM\_-i)*
- **Theoretical Properties:** The authors provide theoretical analyses for MLOO:
 - **Unbiased Gradient Estimation:** Proposition 1 states that the policy-gradient estimator derived from MLOO is an unbiased estimator of the gradient of the expected RMM over N-1 rollouts.
 - **Quadratic Variance Reduction:** Proposition 3 demonstrates that MLOO achieves a quadratic variance reduction (O(1/(N^2 \* T))) compared to the constant variance (O(1/T)) of a simpler Leave-One-Out (RLOO) method, which directly computes variance for single rollouts. This property is critical for stable and sample-efficient RL training. Proposition 2 also characterizes the variance scaling of the RMM itself.
- **RL Training:** The optimization uses the REINFORCE algorithm with RMM\_MLOO\_i as the per-rollout reward. To maintain training stability, a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence regularization term is applied, penalizing significant deviations from the pre-trained reference model's output distribution. An adaptive KL controller manages this regularization.

#### 3.2. Controllability with Goal Conditioning (GCFT)

To enable controllable simulations, RLFTSim employs Goal-Conditioned Fine-Tuning (GCFT), extending the realism-aligned model to respond to externally specified goals.

- **Goal Definition:** Two types of goal criteria are defined:
 - **Hard Target:** Agent's final position must be within 2.0 meters of the goal coordinate.
 - **Soft Target:** Agent must pass within 2.0 meters of the goal coordinate at any point during the rollout.
 - **Goal Polyline:** Contextual map information related to the goal, defined as the map polyline closest to the specified goal coordinate.
- **Goal Representation:** Two methods for integrating goal information into the agent's observation are explored:
 - **Agent Token Embedding Concatenation (cat):** Directly appends continuous goal coordinates to the agent's state vector.
 - **Positional Encoding Indication (ind):** Extends the relative positional encoding between agents and road tokens with a binary indicator, signaling if a road token is part of the goal polyline. This method is noted for potentially easier learning due to its binary nature and enables hybrid simulations where some agents are goal-conditioned while others are not.
- **Hindsight Experience Replay (HER):** To address the sparsity of rewards in goal-reaching tasks, HER is incorporated. In this process (Algorithm 1), multiple rollouts are generated for a given scenario. The "best" rollout (based on RMM) is identified, and the terminal states of its agents are used as *alternative goals*. These alternative goals, which the model actually achieved, are then used to augment the training dataset. When training with these augmented samples, the policy ratio is recomputed based on the achieved alternative goals, improving sample efficiency by providing relevant "successful" experiences.
- **GCFT Reward Design:** For goal-conditioned scenarios, the per-rollout reward combines MLOO with a goal-reaching signal:
 - *R\_GCFT\_i = (1 - λ) \* RMM\_MLOO\_i + λ \* R\_goal\_i*
 - *R\_goal\_i* is a binary reward indicating if an agent successfully reaches its goal. The hyperparameter λ balances the emphasis between realism (RMM\_MLOO) and goal attainment (R\_goal\_i).

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset:** Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) is used for training and evaluation, encompassing training, validation, and test splits with numerous multi-agent scenarios.
- **Base Model:** SMART-tiny, a transformer-based model, is used as the pre-trained imitation learning model.
- **Training Details:** RLFTSim is fine-tuned for a specified number of epochs (e.g., 1 epoch) after the base model's pre-training. Specific hyperparameters for learning rate, KL divergence target, number of rollouts per update, and batch size are defined (Appendix B, Table S1).
- **Evaluation:** Main results are based on the WOSAC private test split leaderboard. Ablation studies utilize a subset of the WOMD validation split. Realism evaluation generates 32 rollouts per scenario, consistent with WOSAC protocol. Evaluation metrics focus on specific evaluation agent IDs (ego vehicle and up to 8 other agents).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings regarding realism enhancement and controllability distillation:

#### 4.1. Simulation Realism Benchmarking (RQ1)

- **State-of-the-Art Realism:** RLFTSim achieved state-of-the-art performance on the WOSAC private test split. It recorded an RMM of 0.7867, surpassing its base model, SMART-tiny (0.7824), and other fine-tuning methods like SMART-tiny CAT-K (0.7856). RLFTSim also led in the interactive metric (0.8129).
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Visual examples demonstrated RLFTSim's ability to correct unrealistic behaviors observed in the baseline SMART-tiny model, such as off-road excursions and collisions with other agents or pedestrians, generating lane-following and rule-respecting behaviors.

#### 4.2. Alternative Reward Signals for Realism Enhancement (RQ2)

- **MLOO Superiority:** An ablation study on the validation set showed that directly optimizing the meta-metric with RMM\_MLOO (0.7830 RMM) was more effective for realism alignment than imitation-based rewards like minADE (0.7801 RMM) or heuristic combinations of collision rate, off-road rate, and minADE (0.7803 RMM). RMM\_MLOO also slightly outperformed RMM\_RLOO (0.7821 RMM), another leave-one-out variant.
- **Statistical Significance:** Paired t-tests confirmed that RMM\_MLOO significantly outperformed all other tested reward formulations on a per-scenario basis (Table S4).
- **Empirical Reward Variance:** Empirical validation confirmed MLOO's theoretical variance reduction properties. MLOO exhibited significantly lower reward variance, scaling approximately as 1/N² with the number of rollouts (N), which contrasts with RLOO's variance, which did not scale effectively with N and plateaued (Figure 2). This indicates MLOO's stability and sample efficiency for RL training.

#### 4.3. Goal-Conditioned Controllability (RQ3 & RQ4)

- **Improved Goal Passing:** All Goal-Conditioned Fine-Tuning (GCFT) variants demonstrated improved goal passing rates compared to the goal-free baseline (Table 3).
- **Goal Representation Impact:** The positional encoding indication (ind) method for goal representation generally maintained higher realism (e.g., 0.7819 RMM with soft goals) compared to direct concatenation (cat) (e.g., 0.7776 RMM with soft goals), while still achieving effective goal reaching.
- **Goal Criterion Impact:** The soft goal criterion (passing within 2.0m of the goal) generally resulted in better passing rates than the hard goal criterion (final position within 2.0m of the goal).
- **Kinematic Controllability:** GCFT models (specifically 'cat, hard' and 'ind, hard' variants) consistently outperformed the baseline SMART-tiny model in reaching goals kinematically perturbed from the ground-truth endpoint across various time horizons (Figure S1d).
- **Maneuver Controllability:** When tested on scenarios with alternative (non-ground-truth) maneuvers, GCFT models significantly outperformed the baseline SMART-tiny. For instance, the 'cat, soft' variant achieved a 75.34% pass rate on alternative maneuvers, compared to 42.89% for the baseline (Table S5). This indicates the model's ability to generalize and generate novel, goal-directed behaviors beyond its pre-training distribution.
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Qualitative examples illustrated GCFT's ability to distill diverse and realistic goal-directed behaviors, such as performing U-turns, left turns, right turns at red lights, or complex parking lot maneuvers for the ego vehicle, which were not consistently generated by the base model (Figure 3b, Figures S6-S8).

#### 4.4. Model Agnosticism

- **Broader Applicability:** RLFTSim's applicability was demonstrated by fine-tuning a different base model, TrafficBots V1.5, which is a continuous-action, VAE-based architecture. This fine-tuning improved TrafficBots V1.5's RMM from 0.7174 to 0.7231, suggesting the framework's versatility beyond discrete-token models (Table S6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLFTSim framework contributes to multi-agent traffic simulation research in several ways, with potential impacts on autonomous driving development and related fields:

- **Enhanced Realism for AV Development:** By achieving state-of-the-art realism in multi-agent traffic simulation, RLFTSim provides a more trustworthy virtual environment for testing and developing autonomous vehicles. This enhanced fidelity is crucial for reducing the "sim-to-real gap," making simulated test results more predictive of real-world performance, especially for complex and rare safety-critical scenarios that are difficult to encounter in reality.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency in RL Training:** The introduction of the Meta-metric Leave-One-Out (MLOO) reward signal represents a methodological advancement in reinforcement learning for simulation. Its low-variance and dense nature enables significantly more sample-efficient training compared to directly using sparse, population-based metrics like RMM. This efficiency is vital for the scalability of RL-based fine-tuning in large-scale simulation environments, potentially reducing computational costs and training time.
- **Distilled Controllability for Targeted Testing:** RLFTSim's ability to distill goal-conditioned controllability into simulation models allows researchers and developers to generate specific, user-defined scenarios. This moves beyond passive realism to active scenario generation, which is invaluable for:
 - **Safety Validation:** Systematically testing AV responses to particular hazards, edge cases, or desired maneuvers.
 - **Behavioral Analysis:** Investigating how AVs interact with specific, controlled human-like behaviors.
 - **Diverse Scenario Generation:** Creating a broader range of test conditions that might not be readily available in recorded datasets.
- **Generalizability Across Architectures:** The demonstration of RLFTSim's effectiveness on both discrete-token (SMART-tiny) and continuous-action (TrafficBots V1.5) architectures suggests its potential as a general fine-tuning framework for various learned simulation models. This model-agnosticism broadens its applicability across the rapidly evolving landscape of traffic simulation technologies.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work highlights the utility of novel reward formulations for complex, population-based metrics in RL. The MLOO formulation, in particular, has potential for application to other domains where population-based metrics are used. Furthermore, the identified limitations, such as the responsiveness of token-based representations and the need for improved realism metrics beyond RMM saturation, provide clear directions for subsequent research.

In summary, RLFTSim offers a practical and theoretically grounded approach to improve the realism and controllability of multi-agent traffic simulators, which can directly contribute to accelerating the safe and efficient development of autonomous driving systems.
