# Model-Based Reinforcement Learning for Control under Time-Varying Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zürich devised an optimistic Model-Based Reinforcement Learning framework capable of adapting to time-varying system dynamics by strategically managing stale data. This approach establishes sublinear dynamic regret guarantees and demonstrates superior performance over stationary MBRL baselines on continuous control tasks and a real-world RC car.

## Problem

- Current Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) typically assumes stationary system dynamics, which does not hold for real-world scenarios like hardware degradation or changing operating conditions.
- Applying standard MBRL in non-stationary environments leads to biased dynamics models, suboptimal control policies, and inaccurate uncertainty estimates due to the accumulation of outdated historical data.
- Existing non-stationary learning solutions often address simpler contexts like tabular RL or bandit problems, lacking frameworks for continuous state-action spaces and non-linear dynamics.

## Method

- An episodic ==Model-Based Reinforcement Learning== (MBRL) framework was introduced, specifically designed for time-varying dynamics using a ==frequentist variation-budget model== within ==Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces==.
- Two data management strategies, ==Full Resets== (**R-OMBRL**) and ==Sliding Window== (**SW-OMBRL**), were developed to restrict the dynamics model training to recent data, preventing bias from stale information.
- For practical implementation, ==Bayesian Neural Networks== (deep ensembles) were used to model dynamics and uncertainty, with policy optimization adapting an ==optimistic MBRL== approach and incorporating ==soft resets== for model parameters.

## Results

- Theoretical analysis established ==dynamic regret bounds== for the proposed algorithms (**R-OMBRL**, **SW-OMBRL**), demonstrating their ability to achieve sublinear dynamic regret in time-varying environments.
- On continuous control benchmarks (e.g., Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper), **R-OMBRL** and **SW-OMBRL** consistently outperformed stationary MBRL baselines by effectively adapting to evolving dynamics and reducing regret accumulation.
- Real-world experiments using an RC car confirmed the algorithms' practical efficacy, with **R-OMBRL** successfully adapting to decaying throttle conditions during a parking maneuver where stationary methods failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard uncertainty estimates from models like Gaussian Processes are insufficient in non-stationary environments, as accumulating stale data introduces a temporal bias not captured by typical variance.
> - Explicitly restricting the influence of outdated data is crucial for maintaining calibrated uncertainty estimates and achieving sublinear dynamic regret in systems with evolving dynamics.
> - There is a bias-variance trade-off in determining the optimal data buffer size: smaller buffers enable rapid adaptation but offer less data for model training, while larger buffers provide more data but risk increased bias from stale dynamics.
