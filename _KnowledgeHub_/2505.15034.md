# RL Tango: Reinforcing Generator and Verifier Together for Language Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> TANGO introduces a framework that concurrently trains an LLM generator and a generative, process-level verifier using reinforcement learning. This approach enhances the reasoning capabilities of 7B/8B-scale models, achieving an average relative improvement of 25.5% on math benchmarks and a 3.3x increase in training efficiency without requiring process-level annotations.

## Problem

- Current reinforcement learning post-training methods for Large Language Models (LLMs) rely on static or discriminatively trained verifiers, which are susceptible to reward hacking and poor generalization.
- Fixed verifiers act as a bottleneck, capping the maximum potential improvements of the LLM generator.
- Traditional process-level reward models (PRMs) require extensive and costly human-annotated step-level labels, hindering scalability.

## Method

- **TANGO** introduces a unified reinforcement learning framework that jointly trains an LLM generator and a generative, process-level LLM verifier in an interleaved manner, enabling mutual reinforcement.
- The generator is trained using a blended advantage of rule-based outcome-level rewards and step-level rewards sampled from the verifier, with the verifier's influence decaying exponentially over time.
- The verifier is also trained via reinforcement learning, but solely on outcome-level correctness rewards, and employs a class-aware reweighting mechanism to address output class imbalance.

## Results

- TANGO achieves an average relative improvement of **25.5%** on five competition-level math benchmarks and **7.3%** on four challenging out-of-domain reasoning tasks for **7B**/**8B**-scale models.
- The framework improves training efficiency by **3.3x**, reaching the same accuracy as vanilla GRPO in 60 generator steps, compared to GRPO's 200 steps.
- TANGO's verifier achieves state-of-the-art F1 scores on the ProcessBench dataset for **7B**/**8B** models without any process-level annotations, demonstrating its ability to infer accurate step-level correctness from outcome-level signals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-evolutionary training, where a continuously improving generator provides richer data for the verifier, and a better verifier provides more accurate feedback, is crucial for unlocking advanced LLM reasoning.
> - A generative and stochastic verifier that produces natural language step-level feedback is more robust against reward hacking and provides stronger, more informative supervision compared to deterministic, logit-based discriminative verifiers.
> - An RL-trained verifier can learn to infer accurate step-level judgments from only outcome-level correctness rewards, eliminating the need for expensive process-level annotations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "RL Tango" addresses a critical challenge in the advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs): enhancing their reasoning capabilities, especially for complex, multi-step tasks. This work is positioned at the intersection of LLM post-training, reinforcement learning (RL), and reward modeling, offering a novel solution to existing limitations.

**Evolution of LLM Reasoning Enhancement:**
Initially, LLMs demonstrated impressive capabilities in various NLP tasks, but often struggled with complex reasoning. Two primary post-training strategies emerged:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This involves training LLMs to mimic human-curated solutions. While effective for learning specific patterns, SFT is inherently imitative, limiting generalization to novel reasoning paths or out-of-distribution problems. The paper cites studies suggesting SFT "memorizes" while RL "generalizes."
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL frames learning as an active exploration process, allowing models to optimize directly for task success through trial and feedback. This approach has shown stronger generalization and has been pivotal in large-scale industrial deployments, such as OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek R1, for unlocking advanced reasoning capabilities.

**Role of Reward Models (Verifiers) in RL for LLMs:**
In RL post-training for LLMs, a "verifier" (or reward model) is crucial. It assesses the quality of generated outputs and provides feedback to guide the LLM generator's policy updates. Reward models are broadly categorized:
- **Outcome Reward Models (ORMs):** These assign a single scalar reward to the final output, providing sparse supervision. They are typically discriminative and trained via SFT on labeled pairs (preferred vs. rejected).
- **Process Reward Models (PRMs):** These provide fine-grained, step-level feedback throughout the generation trajectory, enabling more precise credit assignment and improved training efficiency.

**Limitations of Current Reward Modeling Paradigms:**
A significant limitation across existing RL post-training approaches is their reliance on static or passively trained verifiers:
- **Fixed Verifiers:** Many methods use rule-based metrics or frozen pre-trained reward models. These fixed verifiers restrict the generator's potential improvements and are vulnerable to "reward hacking" when facing distribution changes.
- **Discriminative/SFT-trained Verifiers:** Even online-trained verifiers (like in PRIME) often use discriminative models trained with SFT. SFT-based training limits reasoning capabilities and generalization, akin to the generator's limitations with SFT. Moreover, deterministic, logit-based rewards (common in discriminative PRMs) are susceptible to reward hacking and may not align well with true correctness.
- **Annotation Overhead:** Process-level annotations required for many PRMs are expensive and time-consuming to collect.

**TANGO's Contribution and Niche:**
"RL Tango" directly addresses these limitations by proposing a fundamental shift:
- **Co-evolutionary Training:** Instead of a fixed or passively trained verifier, TANGO concurrently trains *both* the LLM generator and the verifier in an interleaved RL manner. This mutual reinforcement is key, as a continuously improving generator provides richer data for the verifier, and a better verifier provides more accurate feedback for the generator.
- **Generative, Process-Level, RL-Trained Verifier:** This is a central innovation. Unlike previous discriminative or SFT-trained verifiers, TANGO's verifier is:
 - **Generative:** It produces natural language feedback, including step-level assessments and overall judgments, introducing stochasticity that enhances robustness against reward hacking.
 - **Process-Level:** It provides fine-grained, step-by-step verification, offering better credit assignment.
 - **RL-Trained:** It learns its verification policy through RL, allowing it to develop stronger reasoning skills and generalize better, mirroring the benefits of RL for generators.
 - **Annotation-Free (Process-Level):** Crucially, the verifier is trained *solely* on outcome-level verification correctness rewards, eliminating the need for expensive step-level annotations. It learns to infer step-level correctness through the overall outcome.

This places TANGO as a cutting-edge advancement in self-improving AI systems, pushing beyond the current state-of-the-art in LLM post-training by enabling dynamic, robust, and scalable co-learning between generation and verification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind TANGO stems from the observed limitations in current reinforcement learning (RL) post-training methods for Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly their reliance on suboptimal verifiers. The authors identify several key objectives:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Static or Supervised Verifiers:** Current RL approaches for LLMs typically employ verifiers that are either fixed (rule-based or frozen pre-trained models) or discriminatively trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT). These designs suffer from critical drawbacks:
 - **Reward Hacking:** Fixed/SFT verifiers are susceptible to "reward hacking," where the generator learns to exploit flaws in the reward signal rather than improving genuine reasoning. This is particularly problematic in complex domains where optimal reasoning paths are non-trivial.
 - **Poor Generalization:** Verifiers trained discriminatively via SFT tend to generalize poorly beyond their training distributions, limiting the LLM's ability to tackle novel problems or unseen reasoning patterns.
 - **Limited Potential:** A fixed verifier acts as a bottleneck, capping the maximum potential improvement of the LLM generator.

2. **Enable Mutual Reinforcement and Co-evolution:** The authors argue that an ideal system requires both the generator and the verifier to improve synergistically. The objective is to design a framework where:
 - The generator's evolving capabilities expose the verifier to a broader range of reasoning patterns, prompting the verifier to adapt and refine its assessment strategies.
 - The verifier, in turn, provides increasingly accurate and informative feedback, guiding the generator toward more robust and effective reasoning. This creates a "self-reinforcing loop."

3. **Develop a Robust and Generalizable Verifier:** A central goal is to create a verifier that is inherently more robust and generalizable than its predecessors. This is achieved by:
 - **RL-Training:** Training the verifier itself using RL, mirroring the advantages RL offers to generators (active exploration, stronger reasoning skills beyond supervised imitation).
 - **Generative and Stochastic Nature:** Designing the verifier to produce natural language, sampling-based feedback (e.g., "CORRECT" or "INCORRECT" judgments) introduces stochasticity. This generative approach is intended to be more robust against reward hacking compared to deterministic, logit-based discriminative verifiers.
 - **Process-Level Feedback:** The verifier should provide step-level assessments to allow for more precise credit assignment, improving the generator's learning efficiency.

4. **Eliminate the Need for Expensive Process-Level Annotations:** A significant practical motivation is to reduce the annotation burden. Traditional process reward models (PRMs) require detailed, step-level human annotations, which are costly and time-consuming to obtain at scale. TANGO aims to train its process-level verifier solely using readily available *outcome-level* correctness rewards. The hypothesis is that through RL, the verifier can progressively refine its chain-of-thought verification reasoning to align step-level feedback with final correctness outcomes.

5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Reasoning Performance on Challenging Tasks:** Ultimately, the overarching objective is to significantly enhance the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, particularly on competition-level mathematical problems and challenging out-of-domain reasoning tasks, using smaller-scale models (7B/8B parameters). This demonstrates the framework's effectiveness and potential to make advanced reasoning more accessible.

By addressing these motivations, TANGO aims to build a more effective, robust, and scalable co-evolutionary system for LLM reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TANGO proposes a novel, unified RL-based framework that jointly trains an LLM generator ($\pi_g$) and an LLM verifier ($\pi_v$) in an interleaved manner. This creates a self-reinforcing loop where both components continuously improve each other.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework and Interleaved Training

The core idea is to alternate training of the generator and the verifier. The generator policy ($\pi_g$) is updated for $N_g$ steps, followed by the verifier policy ($\pi_v$) for $N_v$ steps, repeating this cycle iteratively. The authors specify using $N_g=3$ and $N_v=1$ to compensate for the generator's slower optimization, indicating a practical consideration for training dynamics.

#### 3.2. RL-based LLM Generator Training

The generator's role is to produce multi-step reasoning trajectories (solutions) $o_g$ given a question $x$. Its learning is guided by a combination of two types of rewards:

- **Rule-based Outcome-Level Rewards ($r_{g,out}$):** This is a binary reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) determined by comparing the extracted final answer $\hat{y}$ from $o_g$ with the gold answer $y$. This provides the ultimate ground truth for task success.

- **Step-Level Rewards from the Verifier ($R_{g,step}$):** This is the innovative component.
 - The verifier $\pi_v$ is queried with the question $x$ and the generator's solution $o_g$.
 - It samples a natural language verification response $o_v$, which contains $K$ step-wise judgments (e.g., "CORRECT" or "INCORRECT").
 - These judgments are converted into numerical rewards ($y_{step,k} \in \{-1, 1\}$) and normalized by $K$ (i.e., $\frac{y_{step,k}}{K}$). This normalization prevents the generator from being biased towards producing longer solutions.
 - This constitutes a *generative, process-level* verifier that provides stochastic, natural language-based step-wise evaluations.

**Advantage Computation for the Generator:**
TANGO computes advantages separately for outcome-level and step-level rewards before blending them. This is a crucial design choice for stability:
- **Outcome-Level Advantages ($\hat{A}^i_{g,out,t}$):** Calculated by normalizing the outcome reward of each generated solution $o^i_g$ within a batch against the mean and standard deviation of all outcome rewards in that batch. This provides a group-normalized comparative signal for final correctness.
- **Step-Level Advantages ($\hat{A}^i_{g,step,t}$):** Calculated by normalizing all individual step rewards across all responses in the batch. The advantage for a given token $t$ is the sum of normalized rewards of all subsequent steps. This offers fine-grained feedback for intermediate reasoning.
- **Blended Advantage ($\hat{A}^i_{g,t}$):** The final advantage is a weighted sum of outcome and step-level advantages: $\hat{A}^i_{g,t} = (1 - \alpha) \hat{A}^i_{g,out,t} + \alpha \hat{A}^i_{g,step,t}$.
 - **Exponential decay for $\alpha$:** The weighting hyperparameter $\alpha$ follows an exponential decay schedule. Initially, step-level supervision (from the verifier) has a stronger influence to encourage exploration of reasoning strategies. As training progresses, $\alpha$ is reduced, gradually shifting focus to the more reliable (gold) outcome-level rewards and mitigating the risk of reward hacking from a potentially imperfect verifier.
 - **Separate Normalization:** The authors highlight that normalizing advantages separately for step and outcome rewards *before* blending is critical for stable learning. Merging rewards first could distort their relative contributions due to scale mismatches.

#### 3.3. RL-based LLM Verifier Training

The verifier's task is to assess the correctness of the generator's solutions. It is trained without any process-level supervision (i.e., no human-annotated step-level labels).

- **Verifier Output:** The verifier generates a verification response $o_v$ that includes step-wise judgments and a final judgment ($y_{final} \in \{0, 1\}$).
- **Reward Design for the Verifier ($r_{v,out}$):** The verifier is rewarded based on two factors:
 - **Correctness of Final Judgment ($r_{v,correct}$):** A binary reward (1 if $y_{final}$ matches the gold correctness of $o_g$, 0 otherwise). This is the primary signal.
 - **Format Score ($r_{v,format}$):** A penalty-based reward (default 1.0, reduced for unmet formatting criteria like missing step justifications or inconsistent step numbering). A hyperparameter $\gamma$ controls its contribution.
 - This means the verifier learns to provide *both* accurate final judgments *and* well-structured step-level explanations, even though it only sees outcome-level truth during its own training.

**Addressing Class Imbalance in Verifier Training:**
A key challenge identified is class imbalance. In early training, the generator produces many incorrect solutions, leading to biased negative supervision for the verifier. This can cause the verifier to collapse to always predicting "incorrect."
- **Class-aware Reweighting:** To counteract this, TANGO introduces a sample-specific scaling factor ($s_+, s_-$) in the verifier's advantage computation. These coefficients are inversely proportional to the square root of the number of correct ($r_{g,out}=1$) and incorrect ($r_{g,out}=0$) generator outputs, respectively. This effectively amplifies the contribution of the less frequent correct samples, ensuring balanced learning. Exponential Moving Averages (EMA) are used for smooth updates of these coefficients.

#### 3.4. Base Models and Implementation Details

- **Generator Base Model:** Qwen2.5-Math-7B (chosen for its mathematical reasoning strengths).
- **Verifier Base Model:** Qwen2.5-7B (chosen for its larger context window to accommodate prompts and generator outputs, notably starting weaker than the generator on math tasks). A Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct version was also tested for generalization.
- **SFT Pre-training:** The generator undergoes SFT using 113K math prompts to learn step-by-step reasoning. The verifier is initialized directly from the base model without SFT.
- **RL Training Data:** 455K math question-answer pairs.
- **Warm-up:** Verifier is warmed up for 40 steps to learn output formatting and achieve reasonable accuracy before guiding the generator.
- **RL Algorithm:** TANGO is compatible with various RL algorithms (GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++); GRPO is used by default.
- **Decoding:** Greedy decoding for evaluation; sampling for rollouts (temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0).
- **Hyperparameters:** Specific learning rates (e.g., $1 \times 10^{-6}$), batch sizes (256, microbatch 4), KL coefficient ($\beta=0.001$), $\gamma=0.8$. $\alpha$ starts higher (0.1 or 0.5) and decays exponentially to $1 \times 10^{-3}$.

#### 3.5. Benchmarks and Evaluation

- **Generator Evaluation:** Pass@1 accuracy on five competition-level math benchmarks (AIME 2025, AIME 2024, AMC 2023, MATH-500, OlympiadBench) and four challenging out-of-domain reasoning tasks (BoardgameQA, CRUXEval, StrategyQA, TableBench).
- **Verifier Evaluation:** Step-level verification F1 score on ProcessBench, a dataset with annotated reasoning errors for math problems.
- **Ablation Study:** An algorithmic reasoning task (last letter concatenation) with gold step-level labels is used to analyze co-evolution dynamics in detail.

This comprehensive methodology details how TANGO integrates a novel, self-improving verifier into the RL training loop, aiming to enhance both the efficiency and ultimate performance of LLM reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that TANGO significantly advances the state-of-the-art for LLM reasoning, particularly for 7B/8B-scale models, through its co-evolving generator-verifier framework.

#### 4.1. Generator Performance and Efficiency

- **Consistent Improvements Across RL Algorithms:** TANGO consistently and substantially improves generator performance when integrated with various vanilla RL algorithms (GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++).
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** Achieves an average relative improvement of 25.5% on five competition-level math benchmarks. For instance, TANGO with GRPO shows remarkable relative gains: 50.4% on AIME 2024, 100.0% on AIME 2025, and 30.0% on AMC 2023.
 - **Out-of-Domain Reasoning:** Delivers an average relative improvement of 7.3% on four challenging out-of-domain reasoning tasks (e.g., logical, code, commonsense, tabular reasoning), highlighting enhanced generalization.
 - Similar trends are observed with RLOO and REINFORCE++, often surpassing GRPO's gains.

- **Enhanced Training Efficiency:** TANGO achieves the same accuracy as vanilla GRPO (after 200 generator steps) in just 60 steps, representing a **3.3x improvement in training efficiency**. Moreover, at 200 generator steps, TANGO's generator achieves 9.1% higher relative accuracy, indicating both faster learning and stronger final performance.

- **Superiority over Existing Reward Model Baselines:**
 - TANGO significantly outperforms both Outcome Reward Model (ORM) and Process Reward Model (PRM) baselines, including PRIME (the only prior work jointly training generator and verifier). This underscores the advantages of TANGO's generative, RL-trained verifier and its co-evolving design. The stochastic, natural language-based feedback is more robust against reward hacking and provides stronger, more informative supervision.

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) for Small Models:** Among 7B/8B-scale reasoning LLMs, TANGO-Qwen-7B establishes new state-of-the-art performance:
 - **Math Tasks:** Averages 49.5% accuracy, with particularly strong scores on difficult competitions like AIME 2024 (26.7%), AIME 2025 (23.3%), and AMC 2023 (70.0%).
 - **Out-of-Domain Tasks:** Averages 62.8% accuracy.
 - These results position TANGO as a leading approach for smaller models, often rivaling or exceeding larger models in specific challenging domains.

#### 4.2. Verifier Performance

- **Consistent Improvement During Training:** The verifier's final verification F1 score steadily improves throughout the training process, as visualized in Figure 1. This confirms that the verifier benefits significantly from the co-evolutionary loop, progressively learning to provide more accurate feedback.

- **State-of-the-Art on ProcessBench (Without Process Labels):** TANGO's verifier achieves state-of-the-art results among 7B/8B-scale models on ProcessBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate step-level verification accuracy for mathematical reasoning.
 - This is a remarkable achievement, given that TANGO's verifier is trained *without any process-level annotations*.
 - It notably excels on the most challenging subsets, OlympiadBench and Omni-MATH, even outperforming the much larger Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct model, despite being initialized from a weaker Qwen2.5-7B base model. This indicates the RL-trained generative verifier effectively learns to infer accurate step-level judgments from outcome-level signals.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis with Gold Step-Level Information (Algorithmic Reasoning)

The ablation study on an algorithmic reasoning task (last letter concatenation), which provides gold step-level labels, provides critical insights into the co-evolution dynamics:

- **TANGO (Joint Training):** Confirms that when both generator and verifier are jointly updated, the generator achieves the best accuracy, and the verifier steadily improves in both step-level and outcome-level F1 scores. This directly validates the hypothesis of mutual reinforcement and the verifier's ability to learn step-level accuracy from outcome-level rewards.

- **Fixed Generator:** If the generator is kept static, the verifier's progress plateaus quickly. This highlights that a continuously improving, diverse generator output distribution is essential for the verifier to continue learning and refining its verification strategies.

- **Fixed Verifier:** A static verifier hinders the generator's learning. Initially, the generator's performance remains flat due to inaccurate step-level feedback from the fixed verifier. While the generator eventually improves as the $\alpha$ schedule shifts focus to gold outcome-level rewards, its final accuracy still lags behind the baseline. This demonstrates that an *evolving* and *accurate* verifier providing informative step-level guidance is crucial, especially in the early stages of training where exploration is key.

In summary, the findings robustly support TANGO's design, showcasing its ability to drive significant improvements in both generator reasoning capabilities and verifier accuracy through a synergistic, interleaved RL training process, all while reducing the reliance on costly step-level annotations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TANGO represents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) post-training, with several key significances and potential impacts:

1. **Novel Paradigm for LLM Post-Training:** TANGO is the first framework to successfully use Reinforcement Learning (RL) for concurrently training both an LLM generator and a *generative, process-level* LLM verifier. This co-evolutionary, RL-on-RL approach moves beyond static or discriminatively SFT-trained reward models, fundamentally changing how LLMs can learn and self-improve.

2. **Addressing Core Limitations of Prior RL-based LLMs:**
 - **Mitigation of Reward Hacking:** By employing an RL-trained, generative, and stochastic verifier, TANGO provides more robust reward signals, reducing the generator's ability to exploit simplistic reward functions. This leads to more genuine skill acquisition rather than superficial optimization.
 - **Enhanced Generalization:** Both the RL-trained generator and verifier demonstrate superior generalization capabilities to out-of-domain and challenging reasoning tasks, surpassing previous methods. This is crucial for deploying LLMs in diverse and unpredictable real-world scenarios.

3. **Efficiency and Scalability:**
 - **3.3x Training Efficiency:** The demonstrated 3.3x speed-up in training efficiency compared to vanilla RL methods is highly impactful. It means that advanced reasoning capabilities can be achieved with fewer computational resources and in less time, making cutting-edge LLM development more accessible.
 - **Reduced Annotation Burden:** Training the process-level verifier solely on outcome-level correctness rewards, without requiring costly and labor-intensive step-level annotations, is a major breakthrough for scalability. This significantly lowers the barrier to developing and deploying sophisticated reasoning LLMs across various domains.

4. **Advancement of Smaller LLM Capabilities:** TANGO enables 7B/8B-scale models to achieve state-of-the-art performance on highly challenging tasks (e.g., AIME competition math problems), often outperforming larger, more resource-intensive models or previous methods in their scale category. This suggests a pathway for smaller, more efficient LLMs to handle complex reasoning, making them more practical for edge computing, specialized applications, and environments with limited resources.

5. **Towards More Autonomous and Self-Improving AI:** The mutual reinforcement mechanism within TANGO offers a glimpse into how AI systems can become more autonomous. The generator provides increasingly diverse and complex reasoning traces, which then pushes the verifier to learn more sophisticated evaluation strategies, and vice-versa. This self-improvement loop is a step towards more general and robust AI.

6. **Transparency and Interpretability:** The generative nature of TANGO's verifier, producing natural language step-by-step judgments, can potentially lead to more transparent and interpretable AI systems. Users or developers can inspect the verifier's reasoning process and pinpoint where an LLM's solution went wrong, facilitating debugging and trust.

7. **Broad Applicability:** While demonstrated primarily on mathematical reasoning and general out-of-domain tasks, the TANGO framework is conceptual and algorithmically flexible. It can be applied to other multi-step reasoning domains such as code generation, scientific problem-solving, or complex decision-making, where step-level feedback is valuable but hard to obtain manually.

In conclusion, "RL Tango" introduces a robust and efficient framework that not only pushes the boundaries of LLM reasoning capabilities but also addresses fundamental challenges in reward modeling and training scalability. Its potential impact lies in fostering the development of more intelligent, generalizable, and resource-efficient LLMs that can tackle complex problems with unprecedented accuracy and transparency.
