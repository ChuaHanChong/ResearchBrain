# PRIMAL: Physically Reactive and Interactive Motor Model for Avatar Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meshcapade and the Max Planck Institute developed PRIMAL, a generative motor system that enables 3D avatars to produce perpetual, realistic, and responsive motions in real-time. This model outperforms existing baselines in motion realism and responsiveness, adapting efficiently to new tasks with limited data and demonstrating effective real-time control in interactive environments.

## Problem

- Most state-of-the-art human motion generation methods produce fixed, non-interactive sequences, lacking real-time responsiveness to dynamic inputs.
- Achieving physical plausibility and natural reactions in avatars often requires complex physics simulations or results in less naturalistic motion compared to data-driven approaches.
- Developing a generalizable, low-level motor system for avatars that can efficiently adapt to diverse, high-level tasks remains a challenge.

## Method

- **PRIMAL** introduces a two-stage approach: a pretraining stage for a generative motor control system and an adaptation stage for high-level behaviors.
- The pretraining uses an autoregressive Transformer-based diffusion model, learning from short 0.5-second unlabeled motion segments conditioned on a single initial state.
- It employs a novel ControlNet-like adaptor for efficient fine-tuning, allowing few-shot semantic action generation and spatial target reaching while keeping the base model frozen.

## Results

- PRIMAL-InContext consistently demonstrates superior motion realism, exhibiting lower Averaged Skating Ratio (ASR) and higher Averaged Non-Collision Ratio (ANCR), and was visually preferred in perceptual studies.
- The model shows enhanced responsiveness to induced impulses, generating natural reactions like kicks or backflips, and effectively controls avatar movement and direction in real-time via Classifier-based Guidance.
- A ControlNet-like adaptor facilitates efficient few-shot adaptation, enabling the learning of personalized semantic actions from as little as 3 minutes of data and precise spatial target reaching.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling human motion as short, 0.5-second segments, conditioned solely on a single initial frame, effectively captures low-level dynamics and avoids overfitting to semantic movements.
> - Perceptually realistic physical effects and responsiveness can be implicitly learned by a generative diffusion model from motion data through kinematic loss functions, without explicit physics simulation.
> - A specialized ControlNet-like adaptor allows for efficient, few-shot adaptation of a pretrained motor system to new tasks like personalized actions or spatial navigation, leveraging a frozen base model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D human motion generation has seen rapid advancements, primarily driven by large motion capture (mocap) datasets and powerful generative models, particularly diffusion models. Existing methods often focus on "offline" generation, conditioned on inputs like text, audio, or scene information, producing highly realistic but pre-scripted motions. However, a significant gap remains in developing **interactive avatars** that can respond in real-time to arbitrary commands, external forces, and environmental inputs, behaving in a "perpetual, realistic, controllable, and responsive manner."

PRIMAL directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel generative motion model that emphasizes interactivity and reactivity. It distinguishes itself from:

- **Offline Motion Generation:** Most text-to-motion or scene-conditioned models (e.g., MDM, OmniControl, MotionLCM) generate fixed-length sequences and are not designed for continuous, real-time interaction or dynamic response to unexpected perturbations. While some, like OMG, use pretrain-finetune strategies, their pretraining focuses on much longer motion segments (up to 300 frames) compared to PRIMAL's short (0.5-second) segments, limiting their real-time reactivity.
- **Physics-Based Motion Generation:** Methods leveraging physical simulation (e.g., PACER++, PhysDiff, InsActor) aim for physical plausibility and can naturally generalize to external forces. However, they often struggle to produce motions as naturalistic as data-driven diffusion models and typically learn policies for a narrow set of motor skills rather than a generic human motion control system. PRIMAL notably achieves **perceptually realistic physical effects without explicit physics simulation**, by learning implicitly from data.
- **Autoregressive Unbounded Motion Generation:** While autoregressive models (e.g., GAMMA, WANDR, DIMOS, CAMDM, DART) are designed for continuous motion, PRIMAL's formulation is unique. Unlike many existing autoregressive diffusion models that predict *N* future frames based on *M* past frames, PRIMAL predicts a short (0.5-second) segment based solely on a **single-frame initial state** (joint positions and velocities). This crucial distinction is argued to prevent overfitting to semantics in limited mocap data, improve generalization, and enhance responsiveness to dynamic changes. It simplifies the training pipeline by eliminating the need for motion tokenizers or scheduled sampling, which are common in other approaches.

In essence, PRIMAL seeks to bridge the gap between the realism of data-driven generative models and the interactivity/physical plausibility often associated with physics-based simulations, positioning itself as a foundational "motor system" for truly interactive digital characters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of PRIMAL is to develop a **generative motor system for interactive avatars** that can exhibit perpetual, realistic, controllable, and responsive motion in 3D space. The authors are motivated by several limitations of existing human motion generation methods:

- **Lack of Interactivity:** Current state-of-the-art motion generation, while realistic, typically produces fixed, non-interactive sequences. Real humans continuously react to commands and environmental stimuli. To feel "alive," an autonomous character needs to be continuously online and responsive, moving on its own yet able to adapt to external inputs.
- **Limited Realism and Responsiveness:** Many methods struggle to achieve the responsiveness and subtle physical realism of real human movements. Issues like foot sliding, ground penetration, or unnatural responses to perturbations persist. The goal is to create motions that appear physically plausible even without explicit physics simulation.
- **Generalization and Foundation Model Capabilities:** Existing autoregressive models, especially when trained on limited mocap data, risk overfitting to specific semantic movements, which restricts their ability to serve as a general foundation for diverse behaviors. The authors aim to create a low-level motor system that implicitly models human dynamics, generalizes beyond specific training data, and provides a robust base for building more complex behaviors.
- **Efficient Adaptation to New Tasks:** Training complex motion models from scratch for every new task is computationally expensive and data-intensive. Inspired by foundation models, a key motivation is to enable efficient adaptation of a pretrained base model to specific tasks (e.g., personalized actions, spatial target reaching) using minimal data.
- **Real-time Performance:** For applications in gaming, AR/VR, and interactive agents, the generated motions must be produced in real-time, allowing for immediate feedback and dynamic interaction.

By addressing these motivations, PRIMAL aims to "breathe life" into avatars, making them more suitable for dynamic and immersive virtual environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRIMAL introduces a novel two-stage learning paradigm for its "Physically Reactive and Interactive Motor model for Avatar Learning."

#### 3.1. Motion Representation

Human motion is represented using the **SMPL-X body model** M(β, r, φ, θ), which includes body shape, root translation, and root/joint rotations. The state for each frame includes joint locations (J) and velocities (˙J). A key insight is that human motion is dominated by physics over short time intervals (around 0.5 seconds, or 15 frames at 30fps) and by semantics over longer ones. PRIMAL's core innovation is to model these **short-term (0.5-second) motion segments** by predicting the future motion from a **single-frame initial state** (x₀, including joint locations and velocities). This implies that, over short horizons, the future motion is assumed to be largely independent of the past, simplifying the learning problem and increasing data availability (any 15-frame segment can be used for training). Motions are canonicalized to a body-centric coordinate system.

#### 3.2. Pretraining Stage: Generative Motor Control

The first stage focuses on learning the low-level motor system using an **autoregressive diffusion model**.
- **Architecture:** The denoising network G(t, Xₜ, x₀) is implemented using a **Transformer Encoder**, leveraging "in-context conditioning" where the diffusion time embedding and initial state embedding are added and then concatenated with the noisy motion segment's embedding. This differs from an alternative "Adaptive LayerNorm (AdaLN)" architecture, with "in-context" proving superior.
- **Training:** The model is trained on a vast amount of **unlabeled short mocap segments** from datasets like AMASS in a fully unsupervised manner. This overcomes data limitations that often plague methods relying on annotated or long-sequence data.
 - **Loss Function:** The training loss combines a standard DDPM "simple" term (L_simple) with two forward kinematics terms (L_FK, L_FKV) that penalize discrepancies between predicted and ground truth joint locations and velocities.
 - **Implicit Physics Learning:** Crucially, the model *does not* explicitly model foot-ground contact or use physics simulation. The authors hypothesize that minimizing the L_FKV term implicitly learns contact information and self-stabilization.
- **Inference:** During inference, given an initial state, the pretrained model generates a 0.5-second motion segment. This process is then **repeated autoregressively** to produce arbitrarily long motion sequences.
 - **Test-time Processing:** To further refine motion quality and address potential artifacts, three efficient post-processing steps are applied: **joint re-projection** (to keep joints aligned with the SMPL-X body), **snapping to the ground** (to prevent floating or penetration), and **inertialization blending** (to smooth transitions between segments).
 - **Real-time Control with Induced Impulses:** The model’s inherent responsiveness allows for real-time interaction by simply perturbing the initial joint velocities. For example, an upward impulse to leg joints can induce a kick, demonstrating physics-like behavior without explicit simulation.
 - **Real-time Control with Classifier-based Guidance (CBG):** To control movement speed and direction, the authors propose two intuitive CBG loss terms (L_move, L_facing). These guide the reverse diffusion process by computing analytical gradients of the objective functions, enabling efficient real-time control of the avatar's trajectory.

#### 3.3. Adaptation Stage: High-Level Behavior Generation

The pretrained model serves as a "base model" for efficient task-specific adaptation, reducing the need for expensive training from scratch.
- **ControlNet-like Adaptor:** A novel **ControlNet-like adaptor** is employed. Unlike some existing ControlNet applications that input the control signal once, PRIMAL's adaptor adds the control embedding to individual blocks of the transformer, similar to OMG. This architecture allows the base model parameters to remain frozen while only updating the adaptor's parameters, making adaptation efficient.
- **Few-shot Semantic Action Generation:** The adaptor can be fine-tuned with a small dataset (e.g., 3 minutes of cellphone mocap data) of personalized actions with labels. The control signal `y` is an action label, mapped to a latent vector. This enables generating infinite personalized movements and natural transitions between actions, controllable via classifier-free guidance (CFG).
- **Spatial Target Reaching:** For spatial control, the control signal `y` is a 2D target location. The adaptor learns to generate motions that reach the specified target, demonstrating continuous spatial control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PRIMAL was rigorously evaluated against state-of-the-art baselines, demonstrating superior performance in motion realism, responsiveness, and adaptability.

#### 4.1. Evaluations on Body Motion Learning (Motion Realism and Responsiveness)

- **Motion Realism:**
 - **Metrics:** Evaluated using Averaged Skating Ratio (ASR), Averaged Non-Collision Ratio (ANCR), Averaged Neural Riemannian Distance (ARD), and Baseline Preference Ratio (BPR) from perceptual studies.
 - **Findings:** PRIMAL-InContext consistently **outperformed data-driven baselines** (DiP, CLoSD, MotionLCM, and re-implemented DART/DiP versions) across most metrics. It achieved a lower ASR (less foot skating), high ANCR (no ground collisions), and a low ARD (more natural poses).
 - **Perceptual Study:** In perceptual studies, PRIMAL-InContext was **visually preferred** over all baselines (low BPR), indicating higher perceived realism of generated motions despite not using explicit physics simulation or foot contact labels.
 - **Architecture Comparison:** The "InContext" architecture was found to be superior to "AdaLN" in terms of motion realism and runtime.
- **Motion Responsiveness (Reactions to Induced Impulses):**
 - **Motivation:** To test if the avatar feels "alive" and can react to external inputs, perturbations were introduced by changing joint velocities.
 - **Findings:** PRIMAL-InContext demonstrated **superior realism and responsiveness** compared to baselines when reacting to induced impulses (e.g., generating kicks, runs, backflips, forward rolls). The generated reactions were described as prompt and natural, generalizing to unseen disruptions. Quantitative metrics (ASR, ANCR, ARD) and qualitative observations (visualizations in Figure 5 and Supplemental Video) supported this.
- **CBG-based Control:**
 - **Findings:** PRIMAL-InContext **significantly outperformed baselines** in controlling movement direction and speed via Classifier-based Guidance, achieving lower L2 distances to target velocities and directions, while maintaining motion realism.

#### 4.2. Evaluations on Model Adaptation

- **Few-shot Semantic Action Generation:**
 - **Context:** Adapted PRIMAL-InContext using a tiny personalized mocap dataset (3 minutes, 5 stylized actions captured by MoCapade3.0).
 - **Findings:** The ControlNet-like adaptor proved **highly effective**. It allowed the model to reproduce personalized actions and generate natural transitions, maintaining the base model's controllability and responsiveness. PRIMAL's adaptor achieved competitive R-precision (classification accuracy for action labels) and better ASR compared to direct finetuning and OmniControlNet, demonstrating efficient and effective learning from limited data.
- **Spatial Target Reaching:**
 - **Findings:** All PRIMAL-based adaptation methods (finetuning, OmniControlNet, and the proposed adaptor) were effective. The proposed ControlNet adaptor led to the **smallest distance error** to the target locations, indicating more precise spatial control, albeit with a slight trade-off in ASR compared to OmniControlNet.

#### 4.3. Practical Demonstration

The effectiveness of PRIMAL was showcased in a **real-time character animation system built in Unreal Engine**, where the avatar exhibited perpetual motion, responded dynamically to controller commands, and reacted naturally to external forces (e.g., "pulling" with a "magnet" or impulsive "zaps"). This demonstrates the practical applicability and immersive potential of the system.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PRIMAL represents a significant step forward in the creation of interactive and believable digital avatars, with substantial implications across various domains:

- **Foundational Motor System for AI Agents:** By formulating the motor system as a generative model learned from short, unlabeled motion segments, PRIMAL provides a robust and generalizable "motor system" that implicitly captures the dynamics of human motion. This approach, akin to foundation models, allows for efficient adaptation to diverse tasks and behaviors, laying the groundwork for more sophisticated and intelligent AI agents.
- **Enhanced Realism and Interactivity:** The ability to generate perpetual, realistic, controllable, and *responsive* motions in real-time sets PRIMAL apart. Avatars can react dynamically to external impulses and commands, making them feel genuinely "alive" rather than being driven by pre-scripted animations. This greatly enhances user immersion and engagement in virtual environments.
- **Data-Driven Physical Plausibility:** Achieving perceptually physical realism without explicit physics simulation is a notable contribution. This simplifies the development pipeline by removing the complexities and computational costs associated with physics engines, while still producing high-fidelity, natural-looking movements, including plausible ground contact.
- **Efficient Adaptation and Personalization:** The ControlNet-like adaptation mechanism allows for rapid and efficient fine-tuning of the base model to specific tasks (e.g., personalized action styles, spatial navigation) with minimal new data. This is particularly valuable for content creators and developers, enabling the creation of unique, expressive avatars tailored to specific needs or users.
- **Broad Application Potential:** The interactive nature and real-time performance of PRIMAL make it highly suitable for:
 - **Gaming:** Replacing traditional motion graphs and motion matching with dynamically generated, responsive animations for more realistic and fluid character control.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR) / Virtual Reality (VR):** Creating more immersive and believable virtual human interactions.
 - **Controllable Video Generation:** Producing dynamic and interactive human motion content for various media.
 - **Interactive Agents:** Building autonomous agents that can navigate and interact with virtual worlds in a natural and responsive manner.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also highlight avenues for future research:
- **Occasional Non-Physical Motions:** While generally realistic, some non-physical motions can occur (e.g., "flying" up after a fall), suggesting that more diverse training data for specific edge cases would be beneficial.
- **Limited Control Inputs:** Currently, control is primarily impulse-based or guided by basic speed/direction. Expanding control to more natural inputs like text or speech, possibly by integrating text-to-motion features into the ControlNet adaptor, is a promising direction.
- **Lack of Environmental Awareness:** The agent lacks awareness of its environment and obstacles, meaning it cannot currently plan paths or avoid collisions. Conditioning the model on scene information is a critical future step for truly autonomous agents.
- **Computational Cost:** While running in real-time, the computational cost might still be prohibitive for widespread adoption in certain game engine contexts, suggesting further optimization is needed to replace traditional motion systems entirely.

Overall, PRIMAL offers a robust and adaptable framework for generating highly realistic and interactive human motion, poised to significantly impact the development of intelligent and immersive digital avatars across numerous applications.
