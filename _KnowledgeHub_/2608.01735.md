# DAPD: Dual-Anchored Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Dual-Anchored Policy Distillation (DAPD) resolves information asymmetry in on-policy self-distillation, mitigating "privilege illusion." It consistently improves model performance across various tasks and scales, reducing unsupported claims by 73% compared to standard OPSD, and demonstrating enhanced generalization.

## Problem

- On-policy (self) distillation (OPSD) for large language models (LLMs) often leads to "privilege illusion," where the student model generates unsupported claims at inference time due to training with privileged information not available during deployment.
- This illusion stems from an "information asymmetry" between a teacher model trained with privileged data and a student model that must predict without it.
- Prior mitigation attempts primarily modify the privileged teacher signal, which often fails to reliably disentangle reproducible guidance from privilege-dependent behaviors.

## Method

- Introduces **Dual-Anchored Policy Distillation (DAPD)**, a unified framework to resolve ==information asymmetry== in OPSD.
- Employs ==Dual-Path Anchoring (DPA)== that uses "Self" distributions as trainable bridges and intermediate supervision targets (anchors) to align model behavior under matched information availability.
- Integrates ==Dual-Source Anchoring (DSA)== which applies DPA in both reference-guided and rollout-guided directions, balancing complementary supervision signals.

## Results

- DAPD consistently achieves superior performance, outperforming OPSD by an average of **+2.00 points** on Qwen3-4B and showing robust gains up to **+3.06 points** at the 32B scale.
- The framework effectively mitigates privilege illusion, reducing the number of "wrong claims" or unsupported assertions by **73%** compared to standard OPSD.
- DAPD demonstrates improved generalization capabilities, achieving the best average score in out-of-distribution evaluations and showing potential for effective reference-free distillation using verified dual rollouts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Information asymmetry between a privileged teacher and an unprivileged student is the fundamental cause of "privilege illusion" in OPSD, not merely teacher quality.
> - Introducing intermediate "anchors" that ensure matched information availability during supervision is key to resolving this asymmetry and reliably transferring reproducible guidance.
> - Balancing guidance from both high-quality reference completions and student-reachable on-policy rollouts offers complementary benefits and improves overall learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language model (LLM) post-training, encompassing supervised fine-tuning (SFT), reinforcement learning (RL), and distillation, is critical for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of pretrained models. On-policy (self) distillation (OPSD) has gained prominence within this landscape. Unlike SFT, which relies on fixed demonstrations, or RL, which uses sparse outcome rewards, OPSD trains models on states visited by the current policy and provides dense, token-level supervision from a teacher.

A common practice in OPSD is to strengthen the teacher by conditioning it on privileged information, such as a reference completion or tool output. However, this approach introduces a known challenge referred to as "privilege illusion." During inference, the student model lacks access to this privileged information but may generate outputs that depend on it, leading to unsupported claims or derivations that cannot be reproduced from its available context. This ultimately degrades performance.

Prior research has attempted to address privilege illusion through two main categories: (1) direct use of privileged information, where the teacher's full supervision, conditioned on privileged data, is distilled; and (2) selective transfer from privileged supervision, where the teacher signal is filtered or reweighted to suppress privilege-dependent behavior. The authors contend that these existing methods often fail to reliably separate reproducible guidance from privilege-dependent behavior, suggesting a structural limitation in that they modify the privileged teacher signal rather than addressing the fundamental information mismatch between teacher and student.

This work positions itself by identifying information asymmetry—the discrepancy between a teacher with privileged information and a student without it—as the root cause of privilege illusion in OPSD. It proposes a novel framework, Dual-Anchored Policy Distillation (DAPD), which aims to resolve this asymmetry by introducing intermediate supervision targets (anchors) and balancing multiple guidance sources, rather than solely modifying the teacher signal. This approach represents a shift from signal-level modifications to a more fundamental re-architecture of the distillation process to ensure information alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify and resolve the root cause of privilege illusion in on-policy (self) distillation (OPSD), a phenomenon that degrades the inference-time performance of post-trained language models.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and hypotheses:

1. **Diagnosis of Privilege Illusion**: The authors hypothesize that privilege illusion arises from "information asymmetry"—the mismatch between the teacher's access to privileged information during training and the student's lack of such information during inference. Existing OPSD methods, which distill a teacher conditioned on privileged information into a student that predicts without it, mix useful, reproducible guidance with privilege-dependent behavior. This mixture, termed "Entangled Distillation," results in the student making confident but unsupported predictions.
2. **Validation of Information Asymmetry**: To validate this hypothesis, the authors conducted an intervention where the student's "None" distribution (without privileged information) in the OPSD objective was replaced with a "Self-conditioned" distribution. This "Privileged Anchor" objective ensured that both the teacher ("Cross") and student ("Self") distributions received full completion information, thereby removing the information asymmetry. Experiments showed that this intervention significantly reduced "wrong claims" (unsupported answer assertions) by 45% and improved reasoning performance by +6.22 points, substantiating information asymmetry as a core problem rather than teacher quality alone.
3. **Limitations of Prior Work**: Current approaches to mitigate privilege illusion either directly use privileged supervision or selectively transfer parts of it. The authors argue that these methods are inherently limited because they modify the teacher signal within an asymmetric information setting, rather than addressing the structural mismatch itself. They suggest that these methods struggle to reliably isolate reproducible guidance.
4. **Importance of Guidance Source**: Beyond information mismatch, the authors highlight that the source of guidance matters. Reference completions are typically reliable for correctness but might be "off-policy" (i.e., outside the current student's generation capabilities). Conversely, on-policy rollouts are student-reachable but may be incorrect or suboptimal. Both sources offer complementary reasoning signals, and an effective distillation strategy should leverage both.

Based on these motivations and diagnoses, the research outlines two key requirements for a robust solution:
1. **Matched Information Availability**: Introduce intermediate supervision targets (anchors) to align reference and rollout behavior under conditions where information availability is matched between the supervising and supervised distributions.
2. **Balanced Guidance Sources**: Integrate and balance guidance from both reliable reference completions and student-reachable on-policy rollouts to enhance overall learning.

The overarching objective is to develop DAPD, a unified framework that directly addresses information asymmetry through matched-information anchoring and leverages complementary guidance sources, thereby alleviating privilege illusion and improving the performance and robustness of language models in post-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DAPD (Dual-Anchored Policy Distillation) is a unified framework designed to address information asymmetry and balance guidance sources in on-policy (self) distillation. It consists of two main components: Dual-Path Anchoring (DPA) and Dual-Source Anchoring (DSA).

#### 3.1. Core Distributions and Objectives

The methodology builds upon three fundamental token distributions for a given completion *s* (either a sampled rollout *y* or a reference completion *y***) and an alternative completion *s***:
- **None distribution (p_s_None)**: p_θ(· | x, s_<t>), where the model predicts the next token without any privileged information. This matches the inference-time student's information.
- **Cross distribution (p_s_Cross)**: p_θ(· | x, s_<t>, s***), where the model predicts while conditioned on the other completion *s***. This acts as a privileged teacher.
- **Self distribution (p_s_Self)**: p_θ(· | x, s_<t>, s), where the model predicts while conditioned on the completion *s* itself. This distribution acts as a trainable bridge, as it shares parameters with `None` and is information-matched with `Cross`.

Based on these distributions, DAPD defines three directed objectives:
- **Entangled Distillation (L_s_ent)**: The original OPSD objective, D(sg[p_s_Cross] || p_s_None), where the `Cross` teacher supervises the `None` student.
- **Inference Anchor (L_s_infer)**: D(sg[p_s_None] || p_s_Self), training `Self` towards `None`, providing an anchor to align behavior without privileged information.
- **Privileged Anchor (L_s_priv)**: D(sg[p_s_Cross] || p_s_Self), training `Self` towards `Cross`, directly aligning behavior when both receive privileged information.

All objectives treat the first argument as a detached teacher and the second as a trainable student. The divergence D is instantiated as a component-clipped, full-vocabulary forward KL divergence.

#### 3.2. Dual-Path Anchoring (DPA)

DPA composes these objectives into two complementary alignment paths for an ordered completion direction *s* → *s***:
- **Unconditioned Path (L_s→s_uncond)**: Aims to align the `None` distributions (which are inference-relevant) by combining `Entangled Distillation` on *s* with the `Inference Anchor` on *s***: L_s_ent + L_s_infer. For the rollout-to-reference direction (y → y***), this becomes L_y_ent + L_y*_infer. The `Entangled Distillation` moves p_y_None towards p_y_Cross, while `Inference Anchor` moves p_y*_Self towards p_y*_None. Crucially, p_y*_Self and p_y_Cross share model parameters and similar conditional distributions, serving as a proxy bridge that implicitly aligns p_y_None towards p_y*_None.
- **Privileged Path**: This path directly uses the `Privileged Anchor` objective, L_s_priv. For y → y***, this aligns self-conditioned and reference-conditioned predictions on rollout tokens under matched information availability.

The total DPA objective for one ordered direction is L_s→s_DPA = L_s→s_uncond + L_s_priv.

#### 3.3. Dual-Source Anchoring (DSA)

DSA applies DPA in both completion directions to balance supervision from reference and rollout sources:
- **Rollout-to-Reference (y → y***)**: The rollout (*y*) is the completion, and the reference (*y***) provides correctness-oriented guidance. This direction uses DPA with *s* = *y* and *s*** = *y***.
- **Reference-to-Rollout (y*** → y)**: The reference (*y***) is the completion, and the rollout (*y*) provides student-reachable, on-policy guidance. This direction uses DPA with *s* = *y*** and *s*** = *y*.

The final DAPD objective combines both directional DPA objectives with a weighting coefficient λ: L_DAPD = λ * L_y→y*_DPA + (1 − λ) * L_y*→y_DPA.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Models**: Qwen3 family (1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B, 32B).
- **Datasets**: OpenThoughts data, specifically the math domain for reasoning, code domain for coding, and a mixture of math, code, and science domains for instruct tasks. Evaluation examples were excluded from training.
- **Benchmarks**:
 - **Reasoning**: AIME24, AIME25, HMMT25.
 - **Coding**: LCB v5, BFCL v3.
 - **Instruction Following**: IFBench.
- **Baselines**: Base (original Qwen3 policy), OPSD, SDFT, SDPO (direct privileged supervision), Purified OPSD, DOPD (selective modification of privileged signal).
- **Implementation**:
 - Training used LoRA (rank 64, scale 128) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
 - Optimizer: Learning rate of 5 × 10^-6, linear 500-step schedule, gradient-norm clipping 0.1, bfloat16, gradient checkpointing, effective batch size 32.
 - Rollout Generation: Temperature 1.1, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, max 1024 new tokens.
 - Privileged Information: Reference completion injected into a special "Reference Solution Begin/End" section in the teacher's user message.
 - Divergence: Component-clipped forward KL (c=0.05) with logits divided by T=1.1.
 - Loss coefficients (λ, β) were tuned and varied across scales (e.g., λ=0.2-0.5, β values 0.5-2).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various benchmarks and Qwen3 model scales demonstrate the effectiveness of DAPD in mitigating privilege illusion and enhancing performance.

1. **Overall Performance Improvements**:
 - On Qwen3-4B, DAPD achieved an average performance of 57.34 across six diverse tasks (reasoning, coding, instruction following).
 - This represents a notable improvement of +2.00 points over standard OPSD and outperforming other baselines that directly use or selectively filter privileged supervision (e.g., +1.09 over Purified OPSD, +3.85 over DOPD). These gains suggest that addressing information asymmetry through matched-information anchoring is more effective than merely modifying asymmetric supervision signals.

2. **Scalability and Robustness Across Model Sizes**:
 - While OPSD's performance gains over the Base model largely diminished at larger scales (e.g., +5.19 at 1.7B reducing to +0.28 at 32B), DAPD maintained consistent and significant improvements.
 - DAPD exhibited gains of +2.41 points at 8B, +2.13 points at 14B, and +3.06 points at 32B over the Base model, consistently outperforming OPSD across all tested scales (1.7B to 32B). This indicates that DAPD's approach of preserving useful reasoning signals via matched-information supervision is particularly beneficial as models scale and their rollouts become more capable.

3. **Out-of-Distribution Generalization**:
 - When trained on reasoning data and evaluated on coding and instruction-following tasks (out-of-distribution), DAPD achieved the best average score of 49.64.
 - It showed a significant gain of +4.82 points on LCB v5 (coding) and +1.37 points on average over OPSD in this OOD setting, while remaining competitive on other tasks. This suggests improved robustness and generalization capabilities due to the matched-information objective.

4. **Mitigation of Privilege Illusion**:
 - A behavioral probe, tracking "wrong claims" (unsupported answer assertions), showed that OPSD led to an increase in such claims during training (from 13.0 to 37.0 per 10,000 generations).
 - The "Privileged Anchor" intervention (replacing OPSD's `None` student with `Self`) reduced late-stage wrong claims by 45%.
 - DAPD further reduced late-stage wrong claims by 73% compared to OPSD, demonstrating its efficacy in improving inference-time behavior and reducing the tendency to "hallucinate" information. Qualitative examples (Figure 4) corroborated this, showing OPSD recalling unsupported answers, while DAPD successfully derived correct solutions.

5. **Ablation Studies for Dual-Path Anchoring (DPA)**:
 - Experiments confirmed that both components of DPA (the unconditioned path combining Entangled Distillation with Inference Anchor, and the privileged path using Privileged Anchor) are necessary.
 - Sequential additions showed incremental improvements: Entangled Distillation alone, then adding the Inference Anchor, and finally adding the Privileged Anchor, each step contributing to better performance. This supports the information-asymmetry analysis that both levels of alignment are crucial.

6. **Ablation Studies for Dual-Source Anchoring (DSA)**:
 - Combining both reference-guided and rollout-guided supervision within DSA yielded superior results (65.28 Avg@12) compared to using either source individually (63.89 for reference-only, 65.09 for rollout-only). This validates the premise that reference and rollout guidance provide complementary information, and their balanced combination is most effective.

7. **Sensitivity and Robustness to Hyperparameters**:
 - The optimal balance (λ weight) between reference-guided and rollout-guided supervision was found to be scale-dependent. Smaller models (1.7B) benefited more from reference guidance (λ=0.5), while larger models (4B, 8B, 14B) showed preference for stronger rollout guidance (λ=0.2). This suggests that larger models, with more reliable rollouts, can leverage their own generated trajectories more effectively.
 - Source-specific Privileged Anchor weights also varied by scale, further indicating a dynamic trade-off between reliability (from reference) and student-reachability (from rollout).

8. **Toward Reference-Free DAPD**:
 - Explorations into reference-free settings showed that using two independent on-policy rollouts (dual-rollout variant) as guidance sources still improved performance over OPSD.
 - Further, applying a correctness verifier to one of the rollouts (verified-rollout variant) led to additional performance gains, suggesting that DAPD can operate without curated references, and the quality of the guidance source (even if self-generated and verified) significantly impacts results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of large language model post-training and has the potential to impact the development and deployment of more robust and reliable LLMs.

1. **Fundamental Problem Identification**: The work provides a clear and empirically validated identification of information asymmetry as the root cause of privilege illusion in OPSD. This reframing moves beyond symptom-level mitigation strategies to address a fundamental architectural flaw in how privileged information is traditionally used in distillation, offering a more robust theoretical foundation for future research in this area.

2. **Novel Methodological Framework**: DAPD introduces a novel, unified framework that directly tackles information asymmetry through its Dual-Path Anchoring (DPA) and Dual-Source Anchoring (DSA) components. By constructing explicit alignment paths under matched information availability and balancing guidance from both reference and rollout completions, DAPD offers a principled solution to the privilege illusion problem.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Scalability**: DAPD consistently and significantly outperforms existing OPSD methods across various reasoning, coding, and instruction-following tasks. Crucially, its benefits persist and even become more pronounced at larger model scales, where the gains from traditional OPSD approaches often diminish. This indicates that DAPD provides a scalable solution for improving the capabilities of increasingly powerful language models.

4. **Improved Model Robustness and Generalization**: The demonstrated improvements in out-of-distribution performance suggest that DAPD fosters more robust and generalizable learning. By reducing reliance on privilege-dependent behavior, models trained with DAPD may be better equipped to handle diverse and unseen scenarios, leading to more reliable real-world applications.

5. **Mitigation of Unwanted Behaviors**: By effectively alleviating privilege illusion, DAPD reduces the tendency of LLMs to make unsupported claims or "hallucinate" derivations based on information they do not possess at inference time. This leads to more trustworthy and factually grounded model outputs, which is critical for high-stakes applications.

6. **Pathway to Reference-Free Distillation**: The exploratory results on reference-free DAPD, utilizing dual rollouts and verifier-approved self-generated content, open up promising avenues for reducing dependence on costly and labor-intensive human-curated references. This could lead to more autonomous and resource-efficient LLM training paradigms, accelerating research and development in self-improving agents.

7. **Informing Adaptive Training Strategies**: The finding that optimal guidance weights vary with model scale suggests that future distillation methods could benefit from adaptive mechanisms to dynamically balance different supervision sources. This could lead to more efficient and tailored training procedures that maximize learning across a spectrum of model sizes and capabilities.

In summary, DAPD represents a significant step forward in the post-training of language models, offering a principled approach to leverage privileged information effectively without inducing detrimental inference-time behaviors. Its impact could be seen in the development of more capable, reliable, and autonomously improvable LLMs.
