# Human2Any: Human-to-Robot Transfer via Constraint-Aware Compositional Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Human2Any is a framework that extracts object-centric interaction priors from human videos and combines them with robot-specific feasibility reasoning to enable skill transfer to diverse robot embodiments. This approach achieves an average success rate of 80% on real-world Franka robot tasks and demonstrates cross-embodiment transfer to a humanoid mobile robot.

## Problem

- High costs and time associated with collecting embodiment-aligned robot demonstrations limit the scalability of robot learning.
- Directly imitating human motions from videos is often infeasible for robots due to significant embodiment mismatches and robot-specific constraints.
- Existing methods struggle to jointly enforce robot feasibility and scene constraints throughout the motion generation process, leading to many infeasible plans.

## Method

- **Human2Any** learns embodiment-agnostic ==object-object interaction priors== (relative pose trajectories) from human videos using a ==conditional diffusion model==.
- Develops robot-specific ==agent-object (grasping) priors== from simulated data for different robot embodiments, also using conditional diffusion models.
- Composes learned priors with robot-side ==feasibility reasoning== and ==motion planning== at test time, employing ==constraint-aware particle filtering== to guide generation towards feasible robot trajectories.

## Results

- Achieved an average **80%** success rate across real-world manipulation tasks on a Franka tabletop robot, including POURINBOWL, HANGMUGTREE, and SORTUTENSILS, without real-world robot demonstrations.
- Demonstrated successful cross-embodiment transfer to an RBY-1 humanoid mobile robot for tasks like POURCUP (**60%** success) and USEROLLER (**70%** success).
- Outperformed baselines in simulation benchmarks, achieving **71%** average success rate on out-of-distribution task variants, and showed that constraint-aware steering enhanced sampling efficiency and generation quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly disentangling transferable object-object interaction knowledge from robot-specific agent-object feasibility constraints enables robust human-to-robot skill transfer.
> - Constraint-aware steering, implemented via particle filtering within the diffusion process, significantly improves the efficiency and quality of generated trajectories by jointly satisfying multiple constraints.
> - Object-centric interaction motion representations derived from human videos generalize effectively to new task contexts, object variations, and diverse robot embodiments without needing specific robot training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation seeks to develop systems capable of generating purposeful and feasible motions to accomplish various tasks. A significant challenge in this area is data collection, as obtaining high-quality, embodiment-aligned robot demonstrations through teleoperation is often time-consuming and expensive. This limitation restricts the scalability and generalization capabilities of behavior cloning (BC) policies, which heavily rely on extensive training data.

Learning from human videos has emerged as a promising alternative for scaling robot learning. Human videos offer an abundant and diverse source of supervision, naturally capturing rich object interactions and manipulation strategies across varied contexts. However, a key difficulty in transferring knowledge from human demonstrations to robots lies in the fundamental embodiment mismatch between humans and robots, alongside variations in scene geometry and robot-specific feasibility constraints (e.g., reachability, joint limits, collisions). Direct imitation of human motion often proves infeasible for robots due to these discrepancies.

Prior research in human-video-based manipulation learning has explored approaches such as visual imitation, motion retargeting, affordance prediction, or the use of intermediate object-centric signals like 2D/3D object flow. While some methods attempt to address embodiment mismatch through learned translation policies or mapping human motion to robot actions, they frequently encounter difficulties in explicitly enforcing robot-specific feasibility, scene geometry, and contact constraints during deployment. Many existing object-centric methods also follow cascaded workflows, where grasp selection or intermediate motion representations are determined before downstream feasibility checks, potentially leading to infeasible compositions.

This work contributes to the landscape by focusing on extracting an embodiment-agnostic interaction structure from human videos, specifically object-centric motion, and then composing this learned knowledge with robot-side feasibility reasoning at test time. It positions itself as an advancement over methods that directly predict robot actions or rely on learned translations, by integrating motion planning and constraint satisfaction into a guided sampling procedure. This approach aims to unify compositional reasoning and constraint satisfaction, addressing the challenge of leveraging diverse human video data for adaptable robot manipulation across different embodiments and task scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central challenge addressed by this research is how to effectively leverage the vast quantities of human video data to learn transferable interaction models that can be successfully adapted to diverse robot embodiments and their specific constraints. The paper identifies several limitations in existing approaches that motivate its proposed framework:

- **Scalability of Robot Data Collection**: Traditional behavior cloning requires extensive, high-quality, embodiment-aligned robot demonstrations, typically gathered through costly and time-consuming teleoperation. This severely limits the scalability of such approaches for learning a wide range of manipulation skills.
- **Limitations of Human Video Imitation**: While human videos are abundant and diverse, directly imitating human body motion is problematic due to the inherent embodiment mismatch between humans and robots. Robot kinematics, joint limits, reachability, and collision constraints differ significantly from human capabilities, rendering many human motions infeasible for robots.
- **Context-Dependency of Trajectory Reproducibility**: A human motion that appears valid in one scene context may be infeasible for a robot in another, or even in the same scene due to robot-specific limitations. The key transferable signal from human videos is not the exact trajectory, but rather the underlying object-centric interaction structure.
- **Ineffectiveness of Direct Action Regression or Learned Translation**: Methods that directly predict robot actions or rely on learned translation policies from human videos often struggle to incorporate robot-specific feasibility and scene geometry constraints at deployment time. This can lead to the generation of locally plausible but globally infeasible robot trajectories, especially for complex, long-horizon tasks involving contacts and precise object manipulations.
- **Lack of Joint Constraint Satisfaction**: Many existing approaches decompose manipulation into stages (e.g., grasp selection, motion generation) but often check feasibility in a cascaded manner. This means that a chosen grasp or intermediate motion might be selected without full consideration of its implications for downstream kinematic or collision constraints, leading to a high rate of failure or inefficient replanning.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the Human2Any framework are:

1. **To learn reusable object-centric interaction priors from human videos**: This involves extracting task-relevant object-object interaction motion, abstracting away embodiment-specific human details, without requiring real-world robot demonstrations in target task contexts.
2. **To represent manipulation through an object-centric factor graph**: This graph is designed to explicitly disentangle human-transferable object-object interaction factors from robot-specific agent-object feasibility factors, enabling robust motion learning and adaptation.
3. **To compose learned interaction priors with robot-side feasibility reasoning and motion planning**: The framework aims to adapt human-derived knowledge to different robot embodiments, scene geometries, and task contexts by integrating these components at test time.
4. **To develop a constraint-aware sampling and scoring procedure**: This procedure should jointly select motion segments that are probable under learned priors while satisfying robot- and scene-specific constraints, thereby producing robot-feasible interaction sequences that preserve the goal-achieving structure observed in human videos.
5. **To validate the approach across diverse manipulation settings and robot embodiments**: This includes demonstrating robust interaction-centric manipulation on both tabletop robotic arms and humanoid mobile robots without reliance on real-world robot training data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Human2Any proposes a framework for human-to-robot transfer that learns object-centric interaction priors from human videos and adapts them to diverse robot embodiments, tasks, and contexts by composing them with robot-side feasibility reasoning and motion planning. The methodology is structured around disentangling embodiment-agnostic interaction knowledge from robot-specific execution constraints.

#### 3.1. Interaction-Centric Task Representation

The framework represents manipulation tasks using a high-level skeleton $S = [s_1, \dots, s_K]$, where each phase $s_k = \langle a_k, O_k \rangle$ specifies an interaction type $a_k$ and the involved objects $O_k$. Three types of interaction are defined:
- **Free-space motion**: Connects interaction phases.
- **Agent-object interaction**: Establishes robot-specific contact with a tool object (e.g., grasping).
- **Object-object interaction**: Describes how a tool object moves relative to a target object (e.g., pouring a cup into a bowl).

The core transferable representation is the object–object interaction trajectory $\tau_{\text{rel}} = \{T_{\text{rel}}(h)\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$, where $T_{\text{rel}}(h) \in SE(3)$ denotes the pose of the tool object relative to the target object at time $h$. This representation captures task-relevant motion while abstracting away human embodiment details. For prehensile manipulation, where the robot rigidly holds the tool object, robot execution can be recovered from this relative trajectory and a grasp pose.

#### 3.2. Learning Interaction Priors

- **Object–Object Priors from Human Videos**:
 - **Data Extraction**: From RGB-D human demonstration videos, tool and target object point clouds ($P_A, P_B$) are segmented and 3D points on the tool object are tracked across frames. These tracked points are transformed into a common world frame using calibrated camera poses.
 - **Relative Trajectory Estimation**: The object-centric relative trajectory $\tau_{\text{rel}}$ is estimated by fitting a rigid transform from the first-frame tool geometry to each subsequent frame. The Kabsch algorithm with RANSAC is employed to reduce the impact of noisy or drifting tracks. Trajectories are trimmed to focus on the interaction segment (e.g., within 0.15–0.2 m of the final interaction pose).
 - **Temporal Smoothing**: The raw trajectories are further refined with temporal smoothing to optimize per-frame rotations and translations, penalizing abrupt changes between consecutive frames.
 - **Diffusion Model Training**: These smoothed $\tau_{\text{rel}}$ trajectories, along with their corresponding point clouds ($P_A, P_B$), form the dataset $D_{\text{os}}$ for training an object-object interaction prior. This prior is instantiated as a conditional diffusion model that predicts 6-DoF pose trajectories conditioned on the point clouds. Rigid transformations are applied to both point clouds and trajectories during training for robustness.

- **Robot-Specific Agent–Object Priors**:
 - **Motivation**: While object-object priors dictate *how* objects interact, the robot needs to know *how* to grasp and control the tool object.
 - **Data Generation**: For each robot embodiment, agent-object priors are trained using simulated grasp data. For a Franka setup, grasp candidates are sampled using geometric heuristics, executed in simulation, and successful grasps are retained. For a dexterous hand (e.g., RBY-1), methods like DRO are used to generate dexterous grasps, which are then curated.
 - **Diffusion Model Training**: Each sample includes a tool-object point cloud ($P_A$) and a robot end-effector trajectory ($\tau_e$) for reaching and grasping. This forms dataset $D_{\text{ro}}$. A separate conditional diffusion model is trained for each robot to learn $p_{\theta_{\text{ro}}}(\tau_e | P_A)$, capturing embodiment-specific grasping behavior. This separation allows the reuse of object-level interaction knowledge across different robots.

#### 3.3. Constraint-Aware Composition

At deployment, the robot receives a context $c$ (robot model, scene point cloud, task skeleton). The challenge is to compose independently plausible motion segments into an executable robot trajectory that satisfies robot and scene constraints.

- **Test-Time Context and Trajectory Assembly ($\Gamma$)**:
 - The framework samples motion segments $x_{1:K}$ for the K phases of the task skeleton.
 - An assembly operator $\Gamma(x_{1:K}, c)$ converts these segments into a full robot trajectory. Agent-object segments are used directly. Object-object segments are converted into end-effector targets using the grasp transform from the preceding agent-object segment (assuming rigid grasp: $T_e(h) = T_{\text{rel}}(h)T_e(0)$).
 - The assembled trajectory is then checked for kinematic feasibility, collision avoidance (using observed scene point clouds via MPLIB), and task-specific constraints.

- **Problem with Rejection Sampling**: Independently sampling segments and then checking feasibility is inefficient. Feasible compositions often occupy a small, constrained region of the joint sample space, leading to many wasted samples.

- **Constraint-Aware Steering**:
 - **Objective**: To approximate the desired posterior distribution $p(x_{1:K} | c, E=1)$, where $E$ denotes the event of a feasible trajectory, proportional to $p(E=1 | x_{1:K}, c) \prod_{k=1}^K p_{\theta_k}(x_k | \tilde{c}_k)$.
 - **Feasibility Likelihood**: This is modeled as $p(E=1 | x_{1:K}, c) \propto \exp(\beta S(\Gamma(\hat{x}_{1:K}, c), c))$, where $S$ scores the assembled trajectory based on in-context constraints (e.g., IK residuals, collision margins, motion-planning feasibility), and $\beta$ controls steering strength.
 - **Particle Filtering**: The posterior is approximated using particle filtering over the joint reverse diffusion process. At each denoising step $t$, $M$ particles are maintained. For each particle, a denoised estimate of the clean segment trajectories ($\hat{x}_{k,0,i}$) is computed using Tweedie's formula.
 - **Scoring and Resampling**: These denoised estimates are assembled by $\Gamma$ and scored by $S$, yielding weights $w_{t,i}$. Particles are then resampled according to these weights before proceeding to the next reverse-diffusion step. This allows steering to gradually reallocate computation towards particles closer to satisfying constraints, even if not fully executable in early steps.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The assembly operator $\Gamma$ utilizes MPLIB for synthesizing free-space motions and verifying kinematic and collision constraints directly on observed scene point clouds, eliminating the need for explicit object mesh models during planning. Robot trajectories are executed using platform-specific controllers: Operational Space Control (OSC) for Franka end-effector targets and joint-position control for the RBY-1 humanoid mobile robot. Perception is handled by tools like SAM 2 for segmentation, FoundationStereo for depth estimation, and CLIP features for object identification.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validates Human2Any across diverse manipulation settings, including real-world experiments on a Franka tabletop setup and an RBY-1 humanoid mobile robot, addressing six hypotheses (H1-H6).

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Composition and Generalization (Simulation)

Table 1 presents the simulation benchmark results across POURINBOWL, HANGMUGTREE, and PREPARETABLE tasks, including in-distribution (ID; S1-S2 variants) and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings.

- **H1: Human2Any can acquire challenging manipulation skills without embodiment-aligned robot data.**
 - Human2Any achieved the strongest overall performance (0.63 ID, 0.71 OOD success rate) compared to baselines. DP3, despite being trained with 100 robot demonstrations per task, showed substantially lower performance (0.22 ID, 0.00 OOD). Im2Flow2Act also underperformed (0.03 ID, 0.00 OOD). This suggests that direct action regression or flow-based action translation struggles with diverse object properties and long-horizon tasks when not explicitly reasoning about robot feasibility.

- **H2: Human2Any generalizes to new task contexts without in-context data.**
 - Human2Any maintained strong performance on OOD variants (0.71 average success rate) with unseen layouts and object arrangements, outperforming baselines significantly (DP3: 0.00, Im2Flow2Act: 0.00). This indicates that the object-object interaction motion representation effectively decouples task-relevant structure from specific training layouts, enabling generalization to novel poses, shapes, and scene contexts.

- **H5: Constraint-aware steering is critical for efficient and feasible composition.**
 - Comparing Human2Any to the "Reject Sampling (No Steering)" ablation, which uses the same learned components but without steering, Human2Any achieved more consistent performance (0.63 ID vs. 0.59 ID, 0.71 OOD vs. 0.55 OOD). This demonstrates that jointly steering motion generation with in-context constraints is more effective and leads to better generalization than independently sampling and filtering candidates.

#### 4.2. Real-world Evaluation

Human2Any was evaluated on real robot platforms (Franka tabletop and RBY-1 humanoid mobile robot) without real-world robot training data for the target tasks.

- **H3: Human2Any can execute real-world manipulation tasks without real-world robot training data.**
 - On the Franka tabletop setup (POURINBOWL, HANGMUGTREE, SORTUTENSILS), Human2Any achieved an average success rate of 0.80 across 10 trials per task (PIB: 8/10, HMT: 7/10, SU: 9/10). Baselines DP3 and Im2Flow2Act performed poorly (DP3: 0.27 avg, Im2Flow2Act: 0.10 avg), with DP3 failing entirely on SORTUTENSILS and Im2Flow2Act on SORTUTENSILS and PREPARETABLE. This confirms that Human2Any can deploy human-derived interaction motions in real scenes without needing real-world robot demonstrations.

- **H4: Human2Any transfers across substantially different robot embodiments.**
 - When deployed on the RBY-1 humanoid mobile robot for POURCUP and USEROLLER, Human2Any achieved success rates of 0.6 and 0.7, respectively. The RBY-1 has significantly different kinematics and a larger workspace compared to the Franka. These results indicate that the learned object-object interaction priors are not tied to a specific robot morphology and can be adapted across diverse robot platforms by resolving embodiment-specific constraints at test time.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Dissecting Key Components

- **Steering improves generation quality and efficiency (H5 revisited).**
 - Qualitative visualizations (Fig. 5a) showed that particle-filtering-based steering effectively reallocates particles from low-feasibility regions towards samples that better satisfy grasp, kinematic, collision, and scene constraints during the denoising process.
 - Quantitatively, Human2Any achieved the highest task-level throughput (successful trials within a one-hour budget) across all simulation domains (Fig. 5b). This demonstrates that steering not only improves the quality of generated motions but also significantly enhances sampling efficiency by reducing computation spent on infeasible candidates.

- **H6: Human2Any benefits from scaled interaction-motion data.**
 - A study on POURINBOWL demonstrated that performance improved with increased quantity and diversity of object-centric interaction trajectories (Fig. 5b). This suggests that Human2Any can effectively leverage larger and more diverse datasets of human video demonstrations, providing indirect evidence for its scalability with future larger human-video sources.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Human2Any presents a methodology for robot manipulation that addresses key limitations in existing learning from demonstration paradigms. Its primary significance lies in enabling the scalable transfer of manipulation knowledge from abundant human videos to diverse robot platforms, significantly reducing the reliance on costly and time-consuming robot-specific data collection.

The framework's core contribution is the explicit disentanglement of embodiment-agnostic object–object interaction priors from robot-specific agent–object feasibility constraints. This architectural separation allows the learning of a universal understanding of how objects interact (e.g., pouring, hanging, placing) from human demonstrations, which can then be flexibly composed with the unique kinematics, grasping capabilities, and environmental constraints of different robots at test time. This modularity is crucial for generalization, as demonstrated by the framework's performance across varied object shapes, scene layouts, and two distinct robot embodiments (Franka arm and RBY-1 humanoid mobile robot).

The introduction of constraint-aware steering within a particle-filtering-based diffusion process is also a significant impact. It moves beyond simple rejection sampling by guiding the motion generation process toward feasible solutions, enhancing both the quality and efficiency of trajectory synthesis. This approach allows for the joint satisfaction of complex, multi-modal constraints (grasp, kinematic, collision) that are often challenging to enforce in cascaded or purely reactive systems.

Potential impacts include:
- **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition**: By leveraging human video data, robots could rapidly acquire new manipulation skills without extensive manual programming or robot teleoperation. This could democratize access to advanced robotic capabilities.
- **Enhanced Generalization**: The ability to generalize across new task contexts, object variations, and robot embodiments, even in zero-shot settings, is critical for deploying robots in unstructured real-world environments.
- **Robustness in Dynamic Environments**: The real-time feasibility reasoning and constraint satisfaction contribute to more robust and reliable robot operation in complex scenes with obstacles and varying object configurations.
- **Foundation for More Complex Systems**: The object-centric interaction priors could serve as a foundational layer for more complex task and motion planning systems, potentially integrating with high-level symbolic planners or language-guided decomposition to achieve longer-horizon, more abstract tasks.

While the current work focuses on prehensile manipulation and assumes a provided task skeleton, the principles established by Human2Any lay groundwork for future extensions. Relaxing the rigid grasp assumption, enabling non-prehensile skills, and integrating with autonomous task planning could further broaden its applicability and impact on the development of highly adaptable and intelligent robotic systems.
