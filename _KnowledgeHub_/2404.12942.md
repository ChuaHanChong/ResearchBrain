# Purposer: Putting Human Motion Generation in Context

> [!summary] Summary
> Purposer generates human motion in 3D scenes using a discrete auto-regressive model (built on PoseGPT) conditioned flexibly on scene point clouds, past/future poses, paths, and action-object targets via a novel dual-branch causal/non-causal conditioning block, outperforming specialized baselines in quality and diversity while enabling chained long-term motion synthesis.

## Problem

- Prior scene-aware motion methods are narrowly specialized to one conditioning type and ignore combinations of scene, past motion, and target goals simultaneously.
- Scarce paired motion-scene data (PROX is two orders of magnitude smaller than AMASS) limits deep models that need large-scale training data.
- Existing scene-conditioned approaches rely on test-time optimization loops to enforce scene constraints, which degrades motion realism and naturalness.

## Method

- ==Discrete motion auto-encoder== (from **PoseGPT**) compresses unconditional motion into a ==quantized latent index sequence== trained on large-scale BABEL data (**Purposer**).
- ==Future-conditioning block== with dual ==causal and non-causal transformer branches== injects time-dependent future signals like paths or target poses into causal auto-regressive prediction.
- ==Multi-context conditioning== combines scene point-cloud embeddings (==PointNet==, prompted), past motion, and action-object target poses (from ==COINS==-style generation) for flexible control and sequence chaining.

## Results

- Adding the future-stream path conditioning improves non-collision score to **71.24-73.28%** versus **55.73%** for the unconditioned PoseGPT baseline on HUMANISE.
- Target pose conditioning raises non-collision by **1.46** points and lowers NLL to **0.42-0.48** while reducing APD due to tighter motion constraints.
- On PROX versus Wang et al. baselines, Purposer achieves much lower FD-static (**29.76-29.84** versus **99.91-111.65**) with comparable or higher contact scores (**99.89-99.96%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prompting scene features as a token works better than concatenating them at the feature level, which can degrade or fail to improve performance.
> - Post-processing optimization to fix collisions improves physical metrics but measurably stiffens and de-naturalizes generated motion.
> - Auto-regressive models trained only on short clips can still produce coherent long-horizon behavior by chaining segments through shared conditioning at boundaries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic and diverse human motion has been an ongoing research problem within computer graphics and vision. Traditionally, efforts have focused on synthesizing motion based on cyclic actions (e.g., walking) or, more recently, on action-conditional 3D human motion generation and textual descriptions. While these methods have advanced the field, many existing approaches exhibit limitations that restrict their applicability, particularly in complex, interactive virtual environments.

**Current Limitations in Motion Generation:**
- **Specialization and Limited Context:** Many state-of-the-art models are specialized to a single conditioning setting (e.g., action labels or past poses) and often do not integrate crucial contextual information such as scene geometry, object semantics, or future goals. This restricts their use in applications like virtual reality or augmented reality where context-aware motion is essential.
- **Data Scarcity for Contextual Motion:** Generating human motion that interacts with a 3D scene requires vast amounts of high-quality, diverse training data that includes detailed scene information. Datasets like PROX, which provide human motion in context, are orders of magnitude smaller than large-scale unconditional motion datasets like AMASS or BABEL. This scarcity hinders the development of powerful conditional models.
- **Reliance on Optimization:** Existing scene-conditioned methods often compensate for data scarcity or model limitations by employing test-time optimization loops. While these loops can enforce scene boundaries and prevent collisions, they can also reduce the naturalness and realism of the generated motion.
- **Challenges with Future Conditioning:** Auto-regressive generative models, due to their causal design, naturally excel at conditioning on past observations. However, they struggle to effectively integrate future-dependent information, such as a target pose or a predefined path, which is critical for goal-oriented motion in complex scenes.

**This paper's contribution** positions itself as a response to these challenges. It aims to develop a learning-based model that can generate human motion in virtual scenes, controlled by diverse contextual information, without being restricted to narrow subsets of conditioning signals or relying on extensive post-processing optimization. By building upon discrete representation learning, the authors seek to leverage large amounts of *unconditional* data while simultaneously integrating *scarce conditional* scene interaction data. The proposed "Purposer" model specifically addresses the future conditioning limitation in auto-regressive architectures, thereby offering a more flexible and comprehensive solution for context-aware human motion generation. It draws inspiration from advancements in discrete auto-regressive models (e.g., PoseGPT, T2M2) and scene interaction synthesis (e.g., COINS, Wang et al.), aiming to unify and extend these capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel method for generating realistic and diverse human motion to populate 3D indoor scenes, enabling seamless interaction with the environment. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Integrate Diverse Contextual Information:** The authors aim to create a single model capable of being controlled by various combinations of conditioning signals. These signals include a specified path within a scene, target poses, past motion observations, and 3D scene geometry represented as point clouds. This is motivated by the observation that human motion is inherently determined by multiple forms of context, and existing models often address only narrow subsets of these.

2. **Overcome Data Scarcity for Context-Aware Motion:** A significant motivation is to address the limitation that state-of-the-art methods often require vast amounts of high-quality and diverse training data, especially for scene-conditioned motion. The proposed model seeks to mitigate this by flexibly exploiting information from open-access large-scale unconditional motion datasets (like AMASS and BABEL) while learning scene interactions from more limited contextual datasets (like HUMANISE and PROX).

3. **Enhance Motion Realism and Naturalness:** The paper aims to improve upon scene-conditioned methods that rely on test-time optimization loops to ensure physical plausibility (e.g., non-collision). While effective for constraint satisfaction, these optimizations can reduce the naturalness and fluidity of the generated motion. A core objective is to achieve realistic and plausible motion through a learning-based approach, minimizing the need for such post-processing.

4. **Enable Future-Dependent Conditioning in Auto-Regressive Models:** A specific architectural objective is to design a novel conditioning block that can effectively handle future conditioning information (e.g., a target goal or a future path) within a causal auto-regressive generative model. This addresses a fundamental limitation of existing auto-regressive approaches, which typically struggle to leverage information from beyond the current timestep.

5. **Provide Semantic Control:** The model is motivated to offer semantic control over generated sequences, allowing users to specify interactions using high-level queries, such as "sit on table" or "lie on couch." This involves integrating action/object semantic pairs to guide motion generation.

6. **Generate Long-Term, Coherent Motions:** Despite being trained on short motion sequences, a key objective is to leverage the flexible conditioning capabilities at test time to chain together short, context-specific motions into longer, coherent sequences that represent complex activities within a scene.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to significantly enhance the applicability of human motion generation in fields such as VR/AR, activity simulation, and synthetic data creation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, dubbed **Purposer**, is a learning-based probabilistic model for generating human motion in 3D indoor scenes. It is structured around a two-stage approach built on neural discrete representation learning, similar to models like PoseGPT.

#### 3.1. Background: Discrete Auto-Regressive Models

Purposer leverages a discrete auto-encoder to transform continuous human motion into a discrete latent space, followed by an auto-regressive model operating within this space.
1. **Discrete Motion Auto-encoder:** An auto-encoder (comprising an encoder $E_p$, a quantizer $Q_p$ with a codebook, and a decoder $D_p$) is trained to compress unconditional human motion sequences into discrete latent representations. A continuous motion sequence of length $T$ is mapped to a discrete sequence of indices $z = \{z^1, \dots, z^{T'}\}$ in the latent space (where $T'$ might be downsampled from $T$). The encoder is causal, meaning $z^t$ depends only on past motion up to a corresponding time step. This auto-encoder is adopted from PoseGPT and trained on the large-scale BABEL dataset.
2. **Auto-regressive Prediction:** Once the discrete latent space is established and frozen, an auto-regressive model $G_p$ is trained to predict the next latent index given the preceding ones. This model maximizes the likelihood of observing a sequence of latent indices, akin to GPT models in natural language processing. Generated latent sequences are then decoded back into continuous human motion by $D_p$.

#### 3.2. Key Innovation: Future Conditioning in Auto-Regressive Models

A central contribution of Purposer is its novel approach to incorporate future-dependent conditioning signals into a causal auto-regressive model.
- **Problem:** Standard causal auto-regressive models predict the next step based only on past information. When conditioned on time-dependent *future* information (e.g., a target pose or a path to follow), these models cannot "see" the future context, potentially degrading output quality.
- **Solution: Two-Branch Network:** Purposer introduces a network with two distinct branches:
 - **Causal Branch:** Processes the input sequence of latent tokens ($z^1, \dots, z^{t-1}$) using causal layers, ensuring predictions $h^l_t$ at any layer $l$ and timestep $t$ depend only on information up to $t-1$.
 - **Non-Causal Branch:** Processes the time-dependent conditioning sequence ($c^1, \dots, c^{T'}$) using non-causal layers. This branch allows information from all timesteps (including future) to propagate, generating conditioning features $g^l_t$.
 - **Injection:** The features $g^l_t$ from the non-causal branch are added to the features $h^l_t$ from the causal branch at each layer ($\tilde{h}^l_t = h^l_t + g^l_t$). This allows the causal prediction mechanism to be informed by the full context, including future information, without violating its causal nature.

#### 3.3. Motion Generation with Context

Purposer integrates several types of contextual information:
1. **Scene Geometry:** The 3D scene is represented as a point cloud and encoded using PointNet. This scene embedding is then used as a *prompt token* (a sequence-wide fixed context) at the beginning of the input sequence for the auto-regressive model.
2. **Past Observations:** The causal design of the auto-encoder and auto-regressive model naturally allows conditioning on observed past motion. If a partial motion sequence is provided, it is encoded into latent indices, and the model continues generation from that point.
3. **Trajectories and Target Poses:**
 - **Trajectories:** A 2D path (e.g., bird's-eye view coordinates) is processed as a time-dependent conditioning sequence using the novel *future conditioning block*.
 - **Target Poses:** A specific future pose (e.g., the last pose in a sequence) can also be used as a time-dependent condition, fed into the future conditioning block.
4. **Semantic Information:**
 - **Action Labels:** Action labels (e.g., "walking," "sitting") are embedded and used as sequence-wide conditioning, either as a prompt or by adding to input tokens.
 - **Target Human-Object Interactions:** To control interactions (e.g., "sit on couch"), the problem is decoupled. A static target pose representing the desired interaction is generated (following COINS) and then used to condition the motion model as a target pose via the *future conditioning block*.

#### 3.4. Training Setup

- **Auto-encoder:** Trained on the BABEL dataset.
- **Auto-regressive Model:** Trained on the HUMANISE dataset, a synthetic dataset that places a subset of AMASS motions into ScanNet scenes.
- **Fine-tuning:** The model is further fine-tuned on the PROX dataset for evaluation on real-world, albeit smaller-scale, data.

#### 3.5. Generating Long-Term Sequences

Despite training on short sequences, Purposer can generate long-term motions by chaining together short, context-specific segments at test time. This is achieved by:
- **Configured Models:** Defining "object interaction" configurations (conditioned on target poses from action-object pairs, action labels, scene, and past motion) and "locomotion" configurations (guided by a path, scene, and past motion).
- **Chaining Mechanism:** The last 'n' poses of a generated sequence (e.g., from an interaction) are used as past observation conditioning for the subsequent sequence (e.g., a locomotion segment), allowing smooth transitions and coherent long-term behavior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Purposer focuses on motion quality, diversity, and physical plausibility within 3D scenes, using both ablation studies and comparisons to state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics

- **Quality:** Fréchet Distance (FD\_static), calculated with a VPoser model on individual frames.
- **Diversity:** Average Pairwise Distance (APD) of motion sequences and APD of specific body markers (APD\_mark).
- **Physical Plausibility:** Non-collision score (measures interpenetration with scene mesh) and Contact score (ensures contact with objects).
- **Generation Quality:** Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) for the auto-regressive generator.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (on HUMANISE dataset)

The ablation studies investigate the impact of various conditioning signals and the proposed architectural components.
- **First Pose Conditioning:** Using the first observed pose as conditioning significantly improves non-collision and contact metrics, guiding the motion in a physically plausible direction.
- **Scene Conditioning:**
 - Integrating scene information at the feature level (concatenation) did not improve performance, and sometimes deteriorated it.
 - Introducing scene information via *token prompting* (as an extra token) was effective, maintaining generation quality (NLL) and improving non-collision scores.
- **Target Pose Conditioning:** Conditioning on a target pose substantially increased the non-collision score (by 1.46%) and improved NLL, indicating better motion quality and interaction. While APD slightly decreased, this is expected as the motion becomes more constrained.
- **Future Stream Component:** The novel future conditioning block (Section 3.2) consistently outperformed counterparts without it.
 - **Locomotion Model (with path):** When using the time-dependent path as input through the future stream, improvements were observed in non-collision, NLL, and maintained contact scores.
 - **Object Interaction Model (with target pose):** This configuration, leveraging the future stream for target pose conditioning, achieved the best performance in terms of motion quality (NLL), non-collision, and contact scores.
The studies also noted that all configurations could generate any action present in the dataset, indicating broad applicability.

#### 4.3. Impact of Post-processing Optimization

Consistent with prior work, the authors investigated the effect of a post-processing optimization step (used by) to improve physical plausibility. While this step slightly enhanced non-collision and contact scores on the PROX dataset, the authors observed that it produced motion that was qualitatively "less natural and stiffer." This suggests that Purposer's learning-based approach generates inherently more natural motion.

#### 4.4. Comparison to State-of-the-Art (on PROX dataset)

Purposer was compared against established scene-conditioned methods by Wang et al. and Wang et al., ensuring fair comparison by matching conditioning settings and incorporating the post-processing optimization step when applicable.
- Purposer demonstrated a favorable trade-off across diversity (APD), quality (FD), and physical plausibility metrics.
- While non-collision scores were comparable to competing approaches, Purposer consistently achieved the *highest contact score*. This indicates a richer and more appropriate interaction of the generated human motion with the 3D scene objects.
- A low FD score was also achieved, suggesting that the generated motions were realistic.
- Qualitative video results (provided in supplementary material) further illustrate the naturalness and improved interaction capabilities of Purposer.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results and Long-Term Generation

Qualitative results (e.g., Figure 5) demonstrated the model's ability to generate appropriate object interactions (e.g., sitting on a couch) from diverse starting positions and orientations when conditioned on a target pose. It also successfully generated realistic locomotion along arbitrarily chosen paths. The ability to chain short, context-specific motions (interaction and locomotion) to form coherent long-term sequences was also demonstrated, highlighting the model's practical utility.

#### 4.6. Limitations

The authors acknowledge two primary limitations:
- As a purely kinematic model, Purposer does not inherently account for physics constraints, which can lead to minor interpenetrations with the scene in some instances.
- The reliance on HUMANISE, a synthetic dataset with short motion clips, means that some subtleties of real-world human-object interactions might not be fully captured. Future work could explore more realistic datasets like SAMP.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Purposer" research introduces a significant advancement in the field of human motion generation, particularly in the context of 3D virtual environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Realism and Scene Interaction:** Purposer moves beyond generic motion generation by deeply integrating various forms of scene context. The consistently high contact scores demonstrate its ability to produce motions that interact meaningfully and plausibly with objects and the environment, leading to more realistic virtual inhabitants. This is a crucial step towards making synthetic human behavior more believable.

2. **Flexible and Multi-Contextual Control:** The model's capacity to be controlled by diverse and combinable conditioning signals—including scene geometry, past motion, future targets (paths/poses), and semantic action-object queries—provides an unprecedented level of control. This flexibility distinguishes it from specialized models and makes it adaptable to a wide array of application scenarios.

3. **Architectural Innovation for Auto-Regressive Models:** The introduction of the novel future conditioning block is a notable technical contribution. It effectively resolves a long-standing limitation of causal auto-regressive models, allowing them to leverage future-dependent information without compromising their core design. This innovation has implications beyond human motion, potentially benefiting other sequence generation tasks where future context is valuable.

4. **Efficient Data Utilization and Scalability:** By building upon neural discrete representation learning, Purposer efficiently leverages large volumes of readily available *unconditional* motion data to learn a powerful motion prior. This strategy mitigates the challenges posed by the scarcity of high-quality *conditional* motion-in-scene data, making the development of complex, context-aware models more feasible.

5. **Generation of Long-Term, Coherent Behaviors:** The ability to chain short, context-specific motion sequences into longer, coherent narratives within a scene is a major practical breakthrough. This enables the simulation of complex activities and workflows, where characters navigate and interact with multiple objects over extended periods, an essential feature for comprehensive virtual environment population.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Purposer can significantly enhance the immersion and realism of VR/AR experiences by populating virtual worlds with intelligent, context-aware agents that move and interact naturally.
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** The model offers a powerful tool for generating diverse and semantically rich synthetic data for training other AI models, such as those for human pose estimation, action recognition, and human-object interaction understanding, particularly in scenarios where real-world data collection is expensive or difficult.
- **Character Animation and Game Development:** Animators and game developers could leverage Purposer to automate the generation of complex character movements and interactions, reducing manual effort and increasing the fidelity of virtual characters.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** Realistic simulations of human behavior and interaction could inform the design and training of robots, allowing them to better understand and respond to human actions in shared environments.
- **Activity Simulation and Training:** For applications requiring the simulation of human activities (e.g., architectural planning, safety training, crowd simulation), Purposer provides a foundation for creating more dynamic and realistic scenarios.

In conclusion, Purposer represents a substantial step forward in human motion generation, offering a versatile, controllable, and robust framework for creating realistic human-scene interactions that can drive a new generation of virtual experiences and intelligent systems.
