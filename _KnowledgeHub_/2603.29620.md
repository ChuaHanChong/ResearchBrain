# Unify-Agent: A Unified Multimodal Agent for World-Grounded Image Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Unify-Agent introduces a unified multimodal agent framework for world-grounded image synthesis, actively acquiring and integrating external knowledge to overcome factual inaccuracies and identity drift in generative models. It achieves a 22-point improvement in overall score on the FactIP benchmark compared to its base model, demonstrating superior factual and visual fidelity for knowledge-intensive concepts.

## Problem

- Existing Text-to-Image (T2I) models struggle to faithfully depict real-world, knowledge-intensive concepts (e.g., specific IPs, historical figures), often suffering from factual inaccuracies or "identity drift".
- Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) rely on static parametric knowledge from pre-training, which is insufficient for less common or frequently updated real-world targets, leading to hallucinations.
- Prior agentic T2I systems are brittle, multi-stage pipelines with decoupled components (LLM planner, retrieval tools, image generator), leading to cascading errors and poor evidence integration.

## Method

- Developed **Unify-Agent**, a unified multimodal agent built on the ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture (**Bagel**) that integrates visual understanding and continuous image generation.
- Implemented an end-to-end ==agentic pipeline== during inference, featuring prompt understanding, ==multimodal evidence searching== (textual and visual), ==evidence-grounded recaptioning==, and visual synthesis.
- Curated a specialized data pipeline of 143K high-quality agent trajectories, including multimodal research traces and structured, generation-oriented recaptions, for fine-tuning the unified model.

## Results

- Unify-Agent achieved a **22-point** overall score improvement on the FactIP benchmark (**73.2** vs. 50.9 for base model) and the best WiScore (**0.77**) on the WiSE benchmark among Unified MLLMs.
- It set a new state-of-the-art overall score (**4.08**) among open-source models on KiTTEN, demonstrating superior text and entity alignment.
- Ablation studies confirmed that multimodal evidence acquisition (text and image search) and evidence-grounded recaptioning are essential for improving factual relevance and image quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A tightly coupled, unified multimodal architecture integrating reasoning, search, and generation within a single model significantly improves factual and identity-consistent image synthesis.
> - Transforming raw retrieved textual and visual evidence into a structured, generation-oriented "recaption" is crucial for effective knowledge integration and downstream synthesis.
> - In a unified model, the interplay between low-level generative latents (VAE) and high-level semantic visual representations (ViT) during recaptioning enhances the interpretation of visual references, improving overall grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the evolving landscape of artificial intelligence, specifically at the intersection of Text-to-Image (T2I) generation, multimodal models, and agentic AI systems.

Current T2I generation models have demonstrated advancements in visual realism, controllability, and stylistic diversity. However, the paper identifies a limitation in their ability to faithfully depict real-world entities and concepts, particularly long-tail or knowledge-intensive concepts such as specific intellectual properties (IPs), historical figures, or scientific phenomena. Existing unified multimodal models (UMMs), while attempting to integrate visual understanding and image generation, primarily rely on static parametric knowledge acquired during pre-training. This reliance can lead to hallucinations or identity drift when encountering concepts not sufficiently represented in their training data, limiting their application in scenarios requiring factual and visual fidelity.

The broader field of AI has seen a shift towards agentic reasoning, where large language models (LLMs) move beyond isolated deliberation to action-oriented planning, often involving external tools and environmental interaction. Previous attempts to incorporate agentic pipelines into T2I systems have largely involved brittle, multi-stage pipelines that loosely connect an LLM planner, retrieval tools, and a separate image generator. These decoupled systems struggle with effective evidence integration, as retrieved textual or visual information may not be directly translatable into precise visual guidance for generation.

Unify-Agent addresses this gap by proposing an end-to-end unified multimodal agent framework. It seeks to move T2I generation from a "closed-book" approach, relying solely on parametric memory, to an "open-book" or "agentic" paradigm that actively acquires and integrates external world knowledge during inference. By unifying reasoning, searching, and generation within a single architecture, this work aims to overcome the limitations of both closed-book UMMs and loosely coupled agentic pipelines, thereby enhancing factual and identity-consistent image synthesis for world-grounded concepts. The introduction of the FactIP benchmark further contributes to the landscape by providing a specialized tool for evaluating models on rare and culturally significant long-tail concepts, a dimension often overlooked by traditional T2I benchmarks focused on aesthetic quality and generic prompt alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified multimodal agent capable of performing world-grounded image synthesis. This involves moving beyond models that rely solely on internalized parametric knowledge to a system that can actively seek and integrate external world knowledge during the image generation process.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing image generation models:

- **Factual and Visual Fidelity for Knowledge-Intensive Concepts:** While modern Text-to-Image (T2I) models excel in generating visually plausible and aesthetically diverse images, they frequently struggle with concepts that require specific real-world knowledge. This includes depicting real people, cultural symbols, rare intellectual properties (IPs), historical scenes, scientific phenomena, and other "long-tail" concepts accurately. For such concepts, visual quality alone is insufficient; factual and visual fidelity to the intended entity is paramount.
- **Limitations of Parametric Knowledge in Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs):** Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) aim to integrate visual understanding and image generation within a shared architecture. However, they typically rely on static parametric memory embedded during pre-training. This fixed knowledge is often insufficient to recover the correct appearance and identity-defining visual cues of less common or frequently updated real-world targets. The model cannot update its world knowledge at inference time, leading to factual inaccuracies, hallucinations, or "identity drift."
- **Inefficiencies of Decoupled Agentic Systems:** Previous attempts to incorporate external knowledge into T2I generation have involved multi-stage pipelines, often stitching together separate LLMs for planning, external search tools, and standalone image generators. This brittle, decoupled approach suffers from several issues:
 - **Cascading Errors:** Errors in one stage can propagate to subsequent stages.
 - **Poor Evidence Integration:** Evidence acquisition, multimodal reasoning, and visual synthesis remain separate, making it challenging to effectively integrate retrieved information. Retrieved text might lack fine-grained visual attributes, and reference images might contain irrelevant elements.
 - **Lack of Unified Reasoning:** The interpretation of retrieved visual references, especially fine-grained visual attributes, is often overlooked by language-based reasoning in decoupled systems.

To address these limitations, the paper proposes Unify-Agent with the following key objectives:

- **Reframing Image Generation as an Agentic Pipeline:** The work aims to reformulate image generation from a passive prompt-to-image mapping into an active, inference-time sequential decision process. This pipeline consists of prompt understanding, multimodal evidence searching, grounded recaptioning, and final synthesis.
- **Developing a Unified End-to-End Architecture:** Instead of decoupled stages, Unify-Agent seeks to build the entire agentic workflow on a unified multimodal model. This architecture is intended to create a mutually reinforcing synergy between generative priors and multimodal understanding, leveraging components like the Vision Transformer (ViT) for high-level semantics and the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for low-level perceptual latents.
- **Constructing a Tailored Multimodal Data Pipeline:** To effectively train the proposed agentic generation process, the research aims to curate a dataset that captures not only final outputs but also intermediate multimodal reasoning, evidence searching, and aggregation steps, including explicit multimodal research traces.
- **Introducing a Comprehensive Factual Benchmark (FactIP):** To rigorously evaluate the model's ability to generate images grounded in factual and long-tail world knowledge, a new benchmark, FactIP, is introduced. This benchmark specifically targets identity consistency and factual faithfulness for rare and culturally significant concepts.

In essence, the motivation is to enable T2I models to accurately depict real-world concepts by actively acquiring and intelligently integrating external knowledge, thereby enhancing their factual and visual reliability for a broader range of practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Unify-Agent system is built upon a unified multimodal model and integrates an agentic pipeline for world-grounded image synthesis. The methodology encompasses the architectural foundation, a specialized data pipeline for training, and the inference-time execution of the agent.

#### 3.1. Architectural Foundation: Bagel

Unify-Agent is built upon Bagel, a Unified Multimodal Model (UMM) that employs a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture. Bagel integrates a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder for multimodal inputs, unifying visual understanding and continuous image generation. It achieves this through dedicated "experts" for each capability:
- **Multimodal Understanding:** Formulated as an autoregressive next-token prediction task, handled by an understanding expert, minimizing negative log-likelihood for textual responses conditioned on multimodal input.
- **Multimodal Generation:** Synthesizes high-fidelity images through a generation expert, formulated as a rectified flow operating within the latent space of a continuous Variational Autoencoder (VAE). It learns a time-conditioned velocity field by minimizing a latent flow-matching objective.

#### 3.2. Motivating Evidence for Agentic Approach

A preliminary study using Bagel demonstrated that injecting external knowledge (text, visual, or both) significantly improved factual generation for rare intellectual properties (IPs) compared to prompt-only generation. Visual injection showed the largest gains. However, simply concatenating raw retrieved text and visual inputs was suboptimal, suggesting the need for a mechanism to transform this knowledge into a more generation-compatible representation, leading to the "recaption" paradigm.

#### 3.3. Problem Formulation

The problem of world-grounded image synthesis is formulated not as a single-step mapping, but as an interleaved generative trajectory. It introduces intermediate variables: cognitive gap assessment (g), textual evidence trace ($\tau_t$), visual evidence trace ($\tau_v$), and evidence-grounded recaption (c). The holistic generative process is defined by a joint distribution $p_\theta(y, c, \tau_t, \tau_v, g | x)$, factorized into four cognitive phases:
1. **Cognitive Gap Detection ($p_\theta(g | x)$):** Assesses the prompt for missing visually critical attributes.
2. **Evidence Acquisition ($p_\theta(\tau_t, \tau_v | x, g)$):** Proactively searches and reasons over textual and visual evidence.
3. **Evidence-Grounded Recaptioning ($p_\theta(c | x, g, \tau_t, \tau_v)$):** Consolidates gathered evidence and original instruction into a structured, generation-oriented recaption.
4. **Visual Synthesis ($p_\theta(y | c, \tau_v)$):** Final image generation, conditioned on the recaption and visual identity anchors.

#### 3.4. Data Pipeline

A specialized data pipeline is constructed to train Unify-Agent and evaluate its capabilities:

- **Training Data Construction (143K high-quality agent trajectories):** Each training sample $D_{SFT} = \{(x, \tau_t, \tau_v, c)\}$ includes the original user prompt (x), textual research trace ($\tau_t$), visual research trace ($\tau_v$), and the final evidence-grounded recaption (c).
 - **Task Source and Prompt Collection:**
 - **IP Pool:** Annotators collected 456K knowledge-intensive, long-tail, and less frequently represented IPs across 12 domains (e.g., Celebrity, Animation, Food). Each IP was summarized into structured metadata using GPT-4o, and two representative seed images were collected.
 - **Prompt Generation:** Gemini 3 Pro was used to create diverse user instructions that retain generation intent (scene, clothing, style) while requiring factual consistency.
 - **Multimodal Research Trace Construction:** Claude Opus 4.6, acting as a teacher agent, generated multi-step research trajectories for each prompt.
 - **Textual Research Trace ($\tau_t$):** The agent formulated a textual query ($q_t$) to an external text search system (e.g., Wikipedia) to obtain textual evidence ($E_t$) for semantic grounding and background context.
 - **Visual Research Trace ($\tau_v$):** Conditioned on the prompt and textual evidence, the agent generated a visual query ($q_v$) for an external image search system. Gemini 3 Flash then acted as a visual evaluator, scoring candidate images on identity consistency, subject salience, clarity, and watermark cleanliness. The top two images were selected as $E_v$.
 - **Evidence-Grounded Recaption Annotation:**
 - **Recaption Function C($x, E_t, E_v$):** The collected multimodal evidence and original instruction were consolidated into a structured, generation-oriented recaption (c). This recaption integrates prompt-level compositional constraints, semantic context from textual evidence, and identity-preserving visual cues from reference images.
 - **Generation-based Validation:** Nano Banana Pro synthesized an image ($\hat{y}$) from the recaption and visual references. GPT-4o, acting as a multimodal judge, compared $\hat{y}$ against the ground-truth image. A reject-sampling strategy was applied: if $\hat{y}$ failed the identity-consistency check after up to five trials, the trajectory was discarded.

- **FactIP Evaluation Benchmark (2,462 curated prompts, 500 for lightweight test):** Derived from the IP pool but disjoint from training data, FactIP evaluates world-grounded image synthesis. Samples are long-tail, knowledge-intensive, visually grounded, difficult for memorization-only models, and diverse across categories.
 - **Evaluation Criterion:** Assessed across four dimensions (0-10 scale by Seed2.0): Clarity, Content, Aesthetics, and Relevance. Relevance, measuring IP identity consistency and factual faithfulness, was highly weighted (0.75) in the overall score.

#### 3.5. Unified Fine-Tuning on Multimodal Agent Trajectories

The Bagel model is fine-tuned on the constructed interleaved multimodal trajectories.
- **Dual-Loss Design:** A combined loss $L_{SFT} = L_{text} + L_{image}$ is used.
 - $L_{text}$: Autoregressive next-token prediction objective (cross-entropy loss) for reasoning, tool invocation, and recaptioning tokens. Special tokens for reasoning/action boundaries are upweighted.
 - $L_{image}$: Latent flow-matching objective (MSE loss) for image generation, aligning generated images with instructions and retrieved evidence.
- **Attention Masking:** A hybrid attention masking strategy is employed. Causal masking for textual components, full attention for retrieved reference images (VAE and ViT tokens). Critically, during image generation, latent flow-matching tokens are restricted to attend only to preceding recaption tokens and reference image tokens, filtering out noisy historical reasoning traces.

#### 3.6. Unify-Agent: Inference-Time Multimodal Agentic Pipeline

The trained Unify-Agent executes a sequential inference-time decision process:
1. **Think (Prompt Understanding & Cognitive Gap Detection):** Interprets the input prompt (x), decomposes it into factors (identity, scene, style), and estimates if external knowledge is missing or ambiguous ($g$). It identifies missing visually-critical knowledge units ($M(x)$).
2. **Research (Sequential Multimodal Evidence Acquisition):**
 - **Textual Evidence Acquisition ($\tau_t$):** Formulates a textual query to obtain semantic context ($E_t$).
 - **Visual Evidence Acquisition ($\tau_v$):** Refines search using textual context to form a visual query and retrieves identity-relevant and context-compatible reference images ($E_v$).
3. **Recaption (Multimodal Grounding into Executable Specifications):** Transforms retrieved evidence into generation-useful grounded constraints.
 - **Identity-preserving constraints:** Captures visual attributes for faithful target identity.
 - **Scene-compositional constraints:** Encodes prompt-specified factors (pose, environment, style).
 - These are integrated into an evidence-grounded recaption (c), a structured textual specification for synthesis.
4. **Generate (Evidence-Grounded Image Synthesis):** The downstream generator synthesizes the image (y) conditioned on the recaption (c) and visual identity anchors ($\tau_v$), ensuring guidance by refined, visually grounded constraints rather than raw reasoning history.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that Unify-Agent significantly improves factual image synthesis capabilities compared to its base model and other baselines, particularly for knowledge-intensive and long-tail concepts.

#### 4.1. FactIP Benchmark Results (Table 1)

- Unify-Agent achieved an Overall score of 73.2, outperforming its base unified model (Bagel, 50.9) by more than 22 points.
- It surpassed strong generation-only baselines such as FLUX.1-dev (28.9) and SD-3.5-large (27.5).
- In the critical "Relevance" dimension, Unify-Agent secured the top scores across Character (67.3), Object (71.8), and Scene (78.2) sub-categories within the Unified MLLM group, indicating superior ability to preserve IP identities and factual visual traits.
- The model's performance approached that of strong closed-source commercial models like Nano Banana-2 (88.5) and Seedream-5 (87.3).

#### 4.2. WiSE Benchmark Results (Table 2)

- Unify-Agent attained the best Overall WiScore of 0.77 within the Unified MLLM category, exceeding BAGEL+CoT (0.70).
- It demonstrated strong performance across most individual knowledge domains, notably excelling in cultural (0.82), biological (0.72), and chemistry (0.70) knowledge.

#### 4.3. KiTTEN Benchmark Results (Table 3)

- Unify-Agent achieved a new state-of-the-art overall score of 4.08 among open-source models, outperforming Imagen-3 (3.50).
- It obtained the highest overall text alignment (4.22) and entity alignment (3.93) scores, indicating robust capability in preserving intricate visual details and adhering to specific entity constraints.

#### 4.4. T2I-FactBench Results (Table 4)

- Within the unified MLLM group, Unify-Agent achieved top overall scores in SKCI (77.4) and MKCC (71.5).
- It recorded an SKCM concept score of 69.2, comparing favorably against commercial models like DALL-E 3 (55.5) in this specific aspect. These results indicate Unify-Agent's ability to capture intrinsic visual attributes of single concepts, adapt them to varied conditions, and handle multi-concept interactions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study (Table 5)

- The full Unify-Agent model showed the best performance, with the largest gain on Relevance (44.9 → 72.4) over the vanilla Bagel baseline, emphasizing its strength in factual grounding and identity fidelity.
- **Pipeline-level ablations:** Removing text search or image search components led to performance degradation, particularly in Relevance, confirming the importance of both textual and visual evidence acquisition. Removing the recaptioning step also caused a substantial drop, indicating that raw retrieved evidence is suboptimal and requires transformation into an executable specification.
- **Constraint-level ablations:** Removing identity-preserving constraints reduced Relevance, while removing scene-compositional constraints negatively impacted Content and Aesthetics, validating the roles of these structured constraints.
- **Recaption Architecture Ablations:** Removing the ViT encoder caused a significant performance drop, especially on Relevance, indicating the importance of high-level semantic visual tokens for understanding reference images. Removing the VAE also degraded performance, suggesting the contribution of low-level perceptual latents.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 5)

- Qualitative comparisons showed that Unify-Agent consistently produced images with superior subject fidelity, more accurate attribute binding, and stronger prompt adherence across diverse knowledge-intensive scenarios (historical figures, fictional characters, products, stylized toys) compared to baselines like Flux-1, Bagel-7b, Hunyuan, and Stable Diffusion. Competing methods often suffered from identity drift or missing attributes.

#### 4.7. Finding: Why Generation Helps Understanding in a Unified Model

- The ablation studies suggested that in a unified model, generation capabilities can enhance understanding. Specifically, the joint availability of low-level generative latents (from VAE) and high-level semantic visual representations (from ViT) within the recaptioning stage allowed the model to interpret retrieved visual references with higher fidelity, leading to better recaptions and subsequently improved grounded image synthesis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Unify-Agent research introduces a paradigm shift in text-to-image generation, moving it from a static, closed-book process to a dynamic, open-book agentic pipeline. This has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Factual Accuracy and Identity Fidelity:** By actively acquiring and integrating external world knowledge, Unify-Agent directly addresses the critical challenge of factual inaccuracies and identity drift in generative models. This is particularly impactful for generating images of rare, long-tail, or knowledge-intensive concepts (e.g., specific historical figures, cultural artifacts, intellectual properties). Such capability is crucial for applications requiring high fidelity, such as digital content creation, educational tools, historical visualization, and product design.
- **Bridging the Understanding-Generation Gap:** The work highlights the value of tightly coupling reasoning, searching, and generation within a unified multimodal architecture. It demonstrates that integrating cognitive gap detection, multimodal evidence acquisition, and grounded recaptioning can lead to a more reliable synthesis process. This integrated approach, where generative priors (like VAE latents) can enhance multimodal understanding, offers architectural insights for future UMM designs.
- **Foundation for More Capable Generative Agents:** Unify-Agent represents an early exploration of agent-based modeling for world-grounded image synthesis. By reformulating generation as an inference-time sequential decision process, it lays groundwork for more sophisticated generative agents capable of complex tasks. Future iterations could involve iterative search, reflection, and replanning, crucial for open-world scenarios beyond single-pass workflows.
- **New Evaluation Standards with FactIP:** The introduction of the FactIP benchmark provides a specialized tool for rigorously evaluating identity consistency and factual faithfulness in generated images. This benchmark goes beyond traditional metrics focused on aesthetic quality or generic prompt alignment, offering a more comprehensive assessment for models operating in knowledge-intensive domains. It can guide future research towards developing models that are not only visually impressive but also factually reliable.
- **Applications in Diverse Domains:** The enhanced ability to generate factually accurate images of specific entities has potential applications across numerous fields:
 - **Creative Industries:** Generating custom content for advertising, gaming, and entertainment that adheres to specific brand guidelines or character designs.
 - **Education:** Creating accurate visual aids for historical events, scientific concepts, or cultural studies.
 - **E-commerce:** Generating product images with specific brand elements and accurate representations.
 - **Research:** Visualizing complex scientific data or theoretical concepts with greater precision.
- **Advancement in Multimodal AI:** The work contributes to the broader field of multimodal AI by demonstrating a successful strategy for integrating symbolic reasoning with continuous generation. It validates the potential of end-to-end unified agent training and encourages further research into how understanding and generation capabilities can mutually reinforce each other within a single model.

In summary, Unify-Agent represents a step towards more intelligent and reliable image generation systems that can operate with a deeper understanding of the real world, paving the way for generative AI to tackle more complex and knowledge-driven visual synthesis tasks.
