# VisMem: Latent Vision Memory Unlocks Potential of Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NUS, Fudan, Tsinghua, and vivo developed VisMem, a framework that integrates a dual latent vision memory system inspired by human cognition into Vision-Language Models. This approach alleviates the visual processing bottleneck, yielding an average 11.8% performance increase across visual understanding, reasoning, and generation tasks with minimal added inference latency.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suffer from a "visual processing bottleneck," leading to a loss of visual grounding and contextualized visual experience during multi-step generation.
- This bottleneck impairs VLM performance on complex tasks requiring fine-grained perception, multi-step reasoning, and visual fidelity, often resulting in visual hallucinations.
- Prior attempts to address this, such as direct training or image/token-level paradigms, incur issues like catastrophic forgetting, high computational costs, or limitations in generating new visual information.

## Method

- VisMem introduces a cognitively-aligned framework for VLMs, integrating ==dynamic short-term (perceptual)== and ==long-term (semantic) latent vision memories==, inspired by human cognitive theory.
- A ==dynamic memory invocation mechanism==, using special tokens, seamlessly triggers and inserts memory content generated by a Query Builder and specialized ==LoRA-based Memory Formers== into the VLM's autoregressive inference.
- A two-stage Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) training strategy is used to optimize both the utility of the generated memory content and the efficiency of the memory invocation process.

## Results

- VisMem achieved an average performance increase of **11.8%** across 12 visual benchmarks, outperforming 15 baseline methods in understanding (**+8.9%**), reasoning (**+16.4%**), and generation (**+10.6%**) tasks.
- The framework exhibited robust cross-domain generalization and effectively mitigated catastrophic forgetting, showing superior knowledge retention compared to direct training methods.
- VisMem maintained broad compatibility across nine diverse base VLM architectures (3B to 38B parameters) and incurred only insignificant additional inference latency, comparable to direct training optimization methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Translating human cognitive memory structures, distinguishing between short-term visually-dominant and long-term semantically-dominant memory, offers an effective paradigm for enhancing VLM visual processing.
> - Dynamically invoking specialized latent vision memories based on the model's cognitive state allows for efficient and context-aware retention and utilization of visual information during complex tasks.
> - Integrating memory through lightweight, non-intrusive components (e.g., LoRA adapters) allows for substantial VLM capability enhancements while preserving base model generalization and maintaining minimal computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and widely acknowledged limitation within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs): the "visual processing bottleneck." Despite their remarkable success in multimodal tasks, current VLMs often struggle with complex visual understanding, reasoning, and generation over prolonged sequences. This bottleneck manifests as a tendency for VLMs to lose grounding in visual evidence, prioritize accumulated textual context, and exhibit a deficit in contextualized visual experience during deep autoregressive decoding. This leads to issues like hallucinations, poor fine-grained perception, and an inability to maintain fidelity over long generative sequences.

The authors contextualize their work by categorizing existing efforts to enhance VLM visual capabilities into four paradigms:
- **Direct Training Paradigm:** This involves fine-tuning or reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize model parameters for specific visual tasks (e.g., SFT, Visual-RFT, VLM-R1, Vision-R1). While these methods can achieve task-specific performance, they often suffer from catastrophic forgetting and sacrifice generalization.
- **Image-Level Paradigm:** Approaches in this category operate in the pixel space by explicitly synthesizing new visual inputs or using tools like bounding boxes (e.g., Sketchpad, DeepEyes, OpenThinkImg). These methods offer image-level "thinking" but come with prohibitive computational costs, high latency, and reliance on external tools.
- **Token-Level Paradigm:** This paradigm constrains operations to visual tokens, focusing on selecting or manipulating existing representations (e.g., ICoT, MINT-CoT, VPT). While more efficient than image-level methods, they are fundamentally non-generative and limited to re-surfacing what the model has already encoded, restricting their ability to introduce novel visual information or reasoning.
- **Latent Space Paradigm:** This promising direction introduces continuous latent contexts during sequential inference (e.g., Coconut, MemGen, Mirage). However, existing methods in this category tend to either focus solely on language models or require auxiliary, often manually labeled, visual data, limiting their direct applicability and effectiveness in fully addressing the VLM visual bottleneck.

VisMem positions itself within the latent space paradigm, but with a crucial distinction: it introduces *dynamic latent vision memories* directly inspired by human cognitive memory theory. This cognitive alignment, particularly drawing from the Dennis Norris Theory distinguishing short-term (visually-dominant) and long-term (semantically-dominant) memory, is a novel approach to overcome the limitations of prior work. By proposing a system that can proactively harness distinct types of vision memory in the latent space, VisMem offers a more holistic and biologically plausible solution to enhance VLM's perceptual fidelity and semantic consistency without the drawbacks of high computational cost or catastrophic forgetting seen in other paradigms. This work contributes to the broader goal of building more robust, intelligent, and human-like multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind VisMem stems from the inherent "visual processing bottleneck" observed in contemporary Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Despite their impressive abilities, VLMs frequently encounter difficulties in tasks demanding intricate visual perception, multi-step reasoning, or maintaining fidelity over extended generative sequences. This limitation arises from a tendency during autoregressive decoding to prioritize accumulated textual context over the initial visual evidence, leading to a loss of visual grounding and a deficiency in contextualized visual experience. The authors highlight that this bottleneck impairs fine-grained visual understanding, efficient reasoning, and robust generation.

To address this, the authors draw inspiration from human cognitive psychology, specifically the Dennis Norris Theory, which posits the existence of distinct short-term (visually-dominant) and long-term (semantically-dominant) memory systems. This cognitive distinction provides a powerful conceptual framework for designing a more effective memory mechanism for VLMs.

Based on this motivation and inspiration, the key objectives of the VisMem framework are:
1. **Develop a Cognitively-Aligned Framework:** To propose VisMem, a novel framework that systematically incorporates dual latent vision memories, analogous to human short-term and long-term memory.
2. **Implement Dynamic Latent Vision Memories:** To design and integrate two specialized memory modules:
 - **Short-term Latent Vision Memory:** Focused on retaining fine-grained perceptual evidence for immediate visual scene enhancement.
 - **Long-term Latent Vision Memory:** Aimed at consolidating abstract, high-level semantic knowledge derived from diverse experiences, providing generalized context for reasoning.
3. **Enable Seamless On-Demand Invocation:** To create a non-intrusive mechanism that allows VLMs to dynamically invoke and insert these latent memory tokens into the autoregressive generation stream during inference. This ensures that both perceptual fidelity and semantic consistency are maintained throughout the "thinking" and generation process.
4. **Enhance Comprehensive Visual Capabilities:** To significantly boost VLM performance across a wide spectrum of visual tasks, including understanding, reasoning, and generation.
5. **Improve Cross-Domain Generalization:** To enable the model to effectively transfer learned visual knowledge and reasoning capabilities to novel, unseen tasks and domains.
6. **Mitigate Catastrophic Forgetting:** To address the common issue in VLM fine-tuning where specific task optimization leads to the degradation of general capabilities or the forgetting of previously acquired knowledge.
7. **Ensure Compatibility and Efficiency:** To design the framework such that it is compatible with various base VLM architectures and sizes, while incurring minimal additional inference latency and computational cost.

By achieving these objectives, VisMem aims to unlock the full potential of VLMs, making them more robust, versatile, and intelligent in handling complex visual-linguistic interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VisMem introduces a cognitively-aligned framework to equip Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with dynamic latent vision memories, comprising both short-term and long-term modules. This is achieved through a two-stage training paradigm based on reinforcement learning.

The core methodology involves two main interactive parts: **Memory Invocation** and **Memory Formation**, carefully orchestrated to enhance VLM capabilities without altering the core model parameters intrusively.

#### 3.1. Memory Invocation

The memory invocation strategy seamlessly integrates with the standard autoregressive generation pipeline of VLMs.
- **Extended Vocabulary:** The vocabulary of the base VLM (`V`) is extended with four special memory-operation tokens: `<m_s_I>`, `<m_s_E>`, `<m_l_I>`, and `<m_l_E>`. These tokens serve as triggers for memory operations, where 's' and 'l' denote short-term and long-term memory, respectively, and 'I' and 'E' denote invocation and end.
- **Token Initialization:** The invocation tokens (`<m_s_I>`, `<m_l_I>`) are initialized using the embedding vector of a delimiter token with slight perturbations and are updated during training. The end tokens (`<m_s_E>`, `<m_l_E>`) are similarly initialized but with a lower learning rate, acting as structural markers.
- **Dynamic Insertion:** During autoregressive decoding, if an invocation token is generated, the corresponding memory former is activated. The generated latent vision memory tokens (short-term or long-term) are then inserted immediately after the invocation token. The corresponding end token is automatically appended, allowing the VLM to resume token-by-token decoding with enriched context. This mechanism addresses the VLM's tendency to prioritize textual context by injecting relevant visual memory directly into the generation stream.

#### 3.2. Memory Formation

The memory formation process is responsible for constructing the content of the latent vision memories. It consists of two key components:
- **Query Builder (B):** This component is a lightweight transformer encoder. Its role is to transform the VLM's current multi-modal cognitive state into an efficient memory query.
 - It takes the combined sequence of visual hidden states from the vision encoder (`{v_1..., v_y}`) and textual hidden states from the language model (`{h_1..., h_z}`).
 - A learnable initial memory query (`Q_init`) is concatenated with these hidden states.
 - The transformer encoder processes this concatenated sequence, and the output corresponding to `Q_init` (the last `K` encoded vectors) forms the contextual-aware memory query `Q`.
 - A masked attention mechanism is employed within the Query Builder to ensure that attention flows only from the query to the hidden states, not in reverse, maintaining query focus.
 - Both short-term and long-term memory pathways share the same Query Builder.
- **Latent Memory Formers (F_s, F_l):** These are two specialized, lightweight LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) adapters. They are attached to the VLM's vision encoder and the final language model, minimizing interference with the core VLM parameters.
 - Upon receiving a memory query `Q` (triggered by an invocation token), the appropriate memory former (`F_s` for short-term, `F_l` for long-term) processes the current token sequence `X`, the query `Q`, and a set of learnable initial memory tokens (`M_init`).
 - `F_s` generates latent tokens (`M_s`) encoding fine-grained, perceptual evidence.
 - `F_l` synthesizes latent tokens (`M_l`) representing abstract, high-level semantic knowledge.
 - These generated latent memory tokens are then seamlessly inserted into the VLM's generation stream, enriching the context for subsequent token prediction. The formers are designed to act as dedicated carriers, internalizing and externalizing visual evidence and semantic knowledge as needed.

#### 3.3. Training Recipe

VisMem employs a two-stage training procedure based on Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to optimize both memory content and invocation strategy.
- **Stage I: Memory Formation Optimization:**
 - **Objective:** Maximize the utility of the generated memory content.
 - **Components Updated:** Query Builder (B) and Memory Formers (F_s, F_l). The policy model (P) remains frozen.
 - **Process:** Initially, memory is randomly invoked upon delimiter detection, establishing basic memory capabilities. This is then expanded to random invocations between delimiters, enabling richer memory interaction trajectories. The optimization aims to maximize the performance improvement compared to trajectories generated without memory, thus enhancing the quality of memory formation.
- **Stage II: Memory Invocation Optimization:**
 - **Objective:** Optimize the policy model (P) to efficiently and accurately invoke the correct type of memory at appropriate times.
 - **Components Updated:** Partial parameters (`θ`) of the policy model (P). Memory formation components (B, F_s, F_l) are frozen.
 - **Process:** This stage focuses on learning optimal invocation patterns, including selecting the correct memory type and avoiding invalid invocations. Two penalties are introduced: a "type penalty" for erroneous memory type selection (e.g., invoking short-term when long-term is better) and a "negative penalty" for invocations that yield negative performance returns. These penalties guide the policy model to make more effective memory invocation decisions.

By meticulously designing these components and training stages, VisMem creates a robust, adaptive, and cognitively-inspired framework that addresses the VLM's visual processing bottleneck effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted across 12 diverse visual benchmarks for understanding, reasoning, and generation unequivocally demonstrate VisMem's superior performance and robust capabilities. The main findings are categorized into three key enhancements and three significant observations:

#### 4.1. Key Enhancements

- **[Enh.1] Advanced and Comprehensive Visual Capabilities:**
 - VisMem delivers a remarkable **11.0% average performance boost** relative to the vanilla VLM across all benchmarks.
 - It consistently **outperforms all 15 baseline models**, including the top-performing direct training methods (Vision-R1, VLM-R1) and image-level methods (OpenThinkImg), by substantial margins (3.0% to 4.9% respectively).
 - The improvements are comprehensive across task types: **+8.9% in visual understanding**, **+14.4% in reasoning**, and **+10.6% in generation** compared to the vanilla model.
 - Detailed subset evaluations (e.g., MuirBench, LogicVista) show significant gains in fine-grained tasks such as counting (+7.0%), visual retrieval (+9.4%), and grounding (+13.1%), as well as reasoning tasks like inductive (+5.7%) and deductive (+7.1%) learning, highlighting the specialized contributions of short-term and long-term memory.

- **[Enh.2] Cross-Domain Generalization:**
 - VisMem demonstrates exceptional cross-domain generalization. When trained exclusively on a limited set of general datasets (Visual CoT and Mulberry), it still achieves **significant performance gains (ranging from +6.9% to +20.2%)** on four unseen target benchmarks (MMVet, MuirBench, MV-Math, MultiTrust).
 - Crucially, its performance gap relative to models trained on full data is considerably smaller than that of baselines (e.g., only 2.3% performance drop for VisMem compared to 5.3% for VLM-R1 on MMVet), indicating robust knowledge transfer.
 - VisMem maintains a strong lead over the second-ranked models (2.7% to 6.8% across benchmarks) in these generalization tests.

- **[Enh.3] Catastrophic Forgetting Alleviation:**
 - In a four-stage continual learning experiment on MMVet, VisMem exhibits significantly **stronger knowledge retention** and effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting, a common pitfall for direct training paradigms.
 - While direct training methods (like SFT, VLM-R1) show substantial performance degradation (over 10% for SFT, near 0.5% retention of initial gains for VLM-R1/Vision-R1 after four stages), VisMem maintains consistent high performance, even improving at later stages by leveraging cross-task generalization. Its performance gap relative to original full-data training remains the smallest among all baselines.

#### 4.2. Key Observations

- **[Obs.1] Compatibility Across Base Models:**
 - VisMem proves to be **robustly compatible** with a wide range of base VLMs, including Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OV-1.5, and InternVL-3.5 families, with parameter sizes spanning from 3B to 38B.
 - It consistently yields **significant performance improvements** across nearly all visual tasks, regardless of the base model's architecture or scale. Smaller models often see greater *relative* gains, while larger models show more pronounced improvements in dense reasoning benchmarks.

- **[Obs.2] Dynamic and Self-Adaptive Memory Invocation:**
 - The dual latent memory system operates dynamically and adaptively. Memory invocation ratios and frequencies are self-adaptive across different tasks.
 - Both short-term and long-term memory invocations show a dynamic downward trend in frequency during the output sequence.
 - **Short-term memory** is more frequently invoked for fine-grained details and multi-image scenarios (e.g., MuirBench), while **long-term memory** plays a more critical role in reasoning tasks (e.g., MV-Math) by providing abstract semantic knowledge. This complementary nature is crucial for optimal performance.

- **[Obs.3] Relatively Low Inference Latency:**
 - VisMem achieves its substantial performance gains with **minimal additional inference latency**.
 - Its average inference latency is comparable to that of direct training optimization and token-level methods, avoiding the prohibitive computational costs and high latency associated with image-level paradigms (which can incur nearly twice the inference time of a vanilla model). VisMem's latency increase is controllable, ranging from 8.2% to 43.8% relative to the vanilla model.

In summary, VisMem not only significantly boosts VLM performance across a broad spectrum of visual tasks but also addresses critical issues like generalization, forgetting, compatibility, and efficiency, setting a new paradigm for latent-space memory enhancement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VisMem framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by effectively addressing the long-standing "visual processing bottleneck." Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

- **Novelty and Cognitive Alignment:** The most distinctive contribution of VisMem is its novel, cognitively-aligned approach to VLM memory. By drawing direct inspiration from human short-term and long-term memory systems (Dennis Norris Theory), it introduces a biologically plausible and intuitively powerful mechanism for VLMs to retain and utilize visual information more effectively. This paves the way for a new paradigm in designing more human-like AI systems that can "perceive and think" about the visual world.

- **Unlocking Advanced Visual Capabilities:** VisMem's ability to achieve an average performance boost of 11.0% across diverse and challenging benchmarks (understanding, reasoning, and generation) is highly impactful. It demonstrates that equipping VLMs with dynamic, dual latent vision memories directly enhances their core visual capabilities, particularly in fine-grained perception and complex multi-step reasoning. This broad improvement suggests a leap forward in VLM intelligence.

- **Robustness and Generalization:** The framework's demonstrated strength in cross-domain generalization and mitigation of catastrophic forgetting are critical for the deployment of truly versatile and reliable AI. The ability to transfer learned visual knowledge to unseen tasks with minimal performance degradation, and to retain prior knowledge during sequential learning, makes VLMs more adaptable and robust to dynamic real-world environments. This directly addresses major limitations of current models that often over-specialize or forget.

- **Practical Efficiency and Compatibility:** A significant impact of VisMem lies in its practical applicability. By operating in the latent space with lightweight LoRA adapters and introducing minimal inference latency, it avoids the prohibitive computational costs and delays associated with image-level interventions. Furthermore, its demonstrated compatibility across various VLM architectures and scales (from 3B to 38B parameters) ensures broad applicability, allowing existing VLM users and developers to integrate this enhancement without extensive re-engineering or resource demands.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** VisMem establishes a new direction for latent-space memory enhancement, encouraging further research into integrating cognitive theories into AI architecture. It opens avenues for exploring more sophisticated memory management, attention mechanisms, and reasoning processes within multimodal models. The availability of the code will facilitate reproducibility and encourage further development by the research community.

- **Potential Real-world Applications:** The enhanced capabilities of VisMem-equipped VLMs have profound implications for numerous real-world applications:
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Improved visual perception and reasoning for self-driving cars, drones, and robots, enabling better situational awareness and decision-making in complex visual environments.
 - **Medical Imaging:** More accurate interpretation of medical images, assisting in diagnosis and treatment planning by retaining fine-grained visual details and semantic context.
 - **Content Generation and Editing:** Creating more coherent and visually consistent long-form visual narratives or interactive content.
 - **Advanced Human-Computer Interaction:** Enabling AI assistants to understand complex visual queries and provide contextually rich responses, leading to more natural and effective interactions.
 - **Scientific Discovery:** Assisting researchers in analyzing complex visual data across various scientific disciplines.

In conclusion, VisMem's innovative blend of cognitive psychology and deep learning offers a powerful solution to core VLM limitations. By making VLMs more capable, robust, efficient, and versatile, it pushes the frontier of multimodal AI towards more truly intelligent and human-aligned systems, with broad and transformative potential across numerous domains.
