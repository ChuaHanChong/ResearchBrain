# Search-Based Spatiotemporal and Multi-Robot Motion Planning on Graphs of Space-Time Convex Sets

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces a search-based framework for time-optimal spatiotemporal motion planning on Graphs of Space-Time Convex Sets (ST-GCS), incorporating an Exact Convex Decomposition (ECD) scheme for dynamic obstacle avoidance. The approach integrates into prioritized multi-robot planning with a windowed coordination mechanism, demonstrating superior scalability by coordinating up to 100 robots in complex environments within minutes while maintaining competitive solution quality.

## Problem

- Existing sampling-based and optimization-based (GCS) motion planners struggle with efficiency and reliability in continuous, dynamic environments, especially when time-augmented state spaces or precise spatiotemporal reservations are needed.
- Monolithic mixed-integer convex programming (MICP) formulations for GCS-based planning in dynamic settings incur high computational demands, limiting scalability.
- Multi-Robot Motion Planning (MRMP) methods often face challenges in integrating continuous-space kinodynamic planning with robust coordination strategies, particularly for high robot counts or complex, dynamic scenarios.

## Method

- A **Graph of Space-Time Convex Sets (ST-GCS)** representation extends spatial ==GCS== into the time dimension, providing a unified framework for continuous trajectories in collision-free space-time.
- A ==best-first search (BFS)== algorithm operates on ==path-indexed states==, leveraging ==admissible heuristics (h_max)== and ==dominance checks (δ_pos)== to efficiently find time-optimal trajectories on ST-GCSs.
- The **Exact Convex Decomposition (ECD)** scheme dynamically updates ST-GCSs by precisely removing occupied regions of moving obstacles or other robot trajectories, ensuring continuous collision avoidance for subsequent planning queries.
- The ST-GCS planning and ECD are integrated into prioritized multi-robot planning (==PP==, ==PBS==) strategies, further enhanced by a ==windowed coordination== scheme that focuses computational effort on relevant time intervals.

## Results

- The single-robot BFS solver, using h_max and δ_pos, solved all **60** benchmark instances within subsecond median runtimes (e.g., **0.1s**), outperforming optimization-based baselines (**6.03s** median for MICP) and sampling-based ST-RRT*.
- Windowed-PBS + BFS demonstrated superior scalability for MRMP, successfully coordinating up to **100 robots** in minutes (e.g., **1.60 min** for 100-robot dense scenario) and consistently scaling across different environments for n ">= " **16** robots.
- The proposed MRMP approach maintained competitive solution quality compared to full-horizon optimal planners while achieving significantly higher success rates for large robot counts, validated through extensive simulations and real-robot deployments on up to **9** differential drive robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating spatiotemporal planning as a search on space-time convex sets allows for continuous-space trajectory optimization while leveraging efficient graph search techniques.
> - Path-indexed search, combined with admissible heuristics and dominance checks, effectively manages the coupled discrete-continuous nature of GCS problems, yielding significant practical speedups over monolithic optimization.
> - Exact Convex Decomposition provides a precise and efficient method for dynamically updating collision-free space, enabling robust handling of dynamic obstacles and inter-robot conflicts in continuous time.
> - Combining continuous-space low-level planning with high-level conflict resolution (PBS) and a dynamic windowed coordination scheme delivers scalable and high-quality solutions for complex multi-robot tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of robotic motion planning, specifically addressing challenges in spatiotemporal planning for single and multiple robots within continuous, dynamic environments. The broader landscape of motion planning is characterized by two primary paradigms: sampling-based methods and optimization-based methods.

Sampling-based planners, such as Probabilistic Roadmaps (PRM) and Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT), offer flexibility in modeling complex configuration spaces. However, their reliance on random exploration can prove inefficient and unreliable in environments with narrow, transient, or geometrically constrained feasible regions, especially when considering time-augmented state spaces where repeated collision checking becomes computationally expensive.

Optimization-based approaches, particularly those leveraging Graphs of Convex Sets (GCS), provide a deterministic framework for reasoning over convex decompositions of free space. While effective for static environments, extending GCS to dynamic settings requires a unified treatment of time, velocity constraints, and dynamic obstacle avoidance. Formulating this as a single large mixed-integer convex program (MICP) typically leads to significant computational demands, limiting scalability.

Multi-Robot Motion Planning (MRMP) introduces additional complexity by requiring robots to coordinate and avoid collisions with each other's trajectories. Existing MRMP techniques vary in how they handle inter-robot conflicts, ranging from coupled methods that reason in composite configuration spaces to decoupled and conflict-based methods like Prioritized Planning (PP) and Conflict-Based Search (CBS/PBS). These methods often struggle with integrating continuous-space kinodynamic planning, especially when precise spatiotemporal reservations are needed.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a novel graph representation, the Graph of Space-Time Convex Sets (ST-GCS), which extends spatial GCS into the time dimension. It then develops a search-based solver for time-optimal planning on ST-GCSs that operates on path-indexed states, aiming for practical speedups over monolithic optimization. The paper further presents an Exact Convex Decomposition (ECD) scheme for dynamically updating ST-GCSs to account for moving obstacles and robot trajectories. By integrating ST-GCS and ECD into prioritized MRMP strategies and introducing a windowed coordination scheme, the research provides a framework intended to be more scalable and robust for continuous-space multi-robot planning in dynamic environments, filling gaps in existing GCS-based and conflict-based MRMP literature.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to present an algorithmic framework for time-optimal spatiotemporal motion planning in continuous spaces, effectively handling dynamic obstacles and facilitating multi-robot coordination. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, each motivated by identified challenges in existing motion planning paradigms.

One key objective is to **formulate time-optimal spatiotemporal motion planning as a graph-search problem on Graphs of Space-Time Convex Sets (ST-GCSs)**. This is motivated by the limitations of traditional spatial GCS in dynamic environments, which require a unified representation that captures both geometric and temporal constraints. By extending convex sets into space-time, the framework aims to allow for continuous trajectories within collision-free regions that are explicitly time-aware, thereby providing a more natural and robust basis for planning in dynamic settings.

A second objective is to **develop a search-based solver that operates on path-indexed states**, accompanied by admissible heuristics and dominance checks tailored for ST-GCSs. This is driven by the computational expense of solving monolithic mixed-integer convex programs (MICP), which often arises when GCS problems are formulated as a single large optimization. The motivation is to leverage the efficiency of graph search algorithms while accounting for the non-local dependencies of cost and feasibility in GCS, where an optimal trajectory depends on the entire path taken, not just the current vertex. The use of heuristics and pruning strategies aims to significantly reduce the search space and improve practical runtime.

A third objective focuses on **presenting the Exact Convex Decomposition (ECD) scheme** to reserve trajectory occupancies in arbitrary-dimensional space-time. This is motivated by the necessity for accurate and dynamic obstacle avoidance in continuous space. For multi-robot systems or environments with moving obstacles, the trajectories of other entities must be precisely removed from the available free space. ECD is designed to update the ST-GCS representation dynamically, ensuring that subsequent planning queries operate on collision-free regions, thereby enabling a unified approach for handling both static and dynamic constraints.

The fourth objective is to **integrate ST-GCS planning and ECD into prioritized planning methods for MRMP**, and to further introduce a windowed coordination scheme to improve efficiency. Current MRMP methods, while sometimes scalable, often struggle with continuous-time, kinodynamic constraints or depend on discrete space-time representations that may lose optimality or completeness. The motivation here is to combine the continuous-space planning capabilities of ST-GCS with established high-level multi-robot coordination strategies like Prioritized Planning (PP) and Priority-Based Search (PBS). The windowed coordination scheme is motivated by the desire to mitigate the computational burden of full-horizon planning by focusing computational effort on relevant time intervals, thereby improving efficiency in large-scale scenarios.

Finally, the research aims to **provide extensive empirical evaluation** to demonstrate substantial speedups over existing planners and strong performance in challenging spatiotemporal scenarios. This is motivated by the need to validate the theoretical and algorithmic contributions against state-of-the-art methods in terms of scalability, solution quality, and practical applicability across various complex environments and robot counts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves establishing a continuous-space representation for spatiotemporal motion planning, developing a search-based algorithm to solve problems on this representation, and integrating these components into a multi-robot coordination framework.

### 4. Spatiotemporal Planning on Graphs of Space-Time Convex Sets (ST-GCSs)

The core representation is an ST-GCS, denoted as $G = (V, E, \mathcal{X})$, where $V$ is the vertex set, $E$ is the edge set, and $\mathcal{X} = \{X_v\}_{v \in V}$ is a collection of convex sets in $(m+1)$-dimensional space-time (m spatial dimensions, 1 time dimension). An ST-GCS can be initialized by extruding a spatial GCS through a time horizon $[0, t_{max}]$. Edges indicate non-empty intersections between space-time convex sets. A trajectory in this framework is a continuous, piecewise-linear path through a sequence of connected space-time convex sets.
The single-robot time-optimal planning problem from a start space-time state $x_s = (p_s, t_s)$ to a goal spatial position $p_g$ (with unconstrained arrival time) is formulated. This involves augmenting the ST-GCS with auxiliary start ($v_s$) and goal ($v_g$) vertices. The objective is to minimize the total elapsed time of the trajectory. Constraints include: path adherence to $G$, all trajectory states remaining within their respective convex sets, continuity between segments, time monotonicity within each segment, and component-wise velocity limits.

### 5. Search-Based Solver on ST-GCSs (Best-First Search - BFS)

A best-first search (BFS) algorithm is developed to solve the time-optimal planning problem on ST-GCSs. Due to the coupling between discrete path choices and continuous trajectory optimization, a standard vertex-indexed graph search is insufficient. Instead, the solver uses **path-indexed search nodes**, where each node records the full prefix path, its optimal conditioned trajectory, cost-to-come, and a search key ($g + \epsilon \cdot h(\pi)$).
The core subproblem, `PathOptimize`, takes a prefix path and solves a convex program to find the optimal continuous trajectory (entry/exit states) along that fixed sequence of convex sets, minimizing time while satisfying constraints.
A key theoretical justification is **Lemma 1**, which proves that for piecewise-linear trajectories and a time-cost objective, set revisitation is unnecessary for optimality, allowing the search to be restricted to simple paths.
To enhance efficiency, the solver incorporates:
- **Admissible Heuristics:** Three types are proposed and combined into `h_max`:
 - `h_mot` (Motion-Only): Estimates remaining cost based solely on velocity limits from the prefix interface to the goal position.
 - `h_tri` (Triplet-Relaxation): Uses precomputed lower-bound costs for sequences of three consecutive vertices (triplets), relaxing continuity between them.
 - `h_tab` (Interface-to-Set Cost Table): Leverages offline precomputed optimal costs from input-graph interfaces to input-graph sets.
- **Dominance Checks:**
 - `δ_set` (Safe Set-Containment): A theoretically safe check that prunes a node if an existing node can reach every state reachable by the current node from its prefix interface, guaranteeing optimality.
 - `δ_state` and `δ_pos`: Heuristic checks that offer more aggressive pruning by comparing only the stored witness states or sampled positions, trading optimality guarantees for faster practical performance.
- **Upper-Bound Pruning:** An incumbent cost, typically obtained from a faster (but potentially suboptimal) vertex-indexed search, is used to prune nodes whose estimated total cost exceeds this bound.

### 6. Exact Convex Decomposition (ECD)

The ECD scheme (Algorithm 2) enables dynamic reservation of spatiotemporal occupancy. Given an ST-GCS and a piecewise-linear trajectory $\tau$ (with object radius $R$), ECD removes the occupied region $\Omega(\tau, R)$ from the ST-GCS. $\Omega(\tau, R)$ is represented as a union of parallelotopes swept by the object's spatial body along each trajectory segment. For each convex set $X_v$ in the ST-GCS that intersects $\Omega(\tau, R)$, ECD iteratively slices $X_v$ using the facets of the intersecting parallelotopes as separating hyperplanes. The resulting collision-free convex subsets (and any residual parts of $X_v$ outside time-bounded intersections) replace the original $X_v$. The connectivity of the updated ST-GCS is then rebuilt by checking intersections among the new sets. This process allows for precise, dynamic updates of the collision-free space.

### 7. Multi-Robot Motion Planning (MRMP)

The ST-GCS planning and ECD are integrated into established high-level MRMP coordination strategies:
- **Prioritized Planning (PP):** Robots are planned sequentially according to a fixed priority order. When planning for robot $\sigma_k$, its low-level query (using the BFS solver) is executed on an ST-GCS that has been updated by ECD to reserve the endpoint-augmented trajectories of all higher-priority robots ($\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_{k-1}$). The "endpoint-augmented" trajectory includes segments where the robot waits at its start position before moving and at its goal position after arrival, ensuring continuous collision avoidance.
 - **Theorem 8** establishes the completeness of PP for well-formed MRMP instances with an ϵ-bounded low-level BFS solver.
- **Priority-Based Search (PBS):** PBS performs a search over partial priority orders to resolve inter-robot conflicts. If a set of trajectories from a PBS node exhibits a collision between robots $i_1$ and $i_2$, two child nodes are created, adding priority constraints $i_1 \prec i_2$ and $i_2 \prec i_1$ respectively. When a priority constraint is added (e.g., $i \prec j$), robot $j$ (and any robots lower-priority than $j$) is replanned, taking into account the endpoint-augmented reserved trajectories of all robots with higher priority.
 - **Theorem 9** establishes the completeness of PBS for well-formed MRMP instances with an ϵ-bounded low-level BFS solver.
- **Reuse of Base ST-GCS Heuristic Values (Lemma 10):** For h_tri and h_tab, heuristics precomputed on the initial base ST-GCS $G_0$ remain admissible for ECD-updated ST-GCSs because ECD only removes feasible space, not adding new, shorter paths. A "projected base interface" maps current path states to their equivalent in $G_0$.
- **Windowed Coordination:** To improve efficiency for large-scale MRMP, both PP and PBS can be applied within a finite planning window $W = [t, t + \Delta t_{plan}]$. ECD reservations are limited to trajectory portions within this window. Only the trajectory prefix within an execution horizon $\Delta t_{exec} \le \Delta t_{plan}$ is committed. A dynamic window adjustment mechanism enlarges $\Delta t_{plan}$ when planning failures occur, resetting it upon success.

### 8. Global Trajectory Optimization (Post-processing)

As a post-processing step, the piecewise-linear trajectories produced by the planner can be smoothed. This involves solving a Quadratic Program (QP) that optimizes sampled spatial positions at fixed time steps. The objective typically minimizes velocity variation and deviation from the initial planned trajectory. Constraints include fixed start/goal positions, velocity limits, containment within the original ST-GCS sets, and sampled linearizations of pairwise robot separation (collision avoidance). For scalability, a sequential rolling-horizon approach can be used.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive empirical evaluation across single-robot spatiotemporal planning and multi-robot motion planning (MRMP) scenarios, demonstrating key findings regarding performance, scalability, and solution quality.

### 10. Single-Robot Spatiotemporal Planning

- **BFS Heuristic Effectiveness:** Ablation studies reveal that the `h_max` heuristic (the maximum of `h_mot`, `h_tri`, and `h_tab`) consistently outperforms other single-component heuristics. It achieved a 100% success rate across 60 instances, reducing median runtime by 5.3–56.2x and expanded nodes by 11.5–56.5x compared to `h_zero`. Increasing the heuristic inflation factor ($\epsilon$) substantially reduced runtime (e.g., up to 91% for `h_tri` at $\epsilon=5$) at the cost of potential, though typically small, optimality gaps.
- **Dominance Check Performance:** Heuristic dominance checks (`δ_state` and `δ_pos`) significantly improved efficiency over the safe `δ_set` check and basic upper-bound pruning. `δ_state` reduced median expanded nodes by 42.7% and runtime by 42.1%, while `δ_pos` yielded reductions of 65.4% and 41.1%, respectively. Both heuristic checks maintained a 0% median optimality gap (relative to `δ_set`), with `δ_pos` having a higher worst-case increase (15.1%) due to its more aggressive pruning.
- **Overall Planner Scalability:** The proposed BFS solvers on ST-GCS (e.g., `BFS (δ_pos + h_max, ϵ=10)`) were the only variants to solve all 60 instances within a 60-second budget, achieving consistently subsecond median runtimes (e.g., 0.1s median for `δ_pos` variant). In contrast, optimization-based baselines (`MICP`, `MICP(g)`) solved fewer instances (31/60 and 59/60, respectively) and had substantially higher median runtimes (6.03s and 1.30s). Sampling-based `ST-RRT*` showed variable performance, solving 57/60 instances but struggling with some 'maze' environments. Discrete `Zeta*-SIPP` solved 41-46 instances depending on discretization, also showing domain dependence.
- **Solution Quality:** The search-based ST-GCS solvers preserved high solution quality. For instance, the `δ_set` variant with $\epsilon=1$ achieved a median cost of 8.53, close to the optimal `MICP` (9.70 median cost on common-success instances). On common-success instances where `MICP` provided optimal solutions, the inflated BFS variants and `MICP(g)` showed 0.0% median cost increase. However, ST-GCS-based planners may yield higher costs than non-ST-GCS baselines in domains like 'iris' where the underlying IRIS-generated convex sets offer only partial coverage of the continuous free space.

### 11. Multi-Robot Motion Planning (MRMP)

- **PBS Node Expansion Rules:** The "number of conflicts (NC)" rule for ordering PBS child nodes provided the strongest scalability for full-horizon PBS, solving 58/60 10-robot instances within 600s, outperforming rules based on sum-of-costs (SoC), makespan (MS), or lazy evaluation.
- **Windowed Coordination Effectiveness:** Dynamic window adjustment within `Windowed-PBS` significantly improved success rates compared to fixed window strategies. For a planning span factor $\alpha=2.5$ (shorter window), dynamic adjustment increased success from 36 to 50 instances. The optimal balance of scalability and quality was observed with dynamic window adjustment at $\alpha=5$ and a full execution horizon ($\beta=1$), achieving the highest success count (55/60 for 20-robot instances). Shorter windows (e.g., $\alpha=2.5$) generally yielded lower SoC/makespan but with reduced success rates.
- **MRMP Scalability:** `Windowed-PBS + BFS` demonstrated superior scalability, solving 338/360 instances for n $\ge$ 10 robots and 51/60 instances for n=20 robots. It was the only planner that consistently scaled across all three domains for n $\ge$ 16 robots. Baselines like `PP + BFS`, `PBS + BFS`, `Windowed-PP + BFS`, `PP + ST-RRT*`, `K-CBS`, and `CB-GCS` solved fewer high-robot-count instances. Runtime analysis for `Windowed-PBS + BFS` indicated that search and optimization within the low-level BFS solver were the dominant computational components, with ECD costs becoming substantial at larger robot counts.
- **MRMP Solution Quality:** `Windowed-PBS + BFS` maintained competitive solution quality. For n $\le$ 10 robots, its median SoC/makespan was 40.0/10.0, comparable to full-horizon BFS planners (e.g., PBS + BFS at 26.7/8.71) while achieving much higher scalability. Feasibility-oriented baselines like `K-CBS` had substantially worse solution quality (median SoC/makespan 66.0/21.7). `PP + ST-RRT*` could achieve higher solution quality in some domains (e.g., 'iris') due to its direct exploration of the continuous free space, but with lower scalability and domain-dependent performance.

### 12. Applications and Demonstrations

The QP-based global trajectory optimization successfully smoothed the planner's piecewise-linear outputs. Large-scale demonstrations showcased `Windowed-PBS + BFS` solving complex 50-robot (four-room, 1.64 min) and 100-robot (dense, 1.60 min) planar coordination tasks, as well as 32-UAV exchange in a 3D sphere and 48-UAVs in a cluttered 3D village. Real-robot deployment on 9 differential drive mobile robots for a rearrangement task (forming letters S, T, G, C, S) validated the planner's practical applicability in an indoor environment, demonstrating collision-free execution with smoothed trajectories.

### 13. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant contribution to spatiotemporal motion planning and multi-robot coordination, particularly in continuous domains. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Robust Continuous-Space Spatiotemporal Planning:** The introduction of the Graph of Space-Time Convex Sets (ST-GCS) provides a robust and theoretically sound framework for representing collision-free regions in continuous time-augmented spaces. This addresses a fundamental challenge in dynamic environments, moving beyond discrete grid-based methods or the complexities faced by sampling-based approaches in tight or transient passages. The ability to model both geometric and temporal constraints within a unified convex set structure is a key advancement.

- **Scalability for High-Density Multi-Robot Systems:** The integration of the search-based ST-GCS solver, the Exact Convex Decomposition (ECD) scheme, and windowed Priority-Based Search (PBS) significantly enhances the scalability of MRMP. The empirical results, demonstrating successful coordination of up to 100 robots in minutes in dense and bottlenecked environments, indicate a substantial leap beyond the capabilities of many existing MRMP planners. This has a direct impact on the feasibility of deploying large robot fleets in complex scenarios.

- **Efficient Hybrid Discrete-Continuous Planning:** By moving from a monolithic mixed-integer convex programming (MICP) approach to a search-based solver on path-indexed states, the framework effectively handles the hybrid discrete-continuous nature of the problem. The development of admissible heuristics and dominance checks for this path-indexed search improves computational efficiency, making optimal or bounded-suboptimal solutions more accessible for practical applications.

- **Dynamic Environment Adaptability:** The ECD scheme provides a precise and efficient mechanism for dynamically updating the collision-free space by removing the occupancy of moving obstacles or previously planned robot trajectories. This is crucial for reactive planning and sequential coordination in non-static environments, enabling subsequent robots to plan against accurate, updated representations of occupied space.

- **Validated Practical Applicability:** The successful demonstrations, including large-scale simulations and real-robot deployments, validate the practical utility of the proposed framework. The post-processing global trajectory optimization further refines the piecewise-linear paths, making them suitable for real-world robot control. This indicates strong potential for applications in automated warehousing, logistics, air traffic management for UAVs, and other fields requiring dense and coordinated robot movement.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays a strong foundation for future research. The identified limitations, such as the piecewise-linear trajectory representation, the assumption of known environments, and the general incompleteness of finite-window coordination, suggest clear directions for extending the work to more complex kinodynamic systems, online replanning, and improved windowing strategies with stronger theoretical guarantees.
