# Agentic Neural Architecture Search

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentNAS bridges LLM architecture design with conventional NAS. An LLM agent loop first searches for a strong seed architecture and training recipe, then decomposes it into a slotted architecture: a scaffold of named interchangeable module slots, identity-default glue slots and a learning-rate slot, which defines a bounded task-specific search space for evolutionary search. It reaches state of the art on 11 of 17 tasks.

## Problem

- Conventional NAS still presupposes hand-engineered search spaces that demand domain expertise and must be rebuilt for every new task and modality.
- LLM-based approaches use the model as a mutation operator or optimizer inside the search loop, conflating space expansion with space exploration.
- No prior work lets an LLM construct a bounded search space handed off to conventional NAS, so the LLM/NAS division of labor stays unmeasured.

## Method

- Phase 1 LLM-driven seed search: a **Planner**, **Code Generator** and **Data Explorer** loop proposes and trains candidates under a ==30%-epoch proxy==, 160 evaluations, returning a seed plus training recipe.
- Phase 2 ==slotted architecture synthesis==: a **Slot Planner**, aided by a **Modularizer** agent, converts the seed into ==module-level slots==, ==identity-default additive glue slots== and a ==learning-rate multiplier slot==.
- Phase 3 runs conventional NAS over the discrete slot space, ==regularized evolution== by default plus random search and GDAS, then fully trains the top 8 candidates.

## Results

- New state of the art on **11 of 17** tasks; on NAS-Bench-360 it wins **8 of 10** tasks with average rank **1.5** versus **3.60** for the expert baseline.
- On the blind Unseen NAS suite it reaches **13.06%** average error versus **15.04%** for the best conventional NAS method and **19.15%** for einspace.
- NAS on top of the seed improves Spherical **45.08** to **38.71**, CIFARTile **11.20** to **8.48**, Chesseract **33.84** to **31.39**; fixed-seed slotted NAS with **160** evaluations beats einspace's **500** on Spherical and CIFARTile.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Split the roles: let the model fix macro-structure, expose combinatorial freedom only at module level, where population-based search actually pays off.
> - LLM sampling saturates within a few hundred evaluations, while combinatorial recombination across slots still finds gains independent samples cannot reach.
> - Search-space quality, not seed quality alone, decides success: slots with no real variation in the layer that matters stall the search.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Neural Architecture Search (NAS) aims to automate the process of designing neural network architectures, historically a labor-intensive task requiring significant human expertise. Over time, NAS methods have become more efficient, with advancements in techniques like weight-sharing and evolutionary algorithms. However, a persistent limitation across these methods is their reliance on manually engineered search spaces. The construction of these spaces demands domain expertise and typically necessitates rebuilding for each new task, thereby constraining the diversity of discoverable architectures.

Recent developments have seen the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) in the context of NAS. LLMs, by encoding broad architectural knowledge from research literature, can generate or mutate network code within an open-ended search space. This capability contrasts with traditional NAS approaches that operate within predefined, bounded spaces. Existing LLM-based approaches have used LLMs as mutation operators, optimizers, or direct architecture generators. However, these methods often conflate two distinct roles: expanding the space of possible architectures and exploring that space. This conflation has limited empirical investigation into the specific value contributions of LLM-driven design versus NAS-driven search, making it difficult to determine an optimal division of labor.

This research addresses this gap by proposing a mechanism that explicitly bridges LLM-driven design and conventional NAS. It positions LLMs not merely as search operators, but as a component capable of *constructing* a task-specific, bounded search space. This space can then be effectively explored by conventional NAS algorithms, which excel at combinatorial search within defined boundaries. The work introduces a framework that allows for the independent measurement of contributions from both LLM-driven space construction and NAS-driven exploration, thereby seeking to clarify their complementary roles. It builds on the observation that bootstrapping NAS with human-designed architectures is beneficial, by having the LLM generate both a strong initial architecture (seed) and the surrounding search space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a mechanism that systematically integrates the architectural design capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) with the search capabilities of conventional Neural Architecture Search (NAS) algorithms. This integration aims to overcome the limitations of manually engineered search spaces and empirically determine the optimal division of labor between LLM-driven design and NAS-driven search.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Limitations of Manual Search Space Engineering:** Traditional NAS methods, despite their increasing efficiency, fundamentally rely on pre-defined search spaces. These spaces require substantial human domain expertise for their creation and often need to be re-engineered for every new task. This manual intervention limits the scalability, generality, and diversity of architectures that can be discovered, acting as a bottleneck in the automation of neural network design.
- **Clarifying the Role of LLMs in NAS:** While LLMs have demonstrated potential in generating architectures within open-ended spaces, existing approaches often merge the responsibilities of expanding the architecture space and exploring it. This conflation prevents a clear understanding of when and how each capability contributes value. The research aims to disentangle these roles by proposing a framework where the LLM's primary contribution is the *creation* of an effective search space, rather than direct exploration.
- **Leveraging Complementary Strengths:** LLMs can leverage their broad knowledge base to propose diverse, high-quality seed architectures and associated design principles. However, their sampling approach does not systematically explore combinatorial interactions between design choices. Conversely, conventional NAS algorithms are proficient at precisely this type of combinatorial optimization within a bounded search space. The research is motivated by the hypothesis that these two capabilities are complementary, with LLMs providing the initial high-level design and search space definition, and NAS performing fine-grained optimization within that space.
- **Automating Search Space Construction:** A core motivation is to eliminate the need for manual engineering of search spaces. By enabling LLMs to decompose a high-quality seed architecture into a "slotted architecture" – a scaffold with named, interchangeable module slots – the system automatically defines a bounded, task-specific search space. This removes a significant barrier to applying NAS to new or underexplored domains.
- **Empirical Study of LLM-NAS Interaction:** The research seeks to provide an empirical understanding of the interaction between LLMs and NAS. This includes quantifying the performance gains from LLM-generated seeds, the additional value delivered by NAS within the LLM-defined space, and the robustness of this division of labor across different LLM capabilities and task types.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named AgentNAS, is structured as a modular three-phase pipeline designed to leverage LLM-driven architectural priors for search space construction and conventional NAS for subsequent exploration.

**Phase 1: LLM-Driven Seed Search**
This phase generates a high-quality initial (seed) architecture and its associated training recipe through an iterative, agent-based process:
- **Planner Agent:** An LLM acts as a "Planner," which orchestrates the search. At each step, it selects one of three actions:
 - `<EXPLORE>`: Launches a separate "Data Explorer" agent to inspect dataset characteristics within a sandboxed Python environment.
 - `<EXPERIMENT>`: Proposes candidate architectures and their training recipes for evaluation.
 - `<STOP>`: Concludes the search and selects architectures for full training.
- **Code Generator Agent:** Implements the architectures proposed by the Planner.
- **Evaluation:** Candidate architectures are trained under a proxy protocol, typically 30% of the proposed full epochs, subject to GPU time and VRAM constraints (e.g., 30-minute GPU cap, 11GB VRAM for RTX 2080 Ti). Only validation metrics are used for in-search selection.
- **Budget:** The search is constrained to 160 evaluations and a patience criterion of 64 consecutive non-improving evaluations.
- **Output:** The Planner returns a seed architecture (its PyTorch source code) and a comprehensive training recipe (optimizer, learning rate, schedule, augmentation, batch size, epochs).

**Phase 2: Slotted Architecture Synthesis**
This phase transforms the seed architecture into a structured search space for NAS.
- **Slot Planner Agent:** An LLM receives the seed's source code, training recipe, and the Data Explorer's report. Its task is to decompose the seed into a "slotted architecture." This involves creating a scaffold where specific components are replaced by named, interchangeable module slots.
- **Modularization:** To prevent an excessively large or unstable search space, a "Modularizer Agent" is employed if the seed architecture exhibits a block-stack pattern (e.g., convolution, normalization, activation composed sequentially). This agent groups these primitives into coherent module-level units before decomposition.
- **Slot Types:**
 - **Module-level slots:** These replace significant architectural components (e.g., a stack of convolutions and activations) while preserving the seed's macro-structural decisions like depth, width progression, and backbone type. The Code Generator agent implements the alternative modules.
 - **Additive glue slots:** Interleaved between module-level slots, these default to an identity operation but allow NAS to introduce complementary components (e.g., additional operations, activation functions, skip connections) without altering the core modules.
 - **Learning rate slot:** A virtual slot is introduced for the learning rate, allowing NAS to adjust it using multipliers of the seed's original learning rate ({0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0}). This accounts for potential changes in parameter count or network complexity introduced by module replacements.
- **Output:** The result is a bounded, task-specific search space defined by these slots and their corresponding alternative modules. The number of slots is capped at 20, and each slot is capped at 8 alternatives.

**Phase 3: Slotted Neural Architecture Search**
This phase explores the search space created in Phase 2 using conventional NAS algorithms.
- **NAS Algorithm:** The default algorithm employed is regularized evolution. Other algorithms, such as random search and GDAS (a Gumbel-Softmax variant of DARTS), were also evaluated for comparison.
- **Search Protocol:** Candidate architectures are trained using the same proxy protocol as in Phase 1 (30% epochs, GPU/VRAM limits). The search is again limited to 160 evaluations and a patience of 64.
- **Population Initialization (Regularized Evolution):** The initial population of 16 architectures for regularized evolution includes the seed, single-slot mutations of the seed, double-slot mutations of the seed, and uniformly-random configurations.
- **Final Training:** After the search, the top 8 architectures (based on proxy validation metrics) from both Phase 1 and Phase 3 are fully trained and evaluated (100% epochs, 24-hour GPU cap). The architecture with the best validation metric is selected, and its test score is reported. If NAS does not improve on the seed, the second-best architecture's score is reported to unmask null-NAS scenarios.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Benchmarks:** The evaluation was conducted on 17 diverse tasks from two benchmark suites: NAS-Bench-360 (10 tasks spanning classification, dense regression, segmentation, multi-label tagging across 1D/2D signals) and Unseen NAS (7 classification tasks designed to test domain-agnostic search, including a blind setting where the LLM received no domain metadata).
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against various published baselines, including fixed architectures (WideResNet-16-4, ResNet-18), differentiable NAS methods (GAEA DARTS, PC-DARTS, DrNAS), evolutionary methods (BonsaiNet, regularized evolution on hNB201), expert-designed architectures, and recent LLM-integrated NAS (einspace).
- **LLMs Used:** Claude Sonnet 4.6 was used for all LLM roles (Planner, Code Generator, Data Explorer, Slot Planner) at a temperature of 0.7, with separate context windows. Ablation studies also utilized Claude Haiku 4.5 and Claude Opus 4.6.
- **Compute:** Each architecture was trained on a single NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti GPU (11 GB VRAM).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy of AgentNAS and the interplay between LLM-driven design and NAS-driven search.

**Overall Performance:**
- AgentNAS achieved state-of-the-art results on 11 out of 17 diverse tasks across the NAS-Bench-360 and Unseen NAS benchmarks.
- On NAS-Bench-360, it secured the best performance on 8 of 10 tasks, achieving an average rank of 1.5, which compares favorably to the next-best baseline (Expert designs) at 3.60.
- On Unseen NAS, operating under a blind setting where the LLM had no domain metadata, AgentNAS recorded the lowest average error of 13.06%, outperforming the per-task-best conventional NAS methods (15.04%).

**LLM Seed Strength:**
- Phase 1, which involved only LLM-driven seed search, produced architectures that already matched or surpassed all published baselines on the majority of tasks. This indicates that an LLM, when granted access to a sandboxed training environment, can function as an architecture designer capable of producing competitive networks without manual intervention or predefined search spaces.

**Additive Value of NAS:**
- Phase 3 NAS consistently delivered additional performance gains on top of the LLM-generated seed for most tasks. Notable improvements were observed on Spherical (from 45.08% to 38.71% error), CIFARTile (from 11.20% to 8.48% error), and Chesseract (from 33.84% to 31.39% error).
- In some cases (e.g., Darcy Flow, GeoClassing), the LLM-generated seed achieved near-ceiling performance, and NAS matched it without further improvement. This suggests that while the LLM provides a strong starting point, NAS can further optimize when the performance landscape allows.

**Limitations in Performance:**
- AgentNAS did not outperform the per-task-best baseline on 6 tasks (NinaPro, PSICOV, AddNIST, MultNIST, CIFARTile, Chesseract). This indicates that LLM-generated search spaces, while generally effective, do not universally dominate carefully hand-designed search spaces, and certain task-specific conventional NAS pairings remain highly competitive.

**Ablation Studies and Analysis:**

- **LLM Seed Search vs. Fixed-Seed NAS:**
 - The full AgentNAS pipeline consistently outperformed variants where NAS was initiated from a fixed, generic architecture (e.g., WideResNet-16-4 or ResNet-18) and refined through Phase 2-3. This demonstrates that the LLM's ability to design a task-adapted seed provides a superior starting point for NAS.
 - Even with a fixed seed, the LLM-constructed slotted space (Phase 2-3 with 160 evaluations) often surpassed the performance of einspace (500 evaluations) on tasks like Spherical and CIFARTile. This suggests that the LLM's capacity to construct an effective search space is a distinct and valuable contribution, independent of the seed quality.

- **NAS Complements LLM Sampling:**
 - Experiments comparing iterated LLM sampling with NAS demonstrated that LLM sampling often saturated in performance within 480 evaluations, producing largely independent proposals. In contrast, NAS, through its combinatorial recombination across slots, consistently delivered further gains beyond the LLM's saturation point on most tasks.
 - This pattern held across LLMs of varying capabilities (Claude Haiku, Sonnet, Opus), confirming the robustness of the division of labor. While stronger LLMs generally produced better initial seeds, the complementary value of NAS remained evident.

- **What Does NAS Change? (Mechanistic Insights):**
 - **Chesseract (Functional Compensation):** For the chess outcome prediction task, NAS frequently introduced functionalities missing in the LLM-generated seed, such as dynamic feature control via ReZero scaling or gated FFN variants. Where gating was already present (e.g., Sonnet's seed), NAS optimized its placement and composition. It also selectively added local mixing operations (depthwise convolutions) to global-only Transformer seeds and pruned redundant components.
 - **CIFARTile (Consistent Channel Attention):** On this tiled image classification task, NAS consistently enhanced channel attention mechanisms. It either added Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks where they were absent or diversified existing ones with complementary mechanisms like Coordinate Attention or ECA. This suggests channel recalibration was a uniformly beneficial design principle for the task.
 - **Darcy Flow (Search Space Quality):** For this dense regression PDE task, all LLMs generated Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) seeds. However, NAS success diverged based on the quality of the *search space* constructed by the LLM. Sonnet's search space failed to improve because it retained a channel-mixing spectral convolution across all alternatives, offering no meaningful variation. In contrast, Opus's more capable LLM included an alternative replacing this with element-wise spectral filtering, providing NAS with a simpler, more parameter-efficient design that it subsequently selected. This highlights that the LLM's contribution extends beyond the seed to the design of structurally diverse and effective search space alternatives.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a novel paradigm for Neural Architecture Search by establishing a clear and empirically validated mechanism for Large Language Models (LLMs) to construct search spaces for conventional NAS algorithms. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-fold:

- **Automating Search Space Engineering:** A primary impact is the significant reduction in the manual effort and domain expertise required to define NAS search spaces. By enabling LLMs to automatically decompose high-quality seed architectures into "slotted architectures," the pipeline automates a traditionally labor-intensive and expert-dependent stage of NAS. This lowers the barrier to applying NAS to new tasks and domains where human architectural priors might be scarce or underdeveloped.
- **Clarifying LLM-NAS Synergy:** The work provides empirical evidence for the complementary strengths of LLM-driven design and NAS-driven search. It demonstrates that LLMs excel at generating strong initial architectural seeds and defining structured search spaces, while conventional NAS is highly effective at exploring combinatorial variations within these automatically defined boundaries. This clarifies the "division of labor" between these two powerful paradigms.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generality:** AgentNAS achieves state-of-the-art performance on a diverse suite of 17 tasks spanning various modalities and problem types, including tasks where LLMs operate under blind conditions. This demonstrates the practical utility and generalization capabilities of the proposed approach beyond conventional, narrowly defined image classification benchmarks.
- **Insights into LLM Capabilities:** The research provides insights into LLMs' capacity for architectural reasoning. It shows that LLMs can not only generate functional code but also infer task characteristics (even under blind settings) and structure complex architectural decisions into modular components suitable for further optimization. The ablation studies, particularly the Darcy Flow example, highlight the importance of the LLM's ability to generate *diverse and meaningful* alternatives within the search space, not just the quality of the initial seed.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research.
 - **Search Space Optimization:** Further investigation into what constitutes an optimal LLM-constructed search space and methods to systematically improve it is a promising direction.
 - **Alternative NAS Algorithms:** Exploring other efficient NAS algorithms, particularly weight-sharing methods, within the slotted framework could yield additional performance gains.
 - **Expansion to Generative Tasks:** Extending the pipeline to more complex domains like generative tasks would further test its generality, though this might necessitate relaxing current computational constraints.
- **Contribution to Automated Scientific Discovery:** More broadly, this work contributes to the growing field of automated scientific discovery and multi-agent systems. By combining LLMs, programmatic evaluators, and evolutionary search, AgentNAS exemplifies how autonomous agents can facilitate the discovery of novel and effective solutions in complex scientific machine learning problems.
