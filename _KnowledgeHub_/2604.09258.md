# Nexus: Same Pretraining Loss, Better Downstream Generalization via Common Minima

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces Nexus, a novel optimizer that improves Large Language Model generalization on downstream tasks and out-of-distribution data while achieving comparable pretraining loss to baselines. It leverages a dual-loop mechanism to encourage geometric closeness of task-specific minima during pretraining, a distinct implicit bias shown to enhance performance without requiring additional computational resources.

## Problem

- Standard LLM pretraining minimizes averaged loss across heterogeneous data sources, but often converges to parameter configurations where individual task-specific minima are geometrically distant.
- This 'distance' between task-specific minima can hinder a model's ability to generalize effectively to unseen downstream and out-of-distribution tasks, even when pretraining loss is low.
- Existing optimizers do not inherently encourage the geometric closeness of task-specific minima, and it was unclear if this specific geometric property could be leveraged for better generalization independently of pretraining loss.

## Method

- Proposed =='closeness of task-specific minima'== as a distinct ==second-order implicit generalization bias==, separate from factors like loss landscape flatness.
- Developed **Nexus**, a novel optimizer that uses a ==dual-loop mechanism== to implicitly maximize the ==cosine similarity== of gradients from different pretraining tasks.
- The inner loop performs multiple ==Normalized SGD== steps on an auxiliary model to compute a =='pseudo-gradient'== that encourages task-specific minima to converge closer, which is then used by a standard outer optimizer.

## Results

- Nexus consistently improved average downstream benchmark accuracy by up to **3.2%** on **2.3B** models and achieved up to **15.0%** higher accuracy on GSM8k, **8.0%** on MATH, and **4.0%** on HumanEval for **3B** models, despite achieving nearly identical pretraining validation loss.
- The generalization benefits of Nexus scale monotonically with increasing model capacity and persist with extended pretraining durations, demonstrating robust and stable improvements.
- Nexus exhibits dynamic balancing, shifting performance gains to general knowledge and reasoning domains when math data is abundant, and resists overfitting to contaminated public datasets, enhancing performance on uncontaminated, sparse domains like coding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Improving the geometric closeness of task-specific minima during LLM pretraining directly enhances downstream generalization and out-of-distribution performance, even when the overall pretraining loss remains comparable to baselines.
> - Maximizing gradient similarity among diverse pretraining tasks serves as a tractable proxy for achieving geometric closeness of task-specific minima, avoiding computationally expensive direct optimization.
> - The multi-step inner loop of the Nexus optimizer is crucial for its 'consensus-seeking' property, dynamically balancing and implicitly prioritizing under-optimized tasks within a heterogeneous data mixture.
