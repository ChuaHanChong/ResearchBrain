# EmbodiSteer: Steering Embodiment-Agnostic Visuomotor Policies with Joint-Space Guidance for Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Deployment

> [!summary] Summary
> EmbodiSteer introduces a training-free framework that enables policies trained with embodiment-agnostic Cartesian actions to execute safely on diverse physical robots by lifting the diffusion process into joint space and applying whole-body collision-aware guidance. The system improved average task success rates by 28.5% in simulation and 36.7% in real-world scenarios, while reducing collision rates by 46.1% and 90.0% respectively compared to base policies.

## Problem

- Large-scale behavior cloning policies, often using Cartesian end-effector actions for scalability, lack awareness of the robot's physical body during deployment.
- Body unawareness leads to task failures due to whole-body collisions in cluttered environments or violations of kinematic limits specific to the deployed robot.
- Existing cross-embodiment generalization methods primarily focus on end-effector behavior, neglecting whole-body constraints and requiring morphology-specific training for safety.

## Method

- **EmbodiSteer** employs a ==training-free== inference framework that lifts a pretrained Cartesian diffusion policy's denoising process into the target robot's joint space using Jacobian-based updates.
- It integrates a CBF-inspired (Control Barrier Function) whole-body collision-aware guidance mechanism, formulated as an efficient Quadratic Program (QP), into each joint-space denoising step.
- The guidance minimizes end-effector and joint-space deviation from the policy's prediction while ensuring whole-body clearance, with a scheduled constraint strength to preserve learned behavior.

## Results

- In simulation, EmbodiSteer increased average task success rates to **64.2%** (a **28.5%** gain over Cartesian-only policies) and reduced average collision occurrence rates to **11.5%** (a **46.1%** reduction).
- On physical UR5 and Franka Panda robots, it achieved 47 out of 60 successful trials (a **36.7%** increase) and reduced collisions to 4 out of 60 trials (a **90.0%** reduction) in obstacle-filled environments.
- The system operates in real-time on physical hardware at **9.61 Hz**, demonstrating its practical deployability for robot manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embodiment-agnostic Cartesian policies can be effectively and safely translated to embodiment-aware joint-space execution at inference time without robot-specific fine-tuning.
> - Applying collision guidance directly within the diffusion denoising loop, rather than as a post-hoc correction, allows for more proactive and integrated whole-body collision avoidance.
> - Balancing task-preserving end-effector behavior with collision avoidance through a constrained optimization approach is crucial for achieving high task success rates in cluttered environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robot imitation learning have been significantly influenced by large-scale behavior cloning methods, including diffusion policies and vision-language-action models. A critical enabler for the scalability of these methods is the adoption of Cartesian end-effector poses as a standardized action representation. This choice renders actions independent of a robot's specific degrees of freedom, joint ordering, or link geometry, thereby facilitating the integration of diverse demonstration data from heterogeneous robots, simulators, and body-free data collection systems such as UMI (Universal Manipulation Interface). This embodiment-agnostic approach forms a foundation for cross-embodiment policy learning.

However, the embodiment-agnostic nature of Cartesian policies introduces a limitation: body unawareness during deployment. This becomes problematic when policies must adhere to robot-specific physical constraints, such as avoiding whole-body collisions in cluttered environments or operating within the kinematic limits of a particular robot's workspace. While training directly in joint space could embed embodiment awareness, joint actions are morphology-specific and incompatible with body-free demonstration sources, complicating zero-shot deployment across diverse robot platforms without extensive robot-specific fine-tuning.

Existing research on cross-embodiment policy generalization primarily addresses shifts in observation, state, and action distributions across different embodiments. Approaches include latent action spaces with embodiment-specific decoders, reusable low-level action primitives, or embodiment-conditioned policy representations. These methods largely overlook the execution-time constraints associated with the target robot's physical body. Consequently, even if end-effector motion effectively transfers, whole-body collisions may still lead to task failures. This work complements these prior efforts by focusing on the inference-time challenge of ensuring feasible and safe execution of an embodiment-agnostic policy on various robot arms, acknowledging that successful cross-embodiment deployment necessitates embodiment-aware joint-space motion that respects whole-body constraints.

The paper builds upon related work in steering diffusion generation, where inference-time guidance can modify pretrained models without retraining. While methods like classifier-free guidance require signals during training, classifier guidance can leverage inference-time gradients. This principle has been applied in robotics for steering diffusion policies towards objectives or user interactions. A related work, EADP, steers embodiment-agnostic policies for aerial manipulators by focusing on low-level controller tracking costs. EmbodiSteer distinguishes itself by specifically targeting collision-aware whole-body execution for robot arms through lifting Cartesian policies into joint-space sampling and applying collision-aware guidance.

In the context of collision-aware visuomotor policies, existing safety mechanisms often prioritize end-effector-level collisions. Approaches involving control barrier functions, model predictive filters, and risk estimators can be integrated as post-processing steps. Some recent works address robot-body safety by encoding whole-body geometry using point clouds, keypoints, or signed distance fields, typically requiring robot-specific training data. EmbodiSteer differentiates from these by employing a pretrained embodiment-agnostic policy as a base and introducing whole-body collision constraints during zero-shot inference, rather than requiring morphology-specific training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to address the limitations of embodiment-agnostic visuomotor policies, particularly their body unawareness during deployment, which renders them susceptible to failures caused by robot-specific physical constraints. The primary motivation stems from the recognition that while Cartesian end-effector actions provide a scalable and unified interface for learning from diverse data sources (including body-free demonstrations), this abstraction inherently lacks information about the specific robot's body.

The key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:
- **Leverage Scalability of Cartesian Policies**: The adoption of Cartesian end-effector poses as an action representation enables the aggregation of large-scale, heterogeneous demonstration data (from different robots, simulators, or body-free systems like UMI). This capability facilitates the development of generalist robot policies, and EmbodiSteer aims to preserve this advantage.
- **Overcome Body Unawareness at Deployment**: Cartesian policies, by design, are unaware of the deployed robot's full body geometry, joint limits, or self-collision properties. This leads to brittleness in real-world scenarios where the entire arm must avoid obstacles in cluttered environments, or where predicted end-effector motions might be kinematically infeasible for a specific robot. The objective is to introduce this missing embodiment awareness at inference time.
- **Enable Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Deployment**: Direct training in joint space would embed embodiment awareness but would be morphology-specific, preventing zero-shot transfer to diverse robots without additional, robot-specific fine-tuning data. The motivation is to achieve safe and feasible execution across different robot embodiments without requiring any robot-specific training or fine-tuning data.
- **Integrate Whole-Body Collision Avoidance**: A significant limitation of existing cross-embodiment generalization methods is their focus on end-effector behavior, often neglecting whole-body constraints. This work specifically aims to develop a mechanism to steer the entire robot arm away from collisions with environmental obstacles.
- **Maintain Learned End-Effector Behavior**: While ensuring whole-body safety is critical, it should not unduly distort the primary end-effector behavior learned by the policy, which is essential for task completion. The objective is to achieve collision avoidance while preserving the policy's intent regarding end-effector motion.
- **Develop a Training-Free Inference Framework**: To avoid the complexities and computational costs associated with retraining or fine-tuning policies for each new robot embodiment or constraint, the research aims to introduce embodiment awareness purely at the inference stage.

In summary, the core objective is to allow robot policies to benefit from the data scalability offered by embodiment-agnostic Cartesian learning, while simultaneously addressing the practical necessity of safe and feasible deployment on diverse physical robot bodies through an efficient, inference-time steering mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EmbodiSteer is designed as a training-free inference framework that operates on a frozen, pre-trained embodiment-agnostic Cartesian diffusion policy. The core idea is to lift the policy's inference-time sampling process into the target robot's joint space, enabling the integration of embodiment-specific constraints.

The methodology proceeds in two main stages:
1. **Joint-Space Denoising with Pretrained Cartesian Diffusion Policy**:
 - **Action Representation**: The base policy generates a chunk of Cartesian end-effector actions, A = [a_1..., a_H], where each action a_i consists of a relative translation and a 6D rotation. The gripper command is also included.
 - **Problem with Inverse Kinematics (IK)**: Directly solving IK for each denoised Cartesian action at every step of the diffusion process would be computationally intensive and impede real-time deployment.
 - **Jacobian-based Update**: Instead, EmbodiSteer treats the Cartesian denoising update as a local task-space residual. It maps this residual to joint space using the target robot's damped pseudoinverse Jacobian J_E⁺(q). This first-order kinematic relation is considered reliable for small Cartesian changes between adjacent denoising steps.
 - **Denoising Initialization**: The process begins by initializing joint-space sampling. Cartesian Gaussian noise samples (a_{T,i}) are converted into 6D pose twists relative to the chunk-start end-effector pose and then mapped to joint perturbations (q_{T,i}) around the current robot configuration (q_start) using the Jacobian. Joint perturbations are clipped to maintain stability.
 - **Joint-Space Denoising Update Loop**: In each denoising step 't' (from T down to 1):
 1. The current noisy joint action chunk Q_t is converted to a Cartesian action chunk A_t using forward kinematics (FK).
 2. The frozen Cartesian denoising network is queried with A_t to predict the denoised Cartesian target A_{t-1}.
 3. The Cartesian denoising residual (ξ_{t,i}), representing the pose twist from a_{t,i} to a_{t-1,i}, is computed.
 4. This residual is mapped back to joint space using J_E⁺(q_{t,i}), yielding a preliminary denoised joint configuration q_diff_{t-1,i}. Joint updates are clipped.

2. **CBF-Inspired Whole-Body Collision-Aware Guidance**:
 - **Rationale**: While joint-space denoising enables consideration of whole-body constraints, directly applying cost-gradient guidance based on collision penalties can inadvertently distort the end-effector trajectory, potentially hindering task completion. EmbodiSteer aims for guidance that prioritizes task-space preservation.
 - **Guidance Formulation**: For each preliminary denoised joint configuration q_diff_{t-1,i}, the method computes a guided joint configuration q_{t-1,i} by solving a constrained optimization problem. The objective minimizes:
 1. The deviation in end-effector pose between q_{t-1,i} and q_diff_{t-1,i} (weighted by W).
 2. The joint-space deviation between q_{t-1,i} and q_diff_{t-1,i} (regularized by λ_cbf).
 The primary constraint is h(q_{t-1,i}) ≥ d_safe, ensuring whole-body clearance, where h(q) is the signed distance field (SDF) value for the robot's body to obstacles, and d_safe is a safety margin.
 - **QP Linearization for Efficiency**: To achieve efficient, per-step updates, the forward kinematics and the safety function h(q) are linearized around q_diff_{t-1,i}. This transforms the optimization into a Quadratic Program (QP) with a single linear inequality constraint. The QP minimizes a quadratic objective function related to the joint-space change (Δq_i) subject to the linearized safety constraint.
 - The constraint strength (γ_t) is scheduled, being weaker in early, noisy denoising steps and stronger in later steps when samples are closer to the data manifold, to prevent premature over-steering.
 - **Solution and Application**: The single-constraint QP is solved efficiently (e.g., in closed form). The resulting optimal joint correction (Δq_cbf_{t-1,i}) is clipped and then applied to q_diff_{t-1,i} to yield the final guided joint configuration q_{t-1,i}.
 - **Whole-Body SDF Representation**: The robot body is approximated by collision spheres attached to its links, and obstacles are represented as known cuboids. The SDF value and gradient are queried for these spheres. A smooth maximum approximation over the top-k largest signed distances is used for the safety function h(q) to provide smoother gradients for guidance.

This integrated approach allows EmbodiSteer to maintain the learned end-effector behavior from the Cartesian policy while dynamically adjusting joint trajectories during inference to avoid whole-body collisions, without requiring robot-specific training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EmbodiSteer involved both simulated and real-world experiments, comparing its performance against several baselines in scenarios requiring precise end-effector motion and whole-body collision avoidance.

**Simulation Results:**
- **Setup**: Three manipulation tasks (PlaceToast, TurnOnFaucet, MakeCoffee) were conducted in ManiSkill. Policies were trained on 200 obstacle-free floating-gripper demonstrations, collecting only wrist-view RGB, end-effector states, and Cartesian actions, ensuring embodiment-agnostic training. Deployment was zero-shot on 9 simulated robot arms with test-time obstacles.
- **Baselines**:
 - **EE (Cartesian-only)**: Direct execution of Cartesian actions.
 - **Joint (Joint-space denoising without guidance)**: Lifts Cartesian sampling to joint space but lacks collision guidance.
 - **EE w/ Sampling (Post-hoc Cartesian)**: Samples multiple Cartesian actions and selects the one with the highest clearance.
 - **EE w/ CBF (Post-hoc Cartesian)**: Performs IK for the Cartesian action, then applies a one-shot CBF-QP correction.
 - **Joint w/ CG (Joint-space denoising with cost-gradient guidance)**: Applies collision-aware guidance using cost gradients during joint-space denoising.
- **Performance without Obstacles**: Both EE and Joint methods achieved high average task success rates (90.0% and 90.7% respectively), demonstrating that the base Cartesian policy transfers well across diverse arms and that joint-space denoising itself preserves end-effector behavior.
- **Performance with Obstacles**:
 - **EE (Cartesian-only)**: Performance significantly degraded, with an average task success rate of 35.7%, a reward of 0.614, and a high collision occurrence rate of 57.6%. This highlights the limitation of end-effector-only deployment when whole-body collision constraints are present.
 - **EE w/ Sampling**: Showed modest improvements, with 38.4% success and 54.8% collision rate, but incurred higher computational costs.
 - **EE w/ CBF**: Provided stronger post-hoc correction, improving success to 57.0% and reducing collisions to 17.9%. However, as a post-denoising step, it could not influence the trajectory generation process.
 - **Joint w/ CG**: Exhibited variable performance. While achieving a low collision rate on PlaceToast (0.2%), its success rate was poor on tasks requiring precision (e.g., 10.3% on TurnOnFaucet). This suggests that collision gradients, while pushing the arm away from obstacles, can significantly distort the end-effector trajectory required for task completion.
 - **EMBODISTEER**: Achieved the best overall balance. It improved the average task success rate to 64.2% (a 28.5% increase compared to EE) and significantly reduced the average collision occurrence rate to 11.5% (a 46.1% reduction compared to EE). This indicates effective whole-body obstacle avoidance while preserving learned end-effector behavior. Per-embodiment comparisons showed consistent gains over baselines.
- **Ablation Studies**: Analysis of constraint strength (γ) for the CBF-QP guidance showed an optimal range: excessively strong guidance could lead to task failure by driving samples too far from the learned policy distribution. Scheduling the guidance strength (weaker in early noisy steps, stronger later) helped preserve task performance while maintaining similar collision rates compared to constant strength guidance.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- **Setup**: EmbodiSteer was evaluated on two physical robots (UR5 and Franka Panda) across three tabletop manipulation tasks (Make Iced Coffee, Put Flower in Vase, Arrange Banana). 200 UMI handheld-gripper demonstrations (body-free, obstacle-free) were used for training a single Cartesian diffusion policy checkpoint, deployed zero-shot without robot-specific fine-tuning. Obstacles were introduced only at deployment.
- **Real-Time Performance**: EmbodiSteer achieved an inference rate of 9.61 Hz on an RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, demonstrating its feasibility for real-time physical deployment.
- **Results**:
 - **No Obstacles**: The base policy demonstrated competence, achieving 49 out of 60 successful trials across tasks and robots.
 - **With Obstacles**: The base policy's performance dropped significantly, with only 25 out of 60 successes and collisions occurring in 58 out of 60 trials. This confirmed the real-world limitation of end-effector-only policies in embodiment-aware execution.
 - **EMBODISTEER**: Recovered performance to 47 out of 60 successes (a 36.7% increase compared to the base policy with obstacles) and substantially reduced collisions to 4 out of 60 trials (a 90.0% reduction).
- **Qualitative Observations**: Visualizations indicated that while the base Cartesian policy often preserved task intent, it frequently collided with obstacles. EmbodiSteer, in contrast, effectively steered joint trajectories to avoid arm-body collisions, enabling task completion in constrained scenarios.

In summary, EmbodiSteer consistently demonstrated improved task success rates and substantially reduced collision rates across a diverse set of simulated and physical robot embodiments and manipulation tasks, outperforming Cartesian-only execution and various steering baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EmbodiSteer framework addresses a significant challenge in robot imitation learning by enabling the zero-shot, embodiment-aware deployment of policies trained on embodiment-agnostic data. Its primary significance lies in bridging the gap between the scalability offered by Cartesian action spaces and the practical necessity for robot-specific whole-body awareness during execution.

The key contributions and potential impact are as follows:
- **Enhanced Deployability of Generalist Policies**: By allowing policies learned from large-scale, heterogeneous, and even body-free datasets to be deployed safely on diverse physical robots without robot-specific fine-tuning, EmbodiSteer contributes to the development of more generalist and reusable robot manipulation policies. This capability reduces the substantial effort and data collection traditionally required for porting policies to new hardware.
- **Improved Safety in Cluttered Environments**: The integration of whole-body collision-aware guidance directly into the inference loop significantly enhances the safety of robot operations. This is crucial for real-world applications in cluttered human environments where physical interaction with obstacles can lead to failures or damage. The demonstrated reduction in collision rates in both simulation and real-world scenarios highlights this impact.
- **Task-Preserving Collision Avoidance**: EmbodiSteer's CBF-inspired guidance mechanism is designed to steer the robot body away from obstacles while minimizing disruption to the learned end-effector trajectory. This task-preserving approach differentiates it from methods where collision avoidance might compromise task precision, thereby increasing overall task success rates in constrained settings.
- **Computational Efficiency**: The methodology avoids computationally intensive inverse kinematics within the diffusion loop by using Jacobian-based updates and efficiently solvable QPs for guidance. This design choice enables real-time deployment on physical hardware, as demonstrated by the practical inference rates achieved.
- **Complementary to Existing Research**: This work complements existing efforts in cross-embodiment policy generalization by focusing on run-time physical constraints, which have often been overlooked. It offers a solution that can be integrated with various Cartesian diffusion policies, enhancing their robustness and applicability.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors**:
- **Local Guidance**: The guidance relies on local linearization and clipped joint corrections, which provides an efficient feasible direction but does not offer formal guarantees of global collision-free motion. This implies that in highly complex or dynamic environments, collisions might still occur.
- **Reactive Nature**: The guidance mechanism is activated only when the robot enters the SDF safety margin. It functions as a local correction rather than a global planner capable of anticipating and planning for optimal collision-free paths far in advance.
- **Potential for Distribution Shift**: Strong guidance, while beneficial for collision avoidance, can sometimes steer the robot into joint states that are outside the distribution of the original Cartesian policy's training data. This could potentially lead to task failures as the policy may not be robust to such out-of-distribution observations. This suggests that broader and more diverse training demonstrations remain important for robust deployment.

Despite these limitations, EmbodiSteer represents a step toward more adaptable and safer robot learning systems, offering a practical framework for deploying scalable, embodiment-agnostic policies in physically constrained environments. Its contribution lies in enabling policies to inherit the benefits of large-scale data while acquiring the embodiment-specific intelligence needed for reliable real-world operation.
