# Video2Sim2Real: Full-Stack Autonomous Dexterous Skill Acquisition from a Single Human Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Video2Sim2Real, a full-stack framework that autonomously acquires dexterous manipulation skills for robots from a single human video. The system achieves an average real-world task success rate of 95.7% by integrating object-centric trajectory refinement with a decoupled sim-to-real transfer strategy for robust policy learning.

## Problem

- Existing methods struggle with perception errors from human hand/object occlusion and the embodiment gap when transferring human video demonstrations to robot dexterous manipulation.
- Learning robust contact-rich robot skills in simulation faces a significant sim-to-real gap, requiring extensive data collection or leading to unsafe behaviors.
- Acquiring dexterous manipulation skills typically demands extensive task-specific supervision, posing a scalability bottleneck for robot learning.

## Method

- An autonomous pipeline reconstructs a simulator-ready ==digital twin== and extracts robot and object motion priors using off-the-shelf foundation models.
- A novel object-centric refinement strategy optimizes robot configurations at key manipulation frames using simulator information, then interpolates to create coherent robot trajectories.
- A decoupled sim-to-real transfer approach employs imitation learning (IL) for keyframe pose distillation (geometry adaptation) and residual reinforcement learning (RL) for local finger adaptation (physics adaptation).

## Results

- The object-centric refinement strategy achieved a **91.4%** average task success rate and **100%** safety in simulation, outperforming RL-based baselines by large margins (e.g., baselines 1.9-**52.9%** success).
- The decoupled sim-to-real policy demonstrated a **95.7%** average real-world task success rate across seven diverse tasks, significantly surpassing a pre-trained pose estimator (**15.7%**) and pure IL/RL methods (3-**45.7%**).
- The framework enabled spatial generalization, successfully planning collision-free robot trajectories for novel object configurations in cluttered environments for 5 out of 7 tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing robot configurations at object-centric keyframes effectively aligns human task intent with robot capabilities, leading to smoother and safer refined trajectories while mitigating the embodiment gap.
> - Decoupling sim-to-real transfer into distinct IL for global geometry adaptation and residual RL for local physics adaptation significantly enhances robustness and real-world performance for contact-rich tasks.
> - Leveraging off-the-shelf foundation models enables autonomous digital twin reconstruction and motion prior extraction, reducing the need for extensive human supervision in the skill acquisition pipeline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The acquisition of dexterous manipulation skills for robots presents a significant challenge due to the need for intricate coordination of arm and finger movements and robust handling of contact-rich object interactions. This complexity typically necessitates extensive task-specific supervision, posing a bottleneck for scalable robot learning. Human manipulation videos offer a potential solution by providing a readily available and rich source of demonstration data across various tasks, objects, and environments.

Prior work has explored methods to leverage human videos for robot skill learning, often by estimating human motions and retargeting them to robot embodiments. However, these approaches face two primary challenges:
1. **Perception Errors:** Hand self-occlusion and hand-object occlusion during manipulation can significantly degrade the accuracy of motion estimation from videos.
2. **Embodiment Gap:** Differences in kinematics and contact dynamics between humans and robots mean that directly retargeted human joint trajectories may not produce the desired object interactions in a robot system. This typically necessitates subsequent trajectory refinement.

Existing refinement strategies in digital twin simulators broadly fall into categories ranging from learning local residuals on retargeted trajectories, using motion-guided rewards, initializing policy learning from pre-contact poses, to entirely discarding human motion and training pure reinforcement learning (RL) policies based solely on object motion. The authors observe that approaches heavily reliant on raw retargeted trajectories are limited by their initial quality, while methods discarding human motion entirely can struggle with the high-dimensional state space of dexterous manipulation.

Furthermore, skills refined in simulation suffer from the sim-to-real gap, where discrepancies between the digital twin and the real world can lead to failures, especially in contact-rich tasks. Addressing this typically involves simulation randomization combined with either imitation learning (IL) or reinforcement learning (RL). However, pure RL can lead to unsafe or less smooth behaviors, while pure IL often requires inefficient data collection across randomized conditions.

This work introduces Video2Sim2Real as a full-stack framework that addresses these challenges. It proposes a novel object-centric refinement strategy that leverages simulator information at key manipulation frames to optimize robot configurations, serving as anchor points for refining retargeted trajectories. To bridge the sim-to-real gap, it introduces a decoupled policy learning strategy that assigns specific roles to IL (for geometry adaptation) and residual RL (for local physics adaptation). Additionally, it integrates a spatial generalization module for handling novel object configurations. The work positions itself as a comprehensive, autonomous system for skill acquisition from single human videos, aiming to overcome the limitations of prior fragmented approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable robots to autonomously and efficiently acquire dexterous manipulation skills from a *single human manipulation video*, without requiring robot demonstrations or expert intervention. This goal is motivated by the desire to alleviate the supervision bottleneck in robot learning by leveraging human videos as a scalable, intuitive, and convenient data source.

The work identifies four key challenges inherent in this problem setup:
1. **Autonomous Operation:** The entire pipeline, from video input to robot execution, must operate autonomously and be broadly applicable across diverse manipulation tasks, objects, and environments. This necessitates robust, off-the-shelf components and minimal human oversight.
2. **Reliable Skill Transfer:** Directly translating human hand motion to robot motion is often unreliable due to inherent differences (embodiment gap) and imperfections in motion estimation. Therefore, a robust refinement mechanism is crucial to produce executable and effective robot actions.
3. **Real-World Robustness:** Since trajectory refinement and policy learning primarily occur in simulation (digital twin), the resulting robot actions must be robust to the sim-to-real gap. This gap, particularly pronounced in contact-rich dexterous manipulation, requires policies that can compensate for discrepancies in geometry and physics between simulated and real environments.
4. **Spatial Generalization:** The acquired skills should ideally generalize beyond the exact object placements shown in the single demonstration video to novel object configurations, possibly in cluttered environments. This requires mechanisms for adapting trajectories to varying initial conditions.

To address these motivations and challenges, the authors propose a full-stack framework with specific objectives for its modular components:
- To autonomously reconstruct a simulator-ready digital twin and extract robot and object motion priors using off-the-shelf foundation models.
- To develop a novel object-centric refinement strategy that identifies object-centric keyframes, optimizes corresponding robot configurations using simulator information, and uses these as anchors to refine the initial retargeted robot motion. This aims to ensure the refined motion achieves the desired environmental impact while preserving temporal coherence.
- To introduce a decoupled sim-to-real transfer strategy that separates robustness concerns: using imitation learning (IL) to recalibrate keyframe robot poses from noisy real-world perception (handling geometry adaptation) and residual reinforcement learning (RL) for local, finger-level adaptations to dynamic hand-object interactions (handling physics adaptation). This aims for more efficient and robust policy learning compared to pure IL or RL approaches.
- To enable spatial generalization to novel object configurations through a collision-aware motion planning module.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Video2Sim2Real is structured as a full-stack framework comprising four main modules: off-the-shelf estimation, trajectory refinement, sim-to-real transfer, and spatial generalization.

#### 3.1. Off-the-shelf Estimation Modules

This module autonomously constructs a simulator-ready digital twin of the scene and extracts motion priors.
- **Digital-Twin Reconstruction:** Given RGB-D human manipulation video frames, a reference RGB-D scene image, and camera parameters, the process involves four stages:
 1. **Semantic Scene Parsing:** Gemini is used with key RGB frames and the reference image to infer a semantic scene description, identifying distinct tabletop objects, the manipulated object, target object (if applicable), and task type.
 2. **Open-Vocabulary Object Segmentation:** SAM3 segments each identified object in the reference image based on its text description, producing 2D binary masks.
 3. **Object-Level 3D Reconstruction:** SAM 3D Objects uses the segmented 2D masks, RGB-D data, and camera intrinsics to reconstruct raw meshes and initial camera-frame poses for each object.
 4. **Simulator Asset Export:** Raw reconstructions are canonicalized, transformed to the table frame, and exported as URDF assets. Mass, inertial parameters, friction, and restitution are estimated from mesh volume and material properties.
- **Motion Estimation:**
 1. **Robot Trajectory:** HaMeR estimates 3D keypoints of the human hand. These are transformed to the robot base frame and then retargeted to robot arm and finger joints using Mink, a differentiable inverse kinematics (IK) solver, which minimizes errors in fingertip and wrist poses. The resulting trajectory is smoothed.
 2. **Object Trajectory:** CoTracker tracks 2D flow points on manipulated and target objects. These are lifted to 3D using depth values and transformed into the table frame.

#### 3.2. Refinement Modules

This module improves the retargeted robot trajectory by anchoring it to optimized object-centric configurations.
- **Keyframe Identification:** Three key manipulation frames are identified using object-motion-based heuristics:
 - **Contact Frame (Tc):** Detected as the first frame with sustained motion of the manipulated object's 2D flow-point centroid.
 - **Interaction Frame (Ti):** For interactive tasks, detected from sustained changes in the relative motion between the manipulated and target object centroids after contact.
 - **Detachment Frame (Td):** Detected as the first frame after contact where the manipulated object's height falls below a threshold above the table.
- **Keyframe Refinement:** Robot configurations at these keyframes are optimized using the reconstructed object mesh, estimated object pose, and task label.
 - **Contact-Frame Refinement:** The raw hand-manipulated-object transform from the retargeted trajectory is replayed and corrected. For grasping tasks, this involves geometry-based correction (top vs. side grasps) and local grasp search using Lightning Grasp, followed by simulation-based filtering for stability. For pushing/pulling tasks, the desired motion direction is estimated from 3D object flow, a surface contact point is selected, and the fingertip normal is aligned against this direction to obtain the hand pose.
 - **Interaction-Frame Refinement:** The manipulated object's pose is estimated from 3D object flow, and combined with the corrected contact-frame hand-object transform to yield the desired interaction-frame robot hand pose.
 - **Detachment-Frame Refinement:** The detachment-frame hand pose is refined to release the object at its desired final pose, estimated from 3D object flow.
- **Trajectory Interpolation:** The refined keyframe robot hand poses are converted to joint-space anchors via IK. The final robot trajectory is generated by interpolating between these anchors and the raw retargeted trajectory, using DLS-based task-space correction and smoothstep blends across task-specific phases (e.g., hand-pose, pre-grasp, grasping, post-grasp tracking for grasping).

#### 3.3. Sim-to-Real Transfer Modules

This module learns policies to bridge the sim-to-real gap, decoupling robustness concerns.
- **Decoupled Policy Learning:**
 - **Imitation Learning (IL) for Keyframe Robot Pose Distillation:** An IL policy is trained to map raw object point clouds directly to keyframe robot hand poses. This handles global geometry discrepancies and enables robustness to local object placement variations. The model is a mask-aware PointNet-style residual network. Training uses supervised residual pose regression with input point clouds augmented with jitter and dropout, and randomized object positions/orientations.
 - **Residual Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Finger Adaptation:** An MLP-based residual RL policy is trained via PPO. It maps observations (current/next reference joint angles, 3D flow centroid, object-motion deltas) to finger joint residuals upon the base trajectory at each step. This policy focuses on local control and physics discrepancies, enabling robust contact interactions. A 3D flow-tracking objective with an action penalty is used as a task-agnostic reward. Training involves randomizing observation, action, control gains, object mesh scale, mass, and friction.
- **Real-World Inference:** The process involves four steps: 1) capture RGB-D data and track object point clouds; 2) distill keyframe poses from the initial point cloud using the learned IL policy; 3) adjust the refined trajectory via IK and interpolation using the distilled poses; and 4) apply the learned residual RL policy at each step to the finger trajectory using real-world feedback, before sending commands to a low-level controller.

#### 3.4. Spatial Generalization Modules

To support broader generalization beyond the video's exact object placement, a collision-aware motion planning module is introduced.
- **Collision-aware Trajectory Planning:** Using the digital-twin simulator and refined trajectory, CuRobo generates feasible robot motions while avoiding collisions. It plans paths to approach the object (based on randomized object positions and the relative contact pose) and transport it to the target (for grasp-and-place tasks, using intermediate waypoints). For push/pull tasks, only the approach is planned, with the interaction phase using linear interpolation.
- **Trajectory Filtering:** Planned trajectories are filtered for finger-environment collisions and implausible arm movements. A short-horizon IK refinement stage is performed after reaching the planned contact pose to align the end-effector with the desired contact pose, using the current manipulated object pose and the recorded relative contact transform.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Video2Sim2Real across seven everyday dexterous manipulation tasks: apple preparation, peach preparation, steak seasoning, toy rearrangement, tissue box handover, book passing, and tray retrieval. Skills are acquired autonomously, refined in simulation, learned, and deployed in the real world.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Refinement Strategy

The proposed object-centric keyframe refinement strategy was compared against five RL-based baselines in simulation: Residual RL, Deep-mimic RL, Pre-Contact Init, Opt-Pre-Contact Init, and Object-only. These baselines represent a spectrum of reliance on retargeted human trajectories. Each baseline was evaluated with both a simple flow-tracking reward and an auxiliary reward variant. Metrics included multi-stage task success rate, safety rate (absence of undesired collisions), and trajectory coherence (RMS jerk for arm and hand).

- **Task Success:** Video2Sim2Real achieved an average task success rate of 91.4% (±22.7%), significantly higher than all baselines, which ranged from 1.9% to 52.9%.
- **Safety:** The method achieved a 100% safety rate (0.0% standard deviation), indicating no observed table collisions. Baselines ranged from 13.8% to 56.7% safety.
- **Trajectory Coherence (RMS Jerk):** Video2Sim2Real produced arm jerk values of 3.7 (±3.7) and hand jerk values of 5.7 (±6.5), which were substantially lower than all baselines, indicating smoother and more coherent motions. Baselines showed significantly higher jerk values, often by orders of magnitude.
- The results indicate that keyframe optimization effectively aligns task intent while preserving the smoothness of human motions through interpolation. RL-based baselines were found to be limited by noisy retargeted trajectories or challenges in high-dimensional exploration.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Sim-to-Real Transfer

The decoupled policy learning method was evaluated against three sim-to-real baselines: Pure-RL (finger-only and arm+finger variants), Pure-IL (using DP3), and FoundationPose.

- **Simulation Robustness (under Randomized Parameters):** Under 50 randomized simulation parameters (object position, orientation, and mesh scale), the decoupled policy demonstrated superior robustness:
 - **Task Success:** It achieved the best average task success rate compared to other sim-to-real baselines.
 - **Safety:** It showed the second-best safety rate (after Pure-IL), while still producing safe behaviors.
 - **Completion Time:** It resulted in the shortest task completion time.
 - **Trajectory Coherence:** It produced the most coherent trajectories (lowest RMS jerk).
 - These findings suggest that the explicit role separation between IL (for geometry adaptation) and residual RL (for local physics adaptation) contributes to robustness.
- **Real-World Transfer (with Object Pose Variations):** Real-world experiments were conducted across the seven tasks with slight perturbations in initial object poses.
 - **Task Success:** Video2Sim2Real achieved an average real-world task success rate of 95.7% (64/70 trials across 7 tasks, 10 trials each), with success rates ranging from 8/10 to 10/10 for individual tasks.
 - In contrast, FoundationPose, relying solely on a pre-trained pose estimator, showed an average success rate of 15.7% (11/70 trials), indicating brittleness for contact-rich manipulation.
 - Ablation studies showed that neither the distillation policy nor the residual RL policy alone achieved comparable performance to their combination, underscoring the importance of the decoupled approach.
 - Pure-IL and Pure-RL baselines generally performed poorly in real-world scenarios, with average success rates of 5.7%, 45.7% (finger-only), and 3.0% (arm+finger), respectively. Pure-IL often resulted in jerky or unsafe motions, while Pure-RL (arm+finger) frequently led to unstable and unsafe arm movements.

#### 4.3. Spatial Generalization beyond the Single Video

The spatial generalization module, leveraging collision-aware motion planning (CuRobo), was evaluated for generating trajectories for novel object configurations in cluttered environments.
- For five of the seven tasks (Peach Preparing, Steak Seasoning, Tissue Box Sharing, Book Passing, Tray Retrieval), the module successfully generated collision-free robot trajectories for significantly different object poses in simulation, even with multiple obstacles.
- For Apple Preparation and Toy Rearrangement, the spatial module-generated trajectories did not consistently lead to successful replay in simulation, attributed to the waypoint pose tolerance of CuRobo. This suggests that for highly precise tasks, policy learning may still be necessary to make trajectory tracking robust under broad spatial generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Video2Sim2Real represents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning by demonstrating a full-stack, autonomous framework for acquiring dexterous manipulation skills from a single human video. The primary impact lies in its ability to address several long-standing challenges in transferring human dexterity to robots.

- **Autonomous Skill Acquisition:** The system's ability to autonomously reconstruct digital twins, extract motion priors, refine trajectories, and learn policies from minimal input (a single RGB-D video) significantly reduces the need for extensive robot data, expert annotations, or motion capture systems. This could democratize access to advanced robot capabilities, making skill acquisition more scalable and accessible.
- **Robust Trajectory Refinement:** The proposed object-centric keyframe refinement method offers an effective strategy for overcoming the embodiment gap and perception errors inherent in human video demonstrations. By optimizing robot configurations based on task intent and simulator information, it produces safer, smoother, and more successful trajectories compared to various RL-based alternatives. This approach provides a balance between leveraging human priors and allowing for necessary robot-specific adjustments.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The decoupled policy learning strategy is a key innovation for bridging the sim-to-real gap in contact-rich manipulation. By explicitly separating geometry adaptation (via IL of keyframe poses) from local physics adaptation (via residual RL for finger movements), the framework achieves superior robustness and real-world performance. This modularity allows each component to specialize, leading to more efficient learning and deployment.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The framework supports not only robust skill replication under local object pose variations but also broader spatial generalization through collision-aware motion planning. This capability allows robots to execute learned skills in novel scene configurations and cluttered environments, extending the practical applicability of the acquired skills.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to promising avenues for future research:
1. **Sparse Keyframe Refinement:** The current refinement strategy relies on sparse keyframes. Extending this to continuous trajectory adjustment could improve performance for tasks requiring complex in-hand reorientation.
2. **Heuristic Keyframe Detection:** The reliance on heuristic-based object-motion keyframe detection could be improved by incorporating more generalizable, pre-trained motion analysis methods for long-horizon dexterous manipulation.
3. **Non-Articulated Object Reconstruction:** The current digital twin reconstruction pipeline supports only non-articulated objects. Incorporating methods for articulated object reconstruction would enable the system to handle a wider diversity of object types.
4. **Hardware Dependency:** Future work could explore leveraging depth estimation methods to support low-cost RGB-only camera setups and introducing auto-calibration methods to eliminate the need for pre-placed markers, thereby enabling in-the-wild learning.
5. **Sensory Integration:** Integrating tactile sensing as an input to the residual RL policy could further enhance its ability to adapt contact dynamics and handle hand-object occlusions during contact-rich task execution.

Overall, Video2Sim2Real establishes a comprehensive and effective framework that moves closer to fully autonomous, generalizable dexterous skill acquisition for robots, with significant implications for robotic automation and human-robot interaction.
