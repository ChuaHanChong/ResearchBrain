# DreamControl: Human-Inspired Whole-Body Humanoid Control for Scene Interaction via Guided Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamControl introduces a two-stage method for autonomous whole-body humanoid control that leverages human motion data from guided diffusion models to generate natural reference trajectories, which are then tracked by a reinforcement learning policy for robust task execution. This approach enables a Unitree G1 robot to perform complex interaction tasks in simulation and in the real world with human-like motions and high success rates, addressing data scarcity for whole-body loco-manipulation.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with autonomous whole-body manipulation and loco-manipulation tasks due to difficulties in coordinating upper and lower body control.
- Acquiring sufficient real-robot teleoperation data for complex, long-horizon skills is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, creating a 'data gap'.
- Direct reinforcement learning for high-dimensional humanoid tasks often produces unnatural, 'robotic' motions that generalize poorly to the real world and struggle with multiple timescales.

## Method

- **Stage 1:** Generate natural, long-horizon human motion reference trajectories using a pre-trained diffusion transformer (OmniControl) conditioned on text and spatiotemporal guidance, then retarget and filter these for the robot.
- **Stage 2:** Train a reinforcement learning (RL) policy to robustly track the generated reference trajectories while simultaneously completing task goals and maintaining dynamic stability.
- Deploy policies to a real robot by retraining with non-privileged observations, using vision models for object localization, and employing a slow-down factor for safe execution.

## Results

- DreamControl achieved the highest success rates in simulation across 9 out of 11 challenging whole-body tasks, significantly outperforming baselines that relied solely on sparse or engineered dense rewards.
- Policies generated human-like motions with lower Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores against human motion datasets and lower average jerk values, and were overwhelmingly preferred by users over baselines.
- The system successfully transferred and executed 6 complex whole-body interaction tasks, including picking, opening drawers, and squatting, on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging readily available human motion data through diffusion models effectively addresses the data scarcity bottleneck for complex humanoid skills.
> - A two-stage approach, decoupling long-horizon motion planning (diffusion prior) from short-horizon robust control (RL), is effective for achieving both naturalness and task completion in whole-body humanoid tasks.
> - Human-inspired motion priors lead to more natural, dynamically stable robot behaviors, which improves sim-to-real transfer and human-robot interaction.
