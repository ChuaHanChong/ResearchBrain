# DreamControl: Human-Inspired Whole-Body Humanoid Control for Scene Interaction via Guided Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamControl introduces a two-stage method for autonomous whole-body humanoid control that leverages human motion data from guided diffusion models to generate natural reference trajectories, which are then tracked by a reinforcement learning policy for robust task execution. This approach enables a Unitree G1 robot to perform complex interaction tasks in simulation and in the real world with human-like motions and high success rates, addressing data scarcity for whole-body loco-manipulation.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with autonomous whole-body manipulation and loco-manipulation tasks due to difficulties in coordinating upper and lower body control.
- Acquiring sufficient real-robot teleoperation data for complex, long-horizon skills is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, creating a 'data gap'.
- Direct reinforcement learning for high-dimensional humanoid tasks often produces unnatural, 'robotic' motions that generalize poorly to the real world and struggle with multiple timescales.

## Method

- **Stage 1:** Generate natural, long-horizon human motion reference trajectories using a pre-trained diffusion transformer (OmniControl) conditioned on text and spatiotemporal guidance, then retarget and filter these for the robot.
- **Stage 2:** Train a reinforcement learning (RL) policy to robustly track the generated reference trajectories while simultaneously completing task goals and maintaining dynamic stability.
- Deploy policies to a real robot by retraining with non-privileged observations, using vision models for object localization, and employing a slow-down factor for safe execution.

## Results

- DreamControl achieved the highest success rates in simulation across 9 out of 11 challenging whole-body tasks, significantly outperforming baselines that relied solely on sparse or engineered dense rewards.
- Policies generated human-like motions with lower Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores against human motion datasets and lower average jerk values, and were overwhelmingly preferred by users over baselines.
- The system successfully transferred and executed 6 complex whole-body interaction tasks, including picking, opening drawers, and squatting, on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging readily available human motion data through diffusion models effectively addresses the data scarcity bottleneck for complex humanoid skills.
> - A two-stage approach, decoupling long-horizon motion planning (diffusion prior) from short-horizon robust control (RL), is effective for achieving both naturalness and task completion in whole-body humanoid tasks.
> - Human-inspired motion priors lead to more natural, dynamically stable robot behaviors, which improves sim-to-real transfer and human-robot interaction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of complex, autonomous interaction with their environment represents a grand challenge in robotics. While impressive strides have been made in humanoid locomotion and motion tracking (e.g., dancing, kung-fu), the transition to "universal assistants" requires robots to perform "whole-body manipulation and loco-manipulation tasks" – actions demanding coordinated control of both upper and lower bodies, often involving physical interaction with objects.

**Current Limitations Addressed by DreamControl:**
- **Simplified Manipulation:** Existing approaches often simplify whole-body problems by fixing the lower body, separating upper and lower body control, or focusing solely on computer graphics. These simplifications limit real-world applicability.
- **Multiple Timescales:** Humanoid control presents challenges due to the need to simultaneously maintain dynamic stability (short-horizon) and execute long-horizon manipulation plans (e.g., grasping distant objects). Direct Reinforcement Learning (RL) often struggles with this, leading to unnatural behaviors or exploration failures.
- **Data Scarcity:** Modern deep learning and imitation learning methods often rely on large datasets, but collecting teleoperation data for whole-body humanoid control is expensive, labor-intensive, and difficult to scale. This "100,000-year data gap" (Goldberg, 2025) is a major bottleneck.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Policies trained purely in simulation often struggle when transferred to the real world, partly due to a lack of naturalness in generated motions.

**DreamControl's Unique Contribution:**
DreamControl positions itself at the intersection of several key research areas:
- **Robot Manipulation:** It builds upon recent successes in diffusion policies for action generation (e.g., Diffusion Policy, $\pi_0$) and on-policy RL for robust control. Unlike prior work that relies on robot teleoperation data, DreamControl leverages the abundance of human motion data.
- **RL for Legged Robots & Humanoids:** It extends the achievements in robust locomotion and motion tracking (e.g., DeepMimic, AMP, ASAP) to fully autonomous, interactive skills. While works like HumanPlus and AMO also demonstrate autonomous execution, they still require teleoperated trajectories. DreamControl's distinction lies in generating these motion plans externally using human data.
- **Character Animation and Motion Models:** The work is heavily influenced by the rich history of statistical priors over human motion, particularly recent generative AI approaches like diffusion models (e.g., Human Motion Diffusion Model). It specifically builds on OmniControl, which allows text and spatiotemporal guidance for human motion generation, and differentiates itself from similar works like OmniGrasp, CloSd, and TokensHSI by focusing on richer, fine-grained guidance for robot deployment and addressing sim-to-real challenges.

By combining the strengths of diffusion models for generating human-like motion priors and RL for robust task execution, DreamControl offers a novel solution to the data scarcity and motion naturalness problems in whole-body humanoid control. It explicitly addresses the multi-timescale nature of these tasks by decoupling long-horizon planning (diffusion prior) from short-horizon robust control (RL).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of DreamControl is to enable humanoid robots to perform **autonomous whole-body skills for complex scene interaction**, moving them beyond pre-programmed demonstrations towards versatile, general-purpose assistants.

The core motivations driving this research stem from the limitations of current approaches in humanoid robotics:
1. **Bridging the Gap to Universal Assistance:** Humanoid robots are envisioned as future assistants, requiring them to interact dynamically with their environment (e.g., opening drawers, picking up objects, squatting). This necessitates coordinated upper and lower body control – "whole-body loco-manipulation" – which remains a substantial challenge.
2. **Overcoming Data Scarcity for Complex Skills:** Learning these complex, long-horizon skills often requires extensive robot teleoperation data for imitation learning, which is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to acquire at scale. The authors are motivated to find a data-efficient alternative.
3. **Enhancing Motion Naturalness and Robustness:** Direct application of RL to high-dimensional, long-horizon tasks often results in unnatural, "robotic" behaviors that generalize poorly to the real world. The goal is to generate motions that are both natural-looking and dynamically robust.
4. **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Unnatural motions or policies that rely heavily on simulation-privileged information hinder the transfer of learned skills from simulation to real-world robots. A key objective is to promote motions that are inherently conducive to real-world deployment.

To address these motivations, DreamControl proposes specific objectives:
- To develop a two-stage methodology that leverages **abundant human motion data** for generating motion priors, thereby reducing reliance on costly robot teleoperation.
- To integrate **diffusion models** for flexible, text- and spatiotemporally-guided long-horizon motion planning.
- To employ **Reinforcement Learning** for robust, task-specific control that tracks these human-inspired motion priors while simultaneously ensuring task completion and dynamic stability.
- To demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of this approach across a **diverse set of challenging whole-body interaction tasks** on a real humanoid robot.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamControl employs a novel two-stage methodology, aptly named for its "dream" of human-like motion guiding a robot's control:

**Stage 1: Generating Reference Trajectory from a Human Motion Prior**

The first stage focuses on creating high-quality, human-like motion plans without requiring real-robot teleoperation data.
1. **Human Motion Prior:** The core idea is to leverage the vast availability of human motion data (e.g., motion capture, video) to learn a prior for natural movements. The authors utilize **OmniControl**, a pre-trained diffusion transformer, which can generate human motion trajectories conditioned on:
 - **Text commands:** Natural language descriptions (e.g., "Pick up the bottle").
 - **Spatiotemporal guidance:** Specifying that a joint (or set of joints) must reach a particular spatial location at a defined time (analogous to image inpainting for motion). This allows connecting the motion plan to environmental interactions (e.g., object location).
2. **Retargeting to Robot Form Factor:** Since OmniControl generates human motions (using SMPL parameterization), these trajectories are **retargeted to the Unitree G1 robot**. This is achieved through an optimization problem (using the PyRoki library) that minimizes discrepancies in keypoint positions, relative angles, and scales for link lengths, while also incorporating physical plausibility residuals (e.g., foot contact, self-collision).
3. **Trajectory Filtering and Refinement:**
 - **Filtering:** A crucial step, as some diffusion-generated trajectories may not be dynamically feasible for a robot or might collide with the scene. Heuristic-based filters are applied (e.g., rejecting trajectories with excessive torso bending, low pelvis height, or scene collisions).
 - **Refinement:** Post-filtering, trajectories are further refined:
 - Prepend an interpolation phase to ensure a consistent starting pose for RL training.
 - Set non-functional arm joints to default positions (e.g., left arm during a right-hand pick) to avoid unnecessary movements.
 - For specific tasks like "Pick," an additional optimization step is performed to minimally modify the trajectory to avoid collisions with the platform, preserving the motion's smoothness.
4. **Novel Task Handling:** For tasks underrepresented in OmniControl's training data (e.g., pulling drawers), an IK-based optimization approach is used, starting from a base trajectory and optimizing joint angles to align with target wrist trajectories.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning with Reference Trajectory**

The generated and refined reference trajectories are then used to inform an RL policy, training the robot to execute the task robustly.
1. **Scene Synthesis:** For each reference trajectory, a corresponding simulated scene is generated. Objects are placed in the environment based on the interaction points and times specified in the reference trajectory (e.g., placing a box where the robot's wrist is expected to close). Environmental parameters like object mass and friction are randomized to enhance policy robustness.
2. **Action and Observation Spaces:**
 - **Action Space:** The 27-DoF Unitree G1 body joint angles and discrete open/closed states for the two 7-DoF hands (Inspire hands in real-world, DEX 3-1 in sim).
 - **Observation Space:** Proprioceptive information (joint angles/velocities, root velocities, gravity), previous action, and a window of **future target trajectory references**. This includes relative joint angles, velocities, root pose, keypoint positions, and target hand states. The paper acknowledges using privileged simulator information (e.g., global root position) and discusses adaptation for real-world deployment.
3. **Rewards:** The RL policy is trained with a multi-component reward function:
 - **Tracking Rewards (Table I):** Dense rewards penalizing deviations from the reference trajectory's joint angles, keypoint positions, root pose, and hand states.
 - **Smoothness & Stability Rewards (Table I):** Penalties for high torques, accelerations, action rate changes, foot sliding, excessive foot contacts, and encouraging parallel foot orientation to the ground.
 - **Task-Specific Sparse Rewards (Table XIII):** Binary rewards triggered upon successful task completion (e.g., object height above threshold for "Pick," drawer open amount for "Open Drawer").
4. **Training:** Policies are trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm within the IsaacLab simulation framework (IsaacSim) on NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs, utilizing 8192 parallel environments over 2000 iterations for each task.

**Sim-to-Real Deployment:**
For real-world deployment, policies are retrained with observations modified to remove simulator-privileged information (e.g., no explicit trajectory reference or root linear velocity, no object physics data). Vision models (OWLv2 for 2D, depth camera for 3D) are used for object localization to provide spatial guidance inputs for the RL policy. A "slow-down factor" is introduced for motion execution on the real robot to improve safety and mitigate sim-to-real gaps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DreamControl's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive evaluations in simulation and real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 robot across a library of 11 challenging whole-body tasks.

**A. Simulation Results (Table II):**
- **Superior Performance:** DreamControl consistently achieves the highest success rates, outperforming all baselines on 9 out of 11 tasks and matching the best on the remaining two. This highlights its ability to combine motion naturalness with task completion.
- **Failure of Sparse Rewards:** The **TaskOnly** baseline (sparse rewards only) yields 0% success across all tasks, underscoring the extreme difficulty of discovering complex whole-body motions purely through sparse signals.
- **Limitations of Engineered Dense Rewards:** **TaskOnly+** (sparse + engineered dense rewards, inspired by previous work) shows improved performance on simpler tasks like "Press Button" and "Precise Punch." However, it significantly struggles with tasks requiring coordinated whole-body movements and dynamic stability (e.g., "Pick from Ground," "Jump"). For instance, in "Jump," the robot might stretch its knees but fails to perform a true jump (Fig. 3), indicating that hand-engineered rewards alone are insufficient for complex, dynamically rich behaviors.
- **Tracking's Role:** **TrackingOnly** (only tracking rewards) performs generally well, especially for tasks that are primarily about motion following. However, it falls short on fine-grained *interactive* tasks like "Pick," where precise object manipulation and task completion are paramount, suggesting that tracking alone doesn't guarantee task success.

**B. Human-ness Comparison (Table III, Fig. 3):**
- **Natural Motions:** DreamControl policies produce significantly more natural and human-like motions compared to baselines.
 - **Lower FID Scores:** DreamControl achieves lower Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores against the HumanML3D dataset, indicating closer alignment with human motion distributions. The "Pick" task is an exception, attributed to a domain gap between human waist-level picking and the shorter G1 robot's shoulder-level picks.
 - **Smoother Trajectories:** DreamControl shows significantly lower average absolute jerk values (Table III, second-to-last column), demonstrating smoother and more fluid movements.
 - **User Preference:** A user study with 40 participants overwhelmingly favored DreamControl's trajectories as more human-like across all tasks (Table III, last column), reinforcing the qualitative assessment.
- **Visual Evidence (Fig. 3):** The visual comparison for the "Jump" task clearly illustrates DreamControl's robot executing a natural bending-and-springing motion, whereas the TaskOnly+ baseline simply lifts off without the preparatory crouch, failing to achieve a human-like jump and often failing the task itself.

**C. Sim-to-Real Deployment:**
- **Successful Real-World Transfer:** DreamControl successfully transfers learned policies to a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot for six tasks: "Pick" (standing), "Bimanual Pick" (varying weights), "Press Button" (standing), "Open Drawer" (different positions), "Precise Punch" (standing), and "Squat" (varying depths).
- **Adaptation for Realism:** Policies are retrained with non-privileged observations, and off-the-shelf vision models (OWLv2 + depth) are used for real-time object localization. Challenges include vision latency (leading to static object estimates) and the need for lower body freezing in some tasks to ensure stability, indicating that perception remains a key bottleneck.
- **Safety and Robustness:** The introduction of a "slow-down factor" for real-world execution highlights a practical approach to mitigating minor sim-to-real gaps and ensuring safety, particularly for precise interaction tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamControl represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics, offering a compelling methodology to tackle long-standing challenges in learning complex, autonomous whole-body skills.

1. **Addresses the "Data Gap":** The most profound impact is its solution to the data scarcity problem. By leveraging readily available human motion data through diffusion priors, DreamControl circumvents the expensive and time-consuming process of collecting robot teleoperation data. This paradigm shift democratizes access to sophisticated motion generation for humanoid control, making it more scalable and sustainable.
2. **Enables Human-Inspired, Natural Motions:** The human motion-informed prior inherently promotes natural-looking and smooth motions. This is critical for two reasons:
 - **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Natural motions are generally more dynamically stable and less prone to "robot-specific" artifacts, bridging the reality gap.
 - **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots that move naturally are more likely to be accepted and interacted with safely and intuitively by humans.
3. **Robust Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation:** The two-stage approach effectively decouples long-horizon planning from robust, short-horizon control. The diffusion prior handles the complex choreography of whole-body movements, while RL ensures stability, balance, and task execution under varying conditions, leading to robust performance across diverse, challenging tasks.
4. **Scalable "Recipe" for Skill Acquisition:** DreamControl provides a generalized framework for training a library of humanoid skills. This "recipe" can be extended to new tasks by simply defining text prompts and spatiotemporal guidance, rather than requiring extensive re-engineering or data collection for each new skill.
5. **Foundation for Future Generalist Humanoids:** The successful deployment on a real Unitree G1 robot, despite current perception bottlenecks, validates the core methodology. This work lays a strong foundation for future research into more capable and general-purpose humanoid robots, with explicit avenues for expansion into:
 - **Skill Composition:** Combining learned primitive skills for more complex, long-horizon scenarios.
 - **Dexterous Manipulation:** Integrating finer control of hands and fingers.
 - **Complex Object Geometries:** Handling a wider variety of objects and interaction types.
 - **Advanced Vision-Based Control:** Overcoming current perception limitations through student-teacher distillation or end-to-end vision-based policies.

In summary, DreamControl pushes the boundaries of autonomous humanoid control by creatively combining generative AI with reinforcement learning. Its ability to generate natural, robust, and task-specific whole-body motions from human data, and successfully deploy them to the real world, represents a crucial step towards realizing the vision of humanoids as truly versatile and intelligent assistants.
