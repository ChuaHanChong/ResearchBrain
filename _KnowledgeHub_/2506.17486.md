# Distilling On-device Language Models for Robot Planning with Minimal Human Intervention

> [!summary] Summary
> The PRISM framework distills powerful cloud-based Large Language Models (LLMs) into efficient Small Language Models (SLMs) that operate directly on robotic hardware for planning tasks. This approach enables on-device SLMs to achieve planning success rates comparable to frontier LLMs while significantly reducing latency and eliminating cloud dependency for robot control.

## Problem

- LLM-enabled robots currently rely on cloud computing, making them vulnerable to unreliable network communication in challenging operational environments.
- On-device Small Language Models (SLMs) have historically shown inadequate performance for complex robot planning tasks, limiting their direct use as alternatives.
- Developing high-quality datasets for robot planning requires extensive human intervention, manual annotation, or reliance on simulators, which is resource-intensive.

## Method

- **PRISM**, a three-module ==self-distillation== framework, automatically synthesizes diverse tasks and environment representations using a generative LLM, requiring minimal human input.
- It employs a closed-loop plan elicitation process, including ==environment masking== and ==plan validation==, to extract high-quality planning demonstrations from a source LLM-enabled planner.
- Target SLMs are trained using ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== with ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== on the collected task-observation-action sequences, enabling efficient on-device deployment (under 5GB memory footprint).

## Results

- **PRISM**-distilled SLMs achieved planning success rates closely matching GPT-4o across three benchmarks (e.g., **92%** vs. **98%** for SPINE, **50%** vs. **50%** for SayCan, **15.54%** vs. **16.49%** for LLM-Planner), representing at least a **93%** performance increase over undistilled SLMs.
- On-device **PRISM** models demonstrated query times within **200 milliseconds**, substantially faster than cloud-based GPT-4o, which averaged **3.64 seconds** (minimal latency) and **4.90 seconds** (200ms added latency).
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of environment masking (**27.3%** performance drop when removed) and plan validation (**45.5%** performance drop when removed) for effective SLM distillation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-performance, cloud-based LLM planning capabilities can be effectively distilled into compact, on-device SLMs that maintain comparable task success rates.
> - Automated data synthesis, through generative LLMs and a closed-loop planning emulation with environment masking and plan validation, is crucial for creating robust training datasets without manual curation.
> - On-device deployment of distilled SLMs provides significantly faster and more deterministic planning execution (under 200ms query times) compared to cloud-dependent LLMs, crucial for real-time robotic applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into robotic systems has advanced capabilities such as contextual reasoning and natural human-robot interaction across diverse applications, including mobile manipulation, autonomous driving, service robotics, and navigation. LLMs leverage extensive internet-scale training data, offering rich semantic knowledge and adaptability, often requiring only a few in-context examples to generalize to new robotic scenarios.

Despite these advantages, the computational scale of frontier LLMs, often comprising hundreds of billions of parameters, necessitates substantial cloud computing infrastructure. This dependency on cloud-hosted models, exemplified by systems utilizing GPT or PALM, renders LLM-enabled robots vulnerable to unreliable communication in environments such as outdoor, industrial, or disaster response settings. While Small Language Models (SLMs) can operate on-device, their performance in robot planning domains has historically been inadequate for widespread adoption, preventing them from serving as viable on-board alternatives.

This work addresses the fundamental challenge of enabling powerful language model-driven robot planning to operate independently of cloud infrastructure. It fits into the broader research landscape by bridging the gap between the high performance of cloud-dependent LLMs and the computational constraints of on-device deployment. It draws upon existing research in:
- **LLM-Enabled Robots:** Acknowledging the success of LLMs in various robotic applications but highlighting their inherent cloud dependency.
- **LLM Distillation:** Utilizing established techniques for training smaller models to emulate the capabilities of larger LLMs. Prior distillation research primarily focuses on virtual domains or general language tasks, with emerging work on self-distillation that generates both queries and responses.
- **Distilling Robot Planners:** Recognizing previous applications of distillation in virtual planning domains (e.g., web search, video games) and some robotic contexts (e.g., multi-robot control, manipulation). However, existing methods often require external knowledge like simulators or curated datasets, and typically distill auxiliary planners rather than direct drop-in replacements for the source LLM.

The presented framework, PRISM, differentiates itself by focusing on self-distillation for robot planning that is grounded in the physical world. It synthesizes all training data directly from an existing LLM-enabled planner and aims to produce a compact, on-device SLM that can directly replace the source LLM, thereby overcoming the limitations of network reliance for real-world robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust and autonomous robot planning capabilities in environments where reliable internet connectivity to cloud-hosted LLMs is not guaranteed. This objective is motivated by the significant limitations imposed by the cloud dependency of current LLM-enabled robotic systems, which restricts their utility in critical applications like environmental monitoring, industrial inspection, and disaster response.

To address these limitations, the authors introduce PRISM (Planning with Robotic dIstilled Small language Models), a framework with the following key objectives:

- **Effective Distillation of SLMs:** The core goal is to distill SLMs that can replicate the planning performance of high-capacity, cloud-based LLM-enabled planners, such as GPT-4o. This involves ensuring that the distilled SLMs maintain a comparable success rate in executing complex robotic tasks.
- **On-Device Deployment:** A central objective is to produce language models compact enough to run directly on a robot's embedded computational hardware. This is defined as models with a memory footprint under 5GB, enabling real-time, closed-loop control without reliance on external compute.
- **Minimal Human Intervention:** The framework aims to reduce the manual effort typically associated with dataset creation and model training for robot planning. This involves automatically synthesizing diverse tasks and environment representations, eliciting plans from a source LLM without requiring manual annotations, simulators, or extensive dataset curation. Users are required only to provide the existing LLM-enabled planner along with its action and observation spaces.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from the observed performance gap between computationally efficient SLMs and powerful, but resource-intensive, LLMs in robot planning. While SLMs can operate on-device, their inherent performance often falls short. Conversely, LLMs offer superior contextual reasoning but demand cloud infrastructure, which introduces latency, costs, and vulnerability in challenging operational environments. PRISM seeks to resolve this trade-off by leveraging the high-quality reasoning of LLMs to automatically generate training data for SLMs, thereby producing capable on-device planners.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRISM operates as a three-module framework designed to distill a small language model (SLM) that replicates the performance of an existing LLM-enabled planner while running on-device with minimal human intervention. The framework takes a source LLM-enabled planner, $\pi_{\text{LLM}}(a_i | o_i, s)$, which processes an observation $o_i$ and a task $t$ to produce an action $a_i$ at planning iteration $i$. The objective is to train a target SLM, $\pi_{\text{SLM}}(a_i | o_i, t)$, to mimic this behavior efficiently.

The methodology comprises the following stages:

#### 3.1. Scenario Generation

This module is responsible for synthesizing diverse robotic tasks and their corresponding textual environment representations.
- **Input:** The action and observation spaces of the source LLM-enabled planner.
- **Tool:** A generative LLM (GPT-4o in this work) is used to create scenarios. This LLM is prompted with the desired observation format, environment size, and number of tasks. A high temperature (set to 1) is employed during generation to promote data diversity.
- **Outputs:** Each scenario consists of an environment (which will produce observations for the planner) and semantically coherent tasks. A description is also generated to ensure semantic coherence.
- **Verification:** All generated environments are checked for adherence to the required representation format of the source planner; erroneous generations trigger re-prompting.
- **Observation Representations:** The framework supports various observation formats, including scene graphs (nodes for regions/objects, edges for traversable paths/visibility, semantic descriptions) and object sets (lists of accessible objects).

#### 3.2. Plan Elicitation

This module extracts high-quality planning demonstrations from the source LLM-enabled planner using the synthesized scenarios.
- **Environment Masking:** For each synthesized scenario, an initial partial observation ($o_1$) is created by randomly obscuring portions of the environment (e.g., removing nodes/edges from a graph, or objects from a set). This simulates partially known environments.
- **Closed-Loop Planning Emulation:** The module emulates the closed-loop interaction between the robot and environment. Given an action $a_i$ from $\pi_{\text{LLM}}$ and the synthesized environment, the module simulates the resulting observation $o_{i+1}$. This involves defining how each action (e.g., `map` to reveal hidden objects, `goto` to change location) affects the environment representation.
- **Iterative Planning and Validation:** The source LLM-enabled planner is seeded with the initial partial observation and iteratively generates actions. This process continues until the planner signals task completion (e.g., via a `done` action) or reaches a predefined maximum number of planning iterations. If the planner times out, the entire sequence is considered invalid and discarded during validation. This validation step is crucial for filtering out suboptimal or erroneous LLM behavior.
- **Dataset Collection:** The output of this module is a dataset $D$ comprising sequences of task-observation-action pairs (rollouts): $D = \{s_i, (o^i_1, a^i_1, \dots, o^i_T, a^i_T)\}_{i=1}^N$.

#### 3.3. Planner Distillation

The collected dataset $D$ is then used to train the target SLM.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The target SLM, such as Llama-3.2-3B, is trained using SFT to minimize the cross-entropy loss over all actions $a^i_1, \dots, a^i_T$ in the dataset. Each action prediction is conditioned on the cumulative context of preceding observation-action pairs.
- **Training Efficiency:** The objective function allows for joint training on all actions, which has been shown to be efficient in multi-turn SFT. To reduce GPU memory footprint during fine-tuning, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is utilized. LoRA adds a low-rank trainable update to pre-trained model weights, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters (e.g., 6 million for a 3 billion parameter Llama with rank 8) without incurring additional inference costs after merging.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

PRISM was evaluated across three distinct LLM-enabled planners:
- **SPINE:** For language-driven navigation, mapping, and exploration in partially-known environments using both indoor/outdoor UGVs and outdoor UAVs. Tasks varied in difficulty (fully-specified vs. under-specified).
- **SayCan:** A hierarchical language-guided planner for tabletop manipulation tasks in the PyBullet simulator. Custom benchmarks with attribute and numeric ambiguities were designed.
- **LLM-Planner:** For language-driven household manipulation tasks in the ALFRED simulator (AI2-THOR environments). It involves few-shot text-to-plan generation.

Baselines included GPT-4o (LLM) and an undistilled Llama-3.2-3B (SLM). The training datasets consisted of 3,200 examples for SPINE, 1,500 for SayCan, and 1,000 for LLM-Planner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PRISM focused on its effectiveness in distilling SLMs comparable to source LLMs, their efficiency for on-device deployment, and the importance of key data synthesis components.

#### 4.1. Model Effectiveness

The performance of PRISM-distilled SLMs was compared against GPT-4o (LLM) and an undistilled Llama-3.2-3B (SLM) across three distinct robot planning domains:
- **Overall Success Rate (Table 1):**
 - GPT-4o achieved success rates of 98.07% for SPINE, 50.0% for SayCan, and 16.49% for LLM-Planner. These figures align with previously reported trends for these planners.
 - The undistilled Llama-3.2-3B SLM demonstrated significantly lower performance, achieving 13.54% for SPINE, 10.0% for SayCan, and 1.76% for LLM-Planner.
 - PRISM-distilled SLMs significantly boosted performance, reaching 92.00% for SPINE, 50.0% for SayCan, and 15.54% for LLM-Planner. This represents at least a 93% performance increase for the SLM across all three planners, closely matching the performance of GPT-4o in each respective domain.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4):** PRISM-distilled models effectively addressed two common failure modes observed in the undistilled SLM: providing semantically incorrect but syntactically valid plans, and failing to generate valid plans altogether. Examples showed PRISM completing tasks where the SLM either failed to identify semantic targets, misused the planner API, or made unwarranted environmental assumptions.
- **Performance Across Difficulty Levels (SPINE, Table 2):**
 - In the simplest scenarios (fully-specified mapping tasks), both GPT-4o and PRISM-distilled SLMs achieved 100% success, while the base SLM reached only 50%.
 - For under-specified tasks, PRISM showed a modest 10% performance decrease compared to GPT-4o, but significantly outperformed the base SLM, which dropped to 0% success in several difficult settings.
 - Exploration tasks, particularly when under-specified, proved most challenging for all models. PRISM-distilled SLMs achieved 75% success, while GPT-4o achieved 83.3%. The undistilled SLM's performance fell to 0% in these more complex scenarios. The results suggest that spatial reasoning remains a comparative challenge for LLM-enabled planners, a weakness inherited by PRISM.

#### 4.2. Model Efficiency

Latency analysis was conducted using SPINE on a UGV platform, comparing PRISM-distilled models with cloud-based GPT-4o queries:
- **Latency Comparison (Figure 5):** PRISM-distilled models demonstrated query times within 200 milliseconds, even under ideal network conditions.
- In contrast, GPT-4o's mean query time was 3.64 seconds with minimal network latency and 4.90 seconds when simulating 200ms of added latency (e.g., satellite internet).
- **Key Advantage:** PRISM offers significantly faster planning execution (over 1 second faster than GPT-4o under realistic deployment conditions) and exhibits reduced variance, providing more deterministic latency. This enables on-device operation without network dependency.

#### 4.3. Importance of Environment Masking and Plan Validation

An ablation study on PRISM's data synthesis components evaluated their contribution to performance:
- **Ablation Results (Table 3):**
 - The full PRISM framework achieved 90.9% success.
 - Removing **environment masking** led to a 27.3% decrease in performance (63.6% success). This variant struggled when tasks required multiple planning iterations, as it was not trained with interactive, partially observed data.
 - Removing **plan validation** resulted in a nearly halved performance (45.5% success). Without validation, the distilled SLM mirrored failure modes of the source LLM, such as repeatedly calling ineffective actions when not properly reacting to new observations.
- **Conclusion:** Both environment masking, for generating diverse interactive scenarios, and plan validation, for filtering out suboptimal LLM behaviors, are critical components for the effectiveness of PRISM-distilled SLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PRISM framework addresses a critical challenge in the field of robotics by enabling advanced language model-driven planning to operate directly on robotic platforms, independent of cloud computing resources. This has several significant implications:

- **Expanded Deployment Capabilities:** By mitigating the reliance on cloud infrastructure and stable network connectivity, PRISM significantly broadens the operational scope of LLM-enabled robots. This enables their deployment in environments with unreliable or non-existent communication, such as remote outdoor areas, industrial facilities, disaster zones, or off-grid monitoring stations, where robust autonomous decision-making is essential.
- **Enhanced Real-Time Performance and Reliability:** On-device operation eliminates network latency and its associated variability, leading to more deterministic and faster planning execution. This is crucial for closed-loop control applications where timely responses are necessary for safe and effective robot operation. The observed efficiency gain of over 1 second compared to cloud-based LLMs underscores this benefit.
- **Cost-Effectiveness and Accessibility:** Distilling powerful LLM capabilities into smaller, efficient SLMs reduces the computational resources required for deployment, potentially lowering operational costs associated with cloud services. Furthermore, PRISM's minimal human intervention requirement for data generation makes sophisticated robot planning more accessible to researchers and developers who may lack the resources for extensive manual data annotation or complex simulator setups.
- **Performance Parity:** The framework demonstrates that SLMs, when appropriately distilled using PRISM, can achieve planning success rates comparable to those of state-of-the-art LLMs like GPT-4o. This validates the feasibility of deploying compact yet highly capable language models for complex robotic tasks.
- **Generalizability Across Platforms and Tasks:** The successful application of PRISM across heterogeneous robotic platforms (UGVs and UAVs), diverse environments (indoor and outdoor, simulated and real-world), and various task types (mapping, exploration, manipulation, household assistance) indicates the framework's versatility and potential for broad applicability.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The release of datasets, trained models, and code promotes reproducibility and provides a foundation for future work, particularly in improving spatial reasoning capabilities of distilled SLMs and exploring compatibility with other action representations, such as formal languages or code.
- **Addressing Safety Concerns:** While acknowledging that distilled SLMs may inherit safety vulnerabilities from source LLMs, the framework is designed to be compatible with existing and future safety guardrails, offering pathways for integrating safety objectives into the distillation process.

In summary, PRISM represents a step toward realizing truly autonomous, intelligent robotic systems capable of operating robustly in challenging real-world environments by effectively translating the reasoning power of large language models into efficient, deployable on-device solutions.
