# FlexWorm: Primitive-augmented Hybrid Contact-motion Planning for Suction-based Multi-segment Deformable Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> FlexWorm plans 3D navigation for suction-based multi-segment soft robots by jointly reasoning over discrete adhesion switching and continuous body deformation. IKHS decomposes the chain into adhesion-induced free blocks for tractable block-wise IK search; PaHS augments it with learned retrieval of validated motion primitives. Matches IKHS success while cutting planning time up to 12x, validated on hardware.

## Problem

- Existing soft-robot locomotion planners rely on hand-designed gaits or morphology-specific pre-scripted motion sequences tied to particular platforms and terrains, not general planning.
- Explicit model-based optimization approaches are either kinematically reduced but limited in accuracy, or physically accurate but too expensive for repeated feasibility checks in long-horizon online search.
- Prior hybrid contact-motion planners target rigid multi-contact robots or mechanism-specific adhesion robots, leaving joint adhesion-switching and deformation reasoning underexplored for suction-based soft chains.

## Method

- **Block-wise IK Hybrid Search (IKHS)**: decomposes the serial chain into free blocks anchored by attached suction pads, reducing full-chain hybrid expansion to localized best-first block IK search under collision, adhesion, and load-deformation constraints.
- ==Load-deformation feasibility manifold==: offline-identified convex polytopes over moment-length-curvature per gravity-angle bin from finite-element-calibrated hardware data, enforced during every IK block update.
- **Primitive-augmented Hybrid Search (PaHS)**: a dual-tower learned observation-primitive embedding retrieves short validated motion segments from an offline library for fast local proposals, falling back to IKHS branching when retrieval fails.

## Results

- Across 60 benchmark tasks spanning clutter, curvature, and surface transitions, both IKHS and PaHS reach **20/20** success in each of three scenarios.
- PaHS cuts planning time from **20.4/11.9/15.6s** (IKHS) to **1.7/1.0/1.8s** across scenarios, an **8.7x-12.0x** speedup, with **92-99%** primitive retrieval hit rates.
- PaHS beats controlled baselines RPR, PWTO, and DBS, which achieve only **30/60**, **31/60**, and **25/60** successes respectively with **10x+** higher planning time and no reduction in deformation cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anchor-induced block decomposition is a general reduction: exploiting adhesion-mode structure converts intractable full-chain hybrid search into local IK problems without sacrificing global feasibility.
> - Learned retrieval only pays off when paired with fallback and refinement; direct primitive replay without IK correction or IKHS fallback fails on most trials due to state mismatch.
> - Guide-path geometry strongly shapes downstream IK feasibility, small guide offsets near contact-critical regions can collapse feasible target counts by over an order of magnitude.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of soft robotics has advanced significantly, with multi-segment suction-based soft robots emerging as promising candidates for inspection and maintenance tasks in challenging environments such as aircraft cavities, pipelines, and other cluttered or fragile structures. These robots offer advantages due to their geometric adaptability, inherent compliance, and safe interaction capabilities, enabling them to conform to irregular surfaces, distribute contact loads, and maintain adhesion across various terrains including walls, ceilings, and transitions.

However, autonomous locomotion for these systems remains a complex challenge. Current approaches frequently rely on manually designed gaits or pre-scripted motion sequences that are often specific to particular environments or robot configurations. This dependency limits their adaptability and generalizability for navigating diverse and complex 3D surfaces.

Broader research in soft robot motion planning can be categorized into several areas:
- **Motion planning via gait generation:** This approach focuses on generating locomotion through predefined deformation patterns, gaits, or morphology-specific controllers. While effective for specific platforms and terrains, these methods often treat planning as gait selection or tuning rather than explicit long-horizon contact-motion planning under dynamically changing 3D geometry and contact constraints. They typically lack the ability to adapt to unforeseen changes or novel environments without extensive re-calibration or redesign.
- **Explicit modeling and optimization:** This line of work incorporates detailed robot models into the planning process. Reduced-order kinematic approximations, such as constant-curvature or multi-rigid-body models, can make search-based planning tractable. Higher-fidelity optimization using continuum or finite-element models can improve physical consistency but incur substantial computational costs. These methods often suffer from high computational expense and morphology dependence, which limits their suitability for long-horizon planning problems requiring frequent feasibility checks during online search.
- **Hybrid contact and motion reasoning:** This area addresses the combined challenge of deciding where and when contacts are established (discrete state) and how the robot's body deforms between these contacts (continuous state). While similar problems exist for rigid multi-contact planners (e.g., for quadruped robots), the hybrid nature of suction-based soft robot locomotion is particularly challenging. Each adhesion mode re-partitions a serial deformable backbone, altering the boundary and load-feasibility conditions for continuous deformation. This is further complicated in pneumatic soft robots by delayed and nonlinear deformation and surface-dependent suction, which introduce considerable uncertainty. Existing adhesion-based robots demonstrate potential but often have planners tied to specific mechanisms and transition patterns.

Against this backdrop, the reported work addresses a significant gap: the lack of a comprehensive planning framework for long-horizon 3D surface-constrained navigation for multi-segment suction-based soft robots that can jointly reason over discrete adhesion switching and continuous body deformation while accounting for geometric, collision, and quasi-static feasibility constraints. The proposed FlexWorm framework aims to provide a generalized solution that overcomes the limitations of manual gait design and high-computational cost model-based optimization by integrating global guidance with efficient local planning and learning-based acceleration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a planning framework that enables serial multi-segment soft robots, equipped with deformable body segments and boundary suction pads, to autonomously navigate complex 3D surfaces. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific, detailed objectives:

1. **Full 3D Navigation on Complex Surfaces:** The framework aims to enable robots to traverse diverse terrains, including transitions between floors, walls, and ceilings, as well as cluttered environments with significant curvature and obstacles. This moves beyond 2D or simplified environments often seen in existing soft robot locomotion studies.
2. **Explicit Handling of Discrete Adhesion Switching and Continuous Body Deformation:** A core objective is to jointly reason about the binary choices of attaching or detaching suction pads (discrete adhesion modes) and the high-dimensional continuous deformation of the robot's soft body segments. This tight coupling is a major challenge that needs to be explicitly addressed, as small changes in segment curvature or elongation can significantly impact the feasibility of contact transitions.
3. **Integration of Geometric, Collision, and Quasi-Static Feasibility Constraints:** The planning process must ensure that the robot's configuration and motion are physically realizable. This includes:
 - **Geometric constraints:** Ensuring suction pads are in proximity to the surface and correctly aligned with surface normals when attached.
 - **Collision constraints:** Preventing unintended collisions between the robot's body and the environment.
 - **Quasi-static feasibility constraints:** Ensuring that segment deformations are physically achievable under gravity-induced loads, which is crucial for compliant robots.
4. **Actuation Agnosticism:** The framework aims to remain independent of the specific actuation mechanism used to achieve segment deformation. This design principle enhances its generalizability and potential applicability to a wider range of soft robot platforms.
5. **Online Adaptation and Recovery under Uncertainty:** A practical objective is to enable the robot to adapt to real-world uncertainties during physical execution, such as those arising from actuation delays, imprecise adhesion, or state estimation errors. The framework should support online recovery strategies to maintain progress despite deviations from the planned trajectory.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from the unique advantages of multi-segment suction-based soft robots for tasks in confined or fragile environments. Their inherent compliance, geometric adaptability, and ability to form safe contact make them ideal for sensitive operations where rigid robots might cause damage or be unable to access. However, existing methods, which largely rely on human-designed gaits or environment-specific scripts, significantly limit their autonomy and broader deployment. The need for a general, autonomous, and robust planning solution that can manage the complex interplay between discrete contact decisions and continuous deformation, while satisfying physical constraints and adapting to uncertainty, is therefore critical for unlocking the full potential of these robots. This research seeks to fill this gap by proposing a principled and efficient planning framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FlexWorm framework employs a hierarchical hybrid planning approach designed for suction-based multi-segment soft robots. It integrates global surface-aware guidance with localized, contact-aware planning, and leverages learned motion primitives for efficiency.

**A. Robot State, Contacts, and Kinematics**
The robot model defines a **hybrid state** consisting of rigid links, deformable segments, and binary adhesion states for each suction pad.
- **Links:** Each link `i` has a pose (`p_i`, `q_i`) and an adhesion state `z_i ∈ {0, 1}` (1 for attached).
- **Segments:** Each segment `i` is parameterized by a body twist `u_i ∈ R^6`, representing its deformation.
- The overall hybrid robot state at step `h` is `(X_h, U_h, Z_h)`.

**Constraints:**
- **Surface and Adhesion Constraints:** For attached pads, world-frame position must be within an `ϵ_d` distance to the surface, and its normal must align with the surface normal within an `ϵ_a` threshold. Persistent adhesion across steps requires minimal pose variation (`ϵ_f`).
- **Collision-Free Constraint:** The robot's geometry must remain collision-free with the environment, excluding intended suction contact patches.
- **Segment Kinematics via Screw Theory:** Segment deformation is modeled using matrix exponentials of body twists, propagating homogeneous transforms between links. A reduced prior `u_i ≈ [0, 0, v_{z,i}, ω_{x,i}, ω_{y,i}, 0]^T` is used, where `v_{z,i}` is length and `(ω_{x,i}, ω_{y,i})` defines curvature, reflecting the dominant actuation modes of the pneumatic prototype.
- **Load-Deformation Feasibility:** A quasi-static feasibility constraint `(M_i, L_i, κ_i) ∈ S_D(γ_i)` is enforced for each segment, where `M_i` is the equivalent load moment, `L_i` is length, `κ_i` is curvature, and `γ_i` is the gravity angle. The feasibility manifold `S_D(·)` is identified offline.

**B. Long-Horizon Hybrid Optimization Problem**
The goal is to find a sequence of hybrid states `(X_{1:H}, U_{1:H}, Z_{1:H})` that minimizes accumulated deformation and unsafe contact while reaching a goal region within a horizon `H`, subject to all defined kinematic, adhesion, collision, and load-deformation constraints. The problem is formulated as a non-convex optimization over a hybrid decision space `({0, 1}^{n_l} × R^{6n_s})^H`, acknowledged as intractable for exact solution.

**C. Proposed Solution: IKHS and PaHS**
The framework comprises two main components: IKHS (Block-wise IK Hybrid Search) and PaHS (Primitive-augmented Hybrid Search).

1. **Core Planner: Block-wise IK Hybrid Search (IKHS)**
 IKHS performs a best-first search over hybrid states, utilizing a guiding surface path and an adhesion-induced block decomposition.
 - **Guiding Surface Path:** An A* search is performed on an augmented Signed Distance Field (SDF)-derived graph. Nodes include position and discretized incoming direction, allowing the guide to penalize non-coplanar transitions, surface-normal changes, and loss of surface support. This path `P` provides global progress measurement and local target proposals.
 - **Adhesion-induced Block Decomposition:** Given an adhesion mode `Z`, the robot chain is partitioned into consecutive "blocks" where attached links act as kinematic anchors. Free blocks can be head-fixed, tail-fixed, or bilateral-fixed, each following specific screw-model propagation rules. This decomposition reduces full-chain planning to local block updates.
 - **Load-Deformation Feasibility Manifold `S_D(·)`:** This manifold is calibrated offline. Approximately 10^3 hardware measurements are used to calibrate a finite-element segment model, which then generates 5x10^5 quasi-static samples. Gravity angles are discretized, and convex polytopes are fitted to conservative subsets of this data, enabling efficient feasibility checks during planning.
 - **Best-First Search:** Nodes in the search frontier are scored by a function `ψ` that balances progress along the guide, deformation effort, and a bonus for under-expanded adhesion modes. The node with the highest score is expanded.
 - **IK-based Expansion (`IKExpand`):** For a selected node and a candidate successor adhesion mode `Z_new`, admissible `Z_new` are enumerated (at most one new attachment per free block). For each affected free block, IKExpand samples target poses for the new attachment pad from a short forward window on `P`. It then solves a reduced Inverse Kinematics (IK) problem for that block, minimizing target pose error and deformation, subject to the load-deformation feasibility constraint (using the pre-computed `S_D(·)` polytopes). The resulting child state is accepted only after passing IK loss, suction pose, collision, and load-deformation checks.

2. **Primitive-augmented Hybrid Search (PaHS)**
 PaHS accelerates IKHS by incorporating a library of validated short-horizon motion primitives.
 - **Primitive Library:** Constructed offline from successful IKHS rollouts. Each primitive `π_h` records a verified local continuation of body deformation and adhesion switching over a short horizon (e.g., `H_p = 4` steps), expressed in the head frame for generality. Each primitive `π_j` is stored with a retrieval key `e_{act,j}`.
 - **Retrieval Embedding:** A dual-tower neural network is trained to learn a common latent space for observations and primitives. An "observation tower" `E_{obs}` encodes the current planning context (local depth image, proprioception, adhesion states, gravity, guide look-ahead). An "action tower" `E_{act}` encodes the canonicalized local rollout descriptor of each primitive. Training uses metric and alignment losses to ensure similar primitives/contexts have nearby embeddings.
 - **Primitive-Augmented Expansion (`PrimExpand`):** When expanding a node, PaHS first attempts a primitive-based expansion. It encodes the current context `e_{obs}`, retrieves the top-K nearest primitives from the library (using a KD-tree), rolls them out for `H_p` steps, and refines them using step-wise block IK under current environmental conditions. If successful, these refined primitives become child nodes, and standard IKHS expansion is skipped for that iteration. If no valid primitive is found after refinement, it falls back to the standard IKHS branching, ensuring search coverage and feasibility. Primitive expansion is attempted once per node.

**D. Overall Analyses**
- **Online Execution and Adaptation:** Plans are executed in a receding-horizon manner. A short deformation schedule is constructed, and rollouts are checked against safety and feasibility. If deviations or violations occur, feedback control (clipped integral pressure commands) is used for segment-level tracking. Persistent mismatches trigger online replanning via a short-horizon PaHS subproblem or replay of nearby validated primitives.
- **Correctness and Completeness:** IKHS is designed to be correct with respect to the modeled constraints, as every accepted child undergoes rigorous validation. PaHS maintains this property by subjecting retrieved primitives to the same IK refinement and feasibility checks. Completeness is limited by finite search budgets, candidate sampling, and beam width.
- **Overall Complexity:** Guide computation is `O(|E_s| log |V_s|)`. IKHS per-node runtime depends on candidate modes and block sizes. PaHS adds `O(d log N_M)` for retrieval, plus rollout/checking and local refinement, with fallback to IKHS.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the FlexWorm framework through extensive simulations and hardware experiments, assessing planning efficiency, retrieval quality, and hardware transferability.

**A. Simulation Setup**
Simulations used a custom transition model, PyBullet for visualization, and GPU-accelerated Open3D for SDF updates. Three benchmark scenarios (S1-S3) were used, presenting complementary challenges:
- **S1:** Floor-wall-ceiling transitions.
- **S2:** Reuse of recurring local transition patterns in clutter.
- **S3:** Guide quality and search coverage under large surface-normal variation.
Twenty navigation tasks were evaluated per scenario.

**B. Results and Analysis**
- **Offline Primitive Library:** A library of approximately 6,000 unique primitive segments was constructed offline from 1,500 IKHS rollouts across the three scenarios, showing the feasibility of learning motion patterns.
- **Overall Planning Performance:**
 - Both IKHS and PaHS achieved 100% success (20/20 trials) in all three scenarios, demonstrating their ability to plan successful trajectories through cluttered terrains, strong curvature, and complex surface transitions.
 - **Planning Time (PT):** PaHS significantly reduced planning time compared to IKHS. Specifically, it achieved speedups of 12.0x in S1 (from 20.4 s to 1.7 s), 11.9x in S2 (from 11.9 s to 1.0 s), and 8.7x in S3 (from 15.6 s to 1.8 s).
 - **Execution Steps (ES) and Deformation Cost (DC):** PaHS achieved lower mean execution steps (from 55.7 to 43.7 across scenarios) without increasing the mean deformation cost, suggesting more efficient and compact plans.
- **Scaling with Library Size and Retrieval Budget:** The retrieval hit rate and planning acceleration improved with increasing primitive library size and retrieval budget. The gains generally saturated beyond a library size of approximately 10,000 entries or a top-K retrieval budget of 50. This indicates that a sufficiently diverse but not excessively large library is effective.
- **Effect of Guide-Path Geometry:** The quality of the guiding path significantly influenced the size and distribution of the IK-feasible regions. Guides that maintained the robot closer to physically plausible contact configurations yielded a much higher count of feasible IK targets, demonstrating the importance of the initial global guidance.
- **Embedding Ablation:** The learned dual-tower embedding for primitive retrieval significantly outperformed random features and pooled PCA features in terms of both hit rate and planning time, in both seen and unseen environments. This validates the effectiveness of the learned latent space in identifying relevant motion primitives.

**C. Baseline Comparison**
PaHS was compared against three baselines and one ablation:
- **Retrieved Primitive Replay (RPR):** Directly replayed retrieved primitives without online adaptation or refinement.
- **Primitive-Warmstarted Trajectory Optimization (PWTO):** Used retrieved segments as warm starts for long-horizon optimization.
- **Discrete Beam Search (DBS):** Discretized deformation and adhesion candidates within a beam search.
- **PaHS-NF (No Fallback):** PaHS with the IKHS fallback mechanism disabled.
- **Results:**
 - PaHS achieved the best overall performance, solving 60/60 tasks with significantly lower planning times than all baselines.
 - RPR and PaHS-NF had substantially lower success rates (30/60 and 39/60 respectively), indicating that direct replay or primitive expansion without robust refinement and fallback is insufficient to maintain feasibility under state mismatch or unrepresented scenarios.
 - PWTO and DBS also showed lower success rates (31/60 and 25/60 respectively) and much higher planning times, execution steps, and deformation costs, suggesting that pure optimization-based or finely discretized search approaches are less efficient for this hybrid planning problem.

**D. Hardware Experiments**
Hardware experiments were conducted on a pneumatic multi-segment soft robot (two segments, three suction pads) in a controlled workspace using motion capture feedback.
- **Tasks:** Randomized planar obstacle avoidance and a plane-to-slope transition onto a 45˚ incline.
- **Results:**
 - The robot successfully navigated 9 out of 10 trials in planar obstacle avoidance, demonstrating online recovery from execution mismatches through replanning.
 - It completed 8 out of 10 trials in the 45˚ climbing task, executing a sequence of contact-aware gaits.
 - The experiments demonstrated the framework's executability on physical hardware and its ability to manage online recovery under real-world uncertainties such as imperfect adhesion, state estimation errors, and pneumatic delays. Moderate deviations were successfully corrected, though large accumulated deviations could prevent recovery.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the autonomous capabilities of multi-segment suction-based soft robots, addressing a fundamental challenge in their deployment. The key contributions and their potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Enabling Autonomous 3D Surface Navigation:** By providing a robust planning framework, this work moves beyond manual gait design and environment-specific scripts, paving the way for truly autonomous 3D navigation of complex surfaces. This is crucial for expanding the applicability of these robots in real-world environments that demand high adaptability.
2. **Tractability for Hybrid Planning Problems:** The introduction of adhesion-induced block-wise planning (IKHS) offers a novel abstraction that simplifies the otherwise intractable problem of jointly optimizing discrete contact decisions and high-dimensional continuous body deformations. This approach makes long-horizon planning computationally feasible for complex soft robot morphologies.
3. **Efficiency through Learning-Augmented Planning:** The primitive-augmented hybrid search (PaHS) significantly accelerates planning by leveraging learned, validated motion primitives. This hybrid strategy combines the efficiency of data-driven proposals with the guarantees of model-based refinement and fallback, achieving substantial speedups (up to 12x) without compromising planning success or physical feasibility. This efficiency is critical for online operation and adaptation in dynamic environments.
4. **Robustness to Real-World Uncertainties:** The framework incorporates mechanisms for online execution, adaptation, and recovery, including feedback control and online replanning. The hardware experiments demonstrate the system's ability to cope with common uncertainties such as actuation delays, imperfect adhesion, and state estimation errors, enhancing the practical utility and reliability of these robots.
5. **Broad Applicability to Soft Robot Designs:** By being actuation-agnostic and explicitly modeling hybrid state, the framework has potential generalizability to various multi-segment soft robot designs beyond the pneumatic prototype used in this study. The approach to identifying the load-deformation feasibility manifold can be adapted for different material and actuation properties.
6. **Potential for Wider Deployment:** The enhanced autonomy, robustness, and efficiency offered by FlexWorm could accelerate the deployment of multi-segment suction-based soft robots in high-value applications. These include critical inspection and maintenance tasks within confined spaces like aircraft wings, nuclear facilities, pipelines, or urban infrastructure, where their compliance and ability to adhere to surfaces provide unique advantages over rigid robotic systems.
7. **Contribution to Hybrid System Planning:** The methodology offers insights into combining discrete and continuous planning domains for highly deformable systems, providing a blueprint for integrating model-based guarantees with learning-based acceleration in complex robotic control problems.

In summary, this research addresses a core challenge in soft robotics by presenting a planning framework that is both theoretically sound and practically effective. Its contributions lay foundational groundwork for future advancements in autonomous soft robot navigation and expand the potential applications of these uniquely capable machines.
