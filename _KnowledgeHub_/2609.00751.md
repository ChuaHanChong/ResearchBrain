# One Print, Many Moves: Monolithic Origami-inspired Folding Actuator for Composable Soft Multi-DoF Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> MONORIGAMI is a single-material, single-print soft folding actuator that programs stiffness anisotropy spatially: thickened origami facets resist off-axis deformation while thin creases stay compliant, converting negative pressure into accurate directional folding. Each unit acts as a composable motion primitive; serial and parallel assemblies mechanically encode multi-DoF trajectories, demonstrated in a 4-DoF wearable haptic device, a 3-DoF kinesthetic joystick, and an underwater modular gripper.

## Problem

- Conventional soft pneumatic actuators cannot restrict undesired deformation, so unintended off-axis motion and instability undermine accurate force application and controlled, repeatable trajectories.
- Errors accumulate along serial chains and couple through closed loops in parallel arrangements, so soft actuators scale poorly to multi-degree-of-freedom robotic systems.
- Prior origami and flexible-hinge fixes need separately fabricated reinforcements, multiple materials, or multistep assembly, and address single actuators rather than composable multi-DoF architectures.

## Method

- Spatially programmed ==stiffness anisotropy== uses discrete wall-thickness tiers so thickened facets bear load while thin creases stay compliant, favoring folding along creases without added stiffeners.
- A modified ==Miura sidewall topology== drops the central facet, converting an overconstrained zero-DoF sealed crease loop into a single-DoF chamber resealed by a flexible panel.
- A ==quasi-static energy model== subtracts crease elastic energy from pressure work, with ==fmincon== optimizing lateral geometry; a modified ==waterbomb skeleton== links modules, SLA-printed monolithically with embedded fluidic channels.

## Results

- Across **nine variants**, blocking torque peaked at **15.72 N·mm** (**0.2 mm** crease, **70°** designed angle) and fell to **9.11 N·mm** at **0.4 mm** and **90°**, contradicting theory.
- Range of motion spanned **70.53°** down to **43.35°** under **-80 kPa**; with a **100 g** load it fell from **56°** to **26°**, endpoint error staying within **±5%**.
- FingerPrint achieved **±17°** roll and pitch, **±20°** yaw, **4.7 mm** z-translation, keeping **>50%** magnitude above **4 Hz**; the **3 g** joystick rendered **0.2-0.55 N** cues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Programming stiffness spatially instead of reinforcing uniformly redistributes compliance into defined motion pathways, weakening the usual trade-off between softness and motion precision.
> - Crease elastic-energy storage can dominate geometric volume gains, so purely geometric torque predictions invert; material dissipation must enter soft-actuator design models.
> - Monolithic printing removes assembly but not post-processing, and single-material fabrication caps facet-crease stiffness contrast, limiting load capacity, miniaturization, and closed-loop sensing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic system performance, scalability, and applicability are fundamentally influenced by actuator design. Traditional rigid robots utilize high-precision rigid actuators, such as electromagnetic motors, which offer accurate motion and high force output. However, these systems often incur significant weight, mechanical complexity, and limited adaptability due to the need for rigid connecting components and their inability to conform to unpredictable environments.

To address some of these limitations, compliant actuators, like Series Elastic Actuators (SEAs), have been developed. These actuators incorporate elastic elements to enhance shock absorption, energy storage, and safety during human interaction. Nevertheless, their compliance is typically confined to joint flexibility, limiting structural-level adaptability across the entire robotic system.

Soft actuators represent a distinct approach, offering continuous deformation and inherent structural adaptability through technologies such as pneumatic actuation, shape-memory alloys, dielectric elastomers, and liquid crystal elastomers. Pneumatic actuation is particularly noted for its capacity to generate large strains and deliver power remotely through lightweight tubing. A persistent limitation for conventional soft actuators, however, is the difficulty in controlling and restricting undesired deformations. This often leads to inaccurate or unstable motion, which hinders their application in tasks requiring precision and limits their scalability to multi-degree-of-freedom (DoF) robotic systems, both in serial configurations where errors can propagate and in parallel configurations where coupling can amplify errors.

Previous efforts to balance compliance with accurate motion have explored techniques such as self-folding actuation, flexible hinge mechanisms, and origami-inspired designs. These approaches integrate structural compliance with stiff elements to constrain unwanted DoFs, aiming to improve accuracy without sacrificing flexibility. Many of these solutions, however, rely on separately fabricated components, multiple materials, or multi-step assembly processes. Furthermore, they have often focused on individual actuators rather than developing a composable architecture where local deformation rules predictably scale to complex multi-DoF systems. Issues such as the fine-tuning of hinge stiffness for additional joints and the accumulation of errors from local stiffness variations, fabrication inconsistencies, or pressure loading remain challenges.

The presented work introduces MONORIGAMI, which addresses these limitations by integrating origami structural principles with monolithic additive manufacturing. This approach aims to directly encode deformation constraints into composable soft actuation modules. By utilizing locally varied wall thickness to create discrete stiffness tiers (facets and creases), the design aims to embed directional compliance and deformation constraints within a single material. This monolithic fabrication strategy, unlike prior fully 3D-printed soft robots focused on digital logic or fluidic circuits, establishes thickness-programmed origami stiffness as a generalized method for achieving directional and composable soft actuation. This integration seeks to provide a reliable and scalable platform for constructing complex multi-DoF soft robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the MONORIGAMI research is to overcome fundamental challenges in soft robotics, specifically the trade-off between inherent material compliance and the ability to achieve accurate, controlled motion, and to facilitate the scalable design of multi-degree-of-freedom (DoF) soft robotic systems. Conventional soft actuators offer adaptability but frequently suffer from uncontrolled deformations, which limit their precision and scalability.

The work identifies three core objectives, framed as contributions, to address these issues:

1. **To establish a general design principle for directionally constrained soft actuation:** The objective is to introduce and validate a design strategy based on spatially programmed stiffness anisotropy. This approach aims to selectively suppress undesired degrees of freedom while preserving compliant folding along prescribed directions. This represents a conceptual shift towards designing soft actuators with inherent control over their deformation pathways, thereby mitigating the traditional trade-off between compliance and precision.

2. **To develop a composable architecture for mechanically programmed multi-DoF motion:** The aim is to design individual actuators as composable motion primitives. These primitives can be linked and oriented in serial or parallel configurations to mechanically encode and produce multi-DoF trajectories. This objective seeks to transform a standalone actuator into a system-level building block, enabling the construction of complex soft robotic mechanisms that maintain compliance and adaptability while achieving controlled and accurate motion.

3. **To create a monolithic manufacturing strategy that encodes deformation logic directly into geometry:** This objective focuses on leveraging monolithic additive manufacturing to integrate structural stiffness, fluidic channels, and mechanical constraints within a single-material, single-print process. The motivation is to eliminate the need for separate reinforcements or post-fabrication assembly, providing a reliable and generalizable method for producing mechanically programmed soft actuation modules at scale.

Beyond these foundational objectives, the research is also motivated by the potential to enable new applications requiring compact multi-axis integration, controlled physical interaction, and geometry-programmed operation across diverse environments, as demonstrated through the development of specific robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MONORIGAMI approach centers on a design strategy that leverages spatially programmed stiffness anisotropy within an origami-inspired geometry, enabled by monolithic 3D printing.

**Design Principle and Actuator Architecture:**
The core design principle involves varying material thickness to create discrete stiffness tiers: compliant creases facilitate folding, while thickened facets provide localized rigidity to resist unwanted deformation. This arrangement is designed to guide actuation along predefined kinematic paths. To achieve a sealed, single-DoF actuation unit, a modified Miura-origami pattern is adopted for the actuator side walls. This modification, involving the removal of a central facet and its replacement with a flexible panel, transforms a theoretically overconstrained (zero-DoF) closed crease network into a single kinematic DoF structure, ensuring both mobility and structural integrity under pneumatic pressurization. For multi-DoF systems, individual MONORIGAMI modules are integrated into a modified waterbomb pattern, which inherently provides three rotational DoFs when expanded. Each actuator is aligned with the folding lines of this skeleton structure, allowing for complex motion trajectories through modular assembly.

**Monolithic Fabrication:**
The entire actuator, including facets, creases, passive joints, and embedded fluidic channels, is fabricated in a single monolithic step using stereolithography (SLA) 3D printing. A Formlabs Form 3 SLA printer with Flexible 80A resin is used, selected for its high resolution and ability to produce air-tight, thin-walled structures. This single-material, single-print process eliminates the need for separate reinforcements or assembly. The fabrication process involves:
1. **Monolithic Fabrication:** Printing the entire device.
2. **Residual Resin Removal:** Using a vacuum compressor and syringe to clear internal chambers via drainage ports.
3. **Drainage Port Sealing:** Filling ports with uncured resin and partially curing with UV light.
4. **Device Curing:** Post-curing the entire device at 60 °C for 4 minutes.
5. **Support Removal:** Manually removing print support structures.

**Programming Actuator Performance:**
A quasi-static geometrical model ($E_{MONO} = E_P - E_{EU}$) is developed to approximate the mechanical energy available for folding. This model considers pressure work ($E_P$) based on volume change and subtracts elastic energy stored in the crease regions ($E_{EU}$). The geometry is decomposed into central and lateral structures, with a target designed angle ($\theta_d$) derived from the central region. Lateral structure parameters are numerically optimized to minimize elastic energy accumulation and maximize $E_{MONO}$.

**Experimental Characterization:**
A set of nine actuator variants were fabricated by systematically varying crease thickness (0.2, 0.3, 0.4 mm) and designed angle (70°, 80°, 90°). These variants were characterized for:
- **Blocking Torque:** Measured the maximum resistive torque under constrained motion at different negative pressures (-20, -40, -60, -80 kPa) to assess pneumatic energy conversion to mechanical output.
- **Range of Motion (RoM):** Measured angular displacement under a -80 kPa square wave input, tracking motion with color markers.
- **Bandwidth:** Evaluated dynamic responsiveness by applying square-wave pressures at frequencies from 0.0625 to 2 Hz.
- **Hysteresis:** Quantified repeatability in free motion by tracking folding angle during cyclic pressurization and release.
- **Load-bearing Capacity:** An actuator (t=0.3 mm, $\theta_d$=80°) was tested under increasing external loads (0g, 20g, 50g, 100g) to assess trajectory accuracy, off-axis material deformation, and response speed.

**System Demonstrations:**
The MONORIGAMI concept was validated through the development of three distinct robotic systems:
1. **4-DoF Wearable Haptic Device (FingerPrint):** Integrates four serial pairs of MONORIGAMI actuators in a modified waterbomb skeleton. Monolithically printed with embedded fluidic channels and a tactor. Tested for workspace, bandwidth of rotational DoFs, and integration with a VR control framework for real-time cutaneous feedback.
2. **3-DoF Kinesthetic-Feedback Joystick:** Utilizes three MONORIGAMI actuators in a delta-inspired parallel configuration. Monolithically printed. Tested with a virtual wall experiment (providing immediate kinesthetic feedback upon boundary contact) and an environmental contact experiment (reproducing follower-side forces).
3. **Modular Soft Robotic Gripper:** Employs heterogeneous serial integration of MONORIGAMI modules, where individual fingers can have different crease thicknesses and designed angles to generate programmed grasp trajectories. Demonstrated reconfigurable two-finger and four-finger operations and underwater capability due to its monolithic and electronics-free design.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the MONORIGAMI actuator design, performance, and application in multi-DoF systems.

**Design Principle and Monolithic Fabrication:**
The study demonstrated that spatially programmed stiffness anisotropy, achieved by patterning material thickness into facets and compliant creases, effectively transforms unconstrained soft deformation into accurate and repeatable folding motions. The monolithic 3D printing process, using a single material (Flexible 80A resin) and a single print, successfully integrates the structural, actuation, and fluidic components without requiring additional reinforcements or assembly steps. This approach enabled the creation of sealed, functional soft actuator modules. The modified Miura-origami pattern proved effective in creating a single-DoF, sealed actuator, while the modified waterbomb pattern served as a suitable kinematic skeleton for composing multi-DoF systems.

**Actuator Performance Programming and Characterization:**
- **Blocking Torque:** Experimental results showed an inverse relationship between blocking torque and both designed angle and crease thickness, contradicting a simple theoretical prediction based solely on volume change. The highest blocking torque measured was 15.72 N·mm (for 0.2 mm crease thickness, 70° designed angle). This discrepancy was attributed to the dominant effect of elastic energy loss in thicker creases and larger designed angles, highlighting the importance of considering material elasticity in design.
- **Range of Motion (RoM):** RoM directly correlated with thinner creases and larger designed angles. The maximum RoM achieved was 70.53° (for 0.2 mm crease thickness, 90° designed angle). A design trade-off was identified: increasing the designed angle increased RoM but decreased blocking torque.
- **Bandwidth:** Actuators with thicker creases (0.3 mm and 0.4 mm) exhibited improved bandwidth, maintaining responses above -3 dB up to approximately 0.5-1 Hz, compared to thinner creases (0.2 mm) that dropped below -3 dB at lower frequencies. This suggests that thicker creases enhance elastic recovery, contributing to faster release phases.
- **Hysteresis:** All actuator designs demonstrated minimal hysteresis during cyclic pressurization and release, indicating repeatable motion with limited energy loss. Complete folding occurred at lower negative pressures for thinner creases (approx. -30 to -40 kPa for 0.2 mm) compared to thicker ones (approx. -60 kPa for 0.4 mm), reflecting greater elastic energy storage in thicker creases.

**Load-Bearing Capacity and Actuation Speed:**
The MONORIGAMI actuators maintained accurate rotational motion under external loads. While increasing loads (up to 100g) reduced RoM (from 56° to 26°) and increased off-axis material deformation (up to -10° at 100g), the endpoint displacement error remained within ±5% of the theoretical trajectory. The actuators demonstrated consistent torque generation and high repeatability in actuation speed and RoM across varying constant load conditions. Crucially, full folding was achieved rapidly (about 50 ms) across all load conditions, indicating that the observed lower bandwidth in free motion was primarily due to release time rather than response time, suggesting improved dynamic performance under load in practical applications.

**System Demonstrations:**
- **4-DoF Wearable Haptic Device (FingerPrint):** This compact device (25 × 25 × 25 mm³, 3g) successfully integrated four serial pairs of MONORIGAMI actuators to achieve ±17° roll and pitch, ±20° yaw, and 4.7 mm z-axis translation. The rotational DoFs maintained over 50% actuation magnitude above 4 Hz. The system was integrated with a VR framework, demonstrating real-time, directional cutaneous feedback based on virtual object interaction.
- **3-DoF Kinesthetic-Feedback Joystick:** A compact (35 × 35 × 35 mm³, 3g), delta-inspired parallel joystick provided rapid kinesthetic feedback (0.2–0.55 N) during virtual wall encounters. In environmental contact tests, the joystick accurately reproduced follower-side interaction forces, preserving vectorial information and demonstrating precise tracking of force magnitude and direction across three axes.
- **Modular Soft Robotic Gripper:** The gripper demonstrated geometry-programmed grasp trajectories (circular or elliptical) by serially arranging actuators with varied crease thicknesses and designed angles. Its modular design allowed for reconfigurable two-finger and four-finger operations. The monolithic, electronics-free construction enabled seamless underwater operation without specialized waterproofing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MONORIGAMI design and fabrication approach offer several significant advancements for the field of soft robotics and its applications.

Firstly, this work addresses a long-standing limitation in soft robotics: the inherent trade-off between compliance and motion accuracy, and the challenges in scaling soft actuators to multi-degree-of-freedom (DoF) systems. By introducing spatially programmed stiffness anisotropy through thickness-defined facets and creases, the MONORIGAMI actuator redirects intrinsic material compliance into predefined, accurate folding directions, thereby suppressing uncontrolled deformations. This represents a conceptual shift in soft actuator design, where softness becomes a precisely engineered geometric property rather than an unconstrained material characteristic.

Secondly, the monolithic additive manufacturing strategy is impactful. Fabricating the entire actuator—including structural components, actuation chambers, and fluidic routing—in a single, single-material 3D print eliminates the need for complex assembly, separate reinforcements, and multi-material processes. This significantly reduces fabrication complexity, cost, and potential failure points, while enabling miniaturization and extensive customization. This accessibility can lower the barrier to entry for researchers and developers in creating sophisticated soft robotic systems.

Thirdly, the development of a composable architecture, where individual MONORIGAMI actuators serve as motion primitives, facilitates the design and mechanical programming of multi-DoF systems. By linking and orienting these modules in serial or parallel configurations, complex trajectories can be realized. This approach mitigates the integration complexity that typically accumulates with increasing DoFs in conventional robotics, offering a scalable pathway for constructing complex soft machines. The ability to tailor system configurations by varying module orientation, connection topology, crease thickness, and designed folding angle provides a versatile platform for diverse applications.

The practical demonstrations across three distinct robotic systems highlight the broad applicability and potential impact of MONORIGAMI:
- **Wearable Haptics:** The 4-DoF FingerPrint device demonstrates the capacity for compact, lightweight, and high-fidelity cutaneous feedback in virtual reality (VR) environments. Its ability to provide multi-axis motion with sufficient bandwidth addresses key requirements for immersive and precise tactile interaction, potentially enhancing user experience in VR/AR, telepresence, and human-computer interaction.
- **Teleoperation:** The 3-DoF kinesthetic-feedback joystick showcases the potential for compact, compliant, and accurate haptic interfaces in teleoperation. By providing rapid, directionally resolved kinesthetic feedback, it can improve operator perception, control, and task performance in remote manipulation, especially in sensitive or constrained environments. The low-mass actuation and high force-to-weight ratio contribute to its responsiveness.
- **Robotic Manipulation:** The modular soft gripper, with its geometry-programmed finger actuation and reconfigurable designs, illustrates versatility in grasping diverse objects. Its monolithic, electronics-free construction enables operation in challenging environments, such as underwater, without specialized sealing, expanding the operational domains for soft robotic grippers.

In conclusion, MONORIGAMI provides a general, composable, accessible, reliable, and scalable platform that addresses long-standing limitations in soft actuator design and fabrication. Its contributions lay foundational groundwork for advancing high-precision soft multi-DoF robotics across various application domains, ranging from human-robot interaction to advanced manipulation in unstructured environments. Future work on post-processing reduction, multi-material printing, and integrated sensing could further enhance its capabilities.
