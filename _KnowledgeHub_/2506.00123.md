# Visual Embodied Brain: Let Multimodal Large Language Models See, Think, and Control in Spaces

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory, Tsinghua University, and SenseTime Research developed VeBrain, a unified framework enabling Multimodal Large Language Models to integrate multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robotic control. This approach reformulates robot control tasks into 2D visual keypoint detection and embodied skill recognition, achieving up to +31.5% average performance gains against other frameworks across diverse benchmarks while preserving general multimodal understanding.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle to extend their intelligence from digital understanding to physical interaction, lacking inherent visual-spatial reasoning and direct physical control capabilities.
- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while designed for robot control, often compromise their general multimodal understanding, creating a 'task gap' or 'knowledge forgetting' when attempting to unify MLLMs and VLAs.
- There is a lack of high-quality, unified instruction datasets that seamlessly integrate and balance diverse capabilities like multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control.

## Method

- Reformulates robot control tasks into text-based MLLM objectives (==2D keypoint detection== and ==embodied skill recognition==) within the 2D visual space of MLLMs.
- Introduces a novel ==Robotic Adapter== component that translates the MLLM's 2D text-based predictions into real-world 3D robot motion policies, comprising a Point Tracker, Movement Controller, Skill Executor, and Dynamic Takeover module.
- Developed ==VeBrain-600k==, a diverse instruction dataset integrating multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control data, augmented with ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== for training.

## Results

- VeBrain achieved the "best trade-off performance" across multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control tasks, yielding up to **+31.5%** average gains against other frameworks.
- The model preserved strong general multimodal understanding capabilities (e.g., **+5.6%** on MMVet compared to Qwen2.5-VL) while remarkably outperforming specialized 2D models on 3D spatial reasoning benchmarks (e.g., **+5.2 CIDEr** on ScanQAval).
- Demonstrated superior robot control performance on both legged robot and robotic arm tasks, achieving significantly higher success rates on complex, long-horizon scenarios (e.g., **5**0%** gain** on 'Complex Transport' compared to baselines).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot control tasks can be effectively reformulated as text-based Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) objectives (2D keypoint detection and embodied skill recognition), allowing MLLMs to leverage their strong understanding for physical control.
> - A specialized robotic adapter is crucial for robustly translating 2D visual predictions from MLLMs into precise 3D real-world robot actions, addressing challenges like ego-view changes and dynamic state awareness.
> - Training a unified embodied AI requires a comprehensive, high-quality instruction dataset that balances multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control, leveraging Chain-of-Thought for complex tasks.
