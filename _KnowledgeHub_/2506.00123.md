# Visual Embodied Brain: Let Multimodal Large Language Models See, Think, and Control in Spaces

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory, Tsinghua University, and SenseTime Research developed VeBrain, a unified framework enabling Multimodal Large Language Models to integrate multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robotic control. This approach reformulates robot control tasks into 2D visual keypoint detection and embodied skill recognition, achieving up to +31.5% average performance gains against other frameworks across diverse benchmarks while preserving general multimodal understanding.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle to extend their intelligence from digital understanding to physical interaction, lacking inherent visual-spatial reasoning and direct physical control capabilities.
- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while designed for robot control, often compromise their general multimodal understanding, creating a 'task gap' or 'knowledge forgetting' when attempting to unify MLLMs and VLAs.
- There is a lack of high-quality, unified instruction datasets that seamlessly integrate and balance diverse capabilities like multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control.

## Method

- Reformulates robot control tasks into text-based MLLM objectives (==2D keypoint detection== and ==embodied skill recognition==) within the 2D visual space of MLLMs.
- Introduces a novel ==Robotic Adapter== component that translates the MLLM's 2D text-based predictions into real-world 3D robot motion policies, comprising a Point Tracker, Movement Controller, Skill Executor, and Dynamic Takeover module.
- Developed ==VeBrain-600k==, a diverse instruction dataset integrating multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control data, augmented with ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== for training.

## Results

- VeBrain achieved the "best trade-off performance" across multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control tasks, yielding up to **+31.5%** average gains against other frameworks.
- The model preserved strong general multimodal understanding capabilities (e.g., **+5.6%** on MMVet compared to Qwen2.5-VL) while remarkably outperforming specialized 2D models on 3D spatial reasoning benchmarks (e.g., **+5.2 CIDEr** on ScanQAval).
- Demonstrated superior robot control performance on both legged robot and robotic arm tasks, achieving significantly higher success rates on complex, long-horizon scenarios (e.g., **5**0%** gain** on 'Complex Transport' compared to baselines).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot control tasks can be effectively reformulated as text-based Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) objectives (2D keypoint detection and embodied skill recognition), allowing MLLMs to leverage their strong understanding for physical control.
> - A specialized robotic adapter is crucial for robustly translating 2D visual predictions from MLLMs into precise 3D real-world robot actions, addressing challenges like ego-view changes and dynamic state awareness.
> - Training a unified embodied AI requires a comprehensive, high-quality instruction dataset that balances multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control, leveraging Chain-of-Thought for complex tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence is rapidly progressing, with Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) achieving remarkable breakthroughs in understanding and generating content across various modalities, particularly text and images. However, a significant frontier remains: extending this intelligence from the digital realm to physical entities, specifically robots. This is where "Visual Embodied Brain" (VeBrain) positions itself, aiming to bridge the gap between abstract multimodal understanding and concrete physical interaction.

Historically, the development of MLLMs has focused primarily on tasks within a 2D visual space, such as image captioning, visual question answering, and multimodal chat. While highly successful in these domains, these models lack inherent capabilities for visual-spatial reasoning (e.g., understanding 3D relationships, distances, and navigations within a physical environment) and physical interaction (e.g., controlling a robot arm or a legged robot).

Concurrently, the robotics community has seen the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models are specifically designed to map multimodal observations (vision, language instructions) directly into executable robot control policies. While VLAs excel in robotic manipulation and locomotion, their training on large-scale robotic datasets often comes at a cost: a significant compromise in their general multimodal understanding capabilities. This trade-off leads to models that are either great at understanding but poor at control, or vice-versa, struggling to achieve a holistic "embodied intelligence."

The core challenge, as identified by the authors, lies in the "fundamental differences" between the objectives of MLLMs and VLAs. MLLMs operate on cross-modal alignment in 2D visual space, producing text-based outputs. Robot control, conversely, demands precise mapping from multimodal inputs to physical motion policies in the real, 3D world. This divergence often results in "knowledge forgetting" or "task conflicts" when attempts are made to unify them directly. Previous attempts to integrate MLLMs with robot control either use MLLMs as high-level planners (which struggle with accurate, low-level control) or train VLAs that sacrifice general understanding.

VeBrain directly addresses this critical dichotomy. Its innovative approach is to **reframe robot control tasks into a format compatible with existing MLLM objectives**, specifically by converting them into text-based tasks (keypoint detection and embodied skill recognition) within the familiar 2D visual space of MLLMs. This unification of objectives and mapping spaces is a significant conceptual leap, allowing MLLMs to leverage their strong understanding and reasoning abilities for robot control without extensive retraining on raw action sequences. Furthermore, the paper highlights the critical lack of suitable, high-quality datasets that seamlessly integrate and balance these diverse capabilities. VeBrain-600k, a carefully curated instruction dataset, directly tackles this data scarcity, providing a rich training ground for the unified framework.

In essence, VeBrain is at the forefront of developing "generalist embodied agents" – systems that not only perceive and understand the world through various senses but can also reason about it spatially and interact with it physically. It aims to achieve this by harmonizing disparate learning paradigms, pushing MLLMs beyond perception to become truly embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **unified framework, named Visual Embodied Brain (VeBrain)**, capable of seamlessly integrating multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robotic control within a single model. The overarching goal is to enable Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to effectively "see, think, and control in spaces" – moving from abstract comprehension to practical physical interaction.

The motivations behind this ambitious objective are multi-faceted:

- **Advancing MLLMs to Physical Entities:** While MLLMs have achieved remarkable success in various vision-language tasks, their intelligence primarily resides in the digital domain. The next natural milestone for MLLMs is their migration to physical entities like robots, allowing them to perceive, reason about, and interact with the real world. This necessitates capabilities beyond traditional multimodal understanding.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - **MLLMs' Embodied Deficiencies:** Current MLLMs, despite their strong multimodal understanding, inherently lack robust visual-spatial reasoning and physical interaction capabilities. They are not designed for direct robot control.
 - **VLAs' Understanding Compromise:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are specifically trained for robot control, mapping observations to control policies. However, this often comes at the expense of their general multimodal understanding, making them less versatile for complex, open-ended tasks that require deep comprehension.
 - **"Task Gap" and Conflicts:** The fundamental difference in learning objectives between MLLMs (cross-modal alignment in 2D visual space) and robot control (precise mapping to physical motion in 3D real-world space) leads to inherent difficulties. Direct combination often results in "knowledge forgetting" and "task conflicts," preventing a synergistic integration.
- **Lack of Unified Data Recipe:** The research community currently lacks comprehensive, high-quality instruction datasets that seamlessly integrate and balance the diverse capabilities of multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control. This data scarcity exacerbates the challenge of training a unified model.
- **Need for Adaptability, Flexibility, and Compositional Capabilities:** Real-world robotic tasks are complex, dynamic, and often require compositional reasoning (e.g., breaking down a long-horizon task into sub-goals, adapting to unexpected situations). A truly embodied AI needs to demonstrate strong adaptability, flexibility, and the ability to compose various skills to solve novel problems.

In essence, the motivation is to overcome the fragmentation in current AI research, where understanding and action capabilities reside in separate, often conflicting, models. VeBrain aims to forge a path towards a more generalist embodied AI by unifying these critical abilities, enabling MLLMs to perceive, think, and act cohesively in the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VeBrain introduces a novel approach to unify multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control by reformulating robotic actions as text-based Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) tasks in a 2D visual space. This core idea allows MLLMs to leverage their inherent strengths for high-level reasoning while enabling precise physical control through a specialized robotic adapter.

#### 3.1. Task Formulations

The methodology is built upon three key principles to bridge the gap between MLLM and robot control:
1. **Unified Input-Output Space:** All tasks are framed as `p(t|x,y;θ)`, where `x` is visual input, `y` is textual prompt, `t` is the textual answer/prediction, and `θ` represents model parameters. This standardizes the interaction across different capabilities.
2. **Robot Control in 2D Visual Space:** Instead of directly predicting complex motor commands in 3D, robot control is decomposed into two MLLM-friendly sub-tasks:
 - **Keypoint Detection:** MLLM predicts 2D coordinates on the image, representing target locations for movement or interaction. This serves as a visual-spatial anchor.
 - **Embodied Skill Recognition:** MLLM predicts textual action commands (e.g., "Turn Right", "Grasp") from a predefined policy pool.
3. **Task-Specific Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** For complex tasks, CoT is integrated into prompts, guiding the MLLM to perform step-by-step reasoning (e.g., environment perception, task planning, current decision-making) before providing the final control signals. This enhances interpretability and problem-solving.

#### 3.2. VeBrain Framework

The VeBrain architecture comprises two main components that operate in a closed-loop fashion:

- **MLLM Architecture:**
 - VeBrain uses **Qwen2.5-VL-7B** as its backbone.
 - It consists of a **vision encoder** (optimized ViT), a **projector** (to align visual features with the language model), and a **Large Language Model (LLM)**.
 - The LLM processes visual features from the vision encoder and textual prompts, and generates textual outputs, which include direct answers for understanding/reasoning tasks, or keypoint coordinates and skill commands for robot control.

- **Robotic Adapter:** This novel component is critical for translating the MLLM's text-based 2D predictions into real-world 3D robot motion policies, addressing the challenges of 2D-to-3D conversion, ego-view changes, and dynamic state awareness.
 - **Point Tracker (LocoTrack):** As the robot moves, its ego-view changes. This module dynamically updates the 2D keypoints predicted by the MLLM to ensure they remain consistent with the robot's current visual perspective. It runs at a high frequency (15Hz) on an edge device.
 - **Movement Controller:** Takes the updated 2D keypoints and converts them into 3D world coordinates using depth information from an RGBD camera and camera calibration. These 3D points are then used to estimate the required movement velocity, which drives a low-level walking policy for legged robots or motion planning for robotic arms.
 - **Skill Executor:** Matches the MLLM's predicted embodied skill (e.g., "Walk", "Jump", "Grasp") to a pre-trained policy within a "policy pool" and executes it. This modular design leverages existing, robust low-level robot skills.
 - **Dynamic Takeover:** Provides robustness by allowing the system to switch control back to the MLLM when the robotic adapter encounters a failure (e.g., target keypoints disappear, subtask completion). This enables the MLLM to re-plan or adapt to unexpected situations, forming a robust closed-loop control.

#### 3.3. VeBrain-600k Data Engine

A high-quality, diverse instruction dataset is crucial for training a generalist embodied AI. VeBrain-600k comprises:
- **Multimodal Understanding (200k conversations):** Collected from open-source datasets (ShareGPT4V, MMInstruct) and augmented with GPT-4o generated data including CoT.
- **Visual-Spatial Reasoning (312k items):** From open-source (GPT4Scene) and self-annotated datasets (ScanNet images). Manual annotations for key information (keypoints) and semi-automated generation of descriptive conversations via GPT-4o, with detailed spatial properties (counting, size, distance). For CoT, Gemini-2.0 was leveraged for its creativity in analyzing complex 3D scenes.
- **Robot Control (88k items):** Collected from scratch by human experts (80+ hours of video episodes and motion states for legged robots and robotic arms). Manual annotation of keypoints and actions for these episodes. GPT-4o was used for generating embodied CoT, thinking from the robot's perspective for planning and decision-making (e.g., obstacle avoidance, interaction).
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation & Quality Verification:** CoT is carefully designed to embed reasoning. A cross-model validation pipeline (Gemini-2.0 to assess GPT-4o CoT) and manual inspection by human experts ensure data quality and plausibility.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

VeBrain is fine-tuned on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct using supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for one epoch on 32 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Only the LLM parameters are trainable, keeping the vision encoder and multimodal projector frozen for efficiency and to preserve their pre-trained capabilities. The inference runs at 0.5Hz on a Tesla A100, while the on-robot tracking runs at 15Hz on NVIDIA Jetson Orin.

This comprehensive methodology allows VeBrain to achieve a delicate balance: leveraging the high-level cognitive abilities of MLLMs for perception, reasoning, and planning, while relying on a robust adapter to translate these insights into precise and dynamic physical actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of VeBrain on over 20 diverse benchmarks, encompassing multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control, demonstrates its superior performance and validates its core design principles.

#### 4.1. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Robotic Adapter:** Equipping Qwen2.5-VL with the robotic adapter alone significantly improved success rates on robot control tasks, highlighting its crucial role in bridging the MLLM's abstract outputs to real-world actions.
- **Preservation of Multimodal Capabilities:** Fine-tuning the MLLM with control data (VeBrain-600k) remarkably preserved its multimodal understanding capabilities. This confirms that VeBrain's design successfully mitigates the "knowledge forgetting" or "task conflict" issues often seen in VLA models.
- **Data Contribution:** Each type of data (multimodal understanding, spatial reasoning, robot control) in VeBrain-600k contributed significantly to improving the model's performance in its corresponding capability, underscoring the importance of a diverse and balanced dataset.
- **Comparison with Frameworks:** VeBrain was rigorously compared against two common paradigms:
 - **MLLM-only (e.g., VLM-PC):** Showed strong multimodal understanding but struggled severely with direct robot control due to the task gap.
 - **VLA (e.g., QUART):** Performed well on robot control but significantly sacrificed multimodal understanding capabilities (e.g., -16.3% on MMVet compared to an MLLM-only baseline).
 - **VeBrain's Superiority:** VeBrain achieved the "best trade-off performance" across all tasks, yielding up to +31.5% average gains against other frameworks, validating its unified objective design.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Understanding Results

- VeBrain demonstrated comprehensive advantages across 13 MLLM benchmarks, outperforming state-of-the-art open-source MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL (+5.6% on MMVet) and even closed-source models in many aspects.
- Existing VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA, DiVLA, ChatVLA) generally performed poorly on these benchmarks, confirming their inherent trade-off. RoboBrain, while integrating more multimodal data, still fell short compared to advanced MLLMs in OCR and chart understanding.
- VeBrain achieved the best normalized average performance of 77.1, indicating its strong general multimodal understanding despite its focus on embodied control.

#### 4.3. Visual-Spatial Reasoning Results

- **3D Spatial Benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, ScanRefer, Multi3DRef):** While 3D-specific MLLMs (e.g., Chat-3D, 3D-LLM) or specialist 2D models (GPT4Scene-HDM) often lead in these tasks, VeBrain, as a generalist MLLM, remarkably outperformed GPT4Scene-HDM on all 3D spatial benchmarks (e.g., +5.2 CIDEr on ScanQAval). This highlights its exceptional ability to infer 3D spatial relationships from 2D visual inputs.
- **VSI Benchmark:** VeBrain outperformed all existing MLLMs, including larger closed-source models like GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5 Pro, on the challenging VSI benchmark (average score of 39.9, +4.0% against Qwen2.5-VL-7B), further confirming its advanced spatial reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.4. Robot Control Results

- **Legged Robot Tasks (7 tasks):** Existing VLA models and MLLMs struggled with complex tasks requiring compositional capabilities (e.g., "Interaction," "Transport," "Complex Find"). VeBrain achieved significantly better results, demonstrating high success rates across easy, middle, and hard locomotion tasks. For instance, it achieved a 50% gain on the long-horizon "Complex Transport" task compared to baselines.
- **Robotic Arm Tasks (7 tasks):** Similarly, VeBrain showed superior performance in manipulation tasks. Common VLAs (OpenVLA, $\pi^0$) had limited success, especially in long-horizon tasks (e.g., $\pi^0$ success rate dropped to 0% on some long-horizon tasks). VeBrain consistently achieved the highest success rates, outperforming $\pi^0$ by up to 80% in the most challenging manipulation tasks.

#### 4.5. Visualizations

Qualitative results (Fig. 3) vividly illustrate VeBrain's "compositional capabilities." For instance, it can correctly infer the hidden location of a chili pepper and execute a multi-step plan (open drawer, grasp) to retrieve it. Another example shows its ability to verify task completion (goods delivered), showcasing combined perception, reasoning, and control.

In summary, VeBrain sets a new benchmark for embodied AI, demonstrating that it is possible to train a unified model that excels simultaneously in general multimodal understanding, detailed visual-spatial reasoning, and robust real-world robot control, overcoming the limitations of previous fragmented approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of VeBrain marks a significant advancement in the pursuit of generalist embodied AI, moving beyond the current dichotomy between powerful language-vision models and specialized robotic control systems. Its significance and potential impact are profound, touching upon both fundamental AI research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Unification Breakthrough in Embodied AI

VeBrain is arguably the first framework to successfully unify multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robust robot control without substantial trade-offs in performance. By elegantly reformulating robot control tasks into a format amenable to MLLMs, it bridges a critical gap. This breakthrough suggests a viable path towards building truly intelligent agents that can "see," "think," and "act" cohesively in complex, dynamic physical environments, moving closer to the vision of general-purpose embodied intelligence.

#### 5.2. Novel Control Paradigm and Architectural Innovation

The core innovation of formulating robot control as 2D visual keypoint detection and embodied skill recognition, coupled with a sophisticated robotic adapter, is a novel control paradigm. This approach simplifies the integration of high-level cognitive abilities (from the MLLM) with precise, low-level robotic execution. The modular design of the robotic adapter (point tracker, movement controller, skill executor, dynamic takeover) provides both efficiency and robustness, crucial for real-world deployment. This architectural ingenuity could inspire new directions in human-robot interaction and robot learning.

#### 5.3. High-Quality Dataset as a Community Resource

The introduction of VeBrain-600k, a carefully curated and diverse instruction dataset featuring multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT), is a substantial contribution to the research community. Such high-quality, comprehensive datasets are vital for training capable generalist models. By combining multimodal understanding, visual-spatial reasoning, and robot control data with rich CoT, VeBrain-600k provides a valuable resource for future research in embodied AI, facilitating the development of more intelligent and adaptable agents.

#### 5.4. Enhanced Generalizability and Adaptability

The extensive experiments demonstrate VeBrain's remarkable adaptability, flexibility, and compositional capabilities across a wide array of tasks and robotic platforms (legged robots and robotic arms). Its ability to handle complex, long-horizon tasks by combining perception, reasoning, and control, as shown in the visualizations (e.g., finding hidden objects, verifying task completion), is a key indicator of its potential for real-world scenarios that demand robust performance in uncertain environments. This enhanced generalizability reduces the need for task-specific engineering.

#### 5.5. Accelerating the Future of Robotics and AI Applications

The successful integration demonstrated by VeBrain has immense potential to accelerate the development of more autonomous and intelligent robotic systems.
- **Smart Manufacturing and Logistics:** Robots that can not only understand complex assembly instructions but also reason about their workspace and manipulate objects precisely.
- **Healthcare and Assistive Robotics:** Robots capable of understanding nuanced human commands, navigating complex home environments, and providing assistance with daily tasks.
- **Household and Service Robotics:** Making domestic robots more capable of understanding user intentions, performing diverse chores, and adapting to changes in the home environment.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Robots that can autonomously perceive, reason about, and navigate challenging terrains, making informed decisions in hazardous situations.

By bringing the cognitive power of MLLMs into the physical world, VeBrain paves the way for a new generation of embodied AI that can perceive, understand, and interact with unprecedented levels of intelligence and autonomy. This work signifies a critical step towards creating truly versatile and capable robots that can seamlessly integrate into various aspects of human life and industries.
