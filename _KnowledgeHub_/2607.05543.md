# GEM-Occ: From Visual Geometry Evidence to Embodied Semantic Occupancy Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed HIOcc, a hierarchical indoor occupancy benchmark, and GEM-Occ, a framework converting transient visual geometry into persistent semantic Gaussian occupancy memory with explicit free-space modeling for embodied agents. This approach enhanced occupancy IoU by up to 3.85 points and semantic mIoU by up to 2.68 points in online room-level mapping, while reducing memory usage by 40% in building-level scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing semantic occupancy methods are often single-frame, local, or offline, limiting their applicability for embodied agents needing continuous, scalable mapping across multi-room environments.
- Current indoor datasets lack a unified protocol for supporting diverse observation geometries (perspective and panoramic) and hierarchical mapping across connected building-scale environments.
- Converting transient visual geometry predictions into persistent, structured memory is challenging, often resulting in fragmented surfaces, inconsistent semantics, and a lack of explicit free-space modeling.

## Method

- Introduced **HIOcc**, a hierarchical indoor occupancy benchmark that unifies ScanNet, ScanNet++, and Matterport3D into a ==sparse semantic occupancy format== for multi-scale evaluation.
- Proposed **GEM-Occ**, a ==Gaussian Evidence Memory== framework that converts local visual geometry predictions into ==semantic Gaussian occupancy evidence== and ==free-space ray evidence==.
- Implemented ==visibility- and uncertainty-aware causal fusion== rules to incrementally update a ==hierarchical Gaussian memory==, enabling explicit distinction between observed free and unobserved unknown space with stability during revisits.

## Results

- HIOcc target construction improved semantic-consistency mIoU from **56.5** to **74.9** through progressive observation-aware filtering stages.
- GEM-Occ achieved an occupancy IoU of **61.37** and semantic mIoU of **57.76** for single-observation local prediction, outperforming prior Gaussian-based methods like GPOcc (**60.69** IoU, **55.08** mIoU).
- In room-level online mapping, GEM-Occ improved occupancy IoU from **52.94** to **56.79** and semantic mIoU from **44.81** to **46.20**, while for building-level mapping, it reduced memory consumption from **1.36** to **0.81 MB/m²** and improved query latency from **49.5** to **33.8 ms**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing occupied surfaces as semantic Gaussian primitives provides more compact volumetric support and robust semantic prediction for persistent memory compared to direct pointmap voxelization.
> - Explicitly incorporating free-space ray evidence is crucial for distinguishing observed empty regions from truly unknown areas, leading to significant improvements in online mapping stability and revisit consistency.
> - A hierarchical memory organization, combined with causal fusion and maintenance operations like pruning and merging, is essential for achieving scalable and efficient semantic occupancy mapping in large, connected indoor environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of 3D perception for embodied agents, framing it as a memory challenge where observations must be accumulated into a persistent, queryable representation of the environment. This falls within the larger domains of robotics perception, embodied artificial intelligence, and 3D scene understanding.

Existing research in semantic occupancy prediction has explored various formulations. Early efforts in indoor semantic scene completion and monocular occupancy primarily focused on predicting local volumes from partial observations or reconstructing static scenes with dense voxel grids. Concurrently, the autonomous driving domain has established occupancy as a scalable representation for surround-view perception, forecasting, and scene generation, leveraging large-scale benchmarks and multi-view or temporal models. More recent advancements, inspired by 3D Gaussian Splatting, have introduced object-centric occupancy representations using compact continuous primitives and Gaussian-to-voxel splatting. These Gaussian-based methods often employ depth-guided or geometry-prior initialization to enhance completion accuracy and reduce empty-space anchors.

Despite these developments, a gap persists in approaches that support long-horizon embodied mapping. Many existing methods are single-frame, local, offline, or bounded by predefined room-scale volumes. They often do not causally update maps, explicitly distinguish free space from unknown space, maintain stability under revisits, or scale effectively from local views to rooms and connected buildings.

Similarly, existing indoor datasets and benchmarks, such as NYUv2, ScanNet, ScanNet++, and Matterport3D, have facilitated progress in RGB-D perception. Occupancy-focused extensions like Occ-ScanNet and EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet support local or room-level prediction. However, they generally lack a unified protocol to support high-fidelity perspective scans, panoramic observations, and connected building-scale environments with hierarchical mapping. This work aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a new benchmark and a corresponding method designed for hierarchical, long-horizon semantic occupancy mapping.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework for hierarchical semantic occupancy mapping that enables embodied agents to build and maintain a persistent, queryable 3D understanding of connected indoor environments over long observational horizons. This encompasses the joint representation of occupied structures, observed free space, unknown regions, and semantic categories.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations identified in the current state of embodied perception:

- **Limited Scope of Existing Occupancy Methods:** Many current semantic occupancy methods operate on a single-frame basis, focus on local predictions, are designed for offline processing, or are constrained to single-room environments. This restricts their applicability for embodied agents that continuously explore and interact with dynamic, multi-room, or multi-floor indoor spaces. A need exists for methods that can incrementally update maps causally, ensuring map stability across revisits and scalability from individual viewpoints to entire buildings.
- **Inadequacy of Current Benchmarks:** Existing indoor datasets, while valuable, do not collectively support the full range of requirements for long-horizon embodied mapping. They often lack unified protocols for handling diverse observation geometries (e.g., perspective RGB-D and panoramic views) and hierarchical mapping across connected spaces. This makes it challenging to evaluate methods that aim to maintain consistent spatial memory across different scales.
- **Challenges in Converting Visual Evidence to Persistent Memory:** Current visual geometry models and pointmap-based evidence, while providing strong metric cues, are primarily transient representations. Naively accumulating pointmaps, for instance, can lead to fragmented surfaces, duplicated geometry, inconsistent semantics, missing free-space information, and propagation of observation errors. These issues highlight the need for a mechanism that converts transient visual geometry predictions into a structured, persistent, and reliably updated memory representation that explicitly models observed free space and unknown regions.
- **Need for Robust Online Map Maintenance:** Embodied agents require maps that are consistently useful at every time step of exploration. This necessitates efficient memory management, effective distinction between observed free and unobserved unknown areas, and a mechanism to ensure consistency when revisiting previously mapped regions.

To address these motivations, the authors set two specific objectives:
1. **Develop HIOcc:** Create a hierarchical indoor occupancy benchmark that unifies existing datasets (ScanNet, ScanNet++, Matterport3D) under a common sparse semantic occupancy format. This benchmark aims to support diverse observation geometries and three complementary evaluation regimes: local semantic occupancy prediction, room-level online occupancy mapping, and building-level mapping across connected panoramic environments.
2. **Propose GEM-Occ:** Design a Gaussian Evidence Memory framework that converts transient local visual geometry predictions into persistent semantic Gaussian occupancy memory. This framework is intended to incorporate visibility- and uncertainty-aware causal fusion rules and explicitly model free-space evidence, addressing the limitations of direct pointmap accumulation and enabling robust, scalable online mapping.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces HIOcc, a hierarchical indoor occupancy benchmark, and GEM-Occ, a Gaussian Evidence Memory framework for semantic occupancy mapping.

#### 3.1. HIOcc: Hierarchical Embodied Occupancy Dataset

**Design Goal:** HIOcc aims to overcome limitations of existing indoor occupancy benchmarks by supporting single-view prediction, online room-level updates, and consistent mapping across connected indoor spaces. It achieves this by unifying ScanNet, ScanNet++, and Matterport3D under a common sparse semantic occupancy format while preserving their native observation geometries.

**Annotation Construction:** The construction pipeline involves several stages:
1. **Source Data Unification:** Semantic labels from ScanNet, ScanNet++, and Matterport3D are unified.
2. **Global Semantic Voxel Source:** Annotated 3D scene geometry from the unified sources is voxelized into a global semantic voxel grid.
3. **Observation-Conditioned Target Generation:** For each viewpoint, a local metric volume is cropped around the camera pose and filtered using visual evidence.
 - **Perspective Views (ScanNet, ScanNet++):** Occupied voxels are transformed to the camera frame, retained within the perspective frustum, and filtered by depth consistency. The default target uses a 240x240x144 grid with 0.02m voxels, covering 4.8x4.8x2.88m.
 - **Panoramic Views (Matterport3D):** Each panorama defines a 120x120x60 target with 0.05m voxels, covering 6x6x3m. First-hit visibility is applied over rectified sub-cameras to retain only the nearest occupied surface along each valid ray, addressing potential long occlusion chains in panoramic views.
4. **Sparse Semantic Occupancy Storage:** Targets are stored as sparse semantic occupancy indices (i, j, k, l) along with grid metadata, coordinate conventions, and validity masks. The occupied label space consists of 11 indoor classes (e.g., ceiling, floor, wall, chair, table). Free and unknown states are derived during training/evaluation from crop geometry and visibility masks, rather than being stored explicitly.

**Annotation Validation:** The annotation process is validated stage-by-stage for semantic consistency using a 2D-3D projection approach. Progressive application of local cropping, frustum filtering, depth/first-hit ray filtering, and multi-view semantic validation improves target quality and semantic-consistency mIoU.

#### 3.2. GEM-Occ: Gaussian Evidence Memory Framework

GEM-Occ is designed to convert transient visual geometry predictions into a persistent, hierarchical semantic occupancy memory.

**Overview:**
The framework processes a causal stream of posed visual observations {(I_t, T_t, K_t)}. A local encoder predicts geometry (D_t), image features (F_t), semantic logits (S_t), and confidence (Q_t) from each observation. Instead of directly voxelizing predicted points, GEM-Occ converts them into:
1. **Semantic Gaussian Occupancy Evidence:** Represents occupied surfaces.
2. **Free-space Ray Evidence:** Represents observed empty space.
These are then fused into a hierarchical memory structure.

**Semantic Gaussian Evidence:**
- For each valid pixel or sub-camera ray, the predicted surface point is unprojected and transformed to the world frame (μ_t_i).
- An occupied semantic Gaussian primitive g_t_i = (μ_t_i, Σ_t_i, α_t_i, p_t_i, η_t_i) is instantiated.
 - μ_t_i: World-frame center.
 - Σ_t_i: Anisotropic covariance, aligned with the viewing ray, with transverse axes from pixel footprint and longitudinal axis from depth uncertainty. This provides compact volumetric support.
 - α_t_i: Occupancy opacity.
 - p_t_i: Semantic distribution (softmax of semantic logits).
 - η_t_i: Evidence confidence.
- **Free-space Ray Evidence:** Samples along the ray *before* the first surface hit are accumulated as free-space evidence. Regions behind the hit remain unknown, preserving the distinction between observed free space and unknown space.

**Hierarchical Gaussian Memory:**
- The persistent map M_t = (G_occ_t, R_free_t) consists of semantic Gaussian occupancy memory (G_occ_t) and a sparse free-space ray cache (R_free_t).
- Each memory Gaussian G_t_j = (μ_t_j, Σ_t_j, ℓ_t_j, p_t_j, w_t_j, n_t_j) stores occupancy log-odds (ℓ), semantic distribution (p), accumulated confidence (w), and observation count (n).
- **Hierarchical Organization:** The memory is organized into local caches, room-level submaps, and a building-level connectivity graph to enable scalability without a dense global voxel tensor.

**Causal Memory Fusion:**
- **Matching:** Incoming evidence primitives (g_t_i) are matched to nearby memory Gaussians (G_t-1_j) using a local spatial search and Mahalanobis distance gating.
- **Fusion:** If a match is found, parameters are updated using confidence-weighted fusion. For example, μ_t_j = (λw_t-1_jμ_t-1_j + η_t_iμ_t_i) / ¯w_t_j, where λ is a temporal decay factor. Covariance and confidence are updated similarly.
- **Free-space Fusion:** Memory Gaussians intersecting the visible free-space segment receive negative log-odds evidence (Δℓ_free), suppressing "floaters." Gaussians behind the first hit are not penalized. This visibility-aware update is crucial for preserving valid but occluded geometry.
- **Memory Maintenance:** To manage memory growth, compatible Gaussians are periodically merged (based on spatial and semantic compatibility), and low-confidence or high-uncertainty primitives are pruned.

**Occupancy Query and Training:**
- **Querying:** The memory can be queried at any 3D location or rasterized to an evaluation grid using Gaussian-to-occupancy splatting. Occupancy probability P_occ(x) is computed by probabilistically accumulating contributions from nearby memory Gaussians (κ_j(x)). The semantic distribution P_sem(x) is obtained via normalized Gaussian-weighted aggregation.
- **Labeling:** A query point is labeled 'occupied' if P_occ(x) exceeds a threshold, 'free' if occupied evidence is weak but ray evidence supports free space, and 'unknown' otherwise.
- **Training:** The local encoder and Gaussian evidence adapter are trained with view-conditioned supervision from HIOcc using a multi-component loss function: L = λ_occL_occ + λ_semL_sem + λ_geoL_geo + λ_rayL_ray.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HIOcc and GEM-Occ involved three embodied occupancy settings: single-observation local prediction, room-level online mapping, and building-level panoramic mapping.

#### 4.1. HIOcc Annotation Validation

The construction of HIOcc targets from source meshes was validated to address semantic mismatch and visibility leakage. The progressive application of observation-aware stages—local cropping, frustum filtering, depth-consistency filtering (for perspective views), and first-hit ray filtering (for panoramas)—consistently improved target quality. The final annotation construction process led to an increase in semantic-consistency mIoU from 56.5 to 74.9, indicating strong alignment between generated targets and view-conditioned occupancy mapping.

#### 4.2. Local Semantic Occupancy Prediction

In the single-observation setting on the ScanNet and ScanNet++ splits of HIOcc, GEM-Occ demonstrated performance improvements compared to prior methods:
- **Overall Performance:** GEM-Occ achieved an occupancy IoU of 61.37 and a semantic mIoU of 57.76.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** This performance surpassed both voxel- and depth-aware methods (e.g., MonoScene, ISO) and other Gaussian-based methods like GPOcc (which achieved 60.69 IoU and 55.08 mIoU).
- **Implication:** The results suggest that the semantic Gaussian evidence, as formulated in GEM-Occ, provides stronger local volumetric support and more accurate semantic prediction than direct accumulation of pointmap-derived or frame-level Gaussian predictions.

#### 4.3. Room-level Online Occupancy Mapping

For causal room-level mapping on HIOcc, where methods incrementally build and update a persistent map over sequential observations, GEM-Occ showed enhanced stability and accuracy:
- **Performance Improvement:** GEM-Occ improved occupancy IoU from 52.94 (GPOcc) to 56.79 and semantic mIoU from 44.81 (GPOcc) to 46.20.
- **Temporal Integration:** The gains were particularly notable in the online setting, indicating GEM-Occ's ability to integrate occupied evidence effectively without corrupting already observed free space or previously mapped regions during revisits. This underscores the benefit of its Gaussian memory and explicit free-space modeling.

#### 4.4. Building-level Occupancy Mapping

Evaluation on the Matterport3D split of HIOcc, which involves pano-centric observations across connected multi-room and multi-floor environments, demonstrated GEM-Occ's scalability and stability for long-horizon mapping:
- **Highest Accuracy and Stability:** GEM-Occ achieved the highest accuracy (53.8 IoU, 46.7 mIoU) and stability metrics (41.5 progress AUC, 86.2 revisit consistency).
- **Scalability Benefits of Hierarchical Design:** Compared to a flat Gaussian memory approach, GEM-Occ's hierarchical design significantly reduced memory consumption from 1.36 to 0.81 MB/m² and improved query latency from 49.5 to 33.8 ms. This indicates a favorable trade-off between accuracy and scalability for large-scale environments.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

A detailed ablation analysis confirmed the contribution of key components within GEM-Occ:
- **Semantic Gaussian Evidence vs. Pointmap Voxelization:** Replacing semantic Gaussian evidence with direct pointmap voxelization reduced room-level mIoU from 46.20 to 41.17, highlighting the superior reliability of semantic Gaussians as persistent map elements. Removing semantic Gaussian evidence entirely led to the largest local semantic degradation.
- **Free-space Ray Evidence:** Eliminating free-space ray evidence had a moderate impact on local prediction but substantially reduced online mIoU and revisit consistency, confirming its importance for distinguishing observed free space from unknown regions during causal mapping.
- **Confidence-weighted Fusion:** The use of confidence-weighted fusion improved temporal stability in the room-level setting.
- **Hierarchical Memory:** Removing the hierarchical memory organization increased memory usage from 0.82 to 1.43 MB/m² without improving local accuracy, demonstrating its primary role in scalability.
- **Pruning and Merging:** These memory maintenance operations were found to be essential for long-horizon efficiency, reducing memory consumption from 1.78 to 0.82 MB/m² with minimal impact on prediction quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI and 3D scene understanding by addressing critical challenges in long-horizon, hierarchical semantic occupancy mapping for indoor environments.

The **HIOcc benchmark** is a significant contribution. By unifying diverse datasets (ScanNet, ScanNet++, Matterport3D) and their native observation geometries under a common sparse semantic occupancy format, it provides a standardized and comprehensive platform for evaluating mapping capabilities across varying scales—from local views to rooms and entire connected buildings. This resource is expected to foster further research and development in hierarchical 3D perception for embodied agents.

The **GEM-Occ framework** introduces a novel approach to building persistent semantic occupancy memory. Its methodology of treating local visual geometry predictions as transient evidence and converting them into semantic Gaussian occupancy primitives, coupled with explicit free-space ray evidence and visibility-aware causal updates, offers several advancements:
- **Improved Map Fidelity and Stability:** GEM-Occ's ability to produce cleaner semantic structures, reduce spurious occupied regions, and maintain map stability under revisits suggests that it offers a more robust and reliable representation of the environment compared to prior methods.
- **Enhanced Free-Space Reasoning:** Explicitly modeling free-space evidence is crucial for embodied agents, enabling them to distinguish between observed empty areas and truly unknown regions. This contributes to safer navigation and more informed exploration strategies.
- **Scalability for Complex Environments:** The hierarchical memory organization (local caches, room-level submaps, building-level graph) allows GEM-Occ to manage memory efficiently and scale to complex, multi-room, and multi-floor indoor environments, which is a prerequisite for real-world embodied applications.

The demonstrated improvements in local prediction accuracy, online map stability, revisit consistency, and building-level scalability indicate that GEM-Occ provides a foundation for more sophisticated embodied behaviors. Potential impacts include:
- **Advanced Embodied Navigation:** Agents equipped with such maps could perform semantic-goal navigation, path planning, and obstacle avoidance more effectively.
- **Enhanced Exploration and Object Search:** Robust semantic occupancy memory would allow agents to track explored areas, identify unknown regions, and efficiently locate objects based on their semantic properties.
- **Complex Human-Robot Interaction:** A detailed and continuously updated semantic understanding of the environment could facilitate more natural and effective interaction between humans and robots in shared spaces.

While the current work focuses on static indoor scenes and relies on local geometry estimates, it lays the groundwork for future extensions to dynamic environments, finer-grained semantics, and direct integration with downstream robotic tasks, ultimately contributing to the development of more capable and intelligent embodied AI systems.
