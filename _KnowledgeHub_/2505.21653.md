# Think Before You Diffuse: Infusing Physical Rules into Video Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffPhy, a framework from Johns Hopkins University and Adobe Research, integrates explicit physical reasoning into video diffusion models to generate physically plausible and photo-realistic videos. It consistently outperforms existing text-to-video models on benchmarks like PhyGenBench and VideoPhy2, with human evaluations confirming higher semantic adherence and physical commonsense.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art text-to-video diffusion models frequently generate videos that lack physical plausibility, exhibiting unrealistic interactions and dynamics.
- These models primarily learn physics implicitly from data, struggling to reliably capture and generalize complex physical principles across diverse scenarios.
- Traditional physics engines offer accuracy but are labor-intensive, require manual specification, and scale poorly for general, complex video generation.

## Method

- The **DiffPhy** framework fine-tunes a pre-trained video ==diffusion model== by infusing explicit physical rules and reasoning derived from language models.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) infer detailed physical attributes, phenomena, and enhanced prompts from user input using Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) evaluate generated video latents against these inferred physical contexts, providing differentiable supervision via specific loss functions and a failure-aware attention injection mechanism.

## Results

- DiffPhy consistently surpasses other text-to-video models across various benchmarks, including PhyGenBench and VideoPhy2, demonstrating improved physical plausibility and semantic adherence.
- The model achieves the highest scores in key physical phenomena detection and physical order verification, accurately representing complex physical events and their sequences.
- Human evaluations confirm DiffPhy's superior performance in generating videos with higher Semantic Adherence and Physical Commonsense compared to strong baselines like CogVideoX and the fine-tuned Wan model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Relying solely on implicit learning from vast video data is insufficient for diffusion models to reliably capture and generalize complex physical laws.
> - Explicitly integrating LLM-derived physical reasoning and MLLM-based verification offers a method to combine the generative power of diffusion models with rule-based physical accuracy.
> - MLLMs can serve as dynamic supervisory signals to guide generative model training, transforming physical evaluations into differentiable loss functions for refined gradient updates and targeted error correction.
