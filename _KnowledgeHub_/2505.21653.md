# Think Before You Diffuse: Infusing Physical Rules into Video Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffPhy, a framework from Johns Hopkins University and Adobe Research, integrates explicit physical reasoning into video diffusion models to generate physically plausible and photo-realistic videos. It consistently outperforms existing text-to-video models on benchmarks like PhyGenBench and VideoPhy2, with human evaluations confirming higher semantic adherence and physical commonsense.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art text-to-video diffusion models frequently generate videos that lack physical plausibility, exhibiting unrealistic interactions and dynamics.
- These models primarily learn physics implicitly from data, struggling to reliably capture and generalize complex physical principles across diverse scenarios.
- Traditional physics engines offer accuracy but are labor-intensive, require manual specification, and scale poorly for general, complex video generation.

## Method

- The **DiffPhy** framework fine-tunes a pre-trained video ==diffusion model== by infusing explicit physical rules and reasoning derived from language models.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) infer detailed physical attributes, phenomena, and enhanced prompts from user input using Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) evaluate generated video latents against these inferred physical contexts, providing differentiable supervision via specific loss functions and a failure-aware attention injection mechanism.

## Results

- DiffPhy consistently surpasses other text-to-video models across various benchmarks, including PhyGenBench and VideoPhy2, demonstrating improved physical plausibility and semantic adherence.
- The model achieves the highest scores in key physical phenomena detection and physical order verification, accurately representing complex physical events and their sequences.
- Human evaluations confirm DiffPhy's superior performance in generating videos with higher Semantic Adherence and Physical Commonsense compared to strong baselines like CogVideoX and the fine-tuned Wan model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Relying solely on implicit learning from vast video data is insufficient for diffusion models to reliably capture and generalize complex physical laws.
> - Explicitly integrating LLM-derived physical reasoning and MLLM-based verification offers a method to combine the generative power of diffusion models with rule-based physical accuracy.
> - MLLMs can serve as dynamic supervisory signals to guide generative model training, transforming physical evaluations into differentiable loss functions for refined gradient updates and targeted error correction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-video (T2V) generation has seen rapid advancements, primarily driven by diffusion models. Models like OpenAI's Sora, Google DeepMind's Veo, and various open-source initiatives (e.g., Wan, Pika, KlingAI) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in producing high-fidelity, visually pleasing, and diverse videos from textual prompts. These models have unlocked new possibilities in content creation, gaming, and even embodied AI simulation.

However, a critical limitation of these cutting-edge models is their struggle with **physical plausibility**. While they excel at synthesizing realistic textures, appearances, and general motion, they often falter when it comes to accurately depicting complex physical interactions, forces, and dynamics. This is because these models primarily learn "physics" implicitly by observing vast amounts of video data. The inherent complexity of real-world physics makes it exceedingly difficult for data-driven models to reliably capture and generalize these principles across diverse scenarios. Generated videos frequently exhibit "unnatural" phenomena, such as objects defying gravity, improper collision responses, or liquids behaving illogically.

Conversely, **traditional computer graphics and simulation approaches** (e.g., using physics engines like NVIDIA PhysX) are designed to produce inherently physically accurate results by defining explicit physical parameters and rules for a scene. These methods are precise but suffer from significant drawbacks: they are labor-intensive, require manual specification of physical properties, and typically scale poorly to complex, real-world scenarios. Consequently, they are often confined to simplified environments or specific physical phenomena like rigid body dynamics or basic fluid simulations.

"THINK BEFORE YOU DIFFUSE" positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms. It aims to bridge the gap between the generative power and visual fidelity of diffusion models and the physical accuracy of simulation engines. The paper recognizes that relying solely on implicit learning from data is insufficient for robust physical understanding in T2V generation. Instead, it proposes a novel approach to *infuse explicit physical rules and reasoning* into the diffusion process, thereby combining the strengths of both data-driven generation and rule-based physical understanding. This work represents a significant step towards developing "world models" for AI, where generated content not only looks real but also behaves realistically according to the laws of physics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable video diffusion models to generate physically-correct and photo-realistic videos from arbitrary user prompts**. This overarching goal is motivated by the recognized shortcomings of existing text-to-video diffusion models, which, despite their visual prowess, frequently fail to synthesize accurate physical effects.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing the "Physics Gap" in Video Diffusion:** Current models learn physics implicitly from data, leading to videos that are visually appealing but often physically implausible. The paper aims to overcome this by introducing explicit physical reasoning.
- **Leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for Physical Context:** User prompts are often brief and lack detailed physical descriptions. A key objective is to use LLMs to infer rich physical context (attributes, phenomena, enhanced prompts) from simple text, thereby providing the diffusion model with a deeper understanding of the desired physical interactions.
- **Developing a "Physics-Aware" Training Strategy:** The research seeks to create a novel training paradigm that can effectively incorporate the inferred physical context. This involves using Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to verify intermediate video latents against physical rules, converting these verifications into differentiable supervision signals, and guiding the diffusion model's gradient updates.
- **Ensuring Semantic Alignment and Commonsense:** While prioritizing physical accuracy, the model must also maintain high visual quality, semantic consistency with the input prompt, and overall physical commonsense.
- **Correcting "Failure Facts":** A specific objective is to implement a mechanism (attention injection) to identify and correct instances where the model fails to generate specific physical phenomena, allowing for targeted refinement.
- **Curating a Dedicated Dataset:** Recognizing the lack of suitable datasets for physics-aware video generation, the authors aim to build a high-quality dataset of real-world videos explicitly labeled with diverse physical actions and events to facilitate effective fine-tuning.

In essence, the paper aims to shift video generation from merely producing visually plausible outputs to creating genuinely believable simulations that adhere to the fundamental laws of the physical world, making generative AI more robust and applicable in demanding scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of DiffPhy is a generic framework that fine-tunes a pre-trained video diffusion model to become physics-aware, leveraging the reasoning capabilities of LLMs and MLLMs. The methodology is structured into several interconnected components:

#### 3.1. Physical Context Reasoning via LLMs

1. **Inferring Rich Physical Context:** Given a user text prompt, a pre-trained LLM (GPT O4-mini for inference, DeepSeek-R1-32B for dataset curation) is used to infer detailed physical information. This goes beyond simple prompt expansion.
2. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** The LLM employs CoT prompting to systematically identify:
 - **Physical Attributes:** Underlying forces (gravity, friction), kinematic relationships (velocity, acceleration), and interaction rules (elastic collisions). This includes identifying initiators, affected entities, and how they interact.
 - **Physical Phenomena:** A list of observable facts or consequences associated with the event (e.g., "candle changes position from upright to lying down" when a candle falls).
 - **Enhanced Prompt:** A more detailed and physically descriptive prompt for video generation, potentially introducing logical entities not explicitly mentioned by the user (e.g., a "cat" if a "candle falls").
3. **Structured Output:** The LLM's output is parsed into machine-readable rules and an enhanced textual prompt.

#### 3.2. Physics-Aware Model Training

DiffPhy fine-tunes a pre-trained video diffusion model (specifically Wan 2.1-14B, a DiT-based model) using the extracted physical context. The training paradigm incorporates a three-step procedure:

1. **Physical Context Verification:** During training, at a sampled diffusion timestep, the predicted latent video clip is decoded into pixel space. A Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) (VideoCon-Physics, based on CLIP and LLaMA-7B) then evaluates this video against the previously reasoned physical context across three dimensions:
 - **Physical Phenomena Alignment:** Verifies if the generated video contains the expected physical facts (e.g., if a ball *actually leaves the racket*). The MLLM assigns a binary score (0: not matched, 1: matched) for each fact.
 - **Physical Commonsense:** Evaluates the overall physical plausibility of the scene, assigning a score from 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest).
 - **Semantic Consistency:** Assesses how well the generated video aligns with the enhanced input text prompt, also scored from 1 to 5.

2. **Continuous Score Estimation:** MLLM outputs textual tokens corresponding to discrete scores (e.g., "1", "one", "not matched"). To provide stable and differentiable supervision, these discrete textual outputs are converted into continuous expected scores using a weighted average of token probabilities (Eq. 1). This smooths the feedback signal for gradient updates.

3. **Loss Function Formulation:** Three MLLM-based loss functions are defined to guide the diffusion model:
 - **Phenomena Loss ($L_{phen}$):** An MSE loss that penalizes deviations from the "matched" state for each physical phenomenon ($||E(f_i) - 1||_2^2$).
 - **Physical Commonsense Loss ($L_{com}$):** An MSE loss that encourages the overall physical plausibility score to be high ($||E(s_{com})/\tau - 1||_2^2$, where $\tau=5$).
 - **Semantic Consistency Loss ($L_{sem}$):** An MSE loss that ensures alignment with the input prompt ($||E(s_{sem})/\tau - 1||_2^2$).

4. **Failure-Aware Refinement (Attention Injection):** For "not matched" physical facts, an auxiliary module injects targeted attention into the diffusion model. This module encodes the failed fact into text embeddings and conditions the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) via a separate cross-attention layer in each block. A ZeroConv layer connects this to the original model, and a LoRA adapter is fine-tuned to reduce cost, allowing the model to specifically learn from and correct failure cases.

5. **Overall Training Objective:** The final loss function combines the original denoising score matching objective ($L_\epsilon$) with the three MLLM-based losses: $L = L_\epsilon + \beta(L_{phen} + L_{com} + L_{sem})$. During training, the original DiT blocks are frozen, and only LoRA layers are trained. An optional iterative refinement step during inference allows for re-evaluating and correcting failure cases.

#### 3.3. HQ-Phy Dataset

The authors curate a new dataset, **HQ-Phy**, comprising approximately 8,000 real-world videos selected from VIDGEN-1M. Each video is associated with a caption, enhanced prompts (generated by DeepSeek-R1-32B), and extracted physical phenomena labels. This dataset, specifically designed for physics-aware video generation, addresses the limitations of existing synthetic or evaluation-only datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DiffPhy demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in generating physically plausible and semantically coherent videos across diverse scenarios, as evidenced by extensive experiments on public benchmarks and human evaluations.

#### 4.1. PhyGenBench Evaluation

- **Overall Superiority:** DiffPhy consistently outperforms a wide range of open and closed-source T2V models (e.g., CogVideoX, Open-Sora, LaVie, Pika, Kling, Wan) on the PhyGenBench benchmark. It achieves the highest average score and the best performance across all four physical domains: Mechanics, Optics, Thermal processes, and Material properties.
- **Detailed Physical Phenomena Capture:** DiffPhy scores highest in both "key phenomena detection" and "physical order verification," indicating its ability to not only include specific physical events but also to arrange them in a logically and physically coherent sequence. This is particularly evident when compared to baseline Wan and the strong closed-source model Kling.

#### 4.2. VideoPhy2 Benchmark Evaluation

- **Significant Improvement over Baselines:** DiffPhy substantially outperforms Wan (the backbone model used for fine-tuning) across all subcategories of VideoPhy2, including "Hard" physical scenarios, "Activity" (sports/physical actions), and "Interaction" (object interactions).
- **Effectiveness of CoT:** Incorporating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for enhanced prompts further boosts DiffPhy's performance, achieving the highest average scores across all video categories in human evaluations. Qualitative examples demonstrate that DiffPhy (with CoT) produces more natural and description-consistent videos compared to others, such as a tennis ball correctly leaving the racket or boxes realistically flying after a crash.
- **Human Evaluation Validation:** Human annotators consistently rate DiffPhy videos higher on both Semantic Adherence (SA) and Physical Commonsense (PC) compared to strong baselines like CogVideoX and Wan. The human evaluations, considered the "definitive standard," confirm the model's ability to generate videos that are both semantically aligned and physically believable. The paper notes discrepancies between model-based and human-based ratings, underscoring the importance of human judgment for nuanced physical plausibility.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of each proposed component:

- **MLLM-based Loss Functions:** Incorporating the combined MLLM-based loss functions ($L_{phen}$, $L_{com}$, $L_{sem}$) yields substantial gains over the baseline Wan, demonstrating the critical role of MLLM supervision.
- **Phenomena Loss and Attention Injection:** The addition of phenomena loss ($L_{phen}$) and attention injection for resolving failure facts further improves performance, highlighting the benefit of targeted error correction.
- **CoT Prompting:** Chain-of-Thought enhanced prompting significantly contributes to improved performance by providing clearer and richer physical context, leading to better prompt understanding and reasoning by the diffusion model.
- **Iterative Refinement:** An optional second-pass refinement during inference, where failure cases from the first pass are corrected via attention injection, provides additional performance improvements, although at the cost of runtime overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of DiffPhy marks a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly for text-to-video synthesis. Its ability to infuse explicit physical reasoning into diffusion models addresses a fundamental challenge that has plagued high-fidelity video generation: the lack of physical plausibility.

The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Reality Gap:** DiffPhy moves generative video models beyond merely photo-realistic appearance to genuinely realistic behavior, making generated content more believable and trustworthy. This is crucial for applications where physical interactions are paramount.
- **Advancing World Models:** By integrating LLM-driven physical reasoning and MLLM-based verification, DiffPhy contributes to the broader goal of building "world models" that not only understand but also accurately simulate the physics of our world. This has implications for embodied AI, robotics, and scientific simulations.
- **Enhanced Content Creation:** For industries like film production, animation, and game development, DiffPhy offers the potential to automate the creation of complex physical scenes with greater accuracy, reducing manual labor and enabling more sophisticated visual effects. Imagine generating dynamic scenes with realistic fluid dynamics, rigid body collisions, or cloth simulations directly from text.
- **Improved Safety and Realism in AI Training Environments:** Physically accurate video generation can create more realistic and robust virtual environments for training autonomous systems, such as self-driving cars or robots, allowing them to learn and practice in conditions that closely mimic the real world.
- **Novel Training Paradigm:** The proposed training strategy, which uses MLLMs as dynamic supervisory signals to guide gradient updates and perform failure-aware refinement, represents a novel approach to incorporating higher-level reasoning into generative models. This paradigm could be adapted to other domains requiring complex logical or causal consistency.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** By leveraging and fine-tuning an open-source model like Wan 2.1-14B and providing a curated dataset, the authors contribute to the accessibility and reproducibility of physics-aware video generation research.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, primarily stemming from the current capabilities of MLLMs. While MLLMs excel at textual physical reasoning, their ability to interpret and evaluate detailed physical alignment in videos remains limited, often producing shallow or generic feedback. This highlights the need for:

1. **More Robust MLLM Video Reasoning:** Future work should focus on developing MLLMs specifically designed for nuanced video interpretation and feedback generation, capable of processing long-sequence video data more effectively.
2. **Expanded and Higher-Quality Datasets:** Scaling up and further refining the curated HQ-Phy dataset with more fine-grained filtering and quality control is crucial for boosting model learning and generalization across even more diverse physical phenomena.
3. **Better Failure Case Resolution:** Exploring more advanced mechanisms for solving failure cases could lead to further improvements in model performance and robustness.

Despite these limitations, DiffPhy represents a significant step towards creating intelligent generative systems that understand and respect the physical laws governing our reality, paving the way for a new generation of physically grounded AI applications.
