# JoyAI-RA 0.5: Scaling Robot Manipulation Learning via Dual Action Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> The JoyAI-RA Team developed JoyAI-RA 0.5, a Vision-Language-World-Action (VLWA) framework that scales robot manipulation learning by effectively integrating diverse data sources through a dual action alignment paradigm. This framework achieved an average task score of 92.0 on seen tasks and robust performance on unseen generalization for real-world AgiBot G1, outperforming baseline models.

## Problem

- The high cost and scarcity of high-quality, task-specific robot data limit the scalability and generalization of robot manipulation policies.
- Existing approaches (VLA models for semantics, WAMs for dynamics) struggle to integrate both, hindering the full utilization of heterogeneous data sources.
- Leveraging vast human egocentric video for robot learning is challenging due to absent action annotations and embodiment discrepancies, preventing its use as a primary data source.

## Method

- Developed **JoyAI-RA 0.5**, a ==Vision-Language-World-Action (VLWA)== framework, to learn from a large-scale corpus of human egocentric, simulation, and real-robot data.
- Implemented a ==dual action alignment== paradigm: implicit alignment infers latent actions from action-free videos for dynamics learning, while explicit alignment unifies reliable human and robot trajectories into a canonical physical action space.
- Integrated an ==inner-outer-loop reinforcement learning== strategy for efficient task adaptation on edge devices and asynchronous refinement of the foundation policy on a central server.

## Results

- JoyAI-RA 0.5 achieved average task score **92.0** on seen tasks (vs. baseline **74.0**) and up to **70.0%** success on unseen pick-and-place tasks via inner-outer-loop RL adaptation, with strong AgiBot G1 generalization.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that both implicit and explicit action alignments are essential and complementary, with implicit alignment boosting unseen performance and explicit alignment enhancing seen-task precision.
- Scaling the human egocentric video pretraining corpus monotonically improved downstream robot performance, with seen-task scores increasing from **47.8** to **85.6** and unseen-task scores from **37.6** to **60.2**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale human egocentric video, when processed with appropriate dual alignment, can serve as a primary and scalable data source for robot manipulation, moving beyond an auxiliary role.
> - Combining implicit latent action learning for dynamics with explicit physical action alignment for precision is crucial for effectively integrating heterogeneous, weakly labeled data while preventing negative transfer.
> - A latent-action-conditioned world model (LAC-WM) effectively transfers generalizable dynamics priors from diverse human videos, significantly improving robustness to unseen task variations and appearance shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation learning faces a fundamental challenge: the scarcity and expense of acquiring high-quality, task-specific robot data. This limitation hinders the development of generalist policies capable of performing diverse tasks across various environments. A promising direction involves leveraging heterogeneous data sources, including human egocentric videos, simulation data, and real-robot demonstrations, which collectively offer a wider range of manipulation experiences. However, these data sources vary significantly in supervision quality and embodiment, often lacking reliable action annotations or presenting mutually incompatible action representations.

Existing approaches typically fall into two categories:
1. **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** These models leverage pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) to acquire semantic priors for instruction following and reasoning. However, they often lack an understanding of physical dynamics, limiting their ability to utilize abundant unlabeled human videos that depict how the physical world evolves under manipulation.
2. **World-Action Models (WAMs):** These models focus on learning predictive physical dynamics directly from visual inputs. While effective at capturing dynamics, they generally lack semantic grounding and often require action annotations, which are frequently unavailable in human videos.

Consequently, VLA models often capture semantics without dynamics, and WAMs capture dynamics without semantics, leading to limitations in fully exploiting heterogeneous data. Latent Action Models (LAMs) have emerged to address some of these issues by inferring embodiment-agnostic latent actions from visual transitions, potentially enabling the use of unlabeled human videos. However, existing LAMs have often not been scaled to very large and heterogeneous video corpora and typically rely on single-view visual transitions, which may limit the captured interaction and geometric information.

JoyAI-RA 0.5 addresses these limitations by proposing a Vision-Language-World-Action (VLWA) framework that integrates physical world-dynamics priors with visual semantics. It aims to scale manipulation learning across diverse data sources by introducing a dual action alignment paradigm and an inner-outer-loop reinforcement learning strategy. This work positions itself as a development towards overcoming the data scarcity bottleneck and enabling generalist robot policies to effectively learn from heterogeneous, weakly labeled, large-scale data, particularly human egocentric videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable the development of generalist robot manipulation policies by effectively learning from diverse, heterogeneous data sources, including human egocentric videos, simulation data, and real-robot demonstrations. The primary motivation stems from the inherent scarcity and high cost of acquiring robot-specific manipulation data, which limits the scalability and generalization capabilities of policies trained exclusively on such data.

The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Leveraging Human Egocentric Video:** Human egocentric video represents a vast and diverse source of manipulation experience. However, its utility for robot learning is challenging due to the lack of reliable action annotations and fundamental differences between human hand trajectories and robot control commands. A key objective is to transform this abundant but weakly labeled human experience into a transferable and scalable training signal for robots, making it a primary axis for scaling manipulation capability, rather than merely an auxiliary source.
- **Integrating Semantics and Dynamics:** Existing VLA models provide strong semantic understanding but lack physical dynamics, while WAMs model dynamics but lack semantic grounding. The work aims to bridge this gap by integrating both physical world-dynamics priors and visual semantics within a unified framework, allowing for comprehensive understanding and control.
- **Addressing Data Heterogeneity:** Different data sources offer varied forms of supervision and originate from different embodiments. The objective is to derive shared, transferable supervision from these heterogeneous sources, preventing negative transfer that can occur from naively pooling incompatible data.
- **Dual Action Alignment:** To achieve transferable supervision, the research proposes "dual action alignment." This involves:
 - **Implicit Action Alignment:** Inferring latent actions from visual state transitions in action-free human, simulation, and robot data. This enables the guidance of a latent-action-conditioned world model in learning physical dynamics that are shared across embodiments, even without explicit action labels.
 - **Explicit Action Alignment:** Grounding reliable human and robot trajectories in a unified physical action space. This involves creating a canonical action representation and expressing actions as camera-frame chunk-relative end-effector movements, thereby providing physically consistent supervision across diverse embodiments.
- **Efficient Task Adaptation and Continuous Improvement:** To enhance policy performance during deployment, particularly for novel tasks or variations, the work aims to implement an inner-outer-loop reinforcement learning strategy. This mechanism is designed to facilitate efficient task adaptation while continuously improving the underlying foundation policy.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to develop robot policies that are generalist, robust, and scalable, by systematically converting disparate data modalities into complementary and mutually reinforcing learning signals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

JoyAI-RA 0.5 proposes a Vision-Language-World-Action (VLWA) framework to scale robot manipulation learning from heterogeneous data through a dual action alignment paradigm and a staged training process.

#### 3.1. Data Preprocessing and Recipe

The framework utilizes a large-scale pre-training corpus integrating three complementary data sources:
- **Human Egocentric Manipulation Data:** Over 53,000 hours, including aggregated public datasets (Egocentric-100K, Xperience-10M, EgoVerse, Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, EgoDex, EPIC-KITCHENS-100) and an in-house EgoLive dataset (20,000+ hours with fine-grained annotations of actions, objects, and attributes).
- **Simulation Data:** Over 11,000 hours from sources like InternData-A1, InternData-M1, Genie Sim 3.0, and BEHAVIOR-1K, providing scalable supervision with explicit robot actions.
- **Real-Robot Data:** Over 8,000 hours from platforms such as AgiBot World Beta, Galaxea Open-World, RoboCOIN, RoboMIND, RoboMIND 2.0, LET, and Baihu Dataset, covering diverse embodiments and real-world conditions.

These heterogeneous sources are processed through a closed-loop data curation pipeline:
- **Egocentric Video Annotation and Hand-Pose Recovery:** Raw human egocentric videos undergo synchronization, calibration, undistortion, hand motion reconstruction (using HaMeR-based estimator for MANO parameters, ORB-SLAM3 for camera motion), and mapping into a canonical hand-motion representation. Videos are segmented into subtasks and annotated with structured language descriptions. Clips with reliable hand poses are used for explicit alignment; others with valid visual/language content are retained for latent-action-based pretraining.
- **Unified Quality and Diversity Curation:** Episodes are first screened for task-independent validity (data structure, visual quality, cross-modal consistency, state-action correctness). Valid episodes then undergo group-relative quality and diversity curation using an embodied video encoder (V-JEPA 2, Q-Former) to extract representations. SemDeDup identifies similar demonstrations within task groups. Multiple quality metrics (derivative statistics, log dimensionless jerk, power spectral density) are applied to retain high-quality, diverse representatives.
- **Representation-Guided Data Acquisition:** The curated corpus is periodically analyzed in the learned representation space to identify underrepresented capabilities, guiding subsequent data collection efforts (hybrid strategy combining open-ended human recording and targeted instructions).

#### 3.2. Dual Alignment for Heterogeneous Data

The core of the methodology is the dual-alignment paradigm, routing data based on its available supervision.

- **Implicit Alignment via Latent Actions:** For videos without reliable physical action labels or with incompatible action formats, a shared variational latent-action model (LAM) is trained across multi-source video corpora. It infers an abstract transition latent (z_t) from consecutive multi-view observations (spatially concatenated images from head, left, right views). This latent action encodes transition-specific changes needed to reconstruct the subsequent observation, rather than redundant appearance information. The LAM is trained with a combination of pixel-level, perceptual, semantic, motion, and geometric reconstruction objectives, along with a variational information bottleneck. The inferred latent actions (z_t) are then used as deterministic offline labels for Latent-Action-Conditioned World Model (LAC-WM) pretraining.
- **Explicit Alignment via Unified Physical Action Space:** For human and robot trajectories with reliable motion or action annotations, explicit alignment converts heterogeneous native formats into physically consistent, cross-embodiment supervision.
 - **130-D Canonical State-Action Representation:** Trajectories are mapped into a shared 130-dimensional representation with fixed physical semantics (e.g., arm joints, end-effector poses, gripper states, fingertip positions, base/head/waist poses). Embodiment-specific adaptors standardize units, coordinate frames, and rotation representations. For human data, a sparse task-space representation uses wrist pose for end-effector slots and fingertip XYZ coordinates.
 - **Camera-Frame Chunk-Relative End-Effector Action:** Wrist or end-effector pose channels are converted into a camera-aligned, chunk-relative action representation. Target poses within an action chunk are transformed into the conditioning camera frame (compensating for camera motion) and then expressed relative to the conditioning end-effector state. This reduces sensitivity to absolute poses and promotes generalist policy learning.

#### 3.3. Model Architecture

The JoyAI-RA 0.5 architecture comprises three main components:
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** Processes multi-view visual observations and language instructions to produce semantic representations (U_t), encoding task objectives, objects, and spatial relationships.
- **Latent-Action-Conditioned World Model (LAC-WM):** Processes visual observations and language instructions to produce dynamics representations (D_t), capturing how manipulation scenes evolve. It acquires this knowledge through latent-action-conditioned pretraining.
- **Flow-Matching Action Expert:** An attention-based network that takes the concatenated semantic (U_t) and dynamics (D_t) representations, along with proprioceptive state (s_t), as input. It predicts a continuous H-step action chunk in the canonical action space via a masked flow-matching objective.

#### 3.4. Training Paradigm

JoyAI-RA 0.5 employs a four-stage training paradigm:
- **Stage 1: LAC-WM Pretraining:** The LAC-WM is pre-trained on implicitly aligned videos using inferred latent actions (or language instructions) as conditioning. It optimizes for flow-matching velocity regression and a temporal-difference term to improve future-video prediction and action-following fidelity. A condition-dropout strategy jointly trains latent-action-conditioned and latent-action-free prediction. The LAC-WM is frozen for subsequent stages.
- **Stage 2: VLWA Pretraining:** Action-annotated human, simulation, and real-robot trajectories (explicitly aligned) are used. The frozen LAC-WM extracts dynamics representations, while the VLM produces semantic representations. The VLM and Action Expert are jointly optimized using masked flow matching for continuous control, alongside VQA and FAST objectives for the VLM to strengthen semantic understanding and action awareness.
- **Stage 3: Target-Robot Post-Training:** The cross-embodiment policy from Stage 2 is further fine-tuned on high-quality demonstrations from the specific target robot. The training formulation is similar to Stage 2, but restricted to target embodiment data and action dimensions.
- **Stage 4: Inner-Outer Loop Reinforcement Learning:** This stage combines rapid task adaptation with continuous foundation-policy improvement.
 - **Inner Loop (Edge Server):** The foundation VLWA is frozen, and a lightweight adapter is trained as a residual policy using an off-policy RL algorithm and human-in-the-loop interaction. This enables efficient task-specific adaptation.
 - **Outer Loop (Central Server):** Asynchronously, the foundation VLWA itself is refined by aggregating successful trajectories from inner-loop interactions (autonomous rollouts and human interventions) with the original post-training data. Improved parameters are periodically synchronized back to the edge. This forms a closed, self-improving system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates JoyAI-RA 0.5 on a real-world AgiBot G1 robotic platform using a dedicated benchmark encompassing seen tasks and unseen generalization scenarios.

#### 4.1. Real-World AgiBot Benchmark Performance

- **Experimental Setup:** The benchmark includes three seen task categories (PnP-Easy, PnP-Hard, Long-Horizon) across six environments, and three unseen generalization dimensions (Object Category & Attribute, Spatial & Topological, Background & Illumination). Each task involved 20 seen and 10 unseen trials, with scores based on subtask completion rates.
- **Comparison with Baseline (π_0.5):** JoyAI-RA 0.5 achieved an average task score of 92.0 on seen tasks, outperforming π_0.5 (74.0). The performance margin increased with task difficulty. On unseen generalization, JoyAI-RA 0.5 also achieved the highest average score, particularly strong under background and illumination variations, suggesting benefits from visual and interaction diversity transferred from human egocentric videos. While π_0.5 maintained a slight advantage in spatial and topological generalization, JoyAI-RA 0.5 demonstrated greater robustness to appearance shifts and stronger in-distribution performance.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning Results

- **Inner-Outer Loop RL Effectiveness:** Experiments on pick-and-place tasks (mouse and headphones) with expanded placement regions (unseen positions) compared different RL strategies. The combination of inner-outer loop RL achieved the highest success rates (70.0% for mouse, 50.0% for headphones), outperforming the original VLWA policy, inner-loop-only, and outer-loop-only adaptations. This indicates that the outer loop enhances the generalization of the foundation VLWA, while the inner loop facilitates rapid task-specific adaptation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Dual Alignment Ablation:**
 - Removing both implicit and explicit alignment resulted in the largest performance drop, particularly for unseen tasks (average score fell to 29.8), indicating the importance of transferable dynamics pretraining and semantic action alignment.
 - Removing implicit alignment primarily reduced unseen performance (46.8), especially under background and illumination variations, supporting its role in transferring visual and interaction diversity from human videos.
 - Removing explicit alignment primarily weakened seen performance (85.7), particularly on precise PnP-Hard tasks, suggesting its necessity for physically consistent supervision and precise execution.
 - The results confirm that implicit and explicit alignments provide distinct and complementary supervision.
- **World Model (WM) Architecture Ablation:** Integrating the WM module consistently improved performance, with a notable absolute increase of 3.1 points on unseen scenes, suggesting that the WM contributes as a generalizable prior.
- **Latent-Action Conditioning Ablation:** The LAC-WM (trained with latent-action conditioning) achieved a seen-task score of 92.1, which was 4.8 points higher than a standard WM without such conditioning (87.3). This supports the notion that latent actions provide a more informative training signal for the world model, leading to more action-sensitive dynamics representations.

#### 4.4. Human Ego-Video Data Scaling Analysis

- **EgoLive Scale and Diversity:** The EgoLive dataset (20,000+ hours, 898,000 episodes, 23.6 million subtasks) was characterized as highly diverse, covering over 600 task scenarios, 2,662 action categories, 53,400 object categories, and 31,400 object attributes. T-SNE visualizations showed that increasing data volume expanded coverage in the action-embedding space, introducing new action types and interaction modes.
- **Scaling with EgoLive Dataset:** As the EgoLive pretraining data scaled from 10% to 100%, held-out human validation loss consistently improved, and downstream robot performance on PnP-Easy and PnP-Hard benchmarks also increased monotonically. Seen-task scores improved from 47.8 to 85.6, and unseen-task scores from 37.6 to 60.2. This indicates that larger ego-video corpora improve pretraining generalization and provide progressively more transferable policy initialization.
- **Scaling LAC-WM with Human Ego-Video:** Varying the human ego-video data volume (10% to 100% of 53,000 hours) while keeping robot data fixed demonstrated monotonic gains in both seen (83.1 to 97.5) and unseen (56.9 to 72.4) task scores. The larger improvements on unseen tasks at earlier scaling stages suggest that additional human video contributes to a stronger, more transferable dynamics prior for the LAC-WM, enhancing robustness to task variations.

These findings collectively support the effectiveness of the dual alignment paradigm and the significant role of large-scale human egocentric video in scaling robot manipulation capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

JoyAI-RA 0.5 introduces a framework designed to address the challenges of data scarcity and heterogeneity in robot manipulation learning. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a method to effectively integrate diverse, weakly labeled data sources—particularly large volumes of human egocentric video—into a cohesive learning signal for robots.

The dual action alignment paradigm is a key contribution, providing distinct mechanisms for different data types: implicit alignment for action-free videos to learn generalizable dynamics, and explicit alignment for action-annotated data to ensure physical grounding and precision. This approach mitigates the negative transfer often associated with naive pooling of incompatible data, enabling instead a complementary reinforcement among heterogeneous sources.

The consistent performance improvements observed with increasing volumes of human egocentric pretraining data suggest that human video can serve as a primary, scalable axis for developing robot manipulation capabilities. This moves beyond its traditional role as merely an auxiliary data source.

The inner-outer-loop reinforcement learning strategy offers a pathway for sustained policy improvement and efficient adaptation in real-world deployment. By combining rapid online task adaptation with asynchronous foundation policy updates, it facilitates continuous learning and generalization.

The potential impact of this work includes:
- **Enabling Generalist Robot Policies:** By leveraging vast, diverse data, the framework contributes to the development of robot policies that can generalize to a wider range of tasks, objects, and environments, reducing the need for costly, domain-specific robot data collection.
- **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition:** The ability to learn from human videos could significantly accelerate the process of teaching robots new skills, making robot deployment more agile and adaptable to evolving needs.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The framework's demonstrated improvements in generalization to unseen objects, backgrounds, and illumination conditions indicate a step towards more robust and reliable robotic systems in varied real-world settings.
- **New Avenues for Data Collection and Utilization:** This research validates the utility of large-scale human video datasets for robotics, potentially encouraging further investment in and development of such resources and methods for their integration.

Overall, JoyAI-RA 0.5 represents an advance in robot learning by offering a structured approach to combine visual semantics and physical dynamics from heterogeneous data, aiming to unlock scalable and generalizable robot manipulation.
