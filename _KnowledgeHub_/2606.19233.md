# Mobile Pedipulation for Object Sliding via Hierarchical Control on a Wheeled Bipedal Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Michigan developed a hierarchical control framework for wheeled bipedal robots to perform dynamic object sliding, termed mobile pedipulation, by integrating a new reduced-order model with NMPC and WBC. The framework enabled a Tron1 robot to slide a 1 kg object laterally from under a desk and scoot a 4 kg object 0.228 meters, while simulations showed the potential to scoot objects up to 23 kg.

## Problem

- Wheeled bipedal robots' capabilities for dynamic interaction and manipulation, particularly non-prehensile object sliding, remained less explored compared to their locomotion abilities.
- Existing planar sliding manipulation methods simplify manipulator dynamics, which is not applicable to highly underactuated wheeled bipedal robots where reaction forces are not directly controllable.
- Prior wheeled bipedal robot control models often relied on simplifications like symmetric configurations, no-slip assumptions, or omitting the hip roll degree of freedom, limiting their applicability to complex 3D dynamics and dynamic object interaction.

## Method

- Developed a hierarchical control framework comprising a robot-object motion planner, a Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control==ler (NMPC), and a Whole-Body Controller (WBC) for mobile pedipulation tasks.
- Introduced a Three Rigid Bodies (TRB) reduced-order model that captures non-linear 3D dynamics, explicitly retains the hip roll degree of freedom, and supports multiple wheel-environment contact modes (rolling, slipping, non-contact) without restrictive assumptions.
- Extended the NMPC with a TRB-Object (TRBO) model to directly incorporate object dynamics and Coulomb friction complementarity constraints, enabling precise object state tracking and robust handling of stick-slip transitions.

## Results

- The Tron1 robot successfully demonstrated mobile pedipulation on hardware by performing lateral sliding to retrieve a 1 kg object from under a desk and by scooting a 4 kg object a total of **0.228** meters over eight cycles.
- Simulation studies indicated the controller's ability to initiate sliding motion for objects up to 23 kg (heavier than the robot's own mass of 20 kg) when the object-ground friction coefficient was **0.3**.
- The TRBO-based NMPC achieved a **100%** success rate and lower object state tracking error in simulation for lateral sliding tasks with object mass mismatch (1-3 kg), outperforming the TRB-based NMPC which only succeeded in **66%** of trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly capturing the hip roll degree of freedom and supporting various wheel-environment contact modes in a reduced-order model is crucial for enabling versatile and dynamic pedipulation behaviors in wheeled bipedal robots.
> - Integrating object dynamics directly into the Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (TRBO-NMPC) improves object state tracking accuracy and enhances robustness to uncertainties, such as object mass mismatch, compared to approaches that exclude object dynamics.
> - Wheeled bipedal robots can leverage their inherent wheel-environment contact stability for effective non-prehensile object sliding, offering a solution for manipulating objects difficult to grasp or located in confined spaces.
