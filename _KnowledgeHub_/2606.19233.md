# Mobile Pedipulation for Object Sliding via Hierarchical Control on a Wheeled Bipedal Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Michigan developed a hierarchical control framework for wheeled bipedal robots to perform dynamic object sliding, termed mobile pedipulation, by integrating a new reduced-order model with NMPC and WBC. The framework enabled a Tron1 robot to slide a 1 kg object laterally from under a desk and scoot a 4 kg object 0.228 meters, while simulations showed the potential to scoot objects up to 23 kg.

## Problem

- Wheeled bipedal robots' capabilities for dynamic interaction and manipulation, particularly non-prehensile object sliding, remained less explored compared to their locomotion abilities.
- Existing planar sliding manipulation methods simplify manipulator dynamics, which is not applicable to highly underactuated wheeled bipedal robots where reaction forces are not directly controllable.
- Prior wheeled bipedal robot control models often relied on simplifications like symmetric configurations, no-slip assumptions, or omitting the hip roll degree of freedom, limiting their applicability to complex 3D dynamics and dynamic object interaction.

## Method

- Developed a hierarchical control framework comprising a robot-object motion planner, a Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control==ler (NMPC), and a Whole-Body Controller (WBC) for mobile pedipulation tasks.
- Introduced a Three Rigid Bodies (TRB) reduced-order model that captures non-linear 3D dynamics, explicitly retains the hip roll degree of freedom, and supports multiple wheel-environment contact modes (rolling, slipping, non-contact) without restrictive assumptions.
- Extended the NMPC with a TRB-Object (TRBO) model to directly incorporate object dynamics and Coulomb friction complementarity constraints, enabling precise object state tracking and robust handling of stick-slip transitions.

## Results

- The Tron1 robot successfully demonstrated mobile pedipulation on hardware by performing lateral sliding to retrieve a 1 kg object from under a desk and by scooting a 4 kg object a total of **0.228** meters over eight cycles.
- Simulation studies indicated the controller's ability to initiate sliding motion for objects up to 23 kg (heavier than the robot's own mass of 20 kg) when the object-ground friction coefficient was **0.3**.
- The TRBO-based NMPC achieved a **100%** success rate and lower object state tracking error in simulation for lateral sliding tasks with object mass mismatch (1-3 kg), outperforming the TRB-based NMPC which only succeeded in **66%** of trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly capturing the hip roll degree of freedom and supporting various wheel-environment contact modes in a reduced-order model is crucial for enabling versatile and dynamic pedipulation behaviors in wheeled bipedal robots.
> - Integrating object dynamics directly into the Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (TRBO-NMPC) improves object state tracking accuracy and enhances robustness to uncertainties, such as object mass mismatch, compared to approaches that exclude object dynamics.
> - Wheeled bipedal robots can leverage their inherent wheel-environment contact stability for effective non-prehensile object sliding, offering a solution for manipulating objects difficult to grasp or located in confined spaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Wheeled bipedal robots are platforms designed to integrate the high mobility of wheels with the maneuverability provided by legs, offering potential for versatile tasks. Prior research has demonstrated the terrain adaptability of these robots in locomotion tasks, including various forms of rolling, stepping, and jumping. However, the capacity of wheeled bipedal robots to dynamically interact with the environment and manipulate external objects has been less explored compared to their fully bipedal counterparts.

The concept of leveraging robot legs for manipulation, termed "pedipulation," presents distinct advantages for specialized tasks where traditional, mounted manipulators might be unsuitable. Wheeled bipedal robots are particularly suited for pedipulation due to their wheel-type end effectors. These wheels allow the robot to maintain continuous ground contact, offering a stable interface for applying forces to objects without the need for periodic contact breaks for balance, which is common in purely legged systems. This characteristic makes them potentially effective for planar object sliding manipulation, where the wheels directly facilitate object movement. Such sliding-based interaction offers a solution for mobile manipulation scenarios involving non-prehensile objects (those difficult to grasp), non-pushable objects (like welcome mats or cardboard sheets flush with the ground), or objects located in confined spaces, such as under furniture or in corners.

Existing literature on planar sliding manipulation, primarily using robotic manipulators, often simplifies manipulator dynamics by assuming precise control over interaction wrenches. This assumption, while valid for fully actuated manipulators, is not applicable to dynamic pedipulation by highly underactuated wheeled bipedal robots, where reaction forces are not directly controllable. The challenge of integrating underactuated robot dynamics with robot-object interaction has led to work in loco-manipulation for legged robots. For instance, unified Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks have been proposed for quadrupedal loco-manipulation by augmenting centroidal dynamics with object dynamics, and single rigid body models have been used for humanoid loco-manipulation where object interaction is treated as external forces. However, these reduced-order formulations are not directly transferable to wheeled bipedal platforms because wheel-ground contact involves distinctive nonholonomic rolling constraints.

Model-based predictive control is a common strategy for wheeled bipedal robots, which often exhibit non–minimum-phase dynamics. Previous efforts have utilized simplified reduced-order models like the wheeled inverted pendulum (WIP) or wheeled-spring-loaded inverted pendulum (W-SLIP) to capture sagittal-plane dynamics, but these are typically valid only when the robot operates around a symmetric configuration. More complex models, such as wheeled rigid-body dynamics, have been used to capture 3D dynamics but often assume no-slip rolling contact and may simplify the hip joint to a single-DoF pitch, neglecting the hip roll joint. This omission limits their applicability to complex motions like lateral stepping and dynamic object interaction.

This work addresses these limitations by developing a hierarchical control framework specifically for wheeled bipedal robots to perform dynamic mobile pedipulation. It aims to bridge the gap by introducing a general reduced-order model that captures non-linear 3D dynamics, explicitly accounts for the hip roll degree of freedom, and supports multiple wheel-environment contact modes (including non-contact, rolling, and slipping), without imposing restrictive assumptions on wheel contact or workspace. This contribution positions the research as a step towards enhancing the dynamic interaction capabilities of wheeled bipedal robots beyond basic locomotion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a hierarchical control framework that enables wheeled bipedal robots to execute planar object sliding tasks using their wheeled legs, a process termed mobile pedipulation.

The motivation for this work stems from several factors:
- **Leveraging Unique Robot Capabilities:** Wheeled bipedal robots possess a combination of wheeled mobility and leg maneuverability. The authors aim to exploit the inherent stability of wheel-environment contact in these robots to apply forces for object manipulation, contrasting with the more complex intermittent contact management required by purely legged systems during manipulation.
- **Addressing Challenging Manipulation Scenarios:** Traditional prehensile manipulation (grasping) is unsuitable for certain objects (e.g., flat, wide, or easily deformable objects like welcome mats) or scenarios where objects are trapped in confined spaces (e.g., under a desk). Sliding-based pedipulation offers a non-prehensile solution for such tasks, expanding the operational versatility of mobile robots.
- **Filling Gaps in Wheeled Bipedal Robot Control:** Existing control frameworks for wheeled bipeds primarily focus on locomotion and often rely on simplified models that do not adequately capture the robot's full 3D dynamics, particularly the hip roll degree of freedom (DoF), or they impose restrictive "no-slip" assumptions on wheel-environment contact. These simplifications limit the robot's ability to perform dynamic interactions and lateral movements essential for pedipulation.
- **Integrating Locomotion and Manipulation:** The research seeks to create a unified control strategy that can simultaneously regulate both the robot's locomotion and its interaction forces with objects. This integration is crucial for dynamic pedipulation tasks where balance, movement, and object interaction are tightly coupled.
- **Enhancing Robustness to Underactuation and Contact Dynamics:** Wheeled bipedal robots are highly underactuated systems, and their interaction with objects involves complex contact dynamics, including stick-slip transitions. The motivation includes developing a framework that can handle these complexities robustly, allowing for precise control of object manipulation while maintaining robot stability.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to expand the functional repertoire of wheeled bipedal robots, enabling them to perform dynamic manipulation tasks that were previously challenging or unfeasible for this class of robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a hierarchical control framework designed to enable dynamic mobile pedipulation for object sliding tasks on a wheeled bipedal robot. This framework integrates a robot-object motion planner with a Nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (NMPC) and a Whole-Body Controller (WBC).

**A. Reduced-Order Model (TRB)**
The core of the control framework is a novel "Three Rigid Bodies (TRB)" reduced-order dynamical model. This model abstracts the wheeled bipedal robot into three main components: a 6-DoF floating-base torso and two wheels. Each wheel is represented by four variables (3D position and accumulated rotation angle), constrained by a massless, extendable rod connecting it to the torso. This simplification assumes that the leg mass and inertia are negligible compared to the torso and wheels, a premise supported by the Tron1 robot's leg mass being less than 10% of its total mass.

- **Hip Roll DoF:** A key feature of the TRB model is its explicit retention of the hip roll degree of freedom, which is crucial for lateral stepping and object interaction behaviors. The model defines the wheel rotation axis based on hip roll and pitch joint positions, ensuring correct kinematic representation.
- **Wheel Contact Kinematics:** A contact frame is defined for each wheel to describe its interaction with the environment. This framework allows the model to support multiple contact modes—no-contact, rolling contact, and slipping contact—by imposing corresponding constraints on the wheel contact velocity. The contact velocity is derived considering the wheel's linear and angular velocities, with an approximation that out-of-axis rotation (due to hip roll and torso angular velocity) is negligible for contact velocity calculations when these rates are small.
- **Equations of Motion (EoM):** The TRB model's dynamics are formulated, describing the translational and rotational motion of the torso and the individual wheels based on internal interaction wrenches (forces and torques between torso and wheels) and Ground Reaction Forces (GRF). The system state comprises the generalized coordinates and their time derivatives, and the inputs are the interaction wrenches and GRFs.

**B. TRB-Object (TRBO) Model**
For robot-object interaction planning, the TRB model is extended to the "TRB-Object (TRBO)" model by incorporating a point-mass object model. The object dynamics are subjected to forces from the wheel and ground contact. The model includes Coulomb friction complementarity constraints to accurately represent stick-slip transitions at the object-ground interface. A phase-dependent Boolean variable `s_k` indicates whether the object is sliding (`s_k = 1`) or sticking (`s_k = 0`), influencing the friction force calculation.

**C. Hierarchical Control Framework Components**

1. **Robot-Object Motion Planner:**
 - **Purpose:** To generate reference robot-object motions, including initiating stick-slip transitions, for pedipulation tasks.
 - **Approach:** This planner formulates a trajectory optimization problem based on simplified 2D TRBO models. For lateral sliding, a frontal plane 2D TRBO model is used, while for scooting, a sagittal plane model is adopted. This simplification reduces computational complexity.
 - **Implementation:** Direct collocation is used to transcribe the optimal control problem into a Nonlinear Programming (NLP) problem, which is then solved by off-the-shelf solvers. Input limit constraints are included.
 - **Output:** Desired robot-object state trajectories and GRF profiles, which serve as references for the NMPC.

2. **Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC):**
 - **Purpose:** To track the reference trajectories from the motion planner, coordinate wheel-leg motion, and precisely regulate contact forces in real-time.
 - **Cost Function:** A quadratic cost function minimizes the tracking error between the robot's current state and the desired state (`x - x_des`) and control effort (`u - u_des`). A terminal cost function is also included to approximate the infinite-horizon cost-to-go, enhancing robustness.
 - **TRB Dynamics Constraint:** The TRB model's EoM are integrated using a semi-implicit Euler scheme to formulate the system dynamics constraints over the prediction horizon.
 - **Wheel Contact Constraints:** Constraints are imposed based on the contact mode:
 - **Rolling Contact (No-slip):** Wheel contact velocity is set to zero or equal to object acceleration if contacting the object.
 - **Friction Cone:** Reaction forces must satisfy Coulomb friction cone constraints, with different friction coefficients for wheel-ground and wheel-object contacts.
 - **Swing Phase:** During stepping, the wheel contact velocity is unconstrained, and GRF is zero.
 - **TRBO NMPC Extension:** For tasks requiring precise object state tracking, the NMPC can be extended to incorporate a simplified 1D object model (along the desired sliding direction) and its dynamics directly into the optimization problem. This extension includes a sliding constraint that enforces the Coulomb friction complementarity, critical for rapid online solution.

3. **Whole-Body Control (WBC):**
 - **Purpose:** To enhance the robustness of the controller by tracking desired task-space accelerations and GRFs, utilizing the robot's full-order dynamics.
 - **Approach:** WBC is formulated as a Quadratic Programming (QP) problem solved at the current operating point. Decision variables include joint accelerations, joint torques, and GRFs.
 - **Tasks:** Tasks are designed to track torso linear and angular accelerations, wheel center-of-mass positions and velocities, and dampen contact velocities.
 - **Implementation:** Full-order dynamics are computed using Pinocchio, and the QP problem is solved efficiently.

**D. System Setup and Implementation**
The framework is validated on the Tron1 wheeled bipedal robot (approx. 20 kg, 8 actuated leg joints) in both simulation (MuJoCo) and hardware. The optimal control problems are implemented using CasADi and solved by the Fatrop solver on a desktop PC, communicating with the robot via LCM. The NMPC runs at approximately 175 Hz, and the WBC at 800 Hz. A Kalman Filter based on onboard IMU and joint encoders estimates robot pose at 1 kHz. A Vicon motion capture system provides object pose feedback at 250 Hz for pedipulation experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents validation results from both locomotion and pedipulation experiments on the Tron1 wheeled bipedal robot, along with simulation studies to evaluate controller robustness and capabilities.

**A. Locomotion Experiment**
To verify the fundamental effectiveness of the control framework, the robot was tasked with tracking various velocity commands from a human-operated joystick. A combined locomotion experiment involved rapid in-place spinning, weaving around obstacle cones, and fast forward acceleration. The results demonstrated that the robot successfully completed all commanded tasks, exhibiting reliable velocity tracking performance and stable control during these diverse motions. This established the controller's capability in managing basic wheeled bipedal locomotion.

**B. Pedipulation Experiments**
Two representative pedipulation motions were experimentally validated: lateral sliding and scooting.

1. **Lateral Sliding:**
 - **Task:** The robot performed an under-desk object retrieval task, requiring it to step sideways onto a 1 kg planar object and slide it out from beneath a desk.
 - **Mechanism:** This task highlights the role of the hip roll joint actuator. The right hip roll joint motor was observed to swing the stepping wheel laterally, while the other leg maintained balance and compensated for gravity. During the actual retrieval, both actuators generated torque to produce a sustained lateral contact force, effectively sliding the object. The robot successfully retrieved the 1 kg object.

2. **Scooting:**
 - **Task:** The robot, with both wheels positioned on a large 4 kg planar object, was commanded to move the object intermittently forward.
 - **Mechanism:** The robot executed a periodic motion involving acceleration followed by rapid braking. This braking action generated a large forward contact force to initiate object sliding. Concurrently, during the braking phase, the vertical force exerted by the robot was reduced. This reduction in vertical force is intended to decrease the static friction at the object–ground contact interface, thereby facilitating sliding.
 - **Result:** In a demonstrated experiment, the robot performed eight scooting cycles, resulting in a total object displacement of 0.228 meters. The GRF trajectories recorded during the experiment provided insight into this cyclic process, confirming the modulated vertical and horizontal forces during sliding phases.

**C. Object Parameter Sweep in Simulation**
To assess the controller's capability for handling varied object properties, a simulation study was conducted on the scooting mode. The maximum object mass the Tron1 robot could scoot was evaluated under different object-ground friction coefficients, while wheel-ground and wheel-object friction coefficients were fixed at 0.9.
- **Result:** The simulations indicated that the Tron1 robot could initiate sliding motion for objects with masses up to 23 kg when the object-ground friction coefficient was 0.3. This mass is significantly heavier than the robot's own mass (approximately 20 kg), demonstrating the potential of this pedipulation skill for manipulating substantial loads in industrial or logistical applications.

**D. TRBO-based NMPC Experiment in Simulation**
The performance of the TRBO-based NMPC was compared against the TRB-based NMPC (which excludes object dynamics) for a lateral sliding task in simulation, specifically evaluating robustness to object mass mismatch. The robot was initialized in contact with an object and tasked to push it away.
- **Setup:** The planner and NMPC used a fixed nominal object mass of 2 kg, while the actual object mass in the simulation varied from 1 kg to 3 kg across six trials.
- **Result:** The TRBO-based NMPC successfully completed all six trials (100% success rate) with lower object state tracking error, despite the mass mismatch. In contrast, the TRB-based NMPC succeeded in only four out of six trials (66% success rate), indicating a limitation when object dynamics are not explicitly considered in the predictive model, especially under model uncertainty. This result suggests that integrating object dynamics directly into the NMPC enhances both tracking accuracy and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of robotics, particularly in the domain of mobile manipulation with wheeled bipedal robots.

**A. Significance**
- **Novel Pedipulation Capability:** This work provides a demonstration of dynamic non-prehensile pedipulation on a wheeled bipedal robot. This is significant as it shows that wheeled bipedal robots can utilize their legs not just for locomotion but also for interacting with objects through controlled sliding, incorporating stick-slip transitions in the planning.
- **Advanced Reduced-Order Modeling:** The proposed Three Rigid Bodies (TRB) dynamical model is a key innovation. By explicitly capturing the hip roll degree of freedom and admitting multiple wheel-environment contact modes (rolling, slipping, non-contact) without restrictive assumptions, it addresses a gap in modeling for wheeled bipedal robots. This model enables more versatile and dynamic interaction capabilities, including lateral movements essential for many pedipulation tasks.
- **Hierarchical Control for Complex Interactions:** The integrated hierarchical control framework, combining a robot-object motion planner, a Nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (NMPC), and a Whole-Body Controller (WBC), provides a robust approach for coordinating locomotion and dynamic object interaction. This framework is shown to effectively track desired trajectories and regulate contact forces.
- **Demonstrated Complex Tasks:** The hardware validation on the Tron1 robot, including non-trivial tasks such as retrieving an object from under a desk via lateral sliding, showcases the practical applicability and effectiveness of the proposed control architecture for real-world scenarios. The scooting motion further demonstrates a novel method for object displacement.
- **Scalability to Heavy Loads:** Simulation results indicating the ability to scoot objects up to 23 kg (heavier than the robot itself) highlight the potential for these robots to handle substantial loads, expanding their utility beyond lighter manipulation tasks.
- **Robustness to Model Mismatch:** The TRBO-based NMPC extension demonstrates improved object state tracking and enhanced robustness to uncertainties in object parameters, such as mass mismatch, compared to approaches that do not integrate object dynamics into the predictive controller.

**B. Potential Impact**
- **Expanded Application Domains:** The ability to perform dynamic non-prehensile manipulation, especially for sliding objects, could enable wheeled bipedal robots to undertake tasks in environments where traditional manipulators or wheeled platforms struggle. This includes navigating cluttered spaces, retrieving items from tight corners, or manipulating flat, non-graspable objects in industrial, service, or domestic settings.
- **Advancement in Mobile Manipulation:** The research contributes to the broader field of mobile manipulation by demonstrating an effective strategy for underactuated systems to perform complex physical interactions. The principles developed here could inform the design and control of other dynamic legged and wheeled-legged platforms.
- **Industrial and Logistics Applications:** The demonstrated capability for manipulating heavy objects through sliding could be particularly impactful in logistics, warehousing, or manufacturing, where robots could assist in moving heavy or awkwardly shaped items that are currently handled by human labor or specialized machinery.
- **Foundation for Future Autonomy:** While current experiments rely on motion capture for object state estimation and predefined contact sequences, the framework lays a foundation for future autonomous pedipulation. Addressing the identified limitations (onboard object state estimation, richer object models, online contact sequence adaptation) could lead to more self-sufficient and adaptable mobile manipulation robots.
- **Inspiration for Robot Design:** The success of leveraging the hip roll DoF and managing complex contact modes might inspire future wheeled bipedal robot designs to prioritize these capabilities for enhanced interaction versatility.

In conclusion, this work significantly advances the capabilities of wheeled bipedal robots by providing a robust and versatile hierarchical control framework for dynamic non-prehensile pedipulation. Its implications extend to opening new operational paradigms for these robots in various challenging manipulation environments.
