# RoboBrain: A Unified Brain Model for Robotic Manipulation from Abstract to Concrete

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, BAAI, and CAS present RoboBrain, an MLLM-based model designed to bridge the gap between high-level human instructions and concrete robotic actions. Leveraging the new ShareRobot dataset, RoboBrain integrates planning, affordance perception, and trajectory prediction, achieving improved performance on established robotic benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) excel at abstract reasoning but lack the capabilities for complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks.
- Existing MLLMs struggle with precise planning, accurate affordance perception (understanding actionable object properties), and reliable end-effector trajectory prediction.
- There is a significant scarcity of large-scale, fine-grained datasets specifically designed for robotic operation tasks that provide detailed planning sub-goals, object affordance regions, and end-effector trajectories.

## Method

- Introduced **RoboBrain**, a unified MLLM based on LLaVA and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, equipped with specialized A-LoRA and T-LoRA modules for affordance and trajectory prediction.
- ==Developed ShareRobot==, a novel, large-scale, and fine-grained heterogeneous dataset derived from Open X-Embodiment (OXE) with multi-dimensional human annotations for task planning, affordance, and trajectory.
- Implemented a multi-stage training strategy that combines general multimodal pre-training with a progressive integration of robotic data, optimizing for both abstract reasoning and concrete action execution.

## Results

- RoboBrain achieved a BLEU-4 score of **55.05** on RoboVQA, outperforming the second-place model by **18.75** points, demonstrating superior planning capabilities.
- The model attained an Average Precision (AP) of **27.1%** for affordance prediction on AGD20K, surpassing Qwen2-VL-7B by **14.6** AP and LLaVA-NeXT-7B by **17.3** AP.
- Trajectory prediction accuracy was substantially improved, with the final model reducing Discrete Fréchet Distance by **42.9%**, Hausdorff Distance by **94.2%**, and Root Mean Square Error by **31.6%** compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified approach integrating planning, affordance perception, and trajectory prediction within a single MLLM significantly enhances robotic manipulation capabilities.
> - The scarcity of large-scale, fine-grained robotic manipulation datasets is a critical bottleneck, and a meticulously curated dataset with multi-dimensional annotations is essential for training concrete robotic skills.
> - A multi-stage training strategy, progressively building from general multimodal understanding to specialized robotic knowledge, effectively enables MLLMs to transition from abstract reasoning to precise physical action.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have propelled the field of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) forward, demonstrating impressive capabilities in tasks like visual question answering, image captioning, and sentiment analysis. These models leverage vast internet-scale multimodal datasets and self-supervised learning to achieve strong visual perception and human language understanding.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: despite their general prowess, current MLLMs exhibit significant limitations when applied to complex robotic scenarios, particularly long-horizon manipulation tasks. This limitation stems from a fundamental lack of three core "robotic brain" capabilities:
1. **Planning Capability:** The ability to decompose complex, abstract instructions into a sequence of manageable, concrete sub-tasks.
2. **Affordance Perception:** The capacity to recognize and interpret the actionable properties (affordances) of objects in an environment, such as graspable regions or pourable surfaces.
3. **Trajectory Prediction:** The foresight to anticipate and generate the complete manipulation path for an end-effector to successfully execute a task.

Existing studies on MLLMs in robotics have largely focused on high-level reasoning, such as planning and subgoal decomposition (e.g., PaLM-E, RT-H, RoboMamba). While these models can generate planning texts and even some actions, they often lack the fine-grained understanding required for executing complex atomic tasks, particularly concerning object interaction and precise movement. The authors explicitly state that this challenge is primarily due to the "scarcity of large-scale, fine-grained datasets specifically designed for robotic operation tasks."

RoboBrain directly addresses this critical gap. It aims to bridge the divide between abstract instruction comprehension and concrete robotic action expression. By introducing a novel, meticulously curated dataset (ShareRobot) and a specialized multi-stage training strategy, RoboBrain pushes MLLMs beyond high-level reasoning to encompass the practical, low-level skills necessary for effective physical interaction. This work represents a significant step towards enabling robots to perform complex real-world tasks more autonomously and intelligently, contributing to the broader goal of embodied AI and AGI. It differentiates itself by focusing on the complete perception-to-action loop, from abstract task understanding to the precise physical execution of manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind RoboBrain stems from the identified limitations of current MLLMs in robotic manipulation. While MLLMs excel at abstract visual and linguistic comprehension, they fall short when it comes to translating high-level human instructions into concrete, actionable steps for robots, especially in long-horizon tasks. This shortfall is attributed to the absence of three crucial "robotic brain" capabilities: precise planning, accurate affordance perception, and reliable trajectory prediction. A significant underlying cause for this deficiency is the lack of large-scale, fine-grained datasets tailored for robotic operation.

Given this motivation, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Unified Robotic Brain Model:** The overarching goal is to create "RoboBrain," an MLLM-based model capable of transforming abstract human instructions into concrete robotic actions by integrating planning, affordance perception, and trajectory prediction capabilities.
2. **Construct a High-Quality, Multi-Dimensional Dataset:** A core objective is to introduce **ShareRobot**, a novel, large-scale, and fine-grained heterogeneous dataset. This dataset is designed to specifically address the data scarcity problem by meticulously labeling multi-dimensional information relevant to robotic manipulation, including task planning sub-goals, object affordance regions, and end-effector trajectories. The dataset aims for high quality through rigorous human annotation and validation, as well as rich diversity across scenes, embodiments, and atomic tasks.
3. **Implement a Multi-Stage Training Strategy:** To effectively imbue RoboBrain with both general multimodal understanding and specialized robotic manipulation skills, a multi-stage training strategy is a key objective. This strategy involves carefully balancing robotic data with general multimodal data, incorporating long videos and high-resolution images, and progressively enhancing the model's capabilities from foundational understanding to concrete action execution.
4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate that RoboBrain, leveraging ShareRobot and the multi-stage training, can achieve state-of-the-art performance across various established robotic benchmarks (like RoboVQA and OpenEQA) and their newly introduced ShareRobot benchmark, thereby validating the effectiveness of their proposed framework.

In essence, the paper aims to equip MLLMs with the necessary "low-level intelligence" to operationalize their high-level reasoning for real-world robotic manipulation, moving from "abstract to concrete."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of "RoboBrain" centers on two complementary pillars: the creation of a novel, multi-dimensional dataset called ShareRobot, and the development of the RoboBrain model itself, powered by a meticulously designed multi-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. ShareRobot Dataset

**Purpose:** To provide fine-grained, multi-dimensional data for task planning, object affordance perception, and end-effector trajectory prediction, thereby enabling MLLMs to perform concrete robotic actions.

**Data Source & Selection:**
- Built upon the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset, a large compilation of robotic data.
- **Rigorous Filtering:** From OXE, 51,403 instances were carefully selected based on strict criteria:
 - **High-resolution images:** Videos with resolutions below 128 pixels or lacking images were excluded.
 - **Accurate descriptions:** Videos with vague or missing descriptions were filtered out.
 - **Success status:** Only videos of successfully completed tasks were retained.
 - **Long video length:** Excluded videos with fewer than 30 frames to ensure sufficient atomic tasks.
 - **Object not covered:** Eliminated videos where target objects or end-effectors were obscured.
 - **Clear trajectories:** Demonstrations with unclear or incomplete trajectories were removed.

**Data Labeling:**
- **Planning Labeling:**
 - 30 frames from each robotic demonstration were used with high-level descriptions.
 - Gemini (a large multimodal model) was initially used to decompose these into low-level planning instructions.
 - Three human annotators meticulously reviewed and refined these instructions for precision.
 - These instructions were then augmented into 1,027,990 question-answer (QA) pairs by designing 5 different templates for each of the 10 question types found in RoboVQA (e.g., Planning Task, Planning with Context Task, Future Prediction Task, Success, Discriminative/Generative Affordance, Past Description).
- **Affordance Labeling:**
 - 6,522 images were selected.
 - Each image was annotated with bounding boxes (l(x), l(y), r(x), r(y)) representing affordance areas relevant to its high-level description.
 - Manual review ensured precise alignment.
- **Trajectory Labeling:**
 - 6,870 images were selected.
 - Each image was annotated with at least three 2D {x, y} coordinates representing the gripper's trajectory for the low-level instruction.
 - Manual review ensured precise alignment.

**Data Statistics:** ShareRobot is described as the largest open-source dataset of its kind, featuring:
- 1,027,990 QA pairs for planning.
- 6,522 images for affordance, 6,870 for trajectory.
- Derived from 23 original OXE datasets, covering 102 scenes and 12 robot embodiments.
- Includes 107 types of atomic tasks, with "pick," "move," "reach," "lift," and "place" being the most frequent.

#### 3.2. RoboBrain Model

**Architecture:**
- Based on the **LLaVA** framework, a popular MLLM architecture.
- Consists of three main modules:
 - **Vision Encoder (ViT):** SigLIP (siglip-so400m-patch14-384), pre-trained on WebLi.
 - **Projector:** A 2-layer MLP to map visual features to the LLM's semantic space.
 - **Large Language Model (LLM):** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, an open-source LLM.
- **Specialized Modules:**
 - **A-LoRA (Affordance LoRA):** A Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) module for affordance perception, enabling the model to output bounding box coordinates for interaction areas.
 - **T-LoRA (Trajectory LoRA):** Another LoRA module for trajectory prediction, enabling the model to output a series of 2D waypoints for end-effector movement.
- **Functionality Flow:** In application, RoboBrain first generates detailed plans, then breaks them into sub-task descriptions for subsequent affordance perception and trajectory prediction.

**Training Strategy (Multi-Stage):**
The training process is divided into two phases to build capabilities incrementally.

- **Phase 1: General OneVision (OV) Training:** Focuses on developing a strong foundational MLLM with general understanding and instruction-following abilities.
 - **Stage 1 (Alignment and General Knowledge Learning):** Uses LCS-558K image-text data to align the Vision Encoder with the LLM via the Projector.
 - **Stage 1.5:** Trains the entire model on 4M high-quality image-text data to enhance general multimodal knowledge.
 - **Stage 2 (Visual Instruction Tuning):** Further trains the entire model with 3.2M single-image data and 1.6M image/video data from LLaVA-OneVision-Data, improving instruction-following, high-resolution image, and video understanding.

- **Phase 2: Robotic Training:** Builds upon the foundational model to empower core robotic capabilities (abstract to concrete).
 - **Stage 3 (Robotic Knowledge Learning):** Utilizes a 1.3M robotic dataset (RoboVQA-800K, ScanView-318K, and a subset of ShareRobot-200K). This stage focuses on improving manipulation planning capabilities, supporting historical frame memory, and high-resolution image input. To prevent catastrophic forgetting, this robotic data is mixed with 1.7M high-quality general image-text data from Phase 1. The entire model is fine-tuned.
 - **Stage 4 (Robotic Multi-Task Learning):** Enhances affordance perception and manipulation trajectory prediction. This stage uses affordance and trajectory data from ShareRobot and other open-source sources, incorporating A-LoRA and T-LoRA modules during training for efficient adaptation to these concrete manipulation capabilities.

**Implementation Details:**
- All experiments were conducted using the Zero3 distributed training strategy on servers equipped with 8xA800 GPUs, highlighting significant computational investment.
- Specific training parameters (image resolution, batch size, epochs, learning rates, optimizers, etc.) are meticulously detailed for each stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted demonstrate that RoboBrain achieves state-of-the-art performance across various robotic tasks and shows strong generalization capabilities on general multimodal benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Robotic Brain Tasks

**Planning Task Evaluation:**
RoboBrain was evaluated against six powerful MLLM baselines (both open and closed-source, including GPT-4V, Claude3, LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-OneVision-7b, Qwen2-VL-7b, and RoboMamba) on three robotic benchmarks: RoboVQA, OpenEQA, and the ShareRobot test set.
- **RoboVQA:** RoboBrain significantly outperformed all baselines, achieving a BLEU-4 score of 55.05, which is 18.75 points higher than the second-place model (RoboMamba at 36.3). This highlights its superior capability in decomposing complex long-range task planning.
- **OpenEQA & ShareRobot:** RoboBrain surpassed all baseline models on both OpenEQA and the ShareRobot benchmark (evaluated using GPT-4o for scoring). This strong performance is attributed to RoboBrain's robust understanding of robotic tasks and its ability to perceive information from long videos, especially its strong planning capabilities across diverse scenes and embodiments.

**Affordance Prediction Evaluation:**
- RoboBrain's affordance prediction capability was tested against Qwen2-VL-7B and LLaVA-NeXT-7B on the AGD20K affordance test set using Average Precision (AP).
- RoboBrain achieved an AP of 27.1%, significantly outperforming Qwen2-VL-7B (12.5% AP) by 14.6 AP and LLaVA-NeXT-7B (9.8% AP) by 17.3 AP. This validates RoboBrain's ability to understand the physical properties of objects and accurately provide affordance information.

**Trajectory Prediction Evaluation:**
- The model's trajectory prediction accuracy was assessed using Discrete Fréchet Distance (DFD), Hausdorff Distance (HD), and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE).
- An ablation study showed the progressive improvements from design choices:
 - Adding 2D start coordinates ("+ Start Points") reduced DFD, HD, and RMSE.
 - Limiting waypoints to 10 ("+ Max Points") had some minor variations.
 - The most effective model, incorporating end-effector positions and special tokens ("+ Spec Token & End Points"), achieved substantial reductions compared to the baseline: DFD decreased by 42.9%, HD by 94.2%, and RMSE by 31.6%.
- The results indicate that adding start points significantly corrects the translational offset, leading to more accurate trajectory predictions.

#### 4.2. Supplementary Experiments and Analysis

- **General Benchmarks:** RoboBrain showed competitive or superior performance on general benchmarks, including structured OCR tasks (AI2D, ChartQA, DocVQA, TextVQA, OCRBench), visual perception, multi-domain reasoning (MMMU, MMStar, MMBench, MME), and real-world understanding (RealWorldQA). This indicates that its robotic specialization does not significantly compromise its general multimodal capabilities.
- **Effectiveness of ShareRobot:** Experiments confirmed that incorporating the ShareRobot dataset significantly enhances RoboBrain's planning capabilities compared to training without it, underscoring the dataset's critical role.
- **Effectiveness of Robotic Data Proportion:** An optimal ratio of 4:6 (robotic data to general data) was identified for training, providing a balanced performance across both robotic and general benchmarks.
- **Ablation Studies of Different Stages:** The multi-stage training strategy was validated, showing that stages 1-3 consistently improve planning performance, while stage 4 specifically enhances affordance and trajectory prediction.

#### 4.3. Visualization

Qualitative results (Fig. 6, Fig. 8, Fig. 9, Fig. 10) visually demonstrate RoboBrain's ability to:
- Interpret human instructions and visual images.
- Engage in multi-turn interactions for planning future steps.
- Assess actions based on real-time image feedback.
- Predict precise trajectories and identify corresponding affordances.
- Examples show successful decomposition of complex tasks into logical sub-steps and accurate prediction of interaction areas and paths. While most cases are successful, a few failure cases (e.g., misidentified objects, spatial awareness issues, neglect of physical constraints) highlight areas for future improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "RoboBrain" research represents a significant leap forward in bridging the gap between high-level AI reasoning and practical robotic manipulation, with profound implications for the future of embodied AI and Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

**Key Significance:**

- **Addresses a Critical Bottleneck in Robotic AI:** The paper directly tackles the long-standing challenge of enabling MLLMs to move beyond abstract comprehension to concrete, actionable outputs for robots. By providing capabilities for planning, affordance perception, and trajectory prediction, RoboBrain equips robots with a more comprehensive "brain" to execute complex, long-horizon tasks that were previously difficult for MLLMs.
- **Introduces a Foundational Dataset (ShareRobot):** ShareRobot is a major open-source contribution to the robotics community. Its large scale, fine-grained multi-dimensional annotations (task planning, object affordance, end-effector trajectory), and rich diversity across embodiments and scenes address a critical data scarcity issue. This dataset will undoubtedly serve as a crucial resource for future research and development in robot learning and embodied AI, accelerating progress in the field.
- **Achieves State-of-the-Art Performance:** RoboBrain's superior performance across multiple robotic benchmarks (RoboVQA, OpenEQA, and ShareRobot's own evaluation set) convincingly demonstrates the effectiveness of their proposed model architecture and multi-stage training strategy. This SOTA result pushes the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve in practical robotic control.
- **Advancement Towards Embodied AGI:** By empowering MLLMs with the ability to understand, plan, and execute actions in a physical environment, RoboBrain moves AI closer to true embodied intelligence. This is a crucial step towards AGI, where AI systems can not only reason abstractly but also interact skillfully and purposefully with the real world.
- **Pioneering a Holistic Approach:** The integration of planning, affordance, and trajectory prediction within a single unified model, trained through a carefully designed multi-stage process, signifies a holistic approach to robotic intelligence. This allows for a seamless transition from high-level human commands to precise low-level robot movements.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Robots equipped with RoboBrain's capabilities could perform more complex and varied tasks in unstructured environments, reducing the need for human intervention and programming. This could revolutionize industries ranging from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and domestic service.
- **Faster Robot Skill Acquisition:** The structured and fine-grained nature of the ShareRobot dataset, combined with the multi-stage training approach, provides a blueprint for efficiently teaching robots new skills and adapting them to novel scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays robust groundwork for several critical future research directions, including:
 - **Improved Spatial Understanding:** Addressing current limitations in spatial awareness.
 - **Advanced Embodied Reasoning:** Further enhancing decision-making in complex, dynamic environments.
 - **Tool Utilization:** Enabling robots to effectively use a wider range of tools.
 - **Long-Text Comprehension:** Improving understanding of more elaborate instructions.
 - **Model Efficiency and Safety:** Crucial considerations for real-world deployment of highly capable robotic systems.

In conclusion, RoboBrain and its accompanying ShareRobot dataset represent a significant contribution to the field of AI and robotics, offering a comprehensive framework for developing intelligent robotic systems capable of understanding and executing complex manipulation tasks from abstract commands to concrete actions.
