# This&That: Language-Gesture Controlled Video Generation for Robot Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents "This&That", a framework that enables robots to understand and execute tasks through a combination of natural language and 2D pointing gestures. It introduces a method for sparsely-conditioned video generation and a robust behavior cloning policy, resulting in superior video plan quality and more accurate robot task execution in ambiguous environments.

## Problem

- Traditional language-only commands and single image goals for robots often suffer from ambiguity, especially in complex or uncertain environments.
- Existing video generative models require dense spatial or temporal conditioning, making them impractical for intuitive human instruction.
- Translating high-level visual plans into low-level robot actions often relies on complex or less robust methods like inverse dynamics or optical flow.

## Method

- A language-gesture conditioned video diffusion model generates robot action plans as videos, fine-tuning Stable Video Diffusion on robotics datasets and incorporating novel sparse 2D gesture conditioning.
- A self-supervised pipeline is used to annotate existing robotics video datasets with gesture information (gripper and object keypoints) for training the video generation model.
- A Transformer-based Diffusion Video to Action (DiVA) module translates the generated video plans into robot actions, using TokenLearner for image embedding compression and temporal noise for training robustness.

## Results

- "This&That" achieves superior quantitative and qualitative video generation, with FID (17.28) and FVD (84.58) substantially lower than state-of-the-art baselines like AVDC (163.93 and 1512.25 respectively) on robotics datasets.
- A human user study showed that language-gesture conditioning yielded 91.7% user alignment success, significantly outperforming language-only baselines (e.g., AVDC at 12.5%) in resolving ambiguity.
- In simulation, the framework achieved 95% pick and 93% place success on in-distribution scenes, and 87% pick and 80% place success on challenging out-of-distribution scenes, outperforming direct behavior cloning and language-only video planners.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining deictic language with sparse 2D gestures provides an unambiguous and intuitive way for humans to instruct robots, particularly in scenes with identical or visually similar objects.
> - Controllable video diffusion models can be effectively adapted using sparse gesture conditioning and fine-tuned on robotics datasets to generate high-fidelity, user-aligned visual plans.
> - A Transformer-based behavior cloning architecture (DiVA) can robustly translate generated video plans into executable robot actions, even with temporal noise during training, demonstrating a low domain gap between real and generated videos for policy learning.
