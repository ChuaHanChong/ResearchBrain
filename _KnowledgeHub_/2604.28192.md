# LaST-R1: Reinforcing Action via Adaptive Physical Latent Reasoning for VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LaST-R1 introduces a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that integrates continuous latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning with an online reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm, Latent-to-Action Policy Optimization (LAPO). This framework achieved a 99.8% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and improved real-world task success by 44% by enabling VLA models to adaptively reason about physical dynamics before acting.

## Problem

- VLA models using latent reasoning are typically confined to static imitation learning, limiting their adaptability and generalization in interactive environments.
- Current reinforcement learning methods for VLA models often optimize directly in the action space, neglecting the critical role of intermediate physical reasoning.
- Fixed-length reasoning horizons in VLA models lead to inefficiencies, either through excessive computation for simple tasks or insufficient depth for complex ones, hindering overall performance and generalization.

## Method

- **LaST-R1** incorporates ==continuous latent Chain-of-Thought== reasoning using ==DINOv3-based== visual embeddings, into the VLA's autoregressive generation before action execution.
- ==Latent-to-Action Policy Optimization (LAPO)==, a novel RL algorithm, jointly optimizes continuous latent reasoning tokens and action generation, treating latents as implicit decision variables.
- An ==adaptive latent CoT== mechanism dynamically adjusts the reasoning horizon by learning to emit a stop token, balancing efficiency with planning depth.

## Results

- **LaST-R1** achieved **99.8% average success** across LIBERO with only one-shot SFT warm-up.
- Real-world: **+44%** average success over warm-up policy across four complex tasks.
- Robust generalization: only **8%** average decrease in real-world unseen conditions (objects, backgrounds, lighting).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly optimizing latent reasoning and action generation through reinforcement learning significantly enhances a VLA model's understanding and modeling of physical dynamics, leading to more robust policies.
> - DINOv3-based latent representations effectively capture comprehensive visual information, providing a compact and powerful means for the VLA model to reason about future states.
> - An adaptive reasoning horizon allows the policy to allocate cognitive resources efficiently, utilizing shorter reasoning paths for simple tasks and deeper reasoning for more complex scenarios, optimizing the inference-planning trade-off.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a significant area of research in robotics, aiming to enable general robotic manipulation through reasoning over complex visual scenes and executing adaptive actions in dynamic environments. This field has progressed from direct observation-to-action mapping towards incorporating reasoning mechanisms, often inspired by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting in large language models.

Initially, VLA models utilized explicit linguistic traces or future state generation for structured guidance, but these approaches often introduced inference latency and discretization bottlenecks, limiting their ability to capture continuous physical dynamics. Consequently, research has shifted towards reasoning in compact latent spaces to accommodate fine-grained, non-verbalizable physical knowledge, establishing an expressive foundation for complex manipulation.

However, existing latent reasoning frameworks have largely been confined to static imitation learning. Such reliance on pre-recorded expert demonstrations restricts their adaptability, generalization, and ability to perform closed-loop environmental interaction, which can lead to compounding errors. Concurrently, online reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a method for VLA post-training, offering improvements in exploration and robustness through trial-and-error interaction with the environment. A notable limitation of current RL-based VLA methods is their primary focus on optimizing actions directly in the action space, often bypassing or not explicitly optimizing the underlying physical reasoning processes. This oversight means that interaction-driven learning is not fully leveraged to refine the model’s understanding of physical dynamics and adapt to complex, interactive environments.

The presented work, LaST-R1, addresses this gap by introducing an RL post-training framework specifically designed to optimize policies that perform "latent reasoning-before-acting." It seeks to bridge the explicit optimization of actions with the implicit optimization of latent reasoning processes, thereby integrating cognitive deliberation with environmental interaction to enhance robustness and generalization in robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. Develop a reinforcement learning post-training framework capable of effectively leveraging and optimizing "latent reasoning-before-acting" policies in VLA models.
2. Propose a novel RL algorithm, Latent-to-Action Policy Optimization (LAPO), that jointly optimizes both the latent reasoning process and the subsequent action generation.
3. Introduce an adaptive latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanism that allows the policy to dynamically adjust its reasoning horizon based on varying environmental states and task complexities.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several limitations observed in the current state of robotic manipulation research:
- **Limitations of Static Imitation Learning:** While latent reasoning VLA models have shown promise in capturing fine-grained physical dynamics, their reliance on static, high-cost expert demonstrations in imitation learning frameworks restricts their adaptability and generalization. They cannot engage in closed-loop environmental interaction, which can lead to compounding errors when faced with novel or dynamic situations.
- **Deficiencies in Current RL Post-training Approaches:** Existing online reinforcement learning methods for VLA post-training primarily focus on optimizing actions directly. These "action-only" policies often bypass or do not explicitly consider the underlying physical reasoning process. Consequently, they may lack the ability to learn and accurately model physical dynamics through interaction, which is crucial for adapting to complex and uncertain environments.
- **Need for Explicit Physical World Modeling:** The authors contend that accurately modeling the physical world is fundamental for robust robotic execution. By embedding Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning directly within the RL optimization loop, the aim is to stimulate profound physical world modeling, which can lead to more robust execution capabilities.
- **Balancing Cognitive Capacity and Computational Efficiency:** Robotic tasks vary significantly in their complexity, requiring different levels of cognitive processing. A fixed reasoning horizon can be computationally inefficient for simple tasks and insufficient for intricate manipulations. An adaptive mechanism is motivated by the need to balance reasoning capacity with computational efficiency, allowing the policy to allocate appropriate reasoning steps based on task demands.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to enhance VLA models' capabilities to reason, adapt, and generalize across diverse and dynamic robotic manipulation scenarios, moving beyond the constraints of static datasets and action-centric RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LaST-R1 framework integrates a VLA model with a novel reinforcement learning algorithm and an adaptive reasoning mechanism.

#### 3.1. LaST-R1 Model Architecture

The LaST-R1 VLA model is built upon the Qwen3-VL-4B architecture, incorporating a SigLIP2-Large visual encoder and an LLM backbone.
- **Input Processing:** Given an input image and language instruction, the SigLIP2-Large encoder processes spatial information into visual embeddings. These are then concatenated with language embeddings and fed into the LLM backbone.
- **Latent Reasoning and Action Generation:** The model autoregressively generates a sequence of latent reasoning embeddings, which are designed to capture structured physical dynamics. These latents then condition the parallel decoding of action tokens. Continuous actions are normalized and discretized into tokens, extending the LLM's vocabulary. A parameter-free action tokenizer maps between continuous actions and discrete tokens, with parallel decoding enhancing inference efficiency.
- **Value Head:** A 4-layer MLP is integrated as a value head, sharing the VLA backbone, to estimate state values for RL.
- **Latent Representation:** To form compact and expressive latent representations of future dynamics, the model uses DINOv3, a vision foundation model. Specifically, the `<CLS>` token from DINOv3 is extracted as a holistic image embedding. Top-k (k=2560) selection based on feature magnitude along the channel dimension is applied to align with the VLA embedding size while retaining salient visual components. These latent targets are precomputed offline, decoupling the foundation model from the policy to avoid additional overhead during training and inference.

#### 3.2. Latent-to-Action Policy Optimization (LAPO)

LAPO is a reinforcement learning algorithm designed to jointly optimize the latent reasoning process and action generation.
- **Problem Formulation:** The policy $\pi_\theta$ maps multimodal observations (visual inputs, language instructions) to an action chunk $a_{t:t+H}$ in SE(3) space. Actions are 7-DoF for single-arm and 14-DoF for dual-arm robots. The objective is to maximize the expected discounted return, similar to standard RL, but explicitly considering the latent reasoning.
- **Rollout Collection:**
 - At each step, the policy generates a sequence of latent embeddings $Z_{old_t} = \{z_{old_{t,i}}\}^{N_z}_{i=1}$ autoregressively.
 - Conditioned on these latents, a sequence of discrete action tokens $C_t = \{a_{t,j}\}^{N_a}_{j=1}$ is produced in parallel.
 - A special `<latent_end>` token is inserted between latent and action sequences, its final hidden embedding used by the value head for state value $v_t$ estimation.
 - The joint log-probability of the action sequence is computed by summing individual token log-probabilities.
 - Sampled latents, actions, their log-probabilities, and state values are stored.
 - A sparse binary reward is assigned based on task success/failure. Advantage estimates $\hat{A}_t$ are computed using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).
- **Policy Optimization:**
 - The model reprocesses the rollout context to yield updated continuous latent embeddings $Z_{\theta_t} = \{z_{\theta_{t,i}}\}^{N_z}_{i=1}$ and updated discrete action log-probabilities.
 - A step-level ratio $r_t(\theta)$ is defined for both the action sequence and continuous latents.
 - For discrete actions, $r_{a_t}(\theta) = \exp(\log \pi_\theta(C_t | \cdot) - \log \pi_{old_\theta}(C_t | \cdot))$.
 - For continuous latents, the distribution of rollout latents $Z_{old_t}$ is approximated by an isotropic Gaussian centered at the current policy output $Z_{\theta_t}$. The latent ratio surrogate is defined as:
 $r_{z_t}(\theta) = \exp \left( -\frac{1}{2\sigma^2} \sum^{N_z}_{i=1} \|z_{old_{t,i}} - z_{\theta_{t,i}}\|^2 \right)$, where $\sigma$ is a hyperparameter for latent variance.
 - The joint LAPO clipped surrogate loss $L_{policy}(\theta)$ is formulated as an expectation over decision steps, minimizing $\min \left( r^k_t(\theta) \hat{A}_t, \text{clip}(r^k_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon_{min}, 1+\epsilon_{max}) \hat{A}_t \right)$ for both latent and action components. This allows environmental rewards to directly shape the latent reasoning space, pulling "good-reasoning" manifolds closer.
 - The total training loss $L_{total}(\theta)$ unifies action loss $L_{action}(\theta)$, latent loss $L_{latent}(\theta)$, and state-value loss $L_{value}(\theta)$ with weighting coefficients $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Latent CoT Reasoning

To dynamically adjust the reasoning horizon:
- **Dynamic Latent Generation:** The `<latent_end>` token is shifted from a deterministic terminator to a dynamically emitted transition signal. If the policy predicts this token with high confidence (p ≥ 0.99), latent generation terminates, and action prediction begins. Emission of this token is restricted to M candidate positions within a maximum reasoning length $N_{max}$.
- **Exploration via Length Sampling:** During RL rollouts, reasoning length is treated as a stochastic decision. Pre-softmax logits for the `<latent_end>` token at M candidate positions are used to form a categorical distribution (with temperature $\beta$) from which the reasoning length index is sampled. This promotes exploration.
- **Adaptive Length Optimization:** An additional policy loss term $L_{end}(\theta)$ is introduced for the `<latent_end>` token, weighted by $\lambda_3$. This term optimizes reasoning efficiency by rewarding shorter reasoning paths for predictable states and penalizing insufficient reasoning for complex scenarios, guided by $\hat{A}_t$. The total training objective now includes $L_{end}(\theta)$.

#### 3.4. Training Pipeline

LaST-R1 VLA is initialized using a pre-trained Qwen3-VL-4B model and undergoes large-scale pre-training across diverse robotic manipulation datasets (400K trajectories, 28M frames from Open-X-Embodiment, DROID, RoboMIND). For downstream tasks, the policy is adapted through a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) warm-up (e.g., one-shot for LIBERO, 30 trajectories for real-world) before engaging in online RL post-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the performance and generalization capabilities of LaST-R1 across simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiment (LIBERO Benchmark)

- **Overall Performance:** LaST-R1 achieved an average success rate of 99.9% on the LIBERO benchmark, establishing a competitive performance against prior state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods. It ranked first across all four task suites: LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Long.
- **Data Efficiency:** With only one-shot supervised warm-up, LaST-R1 surpassed SFT baselines that utilized full expert datasets (e.g., $\pi_{0.5}$ at 96.9%, OpenVLA-OFT at 97.1%).
- **Comparison to RL Baselines:** Compared to the leading RL-based method $\pi_{RL}$ (98.3%), LaST-R1 maintained an advantage, particularly on the LIBERO-Long suite (99.8% vs. 94.0%), indicating improved capability in complex and long-horizon tasks.
- **Convergence Speed:** LaST-R1, optimized with LAPO, exhibited faster convergence speed and higher success rates compared to an Action-Only baseline optimized with standard PPO. The Action-Only PPO baseline struggled with efficiency, especially in LIBERO-Long, converging to suboptimal performance. This suggests that explicit latent CoT optimization during post-training acts as a cognitive buffer, smoothing the RL optimization landscape and improving sample efficiency.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Latent Reasoning:** One-shot SFT with latent reasoning (LaST-R1 Warm-up) achieved 63.9% average success rate on LIBERO, outperforming the Action-Only baseline (51.0%). This improvement was more pronounced in LIBERO-Long (49.6% vs. 26.2%). After RL post-training, LaST-R1 + LAPO achieved 99.9% SR, significantly outperforming Action-Only + PPO (95.2%), supporting that latent reasoning enhances physical understanding for action optimization.
- **Design Choices for Latent Representation:** The DINOv3-based latent representation achieved the highest success rate (99.8%) on LIBERO-Spatial. This outperformed other methods such as Convolution (98.4%), Q-Former (97.2%), and Global Pooling (96.8%), validating the effectiveness of using pre-trained global representations from DINOv3 with top-k selection for preserving spatial and semantic structures.
- **Impact of Latent Reasoning Length:** Performance increased monotonically with fixed latent sequence length from 1 token (96.2% SR) to 8 tokens (98.4% SR) on LIBERO-Spatial, compared to the Action-Only baseline (95.0% SR). This indicates that a longer cognitive horizon provides richer conditional information. A maximum of 8 tokens was chosen for balance between accuracy and inference speed, as gains beyond 4 tokens were marginal.
- **Adaptive CoT Length and <latent_end> Emission Strategy:** An adaptive strategy with M=4 candidate positions for `<latent_end>` emission achieved the best performance (99.8%) on LIBERO-Spatial. This generally improved efficiency and performance over fixed-length reasoning (98.4%). However, setting M too large (M=8, 99.0%) slightly degraded performance, suggesting a moderately constrained adaptive strategy offers the best trade-off.
- **Episode Lengths and Adaptive Reasoning Lengths:** The RL-optimized policy significantly reduced average execution steps compared to SFT warm-up and, in most LIBERO suites, even surpassed the temporal efficiency of expert demonstrations. The adaptive reasoning mechanism, post-LAPO optimization, learned to gravitate towards shorter cognitive horizons (2 or 4 tokens), indicating efficient termination of deliberation once sufficient state representations are formed.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiment

- **Performance on Complex Tasks:** LaST-R1 achieved a 93.75% average success rate (±1.25% std) across four complex real-world tasks (Insert hexagon block, Open bag zipper, Wipe vase with sponge, Open bottle cap), including single-arm and dual-arm settings. This demonstrated up to a 22.5% average improvement over the SOTA $\pi_{0.5}$ SFT baseline (71.25% average SR $\pm$ 4.5% std), which was trained on significantly more data (100 expert trajectories vs. LaST-R1's 30 trajectories + RL).
- **Dual-Arm Coordination:** The method improved dual-arm coordination for contact-rich tasks like "Open bag zipper" and "Wipe vase with sponge." Performance in "Open bottle cap" increased from 45% to 95%, indicating strengthened modeling of temporally evolving physical interactions.

#### 4.4. Generalization Analysis

- **Simulation OOD Generalization:** LaST-R1 + LAPO exhibited continuous and significant out-of-distribution (OOD) improvements across all LIBERO suites, in contrast to the Action-Only + PPO baseline, which showed stagnation or degradation in OOD performance, indicative of overfitting. This suggests that LaST-R1 learns transferable spatial and semantic concepts.
- **Real-World Generalization:** In real-world deployments, LaST-R1 maintained resilient execution under severe unseen perturbations (unseen objects, background variations, lighting conditions). Performance drops were confined to within 15% for novel objects and were near-zero for background and lighting changes, while the full-size SFT baseline $\pi_{0.5}$ experienced catastrophic degradation (up to 45% drop). This indicates that optimizing latent reasoning through environmental feedback fortifies the policy against environmental uncertainties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LaST-R1 framework makes several notable contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, primarily by integrating and optimizing latent reasoning within a reinforcement learning paradigm.

**Significance:**
- **Enhanced Physical World Modeling:** By explicitly embedding Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in a compact latent space and jointly optimizing it with action generation via LAPO, the framework facilitates a deeper understanding and modeling of physical dynamics. This moves beyond merely mapping observations to actions, leading to more robust and accurate execution in interactive environments.
- **Bridging Imitation Learning and Reinforcement Learning:** The work demonstrates a method to effectively post-train VLA models, initially warmed up with limited expert demonstrations, using online reinforcement learning. This approach addresses the limitations of static imitation learning by enabling closed-loop interaction and continuous refinement, while also overcoming the sample inefficiency often associated with end-to-end RL from scratch.
- **Joint Optimization of Reasoning and Action:** LAPO introduces a mechanism where environmental reward signals directly influence and shape both the internal latent reasoning process and the external action generation. This unified optimization framework allows the model to learn not just *what* actions to take, but *why* they are effective by reinforcing beneficial reasoning patterns.
- **Adaptive Reasoning for Efficiency and Performance:** The adaptive latent CoT mechanism allows the policy to dynamically adjust its reasoning horizon based on task complexity and environmental states. This adaptability balances cognitive capacity with computational efficiency, enabling faster execution for simple tasks while allocating more deliberation for intricate manipulations.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The experimental results in both simulation and real-world scenarios indicate that LaST-R1 exhibits strong generalization capabilities to out-of-distribution conditions (unseen objects, backgrounds, lighting) and robustness against environmental uncertainties, which is a critical challenge in real-world robotics.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement of Generalist Robotic Systems:** LaST-R1 contributes to the development of more capable and general-purpose robotic systems that can operate effectively in unstructured and dynamic environments. The ability to reason adaptively and learn from interaction is crucial for tasks requiring high dexterity, precision, and adaptability.
- **Reduced Data Requirements for Real-World Deployment:** The framework's ability to achieve high performance with few-shot warm-up data followed by online RL can significantly reduce the cost and effort associated with collecting large-scale expert demonstrations for real-world robotic tasks.
- **Safer and More Reliable Robot Autonomy:** By enabling robots to better understand and model physical interactions, the approach could lead to safer human-robot collaboration and more reliable autonomous operation, as robots become more adept at predicting and responding to environmental changes.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a foundation for scaling latent reasoning-driven policies in complex robotic manipulation, potentially inspiring further research into more sophisticated latent representations, advanced adaptive reasoning strategies, and novel RL algorithms that more tightly integrate cognitive processes with motor control in diverse robotic embodiments, including multi-fingered dexterous hands and humanoid robots.
- **Applications:** The capabilities demonstrated could be applied to various domains requiring advanced manipulation, such as smart manufacturing, elder care, logistics, and domestic assistance, where robots need to interact with diverse objects and dynamic environments.
