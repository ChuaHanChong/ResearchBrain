# Self-Improvement in Multimodal Large Language Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UT Dallas, University of Toronto, University of Notre Dame, and MBZUAI present the first comprehensive survey on self-improvement in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), systematizing current methodologies for data collection, organization, and model optimization. The work provides a structured overview of techniques enabling MLLMs to autonomously enhance their capabilities, identifies unique challenges, and outlines future research directions in this area.

## Problem

- High costs and human effort dependency in training and scaling Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
- Performance limitations and efficiency challenges of traditional, static training paradigms for MLLMs.
- Absence of a comprehensive, structured overview of self-improvement methodologies specifically within the MLLM domain.

## Method

- Formal definition of self-improvement for MLLMs and a ==five-level spectrum of autonomy==, distinguishing it from self-refinement.
- A ==three-step iterative framework== comprising ==data collection== (e.g., guided generation, negative sampling), ==data organization== (e.g., rule/model/environment-based verification), and ==model optimization== (e.g., SFT, RL, DPO).
- Comprehensive review of seed models, evaluation benchmarks, applications, and challenges unique to MLLM self-improvement.

## Results

- Delivered the first comprehensive survey structuring the nascent field of self-improvement in Multimodal Large Language Models.
- Systematized methodologies into a clear framework encompassing data collection, organization, and model optimization techniques.
- Identified persistent MLLM limitations, such as fine-grained spatial reasoning and long-horizon temporal grounding, even with self-improvement efforts.
- Delineated unique challenges and future research directions like achieving 'Omni I/O' and enhancing robust, generalizable self-improvement mechanisms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-improvement offers a scalable pathway to reduce human annotation costs and enhance MLLM capabilities, including hallucination reduction.
> - The quality and organization of self-generated data, through verification and arrangement, are crucial for effective model enhancement.
> - Performance improvements from self-improvement are typically method-task specific, with limited universal generalization across diverse MLLM capabilities.
> - Evaluation integrity requires preventing 'Judge/Reward Leakage' by ensuring strict separation between data generation/curation and benchmark evaluation.
