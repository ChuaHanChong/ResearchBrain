# Self-Improvement in Multimodal Large Language Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UT Dallas, University of Toronto, University of Notre Dame, and MBZUAI present the first comprehensive survey on self-improvement in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), systematizing current methodologies for data collection, organization, and model optimization. The work provides a structured overview of techniques enabling MLLMs to autonomously enhance their capabilities, identifies unique challenges, and outlines future research directions in this area.

## Problem

- High costs and human effort dependency in training and scaling Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
- Performance limitations and efficiency challenges of traditional, static training paradigms for MLLMs.
- Absence of a comprehensive, structured overview of self-improvement methodologies specifically within the MLLM domain.

## Method

- Formal definition of ==self-improvement== for MLLMs and a ==five-level spectrum of autonomy==, distinguishing it from ==self-refinement==.
- A ==three-step iterative framework== comprising ==data collection== (e.g., guided generation, negative sampling), ==data organization== (e.g., rule/model/environment-based verification), and ==model optimization== (e.g., ==SFT==, ==RL==, ==DPO==).
- Comprehensive review of ==seed models==, evaluation benchmarks, applications, and challenges unique to MLLM self-improvement.

## Results

- Delivered the first comprehensive survey structuring the nascent field of self-improvement in Multimodal Large Language Models, systematizing methodologies into a framework encompassing data collection, organization, and model optimization techniques.
- Identified persistent MLLM limitations, such as fine-grained spatial reasoning and long-horizon temporal grounding, even with self-improvement efforts.
- Delineated unique challenges and future research directions like achieving 'Omni I/O' and enhancing robust, generalizable self-improvement mechanisms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-improvement offers a scalable pathway to reduce human annotation costs and enhance MLLM capabilities including hallucination reduction, though gains are typically method-task specific with limited universal generalization across diverse capabilities.
> - The quality and organization of self-generated data, through verification and arrangement, are crucial for effective model enhancement.
> - Evaluation integrity requires preventing 'Judge/Reward Leakage' by ensuring strict separation between data generation/curation and benchmark evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has experienced rapid advancements, with self-improvement emerging as a critical paradigm to enhance model capabilities without incurring prohibitively high costs in terms of human effort and computational resources. This approach allows models to generate, refine, and utilize their own training data to iteratively improve.

While self-improvement has gained traction in text-only LLMs, its extension to the multimodal domain, involving diverse data sources like images and videos alongside text, presents unique challenges and immense potential. Existing literature includes surveys on general self-improvement in text-only LLMs (e.g., Tao et al., 2024), broad overviews of MLLMs (e.g., Yin et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024a), and specialized surveys on specific MLLM issues like hallucination (e.g., Bai et al., 2024).

This survey by Deng et al. uniquely positions itself as **the first comprehensive overview specifically dedicated to self-improvement in Multimodal Large Language Models.** It fills a critical gap in the existing research landscape by synthesizing and structuring the fragmented knowledge in this nascent yet rapidly growing area. The authors explicitly highlight the distinct challenges MLLMs face, such as modality alignment problems and the inability of many current models to directly generate non-textual data like images, which differentiate MLLM self-improvement from its text-only counterpart. By providing a structured overview and identifying specific methodologies, evaluations, applications, and future challenges, this survey serves as a foundational reference for researchers aiming to push the boundaries of autonomous MLLM development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the recognition that self-improvement offers a crucial pathway to overcome the costly scaling issues and potential performance ceilings inherent in traditional, static training paradigms for MLLMs. The ability of MLLMs to generate their own training data significantly reduces reliance on expensive human annotation, thereby making model development more efficient and scalable. Recent research, as cited by the authors, already demonstrates the potential of this approach to reduce hallucinations and improve general task performance at a relatively low cost.

The key objectives of this survey are multifaceted:
- **Comprehensive Overview:** To provide a thorough and structured overview of the current literature on self-improvement in MLLMs.
- **Methodological Framework:** To categorize and discuss current methods from three core perspectives: data collection, data organization, and model optimization, thereby offering a clear framework for understanding and comparing different approaches.
- **Facilitate Development:** To identify current approaches, compare their differences, and provide clearer guidance to facilitate further research and development in this promising field.
- **Evaluation and Application Analysis:** To summarize commonly used evaluation metrics and benchmarks, compile performance results where applicable, and highlight practical downstream applications, showcasing the real-world impact of self-improvement.
- **Identify Challenges and Future Directions:** To delineate the open challenges unique to MLLM self-improvement and outline potential future research avenues, thereby attracting more researchers to explore this area and guiding strategic development.
- **Establish a Pathway:** Ultimately, the authors aim to establish a "clearer pathway for developing the next generation of MLLMs with better self-improvement mechanisms, moving beyond random exploration with biases."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and structured approach to survey the landscape of self-improvement in MLLMs.
**Formal Definition:** The survey begins by formally defining "self-improvement" as a process of "model update through training" (m₁ = I(m₀, D)), explicitly distinguishing it from "self-refinement," which involves "response update in context" (r₁ = R(r₀, c)). This crucial distinction sets the scope for the subsequent analysis. They also introduce a five-level spectrum of multimodal self-improvement, ranging from L0 (no self-improvement, human-reliant) to L5 (high self-improvement, fully autonomous generation and curation of both images and text).

**Three-Step Framework:** The core of the methodology revolves around a general self-improvement process illustrated as a dynamic, iterative loop comprising three main modules, as shown in Figure 1 and detailed in Figure 2:
1. **Data Collection (§4):** This module focuses on how MLLMs generate their own training data.
 - **Random Sampling:** A straightforward but potentially inefficient method.
 - **Guided Data Generation:** Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and search-based methods (beam search, MCTS) that steer the model towards desired, high-quality responses.
 - **Negative Samples:** Strategies for creating incorrect or suboptimal examples (e.g., poorly designed prompts, distorted images, attention masking) to enhance the model's discriminative abilities.
2. **Data Organization (§5):** This module addresses the crucial step of verifying and processing the collected data before training.
 - **Verification Methods:**
 - **Rule-Based:** Simple, efficient criteria like majority voting or ground truth alignment (e.g., IoU thresholds).
 - **Model-Based:** Leveraging additional models (peer models) or the MLLM's own self-critic mechanism to assess data quality at various granularities (token, sentence, output).
 - **Environment-Based:** Utilizing feedback from real or simulated environments for verification, particularly for agentic MLLMs.
 - **Dataset Arrangement:** Post-processing steps such as filtering, editing, refining outputs, archiving difficult examples for curriculum learning, or yielding reward/judge models.
 - **Collection-Organization Loop:** Emphasizing the iterative nature where organized data can influence subsequent data collection (data-centric iterative evolution) or where improved models produce and organize better data (model-centric recursive improvement).
3. **Model Optimization (§6):** This module details how the organized data is used to update the MLLM's parameters.
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** A widely adopted method for instruction tuning, minimizing cross-entropy loss between predictions and ground truth.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that aim to maximize rewards, particularly useful for preference alignment and reasoning tasks.
 - **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** An offline, reinforcement-learning-free alternative that leverages both preferred and dispreferred outputs to align models with human preferences.
 - **Other Variants:** Mention of enhanced methods with penalty terms and the benefits of curriculum learning across different optimization stages.

**Seed Models (§3):** The survey dedicates a section to "seed models," highlighting the necessity of certain "capability floors" (e.g., basic visual grounding, robust text-in-the-wild handling) for effective self-improvement. It lists commonly used seed MLLMs such as LLaVA, Qwen-VL, InstructBLIP, MiniGPT4, and Video-LLaVA.

**Evaluation and Applications (§7, Appendix B):** The authors review existing datasets created with MLLMs to facilitate self-improvement (e.g., VLFeedback, DeepPerception, OmniAlign-V-DPO). They categorize popular MLLM benchmarks into general knowledge, reasoning, hallucination, medical, video QA, and judging abilities. A "Meta-Analysis Across Benchmarks" provides synthesis of observed patterns. The survey also discusses various downstream applications where self-improvement is impactful, including Math & Science, Control, Healthcare, Personalization, and 3D/Embodied Intelligence.

**Challenges and Opportunities (Appendix C):** Finally, the methodology includes a forward-looking analysis of unique challenges in MLLM self-improvement, such as multi-modality uniqueness, the need for better seed models and emerging modalities, achieving "Omni I/O," addressing biases and robust verification, enhancing generalization, ensuring scalability, and aiming for higher autonomy in the entire R&D process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey synthesizes a wide array of research into the following key findings and observations regarding self-improvement in MLLMs:

- **Iterative Process:** Self-improvement in MLLMs is generally conceptualized as an iterative loop involving a "seed" MLLM, data collection, data organization (verification and arrangement), and model optimization, ultimately leading to an improved MLLM that can serve as the seed for the next iteration.
- **Evolution of Data Collection:** The field is moving beyond simple random sampling to more sophisticated methods. **Guided data generation** (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, search-based methods) helps produce higher quality and relevant data more efficiently. Crucially, the generation of **negative samples** (e.g., through distorted images, misleading prompts, or attention masking) is vital for training models to distinguish between correct and incorrect responses, significantly reducing issues like hallucination.
- **Importance of Data Organization:** The quality of self-generated data is paramount. **Verification methods** are essential, ranging from efficient rule-based checks to more nuanced model-based evaluations (peer models, self-criticism) and robust environment-based feedback for agentic MLLMs. **Dataset arrangement** techniques (filtering, editing, curriculum scheduling) are critical for curating high-quality training sets. The iterative feedback loop between data collection and organization is found to continuously enhance dataset quality and model performance.
- **Diverse Model Optimization Strategies:** Researchers leverage various optimization techniques:
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)** is effective for instruction tuning with high-quality datasets.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, particularly PPO and GRPO, is employed for preference alignment and reasoning, especially when human demonstration data is scarce.
 - **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** has emerged as a standard for preference learning, capable of utilizing both positive and negative samples, offering an offline alternative to RL. These methods are often combined in multi-stage training curricula.
- **Meta-Analysis of Evaluation Benchmarks Reveals Patterns:**
 - **Method-Task Specificity:** Different self-improvement methods yield distinct benefits. Rule- or verification-based RL shows the largest gains on verifiable tasks (e.g., visual math), while preference/AI-feedback data is most effective at reducing hallucinations (e.g., POPE/AMBER) and improving general helpfulness.
 - **Seed Model Quality Matters:** While relative improvement may diminish with stronger seed models, superior seeds consistently lead to higher absolute end-performance.
 - **Cross-Benchmark Inconsistency:** Performance gains in one area (e.g., compositional reasoning) often do not translate directly, or may even regress, in others (e.g., perception-heavy tasks), highlighting the current lack of truly generalizable self-improvement.
 - **Persistent Bottlenecks:** MLLMs consistently struggle with fine-grained spatial reasoning, multi-image/multi-hop consistency, long-horizon video temporal grounding, diagram/chart understanding, and robustness to noisy OCR. Hallucination remains a challenge in open-world scenes without rigorous visual verification.
 - **Judge/Reward Leakage:** A critical finding emphasizes the need for strict separation between data curation mechanisms and evaluation benchmarks to prevent inflated scores and ensure credible claims.
 - **Efficiency Considerations:** Rule-based verification is generally the most computationally efficient, while environment-based verification, though potentially highest quality, can incur significant costs due to simulation complexity. Prompt-guided generation and negative sampling improve data collection efficiency by reducing rejection rates.
- **Current Limitations of MLLMs:** MLLMs face unique challenges compared to text-only LLMs, including modality alignment issues that exacerbate hallucinations and the inability of most current models to generate all necessary multimodal training data (e.g., images directly).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey represents a significant contribution to the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models by providing the first dedicated and comprehensive overview of self-improvement methodologies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Guidance and Standardization:** By offering a structured taxonomy and clear definitions, the survey helps standardize the terminology and categorization of methods within MLLM self-improvement. This clarity is invaluable for new researchers entering the field and provides a common framework for established experts, moving the field "beyond random exploration with biases."
- **Accelerating Research and Development:** The detailed discussion of data collection, organization, and model optimization techniques, along with comparisons of their benefits and drawbacks, equips researchers with a foundational understanding to design more effective self-improvement pipelines. By consolidating current knowledge, it can accelerate the development of next-generation MLLMs that are more autonomous, efficient, and capable.
- **Addressing Scalability and Cost Challenges:** Self-improvement is inherently motivated by the need to reduce human labor and computational costs associated with large-scale data annotation and model training. The survey highlights how various techniques contribute to this goal, offering practical insights for developing more resource-efficient MLLMs.
- **Identifying Critical Research Frontiers:** The explicit outlining of "Challenges and Opportunities" serves as a roadmap for future research. Areas like developing "Omni I/O" capabilities (MLLMs generating all modalities themselves), ensuring robust, bias-free verification, achieving universal generalization, improving scalability, and striving for higher autonomy in the entire R&D process are critical for advancing AI. This structured identification of gaps will channel research efforts into high-impact areas.
- **Driving Real-World Applications:** The survey illustrates the wide range of downstream applications benefiting from self-improved MLLMs, including advancements in Math & Science reasoning, industrial control systems, healthcare diagnostics, personalized AI experiences, and embodied intelligence in 3D environments. This showcases the practical utility and transformative potential of the research, encouraging further investment and application development.
- **Promoting Robust Evaluation Practices:** The meta-analysis of benchmarks and the cautionary note on "Judge/Reward Leakage" are crucial for fostering more rigorous and credible evaluation practices in the field. This emphasis on robust metrics ensures that reported performance gains are genuinely indicative of model improvement.

In essence, this survey serves as a vital compass for the MLLM research community, synthesizing current progress, illuminating unique challenges, and charting a strategic course for the future of self-improving multimodal AI. Its impact will likely be seen in guiding the design of more autonomous, powerful, and generalizable MLLMs across a diverse array of applications.
