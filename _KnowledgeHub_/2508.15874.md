# Spatial Policy: Guiding Visuomotor Robotic Manipulation with Spatial-Aware Modeling and Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for visuomotor robotic manipulation from Tsinghua University integrates explicit spatial modeling and reasoning into a closed-loop perception-to-control pipeline, enhancing robot ability to generate physically plausible visual plans and execute reliable actions. This approach achieved an 86.7% success rate on the Meta-World benchmark and a 59.6% success rate on iTHOR navigation tasks, demonstrating superior performance and robustness in complex environments.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor manipulation frameworks often generate physically implausible visual plans due to a lack of explicit spatial awareness during video generation.
- Difficulty in handling complex, geometry-sensitive manipulation tasks, such as those with occluded targets, due to limited explicit 3D spatial planning.
- Prior spatial reasoning methods primarily focus on semantic or symbolic understanding, lacking integration with continuous visual feedback and executable action rollouts.

## Method

- Introduces the **Spatial Policy** (SP) framework, a closed-loop visuomotor system integrating structured spatial reasoning into its perception-to-control pipeline.
- Utilizes a "spatial plan table," generated by a ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== from robot and object coordinates, as a global condition to guide spatially grounded future video prediction.
- Employs a flow-based diffusion policy for action prediction, conditioned on multimodal inputs including optical flow and images sampled from the generated video, and implements a dual-stage feedback mechanism for real-time spatial consistency.

## Results

- Achieved an **86.7%** overall success rate across 11 Meta-World single-arm tasks, significantly outperforming the best baseline (AVDC-R with **53.7%**).
- Recorded an average success rate of **59.6%** across 12 iTHOR navigation tasks, exceeding AVDC (**31.3%**) and VideoAgent (**34.2%**) by improving global layout consistency and target localization.
- Demonstrated a **1.84x** speedup over VideoAgent in end-to-end runtime and showed the ability for iterative spatial replanning to correct for scene variations and recover from execution failures, including a **30%** success rate on a real-world pick-doll task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding structured spatial intent via a VLM and a "spatial plan table" effectively guides video generation, resulting in physically plausible and task-aligned visual trajectories.
> - Integrating optical flow as a conditioning signal within a diffusion policy robustly translates spatially consistent visual plans into precise, executable actions.
> - A dual-stage feedback mechanism, combining VLM-based video validation and rule-based execution replanning, is critical for achieving robust, long-horizon control and dynamic error recovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of visuomotor robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on hierarchical embodied models that integrate high-level planning with low-level action execution. Recent advancements in this area have seen the emergence of vision-based methods that utilize visual cues to transfer information across different robotic embodiments and environments. These methods often employ generative models to predict future video trajectories, which are subsequently translated into executable actions by a dedicated module.

Previous research in visuomotor robotic manipulation includes approaches like AVDC and VideoAgent, which generate action-annotated video plans or refine them via self-conditioning. CLOVER introduced a closed-loop framework combining video diffusion planning with feedback control. SEER proposed a Predictive Inverse Dynamics Model for end-to-end vision-to-control learning. SuSIE leveraged image-editing diffusion models for subgoal generation. While these methods have demonstrated capabilities, a common limitation identified by the authors is their lack of explicit spatial awareness during video generation and action prediction. This deficiency can lead to physically implausible predictions, such as robotic arms traversing obstacles, or difficulties in handling complex scenarios like occluded targets and geometry-sensitive manipulation tasks. Their spatial modeling is often limited to depth-based cues or appearance-based edits without explicit 3D spatial planning.

Concurrently, spatial reasoning has been explored in embodied AI from various perspectives. Methods such as SpatialVLM, GraspCorrect, 3D-VLA, SPA, and LLM-Planner utilize pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs), 3D vision alignment, point cloud reconstruction, or large language models to extract spatial cues, determine object relations, encode spatial semantics, or infer symbolic plans. However, the authors note that these methods predominantly address semantic or symbolic spatial reasoning and typically lack integration with visual feedback and continuous video prediction for executable rollouts. Many rely on handcrafted prompts or fixed spatial priors, limiting their adaptability to continuous visual planning. This paper aims to bridge this gap by integrating structured spatial reasoning, derived from environmental geometry, directly into a generative video prediction framework, thereby providing a more cohesive approach to spatial understanding and action-aware planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capabilities of visuomotor robotic manipulation frameworks by addressing their lack of spatial awareness. Existing methods often fail to effectively translate high-level visual plans into low-level executable actions, particularly in environments characterized by complexity and geometric sensitivity. This deficiency results in predictions that can be physically inconsistent and limits the robot's ability to adapt to dynamic or spatially constrained scenarios.

The authors identify three specific challenges that motivate the development of their proposed Spatial Policy (SP) framework:

1. **Generating Spatially Relevant Videos:** The first challenge involves developing a mechanism to generate future video trajectories that explicitly model spatial relationships. This includes ensuring correct object-goal positioning and task alignment within the imagined visual plans, preventing physically implausible movements (e.g., passing through walls) that are observed in current models.
2. **Guiding Action Prediction with Spatial Consistency:** The second challenge focuses on how to effectively utilize the spatially-aware generated videos to guide the prediction of executable actions. The translation from visual plan to action must preserve spatial consistency and maintain task relevance, ensuring that the inferred actions accurately reflect the spatial intent embedded in the visual trajectory.
3. **Enabling Real-time Spatial Reasoning Feedback:** The third challenge addresses the need for real-time spatial reasoning during execution. This involves developing a feedback mechanism that can reason over spatial cues to refine plans and actions dynamically, thereby creating a closed-loop system that continuously bridges visual imagination with actual control, allowing for corrections in response to environmental changes or deviations.

To address these challenges, the authors propose the Spatial Policy (SP) framework, which integrates explicit spatial modeling and reasoning through a structured "spatial plan table." This table is designed to guide video generation, facilitate spatially consistent action prediction, and support feedback-driven refinement during policy execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Spatial Policy (SP) framework is a unified, closed-loop visuomotor robotic manipulation system that integrates structured spatial reasoning into its perception-to-control pipeline. The system comprises three interconnected modules:

#### 3.1. Spatial-Conditioned Embodied Video Generation

This module is responsible for generating spatially grounded visual trajectories. It employs a conditional diffusion framework for future video prediction, taking an initial observation frame and a textual task description as input. To instill spatial awareness, a "spatial plan table" is introduced as an auxiliary global condition.

- **Spatial Plan Table:** To derive precise spatial intent, the coordinates of the robot end-effector ($p_{ee} \in R^3$) and the target object ($p_{obj} \in R^3$) are extracted from the simulator. Their relative offset ($\Delta p = p_{obj} - p_{ee}$) is then fed into a pre-trained visual-language model (GPT-4o). This VLM generates a structured spatial plan consisting of sequential subgoals. Each subgoal encodes an atomic action type (e.g., move, grasp), a direction vector, and a scalar distance. The VLM prompt is structured to enforce specific verbs, integer directions ([-1, 0, 1] for x, y, z axes), and positive float distances in meters.
- **Encoding and Integration:** Each subgoal is independently encoded into a fixed-dimensional latent vector. The action type is mapped to an embedding ($e_{act}$) via a learnable embedding table. The direction vector is discretized and embedded ($e_{dir}$) using another learnable embedding table. The distance scalar is projected ($e_{dis}$) through a learnable MLP. These embeddings are concatenated and processed by an MLP to form a subgoal embedding ($e_{subplan}$). All subgoal embeddings are then concatenated with a task text embedding ($z_{task}$) obtained from CLIP to form a composite global condition vector ($T$). This global condition is injected into all layers of a U-Net architecture through conditional normalization mechanisms, guiding the diffusion model to produce spatially grounded video sequences.
- **Conditional Diffusion Formulation:** The video generation model is a U-Net-based denoising diffusion probabilistic model, adapted from the Imagen architecture, consistent with AVDC. It predicts a sequence of seven future frames ($I_{1:T}$) given an initial frame ($I_0$) and the composite condition vector ($T$). The training objective minimizes an MSE loss between sampled noise and the model's denoising network prediction, approximating the posterior distribution of future frames.

#### 3.2. Flow-Based Action Prediction

This module extends previous action prediction methods by incorporating explicit motion cues through optical flow as an additional conditioning signal.

- **Multimodal Spatial Input Encoding:** The policy is conditioned on four types of information: the current image ($I_{cur}$), a goal image ($I_{goal}$) sampled from the generated visual plan, the 3D spatial coordinate of the robotic arm ($p_{cur}$), and a dense flow field ($F_t$) computed between consecutive frames using the Farnebäck method. Images are encoded using a ResNet-based encoder, spatial coordinates via an MLP, and the flow field via a CNN-based encoder. These modality-specific features are concatenated to form a global condition vector.
- **Diffusion Policy-based Action Prediction Module:** Action prediction is formulated as a conditional generative process using a U-Net-based diffusion policy architecture. This models the conditional distribution over action trajectories ($a_{0:H-1}$), where each action includes a 3-DoF end-effector pose and gripper control. The policy is trained via denoising score matching, minimizing an MSE loss with a global condition vector encompassing all encoded modalities. During inference, the model predicts the entire action trajectory in a denoising pass, which is then executed step-by-step. A frame-switching mechanism, based on a composite similarity score (including contour, shape, intensity statistics, SSIM, and inverse optical flow magnitude) between the current observation and the target frame from the predicted video, manages goal frame progression.

#### 3.3. Spatial Reasoning Feedback Policy

This module ensures spatial consistency during execution through a closed-loop correction mechanism that dynamically refines the spatial plan table based on perceptual feedback. It integrates a dual-stage feedback mechanism:

- **Video Validation (Generation Filtering):** During visual planning, a VLM-based consistency checker (GPT-4o) assesses the spatial plausibility of the generated video. This check verifies object-goal alignment, manipulator trajectories, and overall spatial coherence. Videos that pass this check are utilized; otherwise, regeneration is triggered under the same spatial condition. The VLM prompt for this stage is designed to elicit a strict "Accept" or "Reject" response based on object integrity and task progression.
- **Action Execution Replanning:** During action execution, rule-based policy diagnostics monitor temporal progress and detect failures (e.g., positional drift, stagnation). If a failure is detected, the system computes an updated spatial offset between the end-effector and the target object from the latest observation. This offset is re-encoded into a refined spatial subplan via GPT-4o, which then forms an updated condition to replan both the video and the subsequent action policy. This iterative process ensures real-time alignment between visual imagination and robotic execution for robust long-horizon manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Spatial Policy (SP) was conducted across manipulation and navigation tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness compared to existing baselines.

**Quantitative Performance:**

- **Meta-World Benchmark:** SP achieved an overall success rate of 86.7% across 11 single-arm control tasks, outperforming AVDC (19.6%), AVDC-R (43.1%), VideoAgent (22.3%), and VideoAgent-OR (53.7%). On challenging tasks such as Assembly, Hammer, Faucet Open, Button Press Top, Shelf Place, and Basketball, SP achieved 77.5% success, while the best baseline achieved 29.2%. For easier tasks, SP approached 100% success.
- **iTHOR Environment:** SP demonstrated an average success rate of 59.6% across 12 navigation tasks in four room types, surpassing AVDC (31.3%) and VideoAgent (34.2%). Significant improvements were observed in Kitchen (88.3%) and Bedroom (63.3%) tasks, which require precise reasoning in cluttered environments. Performance in Bathroom tasks was comparable to baselines due to geometric constraints.
- **Computational Efficiency:** On Meta-World, SP exhibited an end-to-end runtime of 27.11 seconds per task (VLM inference: 2.43s, video generation: 9.68s, action execution: 0.75s), representing a 1.84x speedup compared to VideoAgent (50s).
- **Human Evaluation of VLM:** Human assessments indicated 82% accuracy in spatial plan table generation and 67% accuracy in replanning decisions, suggesting interpretability and reliability of the spatial reasoning process.

**Qualitative Observations:**

- **Adaptive Trajectories:** In tasks like Shelf Place, SP generated adaptive and spatially coherent paths that corrected for scene variations, unlike baseline methods that produced inflexible trajectories.
- **Improved Interaction Moments:** For the Basketball task, SP allocated more frames to critical grasping phases, resulting in smoother and more reliable manipulation and reduced instances of missing key interaction moments.
- **Scene Consistency in iTHOR:** In iTHOR tasks like Painting and Mirror, SP maintained global layout consistency across viewpoint shifts, with generated frames remaining coherent after rotation and consistent target localization, addressing drift issues observed in baseline trajectories.
- **Iterative Replanning:** The framework demonstrated the ability for iterative spatial replanning (e.g., Figure 4 in the paper), detecting execution stagnation and regenerating video and action sequences based on refined spatial plans.

**Real-World Experiments:**

- SP was evaluated on a real-world pick-doll task using an SO-100 robotic arm. Despite relying on estimated depth (DepthAnything) and incomplete visual observations, SP achieved a 30% success rate across 20 trials, indicating practical viability.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Video Generation Module:** The "Subplan" variant, which uses structured spatial subplans generated by a VLM, achieved an average success rate of 82.0%, outperforming the "Baseline" (63.4%) and "Distance" (79.4%) variants, demonstrating the value of structured spatial abstraction.
- **Action Prediction Module:** The flow-based diffusion policy in SP achieved an average success rate of 63.4%, substantially surpassing AVDC's direct optical flow-to-action mapping (19.6%), particularly on harder tasks.
- **Spatial Condition Injection Strategy:** "Local Cond" (GLIGEN-style partial injection via cross-attention) yielded an average success rate of 84.3%, slightly better than "Global Cond" (uniform injection across U-Net layers) at 82.0%, suggesting improved spatial precision with localized injection.
- **Replanning Mechanism:** Combining "Generation Replanning" with "Execution Replanning" (GR&ER) achieved the highest average success rate of 86.7%, indicating that dual-stage replanning is critical for robust long-horizon control and recovery from failures.
- **Finetuning with Expert-Assisted Recovery Data:** Finetuning with additional trajectories where expert policies corrected failures improved the average success rate from 82.0% to 85.6%, enhancing robustness.

**Error Type Analysis:**

- Human evaluation revealed that 65% of generated videos were correct, with errors primarily from fluency issues (9.1%), robot inconsistency (11.0%), and object inconsistency (14.5%). For action execution, 85.5% of predictions were correct, with remaining errors due to physics violations (9.1%) and spatial mistakes (5.5%). The low spatial error rate for actions suggested effective spatial planning and policy.

**Robustness to Occlusions:**

- Experiments with random masking of input frames demonstrated that the video generation module could produce visually consistent future frames and enable successful task completion despite significant occlusions, indicating robust representation learning.

**Hyperparameter Analysis:**

- The model exhibited consistent high performance across different diffusion noise seeds, with low variance, affirming that structured spatial subplans effectively stabilize the generation process. Analysis of action prediction hyperparameters showed that a broader goal frame selection range (k=20 timesteps) improved generalization, and regressing the noise ϵ performed better than directly predicting the denoised action $\hat{a}_0$.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Spatial Policy (SP) framework contributes to the field of visuomotor robotic manipulation by directly addressing the limitations of existing methods regarding spatial awareness. By integrating explicit spatial modeling and reasoning into the entire perception-to-control pipeline, SP enhances the robot's ability to generate physically plausible visual plans and execute them reliably in complex environments.

The development of a structured spatial plan table, derived from geometric information and processed by a VLM, represents a significant step toward bridging the gap between high-level conceptual understanding and low-level action control. This approach enables the generation of spatially coherent visual trajectories, which are then effectively translated into consistent actions through a flow-based diffusion policy. The dual-stage replanning mechanism, incorporating both VLM-based video validation and rule-based execution feedback, establishes a robust closed-loop control system capable of adapting to real-time environmental changes and recovering from execution failures.

The demonstrated improvements in task success rates across diverse simulated benchmarks (Meta-World and iTHOR), coupled with promising results in real-world robotic experiments, suggest that SP enhances the practicality and reliability of embodied models for a wide range of robotic control applications. The ability to reason about spatial structures and integrate this reasoning into generative models paves the way for more interpretable, robust, and adaptable robotic systems. This work could particularly impact applications requiring precise manipulation in dynamic or unconstrained settings, where accurate spatial understanding is critical for successful task completion.
