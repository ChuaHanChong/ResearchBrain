# VideoVerse: Does Your T2V Generator Have World Model Capability to Synthesize Videos?

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoVerse introduces a comprehensive benchmark to evaluate Text-to-Video (T2V) models' physical commonsense understanding and temporal causality, revealing significant performance gaps between open-source and closed-source models in simulating real-world physics and event sequences.

## Problem

- Existing Text-to-Video (T2V) benchmarks lack sufficient discriminative power and are becoming saturated, failing to adequately evaluate state-of-the-art models' capabilities.
- Current evaluations often neglect event-level temporal causality and a systematic assessment of world knowledge, such as physical laws, material properties, and common sense.
- Prompts in many benchmarks explicitly describe dynamics, allowing models to succeed by surface-level text matching rather than requiring implicit reasoning or understanding of "hidden semantics."

## Method

- Developed **VideoVerse**, a benchmark with ten evaluation dimensions covering both basic T2V abilities and advanced "==world model==" capabilities, including Natural Constraints, Event Following, and Interaction.
- Constructed prompts using a "hidden semantics" design across daily life, scientific experiments, and science fiction domains, compelling T2V models to infer implicit physical or causal relations not explicitly stated.
- Implemented a QA-based evaluation protocol leveraging the Gemini 2.5 Pro ==Vision-Language Model== (==VLM==), supported by fine-grained sub-questions and validated by high human-alignment, to assess video generation outputs.

## Results

- Closed-source models like **Sora-2** and **Veo-3** achieved the **highest overall scores**, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across most dimensions, particularly for world model capabilities.
- A **substantial performance gap** was identified between open-source and closed-source models in "world model level" dimensions (e.g., Mechanics, Interaction, Material Properties), despite comparable performance in basic T2V aspects.
- The fine-grained sub-question evaluation proved effective in diagnosing partial successes and differentiating models with similar overall scores, providing more granular insights into model strengths and weaknesses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art closed-source T2V models consistently outperform open-source models, especially in "world model level" dimensions requiring deeper understanding of physics and causality.
> - Current T2V models struggle significantly with "hidden semantics," frequently failing to infer and generate implicit physical consequences or logical event progressions.
> - The primary limitation for T2V models in event following is not video length, but rather their inherent ability to understand and generate causally linked events and apply world knowledge.
