# VideoVerse: Does Your T2V Generator Have World Model Capability to Synthesize Videos?

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoVerse introduces a comprehensive benchmark to evaluate Text-to-Video (T2V) models' physical commonsense understanding and temporal causality, revealing significant performance gaps between open-source and closed-source models in simulating real-world physics and event sequences.

## Problem

- Existing Text-to-Video (T2V) benchmarks lack sufficient discriminative power and are becoming saturated, failing to adequately evaluate state-of-the-art models' capabilities.
- Current evaluations often neglect event-level temporal causality and a systematic assessment of world knowledge, such as physical laws, material properties, and common sense.
- Prompts in many benchmarks explicitly describe dynamics, allowing models to succeed by surface-level text matching rather than requiring implicit reasoning or understanding of "hidden semantics."

## Method

- Developed **VideoVerse**, a benchmark with ten evaluation dimensions covering both basic T2V abilities and advanced "==world model==" capabilities, including Natural Constraints, Event Following, and Interaction.
- Constructed prompts using a "hidden semantics" design across daily life, scientific experiments, and science fiction domains, compelling T2V models to infer implicit physical or causal relations not explicitly stated.
- Implemented a QA-based evaluation protocol leveraging the Gemini 2.5 Pro ==Vision-Language Model== (==VLM==), supported by fine-grained sub-questions and validated by high human-alignment, to assess video generation outputs.

## Results

- Closed-source models like **Sora-2** and **Veo-3** achieved the **highest overall scores**, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across most dimensions, particularly for world model capabilities.
- A **substantial performance gap** was identified between open-source and closed-source models in "world model level" dimensions (e.g., Mechanics, Interaction, Material Properties), despite comparable performance in basic T2V aspects.
- The fine-grained sub-question evaluation proved effective in diagnosing partial successes and differentiating models with similar overall scores, providing more granular insights into model strengths and weaknesses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art closed-source T2V models consistently outperform open-source models, especially in "world model level" dimensions requiring deeper understanding of physics and causality.
> - Current T2V models struggle significantly with "hidden semantics," frequently failing to infer and generate implicit physical consequences or logical event progressions.
> - The primary limitation for T2V models in event following is not video length, but rather their inherent ability to understand and generate causally linked events and apply world knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Text-to-Video (T2V) generation has experienced rapid advancements, enabling models to translate natural language into coherent and high-quality videos. Early T2V evaluation benchmarks, such as VBench and EvalCrafter, primarily focused on assessing generated videos at a frame level, emphasizing aesthetic quality and image fidelity. Subsequent benchmarks expanded their scope to include semantic alignment, determining whether video content matches the given prompt. More recent efforts, like VBench2 and Video-Bench, employed Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for question-answering-based evaluations, aiming to assess more complex semantic alignment. StoryEval and NeuS-V introduced event-centric evaluation, verifying the occurrence of described events.

However, the continuous evolution of T2V technologies, particularly the development of state-of-the-art models, has begun to reveal limitations in these existing benchmarks. Modern T2V models exhibit increasingly sophisticated capabilities, including strong instruction-following, understanding of world knowledge, and the ability to infer temporal and causal relations among events, often producing high-quality, cinematic videos. This emerging "world model" capability, which involves generating physically plausible events and dynamics beyond explicit textual descriptions, poses new challenges for evaluation. Existing benchmarks are becoming saturated, losing their discriminative power as advanced models achieve near-indistinguishable scores despite clear differences in underlying capabilities. Furthermore, they often rely on fully specified prompts, which do not test a model's ability to infer implicit consequences or integrate real-world knowledge. Crucially, event-level temporal causality, a fundamental property of videos, and a systematic assessment of various aspects of world knowledge remain largely underexplored in current evaluation frameworks.

VideoVerse is introduced to address these gaps by providing a comprehensive benchmark specifically designed to evaluate whether T2V models possess "world model capability." It shifts the focus from surface-level visual correctness and explicit semantic alignment to assessing a model's understanding of complex temporal causality, physical laws, material properties, and common sense, particularly through prompts designed with "hidden semantics." This work positions itself as a necessary progression in T2V evaluation, aiming to rigorously benchmark the rapidly advancing capabilities of T2V generators and guide future research toward more intelligent and robust video synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and introduce **VideoVerse**, a comprehensive benchmark designed to assess whether current Text-to-Video (T2V) models possess "world model capability" by understanding complex temporal causality and world knowledge to synthesize videos.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Saturation of Existing Benchmarks:** Current T2V evaluation metrics, which primarily focus on per-frame aesthetic quality, temporal consistency, and basic semantic alignment, have become insufficient to differentiate between state-of-the-art T2V models. As models improve, they achieve increasingly similar scores on traditional benchmarks, masking actual differences in their underlying capabilities. This saturation limits the usefulness of existing benchmarks for tracking progress and guiding development in a rapidly evolving field.

2. **Lack of Event-Level Temporal Causality Assessment:** Videos are fundamentally defined by sequences of events with inherent temporal and causal relationships. However, prior benchmarks have largely overlooked the systematic evaluation of event-level temporal causality. They may verify if described events occur but often neglect the logical and causal progression between them. A model with "world model capability" should be able to generate a coherent sequence of causally linked events, which is a critical, yet underexplored, property in current evaluation.

3. **Insufficient Assessment of World Knowledge:** The ability of T2V models to understand and incorporate real-world knowledge (e.g., physical laws, material properties, common sense, social conventions) into generated videos is crucial for creating realistic and plausible content. Existing benchmarks lack a systematic and comprehensive framework for evaluating this understanding. Models might generate visually appealing videos, but they often fail to adhere to natural constraints or common sense, indicating a fundamental gap in their "world knowledge."

4. **Limitations of Explicit Prompt Design:** Many existing benchmarks rely on prompts that explicitly describe every expected visual and dynamic outcome (e.g., "an egg falls and cracks"). This design allows models to perform surface-level text matching rather than inferring implicit consequences based on learned world knowledge. To truly assess "world model capability," T2V models should be able to predict and generate unstated dynamics or outcomes when provided with "hidden semantics" in the prompt (e.g., "an egg falls onto a hard floor" should implicitly lead to cracking). This requires physical reasoning rather than mere instruction following.

In response to these motivations, VideoVerse aims to provide a rigorous and discriminative evaluation framework that challenges T2V models to demonstrate a deeper understanding of the world, moving beyond basic instruction following and aesthetic quality to capabilities essential for developing true "world models."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VideoVerse benchmark is constructed through a multi-faceted approach involving prompt design, definition of evaluation dimensions, and a VLM-based evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. Evaluation Dimensions

The benchmark defines ten evaluation dimensions categorized into "Static" and "Dynamic" perspectives, encompassing both foundational T2V abilities and advanced "world model" capabilities. Each dimension is accompanied by corresponding binary evaluation questions. For three dynamic dimensions, additional sub-questions are developed for fine-grained assessment.

- **Static Dimensions (Evaluated from a single frame):**
 - **Natural Constraints (World Model Level):** Assesses adherence to natural scientific laws (e.g., a lake at -20°C should be frozen).
 - **Common Sense (World Model Level):** Evaluates alignment with cultural or general knowledge (e.g., "a tree representative of Japanese culture" implies a cherry blossom).
 - **Attribution Correctness (Basic Level):** Checks if specified objects and their attributes (color, material, shape) appear correctly.
 - **2D Layout (Basic Level):** Assesses the correctness of 2D spatial arrangements.
 - **3D Depth (Basic Level):** Evaluates the accuracy of perspective relationships (foreground/background).

- **Dynamic Dimensions (Evaluated by understanding temporal dynamics):**
 - **Event Following (World Model Level):** Measures if the model generates a temporally causal sequence of events.
 - **Mechanics (World Model Level):** Assesses if object motion adheres to mechanical laws without interaction (e.g., gravity-induced fall).
 - **Interaction (World Model Level):** Evaluates the physical plausibility of interactions involving direct object contact (e.g., shaving with a razor shortens hair).
 - **Material Properties (World Model Level):** Checks if object behavior aligns with its intrinsic material properties (e.g., chocolate melts when heated).
 - **Camera Control (Basic Level):** Determines if specified camera operations (focus, motion trajectory) are correctly executed.

#### 3.2. Prompt Construction

VideoVerse includes 300 carefully curated prompts, integrating multiple evaluation aspects into each. The prompts are sourced from three distinct domains and processed through a unified pipeline:

- **Domains:**
 - **Daily Life:** Videos sampled from ActivityNet Caption, filtered and refined by GPT-4o for temporal causality and concise prompts.
 - **Scientific Experiment:** Manually collected high-school level natural science experiments from the web.
 - **Science Fiction:** Curated imaginative, non-realistic content from VidProM, cleaned by GPT-4o, to test out-of-domain generalization.

- **Unified Pipeline:**
 1. GPT-4o extracts causal relationships between events from the source prompts, forming initial "raw prompts."
 2. Independent human annotators, all holding at least a bachelor's degree, enrich these raw prompts. They select appropriate evaluation dimensions for each prompt and revise the text to incorporate "hidden semantics." This design requires models to infer expected consequences based on world knowledge rather than explicit textual cues (e.g., for "baking soda and vinegar react in the sink," the hidden semantic is "produces bubbles").
 3. For each added dimension, a corresponding binary evaluation question is designed. Annotators periodically review their work to reduce bias and ensure high quality.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocol

A human-preference-aligned, QA-based evaluation pipeline leveraging powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is employed, specifically Gemini 2.5 Pro, to systematically benchmark T2V systems.

- **Mitigation of VLM Hallucination:** Unlike previous benchmarks that expose the full prompt to the VLM, VideoVerse provides dimension-specific binary questions to the VLM, reducing the likelihood of the VLM inferring answers from the text alone without analyzing the video.
- **Temporal Causality Evaluation (Event Following):** The VLM identifies whether each event specified in the prompt occurs in the generated video and extracts the sequence of observed events. The Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) algorithm is then used to compute a score by comparing this observed sequence with the ground truth event sequence.
- **Prompt-specific Dimension Evaluation:** For the other nine dimensions, the VLM answers dimension-specific binary questions. The number of correctly answered questions contributes to the score for these dimensions.
- **Cumulative Scoring Strategy:** The final score for a generated video is a cumulative sum of the LCS score for Event Following and the count of correctly answered binary questions for all other dimensions.
- **Sub-question Evaluation:** For Interaction, Mechanics, and Material Properties, main questions are decomposed into multiple sub-questions. This fine-grained assessment allows for a more diagnostic understanding of a model's performance on intermediate steps of a complex dynamic event.

#### 3.4. Annotation Quality and Robustness Checks

To ensure the reliability of the benchmark:

- **Ambiguity Mitigation:** Prompts with ambiguous hidden semantics are carefully avoided or refined (e.g., specifying "baking soda" and "vinegar" to ensure a unique expected outcome of "bubbles").
- **Annotator Agreement:** Cohen’s κ statistic between two independent annotators for binary questions derived from hidden semantics yielded a score of 0.905, indicating strong agreement.
- **Text Leakage Prevention:** Experiments querying GPT-5.1 and GPT-4o with prompts and questions *without* video input showed very low accuracy (4.2% and 1.9% confident/correct responses, respectively), confirming that answers cannot be inferred from text alone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of leading open-source and closed-source T2V models on VideoVerse using Gemini 2.5 Pro revealed distinct performance patterns and significant gaps.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Model Ranking

- **Sora-2** achieved the highest overall score, establishing state-of-the-art performance across most dimensions, despite a few videos not being generated due to security reviews.
- **Veo-3** closely followed Sora-2, demonstrating similar high performance.
- Closed-source models (Sora-2, Veo-3, Minimax-Hailuo) consistently outperformed all open-source counterparts across almost all evaluation dimensions.
- Among open-source models, **Wan2.2-A14B** achieved the highest overall score. OpenSora2.0 showed strong performance in Natural Constraints and Common Sense, potentially attributed to its conditioning on the powerful T2I model Flux. SkyReels-V2 (L) performed best in the Interaction dimension.

#### 4.2. Performance Gap Between Open-Source and Closed-Source Models

- While open-source models exhibited comparable performance to closed-source models on "basic level" dimensions (e.g., Attribution Correctness, 2D Layout, 3D Depth), their performance diverged significantly at the "world model level" dimensions.
- The gap in world model capabilities was pronounced. For instance, Veo-3 achieved 72.4% of total world-model points (excluding Event Following) compared to 80.3% on basic dimensions, indicating that even advanced models struggle more with deeper world understanding.

#### 4.3. Limitations in World Model Capabilities

The results highlight that current T2V models, even the most advanced ones, still fall short of full "world model capability" in two primary ways:
- **"Hidden" Semantics Following:** Models frequently restrict their generation to surface-level semantics explicitly stated in the prompt, failing to infer and generate implicit or hidden consequences (e.g., a shaving action not resulting in a shorter beard; dry ice not sublimating into vapor at room temperature).
- **Limited Understanding of the Real World:** Models often fail to produce physically or causally reasonable outputs when additional semantic constraints, grounded in real-world knowledge (e.g., physics, chemistry, common sense), are implicitly introduced in prompts.

#### 4.4. Influence of Video Length on Event Following

Contrary to initial assumptions, the study found that longer video generation length did not necessarily translate to better Event Following performance. Veo-3, generating 8-second videos, consistently outperformed open-source models producing 10-second videos. Similarly, Wan2.2-A14B, with 5-second outputs, exceeded other open-source models capable of 10-second videos in Event Following. This suggests that for the prompts in VideoVerse, the current generation lengths already offer sufficient temporal capacity, and limitations stem from world model understanding rather than mere duration.

#### 4.5. Efficacy of Sub-question Evaluation

The decomposition of "Interaction," "Mechanics," and "Material Properties" into fine-grained sub-questions proved effective. It allowed for a more detailed and discriminative assessment, revealing performance differences even between models that had similar overall scores on the single-question evaluation (e.g., CogVideoX1.5 (L) and Hunyuan). This finer granularity helped identify which intermediate steps models successfully generated.

#### 4.6. Alignment with Human Perception

A user study involving 11 volunteers showed high consistency (>90%) between Gemini 2.5 Pro's VLM-based evaluations and human judgments across various dimensions. This validates the reliability of the VLM as an evaluator. While Camera Control exhibited slightly lower consistency, the overall high agreement supports the VLM-driven protocol as a robust proxy for human perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VideoVerse offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of Text-to-Video (T2V) generation and broader AI research:

1. **Addressing a Critical Evaluation Gap:** The benchmark directly addresses the limitations of existing T2V evaluation methods, which have become insufficient to assess the emerging "world model capabilities" of advanced T2V generators. By focusing on "hidden semantics," temporal causality, and diverse world knowledge, VideoVerse provides a more rigorous and discriminative framework.

2. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The detailed findings from VideoVerse highlight that even state-of-the-art T2V models exhibit substantial gaps in understanding implicit temporal causality, common sense, and natural constraints. This indicates new challenges and critical research directions for the development of more capable and intelligent T2V systems, pushing beyond superficial visual quality to deeper semantic and physical understanding.

3. **Facilitating Model Development and Comparison:** VideoVerse provides a standardized, human-preference-aligned, and efficient protocol for benchmarking T2V models. Researchers can now rigorously compare their models' "world model" abilities, identify specific areas of weakness, and track progress more effectively, thereby accelerating advancements in the field.

4. **Promoting the Development of True "World Models":** By explicitly evaluating understanding of physical laws, material properties, interactions, and common sense, the benchmark encourages the development of T2V models that act as more sophisticated "world simulators." This moves the field closer to creating AI systems that can reason about and generate plausible scenarios based on a deeper comprehension of how the world works.

5. **Efficiency and Comprehensive Coverage:** The design of VideoVerse, which integrates multiple evaluation dimensions and events into each prompt and includes sub-question evaluations, enables a comprehensive assessment with a relatively compact set of prompts. This efficiency is particularly valuable given the increasing computational cost associated with generating videos from large T2V models.

6. **Insights into Model Capabilities Beyond Explicit Instructions:** The emphasis on "hidden semantics" compels models to demonstrate implicit reasoning and predictive capabilities, moving beyond simple instruction following. This challenges models to generate expected consequences that are not explicitly mentioned in the text, fostering a new generation of T2V models with more intuitive and intelligent behaviors.

In summary, VideoVerse is positioned to become an influential tool for evaluating and driving the next wave of innovation in T2V generation, pushing models toward a more profound understanding and simulation of the real world.
