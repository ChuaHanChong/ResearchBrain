# ENPIRE: Agentic Robot Policy Self-Improvement in the Real World

> [!summary] Summary
> ENPIRE provides a framework for autonomous robot policy self-improvement, allowing coding agents to refine dexterous manipulation skills directly on physical robots without extensive human supervision. The system achieves up to 99% success rates on challenging real-world tasks like pin insertion and zip-tie cutting, utilizing automated environment feedback and parallelized learning across robot fleets.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic manipulation relies heavily on human supervision for data collection, environment resets, and policy refinement, creating a bottleneck for scaling physical intelligence.
- Existing coding agents for algorithm discovery primarily succeed in digital simulations, lacking robust, closed-loop interfaces for real-world physical experimentation and policy improvement.
- The absence of a repeatable feedback mechanism on physical robots hinders autonomous policy development in non-deterministic real-world environments.

## Method

- **ENPIRE** establishes a ==four-module closed-loop architecture== (Environment, Policy Improvement, Rollout, Evolution) for physical autoresearch, enabling coding agents to manage robot learning as an optimization process.
- The framework first builds agent-friendly environment abstractions, including hard safety constraints, automated outcome verification, and robust reset mechanisms, leveraging procedural tool calls from initial human feedback.
- It then facilitates fully autonomous policy improvement where multiple agents asynchronously test hypotheses on a fleet of physical robots, collaboratively refining policies and sharing successful training recipes.

## Results

- Coding agents within ENPIRE achieved up to **99%** success rates on real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, such as PushT, pin insertion, and zip-tie cutting, demonstrating faster convergence than frontier human-in-the-loop methods.
- Scaling physical autoresearch from one to eight robots drastically reduced wall-clock time for policy improvement; for instance, pin insertion convergence to a near-perfect success rate was cut from over **1.5** hours to approximately 40 minutes.
- The framework enabled the autonomous construction of robust environment interfaces, including reward functions with under 150ms inference latency, and successfully transferred autoresearch experience to improve performance on new tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous coding agents can effectively construct robust real-world environment interfaces, incorporating safety and automated feedback mechanisms, by composing procedural tool calls with limited human guidance.
> - Scaling physical autoresearch across a fleet of robots with decentralized multi-agent collaboration significantly accelerates policy convergence, although it entails a trade-off with increased token and compute costs.
> - Knowledge accumulated through autonomous physical autoresearch, including diverse learning paradigms and environment abstractions, is transferable across tasks, enhancing efficiency for new challenges.
