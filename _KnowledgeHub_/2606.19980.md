# ENPIRE: Agentic Robot Policy Self-Improvement in the Real World

> [!summary] Summary
> ENPIRE provides a framework for autonomous robot policy self-improvement, allowing coding agents to refine dexterous manipulation skills directly on physical robots without extensive human supervision. The system achieves up to 99% success rates on challenging real-world tasks like pin insertion and zip-tie cutting, utilizing automated environment feedback and parallelized learning across robot fleets.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic manipulation relies heavily on human supervision for data collection, environment resets, and policy refinement, creating a bottleneck for scaling physical intelligence.
- Existing coding agents for algorithm discovery primarily succeed in digital simulations, lacking robust, closed-loop interfaces for real-world physical experimentation and policy improvement.
- The absence of a repeatable feedback mechanism on physical robots hinders autonomous policy development in non-deterministic real-world environments.

## Method

- **ENPIRE** establishes a ==four-module closed-loop architecture== (Environment, Policy Improvement, Rollout, Evolution) for physical autoresearch, enabling coding agents to manage robot learning as an optimization process.
- The framework first builds agent-friendly environment abstractions, including hard safety constraints, automated outcome verification, and robust reset mechanisms, leveraging procedural tool calls from initial human feedback.
- It then facilitates fully autonomous policy improvement where multiple agents asynchronously test hypotheses on a fleet of physical robots, collaboratively refining policies and sharing successful training recipes.

## Results

- Coding agents within ENPIRE achieved up to **99%** success rates on real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, such as PushT, pin insertion, and zip-tie cutting, demonstrating faster convergence than frontier human-in-the-loop methods.
- Scaling physical autoresearch from one to eight robots drastically reduced wall-clock time for policy improvement; for instance, pin insertion convergence to a near-perfect success rate was cut from over **1.5** hours to approximately 40 minutes.
- The framework enabled the autonomous construction of robust environment interfaces, including reward functions with under 150ms inference latency, and successfully transferred autoresearch experience to improve performance on new tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous coding agents can effectively construct robust real-world environment interfaces, incorporating safety and automated feedback mechanisms, by composing procedural tool calls with limited human guidance.
> - Scaling physical autoresearch across a fleet of robots with decentralized multi-agent collaboration significantly accelerates policy convergence, although it entails a trade-off with increased token and compute costs.
> - Knowledge accumulated through autonomous physical autoresearch, including diverse learning paradigms and environment abstractions, is transferable across tasks, enhancing efficiency for new challenges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly for dexterous tasks in real-world environments, has historically been characterized by a significant reliance on human supervision and intricate algorithmic engineering. This human-intensive process represents a bottleneck in the pursuit of more general and autonomous physical intelligence. Recent advancements in AI-powered "coding agents" have shown promise in automating algorithm discovery and optimization, primarily within digital environments such as simulations. These agents can generate and refine code, automating aspects of algorithmic research.

This paper addresses a notable gap in this evolving landscape: the extension of autonomous research capabilities to real-world robotic policy learning. While coding agents excel in digital settings, the unique challenges of physical interaction—such as non-deterministic environments, the need for robust real-time feedback, and safe physical operation—have largely confined their successes to simulations. Previous work in agentic self-improvement often relies on inexpensive, fast-iterating substrates like simulations for closing the hypothesis-experiment loop, with real robots typically serving as a deployment target rather than an iterative experimentation medium. Similarly, autonomous research agents in other scientific domains, like chemistry, have either operated in digital spaces or required human execution for physical experiments.

ENPIRE positions itself by formalizing "physical autoresearch" as a distinct problem. It introduces a framework designed to bridge the gap between digital agentic research and real-world robotic policy improvement. By establishing a repeatable physical feedback loop (reset, execute, verify, refine), ENPIRE aims to enable coding agents to autonomously conduct experiments, develop, and refine policies directly on physical robots. This work also contributes to the understanding of scaling autonomous research across multiple physical agents, proposing metrics for evaluating resource utilization in a hardware-constrained environment, a departure from benchmarks focused solely on capability or computational cost in digital settings. It connects to the ongoing research in "Code as Policies" by demonstrating agents generating and optimizing code for robotic control, and in "Agentic Self-Improvement" by closing the learning loop directly on hardware, rather than just in simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable autonomous self-improvement of robot policies for dexterous manipulation tasks in the real world, significantly reducing the reliance on human supervision and algorithmic engineering. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific objectives:

1. **Addressing the Human Supervision Bottleneck:** Dexterous robotic manipulation traditionally requires substantial human involvement in data collection, environment resetting, outcome evaluation, and algorithmic adjustment. As the scale and complexity of real-world policy learning increase, this human labor becomes a limiting factor in the speed at which robots can acquire advanced dexterity. The paper aims to mitigate this by automating the policy improvement lifecycle.

2. **Bridging the Digital-Physical Gap for Coding Agents:** While coding agents demonstrate considerable success in automating algorithm search in digital environments, their application to real-world robotics is hindered by the absence of suitable physical interfaces for closed-loop hypothesis testing. The motivation is to provide these agents with the necessary real-world environment interfaces, allowing them to autonomously deploy policies, evaluate outcomes, and reset physical scenes.

3. **Enabling Repeatable Real-World Feedback Loops:** A core motivation is the establishment of a robust and repeatable feedback loop in the physical world. This loop, comprising scene reset, policy execution, outcome verification, and subsequent refinement, is identified as a missing abstraction required for automating robotics research. The objective is to instantiate this routine within a framework that agents can manage as a controllable optimization procedure.

4. **Formalizing Physical Autoresearch:** The authors aim to formalize the problem of physical autoresearch for dexterous manipulation, defining it as a distinct challenge for both the coding agent and robotics communities. This includes proposing a two-step approach: an initial human-guided phase to construct an automatic environment feedback system, followed by a fully autonomous phase for policy improvement.

5. **Developing Robust Environment Abstractions:** A critical objective is for coding agents to construct autonomous, agent-friendly abstractions of physical interaction. This involves creating task-specific safety constraints, real-time automated verification processes for feedback and credit assignment, and robust automatic reset mechanisms, all implemented through procedural tool calls. This aims to amortize initial human setup costs across subsequent autonomous learning.

6. **Accelerating Autoresearch through Parallelization:** Scaling autonomous research across multiple robots presents challenges in efficiently selecting and verifying hypotheses. The motivation is to develop a mechanism for evolutionary hypothesis selection that accelerates policy improvement by deploying decentralized teams of agents to test training recipes asynchronously.

7. **Quantifying Efficiency in Resource-Constrained Environments:** In real-world robotics, physical resources (robots, hardware) are scarce. The research is motivated to introduce new metrics, Mean Robot Utilization (MRU) and Mean Token Utilization (MTU), to measure the efficiency with which multi-agent physical autoresearch converts allocated resources into research progress, going beyond traditional capability- or cost-per-paper metrics.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to pave a practical and scalable path toward autonomously advancing robotics in real-world settings, thereby contributing to the broader goal of general physical intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ENPIRE framework is designed to enable agentic robot policy self-improvement in the real world through a two-stage process: Environment Construction and Automated Policy Improvement.

#### 3.1. ENPIRE Framework Architecture

ENPIRE comprises four core modules:
- **Environment (EN):** Responsible for automatic scene reset and outcome verification.
- **Policy Improvement (PI):** Launches policy refinement based on feedback.
- **Rollout (R):** Executes policies on single or multiple physical robots in parallel to evaluate performance.
- **Evolution (E):** Coding agents analyze logs, consult literature, and modify training infrastructure and algorithm code to address failure modes.

This closed-loop system allows coding agents to manage robot learning as an optimization procedure, minimizing human intervention.

#### 3.2. Stage One: Environment Construction from Human Feedback (EN)

This initial stage focuses on building an agent-friendly abstraction of physical interaction, providing structured feedback for autonomous policy improvement. This phase involves human guidance to ensure reliability and safety.

- **Hard Safety Constraints:** Coding agents implement rules to restrict the robot's configuration space and kinematic behaviors within safe operational limits. Violating these limits triggers an immediate task failure and an automated reset, safeguarding hardware during unattended operation.
- **Automated Verification:** Agents are tasked with synthesizing binary reward functions using procedural tool calls. Given limited success and failure demonstrations (e.g., videos, proprioception recordings), agents optimize for prediction accuracy and processing latency (e.g., under 150ms). Examples include a reward for pin insertion based on visual alignment, end-effector height, and force estimates, and a dual-view image segmentation-based reward for zip-tie insertion.
- **Automated Reset:** Agents develop tool call sequences to restore the environment to an initial state after task completion or failure. For contact-rich tasks, procedural tool calls are used to precisely position the robot at the onset of critical actions (e.g., inserting a pin), focusing learning on precision bottlenecks. This involves composing perception APIs (like SAM3 for object detection, BundleSDF for 6-DOF pose tracking) and planning APIs (like cuRobo for collision-free trajectory optimization), along with tactile feedback from gripper torque signals.

Once constructed, these safety constraints, automated verification, and automated reset mechanisms form the Environment module (EN). They are exposed to coding agents as immutable Gym APIs, providing feedback and debugging information for subsequent policy improvement.

#### 3.3. Stage Two: Automated Policy Improvement from Real-World Feedback (PIRE)

This stage involves fully autonomous policy refinement using the established environment interfaces.

- **Policy Improvement (PI):** Upon receiving a task description with the objective of maximizing success rate, the coding agent reviews relevant literature, formulates hypotheses, and directly modifies the training codebase. This codebase supports various policy learning paradigms, including heuristic learning, behavior cloning (BC), and online/offline reinforcement learning (RL). The agent invokes environment APIs to log robot trajectories, video recordings, and reward signals during rollouts (Rollout module - R), inspecting these statistics to guide continuous improvement. Agents also tune policy parameters like batch size or hyperparameters.
- **Accelerating Autoresearch via Multi-Agent Scaling (Evolution Module - E):** To enhance scalability, ENPIRE implements a decentralized multi-agent collaboration protocol. N agents are deployed across N physical robots, testing N hypotheses asynchronously. Agents collaborate autonomously via Git, cherry-picking, copying, or merging successful training recipes from their peers to optimize their code search. This allows for distributed hypothesis selection and knowledge transfer.
- **Resource Utilization Metrics:** To quantify efficiency, two metrics are introduced:
 - **Mean Robot Utilization (MRU):** The fraction of research wall-clock time during which a robot is actively executing an experiment.
 - **Mean Token Utilization (MTU):** The fleet's average token consumption during autoresearch, used to calculate a token-to-success ratio.

#### 3.4. Robot System Setup

- **Hardware:** A fleet of eight bimanual YAM robot stations from I2RT. Each station is equipped with two 6-DoF manipulators (YAM arms) with 1-DoF parallel-jaw grippers, cameras (Intel RealSense D405/D435i for RGB-D, top-down, wrist-mounted, and side views), and a workstation (NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU, Intel Core Ultra 9 CPU, 128 GB RAM).
- **Control:** Policy actions are inferred at 30 Hz, with low-level joint controllers running at 100 Hz. Arm joints use PD control with gravity compensation. Grippers operate in a torque-limiting compliant grasp mode for robust and safe interaction.
- **Software Stack:** Hardware control requests are routed through a local FastAPI server. Coordination among stations is entirely Git-based. Agents operate within a sandbox, having elevated autonomy and access to robot data. The PLD-RL pipeline is integrated for online data collection.
- **Coding Agents Benchmarked:** Codex with GPT-5.5 xhigh, Claude Code with Opus 4.7 High, and Kimi Code with Kimi K2.6 thinking.
- **Tasks:** Dexterous manipulation tasks including Push-T, Pin insertion, GPU insertion, and Zip-tie cutting.
- **Success Metric:** Success is defined as completing a task in one rollout, given a fixed number of retries (eight), reflecting both precision and in-context recovery.

**Integration with External Tools:** The methodology leverages existing tools and APIs for perception (SAM3 for open-vocabulary object detection, BundleSDF for 6-DOF pose tracking, RANSAC, OBB), planning (cuRobo for GPU-accelerated collision-free trajectory optimization, Pyroki for IK), and VLM integration (GR00T).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates ENPIRE's capabilities in autonomous policy self-improvement for dexterous manipulation tasks in real-world settings.

#### 4.1. Autonomous Environment Construction and Verification

Coding agents successfully synthesized complex environment interfaces from human feedback.
- **Reward Design:** Agents developed robust reward functions using procedural tool calls. For instance, in pin insertion, a hybrid reward fused visual alignment, end-effector height, and force estimates. For zip-tie insertion, a dual-view image segmentation strategy was designed to verify fastening, optimized to an inference latency under 150ms.
- **Reset Mechanisms:** Agents composed perception (SAM3, BundleSDF), planning (cuRobo), and manipulation skills (pick-and-place, handover) into long-horizon reset functions. An example for GPU insertion involved SAM3-based localization, 3D bounding box estimation (RANSAC, OBB), torque-verified grasping, and collision-free handover.

#### 4.2. Autonomous Policy Improvement on Real-World Tasks

ENPIRE enabled coding agents to achieve high success rates on challenging real-world tasks.
- **Dexterous Tasks:** Agents developed policies achieving up to 99% success rates on tasks such as PushT, pin insertion, and zip-tie cutting.
- **Learning Efficiency:** For pin insertion, policy convergence to 100% was achieved faster than a frontier human-in-the-loop method (Xiao et al., 2025).
- **Diverse Learning Paradigms:** Agents explored and combined various policy improvement regimes, including heuristic learning, tool calling, behavior cloning (BC), and offline or online reinforcement learning (RL), along with parameter tuning (e.g., batch size, BC regularization terms).
- **Simulation vs. Real World:** While all agents solved Push-T in simulation, two of three failed in the real-world Push-T task. This highlights the greater challenge posed by non-deterministic and time-varying real-world physics, motivating the exploration of diverse learning methods and scaling.

#### 4.3. Scaling Policy Learning on a Robot Fleet

The study explored the impact of scaling robot and agent resources on policy improvement.
- **Accelerated Convergence:** Scaling the fleet size from one to eight agents significantly reduced the wall-clock time required to achieve target task performance. For Push-T, time to a 1.0 normalized score decreased from approximately five hours to two hours. For pin insertion, achieving a near-perfect success rate was reduced from over 1.5 hours to approximately 40 minutes. This indicates that ENPIRE can leverage distributed hypothesis selection to accelerate policy improvement.
- **Domain Randomization:** In multi-agent settings, ENPIRE facilitated automatic application of domain randomization (e.g., variations in spatial configurations for GPU insertion), contributing to stronger policy robustness.
- **Resource Utilization Analysis:**
 - **Mean Robot Utilization (MRU):** Decreased as the number of robots increased, suggesting agents spent more time on coordinating activities (e.g., summarizing peer branches) and less time actively operating the robot.
 - **GPU Utilization:** Increased with fleet size.
 - **Mean Token Utilization (MTU):** Grew faster than linearly with fleet size, especially at eight agents. The total token budget required for a successful policy also increased disproportionately to the reduction in wall-clock time, indicating a trade-off between speed and token efficiency.

#### 4.4. Transferring Autoresearch Experience

Knowledge accumulated from multi-agent physical autoresearch demonstrated transferability.
- **Knowledge Transfer:** Insights from the pin insertion task, when summarized and appended to instructions for the GPU insertion task, enabled coding agents to achieve high success rates more efficiently on the new task.

#### 4.5. Synergy between Code-Based Policies and Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs)

ENPIRE facilitated the integration of VLAs with procedural tool calls.
- **Enhanced VLAs:** In the RoboCasa365 simulator, agents improved the success rate of the GR00T VLA by autonomously discovering and utilizing motion planning and detection tools to hover above objects before grasping. This strategy was successfully transferred to a real-world zip-tie cutting task.
- **Simulation Performance:** ENPIRE demonstrated superior performance on the RoboCasa365 benchmark compared to end-to-end VLAs (GR00T) and zero-shot agentic tool use (CaP-X).

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies and Model Comparisons

- **Native Vision:** Coding agents with native visual understanding capabilities performed better than those relying on VLM tools for vision or entirely text-based information.
- **Model/Harness Comparison:** Codex with GPT-5.5 achieved task success faster compared to Claude Code with Opus 4.7 and Codex using the Opus API, indicating differences in model reasoning capabilities and practical overheads of the coding interface.
- **Perception Bottlenecks:** In simulation, SAM3 detection accuracy was a bottleneck for small or ambiguous objects. This was partially mitigated by increasing top-camera resolution and allowing agents to rewrite object prompts, highlighting the role of agentic tool use in perception optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, ENPIRE, that contributes to the advancement of robotics and artificial intelligence in several significant ways:

1. **Enabling Autonomous Robotics Research:** ENPIRE operationalizes "physical autoresearch" by establishing a repeatable, controllable feedback loop directly on real-world robots. This represents a step towards automating the entire policy improvement lifecycle, from environment setup to algorithmic refinement, substantially reducing human intervention in real-world robot learning. This could accelerate the development of robotic capabilities.

2. **Scalability and Efficiency in Real-World Settings:** The demonstration of parallelized autoresearch across a fleet of physical robots highlights a scalable approach to skill acquisition. While revealing a trade-off between faster convergence and increased token/compute costs, the ability to leverage multiple robots to explore hypotheses simultaneously offers a practical pathway for accelerating robotics development in resource-constrained environments. The introduction of Mean Robot Utilization (MRU) and Mean Token Utilization (MTU) provides new metrics for evaluating the efficiency of such systems, fostering further research into optimizing resource allocation.

3. **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation:** By enabling agents to autonomously develop and refine policies to achieve high success rates (e.g., 99%) on complex, dexterous tasks like pin insertion and zip-tie cutting, ENPIRE pushes the boundaries of what autonomous systems can achieve in fine-grained robotic control. This capability could lead to more robust and precise robotic applications in various industries.

4. **Robust Environment Abstractions:** The methodology for coding agents to construct automated safety, verification, and reset mechanisms as reusable APIs is critical. This approach amortizes the initial human effort, making future policy iterations more efficient and reliable. The integration of advanced perception (SAM3, BundleSDF) and planning (cuRobo) tools into these agent-designed environments further enhances their robustness to real-world variability.

5. **Generalization and Transfer of Knowledge:** The observed transferability of autoresearch insights from one task to another (e.g., pin insertion to GPU insertion) suggests a path towards more generalizable and adaptable robotic systems. This could reduce the engineering effort required for new tasks by allowing agents to build upon accumulated "research experience."

6. **Synergy with Advanced AI Models:** The ability of ENPIRE to integrate vision-language-action models (VLAs) with procedural tool calls indicates a promising avenue for combining different AI paradigms. This hybrid approach, where agents can augment learned VLA policies with explicitly designed motion planning and detection tools, could lead to more capable and interpretable robotic behaviors.

Overall, ENPIRE offers a tangible and scalable framework for autonomously advancing robotics in the real world. Its impact could range from accelerating fundamental research in physical intelligence to enabling the deployment of more capable, adaptable, and less human-dependent robotic systems across diverse applications.
