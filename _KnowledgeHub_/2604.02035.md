# Reinforcement Learning for Speculative Trading under Exploratory Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a reinforcement learning framework for sequential optimal stopping in speculative trading, where agents learn optimal entry and exit times under general price dynamics and utility functions. The methodology employs randomized intensity controls with entropy regularization, yielding closed-form optimal policies as Gibbs distributions and providing theoretical convergence guarantees to the original problem.

## Problem

- Determining optimal sequential entry and exit times for speculative trading to maximize discounted expected utility.
- Handling general underlying price processes and diverse utility functions, which are often challenging for traditional optimal stopping methods.
- Balancing exploration of new strategies with exploitation of known profitable opportunities in a model-free setting.

## Method

- The ==sequential optimal stopping== problem is reformulated using ==continuous intensity controls== for entry and exit, modeled as ==Cox processes==, transforming it into a continuous control problem.
- An ==entropy-regularized objective== function is introduced, where randomized policies are defined as probability measures over intensity values, and optimal exploration densities are derived in ==closed-form== as ==Gibbs distributions==.
- A system of ==Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB)== equations is established, and a model-free ==policy iteration algorithm== using ==deep neural networks== is developed for numerical implementation.

## Results

- Numerical solutions of the HJB system demonstrate how value functions and optimal policies evolve with market price, entry price, intensity cap, and exploration temperature.
- Optimal exploration densities show high dispersion for increased exploration (higher temperature η) and concentrate near optimal deterministic actions for reduced exploration (lower η).
- The model-free reinforcement learning algorithm, using deep neural networks, closely approximates the benchmark HJB solutions, particularly with an enhanced training initialization strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Randomized intensities driven by Cox processes provide a direct and theoretically grounded mechanism for exploration within continuous-time optimal stopping problems.
> - Optimal exploration policies naturally emerge as Gibbs distributions, where the probability of choosing an intensity is scaled by its marginal value relative to the exploration temperature.
> - Rigorous error bounds confirm the convergence of the entropy-regularized RL objective to the original optimal stopping problem's value function under specific parameter limits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned within the rapidly expanding field of machine learning, specifically deep and reinforcement learning (RL), applied to quantitative finance. RL offers a model-free approach, enabling agents to learn optimal actions in unknown environments by balancing exploration of new possibilities and exploitation of learned knowledge. This paradigm has demonstrated success across diverse domains, including game playing, large language models, robotics, and energy management.

A central challenge in RL is the systematic management of the exploration-exploitation dilemma. In discrete-time settings, entropy-regularized RL formulations address this by incorporating the entropy of the exploration strategy into the objective function, controlled by a temperature parameter. For continuous-time, state, and action spaces, Wang et al. introduced an exploratory stochastic control framework where Shannon's entropy quantifies the exploration cost, yielding an optimal policy as a probability measure over actions. This foundational framework has been extended to various contexts, including linear-quadratic problems, general exploratory HJB equations, policy gradient and Q-learning algorithms, mean-variance portfolio selection, mean-field games, jump diffusions, and optimal stopping problems.

This paper specifically extends the continuous-time, exploratory RL framework to a sequential optimal stopping problem in speculative trading. While existing literature has applied exploratory RL to one-off optimal stopping (e.g., Dong, Dai et al.), this work distinguishes itself by focusing on the more complex scenario of sequential entry and exit decisions. It also introduces a randomized policy for stopping intensities, which is a key departure from prior works that might use randomized exogenous variables or binary action controls, thereby providing a more direct and intuitive exploration mechanism within the context of optimal stopping. Furthermore, the paper employs a different definition of entropy, relative to the uniform distribution, which facilitates monotonic convergence properties. The analysis also relies on probabilistic techniques, contrasting with some PDE-based arguments found in the extant literature. By addressing sequential stopping with a novel randomization of intensities under a general price process and utility function, this research contributes to bridging stochastic control theory and model-free RL for dynamic financial decision-making.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and analyze a reinforcement learning framework for a speculative trading problem, specifically focusing on sequential optimal stopping decisions for market entry and exit. The goal is to maximize the expected utility derived from the round-trip profit, net of transaction costs, under general utility functions and price processes.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and opportunities in quantitative finance:
- **Sequential Decision-Making:** Financial trading often involves a sequence of interconnected decisions, such as deciding when to enter an investment opportunity and subsequently when to exit it. Traditional optimal stopping problems typically focus on a single stopping event. This paper aims to provide a robust framework for handling such sequential decisions within an RL context.
- **Model-Free Learning:** Financial markets are complex, and their dynamics are often difficult to model accurately with strong assumptions. RL, with its model-free nature, offers a promising alternative by learning optimal strategies directly from interactions with the environment or simulated data, thereby bypassing the need for explicit, potentially misspecified, market models.
- **Balancing Exploration and Exploitation:** In speculative trading, agents must effectively balance exploring new strategies or market conditions with exploiting currently known profitable opportunities. An agent that exploits too much risks missing better strategies, while an agent that explores too much may never capitalize on what it has learned. The exploratory RL framework, with its entropy regularization, provides a systematic mechanism to quantify and manage this trade-off, encouraging diverse behaviors that can lead to discovering more robust or profitable strategies.
- **Generalizability:** Previous applications of continuous-time RL to optimal stopping often have limitations, such as focusing on specific utility functions (e.g., piecewise linear) or price processes (e.g., geometric Brownian motion), or relying on PDE-based methods that suffer from the "curse of dimensionality." This research seeks to develop a more general theoretical and methodological framework applicable to a wider range of diffusion price dynamics and utility functions, including those reflecting behavioral preferences like Prospect Theory.
- **Addressing Limitations of Deterministic Policies:** In classical optimal stopping, the optimal policy is often a deterministic stopping rule. However, in an RL setting, particularly with exploration, a randomized policy can be more beneficial for learning. By explicitly formulating randomized intensities via probability measures, the paper aims to better capture the exploratory spirit of RL, allowing the agent to systematically sample a range of potential stopping rates.

In essence, the work aims to offer a theoretically grounded and practically implementable RL solution for dynamic, sequential trading problems that are free from rigid modeling assumptions, capable of balancing exploration-exploitation, and general enough to handle complex market dynamics and investor preferences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology in this paper progresses through several stages: first, formulating the original speculative trading problem, then relaxing it to an intensity-control problem, subsequently integrating an entropy-regularized exploratory framework, and finally demonstrating a numerical implementation.

#### 3.1. Original Problem Formulation (Sequential Optimal Stopping)

The speculative trading problem is defined as a sequential optimal stopping problem. An agent seeks to find optimal entry ($\tau$) and exit ($\nu$) times, with $\tau \le \nu$, to maximize the discounted expected utility of a round-trip profit. The utility function $U(\cdot)$ is assumed to be continuous, increasing, and H¨older continuous, while the underlying price process $P_t$ follows a general one-dimensional diffusion process with Lipschitz continuous drift and diffusion coefficients. A subjective discount rate $\rho$ is introduced to ensure the problem yields a finite value function. The objective is formalized as $V_{orig}(p) = \sup_{\tau, \nu} E[e^{-\rho\nu} U(\gamma P_\nu - \iota P_\tau - \Psi - R) \mathbf{1}_{\{\nu < \infty\}} | P_0 = p]$, where $\gamma, \iota, \Psi, R$ are constants representing scaling, transaction costs, and reference points.

#### 3.2. Intensity-Relaxed Problem

To convert the optimal stopping problem into a continuous control problem suitable for RL, the entry and exit decisions are reformulated using continuous intensity controls.
- **Augmented State Space:** The original price process $P_t$ is augmented with two auxiliary variables: $J_t \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ indicating the trading regime (0: before entry, 1: holding position, 2: after exit), and $B_t$ recording the price at entry.
- **Randomized Stopping via Cox Processes:** Entry and exit times are modeled as jump times of two Cox processes, $N^\alpha_t$ and $N^\beta_t$. These are driven by deterministic, bounded intensity control processes $\alpha_t, \beta_t \in [0, M]$, where $M$ is an upper bound on the intensity. The intensities are only effective in their respective regimes (e.g., $\alpha_t$ is active only when $J_t=0$). The Cox processes are constructed by time-changing independent unit-rate Poisson seeds.
- **Reward Function:** The reward is defined as an instantaneous utility rate, $r(x,u) = \beta G(p,b) \mathbf{1}_{\{j=1\}}$, which materializes when an exit occurs. The equivalence between maximizing the cumulative instantaneous reward and maximizing the utility at the actual stopping time is formally proven ($V_{inte,1} = V_{inte,2}$).
- **Error Estimate:** An error bound is established between the original problem's value function ($V_{orig}$) and the intensity-relaxed problem's value function ($V_{inte}$). This bound is of the order $CM^{-\kappa/2}$, where $\kappa$ is the H¨older exponent of the utility function, demonstrating that $V_{inte}$ converges to $V_{orig}$ as $M \to \infty$. This implies that a sufficiently high intensity cap $M$ can accurately approximate the original problem, while a finite $M$ can be seen as a regularization for exploration.

#### 3.3. Entropy-Regularized Problem with Randomized Intensities (Exploratory Framework)

The paper then introduces the core exploratory RL framework.
- **Randomized Policies:** Instead of deterministic intensities, the agent chooses probability measures $(\pi^\alpha_t, \pi^\beta_t)$ over the intensity domain $[0, M]$. The expected values of these probability measures, $\bar{\alpha}_t$ and $\bar{\beta}_t$, then drive the Cox processes and influence the state dynamics.
- **Entropy Regularization:** The objective function is augmented by adding Shannon's differential entropy $H(\pi_t)$ of the chosen probability measures, scaled by a "temperature" parameter $\eta$. This entropy is defined relative to the uniform distribution on $[0, M]^2$, ensuring it is nonpositive and enables monotonic convergence. The full exploratory value function is $V^\eta_{ent}(p) = \sup_{\pi} E[\int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t} (\tilde{r}_t^\pi + \eta H(\pi_t)) dt]$.
- **HJB System and Optimal Policy:** A system of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations is derived for the value functions in each trading regime ($V_0(p)$, $V_1(p,b)$, $V_2(p,b)=0$). Crucially, the optimal exploration densities ($\pi^{\alpha,*}_t(\lambda; p)$ and $\pi^{\beta,*}_t(\lambda; p,b)$) are found in closed-form as Gibbs distributions. These distributions indicate the probability of choosing a particular intensity $\lambda$, with the probability weighted by the "economic advantage" (marginal value) of entering or exiting the market, relative to the temperature $\eta$.
- **Error Estimate and Convergence:** A second error bound is established, showing the difference between the entropy-regularized value function ($V^\eta_{ent}$) and the intensity-relaxed value function ($V_{inte}$) is bounded by $CM^2 |\eta \ln \eta|$. Combining this with the previous error estimate, the paper proves that the entropy-regularized value function converges to the original problem's value function as $\eta \downarrow 0$ and $M \to \infty$, specifically under the condition that $M^2 |\eta \ln \eta| \to 0$. This provides a theoretical guarantee for approximating the original problem using the RL framework.

#### 3.4. Numerical Implementation: Pairs-Trading Example

The theoretical framework is applied to a pairs-trading scenario.
- **Model Specification:** The price spread $P_t$ is modeled as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process, a common choice for mean-reverting financial assets. The utility function is an S-shaped power utility, incorporating Prospect Theory preferences (risk aversion for gains, risk seeking for losses, and loss aversion).
- **HJB Solver:** The HJB system is numerically solved using a finite difference method with iteration, providing benchmark solutions for value functions and optimal policies.
- **Offline Policy Iteration Algorithm:** A model-free RL algorithm is designed using deep neural networks to approximate the value functions. This algorithm employs policy iteration, where optimal policies are computed using the closed-form Gibbs distributions, and value functions are updated via TD errors generated from simulated trajectories. A practical modification for training efficiency involves initializing the trading regime for simulations to cover both pre-entry and in-position states.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Theoretical Contributions

The paper provides several theoretical advancements for continuous-time reinforcement learning in sequential optimal stopping:
- **General Framework for Sequential Stopping:** It extends the continuous-time RL framework to dynamic optimization problems involving sequential (entry and exit) stopping, moving beyond prior works that primarily addressed one-off stopping. This formulation accommodates general diffusion dynamics for the underlying asset price and diverse utility functions, enhancing its applicability in financial contexts.
- **Randomized Intensities and Exploration:** A key innovation is the modeling of stopping times via Cox processes driven by *randomized* intensities, represented by probability measures over a bounded intensity range $[0, M]$. This approach aligns with the core principles of exploratory RL by allowing the agent to genuinely explore different stopping rates.
- **Closed-Form Optimal Policies:** The derivation of optimal exploration densities in closed-form as Gibbs distributions is a significant result. These distributions explicitly parameterize the agent's sampling strategy over candidate intensities, providing a direct link between the economic "advantage" of an action and the propensity to take it, governed by the exploration temperature $\eta$.
- **System of Exploratory HJB Equations:** A comprehensive system of HJB equations is derived for the value functions associated with different trading regimes (before entry, holding position, after exit). These equations integrate the entropy regularization term, which drives the randomized policies.
- **Robust Error Estimates and Convergence Guarantees:** The paper establishes detailed error bounds. First, it shows that the intensity-relaxed problem ($V_{inte}$) approximates the original problem ($V_{orig}$) with an error of $CM^{-\kappa/2}$. Second, it demonstrates that the entropy-regularized problem ($V^\eta_{ent}$) approximates the intensity-relaxed problem with an error of $CM^2 |\eta \ln \eta|$. The combined result proves the convergence of the RL objective to the original problem's value function when $M \to \infty$ and $\eta \downarrow 0$, under the condition that $M^2 |\eta \ln \eta| \to 0$. This provides a strong theoretical basis for the proposed RL approach.
- **Novel Entropy Definition:** The use of Shannon's differential entropy relative to the uniform distribution on the action space is a methodological choice that results in nonpositive entropy and ensures monotonic convergence of the value function as $\eta \downarrow 0$.
- **Probabilistic Analysis:** The theoretical analysis relies on probabilistic techniques, offering an alternative perspective compared to PDE-based arguments in some related literature.

#### 4.2. Numerical Results (Pairs-Trading Application)

The numerical implementation using a pairs-trading example with an Ornstein-Uhbeck (OU) process and S-shaped utility demonstrates the practical aspects of the framework:
- **Validation of HJB Solutions:** The HJB system, solved via finite difference methods, provides benchmark solutions for the value functions and optimal policies.
- **Impact of M and η on Value Functions:**
 - The value function $V_0(p)$ (before entry) monotonically increases with both the intensity cap $M$ and the temperature parameter $\eta$. A higher $M$ expands the action space, allowing for potentially better strategies, while higher $\eta$ induces more exploration, which (due to the nonpositive entropy definition) increases the objective.
 - The plots of $V_0(p)$ and $V_1(p,b)$ exhibit expected economic intuitions: $V_0(p)$ generally decreases with $p$ (lower price is better for entry), and $V_1(p,b)$ generally increases with $p$ (higher price is better for exit) and decreases with $b$ (lower entry price $b$ is better for profit).
- **Visualization of Optimal Exploration Densities:**
 - For high $\eta$, the optimal entry density $\pi_{\alpha,*}(\lambda; p)$ shows a more dispersed distribution across different intensities $\lambda$, indicating a preference for exploration. Even at very low or very high prices $p$, there is a non-zero probability of choosing sub-optimal entry intensities.
 - For small $\eta$, the densities become highly concentrated, approaching Dirac measures. For low $p$, the density peaks near $\lambda=M$ (aggressive entry), and for high $p$, it peaks near $\lambda=0$ (no entry). At the free boundary where the agent is indifferent to entering, the density approaches a uniform distribution over $[0,M]$.
 - Similar behavior is observed for optimal exit densities $\pi_{\beta,*}(\lambda; p,b)$, with free boundaries reflecting loss aversion (higher exit price for higher entry price).
- **Comparative Statics for OU Parameters:**
 - **Mean-reversion speed ($\theta$):** Increased $\theta$ leads to higher $V_0$ when $p$ is very low (stronger expectation of price increase), but lower $V_0$ when $p$ is near the mean (confining future price changes to a narrow band, reducing potential profit).
 - **Volatility ($\sigma$):** Increased $\sigma$ generally leads to higher $V_0$, as higher volatility increases the chance of the signal reaching profitable stopping regions sooner, improving the present value of the trade.
- **Performance of Model-Free RL Algorithm:** The offline policy iteration algorithm, using deep neural networks for value function approximation, produces results that closely match the benchmark solutions from the HJB solver. Discrepancies are primarily observed around the free boundaries, which is a common characteristic in numerical solutions of optimal stopping problems. A modified initialization strategy for the trading regime (mixing $J_0=0$ and $J_0=1$) is shown to enhance training efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significance for both theoretical advancements in reinforcement learning and practical applications in quantitative finance.

**Theoretical Significance:**
- **Bridging Stochastic Control and RL:** The paper provides a rigorous mathematical framework that bridges continuous-time stochastic control theory with model-free reinforcement learning for sequential optimal stopping problems. This integration is crucial for applying advanced RL techniques to finance where continuous-time dynamics are prevalent.
- **Genuine Exploration Mechanism:** By defining policies as probability measures over continuous intensity controls and incorporating Shannon's entropy, the framework offers a theoretically sound mechanism for genuine exploration. This contrasts with approaches that might rely on exogenous randomization or binary action spaces, providing a more granular and direct control over exploratory behavior.
- **Robust Convergence Guarantees:** The derivation of comprehensive error bounds and convergence results for the entropy-regularized value function ensures that the RL solution can reliably approximate the optimal solution of the original speculative trading problem under specific parameter limits. This theoretical grounding enhances confidence in the practical utility of the proposed algorithms.
- **Generalizability of Optimal Stopping:** The framework's ability to handle general diffusion processes and utility functions expands the scope of problems that can be addressed using RL, moving beyond restrictive assumptions found in much of the existing literature.

**Practical Impact:**
- **Advanced Algorithmic Trading Strategies:** The proposed methodology can be used to design and optimize sophisticated speculative trading strategies, such as pairs trading, for market entry and exit decisions. Its model-free nature allows it to learn from real or simulated market data without requiring explicit, potentially fragile, assumptions about market dynamics.
- **Handling Complex Investor Preferences:** By accommodating general utility functions, including those embodying behavioral finance aspects like Prospect Theory, the framework can develop strategies that align more closely with realistic investor preferences and risk attitudes.
- **Addressing the "Curse of Dimensionality":** The use of deep neural networks in the model-free RL algorithm offers a potential pathway to overcome the "curse of dimensionality" that often plagues traditional dynamic programming and PDE-based methods for high-dimensional financial problems.
- **Systematic Exploration for Strategy Discovery:** The entropy regularization mechanism provides a systematic way for financial agents to explore a diverse set of trading rules, potentially leading to the discovery of more robust or higher-performing strategies that a purely greedy approach might overlook. This is particularly valuable in evolving market conditions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays groundwork for future extensions, such as tackling multiple round-trip trades (optimal switching problems), incorporating more complex transaction costs, or integrating market impact models, further advancing the state-of-the-art in quantitative finance.
