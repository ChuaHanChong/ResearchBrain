# Dream to Manipulate: Compositional World Models Empowering Robot Imitation Learning with Imagination

> [!summary] Summary
> DREMA introduces a compositional world model that creates physically grounded digital twins using Gaussian Splatting and physics simulations. This model generates realistic training data through imagination, significantly enhancing robot imitation learning, particularly in low-data regimes, and improves real-world task success and generalization to novel object configurations.

## Problem

- Current robot world models often produce unrealistic behaviors or "hallucinations" when applied to real robotics, failing to provide reliable causal predictions.
- Imitation learning for robotics requires extensive and diverse datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to collect in real-world environments, limiting data efficiency.
- Robots struggle to generalize learned policies to novel or out-of-distribution object configurations and environmental variations, hindering robust deployment.

## Method

- **DREMA** constructs a ==compositional world model== by using ==Gaussian Splatting== for photorealistic 3D scene representation and ==open-vocabulary tracking== to decompose scenes into individual, object-centric Gaussian Splat representations.
- It integrates a ==physics engine== (PyBullet) by converting object Gaussian Splats into mesh grids, allowing for physically consistent simulation of object dynamics and robot interactions.
- The framework generates novel, physically plausible training demonstrations through ==equivariant transformations== of existing data, verifying their validity within the physics-powered world model before using them to train imitation learning agents.

## Results

- **DREMA** achieved an average accuracy improvement of **+9.1%** in single-task simulations and **+13.1%** in multi-task settings over a baseline trained with minimal original demonstrations.
- In real-world robot experiments, DREMA nearly doubled the average task success rate to **62.9%** compared to the baseline's **31.7%** and maintained robust performance in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed that combining various equivariant transformations (roto-translation and object rotation) yielded the best results, achieving up to a **10.4%** improvement in mean accuracy over individual augmentations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining photorealistic 3D scene reconstruction with an explicit physics simulator enables the creation of a "learnable digital twin" that supports grounded and reliable robot imagination.
> - Decomposing the scene into object-centric Gaussian Splat representations is crucial for modeling individual object dynamics and facilitating compositional manipulation tasks.
> - Generating physically verified, equivariant data augmentations can significantly improve the generalization and robustness of robot imitation learning policies, especially when real demonstration data is scarce.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper positions itself at the nexus of several rapidly evolving fields: world models, digital twins, 3D scene reconstruction, physics simulation, and imitation learning in robotics.

**World Models and Digital Twins:** Traditional world models (Ha and Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2019, 2020) aim to provide an agent with a predictive representation of its environment, often statistical and implicit. However, applying these to real-world robotics is challenging due to issues like unrealistic behaviors and "hallucinations" when encountering novel configurations (Yang et al., 2023). This work directly addresses this limitation by proposing DREMA as a "learnable digital twin." This bridges the gap between purely statistical, implicit world models and explicit, physics-constrained digital twins. The concept of a digital twin, a high-fidelity virtual replica of a physical system, is gaining traction in robotics for simulation and testing. DREMA moves towards automatically constructing such twins using learned representations, offering a more grounded form of "imagination" for robots.

**3D Reconstruction and Physics Simulation:** The paper leverages recent breakthroughs in real-time photorealistic 3D rendering, specifically **Gaussian Splatting (GS)** (Kerbl et al., 2023), which has revolutionized novel view synthesis. It intelligently combines this with established **physics simulators** (e.g., PyBullet, Coumans and Bai, 2016). While other works have combined reconstruction with physics (Xie et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2024), DREMA distinguishes itself by emphasizing *compositionality* and *object-centricity*, which are crucial for manipulating individual objects and predicting their physical interactions. This moves beyond scene-level reconstruction to a functional understanding of individual entities within the environment.

**Imitation Learning and Data Augmentation:** Imitation learning (IL) is a cornerstone of robot skill acquisition, where policies are learned from expert demonstrations (James et al., 2022). A major bottleneck is the requirement for vast, diverse datasets, which are expensive and time-consuming to collect in the real world (Mandlekar et al., 2023). Existing data augmentation techniques for IL often involve invariant transformations or recombining existing trajectories (Laskin et al., 2020; Pitis et al., 2022). DREMA introduces a novel approach to *generative data augmentation* by allowing the robot to "imagine" entirely new, yet physically plausible, environmental configurations and corresponding actions. This capability directly addresses the data scarcity problem and enhances generalization, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with few-shot imitation learning.

**Object-Centric Learning and Foundational Models:** The work is deeply embedded in the growing trend of object-centric representation learning (Locatello et al., 2020; Seitzer et al., 2023) and the use of large **foundational models**. DREMA explicitly uses open-vocabulary tracking models like DEVA (Cheng et al., 2023) to achieve zero-shot object segmentation, a clear testament to leveraging the power of pre-trained models for perception in robotic systems. This approach allows for automatic scene decomposition, a significant advantage over methods requiring manual object modeling (Torne et al., 2024).

In essence, DREMA integrates state-of-the-art computer vision (Gaussian Splatting, foundational models) with physics-based simulation to create a powerful, compositional world model. This model then serves as a physically grounded imagination engine to augment limited real-world demonstrations, leading to more robust and generalizable policies for robot imitation learning. This holistic approach represents a significant step towards more autonomous and data-efficient robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind DREMA stems from the limitations of current robot learning paradigms, particularly in real-world applications where data collection is arduous and generalization to novel situations is critical. The authors outline several key objectives:

- **Develop a Grounded, Compositional Manipulation World Model:** The primary goal is to create DREMA, a novel world model that is explicitly compositional, object-centric, and controllable. Unlike existing world models that often rely on implicit statistical representations, DREMA aims to be a "learnable digital twin" that faithfully replicates the observed world and its dynamics.
- **Overcome Unrealistic Behaviors and Hallucinations:** Current world models, when applied to real robotics, frequently generate unrealistic behaviors or "hallucinations" because they struggle to directly and explicitly imitate the actual environment. DREMA seeks to resolve this by integrating photorealistic rendering with realistic physics simulation, enabling reliable causal predictions.
- **Bridge the Gap Between Traditional Digital Twins and World Models:** The paper explicitly aims to combine the benefits of traditional digital twins (explicit, physics-driven, accurate for known dynamics) with learned world models (data-driven, capable of learning from observation). This hybrid approach seeks to leverage the strengths of both.
- **Empower Robots with Imagination:** A central motivation is to enable robots to "imagine" novel configurations of objects and predict the future consequences of their actions. This capability is inspired by human cognition (Hawkins et al., 2017) and is deemed fundamental for robots to interact with new and varied environments beyond direct experience.
- **Reduce Data Requirements for Imitation Learning:** Imitation learning typically demands a large, diverse set of demonstrations for robust policy learning, which is impractical and time-consuming in real-world robotics. DREMA aims to alleviate this burden by generating new, high-quality, and physically reliable training data through imagination from a minimal set of real demonstrations. This directly addresses the "data efficiency" problem in robot learning.
- **Improve Generalization and Robustness:** By augmenting training data with imagined scenarios covering broader action and object distributions, DREMA seeks to create policies that are more generalizable and robust to variations in the environment and task, including out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Facilitate One-Shot Policy Learning:** As a highlight, the authors aim to demonstrate that DREMA can empower a real robot to learn novel physical tasks from as little as a single example per task variation, showcasing significant data efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DREMA's methodology revolves around constructing a compositional world model from visual observations and then leveraging this model for data augmentation in imitation learning. The approach integrates several advanced techniques:

- **Compositional World Model (M) Construction:**
 - **Input:** The system takes an RGB-D video (X) of N frames as input.
 - **3D Scene Representation via Gaussian Splatting (GS):** The core of the visual representation is an inverse graphics function `G = h(X)` using Gaussian Splatting. Each Gaussian `g = (p, r, s, α, c)` represents a point in 3D space with position `p`, orientation `r`, scale `s`, color `c`, and opacity `α`. This allows for photorealistic, real-time rendering from arbitrary viewpoints.
 - **Object-Centric Gaussian Splats:** To achieve compositionality, the scene is decomposed into individual objects. Open-vocabulary tracking models, specifically **DEVA** (Cheng et al., 2023), are used to extract segmentation masks (`Y_k`) for each object `k` across all video frames, prompted by general terms like "object" and "table." These masks `Y_k` are then used to mask video frames (`y_{n,k} ⊙ x_{n,k}`) to obtain object-centric instances. Gaussian Splatting is applied to these masked object instances (`G_k = h(X, X_{depth}, Y_k)`) to learn individual object representations. Depth measurements (`X_{depth}`) are incorporated into the GS optimization as an `ℓ1` regularization term (`L_{depth}`) to ensure geometric accuracy, enhancing the original 2DGS method (Huang et al., 2024).
 - **Modeling Robot-Object Dynamics:**
 - **GS to Mesh Conversion:** Gaussian Splat representations `G_k` are not directly suitable for physics simulations. Therefore, they are converted into object mesh grids (`M_k`) using the 2DGS Gaussian Splatting with TSDF (Truncated Signed Distance Function) to extract high-quality meshes.
 - **Physics Engine Integration:** The dynamics (`D`) of the world are modeled using an external **physics engine, PyBullet** (Coumans and Bai, 2016). The physics engine operates on the mesh grids, predicting changes in object positions and orientations (`μ_k, ρ_k`) based on Newtonian mechanics and forces exerted by the robot.
 - **Physical Parameters:** While ideally inferred, constant physical parameters (e.g., mass, friction) are empirically chosen for simplicity in this work.
 - **Robot Model:** The robot arm itself is also treated as an articulated object. Its known kinematics (from URDF files) are used to represent its parts via Gaussian Splats and model its self-dynamics.
 - **Dynamics Propagation:** Changes computed by the physics engine for meshes (`∆μ_k, ∆ρ_k`) are then applied to the corresponding Gaussians `g ∈ G_k` (updating `p'` and `r'`) to render future states realistically.

- **Imagination for Imitation Learning:**
 - **Equivariant Transformations:** DREMA generates novel demonstrations (`ζ_new`) by applying *equivariant transformations* to a small set of real demonstrations. These transformations are rigid (roto-translations) and apply simultaneously to both the 3D positions of objects (initial and target) and the robot's actions (end-effector poses and gripper states). This ensures that the transformed demonstrations remain semantically consistent and task-solving. Two main types are used:
 1. **Roto-translation of the environment and objects:** Applies a rigid transformation (`R_κ`) to all object assets and the environment Gaussians at `t=0`, and then to each action (`a_i = (et_i, er_i, ga_i)`) in the demonstration.
 2. **Object rotation:** Rotates objects around the final position of the action sequence, similarly adapting the action trajectory.
 - **Verification of Transformed Demonstrations:** A crucial step to maintain data quality. After generating a transformed demonstration, DREMA executes it within the physics-powered world model. It then verifies if the final positions of the objects in the imagined trajectory are close to their expected transformed goal positions (`∥ˆl_i − R_i(l_i − P_κ,i) + P_κ,i∥ < τ`). Invalid demonstrations (e.g., unreachable trajectories, objects falling off) are discarded, preventing negative impact on policy learning.
 - **Policy Training:** The augmented dataset (original + verified imagined demonstrations) is used to train an imitation learning agent. The paper uses **PerAct** (Shridhar et al., 2023), a vision-based multi-task transformer for robotic manipulation, as its baseline.

- **Engineering and Real-World Deployment Considerations:** The paper details several practical engineering challenges and their solutions:
 - **2DGS for Accurate Meshes:** Used 2DGS Gaussian Splatting (Huang et al., 2024) with TSDF to extract high-quality meshes, as standard GS does not guarantee surface alignment.
 - **Camera Calibration and Alignment:** Emphasizes accurate camera calibration (Allegro et al., 2024) and alignment between scene reconstruction and the physics simulator.
 - **Low-Resolution Rendering Issues:** Addressed by rendering at higher resolution and then down-sampling to prevent inaccuracies in RGB-D images used by the robot.
 - **Point Cloud Denoising:** Filtered out noisy Gaussians near edges using Open3D's radius outlier removal (Zhou et al., 2018) to improve point cloud accuracy for PerAct's voxel representation.
 - **Flat Surface Extraction:** Used RANSAC (Fischler and Bolles, 1981) and depth filtering to extract the table surface as a fixed convex hull for the simulator.

This comprehensive methodology allows DREMA to create a robust, interactive, and physically grounded world model that can synthesize realistic and task-relevant training data, significantly enhancing imitation learning performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DREMA, conducted in both simulated (RLBench) and real-world environments, demonstrates significant improvements in imitation learning accuracy and robustness, particularly when data is scarce.

#### 4.1. One-Shot Policy Learning: Single- and Multi-task Simulations

- **Performance with Limited Demonstrations:** When trained with a minimal number of original demonstrations (1-5 examples per task variation), PerAct, trained solely on original data, performed poorly due to insufficient data for generalization.
- **DREMA's Superiority (Imagination Only):** DREMA, leveraging approximately 800 imagination-based demonstrations generated from the minimal original data, significantly outperformed PerAct (original data) in single-task settings, achieving an **average accuracy improvement of +9.1%**. This highlights the power of imagination in creating robust policies.
- **DREMA's Further Enhancement (All Data):** When combining imagined and original demonstrations, DREMA achieved an even greater average success rate of **25.1%** across nine single tasks, compared to PerAct's **16.0%** (original data).
- **Outperformance Against Invariant Augmentations:** DREMA consistently outperformed common invariant data augmentation techniques (random patches, table color changes, distractors) by an **average of 5.0%** in single-task settings, demonstrating that its *generative* and *equivariant* augmentations are more effective.
- **Multi-Task Generalization:** In multi-task settings (close jar, slide block, sort shape simultaneously), DREMA (all data) achieved an average accuracy of **35.5%**, representing a **+13.1% improvement** over PerAct (original data), confirming its benefits even for complex multi-task policies.

#### 4.2. Analysis and Ablations

- **Varying Dataset Size:** As the number of original demonstrations increased (from 4/5 to 20), DREMA continued to provide substantial performance gains. For example, PerAct trained on 20 original demonstrations could match DREMA trained with imagined data from just 5 original demonstrations. However, when DREMA leveraged 20 original examples to generate *more* imagined data, it still improved performance by **6.4% to 10.4%**, indicating that novel configurations remain beneficial even with larger initial datasets.
- **Impact of Equivariant Transformations:** Ablation studies on different transformation types confirmed their importance:
 - "Replay" (replaying original demos) performed poorly, demonstrating that mere repetition is insufficient.
 - "Object Rotation" significantly improved accuracy (e.g., +15.2% for close jar over replay).
 - "Roto-translation" had the greatest impact (e.g., +31.2% for close jar over replay), confirming the importance of altering the environment's global pose.
 - Combining transformations in "DREMA + Original (All data)" yielded the best results, achieving up to a **10.4% improvement** in mean accuracy over individual transformations. This highlights the synergistic effect of diverse, valid data augmentations.

#### 4.3. Experiments with a Real Robot

- **World Model Verification:** DREMA demonstrated good accuracy in modeling real-world demonstrations. By comparing predicted final object positions with actual positions, the average localization error was **0.038 meters** across three tasks (pick block, pick shape, push). This indicates that the learned digital twin provides a sufficiently accurate predictive model of the environment.
- **Real-World Policy Performance:** DREMA's imagined demonstrations dramatically improved real-world robot task performance. On average across five tasks, DREMA (all data) achieved a **62.9% success rate**, nearly double the **31.7%** of PerAct trained on original demonstrations alone.
- **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenarios:** PerAct struggled significantly with OOD object configurations, whereas DREMA maintained robust performance in both in-distribution and OOD environments. For example, for "pick block," DREMA achieved 90% in-distribution and 90% OOD success, while PerAct dropped from 55% to 50%. This is a crucial finding for practical robot deployment.
- **Imagination-Only Policy:** Even training with *only* imagined trajectories (without original real-world data) could outperform PerAct's original training in some cases (e.g., +25% on pick block with more examples), further validating the utility of DREMA's generated data.

In summary, DREMA successfully demonstrates that compositional world models, empowered by Gaussian Splatting and physics simulations, can generate high-quality, physically reliable data. This data significantly boosts the generalization capabilities and robustness of imitation learning agents, effectively enabling few-shot policy learning for real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DREMA framework represents a significant advancement in robot learning, with profound implications across several research and application domains.

- **Paradigm Shift for World Models in Robotics:** DREMA proposes a novel conceptualization of robot world models, moving towards "learnable digital twins" that combine the explicit, grounded nature of traditional digital twins with the learned representations of world models. This hybrid approach promises more reliable and physically consistent imagination capabilities for robots, directly addressing the "hallucination" problem prevalent in purely statistical models. It pushes the field towards more interpretable and controllable simulation environments for AI agents.

- **Accelerated and Data-Efficient Robot Learning:** One of the most critical bottlenecks in real-world robotics is the cost and time associated with collecting large, diverse datasets of demonstrations. DREMA’s ability to generate high-quality, novel demonstrations from a minimal set of real-world examples drastically reduces this dependency. This breakthrough has the potential to democratize robot learning, making it accessible for tasks where extensive data collection is infeasible or too expensive. It paves the way for rapid deployment of new skills on robots with significantly less human effort.

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The capacity to "imagine" and train on a wider distribution of object configurations and actions allows DREMA-powered agents to generalize robustly to novel and out-of-distribution scenarios. This is a long-standing challenge in robotics and a key requirement for reliable deployment in unstructured, dynamic environments. The improved generalization demonstrated on real robots is a crucial step towards adaptable and resilient robotic systems.

- **Foundation for Advanced Robot Autonomy:** A compositional, object-centric, and controllable world model provides a robust foundation for more advanced robotic capabilities. Beyond imitation learning, such a model could empower sophisticated planning algorithms, facilitate symbolic reasoning about object interactions, enable proactive problem-solving, and support human-robot collaboration by allowing robots to better understand and predict changes in their environment.

- **Cross-Domain Influence:**
 - **Computer Graphics and Vision:** The work further validates the utility of Gaussian Splatting for tasks beyond novel view synthesis, demonstrating its power in creating functional 3D assets for physics-based simulation. The integration of foundational models for object segmentation showcases a practical pipeline for leveraging large vision models in downstream robotics tasks.
 - **Reinforcement Learning:** While focused on imitation learning, the concept of a physically grounded imagination engine can readily extend to model-based reinforcement learning, allowing agents to explore and learn in complex virtual environments that closely mirror reality.

- **Future Research Directions:** DREMA opens up several exciting avenues for future research. Addressing current limitations, such as handling deformable and articulated objects (e.g., via Dynamic Gaussian Splatting and automatic URDF estimation), incorporating feedback into world model construction for continuous refinement, and scaling to larger, more diverse environments, will further expand its capabilities. Exploring its integration with more advanced policy learning models (like RVT2 or Diffusion Policy) or Visual Language Models (VLMs) for even richer task variations also holds immense promise.

In conclusion, DREMA offers a compelling framework that moves beyond purely statistical predictions to provide robots with a physically grounded, compositional understanding of their world. By enabling robots to "dream to manipulate" in a realistic digital twin, this work substantially lowers the bar for data acquisition in robot learning, fosters greater generalization, and lays critical groundwork for the next generation of autonomous and intelligent robotic systems.
