# Self-Distillation of Hidden Layers for Self-Supervised Representation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Vector Institute developed Bootleg, a self-supervised learning method that uses hierarchical self-distillation of multiple hidden layers to learn more stable and superior image representations. It significantly outperforms comparable single-view baselines like MAE and I-JEPA, achieving over 10% higher accuracy for smaller models on frozen probe image classification, and demonstrates competitive performance in semantic segmentation.

## Problem

- Generative self-supervised learning methods often produce representations lacking high-level conceptual features, necessitating extensive fine-tuning for downstream tasks.
- Predictive self-supervised methods like I-JEPA suffer from training instability and potential representational collapse due to non-stationary, ungrounded targets from final-layer self-distillation.
- Powerful contrastive self-supervised methods typically demand significant computational resources, specific data augmentations, and large batch sizes, limiting their accessibility and generalizability.

## Method

- **Bootleg** uses a ==hierarchical self-distillation== objective where a student ==Vision Transformer== predicts latent representations from multiple, strategically selected hidden layers of an ==Exponential Moving Average== (EMA) teacher network.
- It concatenates independently ==z-scored embeddings== from these target layers to form a single, high-dimensional prediction target for each masked region.
- The method incorporates an improved ==structured masking strategy== with distinct RNG seeds, independent mask size/aspect ratio sampling, and randomized visible patch truncation to enhance robustness and diversity compared to previous approaches.

## Results

- **Bootleg** achieves superior image embeddings, significantly outperforming **MAE** and **I-JEPA** on frozen probe image classification tasks (e.g., **>10%** accuracy gain for ViT-S models).
- It demonstrates competitive performance on semantic segmentation benchmarks and achieves the highest overall accuracy among non-contrastive methods on VTAB classification at ViT-S.
- Ablation studies confirm that using multiple, equispaced hidden layers as targets and employing improved structured masking are critical for both performance gains and training stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling features from multiple hidden layers across the network's depth simultaneously forces the model to capture a diverse range of features, from low-level details to high-level semantic concepts.
> - Integrating earlier, more "stimulus-driven" hidden layer targets into the objective provides stronger grounding, enhancing training stability and preventing representational collapse often seen in final-layer distillation.
> - The use of a robust structured masking strategy is crucial for stable hidden-self-distillation, as uniform random masking can lead to training instability when targeting deeper layers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of self-supervised learning (SSL), a technique for representation learning that efficiently utilizes large volumes of unlabeled data across various domains. The paper identifies a dichotomy in the current landscape of image SSL methods:

- **Generative approaches**, such as Masked Autoencoders (MAE), reconstruct raw, low-level data (e.g., pixels). These methods provide strong grounding for representations but are often computationally inefficient for modalities with high redundancy like imagery. Their training objective does not inherently prioritize the learning of high-level, conceptual features, often necessitating fine-tuning for effective downstream task performance.
- **Predictive approaches**, exemplified by Image-based Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA), focus on predicting high-level abstract embeddings. While aiming for more abstract features, these methods can encounter training instability due to their reliance on non-stationary targets derived from the final layer of a self-distillation process. This circularity can lead to targets that are less stimulus-driven, potentially causing representational collapse during training.

Beyond these two categories, the paper acknowledges the existence of powerful **contrastive methods** (e.g., DINOv3, Franca). These methods, however, typically require extensive computational resources, multiple views of the data generated via domain-specific augmentations, and large batch sizes for effective training, which limits their accessibility and generality.

Bootleg, the method introduced in this paper, is presented as a bridge between generative and predictive paradigms. It aims to combine the grounding benefits of generative methods with the high-level feature learning of predictive methods, while mitigating their respective drawbacks. Critically, Bootleg operates as a single-view and batch-size-independent method, aligning with the computational accessibility and generalizability advantages observed in MAE and I-JEPA, distinguishing it from resource-intensive contrastive approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a self-supervised representation learning method that generates superior image embeddings by addressing the limitations observed in existing generative and predictive SSL architectures. The motivation for Bootleg stems from a detailed analysis of these limitations:

- **Limitations of Generative Approaches (e.g., MAE):** While MAE effectively learns representations useful in pixel-space, these representations often lack the high-level conceptual features required for many downstream tasks. Consequently, MAE-pretrained models typically necessitate extensive fine-tuning to become proficient, limiting their utility in zero-shot or few-shot scenarios. The underlying generative task does not intrinsically guide the model toward learning abstract, semantic properties of the image.
- **Limitations of Predictive Approaches (e.g., I-JEPA):** I-JEPA aims to predict high-level features through a final-layer self-distillation mechanism, where a student network predicts the embeddings of masked image regions generated by an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher network. However, this reliance on final-layer embeddings as targets creates instability. Because the targets themselves are non-stationary and generated by a version of the network being trained, the training objective can become detached from the raw input stimulus. This circularity can lead to degenerate solutions or training collapse, as the model may converge on self-consistent but ungrounded representations. Previous work has indicated that even minor changes to masking strategies can result in substantial performance degradation for I-JEPA.

To overcome these issues, Bootleg introduces a hierarchical self-distillation objective, motivated by several intuitions:

- **Hierarchical Abstraction:** Neural networks, particularly Vision Transformers (ViTs), process input data through successive layers, progressively increasing the abstraction level of their representations. Early layers tend to capture low-level features (e.g., edges, textures), while deeper layers encode high-level semantic concepts (e.g., objects). The optimal level of abstraction for downstream tasks can vary. By tasking the model with predicting latent representations from *multiple hidden layers* of a teacher network, Bootleg forces the student to capture features across this entire hierarchy simultaneously. This approach avoids the need to pre-commit to a single "best" layer for distillation, which may change during training, and ensures the learning of a diverse set of features.
- **Enhanced Stability and Grounding:** The instability of last-layer self-distillation (as in I-JEPA) is attributed to targets being too far removed from the raw input. Bootleg addresses this by including targets from earlier, less-processed hidden layers. These early hidden-layer embeddings are inherently more "stimulus-driven" or "grounded" in the input data. Integrating these lower-level targets into the overall objective provides a grounding mechanism that helps prevent the training from collapsing or converging to degenerate solutions, thus enhancing stability. This concept is likened to predictive coding models of the brain, where neurons predict afferent representations at various hierarchical levels.
- **Information Bottleneck:** The training objective can be viewed through the lens of information theory. The model is required to compress information from multiple levels of abstraction (e.g., multiple target layers across many masked patches, each with high dimensionality) through a bottleneck formed by the visible patch embeddings and a comparatively narrower predictor network. Increasing the number of target layers effectively increases the "bottleneck ratio," compelling the encoder to learn a more comprehensive understanding of the stimulus to reconstruct the diverse target information. This encourages the model to learn meaningful and efficient representations by reducing redundancy across different levels of abstraction.

In summary, Bootleg aims to leverage the rich, multi-level representational capacity of deep networks by making the prediction of these hierarchical features its central self-supervision task, thereby enhancing both the quality and stability of learned representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Bootleg is a hidden-self-distillation training method built upon a Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture, extending principles from I-JEPA. The system comprises three main components: a teacher-encoder, a student-encoder, and a predictor, all implemented as ViTs. The core objective is for the student network to predict latent representations from multiple hidden layers of its teacher network based on incomplete image information.

**Architectural Components:**

- **Teacher-Encoder:** This network processes the complete, unmasked input image. Its weights are updated as an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student-encoder's weights, providing a stable, albeit evolving, target for self-distillation.
- **Student-Encoder:** This network receives a subset of the image patches (the "seen" or "unmasked" patches). It processes these visible patches, along with global tokens (one class token (CLS) and four register tokens), to generate corresponding embeddings.
- **Predictor:** This component takes the patch and CLS embeddings from the student-encoder. For each of the predefined masked regions, it appends an equal number of mask tokens (placeholders for hidden patches) to the student's output. Frozen sin-cos position embeddings are added to all tokens. Four additional predictor-register tokens are also prepended to the sequence. The predictor then processes these tokens in parallel for each masked region, allowing self-attention interactions within each region but preventing interaction between different masked regions. The predictor module is designed with a similar width and depth to MAE's decoder, but its final linear layer is wider to accommodate the increased dimensionality of the concatenated multi-layer targets.

**Masking Strategy:**

Bootleg employs a masking strategy that is conceptually similar to I-JEPA's but incorporates several technical improvements for enhanced robustness and diversity:
- **Patch Tokenization:** Input images are initially split into a grid of patches, as per the ViT tokenizer.
- **Rectangular Masks:** Four rectangular regions are randomly selected for masking, with randomized size and aspect ratio. These regions define the "hidden" patches whose representations the student must predict.
- **Visible Patches:** The complement of the union of these four masks forms the set of "visible" patches, which serve as input to the student-encoder.
- **Technical Improvements over I-JEPA Masking:**
 - **RNG Seeding:** Bootleg uses distinct random number generator (RNG) seeds for each GPU worker, ensuring greater mask diversity across the distributed training batch, unlike I-JEPA's shared seeding which led to redundancy.
 - **Mask Size and Aspect Ratio Sampling:** A bug in I-JEPA where mask size and aspect ratio were correlated has been fixed, allowing independent sampling. The range of sampled mask sizes has also been refined to better match effective distributions.
 - **Alternating Aspect Ratios:** For Bootleg, the four predictor masks can have alternating aspect ratios (e.g., by swapping height and width), which promotes diversity and reduces extreme overlaps, leading to more consistent mask generation.
 - **Background Mask Correction:** An off-by-one error in I-JEPA's background mask sampling, which limited visible regions to a 13x13 top-left square, is corrected in Bootleg. This ensures the student-encoder can see all image regions.
 - **Randomized Truncation:** I-JEPA's fixed truncation strategy for visible patches (always keeping top-left patches) introduced a bias. Bootleg randomizes the edge from which visible patches are truncated, ensuring a more spatially balanced input to the student-encoder.

**Target Generation:**

- **Multi-Layer Targets:** The primary innovation of Bootleg is its use of multiple hidden layers as targets. The teacher-encoder outputs embeddings for all patches (masked and unmasked) across its depth. For each masked patch location, the embeddings from a selected set of transformer blocks ($l \in L$) within the teacher-encoder are extracted ($\bar{z}_l$).
- **Layer Selection:** The target blocks are strategically chosen to be evenly distributed across the teacher's depth to maximize representational diversity. For example, in a 12-block ViT, blocks 1, 4, 8, and 12 are used. For a 24-block ViT, blocks 1, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, and 24 are chosen.
- **Target Standardization:** Each extracted embedding vector ($\bar{z}_{l,j}$) for a given mask location $j$ and depth $l$ is independently z-scored (mean-subtracted and standard deviation-divided) over its embedding dimension ($D$).
- **Target Concatenation:** The independently z-scored embeddings from all selected target layers for a particular masked patch are then concatenated to form a single, high-dimensional target vector (of length $|L| \times D$).
- **Loss Function:** The training objective uses a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between the predictor's output for the masked regions and the corresponding concatenated, z-scored teacher embeddings. A specialized "no-forward MSE loss" function is implemented to reduce computational overhead associated with a large number of targets, where the forward pass returns a dummy value while the backward pass correctly computes gradients.

This methodology aims to enforce a comprehensive understanding of image content by requiring the student to predict a diverse set of features, ranging from lower-level visual properties to higher-level semantic concepts, across various spatial locations, all while maintaining training stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive set of findings demonstrating the effectiveness of Bootleg across various vision tasks and through extensive ablations.

#### 4.1. Illustrative Experiments and Core Concept Validation

Initial experiments confirm the intuitions behind Bootleg.
- **Target Layer Sensitivity:** When modifying I-JEPA to target different single hidden layers, performance initially improves, peaks at mid-depth layers (e.g., block 2 for ViT-S, block 6 for ViT-B), then declines before recovering at the final layer. This suggests that the "most useful" layer varies. Combining multiple targets (a hidden layer + final layer) consistently outperforms single-layer targets.
- **Stability of Hidden-Self-Distillation:** Applying hidden-self-distillation to MAE-like architectures (which typically target pixels) without modifications led to model collapse when targeting deeper layers (beyond block 4). This instability was alleviated by jointly predicting both pixels and hidden layers, or more significantly, by adopting a structured masking strategy (like I-JEPA's or Bootleg's) instead of MAE's uniform random masking. This highlights the crucial role of masking in stabilizing hidden-self-distillation.

#### 4.2. Downstream Task Performance

Bootleg models were evaluated on image classification and semantic segmentation, typically using frozen encoder probes to assess representation quality, and fine-tuning for overall task performance.

- **Image Classification (ImageNet-1K and iNaturalist-21):**
 - **Frozen Probes (Table 1):** Bootleg significantly outperforms comparable single-view baselines (MAE, CrossMAE, data2vec 2.0, I-JEPA) across all tested ViT model sizes (ViT-S/16, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16) and all probe types (Patch, CLS, X-Blk). The largest performance gains, exceeding 10%, were observed for smaller models like ViT-S/16. On iNaturalist-21, a long-tailed, fine-grained classification task with domain shift, Bootleg consistently maintained its performance lead.
 - **Fine-tuning (Table 14):** Bootleg achieves the best or near-best performance across full fine-tuning on 100% of ImageNet-1K, and especially in low-data regimes (1% low-shot full fine-tuning and 1% low-shot LoRA fine-tuning). For 1% fine-tuning, Bootleg showed substantial improvements (e.g., +12.5% for ViT-S and +8.6% for ViT-B over the next best).
- **Semantic Segmentation (ADE20K and Cityscapes):**
 - **Frozen Probes (Table 1):** Bootleg demonstrates competitive performance in semantic segmentation. It significantly outperforms I-JEPA on pixel-level segmentation tasks (kNN, Linear, Block probes), suggesting that distilling features from multiple points along the visual processing pathway enhances the model's ability to learn representations useful for tasks requiring various levels of granularity.
 - **Fine-tuning (Table 14):** Bootleg leads for ViT-S and ViT-B on ADE20K fine-tuning. For ViT-L, data2vec 2.0 and MAE showed stronger performance.
- **VTAB Classification (Tables 16, 17):** Bootleg achieves the highest overall accuracy among non-contrastive methods at ViT-S. It excels particularly on "Natural" and "Specialized" task categories (e.g., medical, satellite imagery), narrowing the performance gap with larger, contrastive state-of-the-art models (DINOv2, Franca, CAPI), despite being trained on less data or fewer epochs. Performance on "Structured" tasks (requiring geometric/spatial reasoning) was competitive but not always leading, with pixel-reconstruction methods like CrossMAE and data2vec 2.0 sometimes performing better.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Extensive ablations clarified the contributions of different Bootleg components.
- **Choice of Target Layers (Table 2, 19, 20):** Targeting the output of every fourth block within the teacher-encoder (e.g., {1, 4, 8, 12} for a 12-block ViT) consistently yielded strong performance across various model sizes. This "equispaced" strategy often matched or surpassed configurations with denser target sets (e.g., all blocks or residuals) for larger models. Concatenating feature vectors from multiple layers (Bootleg's approach) proved superior to averaging them (data2vec-style). Including the tokenizer's raw output as a target was generally detrimental.
- **Integration with Other Methods (Table 3):** Incorporating Bootleg's hidden-self-distillation mechanism (along with necessary architectural and masking adjustments) into MAE, CrossMAE, data2vec 2.0, and I-JEPA consistently improved their performance, with gains of 6-12% on various probes. This suggests that the principle of hidden-self-distillation is broadly beneficial.
- **Comparison to I-JEPA (Table 4):** The most significant individual improvement from Bootleg over I-JEPA's baseline configuration was the change to multi-block targets (+7.0% X-Blk accuracy, +5.7% Linear mIoU). Other notable contributions included improvements in masking implementation and the addition of a CLS token.
- **Masking Strategy (Table 5):** The choice of masking strategy is critical. MAE's uniform random masking led to training instability and collapse with hidden-self-distillation. Structured masking, such as multi-block (I-JEPA/Bootleg) or cyclic block (Franca/CAPI), was essential for stable training. Cyclic block masking sometimes outperformed the multi-block strategy. Larger contiguous masked regions were found to be generally more effective, as they prevent the model from learning shortcuts by predicting missing patches based on immediately adjacent visible context.
- **Predictor Architecture and Target Grouping (Table 25, 26):** Using a self-attention predictor (MAE-style) generally outperformed a cross-attention predictor (CrossMAE-style) for Bootleg. The strategy of having each of the four mask regions predict *all* target layers simultaneously was more effective than dedicating different target layers to different mask regions.
- **Target Standardization (Table 27):** Independently z-scoring each hidden layer's embedding before concatenation (Bootleg's method) yielded better performance than jointly z-scoring the entire concatenated target vector.

#### 4.4. Representational Analysis (Figures 6-10)

Analysis of learned representations revealed:
- **Inter-layer Similarity:** Adjacent layers exhibited high Pearson correlation, which decayed rapidly with increasing layer distance. CKA (Centered Kernel Alignment) scores were higher, reflecting shared geometric structures even when element-wise correlation was low. The chosen target layers (1, 4, 8, 12) indeed sampled distinct regions of the representation space.
- **Spatial Correlation:** Early layers showed localized spatial correlations that diminished with distance. Deeper layers, especially block 12, exhibited uniformly high spatial correlations, indicating a global, coherent representation. The final LayerNorm output partially restored some spatial structure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Bootleg and its underlying principle of hidden-self-distillation presents several significant contributions to the field of self-supervised learning:

- **Improved Representation Quality:** The work demonstrates that by simultaneously tasking a model to predict latent representations from multiple, hierarchically spaced hidden layers of a teacher network, it is possible to learn superior visual representations. These representations are shown to be more effective for a range of downstream tasks, including image classification and semantic segmentation, often outperforming methods that rely on single-layer prediction or pixel-level reconstruction.
- **Enhanced Training Stability for Predictive Methods:** Bootleg offers a method to mitigate the training instability often associated with final-layer self-distillation in predictive SSL architectures like I-JEPA. By integrating predictions from earlier, more "stimulus-driven" hidden layers, Bootleg provides a stronger grounding for the training objective, preventing representational collapse and leading to more robust learning.
- **Increased Accessibility and Generality of SSL:** Bootleg operates without requiring large batch sizes or explicit cross-sample interactions, which are often prerequisites for powerful contrastive learning methods. This design choice makes it more computationally accessible, allowing for effective pretraining on single consumer-grade GPUs. Furthermore, its independence from domain-specific data augmentations enhances its generality, suggesting potential applicability across diverse modalities beyond imagery, such as time series, audio, or 3D point clouds, with minimal modification.
- **Bridging Methodological Gaps:** The research successfully bridges conceptual gaps between generative SSL (which excels at grounding but can lack high-level features) and predictive SSL (which aims for high-level features but struggles with stability). Bootleg effectively combines aspects of both, offering a pathway to acquire both grounded and abstract features.
- **Insights into Learned Representations:** The representational analyses provide valuable insights into how deep networks learn hierarchical features. The observation that different layers specialize in varying levels of abstraction, and that Bootleg's multi-layer targets effectively probe these distinct representational spaces, informs future research into network interpretability and the design of more effective learning objectives. The critical role of structured masking in facilitating stable hidden-self-distillation also underscores its importance for robust SSL.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The findings regarding optimal target layer selection, the benefits of concatenation over averaging for multi-layer targets, and the impact of masking strategies provide a strong foundation for future advancements in SSL. Future work could explore adaptive masking strategies, more sophisticated ways of combining multi-layer targets, or the application of hidden-self-distillation to multimodal or cross-domain learning.

In conclusion, Bootleg contributes a robust and effective self-supervised learning paradigm that advances the state of the art in representation learning, with implications for improved model performance, accessibility, and broader applicability across various artificial intelligence domains.
