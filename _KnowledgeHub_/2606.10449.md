# GuideWalk: Learning Unified Autonomous Navigation and Locomotion for Humanoid Robots across Versatile Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named GuideWalk enables humanoid robots to unify autonomous navigation and terrain-adaptive locomotion using a two-stage training approach with a composite teacher. The method achieves 99.0% navigation success in obstacle terrains and high terrain traversal success rates (e.g., 96.8% for up stairs) in simulation, alongside robust real-world performance on varied and cluttered environments.

## Problem

- Integrating high-level navigation (obstacle avoidance, path planning) with low-level, dynamically feasible locomotion (balance, stable gait) for humanoid robots in complex, unstructured environments.
- Existing approaches often treat navigation and locomotion as separate problems, leading to suboptimal performance or failures when balancing competing requirements (e.g., collision avoidance must not compromise balance).
- Developing an end-to-end framework that can efficiently coordinate continuous dynamic stability with reactive adaptations necessary for obstacle avoidance on versatile terrains.

## Method

- A two-stage training pipeline is introduced, first using DAgger distillation with a composite teacher, followed by ==reinforcement learning== (RL) refinement with an auxiliary behavior cloning objective.
- The composite teacher integrates a Dynamic Window Approach (DWA)-based navigation module for explicit velocity guidance and a pre-trained, privileged locomotion teacher for terrain-adaptive joint actions.
- A student policy is trained with partial observability using dual Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for elevation maps and depth images, combined with proprioception, to output full-body joint actions.

## Results

- Achieved a **99.0%** Navigation Success Rate and 0.65m Proximity to Obstacles in simulation's obstacle terrains, alongside high Terrain Traversal Success Rates (e.g., **96.8%** for up stairs).
- Demonstrated stable, continuous locomotion and effective obstacle avoidance on a physical Kuavo humanoid robot in real-world scenarios, including seamless circumvention of static and sudden obstacles.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both navigation guidance and the locomotion teacher are crucial for performance, and DAgger distillation significantly reduces training time while RL finetuning is essential for robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit velocity guidance from a local planner is crucial for efficient and stable navigation in cluttered environments, preventing oscillatory or inefficient motions.
> - A terrain-adaptive locomotion teacher is essential for encoding dynamically feasible motion priors, significantly improving stability on challenging terrains.
> - Combining DAgger for strong initialization with RL finetuning enables efficient learning and the emergence of robust, advanced behaviors through environmental interaction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robotics research has made progress in achieving dynamic balance, agility, and complex motion generation. However, deploying these robots in real-world environments, which are often cluttered and feature varied terrains, presents a persistent challenge. This challenge arises from the inherent difficulty in coordinating high-level navigation (obstacle avoidance, path planning) with low-level, dynamically feasible locomotion (maintaining balance and stable gait on uneven ground). These two aspects are tightly coupled, as an obstacle avoidance maneuver must be executed with physically achievable motions, and stable locomotion must account for environmental constraints.

Existing approaches typically fall into categories such as:
- **Humanoid Perceptive Locomotion:** Focuses on robust traversal of complex terrains using onboard perception (e.g., depth cameras, LiDAR) to generate terrain representations like elevation maps. While these methods achieve stable motion, they often prioritize terrain traversal and lack explicit mechanisms for obstacle avoidance in cluttered environments.
- **Learning for Local Navigation:** Traditional methods use decoupled perception-planning-control pipelines, which can be limited in complex terrains. Learning-based approaches either employ hierarchical structures (which may restrict fine-grained perceptual information flow) or end-to-end methods (which often struggle to balance multiple objectives like gait stability and obstacle avoidance).
- **Imitation Learning (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) Integration:** Hybrid IL-RL methods have emerged to address challenges like sparse rewards and high sample complexity in RL, using expert demonstrations to accelerate learning or improve exploration.

GuideWalk positions itself by addressing the gap in unifying terrain-aware locomotion with obstacle-aware navigation within a single, end-to-end framework. It aims to develop a system that can effectively balance continuous dynamic stability required for locomotion with the reactive adaptations necessary for obstacle avoidance, an aspect insufficiently addressed by prior work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenge of enabling humanoid robots to operate autonomously and reliably in complex, unstructured real-world environments. While humanoids can execute impressive dynamic movements, their practical deployment requires them to simultaneously navigate without collisions and maintain dynamic stability across diverse terrains. Current methods often treat navigation and locomotion as separate or loosely coupled problems, leading to suboptimal performance or failures when facing competing requirements. For instance, a collision avoidance maneuver must not compromise the robot's balance, and a stable gait must be adaptable to avoid obstacles. The authors identify this tight coupling and the lack of a unified mechanism to efficiently balance these objectives as a significant open challenge.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of GuideWalk are:

- **Unified End-to-End Framework:** To develop a comprehensive framework that integrates traversability-aware navigation guidance with a terrain-adaptive locomotion teacher, enabling a single policy to handle both high-level navigation and low-level control.
- **Decoupled Planning with Explicit Guidance:** To introduce a navigation module that provides explicit velocity guidance, aiming to decouple obstacle avoidance considerations from specific terrain conditions. This is intended to facilitate more robust planning across varied environments.
- **Composite Teacher Distillation:** To propose a scheme for distilling knowledge from a composite teacher into a student policy. This teacher aggregates both goal-directed commands (from navigation guidance) and dynamically consistent actions (from a locomotion teacher), transferring these complementary supervisions to a single, unified policy.
- **Robustness Enhancement through Refinement:** To refine the distilled policy using reinforcement learning augmented with an auxiliary behavior cloning objective. This two-pronged approach aims to improve the policy's robustness and exploration capabilities while ensuring it retains the desirable behaviors learned from the teacher.
- **Experimental Validation:** To validate the proposed approach through extensive simulation and real-world experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness in achieving stable and efficient navigation alongside stable humanoid locomotion across challenging terrains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GuideWalk is an end-to-end framework designed to learn a unified policy for humanoid navigation and locomotion. It employs a two-stage training pipeline that leverages a composite teacher for initial learning, followed by reinforcement learning refinement.

#### 3.1. System Overview

The framework integrates guidance from a composite teacher with policy optimization via RL. The composite teacher comprises a Dynamic Window Approach (DWA)-based navigation guidance and a pre-trained privileged locomotion teacher. This dual-teacher setup provides coherent supervision for both high-level navigation and low-level control.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Pipeline

**Stage 1: DAgger Distillation**
The first stage focuses on rapid acquisition of navigation and locomotion capabilities through Dataset Aggregation (DAgger).
- **Composite Teacher Formulation (π_t):** This teacher is a hierarchical mapping expressed as π_t(˜o) = π_lt(DWA(˜o), ˜o).
 - **Navigation Guidance Module:**
 - Employs a local planner based on the DWA.
 - Inputs: Robot's current pose in a global coordinate frame, target position, and obstacle observations within the Field of View (FoV) from depth cameras.
 - Operation: Samples candidate linear (v) and angular (ω) velocities. For each (v, ω) pair, it simulates future trajectories using a differential-drive kinematic model.
 - Selection: The optimal velocity pair (v*, ω*) maximizes an objective function G(v, ω), which combines weighted terms for heading alignment to the goal, minimum clearance to obstacles, and preference for higher forward speeds.
 - Feasibility: The selection is constrained by a feasible velocity set V_r, which considers kinematic limits (V_s), dynamic acceleration limits (V_d), and collision-free trajectories (V_a). The differential-drive model is intentionally chosen to align with the locomotion teacher's more robust tracking of forward and yaw velocities.
 - **Locomotion Teacher (π_lt):**
 - Formulated as a parameterized policy π_lt: V × Õ → A, where V is the target velocity command, Õ is a privileged observation space (ground-truth terrain elevation, noise-free proprioception, hidden dynamics), and A is the full-body joint action space.
 - Inputs: Privileged observations (˜o) and the target velocity commands (v*, ω*) from the navigation guidance.
 - Outputs: Optimal joint actions (a*) to track the desired motion.
 - Training: Trained via Reinforcement Learning with Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP), using motion capture (MoCap) data to promote human-like and terrain-adaptive locomotion without extensive hand-crafted rewards.
- **Student Policy (π_s):**
 - Operates with partial observability, using a standard observation space (O) that includes proprioceptive and exteroceptive inputs (depth image, height scan).
 - Process: The student interacts with the environment, collecting visited states (o). For each state, the frozen composite teacher (π_t) is queried to provide the "expert" action (a*). These tuples {o, ˜o, a*} are aggregated into a dataset.
 - Optimization: The student policy (π_s) is trained through supervised learning to imitate the teacher's actions, effectively learning both navigation and locomotion behaviors.

**Stage 2: RL Finetune**
The second stage refines the student policy obtained from DAgger using a joint Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Behavior Cloning (BC) framework.
- **RL Objective (L_PPO):**
 - Maximizes the expected cumulative return (Equation 3), with policy parameters updated using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm (Equation 4).
 - Reward Function: A comprehensive reward function is designed (detailed in Appendix A.2) to encourage goal reaching, collision avoidance, agile locomotion, and appropriate terminal behaviors. Key terms include DWA linear and angular velocity tracking (to connect high-level navigation with low-level control), position tracking, heading tracking, standing posture at goal, agile speed, and penalties for undesired termination, joint limits, feet slide, and stalling.
- **Auxiliary BC Loss (L_BC):**
 - An additional loss term (Equation 5) computes the mean squared error between the student's actions and the composite teacher's actions. This is applied to an online trajectory buffer generated by the current student policy.
- **Combined Objective:** The final objective (Equation 6) is a weighted sum of the PPO loss and the BC loss (L = L_PPO + λ L_BC), where λ (set to 0.2) balances exploration-driven improvement from RL with consistency to the teacher's demonstrated behaviors.

#### 3.3. Perception and Network Architecture

- **Perception:** The system utilizes both elevation maps (from LiDAR) and depth images (from a depth camera). Depth images undergo a noise injection pipeline in simulation to mitigate the sim-to-real gap.
- **Locomotion Teacher Network:** Processes elevation map inputs via a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), which are then concatenated with proprioceptive observations and fed into a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to output joint actions.
- **Student Policy Network:** Employs two independent CNNs for elevation maps and depth images, respectively. Their extracted features are flattened, concatenated with proprioceptive observations, and fed into a shared MLP to produce a 26-dimensional action vector. This dual-CNN structure enables the policy to interpret both ground geometry and nearby obstacle structures.

#### 3.4. Hardware Platform and Deployment

The framework is validated on a Kuavo humanoid robot, a 28-DoF platform. Simulation models explicitly account for joint friction, actuator latency, and rotor inertia to enhance sim-to-real transfer. Onboard computation handles all neural network inference, elevation mapping, and low-level communication via a ROS architecture, operating at a control frequency of 50 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from both simulation and real-world experiments, evaluating GuideWalk's performance in unified navigation and locomotion across diverse terrains.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

Experiments were conducted across various challenging terrains, including stair traversal, slope walking, narrow-beam balancing, and obstacle avoidance in cluttered environments.
- **Overall Performance:** GuideWalk enabled the humanoid robot to exhibit smooth, terrain-adaptive, and obstacle-aware behaviors. It achieved continuous motion on stairs by dynamically adjusting step height and body inclination and demonstrated effective spatial awareness and obstacle avoidance in cluttered environments. The method consistently showed lower joint acceleration and smoother trajectories, indicating more physically coordinated dynamics and navigation behaviors.
- **Quantitative Metrics (Table 1):** GuideWalk achieved a high Navigation Success Rate (NSR) of 99.0% and a relatively short Navigation Traversal Time (T) of 15.51 seconds in obstacle terrains. Its Proximity to Obstacles (PO) was 0.65m, indicating a good safety margin. Terrain Traversal Success Rates (TSR) were high across all evaluated terrains, including Flat (100.0%), Up Stair (96.8%), Down Stair (98.5%), Up Slope (98.8%), Down Slope (96.9%), and Beam (99.8%).

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **GuideWalk w/o Nav. guidance:** Removal of the navigation guidance led to a significant degradation in obstacle terrain navigation (NSR dropped to 69.9%) and reduced PO (0.25m), despite comparable performance on other terrains. Trajectory analysis showed unstable, inefficient motion, characterized by directional oscillations and unnecessary detours (Figure 3), highlighting the role of velocity guidance in providing consistent, goal-aligned commands.
 - **GuideWalk w/o Loco. teacher:** This ablation resulted in substantial degradation of stability across challenging terrains, with TSRs dropping significantly (e.g., Beam TSR 0.0%). The robot exhibited aggressive upper-body motions, leading to frequent collisions. This suggested that without explicit gait priors, the student policy relied solely on sparse navigation rewards, leading to inefficient exploration and suboptimal dynamics. The locomotion teacher was identified as crucial for encoding a terrain-conditioned motion manifold that ensures dynamic feasibility and motion naturalness.
 - **GuideWalk w/o Stage 1 (no DAgger distillation):** While the final performance did not significantly alter, training time was substantially prolonged. A single policy update with RL required 0.24 seconds, compared to 0.08 seconds for DAgger, representing a 66% saving in computation time per update. This indicated that DAgger distillation provided a vital initialization, reducing total training time.
 - **GuideWalk w/o Stage 2 (no RL finetune):** Relying solely on imitation learning (IL) degraded navigation performance significantly (NSR dropped to 18.3%), primarily due to compounding distributional shift. Collisions became frequent because the IL objective did not provide explicit feedback or penalties for collision costs.
 - **Full GuideWalk:** The complete framework demonstrated synergy, where IL provided a strong initialization, and RL refined the policy through environmental interaction. This encouraged exploration anchored by teacher guidance, leading to the discovery of emergent behaviors (e.g., lateral adjustments, arm retraction for collision avoidance), which enhanced both navigation and terrain traversal.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

GuideWalk was deployed on a Kuavo humanoid robot across three scenarios, demonstrating its practical applicability and coordination between locomotion and obstacle avoidance.
- **Obstacle-free:** The robot maintained a stable and natural walking gait, with consistent velocity, confirming the framework's ability to balance navigation objectives with motion stability.
- **Static obstacle:** The robot seamlessly altered its heading to circumvent a static obstacle while maintaining continuous locomotion dynamics, showcasing the effectiveness of explicit velocity guidance in mapping terrain perception to responsive actions.
- **Sudden obstacle:** The robot instantaneously adapted to an abrupt obstruction, demonstrating real-time adjustment without explicit replanning. This highlighted how the end-to-end coupling mitigated instability under unexpected environmental disturbances.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GuideWalk framework represents a contribution to the field of humanoid robotics by providing a unified, end-to-end solution for autonomous navigation and locomotion across diverse and cluttered environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Addressing a Core Challenge:** The work directly tackles the complex problem of integrating high-level navigation planning with low-level dynamic locomotion control for humanoids. By demonstrating that a single policy can learn to manage both obstacle avoidance and dynamically stable gait generation, it moves beyond decoupled or hierarchical approaches that often suffer from limitations in complex scenarios.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The framework's ability to achieve stable and effective navigation on varied terrains (stairs, slopes, narrow beams) and in obstacle-rich environments, both in simulation and real-world deployment, indicates a notable improvement in robot autonomy. The explicit velocity guidance and terrain-adaptive locomotion teacher contribute to this robustness.
- **Efficient Training Paradigm:** The proposed two-stage training pipeline, combining DAgger distillation with RL finetune, offers an efficient learning strategy. DAgger provides a strong initial policy, significantly reducing the overall training time compared to purely RL-based methods. The subsequent RL refinement then allows for further optimization and the emergence of nuanced behaviors not explicitly present in the teacher demonstrations.
- **Improved Humanoid Functionality:** The demonstrated capabilities, such as smooth obstacle circumvention and instantaneous adaptation to sudden obstructions, enhance the practical utility of humanoid robots in dynamic and uncertain environments. This can pave the way for humanoids to perform tasks in human-centric spaces that require both agile movement and intelligent navigation.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies limitations, such as the hardware redundancy from using both elevation maps and depth images, and the policy's struggles with complex navigation scenarios like dead-ends due to local guidance. These points suggest clear avenues for future research, including exploring implicit elevation map reconstruction from depth sequences and integrating global planning algorithms. This indicates the work serves as a foundation for further advancements in humanoid intelligence and autonomy.

In summary, GuideWalk provides a robust and efficient methodology for enabling humanoids to perform complex navigation and locomotion tasks in challenging environments, marking progress toward more general-purpose and autonomous humanoid robots.
