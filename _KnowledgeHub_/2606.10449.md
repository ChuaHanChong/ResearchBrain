# GuideWalk: Learning Unified Autonomous Navigation and Locomotion for Humanoid Robots across Versatile Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named GuideWalk enables humanoid robots to unify autonomous navigation and terrain-adaptive locomotion using a two-stage training approach with a composite teacher. The method achieves 99.0% navigation success in obstacle terrains and high terrain traversal success rates (e.g., 96.8% for up stairs) in simulation, alongside robust real-world performance on varied and cluttered environments.

## Problem

- Integrating high-level navigation (obstacle avoidance, path planning) with low-level, dynamically feasible locomotion (balance, stable gait) for humanoid robots in complex, unstructured environments.
- Existing approaches often treat navigation and locomotion as separate problems, leading to suboptimal performance or failures when balancing competing requirements (e.g., collision avoidance must not compromise balance).
- Developing an end-to-end framework that can efficiently coordinate continuous dynamic stability with reactive adaptations necessary for obstacle avoidance on versatile terrains.

## Method

- A two-stage training pipeline is introduced, first using DAgger distillation with a composite teacher, followed by ==reinforcement learning== (RL) refinement with an auxiliary behavior cloning objective.
- The composite teacher integrates a Dynamic Window Approach (DWA)-based navigation module for explicit velocity guidance and a pre-trained, privileged locomotion teacher for terrain-adaptive joint actions.
- A student policy is trained with partial observability using dual Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for elevation maps and depth images, combined with proprioception, to output full-body joint actions.

## Results

- Achieved a **99.0%** Navigation Success Rate and 0.65m Proximity to Obstacles in simulation's obstacle terrains, alongside high Terrain Traversal Success Rates (e.g., **96.8%** for up stairs).
- Demonstrated stable, continuous locomotion and effective obstacle avoidance on a physical Kuavo humanoid robot in real-world scenarios, including seamless circumvention of static and sudden obstacles.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both navigation guidance and the locomotion teacher are crucial for performance, and DAgger distillation significantly reduces training time while RL finetuning is essential for robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit velocity guidance from a local planner is crucial for efficient and stable navigation in cluttered environments, preventing oscillatory or inefficient motions.
> - A terrain-adaptive locomotion teacher is essential for encoding dynamically feasible motion priors, significantly improving stability on challenging terrains.
> - Combining DAgger for strong initialization with RL finetuning enables efficient learning and the emergence of robust, advanced behaviors through environmental interaction.
