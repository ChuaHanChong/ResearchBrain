# Equivariant Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Northeastern University and Boston Dynamics AI Institute developed Equivariant Diffusion Policy (EquiDiff), a method that integrates equivariant neural networks into diffusion models to improve data efficiency and generalization for 6-DoF visuomotor control in robotic manipulation. This approach achieved up to five times higher sample efficiency and significantly better success rates in both simulated and real-world tasks compared to non-equivariant baselines.

## Problem

- Diffusion-based robotic policies, while powerful for multimodal action generation, require substantial amounts of demonstration data to learn their complex denoising functions.
- Existing robot learning methods often struggle to generalize effectively to unseen variations in task configurations or object poses without extensive data collection and augmentation.
- Applying geometric symmetries as inductive biases to closed-loop visuomotor policies for 6-DoF robotic control, particularly within a diffusion framework, remained a significant challenge.

## Method

- The method integrates SO(2)-equivariant neural networks into the denoising function of a ==Diffusion Policy==, leveraging geometric symmetries as an inductive bias.
- A theoretical framework is established, proving that the noise prediction function in a diffusion model is equivariant if the expert policy it models is also equivariant.
- Detailed SO(2) representations for 6-DoF robotic gripper actions in both absolute and relative pose control modes were derived and implemented using `escnn` for equivariant encoding and decoding.

## Results

- EquiDiff (Voxel) achieved an average **21.9%** higher success rate than the best non-equivariant baseline on the MimicGen benchmark when trained with only 100 demonstrations.
- The approach demonstrated up to five times higher sample efficiency in simulation, with policies trained on 200 demonstrations outperforming baselines trained on 1000 demonstrations.
- In real-world robot experiments, EquiDiff successfully learned 6-DoF manipulation policies for tasks like 'bagel baking' (**80%** success) using only 20-60 demonstrations, while non-equivariant baselines largely failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding geometric symmetries directly into the architecture of diffusion models' denoising function drastically enhances sample efficiency and generalization for robotic manipulation tasks.
> - Voxel inputs, by better preserving true geometric symmetries compared to camera views, contribute to superior performance when combined with equivariant network designs.
> - Inherently equivariant network structures provide more substantial performance gains than relying solely on data augmentation to achieve equivariance in complex control problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the confluence of several active and critical research areas in artificial intelligence and robotics: **Diffusion Models for Robot Learning**, **Equivariant Neural Networks**, and **Closed-Loop Visuomotor Control**.

**Diffusion Models in Robotics:** Recent years have seen a surge in the application of diffusion models, originally popular in generative AI, to robotics. "Diffusion Policy" by Chi et al. established a strong baseline by reformulating behavior cloning as a diffusion process, effectively capturing multimodal action distributions from demonstration data. This approach often surpasses traditional explicit policy learning methods. However, a significant challenge remains: the denoising function in diffusion models is inherently more complex and data-intensive to learn compared to a direct state-to-action policy. This paper builds directly upon this foundation, aiming to address the data inefficiency of existing Diffusion Policy approaches.

**Equivariance in Robotics:** Equivariant neural networks embed prior knowledge about symmetries in the data or task directly into the network architecture. This inductive bias allows models to generalize better from fewer data points by inherently understanding how outputs should transform when inputs are transformed (e.g., rotating an object on a table should result in a rotated action, not a completely new one). The benefits of equivariance – improved sample efficiency and generalization – have been widely demonstrated in various robotic tasks and even in molecular generation. Prior work has explored equivariance in SE(3) (3D position and orientation) open-loop planning or SE(2) (2D planar transformations) closed-loop control. A key gap addressed by this paper is the application of equivariance to a *closed-loop visuomotor policy learning* setting using *6-DoF control*, which involves full SE(3) actions for the robot's end-effector.

**Closed-Loop Visuomotor Control:** This area focuses on training robots to perform tasks by directly mapping visual observations to continuous actions, allowing for real-time adaptation and robustness. Traditional explicit policies often struggle with the multimodal nature of expert demonstrations (e.g., multiple valid ways to grasp an object) and long-horizon tasks. Implicit policies, including those based on energy models or diffusion, offer better ways to capture these distributions but come with their own training complexities, particularly the need for extensive data or negative sampling.

"Equivariant Diffusion Policy" represents a novel integration of these fields. It aims to harness the multimodal action generation capabilities of diffusion models while mitigating their data demands by incorporating geometric symmetries through equivariant neural networks. This work distinguishes itself by providing both a theoretical framework and a practical implementation for SO(2)-equivariance in 6-DoF closed-loop visuomotor control, a more expressive and challenging action space than previously tackled by equivariant diffusion methods in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "Equivariant Diffusion Policy" stems from a critical drawback of existing diffusion-based robotic policies: their high data requirements. While Diffusion Policy has proven effective in capturing complex, multimodal action distributions from demonstration data in behavior cloning, the underlying denoising function it learns is substantially more complex than a direct policy mapping. Learning this complex function necessitates a large volume of data, which is often expensive and time-consuming to collect in real-world robotics.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Improve Sample Efficiency and Generalization:** To significantly reduce the amount of demonstration data required to train effective visuomotor policies using diffusion models, and to enhance their ability to generalize to unseen task variations.
2. **Leverage Domain Symmetries as Inductive Bias:** To embed the inherent geometric symmetries of robotic manipulation tasks (specifically, planar rotations about the gravity axis, SO(2)) directly into the neural network architecture of the diffusion denoising function. This acts as a powerful inductive bias, simplifying the learning problem.
3. **Provide Theoretical Justification for Equivariant Diffusion:** To formally analyze and prove the conditions under which the noise prediction function in a diffusion model becomes equivariant, given an equivariant expert policy. This theoretical grounding provides confidence in the proposed architectural choices.
4. **Demonstrate SO(2)-Equivariance for 6-DoF Control:** To show how SO(2) symmetry can be effectively applied to the full 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) control space for robotic manipulation, detailing the representations for both absolute and relative gripper pose control. This extends prior work that often focused on less expressive action spaces (e.g., SE(2) or open-loop SE(3)).
5. **Validate on Challenging Simulated and Real-World Tasks:** To empirically demonstrate the practical benefits of the proposed method in both data-rich simulation environments (MimicGen) and low-data, real-world scenarios, including complex long-horizon tasks.

In essence, the authors aim to make the powerful, generative capabilities of diffusion models more accessible and practical for real-world robotic applications by making them significantly more data-efficient and robust through the principled integration of geometric equivariance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of "Equivariant Diffusion Policy" lies in integrating equivariant neural networks into the denoising process of a Diffusion Policy. The methodology is structured around theoretical analysis, representation of actions, and architectural implementation.

#### 3.1. Theory of Equivariant Diffusion Policy

The paper first establishes a theoretical foundation. It proposes and proves a key proposition: *the noise prediction function (ε) of a diffusion model is equivariant if the expert policy (π) that it models is SO(2)-equivariant*. This means that if rotating an observation leads to a rotated expert action, then rotating a noisy action along with the observation should lead to a correspondingly rotated predicted noise. This theoretical insight justifies the use of equivariant neural networks to learn the denoising function, as they naturally respect these symmetries.

#### 3.2. SO(2) Representation on 6-DoF Action

A critical step for applying equivariance is defining how the robot's actions transform under the chosen symmetry group, SO(2) (planar rotations around the z-axis). The robot's 6-DoF action space corresponds to SE(3) poses (3D position and 3D orientation) for the gripper. The authors rigorously analyze two control modes:
- **Absolute Pose Control (T_{t+1} = A_t):** The action A_t specifies the next absolute pose. By vectorizing the 4x4 SE(3) transformation matrix by column, they derive that the SO(2) action on the vectorized pose a_t can be described by (ρ₁ ⊕ ρ₀²)⁴(g), where ρ₁ is the irreducible representation for a 2D vector rotation and ρ₀ is the trivial representation for scalars. They also provide a simplified 6D rotation representation for the action.
- **Relative Pose Control (T_{t+1} = A_t T_t):** The action A_t specifies a change in pose relative to the current pose T_t. This involves a more complex group action (conjugation: T_g A_t T_g⁻¹). They derive the corresponding linear action ρ_A(g) on the vectorized action and, crucially, find a change-of-basis matrix P to decompose this action into a block-diagonal form consisting of irreducible SO(2) representations (ρ₆⁰ ⊕ ρ₄¹ ⊕ ρ²). This decomposition allows for practical implementation with equivariant neural networks.

#### 3.3. Implementation of Equivariant Diffusion Policy

The proposed "Equivariant Diffusion Policy" (EquiDiff) employs a modular network architecture, primarily built using the `escnn` library for equivariant operations:
- **Encoding Phase:**
 - **Observation Encoder:** For image inputs, an equivariant ResNet processes the agent-view image, a standard ResNet handles the eye-in-hand image, and an equivariant MLP processes the robot states (gripper pose and open width). For voxel inputs, an 8-layer 3D equivariant convolutional encoder replaces the ResNet. These encoders output equivariant embeddings, typically in the form of a regular representation of a discrete subgroup C_u ⊂ SO(2) (e.g., C₈, representing 8 discrete rotations).
 - **Action Encoder:** The noisy action is encoded using an equivariant linear layer, producing an equivariant action embedding.
- **Denoising Phase:** This is the core of the diffusion process. The equivariant observation and action embeddings are split based on their corresponding group elements (e.g., specific rotation angles). Each pair of partial embeddings is then processed independently by a 1D Temporal U-Net (adopted from), which calculates an equivariant noise embedding. Since the same U-Net is applied uniformly across all group elements, the resulting output remains equivariant in the regular representation.
- **Decoding Phase:** An equivariant linear layer decodes the noise embedding to predict the denoising noise ε_k, which is then used in the DDPM inference process.

The method can operate with both image and voxel inputs, with voxel inputs allowing for better alignment with true SO(2) symmetry by eliminating view-dependent distortions present in agent-view images. Rotation augmentation is also integrated into the voxel version to further leverage equivariance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the Equivariant Diffusion Policy (EquiDiff) was conducted rigorously in both simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating significant improvements over baseline methods, especially in data-limited scenarios.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (MimicGen Benchmark)

- **Superior Performance:** EquiDiff, particularly with voxel input and absolute pose control (EquiDiff (Vo) Abs), achieved the best overall performance across 12 diverse manipulation tasks in the MimicGen benchmark. It outperformed the best baseline (Diffusion Policy with 1D Temporal UNet, DiffPo-C) by an average success rate of **21.9%** when trained with only 100 demonstrations.
- **Exceptional Sample Efficiency:** A striking finding was EquiDiff's ability to learn effective policies with very few demonstrations. With just 200 demos, EquiDiff (Vo) Abs outperformed all baselines trained with 1000 demonstrations, highlighting its strong sample efficiency.
- **Input Modality Impact:** While both image (RGB) and voxel inputs showed benefits from equivariance, voxel input generally yielded better results. The authors attribute this to voxels better preserving true geometric symmetries, whereas camera views can introduce symmetry mismatches.
- **Robustness Across Equivariance Levels:** Tasks were categorized into high, intermediate, and low equivariance based on object initialization randomness. EquiDiff showed the most significant performance gains in high-equivariance tasks, as expected, but also demonstrated robust and improved performance in intermediate and low-equivariance tasks, indicating its generalizability even when symmetries are only partial.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study confirmed that both the equivariant structure and the voxel input contribute to performance improvement. However, the **equivariant structure was identified as the more crucial factor**, leading to a more substantial performance drop when removed compared to removing the voxel input.
- **Equivariance vs. Data Augmentation:** A comparison revealed that using inherently equivariant networks (Equi. Net) significantly outperforms implementing equivariance solely through data augmentation (CNN + Aug) on challenging tasks, although augmentation still provides benefits over a plain CNN.
- **SE(2) vs. SE(3) Action Space:** The experiments further confirmed the necessity of a full 6-DoF (SE(3)) action space for complex manipulation tasks, as an SE(2) variation significantly underperformed in tasks requiring out-of-plane rotations.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiments

- **Low-Data Regime Success:** On a Franka Emika robot arm, EquiDiff demonstrated remarkable success in learning effective policies with a very small number of real-world demonstrations (20 to 60) across six manipulation tasks, including a long-horizon 'bagel baking' task. For instance, it achieved an 80% success rate in bagel baking.
- **Baseline Failure:** In contrast, the non-equivariant Diffusion Policy baseline (trained with voxel input) performed poorly across all six real-world tasks in the same low-data regime, often achieving success rates below 10-30%. This stark difference underscores the practical impact of equivariance for real-world robot learning where data collection is expensive.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** A generalization experiment in the 'Bagel Baking' task showed that the learned policy could zero-shot generalize to *unseen* rotated oven poses. This highlights the inherent ability of equivariant models to generalize across symmetry-related variations without explicit training on those variations. Failures observed were primarily due to the robot's joint limits, not a failure of the policy to generate appropriate actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

In conclusion, "Equivariant Diffusion Policy" represents a significant advance in robot learning. By theoretically grounding and practically demonstrating the power of integrating geometric equivariance into diffusion models, it offers a compelling solution for developing data-efficient, generalizable, and robust visuomotor policies for complex 6-DoF robotic manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world scenarios.

#### 5.1. Key Contributions and Impact

The "Equivariant Diffusion Policy" paper makes significant contributions to the field of robot learning, with profound implications and potential impact:

**1. Bridging Data Efficiency and Generative Power:** The most significant impact is the successful marriage of the generative capabilities of diffusion models (for handling multimodal action distributions) with the data efficiency and generalization benefits of equivariant neural networks. This addresses a critical bottleneck in behavior cloning: the high data cost, especially for complex, implicit policies. By enabling effective learning from few demonstrations, the method makes advanced robot learning more accessible and scalable for real-world deployment.

**2. Theoretical and Practical Advancement in Equivariant Robotics:**
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The theoretical proof that the noise prediction function in diffusion models is equivariant, given an equivariant expert policy, provides a strong mathematical foundation for applying equivariant inductive biases in this context.
- **6-DoF Equivariance Framework:** The detailed analysis and derivation of SO(2) representations for 6-DoF SE(3) gripper actions (both absolute and relative control) is a crucial practical contribution. This extends the applicability of equivariant methods to the full complexity of robot manipulation, moving beyond simpler SE(2) or open-loop SE(3) settings.

**3. Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The empirical results in both simulation and the real world underscore the method's ability to generalize to novel object poses and task configurations. The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen oven poses in a real-world setting is particularly impactful, suggesting that robots equipped with this policy can adapt to variations in their environment without needing extensive re-training or data augmentation. This robustness is vital for deployment in dynamic, unstructured environments.

**4. Real-World Applicability:** The striking success on real-world robotic tasks with minimal demonstrations is a major step forward. This could accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous robots in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and home assistance, where manually programming tasks or collecting vast datasets is impractical. The ability to tackle long-horizon tasks like "bagel baking" further showcases its potential for complex, multi-step operations.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors are transparent about several limitations, which also naturally point to future research avenues:
- **Symmetry Mismatch in Vision Systems:** The presence of the robot arm in the observation or inherent camera noise can break perfect symmetry, even with voxel inputs. Future work should focus on vision systems designed to preserve symmetry (e.g., canonicalizing observations, specialized sensors, or more sophisticated equivariant encoders that explicitly handle occlusions).
- **"Incorrect Equivariance":** As highlighted by prior work, applying a symmetry that conflicts with the underlying task or expert demonstrations can harm performance. Research into dynamically adapting symmetries or learning them could address this.
- **Broader Applicability of 6-DoF Equivariance:** While the theoretical analysis for 6-DoF SO(2)-equivariance is general, its application was primarily demonstrated within the diffusion policy framework. Exploring its benefits in other policy learning paradigms (e.g., equivariant versions of BC RNN) could further validate its utility.
- **Expansion to Other Robotic Tasks:** Extending EquiDiff to other complex robotic domains like navigation, locomotion, and mobile manipulation would be a natural next step, potentially unlocking similar benefits in data efficiency and generalization.
