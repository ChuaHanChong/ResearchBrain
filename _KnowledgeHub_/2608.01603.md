# AffordTrajDP: Dynamic Affordance-Guided Visuomotor Policy Learning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AffordTrajDP, developed by researchers at Nanyang Technological University, introduces a dynamic affordance-guided visuomotor policy that generates time-varying affordance trajectories to provide continuous, state-aware guidance for robotic manipulation. This approach significantly enhances precision and robustness in contact-rich tasks, achieving an average success rate of 70.0% in ManiSkill3 simulations and 66.1% on a Galaxea A1 robot, outperforming existing diffusion policy baselines in tasks requiring fine-grained interaction.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor policies, especially those guided by static affordances, struggle with precision and consistency in contact-rich tasks when objects undergo dynamic pose changes after initial contact.
- Current affordance-guided methods rely on static priors (e.g., fixed 3D contact points) that fail to update with the object's evolving pose, leading to misalignment, trajectory drift, and task failures in precision-critical operations.
- Prior work either tracks overall object pose (losing semantic contact information) or retrieves a semantic contact point (losing temporal evolution), lacking a unified signal that combines 'where to contact' and 'how that contact location should dynamically update'.

## Method

- **AffordTrajDP** constructs dynamic affordance trajectories by propagating a retrieved static anchor contact point through the target object's predicted future SE(3) pose trajectory.
- The framework integrates anchor affordance retrieval using semantic correspondence, a ==trajectory diffusion model (DDIM)== for future object pose prediction, and a mechanism to propagate the anchor through these predicted poses.
- The resulting time-aligned dynamic affordance trajectory, along with other observations, then conditions a ==1D conditional U-Net-based diffusion policy== for closed-loop action generation in a receding-horizon manner.

## Results

- AffordTrajDP achieved an average success rate of **70.0%** in ManiSkill3 simulations, outperforming DP3 (**52.2%**), AffordDP (**57.8%**), and SPOT (**62.1%**), with the largest gains observed in precision-critical tasks like PegInsertion (**46.0%** vs. 23.3-40.0% for baselines).
- In real-world experiments, the method attained an average success rate of **66.1%** on a Galaxea A1 robot, demonstrating superior performance across seen and unseen object instances compared to DP3 (**35.0%**) and other ablations.
- On a UR7e robot, AffordTrajDP achieved an average success rate of **72.5%**, significantly outperforming DP3 (**40.8%**), AffordDP (**55.8%**), and SPOT (**54.2%**), and showed a notable improvement in challenging sub-millimeter precision tasks like USB Insertion (**11/30** successes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Static affordance priors are insufficient for maintaining precision and consistency in robotic manipulation tasks where objects' poses dynamically evolve after initial contact.
> - A unified conditioning signal that combines semantic contact point localization and its dynamic temporal evolution relative to the object is necessary for robust generalization and precision in complex manipulation.
> - A trajectory diffusion model provides effective future object pose predictions, which, when used to propagate an anchor affordance, yields a powerful dynamic conditioning signal that adapts to the object's motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation research increasingly focuses on visuomotor policies that leverage visual observations to perform diverse tasks. A significant challenge in this field is the sensitivity of such policies to environmental perturbations, object appearance variations, and novel scene configurations. This sensitivity often leads to performance degradation when policies are deployed in settings different from their training environments.

To address these limitations, several research directions have emerged:
- **Object-centric approaches:** These methods use structured intermediate representations, such as object poses or 3D point flows, to distil task-relevant object states from dense visual inputs, thereby improving generalization by filtering out irrelevant background information.
- **Diffusion-based imitation learning:** Diffusion Policy (DP) and its successors model action prediction as conditional generative modeling, offering stable training and multimodal expressivity. However, initial DP variants, relying on raw pixel inputs, exhibit poor generalization to out-of-distribution settings. Subsequent work, like 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), improved spatial generalization by conditioning on 3D representations such as point clouds, enhancing robustness to object position changes. Other advancements include SIM(3)-equivariant architectures (Equibot) for geometric generalization, 3D flow methods (e.g., 3D flow diffusion policy) predicting future 3D point trajectories, and approaches building 3D semantic fields from multi-view RGB-D observations (GenDP).
- **SE(3) object pose-based policies:** PRISM-DP conditions policies on current-frame object poses, while SPOT predicts future SE(3) object pose trajectories to decouple actions from raw sensory input, aiming for cross-embodiment generalization.
- **Affordance-guided manipulation:** Affordances, which encode task-specific interaction priors (e.g., contact locations, post-contact motion), are a compelling form of object-centric representation. Earlier works treated affordances as fixed spatial properties. AffordDP extended this by retrieving anchor affordance priors (3D contact points paired with demonstration-replayed pose-contact trajectories) and transferring them via one-shot rigid transformations. However, a critical limitation identified in existing affordance-guided methods is their reliance on static affordances. Once transferred, these static priors remain fixed throughout an episode, failing to adapt to the object's evolving pose after contact. This can lead to misalignment in precision-critical tasks, where small deviations accumulate into trajectory drift and potential failure.

AffordTrajDP integrates and extends these paradigms by addressing the identified gap: the absence of a single conditioning signal that jointly preserves both "where on the object to contact" and "how that contact location should update as the object moves." It builds upon diffusion policies and object-centric affordances by introducing dynamic, temporally consistent affordance trajectories, aiming to provide more robust and precise guidance for robotic manipulation, particularly in tasks requiring high accuracy or involving significant object movement during interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust visuomotor policy for robotic manipulation that can maintain precision and consistency in contact-rich tasks, even under object location perturbations or in scenarios requiring fine-grained post-contact adjustments.

The motivation for this research stems from a critical limitation observed in existing affordance-guided imitation learning methods:
- **Static Affordances are Insufficient:** Current affordance-guided policies typically utilize static affordance priors, such as fixed 3D contact points or pre-defined post-contact trajectories, transferred once at the beginning of a manipulation task. While effective in some scenarios, these static priors become inconsistent when the target object's pose changes dynamically, for example, due to initial placement variations or contact-induced movements during execution.
- **Accumulation of Misalignment:** In precision-critical tasks (e.g., insertion, stacking), static affordances fail to update with the object's evolving pose. This leads to a progressive misalignment between the desired interaction point/trajectory and the actual object state, causing trajectory drift, oscillations, and ultimately task failures, especially after initial contact.
- **Missing Joint Conditioning Signal:** The authors diagnose that existing methods provide only partial information. Approaches like PRISM-DP and SPOT track the object's global pose (current or future trajectory) but discard the semantic information about *where* on the object to make contact. Conversely, methods like AffordDP retrieve a semantic contact point but discard *how* that contact location should dynamically update as the object moves. The critical insight and motivation for AffordTrajDP is the need for a unified signal that combines both semantic contact localization and its temporal evolution relative to the object.
- **Enhancing Robustness and Generalization:** By providing dynamic, state-consistent guidance, the authors aim to improve the robustness of policies to environmental perturbations and enhance generalization across diverse task settings, object instances (seen and unseen), and robot embodiments, particularly in tasks where visual generalization alone is insufficient due to limited demonstration data.

To address these motivations, the proposed AffordTrajDP framework aims to generate dynamic affordance trajectories by propagating an anchor affordance through an object's predicted future SE(3) pose trajectory, thereby offering continuous, state-aware guidance throughout the progressive manipulation process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AffordTrajDP is a dynamic framework designed to construct and utilize affordance trajectories for visuomotor policy learning. The pipeline consists of three main stages: anchor affordance generation, dynamic affordance propagation, and action generation via a diffusion policy.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Object-centric Diffusion Policy and Static Affordance-guided Policy

The foundation is a diffusion-based visuomotor policy that models the conditional action distribution. Unlike raw RGB-image-based policies, AffordTrajDP utilizes a 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) backbone, conditioning on point clouds for robustness to background clutter and camera viewpoint shifts. This provides an object-centric 3D geometric representation.
Existing static affordance-guided policies (e.g., AffordDP) augment DP3 with a prior Φ = (𝑐, 𝜏), where 𝑐 is a static contact point and 𝜏 is a post-contact trajectory. These are retrieved from a memory of demonstrations and transferred to the target object via a single rigid-body transformation at the beginning of an episode. The key limitation identified is that Φ remains fixed, failing to track the object's evolving pose after initial contact.

#### 3.2. Architecture Overview

As illustrated in Figure 2, AffordTrajDP integrates three modules:
1. **Anchor Affordance Retrieval:** Identifies and transfers a static contact point (anchor affordance) from an object memory to the target object using semantic correspondence.
2. **Future Pose Trajectory Generation:** Predicts the target object's future SE(3) pose trajectory based on its current estimated 6D pose history.
3. **Dynamic Affordance Propagation and Action Generation:** Propagates the transferred anchor affordance through the predicted pose trajectory via 6D rigid transformations to yield a dynamic affordance trajectory. This time-aligned trajectory then conditions a diffusion policy for closed-loop action generation.

#### 3.3. Anchor Affordance Generation

The process begins by representing a 3D affordance as Φ = (𝑐, 𝜏), where 𝑐 ∈ R^3 is a static contact point (anchor affordance) and 𝜏 ∈ R^(3×H) is the corresponding dynamic affordance trajectory over a horizon H.
- **Retrieval Mechanism:** Instead of direct regression, the anchor contact point 𝑐 is obtained by retrieval. Given an RGB-D observation of a target object, AffordTrajDP retrieves the most semantically similar source object instance from an "affordance memory." This memory is constructed by:
 - Cropping object regions using GroundedSAM.
 - Extracting appearance embeddings with the CLIP image encoder.
 - Storing task label (ℓ), static affordance (𝑐), appearance feature (𝑧), and RGB-D pose estimate (T ∈ SE(3)) for each entry: M = {(ℓ, 𝑐, 𝑧, T)}.
- **Transfer to Target:** At test time, the nearest neighbor within the same task is retrieved based on cosine similarity of cropped-image features. The source contact point 𝑐 is then transferred to the target object via dense visual correspondence between source and target cropped images. The target pixel matching the source contact location is back-projected using the target RGB-D observation to yield the target contact point 𝑐_tgt ∈ R^3, which serves as the anchor.

#### 3.4. Dynamic Affordance Propagation

This module converts the static anchor affordance into a time-varying constraint.
- **Future Pose Estimation:** A trajectory diffusion model is trained to predict future SE(3) object poses. To enhance cross-instance generalization and normalize scene layout, the source object pose is expressed in the target object frame, T_src_tgt(t) = (T_tgt_W(t))^(-1) T_src_W(t), where T_W denotes world-frame pose.
 - **Data Collection for Pose Trajectories:** Object pose trajectories are extracted from demonstrations. GroundedSAM segments the target object, Any6D reconstructs an object mesh from the initial RGB-D view, and FoundationPose tracks the object's 6D pose over time.
 - **Trajectory Diffusion Model:** A Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model (DDIM) is used to model the conditional pose trajectory distribution. The pose x(t) = [t(t); q(t)] ∈ R^7 (translation and quaternion) is vectorized. DDIM's non-Markovian reverse process provides deterministic sampling for a given conditioning history and initial noise, which is crucial for preventing per-step stochastic perturbations from propagating into the affordance trajectory. This also allows for accurate sampling with fewer denoising steps, reducing inference latency. The model is conditioned on the pose history h_t.
- **Affordance Propagation:** The predicted pose trajectory {T_t+k}_(k=0)^H from the diffusion model serves as the temporal backbone. The anchor contact point 𝑐_tgt is propagated through these predicted poses using rigid transformations:
 - 𝑝_t+k = T_t+k ˜𝑐, where ˜𝑐 = [(𝑐_tgt)^⊤, 1]^⊤ is the homogeneous coordinate of 𝑐_tgt.
 - This yields the dynamic affordance trajectory 𝜏_t:t+H = [𝑝_t. . . , 𝑝_t+H], forming Φ_tgt = (𝑐_tgt, 𝜏_tgt). This dynamic trajectory provides time-varying contact guidance that adapts to the target object's motion.

#### 3.5. Action Generation

The generated dynamic affordance trajectory and other observations condition the final action policy.
- **Feature Combination:** The static affordance 𝑐 is encoded by a lightweight MLP. The dynamic affordance trajectory 𝜏 is processed by a Transformer encoder (with a [CLS] token), whose final embedding provides the trajectory feature. Both are combined with other observations to form the conditioning feature f_t.
- **Policy Architecture:** The action generator is a noise prediction network (𝜖_𝜃) based on a 1D conditional U-Net. It starts from Gaussian noise and iteratively denoises for K steps, conditioned on f_t, to obtain a clean action a_0_t using the DDIM reverse update. The policy predicts a 16-step action sequence but executes only the first 8 actions in a receding-horizon manner at 10 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AffordTrajDP was evaluated extensively in both simulation (ManiSkill3) and real-world settings (Galaxea A1 and UR7e robotic arms).

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (ManiSkill3)

- **Tasks:** Six tabletop manipulation tasks (PickCube, StackCube, PegInsertion, PlugCharger, PokeCube, PushCube) ranging from low- to high-precision contact patterns.
- **Baselines:** DP3 (point-cloud-only), AffordDP (static contact point), and SPOT (predicted future SE(3) object pose trajectory).
- **Performance:** AffordTrajDP achieved an average success rate of 70.0%, outperforming DP3 (52.2%), AffordDP (57.8%), and SPOT (62.1%) (Table 1).
 - The largest performance gains were observed in precision-critical tasks like PegInsertion (46.0% vs. 23.3-40.0% for baselines) and PlugCharger (24.7% vs. 11.3-14.7% for baselines).
 - Significant improvements were also noted in StackCube (92.0% vs. 72.0-84.0%) and PushCube (100.0% vs. 84.7-93.3%).
- **Analysis:** The consistent outperformance is attributed to conditioning on a time-varying geometric prior, which allows the policy to track the evolving spatial relationship between the end-effector and the target object, reducing error accumulation from occlusions, sensor noise, and perceptual ambiguity.

#### 4.2. Pose Prediction Accuracy

- **Method:** The pose trajectory diffusion model was evaluated on held-out demonstrations, measuring translation and rotation errors over a 4-step horizon (H=4) under ground-truth and FoundationPose-tracked history.
- **Results:**
 - Under ground-truth history, error increased roughly linearly with horizon length, from 0.59 mm / 0.08° at k=1 to 1.89 mm / 0.25° at k=4.
 - Under FoundationPose-estimated history, errors were consistently higher (e.g., 3.77 mm at k=4), indicating propagation of tracking noise. Rotation error plateaued around 1.3° after k=1.
 - Per-episode-step analysis (StackCube, Figure 6) showed that prediction error at each horizon step remained relatively flat throughout a rollout, with a transient rotation-error spike around the contact event (t ≈ 45) that decayed quickly. It did not compound into growing drift over the episode.
 - FoundationPose's own tracking error was comparable to or larger than the trajectory model's prediction error after contact, suggesting upstream tracking noise as a significant contributor to post-contact pose uncertainty.

#### 4.3. Real-world Results

- **Galaxea A1:** Evaluated on StackCube, PickCup, and AdapterInsertion tasks with both seen and unseen object instances. A single unified policy per task was trained on 30 teleoperated demonstrations.
 - **Ablation Study (Table 2):**
 - **DP3 (Baseline):** Average success rate (Avg. SR) of 35.0%.
 - **DP3+Center Point:** Augmenting DP3 with the object center (non-semantic cue) yielded an Avg. SR of 38.9%.
 - **DP3+Contact Point:** Augmenting DP3 with a semantic contact point improved Avg. SR to 47.2%, outperforming DP3+Center Point on most conditions, especially unseen instances (e.g., AdapterInsertion unseen: 3/30 vs. 1/30; PickCup unseen: 17/30 vs. 12/30), confirming the value of semantic localization.
 - **DP3+Trajectory:** Augmenting DP3 with the pose trajectory alone (without semantic anchor) yielded an Avg. SR of 40.6%. This variant degraded performance below plain DP3 on StackCube unseen instances (6/30 vs. 12/30), indicating that trajectory conditioning is only beneficial when the anchor is semantically grounded, otherwise it can introduce noise.
 - **Ours (Full Method):** Achieved an Avg. SR of 66.1%. It demonstrated the highest success rates across all tasks and instance types. The improvement was most pronounced on the precision-critical AdapterInsertion task (12/30 seen, 6/30 unseen vs. 3/30 seen, 0/30 unseen for DP3), where static priors often drift post-contact. This confirms that both semantic contact retrieval and pose trajectory propagation are necessary and complement each other for robust, generalized performance.
- **UR7e:** Evaluated on PickCup, Ring-on-Peg, Put-in-Bowl, and USB Insertion (Table 3).
 - **Performance:** AffordTrajDP achieved an Avg. SR of 72.5%, significantly outperforming DP3 (40.8%), AffordDP (55.8%), and SPOT (54.2%).
 - Strong performance was observed on PickCup (29/30), Ring-on-Peg (20/30), and Put-in-Bowl (27/30).
 - USB Insertion, a sub-millimeter precision task, remained challenging but still showed a notable improvement for AffordTrajDP (11/30 vs. 1-3/30 for baselines), indicating the approach pushes the boundaries of current precision.
- **Generalization:** The real-world experiments confirmed that dynamic affordance trajectories generalize across different robot arms, camera setups, and task geometries, and are robust to object placement variations and appearance changes, including unseen instances.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of AffordTrajDP lies in its contribution to addressing a critical limitation in current robotic manipulation policies, particularly those relying on affordance-guided imitation learning. The proposed framework introduces a novel approach to provide dynamic, state-consistent guidance, thereby enhancing precision and robustness.

Key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

- **Novel Problem Formulation:** The work formally identifies and addresses the limitation of static affordance priors, which degrade alignment with moving objects in precision-critical tasks. By proposing to construct a state-consistent, time-varying affordance trajectory as the policy conditioning signal, AffordTrajDP introduces a new paradigm for maintaining interaction consistency throughout complex manipulation sequences.
- **Necessity and Sufficiency of Joint Conditioning:** Through controlled ablation studies, the research demonstrates that combining semantic contact retrieval ("where to contact") with pose-trajectory prediction ("how that contact should evolve") is both necessary and sufficient for achieving robust generalization, especially to unseen object instances and in precision-demanding tasks. This finding clarifies the complementary roles of these two information streams, which have previously been studied in isolation.
- **Enhanced Performance in Precision-Critical Tasks:** The empirical validation in both simulation (ManiSkill3) and real-world environments (Galaxea A1, UR7e) consistently shows AffordTrajDP outperforming strong baselines. The most substantial gains are observed in tasks requiring high precision and continuous interaction (e.g., PegInsertion, PlugCharger, AdapterInsertion, USB Insertion), where static priors are prone to drift. This suggests a direct impact on the reliability and capability of robots to perform complex assembly or alignment tasks.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** By leveraging object-centric representations and dynamic affordances, the method demonstrates superior robustness to object placement variations and appearance changes, successfully generalizing to unseen object instances. This is particularly valuable in scenarios with limited demonstration data, where purely visual generalization may fail to bridge appearance gaps.
- **Towards More Adaptive Manipulation:** The ability of AffordTrajDP to provide time-varying guidance that adapts to the object's motion represents a step towards more adaptive and intelligent robotic manipulation. This can reduce the reliance on perfect initial states and enable robots to react to unforeseen movements or minor errors during execution.

Future potential impacts include extending the framework with richer contact semantics (e.g., orientation, force application), integrating uncertainty-aware pose prediction to handle tracking inaccuracies more explicitly, and incorporating tactile or force feedback for enhanced robustness in very high-precision or deformable object manipulation tasks. This research lays a foundation for developing robotic systems that can perform complex, interactive tasks with higher reliability and adaptability in unstructured environments.
