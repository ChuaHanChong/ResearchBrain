# RoboBrain 2.5: Depth in Sight, Time in Mind

> [!summary] Summary
> The BAAI RoboBrain Team developed RoboBrain 2.5, an embodied AI foundation model enhancing robotic reliability through precise 3D spatial reasoning and dense temporal value estimation. This iteration achieves significant improvements in generating collision-free 3D manipulation traces (44% success) and providing robust bidirectional temporal progress feedback across diverse tasks.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI foundation models struggle to bridge the reliability gap between high-level semantic reasoning and precise, physically grounded manipulation in real-world environments.
- Current models suffer from 'metric blindness' in spatial reasoning, lacking absolute depth and scale information, which prevents millimeter-level precision and collision-free 3D trajectory generation.
- Action generation often relies on open-loop prediction, lacking continuous execution monitoring and making it impossible for agents to detect intermediate failures or adaptively recover in long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- The model integrates precise 3D spatial reasoning by predicting 3D coordinates using a decoupled (u, v, d) representation (image-plane coordinates and absolute depth) and generating sequences of collision-free 3D keypoints.
- A dense temporal value estimation mechanism provides continuous, step-aware progress assessments using 'hop labels' and fuses multi-perspective predictions with bi-directional consistency checking.
- The system is trained on an extensive 12.4 million sample multi-modal dataset using a progressive dual-phase strategy and a robust cross-accelerator infrastructure, including non-NVIDIA GPUs.

## Results

- RoboBrain 2.5 achieved an average score of 75.82 on 2D spatial reasoning benchmarks, outperforming general baselines (e.g., Gemini-3-Pro-Preview at 66.14) and embodied baselines.
- The model demonstrated **44%** overall success in generating collision-free 3D manipulation traces on the TraceSpatial benchmark, a substantial improvement over leading generalist VLMs (e.g., Gemini-3-Pro-Preview at **7%** success).
- It exhibited high and balanced VOC+/VOC- scores across diverse datasets (e.g., 98.97 / 98.94 on LIBERO) for temporal value estimation, indicating robust bidirectional temporal understanding, unlike generalist VLMs with significantly lower VOC- scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling 3D coordinate prediction into (u, v, d) simplifies learning 3D geometry from monocular inputs and facilitates versatile 3D task applications.
> - Hop-based relative progress normalization provides a stable and robust supervision signal for temporal understanding by ensuring bounded global progress and addressing cumulative errors.
> - Fusing multi-perspective temporal predictions (incremental, forward-anchored, backward-anchored) with bi-directional consistency checking enhances the reliability of progress estimation for self-correction and high-fidelity reinforcement learning rewards.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

RoboBrain 2.5 emerges in a research landscape where embodied AI foundation models are rapidly evolving, aiming to integrate language, vision, and action capabilities for robotic agents. The current state-of-the-art often demonstrates remarkable success in curated, controlled environments, but a critical "reliability gap" persists in real-world deployments. This gap is primarily due to the difficulty in translating high-level semantic reasoning into precise, physically grounded manipulation, which demands strict adherence to metric constraints, robustness to occlusions and viewpoint shifts, and continuous self-correction.

The paper identifies two fundamental limitations in existing generalist models that hinder real-world reliability:
1. **Metric Blindness in Spatial Reasoning**: Current models typically rely on 2D pixel coordinates or weak topological representations for grounding. This lack of absolute depth and scale information makes it impossible to guarantee millimeter-level precision or generate collision-free 3D trajectories, crucial for complex interactions.
2. **Open-Loop Prediction in Temporal Modeling**: Action generation is often treated as a static sequence prediction task, with sparse external supervision (e.g., success labels). This leaves agents unable to monitor execution progress, detect intermediate failures (like slippage or regression), or adaptively recover in long-horizon tasks.

RoboBrain 2.5 directly addresses these limitations, proposing a paradigm shift from purely semantic reasoners to physically-grounded agents. It positions itself as a "next-generation embodied AI foundation model" that aims to bridge this reliability gap by integrating "Depth in Sight" (Precise 3D Spatial Reasoning) and "Time in Mind" (Dense Temporal Value Estimation). This work builds upon the general perception and reasoning capabilities of its predecessor, RoboBrain 2.0, while introducing crucial upgrades for physically realistic interaction.

By explicitly tackling 3D metric-aware spatial reasoning and dense, continuous temporal progress monitoring, RoboBrain 2.5 pushes the frontier of generalist robots. It directly competes with and significantly outperforms leading general-purpose multimodal LLMs (like Gemini-3-Pro-Preview, GPT-5.2, and Qwen3-VL-8B-Inst.) and other embodied AI baselines (RoboBrain 2.0, Mimo-Embodied) on tasks requiring these specific capabilities. This research is a crucial step towards developing truly autonomous and reliable robots capable of fine-grained manipulation in unstructured, dynamic real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of RoboBrain 2.5 is to **bridge the critical reliability gap** between high-level semantic reasoning and low-level physical interaction in embodied AI systems. The authors explicitly aim for a paradigm shift, transforming embodied AI foundation models from mere "semantic reasoners" into robust "physically-grounded agents."

To achieve this, the paper sets forth two core objectives:

1. **Precise 3D Spatial Reasoning ("Depth in Sight")**:
 - **Objective**: To enable the model to understand and operate within the absolute metric constraints of the 3D physical world. This means moving beyond qualitative 2D pixel-relative grounding to quantitative, depth-aware coordinate prediction.
 - **Motivation**: Real-world manipulation tasks demand millimeter-level precision and collision avoidance. Existing models, with their "metric blindness," cannot guarantee physical compliance, making them prone to failure in tasks requiring fine-grained interaction. The goal is to generate complete 3D manipulation traces as ordered keypoint sequences, inherently encoding spatial planning and respecting physical constraints.

2. **Dense Temporal Value Estimation ("Time in Mind")**:
 - **Objective**: To equip the model with an intrinsic mechanism for continuous, step-aware monitoring of execution progress and understanding of the agent's execution state (progress, stagnation, regression, or error).
 - **Motivation**: Current "open-loop" models lack an internal feedback loop, relying on sparse external success signals. This makes adaptive recovery impossible when intermediate failures occur. Dense temporal feedback is crucial for robust closed-loop control, enabling self-correction and serving as a high-fidelity reward signal for downstream reinforcement learning.

In essence, RoboBrain 2.5 is motivated by the need for embodied intelligence that is not only intelligent in understanding instructions but also reliable and adaptable in executing them in the complex, unforgiving physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboBrain 2.5 builds upon the foundation of RoboBrain 2.0 and leverages the Qwen3-VL architecture, indicating a large-scale multimodal vision-language model (VLM) at its core. Its methodology focuses on two critical enhancements, supported by extensive data curation and a sophisticated training strategy and infrastructure.

#### 3.1. New Features and Technical Approach

1. **Precise 3D Spatial Reasoning**:
 - **Capabilities**: The model is endowed with three complementary skills:
 - **3D Spatial Referring**: Accurately localizing objects and resolving spatial relationships (e.g., "third picture frame from the left").
 - **3D Spatial Measuring**: Estimating absolute, real-world metric quantities (e.g., "1-5 cm above each").
 - **3D Spatial Trace Generation**: Producing a sequence of collision-free 3D keypoints (τ = {p_t}) that describe the complete manipulation procedure.
 - **Task Formulation**: Instead of directly predicting (x, y, z) coordinates in a camera or world frame, the model predicts a decoupled **(u, v, d)** representation—image-plane coordinates (u, v) and absolute depth (d). This is advantageous because it can be trivially projected to 3D using known camera intrinsics (often available in embodied scenarios), simplifying the model's learning task by avoiding implicit camera geometry inference. This formulation also allows for data reusability by easily projecting to 2D traces or specific 3D points.

2. **Dense Temporal Value Estimation**:
 - **Concept**: A vision-based mechanism providing real-time, step-aware progress assessments as temporal value feedback.
 - **Hop-wise Progress Construction**:
 - **Discretization**: Raw multi-view video trajectories are segmented into sub-tasks using human-annotated keyframes. Adaptive sampling within these segments yields dense state sequences, with global progress Φ(s_i) = i/M.
 - **Relative Progress Normalization**: A "hop label" H(s_p, s_q) is introduced, which normalizes the progress from state s_p to s_q relative to the *remaining* distance to the goal (for progress) or the *already covered* distance from the initial state (for regression). This dynamic scaling ensures progress values remain strictly within [−1, 1] and guarantees global progress Φ*(s) stays within when iteratively applied, addressing accumulation errors.
 - **Sampling Strategy**: A balanced set of hop-based training samples is constructed, discretizing hop values and temporal distances, and including "zero-hop" samples to reduce bias towards static segments.
 - **Multi-Perspective Progress Fusion**: To enhance robustness and mitigate error accumulation, three complementary value estimates are fused:
 - **Incremental Prediction**: Recurses on the preceding state's progress and the current hop.
 - **Forward-Anchored Prediction**: Anchored to the initial state (progress = 0).
 - **Backward-Anchored Prediction**: Anchored to the goal state (progress = 1), offering sensitivity near completion.
 The final progress estimate is an average of these three.
 - **Bi-directional Consistency Checking**: To prevent "reward hacking" from OOD (Out-of-Distribution) hallucination during RL, a consistency-aware weighting mechanism is proposed. Discrepancy between forward and backward predictions (∆_norm) is used to derive a confidence weight (w_t), which then conservatively updates the progress state. This acts as a semantic filter, ignoring uncertain updates.

#### 3.2. Training Data

RoboBrain 2.5 is trained on an exceptionally diverse and extensive dataset of approximately 12.4 million high-quality samples, categorized into three core domains:
- **General MLLM Data (2.83M samples)**: Sourced from Honey-Data-1M and LLaVA-Onevision-1.5-Instruct-Data, refined for concise instruction following and balanced class distribution.
- **Spatial Reasoning Data (Multi-source)**: Includes Visual Grounding (LVIS), Object Pointing (Pixmo-Points, RoboPoint), Affordance (PACO-LVIS, RoboPoint), Spatial Understanding (OpenImage, MMScan, 3RScan, ScanQA, SQA3D, SpaceR, CA-1M), Spatial Referring (RefSpatial), and critically, **3D Spatial Reasoning Data (1.74M samples)**. The 3D data, following the TraceSpatial pipeline, integrates 3D scanning datasets (CA-1M, ScanNet) for metric-grounded spatial reasoning and manipulation videos (AgiBot-Beta, DROID, RoboTwin 2.0) for physically plausible manipulation traces.
- **Temporal Prediction Data (Multi-source)**: Covers Ego-View Planning (EgoPlan-IT), ShareRobot Planning, AGIbot Planning, Multi-Robot Planning (RoboOS), Close-Loop Interaction (AI2Thor, Embodied-Reasoner), and the **Dense Value Estimation Data (3.5M samples)**. This last part, following the Dopamine-Reward pipeline, aggregates samples from real-world robots (AGIBot-World, DROID, RoboBrain-X), simulation (LIBERO, RoboCasa, RoboTwin), and human-centric data (EgoDex), ensuring embodiment-invariant progress feedback.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

A progressive dual-phase training strategy is employed:
1. **Stage 1: Foundational Spatiotemporal Learning**: The full model is trained on 8.3 million samples including General MLLM, 2D spatial reasoning, qualitative 3D understanding (text-based QA), and planning/temporal logic (pairwise keyframe comparisons). This stage establishes broad visual semantics and open-loop planning.
2. **Stage 2: Specific Spatiotemporal Enhancement**: Fine-tunes the model on 4.1 million samples, focusing on the newly introduced Metric 3D Spatial Reasoning (point/trajectory generation) and Dense Value Estimation (explicit hop prediction). An anti-forgetting strategy involves replaying 15% of Stage-1 data.

#### 3.4. Infrastructure

The training leverages a robust, multi-dimensional hybrid parallelism strategy built upon RoboBrain 2.0's infrastructure, optimized with:
- **Megatron–Energon** framework for unified data representation and online mixed training of heterogeneous modalities.
- **Uneven pipeline parallelism (FlagScale)** to balance computational load across heterogeneous visual (ViT) and language (LLM) components.
- **Dynamic pre-allocated memory** to prevent GPU memory fragmentation with varying sequence lengths, improving throughput.
- **Cross-accelerator training and inference** capabilities, successfully training on a thousand-device cluster of non-NVIDIA (Moore-Threads) accelerators, demonstrating robust loss convergence and performance matching NVIDIA platforms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboBrain 2.5 demonstrates significant advancements across all evaluated capabilities, consistently outperforming both generalist VLMs and previous embodied AI models. A key aspect of the evaluation is the comparison between RoboBrain 2.5 variants trained on NVIDIA (NV) and Moore-Threads (MTT) GPUs, confirming the robustness of their cross-accelerator training infrastructure.

#### 4.1. 2D Spatial Reasoning Capability (Table 2)

- RoboBrain 2.5 (NV and MTT) achieves a strong average score of **75.82** across five benchmarks (CV-Bench, CrossPoint, RoboSpatial, RefSpatial, EmbSpatial).
- It significantly surpasses general baselines (Gemini-3-Pro-Preview: 66.14, GPT-5.2: 49.33, Qwen3-VL-8B-Inst.: 64.18) and embodied baselines (RoboBrain-2.0: 54.96, Mimo-Embodied: 58.97).
- **Notable improvements**: Achieves 76.30 (MTT) on **CrossPoint** (cross-view point correspondence), vastly outperforming Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (38.60). On **RoboSpatial** (robotics-oriented spatial reasoning), it achieves 73.03 (NV), surpassing all baselines. This indicates enhanced ability to transition from coarse judgment to precise, coordinate-level correspondence and robot-centric perception.

#### 4.2. 3D Spatial Reasoning Capability (Table 3)

- RoboBrain 2.5 excels in metrics that stress metric-grounded and trajectory-aware understanding.
- On **MSMU** (quantitative 3D spatial measuring), it achieves 64.17 (NV), outperforming Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (59.44) and other baselines.
- **TraceSpatial**: This benchmark, critical for 3D spatial tracing, evaluates 3D Start, 3D End, and overall Success (collision-free trajectory). RoboBrain 2.5 achieves remarkable success rates of **83% for 3D Start, 63% for 3D End, and 44% for overall Success** (NV variant). This is a substantial leap compared to Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (19% Start, 25% End, 7% Success) and Qwen3-VL-8B-Inst. (30% Start, 20% End, 6% Success). This is a key validation of the model's ability to generate accurate, collision-free 3D manipulation traces.
- It sets a new SOTA on **VABench-V** (visual trace generation RMSE) with an error of 0.1189 (MTT) and **ShareRobot-T** (robot-centric spatial grounding RMSE) with 0.1164 (NV), demonstrating accurate fine-grained waypoint generation and precise interaction-relevant spatial outputs.

#### 4.3. Temporal Value Estimation (Table 4)

- Evaluated using VOC+ (forward progress) and VOC- (reverse progress), where both should be high for robust temporal understanding.
- RoboBrain 2.5 consistently achieves very high and **balanced** VOC+/VOC- scores across all six diverse datasets (AgiBot, DROID, Galaxea, EgoDex, LIBERO, RoboCasa). For instance, on LIBERO, it reaches 98.97 / 98.94 (NV), indicating near-perfect bidirectional temporal consistency.
- In stark contrast, generalist VLMs (e.g., GPT-5.2, Gemini-3-Pro-Preview) show high VOC+ but significantly **lower VOC-** scores (e.g., GPT-5.2 on AgiBot: 90.02 / **15.91**), indicating they lack robust bidirectional temporal understanding and struggle when videos are time-reversed. This highlights the effectiveness of RoboBrain 2.5's dense, hop-based, and bi-directionally consistent temporal supervision.
- Qualitative examples (Appendix A.2) further demonstrate consistent progress predictions across diverse tasks, robustness to varying temporal sampling intervals, and successful recovery from artificial disturbances in real-world RL rollouts, confirming its ability to provide reliable, high-fidelity reward signals.

#### 4.4. Cross-Accelerator Robustness

The consistent performance between RoboBrain 2.5 variants trained on NVIDIA and Moore-Threads GPUs (e.g., loss convergence within 0.62%, similar benchmark scores) is a significant finding. It validates the robustness and maturity of BAAI's FlagScale/FlagOS infrastructure for large-scale, multi-modal model training across heterogeneous hardware, which is crucial for wider adoption and democratizing advanced AI research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboBrain 2.5 represents a significant advancement in embodied AI, directly addressing critical limitations that hinder the deployment of intelligent robots in real-world, unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Real-World Reliability**: By introducing "Precise 3D Spatial Reasoning" and "Dense Temporal Value Estimation," RoboBrain 2.5 enables robots to transition from executing curated demonstrations to robustly performing complex, fine-grained manipulation tasks in the physical world. This bridges the crucial gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical control, greatly enhancing the reliability and safety of embodied agents.
2. **Foundation for Generalist Robots**: The model transforms existing semantic planners into truly "physically-grounded agents." "Depth in Sight" provides the kinematic feasibility and collision-free trajectories required for precise interaction, while "Time in Mind" ensures execution robustness through continuous self-correction and adaptive recovery. This holistic approach makes RoboBrain 2.5 a more capable foundation model for general-purpose robots.
3. **High-Fidelity Reward Signals for RL**: The dense temporal value estimation acts as a robust, step-aware reward function, significantly improving the efficiency and stability of downstream reinforcement learning. This capability allows robots to learn faster, recover from unforeseen errors, and adapt to dynamic environments, which is vital for long-horizon and contact-rich tasks.
4. **Novel Technical Contributions**: The decoupled (u, v, d) representation for 3D spatial tracing simplifies the learning of 3D geometry from monocular images. Furthermore, the hop-based relative progress normalization strategy, with its theoretical guarantee of bounded global progress, provides a robust and stable supervision signal for temporal understanding, outperforming methods that are susceptible to cumulative errors.
5. **Scalable and Hardware-Agnostic Infrastructure**: The successful demonstration of training and inference across different accelerator platforms (NVIDIA and Moore-Threads) validates BAAI's sophisticated distributed training infrastructure. This contributes to the broader AI ecosystem by demonstrating that cutting-edge foundation models can be developed and deployed beyond proprietary hardware, fostering greater accessibility and innovation.
6. **Ambitious Future Trajectory**: The outlined future work showcases a comprehensive vision for the RoboBrain series:
 - **Unified Generation and Understanding**: Evolving into an embodied world model capable of simulating action outcomes will dramatically improve planning safety and robustness.
 - **Broad Deployment**: Extensive validation on mobile manipulators and humanoids will test and extend the model's generalization capabilities to diverse form factors and complex scenarios.
 - **Scalable Model Family**: Developing specialized variants (e.g., lightweight for edge, "Instruction" vs. "Thinking") promises to optimize for various computational constraints and real-world application requirements.
 - **Self-Evolving Data Engine**: Utilizing RoboBrain 2.5 itself as a verifier to filter and annotate large-scale uncurated videos is a visionary step towards continuous self-improvement and autonomous data generation, creating a powerful flywheel effect for ongoing capability enhancement.

In conclusion, RoboBrain 2.5 marks a significant stride towards creating truly intelligent and reliable embodied AI. By meticulously addressing the challenges of physical grounding and continuous feedback, it paves the way for a future where robots can operate with unprecedented precision and adaptability in our complex world.
