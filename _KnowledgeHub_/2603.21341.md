# RoboAlign: Learning Test-Time Reasoning for Language-Action Alignment in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> KAIST researchers developed ROBOALIGN, a two-stage training framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that directly aligns multimodal large language model (MLLM) reasoning with low-level robotic actions. This approach yielded relative performance improvements of 17.5% on LIBERO, 18.9% on CALVIN, and 106.6% in real-world settings, while enhancing embodied reasoning and preserving general MLLM capabilities.

## Problem

- Improvements in multimodal large language model (MLLM) embodied reasoning often do not reliably translate into enhanced performance for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- A "modality gap" exists where language-based reasoning optimization does not consistently lead to improved low-level action generation.
- MLLMs with high embodied reasoning scores have been observed to yield marginal or even lower VLA performance compared to general MLLM baselines.

## Method

- Introducing **ROBOALIGN**, a two-stage MLLM training framework designed to align reasoning with low-level robotic actions.
- Stage 1 employs ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== with diverse data, including custom VQA and reasoning datasets with zero-shot ==Chain-of-Thought==, to enable low-level ==action token== generation and improve embodied reasoning.
- Stage 2 uses Reinforcement Learning (RL) with ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to refine embodied reasoning and enhance action accuracy, utilizing a ==reward function== based on both format adherence and ==action token sequence similarity==.

## Results

- ROBOALIGN consistently improved VLA performance, achieving relative gains of **17.5%** on LIBERO, **18.9%** on CALVIN, and **106.6%** in real-world robotic tasks.
- The framework notably enhanced success rates for complex, long-horizon tasks, raising LIBERO's "Long" category to **70.0%** and CALVIN's length-5 tasks to **22.2%**.
- Internal representation analysis showed ROBOALIGN boosting K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) accuracy for predicting underlying robot states from **39.06%** to **69.79%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct alignment of MLLM reasoning with low-level action accuracy through reinforcement learning is crucial for consistent VLA performance improvements, effectively bridging the "modality gap."
> - The framework significantly enhances the discriminative power of the MLLM's internal representations, improving the encoding of fine-grained robot state information vital for accurate action generation.
> - ROBOALIGN improves specialized embodied reasoning capabilities within MLLMs while maintaining or even enhancing their general image understanding and reasoning abilities, avoiding performance degradation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated capabilities in robotics by integrating visual perception, language understanding, and common-sense knowledge derived from multimodal large language models (MLLMs). These VLAs aim to translate high-level multimodal understanding into low-level actions, enabling generalizable robotic policies. Current approaches include MLLMs directly predicting discrete action tokens or external action experts operating on MLLM latent states. This paradigm leverages extensive MLLM pre-training for policy development, even with limited robotics data.

However, the efficacy of VLAs is often constrained by the MLLMs' performance on embodied reasoning tasks critical for action generation, such as spatial and temporal reasoning. Researchers have attempted to address these limitations by developing embodied question-answering (VQA) tasks. These tasks encompass questions about high-level actions, spatial relationships, grounding affordance-related locations, and predicting future visual trajectories of end-effectors. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) has been a primary method for these tasks, with more recent applications of reinforcement learning (RL) strategies showing promise in eliciting stronger reasoning capabilities and yielding performance improvements.

Despite these advancements, the correlation between improved embodied reasoning in MLLMs and enhanced VLA performance has been reported as inconsistent and task-dependent, occasionally leading to performance degradation. This discrepancy suggests a modality gap: optimizing embodied reasoning purely through language-based supervision does not consistently translate to improvements in concrete action generation. For instance, MLLMs highly specialized in embodied reasoning, demonstrating high reasoning scores, have been observed to yield lower VLA performance compared to general MLLM baselines. This work proposes an approach to directly bridge this modality gap by aligning MLLM reasoning with low-level actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an observed inconsistency: while MLLMs have shown progress in embodied reasoning (e.g., through vision-question-answering tasks and SFT/RL), these improvements do not reliably translate into enhanced performance for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Previous studies and the authors' preliminary experiments indicate that MLLMs specialized for embodied reasoning often yield only marginal, or even negative, gains in VLA performance, despite achieving high reasoning scores. This suggests a fundamental "modality gap" where optimizing reasoning through language supervision alone does not guarantee improved low-level action generation.

Specifically, the paper notes that models like RoboBrain 2.0, which achieved high reasoning scores and even surpassed commercial models like GPT-4o on certain benchmarks, exhibited the lowest VLA performance in their experiments. This empirical observation highlights that language-based reasoning capabilities do not inherently align with the requirements for precise physical action execution.

The key objectives of this work are to:
1. **Develop a systematic MLLM training framework:** The goal is to create a method, termed ROBOALIGN, that consistently and reliably improves VLA performance.
2. **Bridge the modality gap:** Address the disconnection between MLLM reasoning capabilities and the generation of low-level actions.
3. **Facilitate knowledge transfer:** Enable the effective transfer of knowledge from the MLLM to the VLA component.
4. **Align reasoning directly with action accuracy:** Achieve this by generating low-level action tokens as a direct outcome of embodied reasoning and evaluating the quality of this reasoning based on the accuracy of the generated actions.
5. **Refine reasoning through reinforcement learning:** Utilize RL to improve action accuracy by allowing the model to explore diverse reasoning trajectories and aligning them with precise action execution.
6. **Preserve MLLM general capabilities:** Ensure that the specialization for robotics tasks does not degrade the MLLM's broader image understanding and reasoning abilities.

By addressing these objectives, ROBOALIGN aims to provide a more effective pathway for integrating MLLMs into robust and generalizable robotic control systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ROBOALIGN proposes a two-stage training framework designed to align multimodal large language models (MLLMs) with low-level robotic actions. This process aims to enhance embodied reasoning while preserving the MLLM's general capabilities.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Integrating Low-level Action with MLLM using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The initial stage focuses on equipping the MLLM with the foundational ability to generate low-level action tokens via zero-shot reasoning, while simultaneously maintaining its general vision-language capabilities and improving embodied reasoning skills.

- **FAST Action Tokenization:** The framework adopts FAST tokenization (Pertsch et al., 2025) to represent low-level robotic actions as discrete tokens compatible with MLLMs. Each action is defined as a D-dimensional vector capturing the end-effector's Cartesian position, orientation (roll, pitch, yaw), and gripper state (Open/Close) over a horizon of H timesteps. This action chunk is transformed into the frequency domain using a discrete cosine transform (DCT), then quantized and flattened into a sequence. This sequence is further compressed into discrete tokens using byte-pair encoding (BPE), resulting in T_k = FAST(a_1:H), where each token maps to one of 2K special tokens added to the MLLM’s vocabulary.

- **Data Curation:** A diverse data mixture from four sources is used for SFT:
 - **Open-source SFT datasets:** A variety of existing datasets for general embodied reasoning and multimodal understanding.
 - **ROBOALIGN VQA:** A custom dataset specifically designed for robotic embodied reasoning. This dataset is generated by feeding robot images and associated metadata (e.g., bounding boxes, end-effector states, high/low-level actions) into a large model (gemini-2.5 pro) to automatically create fine-grained VQA, captioning, and grounding QA pairs. This aims to provide richer spatial-temporal information relevant to low-level control, addressing the limitations of existing high-level robot QA datasets.
 - **Reasoning dataset with zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** This dataset is created to encourage explicit, step-by-step reasoning within `<think>...</think>` tags before generating a final answer. It is distilled from a reasoning model pre-trained with GRPO on spatial and robot-related embodied multiple-choice QA (MCQA). Multiple reasoning trajectories are sampled and filtered for quality using rule-based rewards and correctness checks. This component is designed to transfer reasoning ability to FAST token generation.
 - **FAST token generation dataset:** This dataset is created to enable direct prediction of FAST tokens. The MLLM’s vocabulary is extended with special marker tokens (`<ACTION_START>`, `<ACTION_END>`) and 2K FAST tokens. Training data is constructed from the BridgeV2 dataset (Walke et al., 2023) in a QA format, where a robot image and instruction are paired with the corresponding ground-truth sequence of FAST tokens.

- **SFT Process:** The MLLM (based on Qwen2.5VL-7B-Ins) is fine-tuned using this curated data mixture. The vision encoder is frozen, and a cosine scheduler with a learning rate of 2x10^-5 and a warmup ratio of 0.03 is applied for one epoch.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Aligning Embodied Reasoning with Low-level Action via Reinforcement Learning (RL)

Building upon the SFT-trained model, the second stage employs RL to further refine embodied reasoning and directly improve the accuracy of FAST token generation.

- **Data for RL:** The FAST token dataset from Stage 1 is adapted. Each sample's input instruction is augmented with a prompt that explicitly requires reasoning within `<think>...</think>` tags before producing the FAST token sequence.
- **Reward Function:** The reward signal `r` is defined as the arithmetic mean of two components:
 - **Format reward (`r_f`):** A binary reward (0 or 1) indicating whether the model's output adheres to the required reasoning format (e.g., correct use of `<think>` and `<answer>` tags).
 - **Accuracy reward (`r_a`):** A value in `` measuring the accuracy of the generated FAST action tokens. It is computed as the prefix similarity between the generated action token sequence (T_gen_1:n) and the target sequence (T_target_1:m), normalized by the target length: `r_a = (1/m) * max{i ∈ {1, ..., m} : T_gen_1:i = T_target_1:i}`. This encourages both correct formatting and precise action sequences.
- **RL Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO; Shao et al., 2024) is used to optimize the MLLM. GRPO optimizes the policy by maximizing advantages derived from the reward model, while applying a KL penalty against a reference policy. This process involves sampling multiple responses from the current policy, evaluating them with the defined reward, and updating the policy.
- **Implementation Details:** The RL training is conducted using a subset of 12.8K samples from the BridgeV2 FAST token prediction data. All MLLM parameters are trained with a rollout batch size of 512, update batch size of 128, 5 samples per prompt, a constant learning rate of 1x10^-6, and trained for one epoch.

#### 3.3. VLA Conversion and Evaluation

To evaluate ROBOALIGN's effectiveness, the MLLMs trained with this framework (and baselines) are converted into VLAs. This conversion utilizes the Gr00t-N1.5 framework (GEAR, 2025), where a diffusion-based action head is attached to the frozen MLLM backbone. The newly initialized action head is then fine-tuned on benchmark-specific robot datasets (LIBERO, CALVIN, real-world) while the MLLM backbone remains frozen. This ensures a consistent evaluation setup across different MLLM training methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings regarding the effectiveness of ROBOALIGN in enhancing VLA performance and MLLM capabilities.

#### 4.1. VLA Performance Improvements

ROBOALIGN, which integrates SFT and RL, consistently yielded superior VLA performance across simulation and real-world environments compared to models trained with SFT alone or other baseline methods.

- **Overall Performance Gains:** ROBOALIGN achieved relative performance improvements of 17.5% on LIBERO, 18.9% on CALVIN, and 106.6% in real-world settings. These gains were achieved using less than 1% additional data for the RL alignment stage after SFT.
- **LIBERO Benchmark (Table 2):** ROBOALIGN achieved an average success rate of 86.8%, notably improving performance in complex categories. It raised the "Long" category success rate to 70.0% (compared to 63.2% for the base MLLM and marginal gains for other methods) and the "Goal" category from 42.4% to 87.2%. SFT alone provided moderate improvements but did not match the gains from the full ROBOALIGN framework.
- **CALVIN Benchmark (Table 3):** ROBOALIGN consistently improved task completion rates across all sequence lengths, particularly for longer-horizon tasks. It achieved a length-5 success rate of 22.2%, whereas other methods either showed a decline or limited improvement (e.g., base MLLM 18.1%, Language-Only SFT 15.3%).
- **Real-world Experiments (Table 4):** ROBOALIGN also demonstrated effectiveness in real-robot settings, achieving an average success rate of 66.7% across four pick-and-place tasks, a substantial increase over the 32.3% of the base MLLM.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Analyses

- **Compatibility with Different MLLM Backbones (Table 5):** When applied to a different MLLM backbone (Qwen3VL-8B-Ins), ROBOALIGN consistently improved overall performance on LIBERO, with notable gains in the "Long" category (from 60.0% to 78.6%). This indicates the generalizability of the framework across MLLM architectures.
- **Comparison with Embodied Alignment Strategies (Table 6):** The low-level action-based RL alignment in ROBOALIGN proved more effective than alternative RL alignment strategies, such as predicting high-level language actions or 2D visual trajectories of the end-effector. ROBOALIGN showed the largest overall performance improvement and uniquely enhanced long-horizon tasks, whereas other methods had limited impact on such tasks.
- **Comparison with SFT-based Alignment (Table 7):** SFT-based alignment, exemplified by ECoT (Zawalski et al., 2024) which jointly trains reasoning and low-level actions, resulted in performance degradation when compared to ROBOALIGN’s RL-based alignment. This suggests that SFT-based approaches may suffer from limited generalization or forgetting effects, transferring poorly across datasets and task types.
- **Representation Analysis (Table 8):** ROBOALIGN significantly improved the discriminative power of the MLLM's internal representations. A K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) classification experiment showed that ROBOALIGN (SFT+RL) boosted KNN accuracy for predicting underlying robot states from 39.06% (base MLLM) to 69.79%. This indicates that the RL alignment stage enhances the model's ability to encode fine-grained state information, which is critical for accurate action generation.
- **Performance on MLLM Benchmarks (Table 9):** ROBOALIGN not only improved VLA alignment but also enhanced embodied reasoning capabilities within MLLM tasks. It outperformed specialized embodied reasoning models (e.g., Cosmos-Reason1, RoboBrain2.0) on several embodied reasoning benchmarks (Robot-R1 Bench, RoboSpatial, Where2Place, BLINK) while maintaining strong performance on general image understanding (MMStar). This indicates that the RL-based alignment with low-level actions does not degrade, but rather improves, the general capabilities of MLLMs.

#### 4.3. Qualitative and Training Dynamics (Appendix A.5)

During RL training, the accuracy reward steadily increased while the average response length remained stable (Figure 6). Qualitatively, the model's responses evolved from high-level plans (e.g., "approach the cloth") to more concrete, situationally aware action descriptions (e.g., "approach and align its gripper with the cloth... The robot’s current position suggests it is already aligned..."). This indicates successful alignment of reasoning with actionable steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ROBOALIGN framework offers a systematic approach to addressing the documented challenge of inconsistent performance gains in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models derived from Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Modality Gap:** The core contribution is the reliable bridging of the modality gap between language-based embodied reasoning and low-level action generation. This is achieved by directly aligning MLLM reasoning with action accuracy through reinforcement learning, moving beyond indirect language-only supervision. This provides a mechanism for MLLMs to translate their abstract understanding into concrete, precise robotic control.
- **Reliable Performance Improvement:** ROBOALIGN demonstrates consistent and substantial performance gains in VLA tasks across diverse scenarios, including simulation environments (LIBERO, CALVIN) and real-world robot settings. The reported improvements, particularly in complex, long-horizon tasks and goal-conditioned execution, suggest a significant advancement in the robustness and capability of robot policies derived from MLLMs.
- **Data Efficiency in Alignment:** The framework achieves notable performance boosts with a comparatively small amount of data (less than 1% of SFT data) during the reinforcement learning stage. This efficiency can reduce the computational and data collection burden associated with training highly capable robotic agents.
- **Enhanced and Preserved MLLM Capabilities:** Unlike some SFT-based alignment methods that can degrade general MLLM performance, ROBOALIGN enhances embodied reasoning capabilities within MLLMs while maintaining strong performance on general image understanding benchmarks. This suggests a more harmonious integration of specialized robotic knowledge without compromising the MLLM's broad utility.
- **Improved Internal Representations:** The finding that ROBOALIGN leads to more discriminative internal MLLM representations is significant. This implies that the model learns finer-grained features crucial for action generation, contributing to more accurate and effective control policies.
- **Generalizability Across Architectures:** The demonstrated compatibility with different MLLM backbones (e.g., Qwen3VL-8B-Ins) indicates the framework's broad applicability and potential for integration with future MLLM developments.
- **Foundation for Advanced Robotic Control:** By providing a robust method for language-action alignment, ROBOALIGN contributes to the development of more generalist robot policies that can leverage the vast knowledge embedded in MLLMs. This could accelerate progress in fields requiring complex, instruction-driven robotic manipulation.

**Impact Statement (as provided by authors):**
The authors highlight a critical safety implication: if ROBOALIGN is aligned with unsafe action trajectories, VLAs trained using such MLLMs could become biased towards generating unsafe actions. This underscores the necessity of proactively considering and preventing these issues during the training phase, especially given the difficulty of precisely determining MLLM training data post-deployment. This ethical consideration emphasizes the responsibility in dataset curation and safety mechanisms for future applications.
