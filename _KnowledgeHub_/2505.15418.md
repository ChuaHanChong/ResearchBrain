# Guided Policy Optimization under Partial Observability

> [!summary] Summary
> The Guided Policy Optimization (GPO) framework enables efficient utilization of privileged information in partially observable reinforcement learning by co-training a guider with full state access and a learner with partial observations, ensuring the guider's policy remains imitable. This approach consistently achieved superior performance across didactic, noisy continuous control, and memory-based tasks compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning in Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) is difficult due to limited or noisy agent observations.
- Traditional Teacher-Student Learning (TSL) often faces an "imitation gap" where a privileged teacher's optimal policy is too complex or unlearnable for a partially observable student.
- Existing methods struggle to efficiently leverage privileged information or lack theoretical guarantees for learner optimality when using teacher supervision.

## Method

- Introduces Guided Policy Optimization (GPO), a framework co-training a "==guider==" with privileged information and a "learner" with partial observations.
- The guider's policy is continuously aligned with the learner's capabilities through a "backtracking" mechanism, preventing it from becoming "impossibly good."
- The learner primarily updates its policy by minimizing a distance metric to the guider's policy, effectively performing a supervised learning task.

## Results

- All GPO variants achieved optimal performance on didactic tasks like TigerDoor, empirically validating the theoretical guarantees of guided optimality.
- GPO-clip and GPO-penalty consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to various baselines across noisy continuous control tasks in Brax and memory-based tasks in POPGym.
- Incorporating an auxiliary Reinforcement Learning objective for the learner in GPO-penalty and GPO-clip generally accelerated learning and improved overall performance in complex tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The learner's policy updates can be viewed as a constrained policy mirror descent, providing theoretical guarantees for policy improvement towards optimality.
> - The framework effectively divides the learning challenge, with the guider handling complex RL gradient computations using privileged information and the learner performing a simpler supervised task.
> - Explicitly constraining the guider's policy (backtracking) is critical to prevent the "imitation gap" and ensure continuous, actionable supervision for the learner.
