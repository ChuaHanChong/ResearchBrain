# KARL: Knowledge Agents via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Databricks AI Research introduced KARL, a knowledge agent engineered for grounded reasoning over proprietary enterprise data, which achieves Pareto-optimal performance and robust generalization. KARL leverages a novel off-policy reinforcement learning approach and a custom evaluation suite, KARLBench, to surpass leading commercial models like Claude and GPT in cost-efficiency and quality on complex search tasks.

## Problem

- Existing AI systems lack robust capabilities for multi-step information gathering and complex reasoning grounded in proprietary enterprise data.
- Current benchmarks for knowledge agents are limited, failing to capture the diverse search behaviors and real-world complexities found in enterprise settings.
- Prevailing online reinforcement learning methods for large language models are often computationally inefficient, lack robustness, and struggle with out-of-distribution generalization.

## Method

- **KARL** developed **KARLBench**, a multi-capability evaluation suite comprising six distinct, enterprise-relevant search regimes to rigorously assess grounded reasoning.
- An ==agentic synthesis pipeline== generates diverse and challenging training data through iterative question-answer and solution synthesis, incorporating deduplication and quality filtering.
- ==OAPL== (Optimal Advantage-based Policy Optimization with Lagged Inference), an iterative large-batch ==off-policy reinforcement learning== paradigm, is used for post-training, promoting sample efficiency and multi-task generalization.

## Results

- KARL achieves Pareto-optimal performance on KARLBench, outperforming commercial models like Claude 4.6 and GPT 5.2 in quality while maintaining lower cost and latency.
- Models trained with multi-task RL exhibit substantially better generalization, particularly on out-of-distribution tasks within KARLBench, showcasing robust and transferable search behaviors.
- Reinforcement learning reduces search trajectory lengths by **37%**, increases unique document retrieval by **37%** for BrowseComp-Plus, and improves reasoning accuracy, leading to more efficient and effective problem-solving.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-task reinforcement learning significantly improves generalization to out-of-distribution tasks, demonstrating superior adaptability compared to multi-expert distillation.
> - RL training expands the model's problem-solving coverage by converting previously unsolved prompts into partially or fully solved states, rather than merely refining existing capabilities.
> - The learned context compression mechanism is critical for efficient long-horizon search, enabling the agent to identify and retain relevant information while reducing token overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "modern knowledge agents" capable of iteratively querying, retrieving, and reasoning over vast data collections represents a significant frontier in AI research. This work positions itself squarely within the domain of **grounded reasoning**, defined as reasoning that requires access to knowledge beyond the model's parameters. The authors highlight the immense economic value of such capabilities, particularly for enterprises that rely on vast stores of proprietary data inaccessible to general-purpose large language models (LLMs) during their pre-training.

The paper identifies a critical gap in existing research: a "dearth of work that studies model capabilities at the grounded reasoning frontier" compared to more extensively explored areas like common-sense reasoning, math, or coding. Existing "deep research" agents (e.g., OpenAI, 2025a; Zheng et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025) often rely on publicly available web knowledge and black-box web search tools. The authors argue that such approaches do not necessarily generalize to the diverse, proprietary data environments typical of enterprise settings, where tasks demand mastery of various skills like constraint-driven entity search, cross-document report synthesis, and tabular numerical reasoning over internal documents.

To address the limitations of existing benchmarks (e.g., HotpotQA, BrowseComp-Plus, FinanceBench), which capture only a "limited slice" of knowledge agent behaviors, KARL introduces **KARLBench**. This multi-capability evaluation suite is a significant contribution to the research landscape, providing a more comprehensive and enterprise-relevant standard for assessing grounded reasoning. By focusing on closed-corpus benchmarks and avoiding dataset-specific preprocessing, KARLBench aims to ensure that performance gains reflect genuine improvements in retrieval and reasoning, rather than artifact-specific optimizations.

Methodologically, the work stands out by proposing **Optimal Advantage-based Policy Optimization with Lagged Inference policy (OAPL)**, an iterative large-batch off-policy reinforcement learning (RL) paradigm. This approach is presented as a computationally more efficient and robust alternative to prevailing online RL methods (e.g., GRPO by Shao et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2025) that often require complex heuristics for stability, especially with large-scale Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models. The comparison to multi-expert distillation (Liu et al., 2025a; Team, 2026), a popular post-training strategy, further highlights KARL's innovative approach to achieving out-of-distribution generalization.

Ultimately, KARL contributes to the landscape by demonstrating that a combination of tailored synthetic data generation, multi-task reinforcement learning, and strategic test-time compute scaling can yield cost-efficient, high-performing knowledge agents that are Pareto-optimal and exhibit strong generalization capabilities for complex, real-world grounded reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the KARL project is to **develop and evaluate knowledge agents for grounded reasoning across diverse and arbitrary domains, particularly within enterprise settings.** This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and sub-objectives:

1. **Address the Gap in Grounded Reasoning:** Acknowledge the critical need for AI systems that can perform multi-step information gathering and complex reasoning grounded in proprietary enterprise data. Existing models, often trained on public internet data, are ill-suited for these tasks, creating a significant economic opportunity.
2. **Develop a Comprehensive Evaluation Suite:** Overcome the limitations of current benchmarks that only capture narrow aspects of knowledge agent capabilities. Introduce KARLBench to provide a robust, multi-capability evaluation suite spanning six distinct, enterprise-relevant search regimes to assess an agent's ability to acquire, retrieve, and reason over varied content.
3. **Innovate Training Data Generation:** Create a method for synthesizing diverse, grounded, and challenging training data that accurately reflects the complexity of real-world search tasks. This led to the development of an "agentic synthesis pipeline" leveraging long-horizon reasoning and tool use, with an iterative bootstrapping mechanism for self-improvement.
4. **Propose a Robust and Efficient Post-Training RL Paradigm:** Develop a novel reinforcement learning approach, OAPL, that is sample-efficient, robust to trainer-inference engine discrepancies, and naturally extends to multi-task training while promoting out-of-distribution generalization. This aims to simplify the infrastructure complexity associated with large-scale RL for LLMs.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance with Cost-Efficiency:** Engineer an agent that is not only highly performant on challenging grounded reasoning tasks but also Pareto-optimal across cost-quality and latency-quality trade-offs, making it practical for enterprise deployment. The motivation is to deliver SOTA quality at a fraction of the cost and latency of existing closed-source models.
6. **Demonstrate Generalization Across Heterogeneous Tasks:** Prove that models trained across diverse search behaviors, particularly through multi-task RL, can generalize substantially better to unseen tasks and environments compared to models optimized for single benchmarks or trained via distillation.
7. **Explore Test-Time Compute (TTC) for Performance Augmentation:** Investigate how TTC strategies like Parallel Thinking and Value-Guided Search can further boost KARL's performance in a cost and latency-conscious manner, providing flexible deployment options based on computational budget.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KARL employs a sophisticated, multi-pronged approach integrating a custom evaluation suite, an agentic data synthesis pipeline, a novel off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm, and test-time compute scaling strategies.

#### 3.1. Agent Harness

The core of the KARL system is an "agentic search setting" where the agent interacts with a single external tool: **Vector Search**. This design choice isolates the challenges of agentic retrieval and reasoning from broader tool orchestration. The agent iteratively generates search queries, receives retrieved documents, and produces a final answer. A key feature is **context management via automatic compression**, where the agent itself summarizes its interaction history when context length thresholds are met. This compression step is integrated into the RL training, allowing the agent to learn what and how to compress to maximize rewards.

#### 3.2. KARLBench: Multi-Capability Evaluation Suite

To rigorously assess grounded reasoning, KARL introduces **KARLBench**, a benchmark comprising six distinct search regimes:
- **BrowseComp-Plus:** Constraint-driven entity search.
- **TREC-Biogen:** Cross-document report synthesis.
- **FinanceBench:** Long-document traversal with tabular numerical reasoning.
- **QAMPARI:** Exhaustive entity search over encyclopedic text.
- **FreshStack:** Procedural reasoning over technical software documentation.
- **PMBench:** Exhaustive fact search over internal company notes (a new proprietary benchmark).

The corpus for these tasks maintains original document structures to reflect realistic conditions, avoiding specialized preprocessing. Evaluation is unified through **nugget-based completion**, assessing how well an answer covers discrete facts or entities.

#### 3.3. Training a Knowledge Agent via Reinforcement Learning (KARL)

The training methodology centers on reinforcement learning, broken down into data generation and model optimization.

##### 3.3.1. Agentic Synthesis

A two-stage pipeline generates diverse, grounded, and difficult training data:
- **Stage I - Question-Answer Synthesis:** A synthesis agent, equipped with vector search, explores the corpus using few-shot examples to generate novel question-answer pairs grounded in retrieved documents. A **Deduplication Agent** filters out exact or near-duplicates against evaluation sets and within the synthesized data itself.
- **Stage II - Solution Synthesis:** Multiple instances of a Solver Agent attempt to answer the synthetic questions. A **Pass-Rate Filtering** mechanism removes questions that are too easy (all attempts correct) or too hard (all attempts incorrect), focusing on examples with rich learning signals. A **Quality Filter Agent** further screens remaining data for ambiguity or factual inaccuracies in the synthesized ground truth.

##### 3.3.2. Post-training via Iterative Large-Batch Off-Policy RL (OAPL)

KARL utilizes OAPL (Optimal Advantage-based Policy Optimization with Lagged Inference policy), a new post-training paradigm inspired by A*PO and KL-regularized RL.
- **Objective:** Maximize expected reward while controlling policy divergence from a reference model ($\pi_{ref}$) using KL regularization.
- **Loss Function:** A least-square regression loss is employed to learn the optimal policy, leveraging groups of rollouts generated by the reference model.
- **Multi-step Agentic Setting:** The RL optimization incorporates multi-step rollouts, masking out tool outputs and assigning the entire rollout's reward to each segment (including compression steps) for efficiency and learning context management.
- **Iterative Training:** The process is iterative, where the newly trained policy becomes the reference for subsequent data generation and optimization, fostering self-improvement.
- **Advantages:** OAPL is sample-efficient, robust to discrepancies between the trainer and inference engine, and avoids complex heuristics (e.g., clipped importance weighting, data deletion) often required for stable online RL training with large MoE models.

##### 3.3.3. Multi-task RL Post Training

To achieve out-of-distribution generalization, KARL trains on a combination of in-distribution tasks (BrowseComp-Plus and TREC-Biogen), balancing their losses and training tokens. This approach is contrasted with **Multi-Expert Distillation**, where separate experts are trained and then distilled into a single model.

#### 3.4. Scaling KARL via Test-time Compute (TTC)

Two TTC strategies are explored to boost performance:
- **Parallel Thinking:** N independent rollouts are generated for a given prompt. An **Aggregator Agent** then synthesizes a final answer by considering all N rollouts. This aggregator can use tools and generate novel answers, not just pick from existing ones.
- **Value-Guided Search (VGS):** A separate, small value model is trained to predict the probability of success for partial rollouts. During inference, VGS performs parallel tree search, using the value model to select the most promising candidate steps at each turn, leading to N rollouts which are then aggregated (e.g., via Weighted Majority Voting).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several compelling findings demonstrating KARL's superior performance and methodological advantages:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** KARL achieves Pareto-optimal performance on KARLBench across both cost-quality and latency-quality trade-offs, outperforming leading commercial models like Claude 4.6 and GPT 5.2. With sufficient test-time compute, KARL surpasses the strongest closed models in quality.
2. **Robust Generalization:** Models trained across heterogeneous search behaviors (multi-task RL) generalize substantially better, including to out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks within KARLBench. This contrasts with multi-expert distillation, which showed negligible OOD gains with TTC, suggesting RL fosters more general search capabilities.
3. **Cost and Latency Efficiency:** KARL delivers competitive scores at a significantly lower cost (under $0.10 per query for single-call) and latency than other high-performing models. It is even cheaper per query than its base model (GLM 4.5 Air) while achieving over 6 points higher on KARLBench, indicating that RL leads to more efficient search strategies (fewer steps, less token overhead).
4. **Effectiveness of Iterative RL Training:** Multi-iteration training consistently improves performance on both in-distribution (e.g., KARL-TREC from 66.0 to 85.0 on TREC-Biogen) and OOD tasks, demonstrating continuous learning and the development of general search behaviors.
5. **Beyond "Sharpening":** The analysis shows that RL training expands the model's problem-solving coverage (max@k improves across all k), rather than merely sharpening its existing distribution. Previously unsolved prompts are converted to partially or fully solved states, providing direct evidence of new capabilities being learned.
6. **Crucial Role of Context Compression:** Ablation studies confirm that the learned context compression mechanism is vital for long-horizon search, with its removal leading to a substantial drop in accuracy (0.570 to 0.389 on BrowseComp-Plus). RL training improves the model's ability to identify and retain relevant information during compression.
7. **Search Environment Robustness:** KARL demonstrates robustness to changes in embedding models used by the vector search tool, suggesting it learns general search strategies rather than overfitting to specific retrieval quirks.
8. **Test-Time Compute Augmentation:**
 - **Parallel Thinking:** Consistently boosts KARL's performance across all KARLBench tasks, with gains complementary to RL training benefits. The generative aggregator can synthesize answers superior to any single candidate rollout.
 - **Value-Guided Search (VGS):** Achieves higher accuracy on BrowseComp-Plus (70.4) than parallel thinking, with Weighted Majority Voting proving most effective, indicating the utility of the value model's reward signal. VGS also improved the recall of retrieved documents.
9. **Quantitative Behavioral Changes:**
 - **Trajectory Shortening:** KARL learns to solve problems more efficiently, significantly reducing trajectory lengths across all difficulty categories, especially for already "solved" questions.
 - **Increased Search Diversity:** KARL exhibits greater cumulative unique document retrieval (+37% for BrowseComp-Plus, +8% for TREC-Biogen), suggesting more effective exploration.
 - **Improved Reasoning Accuracy:** Answer accuracy improves across all retrieval scenarios, even when only partial or no ground truth documents are retrieved, pointing to enhanced reasoning capabilities.
 - **Search Efficiency:** KARL dramatically reduces "wasteful" searches conducted after all necessary information has been retrieved, while also improving the efficiency of initial document retrieval.
10. **Qualitative Behavioral Shifts:**
 - **Enhanced Search Persistence and Reasoning:** KARL demonstrates improved long-horizon search capabilities and more accurate post-retrieval reasoning compared to baselines.
 - **Adaptive Stopping Policy:** RL training induces a more flexible commitment policy, balancing persistence against efficiency by terminating searches when marginal evidence diminishes, even with partial evidence.
 - **Behavioral Profile:** KARL's search behavior profile (dominated by "Explore then Commit") is more aligned with high-performing commercial models, indicating a learned ability to reliably transition from exploration to commitment within context budgets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The KARL project by Databricks AI Research carries significant implications for both scientific research and practical applications of AI, particularly in enterprise environments.

1. **Enabling Enterprise AI Adoption:** The most immediate impact is on enterprise AI. By developing cost-efficient and high-performing knowledge agents for grounded reasoning over proprietary data, KARL directly addresses a critical business need. Industries such as finance, law, medicine, and manufacturing, which rely heavily on vast, internal data stores, can leverage KARL to unlock new levels of insight, automation, and decision-making capabilities. The focus on Pareto-optimal cost-quality and latency-quality trade-offs makes KARL a highly deployable and economically viable solution.

2. **Advancing Grounded Reasoning Research:** KARL significantly pushes the frontier of grounded reasoning. By identifying and addressing the limitations of existing benchmarks and methodologies, it provides a robust framework (KARLBench) and an effective training paradigm (OAPL) for further research. The demonstration that multi-task RL can teach generalized search behaviors that transfer to out-of-distribution tasks is a crucial step towards building more adaptable and broadly capable AI agents.

3. **Algorithmic Innovation in Reinforcement Learning:** The introduction of OAPL offers a valuable contribution to the RL community. Its demonstrated sample efficiency, robustness, and ability to scale to large-scale MoE models without complex online RL heuristics simplifies the infrastructure and training complexity for developing powerful LLM agents. This could accelerate the development of RL-trained LLMs across various domains.

4. **Demystifying LLM Agent Capabilities:** The detailed quantitative and qualitative analyses provide valuable insights into *how* RL training impacts agent behavior. The findings that RL expands problem-solving coverage (beyond mere "sharpening"), improves search efficiency, increases search diversity, and leads to adaptive stopping policies contribute to a deeper understanding of the mechanisms by which LLMs acquire and refine agentic capabilities.

5. **Blueprint for Self-Improving AI Systems:** The agentic synthesis pipeline with iterative bootstrapping is a powerful concept for creating self-improving AI systems. By using increasingly capable models to generate higher-quality training data, the system can continuously refine its own learning process, reducing reliance on expensive human annotations for complex tasks.

6. **Future Directions for AI Agents:** The paper explicitly points to exciting future work, such as expanding the agent's action space to include structured retrieval, code execution, and compositional sub-agents, as well as developing more sophisticated hierarchical memory management. This vision sets a clear research agenda for building even more powerful and general-purpose knowledge agents, further solidifying Databricks' role in shaping the future of AI.

In conclusion, KARL represents a significant leap forward in the practical deployment and scientific understanding of AI knowledge agents for grounded reasoning. Its comprehensive approach, from benchmark creation to algorithmic innovation and detailed behavioral analysis, sets a new standard for developing enterprise-grade AI solutions.
