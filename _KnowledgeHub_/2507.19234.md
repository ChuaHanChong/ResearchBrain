# Virne: A Comprehensive Benchmark for RL-based Network Resource Allocation in NFV

> [!summary] Summary
> The VIRNE framework introduces a comprehensive benchmark for Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based Network Function Virtualization Resource Allocation (NFV-RA), unifying problem definitions, standardizing algorithm implementations, and offering rigorous evaluation across diverse network scenarios. This platform enables fair comparisons and aims to accelerate research in applying deep RL methods to NFV-RA problems, demonstrating that advanced graph neural network-based RL approaches achieve superior performance and scalability compared to traditional methods.

## Problem

- NFV-RA is an NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem crucial for modern networks, involving mapping virtual networks onto physical infrastructure under various constraints.
- Existing exact solvers are computationally intensive for dynamic, large-scale networks, while heuristic methods often lack adaptability and can yield suboptimal results.
- Research into RL-driven NFV-RA is hampered by a lack of standardized, comprehensive benchmarking frameworks, leading to fragmented evaluations and difficulty in fair comparisons across algorithms.

## Method

- VIRNE provides a unified ==NFV-RA== problem definition, extensible to emerging network scenarios like heterogeneous resources, latency, and energy efficiency.
- It features a modular, five-component architecture supporting versatile customization for network scenarios, event-driven simulation, and integration of over 30 algorithms (exact, heuristic, meta-heuristic, and RL-based).
- For RL-based methods, VIRNE offers a unified pipeline with interchangeable components for MDP modeling (reward functions, feature engineering), policy architectures (e.g., GCN, GAT), and training methods (e.g., PPO, A3C).

## Results

- PPO-DualGAT and PPO-DualGCN consistently delivered superior performance in online simulations across various network topologies, achieving higher Request Acceptance Rate (RAC), Long-term Revenue-to-Cost ratio (LRC), and Long-term Average Revenue (LAR).
- RL-based methods maintained a significantly lower and flatter Average Solving Time (AST) curve compared to meta-heuristics, showcasing better computational scalability with increasing network sizes (e.g., WX500).
- GAT-based models like PPO-DualGAT demonstrated superior adaptability and robustness in emerging network scenarios, including heterogeneous resourcing networks and latency-aware edge networks, where traditional heuristics struggled.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL-based methods, especially those leveraging advanced graph neural networks like PPO-DualGAT and PPO-DualGCN, consistently achieve a better balance between solution quality and efficiency compared to traditional approaches.
> - Effective implementation choices, including moderate fixed intermediate rewards, comprehensive feature engineering (Status and Topological features), and action masking, are critical for robust RL training and performance in complex NFV-RA environments.
> - The benchmark demonstrates that advanced RL policies exhibit stronger generalization across varying network conditions (traffic rates, demand distributions) and superior solvability for larger virtual networks, addressing key practical challenges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Network Function Virtualization (NFV) is a paradigm fundamental to modern networks, including cloud data centers, edge computing, and 5G. It enables the deployment of Virtual Network Functions (VNFs) as software modules on general-purpose servers, offering flexibility and scalability. A core challenge in NFV is Resource Allocation (NFV-RA), which involves mapping service requests, typically modeled as virtual networks (VNs) comprising interconnected VNFs, onto shared physical infrastructure while adhering to various constraints. This is an NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem.

Historically, NFV-RA has been addressed by exact solvers (e.g., Integer Linear Programming) and heuristic methods. Exact solvers aim for optimal solutions but are computationally intensive and impractical for dynamic, large-scale networks. Heuristics, while faster, often rely on manual design and domain-specific knowledge, limiting their adaptability and potentially leading to suboptimal performance in diverse scenarios.

More recently, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising approach for NFV-RA. RL agents can learn efficient allocation policies by interacting with simulated network environments, avoiding the need for high-quality labeled datasets which are difficult to obtain in this domain. However, the advancement of RL-driven NFV-RA research is currently constrained by a lack of standardized and comprehensive benchmarking frameworks. Existing benchmarks are typically limited to specific network scenarios (e.g., cloud environments), implement a narrow range of traditional non-RL methods, and focus primarily on basic effectiveness metrics. This fragmentation hinders fair comparison, robust evaluation, and systematic development of new algorithms, particularly for increasingly complex and diverse network environments.

Virne addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive benchmarking framework designed to unify problem definitions, standardize algorithm implementations, and offer a rigorous evaluation protocol across various network scenarios and practical perspectives. By doing so, it aims to accelerate research and application of deep RL methods for NFV-RA.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Virne are:

1. **Address the Lack of a Systematic Benchmark:** The most significant motivation is the absence of a standardized, comprehensive benchmarking framework for evaluating RL-based NFV-RA methods. Existing benchmarks are fragmented, scenario-specific (e.g., limited to cloud), and implement a narrow set of non-RL algorithms. This makes it difficult for researchers to conduct consistent simulations, perform rigorous evaluations, and make fair comparisons of novel algorithms.
2. **Enable Comprehensive Simulation for Diverse Network Scenarios:** Modern networks span various environments such as cloud data centers, edge computing, and 5G. These environments have distinct resource types, constraints, and service requirements (e.g., latency, energy efficiency, heterogeneous resources). Current benchmarks often fail to offer the flexibility needed to simulate this diversity. Virne aims to provide highly customizable simulation environments to model a wide array of NFV scenarios, accommodating fluctuating resource demands and evolving network conditions.
3. **Facilitate Standardized Implementation and Integration of Algorithms:** The proliferation of various RL-based and traditional NFV-RA algorithms often comes with disparate implementations, making it challenging to reproduce results or integrate new methods. Virne seeks to offer a modular and extensible implementation pipeline. This includes a unified gym-style environment and standardized components for RL agent design (MDP modeling, policy architecture, training methods), integrating over 30 algorithms of various types (exact, heuristic, meta-heuristic, and deep learning-based).
4. **Establish Rigorous Evaluation Protocols Beyond Online Effectiveness:** Traditional evaluation often focuses solely on online effectiveness metrics like acceptance rate or resource utilization. However, for practical deployment, other aspects are crucial. Virne aims to extend evaluation protocols to include practical perspectives such as solvability (ability to find feasible solutions), generalization (performance across varying network conditions), and scalability (effectiveness with increasing problem size). This allows for a more holistic assessment of algorithmic viability.
5. **Provide Insights and Guidance for Future Research:** By conducting extensive experiments within this comprehensive framework, Virne intends to offer in-depth analyses of algorithm performance trade-offs, the impact of different implementation choices (e.g., reward functions, feature engineering), and the characteristics of various methods. These empirical insights are designed to provide actionable guidance for future research directions in both applied machine learning and network optimization.
6. **Accelerate Research and Application:** Ultimately, Virne's overarching goal is to accelerate the development and adoption of deep RL for NFV-RA by providing a readily accessible, unified, and reproducible platform for the research community, bridging the gap between machine learning and networking domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Virne's methodology is structured around a unified problem definition and a comprehensive, modular benchmark architecture, designed to support diverse simulations, streamline algorithm implementation, and enable rigorous evaluation.

#### 3.1. NFV-RA Problem Definition

The paper first establishes a unified NFV-RA problem definition, combining a basic cost optimization model with extensions for emerging scenarios.

- **System Model:** Both user service requests (Virtual Networks, VNs) and physical infrastructure (Physical Network, PN) are modeled as undirected graphs. A VN `G_v` has virtual nodes (`N_v`) and links (`L_v`) with computing (`C(n_v)`) and bandwidth (`B(l_v)`) demands. A PN `G_p` has physical nodes (`N_p`) and links (`L_p`) with available resources (`C(n_p)`, `B(l_p)`). VNs arrive over time with a specified lifetime.
- **Embedding Constraints:** Mapping a VN onto a PN involves two sub-processes:
 - **Node Mapping:** Each virtual node `n_v` is mapped to a unique physical node `n_p`, subject to `C(n_v) ≤ C(n_p)`.
 - **Link Mapping:** Each virtual link `l_v` between two mapped virtual nodes must be routed along a connected physical path `ρ_p` in the PN, such that `B(l_v) ≤ B(l_p)` for all physical links `l_p` in `ρ_p`. One-to-one mapping for virtual nodes within a VN, path connectivity, and resource capacity constraints are enforced.
- **Optimization Objective:** The primary objective is to maximize the revenue-to-cost ratio (R2C) for each successfully embedded instance, defined as `(κ · REV(S)) / COST(S)`, where `κ` is a binary feasibility indicator (1 if feasible, 0 otherwise). `REV(S)` is the sum of virtual node computing and virtual link bandwidth demands. `COST(S)` is the sum of virtual node computing demands and the total bandwidth consumption across all physical paths used (path length multiplied by virtual link bandwidth).
- **Extensions in Emerging Networks:** The framework extends this basic definition to incorporate considerations for:
 - **Resource Heterogeneity:** Multiple resource types (e.g., CPU, GPU, memory) for nodes, requiring simultaneous satisfaction across all types.
 - **Latency Requirements:** Maximum permissible delay `D(l_v)` for virtual links, requiring `D(ρ_p) ≤ D(l_v)` for routed physical paths.
 - **Energy Efficiency:** Including energy consumption `E(n_p)` of physical nodes as part of a multi-objective optimization.

#### 3.2. Virne Benchmark Architecture

Virne is implemented with five key modules, guided by principles of versatile customization, scalable modularity, and intuitive usability:

1. **Simulation Configuration:** Allows users to customize network scenarios and conditions. This includes:
 - **Network Topologies:** Selection from synthetic (e.g., Waxman, FatTree) or realistic (e.g., SDNLib, Topology Zoo) PN and VN topologies.
 - **Resource Availability:** Definition of multiple resource types (CPU, GPU, bandwidth) and their distributions across nodes, links, and the entire network.
 - **Service Requirements:** Specification of additional demands like latency, energy efficiency, and reliability.

2. **Network System:** Functions as an event-driven simulator. It instantiates a PN and processes sequential VN requests. Each request triggers resource scheduling and allocation by the chosen NFV-RA algorithm. The system then evaluates the solution's feasibility and updates network resources, accepting or rejecting the VN.

3. **Algorithm Implementation:** Integrates over 30 NFV-RA algorithms, spanning traditional (exact, heuristic, meta-heuristic) and learning-based methods. For RL-based methods, a unified pipeline is provided:
 - **NFV-RA as a Markov Decision Process (MDP):** The problem is framed as a sequential decision-making process where an agent places virtual nodes onto physical nodes. The state `s_t` represents the embedding status, actions `a_t` are physical node selections, `P` is the state transition, and `R` is the reward function. Virne also supports Multi-Task MDPs for meta-RL and Constrained MDPs for safe-RL.
 - **Unified Pipeline for Efficient Implementations:** RL-based algorithms are decomposed into three interchangeable components:
 - **MDP Modeling:** Defines rewards (no intermediate, fixed, adaptive) and feature engineering (node status, topological features).
 - **Policy Architecture:** Neural networks for state representation and action generation (e.g., MLP, CNN, Attention, GCN, GAT, DualGCN, DualGAT, HeteroGAT).
 - **Training Methods:** RL algorithms for optimizing policies (e.g., MCTS, Policy Gradient, A3C, PPO, DQN).
 - **Implementation Techniques:** Includes action masking (disallowing infeasible actions) and various reward function designs (no, fixed, adaptive intermediate rewards).

4. **Auxiliary Utility:** Enhances usability and analysis with:
 - **System Controller:** Manages simulation workflow and method selection.
 - **Solution Monitor:** Tracks solution feasibility and performance.
 - **Visualization Tools:** Provides interactive visual representations of results.

5. **Evaluation Criteria:** Offers a suite of metrics and practical perspectives for comprehensive assessment:
 - **Performance Metrics:** Request Acceptance Rate (RAC), Long-term Revenue-to-Cost ratio (LRC), Long-term Average Revenue (LAR), and Average Solving Time (AST).
 - **Practicality Perspectives:**
 - **Solvability:** Ability to find feasible solutions under reproducible, static conditions (offline evaluation).
 - **Generalization:** Robustness of performance when pre-trained policies are applied to varying network conditions (traffic rates, demand distributions).
 - **Scalability:** Effectiveness and efficiency (inference time) as network size and complexity increase.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical analysis conducted using Virne provides detailed insights into the performance characteristics of various NFV-RA algorithms.

#### 4.1. Exploration of Implementation Techniques (Section 4.2.1)

Experiments focused on PPO-DualGAT and its variations on the WX100 topology revealed:

- **Impact on RL Training Methods:** PPO demonstrated superior performance, converging rapidly to higher average returns compared to A3C and Policy Gradient, indicating its sample efficiency and robust performance.
- **Impact on Reward Function Design:** A moderate fixed intermediate reward (e.g., 0.1) consistently yielded the best or near-best results across solvers, suggesting its importance in providing sufficient learning signals. Very small or adaptive rewards were less effective.
- **Impact on Feature Engineering Combination:** Combining both Status (node embedding status) and Topological (graph centrality measures) features generally led to improved acceptance rates and revenue, indicating the value of augmenting inputs with global network context, even for graph neural networks.
- **Impact on Action Mask Strategy:** Explicitly preventing agents from selecting infeasible actions via action masking consistently improved performance across all methods, highlighting the importance of constraint enforcement in complex NFV-RA problems.
- **Default Implementation:** Subsequent experiments used PPO as the default RL algorithm, combined with moderate intermediate rewards, comprehensive feature engineering, and action masking.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness in Online Environments (Section 4.2.2)

Online simulations were conducted across WX100, GEANT, and BRAIN topologies with different traffic rates:

- **Overall Performance:** RL-based methods generally offered a more favorable balance between solution quality and efficiency compared to traditional heuristics and meta-heuristics.
- **Advanced RL Agent Performance:** Advanced RL agents, particularly those with dual graph neural network architectures like PPO-DualGAT and PPO-DualGCN, consistently delivered superior performance in terms of RAC, LRC, and LAR.
- **Impact of PN Topology:** The topological characteristics (scale and density) of the Physical Network (PN) impacted the relative performance of GNN architectures. GCN-based methods showed more competitive or better performance on smaller-scale topologies like GEANT and BRAIN, while GATs, with their adaptive aggregation, were more advantageous in denser topologies like WX100.

#### 4.3. Evaluation from Practicality Perspectives (Section 4.3)

- **Solvability via Offline Evaluation:**
 - Experiments with varying Virtual Network (VN) sizes (2 to 10 nodes) showed a general decrease in solution quality (R2C) as VN size increased.
 - RL methods with advanced graph representations (e.g., PPO-DualGAT) exhibited superior solvability, especially for larger VNs, due to their ability to navigate complex search spaces.
 - Traditional heuristics and simpler RL policies generally underperformed compared to top RL methods for larger VNs.

- **Generalization on Network Conditions (Appendix D.2):**
 - **Varying Traffic Rates:** As the average arrival rate (η) increased, RAC generally decreased across all algorithms, but many RL-based algorithms, especially PPO-DualGAT and PPO-DualGCN, demonstrated stability in LRC. Performance differences were more significant in sparser topologies.
 - **Fluctuating Demand Distribution:** When policies trained on a specific VN distribution were tested against changing demand characteristics (resource demands, VN sizes), advanced RL methods like PPO-DualGAT and PPO-DualGCN maintained higher performance and better adaptation compared to others, particularly in managing increased resource demands and topological complexity.

- **Scalability on Network Sizes (Appendix D.3):**
 - **Performance on Large-scale Networks (WX500):** PPO-DualGAT and PPO-DualGCN maintained strong performance on the large WX500 topology, indicating good scalability of their learned policies. Simpler RL models showed limited performance, highlighting the need for effective topological information capture in larger networks. A trade-off between maximizing immediate revenue (LAR) and long-term efficiency (LRC) was observed.
 - **Analysis of Solving Time Scale:** RL-based methods generally maintained a much flatter and lower Average Solving Time (AST) curve compared to meta-heuristics, which showed substantial increases with increasing VN or PN sizes. Traditional heuristics were the fastest but often compromised on solution quality. RL-based methods offered a more favorable balance between solution quality and computational scalability.

#### 4.4. Validation on Emerging Networks (Appendix D.4)

Virne validated algorithmic adaptability in two specific emerging network scenarios:

- **Heterogeneous Resourcing Networks:** In environments with multiple resource types (e.g., CPU, GPU, memory), GAT-based models (PPO-DualGAT, PPO-GAT&S2S) demonstrated superior performance. This was attributed to their attention mechanisms, which can dynamically weigh the importance of different resource types. Simpler RL architectures showed a performance dip.
- **Latency-aware Edge Networks:** The introduction of strict latency constraints generally led to lower RAC and LRC across all algorithms. PPO-DualGAT again showed robust performance, while traditional heuristics struggled to simultaneously satisfy resource and path-level latency constraints, revealing their fundamental weakness in complex scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Virne's introduction as a comprehensive benchmarking framework for RL-based NFV-RA carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the research community and practical deployments:

1. **Standardization and Reproducibility:** By providing a unified problem definition, a modular implementation pipeline, and standardized evaluation protocols, Virne significantly enhances the reproducibility of research in NFV-RA. This allows for fair comparisons between different algorithms, a critical step for objective scientific progress that was previously hindered by fragmented assessments and inconsistent experimental setups.
2. **Acceleration of Algorithm Development:** The modular and extensible design of Virne's pipeline simplifies the process of implementing and testing new algorithms. Researchers can more easily experiment with different RL training methods, neural policy architectures, and feature engineering techniques without needing to build entire simulation environments from scratch. This streamlined workflow is expected to accelerate the development and iteration of novel NFV-RA solutions.
3. **Comprehensive Performance Understanding:** Beyond basic effectiveness metrics, Virne's inclusion of practical evaluation perspectives such as solvability, generalization, and scalability provides a more holistic understanding of an algorithm's practical viability. This enables researchers to identify strengths and weaknesses relevant to real-world deployment challenges, such as adapting to varying network loads, handling diverse request characteristics, or scaling to large infrastructures.
4. **Guidance for Future Research Directions:** The extensive empirical analysis conducted within Virne offers valuable insights into the impact of implementation choices and performance trade-offs. The identification of persistent challenges, such as robust learning for hard constraints, scalability in extremely large networks, and generalizable policies across varying scenarios, provides a clear roadmap for future research in applied machine learning for network optimization.
5. **Bridge Between ML and Networking Communities:** Virne is designed to be accessible to researchers from both the machine learning and networking communities. Its gym-style environment and PyTorch-based implementations are familiar to ML practitioners, while its detailed network modeling and problem definitions resonate with networking experts. This convergence facilitates interdisciplinary collaboration and the cross-pollination of ideas.
6. **Support for Emerging Network Paradigms:** With its customizable simulation capabilities for cloud, edge, and 5G environments, including heterogeneous resources and latency awareness, Virne is directly relevant to the evolving landscape of modern networks. It provides a platform to validate and compare NFV-RA solutions tailored for the unique demands of these emerging paradigms.

Overall, Virne is positioned to serve as a foundational tool that advances the state-of-the-art in RL-based NFV-RA by fostering systematic research, enabling rigorous evaluation, and guiding the development of more robust and practical solutions for dynamic network environments.
