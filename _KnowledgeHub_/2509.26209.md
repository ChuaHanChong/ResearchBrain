# Diversity-Incentivized Exploration for Versatile Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The DIVER framework enhances Large Language Model reasoning by incentivizing global sequence-level diversity through intrinsic rewards, moving beyond token-level exploration. This approach leads to improved performance on mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating stronger generalization to out-of-domain tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs) struggles with exploration due to vast state-action spaces and sparse rewards in complex reasoning tasks.
- Existing LLM exploration methods primarily focus on 'local token-level' diversity, which is insufficient for 'far-sighted, deep exploration' required to discover novel solution patterns.
- Combinatorial explosion in textual state-action spaces leads to deficient exploration and suboptimal sample efficiency, hindering LLMs' reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- **DIVER** introduces a framework that integrates global ==sequence-level diversity== as an intrinsic reward within the GRPO algorithm for LLMs.
- It employs two computationally efficient metrics: Textual Diversity (TD) for general text dissimilarity and Equational Diversity (ED) for mathematical formula differences.
- A potential-based reward shaping mechanism, formally proven to preserve optimal policy invariance, combined with conditional and balanced shaping, is used to integrate diversity incentives and mitigate reward hacking.

## Results

- DIVER achieved an average of 43.0 on six mathematical benchmarks, surpassing OpenReasoner-Zero by +2.0 points, and an average of 58.4 on challenging out-of-domain tasks, outperforming OpenReasoner-Zero by +6.8 points (e.g., +12.5 on GPQA).
- The framework consistently outperformed other exploration RL methods, such as Entropy-RL, by +1.2 points on in-domain tasks and +2.4 points on out-of-domain tasks, with notable gains of +4.6 points on OlympiadBench.
- DIVER demonstrated superior deep exploration, exhibiting more pronounced performance gains at higher Pass@k values (e.g., +6.7 points over Entropy-RL at Pass@32 on AIME25) and its solutions were ranked as 'best' in **76.4%** of cases by an advanced LLM judge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global sequence-level diversity is positively correlated with enhanced LLM reasoning capacity and significantly improves generalization to out-of-domain tasks.
> - Potential-based reward shaping can effectively incorporate intrinsic diversity rewards into RL without altering the optimal policy of the original MDP.
> - Mitigating reward hacking by applying diversity incentives only to correct responses and dynamically balancing exploration vs. exploitation is crucial for successful intrinsic reward learning in LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work operates within the field of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) applied to Large Language Models (LLMs), a paradigm developed to enhance the reasoning capabilities of these models through rule-based verification of their outputs. A fundamental challenge in this area is effective exploration within the vast, high-dimensional textual state-action spaces of LLMs, which often suffer from combinatorial explosion and reward sparsity in complex reasoning tasks. These conditions typically lead to deficient exploration and suboptimal sample efficiency in existing methods.

Current LLM literature has explored various exploration strategies, largely categorized as increasing uncertainty in the policy's action selection. These include techniques such as alleviating policy entropy collapse (Cui et al., 2025b), selectively branching high-entropy tokens (Liu et al., 2025a), and retaining policy gradient updates for high-entropy tokens (Wang et al., 2025). These methods primarily focus on "local token-level" diversity, aiming to help the policy escape local optima and accelerate training by injecting randomness at the individual token level.

The paper argues that while local action-level uncertainty can be informative, it is insufficient for efficient exploration in complex reasoning tasks. It posits that "far-sighted, deep exploration" is necessary, which generates temporally-extended and "global sequence-level" diversity. This concept of optimizing global diversity is recognized in advanced reinforcement learning algorithms (Eysenbach et al., 2019; Grillotti et al., 2024; Celik et al., 2024) for solving intricate tasks. However, the paper identifies a gap: efficient deep exploration mechanisms, particularly those focusing on global sequence-level diversity, remain largely underexplored in the context of LLM reasoning.

The proposed DIVER framework positions itself to address this specific gap by introducing an explicit mechanism to incentivize global sequence-level diversity within LLM reasoning processes. This approach contrasts with token-level diversity strategies and aims to enhance the discovery of novel and effective solution patterns. Related works include those that use Pass@k metrics as training rewards (Chen et al., 2025b) or employ partitioning classifiers to measure diversity (Li et al., 2025), but these do not explicitly optimize global diversity across candidate solutions as DIVER does.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an innovative framework, DIVER (Diversity-Incentivized Exploration for VersatilE Reasoning), that addresses the limitations of exploration in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs). The motivation stems from several interconnected challenges:

1. **Vast State-Action Spaces and Reward Sparsity**: LLM reasoning tasks involve immensely large state-action spaces and often exhibit sparse rewards, meaning that meaningful feedback (correct solutions) is infrequent. This combinatorial explosion makes effective exploration difficult for LLMs, especially under computational constraints.
2. **Limitations of Current Exploration Methods**: Existing LLM exploration techniques predominantly focus on "local token-level" diversity. While these methods can aid in escaping local optima, they are deemed insufficient for "deep exploration" that necessitates broader, "global sequence-level" diversity. This deeper exploration is crucial for discovering novel and versatile reasoning pathways.

To substantiate this motivation, the authors conducted a preliminary empirical study:

- **Study Design**: The study compared two training schemes based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): one utilizing rollouts filtered for high global sequence-level diversity, and another using rollouts filtered for low global diversity. The diversity was quantified using metrics proposed in the paper (Textual Diversity and Equational Diversity).
- **Observations**:
 - During training, the high-diversity scheme showed a slightly lower "solve all" rate but a more significantly lower "solve none" rate. This indicated that encouraging diversity might involve a trade-off where the model explores a wider range of reasoning patterns, accepting fewer uniform successes on easier problems in favor of discovering breakthroughs for harder ones.
 - During testing, models trained with high-diversity rollouts achieved higher average test scores on both in-domain (+1.8 points) and out-of-domain (+2.6 points) benchmarks. The performance improvement was more pronounced for out-of-domain tasks, suggesting that global diversity enhances generalization capabilities.
- **Conclusion**: This empirical evidence established a direct and positive correlation between global sequence-level diversity and the reasoning capacity of LLMs.

Motivated by this finding, the core objective of DIVER is to explicitly incorporate and optimize global sequence-level diversity during RL training. By formulating this diversity as an intrinsic reward, the framework aims to incentivize deep exploration within a semantically structured space, thereby fostering more versatile LLM reasoning abilities and improved generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DIVER framework is designed to promote deep exploration in LLM reasoning by incentivizing global sequence-level diversity. It builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm and integrates novel components for diversity quantification, reward shaping, and reward hacking mitigation.

#### 3.1. Problem Statement and Base Algorithm

The reasoning task is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). DIVER is developed on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), an RLVR baseline that uses a rule-based verifiable reward function. GRPO generates a group of `G` candidate responses `{o_1..., o_G}` for each query `q`. Each response `o_i` receives a binary reward `r_i` (0 or 1) based on a rule-based verification against the ground truth. The policy is updated by maximizing a clipped objective function that uses importance sampling ratios and a KL regularization term, with advantages `A_i` computed from normalized group-level rewards.

#### 3.2. Metrics for Quantifying Sequence-Level Diversity

To quantify diversity at the sequence level, DIVER introduces two metrics:
- **Textual Diversity (TD)**: This metric measures the text-level mutual dissimilarity among responses within a group. It adapts the BLEU score (Papineni et2 al., 2002), typically used for measuring text similarity, by inverting it (1 - BLEU) to quantify dissimilarity. For each response `o_i` in a group, its TD is calculated as the average of its inverted BLEU scores with every other response `o_j` in the group.
- **Equational Diversity (ED)**: Designed specifically for mathematical reasoning tasks, ED captures differences in the mathematical formulas present across group responses. For a response `o_i`, `F(o_i)` denotes the set of formulas extracted from it. `F_-i` is the union of formulas from all other responses in the group. ED for `o_i` is defined as the ratio of unique formulas in `o_i` (those not found in `F_-i`) to the total number of formulas in `o_i`. If no formulas are present, ED is 0. These metrics are designed to be computationally efficient, relying on rule-based calculations rather than large-scale model inference.

#### 3.3. Promoting Global Diversity for Deep Exploration

DIVER integrates these diversity metrics as an intrinsic reward within the GRPO framework:
- **Intrinsic Reward Formulation**: The global diversity `d(o_i)` for each response `o_i` is calculated by averaging its pairwise dissimilarities (from TD or ED) to other responses in its group.
- **Potential-Based Reward Shaping**: To ensure that incorporating the intrinsic reward does not alter the optimal policy, DIVER employs a potential-based reward shaping mechanism (Ng et al., 1999). The intrinsic reward `R_int` for a state transition `(s_t, a_t, s_t+1)` is defined as the difference in potential functions: `R_int(s_t, a_t, s_t+1) = γd(s_t+1) - d(s_t)`. In the LLM context, `s_t` is `[q, o_i,<=t]`, and `s_t+1` is `[q, o_i,<=t+1]`.
- **Cumulative Intrinsic Reward**: For a complete query-response pair `[q, o_i]`, the sum of discounted intrinsic rewards over the trajectory simplifies to `R_int([q, o_i]) = γ^T d([q, o_i])`, where `T` is the terminal step and `d(q)` (diversity of the initial query) is zero. This elegant formulation means the intrinsic reward for a full response is effectively its final global diversity, scaled by the discount factor.
- **Augmented Reward Function**: The new reward function `R'([q, o_i])` combines the original rule-based reward `R([q, o_i])` with the intrinsic diversity reward: `R'([q, o_i]) = R([q, o_i]) + λ * R_int([q, o_i])`, where `λ` is a balancing ratio. This augmented reward `r'_i` replaces `r_i` in GRPO's advantage computation.
- **Optimal Policy Invariance**: A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1, Appendix A) ensures that this specific potential-based reward shaping mechanism preserves optimal policy invariance. Any optimal policy in the transformed MDP `M'` will also be an optimal policy in the original MDP `M`, and vice versa.

#### 3.4. Mitigating Reward Hacking

To prevent the LLM from exploiting the intrinsic diversity reward at the expense of correctness:
- **Balanced Shaping**: The intrinsic reward `r_int_i` is clipped to an upper bound `σ` (`r_int_i = clip(r_int_i; 0, σ)`). Additionally, the balancing ratio `λ` is gradually reduced during training, favoring exploration early and exploitation later, akin to the classic exploration-exploitation trade-off.
- **Conditional Shaping**: Diversity incentives are applied exclusively to *correct* responses: `r'_i = r_i + λ * r_int_i * I(r_i)`, where `I(r_i)` is an indicator function for a correct response. This ensures that diversity is only rewarded when it contributes to valid solutions, aligning the intrinsic reward with the primary objective of accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DIVER spanned various mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks, different model scales, and comprehensive ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Reasoning Performance on Qwen2.5-Math-7B

- **Comparison with Established RLVR Methods**: DIVER-TD and DIVER-ED achieved average scores of 42.3 and 43.0 on six mathematical benchmarks, surpassing OpenReasoner-Zero by +2.0 points. On challenging out-of-domain tasks (ARC-c, GPQA\*, MMLU-Pro), DIVER reached an average of 58.4, outperforming OpenReasoner-Zero by +6.8 points, with notable gains on ARC-c (+10.1) and GPQA (+12.5). DIVER-MIX (combining both TD and ED) yielded slightly higher average scores. These results indicate that encouraging diverse reasoning paths enhances model generalization.
- **Comparison with Exploration RL Methods**: DIVER outperformed Entropy-RL (the best exploration baseline) by +1.2 points on average across in-domain tasks, with a +4.6 point advantage on OlympiadBench. On out-of-domain tasks, DIVER exceeded Entropy-RL by +2.4 points, particularly in conversational reasoning benchmarks like GPQA (+3.5 points). The undirected exploration strategy of GPRO w/ Clip-higher showed weaker performance, suggesting the importance of a strategic approach to exploration.

#### 4.2. Training Dynamics of Exploration Methods

Analysis of training dynamics (Figure 4) indicated that DIVER achieved a balance of high diversity (ED and TD metrics) and controlled entropy levels. In contrast, Pass@k Training and Entropy-RL showed declining diversity, while GPRO w/ Clip-higher maintained diversity but experienced problematic entropy increases. DIVER's "solve all" rate grew more gradually, but its lower "solve none" rate during training suggested effective exploration without compromising overall solution quality.

#### 4.3. Extending DIVER to Different Models

DIVER maintained its effectiveness across various model architectures and scales, including Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B-Base, and LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (Figure 5, Table 9). It consistently outperformed GRPO w/ Clip-higher by +1.7 points on in-domain tasks and +1.5 to +1.9 points on out-of-domain tasks. Furthermore, DIVER demonstrated robust performance with models generating longer responses (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B), indicating its applicability to longer reasoning horizons.

#### 4.4. Exploration Scope and Reasoning Capacity (Pass@k)

- DIVER consistently outperformed baselines across Pass@k metrics (for k ranging from 2 to 32) on both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks (Figure 6, Table 4).
- The performance gains were more pronounced at higher `k` values, demonstrating DIVER's superior deep exploration capabilities. For instance, on AIME25, DIVER achieved a +6.7 point improvement over Entropy-RL at Pass@32, and on OlympiadBench, a +2.1 point improvement over GRPO w/ Clip-higher at Pass@32.
- Extended Pass@k experiments (up to k=1024) confirmed DIVER's consistent superiority, with performance gaps widening as `k` increased (Table 5).
- Qualitative case studies indicated that DIVER generated diverse yet coherent reasoning paths leading to correct solutions, contrasting with baselines that often exhibited broad but unproductive exploration.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Diversity Metrics Validity

Correlation analysis (Table 7) showed strong consistency between TD/ED and LLM-/Embedding-based semantic similarity metrics. Methods with higher TD/ED scores consistently had lower semantic similarity scores, confirming that TD and ED effectively capture meaningful semantic diversity.

#### 4.6. Analysis of Reasoning Quality

Using DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp as a judge model to rank reasoning quality (Table 8), DIVER solutions were ranked as "best" (Rank 1) in 76.4% of cases, more than double the next best method. This suggests that DIVER's diversity measures translate into meaningful improvements in solution quality, not just surface-level variations.

#### 4.7. Mitigating Reward Hacking

An ablation study demonstrated the efficacy of conditional shaping. Applying diversity rewards to incorrect or all responses led to severe performance degradation and response length explosion (Figure 7), indicating reward hacking. The strategy of applying diversity rewards *only* to correct responses successfully constrained exploration to valid solutions and prevented this phenomenon.

#### 4.8. Impact of Longer Horizons

Analysis of diversity calculation horizons (Figure 8, Figure 11) showed that calculating diversity over full responses maximized performance. Shorter horizons resulted in reduced entropy and performance, supporting the hypothesis that global sequence-level diversity across the entire reasoning path is beneficial.

#### 4.9. Hyperparameter Robustness

DIVER exhibited robustness to variations in the diversity reward shaping coefficient (λ) (Figure 10) and showed greater stability to changes in group size and training temperature compared to baselines (Table 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DIVER framework represents a methodological contribution to the field of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly regarding exploration strategies.

**Key Contributions:**
- **Shift in Exploration Paradigm**: The work highlights the importance of "global sequence-level diversity" as a driver for "deep exploration" in LLM reasoning, moving beyond the prevalent focus on local token-level diversity. The initial empirical study provides evidence for a positive correlation between this global diversity and reasoning capacity, especially for generalization to out-of-domain tasks.
- **Principled Methodology**: DIVER introduces computationally efficient metrics (Textual Diversity and Equational Diversity) for quantifying sequence-level diversity. It integrates these metrics into a potential-based reward shaping mechanism that is formally proven to preserve optimal policy invariance, ensuring that the intrinsic rewards guide the agent without altering the fundamental objective.
- **Robustness against Reward Hacking**: The framework incorporates practical heuristics, specifically conditional shaping (incentivizing diversity only for correct solutions) and balanced shaping (clipping rewards and decaying the shaping ratio), to mitigate the risk of reward hacking, which is a common challenge when using intrinsic rewards in complex, high-dimensional environments like LLMs.
- **Demonstrated Performance and Generalization**: Experimental results consistently show DIVER outperforming competitive RLVR baselines and other exploration-focused methods across various mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks. Its superior performance on Pass@k evaluations, especially at higher `k` values, indicates an enhanced ability to generate diverse and correct solutions. The framework also demonstrates stronger generalization capabilities on out-of-domain tasks and adaptability across different LLM scales and architectures. The enhanced reasoning quality, as judged by an advanced LLM, further supports the efficacy of the diversity incentives.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancing LLM Reasoning**: By enabling more effective deep exploration, DIVER could lead to LLMs that are more capable of discovering novel problem-solving strategies and tackling complex reasoning problems that require intricate, multi-step solutions.
- **Improving Generalization**: The observed improvements in out-of-domain task performance suggest that diversity-incentivized exploration can enhance the generalization capabilities of LLMs, making them more versatile across different problem types.
- **Guiding Future Research**: This study may inspire further research into developing more sophisticated global diversity metrics and exploring other forms of deep exploration mechanisms within the context of LLM-based agents.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations. Currently, DIVER focuses on single-turn RLVR. Extending it to multi-turn settings is identified as a promising future direction, as multi-turn interactions are crucial for more complex, real-world applications. Another area for future investigation is the development of more advanced diversity metrics, potentially leveraging LLM-as-a-judge paradigms to capture nuanced semantic differences more effectively.
