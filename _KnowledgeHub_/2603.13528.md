# Learning Actionable Manipulation Recovery via Counterfactual Failure Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Dream2Fix is a framework that generates photorealistic, physically plausible robotic manipulation failure data and corresponding actionable recovery plans from successful demonstrations. This system fine-tunes a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to achieve a 46% recovery rate in real-world benchmarks and a 40% recovery rate in OpenVLA deployments for zero-shot closed-loop failure recovery.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems lack autonomous recovery capabilities from execution errors, as foundation models do not inherently possess the causal understanding for corrective actions.
- Collecting real-world failure data with dense, paired correction annotations is costly, risky, and scarce, while simulation-based data suffers from a significant "sim-to-real" gap.
- Existing visual failure analyzers provide coarse diagnoses (e.g., success/failure) rather than specific, low-level, executable trajectory corrections, creating a gap between detection and actionable recovery.

## Method

- **Dream2Fix** synthesizes photorealistic, physically plausible ==counterfactual failure rollouts== by injecting targeted ==action perturbations== into a generative world model (==Ctrl-World==) using successful real-world demonstrations.
- A rigorous ==multi-verifier mechanism== (VLM, Inverse Dynamics Model, Joint Pose, Point-Tracking verifiers) is employed to filter synthesized data, ensuring task validity, visual coherence, kinematic safety, and dynamic consistency.
- For each verified failure, structured, actionable ==recovery instructions== are deterministically generated as text labels, directly translatable into robot control primitives for ==closed-loop recovery==.

## Results

- The Dream2Fix framework retained **58.1%** of synthesized rollouts after rigorous validation, resulting in a dataset of over 120,000 paired failure-correction samples with good visual fidelity and physical plausibility.
- Dream2Fix-VLM achieved a correction accuracy of **0.813** on the synthetic test set, and **0.472** on a real-world benchmark in a zero-shot setting, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- In real-world robotic deployments, Dream2Fix-VLM enabled closed-loop failure recovery with a **46%** recovery rate on a real-world benchmark and a **40%** recovery rate during OpenVLA task execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Photorealistic and physically plausible synthetic failure data, when rigorously validated, can effectively bridge the data scarcity challenge and sim-to-real gap for training robust robotic recovery policies.
> - Structured, deterministic generation of recovery suggestions, derived directly from the perturbation parameters, provides high-fidelity, actionable supervision for fine-tuning Vision-Language Models.
> - Fine-tuning a VLM with this specialized, validated failure-correction dataset enables it to jointly predict structured failure causes and precise trajectory-level corrections, outperforming general-purpose VLMs in both synthetic and zero-shot real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in foundation models, including vision-language models (VLMs) and vision-language-action (VLA) models, have enhanced robotic manipulation capabilities, particularly in areas like open-vocabulary perception and generalization. Despite these developments, autonomous recovery from execution errors remains a significant challenge for robotic systems. Current models often lack the causal understanding of failure dynamics necessary to synthesize executable corrective actions.

The ability for a robot to diagnose an anomaly, infer its root cause, and then translate that diagnosis into precise trajectory corrections is bottlenecked by the scarcity of relevant data. Collecting real-world failure data, especially with dense, paired correction annotations, is both costly and poses risks to hardware.

Prior approaches to equip foundation models with failure recovery capabilities generally fall into two categories:
1. **Reward signals or preference annotations:** These methods aim to refine policies using temporal heuristics or human-in-the-loop feedback. However, they often yield low-fidelity supervision and are not scalable.
2. **Simulation-based data generation:** This paradigm leverages procedural perturbations in simulators to generate data for training VLMs to output failure taxonomies. While scalable, this approach introduces a substantial "sim-to-real" gap, where models trained on synthetic data may not perform effectively in real-world scenarios due to discrepancies in visual realism and physical dynamics.

A further limitation of existing visual analyzers is their tendency to provide coarse, binary diagnoses (e.g., "success" or "failure," or general failure types) rather than specific, low-level, executable trajectory corrections. This creates a persistent gap between detecting a failure and implementing an actionable recovery. This research addresses this gap by proposing a method to generate photorealistic, physically plausible failure data with paired, actionable corrections directly from successful real-world demonstrations, aiming to overcome the sim-to-real gap and provide the dense supervision required for precise recovery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the existing gap between robotic failure diagnosis and the implementation of actionable recovery strategies. This involves enabling robotic systems to not only detect and classify errors but also to generate precise, executable corrections at the trajectory level.

The motivation behind this work stems from several critical limitations in current robotic manipulation systems and data generation paradigms:
- **Lack of Autonomous Recovery in Foundation Models:** While foundation models have improved nominal task execution, they do not inherently possess the causal understanding or reasoning capabilities required for autonomous recovery from real-world execution errors. Real-world scenarios are prone to unexpected failures that demand precise corrective actions, which current models struggle to synthesize.
- **Data Scarcity for Failure Recovery:** Training models to recover from failures requires extensive datasets of failure instances coupled with corresponding corrective actions. Such data is exceedingly difficult to acquire in the real world due to the infrequency of specific failures, the inherent cost, and the potential hazards to robotic hardware associated with intentionally inducing errors. Furthermore, when failures do occur, they rarely come with dense, trajectory-level correction annotations.
- **Limitations of Simulation-Based Data:** While simulation offers a scalable alternative for data generation, the resulting synthetic data often lacks visual realism and may not accurately reflect real-world physics. This "sim-to-real" gap can compromise the performance of models trained exclusively on simulated data when deployed in physical environments.
- **Insufficient Granularity of Existing Failure Diagnoses:** Prior work in failure analysis predominantly outputs high-level classifications, such as binary success/failure judgments or broad failure taxonomies. These coarse diagnoses are insufficient for guiding a robot toward recovery, which necessitates precise, low-level corrections to its trajectory or gripper actions. There is a clear need for systems that can translate visual anomalies directly into actionable control signals.

To address these motivations, the Dream2Fix framework was developed with the following specific objectives:
- To synthesize photorealistic and physically plausible counterfactual failure rollouts directly from successful real-world robotic demonstrations, thereby mitigating the sim-to-real gap.
- To generate paired failure-correction data without relying on traditional physics simulators.
- To implement a structured verification mechanism to rigorously filter synthesized data, ensuring task validity, visual coherence, kinematic safety, and dynamic consistency.
- To create a high-fidelity dataset of over 120,000 paired failure-correction samples to provide dense, actionable supervision.
- To fine-tune a vision-language model (Dream2Fix-VLM) capable of jointly predicting structured failure causes and precise, trajectory-level corrections, thereby directly mapping visual anomalies to recovery actions.
- To demonstrate improved correction accuracy and enable zero-shot closed-loop failure recovery in real-world robotic deployments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Dream2Fix framework is designed as a data generation pipeline that leverages generative models and structured validation to produce paired failure-correction data, subsequently used to fine-tune a vision-language model for actionable recovery. The methodology comprises three main stages: Photorealistic Failure Generation, Structured Failure Validation, and Learning from Failure.

#### 3.1. Photorealistic Failure Generation

This stage focuses on creating diverse and interpretable failure scenarios from existing successful robot demonstrations.
- **Failure Type Definition:** Four representative failure types are defined to cover common execution errors related to gripper control and spatial accuracy:
 - **Delay close:** Gripper closes too late, resulting in a missed or delayed grasp.
 - **Weak close:** Gripper closes with insufficient force or incompletely, leading to unstable contact or object drops.
 - **Force open:** Gripper fails to close at designated keyframes, remaining open due to policy errors.
 - **Translation:** End-effector pose (x-y states) perturbations during approach or contact, causing misalignment.
- **Base Generative World Model:** Ctrl-World, an action-conditioned generative world model, is used as the base. Unlike models conditioned on task descriptions, Ctrl-World's conditioning on robot actions allows for direct perturbation.
- **Action Perturbation:** Failures are synthesized by injecting targeted action perturbations into the generative world model. These perturbations are applied over a local temporal window around "keyframes" (timesteps of gripper state transitions). Examples include:
 - Adding temporal offsets for `delay close`.
 - Reducing closure magnitude for `weak close`.
 - Inverting gripper actions for `force open`.
 - Introducing Gaussian noise to the end-effector's x and y states for `translation` failures.
 These perturbations are designed to be minimal yet sufficient to produce visible and meaningful deviations. Each synthesized video is annotated with a result label (success/fail), a specific failure type, and a structured text-based correction.

#### 3.2. Structured Failure Validation

To ensure the physical plausibility and visual quality of the synthesized failure cases, a rigorous multi-verifier mechanism is employed. A generated sample is retained only if it passes all verification criteria across four dimensions:
- **VLM Verifier:** A pre-trained VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) is used to assess two aspects:
 - **Task Failure Validity:** Whether the generated clip genuinely represents the intended failure compared to a reference successful execution.
 - **Visual Quality:** Whether the video exhibits severe synthesis artifacts or visual distortions. Only valid failures with good visual quality are kept.
- **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) Verifier:** An IDM (ResNet-50 visual backbone followed by an MLP head) is trained on successful demonstrations to infer the robotic end-effector's state difference (`c_∆s`) from two video frames. This inferred difference is compared to the actual state difference (`∆s`) derived from the recorded actions used for generation. Samples with a discrepancy `e_t,d` exceeding a calibrated threshold `τ` (established from successful reference trajectories) are rejected, ensuring dynamic consistency.
- **Joint Pose Verifier:** A ResNet-50-based predictor estimates per-frame joint angles from the generated video. Joint velocities and accelerations are then computed. Any rollout where predicted joint angles violate joint limits or where velocities/accelerations exceed empirically calibrated thresholds (from successful trajectories) is discarded. This ensures kinematic safety and physical plausibility of joint motions.
- **Point-Tracking Verifier:** CoTracker3 is used to assess temporal coherence by tracking a grid of points across frames. Four complementary metrics are calculated and combined into a total score `S_pt`:
 - `S_smooth`: Measures motion smoothness based on discrete acceleration and spike transients.
 - `S_vis`: Assesses visibility stability of tracked points.
 - `S_topo`: Evaluates local topology stability by tracking pairwise distances of KNN edges.
 - `S_global`: Measures global continuity by assessing affine transform alignment errors and jitter between frames.
Clips with low scores or insufficient tracking confidence are rejected.

#### 3.3. Fix Suggestion Generation

For each verified failure rollout, an actionable recovery is deterministically generated as a structured text label and a natural language summary. This structure allows direct parsing by downstream robot controllers.
- **Structured Schema:** The output adheres to a fixed key-value format (e.g., `RESULT=FAIL; TYPE=translation; STAGE=pre_grasp; FIX_DIR_X=+x; FIX_N_X=2; SUMMARY:...`).
- **Deterministic Generation:** The recovery suggestions are generated directly from the perturbation parameters initially injected to create the failure, ensuring consistent and self-paired supervision.
- **Specifics for Failure Types:**
 - **Gripper Failures:** Specifies a corrected gripper command and a temporal anchor (keyframe) for its execution.
 - **Translation Failures:** Defines an axis-aligned correction direction and a discrete step count along each axis.
This approach avoids free-form recovery generation to maintain output stability and unambiguousness.

#### 3.4. Learning from Failure (VLM Finetuning)

- **Problem Formulation:** Failure recovery is framed as a structured prediction task.
- **Model:** Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct is fine-tuned, referred to as Dream2Fix-VLM.
- **Architecture:** The VLM comprises a frozen vision encoder, a fine-tuned multimodal projector (mapping visual features to language embedding space), and a fine-tuned transformer-based language model.
- **Training:** The model is trained to take a single-view manipulation video and a task instruction as input, and output the structured response (result, failure type, actionable correction, summary) autoregressively. Training is performed on the Dream2Fix dataset.

#### 3.5. Closed-Loop Recovery for VLA

Dream2Fix enables real-world closed-loop recovery by integrating its VLM outputs with robot control primitives.
- **Base VLA Execution:** A base VLA policy is executed on a real robot, and its trajectory is recorded.
- **Failure Detection and VLM Invocation:** The recorded trajectory is downsampled and processed by Dream2Fix-VLM upon failure detection (e.g., by an event-aligned rule or fixed action budget).
- **Parsing and Execution:** A deterministic mapping module parses the VLM's structured actionable recovery output into robot-specific control primitives (e.g., translation deltas, gripper close commands at specific times).
- **Trajectory Replay:** The robot executes the corrected trajectory, and the outcome is recorded. Successfully recovered rollouts can be added back to a training database for further policy improvement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from data quality verification, quantitative VLM evaluations on both synthetic and real-world benchmarks, and real-world robotic experiments.

#### 4.1. Data Quality Verification

- **Retention Rate:** Out of all synthesized rollouts, 41.9% were rejected by the structured validation mechanism, and 58.1% were retained, resulting in a final dataset of over 120,000 paired failure-correction samples. This retention rate suggests that while the generative world model is effective, rigorous verification is essential to ensure data quality.
- **Metric Comparison (Table II):** Synthesized failures were compared against ground-truth successful demonstrations using the verifier metrics.
 - **Temporal Coherence:** Point-tracking scores (S_smooth, S_vis, S_topo, S_global) for synthesized failures remained comparable to those of ground-truth demonstrations (e.g., S_smooth: GT 0.814, Generated 0.823), indicating preservation of temporal smoothness and visual consistency.
 - **Dynamic Consistency:** IDM consistency errors (MAE_xyz and MAE_rpy) showed moderate, controlled discrepancies (e.g., MAE_xyz: GT 0.008, Generated 0.015), suggesting that synthesized failures maintain plausible end-effector transitions.
 - **Kinematic Safety:** Joint pose exceedance rates (ω exceedance @p95 and α exceedance @p95) for synthesized failures were within a controlled range (e.g., ω exceedance: GT 0.114, Generated 0.222), indicating plausible joint motions.
- **Conclusion on Data Quality:** The verification results suggest that Dream2Fix generates failure cases with good visual fidelity and physical plausibility suitable for training and evaluating recovery models.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results of VLM Evaluation

Dream2Fix-VLM was evaluated against several state-of-the-art open-source (LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5/3-VL) and closed-source (Gemini-3-Flash, GPT-4o) VLMs using a unified prompt and structured output format.
- **Dream2Fix Test Set (Synthetic Data):**
 - Dream2Fix-VLM achieved the highest scores across all reported metrics.
 - **Correction Accuracy (Acc):** Increased from 0.197 (GPT-4o) to 0.813, demonstrating a substantial improvement in the ability to produce precise and accurate recovery outputs.
 - **Other Metrics:** Significant gains were also observed in ROUGE-L (0.913), Cosine Similarity (0.941), Binary Success Accuracy (0.950), and LLM-based Fuzzy Match (0.771), indicating better overall understanding and response generation.
- **Real-World Benchmark (Zero-Shot):**
 - Performance metrics were generally lower compared to the synthetic dataset, as expected due to the inherent variability and domain shift in real-world data.
 - **Correction Accuracy (Acc):** Dream2Fix-VLM achieved the strongest performance with an Acc of 0.472, outperforming all baselines (e.g., Gemini-3-Flash at 0.374, GPT-4o at 0.126). This highlights its ability to generalize to real-world scenarios without specific real-world fine-tuning.
 - Some baselines showed high binary success rates (e.g., Gemini-3-Flash 0.980, Qwen3-VL-8B 0.930) but low correction accuracy, suggesting they could often detect failures but struggled to provide actionable recovery instructions. Dream2Fix-VLM demonstrated a better balance between failure detection and actionable correction.

#### 4.3. Downstream Robotic Experiments

Real-world experiments were conducted in two settings to assess the practical impact of Dream2Fix-VLM. The model was applied in a zero-shot manner, having only been trained on synthetic Dream2Fix data.
- **Real-World Benchmark Recovery:** When applying VLM-predicted corrections to failures observed in the Real-World Benchmark, Dream2Fix-VLM achieved a **46% recovery rate**. This compares to Gemini-3-Flash which achieved a 36% recovery rate.
- **OpenVLA Deployment Recovery:** During deployment with OpenVLA on manipulation tasks, Dream2Fix-VLM was used to detect failures and provide real-time corrections. It achieved a **40% recovery rate**, while Gemini-3-Flash resulted in a 30% recovery rate.
- **Conclusion on Downstream Performance:** These results confirm that the corrections predicted by Dream2Fix-VLM can be successfully executed by a physical robot to recover from real-world manipulation failures, demonstrating closed-loop recovery capabilities and practical utility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, Dream2Fix, that addresses a critical challenge in robotic manipulation: autonomous recovery from execution errors. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging Diagnosis and Actionable Recovery:** A primary contribution is moving beyond coarse failure detection to provide precise, trajectory-level corrective actions. This directly addresses the long-standing gap where robots could identify a failure but lacked the capability to generate executable fixes. By providing structured, actionable recovery directives, Dream2Fix enables robots to directly translate visual anomalies into corrective control commands, enhancing their autonomy and robustness.
- **Novel Data Generation Paradigm:** Dream2Fix introduces an innovative approach to generating high-quality failure data. By synthesizing photorealistic, counterfactual failure rollouts directly from successful real-world demonstrations using a generative world model, it bypasses the limitations of both expensive and hazardous real-world data collection and the sim-to-real gap inherent in purely simulated data. The rigorous physical and visual verification mechanism ensures the plausibility and utility of the generated data, which is crucial for training effective recovery policies.
- **Creation of a High-Fidelity Dataset:** The development of a large (over 120,000 samples) and carefully curated dataset of paired failure-correction samples is a valuable resource for the robotics community. This dataset provides dense supervision necessary for training advanced models to learn nuanced failure dynamics and corresponding recovery strategies.
- **Enhanced VLM Capabilities for Robotics:** The research demonstrates that fine-tuning VLMs on this type of synthetically generated, validated data significantly improves their ability to reason about and correct robotic failures. The substantial gains in correction accuracy, even in zero-shot real-world settings, indicate that this approach can effectively transfer learning from synthetic scenarios to physical deployments.
- **Enabling Robust Autonomous Systems:** The ability for robots to diagnose and autonomously recover from their own errors is fundamental for their deployment in complex, unstructured real-world environments. Dream2Fix contributes to the development of more resilient and capable robotic systems by equipping them with an essential self-correction mechanism, reducing the need for human intervention.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work also highlights areas for future improvement, particularly in enhancing the physical consistency and realism of action-conditioned world models. Further refinements in generative models could lead to even higher-fidelity data, expanding the range of failure modes that can be reliably synthesized and addressed. This research lays a foundation for future advancements in robot learning for failure recovery.
