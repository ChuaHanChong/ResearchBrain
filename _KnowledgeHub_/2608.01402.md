# Demystifying When and Why VLAs Fail in Contact-Rich Tasks and How to Fix Them

> [!summary] Summary
> Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with contact-rich manipulation tasks due to precision and force-handling deficiencies. This work from Stanford University diagnoses these issues and introduces FACT (Force-Aware Contact-Rich Manipulation via Timestep Modulation), a method that improves performance by up to +45 percentage points through a refined training schedule and time-aware force injection.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models consistently underperform in contact-rich manipulation tasks that demand precise physical interaction and continuous force regulation.
- Current VLA training paradigms and force integration methods do not adequately address the unique properties of force signals (e.g., sparsity, temporal structure) or the need for fine-grained actions during contact.
- Failures were not clearly understood beyond the general assumption of lacking force feedback, with specific root causes in training procedures or force signal characteristics remaining undiagnosed.

## Method

- A ==Logit-Normal (LN) noise schedule== is employed during post-training to redistribute gradient signal towards the low-noise denoising regime, crucial for learning precise, sub-millimeter contact corrections.
- A ==Time-Aware Force Injection== mechanism integrates force signals by modulating sensitivity based on contact state, incorporating force history as temporal context, and using a ==gradient gate== to handle contact sparsity.
- These solutions are combined into **FACT** (Force-Aware Contact-Rich Manipulation via Timestep Modulation) to address both precision and force-handling deficiencies in VLA models.

## Results

- FACT achieved a **66.0%** average success rate across five real-world contact-rich tasks, surpassing the best prior baseline (ForceVLA) by over **25 percentage points**.
- The Logit-Normal noise schedule alone boosted success rates by up to **+20 pp** on precision-critical tasks and **+45 pp** on force-critical tasks, validating its effectiveness for fine-grained action learning.
- Time-aware force injection provided additional gains of up to **+22.5 pp** on force-critical tasks, with specific components like force history identified as crucial for exploiting temporal force information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA failures in contact-rich tasks arise from distinct issues: precision failures due to undersupervised low-noise denoising and "delta collapse", and force failures from inadequate exploitation of force signal properties.
> - Standard flow-matching noise schedules allocate insufficient gradient signal to the low-noise regime, hindering the learning of sub-millimeter corrections vital for contact-rich manipulation.
> - Effective force integration requires accounting for force signal characteristics like sparsity, temporal context, and context-dependent sensitivity, which are not addressed by simple force concatenation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated capabilities in robotic manipulation across various general tasks such as object retrieval and folding. However, their performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks, which demand precise physical interaction and continuous force regulation, has remained a challenge. These tasks involve scenarios where contact forces fluctuate due to factors like part geometry and material properties, and visual feedback can be degraded by self-occlusion at critical moments.

Prior efforts to address this performance gap have largely focused on augmenting VLA architectures with force feedback. These approaches typically involve specialized fusion modules or using force signals for auxiliary supervision during training. This existing body of work implicitly assumes that the primary limitation is a lack of force sensing or that force signals can be integrated similarly to other sensory modalities (vision, proprioception). Consequently, the underlying causes of failure, particularly those related to the training procedures of VLA models or the specific characteristics of force signals, have been less thoroughly investigated.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by shifting the focus from solely architectural force integration to a diagnostic approach. It aims to identify the root causes of VLA failures in contact-rich tasks, proposing that deficiencies extend beyond simply adding force sensors. The work builds upon the foundation of flow-matching policy formulations, a common paradigm in recent VLA models, and seeks to refine their training and sensory integration mechanisms to enhance performance in precision and force-sensitive interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to understand the specific conditions and reasons why Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models experience failures in contact-rich manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is driven by the observation that despite advances in VLA capabilities for general manipulation, these models consistently underperform in scenarios requiring precise physical interaction and continuous force regulation.

The detailed objectives and motivations are:

- **Diagnose Root Causes of Failure:** The initial objective is to move beyond simply augmenting VLAs with force feedback and instead systematically diagnose the fundamental reasons for their shortcomings in contact-rich environments. The authors hypothesize that failures may stem from a combination of training procedure mismatches and inherent properties of force signals that are not adequately handled by current VLA designs.
- **Identify Distinct Failure Modes:** Through diagnosis, the authors aim to characterize specific, causally distinct failure modes. They propose two such categories:
 - **Precision Failures:** Motivated by the observation that contact tasks require sub-millimeter corrective actions. The authors hypothesize these failures arise because standard flow-matching training paradigms, particularly their noise schedules, undersupervise the low-noise denoising regime crucial for generating these fine-grained actions. The phenomenon of "delta collapse," where action magnitudes become very small during contact, exacerbates this training deficiency.
 - **Force Failures:** Motivated by the fact that force signals are critical for tasks where visual information is ambiguous or insufficient. The authors hypothesize these failures occur because existing force-augmented VLA models do not effectively exploit the unique properties of force signals, such as their sparsity (informative only during contact), temporal structure (history and transients matter), and the need for modulated sensitivity (force influence should vary with contact state).
- **Develop Targeted Solutions for Each Failure Mode:** Based on the identified root causes, the research aims to develop specific, mechanistic interventions. For precision failures, the objective is to modify the training process to better allocate gradient signal to the critical low-noise regime. For force failures, the objective is to design a force injection mechanism that respects and exploits the distinctive characteristics of force signals.
- **Integrate Solutions into a Coherent Framework:** The developed solutions are intended to be combined into a single, comprehensive method capable of addressing both types of failures synergistically. This integrated approach is termed FACT (Force-Aware Contact-Rich Manipulation via Timestep Modulation).
- **Empirically Validate the Proposed Method:** A crucial objective is to rigorously evaluate FACT against established baselines across a diverse set of real-world contact-rich tasks. This evaluation aims to quantify the performance gains and conduct ablation studies to confirm the contribution of each proposed component and the validity of the underlying diagnoses.

The motivation for this work stems from the practical necessity of enabling robots to perform complex assembly, insertion, and manipulation tasks that are ubiquitous in industrial and domestic settings. Current VLA models, despite their generalization capabilities, are limited in these specific domains, hindering the deployment of generalist robots in scenarios requiring robust physical interaction. By systematically dissecting the causes of failure and proposing targeted solutions, this research seeks to bridge a significant gap in robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves a multi-stage approach encompassing diagnosis of failure modes, development of targeted solutions, and comprehensive experimental validation.

#### 3.1. Diagnosis of Failure Modes

The authors diagnose two distinct failure modes in VLA policies during contact-rich tasks:

- **Precision Failures:** These occur when VLA policies struggle to execute the precise, sub-millimeter corrective actions required during contact.
 - **Root Cause 1: Delta Collapse:** During contact, the required end-effector action deltas become very small. This represents a significant distributional shift from free-space manipulation, where actions are larger and more varied.
 - **Root Cause 2: Training Starvation:** The flow-matching action heads, typically used in VLA models like π$_{0.5}$, generate these fine-grained corrections in the low-τ denoising regime. However, commonly used noise schedules (e.g., Beta schedules) concentrate only a small fraction (e.g., 8.9% for τ < 0.2) of their probability mass and thus gradient signal on this critical low-noise regime. This undersupervision leads to a policy that is not adequately trained for the small, accurate corrections demanded by contact.

- **Force Failures:** These occur when force-augmented VLAs fail to effectively use force feedback, particularly in visually ambiguous scenarios.
 - **Root Cause 1: Contact Sparsity:** Force signals are predominantly zero during free-space movement and become informative only during brief contact intervals. Naive concatenation of force signals with other modalities can lead to gradients being biased towards ignoring force input.
 - **Root Cause 2: Temporal Structure:** Force provides information at multiple timescales. Instantaneous readings indicate the current state, while force history reveals dynamics, transients, and cumulative force buildup. Existing methods often fail to capture this temporal context.
 - **Root Cause 3: Sensitivity Modulation:** The influence of force on policy actions should be context-dependent. It should be minimal in free space but significantly heightened during contact, a nuanced interaction often not modeled by simple fusion.

#### 3.2. Proposed Solutions (FACT Components)

The authors propose targeted interventions for each identified failure mode, which are combined into their method called FACT (Force-Aware Contact-Rich Manipulation via Timestep Modulation).

- **Fixing Precision Failures: Logit-Normal (LN) Noise Schedule**
 - To address training starvation in the low-τ regime, the standard Beta noise schedule is replaced with a Logit-Normal (LN) schedule during post-training.
 - The LN schedule, specifically parameterized with a location parameter `m=1.5`, reallocates training signal towards lower τ values. This results in approximately six times more gradient signal being allocated to the τ < 0.2 regime compared to the default Beta schedule, thus reinforcing the learning of fine-grained, contact-correction actions.
 - This modification requires no changes to the VLA model architecture, no additional training data, and introduces no extra parameters.

- **Fixing Force Failures: Time-Aware Force Injection**
 - This mechanism integrates force signals by accounting for their specific properties, as illustrated in Figure 3b.
 - **Contact State (Sensitivity Modulation):** The current force reading (`f_t`) is injected into the AdaRMSNorm layers of the action generation module. A 6-dimensional summary (`¯f_t`) of the most recent force sensor window is passed through a two-layer MLP to produce a per-layer scale modulation (`∆γ`). This `∆γ` dynamically adjusts the policy's responsiveness based on the contact state.
 - **Contact History (Temporal Structure):** A history of the preceding `H=30` steps (approximately 2 seconds) of F/T readings (`{f_t−H , . . . , f_t−1 }`) is encoded independently by a shared causal encoder. The resulting tokens are prepended to the input of the action generation module, providing temporal context for force reasoning.
 - **Contact Gating (Contact Sparsity):** A gradient gate with a threshold `δ` (0.5 N) is applied to block gradients through all force encoding components when no contact is detected. This prevents the force encoders from learning from uninformative near-zero force readings.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Tasks:** Five contact-rich tasks were used:
 - **Precision-critical:** Plug insertion, USB insertion (require sub-millimeter corrections under partial occlusion).
 - **Force-critical:** Key insertion (full occlusion, recognize hard-stop), Button push (probe until force threshold), Board erasing (maintain consistent contact).
- **Hardware:** Experiments used a Franka Research 3 arm with a wrist-mounted Bota SensONE 6-axis F/T sensor, an external Realsense D435 camera, and a wrist-mounted ZED Mini camera.
- **Data Collection:** 100 teleoperated demonstrations per task were collected using a Haply Inverse 3 haptic device, with varied target and home positions. Visual and proprioceptive data were recorded at 15 Hz, and F/T data at 400 Hz (pre-processed with a one-euro filter).
- **Training:** All methods, including baselines, were fine-tuned from a pre-trained π$_{0.5}$ checkpoint using LoRA for 20,000 steps on the collected demonstrations.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against the standard π$_{0.5}$ policy and two force-conditioned baselines: ForceVLA and TA-VLA. These baselines were re-implemented on the π$_{0.5}$ backbone for controlled comparison. Additional evaluations were conducted using the π$_{0}$ backbone.
- **Metrics and Evaluation:** Each method was evaluated on 40 independent real-world rollouts per task, totaling nearly 2,500 rollouts. Success rate (SR %) was reported, and statistical significance was assessed using Fisher’s exact test against the π$_{0.5}$ baseline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated FACT and its components against baselines across five contact-rich tasks, supporting the diagnoses and efficacy of the proposed solutions.

- **Impact of Logit-Normal (LN) Noise Schedule on Precision-Critical Tasks:**
 - Replacing the Beta schedule with LN (π$_{0.5}$ + LN) resulted in improved performance on precision-critical tasks. Plug insertion success rate increased by +20 percentage points (pp) (from 30.0% to 50.0%, p=0.055), and USB insertion by +10 pp (from 37.5% to 47.5%, p=0.249).
 - Gains were also observed on force-critical tasks: Button push increased by +45 pp (from 12.5% to 57.5%, p<0.001), and Key insertion by +22.5 pp (from 15.0% to 37.5%, p=0.020). These gains were achieved without additional data or parameters, supporting the hypothesis that the default schedule undersupervises the contact-correction regime.

- **Impact of Time-Aware Force Injection on Force-Critical Tasks:**
 - Adding time-aware force injection on top of π$_{0.5}$ + LN (i.e., FACT) yielded further significant improvements on force-critical tasks. Button push performance increased by +17.5 pp (from 57.5% to 75.0%, p<0.001) and Key insertion by +22.5 pp (from 37.5% to 60.0%, p<0.001) compared to π$_{0.5}$ + LN. This suggests that explicit force reasoning is essential for tasks where visual observation is ambiguous or insufficient.
 - For precision-critical tasks (plug and USB insertion), the added force signal did not provide statistically significant benefits, indicating that failures in these tasks were primarily due to insufficient denoising time rather than lack of force feedback.
 - Board erasing showed high success rates across all methods (around 90-100%), attributed to the compliant operational-space controller effectively managing sustained contact force, reducing the policy's reliance on explicit force reasoning.

- **Ablation Study of Force Components:**
 - An ablation study of FACT's force components demonstrated that all three (force history, instantaneous reading, gradient threshold) contribute to performance, particularly on force-critical tasks (Table 3).
 - **Force history** showed the largest impact, with success rates dropping by −62.5 pp for button push and −40 pp for key insertion when removed. This supports the importance of temporal integration for force-critical tasks.
 - The **instantaneous force reading** had a smaller but notable effect, especially on key insertion (−25 pp), where the hard-stop peak serves as a critical completion cue.
 - The **gradient threshold** was deemed necessary for force-critical tasks, where contact events carry decisive completion signals.

- **Generality of LN Schedule:**
 - Adding the LN schedule to existing force-augmented baselines (TA-VLA and ForceVLA) on the π$_{0.5}$ backbone consistently improved their performance across selected tasks (Table 2). This indicates that the Beta schedule imbalance is a significant bottleneck, and LN can serve as a compatible enhancement for other flow-based VLAs.

- **Transferability to Different VLA Backbones (π$_{0}$):**
 - FACT was evaluated with the π$_{0}$ backbone (FACTπ$_{0}$), where it achieved 70.0% on plug insertion, 50.0% on key insertion, and 60.0% on button push (Table 4). This performance surpassed both ForceVLAπ$_{0}$ and TA-VLAπ$_{0}$, demonstrating that the principles of LN and time-aware force injection generalize across different flow-matching VLA backbones.
 - A comparison between FACTπ$_{0.5}$ and FACTπ$_{0}$ indicated that π$_{0.5}$ generally performed better on force-critical tasks (over +10 pp), possibly due to its more explicit timestep conditioning. Conversely, π$_{0}$ showed a +12.5 pp advantage on the precision-critical plug insertion task, potentially reflecting differences in its pretraining or state representation beneficial for fine corrections.

- **Overall Performance:**
 - FACT$_{π_{0.5}}$ achieved an average success rate of 66.0% across the five tasks. This significantly outperformed the best prior baseline, ForceVLA$_{π_{0.5}}$ (40.5%), and TA-VLA$_{π_{0.5}}$ (37.5%), with consistent gains observed across both precision- and force-critical task categories.

- **Noise Ablation (Appendix D):**
 - An additional ablation study, where the F/T stream was replaced with i.i.d. Gaussian noise, confirmed that FACT's gains result from genuine exploitation of task-relevant force information rather than mere regularization. FACT's performance dropped significantly (e.g., button push from 60.0% to 5.0%, key insertion from 75.0% to 17.5%) when force signals were randomized, while baselines like TA-VLA showed less or no meaningful dependence on real F/T, suggesting they did not reliably extract force information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a nuanced understanding and targeted solutions for challenges faced by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in contact-rich tasks.

**Conceptual Significance:**
The paper's primary conceptual contribution is the principled decomposition of VLA failures in contact-rich tasks into two causally distinct modes: precision failures and force failures. This reframes the problem beyond simply integrating force sensing, highlighting that issues can stem from the policy training process itself (precision failures) or from an inadequate understanding of force signal characteristics (force failures). This diagnostic approach provides a more granular framework for analyzing and addressing robotic manipulation challenges.

**Methodological Significance:**
The proposed FACT (Force-Aware Contact-Rich Manipulation via Timestep Modulation) method introduces two key mechanisms:
1. **Logit-Normal (LN) Noise Schedule:** This schedule specifically addresses precision failures by reallocating gradient signal to the low-noise denoising regime crucial for sub-millimeter corrections. Its significance lies in its ability to improve performance across flow-based VLA architectures without requiring additional parameters, data, or architectural modifications, making it a broadly applicable and efficient intervention.
2. **Time-Aware Force Injection:** This mechanism addresses force failures by integrating force signals in a manner that accounts for their sparsity, temporal structure, and the need for sensitivity modulation. Its design, incorporating current force readings for adaptive normalization and force history as temporal tokens, represents a refined approach to leveraging haptic feedback effectively, particularly in visually ambiguous scenarios.

**Practical Impact:**
The substantial performance gains demonstrated by FACT (66% average success rate vs. 41% for the best prior baseline across five tasks) indicate a direct practical impact on the reliability of robots performing complex contact-rich tasks. This includes assembly operations, connector insertions, and tasks requiring sustained force regulation. Improved reliability in these domains could accelerate the deployment of generalist robotic systems in industrial, service, and even domestic environments where precise physical interaction is necessary.

**Generalizability and Future Directions:**
The research demonstrates that both the LN noise schedule and time-aware force injection transfer across different flow-matching VLA backbones (π$_{0}$ and π$_{0.5}$) and can improve existing force-augmented baselines. This suggests that the identified failure modes and proposed solutions address general limitations within the flow-matching VLA paradigm, rather than being specific to a single architecture.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as evaluation on a single robot platform and a specific set of tasks. The methods are also tailored to flow-matching action heads and transformer-based architectures. Future research could explore the generalization of these principles to diverse robot kinematics, sensor modalities, alternative VLA architectures (e.g., autoregressive or diffusion policies), and a broader range of contact-rich scenarios with varied geometries and material properties. The conceptual framework and methodological insights provided by this work lay a foundation for developing more robust and capable robotic systems for complex physical interactions.
