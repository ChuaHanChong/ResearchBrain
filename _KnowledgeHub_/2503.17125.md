# LaMOuR: Leveraging Language Models for Out-of-Distribution Recovery in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> LaMOuR, a framework developed at Seoul National University, enables Deep Reinforcement Learning agents to recover from Out-of-Distribution (OOD) states by leveraging Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to generate dense, explicit reward codes. The system guides agents back to a valid state to resume their original tasks, demonstrating effectiveness in complex locomotion and manipulation scenarios.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies are susceptible to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) states, leading to unpredictable actions and task failure in real-world deployment.
- Existing DRL research predominantly focuses on preventing or minimizing OOD states, often neglecting the crucial challenge of enabling agent recovery *after* encountering an OOD state.
- Prior recovery methods, such as SeRO, rely on uncertainty estimation, which degrades in accuracy and limits scalability in complex, high-dimensional environments.

## Method

- **LaMOuR** utilizes a ==Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM)== to generate dense, explicit reward codes for recovery, removing the dependency on uncertainty estimation.
- The LVLM workflow consists of three stages: OOD description (visual observation to textual description), behavior reasoning (inferring necessary recovery actions), and code generation (producing executable reward and evaluation functions).
- A modified reward function dynamically switches between the original task reward and the LVLM-generated recovery reward, complemented by Language Model-Guided Policy Consolidation (LPC) to preserve original task knowledge.

## Results

- LaMOuR demonstrated enhanced sample efficiency and superior recovery performance across four MuJoCo locomotion tasks, outperforming uncertainty-based and other baseline methods.
- The framework successfully enabled agents to recover from OOD states in complex environments, including a DeepMind Humanoid learning to stand up and a ManiSkill2 PushChair robot righting a fallen chair with a **79.8%** success rate from OOD states.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the Behavior Reasoning module for generating exclusively recovery-focused rewards and highlighted the utility of few-shot examples in guiding the LVLM to produce structured, sequential reward signals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Vision-Language Models can effectively translate visual observations of OOD states into precise language descriptions, reason about appropriate recovery behaviors, and synthesize executable Python code for dense reward functions.
> - Dense, explicitly generated reward signals from LVLMs offer superior guidance for OOD recovery compared to uncertainty-based or generic task rewards, particularly in complex, high-dimensional environments.
> - Selective policy consolidation, applied only when the agent is in a valid state, allows agents to acquire new recovery behaviors without exhibiting catastrophic forgetting of their pre-trained original task skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has demonstrated considerable success in various domains, particularly in robotic control. However, a persistent challenge in DRL, akin to other deep learning methodologies, is the susceptibility of learned policies to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) states. When an agent encounters states that deviate significantly from its training distribution, it often produces unreliable actions, leading to task failure.

The existing research landscape addressing OOD states in DRL can be broadly categorized into two main approaches:
1. **Exploration-based methods:** These strategies aim to minimize the occurrence of OOD states by encouraging the agent to explore the state space more thoroughly during training, thereby maximizing state coverage and reducing the likelihood of encountering truly novel states during deployment (e.g.,–).
2. **Prevention methods:** These approaches focus on actively preventing the agent from entering OOD states. This often involves leveraging model-based RL to avoid states with high model uncertainty, regularizing policies to prevent distribution shifts, or employing techniques in offline RL to ensure policies remain within the learned data distribution.

While these methods contribute to mitigating the *impact* or *occurrence* of OOD states, they largely overlook the critical challenge of *recovery* once an agent has inevitably entered an OOD state during real-world deployment. Real-world environments are inherently complex and extensive, making it impractical to completely eliminate OOD situations. For instance, a robot designed for locomotion might slip and fall, entering a state far removed from its training data.

A recent development in addressing this recovery gap was introduced by Kim et al. (SeRO), which proposed retraining agents to return to in-distribution states from OOD situations. This method utilized an auxiliary reward signal derived from epistemic uncertainty estimates. While demonstrating the feasibility of recovery learning, this approach's fundamental reliance on the accuracy of uncertainty estimation presents a significant limitation: uncertainty prediction performance degrades in complex, high-dimensional environments, hindering scalability.

This paper, LaMOuR, situates itself by directly addressing this scalability limitation in OOD recovery. It proposes a novel framework that, unlike previous recovery methods, *does not* depend on uncertainty estimation. Instead, LaMOuR leverages the advanced capabilities of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to generate dense, explicit reward codes that guide the agent back to a "valid state" where its original task can be resumed. By shifting the paradigm from uncertainty-based recovery to language model-guided reward generation, LaMOuR aims to provide a more robust and scalable solution for DRL agents in complex, real-world OOD scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) agents to effectively recover from Out-of-Distribution (OOD) states and return to a valid state where they can successfully continue or resume their original task. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives:

1. **Develop a recovery learning mechanism independent of uncertainty estimation:** A core objective is to overcome the inherent limitations of prior OOD recovery methods, such as SeRO, which rely on the accuracy of uncertainty estimation. In complex environments, where such estimations can be unreliable, this dependency restricts scalability. LaMOuR seeks to provide an alternative that bypasses this issue.
2. **Leverage Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for dense reward generation:** The research aims to utilize the advanced capabilities of LVLMs—specifically their prowess in image description, logical reasoning, and code generation—to automatically construct dense, explicit reward signals. These signals are designed to precisely guide the agent's actions during the recovery process, facilitating a transition from an OOD state back to a state where the original task can be performed.
3. **Prevent catastrophic forgetting of the original task during recovery learning:** While learning new recovery behaviors, the agent must retain its proficiency in the original task. An objective is to implement a mechanism that selectively consolidates the policy, ensuring that the original task knowledge is preserved without hindering the learning of new recovery actions in OOD contexts.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several critical aspects of DRL's application in real-world scenarios:

- **Inherent Susceptibility of DRL to OOD States:** DRL policies, like other deep learning models, are inherently vulnerable to states outside their training data distribution. This susceptibility leads to unpredictable and often unreliable actions, culminating in task failure, particularly in safety-critical applications like robotics.
- **Unavoidability of OOD States in Real-World Deployment:** Despite efforts to minimize or prevent OOD occurrences through exploration or robust training, the vast and complex nature of real-world environments makes it practically impossible to eliminate all novel or unforeseen situations. Agents will inevitably encounter OOD states during deployment, making effective recovery a necessity rather than a preventative measure.
- **Limitations of Existing Approaches:** Current research primarily focuses on reducing the *frequency* or *prevention* of OOD states. However, these methods do not provide a concrete strategy for *recovery* once an OOD state has been entered. The prior recovery method, while a step forward, suffers from scalability issues in complex environments due to its reliance on uncertainty estimation, which often degrades in performance in high-dimensional or less explored state spaces.
- **Potential of Advanced Language Models:** The recent advancements in LVLMs offer a new avenue for addressing this problem. Their ability to process multimodal inputs (e.g., images and text), perform sophisticated logical reasoning, and generate executable code suggests that they could be leveraged to interpret OOD scenarios, infer appropriate recovery actions, and synthesize precise reward functions, thereby automating a historically challenging aspect of RL—reward engineering for complex, transient situations.

By addressing these motivations, LaMOuR seeks to enhance the reliability and robustness of DRL agents, making them more suitable for real-world applications where unexpected events and failures are a part of operation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LaMOuR (Language Models for Out-of-Distribution Recovery) introduces a framework for retraining DRL agents to recover from OOD states by leveraging Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to generate dense recovery rewards. The methodology operates under two primary prerequisites: (1) the agent has already learned the original task and possesses a corresponding policy (π_org), and (2) the agent is already in an OOD state and is aware of it (OOD detection itself is outside the scope of this work). The core components of LaMOuR include recovery reward generation, a modified reward function, and language model-guided policy consolidation.

**A. Recovery Reward Generation**
The process of generating recovery rewards is orchestrated by an LVLM (specifically GPT-4o in this study) and comprises three sequential phases:

1. **OOD Description:**
 - **Input:** A third-person snapshot image (I_OOD) of the agent in its current OOD state, along with a prompt (p_OOD) that guides the LVLM.
 - **Process:** The LVLM analyzes the visual input and the prompt.
 - **Output:** A textual description (d_OOD) that accurately characterizes the agent's OOD state. This step translates visual information into a language format suitable for subsequent reasoning.

2. **Behavior Reasoning:**
 - **Input:** The textual description of the original task (d_task), the generated OOD state description (d_OOD), and a prompt (p_br).
 - **Process:** The LVLM applies chain-of-thought prompting. It is first prompted to identify a "valid state" where the original task can be performed. Subsequently, it infers the necessary sequence of actions or behaviors to transition from the described OOD state back to this identified valid state.
 - **Output:** A textual description of the inferred recovery behavior (d_recovery). This step represents the logical planning phase, determining "how" to recover.

3. **Code Generation:**
 - **Input:** The recovery behavior description (d_recovery), an environment description (d_env) providing details about the agent's state and action space, and a prompt (p_cg). For complex environments, a few-shot example may be included in p_cg to guide the structure of the reward code.
 - **Process:** The LVLM uses the textual descriptions to generate executable code.
 - **Output:**
 - **Dense reward code (c_reward):** This Python code takes the agent's state and action as input and returns a dense, scalar reward designed to reinforce the inferred recovery behavior.
 - **Evaluation code (c_eval):** This Python code takes the agent's state as input and returns `1` if the state is deemed a "valid state" (i.e., where the original task can be successfully executed), and `0` otherwise. This code acts as a real-time state classifier.

**B. Modified Reward Function for Retraining**
The generated `c_reward` and `c_eval` are integrated into a dynamic reward function (r_t) for the agent's retraining process:
$$ r_t = \begin{cases} r_{task} & \text{if } c_{eval}(s_t) = 1 \\ \lambda c_{reward}(s_{t+1}, a_t) & \text{if } c_{eval}(s_t) = 0 \end{cases} $$
Where:
- `r_task` is the reward provided by the environment for the original task.
- `λ` is a scaling coefficient.
This function ensures that the agent receives the recovery reward (`λc_reward`) when it is in an OOD state (where `c_eval(s_t) = 0`), guiding it back to a valid state. Once the agent reaches a valid state (`c_eval(s_t) = 1`), it switches back to receiving the original task reward (`r_task`), allowing it to continue practicing or performing the original task.

**C. Language Model-Guided Policy Consolidation (LPC)**
To prevent "catastrophic forgetting" of the original task while the agent learns recovery behaviors, a policy consolidation mechanism is introduced. Unlike standard regularization against an original policy (which could be detrimental in OOD states where the original policy is unreliable), LPC is applied conditionally:
$$ L_{LPC} = c_{eval}(s_t) D_{KL}(\pi_{\phi}(a_t|s_t) || \pi_{\phi_{org}}(a_t|s_t)) $$
Where:
- `D_KL` is the KL divergence.
- `π_ϕ` is the relearning policy.
- `π_ϕ_org` is the original, pre-trained policy.
If the agent is in an OOD state (`c_eval(s_t) = 0`), `L_LPC` becomes zero, and no policy consolidation occurs, allowing the policy `π_ϕ` to freely learn new recovery actions. If the agent is in a valid state (`c_eval(s_t) = 1`), `L_LPC` applies KL divergence regularization, encouraging `π_ϕ` to remain consistent with `π_ϕ_org`, thereby preserving the original task knowledge.

**D. Reinforcement Learning Algorithm**
The Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm is employed for training the policy (π_ϕ) and Q-functions (Q_θ). The Q-function is updated using the composite reward function (r_t) described above. The policy objective function is augmented to include the LPC loss, balancing entropy maximization, Q-value maximization, and selective policy consolidation.

**E. Experimental Setup**
- **Environments:** The method was evaluated across four modified MuJoCo locomotion tasks (Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2D) and two complex environments (DeepMind Control Suite Humanoid and ManiSkill2 PushChair) representing complex locomotion and mobile manipulation, respectively. OOD states were introduced by manually setting agents/objects into non-standard, "fallen" configurations.
- **Baselines:** Comparisons were made against SeRO (uncertainty-based recovery), SAC-env (original task reward in OOD), SAC-zero (zero reward in OOD), and for PushChair, SAC-T2R (LLM-generated original task reward).
- **Evaluation:** Performance was assessed using learning curves (mean returns and standard deviations) over training steps, and a success rate metric for the PushChair environment.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study was conducted in the PushChair environment to analyze the contribution of each module within the recovery reward generation pipeline (OOD Description, Behavior Reasoning, Code Generation, Few-Shot Example).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with LaMOuR yielded several findings regarding its efficiency, generalization capabilities, and the contribution of its constituent modules.

**A. Evaluation in Four MuJoCo Environments (Recovery Efficiency)**
In the four modified MuJoCo environments (Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2D), LaMOuR consistently demonstrated enhanced sample efficiency during retraining for OOD recovery compared to the baselines.
- LaMOuR exhibited faster learning to return to a valid state and restore original task performance.
- Compared to SeRO, LaMOuR generally achieved superior efficiency, with similar performance observed only in the HalfCheetah environment. This suggests that LaMOuR's dense, explicitly generated reward signals provided more precise guidance for recovery than the uncertainty-based rewards utilized by SeRO.
- Baselines such as SAC-env (using original task reward in OOD states) and SAC-zero (assigning zero reward in OOD states) largely failed to recover or struggled significantly to regain original performance, aligning with observations from prior research.

**B. Evaluation in Complex Environments (Generalization)**
LaMOuR's performance was evaluated in two complex environments to assess its generalization capabilities: the DeepMind Humanoid environment (where the robot starts lying down) and the ManiSkill2 PushChair environment (where a robotic arm must right a fallen chair).
- **Humanoid Environment:** LaMOuR successfully retrained the humanoid agent to stand up from a fallen OOD state and resume its running task. In contrast, all baselines (SeRO, SAC-env, SAC-zero) failed to learn an effective recovery behavior.
- **PushChair Environment:** LaMOuR enabled the dual-arm Panda robot to successfully recover the fallen chair to an upright position and then proceed to push it to the target location. The success rate from OOD states for LaMOuR was 79.8%, comparable to the original policy's 82% success rate from valid states. All baselines, including SAC-T2R (which uses an LLM for task reward generation but not OOD recovery), failed to learn recovery in this complex manipulation task.
- **Reasons for Baseline Failures:** SeRO's failure in complex environments was attributed to the diminished accuracy of uncertainty estimation in environments with higher-dimensional state and action spaces. SAC-T2R's inability to recover from OOD states was because its reward function, generated from the original task description, did not account for or reason about recovery from unforeseen OOD scenarios like a fallen chair.
- **Overall Finding:** These results indicate that LaMOuR generalizes effectively to complex locomotion and mobile manipulation tasks, successfully training agents for recovery where methods relying on uncertainty or generic task rewards falter. This success is attributed to the method's ability to generate dense, explicit reward codes tailored to the specific recovery behavior.

**C. Qualitative Analysis of Recovery Reward Generation**
A qualitative analysis in the PushChair environment provided insight into LaMOuR's reward generation process:
- **OOD Description:** The LVLM accurately described the OOD state (robot and fallen chair) from a visual input.
- **Behavior Reasoning:** The module correctly inferred the recovery behavior—repositioning the chair to an upright position—as a necessary step to return to a valid state.
- **Code Generation:** The LVLM successfully produced:
 - An `evaluation code` (`c_eval`) that correctly identified a valid state based on factors like the robot's end-effector distance to the chair and the chair's tilt angle.
 - A `reward code` (`c_reward`) that provided staged incentives: initially rewarding the robot for approaching the chair, and subsequently for reducing the chair's tilt angle (i.e., bringing it upright). This demonstrated the generation of a detailed, progressive recovery reward.

**D. Ablation Study**
An ablation study in the PushChair environment evaluated the contribution of individual components:
- **Behavior Reasoning Module's Importance:**
 - Ablations without the Behavior Reasoning module (A1, A2) resulted in generated reward codes that included terms related to the *original task* (e.g., "move the chair to the target") alongside recovery rewards. This could lead to local optima where the agent prioritizes the original task prematurely or inappropriately.
 - This finding highlights that the Behavior Reasoning module is crucial for ensuring the generated reward code is exclusively aligned with the desired recovery behavior, preventing the inclusion of extraneous task-related rewards.
- **LVLM's Multimodal Capability (A3):**
 - Directly providing the OOD state *image* to the Behavior Reasoning module (A3) instead of its language description produced equally effective reward code. This indicates the LVLM's robust multimodal input processing, allowing it to interpret OOD states from either visual or textual inputs for reasoning.
- **Few-Shot Example's Importance (A4):**
 - Excluding the few-shot example during Code Generation (A4) led to a reward code that focused solely on the chair's tilt angle, without explicitly rewarding the initial action of *approaching* the chair. While the reward was still aligned with the goal of righting the chair, it presented a more challenging learning problem for the agent, requiring random exploration to initiate contact.
 - This suggests that few-shot examples are important for guiding the LVLM to generate structured, step-by-step reward signals, particularly for recovery behaviors that inherently involve sequential sub-goals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LaMOuR represents a significant contribution to the field of Deep Reinforcement Learning, particularly in addressing the practical challenges of deploying DRL agents in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addresses a Critical Gap in DRL Robustness:** The work directly tackles the problem of OOD state recovery, a fundamental limitation often neglected by existing research that primarily focuses on OOD prevention or minimization. By providing a concrete mechanism for agents to recover from unforeseen failures, LaMOuR enhances the overall robustness and resilience of DRL systems. This is crucial for applications where agents cannot afford to simply fail or get stuck when encountering novel situations.
- **Overcomes Scalability Limitations of Prior Recovery Methods:** LaMOuR successfully moves beyond the reliance on uncertainty estimation, which has been shown to be a bottleneck for scalability in complex environments. By demonstrating effective recovery in high-dimensional tasks like humanoid locomotion and mobile manipulation where uncertainty-based methods fail, LaMOuR offers a more robust and generalizable approach for real-world scenarios.
- **Novel Application of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs):** The research showcases a novel and effective application of advanced LVLMs (like GPT-4o) for automating a complex aspect of DRL: reward engineering for recovery. By leveraging LVLMs for image description, logical reasoning about recovery behaviors, and subsequent code generation for dense rewards, LaMOuR demonstrates the potential for these models to assist in building more adaptive and intelligent autonomous systems. This could pave the way for more automated and less human-intensive reward design in other challenging RL problems.
- **Enhances DRL Reliability and Deployability:** The ability for DRL agents to intelligently recover from OOD situations directly translates to increased reliability. This is particularly impactful for robotic control, where unexpected sensor readings, environmental changes, or physical disturbances can lead to OOD states. Enabling recovery allows robots to continue performing tasks even after minor failures, reducing downtime and the need for human intervention.
- **Potential for Broader Impact Across AI and Robotics:** The principles demonstrated by LaMOuR could extend beyond the specific locomotion and manipulation tasks studied. The concept of using generative AI to define adaptive behaviors and their associated rewards based on contextual understanding of anomalous states has implications for other areas requiring flexible adaptation, such as intelligent agents in complex simulations, autonomous driving, or even human-robot collaboration scenarios where unexpected human actions might occur.
- **Foundation for Future Autonomous Systems:** The paper identifies a promising future direction: integrating OOD detection with the recovery framework. A fully autonomous system that can *detect* OOD states and then *trigger* LaMOuR's recovery mechanism would represent a significant step towards truly self-sufficient and adaptable intelligent agents capable of continuous operation in dynamic environments. This integration could lead to the development of more complete and resilient autonomous systems.
