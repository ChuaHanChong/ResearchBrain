# LaMOuR: Leveraging Language Models for Out-of-Distribution Recovery in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> LaMOuR, a framework developed at Seoul National University, enables Deep Reinforcement Learning agents to recover from Out-of-Distribution (OOD) states by leveraging Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to generate dense, explicit reward codes. The system guides agents back to a valid state to resume their original tasks, demonstrating effectiveness in complex locomotion and manipulation scenarios.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies are susceptible to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) states, leading to unpredictable actions and task failure in real-world deployment.
- Existing DRL research predominantly focuses on preventing or minimizing OOD states, often neglecting the crucial challenge of enabling agent recovery *after* encountering an OOD state.
- Prior recovery methods, such as SeRO, rely on uncertainty estimation, which degrades in accuracy and limits scalability in complex, high-dimensional environments.

## Method

- **LaMOuR** utilizes a ==Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM)== to generate dense, explicit reward codes for recovery, removing the dependency on uncertainty estimation.
- The LVLM workflow consists of three stages: OOD description (visual observation to textual description), behavior reasoning (inferring necessary recovery actions), and code generation (producing executable reward and evaluation functions).
- A modified reward function dynamically switches between the original task reward and the LVLM-generated recovery reward, complemented by Language Model-Guided Policy Consolidation (LPC) to preserve original task knowledge.

## Results

- LaMOuR demonstrated enhanced sample efficiency and superior recovery performance across four MuJoCo locomotion tasks, outperforming uncertainty-based and other baseline methods.
- The framework successfully enabled agents to recover from OOD states in complex environments, including a DeepMind Humanoid learning to stand up and a ManiSkill2 PushChair robot righting a fallen chair with a **79.8%** success rate from OOD states.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the Behavior Reasoning module for generating exclusively recovery-focused rewards and highlighted the utility of few-shot examples in guiding the LVLM to produce structured, sequential reward signals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Vision-Language Models can effectively translate visual observations of OOD states into precise language descriptions, reason about appropriate recovery behaviors, and synthesize executable Python code for dense reward functions.
> - Dense, explicitly generated reward signals from LVLMs offer superior guidance for OOD recovery compared to uncertainty-based or generic task rewards, particularly in complex, high-dimensional environments.
> - Selective policy consolidation, applied only when the agent is in a valid state, allows agents to acquire new recovery behaviors without exhibiting catastrophic forgetting of their pre-trained original task skills.
