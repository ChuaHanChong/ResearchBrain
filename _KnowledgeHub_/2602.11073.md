# Chatting with Images for Introspective Visual Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> The VILAVT model introduces a "chatting with images" framework, which redefines visual reasoning as language-guided feature modulation for large vision-language models. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on 5 out of 8 benchmarks for general, single-image, multi-image, and video-based spatial reasoning tasks, demonstrating improved versatility and robustness to information loss at lower resolutions.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) operating under the 'thinking about images' paradigm suffer irreversible information loss due to single-pass visual encoding.
- Existing 'thinking with images' approaches (tool-based, programmatic, generative imagination) often lack expressiveness for high-level relational intent, incur high latency, or struggle with joint reasoning across multiple visual inputs.
- Challenges in establishing effective cross-modal alignment and grounding between linguistic semantics and low-level visual manipulation interfaces, especially for complex visual semantics or geometric relationships.

## Method

- Introduces the =='chatting with images' paradigm==, which models visual reasoning as generating an iterative trajectory of textual reasoning, natural language inquiries, and targeted visual region re-examinations.
- Develops ==VILAVT==, a novel LVLM featuring a ==dynamic vision encoder== that jointly processes multiple, potentially non-contiguous visual regions conditioned on a natural language inquiry using a hybrid attention strategy.
- Employs a two-stage training strategy: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) on a multi-domain dataset of repurposed and synthesized reasoning trajectories, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) with an outcome-based reward function.

## Results

- VILAVT achieved state-of-the-art results on **5** out of **8** diverse benchmarks, encompassing General VQA, single-image, multi-image, and video-based spatial reasoning tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the 'chatting with images' paradigm and the dynamic vision encoder contributed substantial performance gains over baseline models.
- The model demonstrated improved robustness to information loss at lower visual resolutions, achieving **75.9%** accuracy on SPAR with 512 visual tokens, a **24.8%** gap over a vanilla encoder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling visual reasoning as language-guided feature modulation enables a more robust and fine-grained visual thinking process, overcoming limitations of static encoding and low-level manipulation.
> - A dynamic vision encoder with query-conditioned joint encoding across multiple visual inputs is crucial for effective cross-view interaction and detail preservation, especially in multi-image and video reasoning scenarios.
> - Both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning stages are essential for bootstrapping the model's ability to generate interactive reasoning trajectories and for refining its strategic problem-solving capabilities.
