# Chatting with Images for Introspective Visual Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> The VILAVT model introduces a "chatting with images" framework, which redefines visual reasoning as language-guided feature modulation for large vision-language models. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on 5 out of 8 benchmarks for general, single-image, multi-image, and video-based spatial reasoning tasks, demonstrating improved versatility and robustness to information loss at lower resolutions.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) operating under the 'thinking about images' paradigm suffer irreversible information loss due to single-pass visual encoding.
- Existing 'thinking with images' approaches (tool-based, programmatic, generative imagination) often lack expressiveness for high-level relational intent, incur high latency, or struggle with joint reasoning across multiple visual inputs.
- Challenges in establishing effective cross-modal alignment and grounding between linguistic semantics and low-level visual manipulation interfaces, especially for complex visual semantics or geometric relationships.

## Method

- Introduces the =='chatting with images' paradigm==, which models visual reasoning as generating an iterative trajectory of textual reasoning, natural language inquiries, and targeted visual region re-examinations.
- Develops ==VILAVT==, a novel LVLM featuring a ==dynamic vision encoder== that jointly processes multiple, potentially non-contiguous visual regions conditioned on a natural language inquiry using a hybrid attention strategy.
- Employs a two-stage training strategy: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) on a multi-domain dataset of repurposed and synthesized reasoning trajectories, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) with an outcome-based reward function.

## Results

- VILAVT achieved state-of-the-art results on **5** out of **8** diverse benchmarks, encompassing General VQA, single-image, multi-image, and video-based spatial reasoning tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the 'chatting with images' paradigm and the dynamic vision encoder contributed substantial performance gains over baseline models.
- The model demonstrated improved robustness to information loss at lower visual resolutions, achieving **75.9%** accuracy on SPAR with 512 visual tokens, a **24.8%** gap over a vanilla encoder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling visual reasoning as language-guided feature modulation enables a more robust and fine-grained visual thinking process, overcoming limitations of static encoding and low-level manipulation.
> - A dynamic vision encoder with query-conditioned joint encoding across multiple visual inputs is crucial for effective cross-view interaction and detail preservation, especially in multi-image and video reasoning scenarios.
> - Both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning stages are essential for bootstrapping the model's ability to generate interactive reasoning trajectories and for refining its strategic problem-solving capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Chatting with Images" positions itself within the evolving landscape of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by addressing limitations observed in two predominant reasoning paradigms: "thinking about images" and "thinking with images."

The conventional paradigm, termed "thinking about images," involves LVLMs performing a single-pass visual encoding, where visual inputs are compressed into static tokens, and subsequent reasoning occurs primarily in the language space. This approach often leads to the irreversible loss of fine-grained visual information, particularly when details are crucial or when spatial relationships across multiple images need to be deciphered. For multi-image scenarios, independent encoding of each view and late fusion in the textual domain can obscure geometric relationships, limiting the model's capacity for complex spatial reasoning.

To mitigate these issues, a newer paradigm, "thinking with images," has emerged. This paradigm aims for a more interactive reasoning process where models interleave textual reasoning with dynamic visual manipulations and iterative re-encoding. Existing approaches within "thinking with images" generally fall into two categories:
1. **Tool-based manipulation**: Models invoke predefined external tools (e.g., for image rotation or bounding box drawing) to manipulate visual inputs. A limitation of this method is the black-box nature of external tools, which can hinder end-to-end optimization and confine the model to a predefined, often narrow, toolset.
2. **Programmatic manipulation**: Models generate executable image processing code for custom visual operations. While theoretically versatile, this approach often suffers from high latency due to the code generation-execution loop and restricted expressiveness, primarily operating at a pixel or low-level geometry scale.
3. **Generative imagination**: Some approaches leverage a model's internal generative capacity to synthesize new visual images as intermediate thoughts. This method can be computationally expensive and risks generating content that is ungrounded from the source data, leading to potentially unreliable reasoning.

A common challenge across these "thinking with images" approaches is that they predominantly focus on single-image manipulation. Even when applied to multiple images, they often encode each view independently, and their manipulation interfaces typically lack the expressiveness to convey high-level relational intent. This makes complex tasks requiring nuanced understanding of visual semantics or geometric relationships across distant regions challenging to articulate and execute efficiently.

"Chatting with Images" proposes a new framework designed to overcome the limitations of both paradigms by introducing language-guided feature modulation. It aims to provide a unified, scalable, and expressive mechanism for visual manipulation that bridges high-level linguistic intent with direct feature-level interaction, thereby enabling more robust and fine-grained visual reasoning across single and multiple visual inputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the "chatting with images" framework and the VILAVT model are rooted in addressing the fundamental limitations of existing LVLM reasoning paradigms:

1. **Mitigate Information Loss in Visual Encoding:** Current LVLMs operating under the "thinking about images" paradigm often rely on a single-pass visual encoding. This aggressive compression of high-dimensional visual data into a fixed set of visual tokens inevitably leads to the loss of fine-grained details and spatial nuances. This information loss occurs before any deep reasoning can commence and is exacerbated in scenarios requiring an understanding of subtle visual cues or interactions between distant regions. The motivation is to enable LVLMs to dynamically retrieve and re-encode task-relevant details as needed, preventing this premature information attenuation.

2. **Enhance Cross-Modal Alignment and Grounding:** While the "thinking with images" paradigm attempts iterative visual manipulation, existing methods often struggle with insufficient grounding of visual states in linguistic semantics. Tools or programmatic manipulation interfaces are typically low-level (e.g., pixel-level operations), making it difficult to express complex cognitive intent or relational concepts using language. This impairs effective cross-modal alignment, especially for tasks that require reasoning over visual semantics or geometric relationships across multiple images or distant regions within an image. The objective is to establish a tighter coupling between linguistic reasoning and visual state updates, allowing language to directly guide and interpret visual manipulations.

3. **Improve Expressiveness and Scalability of Visual Manipulation:** Existing "thinking with images" approaches face challenges related to the expressiveness and scalability of their manipulation interfaces. Tool-based methods are limited by predefined toolsets and a black-box nature, restricting generalization. Programmatic methods, though theoretically versatile, incur high latency and primarily offer pixel-level control, making it cumbersome to express high-level relational intents (e.g., "find the car parked incorrectly"). The motivation is to develop a visual manipulation mechanism that is both scalable and highly expressive, using natural language as a unified control interface. This aims to reduce reliance on extensive handcrafting of specialized tools or brittle program chains.

4. **Enable Joint Multi-Image and Video-based Reasoning:** A specific challenge for multi-image and video-based tasks is the common practice of encoding each visual input independently and aggregating information only in the textual domain. This approach often fails to capture intricate geometric or temporal relationships across views. A key objective is to facilitate joint re-encoding over multiple image regions, allowing for cross-view interactions directly in feature space and enabling the model to build a coherent understanding of dynamic and spatial contexts.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond static, information-losing visual processing and towards an introspective, interactive, and language-guided visual thinking process that dynamically modulates visual perception based on high-level cognitive intent, leading to more robust and accurate multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed "chatting with images" framework redefines visual reasoning as a process of language-guided feature modulation. This methodology is instantiated in VILAVT, a novel LVLM, and supported by a two-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. The "Chatting with Images" Paradigm

The framework models visual reasoning as generating a trajectory $\tau = (s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_T)$, where each step $s_t$ is a triplet $(r_t, q_t, z_t)$:
- $r_t$: Represents the model's textual reasoning or internal thought process.
- $q_t$: A natural language inquiry that serves as an expressive language prompt, guiding the vision encoder to perform a targeted re-computation of visual features.
- $z_t$: A set of target visual regions, defined as $\{(n_i^t, b_i^t)\}_{i=1}^{M_t}$, where $n_i^t$ is an index to a visual source (original image or a previously produced image) and $b_i^t$ is a bounding box specifying the sub-region to re-examine.

The iterative process unfolds as follows:
1. **Initial Encoding**: All input images/frames ($I$) are initially encoded independently into visual token embeddings by a vanilla vision encoder $V(I, \emptyset)$, producing $f_0$.
2. **Stepwise Reasoning**: The language model ($M$) generates an action triplet $s_t = (r_t, q_t, z_t)$, comprising a thought, a natural language inquiry, and a set of target regions.
3. **Targeted Re-encoding**: The framework crops the specified visual regions $C_t$ from the source images. To enhance detail, each region is upscaled by a factor of 2 (capped at original dimensions). The dynamic vision encoder ($V$) then jointly processes the textual inquiry $q_t$ and the upscaled regions $C_t$ to produce a new feature set: $f_t = V(C_t, q_t)$. This allows for cross-view interactions directly in the feature space.
4. **Iteration**: These newly generated visual token embeddings $f_t$ are passed back to the language model, enriching its context and informing the generation of the next reasoning step $s_{t+1}$. This iteration continues until a final answer is reached.

#### 3.2. The Architecture of the Vision Encoder

VILAVT introduces a dynamic vision encoder designed to interpret textual inquiries and jointly process a variable number of potentially non-contiguous image regions.
- **Structure**: Given a set of $U$ images $C = \{i_u\}_{u=1}^U$ and a textual inquiry $q$, the dynamic vision encoder $V$ is formulated as $V(C, q) = V_e(P_1 \oplus P_2 \oplus \cdots \oplus P_U \oplus h_q)$, where $h_q = V_m(q)$.
 - $V_e$: A vision Transformer that encodes visual and textual embeddings.
 - $V_m$: A lightweight language Transformer that encodes the inquiry $q$ into a query embedding $h_q$. This component is initialized from Qwen3-0.6B-Embedding and kept frozen during training.
- **Unified Input**: Each image $i_u$ is projected into its patch embeddings $P_u$. These patch embeddings are then concatenated ($\oplus$) with the query embedding $h_q$ from $V_m$ to form a single input sequence for $V_e$. A left-to-right sequence of 2D positional embeddings is applied to this entire sequence.
- **Hybrid Attention Strategy**: Within $V_e$, a hybrid attention mechanism balances computational cost and expressive power:
 - **Inter-full attention**: In designated interaction layers (layers 17-32), full self-attention is applied across all visual tokens (from multiple images) and also attends to the textual inquiry, enabling query-conditioned cross-view interactions.
 - **Intra-full attention**: In other designated layers (layers 8, 16), attention operates within individual images.
 - **Sparse window attention**: In the remaining layers, attention is restricted to local patch windows for efficiency.
This design allows for query-guided cross-region interactions over multiple visual inputs during feature extraction, distinguishing it from approaches like InstructBLIP that primarily use query tokens for compression.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Strategy

VILAVT is trained using a two-stage curriculum.

#### 3.4. Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)

- **Objective**: To bootstrap the model's ability to generate reasoning trajectories consistent with the "chatting with images" paradigm.
- **Data Construction**: A large-scale, multi-domain dataset, $D_{SFT}$, is created from three sources (Table 4, Appendix A):
 - **Repurposed "Thinking with Images" Trajectories**: Existing datasets featuring tool-based (e.g., VGR) or programmatic (e.g., Thyme) manipulation are translated. A powerful teacher LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) is prompted to convert external actions (tool calls or code blocks) into the $(r_t, q_t, z_t)$ triplet format, unifying heterogeneous interfaces into natural language-guided inquiries.
 - **Synthesized Spatial Reasoning Trajectories**: For complex spatial reasoning datasets (e.g., SpaceR, SPAR, Vica) lacking explicit reasoning paths, a dedicated pipeline is used. This pipeline first performs parallel knowledge mining (object detection, grounding, camera motion estimation) and holistic captioning. This rich context is then fed to the teacher LVLM to generate high-quality "chatting with images" trajectories.
 - **Augmented Text-Only Trajectories**: For simpler questions where visual re-examination is unnecessary, purely textual reasoning chains are included (either from existing annotations or generated via rejection sampling by Qwen2.5-VL-72B).
- **Optimization**: The model $\pi_\theta$ is trained on this combined dataset using a standard maximum likelihood objective.

#### 3.5. Reinforcement Learning (RL)

- **Objective**: To refine the model's strategic problem-solving abilities by directly optimizing for task outcomes and exploring the reasoning space.
- **Reward Function**: An outcome-based reward function $R(\tau) = 1(R_{correct} > 0)(R_{correct} + R_{format})$ is used for each rollout trajectory:
 - $R_{correct}$: Measures answer correctness (binary for multiple-choice, Mean Relative Accuracy for numerical questions). A strict gating mechanism ensures reward accumulation only for correct final answers.
 - $R_{format}$: Binary score (1/0) indicating the syntactic validity of the trajectory and bounding boxes.
- **Optimization**: The policy $\pi_\theta$ is optimized using the GRPO algorithm (Shao et al., 2024), a robust policy gradient method. During RL rollouts, multiple candidate reasoning paths are generated per query, allowing the model to learn from successful and unsuccessful explorations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented in the paper "Chatting with Images" demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed VILAVT model and its underlying reasoning paradigm through extensive experimentation.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Versatility

- VILAVT was evaluated across a diverse suite of eight benchmarks, encompassing General Visual Question Answering (VQA) (HRBench-4K, HRBench-8K), single-image spatial reasoning (SpatialEval-Real, EmbSpatial), multi-image spatial reasoning (ERQA, SPAR-Bench, MMSI-Bench), and video-based spatial reasoning (VSI-Bench).
- The model achieved state-of-the-art results on 5 out of 8 benchmarks, notably demonstrating strong gains on complex multi-image and video-based spatial reasoning tasks.
- VILAVT exhibited a balanced performance, maintaining competitiveness on general VQA tasks even against models specifically designed for such tasks (e.g., Thyme), while simultaneously excelling in spatial reasoning. This suggests a higher degree of versatility compared to baseline models which often specialize in narrow domains.
- A comparative analysis with models from "non-thinking," "thinking about images," and "thinking with images" paradigms indicated that prior methods often suffer from task-specific specialization, failing to generalize across different reasoning complexities. VILAVT, by contrast, leveraged its language-guided, unified visual interaction framework to achieve broad capability.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies on Training Stages and Model Components

Ablation studies were conducted to isolate the contributions of different components and training stages:
- **Importance of the "Chatting with Images" Paradigm**: Comparing VILAVT-SFT (our model trained with SFT only, using the proposed paradigm) against Qwen2.5-VL-7B-SFT (the base model fine-tuned on question-answer pairs without reasoning chains) revealed that VILAVT-SFT achieved substantial improvements. This indicated that the gains were primarily attributed to the interactive reasoning framework itself rather than merely data scaling or standard fine-tuning.
- **Importance of Training Stages**: VILAVT-SFT significantly outperformed the base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), confirming that supervised fine-tuning is effective in enabling the interactive reasoning format. Subsequently, the full VILAVT model (with RL) consistently surpassed VILAVT-SFT across all benchmarks. This suggests that reinforcement learning further refines the model's interactive reasoning strategies beyond what is learned through supervised demonstrations.
- **Importance of the Dynamic Vision Encoder**: Under identical SFT and partial RL schedules (VILAVT$_{400}$), the variant employing the dynamic vision encoder consistently outperformed a variant equipped with a vanilla ViT (VILAVT$_{400}$ w/ vanilla ViT). This was particularly evident on HRBench-8K and VSI-Bench, indicating that query-conditioned joint encoding within the vision encoder is crucial for performance gains, complementing the iterative reasoning process.

#### 4.3. Analysis under Different Resolutions

- Experiments on a subset of the SPAR dataset demonstrated that VILAVT maintained robustness to information loss at lower resolutions. At 512 visual tokens, VILAVT achieved 75.9% accuracy, marking a 24.8% performance gap over a vanilla encoder (51.1%). This indicates that the architectural modifications are effective in preserving performance in information-scarce scenarios.
- Ablations further suggested that joint multi-image encoding was the primary contributor to this robustness on multi-view benchmarks, with inquiry-conditioning providing additional, consistent performance gains.
- These findings were generalized on HRBench-4K, where VILAVT consistently outperformed the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B across various resolutions, including low-information settings. The performance gap between VILAVT-SFT and the full VILAVT persisted across resolutions, affirming that RL refines reasoning strategies under different resolution constraints.

#### 4.4. Case Studies and Attention Visualization

- Qualitative analysis (Figure 10) on a video-based spatial reasoning task from VSI-Bench illustrated VILAVT's ability to solve complex problems. It effectively used expressive language prompts to guide joint, relational re-encoding of features across key frames, enabling the construction of a coherent spatial map.
- On high-resolution perception tasks (HRBench-4K, Figures 11-13), VILAVT's coarse-to-fine strategy, involving targeted cropping and zooming guided by language inquiries, allowed it to correctly identify fine-grained details where other models, including proprietary ones, failed or provided less explicit reasoning.
- Attention map visualizations (Figure 5, 14) demonstrated that inquiry-conditioned encoding in VILAVT resulted in sharper and more localized attention on task-relevant regions. This indicated that incorporating the textual inquiry into the vision encoder facilitated more precise perception and fine-grained detail extraction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "chatting with images" framework and the VILAVT model represent a notable advancement in the field of large vision-language models, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Visual Reasoning

The introduction of "chatting with images" as a new reasoning paradigm is a significant conceptual contribution. It reframes visual manipulation not as a discrete external tool invocation or code execution, but as language-guided feature modulation. This paradigm directly addresses the limitations of current LVLMs, specifically the information loss inherent in static "thinking about images" approaches and the low expressiveness and scalability issues of visual prompts in existing "thinking with images" methods. By leveraging the compositional power of language to articulate high-level cognitive intent, the framework enables a tighter coupling between linguistic reasoning and dynamic visual state updates.

#### 5.2. Architectural Innovation for Introspective Visual Perception

The development of VILAVT, equipped with a dynamic vision encoder, is a key technical contribution. This encoder is explicitly designed to support introspective visual thinking by jointly processing multiple, potentially non-contiguous images conditioned on a natural language inquiry. This allows for cross-view interactions at the feature level, a capability largely absent in prior approaches that often fuse information only in the textual domain. The hybrid attention strategy within the encoder is crucial for balancing computational efficiency with the ability to perform query-guided, fine-grained visual re-encoding.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance and Versatility

The extensive empirical validation across eight diverse benchmarks demonstrates that VILAVT achieves strong and consistent improvements, securing state-of-the-art results on a majority of them. The particularly pronounced gains on complex multi-image and video-based spatial reasoning tasks highlight the effectiveness of the language-guided joint re-encoding for understanding intricate relationships. The model's demonstrated versatility, outperforming or remaining highly competitive with specialized models across different task types, suggests a move towards more general-purpose visual reasoning agents.

#### 5.4. Robustness to Information Scarcity

The analysis under varying resolutions indicates that VILAVT's dynamic vision encoder is more robust to information loss. This capability to preserve detail and integrate cross-view information effectively, even when visual inputs are compressed, is critical for real-world applications where image quality or resolution may vary.

#### 5.5. Potential Impact on Applications

The advancements presented in this work have the potential to significantly impact applications requiring robust and reliable visual grounding. These include:
- **Grounded Question Answering**: Enabling more accurate and nuanced answers to complex visual questions by allowing models to dynamically "inspect" relevant visual evidence.
- **Educational Tools**: Assisting in learning environments by providing detailed, interactive explanations of visual content.
- **Scientific and Engineering Assistance**: Supporting professionals in fields requiring detailed analysis of visual data, such as medical imaging, remote sensing, or architectural design, by enabling more precise and context-aware visual reasoning.
- **Autonomous Systems**: Improving the perception and decision-making capabilities of robots and other autonomous agents by providing a more sophisticated mechanism for understanding their visual environment.

By bridging high-level declarative intent with low-level feature modulation, "chatting with images" opens promising avenues for developing more compositional and general-purpose visual reasoning agents that can engage in human-like introspective visual thinking.
