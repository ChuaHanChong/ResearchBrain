# LessMimic: Long-Horizon Humanoid Interaction with Unified Distance Field Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> The LESSMIMIC framework enables humanoid robots to perform long-horizon interactions with diverse physical objects by utilizing unified Distance Field representations. This system achieves 80-100% success rates on object generalization tasks across varying scales and robustly executes sequences of up to 40 tasks, reliably transferring these learned capabilities to a real-world robot using only vision.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid interaction methods suffer from either rigid coupling to specific motion references, which limits generalization to novel object geometries, or reliance on extensive task-specific reward engineering, hindering flexible skill composition.
- Conventional geometric representations like voxels or meshes are often computationally intensive for real-time whole-body control or lack the continuous distance information crucial for contact-aware interaction.
- Achieving truly autonomous, generalizable, and adaptable humanoid behavior requires a unified representation that can decouple interaction logic from specific motion patterns.

## Method

- A novel ==Distance Field (DF)== based representation is introduced, capturing continuous geometric and dynamic interaction cues (signed distance, surface normals, decomposed velocities) for humanoid links, which is invariant to global object pose and scale.
- A three-stage training pipeline is employed, starting with behavior cloning and DAgger distillation from a reference-based teacher to a DF-based student policy.
- The policy is further refined using reinforcement learning with Adversarial Interaction Priors (AIP) in procedurally randomized environments to promote geometric generalization, followed by visual-motor distillation for real-world deployment using egocentric depth observations.

## Results

- The system achieved 80-**100%** success rates on `PickUp` and `SitStand` tasks across a wide range of object scales (**0.4x** to **1.6x**) and successfully generalized to entirely unseen object shapes, significantly outperforming existing baselines.
- A single LESSMIMIC policy demonstrated robust long-horizon skill composition, achieving **61.7%** success on sequences of 5 randomly ordered heterogeneous tasks and maintaining viability for up to 40 consecutive task instances without environment resets.
- Capabilities reliably transferred to a physical humanoid robot, achieving 10/10 real-world success on `PickUp` and `SitStand` tasks, with the vision-based variant also showing strong performance and successfully demonstrating long-horizon real-world sequences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distance Fields provide a geometrically invariant and unified representation of humanoid-object interaction, abstracting local contact patterns from global object properties, which is key for robust generalization across object shapes and scales.
> - Adversarial Interaction Priors (AIP) effectively guide the reinforcement learning process, encouraging the policy to learn and maintain geometry-consistent interaction patterns in procedurally varied environments.
> - A multi-stage learning approach, combining initial behavior cloning for skill acquisition, reinforcement learning for generalization, and visual distillation for deployment, is effective for creating versatile and autonomous humanoid agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of autonomous, long-horizon interaction with physical environments is a central, long-standing challenge in embodied intelligence. The paper contextualizes its contribution by identifying a critical representational bottleneck in existing approaches, which broadly fall into two categories: reference-based and reference-free methods.

**Reference-based methods** (e.g., HDMI, ResMimic, VisualMimic) leverage pre-recorded human demonstrations or motion references to generate high-fidelity robotic movements. While these methods can produce impressive, dynamic motions, they suffer from fundamental limitations:
1. **Geometric specialization:** Policies become rigidly coupled to the specific object geometries and configurations present in the training data, leading to brittle performance on novel shapes or scales. They essentially "memorize" specific kinematic trajectories.
2. **Lack of flexibility:** Any deviation from the reference trajectory is treated as an error, hindering adaptive responses to unforeseen perturbations or changes in the environment. Recent attempts to introduce higher-level planners or human-in-the-loop teleoperation mitigate this but do not resolve the core representational issue.

**Reference-free methods** (e.g., Op3-Soccer, PhysHSI) discard motion references to gain flexibility. However, without a principled interaction signal, these methods typically resort to extensive task-specific reward engineering and hand-crafted interaction cues. This results in specialized, isolated policies that struggle to generalize across tasks or compose skills over extended horizons. They lack a unified framework for diverse interactions.

The paper argues that neither paradigm provides a "geometry-aware, task-unified representation that decouples interaction logic from specific motion patterns," which is essential for generalizable, adaptive, and composable humanoid behavior.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts its chosen representation, **Distance Field (DF)**, with other common geometric representations:
- **Voxel-based and occupancy representations** are memory and computationally intensive, precluding real-time whole-body control.
- **Point-based and mesh-based representations** capture surface geometry but lack the continuous distance information crucial for contact-aware control.
- **Implicit neural representations** offer continuous geometry but are often too slow for high-frequency control loops due to their inference cost.

LESSMIMIC positions itself as a solution to these limitations by advocating for the Distance Field as the core representation. By leveraging DFs, the authors propose a **reference-free** approach that offers a **unified observation space** across tasks and supports **autonomous long-horizon skill composition**, addressing the key shortcomings of prior work as summarized in their comparative Table I. This work represents a significant step towards creating more autonomous, adaptable, and general-purpose humanoid robots, moving beyond specialized solutions to a more unified and scalable framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of the LESSMIMIC research is to enable humanoid robots to autonomously and robustly interact with diverse physical environments over extended periods, exhibiting human-like fluidity and adaptability. This ambitious goal translates into several key objectives and underlying motivations:

1. **Overcome Representational Bottlenecks:** The primary motivation stems from the limitations of current interaction representations. Existing methods either rely on rigid, reference-based motion scripts that are not generalizable to novel object geometries, or on task-specific, hand-crafted rewards that impede skill composition. The authors aim to develop a unified representation that inherently supports generalization and composition.
2. **Achieve Geometric Generalization:** A crucial objective is for a single policy to generalize to unseen object shapes and scales without requiring retraining. Humanoids should be able to pick up objects of different sizes, push various containers, or sit on chairs of varying heights, treating the local geometry of interaction as invariant rather than memorizing specific configurations.
3. **Enable Reference-Free Inference:** The research seeks to eliminate the need for motion references during policy execution. This is critical for achieving true autonomy, as relying on pre-recorded demonstrations limits real-time adaptation and recovery from unexpected situations. The policy should infer appropriate actions solely from current perceptual and proprioceptive states.
4. **Support Long-Horizon Skill Composition:** A major challenge is to allow a single policy to seamlessly execute a sequence of heterogeneous interaction skills (e.g., push, pick up, carry, sit down) without explicit resets or task-specific planners. The goal is implicit skill transitions driven by a unified understanding of interaction.
5. **Facilitate Online Failure Recovery:** The policy should be robust to perturbations and able to recover from failures by re-initiating tasks or adapting its actions, a capability severely hampered by rigid, reference-tracking approaches.
6. **Transition to Vision-Only Deployment:** For practical real-world application, humanoid robots cannot rely on external motion capture (MoCap) systems. Therefore, a key objective is to distill the learned control policy into a vision-based counterpart that operates solely on egocentric depth observations, making it deployable in unstructured environments.
7. **Ground Interaction in Local Geometry:** The core motivation for adopting Distance Fields (DFs) is the belief that local geometric relationships – such as surface distances, gradients, and velocity decompositions relative to an object's surface – are intrinsically invariant to global object properties (shape, size, pose). By grounding interaction in these local cues, the policy can learn abstract interaction patterns rather than specific movements, thus achieving robustness and generalizability.

In essence, LESSMIMIC is motivated by the desire to move humanoid robotics from "mimicking" specific motions to "understanding" and "adapting" to the geometry of interaction, enabling a new level of autonomous, versatile, and robust robotic behavior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LESSMIMIC introduces a novel, reference-free framework centered on Distance Fields (DF) as a unified interaction representation, trained through a three-stage pipeline.

**A. DF-based Interaction Representation**
The cornerstone of LESSMIMIC is its representation of humanoid-object interaction using Distance Fields (DFs). A DF, denoted Φ: R³ → R, assigns to every point in space its signed distance to the nearest object surface. This continuous, differentiable field provides:
- **Distance to surface (Φ(x_t))**: For any humanoid link at position x_t, this directly indicates proximity to an object.
- **Surface orientation (∇Φ(x_t))**: The gradient of the DF at x_t provides the local surface normal, crucial for understanding contact direction.

To capture the dynamics of interaction, the robot's linear velocity (v_t) for each link is decomposed using the local surface normal:
- **Normal velocity (v_norm)**: The component of velocity along the surface normal, indicating approach or force application intensity.
- **Tangential velocity (v_tan)**: The component of velocity within the tangent plane, representing sliding or traversal across the surface.

For each task-relevant humanoid link, these geometric and dynamic cues are combined into a per-link tuple: u_t = [Φ(x_t), ∇Φ(x_t), v_norm, v_tan]. To capture the temporal evolution of interaction, the framework constructs an "interaction representation" I_t as a history of these tuples over a fixed temporal window. Critically, this representation is **invariant to the object's global pose and scale**, meaning interactions with objects of varying sizes and shapes exhibit similar local geometric signatures. This invariance is key to generalization. Finally, I_t is encoded into a compact latent vector z_t using a Variational Auto-Encoder (VAE) to reduce dimensionality, smooth sensor noise, and provide a fixed-size input to the policy.

**B. Three-Stage Training Pipeline**

1. **Interaction Skill Pre-Training (Behavior Cloning with DAgger)**
 - **Teacher Policy (π_mimic):** A high-quality teacher policy, specifically ResMimic, is used to generate physically valid interaction data in simulation. This teacher tracks retargeted human MoCap motions, augmented with a residual module to compensate for dynamic mismatches during contact. It has access to privileged information (full-body reference motions).
 - **Student Policy (π_base):** The target policy, π_base, is initialized through behavior cloning on the data generated by π_mimic. Unlike the teacher, π_base operates on an inference-time observation space, o_base = [proprioception, sparse root-trajectory command, DF interaction latent z_t], without access to reference motions.
 - **DAgger-style Distillation:** To mitigate covariate shift, π_base is iteratively rolled out in the environment, and the teacher π_mimic provides corrective actions for the states encountered by the student. This ensures the student learns to recover from its own deviations.
 - **Architecture:** π_base is implemented as a Transformer, chosen for its ability to capture long-range temporal dependencies in the interaction history.

2. **Discriminative Post-Training (Reinforcement Learning with Adversarial Interaction Priors - AIP)**
 - **Motivation:** Pre-training on a fixed dataset encourages memorization. This stage aims to promote genuine geometric generalization by fine-tuning the policy with Reinforcement Learning (RL) in procedurally augmented environments.
 - **Environment Setup:** Object geometries (scale, shape, surface properties) are randomized across episodes. Crucially, motion-tracking rewards and hand-crafted shaping terms are excluded, as reference motions are invalid for randomized objects.
 - **Adversarial Interaction Priors (AIP):** Inspired by AMP, AIP uses a discriminator D to distinguish interaction latents (z_t) generated by the policy on novel objects from a reference buffer (B_ref) of valid interaction latents. A least-squares GAN objective trains the discriminator.
 - **Policy Optimization (RL):** The policy is trained to maximize a composite reward: r_t = r_task + λ_i * r_interact + λ_s * r_style.
 - r_task: Penalizes deviation from the target root command.
 - r_interact: Derived from the AIP discriminator, rewarding geometrically valid interaction patterns (i.e., making D(z_t) predict "real").
 - r_style: A standard AMP loss, regularizing for natural whole-body motion.
 - This stage, yielding π_full, ensures the policy learns underlying geometric rules of interaction rather than specific motion patterns.

3. **Visual-Motor Policy Distillation (for Real-World Deployment)**
 - **Goal:** To enable deployment without MoCap, π_full is distilled into a vision-based policy π_vis that operates on egocentric depth observations.
 - **Input:** π_vis receives o_vis = [proprioception, root command, history of egocentric depth frames (S_t)]. A visual encoder (E_ϕ) maps S_t to the DF latent z_t.
 - **Distillation Process:** A DAgger-style procedure is employed, where π_vis interacts with the environment, and the frozen teacher π_full provides supervisory actions. The student minimizes MSE loss against the teacher's actions.
 - **Domain Randomization:** Extensive randomization (camera extrinsics, depth noise, physical properties) is applied during distillation to enhance sim-to-real transferability.

This comprehensive methodology, combining DF-based perception, multi-stage learning with adversarial priors, and visual distillation, allows LESSMIMIC to acquire generalizable, long-horizon humanoid interaction skills ready for real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of LESSMIMIC rigorously demonstrates its capabilities across object generalization, long-horizon skill composition, and real-world deployment, significantly outperforming existing baselines and validating the design choices through ablation studies.

**A. Generalization on Object Sizes and Shapes:**
- **Superior Robustness:** LESSMIMIC (MoCap) achieved remarkable success rates of 80–100% on `PickUp` and `SitStand` tasks across a wide range of object scales (0.4x to 1.6x, with 1.0x being the training scale). This contrasts sharply with reference-based baselines (HDMI, ResMimic, VisualMimic) which degraded severely at scales deviating from the training distribution.
- **Outperformance:** For `SitStand` and `Push`, LESSMIMIC outperformed the strongest baselines by 15–20% at larger object sizes. It also achieved the highest success rate for `Carry` under scale variation.
- **Shape Generalization:** The policy successfully generalized to entirely unseen object shapes, such as picking up a soccer ball (spherical) despite being trained only on boxes and cylinders (Fig. 5b), demonstrating its ability to learn abstract geometric interaction rather than memorizing specific object forms.

**B. Long-Horizon Skill Composition:**
- **Unprecedented Sequence Length:** A single LESSMIMIC (MoCap) policy achieved 61.7% success on sequences composed of 5 randomly ordered heterogeneous tasks and maintained 2.1% viability even for sequences of up to 40 consecutive task instances without environment resets.
- **Implicit Transitions:** This capability emerges from the unified DF representation, allowing implicit skill transitions based on changing geometric contexts, rather than explicit sequencing or task-specific policies.
- **Baseline Collapse:** All ablated variants and baselines collapsed to zero success beyond very short sequences, underscoring the necessity of every component in LESSMIMIC for long-horizon performance.

**C. Distilling LESSMIMIC to Visual Input (Vision-Only Deployment):**
- **Retained Performance Trends:** The vision-based variant (LESSMIMIC Vision) largely preserved the overall performance trends of the MoCap-based model, albeit with a moderate reduction in success rates across tasks.
- **Superior to Vision-Guided Baselines:** For `PickUp` under scale generalization, the vision-based policy achieved 63.7–99.7% success at scales where all baseline methods (including vision-guided ones like VisualMimic) exhibited near-zero performance. This indicates that while depth observations introduce some uncertainty, the underlying DF-based interaction logic remains robust.

**D. Ablation Study Insights:**
- **Adversarial Interaction Prior (AIP):** Removing AIP significantly reduced robustness to scale variation, confirming its critical role in enforcing geometry-consistent interaction across diverse object geometries.
- **Synthetic Physicalization:** Its removal severely impacted contact-rich tasks like `Carry`, highlighting the importance of physically feasible training data when sustained contact is required.
- **Geometry Randomization:** Disabling randomization caused severe overfitting, leading to near-zero success outside training scales, validating its necessity for scale-invariant generalization.
- **RL Fine-tuning:** Without RL fine-tuning, performance was limited, demonstrating that behavior cloning alone is insufficient to adapt to novel geometries.
- **Transformer Architecture:** Replacing the Transformer with an MLP severely degraded performance on `PickUp` and `Carry`, indicating that the Transformer's capacity to capture temporal dependencies is essential for multi-skill interactions.

**E. Real-World Deployment:**
- **Reliable Transfer:** Real-world experiments on a physical humanoid platform confirmed that the capabilities reliably transfer from simulation.
- **High Success Rates:** The MoCap-based model achieved 10/10 success on `PickUp` (22cm³) and `SitStand` (46cm), with over 90% root trajectory tracking accuracy.
- **Viable Vision-Based Performance:** The vision-based variant achieved 8/10 and 7/10 success on the two `PickUp` conditions, with slightly reduced but still strong tracking accuracy.
- **Long-Horizon Real-World Demo:** Successfully demonstrated long-horizon skill composition in the real world, such as pushing a cabinet then picking up and carrying a box (Fig. 6).

In summary, LESSMIMIC significantly advances the state-of-the-art in humanoid-object interaction by demonstrating unprecedented generalization, long-horizon composition, and real-world transfer, primarily attributed to its novel DF-based representation and multi-stage learning approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LESSMIMIC represents a substantial advancement in the field of embodied intelligence and humanoid robotics, with significant implications and potential impacts across several domains:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Interaction Representation:** The most significant contribution is establishing the Distance Field (DF) as a highly effective, unified, and inherently shape/scale-invariant representation for complex humanoid-object interaction. This moves beyond the limitations of motion-centric or task-specific representations, offering a powerful abstraction that captures the core geometric logic of interaction. This could influence how researchers approach perception and control for contact-rich tasks in robotics more broadly.

2. **Unprecedented Generalization Capabilities:** By enabling a single policy to generalize robustly to unseen object shapes and scales without retraining, LESSMIMIC addresses a critical bottleneck in robot autonomy. This means robots could operate in dynamic, unstructured environments with novel objects, reducing the need for extensive data collection or re-training for every new item encountered. This is a crucial step towards truly versatile robots.

3. **Breakthrough in Long-Horizon Skill Composition:** The demonstration of a single policy executing up to 40 sequential and heterogeneous tasks without resets is a remarkable achievement. This capability for seamless, implicit skill transition, driven by a unified geometric understanding, brings humanoid robots closer to performing complex, multi-stage human-like activities in real-world settings, such as tidying a room or assisting in a factory.

4. **Enabling Reference-Free and Vision-Only Autonomy:** Eliminating the reliance on motion references and MoCap systems is vital for practical deployment. The successful distillation of the policy to operate solely on egocentric depth observations makes LESSMIMIC much more portable, scalable, and deployable in diverse, uninstrumented environments, accelerating the transition from simulation to real-world applications.

5. **Enhanced Robustness and Failure Recovery:** The continuous geometric feedback offered by DFs provides a rich signal for adaptive control, allowing the humanoid to recover from perturbations (e.g., re-initiating a pickup after dropping an object). This inherent robustness is essential for reliable operation outside of controlled lab settings.

6. **Scalable Path for General-Purpose Humanoids:** By providing a unified framework for learning diverse interaction skills and generalizing them across objects, LESSMIMIC offers a more scalable and efficient pathway for developing general-purpose humanoid robots. This could reduce the engineering overhead associated with developing specific solutions for myriad interaction scenarios.

7. **Impact on Related Fields:**
 - **Robot Control:** The method for decomposing velocity into normal and tangential components relative to the DF surface provides a powerful tool for contact-aware control.
 - **Computer Vision:** The visual distillation stage demonstrates how egocentric depth features can be effectively processed to derive abstract geometric cues, potentially inspiring new perception architectures.
 - **Cognitive Science:** The success of grounding complex interaction in local geometric relationships could provide insights into how biological systems simplify and generalize their physical interactions.

While current limitations (e.g., generalization to articulated or deformable objects, robustness under partial observability) are acknowledged, LESSMIMIC lays a strong foundation. Its contributions mark a significant step towards the long-term goal of truly intelligent, autonomous, and adaptable humanoid robots capable of interacting with the world in a flexible and generalizable manner.
