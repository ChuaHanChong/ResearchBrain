# TinySDP: Real Time Semidefinite Optimization for Certifiable and Agile Edge Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> TinySDP, developed by researchers from Columbia University and MIT, provides a framework for real-time semidefinite optimization to enable certifiable and agile model-predictive control on microcontrollers navigating nonconvex obstacles. The system consistently achieved collision-free navigation with paths 31-73% shorter and 4-70 times more precise than baselines, running onboard a Crazyflie quadrotor at 25 Hz.

## Problem

- Existing embedded Model Predictive Control (MPC) implementations struggle with robustly handling nonconvex obstacle constraints, often relying on local approximations that can fail in complex geometries or require impractical safety margins.
- Traditional Semidefinite Programming (SDP) offers principled methods for robust geometric guarantees but is computationally prohibitive and memory-intensive for real-time, resource-constrained embedded systems.
- Current real-time collision avoidance methods often lack explicit a posteriori safety guarantees, making them unsuitable for safety-critical robotic applications.

## Method

- A per-stage, convex, semidefinite relaxation of nonconvex, quadratic, disk-based obstacle avoidance constraints is integrated into the ==MPC== formulation using lifted state-input moment matrices.
- An efficient Riccati-based ADMM solver, adapted from TinyMPC, is employed to handle the PSD constraints, leveraging offline-cached steady-state Riccati quantities for real-time performance on embedded hardware.
- An a posteriori rank-1 safety certificate is introduced, which calculates a 'trace gap' to verify if the relaxed solution corresponds to a collision-free physical state, ensuring explicit geometric safety guarantees.

## Results

- TinySDP achieved collision-free navigation in challenging static (U-shape cul-de-sac) and dynamic (moving gap) environments, generating paths 31-**73%** shorter and 4-70 times more precise than state-of-the-art baselines like TinyMPC-LIN and RPCBF.
- The system was successfully deployed on a resource-constrained Crazyflie quadrotor (STM32F405 microcontroller), running at 25 Hz with an average MPC solve time of **14.842** ms and a modest memory footprint of **139.0** KB RAM.
- The online safety certificate (trace gap approx. 0) remained valid at every control step during real-world dynamic obstacle avoidance trials, confirming the preservation of rank-1 geometry and certifiable safety even under real-world disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SDP-based safety guarantees, traditionally limited to offline or high-performance computing, can be made tractable for real-time embedded control by structuring the problem to fit a cached-Riccati ADMM solver.
> - By introducing auxiliary second-order moment variables and per-stage PSD constraints, nonconvex obstacle avoidance can be formulated as a convex problem, allowing for more robust and global reasoning compared to local approximations.
> - The 'trace gap' certification method provides an effective way to translate relaxed SDP solutions into explicit, mathematically certifiable safety statements for the physical system, crucial for reliable autonomous operation.
