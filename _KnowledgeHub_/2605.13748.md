# TinySDP: Real Time Semidefinite Optimization for Certifiable and Agile Edge Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> TinySDP, developed by researchers from Columbia University and MIT, provides a framework for real-time semidefinite optimization to enable certifiable and agile model-predictive control on microcontrollers navigating nonconvex obstacles. The system consistently achieved collision-free navigation with paths 31-73% shorter and 4-70 times more precise than baselines, running onboard a Crazyflie quadrotor at 25 Hz.

## Problem

- Existing embedded Model Predictive Control (MPC) implementations struggle with robustly handling nonconvex obstacle constraints, often relying on local approximations that can fail in complex geometries or require impractical safety margins.
- Traditional Semidefinite Programming (SDP) offers principled methods for robust geometric guarantees but is computationally prohibitive and memory-intensive for real-time, resource-constrained embedded systems.
- Current real-time collision avoidance methods often lack explicit a posteriori safety guarantees, making them unsuitable for safety-critical robotic applications.

## Method

- A per-stage, convex, semidefinite relaxation of nonconvex, quadratic, disk-based obstacle avoidance constraints is integrated into the ==MPC== formulation using lifted state-input moment matrices.
- An efficient Riccati-based ADMM solver, adapted from TinyMPC, is employed to handle the PSD constraints, leveraging offline-cached steady-state Riccati quantities for real-time performance on embedded hardware.
- An a posteriori rank-1 safety certificate is introduced, which calculates a 'trace gap' to verify if the relaxed solution corresponds to a collision-free physical state, ensuring explicit geometric safety guarantees.

## Results

- TinySDP achieved collision-free navigation in challenging static (U-shape cul-de-sac) and dynamic (moving gap) environments, generating paths 31-**73%** shorter and 4-70 times more precise than state-of-the-art baselines like TinyMPC-LIN and RPCBF.
- The system was successfully deployed on a resource-constrained Crazyflie quadrotor (STM32F405 microcontroller), running at 25 Hz with an average MPC solve time of **14.842** ms and a modest memory footprint of **139.0** KB RAM.
- The online safety certificate (trace gap approx. 0) remained valid at every control step during real-world dynamic obstacle avoidance trials, confirming the preservation of rank-1 geometry and certifiable safety even under real-world disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SDP-based safety guarantees, traditionally limited to offline or high-performance computing, can be made tractable for real-time embedded control by structuring the problem to fit a cached-Riccati ADMM solver.
> - By introducing auxiliary second-order moment variables and per-stage PSD constraints, nonconvex obstacle avoidance can be formulated as a convex problem, allowing for more robust and global reasoning compared to local approximations.
> - The 'trace gap' certification method provides an effective way to translate relaxed SDP solutions into explicit, mathematically certifiable safety statements for the physical system, crucial for reliable autonomous operation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in safe motion planning and control for robotic systems operating in dynamic environments, particularly on resource-constrained embedded platforms. Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a widely adopted framework for such problems due to its ability to incorporate system dynamics and constraints. Recent advancements have enabled MPC deployment on microcontrollers through methods like OSQP, CVXGEN, ECOS, SCS, and notably, TinyMPC, which exploits Riccati structure for efficiency.

Despite these advancements, robust obstacle avoidance with formal guarantees remains an area of active research for embedded MPC. Current real-time implementations often rely on conservative over-approximations or local convexifications, such as linearized distance constraints or tangent half-spaces. These approximations can be fragile in complex scenarios like narrow passages, cul-de-sacs, or with dynamic obstacles, potentially leading to collisions or requiring extensive, scenario-specific tuning of safety margins. Similarly, barrier function methods for obstacle avoidance also frequently necessitate significant tuning, and while Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability can create tuning-free barrier functions, it struggles with high-dimensional systems. Learning-based barrier functions, while promising, often involve neural networks that are too computationally intensive for real-time embedded deployment.

Semidefinite Programming (SDP) offers an alternative approach. It provides a principled framework for formulating nonconvex geometric constraints as convex problems by lifting quadratic or polynomial constraints into higher-dimensional positive semidefinite cones. This method has been applied successfully in offline trajectory optimization, state estimation, localization, and sum-of-squares optimization, offering robust global guarantees. However, traditional general-purpose SDP solvers are computationally expensive and memory-intensive, rendering them unsuitable for real-time, receding horizon control, especially on embedded systems.

TinySDP positions itself at the intersection of these fields, aiming to bridge the gap between the theoretical advantages of SDP-based safety guarantees and the practical computational and memory limitations of embedded real-time MPC. It seeks to integrate the global reasoning capabilities of SDP with the real-time performance of embedded optimization techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable real-time semidefinite optimization for certifiable and agile edge robotics, specifically for model-predictive control on microcontrollers with nonconvex obstacle constraints. This overarching goal is motivated by several key challenges and opportunities:

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Embedded MPC for Obstacle Avoidance:** Existing embedded MPC implementations struggle with robustly handling nonconvex obstacle constraints. They often rely on local approximations (e.g., linearized tangent half-spaces) that can fail in complex geometries (e.g., cul-de-sacs, narrow passages, dynamic obstacles) or require impractically large, manually tuned safety margins. This results in solutions that are either unsafe or overly conservative, hindering agile navigation.
- **Leveraging the Principled Nature of Semidefinite Programming (SDP):** SDP provides a mathematically rigorous method for convexly relaxing nonconvex quadratic constraints, offering stronger, more global geometric guarantees compared to local approximations. The motivation is to bring this robust framework to real-time control.
- **Overcoming Computational Barriers of SDP for Embedded Systems:** Traditional SDP solvers are computationally prohibitive for real-time, resource-constrained environments due to their high computational cost (e.g., cubic scaling with matrix dimensions) and memory demands. A core motivation is to devise a method that can exploit problem structure to make SDP tractable for embedded platforms.
- **Enabling Certifiable Safety:** The goal is not just to avoid collisions, but to provide explicit, a posteriori geometric safety guarantees at each timestep, transforming relaxed solutions into certifiable safety statements. This is crucial for applications requiring high levels of assurance.
- **Facilitating Agile and Efficient Navigation:** By providing less conservative and more globally optimal solutions for obstacle avoidance, the research aims to enable robots to navigate more efficiently and along shorter paths, even in challenging, dynamic environments.
- **Demonstrating Real-World Feasibility:** The motivation extends to validating the proposed approach on actual hardware (a Crazyflie quadrotor) to prove its practical applicability and performance on typical embedded platforms.

In pursuit of these motivations, the specific objectives of TinySDP include:
1. **Developing an embedded-compatible SDP solver:** This involves integrating positive-semidefinite cone projections into a cached-Riccati-based ADMM solver, specifically designed to leverage computational structure for embedded tractability.
2. **Formulating a structured SDP relaxation:** The method aims for a per-stage, convex, semidefinite relaxation of nonconvex, quadratic, disk-based obstacle avoidance constraints that is small, structured, and compatible with Riccati-based MPC.
3. **Designing an a posteriori rank-1 safety certificate:** This certificate is intended to convert relaxed solutions into explicit geometric guarantees, ensuring the true robot position avoids all obstacles when the certificate holds.
4. **Evaluating performance on challenging benchmarks:** The research seeks to demonstrate superior performance in terms of collision avoidance, path length, and goal precision compared to state-of-the-art baselines in scenarios known to cause failures in local methods.
5. **Validating hardware deployment:** The project aims to demonstrate that semidefinite constraints can be enforced at real-time rates on resource-constrained microcontrollers for agile robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TinySDP integrates semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxations into a real-time Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework suitable for embedded systems. The methodology involves a structured lifting of the MPC problem, an efficient Riccati-based ADMM solver, and an a posteriori safety certificate.

#### 3.1. PSD-Relaxed Lifted MPC Formulation

The approach starts with a standard finite-horizon MPC problem for a discrete-time linear time-invariant system, augmented with specific constraints:
- **Input Bounds:** Standard box constraints on control inputs ($u_{\min} \le u_k \le u_{\max}$).
- **Obstacle Avoidance:** Nonconvex constraints formulated as disk-based keep-out zones: $||p_k - c_j||_2^2 \ge r_j^2$, where $p_k$ is the planar position component of the state, $c_j$ is the obstacle center, and $r_j$ is its radius.

To address the nonconvexity of obstacle constraints while maintaining a convex optimization problem, the paper employs a semidefinite lifting technique:
- **Lifted Variables:** Auxiliary second-order moment variables are introduced for the state and input outer products: $X_k = x_k x_k^\top$, $XU_k = x_k u_k^\top$, $U X_k = u_k x_k^\top$, $U U_k = u_k u_k^\top$. These are combined into lifted state ($\bar{x}_k$) and input ($\bar{u}_k$) vectors. The initial lifted state $\bar{x}_0$ is constructed to be rank-consistent.
- **Lifted Dynamics:** The original linear dynamics ($x_{k+1} = Ax_k + Bu_k$) are shown to remain linear in the lifted variables ($\bar{x}_{k+1} = \bar{A} \bar{x}_k + \bar{B} \bar{u}_k$), where $\bar{A}$ and $\bar{B}$ involve Kronecker products of the original system matrices.
- **Lifted Costs:** Quadratic stage and terminal costs ($x_k^\top Q x_k + u_k^\top R u_k$) are rewritten as linear functions of the lifted variables after applying projection matrices $C_x$ and $C_u$ to extract physical variables.
- **Lifted Geometric Constraints:** The nonconvex disk obstacle avoidance constraint $||p_k - c_j||_2^2 \ge r_j^2$ is expanded and then rewritten using the lifted position block $X_k^{(p)} := C_p X_k C_p^\top$ (where $p_k = C_p x_k$) to yield a linear inequality constraint in the lifted variables: $\text{trace}(X_k^{(p)}) - 2c_j^\top p_k + ||c_j||_2^2 \ge r_j^2$. This constraint is exact if $X_k = x_k x_k^\top$.
- **Per-Stage PSD Constraint:** A crucial step is the introduction of a positive semidefinite constraint on an augmented state-input moment matrix $M_k(\bar{x}_k, \bar{u}_k) \succeq 0$. This matrix encapsulates the lifted state, input, and their outer products. While in the exact (non-relaxed) case $M_k$ would be rank-1, relaxing it to $M_k \succeq 0$ creates a convex problem allowing for the assessment of relaxation tightness a posteriori. Physical variables $x_k$ and $u_k$ remain explicit.

The resulting MPC problem is a convex optimization problem over lifted variables, subject to linear lifted dynamics, lifted quadratic costs, input bounds, lifted geometric inequalities, and per-stage positive semidefinite constraints.

#### 3.2. Riccati–ADMM Solver

To solve this convex problem efficiently on embedded systems, TinySDP adapts the TinyMPC framework, which uses an Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) combined with Riccati recursion:
- **ADMM Structure:** ADMM separates the problem into a primal update, a positive semidefinite (PSD) projection, and a dual update. Slack variables $S_k$ (for $M_k = S_k$) and scaled dual variables $H_k$ are introduced. The `svec(·)` operator is used to compactly store symmetric matrices as vectors, reducing memory.
- **Primal Update (Riccati Step):** When $S_k$ and $H_k$ are fixed, the primal subproblem (minimizing the augmented Lagrangian with respect to $\bar{x}$ and $\bar{u}$) becomes a lifted Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problem. The PSD-augmented term from the augmented Lagrangian adds linear cost terms to the problem. This LQR problem can be solved efficiently using a backward-forward Riccati sweep, leveraging cached steady-state Riccati quantities computed offline (as in TinyMPC).
- **PSD Projection:** The slack update involves projecting $M_k(\bar{x}_k^+, \bar{u}_k^+) + H_k$ onto the positive semidefinite cone. This projection has an analytic solution, requiring an eigenvalue decomposition of the matrix, followed by clamping negative eigenvalues to zero and reconstructing the matrix.
- **Dual Update:** The dual variables $H_k$ are updated via a simple gradient ascent step.
- **Offline Caching:** Prior to online operation, lifted dynamics, costs, and steady-state Riccati quantities are computed and cached to reduce online computational load.

#### 3.3. Overall TinySDP Algorithm

The TinySDP algorithm operates in a receding-horizon MPC loop. Offline, it prepares the cached Riccati data. Online, at each control timestep, it solves the finite-horizon PSD-relaxed MPC problem using the ADMM iterations. Within each ADMM iteration, the cached Riccati quantities are used for the primal step, followed by the PSD projection and dual updates. The first control input from the optimized sequence is then applied.

#### 3.4. A Posteriori Rank-1 Safety Certificate

To provide explicit geometric safety guarantees, the approach includes an online safety certificate:
- **Trace Gap ($\Delta_k$):** Defined as $\text{trace}(X_k^{(p)}) - ||p_k||_2^2$, this scalar measures the discrepancy between the relaxed lifted position block and the true physical position's second moment. $\Delta_k = 0$ indicates perfect consistency (rank-1).
- **Lifted Margin ($\eta_{k,j}$):** This is the value of the linear obstacle-avoidance constraint enforced by the solver: $\text{trace}(X_k^{(p)}) - 2c_j^\top p_k + ||c_j||_2^2 - r_j^2$.
- **Certification Logic:** The true squared clearance to obstacle $j$ is $\delta_{k,j} := ||p_k - c_j||_2^2 - r_j^2$. An identity links these: $\delta_{k,j} = \eta_{k,j} - \Delta_k$. Therefore, if the minimum lifted margin $\eta_{\min,k} \ge 0$ and the absolute trace gap $|\Delta_k|$ is less than or equal to $\eta_{\min,k}$, then $\delta_{k,j} \ge 0$ for all obstacles $j$, mathematically certifying collision-freedom for the physical state $p_k$.
- **Online Safety Monitor:** A runtime monitor is integrated. If the certificate (23) fails for a planned solution, the system discards the unsafe control input and executes a fallback stop-and-hover policy, preventing uncertified actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TinySDP involved comparative simulations against baseline methods on challenging static and dynamic obstacle scenarios, culminating in a real-world hardware deployment.

#### 4.1. Simulation: Static U-Shape Cul-de-Sac

- **Performance:** TinySDP consistently achieved collision-free navigation across four initial conditions in a concave U-shaped cul-de-sac. It reached the goal with high precision (within 0.02 m).
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **TinyMPC-LIN (linearized tangent half-spaces) and TinyMPC-HOCBF (higher-order control barrier functions)** with zero safety margin failed (collided) in all scenarios due to their reliance on local information and inability to enforce global geometric keep-out.
 - **Tuned TinyMPC-LIN (with 3.1 m safety margin):** While it could navigate safely, the margin was impractical (approximately 30 times the Crazyflie diameter), and it forced the solver to terminate 1.4 m away from the actual goal due to overlapping inflated obstacle boundaries.
 - **TinyMPC-HOCBF (with 3m margin):** Continued to fail (collide) regardless of the safety margin, highlighting limitations of local reactive methods in such trap-like geometries.
 - **RPCBF (sampling-based policy safety filter):** Successfully remained collision-free with a 0m margin but generated significantly longer and more conservative trajectories.
- **Path Efficiency:** TinySDP produced paths 31–73% shorter than RPCBF and 2–59% shorter than the tuned TinyMPC-LIN, while achieving 4–15 times more precision in goal reaching than RPCBF and 70 times more precision than tuned TinyMPC-LIN.

#### 4.2. Simulation: Dynamic Obstacles (Moving Gap)

- **Performance:** TinySDP successfully navigated a scenario with a static obstacle and two moving obstacles that periodically closed a narrow passage. It maintained positive clearance and the rank-1 certificate (∆k ≈ 0) remained valid at every timestep, demonstrating its ability to anticipate dynamic obstacles.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Zero-margin TinyMPC-LIN and TinyMPC-HOCBF:** Both collided early due to insufficient anticipation of fast-moving geometries.
 - **Tuned TinyMPC-LIN (1.5 m margin) and TinyMPC-HOCBF (3 m margin):** Achieved safety, but with impractical margins (17–30 times the drone's size).
 - **RPCBF:** Remained collision-free but with significantly longer, conservative paths.
- **Path Efficiency:** TinySDP achieved paths 30% shorter than RPCBF and 43% shorter than the tuned TinyMPC-HOCBF. It was 3.8–4.3 times more precise in goal reaching than RPCBF and TinyMPC-HOCBF. The tuned TinyMPC-LIN achieved a slightly shorter path in one specific case due to a fortuitous timing that allowed it to bypass an active avoidance maneuver, whereas TinySDP actively negotiated the obstacle.

#### 4.3. Simulation: Extension to 3D Obstacle Avoidance Demos

- **Generalizability:** The paper demonstrated that the lifting framework extends naturally to 3D spherical obstacles.
- **Scenarios:** TinySDP successfully navigated two dynamic 3D scenarios: "Sweeping Barrier" (requiring early nonplanar deviation) and "Vertical Gate" (forcing timed ascent-descent maneuvers). In both cases, it reached the goal without collisions and with positive clearance.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment: Dynamic Obstacles on Crazyflie

- **Hardware Validation:** TinySDP was deployed on a resource-constrained Crazyflie 2.1 Brushless quadrotor (STM32F405 microcontroller, 168 MHz, 192 KB SRAM).
- **Real-time Performance:** The solver ran onboard at 25 Hz with a 20-step horizon. The ADMM was capped at 5 iterations per control update. The average MPC solve time was 14.842 ms, leaving approximately 25.2 ms margin within the 40 ms control period.
- **Resource Footprint:** The compiled firmware used 272.4 KB of flash and 139.0 KB of on-chip RAM. The PSD-specific overhead was modest: 1.47 KB static memory, ~720 B peak additional stack, and 2.5–3.4 KB flash.
- **PSD Projection Efficiency:** For the planar (2D position) application, a 3x3 moment matrix was used, and the PSD projection via specialized small-matrix eigendecomposition was not a computational bottleneck.
- **Certifiable Safety in Reality:** During a dynamic obstacle avoidance task (Crazyflie avoiding a sweeping robotic arm), the online safety certificate (∆k ≈ 0) remained valid at every control step across five independent trials. This confirmed the preservation of rank-1 geometry and thus certifiable safety even under real-world disturbances and finite-precision arithmetic. The drone successfully anticipated and deviated from the moving obstacle.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TinySDP represents a notable advancement in the field of robotic motion planning and control, particularly for resource-constrained systems. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Enabling Certifiable Safety on Embedded Platforms:** This work demonstrates the feasibility of integrating advanced semidefinite programming techniques, traditionally confined to offline or high-performance computing, directly onto embedded microcontrollers. This provides a mechanism for real-time, mathematically certifiable collision avoidance, which is a significant departure from previous embedded methods that relied on local approximations or conservative heuristics. For safety-critical robotic applications (e.g., autonomous drones, mobile robots operating in proximity to humans or valuable assets), the ability to offer explicit geometric safety guarantees at high rates is crucial.

- **Bridging the Gap Between Theory and Practice:** TinySDP successfully bridges the divide between the theoretical robustness of SDP-based methods and the practical constraints of real-time embedded systems. By demonstrating that SDP-based safety constraints can be incorporated efficiently when solver design respects MPC structure (e.g., cached-Riccati ADMM), the research establishes a methodological pathway for integrating more sophisticated convex safety models into high-rate controllers.

- **Enhanced Agility and Efficiency:** The comparative results indicate that TinySDP enables more agile and efficient navigation. By producing significantly shorter and more precise paths compared to state-of-the-art baselines, TinySDP allows robots to operate closer to optimality without sacrificing safety. This can lead to reduced energy consumption, faster task completion, and improved performance in dynamic, cluttered environments where conservative planning can lead to deadlocks or extensive detours.

- **Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The successful navigation of complex scenarios like cul-de-sacs and dynamic moving-gap obstacles, where local methods often fail or require impractical safety margins, highlights the robustness of TinySDP's global geometric reasoning. This is particularly relevant for unpredictable real-world deployments.

- **Methodological Innovation:** The integration of per-stage semidefinite lifting with a cached-Riccati-based ADMM solver and an a posteriori rank-1 safety certificate is a methodological contribution. It provides a template for how other complex convex relaxations could potentially be adapted for embedded real-time control by carefully exploiting problem structure and separating optimization from certification.

- **Motivation for Future Research:** This work opens avenues for future research. It motivates the development of more low-compute and low-memory footprint solvers for real-time control, the extension to full 3D obstacle avoidance on microcontrollers (which currently presents a computational trade-off with larger moment matrices), richer obstacle representations beyond disks/spheres, and the integration of proactive recovery guarantees (e.g., via forward reachability analysis) to complement the current reactive fallback policy.

In summary, TinySDP's significance lies in its capacity to empower edge robotics with advanced, certifiable safety capabilities, transforming complex theoretical optimization into practical, real-time control solutions for autonomous systems operating in challenging and dynamic environments.
