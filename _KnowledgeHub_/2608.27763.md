# Fast Weight Attention for Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Falcon (Fast Weight Attention) recasts recurrent state updates as online continual learning: the fixed-size state is a fast linear predictor trained on the prefix-aligned pair (phi(k_{t-1}), v_t) rather than the usual same-step pair. Normalized first-order updates give six rules, scalar, per-column, and sliding-window, for squared-error and inner-product objectives, with chunk-parallel forms; they match strong recurrent baselines in language modeling and improve length extrapolation on variable-digit addition.

## Problem

- Transformer attention costs quadratic compute and a growing KV cache, so long-context modeling needs fixed-size recurrent state without catastrophic interference from new evidence.
- Prior SSMs and fast-weight models state update rules architecturally, leaving the local learning objective and its temporal alignment implicit and unexamined.
- Common recurrences bind the same-step key-value association, which is causal but optimizes a different internal objective than read-after-write prefix prediction actually requires.

## Method

- Prefix-aligned next-latent formulation: the ==fast-memory training pair== is the previous step's key feature mapped to the newly revealed value, making the state an autoregressive linear predictor.
- **Falcon-1/2/3** are ==NLMS-style normalized regression== writes, scalar plasticity, per-value-channel gain vector, and a sliding-window mini-batch residual, with decay lambda controlling shrinkage.
- **Falcon-1A/2A/3A** are inner-product counterparts using write-magnitude rather than curvature normalization, plus recurrent, masked-parallel and ==chunk-parallel== forms with stable positive-decay renormalization.

## Results

- At 124M-130M parameters on 50B FineWeb-Edu tokens, Falcon-1.3 reaches 17.10 validation perplexity versus 17.32 for Gated DeltaNet and 17.38 for the Transformer.
- Falcon-1A.2 gets the best zero-shot 8-task average at 49.30 and Falcon-1.3 the best recurrent one-shot average at 49.54, above DeltaNet's 48.50.
- On variable-digit addition trained to 32 digits, Falcon-3A.3 reaches 87.2 mean out-of-distribution accuracy versus 82.9 for RetNet, 75.2 Mamba-2, 65.8 Transformer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naming the local objective a recurrent state implicitly optimizes exposes design axes, temporal alignment, plasticity, forgetting and rehearsal, that architecture-level gating descriptions hide.
> - Classical adaptive-filtering normalization such as NLMS imports directly into fast-weight updates, giving scale-robust online writes that still admit chunk-parallel training.
> - Perplexity gains were small while length extrapolation gains were large, so causal alignment mainly helps tasks dominated by exact storage and carry propagation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The research addresses challenges in long-context modeling within sequence processing, particularly concerning the computational scalability and continual learning aspects. Transformers, while effective in capturing global dependencies, incur quadratic computational and memory costs (O(N^2)) with sequence length, which becomes a bottleneck for long contexts. This limitation has motivated the development of recurrent alternatives.

The paper positions its work in relation to several existing paradigms:
- **Recurrent Alternatives**: Models such as Linear Attention, Fast Weight Programmers, Delta Networks, RWKV, and Mamba demonstrate that recurrent architectures can achieve competitive performance with linear (O(N)) training and constant (O(1)) per-step inference complexity.
- **State Space Models (SSMs)**: Modern SSMs like Mamba and Mamba-2 process sequences through a compressed latent state. Mamba-2, in particular, established a theoretical bridge through Structured State Space Duality (SSD), demonstrating an equivalence between recurrent SSMs and a form of causal linear attention, enabling efficient chunk-parallel training.
- **Fast-Weight Models**: These models, including Fast Weight Programmers and Delta Networks, conceptualize sequence modeling as the online learning of a value-retrieval function, where a fast-weight state matrix is updated in response to input.
- **Continual Learning**: The paper frames long-context modeling as a continual-learning problem where a model must integrate new information online without experiencing catastrophic interference. While Transformers manage this with a growing key-value (KV) cache, SSMs and fast-weight models aim to compress this fast memory into a fixed-size recurrent state.

This work specifically re-examines the underlying online learning objectives and temporal alignment mechanisms within these recurrent and fast-weight architectures. It aims to make explicit the local objectives that drive state updates, distinguish them from common implicit conventions, and derive normalized updates that are compatible with modern efficient training paradigms like chunk-parallel processing. The objective is to unify temporal alignment, plasticity, forgetting, and bounded rehearsal within a single framework.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The core objective of this research is to formalize and improve recurrent sequence modeling by explicitly defining the online learning problem that governs the updates of a fixed-size recurrent memory state. The authors identify several motivations and specific goals:

- **Clarify Temporal Alignment**: Many existing recurrent architectures, including linear attention and fast-weight models, employ state-update rules that are often presented at an architectural level, leaving the specific local objective and temporal alignment implicit. The paper highlights a convention mismatch: while many recurrences bind the "same-step" pair (`(ϕ(k_t), v_t)`), the autoregressive prefix-prediction objective requires pairing the newly revealed target `v_t` with the prefix feature that was available when it was predicted, specifically `ϕ(k_t-1)`. A primary objective is to identify and consistently use this "next-latent" causal pair (`(ϕ(k_t-1), v_t)`) under read-after-write semantics.
- **Derive Objective-Matched Updates**: Building on the identified causal alignment, the research aims to derive normalized first-order updates for the recurrent state. These updates are explicitly grounded in standard online optimization problems:
 - **Squared-Error Regression**: Interpreting the state as a fast linear predictor, updates are derived from an instantaneous ridge-regression loss. This connects to and extends classical Delta Networks.
 - **Negative Inner-Product**: An alternative objective encouraging alignment between state prediction and target, which leads to additive (Hebbian-like) writes.
- **Enhance Stability and Control**: The derived updates incorporate normalization principles (analogous to Normalized Least Mean Squares, NLMS) to ensure numerical stability and scale robustness. Additionally, the framework introduces explicit controls for plasticity (gain parameters `β_t`) and forgetting (regularization coefficients `λ_t`), which are integrated into the step-size calculation.
- **Develop Varied Update Mechanisms**: To explore different levels of adaptivity and context aggregation, the authors aim to introduce a family of variants:
 - Scalar updates (Falcon-1/1A): uniform plasticity across all value channels.
 - Per-column updates (Falcon-2/2A): individualized plasticity for each value channel.
 - Sliding-window mini-batch updates (Falcon-3/3A): aggregating information over a recent history window to capture local dependencies and reduce noise.
- **Ensure Compatibility with Efficient Training**: A key practical motivation is to ensure that these theoretically grounded, causally aligned update rules remain compatible with efficient, parallel training paradigms prevalent in modern recurrent sequence models, particularly SSD-style chunk-parallel training. This involves developing mathematically equivalent parallel forms (e.g., using Gram/WY representations or structured attention masks).
- **Separate and Control Architectural Elements**: The overarching motivation is to create a framework that explicitly separates and allows for controlled manipulation of temporal alignment, plasticity, forgetting, and bounded rehearsal within recurrent sequence models. This can lead to more interpretable models and potentially improved performance in tasks requiring robust long-term memory.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The methodology of this research centers on recasting recurrent state updates in sequence models as an explicit online continual learning problem. The approach involves:

#### 3.1. Causal Alignment and Online Learning Objective

- **Read-After-Write (RAW) Semantics**: The paper adopts a "read-after-write" convention, where the updated state `S_t` (after observing `v_t`) is used to predict the next token (`t+1`).
- **Next-Latent Prediction**: For autoregressive prefix-prediction, the causally aligned training pair for updating the fast memory `S` at step `t` is identified as `(x_t := ϕ(k_t-1), y_t := v_t)`. This means the target `v_t` is bound to the key `k_t-1` that was available *before* `v_t` was observed. This is distinguished from the "same-step" `(ϕ(k_t), v_t)` pairing found in some other models.
- **Online Gradient Descent (OGD)**: The recurrent state `S_t` is viewed as an online linear predictor. Its updates are derived by taking a single gradient step on an instantaneous loss function, rather than relying on purely additive or architecturally specified rules.

#### 3.2. Derived Fast-Weight Update Rules (Falcon Family)

The paper derives two main families of updates based on different instantaneous objectives, each with scalar, per-column, and sliding-window variants:

- **Regression Loss (Delta Network — Falcon-1, Falcon-2, Falcon-3)**:
 - **Objective**: Instantaneous ridge-regression loss: `ℓ_t(S) = 1/2 ||S^T x_t - y_t||^2_2 + λ_t/2 ||S||^2_F`. The `L_2` regularization term (`λ_t`) serves as a forgetting mechanism.
 - **Falcon-1 (Scalar Plasticity)**: Applies a single scalar learning rate `η_t` across all `d_v` value channels. The update is `S_t = (1 - η_tλ_t)S_t-1 + η_t x_t r_t^T`, where `r_t = y_t - S_t-1^T x_t` is the residual.
 - **Falcon-2 (Per-Channel Plasticity)**: Extends Falcon-1 by allowing a vector `η_t ∈ R^dv` of learning rates, enabling each value channel (column of `S`) to have its own plasticity. This is possible because the regression loss is separable across value dimensions.
 - **Falcon-3 (Sliding-Window Mini-batch)**: Takes a mini-batch gradient step over a window `I_t` of `B` recent causal pairs. The objective is the window-averaged squared loss. All residuals are evaluated at the pre-update state `S_t-1`.

- **Inner-Product Loss (Linear Attention/Mamba-2 — Falcon-1A, Falcon-2A, Falcon-3A)**:
 - **Objective**: Negative inner-product loss: `ℓ_ip_t(S) = -⟨S^T x_t, y_t⟩ + λ_t/2 ||S||^2_F`. When `λ_t=0`, this results in purely additive (Hebbian) writes.
 - **Falcon-1A (Scalar Inner-Product Write)**: `S_t = (1 - η_tλ_t)S_t-1 + η_t x_t y_t^T`.
 - **Falcon-2A (Per-Channel Inner-Product Write)**: Each value channel `j` has its own learning rate `η_j,t`.
 - **Falcon-3A (Sliding-Window Inner-Product Write)**: Writes the window-averaged cross-covariance `N_t^(B)` instead of a residual-driven update.

#### 3.3. Normalization and Stability

- **Normalized Step Sizes**: For numerical stability, particularly under varying input magnitudes, `η_t` is normalized. For regression, `η_t = β_t / (||x_t||^2_2 + λ_t + ε)` based on the smoothness constant of the loss. For inner-product, a similar form is used, but `||x_t||^2_2` acts as a write-magnitude normalizer rather than a curvature one.
- **RMS Normalization (RMSNorm)**: Query (`q_t`) and key (`k_t`) projections are RMS-normalized before forming dot or outer products to stabilize both read and write streams.
- **Positive Decay Renormalization**: For numerical stability in long sequences, especially in mixed precision, multiplicative decay factors (`γ_t`) are handled in log-space, and chunk-local renormalization is applied to prevent underflow/overflow.

#### 3.4. Parallel Implementation Strategies

The paper provides chunk-parallel implementations for the derived recurrences, compatible with SSD-style training:
- **For Regression Variants (Falcon-1/2/3)**: These updates, which involve rank-one edits (`I - η_t x_t x_t^T`), are parallelized using a WY/Gram matrix representation. This approach converts the recurrence into a form that can be solved via batched triangular solves within chunks, propagating a fixed-size state across chunks.
- **For Inner-Product Variants (Falcon-3A)**: The recurrent sliding-window update is unrolled into a form resembling unnormalized dot-product attention with a structured causal mask. This allows for fully vectorized, GPU-parallel training over the sequence dimension.
- **Low-Rank Affine Recurrence (Falcon-3)**: Falcon-3's update is mapped to a rank-B affine recurrence, enabling the use of the ParallelFlow `tensorInv` algorithm for efficient chunk-parallel computation.

#### 3.5. Experimental Design

- **Language Modeling**: Evaluated on 124M–130M parameter models trained on FineWeb-Edu (50B tokens), assessing perplexity and downstream zero-shot/one-shot task accuracy.
- **Arithmetic Extrapolation**: Used variable-length multi-digit addition as a controlled diagnostic task to stress-test causal storage and length extrapolation capabilities, measuring teacher-forced target-suffix accuracy.

### 4. Main findings and results

The empirical evaluation focused on selected Falcon variants in language modeling and arithmetic tasks.

#### 4.1. Language Modeling Experiments (124M-130M parameters)

- **Perplexity on FineWeb-Edu Validation**:
 - Falcon-1.3 (scalar regression with QK-RMSNorm, ctxη-ctxλ) achieved a perplexity of 17.10, which was the lowest (best) among all evaluated recurrent models, including baselines like Gated DeltaNet (17.32) and Mamba-2 (17.70), and competitive with the Transformer baseline (17.38).
 - Falcon-1A.3 (scalar inner-product with QK-RMSNorm, ctxη-ctxλ) achieved 17.40, making it the best evaluated inner-product variant.
- **Downstream Task Accuracy (Zero-shot)**:
 - Falcon-1A.2 (scalar inner-product with QK-ℓ2-norm, ctxη-ctxλ) achieved the highest average zero-shot accuracy (49.30) among the listed models, slightly surpassing the Transformer (48.16) and other recurrent baselines.
 - Falcon-1.3 (scalar regression) achieved an average of 49.18.
- **Downstream Task Accuracy (One-shot)**:
 - Falcon-1.3 exhibited the best recurrent one-shot average accuracy at 49.54, closely trailing the Transformer (49.67).
- **Normalization and Gating Ablations**:
 - QK-RMSNorm generally improved FineWeb-Edu perplexity compared to QK-ℓ2-norm in inner-product variants.
 - Context-conditioned `η` (step size) generally improved small-model zero-shot and one-shot averages compared to context-conditioned `β` (gain) variants.

These language modeling results indicate that the proposed aligned and normalized fast-weight updates allow recurrent models to maintain competitive quality against Transformer and other recurrent baselines.

#### 4.2. Variable-Length Arithmetic Addition Tasks

- **Length Extrapolation**: This task evaluated the models' ability to handle multi-digit addition beyond their training length, specifically for 33–48 digits (trained on 1–32 digits).
 - Falcon-3A.3 (sliding-window inner-product with QK-RMSNorm, ctxη-ctxλ, B=4) demonstrated the best extrapolation performance, achieving a mean accuracy of 87.2% across the 33–48 digit range.
 - Falcon-1A.3 (scalar inner-product with QK-RMSNorm, ctxη-ctxλ) also performed well, with a mean accuracy of 85.9%.
 - Both Falcon-3A.3 and Falcon-1A.3 significantly outperformed the Transformer (65.8%), RetNet/LightningAttn (82.9%), and Mamba-2 (75.2%) baselines.
 - Falcon-1.3 (scalar regression) showed lower performance on this task (68.8%) compared to its inner-product counterparts.

The arithmetic addition results suggest that the proposed shifted, normalized updates, particularly the inner-product variants (especially with sliding windows), enhance the models' ability to store and process information causally, which is critical for tasks requiring precise length extrapolation and carry propagation. This demonstrates a specific strength in memory-intensive tasks.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research introduces a framework that reformulates recurrent sequence modeling through the lens of online continual learning with explicit fast-memory objectives. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Formalization of Fast-Weight Updates**: The work provides a formal, optimization-driven derivation for fast-weight updates, grounding them in classical online learning principles (e.g., ridge regression, NLMS). This moves beyond purely architectural or heuristic update rules, offering a more principled approach to designing recurrent memory mechanisms.
- **Explicit Temporal Alignment**: By rigorously identifying and consistently applying the "next-latent" causal training pair (`(ϕ(k_t-1), v_t)`) under read-after-write semantics, the paper addresses a critical, often implicit, aspect of autoregressive sequence modeling. This explicit alignment ensures that the fast memory is updated with information that was genuinely available at prediction time, potentially improving causal coherence and learning efficiency.
- **Enhanced Control and Stability**: The derived normalized updates, incorporating specific gain and regularization parameters (`β_t`, `λ_t`), offer explicit controls over plasticity and forgetting. This fine-grained control can lead to more stable and robust learning in dynamic environments. The use of RMSNorm and log-space renormalization further enhances numerical stability, which is crucial for training large models over long sequences.
- **Compatibility with Modern Hardware Architectures**: The development of chunk-parallel implementations (using WY/Gram decompositions for regression and structured attention masks for inner-product variants) ensures that these theoretically robust learning rules are practical and efficient for training on current GPU hardware, compatible with paradigms like Structured State Space Duality (SSD). This bridges the gap between theoretical online learning and high-throughput neural network training.
- **Competitive Performance and Extrapolation**: The empirical results demonstrate that the proposed Falcon variants not only maintain competitive language modeling quality against contemporary Transformer and recurrent baselines but also show superior length extrapolation capabilities on algorithmic tasks like multi-digit addition. This suggests a more robust and scalable approach to handling long-term dependencies and memory in sequences.
- **Framework for Future Research**: By separating and explicitly addressing distinct components such as temporal alignment, plasticity, forgetting, and bounded rehearsal, the research provides a modular framework. This could facilitate future investigations into each of these areas, allowing for targeted improvements in recurrent sequence models for various continual learning scenarios, meta-learning, and long-context applications where effective memory management is paramount. For instance, further research could explore more sophisticated context-conditioned `β` and `λ` mechanisms or adaptive windowing strategies.
