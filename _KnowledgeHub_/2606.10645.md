# ManiSplat: Manipulation Trajectory Synthesis from Monocular Video via Decoupled 3D Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> ManiSplat reconstructs controllable 3D Gaussian digital twins of dynamic manipulation scenes directly from monocular robotic videos, explicitly disentangling robot, object, and background components. It achieves superior dynamic reconstruction quality with a PSNR of 32.31 and reduces object pose estimation errors to 0.5864 cm translation and 5.185 degrees rotation, while also enabling topology-preserving data augmentation for robotic policy learning.

## Problem

- Existing dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods lack explicit object-level disentanglement or are not designed for interaction-driven scenes with articulated robots and manipulable objects.
- Current Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) pipelines often rely on static scene scans and pre-aligned assets, limiting their ability to create digital twins from raw, dynamically captured robotic videos.
- Robotic policy learning is hindered by data scarcity, necessitating methods to augment demonstration data while maintaining physical consistency and task fidelity.

## Method

- A ==graph-structured disentangled representation== models the scene with independent Gaussian subfields for the static background, articulated robot (driven by URDF kinematics), and manipulable objects with time-varying poses.
- A ==task-oriented spatio-temporal alignment== module segments videos into skill and motion phases, using hybrid pose estimation (kinematic constraints for motion, tracking for skills) and appearance alignment with dynamic masks.
- A ==topology-preserving data augmentation== pipeline applies rigid transformations to skill segments and re-plans motion segments to synthesize diverse, physically consistent manipulation trajectories from single demonstrations.

## Results

- ManiSplat achieved superior dynamic reconstruction performance, with a PSNR of **32.31** and SSIM of **0.951** for full images, outperforming 4DGS, DeformableGS, and HUGS on synthetic data.
- The method demonstrated high object pose estimation accuracy on synthetic data, yielding an average translation error of **0.5864 cm** and rotation error of **5.185°**, a reduction compared to baselines.
- It successfully generated photorealistic and physically consistent novel scenarios and trajectories from single real-world demonstration videos, proving its capability for data augmentation in robotic learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling dynamic scene components (robot, object, background) within a graph-structured Gaussian representation is crucial for precise control and accurate reconstruction of interaction-driven manipulation tasks.
> - Leveraging task-specific phases (skill vs. motion) and combining kinematic data with tracking-based methods significantly improves the accuracy and temporal stability of object pose estimation during complex robotic interactions.
> - Applying rigid transformations to core interaction 'skill' segments and adaptively re-planning 'motion' segments provides an effective strategy for data augmentation that preserves task topology and physical feasibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in ManiSplat operates at the intersection of 3D computer vision, particularly novel view synthesis and dynamic scene reconstruction, and robotics, specifically the Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) paradigm and robotic manipulation learning.

The foundation for this research lies in **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)**, a recent advancement that enables high-fidelity static 3D scene reconstruction and real-time rendering. Subsequent research has extended 3DGS to **dynamic scene reconstruction**, with methods such as Deformable 3D Gaussian, 4D-GS, MotionGS, and Spacetime Gaussian. These approaches typically encode motion implicitly within the Gaussian representation, treating Gaussians as functions of time. However, a limitation of these methods is their lack of explicit object-level disentanglement, which is crucial for scenarios involving multiple independent moving entities.

To address this, other works like Ex4DGS, HUGS, EgoGaussian, SplitGaussian, and BézierGS have introduced **explicit separation of static and dynamic components**. While these methods improve geometric stability and temporal coherence, they primarily target *passive dynamic scenes*, such such as driving scenarios or human motion, where dynamics are observed rather than actively induced by an articulated agent. They are often insufficient for *interaction-driven environments* involving robotic manipulators and manipulable objects, where complex contact interactions and abrupt pose changes are common. IGFuse explored interactive Gaussian reconstruction, but it relied on multi-state scene scans rather than continuous video streams of manipulation.

Concurrently, the **Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) paradigm** has gained prominence in robotics, aiming to bridge real-world perception and simulation for robotic learning. Recent studies have integrated 3DGS into robotic pipelines to enhance photorealism and physical consistency. Examples include Robo-GS, RL-GSBridge, SplatSim, RoboGSim, DREMA, and RE3SIM, which construct learnable digital twins and enable differentiable rendering for embodied policy learning. However, a common constraint in many of these R2S2R frameworks is their reliance on *static scene scans* and *pre-aligned assets* for geometry and pose alignment. This makes it challenging to model robots and manipulated objects directly from dynamically captured videos, which are characteristic of real-world robotic demonstrations.

ManiSplat positions itself by addressing the gap left by these existing approaches. It tackles the challenge of reconstructing *interaction-driven dynamic 3D scenes* directly from *monocular ego-view robotic videos*. By introducing a framework that explicitly disentangles robot, object, and background components within a structured representation, and by developing mechanisms to handle the unique dynamics of manipulation tasks, ManiSplat aims to provide a unified solution for creating simulation-ready digital twins that overcome the limitations of passive dynamic scene modeling and static asset reliance in R2S2R pipelines. Furthermore, it contributes a data augmentation strategy to alleviate data sparsity in robotic policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ManiSplat is to reconstruct controllable and decoupled Gaussian digital twins of dynamic and interactive 3D scenes directly from monocular ego-view robotic videos. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified challenges and limitations in current computer vision and robotics research:

1. **Fundamental Challenge of Dynamic Scene Reconstruction**: Reconstructing dynamic and interactive 3D scenes from real-world observations is a persistent challenge. While 3D Gaussian Splatting has advanced static reconstruction, extending it to complex interactive environments with articulated robots and manipulable objects remains difficult due to the intricacies of contact interactions and abrupt pose changes.

2. **Limitations of Existing Dynamic 3DGS Methods for Manipulation**:
 - Many dynamic 3DGS frameworks encode motion implicitly without explicit object-level disentanglement. This makes them unsuitable for scenes where individual components (robot, object) need to be independently controlled or analyzed.
 - Decoupled dynamic 3DGS methods often focus on passively observed dynamics (e.g., human movement, driving scenes). They are not designed to handle *interaction-driven scenarios* where dynamics are actively induced by articulated agents like robotic arms, leading to unique challenges in modeling complex contact and rapid state transitions.

3. **Limitations of R2S2R Pipelines for Dynamic Capture**: The Real2Sim2Real (R2S2R) paradigm is effective for robotic learning, but existing 3DGS-based R2S2R methods typically rely on *static scene scans* and *pre-aligned assets*. This dependency limits their ability to model robots and objects directly from *dynamically captured videos*, which are characteristic of real-world robot demonstrations and crucial for capturing the nuances of interaction. There is a need for methods that can process raw, dynamic video data to generate simulation-ready assets.

4. **Need for Controllable and Simulation-Ready Digital Twins**: For downstream robotic tasks and policy learning, digital twins must be not only visually high-fidelity but also *controllable* and *physically consistent*. This requires an explicit, disentangled representation of scene components that can be manipulated within a simulation environment.

5. **Addressing Data Scarcity in Robotic Learning**: Training robust robotic policies often requires vast amounts of diverse demonstration data. Real-world data collection can be time-consuming and expensive. A method to effectively augment existing demonstration data, generating physically feasible and spatially diverse synthetic trajectories from a single real-world demonstration, is highly motivated to overcome data sparsity and improve policy generalization.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Develop a unified framework for reconstructing interaction-driven dynamic Gaussian scenes directly from monocular ego-view robotic videos.
- Create a disentangled representation that explicitly separates robots, objects, and the background, enabling independent control and optimization.
- Introduce robust spatio-temporal alignment mechanisms to accurately estimate object poses and ensure visual consistency during complex interactions.
- Develop a data augmentation pipeline that can synthesize diverse, physically consistent manipulation trajectories from limited real-world demonstrations.
These objectives collectively aim to bridge the gap between high-fidelity dynamic scene reconstruction and the practical demands of robotic learning and simulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ManiSplat's methodology is structured around three core components: a Graph-Structured Disentangled Representation, a Task-Oriented Spatio-Temporal Alignment module, and a Topology-Preserving Data Augmentation pipeline, all integrated within a unified optimization framework.

#### 3.1. Graph-Structured Disentangled Representation

To enable precise control and manipulation, ManiSplat models the 3D scene as a dynamic scene graph. This graph consists of three types of independently optimizable Gaussian subfields:
- **Background Node ($\mathcal{G}_{bg}$)**: This node contains static Gaussian primitives representing the environment, which remain fixed in world coordinates.
- **Robot Node ($\mathcal{G}_{robot}$)**: The robot arm is modeled as a collection of articulated Gaussian clusters. Its geometry and movement are explicitly driven by a URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) kinematic chain. Given the robot's joint angles $q_t$ at time $t$, Gaussians attached to each link $l$ (denoted $\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{robot}^{(l)}$ in canonical space) are transformed into world space using forward kinematics $T_{base \to l}(q_t)$. This allows the robot's Gaussian representation to dynamically change based on its kinematic state.
- **Object Nodes ($\mathcal{G}_{v}^{obj}$)**: Each manipulable object $v$ is represented by its own canonical Gaussian set $\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{v}^{obj}$ and a time-varying pose $T_v(t) \in SE(3)$. The object's world-space representation is obtained by applying its current pose transformation to its canonical Gaussians.
The complete scene at any time $t$ is formed by the union of these transformed subfields: $\mathcal{G}(t) = \mathcal{G}_{bg} \cup \mathcal{G}_{robot}(t) \cup \{\mathcal{G}_{v}^{obj}(t)\}_v$. This explicit decoupling facilitates independent rendering and manipulation of each scene component.

#### 3.2. Task-Oriented Spatio-Temporal Alignment

Accurate estimation of object poses $T_v(t)$ is critical for consistent scene reconstruction. Instead of relying solely on general dynamic tracking, ManiSplat leverages the inherent structure of manipulation tasks through a task-oriented approach:

- **Interaction-Aware Phase Segmentation**: The robotic demonstration video is segmented into two distinct phases based on the distance $d(t)$ between the robot's end-effector (EE) and the target object, using a proximity threshold $\tau$:
 - **Skill Phase (S)**: When $d(t) < \tau$, representing critical interactions such as grasping or placing.
 - **Motion Phase (M)**: When $d(t) \ge \tau$, representing free-space transport, approaching, or returning.
 This segmentation allows for phase-specific handling of object dynamics. For example, a pick-and-place task might be segmented as $M_{approach} \to S_{pick} \to M_{transfer} \to S_{place} \to M_{return}$.

- **Hybrid Pose Estimation**: Pseudo-ground-truth pose trajectories ($T_{v}^{pseudo}(t)$) are constructed using phase-specific geometric constraints:
 - **Static Constraint (for $M_{approach}$, $M_{return}$ phases)**: When the object is not being manipulated, its pose is fixed to a stable initial or final state.
 - **Attached Constraint (for $M_{transfer}$ phase)**: During transport, the object is assumed to be rigidly attached to the gripper. Its pose is derived from the robot's accurate end-effector pose $T_{ee}(t)$ and a constant relative transformation $T_{rel}$ (captured at the end of the grasp).
 - **Tracking-Based Constraint (for $S_{pick}$, $S_{place}$ phases)**: For complex, non-rigid interactions, a point tracker (CoTracker) is used to track a set of 3D keypoints on the object. The optimal object pose $T(t)$ is then determined by minimizing the registration error between the canonical keypoints and their tracked positions.

- **Appearance Alignment**: To bridge the domain gap between the URDF-rendered robot and its real counterpart, and to disentangle dynamic objects from the static background, appearance consistency is enforced:
 - SAM2 is employed to generate dynamic masks ($M_{dyn}$) for moving components (robot and object) in each video frame.
 - A masked $L_1$ and $L_{SSIM}$ loss ($L_{app}$) is applied between the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the real image $I$, masked by $M_{dyn}$, to ensure photometric consistency of dynamic elements.
 - An opacity mask-consistency loss ($L_{mask}$) is introduced between the rendered alpha map of dynamic Gaussians ($O_{\mathcal{G}_{dyn}}$) and the SAM2 mask $M_{dyn}$. This promotes sharp boundaries and accurate disentanglement of dynamic entities.

#### 3.3. Optimization

The framework jointly optimizes the Gaussian parameters and the scene graph structure using a comprehensive loss function:
$L_{total} = \lambda_{render}L_{render} + \lambda_{app}L_{app} + \lambda_{mask}L_{mask} + \lambda_{pose}L_{pose}$
- $L_{render}$: Standard photometric loss from vanilla 3DGS, ensuring pixel-level color and structural consistency.
- $L_{app}$: Appearance alignment loss as described above.
- $L_{mask}$: Opacity mask consistency loss as described above.
- $L_{pose}$: A pose loss that supervises the object trajectories $T_v(t)$ based on the hybrid estimated pseudo-ground-truth poses $T_{v}^{pseudo}(t)$, using a distance metric in $SE(3)$.

The optimization uses the Adam optimizer for 30,000 iterations. Initial object poses are obtained via GenPose++, and CoTracker is used for dynamic point tracking.

#### 3.4. Topology-Preserving Data Augmentation

To scale demonstration data for policy learning, ManiSplat introduces a "Skill-Preserving, Motion-Adaptive" data augmentation strategy:
- **Skill Augmentation via Rigid Transformation**: For identified Skill segments, a random rigid transformation ($T_{aug}$) is applied to the object's pose. To maintain the physical integrity of the interaction, the robot's end-effector trajectory during that skill segment is subjected to the *same* transformation. This effectively transposes the core interaction mechanics to a new spatial configuration.
- **Motion Generation via Re-planning**: Motion segments (transitions between skills) are re-planned. Given the augmented terminal state of the preceding skill and the initial state of the subsequent skill, a motion planning framework generates a new, collision-free trajectory that connects these states in the modified environment. This adaptive re-planning ensures kinematic feasibility while preserving the high-level task logic.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates ManiSplat using both synthetic data from the RoboTwin simulation platform (providing ground truth) and real-world data captured with an AgileX dual-arm manipulator. Comparisons are made against 3DGS, 4DGS, DeformableGS, and HUGS.

#### 4.1. Dynamic Reconstruction

- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 3)**: ManiSplat demonstrated the ability to maintain sharp object boundaries and stable appearance during rapid manipulator movements and complex interactions. In contrast, 4DGS and DeformableGS, lacking explicit decoupling, exhibited artifacts and motion blur. HUGS, while a decoupled method, produced blurry object details due to over-smoothing of abrupt pose changes typical during grasping, failing to capture discrete physical events. ManiSplat's multi-stage pose handling, leveraging robot kinematics in motion phases and tracking in skill phases, contributed to its stability in both pose and appearance.
- **Quantitative Analysis (Table 1)**: ManiSplat achieved superior performance across full image, robot, and object regions in terms of PSNR and SSIM metrics.
 - 3DGS performed lowest due to its static scene modeling.
 - 4DGS and DeformableGS showed improved overall metrics but poor performance in disentangled robot and object regions, confirming their limitations in object-level decoupling.
 - HUGS, when provided with ground-truth robot joint angles for fair comparison, yielded robot reconstruction quality comparable to ManiSplat. However, its object reconstruction metrics were notably lower, which the authors attribute to its over-smoothing of complex contact interactions.
 - ManiSplat obtained the highest PSNR (32.31) and SSIM (0.951) for full images, and strong performance for robot (PSNR 26.07, SSIM 0.838) and object (PSNR 26.85, SSIM 0.872) regions, indicating accurate capture of complex interaction dynamics.

#### 4.2. Pose Estimation

- **Pose Estimation Accuracy (Table 2)**: On the synthetic RoboTwin dataset, ManiSplat achieved the lowest average translation error ($E_t$) of 0.5864 cm and rotation error ($E_R$) of 5.185 degrees. This represents a reduction in error compared to GenPose++ (0.9965 cm, 7.109 degrees) and HUGS (0.9936 cm, 7.004 degrees). This improvement is attributed to the framework's use of kinematic data for pose refinement, which helps mitigate estimation drift.
- **Trajectory Analysis (Figure 4)**: Visualizations of real-world trajectories indicated that ManiSplat produced more temporally stable object pose trajectories, exhibiting less high-frequency noise compared to the GenPose++ baseline. Crucially, the method demonstrated the ability to accurately capture abrupt pose transitions during critical interaction events (e.g., gripper engagement), which single-frame estimation methods often obscure with noise. This stability in motion phases and precision in skill phases supports accurate digital twin reconstruction.

#### 4.3. Data Augmentation

- **Qualitative Demonstration (Figure 5)**: The framework successfully generated photorealistic novel scenarios from a single source video. An example demonstrates augmenting a flower grasping task by altering the flower's position and orientation while keeping the vase stationary. This capability highlights the method's potential for generating diverse training data while preserving interaction fidelity, which is valuable for scaling datasets for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model training.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study (Table 3) evaluated the contribution of individual loss components:
- **$L_{pose}$ (Pose constraint)**: Provided the most significant performance gain, improving PSNR from 30.08 to 31.15. This confirms its role in ensuring geometric stability and reducing high-frequency jitter.
- **$L_{app}$ (Appearance alignment)**: Further refined rendering quality, increasing PSNR to 32.24, by enforcing photometric consistency across the sequence.
- **$L_{mask}$ (Opacity mask supervision)**: While yielding a marginal improvement in global metrics (PSNR to 32.31), it was considered essential for obtaining high-quality decoupled representations. It effectively eliminated floaters and ensured sharp, physically plausible boundaries between the robot manipulator and manipulated objects, which is critical for realistic re-composition in data augmentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ManiSplat presents a significant contribution to the fields of 3D computer vision and robotics by addressing several long-standing challenges in dynamic scene reconstruction and Real2Sim2Real transfer.

1. **Bridging the Gap in Dynamic 3DGS**: The research effectively extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to complex, interaction-driven robotic scenarios. It overcomes the limitations of previous dynamic 3DGS methods that either lacked explicit object-level disentanglement or were designed for passive, non-interactive dynamics. By explicitly modeling interaction-induced dynamics, ManiSplat facilitates a deeper understanding and control of robotic manipulation tasks within Gaussian representations.

2. **High-Fidelity, Controllable Digital Twins from Monocular Video**: ManiSplat provides a pipeline for creating high-fidelity, simulation-ready Gaussian digital twins directly from monocular ego-view robotic videos. This capability reduces the reliance on costly multi-state scans or pre-aligned assets, making the process of generating digital twins more accessible and efficient for real-world robotic deployments. These controllable digital twins can serve as foundational assets for various downstream robotic applications.

3. **Enhanced Support for Robotic Tasks and Policy Learning**: The disentangled representation allows for independent modeling and control of robots, objects, and the environment. This is crucial for robotic learning tasks such as imitation learning, reinforcement learning, and task planning, where explicit control over individual scene elements is required. The generated digital twins are designed to be physically consistent and simulation-ready, facilitating robust Real2Sim2Real transfer.

4. **Addressing Data Scarcity through Topology-Preserving Augmentation**: A key impact lies in the method's ability to synthesize diverse, physically feasible, and spatially varied trajectories from a single real-world demonstration. This "Skill-Preserving, Motion-Adaptive" data augmentation strategy is valuable for mitigating data sparsity, a common bottleneck in training data-hungry robotic policies. By generating large-scale synthetic data, ManiSplat can potentially accelerate the development and improve the generalization capabilities of vision-language-action (VLA) models and other embodied AI systems.

5. **Robust Pose Estimation for Dynamic Interactions**: The task-oriented spatio-temporal alignment, particularly the hybrid pose estimation mechanism, improves the accuracy and temporal stability of object pose trajectories during complex interactions. This is critical for reliable reconstruction and simulation, as it handles both continuous motions and abrupt, discrete contact events with greater precision than previous methods.

While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding rendering quality degradation with large spatial offsets in augmented scenes, the overall framework offers a scalable approach for transforming raw robotic video data into versatile digital assets. Future work on incorporating more geometrically constrained representations or fusing multiple reference demonstrations could further enhance its robustness and applicability.
