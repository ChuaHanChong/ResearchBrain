# SIEVE: Structure-Aware Data Selection for Imitation Learning with VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A structure-aware data selection method named SIEVE improves Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model training by curating compact, informative demonstration subsets based on reusable visuo-motor primitives and transition interfaces. This approach achieved an average success rate of 56.3% using 50% of the data and training steps, surpassing the 51.8% success rate of full dataset training on the Bridge-V2 benchmark.

## Problem

- Large-scale robotic demonstration datasets for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often contain redundancy, noise, and suboptimal examples, hindering efficient imitation learning.
- Current data selection methods for imitation learning suffer from a granularity mismatch, being either too global (trajectory-level) or too local (state-action-level) to effectively identify reusable behavioral structures.
- Training VLA models on unfiltered large datasets leads to inefficient learning, wasted computational resources, and policies that struggle with consistency or generalization.

## Method

- **SIEVE** segments trajectories using end-effector state flips, encodes segments with a pretrained video encoder (==V-JEPA2==), and clusters them via ==Mini-Batch K-Means== to discover reusable ==visuo-motor primitives==.
- It allocates the data selection budget across discovered ==composition patterns== and transitions by greedily maximizing a reuse-aware, ==logarithmic utility== objective to ensure broad structural exposure.
- Within each allocated pattern, SIEVE selects "==medoid==" trajectories, which are most representative and central, to provide stable and consistent supervision for ==behavior cloning==.

## Results

- SIEVE achieved a **56.3%** average success rate on the Bridge-V2 benchmark using only **50%** of the training data and steps, exceeding the **51.8%** success rate of training on the full dataset with twice the resources.
- The method consistently outperformed baseline selection strategies and full-data training across multiple robotic datasets (Bridge-V2, Fractal, GR00T-X-Sim) and different VLA model architectures.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that both primitive and transition exposure components of structural allocation, as well as the selection of learning-friendly medoid trajectories, were critical for SIEVE's performance benefits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective imitation learning benefits from data selection that identifies and prioritizes reusable visuo-motor primitives and their transition interfaces, rather than relying solely on data volume.
> - An intermediate granularity for data selection, beyond individual states or full trajectories, is essential for capturing coherent and compositional behavioral structures in robot demonstrations.
> - For behavior cloning, selecting demonstrations that are central and stable (medoids) within identified behavioral patterns yields more consistent and effective learning signals than selecting diverse or atypical examples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control, which typically rely on imitation learning (IL) from large-scale demonstration datasets. While the availability of vast datasets has enabled progress in VLA models, the paper identifies that simply increasing data volume does not necessarily lead to improved policy performance. This is attributed to common issues in large datasets, including redundancy, noise, suboptimal demonstrations, and uneven coverage of behaviors.

Current data selection methods for imitation learning typically operate at two granularities:
- **Trajectory-level methods** assess demonstrations based on global signals like representation similarity, reliability, or task feedback. These methods may oversimplify long-horizon behaviors by collapsing them into a single score and can require costly additional model training for feedback-based approaches.
- **State-action-level methods** use fine-grained signals such as mutual information or local similarity. These methods are often considered too localized to capture coherent, long-horizon task semantics effectively, or are primarily designed for local pruning rather than broader structural curation.

The paper argues for a need to bridge this "granularity mismatch" by developing a data selection approach that operates at a mid-level of abstraction, capturing reusable behavioral structures that are more complex than individual state-action pairs but more structured than a single trajectory score. This approach is positioned as a contribution to making VLA imitation learning more efficient and effective, particularly in the context of increasingly large and heterogeneous robotic datasets. The proposed method draws inspiration from the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, which suggests that useful data facilitates compression by exposing repeating patterns that a learner can internalize and reuse.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective data selection method, named SIEVE, to curate a compact and informative subset from large robot demonstration datasets for training VLA models through imitation learning. The motivation stems from the observed limitations of current VLA training paradigms:

1. **Inefficiency of Unfiltered Large Datasets:** VLA models, trained via imitation learning, often suffer from the quality issues present in large-scale datasets. These issues include a high degree of redundancy (near-duplicate behaviors), the presence of noisy or suboptimal human demonstrations, and an uneven distribution of task coverage. Training on such unfiltered data can lead to inefficient learning, wasted computational resources, and policies that struggle with consistency or generalization, as the model is repeatedly exposed to redundant or inconsistent supervision.

2. **Addressing Granularity Mismatch in Existing Selection Methods:** Current data selection techniques are identified as having a granularity mismatch. Trajectory-level methods provide a global view but may obscure crucial internal stages or behavioral compositions within long-horizon tasks. Conversely, state-action-level methods, while fine-grained, can be too myopic to capture the overarching semantics of complex tasks or coherent behavioral sequences. The authors argue that effective data selection requires an intermediate level of detail that can characterize reusable behavioral patterns.

3. **Leveraging Reusable Behavioral Structures:** Inspired by the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, the authors hypothesize that "useful demonstrations are those that expose reusable behavioral regularities." This suggests that a learner benefits from data where patterns can be compressed and reused, rather than redundantly encoded. For robotic imitation learning, such structure is naturally represented as compositions of atomic behavior primitives and their transition interfaces. The objective is to design a method that identifies and prioritizes demonstrations rich in these reusable primitive compositions and transitions.

4. **Ensuring Learning-Friendly Supervision:** Beyond identifying structural content, a key motivation is to select demonstrations that provide stable and predictable action supervision for behavior cloning (BC). Noisy or atypical realizations of the same behavior pattern can introduce inconsistencies, making it harder for the policy to learn robust state-action mappings. Therefore, the method aims to select central and stable instantiations of identified behavior patterns, minimizing ambiguity in the training signal.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Develop a **structure-aware** data selection framework that identifies and prioritizes demonstrations based on their reusable visuo-motor primitives and transition interfaces.
- Allocate data selection budgets to maximize exposure to diverse and reusable structural components under diminishing returns.
- Select **learning-friendly** concrete trajectories that provide consistent and stable supervision for behavior cloning within each identified structural pattern.
- Demonstrate that this approach can yield a compact, high-quality dataset that improves VLA model performance and efficiency, potentially outperforming training on the full, unfiltered dataset with reduced computational cost.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SIEVE is proposed as a three-stage framework for structure-aware data selection in VLA imitation learning: Primitive Discovery, Structural Exposure Allocation, and Learning-Friendly Trajectory Selection.

#### 3.1. Primitive Discovery

This stage aims to identify reusable visuo-motor behavior units, or "primitives," from the raw demonstration trajectories.

- **Trajectory Segmentation:** Each raw demonstration trajectory is segmented based on physically grounded interaction boundaries. Specifically, "end effector state flips" (e.g., gripper grasp/release events) are used as segmentation points. To ensure robust segmentation and suppress noise, a state transition is only accepted if it persists for five consecutive frames. This process decomposes each trajectory T into a sequence of segments {S_1..., S_L}.
- **Segment Representation:** For each segment S_j, a compact visual representation is extracted. This involves uniformly sampling 8 frames from the segment and encoding them using a pretrained video encoder, V-JEPA2. The representations of the start, middle, and end frames of the segment are concatenated to capture both semantic content and coarse temporal structure. This concatenated feature is then reduced to 256 dimensions using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to suppress noise, enhance clustering stability, and reduce computational overhead, yielding a segment representation z_j.
- **Primitive Discovery via Clustering:** The segment representations are clustered using Mini-Batch K-Means to discover a vocabulary of primitives. The optimal number of clusters, K, is not manually specified but determined automatically. A subset of trajectories is sampled, and for each candidate K, segments are clustered. Each trajectory is then summarized by the set of clusters (primitives) it covers. The optimal K is chosen by maximizing a reuse-aware criterion: $(1 - J) \log R$.
 - **Trajectory-level discriminability (J):** Measures how distinct trajectories remain when represented by their primitive coverage. It is computed as the median of the average pairwise Jaccard similarities of the cluster sets across all sampled trajectories. A lower J indicates better preservation of structural distinctions.
 - **Cross-trajectory reuse (R):** Quantifies how broadly each discovered cluster (primitive) recurs across different trajectories. It is defined as the median occurrence count of each cluster across all trajectories. A higher R implies more widely applicable behavior units.
 After K* is selected, Mini-Batch K-Means is rerun on all segment representations to finalize the primitive clusters.

#### 3.2. Structural Exposure Allocation

Following primitive discovery, each trajectory is represented as an ordered sequence of primitives, termed a "composition pattern." This stage allocates the data selection budget across these patterns to maximize exposure to reusable structures.

- **Composition Patterns and Transitions:** A trajectory T_i is represented as an ordered sequence P_i = [c_1..., c_|P_i|] of discovered primitives. "Transitions" are defined as the local compositional interfaces between adjacent primitives (e.g., (c_j -> c_{j+1})). For single-primitive trajectories, a terminal null state is introduced to define a terminal transition (c_1 -> ∅), ensuring every trajectory has at least one transition.
- **Structural Exposure Objective:** The dataset contains a set P of unique composition patterns, each corresponding to a "pattern bucket" of trajectories. The selection budget B (vector of b_ℓ, number of trajectories for pattern P^(ℓ)) is optimized by maximizing an objective function F(B). This objective sums logarithmic terms for the occurrences of all discovered primitives (c ∈ C) and transitions (e ∈ E) among the selected trajectories.
 - F(B) = Σ_{c∈C} w_c log(1 + n_c(B)) + Σ_{e∈E} w_e log(1 + n_e(B)), where n_c(B) and n_e(B) are the counts of primitive c and transition e.
 - **Weights (w_c, w_e):** These are defined by the reuse frequency of primitives and transitions across the composition-pattern space (q_c/|P| and q_e/|P|). Primitives and transitions appearing in more distinct composition patterns receive higher weights, reflecting their broader applicability.
 - **Logarithmic utility:** This introduces diminishing returns, encouraging the selection to expose a broader set of unique structures rather than repeatedly reinforcing already well-covered ones.
- **Greedy Optimization:** The budget allocation B is optimized greedily. Starting with no samples, in each step, one additional sample is allocated to the composition pattern P^(ℓ) that yields the largest marginal gain in F(B). This process continues until the total selection budget is exhausted.

#### 3.3. Learning-Friendly Trajectory Selection

After the budget is allocated to different composition patterns, this final stage selects the actual trajectories within each pattern bucket.

- **Motivation:** For behavior cloning, demonstrations with consistent state-action mappings are more beneficial. The paper posits that trajectories near the center of a composition pattern's representation space are less likely to be outliers or ambiguous, thus providing more stable supervision.
- **Trajectory Representation:** For each trajectory T_i within a pattern P^(ℓ), a trajectory representation x_i is constructed by concatenating the segment representations (z_j) along its primitive sequence.
- **Medoid Selection:** Within each composition-pattern bucket, trajectories are compared using cosine similarity between their x_i representations. The "medoid trajectory" is identified as the one with the highest aggregate similarity to all other trajectories in that bucket. The B[ℓ] trajectories with the smallest distances (1 - cosine similarity) to this medoid are then selected. This strategy aims to retain central, stable, and representative demonstrations that are conducive to effective imitation learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated SIEVE's performance across multiple datasets, VLA models, and benchmarks, comparing it against competitive baselines.

#### 4.1. Main Results on Bridge-V2 with Qwen3-VL-4B-GR00T

- **Overall Performance:** SIEVE consistently achieved the highest average success rate across all tested selection budgets (50% and 70% of data) and training schedules.
- **Efficiency Gains:** With a 50% data selection budget (26.5K demonstrations) and proportionally reduced training steps (25K steps), SIEVE yielded an average success rate of 56.3%. This surpassed the performance of Full-Training, which used the complete dataset (53K demonstrations) and twice the training steps (50K steps) and achieved an average success rate of 51.8%. This indicates SIEVE's ability to identify a more effective and compact training subset, leading to both performance improvements and substantial reductions in computational resources.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - Under the 50% selection budget, SIEVE (56.3% avg success with 25K steps; 59.4% avg success with 50K steps) consistently outperformed SCIZOR (52.2% and 55.5%), DemInf (43.2% and 46.6%), and Random (39.6% and 40.4%).
 - Under the 70% selection budget, SIEVE (62.3% avg success with 35K steps; 62.5% avg success with 50K steps) also maintained its lead over all baselines.
- **Balanced Task Performance:** While some baselines like SCIZOR showed strong performance on specific tasks (e.g., Put Eggplant In Basket), their performance could drop significantly on others. SIEVE demonstrated more balanced and competitive performance across all four manipulation tasks in SimplerEnv-WidowX.
- **Generalization under Distribution Shift:** The evaluation in SimplerEnv, which involves unseen kitchen backgrounds and randomized object configurations, demonstrated that SIEVE-selected subsets generalize effectively under distribution shift.

#### 4.2. Extended Results

- **Performance Across Different Datasets:** SIEVE consistently achieved the highest average success rates when applied to other datasets:
 - **Fractal (Google Robot):** SIEVE achieved 76.4%, outperforming Full-Training (75.0%) with only 50% of data and 50% of training steps.
 - **GR00T-X-Sim (Humanoid Robot):** SIEVE achieved 54.8%, which was the highest among all methods, including Full-Training (52.7%) and Random (53.5%). The paper notes that Random selection slightly outperforming Full-Training in this simulated-to-simulated setting might be due to more effective optimization per sample in a relatively in-domain environment.
 These results indicate SIEVE's applicability across different robot embodiments and evaluation benchmarks.
- **Performance Across Different Models:** SIEVE's effectiveness generalized to different VLA models.
 - With **Qwen3-VL-4B-GR00T**, SIEVE achieved 56.3% (avg), outperforming Random (39.6%) and Full-Training (51.8%).
 - With **Qwen3-VL-4B-OFT**, SIEVE achieved 56.5% (avg), significantly outperforming Random (26.3%) and Full-Training (38.8%).
 The gains were particularly pronounced on certain tasks, suggesting that SIEVE's benefits are not tied to a specific VLA model architecture.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study and Further Analysis

- **Ablation of Structural Exposure Allocation:**
 - Removing the **transition exposure term** led to a decrease in average success rate from 56.3% to 50.8%.
 - Removing the **primitive exposure term** resulted in a decrease to 51.6%.
 Both primitives and transitions contribute to effective data selection, with transition interfaces appearing to provide a larger contribution, suggesting their importance in modeling how behavioral units connect.
- **Ablation of Learning-Friendly Trajectory Selection:**
 - Replacing the medoid selection with **"Most-Dissim"** (selecting most diverse trajectories within a bucket) reduced the average success rate from 56.3% to 40.1%. This indicates that maximizing diversity within a pattern bucket by selecting atypical examples is detrimental for imitation learning.
 - Using **random within-bucket selection** performed better than Most-Dissim but still trailed SIEVE, achieving 53.7%.
 These results validate the choice of selecting central and stable demonstrations within each composition pattern for behavior cloning.
- **Composition Pattern Redistribution:** Analysis of the composition pattern distribution showed that SIEVE shifted the selected subset away from a few frequency-dominated patterns (many single-primitive) towards a more balanced distribution, with a higher proportion of multi-primitive sequences. This suggests that SIEVE effectively prioritizes structurally richer patterns, thereby exposing more diverse and reusable primitives and transitions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces SIEVE, a data selection method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) imitation learning that emphasizes the structural composition of demonstrations. This work demonstrates that an explicit focus on reusable primitives and transition interfaces, combined with the selection of learning-friendly trajectory realizations, can significantly enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of VLA model training.

The primary significance lies in its ability to **outperform training on full, unfiltered datasets while using substantially fewer resources**. Specifically, SIEVE achieved higher average success rates using only 50% of the demonstrations and 50% of the training steps compared to full-data training. This finding suggests that the quality and structural information content of a dataset can be more critical than its sheer size for VLA models.

Potential impacts include:

- **Increased Training Efficiency:** By reducing the required training data and computational steps, SIEVE can accelerate the development and iteration cycles for VLA models. This translates to lower energy consumption and faster experimentation, which are important considerations for large-scale machine learning and robotics.
- **Improved Policy Performance and Generalization:** The selected subsets lead to policies with higher success rates and demonstrate better generalization capabilities across different tasks, datasets, and VLA model architectures. This indicates that SIEVE helps models internalize more robust and reusable behavioral structures, leading to more capable robots.
- **Guidance for Data Collection and Curation:** The principles underlying SIEVE – identifying reusable primitives and transitions, and favoring stable demonstrations – can inform future strategies for robot data collection and curation. Instead of solely focusing on quantity, efforts could be directed towards generating or annotating data that explicitly provides diverse and consistent structural patterns.
- **Advancement in Imitation Learning:** The work contributes to the broader field of imitation learning by offering a novel perspective on data utility, moving beyond purely local or global metrics to a structure-aware, mid-level granularity. This paradigm may open new avenues for research into how robots can learn complex, long-horizon behaviors more effectively from human demonstrations.
- **Broader Applicability:** The consistent performance across different robot embodiments (WidowX, Google Robot, Humanoid) and VLA model architectures (Qwen3-VL-4B-GR00T, Qwen3-VL-4B-OFT) suggests that SIEVE's approach is robust and broadly applicable within the VLA domain.

Overall, SIEVE represents a practical advancement towards more intelligent and resource-efficient training of robotic policies, highlighting the importance of curated, structure-aware data in the era of large-scale VLA models.
