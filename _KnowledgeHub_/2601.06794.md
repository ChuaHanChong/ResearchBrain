# No More Stale Feedback: Co-Evolving Critics for Open-World Agent Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ECHO, a framework for training Large Language Model agents, introduces a co-evolutionary paradigm where the policy and critic are jointly optimized to address "critic staleness" in critique-guided reinforcement learning. This approach achieved an average gain of 7.28 points over standard GRPO across four open-world benchmarks, with relative improvements of up to 42% on DeepSearch, by adapting feedback to the agent's evolving failure patterns.

## Problem

- Existing critique-guided reinforcement learning for LLM agents relies on static or asynchronously updated critics, which become "stale" as the agent's policy evolves and its error patterns shift.
- Traditional RL with scalar rewards often provides sparse and non-diagnostic feedback, limiting the efficiency and effectiveness of LLM agent training.
- The assumption that optimal critique strategies are stationary hinders continuous and effective long-horizon improvement for LLM agents.

## Method

- The **ECHO** framework employs a synchronized co-evolutionary loop, jointly optimizing both the LLM agent's policy and its critic for adaptive learning.
- It utilizes a ==Cascaded Evolutionary Rollout== mechanism to generate multi-view diagnostic critiques and subsequent conditional refinements of agent trajectories.
- A ==Saturation-Aware Reward Design== is introduced for the critic, which non-linearly weights improvements to better incentivize "last-mile" performance gains, especially as agents approach optimal behavior.

## Results

- ECHO consistently outperformed standard GRPO and various proprietary models, achieving an average gain of **7.28 points** across four open-world benchmarks (WebShop, ALFWorld, SciWorld, DeepSearch).
- Specific improvements include a **42%** relative increase on DeepSearch and a **9%** increase on WebShop using the Qwen3-4B backbone model, demonstrating efficacy in multi-step diagnosis and repair tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that disabling co-evolution (freezing the critic) significantly degraded performance due to stale critiques, and saturation-aware reward design consistently improved performance, especially for near-ceiling solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Empirical evidence confirms that LLM agent "fail-patterns drift" significantly during on-policy learning, rendering static or frozen critics ineffective.
> - Critics must adapt their diagnostic focus in lockstep with the agent's evolving policy to provide continuously relevant and actionable feedback.
> - Saturation-aware reward shaping, which non-linearly values improvements, is essential for guiding agents to achieve subtle, high-score "last-mile" refinements that linear rewards often fail to capture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents capable of navigating complex tasks through environmental interactions is a significant area within reinforcement learning (RL). A fundamental challenge in this field is the sparsity and lack of actionability of traditional outcome-based reward signals, which provide little diagnostic information for effective policy refinement. This limitation often leads to data inefficiency in training LLM agents.

To address this, recent research has introduced linguistic critics, which augment sparse rewards with natural-language feedback, offering more granular guidance. Existing approaches to linguistic critics generally fall into two categories:
1. **Template-based critiques:** These methods use pre-defined or hand-crafted hints to guide agents. While computationally inexpensive, they often lack the adaptability to provide feedback tailored to the agent's specific actions or evolving state. Examples include HINT, LUFFY, GHPO, ADHint, StepHint, and Scaf-GRPO, which vary in the structure and granularity of their pre-defined guidance.
2. **Training-based critics:** These involve independently fine-tuned, separate critic models designed to generate more informative and diagnostic feedback. Early attempts focused on single-stage fine-tuning using curated critique datasets. More advanced methods like Refinement-oriented Critique Optimization (RCO) and multi-stage training pipelines such as CGI and CTRL have been developed to improve the utility and stability of these critics.

A common limitation across most training-based critic methods is their decoupled nature from the policy's learning process. Critics are typically trained off-policy and then either frozen or updated asynchronously. This approach assumes that the optimal critique strategy remains stationary. However, in on-policy RL, the agent's policy continuously evolves, causing a shift in its trajectory distribution and, consequently, its failure patterns. An early-stage policy might make coarse mistakes, benefiting from high-level hints, while a later-stage policy might be bottlenecked by subtle, hard-to-localize defects. A critic trained and frozen on an earlier distribution can become "stale," providing feedback that is redundant, miscalibrated, or even misleading for the current policy. This critic staleness is identified as a fundamental barrier to sustained improvement and sample efficiency in critique-guided RL.

This work, ECHO, addresses this specific limitation by proposing a framework where the critic is not a stationary supervisor but a "co-evolving module." It aims to synchronize the optimization of the policy and the critic, ensuring the critic's feedback remains pertinent to the policy's evolving error patterns. By tackling critic staleness directly, ECHO seeks to advance the effectiveness and efficiency of critique-guided reinforcement learning for open-world LLM agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation of "critic staleness" in existing critique-guided reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms for Large Language Model (LLM) agents. Current methods often treat the critic as a static or offline component, decoupled from the policy's dynamic learning process. In on-policy RL, as an agent's policy improves, its error patterns naturally shift—from basic, high-level errors in early stages to more subtle, complex flaws later on. A stationary critic, trained on an earlier distribution of failures, becomes increasingly misaligned with the evolving policy, providing feedback that loses utility, can be redundant, miscalibrated in granularity, or even misleading. This diminishing utility of feedback fundamentally limits sample efficiency and the capacity for sustained improvement in long-horizon tasks.

To address this, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **To empirically identify and demonstrate critic staleness:** The research aims to show that freezing the critic leads to a measurable decay in the utility of its critiques as the agent's policy improves over time. This validates the hypothesis that the critic's relevance is dependent on its synchronization with the policy's evolution.
2. **To propose a co-evolutionary framework for policy and critic optimization:** Introduce ECHO (Evolving Critic for Hindsight-Guided Optimization), a novel paradigm that fosters a symbiotic optimization loop where the policy and critic are jointly and synchronously optimized. This aims to continuously calibrate the critic's diagnostic precision to the policy's shifting failure patterns.
3. **To develop stable and sample-efficient mechanisms for co-evolution:**
 - **Cascaded Rollout Mechanism:** Design a diagnostic-and-corrective rollout process that generates "group-structured" trajectories. This mechanism enables relative advantage estimation, allowing both the policy and critic to learn efficiently from diverse samples.
 - **Saturation-Aware Gain Shaping Objective:** Address the challenge of learning plateaus, particularly when the policy's performance approaches a ceiling. This objective rewards the critic for inducing incremental improvements in high-performing trajectories, preventing the "equidistant fallacy" of linear reward metrics and encouraging the critic to diagnose subtle flaws.
 - **Dual-Track GRPO Updates:** Implement a synchronized Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) mechanism to update both the policy and critic in lockstep, ensuring their mutual alignment and robust co-evolution.

By pursuing these objectives, the researchers aim to overcome the limitations of stale feedback, enabling LLM agents to achieve more stable training and higher long-horizon task success in open-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ECHO (Evolving Critic for Hindsight-Guided Optimization) is designed as a co-evolutionary framework that fosters a symbiotic optimization loop between a Policy Pθ (the Actor) and a Critic Cψ (the Diagnostic Evolver). This framework transforms the refinement process into a self-improving system where evaluative precision and execution capability evolve in tandem.

#### 3.1. Cascaded Evolutionary Rollout

To facilitate the joint optimization, ECHO employs a cascaded rollout mechanism that generates group-structured trajectories through a two-stage diagnostic-and-corrective cycle.

- **Stage 1: Multi-view Diagnosis:**
 - Given an initial query `q`, the policy `Pθ` first generates an initial trajectory `τo`.
 - An external reward model `R` evaluates `τo` to obtain a baseline score `so = R(q, τo)`.
 - Conditioned on `q`, `τo`, and `so`, the critic `Cψ` is invoked `N` times independently to produce a set of diverse diagnostic feedbacks `GC = {c(j)o}N_j=1`. The inclusion of `so` allows the critic to provide "score-aware" explanations, identifying specific gaps preventing higher rewards.

- **Stage 2: Conditional Refinement:**
 - Following diagnosis, the policy `Pθ` refines its actions. For each critique `c(j)o`, an augmented input `˜q(j) = (q, c(j)o)` is formed.
 - The policy then samples a corresponding set of refined trajectories `τ(j)r ∼ Pθ(· | ˜q(j))`.
 - Each refined trajectory is evaluated by the reward model `R` to yield post-correction scores `s(j)r = R(q, τ(j)r)`.
 - This process yields the baseline score `so`, the critique group `GC`, and the refinement group `GP = {τ(j)r}N_j=1`, which serve as empirical signals for co-evolutionary optimization.

#### 3.2. Saturation-Aware Reward Design

To address the "equidistant fallacy" of linear reward metrics (e.g., `∆s = sr − so`), which fail to account for the increasing difficulty of achieving improvements as performance approaches a ceiling, ECHO introduces a saturation-aware reward design.

- The reward space is hypothesized to be non-linear, governed by a difficulty weighting function `ω(s) = 1 / (1 − s + η)`, where `η > 0` is a smoothing hyperparameter. This function captures the surging marginal effort required for increments near perfection.
- The intrinsic gain `g(so, sr)` of a refinement is defined as the path integral of `ω(s)` from `so` to `sr`: `g(so, sr) = ∫(from so to sr) ω(s)ds = ln((1 − so + η) / (1 − sr + η))`.
- This gain function possesses three properties: it is saturation-aware (larger gain for same `∆s` in higher-score regions), additive (path-consistent), and antisymmetric (`g(so, sr) = −g(sr, so)`).
- This intrinsic gain `rc = g(so, sr)` is directly used as the critic reward.

#### 3.3. Synchronized Co-evolutionary Optimization

The co-evolution is operationalized as a synchronized dual-track alignment problem using Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

- **Dual-Track Advantage Estimation:** To maximize sample efficiency, group-relative advantages are computed for both models:
 - For policy `Pθ`, `A(j)P` is computed by normalizing scores `s(j)r` within `GP`, allowing the policy to identify effective refinement paths from diverse diagnostic samples.
 - For critic `Cψ`, `A(j)C` is derived by group-relative normalization of the saturation-aware rewards `r(j)c`, enabling the critic to converge on effective feedback by amplifying high-score gains.

- **Synchronized Update:** Both `Pθ` and `Cψ` are updated by maximizing a shared GRPO surrogate objective `J(ϕ)`, which incorporates advantage-weighted likelihood and a KL divergence constraint:
 `J(ϕ) = E[ (1/N) Σ(from i=1 to N) (1/|oi|) Σ(from t=1 to |oi|) min(ρi,t(ϕ)Ai, clip(ρi,t(ϕ), 1−ϵ, 1+ϵ)Ai) ] − βDKL(Mϕ || Mref)`.
 Here, `ϕ ∈ {θ, ψ}` represents the parameters, `oi` is the generated sequence, `ρi,t(ϕ)` is the importance sampling ratio, and `Ai ∈ {AP, AC}` is the respective group-relative advantage. This ensures the critic's diagnostic focus continually calibrates to the policy's evolving failure patterns.

**Implementation Details (from Appendix B):**
- Experiments utilized Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen2.5-7B as backbone models.
- The critic `Cψ` typically uses the same backbone as the policy `Pθ`.
- Learning rates were set to `lrθ = lrψ = 1 × 10−6`.
- The rollout group size `N` was 8.
- The smoothing hyperparameter `η` for the saturation-aware reward was set to 0.1.
- A specific prompt template was used for the critic model to ensure grounded, actionable suggestions in a fixed format (Box A.1).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates ECHO across four open-world environments: WebShop, ALFWorld, SciWorld, and DeepSearch. The findings address three main research questions.

#### 4.1. RQ1: How effective is ECHO for open-world agent learning?

ECHO consistently demonstrated improved performance compared to standard GRPO and competitive baselines.
- **Performance Gains:** As shown in Table 1, ECHO surpassed GRPO under the same training budget, indicating that synchronized, on-policy critiques enhance data efficiency by reducing unproductive exploration. Notable improvements include a 42% relative increase on DeepSearch (from 33.25 to 47.25) and a 9% relative increase on WebShop (from 82.37 to 90.03) for the Qwen3-4B model. For more complex environments, ALFWorld and SciWorld, ECHO also yielded consistent gains.
- **Competitive Results:** ECHO achieved competitive results against stronger proprietary models (GPT series, Gemini-2.5-pro, Claude-Sonnet-4.5) and large open-source models (Qwen3-235B-A22B, DeepSeek-R1-0528), matching or surpassing most of them on multiple benchmarks, except for GPT-5 on DeepSearch.
- **Generalizability Across Backbone Sizes:** When evaluated on Qwen2.5-7B, ECHO similarly improved over GRPO, demonstrating its applicability across different model capacities and consistent benefits regardless of the backbone size.
- **Comparison with Other Critique-Guided Baselines (Appendix C.1):** ECHO consistently outperformed RCO and LUFFY across all tasks (Table 4). This suggests that ECHO's improvements stem from adaptive diagnostic feedback provided by the co-evolving critic, rather than reliance on strong external guidance or teacher hints.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Does fail-pattern drift happen during on-policy learning?

This section investigates the dynamic nature of agent failures and the implications for static critics.

- **How Failures Change Over Training:** Analysis of failed trajectories from early, intermediate, and late training phases using t-SNE visualization (Figure 3) revealed clear distributional drift of failure patterns across all four environments.
 - In WebShop and DeepSearch, failure modes formed compact clusters that substantially shifted over time, indicating a change in dominant error causes rather than merely a reduction in existing mistakes.
 - In more complex environments like ALFWorld and SciWorld, failure distributions were more dispersed and partially overlapped, suggesting higher failure-mode diversity and some persistent errors, yet the density mass still migrated across phases, confirming non-stationary dominant failure patterns.

- **Limitations of Frozen Critics under Failure-Pattern Drift:** Experiments where the critic was frozen while other ECHO components remained active (Table 2, Figure 5) demonstrated performance degradation.
 - **Final Performance Drop:** A frozen critic consistently led to lower final performance across all environments, highlighting the importance of synchronized critiques. The degradation was more severe in ALFWorld and SciWorld, occasionally underperforming standard GRPO, suggesting that stale, miscalibrated critiques can become noise that hinders policy refinement.
 - **Training Dynamics:** Training curves (Figure 5) showed phase-dependent effects. On WebShop, a frozen critic initially performed well but its improvement plateaued, being overtaken by ECHO, which aligns with the idea that later-stage fine-grained errors are poorly addressed by stale critiques. In ALFWorld and SciWorld, ECHO's advantage became visible in the mid-to-late stages, indicating a calibration period for the critic. On DeepSearch, ECHO showed steeper early-stage improvement, hypothesized to be due to rapid correction of systematic early failures. These dynamics support the claim that critique strategies are non-stationary, and co-evolution helps maintain utility.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Why is the saturation-aware (SA) reward design effective?

This section examines the impact of the saturation-aware gain shaping.

- **Impact on Performance:** Disabling SA shaping (Table 2) resulted in consistent performance drops on WebShop and SciWorld. The degradation was more pronounced on WebShop, which tends to reach closer to saturation, where last-mile improvements are critical.
- **Refinement Effectiveness Visualization:** Density scatter maps of pre-refinement (`so`) and post-refinement (`sr`) rewards (Figure 4) showed that SA shaping concentrated substantially more probability mass in the `sr > so` (improvement) region for both WebShop and SciWorld, indicating more reliable reward-increasing refinements.
- **Last-Mile Improvement:** In the high-score regime (near-ceiling), SA shaping exhibited higher density in the upper-left area (trajectories starting near full reward and improving), indicating a better ability to convert near-correct trajectories into full-reward solutions. In contrast, linear rewards often kept samples close to the diagonal, struggling with small but critical gains near the ceiling.

**Additional Findings from Appendix C:**
- **Effectiveness of Critique-Guided Refinement (C.2):** Comparing critic-guided refinement with no-critic regeneration (Table 5) showed that critiques provide actionable guidance beyond simple re-sampling. Relative gains increased in later training phases, indicating growing effectiveness of the co-evolving critic.
- **Critique–Refinement Alignment and Granularity Evolution (C.3):**
 - **Alignment:** An external evaluator (Gemini-2.5-pro) assessed whether refinements resolved issues identified in critiques. The "issue-addressed rate" (Table 6) improved significantly from ~75% in early phases to over 90% in intermediate and late phases, demonstrating that the policy increasingly learns to act on diagnostic feedback.
 - **Granularity:** Critiques evolved from coarse-level guidance in early stages to predominantly mid-level and fine-grained guidance in later stages (Table 6). Fine-grained critiques became substantially more prevalent in the late phase, reflecting the critic's adaptation to focus on precise error localization as the policy improved. This shift in granularity is considered a driver for sustained performance gains.
- **Training Time Analysis (C.4):** The computational overhead of ECHO was modest (Table 7). The additional stages (initial trajectory generation, critic evaluation, updates) introduced marginal overhead. The primary increase in training time came from the refinement stage, which involves processing longer contexts. Overall, ECHO incurred an average increase of approximately 15% over GRPO, which was considered a favorable trade-off given the substantial performance gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces ECHO (Evolving Critic for Hindsight-Guided Optimization), a co-evolutionary framework that addresses the problem of "critic staleness" in critique-guided reinforcement learning for LLM agents. By identifying that a static critic's feedback becomes misaligned and loses utility as an agent's policy evolves and its failure patterns shift, ECHO proposes a synchronized optimization approach where both the policy and the critic learn in tandem.

The core significance of this work lies in:
- **Mitigating Critic Staleness:** By allowing the critic to co-evolve with the policy, ECHO ensures that diagnostic feedback remains relevant and actionable throughout the training process. This directly addresses a previously unmitigated challenge that limited the sustained effectiveness and sample efficiency of critique-guided RL.
- **Enhancing Training Stability and Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate that ECHO leads to more stable training dynamics and consistently higher long-horizon task success across diverse open-world environments (WebShop, ALFWorld, SciWorld, DeepSearch). The framework's ability to maintain high utility of critiques, even when agents are tackling subtle, last-mile errors, is crucial for pushing performance ceilings.
- **Improved Refinement Quality:** The cascaded evolutionary rollout mechanism, combined with saturation-aware gain shaping, enables the critic to provide more precise and impactful guidance. The saturation-aware reward, in particular, incentivizes the critic to diagnose and facilitate improvements in high-performing trajectories, which is critical for achieving near-optimal solutions.
- **Generalizability Across Model Scales:** The demonstrated effectiveness of ECHO across different backbone model sizes (Qwen3-4B and Qwen2.5-7B) suggests its broad applicability within the LLM agent development landscape.

The potential impact of ECHO extends to various applications requiring robust and adaptive LLM agents, such as complex web navigation, embodied AI tasks, scientific discovery, and advanced information retrieval. By providing a mechanism for continuous alignment between diagnostic feedback and agent capabilities, this framework could accelerate the development of more intelligent, efficient, and resilient AI agents in open-world settings.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge two primary limitations. First, ECHO's effectiveness is contingent on the quality and calibration of the external reward model used for evaluation. Noisy, biased, or underspecified rewards could lead the critic to optimize towards evaluator artifacts rather than genuinely diagnostic feedback, potentially causing misalignment. Second, the current implementation uses separate models for reward evaluation and critique generation. A natural next step, as suggested by the authors, is to unify these functions into a single model. This integration could simplify the training pipeline and enhance consistency between "what is rewarded" and "what is suggested," potentially leading to further improvements in agent learning.
