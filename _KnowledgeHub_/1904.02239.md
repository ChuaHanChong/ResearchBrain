# Hyperbolic Image Embeddings

> [!summary] Summary
> This work explores applying hyperbolic geometry to computer vision, demonstrating that image embeddings in negatively curved spaces can enhance performance in tasks like few-shot classification and person re-identification. It reveals that visual data exhibits intrinsic hyperbolicity, which these embeddings effectively capture.

## Problem

- Traditional Euclidean or spherical embedding spaces may not optimally represent the hierarchical structures present in many visual datasets.
- Conventional geometric frameworks often struggle to embed tree-like or hierarchical relationships with low distortion.
- A lack of quantitative methods existed to assess the intrinsic 'hyperbolicity' of visual datasets, hindering the informed application of hyperbolic embeddings.

## Method

- The authors adapted the ==Poincaré ball model== and its operations (e.g., ==Möbius addition==, ==geodesic distance==) for use within neural networks.
- A hybrid approach integrates standard Euclidean CNNs for feature extraction with hyperbolic layers for the final embedding and classification stages.
- ==Gromov δ-hyperbolicity== was introduced as a metric to quantitatively assess dataset hyperbolicity and guide the selection of the optimal curvature parameter for hyperbolic embeddings.

## Results

- Hyperbolic Prototypical Networks achieved absolute accuracy improvements in few-shot classification, notably over **10%** on CUB and approximately **4-5%** on MiniImageNet compared to Euclidean baselines.
- In person re-identification tasks, hyperbolic models generally outperformed their Euclidean counterparts, especially for lower embedding dimensionalities (e.g., Rank1/mAP of **75.9/51.9** vs. **68.0/43.4** on Market-1501 with 32-dim embeddings).
- Uncertainty quantification was enhanced, as the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance for embedding distances to the origin (e.g., **0.868**) surpassed that of maximum predicted probabilities (e.g., **0.834**) in distinguishing MNIST from Omniglot data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual datasets, when processed by deep feature extractors, possess a measurable intrinsic hyperbolicity (e.g., MiniImageNet with a δrel of 0.17), indicating their suitability for negatively curved representations.
> - The distance of an embedding from the origin in hyperbolic space effectively indicates model uncertainty and can distinguish in-domain from out-of-domain data more clearly than maximum predicted probabilities.
> - Hyperbolic spaces, with their exponential volume growth, are well-suited to capture hierarchical and ambiguous-to-specific relationships inherent in visual data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern computer vision tasks such as image classification, retrieval, and few-shot learning primarily utilize Euclidean or spherical embedding spaces. In these conventional approaches, class boundaries are typically defined by linear hyperplanes, and similarity is measured using Euclidean distances or spherical geodesic distances (cosine similarity). These geometries possess zero (Euclidean) or positive (spherical) curvature, which dictates the structural properties of the learned embeddings.

A recent development in natural language processing (NLP) has demonstrated the advantages of hyperbolic embeddings for representing hierarchical data structures, such as taxonomies or word relationships. Hyperbolic spaces, characterized by negative curvature and exponential volume expansion, are inherently suited for embedding tree-like or hierarchical data with low distortion. This success in NLP tasks, including neural machine translation, inspired the current work.

This paper extends the application of hyperbolic geometry from NLP to computer vision. It investigates whether visual data also exhibits hierarchical properties that could be better captured by hyperbolic embeddings, thereby addressing limitations inherent in Euclidean and spherical embedding spaces for certain computer vision tasks. The work introduces methods to integrate hyperbolic neural network layers, previously proposed for NLP, into existing computer vision architectures, thus proposing a novel geometric framework for learning image representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to demonstrate that hyperbolic embeddings can offer a superior alternative to conventional Euclidean and spherical embeddings in various practical computer vision scenarios. The authors aim to integrate recently developed hyperbolic network layers into standard computer vision architectures and evaluate their performance across several tasks.

The motivation for exploring hyperbolic image embeddings stems from the hypothesis that many visual datasets possess an intrinsic hierarchical structure that is poorly represented by Euclidean or spherical geometries. The authors articulate several specific instances where such hierarchies manifest in computer vision:

- **Image Retrieval:** A high-level overview photograph may be hierarchically related to numerous close-up images of distinct details within that scene. Similarly, in general classification tasks, an image containing multiple class representatives can be seen as superordinate to images featuring isolated instances of those classes. Representing such composite-to-detail relationships necessitates a space capable of embedding hierarchies.
- **Image Quality and Ambiguity:** In tasks like face recognition, degraded images (e.g., blurry or low-resolution faces) are inherently more ambiguous and could correspond to multiple distinct high-resolution identities. These generic or ambiguous images form a hierarchy where they relate to a broader set of specific, clear images. Hyperbolic spaces, with their exponential volume growth towards the boundary, offer a natural mapping where generic/ambiguous items can reside closer to the origin, and specific items closer to the boundary, allowing the distance to the origin to serve as a measure of uncertainty.
- **Conceptual Hierarchies:** Many natural hierarchies prevalent in NLP, such as animal taxonomies (e.g., felines vs. pinnipeds), also extend to the visual domain, where different species or visual concepts can be grouped hierarchically based on shared and distinct visual features.

The authors postulate that the exponential expansion of hyperbolic spaces, in contrast to the polynomial expansion of Euclidean spaces, makes them continuous analogues of trees. This property is considered more suitable for capturing the underlying hierarchical structure present in visual data, thereby leading to more accurate and meaningful embeddings. A secondary objective is to propose a method for quantitatively evaluating the hyperbolicity of a dataset using Gromov δ-hyperbolicity, which can also guide the selection of appropriate curvature parameters for hyperbolic embeddings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper involves three main components: understanding hyperbolic geometry, defining operations within this geometry for neural networks, and developing a quantitative measure for dataset hyperbolicity.

**Hyperbolic Spaces and Operations:**
The authors adopt the Poincaré ball model, $D_n = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x\| < 1\}$, for $n$-dimensional hyperbolic space. This model is endowed with a Riemannian metric that introduces a constant negative sectional curvature. A crucial aspect is the introduction of a hyperparameter $c \ge 0$, which modifies the curvature of the Poincaré ball (denoted $D_n^c$). As $c \to 0$, hyperbolic formulas converge to their Euclidean counterparts, allowing for a balance between hyperbolic and Euclidean geometries. Key operations adapted for this space include:

- **Möbius Addition ($\oplus_c$):** A generalization of vector addition for points within the Poincaré ball, crucial for defining linear-like operations.
- **Geodesic Distance ($d_c$):** The shortest path distance between two points in the hyperbolic space.
- **Exponential and Logarithmic Maps ($\text{exp}_c, \text{log}_c$):** These bijective maps facilitate conversion between Euclidean tangent spaces (effectively $\mathbb{R}^n$) and the hyperbolic Poincaré ball. Specifically, $\text{exp}_c(0)$ and $\text{log}_c(0)$ are used for mapping Euclidean feature vectors into the hyperbolic space and vice versa, centered around the origin.
- **Hyperbolic Averaging (HypAve):** Generalizes the Euclidean mean operation. To simplify computation, this is performed in the Klein model of hyperbolic space, where it takes a straightforward weighted average form using Lorentz factors, with results then converted back to the Poincaré model.

**Integration into Neural Networks:**
The models proposed are "hybrid" in nature. Standard convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which intrinsically perform Euclidean operations, are used as feature extractors (backbones). The output of the last hidden layer of these Euclidean CNNs is then mapped into the Poincaré ball using the exponential map. Subsequent operations, such as classification or similarity calculations, are performed in the hyperbolic space using adapted layers:

- **Hyperbolic Linear Layer:** Generalizes Euclidean linear layers by applying a M¨obius matrix-vector product and Möbius addition for bias.
- **Hyperbolic Concatenation:** A generalized version of vector concatenation, necessary when combining hyperbolic vectors.
- **Hyperbolic Multiclass Logistic Regression (MLR):** This layer extends multinomial logistic regression to hyperbolic spaces by defining logits as distances to hyperbolic hyperplanes.

Numerical stability is maintained through techniques such as norm clipping after applying the exponential map, ensuring that embeddings remain within the valid region of the Poincaré ball.

**Hyperbolicity Estimation (Gromov δ-Hyperbolicity):**
To quantitatively assess the intrinsic hyperbolicity of a dataset and aid in selecting the curvature parameter $c$, the authors propose using Gromov δ-hyperbolicity. This metric describes how "tree-like" a metric space is; a δ-hyperbolicity of 0 corresponds to a tree. The calculation involves computing pairwise Gromov products for points in the dataset and then finding the largest coefficient in a specific matrix product. To make this measure scale-invariant, a relative δ-hyperbolicity, $\delta_{rel}(X) = \frac{2\delta(X)}{\text{diam}(X)}$, is used. This allows for a comparison between different datasets and the theoretical hyperbolicity of the Poincaré ball. An empirical formula is then derived to estimate the optimal curvature parameter $c(X)$ for a given dataset based on its $\delta_{rel}$ value. For image datasets, Euclidean distances between features extracted by pre-trained CNNs (VGG19, ResNet34, Inception v3) serve as the underlying metric for this analysis.

**Experimental Tasks:**
The methodology is evaluated across several computer vision tasks:
1. **Uncertainty Measurement:** A hyperbolic classifier is trained on MNIST, and its performance and embedding distribution are tested on Omniglot. The distance to the origin in the Poincaré ball is investigated as a measure of model confidence/uncertainty.
2. **Few-shot Classification:** The prototypical networks (ProtoNets) approach is adapted to hyperbolic geometry by replacing Euclidean mean operations with HypAve and using hyperbolic distance for similarity. This is evaluated on MiniImageNet and Caltech-UCSD Birds-200-2011 (CUB) datasets with different backbone CNNs (4-Conv and ResNet18).
3. **Person Re-identification:** A classification baseline with a ResNet50 backbone is compared against its hyperbolic counterpart, which uses hyperbolic MLR, on Market-1501 and DukeMTMC-reID datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings across various experiments, supporting the hypothesis that hyperbolic embeddings can be beneficial for visual data.

**Dataset Hyperbolicity Assessment:**
The calculation of relative δ-hyperbolicity ($\delta_{rel}$) for various image datasets (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, CUB, MiniImageNet) revealed values significantly closer to 0 than to 1. For example, using VGG19 features, MiniImageNet yielded a $\delta_{rel}$ of 0.17, while CIFAR10 was 0.23. These values suggest that visual datasets, when represented by deep features, exhibit a degree of intrinsic hyperbolicity. This observation provides quantitative support for the application of hyperbolic representations. Based on these $\delta_{rel}$ values, the authors proposed an empirical formula to estimate the appropriate curvature $c$ for the Poincaré disk, with $c \sim 0.33$ being a general estimate, although specific adjustments were sometimes found to yield better empirical results.

**Distance to Origin as Uncertainty Measure:**
In an experiment involving training a hyperbolic convolutional neural network on MNIST and evaluating it on the Omniglot dataset, the distance of an image's embedding to the origin of the Poincaré ball was investigated as a measure of model uncertainty. The results showed that ambiguous or out-of-domain images (Omniglot) were embedded closer to the center of the Poincaré ball, while clear, familiar images (MNIST) were pushed towards the boundary. Quantitatively, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between the distributions of distances to the origin for MNIST and Omniglot was consistently higher (e.g., 0.868 for n=2) than the distance between distributions of maximum predicted probabilities ($p_{max}$) from an equivalent Euclidean classifier (e.g., 0.834 for n=2). This indicates that the hyperbolic distance to the origin is a more effective indicator of dataset dissimilarity and model uncertainty.

**Few-shot Classification Performance:**
The "Hyperbolic ProtoNet" (an adaptation of Prototypical Networks using hyperbolic averaging and distance) demonstrated substantial performance improvements over its Euclidean counterpart, especially in low-data regimes.

- **MiniImageNet:**
 - Using a 4-Conv backbone, the Hyperbolic ProtoNet achieved 54.43% accuracy for 1-shot 5-way classification and 72.67% for 5-shot 5-way. This represents an absolute improvement of approximately 4.9% (1-shot) and 4.4% (5-shot) compared to the standard ProtoNet (49.42% and 68.20% respectively). The hyperbolic model also outperformed many other advanced methods reported in the literature, sometimes even those with larger architectural capacities.
 - With a ResNet18 backbone, Hyperbolic ProtoNet achieved 59.47% (1-shot) and 76.84% (5-shot), improving over the ProtoNet+ baseline (56.50% and 74.20%).
- **Caltech-UCSD Birds (CUB):** For the 4-Conv backbone, the Hyperbolic ProtoNet recorded 64.02% accuracy for 1-shot 5-way and 82.53% for 5-shot 5-way. This constitutes an absolute improvement of over 10% in both settings compared to the standard ProtoNet (51.31% and 70.77%). The hyperbolic approach also surpassed several other algorithms on this dataset.

**Person Re-identification Performance:**
For person re-identification tasks on Market-1501 and DukeMTMC-reID datasets, the hyperbolic version of a classification baseline (using hyperbolic multiclass logistic regression) generally showed better performance than its Euclidean counterpart. The advantage was more pronounced for smaller embedding dimensionalities. For example, with 32-dimensional embeddings and learning rate schedule #2, the hyperbolic model achieved Rank1/mAP of 75.9/51.9 on Market-1501, compared to the Euclidean baseline's 68.0/43.4. Similarly, on DukeMTMC-reID, the hyperbolic model achieved 62.2/39.1 versus 57.2/35.7 for the Euclidean baseline. While the improvements were less dramatic for higher dimensionalities (e.g., 128-dim), the hyperbolic models still demonstrated competitive or superior results in most configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work marks a significant step in extending the application of hyperbolic geometry to the field of computer vision. Prior to this research, hyperbolic embeddings primarily found success in natural language processing for representing hierarchical data. This paper demonstrates that a similar benefit can be realized for visual data, suggesting that images or their abstract features often possess an underlying hierarchical structure that is better captured by negatively curved spaces than by traditional Euclidean or spherical geometries.

The direct application of hyperbolic neural networks to computer vision tasks, particularly in few-shot learning and person re-identification, provides empirical evidence of performance gains. The consistent improvements observed in accuracy for few-shot classification (e.g., over 10% absolute gain on CUB and notable gains on MiniImageNet) indicate that hyperbolic embeddings can lead to more discriminative and geometrically aligned representations for visual concepts, especially when training data is scarce. This is impactful for applications requiring rapid adaptation to new classes with limited examples. For person re-identification, the generally superior performance, particularly at lower embedding dimensions, suggests that hyperbolic spaces could enable more compact and efficient representations while maintaining or improving matching accuracy.

A notable contribution is the proposal of using Gromov δ-hyperbolicity as a quantitative measure to assess the "hyperbolicity" of a dataset. This diagnostic tool provides practitioners with a method to determine the suitability of hyperbolic embeddings for a specific dataset and to estimate an appropriate curvature parameter, moving beyond purely empirical parameter tuning. This can streamline the adoption of hyperbolic models and foster a deeper understanding of the intrinsic geometry of various datasets.

The "hybrid" approach, where existing Euclidean CNN backbones are leveraged for feature extraction and only the final embedding and classification layers are adapted to hyperbolic geometry, ensures compatibility with current deep learning paradigms. This facilitates the integration of hyperbolic embeddings into existing systems without requiring a complete overhaul of neural network architectures, thereby lowering the barrier to adoption.

Potential future impacts include:
- **Improved Few-shot Learning:** The significant gains in few-shot classification could lead to more robust models for domains where data annotation is costly or new classes emerge frequently.
- **Efficient Representations:** For tasks like re-identification, the ability to achieve good performance with smaller embedding dimensions could translate to reduced memory footprint and faster comparisons in large-scale retrieval systems.
- **Novel Geometric Insights:** This work opens new avenues for exploring the geometric properties of learned image representations, potentially inspiring the development of novel architectures or loss functions that explicitly account for hierarchical structures in visual data.
- **Uncertainty Quantification:** The finding that distance to the origin in hyperbolic space correlates with model uncertainty offers a novel method for out-of-distribution detection or confidence estimation in computer vision.

However, the authors also acknowledge areas for future work, such as gaining a better theoretical understanding of *when* and *why* hyperbolic geometry provides benefits and addressing numerical stability issues that can arise from fixed-precision arithmetic in different hyperbolic models.
