# SPADE: Sketch-guided Path Planning Augmented with Diffusion Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> SPADE introduces a framework for sketch-guided path planning for Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) that augments behavioral cloning with image-conditioned diffusion models, improving generalization to unseen environments and enhancing path quality while maintaining real-time inference for on-robot deployment. The approach yielded a 39.1% lower Absolute Pose Error and 33.5% lower Fréchet Inception Distance compared to baseline methods, utilizing significantly fewer parameters.

## Problem

- Traditional path planning algorithms struggle to incorporate nuanced human operational preferences, requiring complex reward engineering or hardware solutions.
- Imitation Learning methods often face challenges with high-quality data acquisition in specialized environments and limited generalization to novel environments.
- Prior sketch-guided path planning systems (like SKIPP) suffered from poor generalization to new maps and reliance on deprecated, non-open-source data annotation tools.

## Method

- An overhauled, open-source data annotation tool built on ROS 2 facilitates robust data collection for diverse expert demonstrations.
- A novel training strategy called ==Diffusion== Expert Augmented Training integrates high-capacity diffusion models to guide a compact behavioral cloning (BC) network.
- The approach extends Diffusion-Augmented Behavioral Cloning (DBC) with image-conditioned diffusion models (Cond-DBC) that specifically model the conditional probability of paths given environmental context.

## Results

- The Cond-DBC approach achieved a **39.1%** lower Absolute Pose Error (APE), **33.5%** lower Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), and **60%** lower Hd19 (artifact count) compared to a large BC baseline model, while using **93.8%** fewer trainable parameters.
- During cross-behavior transfer (L-shape to U-shape via fine-tuning), the complete SPADE framework improved FID by **37.1%**, APE by **23.1%**, and reduced artifact count by **62%** compared to fine-tuning a standard SKIPP BC model.
- For medium and large model sizes, both DBC and Cond-DBC achieved near-zero artifact rates (0-10 instances exceeding 19 Hausdorff distance), indicating highly regular and artifact-free path generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating diffusion models, particularly image-conditioned ones, into behavioral cloning consistently enhances the generalization capabilities and overall quality of generated paths for AMRs.
> - The Cond-DBC approach provides more targeted and interpretable guidance signals during training, effectively reducing path artifacts and improving alignment with expert demonstrations.
> - It is possible to transfer the representational power of large diffusion models to lightweight behavioral cloning networks, enabling real-time, on-edge deployment on resource-constrained robotic systems without their inherent high inference latency.
