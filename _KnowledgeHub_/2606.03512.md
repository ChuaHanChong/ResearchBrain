# SPADE: Sketch-guided Path Planning Augmented with Diffusion Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> SPADE introduces a framework for sketch-guided path planning for Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) that augments behavioral cloning with image-conditioned diffusion models, improving generalization to unseen environments and enhancing path quality while maintaining real-time inference for on-robot deployment. The approach yielded a 39.1% lower Absolute Pose Error and 33.5% lower Fréchet Inception Distance compared to baseline methods, utilizing significantly fewer parameters.

## Problem

- Traditional path planning algorithms struggle to incorporate nuanced human operational preferences, requiring complex reward engineering or hardware solutions.
- Imitation Learning methods often face challenges with high-quality data acquisition in specialized environments and limited generalization to novel environments.
- Prior sketch-guided path planning systems (like SKIPP) suffered from poor generalization to new maps and reliance on deprecated, non-open-source data annotation tools.

## Method

- An overhauled, open-source data annotation tool built on ROS 2 facilitates robust data collection for diverse expert demonstrations.
- A novel training strategy called ==Diffusion== Expert Augmented Training integrates high-capacity diffusion models to guide a compact behavioral cloning (BC) network.
- The approach extends Diffusion-Augmented Behavioral Cloning (DBC) with image-conditioned diffusion models (Cond-DBC) that specifically model the conditional probability of paths given environmental context.

## Results

- The Cond-DBC approach achieved a **39.1%** lower Absolute Pose Error (APE), **33.5%** lower Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), and **60%** lower Hd19 (artifact count) compared to a large BC baseline model, while using **93.8%** fewer trainable parameters.
- During cross-behavior transfer (L-shape to U-shape via fine-tuning), the complete SPADE framework improved FID by **37.1%**, APE by **23.1%**, and reduced artifact count by **62%** compared to fine-tuning a standard SKIPP BC model.
- For medium and large model sizes, both DBC and Cond-DBC achieved near-zero artifact rates (0-10 instances exceeding 19 Hausdorff distance), indicating highly regular and artifact-free path generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating diffusion models, particularly image-conditioned ones, into behavioral cloning consistently enhances the generalization capabilities and overall quality of generated paths for AMRs.
> - The Cond-DBC approach provides more targeted and interpretable guidance signals during training, effectively reducing path artifacts and improving alignment with expert demonstrations.
> - It is possible to transfer the representational power of large diffusion models to lightweight behavioral cloning networks, enabling real-time, on-edge deployment on resource-constrained robotic systems without their inherent high inference latency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) are integral to modern industrial operations, necessitating robust path planning capabilities. Traditional path planning algorithms, such as A* and Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT), excel at identifying optimal geometric routes but often struggle to incorporate nuanced human operational preferences. These preferences, which may include maintaining specific clearances, favoring certain corridors, or executing smoother turns, are frequently difficult to formalize into objective functions or cost parameters. Conventional approaches to integrate such preferences often involve intricate reward engineering or hardware-intensive solutions.

Imitation Learning (IL) has emerged as a method to address this challenge by enabling robots to acquire behaviors directly from expert demonstrations. IL can translate the implicit knowledge of domain specialists into executable navigation strategies. However, the efficacy of IL in real-world scenarios, particularly industrial settings, is constrained by several factors: the difficulty in acquiring high-quality demonstrations due to limited teleoperation flexibility and privacy concerns, the limited generalization of learned policies to novel environments, and challenges in the robustness of demonstration collection.

A prior work, "End-to-end Sketch-Guided Path Planning through Imitation Learning for Autonomous Mobile Robots (SKIPP)," sought to overcome these data acquisition constraints by allowing operators to sketch desired paths on a map. SKIPP employed behavioral cloning (BC) with a U-Net architecture to convert these sketches into executable navigation commands. While SKIPP demonstrated the capacity to learn sketched behaviors, it encountered limitations in generalizing these behaviors to new, unseen maps.

Concurrently, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) and their variants have advanced as powerful generative models capable of high-quality data synthesis. These diffusion models have seen increasing application in robotics, including visuomotor control (Diffusion Policy) and collision-free trajectory generation (Motion Planning Diffusion). A technique known as Diffusion-Augmented Behavioral Cloning (DBC) has been proposed to enhance the generalization of standard behavioral cloning by leveraging an offline-trained, high-capacity diffusion model to guide a compact BC network during training. This approach aims to transfer the structural knowledge of the diffusion model without incurring its high inference latency.

The present work, SPADE, builds upon these developments by directly addressing the identified limitations of the SKIPP framework. It aims to improve both the data annotation process for expert demonstrations and the generalization capabilities of the underlying behavioral cloning models. By integrating advanced diffusion model augmentation, specifically image-conditioned diffusion, SPADE seeks to create a more robust, adaptable, and deployable path planning solution for AMRs that can incorporate human preferences efficiently and generalize effectively across varied environments, while maintaining real-time inference properties suitable for resource-constrained robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an enhanced framework for sketch-guided path planning for Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) that overcomes the challenges of limited generalization to unseen environments and insufficient robustness in expert demonstration collection, which were prevalent in previous approaches like SKIPP.

The motivation for this work stems from several key limitations identified in existing methodologies:

1. **Challenges with Traditional Path Planning for Human Preferences:** Conventional path planners are efficient for geometric constraints but lack inherent mechanisms to interpret and execute nuanced human preferences for robot motion, such as preferred clearances, specific turning radii, or favored routes. Integrating these preferences often requires complex and non-intuitive reward engineering or specialized hardware, making them difficult for non-technical users to implement.

2. **Limitations of Imitation Learning Data Acquisition:** While imitation learning offers a solution by enabling robots to learn directly from human demonstrations, collecting high-quality, diverse expert data in specialized environments (e.g., industrial settings) is often resource-intensive. Constraints like limited teleoperation flexibility and privacy concerns regarding sensor data (e.g., RGB images) contribute to the scarcity and high cost of such demonstrations.

3. **Generalization Issues in Prior Sketch-Guided Planning (SKIPP):** The SKIPP framework provided an intuitive method for users to sketch desired paths, which were then translated into robot commands via behavioral cloning. However, SKIPP, built on standard behavioral cloning with a U-Net backbone, demonstrated limitations in generalizing the learned behaviors to new, previously unencountered maps or environmental configurations. The reliance on a deprecated simulator (NVIDIA ISAAC SDK's Flatsim) also restricted its maintainability and scalability, hindering the robustness of the data annotation process.

To address these motivations, SPADE sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Overhaul the Data Annotation Process:** Develop and release a fully open-source, robust data annotation tool built on ROS 2. This tool is intended to enhance data accessibility and quality for both operators and researchers, ensuring reproducibility and extensibility for future work, thereby mitigating the maintenance and scalability issues of prior systems.
- **Improve Model Generalization through Diffusion Augmentation:** Introduce a novel training strategy that integrates diffusion-based augmentation into baseline behavioral cloning models. This aims to enable the compact behavioral cloning network to generalize more effectively to new environments and behaviors by learning from a high-capacity diffusion expert.
- **Leverage Image-Conditioned Diffusion Models:** Extend the Diffusion-guided Behavioral Cloning (DBC) pipeline by incorporating image-conditioned diffusion models. This modification provides more targeted and powerful guidance signals during training by explicitly modeling the conditional probability of paths given environmental context, enabling context-aware path generation.
- **Achieve Real-time, On-edge Deployment:** Ensure that the enhanced framework retains the real-time, on-edge inference properties necessary for deployment on resource-constrained Autonomous Mobile Robots. This involves guiding a lightweight behavioral cloning network with a larger diffusion model such that the benefits of the diffusion model's representational power are transferred without its computational overhead during deployment.

By pursuing these objectives, SPADE seeks to provide a practical and deployable solution for preference-aware path planning that is both intuitive for human operators and robust in its robotic execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SPADE framework introduces enhancements to sketch-guided path planning through two primary contributions: an overhauled data annotation tool and a novel diffusion-augmented training strategy.

#### 3.1. Data Annotation Tool

Addressing the limitations of SKIPP's original data acquisition mechanism, which relied on a deprecated simulator and was not open-source, this work develops a new annotation pipeline built on ROS 2.

- **Environment Setup:** A 2D global occupancy grid is converted into a 3D Gazebo world using the open-source `map2gazebo` ROS package. For robot control and navigation, the TurtleBot3, a ROS-based differential-drive mobile robot, is employed. The Nav2 framework provides modular components for real-time localization, planning, and obstacle avoidance within the simulated environment.
- **User Interaction:** Users define navigation paths by interactively selecting waypoints within RViz2, which are then published to Nav2's action server for sequential goal execution. This process allows the robot to generate trajectories that conform to user-defined shapes.
- **Data Recording:** Data is recorded across three modalities: robot path, end-goal, and robot pose. These are extracted from the `/waypoints`, `/amcl_pose`, and `/local_costmap` topics, respectively.
- **Post-processing:** A spline-based smoothing function is applied to each recorded path. This resampling process transforms the paths into continuous, evenly spaced curves, which reduces noise and irregularities, thereby enhancing training stability and generalization.
- **Dataset Generation:** Using this tool, a dataset comprising 20,000 instances is generated. This includes 10,000 L-shape and 10,000 U-shape paths, evenly distributed across ten open-source occupancy grids. Each instance is represented as four 128x128 binary images (occupancy grid, start point, end point, and path). An additional test dataset of 2,000 instances (1,000 for each shape) is collected based on an industrial map.

#### 3.2. Diffusion Expert Augmented Training

The second core contribution is an enhanced training framework that integrates diffusion models into the behavioral cloning pipeline. This extends the standard behavioral cloning (BC) approach previously used by SKIPP.

- **Baseline Behavioral Cloning (BC):** In the original SKIPP, a BC policy (U-Net backbone) was trained to map observed states to expert actions by minimizing a pixel-wise binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss between the generated and expert path images. This approach models the conditional probability *p(a|s)*.
- **Diffusion Model–Augmented Behavioral Cloning (DBC):** SPADE incorporates the Diffusion Model–Augmented Behavioral Cloning (DBC) principle. In this setup, a high-capacity diffusion model is trained offline to capture the joint probability distribution *p(a,s)* of state-action pairs from the expert dataset. This diffusion model then serves as an auxiliary signal during the training of a compact BC network. The diffusion model receives a four-channel tensor (occupancy grid, start, goal, and path) and predicts all four channels jointly.
- **Conditional Diffusion Expert Augmentation (Cond-DBC - Proposed Extension):** This work extends the DBC framework by replacing the base (unconditional) diffusion model with an *image-conditioned* diffusion model. This modified model takes the state (occupancy grid, start, and goal) as conditioning inputs and learns to predict only the path channel, effectively modeling the conditional probability distribution *p(a|s)*. Conditioning is implemented using FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) layers, which modulate the diffusion network's activations based on the input state. This provides more targeted guidance to the BC network on whether a generated path aligns with expert demonstrations given a specific environment and start-end configuration.
- **Training Objective:** The diffusion model estimates the likelihood of a path originating from the expert dataset by comparing reconstructed noise between generated and expert samples. The Diffusion Margin Loss (L_DM) is defined as the difference between the diffusion reconstruction loss on the agent's generated path (L_diff,agent) and the expert's path (L_diff,expert), capped at zero: L_DM = max(L_diff,agent - L_diff,expert, 0). The total training objective combines the standard BC loss (L_BC) with the diffusion margin loss: L_total = L_BC + λL_DM, where λ is a balancing weight. In Cond-DBC, the diffusion process and its associated loss are applied specifically to the path channel, using other inputs for conditioning.
- **Model Architectures:**
 - **BC Models:** U-Net architectures with varying capacities: small (117,025 parameters), medium (1,926,209 parameters), and large (31,114,785 parameters). Inputs are 3x128x128 tensors representing the local occupancy grid, start, and end points.
 - **Unconditional DDPM:** A ConvNext backbone with approximately 1 billion parameters. Input is a 4x128x128 tensor, including the path as an additional modality.
 - **Conditional Diffusion Model (Cond-DBC Expert):** Inspired by, this model incorporates FiLM conditioning layers and has 9,218,561 trainable parameters. It takes a 1x128x128 noise matrix as input, conditioned by a 3x128x128 tensor representing the occupancy grid, start, and end points.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics

Three metrics are employed to evaluate the generated paths:

- **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID):** Measures the distributional similarity between generated and real path datasets. Lower FID indicates better alignment of path characteristics.
- **Absolute Pose Error (APE):** Quantifies the average pose-wise Euclidean distance deviation between a generated trajectory and its corresponding ground truth expert demonstration. Lower APE signifies paths that closely follow expert trajectories.
- **Hausdorff Distance (Hd19):** Measures the maximum positional deviation between two paths, assessing worst-case alignment. A modified metric, `Hd19`, counts the number of instances where the Hausdorff distance exceeds a threshold of 19, indicating the presence of artifacts (e.g., disconnected segments, extraneous pixels). Lower `Hd19` counts are desirable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance of SPADE compared to baseline methods and its components.

**Standalone Diffusion Model Performance:**
- An unconditional DDPM achieved low FID scores (2.43 for U-shape, 4.84 for L-shape) but exhibited high inference latency, approximately 45 seconds.
- A conditional DDPM achieved slightly higher FID scores (6.75 for U-shape, 13.43 for L-shape) and the lowest APE values (2.74 cm for U-shape, 1.42 cm for L-shape), with a reduced inference time of approximately 18 seconds. Both standalone diffusion models were deemed unsuitable for real-time deployment due to their latency.

**Benchmark Comparison (Table 1):**
- **General Performance Improvement:** The integration of diffusion models, whether unconditional (DBC) or conditional (Cond-DBC), consistently led to lower FID and APE values across all tested Behavioral Cloning (BC) model sizes (small, medium, large) compared to the baseline SKIPP BC approach.
- **Cond-DBC Superiority:** Cond-DBC generally provided stronger results than DBC across most metrics and model sizes. For instance, the medium-sized Cond-DBC achieved a 39.1% lower APE, 33.5% lower FID, and 60% lower Hd19 compared to the large BC baseline model, despite using 93.8% fewer trainable parameters.
- **Generalization to Diffusion Level:** The large Cond-DBC model demonstrated performance in FID and APE that was not statistically significantly different from the standalone conditional DDPM for the L-shape behavior, even on unseen maps.
- **Artifact Generation (Hd19):**
 - Baseline BC models showed higher artifact counts, particularly for the small architecture (28-52 instances > 19 Hausdorff distance).
 - DBC, when applied to small models, paradoxically increased artifact generation (62-124 instances), suggesting potential issues with a low-capacity student network interpreting the unconditional joint distribution signal.
 - Cond-DBC maintained artifact counts comparable to or lower than baseline BC, even for small models, indicating that the image-conditioned guidance provided more focused and interpretable signals. For medium and large models, both DBC and Cond-DBC achieved near-zero artifact rates (0-10 instances).

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Cross-Behavior Transfer (L-shape to U-shape via Fine-tuning - Table 2):**
 - The complete SPADE framework (augmented BC with pre-trained L-shape conditional diffusion expert) achieved the strongest performance.
 - Compared to fine-tuning the standard SKIPP BC model, SPADE improved FID by 37.1%, APE by 23.1%, and reduced artifact count (Hd19) by 62%.
 - When compared to fine-tuning the augmented BC model *without* the SPADE pipeline, the diffusion-based expert guidance provided additional improvements: 6.2% in FID, 3.72% in APE, and 26.92% in Hd19. This suggests that the diffusion expert contributes to effective cross-behavior knowledge transfer.

- **Training from Scratch (U-shape Behavior - Table 3):**
 - Training a model with the complete SPADE pipeline (augmented BC with a pre-trained diffusion expert) from scratch on the U-shape behavior resulted in an FID score of 44.14, APE of 8.26 cm, and Hd19 of 7.
 - Compared to training a standard SKIPP BC model from scratch, the SPADE pipeline improved FID by 4.21% and reduced Hd19 by 63.16%.

These findings collectively indicate that the SPADE framework, particularly with the Cond-DBC approach, enhances generalization and reduces artifacts while enabling the use of significantly smaller models suitable for real-time robotic deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SPADE framework presents advancements in sketch-guided path planning for Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs), with implications for both research and practical deployment.

One of the primary significances of this work is the **demonstrated improvement in model generalization and path quality**. SPADE, specifically using the Cond-DBC approach, consistently outperforms the baseline SKIPP method. The results indicate a 39.1% reduction in Absolute Pose Error (APE) and a 33.5% lower Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), suggesting that the generated paths more closely resemble expert demonstrations and exhibit superior distributional fidelity. This enhanced generalization is observed across varying environments and behavior types (L-shape and U-shape), which is a critical requirement for AMRs operating in dynamic and diverse real-world settings.

A key impact is the **significant increase in computational efficiency for real-time deployment**. SPADE achieves performance comparable to high-capacity, standalone diffusion models (which have inference times of 18-45 seconds) while utilizing significantly fewer trainable parameters. For instance, a medium-sized Cond-DBC model achieves competitive performance with approximately 93.8% fewer parameters than a large baseline model. This reduction in model complexity allows for on-robot inference without requiring specialized hardware accelerators or reliance on cloud computing, addressing a major practical constraint for resource-limited embedded robotic systems. This bridges the gap between the representational power of large generative models and the strict latency, memory, and power constraints of real-world robotic applications.

The development of an **overhauled, open-source data annotation tool built on ROS 2** contributes to research reproducibility and extensibility. By addressing the maintainability and scalability issues of previous proprietary or deprecated data acquisition systems, this tool can facilitate further research and development in learning-from-demonstration for robotics.

The **ablation studies validate the effectiveness of diffusion-based expert guidance** for transfer learning and training from scratch. The framework's ability to effectively transfer knowledge between different behaviors (e.g., L-shape to U-shape) and adapt to new environments through fine-tuning, with improved FID and reduced artifact generation, highlights its potential for developing adaptable and versatile robotic policies with minimal new data collection.

In practical terms, SPADE's ability to encode and replicate nuanced human preferences for robot navigation without complex reward engineering or high computational overhead has the potential to make learning-based, preference-aware path planning more viable for industrial and logistics AMRs. This can lead to robots that operate more predictably and conform to human operational expectations, improving safety, efficiency, and user acceptance in automated environments.

Future directions, such as extending the framework to dynamic environments with moving obstacles, developing more sophisticated diffusion expert training strategies, and incorporating multi-modal diffusion conditioning (e.g., temporal information or high-level task specifications), suggest avenues for further enhancing the framework's robustness and applicability to increasingly complex real-world scenarios.
