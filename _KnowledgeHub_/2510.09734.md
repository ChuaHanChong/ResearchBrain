# ARROW: An Adaptive Rollout and Routing Method for Global Weather Forecasting

> [!summary] Summary
> ARROW introduces an adaptive rollout and routing method for global weather forecasting, addressing limitations in spatiotemporal dependency modeling and autoregressive rollout. The system consistently surpassed existing data-driven baselines, showing an average improvement of approximately 9.3% in RMSE and 10% in ACC for medium-range forecasts by dynamically selecting optimal time intervals.

## Problem

- Existing data-driven global weather forecasting models often fail to adequately model multi-scale spatiotemporal dependencies, treating different temporal scales in isolation and simplifying Earth's spherical geometry.
- Current autoregressive rollout schemes for long-term forecasts are inflexible, using fixed or greedy intervals that do not adapt to the dynamic and non-uniform evolution of atmospheric conditions, leading to suboptimal error accumulation or missed rapid changes.

## Method

- A ==Multi-Interval Forecasting== Model (MIFM) is pre-trained to capture multi-scale temporal correlations and circular spatial dependencies using Ring Positional Encoding (RPE) and a Shared-Private Mixture-of-Experts (S&P MoE) with auxiliary losses.
- An Adaptive Rollout Scheduler (AR Scheduler) is fine-tuned using Deep Q-Network (DQN) reinforcement learning to dynamically select the most suitable time interval for forecasting based on the current weather state and temporal context.
- An alternating optimization algorithm jointly trains the AR Scheduler and fine-tunes the MIFM, allowing the model's parameters to adapt to the errors induced by its learned adaptive rollout policy.

## Results

- ARROW consistently outperformed all data-driven baselines, achieving an average improvement of approximately **9.3%** in RMSE and **10%** in ACC over the second-best models for medium-range global weather forecasts (Day 5 to Day 14).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Ring Positional Encoding (RPE) and the Shared-Private Mixture-of-Experts (S&P MoE) with auxiliary losses are essential components for the model's enhanced accuracy.
- The Adaptive Rollout Scheduler dynamically selected finer time intervals during periods of significant geopotential regime shifts and extreme weather events, demonstrating its capability to adapt to non-uniform atmospheric evolution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling the Earth's spherical geometry with Ring Positional Encoding and capturing both shared and interval-specific atmospheric dynamics through a Shared-Private Mixture-of-Experts improves the accuracy of global spatiotemporal forecasting.
> - An adaptive, reinforcement learning-driven rollout strategy can dynamically adjust forecasting intervals based on atmospheric conditions, effectively balancing the need to mitigate error accumulation and capture rapid changes.
> - Co-training the forecasting model with an adaptive rollout scheduler through alternating optimization allows the model to learn and adapt to its own dynamic inference strategy, leading to more robust performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Global Weather Forecasting (GWF) is a foundational task with applications across diverse sectors such as energy, economics, agriculture, and individual planning. Historically, weather prediction relied on Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) methods, which involve solving Partial Differential Equations derived from atmospheric dynamics. While physically grounded, NWP models are computationally intensive, particularly for global scales, and do not directly benefit from increased observational data in terms of fundamental model refinement.

In response to the computational demands of NWP, data-driven forecasting models have emerged. These models leverage large historical datasets and deep learning techniques to approximate atmospheric dynamics. A common strategy in data-driven GWF is the iterative prediction paradigm, where a short-interval forecasting model is trained and then repeatedly applied autoregressively to generate long-term forecasts. Examples include FourcastNet, which uses a 6-hour interval model iteratively, and Pangu-weather, which employs multiple single-interval forecasting models (SIFMs) with predefined greedy rollout strategies.

Despite advancements, data-driven methods for GWF exhibit two primary limitations that ARROW aims to address:
1. **Insufficient modeling of spatiotemporal dependencies:** Existing methods frequently treat forecasts at different temporal scales in isolation, ignoring the inherent multi-scale correlations and shared physical principles governing atmospheric dynamics. Furthermore, many Transformer-based models, while powerful, simplify the Earth's spherical geometry by treating it as a flat 2D image, which can distort the representation of global atmospheric phenomena.
2. **Inflexible autoregression rollout schemes:** Atmospheric evolution is not uniform; it includes periods of gradual change and abrupt transitions. Current autoregressive strategies often employ fixed-interval rollouts or greedy schemes that do not dynamically adapt to the evolving weather state. This inflexibility can lead to either excessive error accumulation over long stable periods or a failure to capture rapid, fine-grained atmospheric variations during transitional phases.

ARROW positions itself within this landscape by introducing a novel approach that unifies multi-scale temporal and circular spatial dependency modeling within a single framework, coupled with an adaptive, reinforcement learning-based rollout strategy. This aims to overcome the identified limitations of current data-driven models, potentially establishing a more robust paradigm for GWF.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the accuracy and adaptability of global weather forecasting, particularly for medium-range predictions, by addressing the limitations of current data-driven methods. This overarching goal is broken down into specific objectives and motivations:

**Addressing Insufficient Modeling of Spatiotemporal Dependencies:**
- **Motivation:** Atmospheric dynamics are intrinsically multi-scale, with phenomena evolving and interacting across various temporal horizons. Current practices of training independent Single Interval Forecasting Models (SIFMs) for different time intervals overlook these correlations, leading to increased computational overhead and suboptimal modeling. Additionally, the Earth's spherical geometry is fundamental to global weather patterns, yet many models simplify it to a flat representation, introducing inaccuracies.
- **Objective:** Develop a Multi-Interval Forecasting Model (MIFM) capable of simultaneously capturing:
 - **Multi-scale temporal correlations:** By allowing the model to learn shared patterns and interval-specific characteristics across different time scales within a unified architecture.
 - **Circular spatial dependencies:** By accurately encoding the Earth's spherical latitude structure to prevent distortions in representing global atmospheric phenomena.

**Addressing Inflexible Autoregression Rollout Schemes:**
- **Motivation:** Atmospheric evolution is inherently dynamic and non-uniform. A fixed-interval or greedily chosen rollout strategy cannot optimally adapt to these varying conditions. For instance, using small intervals throughout a stable period can lead to unnecessary error accumulation, while large intervals during rapid transitions may miss critical fine-grained changes. An effective strategy should balance these two aspects. Traditional Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) methods often use adaptive time-stepping, suggesting a similar adaptive approach could benefit data-driven models.
- **Objective:** Design an Adaptive Rollout Scheduler (AR Scheduler) that can dynamically select the most suitable time interval for forecasting based on the current weather state and temporal context. This aims to:
 - Mitigate error accumulation by selecting larger intervals during stable atmospheric phases.
 - Capture rapid transitions accurately by employing finer intervals when atmospheric dynamics are highly active.
 - Integrate this adaptive strategy with model fine-tuning to ensure the model's parameters remain optimal under the learned rollout policy.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the critical role of accurate weather forecasting in various real-world applications and the desire to push the capabilities of data-driven models beyond their current limitations, ultimately aiming to achieve state-of-the-art performance and establish a more robust forecasting paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ARROW's methodology is structured into two main stages: the pre-training of a Multi-Interval Forecasting Model (MIFM) and the fine-tuning of an Adaptive Rollout Scheduler (AR Scheduler).

**Stage 1: Multi-Interval Forecasting Model (MIFM) Pre-training**
This stage focuses on developing a unified model that can generate accurate one-step forecasts across different time intervals while explicitly modeling spatiotemporal dependencies.

- **Input Processing:** The initial weather state, `X_0`, is first converted into a sequence of tokens (`~Z`) using a weather-specific tokenizer.
- **Ring Positional Encoding (RPE):** To address the Earth's spherical geometry, RPE is introduced. Unlike conventional positional encodings that might treat spatial endpoints as distant, RPE explicitly encodes the circular latitude structure. For a 2D grid, it applies circular encoding along the longitude (where 180°W and 180°E are adjacent) and standard positional encoding along the latitude. This creates a weather embedding `Z^(0) = ~Z + RPE`.
- **Arch Blocks and Shared-Private Mixture-of-Experts (S&P MoE):** The weather embedding `Z^(0)`, along with the embedded time interval `delta`, passes through `N` Arch blocks. Each Arch block incorporates:
 - **Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN):** Conditions the Transformer layers on the specific time interval `delta`.
 - **Shared-Private Mixture-of-Experts (S&P MoE):** This component is designed to capture both common and specific atmospheric dynamics across different time intervals. Each token is routed to a shared Feed-Forward Network (FFN) and a selection of `k` private FFNs (`M` total private experts). The gating value for routing is perturbed by an interval-specific noise (`b_delta`), allowing for dynamic expert selection.
 - **Auxiliary Losses for S&P MoE:** Two auxiliary cross-entropy losses are introduced to manage expert utilization:
 - `aux-loss1`: Encourages divergence between the noise distributions of different time intervals, promoting specialization among private experts for interval-specific features.
 - `aux-loss2`: Encourages the aggregated noise distribution across all experts to be uniform, ensuring balanced load and utilization of all private FFNs.
- **Optimization:** The MIFM is pre-trained by jointly optimizing a randomized dynamics forecasting loss (`L_delta`), which measures the error in predicting weather changes over a random time interval `delta`, and the total auxiliary loss (`L_aux = -aux-loss1 + alpha * aux-loss2`).

**Stage 2: Adaptive Rollout Scheduler (AR Scheduler) Fine-tuning**
This stage develops a reinforcement learning agent to dynamically select time intervals during autoregressive rollout, optimizing for accuracy and computational efficiency.

- **Weather Forecasting Environment (`Psi`):** This environment is defined by the pre-trained ARROW (MIFM) and the weather dataset used in Stage 1. The AR Scheduler interacts with this environment.
- **Reinforcement Learning Setup:**
 - **State Space (S):** At each step `t`, the state comprises the current predicted weather state (`X_tau_(t-1)`), date and time information, and temporal position within the trajectory (travel time, remaining time, target lead time). These are converted into weather (`Z_weather`) and temporal (`Z_time`) embeddings.
 - **Action Space (A):** A discrete set of available time intervals, such as {6h, 12h, 24h}.
 - **Reward Function (R):** The immediate reward `r_tau` is defined as the negative latitude-weighted Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of the prediction at that step. A penalty term (`omega`) is added to discourage excessively long rollout trajectories, aiming to maximize the trajectory return `G_Gamma`.
- **Deep Q-Network (DQN):** A DQN is employed to estimate the state-action value function `q(s, a)`. The temporal and weather embeddings are concatenated, processed by a Multi-Head Self-Attention module, and fed into a head to output value estimates for each possible action.
- **Alternating Optimization:** A key challenge is the bi-level optimization problem, where the optimal policy (`q(s,a)`) depends on the environment (`Psi`), and `Psi` (i.e., the MIFM parameters) is affected by the policy. ARROW addresses this through an alternating optimization algorithm:
 1. The DQN (`q_main`) is updated by minimizing the temporal difference loss (`L_Psi`).
 2. The pre-trained MIFM is fine-tuned using a multi-step fine-tuning loss (`L_q_target`) on rollout trajectories generated by the current AR Scheduler policy (`pi_ARROW-P`). This process stops the gradient for excessively long trajectories (`T_max` steps) to manage error accumulation.
 This alternating process allows `Psi` and `q(s,a)` to gradually converge towards an optimal state, where the model is fine-tuned to the errors induced by its adaptive rollout strategy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ARROW demonstrates its effectiveness in global weather forecasting across various lead times and meteorological variables.

- **Overall Performance Comparison:**
 - ARROW consistently achieved superior performance compared to all data-driven baselines, including Keisler, GraphCast, Pangu-weather, FourcastNet, and Stormer. This superiority was observed in both Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) (lower is better) and Anomaly Correlation Coefficient (ACC) (higher is better) across a nine-day evaluation period (Day 5 to Day 14) for six key meteorological variables (T2m, U10, V10, TCC, Z500, T850).
 - Quantitatively, ARROW demonstrated an average improvement of approximately 9.3% in RMSE and 10% in ACC over the second-best data-driven models.
 - The model also showed strong short-term forecasting capabilities, occasionally outperforming the Integrated Forecast System (IFS), a high-resolution numerical weather prediction model.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effect of Ring Positional Encoding (RPE):** Replacing RPE with a standard 2D learnable positional encoding, originally designed for images, resulted in a deterioration of both RMSE and ACC at the 72-hour lead time. This indicates that RPE's explicit modeling of the Earth's circular latitude structure is beneficial for global weather representation.
 - **Effect of Shared-Private Mixture-of-Experts (S&P MoE):** Substituting the S&P MoE with a standard Feed-Forward Network (FFN) led to performance degradation. This finding supports the design premise that capturing both shared and interval-specific atmospheric variations across different time scales is critical for accurate forecasting.
 - **Effect of Auxiliary Losses:** Removing either `aux-loss1` (promoting individual expert characteristics) or `aux-loss2` (promoting global expert load balance) resulted in a decline in forecasting accuracy. This confirms the importance of these losses in enabling specialized and balanced utilization of the private experts within the S&P MoE.

- **Effect of Adaptive Rollout Strategy:**
 - Comparison with alternative rollout strategies (Random, Naive, Greedy) at a 138-hour lead time for T2m and T850 demonstrated the effectiveness of ARROW's adaptive rollout. The adaptive strategy outperformed all others, including the greedy strategy (which aims to reduce error accumulation but lacks fine-grained adaptation) and the naive strategy (fixed small interval). This validates the AR Scheduler's ability to learn and implement a robust dynamic forecasting strategy.

- **Case Studies:**
 - **Siberian Cold Wave (January 22-27, 2018):** ARROW accurately predicted the trajectory and temperature variations of a significant cold wave, including a topographically shielded region that remained unaffected. This illustrates its capability in handling extreme weather events and capturing fine-grained spatial details.
 - **Cloud Cover (May 10-11, 2018):** The model successfully predicted global cloud cover one day in advance, identifying specific regions of low cloud cover. This demonstrates its utility for applications like photovoltaic power scheduling.
 - **Qualitative Comparison (Appendix J):** Visual comparisons with Pangu-weather, FourCastNet, and Stormer for various meteorological variables at a 234-hour lead time showed that ARROW produced forecasts closely matching ground truth, with minimal artifacts and superior global consistency. Other models exhibited more spatial noise, over-smoothing, or distortions.

- **AR Scheduler Analysis (Appendix I):**
 - **Interval Distribution:** Analysis of the AR Scheduler's decisions revealed that shorter lead times (e.g., 120h) tended to use 6-hour intervals more frequently than longer lead times (e.g., 354h). This suggests a learned trade-off where finer temporal resolution is prioritized for shorter, less error-prone trajectories, while longer trajectories use larger intervals to manage cumulative error.
 - **Decision-Making Correlation:** Periods with a high frequency of 6-hour interval selections by the AR Scheduler correlated with periods of significant geopotential regime shifts (e.g., late August to early September 2018). These periods corresponded to real-world extreme weather events (e.g., Hurricane Lane, Typhoon Jebi, Tropical Storm Gordon). This indicates that the AR Scheduler successfully adapts its interval choices to atmospheric conditions requiring finer temporal granularity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ARROW introduces methodological advancements that have implications for the field of global weather forecasting and its downstream applications.

- **Advancement in Data-Driven Weather Forecasting:** The work addresses two fundamental limitations of existing data-driven weather models: the insufficient modeling of multi-scale spatiotemporal dependencies and the inflexibility of autoregressive rollout schemes. By offering a comprehensive solution to these challenges, ARROW contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in this domain.

- **Improved Accuracy and Reliability:** The consistent outperformance of ARROW against multiple data-driven baselines in both RMSE and ACC metrics across various lead times and variables indicates a notable improvement in forecast accuracy and reliability. This enhanced precision is crucial for decision-making across diverse sectors.

- **Enhanced Spatiotemporal Modeling:** The Multi-Interval Forecasting Model (MIFM), with its Shared-Private Mixture-of-Experts (S&P MoE) and Ring Positional Encoding (RPE), provides a more nuanced approach to modeling atmospheric dynamics. RPE's accurate representation of Earth's spherical geometry and S&P MoE's ability to capture both common and interval-specific temporal patterns contribute to a more faithful representation of global weather phenomena.

- **Adaptive Forecasting Paradigm:** The Adaptive Rollout Scheduler (AR Scheduler) marks a departure from static or greedy rollout strategies. By dynamically selecting time intervals based on current weather states, ARROW can better balance the trade-off between mitigating error accumulation and capturing fine-grained atmospheric changes. This adaptive capability makes the forecasting system more responsive to the non-uniform nature of atmospheric evolution, mimicking adaptive solvers in traditional Numerical Weather Prediction.

- **Broad Societal and Economic Impact:** Accurate medium-range global weather forecasts serve as vital upstream information for numerous applications. ARROW's improved performance can potentially enhance:
 - **Energy Management:** More precise cloud cover forecasts can optimize photovoltaic power generation scheduling.
 - **Agriculture:** Better predictions of temperature and precipitation patterns can support crop planning and resource management.
 - **Transportation:** Improved forecasts of wind and temperature can aid in traffic flow prediction and route optimization.
 - **Disaster Preparedness:** Accurate forecasting of extreme events like cold waves, hurricanes, and typhoons can facilitate early warning systems and more effective response strategies.
 - **Economic Decision-Making:** Greater certainty in weather outlooks can support various economic activities dependent on climatic conditions.

- **Future Research Directions:** The authors indicate plans to explore reinforcement learning for local weather forecasting and further integrate physics-guided approaches. This suggests that the principles established by ARROW could be extended to other scales and combined with physical constraints to develop even more robust and interpretable models. The availability of the code also promotes reproducibility and further research in this area.
