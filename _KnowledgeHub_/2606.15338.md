# SimWeaver: Zero-Shot RGB Sim-to-Real for Deformable Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SimWeaver facilitates zero-shot RGB sim-to-real transfer for deformable object manipulation, achieving an average real-world success rate of 91.30% across five diverse tasks using policies trained with 200 simulated demonstrations.

## Problem

- Real-world data collection for deformable object manipulation is labor-intensive and costly due to complex object properties and high-dimensional state spaces.
- Current physics simulators often lack the fidelity and reliability for deformable objects, causing a significant sim-to-real gap, particularly in contact dynamics and determinism.
- Existing sim-to-real approaches for pixel-based policies rely on human teleoperation or manual calibration per task, limiting scalability and robust generalization across diverse materials.

## Method

- **SimWeaver**-Sim: A robust deformable simulator featuring an ==active-collision-region scheme== for stable robot-deformable interactions and physical parameterization.
- SimWeaver-Syn: An automatic, topology-aware trajectory synthesizer that generates high-quality, deterministic demonstrations without human teleoperation.
- SimWeaver-Real: A comprehensive sim-to-real protocol employing physics-driven cloth state randomization and sensor-distribution-aware photometric augmentation for visual alignment.

## Results

- Achieved an average **91.30%** real-world success rate across five diverse deformable manipulation tasks with zero-shot RGB sim-to-real transfer, utilizing only 200 simulated demonstrations per task.
- SimWeaver-Sim demonstrated **100%** task and grasp success with **0%** penetration or explosion in bimanual grasping tests, outperforming other simulators in reliability and efficiency.
- Reduced the cost of generating usable trajectories to $**0.03** per trajectory, offering two orders of magnitude cost reduction compared to real-robot collection and over a factor of three reduction compared to prior sim-to-real methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust collision handling and deterministic replay in simulation are crucial for achieving high-fidelity deformable manipulation and effective zero-shot transfer.
> - Automated, topology-aware trajectory synthesis can generate high-quality, stable demonstrations more efficiently and reliably than human teleoperation or learned models.
> - Carefully calibrated sensor-distribution-aware photometric augmentation is essential for bridging the visual sim-to-real gap, especially for RGB observations of challenging materials.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant progress, particularly with rigid objects, where policy learning can benefit from large datasets, often following approximate power-law relationships with demonstration count. However, scaling this success to deformable objects presents substantial challenges due to their high-dimensional configuration spaces, non-linear shape variations, and complex interactions with rigid manipulators. Real-world data collection for deformable objects is notably labor-intensive, costly, and difficult to scale, prompting extensive exploration of synthetic demonstrations via physics-based simulation.

Existing simulation approaches, while effective for rigid manipulation, have largely failed to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for deformable objects without requiring real-world fine-tuning or extensive manual calibration. Several factors contribute to this limitation:
- **Simulator Fidelity:** Widely used simulators (e.g., NVIDIA Isaac Sim) often exhibit unreliable contact dynamics for thin structures and do not consistently capture realistic physical behavior of deformable materials, leading to a significant sim-to-real gap. Concurrent VBD-based solvers have improved fidelity but still present failure modes such as cloth-rigid penetration, grasp instability, and replay non-determinism.
- **Observation Modality Shift:** The community has often shifted towards depth and point-cloud representations for deformable manipulation due to the unresolved sim-to-real gap for raw visual (RGB) inputs. However, point-cloud methods can be unreliable when depth data is noisy, incomplete, or for visually challenging materials (e.g., dark, reflective, low-texture, self-occluded fabrics).
- **Data Generation Constraints:** Prior pixel-based sim-to-real work, such as the concurrent SIM1 which achieved 87% real-world success on T-shirt folding, has relied on per-task expert-guided material calibration and teleoperated demonstrations for trajectory generation. These constraints limit scalability, autonomy, and applicability beyond specific folding tasks.
- **Limited Asset Diversity:** Simulation benchmarks often simplify deformable objects, with plastic bag manipulation, for example, largely remaining a real-robot-first domain due to the complexity of simulating thin-shell deformation, 2-torus handles, and self-contact.

This work positions itself to address these limitations by developing a comprehensive sim-to-real framework that aims to enable zero-shot RGB transfer for diverse deformable manipulation tasks. It seeks to provide a teleoperation-free, scalable, and calibration-free solution that is robust to visually challenging materials and extends to complex deformable objects like plastic bags. The paper contrasts its approach with existing deformable simulators, sim-to-real methods (both point-cloud and RGB), and trajectory generation techniques, identifying specific gaps in reliability, scalability, and generalization that SimWeaver aims to bridge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve zero-shot RGB sim-to-real transfer for deformable object manipulation, enabling policies trained purely in simulation to perform effectively on real robotic hardware without requiring real-world fine-tuning, teleoperation, or per-task calibration. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific challenges in the field:

1. **Overcoming Real-World Data Collection Bottlenecks:** Collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world data for deformable manipulation is resource-intensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale. This limitation significantly hinders the application of data-driven policy learning for deformable objects. The motivation is to leverage the scalability and cost-efficiency of simulation to generate training data.

2. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Deformable Objects:** Existing simulators often lack the fidelity required for robust zero-shot transfer in deformable manipulation. This is due to issues such as unreliable contact dynamics (e.g., for thin fabrics), inaccurate physical behavior, cloth-rigid penetration, grasp instability, and non-deterministic replay of trajectories. A key motivation is to develop a simulator that provides a higher degree of physical realism and contact reliability, thereby reducing the sim-to-real gap.

3. **Eliminating Dependence on Teleoperation and Manual Calibration:** Many current approaches, including prior work on RGB sim-to-real, rely on human teleoperation for generating expert demonstrations and manual, per-task material calibration. This introduces human bias, labor costs, and limits the autonomy and scalability of the training pipeline. The objective is to automate the demonstration generation process entirely and remove the need for manual calibration of physical parameters.

4. **Enabling Robust Pixel-Based Policy Learning:** While the community has often gravitated towards depth or point-cloud observations to mitigate RGB sim-to-real challenges, these modalities are susceptible to noise and incompleteness when dealing with visually difficult materials (e.g., dark, reflective, or low-texture fabrics). The motivation is to advance RGB end-to-end policy learning, which offers robustness to such visual challenges, by effectively closing the pixel-based sim-to-real gap.

5. **Supporting Diverse and Complex Deformable Materials:** Many existing deformable manipulation benchmarks and simulators are tailored to standard fabrics. There is a need to extend capabilities to more complex and challenging deformable objects, such as plastic bags with distinct handle structures and complex self-contact behaviors, which have historically been difficult to simulate reliably.

To achieve these objectives, the research sets out to:
- Develop **SimWeaver-Sim**, a reliable deformable simulator featuring robust collision handling, penetration prevention, trajectory replay determinism, and physically measurable parameters.
- Create **SimWeaver-Asset**, an extensible framework for diverse deformable assets with measurement-backed physical properties and single-image generation capabilities.
- Design **SimWeaver-Syn**, a deterministic, topology-aware trajectory synthesizer that generates high-quality demonstrations without teleoperation or learned generative models.
- Establish **SimWeaver-Real**, a robust sim-to-real protocol incorporating physics-driven cloth state randomization and sensor-distribution-aware photometric augmentation to align visual observations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SimWeaver framework integrates four core components designed to collectively achieve zero-shot RGB sim-to-real transfer for deformable manipulation: SimWeaver-Sim, SimWeaver-Asset, SimWeaver-Syn, and SimWeaver-Real.

#### 3.1. SimWeaver-Sim: Reliable Deformable Simulation

SimWeaver-Sim is built upon the RGBench simulator, with significant enhancements to improve contact reliability for closed-loop robot manipulation. The method focuses on addressing critical failure modes identified in widely-used solvers (e.g., NVIDIA Isaac Sim PBD, VBD-based methods), such as instability, missed grasps, rigid-flexible penetration, and non-determinism during contact-rich interactions.
- **Active-Collision-Region Scheme:** A key innovation is a simple yet effective strategy for stabilizing robot-deformable interactions. It defines a "collision-forbidden region" around each gripper jaw where deformable self-collision handling is temporarily disabled. Concurrently, an "active collision region" is adaptively computed where cloth self-collisions remain enabled and are robustly resolved. This mechanism suppresses grasping jitter and oscillation when the gripper closes by contracting the active region, and expands it when opening to allow for stable intersection resolution. This approach is designed to eliminate penetration artifacts and unstable contacts without introducing observable visual artifacts.
- **Physical Parameterization:** Unlike many simulators that rely on abstract parameters, SimWeaver-Sim uses physical parameters (e.g., mass per unit area, bending stiffness, stretch, friction) that directly correspond to real-world measurements. These values are drawn from a fabric measurement library, ensuring physical grounding.

#### 3.2. SimWeaver-Asset: Extensible Asset Framework

SimWeaver-Asset provides a comprehensive collection of deformable meshes and a system for generating new ones, all integrated with physically accurate properties.
- **Diverse Asset Library:** It includes approximately 2,000 garment meshes (from CLOTH3D) and extends to categories underrepresented in prior datasets, such as plastic bags with handle structures. All assets share a unified triangle-mesh representation.
- **Measurement-Backed Physical Properties:** Material properties for assets are assigned from the RGBench fabric measurement library. This ensures that simulated materials behave according to real-world measurements (e.g., ASTM standards for stiffness and stretch), bypassing the need for manual, per-asset parameter tuning.
- **Single-Image Asset Generation:** The framework integrates EmbodiedGen to generate new 3D assets from single images. This pipeline also infers fabric categories and samples physical properties from the RGBench library, allowing generated assets to have measurement-backed dynamics and be directly usable in the simulator.

#### 3.3. SimWeaver-Syn: Automatic Topology-Aware Trajectory Synthesis

SimWeaver-Syn generates training demonstrations deterministically, without requiring human teleoperation, learned generative models, or post-hoc filtering.
- **Topology-Adjacency Selection (TAS):** Manipulation semantics are captured by a labeled graph G=(V, E) on the canonical mesh of the asset. V represents semantic landmarks (e.g., cloth corners, T-shirt neck/sleeve points, bag strap tips), and E defines task-feasible bimanual grasp pairs. For symmetric assets, G is automatically extracted; for others, landmarks are labeled once per asset class. The selection process `(l*, r*) = arg max (u,v) ∈ E ∩ F(obs_t) S(u, v; obs_t)` restricts grasp candidates to topologically valid and geometrically feasible pairs.
- **Topology-Aware State Predicates:** A set of closed-form predicates evaluates the feasibility of grasp candidates on the current cloth observation `obs_t`. These include `Occlusion-above` (detects objects above a candidate), `Layer separation` (identifies multi-layer overlaps), `Reachability`, `Anti-cross-arm safety`, and `Surface exposure`. These predicates ensure that selected grasps are physically viable, deterministic, and free of label noise.
- **Closed-Loop Synthesis:** To handle common grasp failures, the synthesis process incorporates a closed-loop mechanism. Each grasp attempt is verified, and if it fails (e.g., the cloth is not truly grasped), the system re-observes the cloth, re-evaluates candidate grasps, and retries up to a maximum number of attempts while anchoring already-verified arms.
- **Constrained Planner:** Realizes the selected grasp pairs into smooth, executable trajectories using a constrained planner (e.g., TOPP-RA with Pinocchio IK). Diversity in demonstrations is achieved through randomized simulator initializations.

#### 3.4. SimWeaver-Real: Sim-to-Real Protocol

This component addresses the sim-to-real gap at both physical and perceptual levels to ensure effective zero-shot transfer.
- **Physics-Driven Cloth State Randomization:** Instead of simple pose randomization, cloth initial states are generated using task-conditioned physics primitives (e.g., "pin-and-release," "random-fold-and-settle"). This allows the simulator to produce physically plausible and diverse configurations that cover the high-dimensional state space of deformable objects.
- **Sensor-Distribution-Aware Photometric Augmentation:** To mitigate visual disparities from real-world camera sensors (e.g., RealSense D435i), a photometric augmentation pipeline is applied during training. The ranges for brightness, hue, contrast, saturation, and sharpness are fitted to empirically measured per-camera statistics, addressing issues like color bias and gain-loop channel drift. This augmentation is shown to be critical for transfer; disabling it leads to 0% real-world success.
- **Conventional Domain Randomization:** Standard domain randomization techniques are applied to robot joint home-pose noise, table height, table texture, and lighting. Physical parameters for deformable objects are fixed based on real-world measurements from SimWeaver-Asset. A scrambled Sobol quasi-random sequence is used for sampling randomization axes to improve coverage within the 200-episode budget per task.
- **Policy Training:** A π0.5 VLA policy is fine-tuned on the 200 SimWeaver-Syn-generated demonstrations per task, consuming RGB observations from multiple cameras and bimanual joint states.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings demonstrating the effectiveness of the SimWeaver framework for zero-shot RGB sim-to-real deformable manipulation.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot RGB Sim-to-Real Success Across Diverse Tasks

SimWeaver achieved an average real-world success rate of 91.30% across five diverse deformable manipulation tasks, utilizing zero-shot transfer from policies trained on only 200 simulated demonstrations per task. This was accomplished without teleoperation or per-task calibration.
- **Task-specific success rates:**
 - Snack packaging: 86.96%
 - Garment folding: 91.30%
 - Garment unfolding: 82.61%
 - Silk unfolding: 95.65%
 - Silk grasping: 100.00%
- These tasks encompassed plastic-bag manipulation, structured garment handling, and thin-fabric manipulation, covering a range of deformable complexities. The lowest success rates were observed in tasks requiring recovery from wrinkled or arbitrarily draped initial states (snack packaging, garment unfolding).

#### 4.2. Simulator Reliability and Efficiency

A quantitative comparison of SimWeaver-Sim against NVIDIA Isaac Sim (PhysX particle cloth) and a Newton VBD-based simulator demonstrated its superior reliability and efficiency for bimanual garment grasping.
- **Task Success:** SimWeaver-Sim achieved 100% task and grasp success, contrasting with 0% for Isaac Sim and 0% task success (77.5% penetration, 22.5% explosion) for Newton VBD.
- **Failure Modes:** SimWeaver-Sim exhibited 0% penetration and 0% explosion rates, which are significant failure modes in the compared simulators.
- **Efficiency:** SimWeaver-Sim was the most efficient, with an average per-step time of 4.44 ms, compared to 7.80 ms for Isaac Sim and 10.38 ms for Newton VBD. These reliability and efficiency improvements directly contribute to generating high-quality usable training data.

#### 4.3. Trajectory Synthesis Quality and Replay Stability

SimWeaver-Syn's ability to generate high-quality, deterministic trajectories was validated through ablation studies and cross-method comparisons.
- **Ablation Study (T-shirt flatten):** Full SimWeaver-Syn achieved an 89.7% pass rate with 100/100 replay stability. Removing the closed-loop iteration reduced the pass rate to 65.0%, primarily due to 'Lift fail' (35%). Further removing Topology-Adjacency Selection (TAS) dropped the pass rate to 56.3% ('Lift fail' at 43.7%). This indicates that both closed-loop retry and topology-aware pair selection are critical for reliable synthesis.
- **Cross-Method Comparison (T-shirt fold):** SimWeaver-Syn achieved a 97.2% pass rate and 100/100 replay stability, substantially outperforming the concurrent SIM1 which had a 24.0% pass rate and only 13/100 replay stability. SIM1 experienced high rates of 'Lift fail' (43%) and 'Stall fail' (32%), attributed to both generator-side and simulator-side failures. The replay test isolated simulator-side contact-resolution determinism, highlighting SimWeaver-Sim's robustness.

#### 4.4. Sample Efficiency and Generalization on Silk Grasping

Experiments on silk grasping, a challenging task due to weak texture and specular reflection, demonstrated the sample efficiency and generalization capabilities of sim-trained policies.
- **In-Distribution Scaling:** Sim-trained policies (with Domain Randomization, DR) showed comparable or superior performance to real-data trained policies at small data scales, reaching 100% success with 200 demonstrations. This suggests that the consistent synthesized trajectories are more sample-efficient than noisy teleoperated demonstrations.
- **Generalization Under Distribution Shift:** Under visual distribution shifts (texture, lighting, and rotation), the sim-trained policy (200 demos + DR) maintained 100% success. In contrast, a real-data trained policy (100 demos) experienced significant performance drops: 13.0% (texture), 69.6% (lighting), and 8.7% (rotation). This indicates that the simulator-only DR axes contribute to strong generalization.

#### 4.5. Cost Efficiency

SimWeaver demonstrated substantial cost efficiency for generating usable trajectories compared to prior methods and direct real-robot collection.
- **Throughput:** SimWeaver achieved 2824 usable trajectories per day, significantly higher than SIM1 (710 traj/day) and real-robot collection (104 traj/day).
- **Unit Cost:** The cost per usable trajectory was $0.03 for SimWeaver, $0.10 for SIM1, and $2.71 for real-robot collection, representing a two-orders-of-magnitude reduction compared to real-robot collection and over a factor of three reduction compared to SIM1.

#### 4.6. Criticality of Sensor-Aware Photometric Augmentation

Ablation studies confirmed that the sensor-distribution-aware photometric augmentation is essential for sim-to-real transfer. Disabling this component resulted in a 0% real-world success rate across all five tasks.

#### 4.7. Point-Cloud Baseline Limitations

A DP3 point-cloud baseline trained on the same data failed on all real tasks. This failure was attributed to fundamental coverage degradation of the point-cloud modality on visually challenging materials and robot grippers in the real-world scene, rather than an inherent limitation of point-cloud methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SimWeaver framework represents an advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for deformable objects, and carries several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Zero-Shot RGB Sim-to-Real for Deformables:** SimWeaver addresses a long-standing challenge by demonstrating reliable zero-shot RGB sim-to-real transfer for deformable manipulation. This achievement facilitates the deployment of policies trained entirely in simulation to real-world robots without costly and time-consuming real-world fine-tuning or manual calibration, expanding the practical applicability of robotic systems for handling flexible materials.

- **Scalable and Cost-Efficient Data Generation:** By achieving high real-world success with a limited number of simulated demonstrations (200 per task) and demonstrating significantly higher throughput at a substantially lower cost than prior simulation methods and real-world data collection, SimWeaver provides a scalable solution for generating training data. This reduces reliance on laborious human teleoperation and costly real-robot operation, accelerating the development cycle for deformable manipulation tasks.

- **Robustness to Visual Challenges:** The pixel-based approach, combined with sensor-distribution-aware photometric augmentation, enables robust manipulation of visually challenging materials such as dark, low-texture, and reflective fabrics, as well as complex objects like plastic bags. This is a notable advantage over depth or point-cloud methods, which often struggle with such visual conditions, thereby broadening the range of materials robots can effectively interact with.

- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated ability of sim-trained policies to maintain high performance under significant visual distribution shifts (texture, lighting, rotation) highlights the framework's contribution to generalization. This is crucial for deploying robotic systems in diverse and uncontrolled real-world environments where variations in appearance are common.

- **Foundation for Reliable Deformable Simulation:** The improvements to simulator reliability (SimWeaver-Sim), including robust collision handling, penetration prevention, and trajectory determinism, provide a more trustworthy foundation for generating high-fidelity synthetic data. This directly impacts the quality and transferability of learned policies.

- **Automated and Deterministic Trajectory Synthesis:** The topology-aware, closed-loop trajectory synthesizer (SimWeaver-Syn) eliminates the need for human teleoperation or noisy learned generative models. This automation streamlines the data generation pipeline, making it more autonomous, consistent, and less prone to human variability or generator-induced failures.

- **Extensible and Physically Grounded Asset Ecosystem:** SimWeaver-Asset, with its measurement-backed physical parameterization and single-image asset generation, fosters the growth of diverse and physically accurate deformable asset libraries. This capability supports wider research and application by providing a consistent and physically accurate basis for simulating various materials.

- **Accelerated Research and Industrial Adoption:** The framework's ability to reduce development costs and time could significantly accelerate research in deformable object manipulation. It also has the potential to impact industries such as textiles, apparel manufacturing, packaging, logistics, and even healthcare, where handling flexible and deformable items is a common challenge. The planned release of components aims to further support the broader robotics community.
