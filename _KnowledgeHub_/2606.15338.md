# SimWeaver: Zero-Shot RGB Sim-to-Real for Deformable Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SimWeaver facilitates zero-shot RGB sim-to-real transfer for deformable object manipulation, achieving an average real-world success rate of 91.30% across five diverse tasks using policies trained with 200 simulated demonstrations.

## Problem

- Real-world data collection for deformable object manipulation is labor-intensive and costly due to complex object properties and high-dimensional state spaces.
- Current physics simulators often lack the fidelity and reliability for deformable objects, causing a significant sim-to-real gap, particularly in contact dynamics and determinism.
- Existing sim-to-real approaches for pixel-based policies rely on human teleoperation or manual calibration per task, limiting scalability and robust generalization across diverse materials.

## Method

- **SimWeaver**-Sim: A robust deformable simulator featuring an ==active-collision-region scheme== for stable robot-deformable interactions and physical parameterization.
- SimWeaver-Syn: An automatic, topology-aware trajectory synthesizer that generates high-quality, deterministic demonstrations without human teleoperation.
- SimWeaver-Real: A comprehensive sim-to-real protocol employing physics-driven cloth state randomization and sensor-distribution-aware photometric augmentation for visual alignment.

## Results

- Achieved an average **91.30%** real-world success rate across five diverse deformable manipulation tasks with zero-shot RGB sim-to-real transfer, utilizing only 200 simulated demonstrations per task.
- SimWeaver-Sim demonstrated **100%** task and grasp success with **0%** penetration or explosion in bimanual grasping tests, outperforming other simulators in reliability and efficiency.
- Reduced the cost of generating usable trajectories to $**0.03** per trajectory, offering two orders of magnitude cost reduction compared to real-robot collection and over a factor of three reduction compared to prior sim-to-real methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust collision handling and deterministic replay in simulation are crucial for achieving high-fidelity deformable manipulation and effective zero-shot transfer.
> - Automated, topology-aware trajectory synthesis can generate high-quality, stable demonstrations more efficiently and reliably than human teleoperation or learned models.
> - Carefully calibrated sensor-distribution-aware photometric augmentation is essential for bridging the visual sim-to-real gap, especially for RGB observations of challenging materials.
