# ContactGuard: Pre-Contact Execution Monitoring with Action-Conditioned Latent World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ContactGuard is a pre-contact execution monitor for chunked visuomotor policies. A JEPA-style multi-view latent world model, trained on unlabelled robot trajectories, rolls the policy's own planned action chunk forward in embedding space; a frozen logistic probe scores the predicted post-contact latent and aborts before the gripper closes. Across four real grasp tasks it reaches 0.898-0.992 live AUC without touching the policy.

## Problem

- Conventional manipulation failure detectors fire only after contact, when a push, slip, or object disturbance has already changed the scene and cannot be undone.
- Pixel-level video-prediction world models waste capacity on photorealism monitoring doesn't need; prior imagined-future monitors jointly train policy and dynamics, so they can't attach to an unchanged black-box policy.
- Single-view monitors lose the gripper-object contact region under occlusion, and direct probes on small labelled grasp sets generalise poorly.

## Method

- ==Multi-view latent world model==: shared ==ViT-Tiny== encodes three cameras, mean-pools to a 192-d latent, and an ==AdaLN-zero causal Transformer== predicts next latents under actions with ==SIGReg anti-collapse regularisation==.
- ==Frozen linear failure probe==: an ==l2-penalised class-balanced logistic regression== reads only the K-step predicted latent, separating task-agnostic dynamics learning from small-data outcome supervision.
- ==Online pre-contact gate==: scan the chunk for the commanded gripper open-to-close transition, anchor 15 frames before it, roll out 30 steps, and abort if predicted failure probability exceeds a per-task frozen threshold.

## Results

- Live **50**-rollout monitoring beats single-view LeWM and Direct-linear on all four tasks: cup AUC **0.992** vs **0.928**/**0.661**, with false-abort rate cut to **0.120** and balanced accuracy **0.940**.
- On larger offline pools ContactGuard scores **0.978-0.992** AUC, above matched current-latent probes (**0.840-0.923**), SAFE (**0.844-0.925**), and far above FAIL-Detect and RND.
- Shuffling action chunks collapses AUC to near chance (**0.483-0.535**); swapping a failed attempt's chunk under a fixed observation raises predicted failure probability by **+0.25** to **+0.65**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rolling actions through pretrained latent dynamics yields a more linearly separable outcome feature than the current latent, so a low-capacity probe suffices on small labelled sets.
> - A monitor can be a policy-decoupled verifier: consuming only observations and the emitted action chunk makes it attachable to any black-box chunked policy.
> - Naive proprioceptive fusion hurt in this small-data regime, dropping AUC on all four tasks by acting as a shortcut that displaces grasp-relevant visual dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "ContactGuard" addresses a fundamental challenge in robot manipulation, specifically in contact-rich tasks: the untimely detection of failures. Conventionally, many visuomotor policies only detect errors after the robot has physically committed to an interaction, leading to consequences such as nudging an object, missing a grasp, or disturbing the scene. This issue is particularly pronounced in setups relying on wrist-mounted cameras, where close-up views of the gripper and object near contact may still not prevent a poor approach from causing immediate failure. The paper positions its contribution within several interconnected research areas:

**Latent World Models for Robot Manipulation:** This field focuses on learning compact dynamics from pixel data for applications like model-based reinforcement learning (RL) and policy learning. Prior work, such as Dreamer, TD-MPC, and LeWorldModel, utilizes latent spaces for imagining trajectories, model-predictive control, or end-to-end learning of latent dynamics. ContactGuard adopts this latent prediction paradigm but specifically applies it to *failure monitoring* rather than planning or policy synthesis. The paper emphasizes that for monitoring, the model needs to preserve outcome-relevant information in its latent space, rather than generating photorealistic pixel-level predictions.

**Failure Prediction and Execution Monitoring:** Research in this area aims to enhance robot robustness by predicting and detecting manipulation failures. Existing methods include visual and vision-language detectors for success/failure, reward models, trajectory rarity monitoring, and runtime distribution shift detection (e.g., FIPER, FAIL-Detect, RND, SAFE). More recent work like SIRIUS and Sirius-Fleet explores joint policy and latent dynamics training for runtime monitoring. ContactGuard differentiates itself by focusing on *pre-contact* prediction and operating as a *policy-decoupled* monitor. It treats the underlying visuomotor policy as a black-box action proposer, using an independently trained world model to verify proposed action chunks without requiring modifications or joint training with the policy itself. This separation aims to improve practical deployability.

**Chunked Visuomotor Policies and Test-Time Verification:** Modern imitation learning approaches, such as ACT and Diffusion Policy, often generate actions in "chunks," which represent meaningful interaction events like gripper closure or object contact. Some methods use learned rewards or world models to score multiple candidate actions for test-time verification. ContactGuard operates within this context by evaluating a *single* proposed action chunk from an existing policy, without searching for alternative actions or modifying the action generator. It aims to provide a pre-contact veto signal for that specific chunk.

In summary, ContactGuard’s contribution is a novel synthesis of these areas, offering a system that leverages action-conditioned latent world models to perform pre-contact execution monitoring in a policy-agnostic, real-time manner, aimed at preventing failures before physical interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for ContactGuard stems from the observation that contact-rich manipulation tasks are highly susceptible to small errors near contact, which can rapidly lead to failure. Such failures are difficult to avoid for visuomotor policies operating under conditions of occlusion, clutter, deformable objects, or distribution shifts. Crucially, once physical contact occurs and the scene is disturbed (e.g., an object is nudged, slips, or is grasped incorrectly), correcting the error becomes significantly more challenging, if not impossible, within the current execution sequence.

This problem motivates the concept of **pre-contact execution monitoring**. Instead of merely detecting that a robot has already failed, the objective is to proactively assess whether an action chunk the robot is *about to execute* is likely to lead to failure. Chunked visuomotor policies naturally lend themselves to this approach, as an action chunk frequently encapsulates a coherent interaction event, such as gripper closure. If the robot can evaluate the potential consequences of such a chunk *before* committing to it, it gains the ability to abort a suboptimal interaction, thereby preventing undesirable scene changes.

The specific objectives for the ContactGuard system are detailed as follows:
- **Formulate Pre-Contact Execution Monitoring:** To formally define the problem of evaluating the likely consequence of an imminent contact action *before* the robot commits to its execution within the context of chunked visuomotor policies.
- **Develop an Action-Conditioned Latent World Model Monitor:** To introduce ContactGuard as a system that rolls out compact multi-view visual embeddings under a policy’s planned action chunk. This approach aims to predict short-horizon consequences in a latent visual space, avoiding the computational cost and ambiguity associated with pixel-level video prediction. The choice of a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) principle is motivated by the need for a representation that preserves outcome-relevant information without requiring photorealistic reconstruction.
- **Demonstrate Superiority of Predicted Future Latents:** To empirically demonstrate that the information contained within these predicted future latents is more informative for failure prediction than relying solely on current observations or raw planned actions. This objective is tested through comparisons with various ablations.
- **Enable Live Robot Pre-Contact Aborts:** To deploy ContactGuard on a real robot to enable pre-contact abort signals. A critical design goal is to achieve this without requiring candidate action search, pixel-level video prediction, or any modification to the underlying visuomotor policy, treating it as a black-box proposer. This promotes practical applicability and ease of integration into existing robot systems.

By addressing these objectives, ContactGuard seeks to enhance the reliability and robustness of robotic manipulation systems, particularly in scenarios demanding precise interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ContactGuard integrates an action-conditioned latent world model with a lightweight failure probe to perform pre-contact execution monitoring. The methodology separates the learning of general visual dynamics from task-specific outcome supervision.

#### 3.1. Problem Setting and Data

The system utilizes two distinct datasets:
- **World Model Dataset ($D_{wm}$):** Consists of unlabelled robot trajectories, including synchronized multi-view observations ($o_{1:V, t}$) from $V$ fixed cameras and corresponding joint-space actions ($a_t$). This dataset is used exclusively for training the latent world model, without any success/failure labels.
- **Probe Dataset ($D_{probe}$):** Comprises a smaller collection of labelled pre-contact grasp clips. Each clip includes a short multi-view observation history ($o_{1:V, t-C+1:t}$), the planned action chunk ($a_{t:t+K-1}$), and a binary label ($y \in \{0, 1\}$) indicating failure or success. This dataset is used to train the failure probe *after* the world model has been frozen.

#### 3.2. Multi-View Latent World Model

At its core, ContactGuard employs a JEPA-style latent world model, adapting the LeWorldModel architecture.
- **Visual Encoder ($E_\phi$):** A shared ViT-Tiny model independently encodes observations from each of the $V$ cameras. The resulting per-view embeddings are mean-pooled and then projected through a learned linear layer to produce a single, fixed-dimension latent embedding $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (D=192). This aggregation strategy allows the latent dimension to remain consistent regardless of the number of cameras and provides robustness to partial occlusions.
- **Action Embedder:** A compact network, consisting of a 1x1 temporal convolution and a two-layer MLP, maps each joint-space action $a_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ into the same $D$-dimensional latent space.
- **Causal Predictor ($P_\theta$):** This component is a Transformer-based network (four AdaLN-zero-conditioned blocks with 8 attention heads) designed to predict the next latent state. It takes a context of $C$ preceding latents ($z_{t-C+1:t}$) and the current action ($a_t$) as input to predict $\hat{z}_{t+1}$. A two-layer MLP forms the final prediction projection head.
- **Training:** The predictor ($P_\theta$) is trained with a teacher-forced one-step next-embedding regression objective, minimizing an MSE loss between predicted and ground-truth future latents. Additionally, a SIGReg anti-collapse regularizer is applied to encourage the latent distribution to match a standard Gaussian on random projections, ensuring stable learning of compact representations. Training uses a context window of $C=3$ frames and $L=5$ rollout targets per sample.
- **Inference:** During deployment, the trained predictor is rolled out autoregressively for $K$ steps, with predicted latents feeding back into the context for subsequent predictions, using the policy's planned action chunk.

#### 3.3. Linear Failure Probe on Predicted Latents

Once the visual encoder ($E_\phi$) and causal predictor ($P_\theta$) of the world model are trained, they are frozen. A lightweight linear failure probe is then trained.
- **Input Feature:** For each labelled pre-contact clip from $D_{probe}$, the frozen world model is used to roll out actions starting $k_{pre}$ frames before the expected gripper closure. The predicted latent $\hat{z}_{t+K}$ at $K$ steps into the future (chosen to correspond to a moment shortly after planned gripper closure) serves as the input feature for the probe. This feature is standardized using training-split statistics.
- **Classification:** A linear logistic regression probe learns to map the standardized $\hat{z}_{t+K}$ to a probability of failure $P(\text{fail} | \hat{z}_{t+K})$.
- **Probe Training:** The probe parameters (weights $w$ and bias $b$) are fit on the training portion of $D_{probe}$ using an $\ell_2$-penalized, class-balanced logistic loss. Class balancing compensates for potential success/failure imbalances in the dataset. The trained probe is then fixed for all subsequent evaluations.

#### 3.4. Online Pre-Contact Grasp Monitor

At deployment, ContactGuard operates as a policy-decoupled verifier alongside an existing chunked visuomotor policy (e.g., ACT), performing control at 30 Hz.
- **Trigger:** A task-specific trigger, such as the commanded gripper open-to-close transition, identifies an imminent contact event. The monitor activates when this planned closure is within $k_{pre}$ frames (e.g., 15 frames, or 0.5s) of the current time.
- **Anchor:** Prediction is anchored at this pre-contact time.
- **Rollout and Score:** The frozen world model's encoder processes the current multi-view observation history. The predictor then performs an autoregressive rollout for $K=30$ steps (reaching 0.5s post-closure) using the policy's planned action chunk. The frozen failure probe scores the resulting predicted future latent $\hat{z}_{t+K}$.
- **Abort Decision:** If the predicted probability of failure $P(\text{fail})$ exceeds a predefined threshold $\tau$ (selected on a validation split), the robot aborts execution before the gripper closes. Otherwise, the policy continues without interruption. This process runs within the chunk execution window, ensuring timely decisions.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Robot System:** A 14-DoF AgileX Piper dual-arm robot equipped with three synchronized RGB cameras.
- **Tasks:** Four real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks: pick-and-place with a cup, pick-and-place with a box, pencil grasping in a notebook, and towel folding.
- **Policy:** ACT serves as the underlying chunked visuomotor policy.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Metrics include Precision, Recall, False-Abort Rate (FAR), ROC AUC, and Balanced Accuracy. Live real-robot evaluations involve N=50 monitored rollouts per task. When an abort is triggered, the robot pauses with the gripper open for 5 seconds, then resumes the action chunk to record the counterfactual outcome.
- **Baselines:** Comparisons are made against a single-view LeWM, Direct-linear (direct classification from anchor latent and action chunk), Current latent (probe on current state), and established external failure detectors (FAIL-Detect, RND, SAFE).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ContactGuard focused on its ability to predict grasp outcomes in real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, its reliance on imagined future states and specific actions, and its real-time performance.

#### 4.1. Closed-Loop Grasp-Outcome Prediction

Evaluations on live robot rollouts demonstrated ContactGuard's performance in preventing failures:
- **Multi-view vs. Single-view:** ContactGuard, leveraging multi-view visual information, consistently achieved higher balanced accuracy and ROC AUC compared to the single-view LeWM baseline across all four tasks. For example, on the Cup task, ContactGuard reached an AUC of 0.992, significantly higher than LeWM's 0.928. This improvement was most pronounced in tasks where individual viewpoints are prone to occlusion, such as the Towel task.
- **Benefit of Latent Imagination:** The "Direct-linear" baseline, which attempts to predict failure directly from the anchor latent and planned action chunk without imagination, consistently performed worse than ContactGuard in AUC on all live tasks. This indicated that rolling actions through the pretrained latent dynamics produces a more discriminative representation for failure prediction. Similarly, the "Current latent" ablation, which uses the same encoder and probe but classifies the current latent instead of the predicted future latent, also showed lower AUC across tasks, further isolating the value of imagining future consequences.
- **Comparison to External Detectors:** In offline evaluations on larger grasp pools, ContactGuard achieved the highest ROC AUC across all four tasks when compared against external failure detectors such as SAFE, FAIL-Detect, and RND. This suggests that its action-conditioned latent imagination provides a more accurate signal for pre-contact failure risk in this context.
- **Task-Specific Abort Behavior:** While overall performance was strong, ContactGuard exhibited varied abort behaviors across tasks. For instance, the Cup task showed clean separation of success and failure scores, leading to high precision and recall. The Towel task had a higher false-abort rate despite catching most failures. The Pencil task showed higher precision but lower recall, indicating some missed failures.

#### 4.2. Offline Diagnostics: Information Source and Proprioceptive Input

Diagnostic experiments probed the sources of information ContactGuard utilizes:
- **Proprioceptive Input:** Counterintuitively, removing a 28-dimensional proprioceptive robot state (joint positions and efforts) from the single-view LeWM variant actually *improved* test AUC across all four tasks. The authors suggest that in their small-data regime, naive state fusion might act as a domain-specific shortcut, potentially displacing more relevant visual dynamics learning.
- **Action and Latent Information:**
 - Replacing the predicted future latent ($\hat{z}_{t+K}$) with the current anchor latent ($z_t$) consistently reduced AUC, confirming that the system benefits from forecasting consequences rather than merely assessing the current state.
 - Corrupting the proposed action chunk (by shuffling, zeroing, or using mean actions) before rollout severely degraded AUC to near-chance levels on all tasks. This conclusively demonstrated that ContactGuard's verdict is not based on generic motion cues or current visual risk alone, but specifically on the imagined consequence of the *proposed action*.
 - An additional "action-swap test" reinforced this finding: holding the initial observation fixed while replacing the planned action chunk with one from a failed attempt substantially increased the predicted failure probability.

#### 4.3. Runtime Analysis

The real-time feasibility of ContactGuard was confirmed through runtime measurements on an NVIDIA RTX 5090.
- The full encode-rollout-probe pass for a deployment horizon of K=30 steps (1.0 second forecast) took approximately 19.18 ms. This latency is well within the typical pre-closure slack, indicating that the monitor can provide timely decisions without bottlenecking robot control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ContactGuard presents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation by introducing a robust and practical system for **pre-contact execution monitoring**. The ability to predict and prevent failures *before* they occur, rather than merely detecting them after the scene has been disturbed, represents a crucial step towards more reliable and autonomous robot operation in complex, contact-rich environments.

**Key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:**
- **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness:** By acting as a predictive verifier that can veto likely failure-inducing actions, ContactGuard directly contributes to improving the overall reliability and robustness of visuomotor policies. This is particularly valuable in tasks requiring precise interactions, where small errors can lead to immediate failure.
- **Policy-Decoupled Design:** A major practical advantage is its design as a policy-decoupled monitor. It treats the underlying visuomotor policy as a black-box proposer, consuming only its output action chunk and current observations. This independence means ContactGuard can be readily integrated with existing, unmodified policies, avoiding the need for costly joint training or complex policy alterations. This feature significantly lowers the barrier to adoption in real-world robotic systems.
- **Efficient Learning and Operation:** The methodology leverages unlabelled robot trajectories for training the core latent world model, requiring only a smaller, labelled dataset for the lightweight failure probe. This addresses a common challenge in robotics concerning data acquisition. Furthermore, by predicting in latent space rather than generating pixel-level video, the system remains computationally efficient, enabling real-time operation within critical pre-contact windows.
- **Validation of Latent Imagination:** The empirical results demonstrate that action-conditioned latent imagination provides superior failure prediction signals compared to current-state monitoring or direct classification. This validates the utility of learned latent dynamics for high-stakes decision-making beyond traditional planning or representation learning.
- **New Paradigm for Safety:** ContactGuard introduces a valuable safety mechanism that can prevent costly or dangerous physical interactions, fostering safer and more predictable robot deployment.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **No Recovery Mechanism:** ContactGuard currently prevents failures by abstaining (aborting execution) but does not include an internal recovery module to complete the task after an abort. This requires an external system for re-planning or selecting new actions.
- **Focus on Imminent Contact:** The system is tailored for imminent contact events whose outcome is determined within the next action chunk. Extending it to longer-horizon skills or more complex sequences would necessitate hierarchical or repeated event-level monitoring.

**Future Work:**
The identified limitations suggest several avenues for future research:
- **Closed-Loop Recovery:** Developing external recovery modules or integrating with multi-sample policies (e.g., Diffusion, Flow Matching) to enable the robot to select a new action chunk or replan from the preserved scene after an abort.
- **Hierarchical Monitoring:** Extending the framework to monitor longer-horizon skills through hierarchical event-level prediction or sequential monitoring of multiple interaction phases.
- **General-Purpose Triggers:** Designing more generalized triggers for diverse contact events beyond simple gripper closure, reducing task-specific engineering.

Overall, ContactGuard represents a significant step towards enabling robots to proactively avoid errors, contributing to a future where autonomous manipulation is not only capable but also reliably safe and robust.
