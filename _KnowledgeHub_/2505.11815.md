# UniMoCo: Unified Modality Completion for Robust Multi-Modal Embeddings

> [!summary] Summary
> UniMoCo introduces a framework for robust multi-modal embeddings by integrating a lightweight modality-completion module within a Large Vision-Language Model backbone, specifically designed to handle diverse and incomplete modality combinations. The model achieved an overall average score of 63.2 on the Massive Multi-modal Embedding Benchmark (MMEB), demonstrating superior performance and consistent robustness across varying input modalities.

## Problem

- Existing multi-modal embedding models struggle with diverse and incomplete modality combinations in real-world scenarios, like text-only inputs or missing images.
- An "inherent bias" in current models causes performance degradation on modality combinations that are less represented in training data.
- Conventional dual-encoder architectures are not robust to fine-grained cross-modal reasoning or when one modality is absent during inference.

## Method

- It introduces UniMoCo, an architecture built on a ==Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) backbone==, incorporating a lightweight ==modality-completion module==.
- The modality-completion module generates ==pseudo visual embeddings== directly from text, leveraging a compact language model and an auxiliary vision encoder to ensure consistent representations when visual input is absent.
- A unique training strategy combines ==contrastive learning with an auxiliary loss==, which explicitly aligns embeddings from complete inputs and their modality-completed pseudo versions.

## Results

- UniMoCo achieved an overall average score of **63.2** on the MMEB, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by a margin of up to **7.8 points** in classification tasks.
- The model demonstrated robust and consistent performance across diverse and incomplete modality combinations, effectively mitigating the "modality combination bias" seen in traditional architectures.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the padding strategy and the additional vision encoder in the modality-completion module, along with the auxiliary loss, were crucial for the observed performance gains and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Traditional multi-modal embedding models exhibit a quantifiable "modality combination bias," leading to performance drops on less common input configurations.
> - Generating lightweight "pseudo visual embeddings" from text is an effective and efficient method to achieve modality completeness without the computational cost of full image generation.
> - Explicitly aligning embeddings from original and modality-completed inputs through an auxiliary loss is critical for developing robust and bias-mitigated multi-modal systems.
