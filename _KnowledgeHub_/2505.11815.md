# UniMoCo: Unified Modality Completion for Robust Multi-Modal Embeddings

> [!summary] Summary
> UniMoCo introduces a framework for robust multi-modal embeddings by integrating a lightweight modality-completion module within a Large Vision-Language Model backbone, specifically designed to handle diverse and incomplete modality combinations. The model achieved an overall average score of 63.2 on the Massive Multi-modal Embedding Benchmark (MMEB), demonstrating superior performance and consistent robustness across varying input modalities.

## Problem

- Existing multi-modal embedding models struggle with diverse and incomplete modality combinations in real-world scenarios, like text-only inputs or missing images.
- An "inherent bias" in current models causes performance degradation on modality combinations that are less represented in training data.
- Conventional dual-encoder architectures are not robust to fine-grained cross-modal reasoning or when one modality is absent during inference.

## Method

- It introduces UniMoCo, an architecture built on a ==Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) backbone==, incorporating a lightweight ==modality-completion module==.
- The modality-completion module generates ==pseudo visual embeddings== directly from text, leveraging a compact language model and an auxiliary vision encoder to ensure consistent representations when visual input is absent.
- A unique training strategy combines ==contrastive learning with an auxiliary loss==, which explicitly aligns embeddings from complete inputs and their modality-completed pseudo versions.

## Results

- UniMoCo achieved an overall average score of **63.2** on the MMEB, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by a margin of up to **7.8 points** in classification tasks.
- The model demonstrated robust and consistent performance across diverse and incomplete modality combinations, effectively mitigating the "modality combination bias" seen in traditional architectures.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the padding strategy and the additional vision encoder in the modality-completion module, along with the auxiliary loss, were crucial for the observed performance gains and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Traditional multi-modal embedding models exhibit a quantifiable "modality combination bias," leading to performance drops on less common input configurations.
> - Generating lightweight "pseudo visual embeddings" from text is an effective and efficient method to achieve modality completeness without the computational cost of full image generation.
> - Explicitly aligning embeddings from original and modality-completed inputs through an auxiliary loss is critical for developing robust and bias-mitigated multi-modal systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multi-modal machine learning, particularly concerning vision-language models (VLMs), has witnessed rapid advancements, with models like CLIP, BLIP, SigLIP, and ALIGN demonstrating impressive capabilities in aligning visual and textual information for tasks such as information retrieval, visual grounding, and classification. The emergence of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) has further propelled the understanding and reasoning abilities across modalities.

UniMoCo positions itself directly within this vibrant landscape, acknowledging the success of existing LVLMs as backbones for multi-modal representation learning, as exemplified by recent works like VLM2VEC. However, the authors identify a critical limitation in the current state-of-the-art: a lack of robustness when confronted with diverse and often incomplete modality combinations in real-world scenarios. Traditional dual-encoder architectures, while effective for aligning paired image-text data, often fall short in fine-grained cross-modal reasoning and struggle to maintain performance when one modality is absent or when unseen combinations appear during inference. This leads to an "inherent bias" towards the modality combinations most frequently seen during training, degrading performance in less common, but equally important, scenarios.

The paper differentiates itself from related work by offering an architectural innovation rather than solely focusing on training strategies or data augmentation. While previous approaches to handling missing modalities have included learning shared modality-invariant spaces (e.g., in CLIP, BLIP), generative synthesis of proxy modalities (e.g., using diffusion models), or expert-based and transformer-based methods, UniMoCo proposes a lightweight, integrated modality-completion module. This module directly addresses the challenge of creating consistent representations by generating *pseudo visual embeddings* from text, rather than computationally intensive full image generation, and integrates this process seamlessly into an LVLM backbone. This targeted approach to modality completeness and alignment within a unified embedding space represents a significant contribution to making multi-modal models more adaptable and robust for practical applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind UniMoCo stems from a crucial observation regarding the performance of existing vision-language models in real-world multi-modal embedding tasks. While current models have advanced considerably, they often face significant challenges when queries and targets involve diverse and incomplete modality combinations. For instance, a query might consist of both text and an image, while the target is only text, or vice versa, or both might be multi-modal. The authors highlight that the inherent imbalance of modality combinations in training data leads to a "modality combination bias," where models struggle to align all possible combinations within a unified embedding space, resulting in degraded performance at inference for underrepresented or unseen scenarios.

Given this limitation, the key objectives of the UniMoCo research are:
1. **Develop a novel vision-language model architecture (UniMoCo)** specifically designed to generate robust multi-modal embeddings capable of handling diverse and incomplete modality combinations.
2. **Introduce a modality-completion module** that can effectively generate visual features from textual inputs when the actual visual modality is absent, thereby ensuring "modality completeness" for both queries and targets within the embedding space.
3. **Formulate a specialized training strategy** that aligns embeddings from both original complete inputs and modality-completed (pseudo) inputs. This strategy aims to ensure consistency and coherence across the embedding space, regardless of the input's original modality completeness.
4. **Quantify and mitigate the inherent bias** in conventional multi-modal embedding approaches caused by imbalanced training data distributions, demonstrating UniMoCo's ability to maintain consistent performance across various modality combinations.
5. **Achieve superior performance** compared to existing state-of-the-art methods across a wide range of multi-modal embedding tasks (classification, retrieval, VQA, visual grounding) while demonstrating consistent robustness.

In essence, UniMoCo seeks to bridge the gap between idealized multi-modal model training on complete data and the messy reality of diverse, often incomplete, multi-modal inputs, thereby making VLMs more practical and reliable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniMoCo proposes a novel architecture and a tailored training strategy to address the challenges of diverse and incomplete modality combinations in multi-modal embedding tasks. The overarching goal is to project both queries and candidate targets into a shared high-dimensional embedding space, enabling robust similarity matching for downstream tasks.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The model takes textual inputs (including a task-defining instruction and specific content) and optional visual inputs. It encodes them into compact embeddings $E \in \mathbb{R}^d$. For a query $q$ and candidate targets $\{c_1, \ldots, c_n\}$, the model computes their embeddings $E_q = f(q)$ and $E_{c_i} = f(c_i)$, identifying the best match $c^*$ through similarity matching, typically temperature-scaled cosine similarity.

#### 3.2. UniMoCo Architecture

The architecture leverages a Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) as its backbone, which comprises a Large Language Model (LLM), a vision encoder, and a projector. The core innovation is the **modality-completion module**, which activates when the visual modality is absent in the input.

- **Modality-Completion Module:** Unlike approaches that generate full images using diffusion models (which are computationally heavy and can introduce biases from image generation tasks), UniMoCo employs a compact language model that directly converts text into **pseudo visual embeddings**.
 - **Text-to-Image (T2I) Model:** This component is a lightweight language model designed to transform textual information into representations that mimic visual features.
 - **Additional Vision Encoder:** A supplementary vision encoder is included to process these pseudo visual embeddings. This is crucial because embeddings from the T2I model might not perfectly align with those generated by the primary vision encoder from real images. This additional encoder helps bridge that consistency gap.
 - **Padding Strategy:** To ensure dimensional consistency, when textual input is processed by the modality-completion module, it is concatenated with padding tokens. This aligns the input length of the T2I model with the fixed number of visual tokens produced by the primary vision encoder from authentic images, thus creating pseudo visual embeddings of comparable length to real image embeddings.

- **Workflow:**
 - **Complete Multi-modal Inputs (Image + Text):** The modality-completion module is bypassed, and the LVLM functions conventionally.
 - **Text-Only Inputs:** The textual input is simultaneously processed by the LLM and the modality-completion module. The latter generates pseudo visual tokens, which then substitute for the missing image representations. This unified approach ensures that all inputs, regardless of their initial modality completeness, are represented consistently in the embedding space.

#### 3.3. UniMoCo Training Strategy

To optimize the model for robustness across modality combinations, UniMoCo employs a composite loss function: $L = L_1 + \alpha L_2$.

- **$L_1$ (Contrastive Learning Loss):** This is a standard InfoNCE loss, aiming to learn discriminative representations. For a batch of query-target pairs, it minimizes the distance between positive pairs and maximizes the separation from negative pairs in the embedding space.
- **$L_2$ (Auxiliary Loss):** This is the crucial component for modality alignment. It addresses the discrepancy between modality-complete and modality-missing inputs.
 - For any input (query $q$ or target $c_i$) that *originally contains both image and text*, a *pseudo counterpart* ($q'$ or $c_i'$) is constructed. This counterpart is created by removing the real image component and instead generating pseudo visual tokens through the modality-completion module based solely on the text.
 - The $L_2$ loss then calculates the cross-entropy between the embeddings of the original complete input ($E_q$ or $E_{c_i}$) and its modality-completed pseudo counterpart ($E_{q'}$ or $E_{c_i'}$). This explicitly aligns the pseudo visual embeddings with the real visual embeddings, forcing the modality-completion module to produce high-quality, consistent representations.
 - In cases where the input naturally lacks visual content, the pseudo counterpart becomes identical to the original input, and its contribution to $L_2$ is nullified, ensuring the loss only applies where meaningful alignment is required.
- **Hyperparameter $\alpha$:** This positive scalar controls the relative importance of the auxiliary loss ($L_2$) in the overall objective, balancing discriminative embedding learning with modality-invariant representation learning.

This two-pronged training approach ensures that UniMoCo not only learns powerful representations for matching but also maintains consistency across modalities, bridging the gap when visual information is absent.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of UniMoCo on the Massive Multi-modal Embedding Benchmark (MMEB) demonstrates its superior performance and robustness compared to existing methods.

- **Overall Performance:** UniMoCo consistently outperforms all baseline methods, including prominent VLMs like CLIP, OpenCLIP, BLIP2, SigLIP, UniIR, and VLM2VEC, both in non-fine-tuned and fine-tuned settings. The UniMoCo variant utilizing Qwen2-VL-7B as its backbone achieved the highest overall average score of 63.2 across 36 evaluation benchmarks. This included scores of 67.0 on in-distribution datasets and 58.4 on out-of-distribution datasets, highlighting strong generalization capabilities.
 - Significant gains were observed across all meta-tasks: classification (+7.8 points over the best fine-tuned baseline), VQA (+3.3), retrieval (+2.7), and grounding (+2.9).

- **Robustness Across Modality Combinations:** A key finding is UniMoCo's ability to maintain consistent performance across diverse modality combinations: (T+I, T), (T, T+I), and (T+I, T+I). While some baselines might perform comparably on the most dominant modality combination in training data (e.g., VLM2VEC on (T+I, T)), UniMoCo showed significant superiority on other combinations, demonstrating its unified alignment capabilities.

- **Mitigation of Modality Combination Bias:** A dedicated analysis (Figure 4) rigorously quantified the "modality combination bias" in traditional architectures.
 - **VLM2VEC (Traditional Approach):** Demonstrated strong performance when evaluated on tasks matching the dominant modality combination in its training set, but suffered significant performance deterioration on other, less represented combinations. This confirmed that conventional methods are highly susceptible to imbalances in training data.
 - **UniMoCo:** Showed remarkable robustness, maintaining consistently high performance across all evaluation benchmarks regardless of the dominant modality combination in the training data. This unequivocally validates UniMoCo's effectiveness in mitigating the critical issue of modality bias.

- **Ablation Study: Modality-Completion Module (Table 2):**
 - Simply using a T2I model without modifications for modality completion led to performance degradation, primarily due to length mismatch between generated pseudo visual embeddings (short, from text) and real image embeddings (long, from image patches).
 - The proposed **padding strategy** was crucial, significantly enhancing performance by aligning the T2I output length with real image token counts.
 - The **additional vision encoder** further contributed to performance gains, demonstrating its role in better aligning the characteristics of pseudo visual embeddings with real ones.
 - The **synergistic combination** of both padding and the additional encoder yielded the best results, especially for tasks heavily dependent on robust modality completion (e.g., (T+I, T+I) and (T, T+I)).

- **Ablation Study: T2I Model Scale (Table 3):** Larger T2I models (e.g., Qwen2-7B vs. Qwen2-0.5B/1.5B) consistently improved UniMoCo's embedding quality across all benchmarks. This aligns with scaling laws in language models, indicating that increased capacity in the T2I component leads to more accurate and high-fidelity pseudo visual embedding generation.

- **Ablation Study: Training Strategy ($\alpha$ parameter) (Table 4):**
 - Integrating the **auxiliary loss ($L_2$)** significantly boosted performance across all metrics, including downstream task performance, generalization to out-of-distribution data, and robustness to diverse modality combinations.
 - An optimal $\alpha$ value of 0.2 was identified. Increasing $\alpha$ beyond this point led to performance degradation, suggesting that an excessive weighting of the auxiliary loss could inadvertently shift the primary training objective away from learning effective multi-modal embeddings.
 - The most pronounced improvements from $L_2$ were observed in (T+I, T) and (T, T+I) datasets, directly confirming its role in bridging the gap when the image modality is missing. The benefits even extended to (T+I, T+I) configurations, likely due to improved alignment between different vision encoders.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniMoCo framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multi-modal machine learning, particularly for robust embedding tasks. Its contributions hold substantial significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Real-World Scenarios:** The most critical impact of UniMoCo is its ability to effectively handle diverse and incomplete modality combinations. Real-world data is often messy, with missing or partial information being common. By ensuring "modality completeness" through the generation of pseudo visual embeddings and a specialized alignment strategy, UniMoCo makes multi-modal systems far more robust and reliable in practical deployments where full modality availability cannot be guaranteed.

2. **Mitigation of Modality Combination Bias:** UniMoCo provides a concrete solution to the inherent bias observed in conventional approaches, where models tend to perform poorly on modality combinations underrepresented in training data. By quantifying this bias and demonstrating UniMoCo's consistent performance across varying data distributions, the paper advocates for fairer and more generalizable multi-modal AI systems. This is crucial for applications demanding consistent performance across all input types, preventing system failures in edge cases.

3. **Architectural Innovation for LVLMs:** The proposed modality-completion module, integrating a lightweight text-to-image model and an auxiliary vision encoder, is a novel architectural design. Unlike prior generative methods that produce full images, UniMoCo's direct generation of pseudo visual embeddings is computationally efficient and more precisely aligned with the goal of embedding consistency. This innovation offers a new paradigm for building robust LVLM backbones.

4. **Improved Generalization and Performance:** The consistent outperformance of UniMoCo across a wide range of multi-modal tasks (classification, VQA, retrieval, visual grounding) and across both in-distribution and out-of-distribution datasets underlines its superior generalization capabilities. This translates to more effective information retrieval systems, better visual question answering, and more accurate visual grounding in complex scenes.

5. **Implications for Future Multi-modal AI:** UniMoCo highlights the necessity of explicitly addressing modality completeness and alignment within the model architecture and training strategy. This work could inspire future research to integrate similar robustness-enhancing modules into next-generation multi-modal models, moving beyond simple data augmentation or pre-training on massive paired datasets.

**Potential Impact in Specific Domains:**
- **Information Retrieval:** Users can submit queries with varying modality compositions (e.g., text-only product descriptions, an image with a short text caption) and expect consistent and accurate retrieval results.
- **Visual-Language Assistants:** Voice or text-based queries lacking visual context could be "completed" internally, allowing for more robust interaction with visual data.
- **Accessibility:** Models could better process inputs from users with sensory limitations, for example, inferring visual context from detailed text descriptions.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge that their focus has been on structural innovations, leaving avenues for future work such as exploring data enhancement techniques or advanced contrastive learning optimizations in conjunction with UniMoCo. Furthermore, testing on an even broader range of diverse datasets would further solidify its generalizability. These suggestions provide a clear roadmap for extending UniMoCo's capabilities and impact.

In conclusion, UniMoCo offers a robust and innovative solution to a critical problem in multi-modal AI, paving the way for more reliable, bias-mitigated, and adaptable vision-language models in real-world applications.
