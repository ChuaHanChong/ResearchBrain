# From Foresight to Forethought: VLM-In-the-Loop Policy Steering via Latent Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called FOREWARN enables Vision Language Models (VLMs) to steer multi-modal generative robot policies by translating predicted low-level robot behaviors from a latent world model into natural language narrations. This approach, developed by researchers at Carnegie Mellon University and UC Berkeley, significantly enhances task success rates and generalization to novel task descriptions in complex robotic manipulation by bridging the VLM's high-level understanding with fine-grained robot actions.

## Problem

- Generative imitation-based robot policies frequently exhibit unexpected failures or context-inappropriate behaviors during runtime in dynamic, open-world environments.
- Traditional policy steering methods often rely on difficult-to-obtain physics models or hand-crafted reward functions, and retraining policies for failures is costly and time-consuming.
- Off-the-shelf Vision Language Models (VLMs), despite strong open-world reasoning, struggle to understand and verify fine-grained low-level robot actions due to a significant representational gap.

## Method

- The **FOREWARN** (Filtering Options via REpresenting World-model Action Rollouts as Narration) framework separates policy steering into two stages: foresight (predicting outcomes) and forethought (evaluating outcomes).
- It uses a pretrained ==latent world model== (**Dreamerv3**) to efficiently predict sequences of future latent states from candidate low-level robot action plans.
- A Vision Language Model (**Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct**) is fine-tuned to ==narrate detailed robot behaviors== from these predicted latent states, and then performs a ==multiple-choice question answering== task to select the best action plan based on user intent and generated narrations.

## Results

- FOREWARN significantly outperformed baselines in generating accurate behavior narrations, showing over **50%** improvement in GT Accuracy compared to direct VLM interpretation of raw actions.
- The framework achieved substantially higher task success rates (e.g., 0.30 to 0.80 for the Cup task) and maintained high performance with minimal degradation when exposed to novel task descriptions, unlike other methods that suffered severe drops.
- FOREWARN demonstrated robustness to environmental changes (object appearances, backgrounds) and achieved a faster inference time of 3.7 seconds compared to 22.0 seconds for VLM-Act, due to efficient latent-space communication.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling action prediction (foresight) from outcome evaluation (forethought) allows specialized models to handle complex robot policy steering.
> - Aligning Vision Language Model (VLM) reasoning with latent world model predictions through natural language narration effectively bridges the representational gap between high-level VLMs and low-level robot actions.
> - Reasoning over fine-grained textual narrations of predicted outcomes enables VLMs to generalize robustly to novel task descriptions and environmental variations, which is challenging for direct latent-to-category mapping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical challenge in modern robotics: the deployment of **generative imitation-based policies** in real-world settings. While these policies have shown immense promise in learning complex, multimodal behaviors from demonstrations, they often exhibit "nuanced and unexpected failures" at runtime, ranging from complete task failures to inappropriate behaviors that deviate from user intent. This problem is particularly acute in dynamic, open-world environments where variations are difficult to anticipate during training.

The paper positions its solution within the paradigm of **runtime policy steering**. This approach aims to mitigate failures not by retraining the base policy (which is often expensive and time-consuming) but by using an "external verifier" to select the most appropriate low-level actions from those proposed by an imperfect generative policy. This falls under the broader umbrella of **safety and robustness in AI systems**, a burgeoning field crucial for the safe deployment of autonomous agents.

A central theme in contemporary AI research, particularly in robotics, is the integration of **Vision Language Models (VLMs)**. VLMs offer powerful open-world reasoning capabilities and commonsense understanding, making them attractive candidates for intelligent verification. However, a significant "representational gap" exists: off-the-shelf VLMs are trained on high-level text and images, struggling to understand the fine-grained consequences of low-level robot actions. This paper directly tackles this gap, which is a major hurdle for leveraging foundation models in embodied AI.

The work also draws heavily from the field of **world models** in reinforcement learning. World models are used for "foresight"—predicting future states and outcomes given actions—a critical component for model-predictive control. By leveraging a latent world model, the authors align with a growing trend towards learning predictive models of the environment to enable more intelligent and proactive decision-making in robotics.

Furthermore, the paper explicitly frames its approach within the **"learning to search" (L2S) framework**. L2S principles suggest that verifying a good plan is often easier than generating one from scratch. By decoupling prediction from verification, FOREWARN aligns with this principle, contributing to the understanding of how learned verifiers can guide policy execution efficiently. This contrasts with purely end-to-end VLM-based approaches that attempt to solve both prediction and verification simultaneously, often leading to sample inefficiency and poor generalization.

Finally, in the context of **failure detection and prediction**, this work contributes to the "predictive category" of methods. Unlike post-hoc analysis or runtime monitoring that activate *after* a failure occurs, FOREWARN anticipates future outcomes and steers the policy *preemptively*, a critical capability for reliable robotic operation. This comprehensive integration of cutting-edge techniques from various AI subfields positions FOREWARN as a significant step towards more autonomous, robust, and interpretable robotic systems capable of operating reliably in complex, human-centric environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework that enables **Vision Language Models (VLMs) to effectively steer multi-modal generative robot policies at runtime**, thereby enhancing task success and alignment with user intent in open-world settings.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges in deploying advanced robot policies:

- **Runtime Failures of Generative Policies:** Even powerful generative imitation-based policies, trained on diverse demonstrations, frequently exhibit unexpected failures during deployment. These failures can range from outright task failures (e.g., dropping a cup) to subtle, context-inappropriate behaviors (e.g., placing a gripper inside a cup of water when serving a guest).
- **Cost of Retraining:** A common mitigation strategy for such failures is to collect more demonstration data and retrain the policy. However, this is often prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and not always indicative of a fundamental policy limitation (the "right" behavior might already be in the policy's distribution but not reliably selected).
- **Limitations of Traditional Policy Steering:** Runtime policy steering offers an attractive alternative by using an external verifier to select optimal action plans. However, traditional verifiers often rely on physics-based models (hard to obtain in open worlds) or hand-crafted reward functions (difficult to specify for nuanced human preferences).
- **Representational Gap for VLMs:** While VLMs possess strong open-world reasoning and commonsense capabilities, making them ideal candidates for verification, they are not inherently trained to understand low-level robot actions (e.g., end-effector poses, gripper states). Directly asking an off-the-shelf VLM to verify raw action plans is ineffective, as these actions are fundamentally different from the text and images VLMs are trained on.

To address these motivations, the core idea behind FOREWARN is to **decouple the VLM's burden of predicting action outcomes (foresight) from evaluating them (forethought)**. This modular approach allows each component to leverage its specific strengths: a latent world model handles the complex foresight task by imagining future robot states given low-level actions, while the VLM focuses on the forethought task—reasoning about these imagined outcomes in a human-interpretable way. The crucial innovation lies in **aligning the VLM to reason directly about the predicted latent states** by translating them into natural language narrations, thereby bridging the representational gap and unlocking the VLM's potential as a powerful, open-vocabulary verifier for real-time policy steering.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **FOREWARN (Filtering Options via REpresenting World-model Action Rollouts as Narration)**, tackles policy steering by explicitly separating the complex problem into two main stages: foresight (predicting outcomes) and forethought (evaluating outcomes).

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The authors formalize policy steering as a stochastic model-predictive control problem (Eq. 1). Given a current observation ($o_t$) and $K$ candidate action plans ($a_t^i$) from a base policy ($\pi$), the goal is to select the plan ($a_t^\star$) that maximizes an expected reward $R(o_t; \ell)$, where $\ell$ is a language-based task description. This formulation highlights the need for both predicting future observations ($P(\cdot|o_t, a_t)$) and evaluating them against user intent ($R(\cdot; \ell)$).

FOREWARN's key insight is to approximate this by:
- Using a **probabilistic latent dynamics model** ($f_\phi$) to predict future latent states ($z_t$) instead of raw observations.
- Leveraging a **fine-tuned VLM** ($R_{\text{VLM}}^\psi$) to evaluate these predicted latent states, effectively serving as an implicit reward function conditioned on the task description ($\ell$). This leads to the objective in Eq. 2.

**B. Foresight: Predicting Outcomes via Latent World Models:**
1. **World Model Choice:** The authors adopt **Dreamerv3**, a state-of-the-art recurrent world model, for predicting future action outcomes.
2. **Architecture:** The world model, $W_\phi = (E_\phi, f_\phi, D_\phi)$, consists of:
 - An **encoder** ($E_\phi: O \rightarrow Z$) that maps high-dimensional observations (images and proprioceptive states) into a lower-dimensional latent space ($Z$).
 - A **recurrent dynamics model** ($f_\phi$) that predicts future latent states given current latent states and actions.
 - A **decoder** ($D_\phi$) that can reconstruct observations from latent states.
3. **Pretraining:** The world model is pretrained on an offline dataset ($D_{WM}$) consisting of robot observation-action trajectories. Crucially, this dataset includes both successful and failed rollouts from the base policy, along with additional demonstrations. This ensures the world model can accurately predict the outcomes of both desired and undesired actions. The training loss encourages informative latent states for image decoding and accurate next-state prediction.
4. **Deployment:** After training, the world model ($E_\phi$ and $f_\phi$) is frozen. For a given action plan, it efficiently rolls out and predicts a sequence of future latent states.

**C. Forethought: Latent-Text Alignment for Outcome Reasoning and Policy Steering:**
This is the core innovative step that bridges the VLM's capabilities with the world model's predictions.

1. **VLM Backbone:** The **Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct** model is used as the VLM backbone.
2. **Architectural Adaptation:** The key modification involves replacing Llama's original Vision Transformer (ViT) with the world model's encoder ($E_\phi$) and dynamics model ($f_\phi$). A new linear projection layer is introduced to map the world model's latent states directly into the VLM's text embedding space, essentially treating the latent states as "latent tokens" that the VLM can process alongside text.
3. **VLM Finetuning (Latent-Text Alignment):**
 - A Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset is constructed from the same offline data used for the world model.
 - For each trajectory, the world model predicts a sequence of latent states. These latent states are downsampled to reduce redundancy (e.g., 64 steps to 16 latent states).
 - The VLM is fine-tuned (using LoRA for efficiency) to narrate the real-world behavior of the robot (e.g., "the robot grasps the cup by the handle") given the sequence of predicted latent states and a general task prompt. This focuses the VLM on generating *fine-grained motion details* and potential failure modes, not just high-level semantics.
4. **VLM-In-the-Loop Policy Steering (Runtime):**
 - **Action Plan Generation:** At runtime, the base generative policy ($\pi$) samples a large number of candidate action plans (e.g., 100). These are then aggregated into a smaller set of distinct modes (e.g., 6 modes using Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS)).
 - **Latent Rollout:** For each of these 6 candidate action plans, the frozen world model ($f_\phi$) predicts the most likely sequence of future latent states.
 - **Behavior Narration:** The fine-tuned VLM (using the modified architecture) takes each sequence of predicted latent states as input and generates a natural language *behavior narration* describing the outcome of that action plan.
 - **Policy Steering (MCQA):** Finally, the *same fine-tuned VLM* is queried again. It receives the user's overall task description ($\ell$) and the 6 generated behavior narrations (one for each candidate action plan). The VLM then acts as a verifier, performing a **multiple-choice question answering (MCQA)** task to select the best action plan that aligns with the task goals and user preferences, providing an explanation for its choice.
 - The robot then executes the action plan selected by the VLM.

This modular, "divide-and-conquer" approach allows FOREWARN to leverage the strengths of specialized models, with the latent-text alignment being the critical enabler for the VLM's effective reasoning about low-level robot behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors evaluate FOREWARN across three real-world robotic manipulation tasks: **Cup** (grasping a cup by handle/rim), **Bag** (picking a deformable bag, avoiding crushing), and **Fork-to-Bowl Transfer** (multi-phase, longer horizon task). The base generative policy is a Diffusion Policy trained on 100 teleoperated demonstrations per task.

**A. From Action Rollouts to Behavior Narration (Table I, Fig. 3):**
This experiment evaluates the ability of the VLM to accurately describe predicted robot behaviors.
- **Key finding:** FOREWARN significantly outperforms all baselines (VLM-Act, VLM-Img, VLM-Img-Oracle) in generating accurate behavior narrations (measured by GT Accuracy and LLM Score).
 - **Value of Explicit Outcome Prediction:** VLM-Act, which directly tries to interpret raw actions without a world model, performs poorly (<50% GT Accuracy). FOREWARN, by using an explicit world model for outcome prediction, shows a >50% improvement, demonstrating that decoupling prediction from narration is crucial.
 - **Value of VLM Fine-Tuning:** VLM-Img and VLM-Img-Oracle (using GPT-4o on decoded/ground-truth images) also struggle to capture fine-grained motion details, falling behind FOREWARN by at least 30%. This highlights that even advanced off-the-shelf VLMs require fine-tuning for nuanced robot behavior understanding, and directly feeding them images is insufficient.
- **Qualitative examples (Fig. 3):** Visually confirm that FOREWARN produces consistent and accurate narrations, capturing subtle contact details, whereas baselines often hallucinate or misinterpret.

**B. Policy Steering for Open-World Alignment (Table II, Fig. 4):**
This experiment evaluates the closed-loop policy steering performance, focusing on robustness to novel task descriptions.
- **Performance with Training Task Descriptions:** FOREWARN significantly improves success rates over the base policy (e.g., from 0.30 to 0.80 for Cup, 0.20 to 0.70 for Bag and Fork). It performs comparably to Classifier-Dyn-Latent and better than VLM-DynLat-Category and VLM-Act.
- **Robustness to Novel Task Descriptions (Generalization):** This is where FOREWARN truly shines. When task descriptions are altered (e.g., "handle covered with oil" for Cup, "maximize stability" for Bag, "maximizing contact region" for Fork), FOREWARN maintains high success rates (e.g., 0.80 for Cup, 0.70 for Bag), showing minimal degradation. In stark contrast, VLM-DynLat-Category and Classifier-Dyn-Latent, which directly map latent states to success/failure categories, suffer severe performance drops (e.g., Classifier-Dyn-Latent drops to 0.00 for Cup and 0.10 for Bag). This demonstrates the superior generalization capability of FOREWARN, attributing it to the VLM's ability to reason over interpretable textual narrations rather than just latent features. VLM-Act consistently performs poorly.
- **Qualitative Examples (Fig. 4, Appendix B2):** Illustrate FOREWARN's step-by-step reasoning, showing how it generates narrations for different modes and then selects the optimal one based on the given task context.
- **Generalization to Environmental Changes (Fig. 5):** The system shows robustness to variations in object appearances (colors, sizes) and background changes, maintaining performance with small drops.
- **Policy Steering Speed (Table III):** FOREWARN achieves a total inference time of 3.7 seconds, significantly faster than VLM-Act (22.0 seconds), primarily due to efficient latent-space communication between the world model and VLM, avoiding costly image encoding/decoding.

**C. Supplementary Experiments & Analyses (Appendix B2):**
- **Failure Monitoring:** FOREWARN functions effectively as a preemptive failure monitor, consistently achieving high accuracy (above 75%) and balanced True Positive/Negative Rates across both training and novel task descriptions, unlike baselines that show sharp performance degradation with unseen tasks. This further supports the strength of its modular design and reasoning capabilities.
- **Component-level Analysis (Table X):** Reinforces that FOREWARN's overall strong performance comes from high accuracy in *all* its components (world model prediction, behavior narration, and VLM reasoning), while other methods bottleneck in specific stages (e.g., VLM-Act's poor narration, or Classifier-Dyn-Latent's/VLM-DynLat-Binary's inability to generalize reasoning to novel tasks).

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that FOREWARN's modular architecture, specifically the latent-text alignment and VLM-in-the-loop reasoning over behavior narrations, enables robust, generalizable, and interpretable policy steering in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "From Foresight to Forethought: VLM-In-the-Loop Policy Steering via Latent Alignment" carries significant implications for the field of robotics and embodied AI, addressing several key challenges in deploying intelligent systems in real-world scenarios.

1. **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness of Robot Policies:** The most immediate impact is on improving the success rates of generative robot policies. By proactively steering the robot's behavior away from potential failures or context-inappropriate actions, FOREWARN significantly boosts the reliability of robotic systems. This is critical for moving robots out of controlled lab environments and into more dynamic, unconstrained settings.

2. **Unlocking VLMs for Embodied Control:** The paper provides a crucial blueprint for effectively integrating Vision Language Models (VLMs) into low-level robot control. By devising a clever latent-text alignment strategy, it overcomes the significant "representational gap" that previously limited VLMs to high-level semantic tasks. This demonstrates how VLMs can be made to understand and reason about fine-grained sensori-motor data, paving the way for more intelligent, adaptable, and human-aware robotic behaviors driven by large foundation models.

3. **Generalization to Novel Task Contexts:** A major strength of FOREWARN is its demonstrated ability to generalize to novel task descriptions and environmental variations. This is a holy grail in robotics, as hand-crafting rules or collecting exhaustive data for every possible scenario is infeasible. By allowing the VLM to reason abstractly over narrated behaviors and user intent, the system becomes significantly more flexible and robust to changes not explicitly seen during training, making it highly valuable for real-world applications.

4. **Interpretable and Explainable AI in Robotics:** The generation of natural language behavior narrations provides human-understandable explanations for the robot's planned actions and the VLM's selection process. This interpretability is crucial for building trust between humans and robots, enabling easier debugging, and allowing non-expert users to understand why a robot is behaving in a particular way. This aligns with a growing demand for explainable AI in safety-critical domains.

5. **Efficient Policy Refinement:** FOREWARN offers a compelling alternative to costly and time-consuming policy retraining or human intervention. By performing runtime steering, it leverages the inherent multi-modality of generative policies without needing additional demonstrations or expert re-annotations for every failure mode, thus accelerating the deployment pipeline for complex robot skills.

6. **Modular and Scalable Architecture:** The modular "divide-and-conquer" approach, separating foresight (world model) from forethought (VLM), offers a robust and potentially scalable framework. As world models become more accurate and VLMs become more capable, FOREWARN's performance can progressively improve by simply upgrading its underlying components, rather than requiring an entirely new end-to-end model. The efficiency gains observed (faster inference than end-to-end VLM approaches) further support its practicality.

**Potential Future Directions and Limitations (as noted by authors):** While highly impactful, the authors acknowledge limitations, including the assumption of a sufficiently competent base policy (i.e., the desired behavior must be generatable), the current world model's prediction accuracy with limited data, and the computational overhead of large autoregressive models. Future work can explore detecting when the base policy is entirely insufficient, improving world model robustness with richer visual features, and optimizing inference speed. Nevertheless, FOREWARN represents a significant step towards more autonomous, robust, and intuitive robot control systems, particularly for manipulation tasks in dynamic and open-ended environments.
