# A Rapid Instrument Exchange System for Humanoid Robots in Minimally Invasive Surgery

> [!summary] Summary
> A rapid instrument exchange system for humanoid robots performing minimally invasive surgery was developed, leveraging immersive teleoperation with first-person view and a single-axis compliant docking mechanism. The system demonstrated an average full exchange cycle time of 48 seconds for expert operators and validated the technical feasibility and robustness of humanoid robots for surgical instrument management.

## Problem

- Existing surgical robot platforms are costly, have large footprints, and require specialized operating room layouts, limiting widespread adoption.
- Humanoid robots, with dual-arm configurations, inherently lack the ability to hold multiple surgical instruments simultaneously, hindering their utility in complex multi-step procedures.
- Current rapid instrument exchange solutions are tailored for specialized industrial or medical robot architectures and are challenging to adapt to biomimetic humanoid platforms with strict spatial constraints.

## Method

- An ==immersive teleoperated== instrument rapid exchange system was proposed, using a master-slave architecture with a **Unitree G1** humanoid robot as the slave and an HMD/spatial controllers for the operator.
- A low-latency instrument exchange mechanism was designed, featuring a robot-side ==active component== and an instrument-side ==passive component==, with an ==auto-locking terminal== and a repository-assisted unlocking system.
- The system integrates real-time ==first-person view (FPV)== through an HMD with spatial pose mapping from controllers, enabling intuitive and precise teleoperation for instrument docking and release.

## Results

- Expert operators achieved a full instrument exchange cycle (detachment and attachment) in an average of **48 seconds**, while novice operators completed the same task in **98 seconds**.
- The system demonstrated high operational success rates for localized instrument docking and detachment, with failures primarily attributed to instrument pose misalignment during insertion.
- Novice subjects exhibited a rapid learning curve for localized instrument manipulation tasks, but the time taken for larger-scale spatial positioning of the robot did not show similar convergence, indicating limitations in current teleoperation for complex, long-distance movements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single-axis compliant docking mechanism combined with an environmental constraint release system can enable stable and precise instrument exchange for humanoid robots despite their high biomimetics and spatial limitations.
> - Immersive teleoperation with high-fidelity FPV significantly reduces the cognitive load and complexity associated with fine-tuned pose alignment during surgical instrument docking.
> - While localized instrument attachment and detachment show rapid learning curves, the macroscopic spatial movements of the humanoid robot during instrument selection or repositioning pose stability challenges for teleoperation, suggesting a need for more advanced automation in surrounding facilities.
