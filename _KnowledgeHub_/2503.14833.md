# Curiosity-Diffuser: Curiosity Guide Diffusion Models for Reliability

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northwestern Polytechnical University developed Curiosity-Diffuser, a framework guiding conditional diffusion models with Random Network Distillation (RND) to generate reliable robotic behaviors that align with training data. This approach achieved an average task score of 77.8 in D4RL MuJoCo tasks and improved success rates in AntMaze environments up to 58.0% for 'Antmaze-Large-Play', while introducing the K-Sim metric to quantify behavior similarity.

## Problem

- Robotic imitation learning systems frequently exhibit limited reliability and "hallucinations" when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) states, posing safety concerns.
- State-of-the-art imitation learning methods often achieve success rates below 100%, indicating instability that hinders robust real-world deployment.
- There is a need for efficient quantitative metrics to evaluate policy reliability by measuring the similarity of generated behaviors to training data.

## Method

- Introduces **Curiosity-Diffuser**, which integrates a ==Random Network Distillation (RND)== module to assess the familiarity or novelty of generated trajectories relative to training data.
- Utilizes RND's prediction error as a direct ==curiosity guidance signal== for ==conditional diffusion models==, steering the policy away from OOD behaviors during inference.
- Combines RND-based reliability guidance with traditional ==reward-based guidance== to balance task completion with adherence to familiar, safe behaviors.

## Results

- Curiosity-Diffuser achieved an average task score of **77.8** across D4RL MuJoCo datasets (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d), outperforming all compared baselines.
- Improved success rates in sparse-reward AntMaze tasks, increasing from **22.0%** to **38.0%** for "Antmaze-Medium-Play" and **38.0%** to **58.0%** for "Antmaze-Large-Play" compared to the Diffuser baseline.
- Yielded higher K-Sim scores (e.g., **0.949** vs. **0.939** in "Antmaze-Medium-Play"), quantitatively confirming that generated behaviors are more similar to training data and thus more reliable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Curiosity, typically used for exploration in reinforcement learning, can be repurposed to promote reliability in imitation learning by guiding generative models towards in-distribution behaviors.
> - Integrating RND-based familiarity assessment as a guidance signal in diffusion models effectively mitigates policy "hallucinations" and overgeneralization to OOD states.
> - Achieving optimal performance requires a balanced combination of reward-based guidance and RND-based reliability guidance, as excessive reliance on either can be detrimental.
