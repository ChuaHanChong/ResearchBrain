# Curiosity-Diffuser: Curiosity Guide Diffusion Models for Reliability

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northwestern Polytechnical University developed Curiosity-Diffuser, a framework guiding conditional diffusion models with Random Network Distillation (RND) to generate reliable robotic behaviors that align with training data. This approach achieved an average task score of 77.8 in D4RL MuJoCo tasks and improved success rates in AntMaze environments up to 58.0% for 'Antmaze-Large-Play', while introducing the K-Sim metric to quantify behavior similarity.

## Problem

- Robotic imitation learning systems frequently exhibit limited reliability and "hallucinations" when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) states, posing safety concerns.
- State-of-the-art imitation learning methods often achieve success rates below 100%, indicating instability that hinders robust real-world deployment.
- There is a need for efficient quantitative metrics to evaluate policy reliability by measuring the similarity of generated behaviors to training data.

## Method

- Introduces **Curiosity-Diffuser**, which integrates a ==Random Network Distillation (RND)== module to assess the familiarity or novelty of generated trajectories relative to training data.
- Utilizes RND's prediction error as a direct ==curiosity guidance signal== for ==conditional diffusion models==, steering the policy away from OOD behaviors during inference.
- Combines RND-based reliability guidance with traditional ==reward-based guidance== to balance task completion with adherence to familiar, safe behaviors.

## Results

- Curiosity-Diffuser achieved an average task score of **77.8** across D4RL MuJoCo datasets (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d), outperforming all compared baselines.
- Improved success rates in sparse-reward AntMaze tasks, increasing from **22.0%** to **38.0%** for "Antmaze-Medium-Play" and **38.0%** to **58.0%** for "Antmaze-Large-Play" compared to the Diffuser baseline.
- Yielded higher K-Sim scores (e.g., **0.949** vs. **0.939** in "Antmaze-Medium-Play"), quantitatively confirming that generated behaviors are more similar to training data and thus more reliable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Curiosity, typically used for exploration in reinforcement learning, can be repurposed to promote reliability in imitation learning by guiding generative models towards in-distribution behaviors.
> - Integrating RND-based familiarity assessment as a guidance signal in diffusion models effectively mitigates policy "hallucinations" and overgeneralization to OOD states.
> - Achieving optimal performance requires a balanced combination of reward-based guidance and RND-based reliability guidance, as excessive reliance on either can be detrimental.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the field of robotic imitation learning, which has seen substantial advancements due to deep neural networks and extensive demonstration datasets. Modern approaches, including transformer-based methods like ACT, diffusion policies, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, have expanded the capabilities of robots across diverse tasks and architectures. These developments have enabled various real-world applications, such as quadruped robots performing manipulation tasks, underwater robots for debris collection, and humanoid robots executing complex movements.

Despite these advancements, a significant challenge in imitation learning systems is their limited reliability, particularly in complex or multi-task scenarios. Empirical observations indicate performance gaps, with systems like ACT and OpenVLA achieving success rates below 100%, and multi-task 3D Diffusion Policy showing success rates exceeding 50% in only a fraction of its tasks. This instability is exacerbated by the tendency of neural network models to "hallucinate" when encountering unfamiliar states, leading to inaccurate behaviors that pose safety concerns in real-world robotic deployments. This issue parallels observations of factual inconsistency in large language models.

Current efforts to address reliability include scaling up datasets to mitigate long-tail distribution problems (e.g., RT-X, RDT) and incorporating reinforcement learning fine-tuning to introduce optimal strategy preferences (e.g., HIL_SERL). Another relevant area is curiosity-driven methods in reinforcement learning, which typically promote exploration by rewarding novelty. This paper, however, adapts the concept of curiosity in an inverse manner for imitation learning: instead of encouraging novelty, it aims to reduce it during decision-making to ensure generated behaviors remain close to the training data, thereby preventing errors and "hallucinations" during generalization. This repurposes curiosity as a mechanism for evaluating the reliability of an imitation learning model's output.

The work builds upon the foundation of guided diffusion models, which have been widely applied in generative tasks, including robot decision-making. Prior research, such as the Diffuser method, has utilized classifier guidance to steer diffusion models based on reward preferences from offline reinforcement learning. Other methods, like Decision Diffuser, have expanded guidance types to incorporate trajectory constraints. The proposed Curiosity-Diffuser specifically relates to ReDiffuser, which also employs Random Network Distillation (RND) for reliability estimation. However, a key distinction is that ReDiffuser uses RND for selecting among multiple sampled trajectories, whereas Curiosity-Diffuser integrates RND-based reliability as a direct guidance signal during the diffusion model's generation process, aiming to produce reliable trajectories more efficiently.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a fundamental bottleneck in robotic intelligence: the inherent instability of neural network models. Unlike traditional control models, neural networks often lack a well-defined convergence domain and stability guarantees. In the context of imitation learning, this instability manifests as "hallucinations," where policies trained on demonstration data generate inaccurate or unexpected behaviors when presented with novel or out-of-distribution (OOD) states. Such unreliable behavior poses significant safety risks and acts as a critical barrier to the widespread application of robotic intelligence in physical environments.

Specifically, the authors observe that existing state-of-the-art imitation learning methods, despite their advancements, frequently exhibit limited reliability. For instance, frameworks like ACT and OpenVLA, even with extensive training data, often achieve success rates that are below those required for robust real-world deployment. This reliability deficit is attributed to the models' tendency to overgeneralize or infer in unfamiliar state spaces, leading to deviations from learned, safe behaviors.

Against this background, the key objectives of the Curiosity-Diffuser work are:

1. **Enhance Policy Reliability:** To guide conditional diffusion models to generate trajectories that exhibit lower "curiosity." This implies that the generated behaviors should align more closely with the distribution of the training data, thereby reducing the likelihood of "hallucinations" and improving the overall reliability of the policy.
2. **Integrate Curiosity for Guidance:** To implement a mechanism, specifically utilizing a Random Network Distillation (RND) curiosity module, to quantitatively assess the alignment of a model's proposed behavior with its training data. This assessment is then used as a guiding signal during the inference process of a conditional diffusion model.
3. **Minimize Overgeneralization:** By minimizing the "curiosity" score through classifier guidance diffusion, the aim is to restrict the model's output during inference to remain within the observed data distribution, thereby actively reducing overgeneralization to OOD states.
4. **Develop an Efficient Reliability Metric:** To propose and implement a computationally efficient metric, termed K-Sim, for evaluating the reliability of policy outputs. This metric measures the similarity between generated behaviors and the training dataset, facilitating quantitative research into reliability learning.
5. **Validate Effectiveness:** To empirically demonstrate through simulation that the proposed Curiosity-Diffuser method not only improves task performance but also generates behaviors that are objectively more similar to the training data, thus confirming its ability to enhance policy reliability.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more reliable and stable robotic policies derived from imitation learning, and its core objective is to achieve this by leveraging a curiosity-based mechanism to guide generative models away from unpredictable, out-of-distribution behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Curiosity-Diffuser method integrates a Random Network Distillation (RND) module for reliability assessment with a conditional diffusion model to guide the generation of reliable trajectories. The overall framework comprises three main components: a diffusion model for decision-making, an RND-based model evaluation for guidance, and a novel K-Sim metric for similarity measurement.

**A. Diffusion Model for Decision Making**
The method employs a diffusion model that processes data through a forward diffusion process (adding noise) and an iterative denoising process (reconstructing data). Trajectories, denoted as `τ`, consist of interleaved states and actions `(s_0, a_0..., s_T, a_T)`. The denoising process `p_θ(τ_{i-1}|τ_i)` is approximated as a Gaussian distribution, with its mean `μ_θ` parameterized by `θ`. The model learns to predict noise by minimizing the error in this prediction (Equation 2).

For robotic decision-making, the diffusion model is adapted for conditional generation, allowing it to predict optimal behaviors or trajectories satisfying certain constraints. This is achieved through a guided diffusion model (Equation 3): `p_θ(τ_{i-1}|τ_i, y(τ_i)) ≈ N(τ_{i-1}; μ_θ + αΣg, Σ)`. Here, `y(τ_i)` represents conditional information (e.g., task completion likelihood), `α` is a weighting factor for guidance, and `g` is the guidance information. In the context of offline Reinforcement Learning (RL), `g` can be the gradient of the cumulative reward `∇_τ J(μ_θ)` (Equation 4), effectively maximizing the probability of "good" trajectories. A key advantage is the decoupled training: the policy network (`θ`) learns from diverse trajectories, while the classifier network (`ϕ`) for guidance is trained independently based on trajectory categories.

**B. RND-based Model Evaluation**
To enhance reliability, the paper introduces RND as a model evaluation mechanism. RND assesses the novelty or familiarity of a state or trajectory relative to the training data. It consists of two neural networks: a fixed-parameter random encoder `f_ξ(τ)` and a learnable prediction network `f_η(τ)`. Both take a trajectory segment `τ_i` (from diffusion steps) as input. The prediction network `f_η` is trained to minimize the squared difference `||f_η(τ_i) - f_ξ(τ_i)||^2` (Equation 5). A smaller difference indicates higher familiarity and, conversely, higher reliability of `τ_i`.

This difference is leveraged for RND-based guidance `g_2`. Since the prediction error is inversely proportional to reliability, minimizing this error corresponds to maximizing reliability. Thus, `g_2` is defined as the negative gradient of the squared difference: `g_2 = -∇_τ ||f_η(τ_i) - f_ξ(τ_i)||^2 = ∇_τ C(μ_θ)` (Equation 6), where `C(μ_θ)` represents the "curiosity" or novelty. This gradient guides the diffusion model to generate trajectories that are more familiar to the RND module.

To ensure that familiar trajectories also lead to task completion, two improvements are implemented:
1. The RND prediction network is trained solely on successful data.
2. RND guidance (`g_2`) is combined with reward-based guidance (`g_1`) using a weighting coefficient `λ`: `g = g_1 + λg_2` (Equation 7). This combined guidance term encourages the generation of plans that balance task achievement with adherence to familiar, reliable behaviors, preventing the model from solely pursuing novelty or only staying within the data distribution without completing the task.

**C. Similarity Measurement (K-Sim)**
To quantitatively evaluate the effectiveness of RND guidance in producing reliable, data-similar behaviors, the authors introduce K-Sim, a computationally efficient similarity metric. Traditional brute-force similarity search across large datasets is impractical. K-Sim addresses this by:
1. **Preprocessing:** Applying K-Means clustering to the states `s_j` in the training dataset, dividing it into `m` clusters.
2. **Efficient Search:** For each decision `(s_i, π(s_i))` during testing, the algorithm first identifies the K-Means cluster whose center is closest to `s_i`.
3. **Refined Search:** Within this identified cluster, a small subset of states most similar to `s_i` is selected. From this subset, the action `a_k` most similar to `π(s_i)` is found, forming the nearest training pair `(s_ki, a_ki)`.
4. **Similarity Calculation:** The K-Sim score is then computed as the average inverse norm distance between the test decision `(s_i, π(s_i))` and its nearest training pair `(s_ki, a_ki)`: `K_sim = (1/n) * Σ_i min(1, γ / ||(s_i, π(s_i)), (s_ki, a_ki)||^2)` (Equation 8). The parameter `γ` is a proportional coefficient, and `min(1...)` constrains the score to `(0, 1]`. A score closer to 1 indicates higher similarity to the training data and thus higher reliability. This approach significantly reduces computational complexity compared to a full dataset traversal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of Curiosity-Diffuser was evaluated through simulations on the D4RL MuJoCo and AntMaze environments, comparing it against various imitation learning and offline reinforcement learning baselines. The evaluation focused on task performance and the proposed K-Sim metric for reliability.

**A. Simulation Performance Enhancement**

1. **MuJoCo Tasks:**
 - Curiosity-Diffuser demonstrated an improvement in task performance over the baseline Diffuser method. Across the "Medium," "Medium-Replay," and "Medium-Expert" datasets for HalfCheetah, Hopper, and Walker2d, Curiosity-Diffuser achieved an average score of 77.8, which was the highest among all compared methods, including BC, MOPO, MBOP, SynthER, MOReL, DT, DD, AdaptDiffuser, IQL, and Diffuser.
 - The performance gain was particularly noticeable in the "Medium-Expert" group. For instance, in Hopper, it improved from 105.2 (Diffuser) to 108.3, and in HalfCheetah, from 86.3 to 88.3. This outcome is attributed to the presence of high-quality expert demonstrations in this dataset, which allowed the RND guidance to steer the policy towards more effective behaviors.
 - In contrast, improvements were less pronounced or slightly varied in the "Medium" and "Medium-Replay" groups, which contain lower-quality data. The authors suggest that RND guidance with lower-quality data might have a less positive or even a negative impact on task completion in some instances.

2. **AntMaze Tasks:**
 - In the AntMaze environment, characterized by sparse rewards and complex robot control, Curiosity-Diffuser showed substantial improvements, especially in the "Play" group. For "Antmaze-Medium-Play," the success rate increased from 22.0% (Diffuser) to 38.0%, and for "Antmaze-Large-Play," from 38.0% to 58.0%.
 - This significant improvement under sparse reward conditions highlights the utility of RND guidance. It helped the robot follow familiar paths, mitigating failures caused by "hallucinations" that might arise from out-of-distribution generalization in the absence of informative rewards.
 - In the "Diverse" group, where both start and target points are randomized and data quality was observed to be lower, the performance difference was minimal (e.g., 24.0% to 26.0% for Antmaze-Medium-Diverse).
 - Overall, Curiosity-Diffuser achieved an average success rate of 43.0% in AntMaze, outperforming all baselines.

3. **Qualitative Analysis (AntMaze):**
 - Visualizations of robot paths in AntMaze revealed that Curiosity-Diffuser enabled smoother trajectories towards the target. Even when the robot exhibited minor hesitations (e.g., stepping in place), the combined RND and reward guidance allowed it to re-enter familiar and successful trajectories.
 - Conversely, the Diffuser method (without curiosity guidance) generated more erratic movement trajectories, indicative of the model producing "hallucinations" during inference. These erratic behaviors led to accumulation of action errors, precision issues, and the robot becoming stuck in unfamiliar states near the target, unable to complete the task.

4. **Guided Mixing Ratio (Ablation Study):**
 - An ablation study on the weighting factor `λ` for RND guidance showed a non-monotonic relationship with task performance. As `λ` increased from 0 (reward-only guidance), performance initially improved, but then declined past a certain threshold. This indicates that an excessive reliance on RND-based guidance (i.e., prioritizing familiarity too much) can hinder task completion.
 - The findings support the necessity of both reward-based guidance (to drive task completion) and RND-based guidance (to ensure reliability and prevent hallucinations), with an optimal balance being crucial for robust performance.

**B. K-Sim Testing**

- The K-Sim metric was used to evaluate the similarity of generated trajectories to the training data in the AntMaze environment.
- Curiosity-Diffuser consistently yielded higher K-Sim scores compared to the Diffuser baseline. For example, in "Antmaze-Medium-Play," Curiosity-Diffuser achieved a K-Sim score of 0.949, compared to Diffuser's 0.939. Similar trends were observed across "Antmaze-Large-Play," "Antmaze-Medium-Diverse," and "Antmaze-Large-Diverse."
- Scores closer to 1.0 indicate greater similarity to the training dataset. These results quantitatively support the claim that RND-guided diffusion effectively suppresses "out-of-distribution (OOD)" generalization, leading to more reliable and data-aligned behaviors during decision-making. The computation of these scores was completed efficiently due to the K-Means based approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a notable challenge in modern robotic imitation learning: the instability and "hallucination" phenomena observed in neural network policies. By proposing the Curiosity-Diffuser, the authors contribute a method designed to enhance the reliability of robot behaviors, which is a critical factor for the safe and effective deployment of intelligent robots in physical environments.

The primary significance lies in its approach to integrating curiosity-driven evaluation with generative models for policy synthesis. The repurposing of Random Network Distillation (RND) – traditionally used for exploration in reinforcement learning – to actively guide conditional diffusion models towards familiar, in-distribution trajectories represents a methodological advancement. This mechanism directly tackles the issue of overgeneralization, a common source of unreliable behavior in neural network-based policies.

The demonstrated improvements in task performance, particularly in reward-sparse environments like AntMaze, suggest practical utility. In such scenarios, where explicit reward signals are infrequent, guiding the model to adhere to known, successful paths can be crucial for achieving goals while avoiding unsafe or inefficient deviations. This makes the Curiosity-Diffuser potentially well-suited for imitation learning applications where reward engineering is challenging or where demonstrations provide the primary source of behavioral information.

Furthermore, the introduction of K-Sim as a computationally efficient metric for evaluating policy reliability is a valuable contribution to the research community. This metric offers a quantitative means to assess the similarity of generated behaviors to the training data, thereby providing a more objective measure of a policy's tendency to operate within its learned distribution. This can facilitate future comparative studies and guide the development of more reliable imitation learning algorithms.

The overall impact of this work is positioned towards increasing the trustworthiness and robustness of autonomous systems. By reducing the occurrence of "hallucinations" and ensuring that robotic policies operate within a reliably understood domain, Curiosity-Diffuser contributes to the broader goal of making robotic intelligence safer and more deployable in real-world, mission-critical applications where unpredictable failures cannot be tolerated. The method's generalizability, stemming from the versatility of RND and diffusion models, suggests its potential applicability across a range of robotic tasks and environments.

The authors also identify future work in improving model evaluation methods by analyzing internal activation features of neural networks. This indicates an ongoing commitment to refining the mechanisms for ensuring policy reliability, suggesting that the presented work is a step within a larger research agenda focused on robust and trustworthy AI for robotics.
