# DexGraspVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Framework Towards General Dexterous Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> DexGraspVLA, a vision-language-action framework from Peking University, achieves an unprecedented 90.8% aggregated success rate for dexterous grasping in cluttered scenes across 1,287 unseen object, lighting, and background combinations. The framework leverages foundation models to iteratively transform diverse language and visual inputs into domain-invariant representations, enabling robust closed-loop control and strong zero-shot generalization for language-guided manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous grasping methods struggle to generalize effectively to diverse, unseen objects and environments in challenging real-world cluttered scenes.
- Imitation learning, while avoiding the sim-to-real gap, suffers from a generalization bottleneck as it typically fails beyond its specific training data due to domain shift and the impracticality of exhaustive data collection.
- Integrating vision-language foundation models (VLMs) for closed-loop robotic control often requires massive datasets for end-to-end training or results in open-loop systems vulnerable to domain shift.

## Method

- **DexGraspVLA** introduces a hierarchical framework with a high-level VLM planner (Qwen) for task decomposition and generating domain-invariant bounding box affordance signals.
- A low-level controller utilizes frozen foundation models (SAM for masking, DINOv2 for visual features) to create domain-invariant representations by tracking objects and extracting robust visual features.
- A ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== learns a closed-loop policy to predict multi-step action chunks based on these fused, domain-invariant features, enabling continuous control and responsiveness to perturbations.

## Results

- Achieved an unprecedented **90.8%** aggregated success rate for dexterous grasping in cluttered scenes across 1,287 zero-shot combinations of unseen objects, lighting, and backgrounds, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art VLA baselines.
- Demonstrated robust closed-loop control, handling adversarial objects (transparent, deformable, reflective), human-induced perturbations, and successful re-grasps after initial failures.
- Achieved an **89.6%** task success rate for free-form, long-horizon language-guided tasks like "Clear the table" and successfully extended to nonprehensile grasping with an **84.7%** aggregated generalization performance without architectural changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging frozen vision-language foundation models (Qwen, SAM, DINOv2) is critical for transforming diverse, domain-variant raw inputs into domain-invariant representations, which is key for imitation learning to achieve strong generalization.
> - A hierarchical design, where a VLM handles high-level planning and task decomposition into object-centric instructions, effectively guides a robust low-level controller for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
> - By establishing domain-invariant representations, the framework enables efficient learning from a relatively modest dataset (2,094 demonstrations) to achieve broad generalization, mitigating the data intensity typical of end-to-end VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

DexGraspVLA tackles dexterous grasping, a fundamental yet persistently challenging problem in robotics, especially in the context of creating general-purpose robots capable of operating in diverse, unseen, and cluttered real-world environments. The paper positions itself within the broader landscape by first acknowledging existing approaches and their limitations, then proposing an innovative integration of recent advancements.

Historically, dexterous grasping methods have largely fallen into two categories:
- **Two-stage pipelines:** These methods typically predict a grasp pose from static perception and then execute an open-loop motion plan to reach it (e.g., Chen et al., 2024). While modular, they heavily rely on precise calibration and mechanical accuracy, making them vulnerable to disturbances and real-world uncertainties.
- **End-to-end paradigms:** These approaches, primarily based on reinforcement learning (RL) or imitation learning (IL), aim for closed-loop control by continuously adjusting actions based on real-time feedback.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Has achieved impressive feats in simulation (e.g., Akkaya et al., 2019), but the inherent complexity of real-world physics leads to a significant "sim-to-real gap," making direct transfer challenging.
 - **Imitation Learning (IL):** Learns directly from human demonstrations, thereby circumventing the sim-to-real gap. However, IL traditionally struggles with **generalization** beyond its training data. Collecting sufficient expert trajectories to cover the full spectrum of objects, environments, and variations encountered in real-world general grasping is practically impossible. This generalization bottleneck for IL is a central challenge that DexGraspVLA aims to address.

The rapid emergence of **vision and language foundation models (FMs)**, such as DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023), SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023), and large language models like Qwen-VL (Bai et al., 2023), represents a transformative development. Pre-trained on internet-scale data, these models possess remarkable world knowledge and strong generalization capabilities over visual and linguistic inputs. Robotic research has naturally begun integrating these FMs into **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for decision-making.

Within this nascent VLA landscape, two main integration strategies have emerged:
- **Direct end-to-end training:** Approaches like OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024) and π0 (Black et al., 2024) fine-tune large VLMs directly on robot data. While promising, this demands massive, manually collected demonstrations (O’Neill et al., 2023) to achieve diversity, yet still exhibits reduced performance on unseen scenarios and requires fine-tuning for new conditions, indicating issues with catastrophic forgetting and limited generalization.
- **Modular frameworks with frozen FMs:** Some works leverage frozen foundation models to robustly infer task affordances (e.g., Huang et al., 2024; Stone et al., 2023), guiding either motion planning or a learned action head. However, these often suffer from dependence on accurate calibration, substantial human design, open-loop control issues, or a vulnerability to domain shift when raw visual inputs are still directly mapped to actions.

DexGraspVLA strategically positions itself in this landscape by addressing the critical gap in achieving **generalizable closed-loop policies with foundation models**. Its key contribution is demonstrating that by using foundation models to **iteratively transform diverse and domain-varying language and visual inputs into *domain-invariant representations***, imitation learning can be applied efficiently and effectively. This mitigates the domain shift problem, allowing a learned policy to generalize strongly to novel scenarios, as these scenarios are translated into representations that the policy has effectively learned from. This hybrid approach combines the extensive world knowledge and generalization ability of FMs with the action modeling capacity of imitation learning, offering a more robust and adaptive solution than previous methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of the DexGraspVLA research is to develop a general-purpose robotic system capable of robust and versatile dexterous grasping in challenging, unstructured real-world environments, guided by natural language instructions. The specific objectives and motivations underpinning this work are:

- **Achieve Generalization in Dexterous Grasping:** The primary objective is to enable a robot to grasp diverse objects across arbitrary, unseen scenarios (variations in objects, lighting, backgrounds, clutter) with high success rates. This directly addresses the fundamental limitation of existing dexterous grasping approaches that typically rely on restrictive assumptions like single-object settings or limited environments, thereby showing constrained generalization.
- **Overcome Imitation Learning's Generalization Bottleneck:** While imitation learning offers benefits over reinforcement learning by avoiding the sim-to-real gap, its struggle to generalize beyond the training data remains a major hurdle. The paper aims to develop a strategy that allows imitation learning to be effective even with limited expert data by making the input representations domain-invariant.
- **Effectively Leverage Foundation Models for Robotics:** Recognize the immense potential of vision and language foundation models (VLMs) with their internet-scale world knowledge and generalization. The objective is to harness these capabilities not merely for perception or high-level reasoning, but to integrate them strategically into a closed-loop action policy for robust manipulation.
- **Develop a Robust Closed-Loop Control System:** Unlike open-loop systems that are fragile to disturbances, the motivation is to build an adaptive system that can continuously adjust its actions based on real-time feedback, capable of handling unforeseen events like human perturbations or initial grasp failures.
- **Enable Language-Guided, Long-Horizon Tasks:** Beyond single-object grasping, the research aims to enable robots to understand and execute complex, free-form, long-horizon instructions, requiring embodied reasoning and sequential task completion in cluttered scenes.
- **Demonstrate Broad Applicability:** The motivation extends to proving the framework's versatility beyond standard prehensile grasping, showing its adaptability to other dexterous manipulation skills, such as nonprehensile tasks (e.g., pushing objects to an accessible pose before grasping).

In essence, the authors are motivated by the gap between current robotic manipulation capabilities and the demands of true general-purpose autonomy in unstructured human environments. They propose that by intelligently combining the representational power of foundation models with the action learning capabilities of imitation learning, this gap can be significantly narrowed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexGraspVLA introduces a **hierarchical and modular Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework** that strategically integrates foundation models with imitation learning to achieve robust generalization. The core methodological insight lies in iteratively transforming diverse language and visual inputs into *domain-invariant representations* before applying imitation learning. This alleviates the critical problem of domain shift, making the learned policy highly generalizable.

The framework comprises two main components: a **high-level planner** and a **low-level controller**.

#### 3.1. High-Level Planner

The planner is built upon an off-the-shelf, pre-trained **Qwen VLM (Vision-Language Model)**. Its responsibilities are:
- **Task Decomposition:** Given a free-form user prompt (e.g., "clear the table"), the planner decomposes it into a sequence of individual, object-level grasping instructions (e.g., "grasp the cookie").
- **Affordance Signal Generation:** For each instruction, the planner identifies the target object by predicting a **bounding box** in the head camera image. This bounding box serves as a crucial *domain-invariant affordance signal*. While language phrasing and visual appearances can vary widely (domain-variant), the bounding box format provides a consistent target localization for the controller, simplifying its learning task.
- **Execution Monitoring and Task Completion:** The planner monitors the controller's execution, assesses grasp outcomes, initiates robot resets after each attempt, and proposes updated instructions based on the evolving scene until the entire user prompt is fulfilled.

#### 3.2. Low-Level Controller

The controller is responsible for executing the grasping instruction guided by the planner's bounding box. Its design focuses on creating domain-invariant representations for effective imitation learning:
- **Object Mask Tracking:** The bounding box from the planner is fed to **SAM (Segment Anything Model)** to generate an initial binary mask of the target object. This mask is then continuously tracked over time by **Cutie (Continuous Tracking via object mask)**, ensuring accurate object identification even in cluttered and dynamic scenes.
- **Domain-Invariant Visual Feature Extraction:** To overcome the challenge of high variability in raw pixel-level inputs, DexGraspVLA employs **frozen DINOv2 (Vision Foundation Model)** as a feature extractor. DINOv2, trained without supervision on internet-scale images, is known to produce robust and semantically rich visual features that are comparatively invariant to distracting visual factors like lighting, backgrounds, or textures. Separate DINOv2 encoders extract features from both the head camera (`z_h_t`) and wrist camera (`z_w_t`) images.
- **Multimodal Input Fusion:**
 - The tracked object mask (`m_t`) is projected into the head image feature space using a randomly initialized ViT and then concatenated patch-wise with the head camera features (`z_h_t`).
 - These combined head features, wrist camera features (`z_w_t`), and robot proprioception (`s_t` – arm and hand joint angles) are mapped into a common embedding space using separate MLPs.
 - These embeddings are then concatenated to form a comprehensive, domain-invariant observation feature sequence (`_z_obs_t`).
- **Action Prediction via Diffusion Policy (DiT):** The policy `pi` is learned using a **Diffusion Transformer (DiT)**, following the paradigm of diffusion policies.
 - **Action Chunking:** Instead of predicting a single action, the DiT predicts a *chunk* of `H` future actions (`A_t`).
 - **Denoising Process:** During training, Gaussian noise is added to the ground-truth action chunk. The DiT, conditioned on the observation feature sequence (`_z_obs_t`) and the diffusion timestep, learns to predict and remove this noise. The training objective is to minimize the noise prediction error.
 - **Inference:** At inference time, the DiT iteratively denoises a randomly sampled Gaussian noise, reconstructing the intended multi-step action sequence from the learned distribution.
 - **Receding Horizon Control:** Only the first `H_a` actions from the predicted chunk are executed before a new action chunk is predicted, enabling continuous closed-loop control and responsiveness.

#### 3.3. Data Collection

The system is trained using imitation learning on a manually collected dataset. This dataset comprises **2,094 successful demonstrations** of dexterous grasping in cluttered scenes, using 36 diverse household objects (varying in size, weight, geometry, texture, material, and category). Each episode records raw camera images, robot proprioception, **object masks (labeled consistently with the controller's requirements)**, and actions. Demonstrations are collected via kinesthetic teaching, with surrounding objects randomized between episodes to introduce variability. This focused data collection, combined with the domain-invariant representation strategy, aims to make efficient use of limited expert data for broad generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DexGraspVLA demonstrates highly impressive performance across a wide range of challenging dexterous grasping tasks, particularly excelling in generalization to unseen scenarios.

- **Exceptional Large-Scale Generalization (Zero-Shot):** The framework achieves an unprecedented **90.8% aggregated success rate** for grasping in cluttered scenes across **1,287 unseen object, lighting, and background combinations**. This includes 91.1% on 360 unseen objects, 90.5% on 6 unseen backgrounds, and 90.9% under 3 unseen lighting conditions, all in a "zero-shot" environment (i.e., evaluated in a different physical environment than where the demonstrations were collected, without any fine-tuning). Allowing for up to three attempts further boosts the success rate to 96.9%. Qualitatively, DexGraspVLA handles transparent, deformable, reflective, and camouflaged objects, adapts to diverse geometries, tolerates human-induced perturbations, and performs re-grasps after failed attempts.
- **Superior Baseline Comparison:** DexGraspVLA consistently achieves over 90% success across all challenging settings, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art VLA baselines (OpenVLA, π0, RDT) fine-tuned on the same dataset. These baselines exhibit steep performance declines, particularly on unseen visual variations, suggesting overfitting to training domains. For instance, π0 (Full FT) achieved 75% on seen objects but only 45.8% on unseen objects, whereas DexGraspVLA maintained above 90% in both. This highlights DexGraspVLA's superior generalization.
- **Validation through Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies confirm the critical role of frozen, pre-trained vision foundation models in achieving generalization. DexGraspVLA (using frozen DINOv2) achieves 98.6% success on single-object grasping (seen and unseen). In contrast, ablated variants that allow DINOv2 encoders to be trainable or replace them with smaller, trainable ViTs (effectively an enhanced Diffusion Policy) perform significantly worse (e.g., 50.5% aggregated for ViT-small). This demonstrates that mapping raw inputs directly to actions leads to generalization failures due to domain shift, while frozen foundation models provide the necessary domain-invariant representations.
- **Consistent Internal Model Behavior:** Empirical analysis reveals that the internal model behaviors of DexGraspVLA remain consistent across drastically varying visual environments. DINOv2 features, when visualized, appear robust and consistent for the target object despite changes in background, lighting, and table texture. The diffusion-based action head's cross-attention maps consistently focus on the target object, validating the design principle of transforming diverse raw inputs into invariant representations for effective imitation learning.
- **Effective Long-Horizon Task Execution:** DexGraspVLA achieves an 89.6% aggregated task success rate for free-form, long-horizon prompts like "Clear the table" or "Grasp all green objects." This demonstrates the high-level planner's strong grounding and embodied reasoning capabilities (94.3% instruction proposal, 98.4% bounding box accuracy, 96.3% completion check) coupled with the low-level controller's robust grasping (over 91% success within long-horizon tasks).
- **Extension to Nonprehensile Grasping:** The framework successfully extends its applicability to challenging nonprehensile grasping tasks (e.g., pushing flat objects to an edge before grasping), achieving an 84.7% aggregated generalization performance with only a modest number of demonstrations and no architectural changes. This showcases the generality of its hierarchical design and the effectiveness of using bounding boxes as affordance guidance on domain-invariant representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexGraspVLA framework represents a significant leap forward in general dexterous grasping and holds substantial potential for impact across several dimensions:

- **Breakthrough in Generalization for Dexterous Grasping:** The most significant contribution is setting a new state-of-the-art for generalization in dexterous grasping. Achieving over 90% success rate in zero-shot, highly cluttered scenes with thousands of unseen object, lighting, and background combinations is an unprecedented feat. This demonstrates a level of adaptability previously thought impractical for real-world robotic systems learning from demonstrations.
- **Paradigm Shift for Imitation Learning:** DexGraspVLA offers a compelling solution to the long-standing generalization bottleneck of imitation learning. By demonstrating that strategic leveraging of frozen foundation models can transform diverse, domain-variant inputs into *domain-invariant representations* suitable for imitation learning, it unlocks a more data-efficient and robust pathway for learning complex robot behaviors. This approach could inspire similar strategies across other robot learning problems where real-world diversity poses a challenge.
- **Robust and Adaptive Robot Systems:** The ability to robustly handle adversarial objects, human disturbances, and failure recovery in closed-loop control makes DexGraspVLA a highly practical framework. This robustness is crucial for deploying robots in unpredictable human environments (e.g., homes, warehouses, healthcare).
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction through Language:** The strong performance in free-form, long-horizon language-guided tasks signifies progress towards more intuitive and natural human-robot interaction. Robots equipped with such capabilities can understand and execute complex instructions, moving beyond pre-programmed tasks to genuinely assist humans in dynamic settings.
- **Scalability and Data Efficiency:** Learning such complex dexterous behaviors with a relatively modest dataset of 2,094 demonstrations, especially when compared to the massive datasets often required by end-to-end VLA models, highlights the framework's data efficiency. This is a critical factor for the practical adoption of robotic learning in scenarios where expert data collection is expensive and time-consuming.
- **Foundation for Broader Robotic Manipulation:** The successful extension to nonprehensile grasping, without architectural modifications, demonstrates the inherent generality of the framework's design principles. This suggests that DexGraspVLA could be a foundational architecture for learning a wider array of dexterous manipulation skills, paving the way for more versatile and autonomous robotic agents.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly outlines future work, including addressing functional grasping, subsequent manipulation, and integrating tactile sensing. The strong foundation laid by DexGraspVLA provides a robust platform for these advancements, potentially leading to robots that not only grasp objects but also skillfully use them in complex tasks.

In conclusion, DexGraspVLA represents a significant methodological and empirical contribution to the field of robotic manipulation. By demonstrating robust, zero-shot generalization in dexterous grasping through an innovative hierarchical VLA framework that leverages foundation models for domain-invariant representations, it brings us closer to realizing truly general-purpose, language-guided robots capable of operating effectively in the complexity of the real world.
