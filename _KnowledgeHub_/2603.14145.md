# MMOU: A Massive Multi-Task Omni Understanding and Reasoning Benchmark for Long and Complex Real-World Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA and University of Maryland researchers developed MMOU, a benchmark with 15,000 questions from nearly 9,000 long, complex real-world videos, to evaluate multimodal large language models (MLLMs) on strictly joint audio-visual reasoning. Current MLLMs achieve up to 64.2% accuracy, significantly below human performance of 84.3%, highlighting challenges in temporal understanding and integrated cross-modal perception.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs often struggle with joint reasoning across multiple modalities, especially when processing long, complex, and real-world video content.
- Current multimodal benchmarks frequently omit or treat audio as auxiliary, focus on short video clips, or lack the scale and task diversity to assess comprehensive, long-term audio-visual understanding.
- A lack of robust diagnostic tools exists to systematically evaluate MLLMs' ability to integrate omni-modal signals (visual, audio, textual) and identify specific reasoning weaknesses.

## Method

- Introduced **MMOU**, a benchmark featuring 15,000 multiple-choice questions derived from 9,038 real-world videos averaging 11.8 minutes in length, designed to necessitate explicit integration of audio and visual information.
- Established a taxonomy of ==13 fundamental audio-visual reasoning skills==, curated through a ==multi-stage, expert-driven annotation pipeline== to ensure questions strictly require joint cross-modal understanding.
- Evaluated over 20 state-of-the-art MLLMs, including both closed-source and open-source models, alongside modality-restricted, text-only, and cascaded baselines, using ==micro-averaged accuracy== and a supplementary open-ended evaluation.

## Results

- Human annotators achieved **84.3%** accuracy, while Gemini 2.5 Pro scored **64.2%** and the top open-source model, MiniCPM-o 4.5, achieved **46.8%** overall accuracy on MMOU.
- Modality-restricted models (e.g., vision-only **44%**, audio-only **17.7%**) and cascaded approaches (**28.1-33.1%**) demonstrated considerably lower performance, validating the benchmark's strict requirement for integrated audio-visual understanding.
- Model accuracy consistently decreased as the temporal position of the supporting evidence shifted toward the end of long videos, highlighting a limitation in long-term context retention and temporal reasoning across all models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs exhibit a substantial performance gap compared to human capabilities, with the best model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) scoring 64.2% accuracy against human annotators' 84.3%.
> - The benchmark successfully enforces true cross-modal reasoning, as unimodal and cascaded models perform significantly worse than end-to-end multimodal systems, confirming the necessity of joint audio-visual perception.
> - All evaluated models struggle with fundamental reasoning skills and long-horizon temporal understanding, showing a consistent degradation in accuracy when relevant evidence appears later in extended videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) represents a significant advancement in Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), enabling traditional Large Language Models (LLMs) to process and reason over diverse modalities such as text, images, audio, and video. Current MLLMs have demonstrated proficiency in tasks within isolated modalities, such as visual question answering or audio captioning, often surpassing previous benchmarks.

However, a notable limitation in the current landscape is the restricted ability of these models to perform joint reasoning across multiple modalities, particularly when confronted with long, complex, and real-world video content. Existing MLLMs are frequently optimized for single-modality reasoning (e.g., vision-only or audio-only) and often struggle to integrate audio and visual cues in a unified manner, a capability fundamental to human cognition. This limitation is partly attributed to an imbalance in available training data and benchmarks, where single-modality datasets are more abundant and cover a broader range of tasks than their multimodal counterparts.

In terms of evaluation, while substantial progress has been made in benchmarking LLMs and vision-only/audio-only MLLMs, holistic evaluation of MLLMs for genuine cross-modal reasoning remains underdeveloped. Many existing video benchmarks either entirely omit audio or treat it as an auxiliary component, and predominantly focus on short video clips. Consequently, these benchmarks often fail to capture the long-term temporal dependencies and the nuanced, tightly coupled audio-visual reasoning required for comprehensive understanding of real-world videos. Prior audio-visual datasets exist (e.g., VALOR, AVQA, MusicAVQA, AV-Odyssey, AVHBench), but they frequently limit questions to a single dominant modality, focus on short-duration videos, or operate at a small scale with limited task diversity. More recent benchmarks (e.g., WorldSense, DailyOmni, OmniBench, OmniVideoBench, UNO-Bench) have begun to address complex joint audio-visual evaluation but still exhibit constraints regarding video length, scale, or the strict requirement for cross-modal integration.

The MMOU benchmark addresses this gap by providing a diagnostic evaluation framework specifically designed to assess MLLMs' ability to jointly reason over omni-modal signals in long, complex, real-world videos. It aims to systematically expose the limitations of current models in scenarios that necessitate deep integration of audio and visual information over extended temporal contexts, thereby contributing to the advancement of more robust and generalizable multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce MMOU, a new benchmark specifically designed to systematically evaluate multimodal understanding and reasoning in MLLMs under challenging, real-world conditions, focusing on omni-modal (visual, audio, and textual) signals within long and complex videos.

The motivation for developing MMOU stems from several identified limitations in the current state of multimodal AI:

- **Lack of Joint Omni-Modal Reasoning Evaluation**: Current MLLMs typically excel in isolated visual or audio tasks but exhibit weaknesses in jointly perceiving and reasoning across modalities in a manner similar to human cognition. This limitation hinders their application in real-world scenarios where multimodal cues are intrinsically linked and must be integrated for comprehensive understanding.
- **Insufficient Benchmarking for Long and Complex Videos**: Existing multimodal benchmarks often overlook the audio modality, treat it as secondary, or primarily focus on short video clips. This approach fails to capture the complexity of real-world videos, which frequently demand understanding of long-term temporal dependencies and tightly coupled reasoning over synchronized audio and visual streams spanning extended durations.
- **Need for Robust Diagnostic Tools**: To advance MLLMs towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), a comprehensive and diagnostic evaluation framework is necessary. Such a benchmark must be capable of identifying specific strengths and systematic failure modes of models, guiding research efforts effectively.
- **Requirement for Strict Cross-Modal Dependency**: A core motivation for MMOU is to ensure that all questions necessitate the simultaneous integration of audio and visual information. The design explicitly excludes questions that can be answered by relying on a single modality, thus enforcing true cross-modal reasoning.
- **Diverse Skill Coverage**: To provide a holistic assessment, the benchmark aims to cover a broad spectrum of fundamental reasoning skills (13 distinct categories). These skills are carefully curated to ensure they require evidence integration across modalities and time.
- **High-Quality and Scale**: The development of a large-scale benchmark with meticulously curated questions and a substantial volume of diverse, long-form, real-world videos is motivated by the need for a rigorous and representative evaluation platform. Manual annotation by professional annotators further ensures high quality and reasoning fidelity.
- **Real-world Applicability**: By using web-collected videos from diverse domains and subcategories, the benchmark seeks to evaluate models in conditions reflective of practical deployment, where videos often exhibit rich, tightly coupled audio-visual content.

In summary, MMOU is motivated by the critical need for a benchmark that moves beyond isolated modality evaluation and short clips, focusing on the challenging task of integrated omni-modal understanding and reasoning in long, complex, and temporally dense real-world video content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MMOU benchmark was constructed through a multi-stage, expert-driven process designed to ensure high-quality, complex, and truly omni-modal evaluation.

**Benchmark Scale and Characteristics:**
MMOU comprises 15,000 multiple-choice Question-Answering (QA) pairs derived from 9,038 real-world videos. The videos are characterized by their extended duration, averaging 711.6 seconds (approximately 11.8 minutes), with lengths ranging from 7 seconds to 7255 seconds (over 120 minutes). All videos are sampled at 720p resolution. The dataset spans 10 major categories and 36 fine-grained subcategories, encompassing diverse domains such as academic lectures, sports, travel, and gaming, ensuring content variety.

**Skill and Task Curation:**
A taxonomy of 13 fundamental audio-visual reasoning skills was established to capture various challenges in long-form, real-world videos. Each skill is designed to necessitate explicit integration of audio and visual information. These skills include:
- **Temporal Understanding and Event Sequencing:** Reasoning about the order, progression, and dependencies of audio-visual events over time.
- **Sub-scene Understanding:** Identifying and interpreting semantically significant segments within long videos.
- **Holistic Video Reasoning:** Global comprehension of the video’s main activity or theme.
- **Inference and Context Understanding:** Deducting unstated intentions, causes, or situational context from multiple audio-visual cues.
- **Needle-in-the-haystack Reasoning:** Localizing and reasoning about specific moments in long videos.
- **Referential Grounding:** Linking audio references to visual entities or vice versa.
- **Counting and Comparative Reasoning:** Quantitative and relational reasoning over audio-visual events.
- **Object Interaction Reasoning:** Understanding actions on objects and their transformations.
- **Audio-Visual Stitching:** Reasoning over edited segments, understanding narrative continuity.
- **Tracking Spurious Correlations:** Identifying surprising or unintuitive audio-visual evidence.
All questions are additionally tagged with "audio-visual understanding," explicitly ensuring joint reasoning across both modalities and excluding unimodal solvable questions.

**Data Collection, Curation, and Annotation Pipeline:**
1. **Skill and Task Curation:** The 13 skill categories were predefined to guide annotation and ensure comprehensive coverage of omni-modal reasoning.
2. **Video Domain Selection:** 10 major and 36 fine-grained subcategories were systematically chosen to ensure broad coverage of real-world scenarios and exercise distinct combinations of the targeted skills.
3. **Source Video Collection:** 9,038 publicly available, real-world videos were collected from online platforms (e.g., YouTube). Videos were selected to align with the skill taxonomy, support at least one high-quality question, and prioritize natural, authentic content.
4. **Expert Question Generation:** Eleven professionally trained human experts, with diverse backgrounds in writing, linguistics, and STEM fields, generated open-ended question-answer pairs. Annotators were instructed to watch videos in full, create questions requiring joint audio and visual understanding (avoiding yes/no or text-only solvable questions), and annotate the earliest and latest timestamps of supporting evidence. Each question was tagged with one or more skill categories.
5. **Distractor Generation:** The initial open-ended questions were converted to a multiple-choice format. GPT-5.2 was utilized to generate nine hard distractors for each question, resulting in ten answer options. Distractors were designed to be challenging, with half being semantically plausible and video-grounded, and the other half intentionally out-of-context. To further increase difficulty and mitigate positional biases, "None of the above" was used as the correct answer in 13% of QAs and as an incorrect option in another 13% of QAs. Answer-relevant evidence was deliberately placed at diverse temporal locations within videos to avoid positional biases.
6. **Quality Control and Filtering:** A separate group of expert reviewers performed rigorous quality control on a 20% sample of the QA pairs. This process ensured questions were relevant, grammatically correct, accurately tagged, and had correct answers. Instances with ambiguity, redundancy, triviality, misaligned timestamps, or weak audio-visual grounding were removed.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Baselines**: Over 20 state-of-the-art models were evaluated, including:
 - **Audio-Visual Multimodal LLMs**: Both closed-source (Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Pro) and open-source (Qwen 2.5/3-Omni, Phi-4 Multimodal, Gemma 3n, MiniCPM-o 4.5, Video-LLaMA 2, OmniVinci, Baichuan-Omni-1.5).
 - **Modality-Restricted MLLMs**: Vision-only (Qwen3/2.5-VL series) and Audio-only (Audio Flamingo 3, Qwen3-Omni in audio-only mode) models to assess unimodal contributions.
 - **Text-Only LLMs**: Qwen3-235B, GPT-5.2, and GPT-4o mini, to establish a baseline for questions answerable by text priors alone.
 - **Cascaded Models**: Architectures where audio and visual captions were generated separately (using Qwen3-Omni and Qwen3-VL) and then fused as context for a text-only LLM (Qwen3-235B or GPT-5.2).
- **Evaluation Metric**: Micro-averaged accuracy for multiple-choice questions.
- **Evaluation Protocol**: Models selected exactly one answer from the provided options. To mitigate option-order bias, the order of choices was randomized five times, and the majority-selected answer was chosen. Multiple prompt variants were tested, and the best-performing configuration was reported.

**Open-Ended Evaluation (for in-depth analysis):**
A supplementary open-ended evaluation was conducted where models generated free-form answers without predefined options. GPT-5 served as an LLM judge, using a four-dimensional rubric (Correctness, Completeness, Faithfulness, Clarity) scored on a 1-5 scale. A weighted overall score was calculated (0.5 * Correctness + 0.5/3 * (Completeness + Faithfulness + Clarity)). This aimed to differentiate models based on generative capabilities versus recognition/elimination strategies in MCQs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of over 20 state-of-the-art multimodal models on the MMOU benchmark revealed several key findings:

**Overall Performance Gaps:**
- A substantial performance gap exists between current models and human-level capabilities. Human annotators achieved an average accuracy of 84.3%.
- The best-performing closed-source model, Gemini 2.5 Pro, achieved an overall accuracy of 64.2%.
- The strongest open-source model, MiniCPM-o 4.5, reached an accuracy of 46.8%. This 24.7% relative performance drop highlights significant challenges for open-source models compared to proprietary ones. The authors hypothesize Gemini's stronger performance may be due to pre-training on YouTube videos.

**Necessity of Cross-Modal Understanding:**
- Modality-restricted models demonstrated significantly lower performance, confirming the strict audio-visual dependency embedded in MMOU questions.
- Vision-only models, such as Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct, achieved only 44% accuracy, indicating that visual information alone is insufficient to answer the questions.
- Audio-only models, including Audio Flamingo 3 (17.7%) and Qwen3-Omni (35.6%) operating in audio-only mode, performed poorly, further validating that unimodal reasoning is inadequate for MMOU.

**Limitations of Text-Only and Cascaded Models:**
- Text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-5.2 (40.7%) and Qwen3-235B (37.5%) performed close to random chance without audio-visual inputs. This validates the benchmark's design in minimizing textual biases and necessitating true, temporally grounded audio-visual perception.
- Cascaded models, which generate separate audio and visual captions and then feed them to a text-only LLM, also struggled. For example, Qwen3-(VL+O-A) + Qwen3-235B achieved 33.1%, and Qwen3-(VL+O-A) + GPT-5.2 achieved 28.1%. This indicates that providing a rich contextual audio-visual summary is insufficient, emphasizing the need for joint, end-to-end cross-modal perception rather than decoupled processing.

**Skill-wise Performance Analysis:**
- All models, including the best-performing ones, exhibited substantial weaknesses across fundamental skills.
- Closed models consistently outperformed open models across most skill categories.
- However, all models struggled particularly with basic yet essential skills such as temporal understanding, counting, and needle-in-the-haystack reasoning.

**Temporal Position Sensitivity:**
- Model accuracy consistently degraded as the relevant evidence for the answer appeared later in the video.
- A sharp drop in performance was observed for evidence located toward the end of long video sequences. This trend was consistent across both open and closed models, highlighting a fundamental limitation in long-horizon temporal reasoning and context retention in current multimodal systems.

**Open-Ended Evaluation Insights:**
- **Overall Performance**: Gemini 2.5 Pro achieved the highest overall score (3.90) in open-ended generation, outperforming other models like Qwen3-Omni-30B-Instruct (2.86) and OmniVinci (2.64). This format more clearly separated model capabilities.
- **Dimension Analysis**: Top models, such as Gemini 2.5 Pro and Qwen3-Omni-30B, showed strong Faithfulness (3.80 and 3.36) and Clarity (4.62 and 4.62), suggesting they articulate responses clearly and avoid severe hallucinations. However, their Correctness (3.71 vs. 2.27) and Completeness (3.86 vs. 2.34) remained considerably lower, indicating ongoing challenges in accurately comprehending and fully addressing questions.
- **Skill-Specific Performance (Gemini 2.5 Pro)**: Holistic Reasoning showed the highest scores in Correctness (4.24), Completeness (4.34), and Faithfulness (4.18), while Counting was the most challenging category with the lowest scores across these dimensions (Correctness 3.01, Completeness 3.27, Faithfulness 3.50). Clarity remained consistently high across all skill types.
- **Open-Ended vs. Multiple-Choice Discrepancy**: Models answered a significant fraction of questions correctly in MCQ format even when they performed poorly in open-ended generation (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro answered 21.1% correctly in MCQ when its open-ended correctness was below 2 out of 5). This suggests that MCQ provides scaffolding, potentially overestimating true understanding, and that models may possess underlying knowledge but struggle with articulation in a free-form manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MMOU benchmark holds several significances and carries potential impacts for the advancement and responsible deployment of multimodal AI:

- **Diagnostic Tool for MLLM Limitations**: MMOU serves as a crucial diagnostic instrument for the research community. By systematically exposing the substantial performance gaps and specific failure modes of current multimodal models in understanding and reasoning over long, complex, omni-modal real-world videos, it provides concrete insights into where and why existing systems fail. This diagnostic capability is essential for targeted research and development efforts.
- **Driving Next-Generation MLLM Development**: The benchmark's emphasis on strictly joint audio-visual perception, long-term temporal reasoning, and diverse skill categories challenges the current limitations of MLLMs. It is expected to stimulate the development of more robust, reliable, and genuinely omni-modal models that can better integrate information across modalities and time, thereby pushing closer to the goal of Artificial General Intelligence.
- **Relevance to Real-World Applications**: The use of real-world, web-collected videos across diverse domains directly addresses the requirements of practical applications involving long-form video understanding. This includes fields such as educational content analysis, media monitoring, content creation and editing, surveillance, and human-computer interaction, where comprehensive multimodal comprehension is critical.
- **Highlighting Risks and Promoting Responsible AI**: By revealing systematic failure modes, MMOU implicitly highlights the risks associated with deploying current AI systems in high-impact settings where errors or misinterpretations can have significant consequences. This underscores the necessity of rigorous evaluation, transparent reporting of limitations, and continued research before widespread real-world deployment.
- **Fostering Benchmarking Evolution**: The benchmark contributes to the evolution of multimodal evaluation paradigms by moving beyond unimodal or short-clip assessments. Its detailed annotation protocol, rigorous quality control, and the inclusion of open-ended evaluation aspects provide a comprehensive model for future benchmark design.
- **Acknowledging and Addressing Biases**: The paper openly acknowledges limitations such as potential content biases inherent in publicly available web videos and the possibility of train-test leakage for closed-source models. This transparency encourages future research to expand evaluation to more diverse, unstructured data sources and explicitly address fairness and representational biases, promoting more equitable AI development.
- **Encouraging Open Science**: While the benchmark itself is a tool for evaluation, its public release (via Hugging Face) encourages broader participation and reproducibility within the research community, fostering collaborative progress in multimodal AI.

Overall, MMOU is positioned to positively influence the field by encouraging transparent evaluation, guiding research toward models with superior cross-modal reasoning abilities, and contributing to the safer and more effective development of multimodal AI.
