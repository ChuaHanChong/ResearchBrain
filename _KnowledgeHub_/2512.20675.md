# Revisiting the Learning Objectives of Vision-Language Reward Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This work demonstrates that a simple triplet loss objective, when used for finetuning Vision-Language Models for reward modeling, often surpasses or rivals the performance of more complex state-of-the-art objectives under controlled experimental conditions. It suggests that observed performance gains in previous methods might stem from differences in pre-training data and model architecture rather than objective complexity.

## Problem

- Developing generalizable reward functions for embodied AI agents is challenging, often relying on laborious, domain-specific human supervision.
- Prior research on VLM-based reward models lacked systematic comparison, with methods pre-trained on different datasets and architectures, making it difficult to attribute performance gains to specific learning objectives.
- Existing complex learning objectives for finetuning VLMs on video demonstrations sometimes struggled with domain shifts, action specificity, and complex task dynamics.

## Method

- A unified experimental framework was established, controlling for the VLM backbone (SigLIP2 with ==LoRA==), finetuning data (Meta-World expert demonstrations), and evaluation environments (held-out Meta-World tasks).
- Popular learning objectives (TCN, VIP, R3M, LIV, and their language adaptations) were systematically re-evaluated alongside a newly proposed ==simple triplet loss baseline==.
- Performance was quantified using two metrics: consistency with ground-truth reward (==ranking accuracy==) and alignment with expert progress (==Value-Order Correlation - VOC==).

## Results

- The triplet loss achieved the highest overall accuracy (**68.88%** average) in ranking consistency with ground-truth reward across held-out Meta-World tasks, outperforming all other objectives.
- TCN-based objectives performed similarly to each other, securing the second-best tier (e.g., L_R3M at **66.40%** average accuracy), while VIP-based objectives showed considerably lower average accuracies (e.g., L_LIV at **52.07%**).
- In terms of alignment with expert progress (VOC), the triplet loss demonstrated strong positive correlations and generally outperformed VIP-based methods, but all objectives, including triplet loss, struggled significantly on more complex multi-step tasks like 'door-open' (e.g., L_Triplet multi-view VOC was **6.70%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A simple triplet loss, which aligns language goals with later images and pushes earlier images away, proved surprisingly effective and robust in VLM-based reward modeling.
> - The performance advantages observed in prior, more complex learning objectives might be primarily due to variations in pre-training data, model architectures, and evaluation setups, rather than the inherent sophistication of the objectives themselves.
> - VIP-based objectives showed inconsistent and often poor performance, struggling to capture monotonic progress accurately, highlighting limitations in their design for reward modeling.
