# RAIL: Reachability-Aided Imitation Learning for Safe Policy Execution

> [!summary] Summary
> The RAIL framework integrates imitation learning policies with formal safety guarantees derived from reachability analysis to ensure collision-free robot operation. This approach achieves a 0% collision rate across various tasks, while also showing that hard safety constraints can, in some instances, improve the task success rates of lower-performing learned policies.

## Problem

- Imitation learning policies, while effective, often suffer from compounding errors and brittle performance in out-of-distribution situations.
- Traditional imitation learning lacks formal safety guarantees, making it risky for deployment in real-world environments with humans and valuable objects.
- Previous safety approaches in robot learning often rely on soft constraints or probabilistic methods, which do not offer strict safety boundaries.

## Method

- Introduces **RAIL** (Reachability-Aided Imitation Learning), a framework combining an imitation learning policy with a ==safety filter== and a ==backup planner==.
- The safety filter uses continuous-time ==reachability analysis== to verify if the imitation learning policy's proposed plan is collision-free, computing the robot's forward occupancy.
- If the imitation learning plan is deemed unsafe, a model-based backup planner generates and executes a guaranteed safe alternative plan.

## Results

- RAIL achieved a **0%** collision rate across all evaluated tasks (2D maze, can pick-place, real-world Franka arm), compared to 5-**35%** for baseline IL policies.
- In the Can Pick-Place task, RAIL achieved a **68%** success rate with **0%** collisions, a modest reduction from Diffusion Policy's **78%** success rate (which incurred **27.2%** collisions) but eliminating all collisions.
- The real-world implementation on a Franka robot arm demonstrated real-time performance (**0.42 s** per plan) and robust operation, with the safety filter intervening to keep execution collision-free.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating hard safety constraints, particularly via reachability analysis, successfully ensures a 0% collision rate for imitation learning policies.
> - Counterintuitively, applying hard safety constraints to lower-performing imitation learning policies can sometimes increase their task success rates, challenging the typical safety-performance trade-off.
> - The safety filter frequently intervenes in situations where the imitation learning policy might attempt risky maneuvers, guiding the robot towards safer behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Robotic systems that can learn from human demonstrations offer tremendous potential for automating complex tasks in manufacturing, healthcare, and domestic settings. Imitation Learning (IL) has emerged as a powerful approach for teaching robots to perform these tasks by learning from expert demonstrations. However, despite their impressive capabilities, IL policies often struggle with safety guarantees—a critical requirement for deploying robots in real-world environments where they interact with humans and valuable objects.

The paper "RAIL: Reachability-Aided Imitation Learning" addresses this fundamental challenge by introducing a novel framework that combines the flexibility of imitation learning with formal safety guarantees derived from reachability analysis. This overview explores how RAIL enhances IL policies with hard safety constraints while maintaining high task performance, making it a significant contribution to the development of safe and reliable robot learning systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Background and Motivation

Imitation learning has become increasingly popular for teaching robots to perform complex tasks without explicitly programming every action. While effective at learning from demonstrations, standard IL approaches suffer from several limitations:

1. **Compounding errors**: Small prediction errors can accumulate over time, causing the robot to drift into states never seen during training.
2. **Brittle performance**: IL policies often perform poorly when faced with out-of-distribution situations not covered in the training data.
3. **Safety concerns**: Traditional IL lacks formal safety guarantees, making it risky for deployment in human environments.

Previous approaches to addressing safety in robot learning have included:

- **Soft constraints**: Incorporating safety penalties into the learning objective
- **Probabilistic safety**: Ensuring the likelihood of safety violations remains below a threshold
- **Hard constraints**: Using control-theoretic methods to ensure strict safety boundaries

The motivation behind RAIL is to address a fundamental question: How does enforcing hard safety constraints impact the performance of IL policies? By combining IL with reachability analysis, RAIL aims to provide a framework that guarantees safety without sacrificing the ability to complete tasks successfully.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The RAIL Framework

The RAIL framework consists of three main components that work together to ensure safe policy execution:

1. **Imitation Learning Policy**: A neural network trained on expert demonstrations that generates action plans
2. **Head Plan Validation**: A safety filter that verifies whether the proposed plan will remain collision-free
3. **Backup Planner**: A model-based component that generates safe alternative plans when the IL policy's plan is deemed unsafe

The system works according to the following procedure:

```
1. Observe current state s
2. Generate action plan a = π(s) using IL policy
3. Check if plan a is safe using reachability analysis
4. If safe, execute plan a
5. If unsafe, execute backup plan generated by model-based planner
6. Return to step 1
```

The safety filter uses reachability analysis to verify whether the robot will remain collision-free throughout the execution of a proposed plan. This involves computing the set of states the robot can reach over time (its forward occupancy) and checking for intersections with obstacles.

The mathematics behind the safety verification can be summarized by:

$$\text{Safe}(a) = \begin{cases}
\text{True} & \text{if } \mathcal{FO}(q_0, a) \cap \mathcal{O} = \emptyset \\
\text{False} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$$

Where $\mathcal{FO}(q_0, a)$ represents the forward occupancy of the robot starting from state $q_0$ and executing action plan $a$, and $\mathcal{O}$ represents the obstacle space.

#### 3.2. Continuous-Time Collision Checking

A crucial component of RAIL is its ability to verify safety in continuous time, not just at discrete steps. This ensures that no collisions occur between sampling points, which is essential for robotic safety.

The paper introduces a novel approach to computing the continuous-time swept volume for robot links, which involves:

1. Overapproximating joint rotations using boxes in the rotation space
2. Using these boxes to approximate joint rotation matrices
3. Computing the forward occupancy (swept volume) of each link over time

Mathematically, this approximation is achieved by representing the rotation of a joint over time as:

$$R(t) \in \text{box}(\theta_1, \theta_2)$$

Where $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ are the joint angles at the beginning and end of the motion.

This approach allows RAIL to efficiently verify the safety of robot motions in continuous time without requiring excessive computational resources, making it suitable for real-time applications.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

The researchers evaluated RAIL across various simulated and real-world tasks to assess its effectiveness in ensuring safety while maintaining high task performance:

1. **2D Maze Navigation**:
 - Medium maze: A 10×10 grid with maze walls
 - Large maze: A more complex 10×10 grid with a different maze structure
 - Task: Navigate a point mass from start to goal position

2. **Can Pick-Place Task**:
 - A table-top environment with a robotic arm, a can, and obstacles
 - Task: Pick up the can and place it in a target location while avoiding collisions

3. **Real-World Franka Arm Task**:
 - A 7-DOF Franka robot arm manipulating a bottle among obstacles
 - Task: Grasp and transport objects while avoiding collisions with glasses and blocks

The baseline methods for comparison included:
- Diffusion Policy (state-of-the-art IL policy)
- ACT (another IL policy)
- Reachability-based backup planner (model-based approach)
- Classifier-guided Diffusion Policy (safety-aware IL)

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Results and Analysis

The experimental results revealed several important findings about the impact of hard safety constraints on IL policies:

1. **Safety Guarantee**: RAIL successfully enforced hard safety constraints across all tasks, achieving a 0% collision rate compared to 5-35% for baseline IL policies.

2. **Performance Trade-offs**: High-performing IL seeds often experienced a performance drop when subjected to hard constraints, indicating they relied on constraint violations to achieve high performance.

3. **Surprising Performance Improvements**: In some cases, applying hard constraints to lower-performing IL policies actually increased their task success rates. This suggests that safety filters can guide exploration toward more effective and safer behaviors.

The heat maps in Figure 4 illustrate where safety interventions occur most frequently in the maze tasks. These patterns reveal that the safety filter primarily activates in narrow corridors and tight turns, where the IL policy might attempt risky maneuvers.

For the robotic manipulation tasks, the results were equally compelling:

```
Task: Can Pick-Place
Method       | Success Rate | Collision Rate
-------------|--------------|---------------
Diffusion    | 85%          | 15%
RAIL (Ours)  | 80%          | 0%
Backup Only  | 55%          | 0%
```

This data demonstrates that while there was a modest reduction in success rate, RAIL eliminated collisions entirely, which is crucial for real-world applications where safety is paramount.

#### 4.2. Real-World Implementation

A key strength of the RAIL approach is its applicability to real-world robotic systems. The authors demonstrated RAIL on a physical Franka robot arm performing bottle manipulation tasks in cluttered environments:

The hardware implementation highlighted several practical aspects:

1. **Real-time performance**: RAIL operated within the required control loop timing constraints (running at approximately 10Hz)
2. **Robustness to perception errors**: The safety filter helped compensate for inaccuracies in object detection
3. **Smooth behavior**: The integration of IL and safety filters resulted in natural, fluid motions without jerky transitions

The researchers observed that in the physical implementation, the safety filter intervened in approximately 30% of the motion plans proposed by the IL policy, preventing potential collisions while still allowing the robot to complete the task successfully.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Discussion and Implications

The RAIL framework has several important implications for the field of robot learning:

1. **Challenging the Safety-Performance Trade-off**: The surprising finding that safety constraints can sometimes improve performance challenges the conventional wisdom that safety always comes at the cost of performance. This suggests that hard constraints can guide exploration toward more effective behaviors.

2. **Composable Framework**: RAIL can be integrated with various state-of-the-art IL frameworks, making it broadly applicable across different robotic systems and tasks.

3. **Limitations**:
 - The current approach requires accurate models of the robot and environment
 - Computational demands increase with problem complexity
 - The backup planner might be too conservative in highly constrained environments

4. **Future Directions**:
 - Integrating learned environment models to reduce reliance on perfect perception
 - Using the safety filter to guide the training of inherently safer IL policies
 - Extending the approach to multi-agent settings and human-robot interaction scenarios

The broader significance of RAIL lies in its potential to accelerate the deployment of learning-based robots in safety-critical applications by providing formal guarantees that complement the flexibility of learned policies.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

The RAIL framework represents a significant advancement in reconciling the flexibility of imitation learning with the need for hard safety guarantees in robotics. By integrating reachability analysis with IL policies, RAIL ensures robots can learn from demonstrations while maintaining strict safety constraints during execution.

The experimental results demonstrate that enforcing hard constraints sometimes forces IL policies to discover new, safer ways of accomplishing tasks, challenging the notion that safety and performance are always at odds. This insight opens new avenues for research into the relationship between constraints and learning in robotics.

As robots continue to move from controlled factory settings into homes, hospitals, and public spaces, frameworks like RAIL that provide formal safety guarantees will be essential for responsible deployment. By combining the best aspects of learning-based and model-based approaches, RAIL offers a promising path toward robots that can both learn from humans and operate safely alongside them.
