# Contrast, Imitate, Adapt: Learning Robotic Skills From Raw Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> CIA is a three-stage framework (Contrast-Imitate-Adapt) for learning robot manipulation skills from raw human videos without action labels. It trains an interaction-aware alignment transformer to learn task priors, a trajectory GAN to imitate action priors, then adapts via GAN inversion plus limited real interaction guided by trajectory semantic directions. Achieves up to 90% success across six real-world tasks, beating offline RL and behavior cloning baselines.

## Problem

- Behavior cloning methods learning from human videos require paired robot action labels, which raw internet videos never provide, limiting scalability beyond lab-collected demos.
- Prior reward-learning-from-video methods demand consistent viewpoints and similar layouts between human and robot execution videos, restricting generalization to new scenarios.
- Most RL methods explore vast action spaces from scratch instead of exploiting action priors in videos, causing low sample efficiency and unsafe real-world interaction.

## Method

- **IAAformer**: an ==interaction-aware alignment transformer== with self- and ==cross-attention== over action and object encoders, trained with a ==hindsight interaction contrastive loss== and ==temporal monotonic loss== to temporally align video pairs and yield alignment-distance rewards.
- **TrajGAN**: a transformer-based ==GAN== trained on retargeted human wrist trajectories (relative to a Pelvis anchor point) to imitate action priors and generate 7-dimensional trajectories independent of viewpoint.
- **Inversion-Interaction** adaptation: ==GAN inversion== initializes coarse trajectories from object position, then a ==Trajectory-Semantic Cross-Entropy Method (TS-CEM)== samples unsupervised semantic directions in TrajGAN's latent space for safe, sample-efficient refinement using IAAformer-computed rewards.

## Results

- CIA reaches **90%** success on Grasp and up to **97%** on Close-refrigerator across six real tasks after two interaction iterations, versus **27-57%** for CQL and behavior-cloning baselines.
- IAAformer beats TCC, LAV, and CASA on phase classification, phase progression, and Kendall's Tau, e.g. **78.94%** vs **63.07%** phase classification on Open-refrigerator.
- Ablations show removing GAN inversion drops success to **0.27/0.17** (Grasp/Open-drawer), removing interaction drops to **0.43/0.37**, and removing IAAformer as reward drops to **0.37/0.43**, versus **0.90/0.87** for full CIA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating task priors (what constitutes correct progress) from action priors (how to move) lets a system learn from viewpoint- and embodiment-mismatched demonstrations without paired actions.
> - Latent-space semantic-direction sampling gives a principled, low-dimensional action space for safe real-world exploration, avoiding random search in raw high-dimensional trajectory space.
> - GAN inversion doubles as a fast confidence estimator: large inversion residual flags scenarios too far from training distribution before risking real-world execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The acquisition of general-purpose manipulation skills by intelligent agents in open environments is a significant challenge in robotics. Traditional approaches, such as Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL), have achieved notable successes in specific tasks like grasping and pouring. However, these methods often encounter difficulties when scaling to diverse real-world scenarios. RL methods frequently rely on simulations, which can limit generalization to varied physical environments. IL methods, while effective, typically require labor-intensive data collection through teleoperation or kinesthetic teaching, confining their applicability to controlled laboratory settings.

In contrast, human videos offer a vast, readily available, and diverse data source for robot learning, featuring various viewpoints, operating embodiments, and object instances. The challenge lies in extracting actionable knowledge from these unstructured, "raw" videos. Prior work on learning from videos has attempted to address this. Some IL methods, like Behavior Cloning, map visual inputs to robot actions, but they necessitate corresponding action labels, which are generally unavailable in raw human videos. Other RL approaches learn reward functions from videos, for instance, by training video classifiers or embodiment-agnostic representations to measure similarity between robot executions and human demonstrations. These methods, however, often impose strict requirements for consistent viewpoints and similar environmental layouts between human and robot videos, limiting their flexibility. Furthermore, many RL methods explore action spaces from scratch, leading to low sample efficiency and extended training times.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by aiming to overcome these identified limitations. It seeks to develop a framework that can learn robotic skills from raw human videos without requiring explicit robot actions, consistent viewpoints, or similar layouts, while also addressing issues of sample efficiency. The work integrates concepts from self-supervised temporal alignment for understanding task progression and generative models for action prior learning, followed by a sample-efficient policy improvement method. This approach contributes to enhancing the generalization capabilities of robots in real-world environments by leveraging the richness of internet-scale human video data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to learn diverse manipulation skills directly from raw human videos and to generalize these learned skills effectively to novel, previously unseen scenarios. The authors identify several critical issues in existing approaches that motivate their proposed framework:

1. **Absence of Robot Action Labels:** Many imitation learning methods rely on demonstrations that include corresponding robot action data. However, raw human videos typically lack such labels, making direct application of these methods impractical and limiting their scalability beyond controlled laboratory environments.
2. **Viewpoint and Layout Consistency Requirements:** Current reward learning methods for reinforcement learning often assume consistent viewpoints or similar environmental layouts between the human demonstration videos and the robot's execution videos. This assumption restricts the utility of diverse human videos and limits the scenarios in which a robot can operate.
3. **Low Sample Efficiency in Reinforcement Learning:** Standard reinforcement learning algorithms, especially when exploring large action spaces, often exhibit low sample efficiency. This translates to extensive interaction time with the real environment, which can be both time-consuming and potentially hazardous for physical robots.
4. **Limited Generalization to Novel Scenarios:** Many existing methods struggle to generalize learned skills to diverse real-world settings that differ from the training environment, including variations in object instances, positions, and overall scene layouts.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a core insight: to learn *task priors* by contrasting video pairs, and *action priors* by imitating trajectories extracted from videos. These task priors are then utilized to guide the robot's actions, enabling adaptation to novel scenarios. This insight forms the foundation of their three-stage framework, Contrast-Imitate-Adapt (CIA), which aims to provide a comprehensive solution for robust and generalizable robotic skill learning from human video demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Contrast-Imitate-Adapt (CIA), is a three-stage skill learning system designed to extract knowledge from raw human videos and adapt it to novel robotic execution scenarios.

**A. Priors Extraction and Transformation**
The initial stage focuses on processing raw human videos to extract relevant information and transform it into a format usable by the robotic system.

1. **Extracting Human Priors**: State-of-the-art computer vision models are employed:
 - **Object Detection**: Grounding DINO is used to detect bounding boxes of task-relevant objects ($B_{obj}$).
 - **3D Human Pose Estimation**: OSX, a 3D human mesh recovery model, estimates the joint rotation ($\theta_{body}$) of the SMPL-X human model, from which 3D ($P_{3D}$) and 2D ($P_{2D}$) keypoints are computed. Wrist rotation ($\theta_{wrist}$) is also derived.
 - **Contact Detection**: A Faster-RCNN-based model predicts the binary hand-object contact state ($c$).
 - **Data Smoothing**: Savitzky-Golay filtering is applied across timesteps to mitigate noise in single-frame detections.

2. **Transforming to Learnable Action Priors**: To address varying viewpoints and coordinate systems, a transformation is applied:
 - The Pelvis joint of the SMPL-X model serves as a human anchor point.
 - A fixed vertical offset point of the robotic arm base is designated as the robot origin.
 - The root body rotation is set to zero to mitigate viewpoint influence.
 - Relative 3D position ($P'_{3D}$) and wrist rotation ($\theta'_{wrist}$) from the Right Wrist joint to the Pelvis joint are calculated through forward kinematics. These relative trajectories, termed "action priors," are designed to be deployable to robots.
 - The final set of priors includes $P'_{3D}$, $\theta'_{wrist}$, $P_{2D}$, $c$, $B_{obj}$, and corresponding cropped object images ($I_{obj}$).

**B. Learning Task Priors through Temporal Alignment (IAAformer)**
The Interaction-Aware Alignment transformer (IAAformer) learns task priors by establishing frame-by-frame consistency between video pairs, thereby enabling it to measure the quality of robot execution against human demonstrations.

1. **Architecture of IAAformer**: IAAformer comprises three modules:
 - **Interaction-Aware Transformer Encoder**: This module models hand-object interaction. It takes action priors ($P_{2D}$, $P'_{3D}$, $\theta'_{wrist}$, $c$) and object information ($B_{obj}$, $I_{obj}$) as inputs. An Action Encoder processes action priors with a nonlinear projection layer, learnable positional embeddings, and a self-attention mechanism. An Object Encoder extracts features from cropped object images using a pre-trained Resnet50 and concatenates them with bounding box features. Crucially, **Cross-Attention mechanisms** explicitly model the interaction by querying object features with action keys and values, integrating object changes into action representations.
 - **Temporal Transformer Encoder**: Following a 1D convolutional layer, standard multi-head self-attention and MLP blocks model global temporal dependencies across the sequence.
 - **Projection Head**: A layer normalization and projection head merge frame features into a 128-dimensional embedding ($z^h_t$).

2. **Matching Loss**: Self-supervised losses are used to train IAAformer:
 - **Temporal Cycle-Consistency (TCC) Loss**: Addresses unknown frame-level correspondences between two videos ($V^h_i$, $V^h_j$). It finds the nearest neighbor of a frame in one video within the other, and then checks if cycling back leads to the original frame. A soft nearest neighbor is calculated using softmax and cosine similarity with a temperature parameter ($\lambda_{temp}$) to enable gradient backpropagation.
 - **Hindsight Interaction Contrastive Loss ($L_{hic}$)**: Aims to improve interaction awareness by generating failure experiences from successful human demonstrations. Hindsight augmentation involves:
 - *Action Augmentation*: Adding Gaussian noise to 3D body joint rotations or replacing action features with those from other videos.
 - *Object Augmentation*: Replacing object features of one video with those from another.
 The loss maximizes similarity between positive pairs and minimizes it with negative samples, including the hindsight augmented features.
 - **Temporal Monotonic Loss ($L_{mon}$)**: Ensures the monotonicity of temporal alignment by minimizing deviations in a differentiable matching index, which averages frame indices weighted by matching probabilities.
 - **Total Loss**: $L_{total} = \gamma_{tcc}L_{tcc} + \gamma_{hic}L_{hic} + \gamma_{mon}L_{mon}$, with empirically determined weights.

**C. Policy Learning by Inversion-Interaction**
This stage learns a policy to imitate actions and adapt to novel scenarios, using the IAAformer for reward generation.

1. **Trajectory Generation Model (TrajGAN)**: A transformer-based Generative Adversarial Network (TrajGAN) is trained to generate complex trajectories (3D keypoints, wrist rotation, gripper state). It samples an 8-dimensional Gaussian noise vector and outputs a trajectory. A contact discriminator ($D_c$) is added to predict the true probability of contact points.

2. **GAN Inversion for Fast Trajectory Initialization**:
 - To adapt TrajGAN-generated trajectories to specific environmental conditions (e.g., target object positions), optimization-based GAN inversion is used.
 - Given an environmental variable ($VE$) representing the target object's position, the latent variable ($v$) of the pre-trained TrajGAN is optimized to minimize the distance between $VE$ and the contact points ($C(\tau)$) in the generated trajectory ($\tau = G(v)$). This provides a coarse, task-relevant initial trajectory.
 - A success confidence score ($\theta_{success}$) is computed from the inversion loss to reject executions in scenarios too far from the training data distribution.

3. **Safe and Efficient Trajectory Refinement via Interaction (TS-CEM)**:
 - The initialized trajectory may still require refinement due to minor inaccuracies. A Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) variant, Trajectory Semantic-based Cross-Entropy Method (TS-CEM), is introduced for safe and sample-efficient policy improvement through real-world interaction.
 - **Unsupervised Semantic Direction Discovery**: The authors leverage the property that GANs spontaneously encode semantic attributes in their latent space. By analyzing the weight matrix ($A$) of the first fully connected layers of TrajGAN, semantic directions ($n_k$) are discovered as eigenvectors of $A^T A$ corresponding to the largest eigenvalues. These directions allow for controllable editing of trajectories.
 - **Optimization Procedure**:
 - Starting from the initialized latent variable ($v_{init}$), TS-CEM samples semantic directions ($n_k$) and editing scales ($\beta$) from learned Gaussian distributions ($\mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \sigma_k)$ and $\mathcal{N}(\mu_\beta, \sigma_\beta)$).
 - New trajectories ($\tau_{new} = G_\theta(v_{init} + \beta n_k)$) are generated and executed on the robot.
 - **Reward Function**: The IAAformer computes the alignment distance ($Rew = \sum ||z^r_t - \hat{z}^h_t||^2$) between the robot's execution video ($V^r$) and a randomly selected human demonstration ($V^h$).
 - Trajectories are ranked by reward, and the top-performing "elite" trajectories are used to update the distribution parameters ($\mu_k, \sigma_k, \mu_\beta, \sigma_\beta$) for the next iteration, promoting efficient exploration and refinement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental results across six real-world manipulation tasks using a Realman RM65 robotic arm with an Inspire five-finger dexterous hand.

**Overall Performance and Comparison to Baselines:**
- **Superior Performance:** CIA consistently and significantly outperformed several state-of-the-art baselines, including Behavior Cloning (BC), Conservative Q-learning (CQL) with a video classifier (CQL-CLS), CQL with CASA representations (CQL-CASA), and CQL trained with IAAformer rewards (CQL-ours).
- **Iterative Improvement:** CIA demonstrated iterative improvement in success rates. For instance, in most tasks, CIA with two interaction iterations achieved success rates exceeding 90%, whereas initial attempts (CIA-w/o-Iter) were considerably lower.
- **Baseline Limitations:** Offline RL methods (CQL variants) struggled to complete tasks and showed poor generalization. The BC method also exhibited inaccuracies, particularly at critical contact points. The superior performance of CQL-ours over other CQL variants indicated the effectiveness of IAAformer's reward function in identifying quality interaction trajectories.

**IAAformer Evaluation and Temporal Alignment:**
- **Enhanced Alignment:** IAAformer outperformed previous temporal alignment methods such as TCC, LAV, and CASA across three metrics: Phase Classification, Phase Progression, and Kendall's Tau. This was demonstrated on both "Open refrigerator" and "Grasp" tasks.
- **Importance of Interaction Modeling:** The explicit modeling of hand-object interaction through self- and cross-attention mechanisms, along with the inclusion of object information as input, was critical for IAAformer's superior performance. Methods relying solely on image features or 3D human skeletons (like CASA) exhibited limitations due to a lack of explicit interactive cues.
- **Loss Function Effectiveness:** Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of the proposed loss functions:
 - The hindsight interaction contrastive loss ($L_{hic}$) improved the representation's ability to understand the task process, enabling IAAformer to distinguish mismatches even with minor action augmentations.
 - The temporal monotonic loss ($L_{mon}$) ensured consistent alignment order over time.
 - Attention mechanisms further enhanced overall temporal alignment performance.

**Trajectory Initialization and Controllable Editing:**
- **Effective Initialization:** GAN inversion proved effective for fast trajectory initialization. By minimizing the distance between environmental target object positions and the generated trajectory's contact points, it rapidly provided coarse but relevant trajectories for diverse tasks like Grasp, Open refrigerator, and Open drawer.
- **Semantic Control:** Unsupervised discovery of semantic directions in TrajGAN's latent space enabled controllable and meaningful edits to generated trajectories. Experiments on the Grasp task showed that specific semantic directions could systematically modify trajectory attributes, such as movement along the z-axis, which is crucial for safe and targeted exploration in real environments.

**Ablation Analysis of Inversion-Interaction Components:**
- **Necessity of Initialization:** The "CIA-w/o-inversion" variant, which sampled trajectories randomly without GAN inversion initialization, performed poorly, underscoring the importance of efficient initialization for finding optimal trajectories.
- **Value of Interaction:** "CIA-w/o-interactions," relying only on initialization without subsequent policy improvement through interaction, achieved lower success rates than the full CIA framework, demonstrating the necessity of refinement.
- **IAAformer as Reward Function:** "CIA-w/o-IAAformer," which replaced IAAformer with a generic video classifier for rewards, also showed limited performance, validating IAAformer's role in providing an appropriate objective function for policy improvement.

**Adaptation Capabilities:**
- **Novel Scenario Layouts:** CIA successfully adapted to various scenario layouts, including different object positions and heights, and varied backgrounds, indicating robust generalization.
- **Novel Object Instances:** The framework demonstrated adaptability to diverse object instances, such as different types of fruits, beverage containers, and varying sizes of refrigerators and drawers. The success rates across tasks confirmed this ability.

**Failure Analysis:**
Identified failure modes included inaccuracies in matching generated trajectory contact points with environmental variables (e.g., drawer handles) and issues related to robot hand positions or postures (e.g., objects sliding out of the gripper), suggesting areas for future refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, Contrast-Imitate-Adapt (CIA), that addresses several limitations in the field of robotic skill learning from human video demonstrations. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Learning from Unlabeled, Diverse Data**: CIA offers a method for robots to learn complex manipulation skills directly from raw human videos, which typically lack corresponding robot action labels. This is a crucial step towards leveraging the vast and diverse repository of human activity videos available online, moving beyond the constraints of laboratory-collected, labeled demonstration datasets.
2. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities**: By decoupling task priors and action priors, and incorporating an adaptation stage, the framework improves the robot's ability to generalize to novel scenarios, object instances, and varying environmental layouts that differ significantly from the human demonstration videos. This generalization capacity is fundamental for deploying robots in unstructured, real-world environments.
3. **Robust Task Understanding through Interaction-Aware Alignment**: The proposed IAAformer contributes a novel self-supervised temporal alignment mechanism that explicitly models hand-object interactions. This enhanced understanding of task-relevant processes allows IAAformer to serve as a robust, non-human-supervised reward function for policy improvement, a significant advantage for autonomous robot learning.
4. **Sample-Efficient and Safe Real-World Interaction**: The Inversion-Interaction method, particularly the Trajectory Semantic-based Cross-Entropy Method (TS-CEM), provides a practical approach to policy refinement in real environments. By initializing trajectories through GAN inversion and intelligently exploring the latent space via unsupervised semantic directions, the method aims to enhance sample efficiency and ensure safer robot interaction during learning, mitigating risks associated with random exploration.
5. **Modular and Extensible Framework**: The three-stage Contrast-Imitate-Adapt structure offers a modular approach to skill learning. Each component addresses a specific challenge, and potential future advancements in areas like computer vision for prior extraction or generative models could be integrated to further improve the framework.

The ability to learn from abundant human video data, generalize effectively, and refine policies efficiently in real-world settings can accelerate the development of more capable and versatile robotic systems. This work lays groundwork for robots that can acquire a broader range of general-purpose manipulation skills relevant to daily life and various industrial applications. Future research directions suggested by the authors, such as integrating vision-language models for task priors and developing robust closed-loop control, indicate continued avenues for advancing robot autonomy based on this foundation.
