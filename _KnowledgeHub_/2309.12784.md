# Learning to Walk and Fly with Adversarial Motion Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> A learning-based method leveraging Reinforcement Learning and Adversarial Motion Priors enables an aerial humanoid robot to autonomously execute smooth, energy-efficient transitions between legged and aerial locomotion. The robot adaptively switches modes based on environmental context and terrain, achieving both human-like walking and optimized flying behaviors.

## Problem

- Developing autonomous and smooth transitions between legged and aerial locomotion for humanoid robots.
- Overcoming limitations of traditional methods like computationally intensive trajectory optimization or reliance on explicit state machines for mode switching.
- Addressing the challenge of enabling robots to implicitly decide when and how to switch locomotion modes based on environmental feedback.

## Method

- Proposed a ==Reinforcement Learning (PPO)== framework integrated with Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) to learn a control policy.
- Incorporated a dual reward function, combining task-specific goals (waypoint tracking, energy efficiency) with a style reward derived from a discriminator.
- Leveraged two distinct motion datasets as priors: human motion capture for walking and trajectory optimization for flying.

## Results

- The iRonCub robot autonomously performed smooth transitions between walking, flying, taking off, and landing in simulation.
- The robot exhibited terrain-aware locomotion, using an elevation map to switch to flying over inaccessible ground like wide gaps or high obstacles.
- Policies trained with both walking and flying motion priors achieved the most efficient locomotion, demonstrating lower thrust usage compared to policies with no priors or single priors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial Motion Priors enable the spontaneous emergence of multimodal locomotion and adaptive mode switching without explicit high-level planners or state machines.
> - The integration of both walking (human-like) and flying (optimized) motion priors is crucial for achieving efficient, versatile, and natural-looking multimodal behaviors.
> - A thrust penalization term in the reward function effectively guides the robot towards energy-efficient locomotion, preferring walking over flying when feasible.
