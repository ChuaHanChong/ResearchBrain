# AnySat: One Earth Observation Model for Many Resolutions, Scales, and Modalities

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified Earth Observation model called AnySat integrates diverse resolutions, scales, and modalities, employing self-supervised learning to achieve state-of-the-art results on several geospatial tasks while demonstrating strong generalization to unseen data. The model reduces computational costs for advanced environmental prediction by enabling training on highly heterogeneous datasets simultaneously.

## Problem

- Existing Earth Observation (EO) models require fixed input configurations, limiting their applicability across varied spatial resolutions, temporal frequencies, and spectral modalities.
- Current EO foundation models struggle to generalize across the full spectrum of EO data heterogeneity, necessitating extensive retraining for different data types.
- Lack of a single, unified framework to leverage the inherent spatial alignment of multimodal EO data from diverse sensors for robust self-supervised learning.

## Method

- Implements a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with a ==student-teacher== framework for self-supervised learning on multimodal EO data.
- Utilizes scale-adaptive spatial encoders that map diverse input patches into fixed-size embeddings, enabling resolution invariance without explicit input rescaling.
- Employs a comprehensive masking strategy (patch dropping, modality and temporal masking) during training to predict latent features from partial inputs, coupled with a contrastive loss.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on GeoPlex datasets, including +**0.9** weighted F1 on TreeSatAI-TS and +2.8 macro F1 for classification on PASTIS-HD.
- Demonstrated strong generalization by outperforming existing SOTA models on six external datasets, with notable improvements such as +10.2 mIoU on BraDD-S1TS and +11.0 Overall Accuracy on TimeSen2Crop.
- Exhibited robustness to unseen sensor configurations and modalities, achieving SOTA or competitive results on benchmarks with linear probing, demonstrating high feature transferability and computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning in a latent feature space via JEPA is more robust for heterogeneous and noisy EO data than traditional pixel-level reconstruction.
> - Simultaneous self-supervised training on a diverse collection of datasets (GeoPlex) fosters highly transferable and generalizable representations across regions and sensor types.
> - Scale-adaptive patch encoders effectively manage diverse spatial resolutions within a unified architecture, preventing information loss from resizing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Earth Observation (EO) is experiencing a revolution driven by the increasing availability of diverse satellite and aerial imagery. However, unlike traditional computer vision, which often deals with relatively uniform natural images (e.g., RGB from standard cameras), EO data presents immense variability. This heterogeneity spans multiple dimensions: **spatial resolution** (from sub-meter to kilometers per pixel), **temporal resolution** (single snapshots to daily or weekly time series), **spectral modalities** (RGB, infrared, SAR, multispectral, hyperspectral), and **scale** (individual objects to large geographical regions).

Existing EO foundation models, while powerful, typically suffer from a critical limitation: they are designed for and trained on data with specific input configurations. This means a model trained on Sentinel-2 optical time series cannot directly process high-resolution aerial imagery or SAR data without significant architectural modifications and extensive retraining. This "fixed input configuration" problem severely hinders their practical applicability and scalability, as it defeats the purpose of a "foundation model" that should ideally generalize across diverse data types. Recent efforts have tried to address parts of this challenge, such as models adapting to varying spectral resolutions (e.g., DOFA), temporal dynamics (e.g., OmniSat, UTAE), or spatial scales (e.g., ScaleMAE). However, none have comprehensively tackled the simultaneous variability across *all* these dimensions—resolutions, scales, and modalities—within a single, unified framework.

This paper directly addresses this crucial gap by proposing **AnySat**, a novel Earth Observation model designed to be truly versatile. It aims to transcend the limitations of current models by enabling simultaneous self-supervised training on highly heterogeneous data, integrating various resolutions, scales, and modalities. AnySat leverages the inherent spatial alignment of multimodal EO data, recognizing that different sensors observing the same geographical area share underlying semantics.

The methodological innovation lies in combining the **Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)** with **scale-adaptive spatial encoders**. JEPA, a self-supervised learning paradigm, moves beyond pixel-space reconstruction (common in masked autoencoders like MAE) to predict features in a latent embedding space. This is particularly advantageous for EO, where environmental factors (clouds, shadows, acquisition angles) make pixel-level reconstruction challenging and often less semantically robust. By reconstructing in feature space, AnySat can learn more consistent and meaningful representations despite observational noise. The scale-adaptive encoders further enhance versatility by handling inputs with widely differing spatial resolutions without requiring input resizing, a common source of inefficiency and information loss in other models.

By building on principles of self-supervised learning (which is well-suited to the abundance of unlabeled EO data) and addressing the multi-faceted heterogeneity problem, AnySat marks a significant step towards developing a truly **generalist foundation model for Earth Observation**. Such a model would drastically reduce the need for task-specific model development and large labeled datasets, accelerating progress in environmental monitoring and geospatial intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the research presented in "AnySat: One Earth Observation Model for Many Resolutions, Scales, and Modalities" is to **develop a single, highly versatile Earth Observation (EO) model capable of learning from and adapting to the intrinsic diversity of EO data across resolutions, scales, and modalities.** The authors aim to overcome the limitations of existing EO models that typically require fixed input configurations and extensive retraining for different data types.

The key motivations driving this research are:

- **Addressing Data Heterogeneity:** Earth Observation data is inherently complex and varied. It encompasses a vast spectrum of sensor types (optical, radar, lidar), spatial resolutions (from sub-meter aerial imagery to kilometer-scale satellite data), temporal frequencies (single-date images to dense time series), and spectral bands. Existing models struggle to accommodate this entire spectrum within a single framework.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Current Foundation Models:** While foundation models have shown great promise in computer vision, their application to EO is hampered by the fixed input requirements of most architectures. Current EO foundation models are often modality-specific or resolution-specific, requiring separate training pipelines for different data types. This necessitates retraining from scratch or significant adaptation, which is inefficient and counter-productive to the goal of a universal model.
- **Leveraging Multimodal Synergy through Self-Supervision:** The authors recognize that multiple observations of the same geographical area, even from distinct sensors, share underlying semantic information. This spatial alignment across modalities provides a rich source of self-supervision. The motivation is to harness this synergy to learn robust and generalizable representations without relying on vast amounts of costly human-labeled data.
- **Eliminating Modality-Specific Decoders:** A practical challenge in multimodal learning is the need for separate decoders for each modality if reconstruction is done in pixel space. This complicates model architecture and limits flexibility. By adopting a feature-predictive approach, the authors aim to bypass this requirement, making the model more streamlined and adaptable to a wider array of sensors.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** The ultimate motivation is to demonstrate that a unified, self-supervised model can not only handle diverse data but also achieve competitive, if not superior, performance on a wide range of downstream environmental monitoring tasks, including classification, segmentation, and change detection.
- **Enabling Seamless Integration of New Data:** The authors envision a model that can easily integrate new datasets for both training and inference, regardless of their specific characteristics, fostering the development of more adaptable and scalable geospatial AI solutions for global environmental monitoring.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AnySat’s methodology is built upon two core innovations: a novel multimodal Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) for self-supervised learning and scale-adaptive spatial encoders. These components enable the model to process and learn from highly heterogeneous Earth Observation data simultaneously.

#### 3.1. Architecture

The AnySat architecture processes multimodal observations of geographical tiles, which are first partitioned into spatially aligned patches.

- **Spatially Consistent Patching:** Input tiles of a fixed ground extent ($S \times S$ meters) are observed across multiple modalities ($M$). Each modality $m \in M$ has distinct resolution ($R_m$), temporal observations ($T_m$), and channel count ($C_m$). Tiles are subdivided into non-overlapping patches of a constant ground size ($P \times P$ meters). Crucially, all modalities share the same spatial patch layout, ensuring consistency despite differing pixel dimensions per patch.
- **Scale-Adaptive Patch Encoder ($\phi_{patch}$):** This module is designed to map any input patch token $x_p^m$ (representing patch $p$ in modality $m$) into a fixed-size embedding $f_p^m \in \mathbb{R}^E$, irrespective of its original resolution ($R_m$) or the chosen patch size ($P$). This adaptability is achieved through a three-stage process:
 1. **Subdivision into Sub-patches:** Each patch token is subdivided into fixed-size sub-patches of $\delta_m \times \delta_m$ pixels. The spatial dimensions of these sub-patches are then flattened. The size $\delta_m$ can vary per modality (e.g., 1 pixel for VHR images, 10 pixels for time series).
 2. **Modality-Specific Projection ($\phi_{proj}^m$):** Each flattened sub-patch is projected into an $E$-dimensional embedding using a modality-specific MLP. For multi-temporal modalities ($T_m > 1$), a Lightweight Temporal Attention Encoder (LTAE) collapses the temporal dimension before projection.
 3. **Shared Spatial Transformer ($\phi_{trans}$):** Positional encodings (based on ground sampling distance) are added to the sub-patch embeddings. A shared transformer network then aggregates these sub-patch embeddings into a single representation $f_p^m$ for the entire patch, using a CLS-like token. The critical insight here is that varying patch sizes $P$ only change the *number* of sub-patches fed to $\phi_{trans}$, not the sub-patch embedding size, enabling resolution invariance without input rescaling.
- **Modality-Combiner Network ($\phi_{comb}$):** This module integrates the individual modality embeddings $f_p^m$ from all available modalities into a unified multimodal representation $f_p^\star$ for each patch $p$. It uses a cross-attention-based architecture, similar to OmniSat, where positional encodings are added to each $f_p^m$, followed by self-attention blocks, and finally cross-attention to aggregate information into a single patch embedding.

#### 3.2. Training

AnySat employs a self-supervised training paradigm based on the JEPA framework, adapted for multimodal EO data. It utilizes a student-teacher network setup, where the student learns to predict the teacher’s embeddings.

- **Student-Teacher Framework:** The student network comprises $\phi_{patch}^S$, $\phi_{comb}^S$, and a predictor network $\phi_{pred}^S$. The teacher network consists of $\phi_{patch}^T$ and $\phi_{comb}^T$, with its weights being an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's weights. The teacher processes complete, unmasked inputs.
- **Masking Strategy:**
 - **Patch Dropping:** Random rectangular regions within a tile are selected, and all modalities for patches ($p \in K$) within these regions are completely dropped from the student's input.
 - **Modality & Temporal Masking:** For the *remaining* un-dropped patches ($p \in \bar{K}$), a subset of modalities is randomly masked ($L \subset \bar{K} \times M$), ensuring at least one modality remains unmasked per patch. Masked token embeddings are replaced by a learned fixed value $f_{mask}$. Additionally, 50% of timestamps are randomly masked for time series data.
- **Loss Functions:**
 - **Contrastive Loss ($L_{con}$):** An InfoNCE loss is applied to the student's patch embeddings ($f_p^m,S$) before masking. It encourages embeddings from different modalities of the *same* spatial patch to be similar, while being dissimilar to embeddings from *other* spatial patches, thus enforcing cross-modality consistency.
 - **JEPA Loss ($L_{JEPA}$):** The core self-supervised objective. The student's predictor network ($\phi_{pred}^S$) reconstructs the multimodal embeddings for the *dropped* patches ($p \in K$). This prediction ($f_p^\star,pred$) is compared via an $L_2$ distance to the corresponding embeddings generated by the teacher network ($f_p^\star,T$), which saw the full, unmasked input. This latent-space reconstruction avoids the complexities of pixel-level reconstruction in EO.
- **Training with Multiple Datasets (GeoPlex):** A significant aspect of AnySat is its ability to train on a collection of highly diverse datasets simultaneously using shared weights. The authors compiled **GeoPlex**, consisting of 5 multimodal datasets (TreeSatAI-TS, FLAIR, PLANTED, S2NAIP-URBAN, PASTIS-HD) featuring 11 distinct sensors across various resolutions, scales, and modalities. During training, a dataset and patch size are randomly selected per iteration, allowing the single AnySat model to learn from this broad spectrum of EO data.

#### 3.3. Downstream Tasks

After self-supervised pretraining, AnySat can be adapted to supervised downstream tasks through fine-tuning or linear probing.

- **Classification:** For tile-level classification, a `[CLS]` token is integrated into the combiner network, producing a tile-level embedding that is then fed to a linear classifier.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** To achieve pixel-level predictions, a dense feature map is created at the sub-patch scale by concatenating sub-patch embeddings ($\phi_{proj}^m$ outputs) with multimodal patch embeddings ($\phi_{comb}$ outputs). An MLP then maps these features to pixel-level logits. This approach leverages the fine-grained information from sub-patches.
- **Probing:** AnySat supports linear probing, where only a lightweight linear layer is trained on top of the frozen pretrained features. This evaluates the quality and transferability of the learned representations with minimal trainable parameters.
- **New Sensor Configurations:** AnySat demonstrates robust generalization. For missing modality channels, a learned scalar value is used. For entirely new sensors not present in the pretraining mix, a new projector ($\phi_{proj}^m$) is randomly initialized and fine-tuned along with other free parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AnySat demonstrates significant advancements in Earth Observation modeling, showcasing its versatility and robust performance across a wide array of tasks, datasets, and sensor configurations.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance on GeoPlex Test Sets:**
 - AnySat achieves new state-of-the-art (SOTA) results on two key GeoPlex datasets: **TreeSatAI-TS** (multilabel tree species classification, +0.9 weighted F1 score) and **PASTIS-HD** (crop mapping, +2.8 macro F1 for classification and +0.2 mIoU for semantic segmentation).
 - It also performs competitively, achieving near SOTA results on **PLANTED** and **FLAIR**, even against models trained on significantly larger or more specific datasets (e.g., ViViT models on 80% more data for PLANTED, or UT&T with access to full Sentinel-2 tiles for FLAIR).
 - Pretraining on GeoPlex consistently improves performance across these datasets, indicating that simultaneous training on diverse modalities and scales leads to richer and more robust feature representations, particularly beneficial for smaller datasets and classification tasks.

- **Exceptional Generalization to External Datasets:**
 - AnySat significantly outperforms existing SOTA models on six external datasets not included in its pretraining, demonstrating strong spatial and domain generalization capabilities. Notable improvements include:
 - **SICKLE** (crop mapping): +3.6 mIoU.
 - **BraDD-S1TS** (deforestation detection): +10.2 mIoU.
 - **TimeSen2Crop** (crop classification): +11.0 Overall Accuracy (OA).
 - These gains are particularly impressive given that GeoPlex primarily covers the Northern Hemisphere, while the external datasets span global regions.
 - AnySat also achieves SOTA or competitive results on GeoBench and PANGAEA-Bench benchmarks, often outperforming other foundation models.

- **Robustness to New Sensor Configurations:**
 - AnySat successfully adapts to unseen sensor configurations, such as Landsat-8 data in SICKLE requiring additional bands not present in its training mix, or TimeSen2Crop with only 9 of 10 Sentinel-2 bands.
 - It achieves SOTA performance even on datasets with modalities or configurations never seen during pretraining (e.g., single-date Sentinel images for **So2Sat** and **Sen1Flood11**, or single-pixel time series for **TimeSen2Crop**).
 - For entirely new sensor types, like HLS data in **HLS Burn Scar**, AnySat (after fine-tuning a new projector) outperforms models like Prithvi, which were trained on vast amounts of specific HLS imagery, highlighting its strong generalization capacity.

- **Efficacy of Linear Probing:**
 - The model’s features are highly expressive, as demonstrated by its strong performance with linear probing. AnySat linearly probed on BraDD-S1TS outperforms all specialized approaches, and on Sen1Flood11, it surpasses fine-tuned UperNet segmentation heads of other foundation models with orders of magnitude fewer trainable parameters. This underscores the quality and transferability of the self-supervised learned representations.

- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Contrastive Loss:** Essential for learning discriminative features, especially for classification tasks, as its removal led to a substantial decrease in classification performance (-4.3 F1 score).
 - **Sub-patches for Segmentation:** Crucial for fine-grained spatial information, with "naive" semantic segmentation (without utilizing sub-patch features) resulting in a 2.4 mIoU drop.
 - **Masking Strategy:** While block masking (JEPA's strategy) performed slightly better overall than random token dropping, the effects were less pronounced than in natural image contexts, and modality/temporal masking contributed to memory efficiency.

- **Computational Efficiency:** Pretraining on GeoPlex required 1760 GPU-hours (NVIDIA H100), with fine-tuning taking 10-40 hours and linear probing approximately 2 hours. Inference speed is competitive (3ms per mono-date tile), matching or exceeding specialized models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AnySat represents a pivotal advancement in Earth Observation research, offering a new paradigm for handling the inherent complexity and diversity of geospatial data. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Towards a Unified Earth Observation Foundation Model:** AnySat makes a substantial step towards realizing a truly generalist foundation model for Earth Observation. By demonstrating that a single model can learn robust representations from widely heterogeneous data (varying resolutions, scales, and modalities) simultaneously, it moves beyond the current fragmented landscape of task-specific or modality-specific models. This unified approach can simplify model development, reduce computational overhead for new tasks, and accelerate scientific discovery.

- **Enhanced Accessibility and Efficiency of EO Data Analysis:** The ability of AnySat to generalize across diverse sensor inputs and tasks with minimal fine-tuning or even linear probing significantly lowers the barrier to entry for advanced EO analytics. Researchers and practitioners will no longer need to retrain bespoke models for every new dataset or sensor combination, democratizing access to powerful AI tools for environmental monitoring, urban planning, agriculture, and disaster response. This efficiency gain is critical for large-scale, dynamic applications.

- **Robust Feature Learning and Transferability:** The impressive performance in linear probing scenarios, where only a small linear layer is trained, highlights that AnySat learns highly expressive and semantically meaningful features during self-supervised pretraining. These robust representations are highly transferable, allowing the model to adapt to unseen tasks and regions with high accuracy and low computational cost, even for novel sensor configurations.

- **Catalyst for Global Environmental Monitoring:** The model's demonstrated capacity for tasks like land cover mapping, crop type classification, deforestation detection, and flood/burn scar segmentation is directly applicable to urgent global environmental challenges. By providing more accurate and timely insights from diverse satellite data, AnySat can support better policy-making, resource management, and climate change mitigation efforts worldwide. Its strong spatial generalization, even to regions not covered in its training data, is crucial for truly global applications.

- **Advancing Self-Supervised Learning in EO:** AnySat's successful application of a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to multimodal EO data, combined with scale-adaptive encoders, provides a powerful architectural blueprint. This work validates the benefits of latent-space reconstruction over pixel-space methods for noisy and complex EO data, inspiring further research into more robust and generalized self-supervised learning techniques tailored for geospatial intelligence.

- **Value of Curated Heterogeneous Datasets:** The compilation of GeoPlex, a collection of diverse multimodal datasets, underscores the importance of high-quality, varied training data for developing generalist EO models. This contributes to the growing "data-centric AI" movement in Earth Observation, emphasizing that diversity and curation of data are as crucial as model architecture for achieving broad generalization.

- **Open Science and Reproducibility:** By making their code and models publicly available, the authors foster reproducibility and encourage further development and application within the broader scientific community. This openness is essential for building upon their foundational work and accelerating innovation in EO AI.

In summary, AnySat pushes the boundaries of Earth Observation AI by providing a robust, flexible, and efficient framework for processing the vast and heterogeneous landscape of geospatial data. Its impact will likely be felt across scientific research, environmental management, and commercial applications, paving the way for more comprehensive and accessible monitoring of our planet.
