# AnySat: One Earth Observation Model for Many Resolutions, Scales, and Modalities

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified Earth Observation model called AnySat integrates diverse resolutions, scales, and modalities, employing self-supervised learning to achieve state-of-the-art results on several geospatial tasks while demonstrating strong generalization to unseen data. The model reduces computational costs for advanced environmental prediction by enabling training on highly heterogeneous datasets simultaneously.

## Problem

- Existing Earth Observation (EO) models require fixed input configurations, limiting their applicability across varied spatial resolutions, temporal frequencies, and spectral modalities.
- Current EO foundation models struggle to generalize across the full spectrum of EO data heterogeneity, necessitating extensive retraining for different data types.
- Lack of a single, unified framework to leverage the inherent spatial alignment of multimodal EO data from diverse sensors for robust self-supervised learning.

## Method

- Implements a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with a ==student-teacher== framework for self-supervised learning on multimodal EO data.
- Utilizes scale-adaptive spatial encoders that map diverse input patches into fixed-size embeddings, enabling resolution invariance without explicit input rescaling.
- Employs a comprehensive masking strategy (patch dropping, modality and temporal masking) during training to predict latent features from partial inputs, coupled with a contrastive loss.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on GeoPlex datasets, including +**0.9** weighted F1 on TreeSatAI-TS and +2.8 macro F1 for classification on PASTIS-HD.
- Demonstrated strong generalization by outperforming existing SOTA models on six external datasets, with notable improvements such as +10.2 mIoU on BraDD-S1TS and +11.0 Overall Accuracy on TimeSen2Crop.
- Exhibited robustness to unseen sensor configurations and modalities, achieving SOTA or competitive results on benchmarks with linear probing, demonstrating high feature transferability and computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning in a latent feature space via JEPA is more robust for heterogeneous and noisy EO data than traditional pixel-level reconstruction.
> - Simultaneous self-supervised training on a diverse collection of datasets (GeoPlex) fosters highly transferable and generalizable representations across regions and sensor types.
> - Scale-adaptive patch encoders effectively manage diverse spatial resolutions within a unified architecture, preventing information loss from resizing.
