# SpecEyes: Accelerating Agentic Multimodal LLMs via Speculative Perception and Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> SpecEyes introduces an agentic-level speculative acceleration framework for multimodal large language models (MLLMs), employing a lightweight model to preemptively answer queries and a novel confidence gating mechanism to bypass costly iterative perception and planning loops. This approach significantly boosts MLLM throughput (up to 3.35x speedup) while maintaining or improving accuracy (up to +6.7%).

## Problem

- Agentic MLLMs, while capable of advanced reasoning through iterative visual tool invocation, suffer from a 'latency explosion' where response time scales linearly with agentic depth due to sequential dependencies.
- The stateful nature of iterative tool use in agentic MLLMs prevents effective GPU batching, leading to 'concurrency collapse' and underutilization of hardware, which severely limits system throughput.
- Existing acceleration methods primarily target individual token generation or reasoning steps, failing to address the fundamental sequential bottleneck imposed by the multi-turn perception and reasoning loop.

## Method

- **SpecEyes** employs a four-phase pipeline: ==heuristic tool-use judgment==, ==speculative prediction== by a small non-agentic MLLM, ==cognitive gating== via answer separability, and agentic fallback to a large MLLM for complex queries.
- A novel ==answer separability score== (specifically, the minimum separability across all tokens) is introduced as a robust, calibration-free confidence metric for the small MLLM to self-verify its speculative answers.
- A ==heterogeneous parallel serving architecture== processes stateless speculative phases in batch, funneling only the rejected or genuinely tool-requiring queries to the computationally intensive, sequential agentic MLLM.

## Results

- SpecEyes achieved an average speedup of **1.73x** and improved average accuracy from **81.39%** to **84.26%** when using DeepEyes as the agentic backbone, with a maximum speedup of **3.35x**.
- The framework demonstrated robust performance, notably increasing accuracy on POPE benchmarks (e.g., from **78.43%** to **85.13%** on Adversarial split) while achieving 2.13-**2.19x** speedup, suggesting mitigation of hallucination errors.
- The proposed 'min-token' answer separability score (S_min_sep) showed superior discriminability between correct and incorrect speculative answers compared to other confidence metrics, enabling an optimal accuracy-speedup trade-off.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial portion of queries directed at agentic MLLMs do not necessitate deep, multi-step tool-assisted reasoning and can be accurately resolved by a lightweight, tool-free model.
> - Standard probability-based confidence metrics are often miscalibrated and unreliable for critical gating decisions in a speculative system, necessitating a more robust, scale-invariant measure of decision margin.
> - Aggregating token-level confidence using the minimum separability score across all generated tokens acts as an effective 'worst-case guard,' ensuring accuracy is preserved by triggering fallback even for single ambiguous tokens.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has progressed from models that process visual information in a single pass to "agentic" MLLMs. Early MLLMs, such as those employing a static, single-pass visual perception paradigm, encoded an image once and generated a response without further visual interaction. Recent advancements have introduced an agentic paradigm, where MLLMs actively invoke external perception tools (e.g., zoom-in, crop, OCR) in iterative loops of perception, reasoning, and tool calling. This allows for progressive refinement of visual understanding and enables MLLMs to excel in complex visual tasks requiring fine-grained inspection, multi-step compositional reasoning, and active information seeking. Examples include OpenAI o3 and Gemini Agentic Vision.

While effective, this agentic paradigm introduces significant computational challenges. The iterative, stateful nature of tool invocation creates a sequential bottleneck, termed "agentic depth." Each step in the reasoning and tool-calling cycle depends on the observation from the previous step, leading to linear scaling of end-to-end response time with agentic depth. Furthermore, the per-query state mutation during tool use hinders GPU batching, limiting system-level concurrency and leaving hardware parallelism underutilized. This renders agentic MLLMs substantially slower than their non-agentic counterparts, posing a barrier to their practical deployment.

Existing approaches to enhance efficiency in LLMs and MLLMs have primarily focused on accelerating individual steps within a fixed reasoning trajectory. Token-level speculative decoding, for instance, uses a smaller draft model to propose tokens for a larger model to verify, accelerating token generation. However, these methods operate *within* the agentic pipeline, leaving the multi-turn perception and reasoning loop fully serial. Other techniques like multimodal token pruning, temporal compression, and adaptive computation methods aim to reduce per-step computation or bypass layers for easier inputs. While these improve per-step efficiency, they do not eliminate the repeated tool invocations that dominate agentic latency, nor do they question the necessity of the entire agentic loop for every query.

SpecEyes addresses this specific gap by proposing an "agentic-level" speculative acceleration framework. It re-conceptualizes speculation, moving beyond token or semantic levels, to bypass entire tool-use loops when appropriate. This work distinguishes itself by asking whether the agentic loop is necessary for *every* query, rather than merely accelerating components within it. By leveraging a lightweight, tool-free MLLM to rapidly provide speculative answers for a significant fraction of queries, SpecEyes aims to fundamentally break the sequential bottleneck and improve system throughput, positioning itself as a solution to a previously unaddressed system-level challenge in agentic MLLM deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for SpecEyes stems from the efficiency crisis observed in agentic multimodal large language models (MLLMs). These models, while demonstrating advanced reasoning capabilities through iterative visual tool invocation, suffer from significant sequential overhead. This overhead is characterized by:

1. **Latency Explosion:** Each query triggers a cascade of tool-calling steps, referred to as "agentic depth" (D). Because each step causally depends on the observations from the previous step, the entire process is strictly sequential. The end-to-end response time for a single query thus grows linearly with D, as each reasoning-and-tool cycle must complete before the next can commence.
2. **Concurrency Collapse:** The stateful nature of agentic MLLMs means that each query's tool-use chain modifies a unique, per-query state. This strict data dependency effectively nullifies the benefits of GPU batching for agentic operations, as the model can only advance one step at a time per query. Consequently, massive hardware parallelism remains idle, severely limiting system throughput under concurrent workloads.

These issues render agentic MLLMs orders of magnitude slower than their non-agentic counterparts, posing a fundamental barrier to their practical deployment in real-world applications where low latency and high throughput are critical.

The key insight motivating SpecEyes is the observation that a substantial fraction of queries directed at agentic MLLMs may not necessitate deep, multi-step, tool-assisted reasoning. For these queries, a lightweight, tool-free vision model could potentially provide correct answers directly from the initial image, provided a reliable mechanism exists to identify such queries. This insight leads to the concept of a heterogeneous "think fast, think slow" architecture.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of SpecEyes are:

1. **Break the Sequential Bottleneck:** To design an agentic-level speculative acceleration framework that can bypass entire tool-use loops for queries that do not require them, thereby mitigating the linear latency scaling and enabling higher concurrency.
2. **Preserve or Enhance Accuracy:** To achieve significant speedups without sacrificing the accuracy provided by the full agentic MLLM. The framework should ensure that complex queries still benefit from the deep reasoning capabilities of the large agentic model.
3. **Develop a Robust Cognitive Gating Mechanism:** To create a reliable, label-free, and scale-invariant method for the lightweight model to self-verify its confidence in a speculative answer. This mechanism is crucial for deciding whether to accept the speculative output or to fall back to the more expensive agentic model.
4. **Maximize System Throughput:** To design a serving architecture that leverages the stateless nature of the lightweight speculative model to process queries concurrently, converting the rate of speculative acceptance into multiplicative throughput gains.

In essence, SpecEyes aims to provide a practical solution to the efficiency challenges of agentic MLLMs by introducing a speculative paradigm at the agentic level, allowing for intelligent bypassing of costly computational pathways based on estimated query complexity and model confidence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpecEyes is proposed as an agentic-level speculative acceleration framework that addresses the stateful bottleneck of agentic MLLMs. The methodology involves formalizing this bottleneck, implementing a four-phase speculative pipeline, developing a cognitive gating mechanism, and designing a heterogeneous parallel serving architecture.

#### 3.1. Modeling the Stateful Bottleneck of Agentic MLLMs

Agentic MLLMs are formalized as stateful reasoning systems A = (S, T, π), where S is the state space, T is a set of perception tools, and π is a policy for tool invocation and token generation. A query `q` on image `I` generates a state trajectory {s₀, s₁..., s_D} over D steps, where `s₀ = (q, I)`. Each step `d` involves an action `a_d = π(s_d)` which either calls a tool `t_d` or generates a final answer. State transitions `s_{d+1} = f(s_d, t_d(s_d))` show that subsequent tool selections are causally dependent on prior observations (`p(a_{d+1} | s_{d+1}) ≠ p(a_{d+1} | s₀)`). This causal dependency makes the agentic pipeline inherently sequential, leading to an end-to-end latency `L_agent(q)` that scales linearly with agentic depth D (Eq. 3). At the system level, this strict serialization limits concurrency, bounding throughput `Θ_agent` by `B / ΣL_agent(q_i)` for a batch of B queries (Eq. 4). This formalization underscores the need to eliminate unnecessary tool invocations.

#### 3.2. SpecEyes: Agentic-Level Speculative Reasoning (Four-Phase Pipeline)

SpecEyes implements a four-phase pipeline to exploit the observation that many queries do not require deep agentic reasoning. It uses a small, non-agentic MLLM (M_S) for speculative answers and falls back to a large agentic MLLM (M_L = A) when necessary.

- **Phase I: Heuristic Tool-Use Judgment:** For an incoming query `q` and image `I`, the large agentic model M_L uses a lightweight binary classification head to determine if tool invocation is necessary (`g(q, I) ∈ {0, 1}`). A prompt `P_judge` guides M_L to assess tool necessity. Queries judged `g=0` (answerable from global image) proceed to Phase II; queries judged `g=1` (potential tool need) are immediately routed to Phase IV (agentic fallback). This phase is executed by M_L due to its tool-calling expertise but incurs negligible overhead.
- **Phase II: Speculative Prediction:** Queries passing Phase I (`g=0`) are forwarded to M_S. M_S generates a speculative answer `ˆy_S` and its full output logit distribution. This inference is stateless and can be performed concurrently for all queries in the batch, requiring no tool execution.
- **Phase III: Small MLLM Confidence Switching (Cognitive Gating):** The logits from Phase II are evaluated by a cognitive gating function `S_sep` (detailed below) to quantify M_S's confidence without ground-truth labels. A scalar separability score `S_sep(ˆy_S)` is computed. If `S_sep(ˆy_S) ≥ τ` (a calibrated threshold), the speculative answer is accepted and returned, completely bypassing the agentic pipeline. Otherwise, the query is rejected and proceeds to Phase IV.
- **Phase IV: Agentic Fallback:** Queries that either fail Phase I (judged `g=1`) or Phase III (rejected by cognitive gate) are routed to the full agentic model M_L. M_L executes its complete stateful perception-reasoning loop, performing multi-step reasoning with access to all tools. This phase acts as a safety net, mitigating accuracy loss for complex or ambiguous queries.

The end-to-end expected latency `E[L_SpecEyes]` is expressed as `c_J + β c_S + (1 − βα) L_agent` (Eq. 9), where `c_J` is judgment cost, `c_S` is small model cost, `β` is tool-free screening ratio, and `α` is cognitive gate acceptance rate. When `βα` is high, the latency is dominated by the lightweight front-end costs.

#### 3.3. Small MLLM Cognitive Gating via Answer Separability

The efficacy of SpecEyes relies on the confidence switching mechanism in Phase III. The paper proposes an "answer separability score" `S_sep`, addressing limitations of probability-based confidence metrics (e.g., geometric mean of max softmax probabilities `S_log`). `S_log` suffers from softmax miscalibration and high scores for low-entropy tokens, making it unreliable for critical gating.

- **Answer Separability Score:** For each token `n` with logit vector `ℓ^{(n)}`, the sorted logits are `ℓ^{(n)} ≥ ℓ^{(n)} ≥...`. The token-level separability `S^{(n)}_sep` is defined as `(ℓ^{(n)} − μ^{(n)}_K) / (σ^{(n)}_K + ϵ)` (Eq. 12). `μ^{(n)}_K` and `σ^{(n)}_K` are the mean and standard deviation of the top-K logits `{ℓ^{(n)}..., ℓ^{(n)}[K]}`. This metric quantifies the decision margin between the top prediction and its competitors, providing a scale-invariant signal that models the competitive landscape among candidates.
- **Token-to-Answer Aggregation:** Token-level `S^{(n)}_sep` scores are aggregated to an answer-level confidence. Three strategies are considered: mean (`S_mean_sep`), minimum (`S_min_sep`), and bottom-r fraction mean (`S_bottom_sep`). `S_min_sep` (the minimum separability score across all tokens) is adopted as the default, based on a risk-theoretic argument (Proposition 1). This strategy acts as a conservative "worst-case guard," triggering fallback if any token in the answer exhibits low separability, thus prioritizing precision and preserving agentic pipeline accuracy.

#### 3.4. Heterogeneous Parallelism for Throughput Acceleration

SpecEyes enhances system throughput by organizing the four phases into a heterogeneous parallel funnel.

- **Batch-Parallel Front-End:** Phase I (screening) and Phase II (speculative inference) are stateless, single-turn forward passes. They are fully batch-parallelizable, incurring a parallel front-end cost of `c_J + c_S` for a batch of B queries.
- **Funnel-Shaped Serving:** Accepted queries (`αβB`) are returned immediately. The remaining residual set `R` (gating-rejected `(1 − α)βB` and tool-required `(1 − β)B` queries) falls back to the sequential agentic execution of M_L. Since `c_J + c_S` is small compared to the agentic cost for a batch, the batch time is dominated by the agentic fallback for the size `|R| = (1 − βα)B`. This yields a throughput speedup `Θ_SpecEyes / Θ_agent ≈ 1 / (1 − βα)` (Eq. 16), which is jointly governed by the screening ratio `β` and the gate acceptance rate `α`.

#### 3.5. Experiment Setups

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations are conducted on V\* (Direct Attributes, Relative Position), HR-Bench (4K, 8K), and POPE (Adversarial, Popular, Random splits).
- **Models:** The small non-agentic model (M_S) is Qwen3-VL-2B. The large agentic model (M_L) is instantiated with DeepEyes and Thyme, both capped at 5 tool-use steps.
- **Implementation Details:** Greedy decoding (temperature 0) is used. Latencies include tool execution time. For cognitive gating, K=64, ϵ=10⁻⁶, and min-token aggregation is the default. The gating threshold τ is empirically calibrated for each benchmark. All experiments were run on a single NVIDIA A100 40 GB GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SpecEyes achieves significant speedups and throughput improvements while maintaining or enhancing accuracy across diverse multimodal benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

Table 1 presents the main results, comparing SpecEyes (with various confidence aggregation strategies) against agentic baselines (DeepEyes and Thyme) and SpecReason, using Qwen3-VL-2B as the speculative model.

- **SpecEyes (min) Performance:** Among the four confidence aggregation strategies tested, SpecEyes (min) consistently yields the strongest accuracy-speed profile.
 - **With DeepEyes backbone:** SpecEyes (min) achieves an average speedup of 1.73× and improves average accuracy from 81.39% to 84.26%.
 - On V\* Bench, it matches the baseline accuracy on Direct Attributes (90.43%) with 1.53× speedup and boosts Relative Position accuracy from 82.89% to 89.47% with 1.90× speedup.
 - POPE benefits most significantly, showing 2.13–2.19× speedup, with accuracy consistently above baseline (e.g., Adversarial: 78.43% → 85.13%). This suggests that bypassing unnecessary tool trajectories can also mitigate hallucination errors.
 - HR-Bench shows moderate speedups (1.08–1.13×), indicating that these queries more frequently require fine-grained, tool-assisted inspection.
 - **With Thyme backbone:** SpecEyes (min) yields an average speedup of 1.42× and increases accuracy from 82.29% to 83.99%. The per-benchmark patterns are similar, with POPE showing the highest speedups (1.70–1.78×), V\* providing solid gains (1.32–1.42×), and HR-Bench remaining more constrained (0.95–1.01×). The marginal sub-1× speedup on HR-Bench 8K occurs when the `βα` value is low, causing the fixed cost of running M_S to slightly exceed savings.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines

- **SpecReason:** This method, which accelerates individual reasoning steps, consistently decelerates overall inference (0.37–0.61× with DeepEyes; 0.43–0.53× with Thyme). This is attributed to the small model's lack of structured tool-calling capability and the overhead incurred from additional tokens and turns (averaging 414 tokens and 3.48 rounds). SpecReason also showed sharp accuracy degradation on POPE (as low as 49.10%). In contrast, SpecEyes bypasses entire tool-use chains, avoiding this overhead.
- **Qwen3-VL-2B (draft only):** This configuration, where only the small model generates answers, provides an upper bound on speedup (4.13× average) but at a notable accuracy cost (78.93% average). SpecEyes demonstrates the ability to capture a significant portion of these latency savings while preserving the full reasoning quality of the large agentic model.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Confidence Calibration (Figure 3)

The discriminability of confidence scores between correct and incorrect samples from M_S on V\* was assessed.
- `S_log` (probability-based) and `S_mean_sep` (mean separability) exhibited small peak distances (∆) and heavy overlap between correct and incorrect distributions, indicating poor discriminability due to softmax overconfidence or averaging effects.
- `S_bottom_sep` improved ∆ by focusing on low-separability tokens but still showed residual overlap.
- `S_min_sep` achieved the largest ∆, showing a sharp bimodal separation where incorrect samples clustered at low scores and correct samples formed a distinct high-score mode. This supports Proposition 1 and explains why `S_min_sep` facilitates an optimal accuracy-speedup trade-off by enabling a single threshold to maximize acceptance while preserving accuracy.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Gating Threshold (Figure 4):** Varying the gating threshold for `S_min_sep` demonstrated a clear trade-off: lowering the threshold monotonically increased the acceptance ratio and speedup, while accuracy degraded gracefully. V\* and POPE maintained accuracy near or above baseline over a wide threshold range, confirming that many queries can be safely bypassed. HR-Bench was more sensitive, with accuracy dropping below 0.97, reflecting a higher proportion of genuinely tool-requiring queries. The threshold acts as a smooth control knob for navigating the accuracy-efficiency Pareto front.
- **Batch Size (Figure 5):** Increasing batch size consistently improved end-to-end speedup, with accuracy remaining unchanged. This aligns with the heterogeneous funnel design, where the stateless speculative stage is highly batchable and its per-query overhead is amortized. Diminishing marginal speedup gains were observed at larger batch sizes as the largely sequential, stateful agentic fallback stage became the bottleneck. Datasets with higher bypass rates (V\*, POPE) benefited more from batching.
- **Top-K in Separability Computation (Figure 6):** Increasing the K parameter (number of top logits considered) in `S_sep` monotonically improved speedup but degraded accuracy. Larger K values diluted the contrastive signal, inflating confidence estimates. K=64 was chosen as a balanced default, providing strong speedup while matching baseline accuracy on V\* Direct Attributes and achieving good performance on Relative Position.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SpecEyes framework addresses a critical and previously overlooked system-level bottleneck in agentic multimodal large language models (MLLMs): the inherent sequential and stateful nature of iterative tool invocation. This bottleneck imposes prohibitive latency and limits system concurrency, hindering the practical deployment of advanced agentic MLLMs.

The significance of this work lies in several key aspects:

1. **Novel Paradigmatic Shift:** SpecEyes introduces the concept of agentic-level speculative acceleration, extending the speculation paradigm from individual tokens or semantic steps to entire agentic pipelines. This allows for the intelligent bypassing of potentially extensive and costly tool-use loops when they are not strictly necessary.
2. **Robust Confidence Mechanism:** The introduction of the answer separability score (S_sep) provides a novel, calibration-free, and scale-invariant method for the lightweight speculative model to assess its own confidence. The "min-token aggregation" strategy, acting as a worst-case guard, ensures that accuracy is preserved even when accelerating inference, a crucial aspect for maintaining the reliability of agentic systems.
3. **Efficient System Design:** The heterogeneous parallel funnel architecture effectively exploits the stateless concurrency of the small speculative model to mask the stateful, serial execution of the large agentic model. This design converts per-query latency savings into significant system-level throughput gains, which is vital for serving agentic MLLMs under concurrent workloads.
4. **Tangible Performance Improvements:** Extensive experiments demonstrate substantial speedups (up to 3.35×) over agentic baselines while preserving or even improving accuracy (up to +6.7%). This quantitative improvement signifies a step towards making powerful agentic MLLMs more feasible for real-world applications requiring rapid responses and high user concurrency.
5. **Reduced Computational Footprint:** By judiciously avoiding unnecessary resource-intensive tool invocations, SpecEyes has the potential to reduce the overall computational cost and energy consumption associated with running agentic MLLMs, contributing to more sustainable AI development and deployment.

The potential impact of SpecEyes extends to various domains where agentic MLLMs could be deployed, such as intelligent assistants, automated visual analysis, and interactive systems. By making these models faster and more efficient, SpecEyes contributes to their broader adoption and integration into real-time applications. The work also opens avenues for future research, such as "multi-depth speculation," which could further enhance efficiency by allowing the speculative model a bounded number of lightweight tool calls, thereby reducing fallbacks even for moderately complex queries. This iterative refinement of the speculative paradigm promises continued advancements in the efficiency of agentic AI systems.
