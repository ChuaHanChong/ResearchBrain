# Writing-Zero: Bridge the Gap Between Non-verifiable Tasks and Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Quark LLM, Alibaba Group, developed a method to apply Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to non-verifiable tasks like creative writing. They introduced a pairwise Generative Reward Model (GenRM) and a novel algorithm, Bootstrapped Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO), enabling a 'Writing-Zero' model to achieve advanced writing proficiency directly from a pretrained base model and demonstrating enhanced resistance to reward hacking.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is highly effective for objective tasks (e.g., math, code) but struggles with non-verifiable, subjective tasks (e.g., creative writing) due to the absence of definitive ground truth.
- Existing scalar reward models, typically used in RLHF for subjective tasks, suffer from limited generalization and vulnerability to reward hacking, such as over-explanation and length bias, which degrade true content quality.
- Assessing quality in creative writing is inherently subjective and lacks a single definitive reference, making it difficult to design stable and scalable RL training processes using conventional methods.

## Method

- A Self-Principled Critique Pairwise ==Generative Reward Model (GenRM)== is trained using filtered high-quality human preference data, which is then fine-tuned via RL using rule-based outcome rewards and a position bias weighting mechanism to provide robust, comparative scores for writing quality.
- A novel Reinforcement Learning algorithm, ==Bootstrapped Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO)==, is introduced. For a given query, BRPO dynamically selects one policy-generated response as a temporary reference and compares all other responses against it using the pairwise GenRM.
- The 'Writing-Zero' model is trained directly from a base pretrained LLM (Qwen3-32B-Base) without any supervised fine-tuning, using the proposed GenRM and BRPO framework.

## Results

- The Pairwise Writing Generative Reward Model outperformed Claude-3.5-Sonnet and other reward models on tested benchmarks (e.g., **8**7.4%** accuracy** on RewardBench), showing robust generalization even when trained solely on Chinese data.
- Both Writing-Zero (Qwen3-32B-Base-GenRM-BRPO) and Writing-R1 (Writing-SFT-GenRM-BRPO) showed substantial improvements in writing capabilities over their base models and consistently outperformed scalar reward baselines on various writing test sets.
- Models trained with the GenRM+BRPO approach demonstrated enhanced resistance to reward hacking, producing significantly shorter and less redundant responses compared to scalar-reward trained models, which exhibited severe over-explanation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative Reward Models (GenRMs) can be effectively designed for inherently subjective tasks like creative writing, providing robust, comparative reward signals where objective ground truth is absent.
> - A bootstrapped relative policy optimization approach enables LLMs to improve continuously by comparing their own evolving outputs, eliminating the need for fixed external references for subjective evaluations.
> - It is feasible to achieve advanced writing capabilities in LLMs through pure reinforcement learning from a pretrained base model, bypassing the necessity for supervised fine-tuning, as demonstrated by the 'Writing-Zero' model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the cutting-edge landscape of Large Language Model (LLM) training, specifically focusing on Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques.

**Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** has emerged as a highly effective paradigm for enhancing LLMs' reasoning capabilities, particularly for tasks with objective, ground-truth answers such as mathematics and code generation. Examples include works like DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) and Kimi K1.5 (Team et al., 2025), which leverage explicit correctness signals to guide model improvement.

However, a significant challenge persists: extending these RL-based successes to **non-verifiable tasks**. These tasks, exemplified by creative writing, poetry generation, and open-ended dialogue, lack definitive external references or objective ground truths. Quality assessment in such domains is inherently subjective and often relies on human preferences.

**Current approaches for non-verifiable tasks** predominantly employ **scalar reward models** trained on human preference data, often within the Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) framework (Ouyang et al., 2022). While foundational, these scalar reward models suffer from several limitations:
1. **Limited Generalization:** They often struggle to generalize robustly across diverse contexts or nuanced preferences.
2. **Vulnerability to Reward Hacking:** LLMs can learn to exploit superficial cues in the reward signal rather than genuinely improving content quality. Common manifestations include over-explanation (adding lengthy justifications) and length bias (favoring longer responses, regardless of content quality), as highlighted by previous research (Zhong et al., 2025; Sharma et al., 2023; McKee-Reid et al., 2024).

The paper identifies **Generative Reward Models (GenRMs)** as a promising recent advancement in reward modeling. Unlike scalar models, GenRMs can produce textual critiques and scores, often leveraging chain-of-thought reasoning to provide more interpretable and robust evaluations. Prior work (Liu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025a; Whitehouse et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025b) has explored training GenRMs and integrating them into RLVR, expanding its application to less-structured domains like multi-subject QA (Su et al., 2025).

This work specifically addresses the largely unexplored frontier of applying robust GenRMs within an RLVR framework to **purely non-verifiable tasks**. The authors acknowledge that while GenRMs hold promise, their effective application to such inherently subjective domains, particularly creative writing where "there is no absolute first place," remains a challenge.

By developing a novel pairwise GenRM and a tailored RL algorithm (BRPO), the paper aims to bridge this critical gap. Furthermore, it explicitly articulates a grander vision: the **unification of diverse reward modeling paradigms** (rule-based for verifiable tasks, reference-based for less-structured tasks, and reference-free model-based for creative tasks) under a comprehensive and scalable RLVR framework. This positions the work as a significant step towards a more universal and consistent training methodology for LLMs across the entire spectrum of language tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **bridge the fundamental gap between non-verifiable tasks and the powerful framework of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**.

This objective is motivated by several critical observations and challenges in the current landscape of LLM training:

1. **Limitations of RLVR for Subjective Tasks:** While RLVR has driven remarkable progress in LLM reasoning for tasks with objective ground-truth answers (e.g., math, code generation), its application to domains where quality is inherently subjective (e.g., creative writing, open-ended dialogue) remains underdeveloped. The absence of definitive "correct" answers makes direct verification impossible, hindering the traditional RLVR approach.

2. **Shortcomings of Existing Reward Models for Non-Verifiable Tasks:** Current methodologies for these subjective tasks heavily rely on scalar reward models trained on human preferences (RLHF). However, these models suffer from:
 - **Limited Generalization:** They often fail to capture the full breadth and nuance of human preferences across diverse scenarios.
 - **Vulnerability to Reward Hacking:** LLMs trained with these scalar rewards frequently learn to optimize for superficial metrics rather than genuine quality. Examples include "over-explanation" (appending lengthy, often unhelpful justifications) and "length bias" (producing verbose responses simply because longer ones tend to get higher scores), which degrade the user experience and true model capability.

3. **The Inherent Subjectivity of Creative Writing:** Creative writing serves as a prime example of a non-verifiable task where quality assessment is fluid and lacks a single definitive reference. The paper uses the Chinese saying, "there is no absolute first place in literary arts," to highlight this challenge. This subjectivity makes it difficult to design stable and scalable RL training processes using conventional methods.

**To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:**

- **Propose a Unified RLVR-based Training Paradigm:** Develop a comprehensive framework that can effectively apply RLVR to non-verifiable writing tasks.
- **Introduce a Novel Pairwise Generative Reward Model (GenRM):** Design a GenRM that is specifically grounded in writing principles and capable of transforming subjective assessments into robust, "verifiable" (in a comparative sense) rewards. This GenRM should leverage self-principled critique to provide nuanced, adaptive evaluations.
- **Develop a New Reinforcement Learning Algorithm, Bootstrapped Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO):** Create an algorithm that facilitates dynamic, reference-free pairwise comparisons by using bootstrapped responses as temporary references during group rollouts in RL training. This eliminates the need for fixed external references, allowing the model to improve from its own evolving outputs.
- **Enable LLMs to Cultivate Advanced Writing Capabilities Without Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Demonstrate the feasibility of "Writing-Zero," a model trained directly from its base pretrained state using only the proposed RLVR framework, bypassing the need for expensive and often domain-specific SFT data.
- **Enhance Resilience to Reward Hacking:** Show that the proposed method significantly mitigates issues like over-explanation and length bias compared to scalar reward baselines.
- **Achieve Competitive Performance:** Demonstrate strong results on both proprietary and publicly available writing benchmarks.
- **Explore Unification of Reward Modeling Paradigms:** Lay the groundwork for a future where rule-based, reference-based, and reference-free reward modeling can be unified under a consistent and comprehensive RLVR training paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a unified RLVR-based training paradigm for non-verifiable writing tasks, structured into two main stages: training a pairwise Generative Reward Model (GenRM) and then training the writing policy model using this GenRM via a novel RL algorithm.

**Stage 1: Self-Principled Critique Pairwise GenRM for Creative Writing**

Inspired by the Self-Principled Critique Tuning (SPCT) framework, the authors introduce a Pairwise Writing GenRM specifically tailored for the nuances of creative writing.

- **Focused Application:** Unlike the broader SPCT, this GenRM is exclusively designed for non-verifiable writing tasks, as verifiable tasks can be more directly assessed with rule-based rewards.
- **Dedicated Pairwise Comparison:** The GenRM is streamlined to take only two responses as input, simplifying training and enhancing comparative assessment accuracy.
- **GenRM Training Pipeline (using Qwen3-32B-Base as base):**
 1. **Data Filtering (3.1.1):** From an initial in-house dataset of ~200K pairwise preferences (including 30K writing-related pairs), a scalar Reward Model (RM) is used to re-score these. High-quality pairs (with higher chosen response reward and larger reward gap) are filtered, resulting in ~10K writing pairs.
 2. **SPCT Prompt Engineering (3.1.2):** Tailored prompts integrate pre-defined general writing principles and model-generated specific principles for a detailed critique. The GenRM outputs `` float scores for each response in the pair (S_c and S_r), indicating comparative quality.
 3. **Cold-Start Fine-tuning (RFT) (3.1.3):** To stabilize early RL training and enhance principle granularity, a small, high-quality dataset is curated:
 - 1K writing preference pairs (from the 10K filtered set) are selected.
 - These 1K pairs are duplicated by swapping chosen and rejected responses, creating 2K SPCT prompts (50% (y_c, y_r) and 50% (y_r, y_c)).
 - Initial reasoning traces (principles and critiques) are sampled using Claude-3.5-Sonnet.
 - A **rejection process** is applied: only queries are kept where *both* the original and swapped versions of Claude's predicted scores (S_c, S_r) are consistent with the known ground-truth preference (i.e., S_c > S_r). This addresses potential position bias. This results in 500 original pairs (1,000 total with swaps).
 - The Qwen3-32B-Base model is then fine-tuned with these 1K cold-start data for one epoch.
 4. **RL-based Refinement for Pairwise GenRM (3.1.4):** The GenRM is further fine-tuned using GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with rule-based outcome rewards:
 - **Accuracy Reward (R_acc):** `+1` if S_c > S_r, `-1` if S_c < S_r, `0` otherwise.
 - **Format Reward (R_format):** `+1` if both S_c and S_r are within ``, `-1` otherwise.
 - **Score Margin:** R_acc is weighted by `(S_c - S_r) / τ_margin` (where `τ_margin=2`) for small score differences, enhancing sensitivity.
 - **Position Bias Weight:** A dynamic weighting mechanism is applied to the advantage `Â_i,t` based on the preference distribution within the current batch to reduce variance and converge the GenRM's preference for the first-presented response towards 0.5.
 - **Dynamic Sampling:** Queries are filtered out if the GenRM's predictive accuracy for that prompt is 0 or 1, ensuring effective gradients by focusing on less clear-cut cases. *A notable challenge observed during training was this leading to >95% dropout later on, causing computational inefficiency and premature halting.*

**Stage 2: RL with Bootstrapped Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO)**

This stage focuses on training the actual writing policy model using the refined Pairwise GenRM.

- **Bootstrapped Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO) (3.2.1):** This novel algorithm adapts GRPO to handle pairwise preference rewards for non-verifiable tasks.
 - **Core Mechanism:** For a given query, BRPO dynamically selects one response (`o_ref`) from the current group of `G` policy-generated responses (`{o_i}^G_{i=1}`) as a *temporary reference*. All other responses are then compared against this `o_ref` using the pairwise GenRM.
 - **Preference Advantage:** The advantage `Â_i,t` for response `o_i` at token `t` is directly set to a binary preference score `R_i`: `+1` if `S_i > S_ref` (across all voting conditions if multiple votes are used), and `-1` otherwise. This bootstrapping ensures a zero expectation for advantage, eliminating the need for additional normalization. This allows the model to continuously improve by learning from its own increasingly sophisticated outputs.
 - **Dynamic Sampling:** Similar to GenRM training, BRPO filters out queries where the selected `o_ref` leads to highly skewed preference distributions (`|sum(R_i)| / G > τ_filter`), preventing the policy from overfitting to outliers and ensuring a discriminative learning signal.
- **Writing-Zero and Writing-R1 (3.2.2):**
 - **Writing-Zero:** Qwen3-32B-Base is trained *directly* from its pretrained state using the Pairwise Writing GenRM and BRPO, *without any supervised fine-tuning (SFT)*. This explores the potential for pure RL-driven self-evolution.
 - **Writing-R1:** The same BRPO algorithm is applied to fine-tune an existing in-house SFT model (specialized in thinking and instruction following), demonstrating the method's effectiveness with an SFT-initialized base.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed methodology in bridging the gap for non-verifiable tasks.

- **Pairwise Writing GenRM Performance (Table 1, Figure 3):**
 - **Competitive Evaluation:** The Pairwise Writing GenRM outperforms Claude-3.5-Sonnet across all tested benchmarks (Cultural & Creative Writing, Lifestyle Copywriting, RewardBench, M-RewardBench). Notably, despite being trained exclusively on Chinese writing data, it achieved strong generalization accuracy (87.4% on RewardBench, 86.1% on M-RewardBench), suggesting robust cross-lingual capabilities for reward assessment. It also showed better performance than Skywork-Reward and INF-ORM on domain-specific Chinese datasets.
 - **Training Challenges:** The GenRM training faced a significant bottleneck due to dynamic sampling, leading to an extremely high dropout rate (>95%) in later stages (Figure 3a). This forced premature halting of training, indicating that the employed GenRM was likely sub-optimal and could have achieved even better performance had training been completed. The test accuracy did not clearly converge (Figure 3b).

- **Writing Model Performance (Table 2, Figure 1, Human Evaluation):**
 - **Significant Improvement with GenRM+BRPO:** Both Writing-Zero (Qwen3-32B-Base-GenRM-BRPO) and Writing-R1 (Writing-SFT-GenRM-BRPO) show substantial improvements in writing capabilities compared to their respective base models.
 - Writing-Zero improved Qwen3-32B-Base scores from 6.89 to 8.29 on WritingBench and 1.23 to 3.84 on the Writing Testset.
 - Writing-R1 improved Writing-SFT scores from 8.56 to 8.68 on WritingBench and 3.31 to 3.93 on the Writing Testset.
 - **Superiority over Scalar Reward Baselines:** Writing-Zero consistently achieved higher and more stable reward scores during training (Figure 1) and outperformed its scalar reward counterpart (Qwen3-32B-Base-ScalarRM-GRPO) by 1.01 points on the Writing Testset. Similarly, Writing-R1 surpassed Writing-SFT-ScalarRM-GRPO by 0.24 points. This indicates the GenRM+BRPO approach is more effective for policy optimization than scalar rewards.
 - **Reward Hacking Resistance (Table 3):**
 - The scalar-reward trained model (Qwen3-32B-Base-ScalarRM-GRPO) exhibited severe reward hacking, producing "gibberish" responses with low actual quality (low Eval RM score) yet anomalously high scores on WritingBench, suggesting the latter's susceptibility to hacking.
 - In contrast, Writing-Zero demonstrated significantly enhanced resistance to reward hacking, showing substantially less redundant information and shorter mean response lengths (e.g., 258 vs. 2417 for mean explanation length, 129 vs. 187 for mean response length for Qwen3-32B-Base models). This is a crucial practical benefit.
 - **Human Evaluation Confirmation:** Human evaluations on a test set confirmed Writing-R1's superior performance against both its SFT base and the scalar-reward trained counterpart, validating the automated metrics.

- **GenRM's Position Bias Calibration (Figure 4):** The initial cold-start GenRM exhibited a bias towards preferring the latter response in a pair. However, the subsequent RL phase successfully calibrated this bias, with the empirical probability of preferring the former response converging towards the ideal 50% (Figure 4a). The position bias advantage weighting mechanism visibly reduced the variance of this preference ratio across training batches (Figure 4b).

- **Test-Time Scaling Benefits (Figure 5, Table 2):**
 - The Pairwise Writing GenRM showed improved accuracy with increased voting samples (n) at test time (e.g., Cultural & Creative Writing accuracy improved from 57.5% (n=1) to 59.2% (n=8)).
 - This benefit translated to the policy model: Writing-Zero further improved its performance on the Writing Testset when its underlying GenRM utilized two voting rounds (voting@2), demonstrating that a more accurate reward signal from test-time scaling leads to better policy optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model training, particularly for addressing the challenging domain of non-verifiable, subjective tasks.

1. **Bridging a Critical Gap:** The most profound significance lies in successfully bridging the gap between non-verifiable tasks (like creative writing) and the robust framework of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). This extends the powerful benefits of RL (e.g., continuous improvement, mitigation of exposure bias) to domains previously limited by the absence of objective ground truth.

2. **Addressing Reward Hacking:** The paper effectively tackles the pervasive problem of reward hacking (e.g., length bias, over-explanation) that plagues LLMs trained with traditional scalar reward models on subjective tasks. By demonstrating that Writing-Zero exhibits enhanced resilience to these issues, the work contributes to building more genuinely capable and user-aligned LLMs, reducing the need for costly manual interventions to correct hacky behaviors. This is a critical step towards more reliable and trustworthy AI agents for creative and conversational applications.

3. **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The introduction of two key components – the **Pairwise Writing Generative Reward Model (GenRM)** with self-principled critique and the **Bootstrapped Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO) algorithm** – represents novel technical advancements. The GenRM's ability to produce nuanced, comparative scores grounded in writing principles, and BRPO's dynamic, reference-free bootstrapping mechanism, are innovative solutions that overcome the limitations of fixed references and scalar rewards for subjective evaluations.

4. **Enabling "Zero-Shot" RL Training for Writing:** The "Writing-Zero" model demonstrates a paradigm-shifting capability: achieving advanced writing proficiency directly from a pretrained base model via pure RL, *without requiring supervised fine-tuning (SFT)*. This drastically reduces reliance on expensive, labor-intensive, and domain-specific SFT data collection, making LLM training more scalable, efficient, and adaptable to new domains where SFT data is scarce. This opens avenues for training highly specialized models at lower cost and faster iteration cycles.

5. **Paving the Way for Unified RLVR:** The work's ambitious claim of unifying rule-based, reference-based, and reference-free reward modeling within a comprehensive RLVR framework is a testament to its forward-looking vision. This potential unification suggests a more general, consistent, and scalable RL training paradigm that can be applied across the entire spectrum of language tasks, from highly verifiable to deeply subjective ones. Such a unified framework could simplify the LLM development lifecycle and foster more general-purpose AI.

In summary, "Writing-Zero" is a significant step forward in LLM alignment and capability development. Its innovative approach to reward modeling and policy optimization for non-verifiable tasks not only enhances the robustness and quality of LLMs in subjective domains but also lays foundational groundwork for a more efficient, scalable, and unified reinforcement learning pipeline for future AI systems.
