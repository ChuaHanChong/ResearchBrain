# Solving Deep Reinforcement Learning Tasks with Evolution Strategies and Linear Policy Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> A study from Leiden University evaluated Evolution Strategies (ES) against gradient-based Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) using linear policy networks across various tasks. The work shows ES successfully optimize linear policies for many RL benchmarks, often outperforming DRL methods when both are restricted to linear architectures, and achieving performance comparable to deep DRL on select complex environments.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) often requires complex algorithms, extensive training durations, and large neural network architectures.
- Reproducibility of DRL results is a challenge, frequently influenced by random seeds and hyperparameter choices.
- There is a question whether the complexity commonly associated with current DRL solutions is always necessary for established benchmark tasks.

## Method

- Benchmarked three ==Evolution Strategies== (**CSA-ES**, **sep-CMA-ES**, **CMA-ES**) against prominent gradient-based DRL algorithms (**DQN**, **PPO**, **SAC**) and ==Augmented Random Search (ARS)==.
- Optimized ==policy networks== consisting of a single ==linear layer== for ES, ARS, and constrained linear DRL versions across classical control, Box2D, MuJoCo, and Atari environments.
- Employed game Random Access Memory (RAM) state as input for Atari tasks to evaluate ==linear policies== without complex visual processing.

## Results

- ES and ARS consistently found optimal linear policies for classical control tasks (e.g., CartPole, Acrobot), often outperforming deep DRL, which struggled with linear policies.
- For Atari games using RAM input, ES methods frequently achieved higher scores than linear DQN and, in some cases (e.g., Atlantis, Boxing, SpaceInvaders), matched or exceeded the performance of deep pixel-based DQN.
- Linear ES algorithms consistently outperformed their linear gradient-based counterparts across various MuJoCo tasks, with CMA-ES finding linear policies for Humanoid-v4 that were competitive with deep SAC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolution Strategies (ES) can effectively optimize linear policy networks, frequently outperforming gradient-based DRL when DRL is similarly constrained to linear architectures.
> - The complexity of deep neural networks commonly used in DRL might be unnecessary for many benchmark tasks, as simpler linear policies often suffice.
> - ES offer advantages such as algorithmic simplicity, inherent parallelizability, and a lower propensity to converge to local optima compared to gradient-based DRL methods.
