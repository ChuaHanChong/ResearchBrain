# EmbodiedSplat: Personalized Real-to-Sim-to-Real Navigation with Gaussian Splats from a Mobile Device

> [!summary] Summary
> EmbodiedSplat personalizes embodied navigation by reconstructing the deployment environment from a handheld iPhone capture: DN-Splatter trains depth- and normal-regularized 3D Gaussian Splats, Poisson reconstruction yields a mesh loaded into Habitat-Sim, and a pre-trained ImageNav policy is fine-tuned inside it for 20M steps. Real-world success rises 20% over HM3D and 40% over HSSD zero-shot baselines, with 0.87-0.97 sim-vs-real correlation.

## Problem

- Synthetic simulation datasets like HSSD lack real-world photorealism and scale, so policies trained inside them generalize poorly to physical deployment environments.
- Real-scan datasets such as Matterport3D and HM3D need costly capture rigs and labor-intensive pipelines, making per-deployment scene collection impractical and biasing coverage toward apartments.
- Prior Gaussian-Splatting navigation work stays modular or simulation-only, never demonstrating end-to-end policy learning plus real-robot sim-to-real transfer in the captured deployment scene.

## Method

- Capture stage: 20-30 minute handheld iPhone RGB-D recording via Polycam, processed in Nerfstudio into 1000 low-blur posed RGB-depth frames, plus a Polycam mesh for comparison.
- Reconstruction stage: **DN-Splatter** trains ==3D Gaussian Splats== for 30k iterations with sensor-depth weight 0.2, smoothness and Metric3D-V2 ==normal regularization==, then ==Poisson reconstruction== produces a Habitat-Sim-ready mesh.
- Training stage: largest-navmesh-island episode generation (1000 train, 100 validation per scene), then 20M-step ==fine-tuning== of a ==VC-1== LSTM ==ImageNav== policy at learning rates 2.5e-6 and 6e-7.

## Results

- Real lounge deployment on a Stretch robot: HM3D zero-shot **50%** success rises to **70%** after fine-tuning; HSSD zero-shot **10%** rises to **50%** (Polycam) and **40%** (DN mesh).
- In simulation, 20M-step fine-tuning lifts HM3D-pretrained policies above **90%** and HSSD-pretrained above **80%** per scene, versus roughly **60%** and far lower zero-shot scene averages.
- Sim-vs-real correlation reaches **0.87-0.97**; scratch-trained overfitted policies still hit **50%** real success on the Polycam mesh but only **10%** on the DN mesh.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A short consumer-phone capture plus brief fine-tuning can beat large-scale pretraining alone: personalization to the deployment scene outweighs raw dataset size.
> - Longer pretraining stops paying off - zero-shot transfer to captured scenes plateaus or degrades past 400M steps while in-domain validation keeps improving.
> - Reconstruction visual fidelity, not geometry alone, drives transfer: success correlates positively with splat validation PSNR and negatively with scene scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI primarily relies on simulated environments for the training and evaluation of robotic agents. A persistent challenge in this domain is the "sim-to-real gap," which refers to the discrepancy between performance in simulation and performance in real-world deployment. This gap arises from several factors:

- **Simulation Fidelity:** Existing simulation environments often use either fully synthetic scenes that lack the photorealism and complexity of the real world (e.g., HSSD) or high-fidelity real-world reconstructions that are labor-intensive and expensive to create (e.g., Matterport3D, HM3D). Synthetic environments struggle to capture real-world variability, while high-fidelity reconstructions are not easily scalable or adaptable to novel deployment scenarios.
- **Accessibility and Cost:** Capturing real-world environments for simulation typically requires specialized and expensive hardware (e.g., Matterport cameras) and extensive post-processing, making it impractical for rapid prototyping or personalization to diverse, transient deployment settings.
- **Personalization:** Policies trained on large, general datasets may not perform optimally in specific, unseen real-world environments due to domain shift. The ability to quickly adapt or personalize policies to a target environment is crucial for practical robot deployment.

Recent advancements in 3D scene representation, particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS), offer a potential solution by enabling high-quality scene reconstruction from casual, consumer-grade hardware like mobile phones. These methods provide high visual fidelity and efficient novel-view synthesis. While GS has been explored for various applications, its potential for generating simulation environments for embodied robot navigation, specifically for addressing the sim-to-real transfer problem in an accessible manner, has remained largely unaddressed. Prior works using GS for navigation, such as GaussNav and SplatNav, have focused on specific navigation tasks or modular policy components, often without demonstrating end-to-end policy learning or robust sim-to-real transfer for image-goal navigation in indoor environments.

EmbodiedSplat aims to bridge this gap by leveraging these advances in 3D reconstruction to create a streamlined, cost-effective pipeline for personalized policy training and deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether low-effort mobile phone video captures can be utilized to generate 3D meshes suitable for training Embodied AI navigation policies, thereby facilitating effective sim-to-real transfer to a target environment. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

- **Addressing the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The primary motivation is to mitigate the sim-to-real gap that hinders the deployment of embodied agents from simulation to real-world robots. The authors propose that personalized training within reconstructed target environments can improve real-world performance.
- **Improving Accessibility and Scalability of Scene Capture:** Current methods for creating high-fidelity simulation environments are costly and time-consuming. The research aims to develop an efficient and cost-effective pipeline using readily available consumer-grade hardware (smartphones) to democratize scene collection and enable rapid adaptation of policies to new environments.
- **Enabling Policy Personalization:** Recognizing that general pre-trained policies may not generalize well to out-of-distribution real-world scenarios, the work seeks to enable the personalization of navigation policies. This involves training agents directly on high-fidelity 3D models of the specific deployment environment, constructed from low-cost data.
- **Exploring 3D Gaussian Splatting for Embodied AI:** The research is motivated by the potential of 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) to generate high-quality scene representations efficiently. The authors aim to explore its application in creating simulation-ready meshes for end-to-end policy learning in image-goal navigation, a largely unexplored area compared to its use in other robotic tasks.
- **Comprehensive Analysis of Training and Reconstruction Strategies:** Beyond simply demonstrating a pipeline, the authors are motivated to conduct a detailed analysis. This includes evaluating the impact of different mesh generation pipelines (GS-based vs. Polycam), mesh quality, pre-training datasets (synthetic vs. real-world), and training strategies (zero-shot vs. fine-tuning vs. training from scratch) on sim-to-real predictivity and real-world navigation performance.
- **Open-Sourcing Resources:** The motivation includes providing an open-source codebase and dataset to foster further research and ensure reproducibility within the Embodied AI community.

In essence, the work seeks to establish a practical framework that makes high-quality scene collection and personalized agent training more accessible, efficient, and effective for real-world robot deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EmbodiedSplat methodology proposes a pipeline for integrating real-world scenes into the Habitat-Sim simulator and subsequently deploying trained agents in the real world. The pipeline consists of four main stages: scene capture, 3D scene reconstruction, ImageNav episode generation and training in simulation, and real-world deployment.

#### 3.1. Scene Captures and Datasets

- **Pre-training Scene Datasets:** For pre-training zero-shot baseline policies, two existing datasets are used:
 - **HM3D:** A large-scale dataset comprising 1,000 building-scale 3D reconstructions from real-world locations, primarily apartment-style scenes. It is split into 800 training and 100 validation scenes.
 - **HSSD:** A synthetic dataset with 211 realistic environments, split into 134 training and 33 validation scenes.
- **Captured Scenes:** To evaluate the pipeline's real-world feasibility and conduct experiments on out-of-distribution environments, the authors captured several indoor scenes (e.g., lounge, classroom, conference rooms) within a university setting using an iPhone 13 Pro Max.
 - The capture process utilized the **Polycam application** to record RGB-D data, following a procedure inspired by the MuSHRoom dataset.
 - **Nerfstudio** was then used to process the raw RGB-D data, aligning and sampling 1000 RGB-depth frames with corresponding poses.
 - Captures were performed manually without a gimbal, taking approximately 20-30 minutes per scene.
 - Polycam also provides its own reconstructed meshes, referred to as **POLYCAM meshes**, for comparison.
- **MuSHRoom Dataset:** This multi-sensor dataset of 10 real-world scenes (captured with iPhone, Kinect, and Faro Scanner) was used for benchmarking depth and normal encoder selections. Specifically, iPhone long-sequences were used for training and evaluation.

#### 3.2. 3D Scene and Mesh Reconstruction

- The primary method for mesh reconstruction is **DN-Splatter**, selected for its performance and ease of integration with Habitat-Sim.
- DN-Splatter trains 3D Gaussian Splats (GS) using depth and normal regularization, along with smoothness losses, to maintain geometric consistency.
- For the depth input, the **ground truth depth from the iPhone** captures was used, as a benchmark showed it outperformed other monocular depth encoders (e.g., DepthAnything-v2, Metric3D-v2, UniDepth-v2, ZoeDepth).
- For normal estimation, **Metric3D-V2** was chosen as the normal encoder based on benchmarking against other encoders (e.g., DSINE, Omnidata) on the MuSHRoom dataset, demonstrating superior mesh quality (higher F-score).
- The GS models were trained for 30,000 iterations using default hyperparameters, including sensor depth weight $\lambda_d = 0.2$.
- After GS training, the rendered depth and normal maps from training views were back-projected to create a point-set. **Poisson reconstruction** was then applied to generate a mesh in `.ply` format. These meshes are referred to as **DN meshes**.
- The `.ply` meshes were converted to `.glb` format using **Blender**, ensuring correct coordinate system alignment for Habitat-Sim.
- The entire reconstruction process (capture, GS training, meshing) takes approximately 1.5-2.5 hours per scene.

#### 3.3. ImageNav Episode Generation

- **Navmesh Generation:** For all scenes (HM3D, HSSD, Captured, MuSHRoom), Habitat's navmesh islands are used to define navigable areas. For Captured and MuSHRoom scenes, only the largest navmesh island was selected due to their smaller scale compared to HM3D.
- **Episode Creation:** Building on Habitat's scripts for PointNav, ImageNav episodes are generated by pairing PointNav goal locations with an Image-goal sensor.
 - For HM3D and HSSD, 10,000 training episodes and 25 validation episodes per scene were generated.
 - For Captured and MuSHRoom scenes, 1,000 training episodes and 100 evaluation episodes per scene were generated.
- **Validity Checks:** Start and goal locations are sampled with checks for reachability, non-zero distance, and a navmesh island radius exceeding 2m to prevent navigation on objects.

#### 3.4. Agent, Training, and Evaluation Details

- **Agent Embodiment:** A Hello Robot Stretch robot embodiment is used in Habitat Simulator, modeled as a cylinder (height 1.41m, radius 0.3m) with an RGB camera at 1.31m height and 42$^\circ$ horizontal FOV.
- **Policy:** A 2-layer LSTM policy is employed, taking previous action embedding, visual observation embeddings, and goal image embeddings as input. It outputs discrete actions: MOVE FORWARD, TURN LEFT, TURN RIGHT, or STOP.
- **Visual Encoder:** The VC-1-Base visual encoder processes resized 160x120 images, with shared weights for goal and observation encoders.
- **Training Algorithm:** DD-PPO is used, with an ImageNav reward function (Eq. 1 in paper) adapted from, including success, angle success, slack penalty, and collision penalty components.
- **Hyperparameters:** AdamW optimizer with a weight decay of 10$^{-6}$. Learning rates are 2.5 x 10$^{-4}$ for the policy and 1.5 x 10$^{-6}$ (HM3D/other) or 1.5 x 10$^{-5}$ (HSSD) for the visual encoder. Fine-tuning uses reduced learning rates (2.5 x 10$^{-6}$ for LSTM, 6 x 10$^{-7}$ for visual encoder).
- **Evaluation Metric:** Success Rate (SR) is the primary metric. An episode is successful if the agent stops within 1m of the goal location before 1000 steps (simulation) or 100 steps (real-world).
- **Sim-to-Real Predictivity:** The Sim-vs-Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) is used to quantify how well simulation performance predicts real-world results.
- **Real-world Deployment:** Policies are deployed on a physical Stretch robot in a `lounge` scene. A Flask server on a remote compute node handles policy inference, receiving image observations from the robot via ROS and sending back actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings across various experiments, evaluating zero-shot, fine-tuned, and overfitted policies in simulation and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Performance on New Scenes

- **HM3D Pre-trained Policy:** When evaluated zero-shot on the authors' "Captured scenes" (university environments), the HM3D pre-trained policy showed varying success rates.
 - Smaller scenes (`conf a`, `conf b`) achieved relatively high success rates (e.g., `conf a` 85% on DN mesh, 82% on POLYCAM; `conf b` 88% on DN mesh, 79% on POLYCAM).
 - Larger, out-of-distribution scenes (`classroom`, `lounge`) showed significantly lower success rates (e.g., `classroom` 53% DN, 42% POLYCAM; `lounge` 50% DN, 76% POLYCAM). This indicates a challenge in generalizing from apartment-style HM3D scenes to diverse, larger environments.
 - On MuSHRoom scenes, `sauna` and `honka` (DN meshes) achieved 95% SR, while the larger `activity` scene showed decreased performance.
 - On average, both DN and POLYCAM meshes yielded similar zero-shot success rates (~60%) for the HM3D policy.
- **HSSD Pre-trained Policy:** The HSSD pre-trained policy (from synthetic data) exhibited substantially lower zero-shot success rates across all "Captured scenes" compared to HM3D. For instance, `classroom` yielded only 1% SR for both mesh types, and `lounge` achieved 14% SR on POLYCAM. This suggests that policies trained on purely synthetic datasets struggle more with real-world scene generalization.

#### 4.2. Impact of Fine-Tuning

- Fine-tuning pre-trained policies on the specific "Captured scenes" for 20M additional steps consistently improved performance in simulation across all tested scenes and mesh types.
- For the HM3D pre-trained policy, fine-tuning resulted in validation success rates approaching or exceeding 90% across different meshes.
- Similarly, for the HSSD pre-trained policy, fine-tuning led to significant improvements, with most policies achieving over 80% success rates.
- These gains were particularly notable in larger, complex environments like `classroom` and `lounge`, demonstrating the effectiveness of the pipeline in adapting policies to new scene types beyond their original training distribution.

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Transfer

- Real-world evaluations were conducted on a Stretch robot in the `lounge` scene.
- The zero-shot HM3D policy achieved a 50% success rate, which was lower than results reported in prior work on different real-world scenes, attributed to the structural and semantic differences of the `lounge` scene from HM3D training data.
- Fine-tuning the HM3D policy on reconstructed meshes of the `lounge` scene (POLYCAM and DN) improved real-world success rates to 70%.
- For HSSD, zero-shot performance was 10%. Fine-tuning improved this to 50% with POLYCAM and 40% with DN mesh.
- The **Sim-to-Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC)** between simulation and real-world performance for DN and POLYCAM meshes ranged from **0.87 to 0.97**. This indicates a strong positive correlation, suggesting that improvements observed in simulation on these reconstructed meshes translate to improved real-world performance.

#### 4.4. Ablations and Analysis

- **Necessity of Large-Scale Pre-training:** Policies "overfitted" (trained from scratch for ~100M steps) exclusively on a single `lounge` scene's reconstructed meshes (without large-scale pre-training) achieved near-perfect simulation performance. In real-world deployment, the overfitted policy on the POLYCAM mesh achieved a 50% success rate, while the DN mesh counterpart achieved 10%. This suggests that mesh-based training can yield non-zero real-world performance even without extensive pre-training, and highlights a difference in visual fidelity between POLYCAM (which preserves original image details) and DN meshes (based on learned GS colors).
- **Reconstruction Quality and Scene Scale:** A negative correlation was observed between HM3D zero-shot validation success rate and the average shortest distance of validation episodes (scene scale); larger scenes generally led to lower success rates. Conversely, a positive correlation existed between success rate and the validation PSNR of the corresponding 3D Gaussian Splats; higher PSNR correlated with improved success rates. MuSHRoom captures generally exhibited higher PSNRs, potentially due to gimbal-stabilized captures.
- **Impact of Continuous Pre-training:**
 - For HM3D pre-training, zero-shot success rates on "Captured scenes" (POLYCAM and DN meshes) initially improved but plateaued or deteriorated after approximately 400M steps, despite continued improvements on the HM3D validation set.
 - For HSSD pre-training, similar trends were observed, with zero-shot performance on "Captured scenes" plateauing after about 300M steps.
 - This indicates that prolonged pre-training on large datasets may not proportionally improve zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution scenes beyond a certain point. POLYCAM meshes generally outperformed DN meshes for HM3D pre-training, while for HSSD, the trend was less distinct.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EmbodiedSplat research introduces a pipeline that demonstrates a method for bridging the real-world and simulated environments for embodied agent training, offering several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By enabling policy fine-tuning on high-fidelity, environment-specific reconstructions, the work directly addresses the sim-to-real gap. The demonstrated strong sim-to-real correlation (0.87-0.97 SRCC) indicates that performance gains observed in simulated reconstructions can reliably predict and lead to improved real-world performance, which is a critical step for practical robot deployment.
- **Increased Accessibility and Cost-Effectiveness:** The use of consumer-grade mobile phones for scene capture, combined with efficient 3D Gaussian Splatting for reconstruction, significantly reduces the cost and effort traditionally associated with creating high-fidelity simulation environments. This makes sophisticated scene collection and agent training more accessible to a broader range of researchers and developers, potentially accelerating progress in Embodied AI.
- **Personalized Agent Training:** The pipeline facilitates rapid personalization of navigation policies to specific deployment environments. This capability is crucial for robots operating in diverse and dynamic real-world settings, allowing agents to adapt to novel structural and semantic characteristics of a target location that may be out-of-distribution for large pre-trained datasets.
- **Foundation for Scalable Scene Collection:** The efficiency of smartphone-based capture and GS reconstruction offers a scalable approach to collecting diverse real-world scene data. This could lead to larger and more varied datasets that better represent the complexity of real-world scenarios, ultimately improving the robustness and generalization of embodied agents.
- **Insights into Training Strategies and Reconstruction Quality:** The comprehensive analysis of zero-shot vs. fine-tuned policies, overfitting, and the relationship between reconstruction quality (PSNR) and scene scale provides valuable insights for future Embodied AI research. For example, the finding that even policies overfitted on a single reconstructed scene can yield non-zero real-world performance, and the differential performance between POLYCAM and DN meshes, highlight nuances in visual fidelity and training requirements.
- **Contribution to Open Science:** The commitment to an open-source codebase and dataset supports reproducibility and encourages further research in this domain, fostering collaboration and cumulative progress.

**Potential Future Directions (as suggested by the authors):**
- Integrating GS directly into the training process, replacing visual observations and goal images, to enhance learning efficiency.
- Extending the application of Gaussian Splats to more complex embodied AI tasks, such as rearrangement and mobile manipulation, which require detailed and dynamic room- and object-level scene representations.
- Further investigating the impact of capture stability (e.g., using a gimbal) on reconstruction quality and policy performance.

Overall, EmbodiedSplat represents a contribution toward making embodied AI systems more adaptable and practical for real-world applications by democratizing the creation of high-fidelity, personalized simulation environments.
