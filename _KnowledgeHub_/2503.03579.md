# A Generative System for Robot-to-Human Handovers: from Intent Inference to Spatial Configuration Imagery

> [!summary] Summary
> A generative system for robot-to-human handovers was developed, integrating a multimodal large language model for human intent inference and a text-guided diffusion model for generating unified spatial configurations of the human hand, object, and robotic gripper. The system achieved robot execution success rates between 80% and 93% on various objects in real-world scenarios, demonstrating improved safety and naturalness in human-robot interaction.

## Problem

- Existing robot-to-human handover research often focuses on mechanical aspects (grasping, motion planning) without effectively inferring complex human intent from multimodal cues.
- Current intent inference systems frequently overlook the critical visual cue of the human receiving hand, relying primarily on verbal commands or general object context.
- Generating feasible 3D handover configurations typically involves separate generation of human and robot poses, which can lead to spatial conflicts, impractical distances, or safety issues during actual handover.

## Method

- A three-stage system is proposed: handover intent inference (using Gemma 2 MLLM, Whisper, and RGB-D camera), configuration imagery (Text2HOI diffusion model for human hand pose, ==Multi-GraspLLM== for robotic grasp angle), and pose matching.
- The system interprets human handover intent from multimodal inputs, combining verbal instructions and visual input of the receiving human hand to recognize the target object and desired hand type.
- An integrated 3D handover configuration is generated by simultaneously considering the human receiving hand, the object, and the robotic gripper through a text-guided diffusion model, optimized to ensure collision-free gripper placement.

## Results

- Gemma 2:9b achieved the highest average accuracy of **44.24%** in inferring handover intent from 'fuzzy' (general task description) conditions, demonstrating strong contextual understanding.
- The generative method produced natural-looking receiving hand poses and reliably positioned the robotic gripper to prevent contact with the human hand, contributing to safer handovers.
- Real-world robotic arm execution resulted in success rates between **80%** and **93%** across five diverse objects, with the 'imagination time' (intent inference and configuration generation) averaging approximately 3 seconds, or one-third of the total handover time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) can effectively infer nuanced human handover intent from combined verbal and visual cues, even outperforming scenarios with ambiguously similar-sounding object names when full context is provided.
> - Generating an integrated 3D configuration for the human hand, object, and robot gripper simultaneously through a diffusion model ensures spatial coherence and enhances safety by design, unlike separate pose generation methods.
> - Aligning generated handover configurations with the real-world human receiving hand is robustly achieved by defining and matching local coordinate frames based on the hand's geometric center, direction, and palm normal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human-robot interaction (HRI) is experiencing growth, particularly in collaborative robotics where humans and robots work in close proximity. Object handover is a fundamental aspect of HRI, with existing research broadly categorizing handovers into robot-to-human, human-to-robot, and robot-to-robot scenarios. This study specifically addresses robot-to-human handover, where the robot acts as the giver.

Previous research in robot-to-human handovers has predominantly concentrated on mechanical aspects such as grasping strategies, motion planning, and optimizing delivery positions to ensure stability and success. Many approaches employ multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) for environmental understanding and human intent inference, leveraging language and vision inputs. Examples include language-guided grasping methods like FLarG, which uses CLIP for object localization, and GraspCLIP, which integrates visual and linguistic information for task-oriented grasping. RefTR combines BERT and ResNet for cross-modal fusion to improve grasping success rates. While these studies consider object-related linguistic and visual information, they often do not explicitly account for the human receiving hand, which is a critical cue for inferring handover intent.

Concurrently, the generation of feasible 3D handover configurations is a complex task, necessitating the consideration of spatial relationships between the human hand, object, and robotic gripper. Diffusion models have emerged as a method for generating 3D objects and grasp configurations from textual descriptions. Datasets like GRAB, ContactPose, H2O, and Multi-GripperGrasp provide data on human and robotic grasps. Some studies, such as Contact2Grasp and DiffH2O, utilize diffusion models to generate human grasp poses or hand-object interaction sequences. GraspLDM applies diffusion models to generate 6-DOF robotic grasp poses. ContactHandover optimizes robotic grasping by predicting human contact points. However, a limitation in existing generative approaches is that human grasp poses and robotic gripper poses are often generated separately, potentially leading to spatial conflicts, impractical distances, or safety concerns during actual handover.

This work addresses these gaps by proposing a system that emulates human cognitive processes in handover, specifically focusing on inferring human handover intent from multimodal cues (language and vision, including the receiving hand) and generating an integrated spatial handover configuration (involving the gripper, object, and human hand simultaneously) using a diffusion-based model, thereby mimicking human motor imagery. This integrated approach aims to facilitate more natural, fluent, and safe robot-to-human handovers by overcoming the fragmentation of intent understanding and pose generation in previous systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel system for robot-to-human object handover that emulates key aspects of human coworker interactions. This involves moving beyond conventional focus on just grasping strategies and motion planning to address two specific, interconnected challenges:
1. **Inferring human handover intents:** This aims to enable robots to understand not only explicit verbal commands but also implicit expressions and visual cues, particularly from the human receiving hand.
2. **Imagining spatial handover configuration:** This seeks to allow robots to mentally simulate the final handover gesture, considering the spatial relationships among the robot's gripper, the object, and the human hand, akin to human motor imagery.

The motivation for these objectives stems from observations of natural human-to-human handovers, where the giver effectively interprets complex multimodal cues (e.g., receiver's extended hand, verbal instructions) to infer the appropriate moment and manner of transfer. Cognitive studies indicate that humans mentally simulate actions, including the spatial configuration of a handover, before execution. Replicating such human-like behavior in robotic systems is considered crucial for enhancing collaboration, naturalness, and safety in dynamic environments (e.g., factories, laboratories) where humans and robots operate side-by-side.

Current robotic systems face several challenges in achieving this human-like capability:
- **Comprehending complex tasks:** Robots often struggle with the nuanced and sometimes implicit nature of human intent, requiring advanced reasoning and perception. The human receiving hand itself serves as a critical, yet often overlooked, intent signal.
- **Generating suitable spatial handover configurations:** Current methods often generate individual poses for the human and robot separately, which can lead to spatial conflicts or suboptimal transfer gestures. There is a need for methods that generate an integrated configuration where the relative poses of all involved entities are considered simultaneously.

To address these challenges, the research proposes to decompose the handover task into intent recognition and configuration generation, leveraging advanced AI models to achieve more fluent and human-like handovers. The overall motivation is to advance collaborative robotics by enabling robots to interpret human cues more effectively and generate appropriate, safe, and natural grasp configurations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system for robot-to-human object handover is structured into three main stages: handover intents inferring, handover configuration imagery, and handover pose matching. The system integrates multimodal perception, generative AI models, and a pose alignment strategy.

**A. Handover Intents Inferring**
The first stage focuses on interpreting human handover intent from multimodal cues.
- **MLLM Selection:** Gemma 2, an MLLM, is utilized as the core "brain" for intent understanding due to its capabilities in integrating visual and linguistic descriptions.
- **Multimodal Input Processing:**
 - **Language Input:** Speech recognition is performed using Whisper, a speech-to-text model, which converts real-time verbal instructions (triggered by "Hey robot") into textual input (T).
 - **Vision Input:** An RGB-D camera captures an image of the receiving human hand (I) when a voice signal is recognized.
- **Intent Inference:** The processed text-image pair (T, I) is fed into the MLLM. A structured dialogue template (as shown in Fig. 4) is employed to guide the MLLM to recognize the tool name and the type of receiving hand (e.g., "left hand," "right hand"). The model generates a templated output, such as "Pass the game controller to right hand of human," ensuring consistency and clarity.

**B. Handover Configuration Imagery**
This stage involves generating the spatial handover configuration, mimicking human motor imagery. It comprises two generative modules:
- **Receiving Hand Pose Generation:**
 - The Text2HOI model, a text-guided diffusion model, is used to generate the receiving human hand pose from the inferred task description (T) and the 3D point cloud of the object (x_obj).
 - The human hand is represented using the MANO model, which provides a deformable mesh surface parameterized by shape (β) and pose (θ) parameters. The hand representation for the diffusion model includes 3D translation (t_h) and 6D rotation parameters (θ_h).
 - Text2HOI utilizes a transformer for denoising the MANO hand representation. Conditional inputs include a contact map generated by CT-CVAE (based on the object's point cloud) and an embedding vector from CLIP (encoding the task description). This guides the diffusion model to predict the desired receiving hand pose (x_hand).
- **Robotic Gripper Grasping Angle Generation:**
 - The Multi-GraspLLM is employed to predict potential grasp angles for the robot's gripper, using the same inputs: the object point cloud and a prompt based on the object name from the task description (e.g., "How can 2-fingers parallel gripper grasp the game-controller?").
 - The LLM generates grasping pose parameters as a homogeneous transformation matrix (H), encoding the gripper's position and orientation.
- **Optimal Grasp Angle Selection:**
 - To prevent contact between the gripper and the human hand, an optimization strategy is implemented. This strategy aims to position the gripper on the opposite side of the object relative to the human hand.
 - The objective function minimizes the angle between the gripper's direction vector (v_g) and the human hand's direction vector (v_h) while maximizing the distance between their respective center points (p_g and p_h). The hand's direction vector is computed from MANO key points (wrist to middle finger tip). This ensures a safer, collision-free handover.

**C. Handover Pose Matching**
The generated handover configuration (imagined hand, object, gripper) must be aligned with the actual receiving human hand in the real world.
- **Coordinate Frame Definition:** A local coordinate frame for both the imagined and real human hands is defined. The geometric center of the hand (c) serves as the origin. The hand's direction vector (d) (from wrist to middle finger tip) defines the positive x-axis. The palm normal vector (p) (outward from the palm) defines the positive z-axis, with the palm plane formed by the x and y axes.
- **Matching Process:** The process ensures alignment by:
 1. Coinciding the coordinate origins (c_1 and c_2).
 2. Aligning the direction vectors (d_1 and d_2).
 3. Minimizing the angle between the palm normal vectors (p_1 and p_2).
- **Transformation Matrix Calculation:** A homogeneous transformation matrix (H) is constructed using rotation matrices (R_1, R_2) derived from the direction and normal vectors of the imagined and real hand poses, and the difference in their origins.
- **End-Effector Pose Transformation:** The robot's end-effector's final position (p_hat) and quaternion (q_hat) in the world frame are determined by transforming its initially generated position (p_0) and quaternion (q_0) using the calculated transformation matrix H.

**Implementation Environment:**
The system was implemented on a 6-DOF UR5e robotic arm equipped with a 2FG7 two-finger parallel gripper. An Intel RealSense D435i RGB-D camera was used for environmental perception. Isaac Sim served as the simulation environment, and communication between the computer and robot was managed via ROS2 Humble.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented findings from experiments conducted to evaluate the system's ability to infer intent, generate configurations, and execute handovers.

**A. Inferring Test of Intention**
Evaluation of MLLMs' ability to interpret handover intent from language and images was performed using 30 hand images, 16 objects, and 30 task descriptions per object across three ambiguity levels:
- **Clear:** Direct descriptions and explicit object naming.
- **Foggy:** Descriptions focusing on task and object, with similar-sounding names (e.g., "screw" and "screwdriver").
- **Fuzzy:** General task descriptions without explicit object names, requiring contextual inference.

Key observations from the MLLM performance (measured by average accuracy, where both correct hand type and object name were required):
- **Gemma 2:9b** demonstrated the highest overall performance, achieving an average accuracy of 44.24% in the fuzzy test.
- **Qwen2.5:3b** also showed competitive performance (40.67% average accuracy) despite having significantly fewer parameters than Gemma2, suggesting that model parameter size does not directly correlate with performance in all scenarios.
- **Accuracy Levels:** Clear task descriptions consistently yielded the highest average accuracy. Interestingly, the foggy level resulted in lower accuracy than the fuzzy level. This indicated that MLLMs could infer required items based on a complete scene description and intended use, even without explicit object names, outperforming scenarios where similar-sounding but incorrect object names might confuse the model.
- **Failure Cases:** Most failures in intent recognition were attributed to:
 - **Side views of the hand:** These significantly affected recognition accuracy.
 - **Multiple tool options:** When a scene allowed for multiple functionally similar tools, the model frequently made incorrect judgments.

**B. Visualization of Robotic Imagery**
The generative method was applied to 16 diverse daily household objects (tools, kitchenware, electronics).
- **Natural Hand Poses:** The generated receiving hand poses appeared natural and aligned with realistic human receiving gestures for most objects.
- **Safety:** The generated configurations effectively prevented contact between the robotic gripper and the human hand, thereby contributing to a safer handover.
- **Guidance for Robotics:** The generated configurations were considered to provide reliable guidance for the robotic arm by establishing well-aligned spatial relationships among the human hand, gripper, and object.

**C. Execution Testing**
The complete handover system was tested on a UR5e robotic arm in both simulation and real environments using five selected objects.
- **Success Rates:** The robot execution success rates ranged from 80% to 93% across the five objects (scissors: 0.80, game controller: 0.87, pincer: 0.90, knife: 0.93, eyeglasses: 0.80). The data indicated that the system's success rate was not directly dependent on the specific object.
- **Time Analysis:** The total time for a handover averaged approximately 9-10 seconds. The "imagination time" (for intent inference and configuration generation) accounted for roughly one-third of the total time (around 3 seconds), while the "execution time" (for robot motion planning and movement) constituted the remaining two-thirds (around 6-7 seconds).
- **Failure Cases:** The main causes of failure during execution included:
 - **Unfavorable receiving hand angles:** These could lead to kinematic singularities or suboptimal motion planning for the robot.
 - **Engineering challenges:** Issues such as gripper actuation problems and incomplete ROS2 packages were also noted as contributing to failures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a novel and integrated approach to robot-to-human object handover, addressing limitations in existing methodologies and aiming to enhance collaborative robotics.

**Significance:**
- **Emulation of Human Cognition:** The system's core significance lies in its emulation of human cognitive processes during handover, specifically intent inference and motor imagery. By integrating multimodal MLLMs for understanding nuanced human cues and diffusion models for generating holistic spatial configurations, the system moves beyond purely mechanical handover solutions.
- **Integrated Spatial Configuration Generation:** Unlike previous methods that often generate human and robot poses separately, this work generates an integrated 3D handover configuration that simultaneously considers the relative poses of the robotic gripper, the object, and the human hand. This fosters spatial coherence and safety.
- **Multimodal Intent Interpretation:** The use of MLLMs to interpret both verbal instructions and visual gestural cues, particularly incorporating the human receiving hand, represents a more comprehensive approach to understanding human intent in handover scenarios. This addresses a gap where the receiving hand was often overlooked.
- **Improved Safety and Naturalness:** The strategy for optimal grasp angle selection, which positions the gripper opposite the human hand, contributes to safer human-robot interaction by minimizing collision risks. The natural-looking generated hand poses and overall fluent execution enhance the naturalness of the handover process, improving the human experience.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** By enabling robots to understand human intent more deeply and execute handovers in a human-like, intuitive manner, this system could significantly improve the fluidity and effectiveness of human-robot collaboration in various settings. This is crucial for the wider adoption of cobots in dynamic and shared workspaces.
- **Applications in Diverse Environments:** The approach is applicable to environments such as factories, laboratories, healthcare settings, and domestic assistance, where seamless and safe object exchange between humans and robots is essential.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for future research in several areas:
 - **Advanced Intent Understanding:** Incorporating more subtle human cues like eye gaze, micro-expressions, or muscle tension could lead to even more robust intent inference.
 - **Dynamic Adaptation:** Extending the system to account for dynamic adjustments from the human (e.g., changing hands or gestures mid-handover) would enable real-time adaptability.
 - **Tool Functionality and Contextual Reasoning:** Integrating knowledge about tool functionality and how different object regions affect human interaction would allow for more intelligent and context-aware handovers.
- **Advancement in Generative Models for Robotics:** The application of diffusion models for generating complex 3D human-robot interaction configurations demonstrates a promising direction for using generative AI in robotic motor control and planning.

In conclusion, this research offers a step toward more intelligent, intuitive, and safer robot-to-human interaction by addressing the cognitive and spatial challenges of object handovers, contributing to the development of more capable collaborative robotic systems.
