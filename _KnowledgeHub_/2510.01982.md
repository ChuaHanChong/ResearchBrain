# Fine-Grained GRPO for Precise Preference Alignment in Flow Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed Granular-GRPO (G²RPO), a framework for aligning flow-based generative models with human preferences by introducing Singular Stochastic Sampling and Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration. This approach improves image quality and prompt adherence, achieving up to 6.52% relative improvement on HPS-v2.1 scores over DanceGRPO and superior performance across multiple reward models like Unified Reward.

## Problem

- Existing flow-based GRPO methods suffer from a sparse reward problem, where the final reward is uniformly attributed to all denoising steps, making precise credit assignment difficult.
- Current evaluation of sampling directions is often incomplete, relying on a fixed number of subsequent denoising steps, which provides only a single-granularity assessment.
- These limitations hinder the stability, efficiency, and overall performance ceiling of preference alignment in flow models, potentially leading to policy collapse or overfitting to specific reward biases.

## Method

- Developed ==Singular Stochastic Sampling== (SSS) to localize stochasticity to a single critical denoising step, making the variance in rewards directly attributable to that specific perturbation for precise credit assignment.
- Introduced Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration (MGAI) to evaluate sampling directions across multiple subsequent denoising granularities, generating images at various levels of detail to provide a comprehensive assessment.
- Integrated SSS and MGAI into an enhanced GRPO objective function, where advantages are computed based on the precisely attributed, multi-granularity rewards, leading to more robust model updates.

## Results

- G²RPO without MGAI (using SSS alone) achieved a **6.52%** relative improvement in HPS-v2.1 score over DanceGRPO, demonstrating the efficacy of precise reward signals.
- The full G²RPO framework outperformed baselines (DanceGRPO, MixGRPO) across various reward models, including a higher Unified Reward score (3.783 vs. MixGRPO's 3.661), indicating improved robustness and alignment with human preferences.
- G²RPO demonstrated stronger robustness to varying inference steps, maintaining significant performance improvements even when inference steps were reduced to 10 or 20, and showed superior detail fidelity and adherence to complex text prompts qualitatively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Localizing stochasticity to a single step dramatically improves the precision of reward attribution, enabling more effective credit assignment in online reinforcement learning for generative models.
> - Evaluating denoising directions across multiple granularities (e.g., coarse to fine) provides a more robust and comprehensive assessment of quality, preventing overfitting to specific reward model biases and enhancing generalization.
> - A unified advantage derived from multi-granularity evaluations mitigates domain collapse and leads to more stable and holistic improvements in generated content across various quality dimensions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of generative models and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), specifically targeting the alignment of flow-based generative models with human preferences.

**Generative Models:** The landscape of visual content creation has been revolutionized by diffusion models and, more recently, flow models. These models, like Flux.1-dev used in this paper, offer unprecedented capabilities in generating high-quality images and videos. However, generating visually appealing and semantically accurate content that truly aligns with diverse and often subjective human preferences remains a significant challenge.

**Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):** To address the preference alignment challenge, RLHF has emerged as a powerful paradigm, initially gaining prominence in Large Language Models (LLMs) with algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). RLHF aims to fine-tune generative models based on human feedback, often collected as pairwise comparisons, to better capture nuanced human tastes and instructions.

**RLHF for Visual Generative Models:** The success of RLHF in LLMs has led to its adaptation for visual generative models, with pioneer works like DDPO applying PPO to diffusion models. These early adaptations, however, faced inherent RL challenges such as high variance, low efficiency, and critically, *sparse reward signals*.

**Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Within the RLHF ecosystem, GRPO stands out as a "value-free" online reinforcement learning approach. Unlike PPO, GRPO optimizes policies based on *relative preferences* within a group of samples, eliminating the need for a separate value model and offering greater flexibility and scalability. Its efficacy has been demonstrated in leading LLMs like DeepSeek-R1.

**Flow-based GRPO:** The application of GRPO to flow models involves converting the deterministic Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) sampler into a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) formulation. This conversion introduces stochasticity at each denoising step, allowing the generation of a *group* of diverse samples necessary for GRPO's comparative optimization. Existing methods like Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO have implemented this, with MixGRPO further improving efficiency through a hybrid ODE-SDE approach.

**Current Limitations Addressed by G2RPO:** The paper directly tackles two critical limitations prevalent in these existing flow-based GRPO methods:
1. **Sparse Reward Feedback:** When SDE sampling is applied across multiple steps, the final reward signal is uniformly attributed to each individual step. This "long-range stochasticity injection" makes precise credit assignment difficult, leading to imprecise and sparse rewards that hinder effective optimization.
2. **Incomplete Evaluation (Fixed Granularity):** Current methods rigidly associate each denoising direction with a fixed number of subsequent denoising steps, resulting in images generated at a single granularity. This prevents a comprehensive assessment of the sampling direction's quality across different levels of detail or structural coherence, missing the robustness aspect that an "ideal" direction should exhibit.

"Fine-Grained GRPO" (G2RPO) directly addresses these core issues, aiming to refine the reward signal and enhance the evaluation comprehensiveness, thereby pushing the "optimization ceiling" for preference alignment in flow models. It represents an evolution in how RLHF is applied to visual generation, moving towards more intelligent and effective utilization of reward signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel online reinforcement learning framework, Granular-GRPO (G²RPO), that enables **fine-grained and comprehensive evaluation of sampling directions** in flow-based generative models. This, in turn, aims to achieve more precise and robust alignment of model outputs with human preferences.

The motivation for G²RPO stems from two significant limitations identified in existing flow-based GRPO methods:

1. **The Sparse Reward Problem (Motivation for Singular Stochastic Sampling):**
 - **Issue:** Traditional GRPO methods using SDE sampling throughout the denoising process generate a final image whose reward is then uniformly assigned to every preceding SDE step (as illustrated in Figure 1b). This creates a long credit-assignment chain. It becomes challenging to accurately determine which specific stochastic perturbation at an earlier step contributed to the final reward, leading to "sparse" and imprecise feedback.
 - **Consequence:** This sparsity hinders stable and efficient learning, making it difficult for the model to effectively optimize its policy based on the delayed and diluted reward signal. The model struggles to link specific actions (denoising directions) to desired outcomes.

2. **The Incomplete Evaluation Problem (Motivation for Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration):**
 - **Issue:** Current methods rigidly link each denoising direction to a fixed number of subsequent denoising steps, resulting in images with a single, predetermined granularity (e.g., fixed total sampling steps). This "single granularity" evaluation (Figure 1c) provides only a partial assessment of a sampling direction's quality. An intuitively "good" denoising direction should ideally lead to high-quality results robustly, regardless of minor variations in the remaining denoising trajectory's granularity.
 - **Consequence:** A fixed-granularity evaluation can be biased, failing to capture how a sampling direction performs across different levels of detail or abstraction. This limits the comprehensiveness of the group comparison and, consequently, the effectiveness of the GRPO optimization, as the model might overfit to a specific rendering detail rather than robust overall quality.

To overcome these motivations, G²RPO sets out to:
- **Precisely localize stochasticity:** To establish a strong, direct correlation between injected noise and the resulting reward, enabling more accurate credit assignment.
- **Provide a comprehensive assessment:** To evaluate sampling directions considering their impact across multiple denoising granularities, ensuring a more robust and holistic understanding of their quality.

By addressing these issues, G²RPO aims to provide a denser, more accurate, and more informative reward signal, thereby enhancing the training stability, efficiency, and ultimate performance ceiling of GRPO-based preference alignment in flow models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Granular-GRPO (G²RPO) introduces a novel framework that integrates two key components to achieve fine-grained and comprehensive evaluation of sampling directions: **Singular Stochastic Sampling** and **Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration (MGAI)**. The overall framework is depicted in Figure 2.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Flow-Based GRPO

The paper first reviews the standard flow-based GRPO setup. Denoising is framed as a multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP), where a flow model (policy `p_θ`) generates a trajectory from noisy input `x_T` to a clean image `x_0` based on a text prompt `c`.
- **ODE Sampling:** Flow models typically use deterministic ODEs (`dx_t = v_θ(x_t, t)dt`) for denoising.
- **SDE Sampling:** For GRPO's online RL requirement (which needs grouped outputs and stochastic exploration), the ODE is converted to an equivalent SDE (`dx_t = (v_θ(...) + σ_t^2/(2t)(...))dt + σ_t dw_t`) [Eq. 2, 3]. This injects noise (`ϵ`) at each step, allowing multiple denoising directions from the same state.
- **GRPO Training:** A group of `G` images `{x_i_0}` are generated, rewarded by `R(x_i_0, c)`, and then their advantages (`A_i_t`) are computed relative to the group mean and standard deviation [Eq. 4]. Critically, in traditional methods, this final advantage `A_i_0` is uniformly broadcast to all previous steps `A_i_t`. The policy `p_θ` is then optimized using a PPO-like objective function [Eq. 5, 6, 7] to maximize these advantages.

#### 3.2. Singular Stochastic Sampling (SSS)

This strategy directly addresses the sparse reward problem by confining the stochasticity to a single step.
- **Traditional Problem:** Injecting SDE noise at *every* step makes it difficult to assign precise credit to any single perturbation, leading to sparse rewards.
- **G²RPO's Approach:**
 1. **Focus on Critical Steps:** The method prioritizes the early denoising timesteps (`k ∈ M = {T, T-1..., ⌊T/2⌋}`), where the SDE's exploration space is larger and more influential on the overall trajectory.
 2. **Isolate Stochasticity:** For a selected timestep `k` (within `M`), the process starts from a common noisy sample `x_k` obtained via *deterministic ODE sampling* from `x_T`.
 3. **Single-Step SDE Perturbation:** At this specific step `k`, SDE sampling is applied to generate `G` distinct denoising directions, yielding `G` subsequent noisy states `{x_i_{k-1}}` [Figure 2].
 4. **Deterministic Completion:** From each `x_i_{k-1}`, the remaining `k-1` denoising steps are completed using *deterministic ODE sampling* to produce `G` final images `{x_i_{0←k}}`.
- **Benefits:** By localizing stochasticity to a single step, the variance in rewards among the `G` final images is entirely attributable to the SDE sampling at step `k`. This creates a strong, step-aware correlation between injected noise and the reward signal, allowing for precise advantage computation `A_i_k` [Eq. 8]. It also enhances training efficiency by allowing the reuse of the common ODE-computed `v_k` for `x_k`.

#### 3.3. Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration (MGAI)

This module tackles the incomplete evaluation problem by assessing sampling directions across different denoising granularities.
- **Traditional Problem:** Evaluating a denoising direction with a fixed number of subsequent ODE steps provides a single-granularity view, which may be inconsistent or biased (Figure 3).
- **G²RPO's Approach:**
 1. **Multi-Granularity Denoising:** After SSS generates the group of intermediate noisy states `{x_i_{k-1}}` (from step `k`), *each* `x_i_{k-1}` undergoes *multiple* subsequent ODE denoising processes.
 2. **Granularity Control:** A set of integer scaling factors `Λ = {λ_1, λ_2..., λ_J}` is defined. Each `λ_j` determines an interval for sampling steps from the remaining `k-1` steps. For example, `λ=1` means every step, `λ=2` means every second step, etc., creating coarse-grained (`λ` large) and fine-grained (`λ` small) versions of the denoising trajectory [Figure 2].
 3. **Group of Groups:** For each initial `x_i_{k-1}`, this process yields `J` denoised images (one for each `λ_j`), resulting in `G × J` final images.
 4. **Advantage Integration:** For each granularity `λ_j`, a group of rewards `{R(x_i,j_0←k, c)}` is obtained, and intra-group advantages `{A_i,j_k}` are computed. These `J` advantages (for each `x_i_{k-1}`) are then aggregated by summing them to create a unified, "mixed" advantage `A_i,mix_k` [Eq. 11].
- **Benefits:** This multi-dimensional evaluation provides a more robust and comprehensive assessment of the single-step SDE sampling direction. It accounts for both coarse-grained structural coherence and fine-grained textural details, leading to more stable model updates and mitigating the risk of policy collapse or overfitting to specific reward model biases. The GRPO objective is then optimized using these `A_i,mix_k` values [Eq. 12].

The full training process is summarized in Algorithm 1, integrating SSS for precise stochasticity and MGAI for comprehensive evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the superior performance and robustness of Granular-GRPO (G²RPO) across various metrics and settings compared to existing flow-based GRPO baselines.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation (Table 1)

- **Outperformance:** G²RPO consistently outperforms Flux.1-dev (baseline flow model), DanceGRPO, and MixGRPO on multiple reward models, including HPS-v2.1 (HPS), CLIP Score (CLIP), Pick Score (PS), Image Reward (IR), and the advanced Unified Reward (UR).
- **Impact of Singular Stochastic Sampling (SSS):** When trained solely with HPS-v2.1, G²RPO without MGAI (labeled "G²RPO w/o MGAI") achieves a 6.52% relative improvement in HPS score over DanceGRPO. This validates the effectiveness of SSS in providing a precise reward signal and enhancing the GRPO optimization ceiling.
- **Impact of Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration (MGAI):** In the more robust multi-reward training setting (HPS-v2.1 and CLIP Score), the full G²RPO (with MGAI) achieves the highest scores across almost all metrics, including a substantial improvement on Unified Reward (3.783 vs. MixGRPO's 3.661), which is a state-of-the-art LLM-enhanced reward model. This demonstrates MGAI's ability to provide a comprehensive evaluation, leading to more robust models that generalize better to out-of-domain metrics and avoid model "hacking."

#### 4.2. Qualitative Comparison (Figure 4)

- The visual examples clearly illustrate G²RPO's superior ability to align with human preferences. Images generated by G²RPO exhibit enhanced detail fidelity and improved consistency with the text prompt.
- For instance, in the "chess pieces" example, G²RPO accurately renders specific expressions and nuances. In the "poster" example, it not only adheres to spatial requirements but also generates clearer reflections and a more consistent aesthetic style. This qualitative superiority complements the quantitative gains.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on MGAI (Table 2)

- The ablation study varying the set of granularities `Λ` for MGAI confirms its value. As the number of selected granularities increases (e.g., from `{1}` to `{1, 2, 3}`), the performance on various reward models generally improves, especially on CLIP, IR, and UR.
- This indicates that a more diverse and comprehensive evaluation across different granularities leads to more robust assessment of sampling directions and, consequently, better model optimization.

#### 4.4. Further Exploration of Varying Inference Steps (Table 3)

- G²RPO demonstrates stronger robustness to varying inference step configurations. Even when total inference steps are significantly reduced (to 20 or 10 steps), G²RPO maintains substantial performance improvements over baselines across both in-domain and out-of-domain metrics. This highlights that the multi-granularity assessment learned during training translates into a more adaptable and efficient model at inference time.

#### 4.5. Additional UniGenBench++ Evaluation (Supplementary Material, Table 5)

- This comprehensive benchmark evaluation further supports the findings. While SSS (G²RPO w/o MGAI) improves specific fine-grained metrics like `Attribute`, `Relation`, and `Text`, it can sometimes lead to slight degradation in broader categories like `Style` and `Layout` due to overfitting to reward model biases.
- Crucially, the full G²RPO (with MGAI) achieves significant improvements across the `Overall` score and many fine-grained dimensions of UniGenBench++, including `Attribute`, `Relation`, `Logic.Reason.`, `Grammar`, `Compound`, and `Text`. This confirms MGAI's role in mitigating domain collapse and achieving a holistic improvement by evaluating samples at both coarse (structural) and fine (textural) levels.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Granular-GRPO (G²RPO) framework represents a notable advancement in the field of aligning generative models with human preferences, offering several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Preference Alignment in Generative AI:** G²RPO pushes the boundaries of RLHF for visual generative models, specifically flow models. By addressing critical limitations like sparse reward feedback and incomplete evaluation, it enables more precise, stable, and comprehensive optimization, leading to generated content that better aligns with complex human desires and aesthetic standards. This directly contributes to making generative AI more useful and controllable.

2. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration (MGAI) module is particularly impactful. By evaluating denoising directions across various granularities, G²RPO learns a more robust policy. This helps mitigate the risk of "model hacking" or overfitting to biases of a single reward model, leading to better generalization to out-of-domain metrics and a more consistent quality of generated images. The demonstrated robustness to varying inference steps is a practical benefit, allowing for faster generation without significant quality degradation.

3. **Refined Online Reinforcement Learning for Generative Models:** The Singular Stochastic Sampling strategy offers a principled way to improve credit assignment in online RL settings where actions (denoising steps) are sequential and outcomes (final image quality) are delayed. This approach could inspire similar fine-grained reward mechanisms in other iterative generative processes or sequential decision-making tasks where precise attribution of credit is challenging.

4. **Higher Quality and Fidelity in Generated Content:** The qualitative results visibly demonstrate G²RPO's ability to produce images with superior detail fidelity and adherence to complex text prompts. This direct improvement in image quality and semantic alignment makes the generated outputs more usable for creative professionals, designers, and general users.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The concepts introduced by G²RPO – localized stochasticity and multi-granularity evaluation – provide a strong foundation for future research. This could include exploring adaptive granularity selection, dynamic credit assignment mechanisms, or integrating even more sophisticated reward signals that capture a wider range of human preferences across different levels of abstraction. The comprehensive evaluation benchmarks like UniGenBench++ used by the authors further support this ongoing research direction.

6. **Practical Efficiency Considerations:** While MGAI introduces some training-time overhead due to generating multiple granularities, this is a one-time cost that does not affect inference speed. Moreover, the Singular Stochastic Sampling strategy contributes to training efficiency by allowing reuse of computed denoising directions, partially offsetting the MGAI overhead. The ability of the model to perform well with fewer inference steps after training also points to potential practical efficiency gains during deployment.

In conclusion, G²RPO marks a significant step towards developing more intelligent and human-centric generative AI systems. By refining how generative models learn from feedback, it opens avenues for producing more controllable, higher-quality, and aesthetically pleasing content across diverse applications.
