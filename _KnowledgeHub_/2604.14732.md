# World-Value-Action Model: Implicit Planning for Vision-Language-Action Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> The World–Value–Action (WAV) model unifies vision–language–action systems with implicit planning capabilities by integrating language-conditioned video generation, trajectory value estimation, and action decoding. This framework enhances long-horizon decision-making, achieving 98.1% average success on the LIBERO benchmark and 75.6% on real-world dual-arm robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models rely on direct action prediction, which limits their capacity for long-horizon reasoning and evaluation of multi-step action consequences.
- Current world models can predict future states but generally lack a direct or principled method for evaluating trajectory utility or selecting optimal actions.
- Explicit planning in high-dimensional action spaces suffers from a "curse of feasibility," where the probability of sampling dynamically feasible trajectories decays exponentially with the planning horizon.

## Method

- The **WAV** (==World–Value–Action==) model integrates three ==Diffusion Transformer== (DiT)-based modules: a ==language-conditioned video generation== module, a ==trajectory value== module, and an ==action decoding== module.
- It employs an ==iterative latent-space trajectory optimization==, inspired by ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI), to progressively refine adaptive noise distributions for video and value predictions.
- Planning is conceptualized as an ==implicit inference process== within learned latent spaces, allowing the model to concentrate probability mass on high-value and dynamically feasible trajectories.

## Results

- The **WAV** model achieved an average success rate of **98.1%** across all LIBERO benchmark task suites, including **94.4%** on complex long-horizon compositional tasks.
- In real-world experiments on a dual-arm robotic platform, WAV demonstrated a **75.6%** average success rate, outperforming the GE-ACT baseline (**35.6%**) on challenging manipulation tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the latent trajectory planning component is crucial for performance, particularly in long-horizon scenarios, improving success by **1.7** percentage points on average.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent-space planning can mitigate the "curse of feasibility" by exponentially increasing the probability of sampling dynamically and semantically feasible trajectories compared to direct action-space planning.
> - Iterative inference is necessary to refine the latent distribution, progressively concentrating probability mass on high-value and semantically aligned feasible trajectories.
> - Unifying a generative world model, a value function, and an action decoder within a single framework enables synergistic, value-guided action selection for long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically within Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models. VLA models integrate multimodal observations (visuals), natural language instructions, and robot actions to enable agents to operate in physical environments.

Current VLA approaches predominantly rely on direct action prediction. This methodology often treats each decision step as an independent event, which can restrict the model's capacity for long-horizon reasoning and the evaluation of action consequences. This limitation is particularly evident in complex tasks requiring multi-step planning and temporal coherence.

Concurrently, research in world models focuses on predicting future observations to capture environmental dynamics. While these models provide representations of how the world evolves, they typically lack mechanisms for principled trajectory evaluation or action selection. Some recent efforts attempt to unify world modeling with policy learning, but often depend on supervised learning from static datasets, which can limit their ability to model fine-grained dynamics and assess long-horizon trajectory quality. Another related area is the use of learned world models for data augmentation in reinforcement learning (RL), though this can be constrained by the world model's generalization capabilities, potentially leading to compounding errors in complex scenarios.

Model-based RL, in contrast, emphasizes the combination of predictive modeling and trajectory evaluation for effective long-horizon decision-making, conceptualizing planning as a process of refining candidate futures based on their expected value. The proposed WAV model positions itself within this broader context by addressing the question of whether planning can be realized as an implicit inference process rather than an explicit optimization module. It aims to integrate a learned world model and a value function within a VLA framework, enabling planning capabilities that address the limitations of direct action prediction and the "curse of feasibility" in high-dimensional action spaces.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework that endows Vision–Language–Action (VLA) systems with implicit planning capabilities, thereby enabling them to perform robust long-horizon decision-making in complex physical environments. The motivations for this work stem from several identified limitations in existing approaches:

1. **Limitations of Direct Action Prediction in VLA Models:** Existing VLA models often predict actions directly, treating each decision step independently. This approach restricts their ability to reason over extended sequences of actions (long-horizon trajectories) and to evaluate the potential consequences of these actions. Consequently, these models often exhibit reduced performance in tasks requiring coherent multi-step decision-making or strategic foresight.
2. **Lack of Principled Trajectory Evaluation in World Models:** While world models can predict future states and observations, they typically do not provide an inherent mechanism for evaluating the utility or desirability of these predicted trajectories or for systematically selecting actions based on such evaluations. This gap prevents world models from directly facilitating effective long-horizon planning.
3. **The "Curse of Feasibility" in High-Dimensional Action Spaces:** Long-horizon planning in VLA tasks faces a fundamental challenge: the dimensionality of the action space and corresponding trajectory space grows linearly with the planning horizon. The set of dynamically feasible trajectories, which adhere to physical constraints, scene geometry, and task semantics, occupies a rapidly diminishing fraction of this high-dimensional space. Direct sampling or search in this ambient action space becomes exponentially inefficient as the horizon increases, making it highly improbable to discover feasible, let alone optimal, trajectories. This phenomenon is termed the "curse of feasibility."
4. **Necessity of Iterative Refinement for High-Value Trajectories:** Even if a method could efficiently sample feasible trajectories, the feasible set still contains trajectories with widely varying task returns. One-shot sampling from a distribution over feasible trajectories does not guarantee the discovery of high-value, task-optimal solutions within practical computational budgets, particularly as task complexity and horizon increase. This highlights the need for mechanisms that can progressively concentrate probability mass toward high-value regions within the feasible set.

To address these motivations, the WAV model aims to:
- Learn a structured latent representation of future trajectories conditioned on visual observations and language instructions.
- Integrate a learned world model to predict future states and a trajectory value function to evaluate their long-horizon utility.
- Formulate action generation as an inference process in this latent space, where the model iteratively refines the probability distribution to concentrate on dynamically feasible and high-value trajectories.
- Demonstrate that latent-space inference can mitigate the exponential decay of feasible trajectory probability inherent in direct action-space planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The World–Value–Action (WAV) model is presented as an end-to-end modular framework designed for long-horizon reasoning and decision-making through implicit latent planning. The methodology encompasses theoretical foundations, a unified architectural design, and an iterative inference procedure.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Analysis of Latent Planning

The paper provides a theoretical analysis to underscore the limitations of action-space planning and the advantages of latent planning:

- **Action-Space Planning and the Curse of Feasibility (Lemma 4.1):** The analysis formally states that in high-dimensional trajectory spaces (where dimension grows linearly with horizon $H$), the probability of sampling even approximately feasible trajectories decays exponentially with $H$ under uniform exploration. This is attributed to the vast mismatch between the ambient dimensionality of the trajectory space and the much lower intrinsic dimensionality of the feasible trajectory manifold, which is constrained by visual, physical, and semantic requirements.

- **Latent Planning as Probability Mass Reweighting (Proposition 4.2):** Latent planning addresses this bottleneck by reshaping the sampling distribution. Instead of directly sampling actions, it samples a latent variable and decodes it into a full trajectory via a learned generative model. When this generator effectively captures the manifold of feasible behaviors, latent sampling concentrates probability mass on dynamically and semantically valid trajectories. The proposition demonstrates that this approach leads to exponentially higher probabilities of sampling feasible trajectories compared to uniform action-space sampling as the horizon increases. This benefit arises from reweighting the search toward relevant regions rather than merely dimensionality reduction.

- **Necessity of Iterative Latent Planning (Corollary 4.3):** While latent planning improves the probability of finding feasible trajectories, feasibility alone is insufficient for optimal decision-making because the feasible set still contains trajectories with varying task returns. The corollary argues that one-shot latent sampling, under subexponential sample budgets, cannot guarantee the discovery of near-optimal trajectories with high probability. Therefore, iterative inference procedures are necessary to progressively concentrate probability mass within the feasible set toward trajectories that are both semantically aligned and high-value.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture and Training Objectives

The WAV model architecture consists of three interconnected modules:

- **Language-Conditioned Video Generation Module (W):** This module predicts future visual trajectories based on past observations and language instructions. It employs a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone, leveraging a frozen T5-XXL encoder for language embeddings. It conditions predictions on multi-view visual history, stochastic latent noise variables, and language semantics, enabling it to capture task intent and spatiotemporal dynamics. The video generation operates autoregressively to produce temporally coherent visual rollouts.

- **Trajectory Value Module (V):** This module, also based on a DiT architecture, operates on latent trajectory representations. Visual features from the video generation module are transformed into temporally structured tokens. A latent value token is initialized and refined through cross-attention with these video features, resulting in a latent trajectory value embedding.

- **Action Decoding Module (A):** The action decoder similarly uses a DiT-based autoregressive architecture. It initializes latent action tokens and sequentially integrates video and value features to predict executable robot actions. This module uses cross-attention to incorporate both visual context and the learned trajectory value.

The training strategy for WAV employs a **flow matching** approach, a method for training generative models that map a simple base noise distribution to a complex target data distribution. The training proceeds in three stages:

1. **Video Generation Module Training:** The video generation module is initially trained to predict future visual segments using a video flow loss, aiming to match generated videos to target video trajectories.
2. **Trajectory Value Module Training:** With the video module frozen, the trajectory value module is trained to predict cumulative discounted rewards (state-values). The reward function used is a rule-based dense reward, similar to prior work (ReinboT), incorporating terms for task progress, behavior smoothness, and task completion.
3. **Joint Training with Action Flow Loss:** Finally, all modules are jointly trained using an action flow loss. This loss encourages the action decoding module to predict target robot actions, leveraging the denoised video and value features.

#### 3.3. Latent Planning and Iterative Inference

Inspired by Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, WAV implements an iterative latent-space trajectory optimization during inference (Algorithm 1). This process adaptively updates the noise distributions of the video and value modules:

1. **Iterative Sampling and Evaluation:** In each iteration, $M$ groups of video latent noise are sampled from an iteratively refined distribution. These are denoised by the video generator to produce predicted future visual trajectory features. For each video feature, $N$ groups of trajectory value flow noise are sampled and denoised by the trajectory value module to yield predicted state-values.
2. **Scoring and Elite Selection:** The Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) of the value predictions is computed for each exploration to provide a stable assessment of trajectory quality. The score for each video exploration is defined as the most reliable (highest SNR) among its $N$ value estimates.
3. **Distribution Update:** Based on these scores, the top $K_1$ elite video explorations are selected. The video noise distribution's mean and standard deviation are updated using the empirical statistics of their corresponding latent variables. Similarly, the top $K_2$ value evaluations (based on SNR) are used to update the value noise distribution.
4. **Smoothing:** To prevent distribution collapse and enhance stability, variance decay and exponential smoothing are applied to the updated means and standard deviations of both noise distributions.
5. **Action Prediction:** After $K$ iterations, optimized latent variables are sampled from the refined distributions, and these are fused by the action decoding module to predict the final actions for decision-making.

This iterative process allows the model to progressively concentrate probability mass on trajectories that are both dynamically feasible and aligned with high expected returns, addressing the challenges identified in the theoretical analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate the WAV model's performance in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, focusing on generalization, long-horizon reasoning, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Generalization Comparison in Simulation

- **Setup:** The WAV model was evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark, a comprehensive suite designed to test various aspects of generalization (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long) in multi-task and lifelong robotic manipulation. A single WAV model was deployed across all four task suites.
- **Results:** The WAV model achieved an average success rate of 98.1% across the LIBERO benchmark, demonstrating competitive or state-of-the-art performance. Notably, it exhibited strong performance on the "Long" suite, which comprises complex long-horizon compositional tasks, achieving 94.4%.
- **Ablation Study (W/o Latent Trajectory Planning):** An ablation study, where latent trajectory planning was removed, resulted in an average performance drop of 1.7 percentage points across the benchmark. The most significant degradation occurred in the "Long" suite, where the success rate decreased from 94.4% to 91.8%. This suggests that trajectory-level planning is critical for robust and generalizable task execution, with its benefits becoming more pronounced as the task horizon increases.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation

- **Setup:** WAV was deployed on a real-world dual-arm robotic platform named Piper. The evaluation included challenging manipulation tasks such as bowl organization, towel flattening, and a multi-step drawer task (open, place object, close). For comparison, GE-ACT, a baseline model with similar architecture and parameter count, was used. A strict binary success metric was applied.
- **Results:** WAV demonstrated a substantially higher average success rate (75.6%) compared to the GE-ACT baseline (35.6%) across the real-world tasks. Qualitative analysis indicated that GE-ACT frequently failed due to inaccurate action execution and weak spatial grounding, leading to accumulating errors in long-horizon tasks. In contrast, WAV produced more consistent and coherent multi-step behaviors, attributed to its implicit planning capability which anticipates downstream consequences of actions, thereby mitigating error accumulation. This advantage was particularly evident in tasks requiring multi-step coordination.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Further ablation studies investigated the impact of various hyperparameters:

- **Effect of Iteration Counts (K) and Sampling Sizes (M, N):**
 - Increasing the iteration count $K$ from 1 to 5 led to improved success rates, indicating the effectiveness of iterative latent planning. Beyond $K=5$, gains became marginal.
 - Performance was sensitive to the number of video latent noise samples $M$, with steep improvements observed in the low-sample regime, highlighting the importance of exploring diverse future trajectory hypotheses.
 - The influence of trajectory value noise samples $N$ was comparatively milder, with performance saturating earlier, suggesting diminishing returns from excessively dense value evaluation.

- **Effect of Smoothing Parameters ($\alpha$, $\beta$):**
 - Small values of $\alpha$ (for video noise distribution smoothing) resulted in a sharp drop in success rate, indicating that insufficient smoothing led to unstable distribution updates. Performance improved with increasing $\alpha$ and then plateaued.
 - A similar, though milder, trend was observed for $\beta$ (for value noise distribution smoothing). This suggests that stabilizing the latent distribution updates is crucial, but overly strong smoothing offers limited additional benefit.

- **Effect of Elite Selection ($K_1$, $K_2$):** Performance remained relatively stable across a range of $K_1$ (elite video explorations) and $K_2$ (elite value evaluations). However, extremely small values for these parameters led to noticeable performance degradation, implying that a sufficient number of elite samples is necessary for stable distribution updates.

#### 4.4. Performance–Efficiency Trade-off

- Increasing the number of iterations from 0 to 3 consistently improved performance. However, beyond 3 iterations, performance gains saturated while computational costs, particularly inference time, continued to rise. This indicates that $K=3$ offers a balance between effectiveness and efficiency, achieving strong performance with moderate computational overhead. This behavior supports the notion of diminishing returns for iterative refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The World–Value–Action (WAV) model represents a contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence by introducing a unified framework that integrates perception, reasoning, and action through implicit latent trajectory planning.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:

1. **Addressing Long-Horizon Planning Limitations:** WAV directly tackles the challenge of long-horizon decision-making in VLA systems, a known limitation of many existing direct action prediction approaches. By learning a structured latent representation of future trajectories and incorporating a trajectory value function, it enables systems to reason over extended sequences of actions and evaluate their consequences.
2. **Mitigating the "Curse of Feasibility":** The theoretical analysis provides a principled explanation for why latent planning is more effective than direct action-space sampling. By reshaping the search distribution to concentrate probability mass on dynamically feasible and high-value regions, WAV significantly reduces the complexity associated with planning in high-dimensional trajectory spaces. This offers a fundamental advantage for robotic tasks involving complex physical interactions and semantic constraints.
3. **Demonstrated Performance Improvements:** Empirical results across both simulated benchmarks (LIBERO) and real-world robotic platforms (Piper) demonstrate consistent and significant improvements in task success rate, generalization ability, and robustness compared to state-of-the-art baselines. These gains are particularly notable in long-horizon and compositional scenarios, validating the effectiveness of the implicit planning mechanism.
4. **Unifying Prediction, Evaluation, and Control:** The framework suggests that planning can emerge as a natural inference process within structured generative models. This perspective offers a promising direction for unifying distinct components of intelligent agents—namely, predicting future states (world model), evaluating their utility (value function), and selecting actions (policy)—into a cohesive learning system.

**Potential Impact:**

The WAV model's capabilities could lead to more autonomous and versatile embodied agents capable of performing complex tasks in unstructured environments. Its enhanced robustness and generalization could accelerate the deployment of robots in various applications, including logistics, manufacturing, and service robotics. The theoretical insights into latent planning also offer a foundation for future research in model-based reinforcement learning and generative modeling for control.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The paper acknowledges that a main limitation of the current work is the time and storage overhead required for model deployment. Future research directions include:

- **Scalability and Efficiency:** Exploring methods to reduce the computational and memory footprint of the WAV model to enable more efficient real-time closed-loop deployment on physical robotic systems.
- **Richer Multimodal Instructions:** Extending the framework to incorporate richer and more diverse multimodal instructions, potentially allowing for more nuanced and adaptable task specifications.
- **Further Unification:** Continuing to explore how prediction, evaluation, and control can be more deeply unified within structured generative models, potentially leading to more sample-efficient and generalizable learning paradigms for embodied AI.
