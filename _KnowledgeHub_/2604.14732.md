# World-Value-Action Model: Implicit Planning for Vision-Language-Action Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> The World–Value–Action (WAV) model unifies vision–language–action systems with implicit planning capabilities by integrating language-conditioned video generation, trajectory value estimation, and action decoding. This framework enhances long-horizon decision-making, achieving 98.1% average success on the LIBERO benchmark and 75.6% on real-world dual-arm robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models rely on direct action prediction, which limits their capacity for long-horizon reasoning and evaluation of multi-step action consequences.
- Current world models can predict future states but generally lack a direct or principled method for evaluating trajectory utility or selecting optimal actions.
- Explicit planning in high-dimensional action spaces suffers from a "curse of feasibility," where the probability of sampling dynamically feasible trajectories decays exponentially with the planning horizon.

## Method

- The **WAV** (==World–Value–Action==) model integrates three ==Diffusion Transformer== (DiT)-based modules: a ==language-conditioned video generation== module, a ==trajectory value== module, and an ==action decoding== module.
- It employs an ==iterative latent-space trajectory optimization==, inspired by ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI), to progressively refine adaptive noise distributions for video and value predictions.
- Planning is conceptualized as an ==implicit inference process== within learned latent spaces, allowing the model to concentrate probability mass on high-value and dynamically feasible trajectories.

## Results

- The **WAV** model achieved an average success rate of **98.1%** across all LIBERO benchmark task suites, including **94.4%** on complex long-horizon compositional tasks.
- In real-world experiments on a dual-arm robotic platform, WAV demonstrated a **75.6%** average success rate, outperforming the GE-ACT baseline (**35.6%**) on challenging manipulation tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the latent trajectory planning component is crucial for performance, particularly in long-horizon scenarios, improving success by **1.7** percentage points on average.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent-space planning can mitigate the "curse of feasibility" by exponentially increasing the probability of sampling dynamically and semantically feasible trajectories compared to direct action-space planning.
> - Iterative inference is necessary to refine the latent distribution, progressively concentrating probability mass on high-value and semantically aligned feasible trajectories.
> - Unifying a generative world model, a value function, and an action decoder within a single framework enables synergistic, value-guided action selection for long-horizon tasks.
