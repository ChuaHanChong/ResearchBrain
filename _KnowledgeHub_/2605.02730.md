# Perceptual Flow Network for Visually Grounded Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> PFlowNet introduces a framework for visually grounded reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models by optimizing a structured latent "Perceptual Flow" to guide reasoning. This method mitigates hallucination and language bias by exploring reasoning-oriented perceptual behaviors, achieving state-of-the-art results with a 10.4% average improvement on TreeBench and 18.4% on MME-RealWorld-Lite compared to the base Qwen3-VL-8B.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently suffer from hallucinations and poor interpretability, especially in fine-grained visual reasoning tasks.
- Existing methods that distill geometric priors from visual experts (like GroundingDINO) for LVLMs often lead to a "tunnel vision effect," where overly precise geometric evidence excludes necessary broader contextual information.
- Geometric priors optimized for object detection are not inherently optimal for complex visual reasoning, creating a mismatch between expert utility and reasoning needs.

## Method

- **PFlowNet** defines "==Perceptual Flow==" as a structured latent trajectory of visual thoughts, comprising a planning state (query decomposition) and perceptual states (grounded observations with RoIs and captions), decoupling perception from reasoning.
- The framework employs a progressive training paradigm, starting with data curation and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to teach the model to generate beneficial perceptual flows.
- A ==Variational Reinforcement Fine-Tuning== (RFT) strategy with a multi-dimensional reward function (balancing quality and reasoning efficacy) and a ==vicinal geometric shaping== scheme is used to foster reasoning-oriented perceptual behaviors while maintaining visual reliability.

## Results

- PFlowNet achieved an overall **10.4%** improvement on TreeBench and **18.4%** on MME-RealWorld-Lite compared to the Qwen3-VL-8B base model, setting new state-of-the-art records on 17 out of 19 sub-tasks across general-purpose benchmarks.
- The framework delivered state-of-the-art results on fine-grained benchmarks, including a **13%** gain on V* Bench, 8-**8.8%** on HR-Bench, and 2.5-**7%** on ScreenSpot over the Qwen3-VL series, matching the performance of the larger Qwen3-VL-32B model with the 8B variant.
- PFlowNet demonstrates robust test-time scaling capabilities and an improved performance-efficiency trade-off, achieving comparable or superior performance to agentic frameworks with significantly shorter context lengths and reduced inference latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strict geometric precision, while beneficial for localization, can induce a "tunnel vision effect" that hinders comprehensive visual reasoning by limiting contextual understanding.
> - Decoupling optimizable perceptual behaviors from the reasoning process allows for more effective visually grounded reasoning through self-conditioned autoregressive generation.
> - A multi-dimensional reward function combined with vicinal geometric shaping enables LVLMs to explore a broader range of effective perceptual behaviors that enhance reasoning utility beyond just geometric precision, preserving visual reliability without rigid alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across various visual tasks by integrating vision encoders with Large Language Models (LLMs) and cross-modal alignment techniques. Despite their advancements, LVLMs exhibit limitations in interpretability and are susceptible to generating hallucinations, particularly in scenarios requiring fine-grained visual understanding.

A line of research to enhance LVLM reliability involves distilling geometric priors from visual expert models, such as GroundingDINO, and incorporating them into LVLMs through Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR). These methods aim to anchor intermediate reasoning processes in visual evidence by maximizing geometric consistency between LVLM predictions and expert priors. However, the authors identify a potential misalignment: visual experts are primarily optimized for object detection and precise localization, which may not directly translate to optimal utility for visual reasoning tasks. The paper posits that such precise geometric priors can lead to a "tunnel vision effect," potentially excluding broader contextual information necessary for comprehensive understanding and consequently degrading reasoning performance. This observation forms the basis for the proposed Perceptual Flow Network (PFlowNet), which seeks an alternative approach to visually grounded reasoning that moves beyond rigid adherence to expert-derived geometric priors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an interpretable and effective visual reasoning framework for LVLMs that mitigates language bias and hallucination without relying on suboptimal geometric priors from visual experts. The authors investigate whether geometric priors optimized for object detection are truly optimal for visual reasoning, highlighting a fundamental mismatch.

The motivation for PFlowNet stems from a preliminary study conducted using the Qwen2.5-VL model family on the V* benchmark. This benchmark includes tasks requiring direct attribute recognition and spatial relation reasoning, supported by fine-grained expert annotations. The study involved generating varying geometric priors by isotropically expanding original expert annotations and feeding models with these evidence crops instead of full images. The key observation was that the most geometrically precise prior (i.e., the expert annotation itself) did not yield the highest reasoning performance. This counterintuitive result suggested that strict geometric precision, while beneficial for localization, could induce a "tunnel vision effect," preventing the model from incorporating necessary context for comprehensive reasoning. Approximating "golden evidence" through heuristic transformations of expert priors was deemed intractable due to its instance-specific nature.

To address this challenge, the authors propose PFlowNet with three specific innovations:
1. **Perceptual Flow:** A structured latent trajectory formulation that characterizes LVLM perceptual behaviors, facilitating optimization through hierarchical variational objectives (e.g., Sub-Trajectory Balance). This flow comprises a planning state (language sequence for query decomposition) and perceptual states (a chain of grounded observations, each with a Region of Interest and a descriptive caption).
2. **Decoupled Framework:** A separation of optimizable perceptual behaviors from the model’s reasoning process. This enables visually grounded reasoning via a self-conditioned autoregressive generation, where the perceptual flow guides subsequent reasoning.
3. **Variational Reinforcement Fine-Tuning Strategy:** This strategy integrates a multi-dimensional reward function with a vicinal geometric shaping scheme. Its purpose is to foster reasoning-oriented perceptual behaviors while preserving visual reliability, moving beyond rigid alignment with static geometric priors. The goal is to allow the model to approximate the posterior of idealized perceptual behaviors by sampling from an optimized intrinsic distribution.

These innovations collectively aim to enable LVLMs to explore a broader range of effective perceptual behaviors that enhance reasoning utility, rather than solely focusing on geometric precision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PFlowNet addresses visually grounded reasoning by decoupling perception from reasoning within a self-conditioned generation process. The overall architecture consists of two main stages: perceptual flow generation and flow-guided reasoning.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Perceptual Flow Definition

The research formalizes Visually Grounded Reasoning (VGR) for an LVLM $M_\theta$ by considering the visual reasoning trajectory (e.g., a sequence of Regions of Interest or RoIs) as a latent variable $Z$. Hallucination is attributed to an ill-posed posterior $P(Z|X,Y)$ that assigns probability to invalid trajectories. VGR aims to maximize the expected log-likelihood of generating the correct answer $Y$ while anchoring the latent visual rationales $Z$ to a "golden visual trajectory" $G$. The set of valid visual trajectories $S_V$ is defined as the $\sigma$-neighborhood of $G$.

The concept of **Perceptual Flow** $Z = (z_0 \to z_1 \ldots z_K)$ is introduced as a structured latent trajectory explicating visual "thoughts." It includes:
- **Planning State ($z_0$):** A language sequence (e.g., enclosed by `<analyze>` and `</analyze>`) that decomposes the input query and identifies relevant visual candidates.
- **Perceptual States ($z_{\geq 1}$):** A chain of grounded observations (e.g., enclosed by `<localize>` and `</localize>`). Each state $z_k = \langle r_k, c_k \rangle$ consists of a Region of Interest (RoI) $r_k$ (in relative coordinates) and a corresponding descriptive caption $c_k$.

The joint distribution of the grounded output $Y$ and perceptual flow $Z$ given multimodal input $X = \langle I, T \rangle$ (image $I$, instruction $T$) is factorized as $p_\theta(Y, Z | X) = p_\theta(Z | X) p_\theta(Y | Z, \langle X, I_{RoI} \rangle)$, where $I_{RoI}$ denotes image regions conditioned on $Z$.

#### 3.2. Training Data Curation and Cold Start

The training process employs a progressive paradigm, starting with data curation and supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
- **Data Collection:** High-quality data is curated from existing multimodal VQA datasets (LLaVA, VGR, ArxivQA, VLM-R3, ThinkLite-VL), filtered for task difficulty, typology, and evidence distribution. Principles include diverse tasks (fine-grained and general-purpose) and diverse RoIs (cross-expert annotation to ensure broad spatial coverage).
- **Flow Synthesis:** For each sample with expert RoIs, the RoIs are randomly expanded to mitigate inductive bias. Teacher models (e.g., Gemini3flash, GPT-4o) are then prompted to generate: (i) a Planning State ($z_0$) by identifying critical visual content, and (ii) Perceptual States ($z_{\geq 1}$) with detailed captions for each expanded RoI. These form the synthetic perceptual flow $Z_s$.
- **Verification & Difficulty Control:** A verifier filters synthesized flows. Samples are categorized: trivial/unreliable flows are dropped; others are assigned to either a cold-start dataset or an RFT dataset based on performance gain induced by $Z_s$.
- **Cold Start (SFT):** The LVLM policy is initialized via SFT by minimizing cross-entropy loss between $p_\theta(Z|X)$ and $Z_s$ on the cold-start dataset. This phase teaches the model to generate perceptual flows beneficial for reasoning.

#### 3.3. Variational Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT)

Following SFT, PFlowNet undergoes variational RFT to better approximate the target posterior $P_V(Z|X,Y)$, relaxing the reliance on heuristic flow synthesis. The RFT objective is derived from the Sub-Trajectory Balance (SubTB) principle, which provides dense intermediate supervision. The objective $L_{vRFT}(\theta)$ is an expectation over data tuples $(X,Y,E)$ and sampled flows $Z$, minimizing the squared log-ratios of flow values across sub-trajectories.

- **Reward Design:** A multi-dimensional reward function $R(z_{0:k}\top)$ is designed to evaluate both the **quality** and **reasoning efficacy** of a sub-flow $z_{0:k}$.
 - **Quality Term:** A contrastive term $\prod_{i=1}^k (p^+_\phi(z_i) / p^-_\phi(z_i))$ where $p^+_\phi(z_i) = p_\phi(c_i | I_{r_i})$ (caption likelihood given zoomed-in evidence) and $p^-_\phi(z_i) = p_\phi(c_i | I \setminus I_{r_i})$ (caption likelihood given complementary region). Maximizing this term encourages captions to be more specific to the relevant visual evidence and less influenced by generic language priors.
 - **Reasoning Efficacy Term:** Measured by $\log p_\phi(Y | z_{0:k}\top, X)$, which quantifies the information gain provided by the sampled flow for deriving the target response $Y$.
- **Vicinal Geometric Shaping:** To prevent excessive exploration outside valid visual supports, a vicinal geometric shaping scheme is introduced. This scheme does not enforce rigid alignment but constrains variational inference to an $\epsilon$-vicinity $B_\epsilon(E)$ around the expert prior $E$. The Chamfer IoU distance $d_{IoU}(A, B)$ quantifies similarity between RoI sets. An energy weight $\omega_\lambda(z_{0:k}, E) := \exp(-\lambda I(z_{0:k} \notin B_\epsilon(E)))$ penalizes flows outside this vicinity. The final shaped reward is $R_\lambda(z_{0:k}\top) = R(z_{0:k}\top) \omega_\lambda(z_{0:k}, E)$, balancing exploration with visual reliability.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical analysis establishing a provable performance guarantee for PFlowNet.
- **Total Variation Distance Bound (Theorem 3.1):** Under certain assumptions, a bound on the Total Variation (TV) distance between the optimal policy $p_{\theta^\star}(Z|X)$ and the target valid posterior $P_V(Z|X,Y)$ is derived. This bound depends on $s_V$ (probability mass of valid support), $s_B$ (probability mass of expert vicinity), and hyperparameters $\lambda$ (shaping intensity) and $\epsilon$ (vicinal radius).
- **Guaranteed Improvement (Theorem 3.4):** This theorem states that for any $\epsilon$ such that the expert vicinity $B_\epsilon$ is within the valid support $S_V$, there exists an optimal shaping intensity $\lambda^\star$ where PFlowNet's TV distance bound is strictly less than that of standard MLE ($1 - s_V$) and expert-guided RLVR ($1 - q$, where $q = s_B/s_V$). Furthermore, for fixed $\lambda^\star$, the bound is strictly decreasing as $q$ increases (i.e., as $\epsilon$ increases within the valid region). This implies PFlowNet strictly tightens the idealized TV bounds of baseline methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PFlowNet was initialized from Qwen3-VL-8B and evaluated against both general-purpose LVLMs and visually grounded reasoning models across 15 benchmarks, covering general-purpose and fine-grained visual tasks.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **General-purpose Tasks:** PFlowNet demonstrated robust capabilities in both perception and reasoning. It achieved overall improvements of 10.4% on TreeBench and 18.4% on MME-RealWorld-Lite compared to the vanilla Qwen3-VL-8B. PFlowNet surpassed existing grounded RLVR methods (e.g., TreeVGR, Pixel Reasoner) and agentic frameworks (e.g., DeepEyes, Thyme), achieving the best average performance and setting new SOTA records on 17 out of 19 sub-tasks across these benchmarks.
- **Fine-grained Visual Understanding:** PFlowNet achieved SOTA results across all fine-grained benchmarks, including V* Bench, HR-Bench (4K/8K), and ScreenSpot. It showed clear gains of 13% on V*, 8%/8.8% on HR-Bench (4k/8k), and 2.5%–7% on ScreenSpot over the Qwen3-VL series, despite Qwen3-VL having architectural improvements for fine-grained capabilities. These improvements were concentrated in reasoning-oriented subsets like spatial reasoning. PFlowNet, built on Qwen3-VL-8B, matched the performance of the larger Qwen3-VL-32B on these challenging tasks (e.g., 90.6% vs. 87.4% on V*).

#### 4.2. In-depth Analysis

- **Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:** PFlowNet exhibited a balance between performance and efficiency. It achieved comparable or superior performance to agentic frameworks while incurring significantly shorter context lengths and reduced inference latency, by replacing complex tool executions with structured perceptual flows. Compared to TreeVGR, PFlowNet improved perceptual quality and utility with affordable computational costs.
- **Test-Time Scaling:** The variational objective of PFlowNet enabled robust test-time scaling capabilities, allowing for diverse rationale distribution. In contrast, TreeVGR, a grounded RLVR method, showed negligible performance gains when computational budget (k) scaled up, particularly on challenging benchmarks like V* Bench and TreeBench. This indicated that TreeVGR's rigid alignment with sparse expert trajectories led to a sharp, less diverse rationale distribution.
- **Case Study (Qualitative):** Qualitative examples showed that PFlowNet enabled precise yet comprehensive exploration of visual evidence, anchoring the reasoning process to perceptual outcomes. Unlike TreeVGR, which sometimes produced geometrically precise but semantically misaligned boxes, PFlowNet's dense contrastive rewards enforced strict visual-textual dependency, leading to faithful interpretability. PFlowNet was observed to prioritize precise localization before expanding its visual scope, attributed to the sequence-level dIoU shaping energy.
- **Character-level Output Length:** The generated flow lengths in PFlowNet remained consistent across benchmarks for the same number of RoIs, suggesting flow length is primarily governed by required visual evidence. Model-generated flows were generally shorter than synthetic flows, possibly due to the planning state's lack of direct RFT supervision and the contrastive reward encouraging concise captions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Framework & Reward Design:** SFT and synthetic flows were confirmed to be effective. The proposed RFT strategy provided further substantial gains. Both quality and efficacy rewards collaboratively contributed during RFT, with the contrastive formulation being crucial. The perceptual flow itself served as a critical explicit semantic anchor, guiding reasoning more directly than raw visual features; its removal led to severe performance degradation.
- **Geometric Shaping:** Geometric shaping stabilized training and allowed for broader exploration than typical KL regularization, mitigating SFT inductive bias. An initial drop and subsequent resurgence in dIoU during training reflected a healthy exploration-to-exploitation transition. The optimal vicinal radius $\epsilon$ and shaping intensity $\lambda$ were empirically determined, consistent with theoretical analyses: excessive $\epsilon$ led to over-exploration into invalid supports, while too small $\epsilon$ enforced expert bias. Small $\lambda$ caused training instability, while large $\lambda$ restricted exploration.
- **Cross-Scale Evaluations:** PFlowNet demonstrated scalability across Qwen3-VL 4B and 32B variants. Even with efficient training regimes, both SFT and RFT stages consistently yielded clear performance improvements on general-purpose and fine-grained tasks across different model scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PFlowNet introduces a novel framework for visually grounded reasoning in LVLMs by moving beyond rigid adherence to geometric priors derived from visual experts. Its core contribution lies in defining and optimizing "Perceptual Flow" – a structured, self-conditioned latent trajectory that explicates visual thoughts. This approach allows LVLMs to explore reasoning-oriented perceptual behaviors while maintaining visual reliability.

The theoretical analysis, including a provable performance guarantee with a tighter Total Variation distance bound compared to standard MLE and expert-guided RLVR, provides a formal basis for PFlowNet's effectiveness. Empirically, PFlowNet achieves competitive results, setting new SOTA records on challenging benchmarks like V* Bench and MME-RealWorld-lite, and demonstrating substantial improvements over base LVLMs and existing grounded reasoning methods.

PFlowNet's ability to decouple perception from reasoning and its use of a multi-dimensional reward function with vicinal geometric shaping contribute to more interpretable and accurate visual reasoning. This work has the potential to:
- **Mitigate Hallucination and Language Bias:** By providing a structured, verifiable visual rationale, PFlowNet can enhance the trustworthiness and factual consistency of LVLM outputs.
- **Improve Fine-grained Visual Understanding:** The framework's demonstrated superior performance in tasks requiring precise localization, attribute recognition, and spatial reasoning indicates its impact on applications demanding detailed visual analysis.
- **Enhance Efficiency and Scalability:** PFlowNet's favorable performance-efficiency balance and robust test-time scaling suggest its applicability in resource-constrained environments or where diverse reasoning paths are beneficial.

The limitations acknowledged by the authors, such as reliance on idealized theoretical assumptions, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and the current lack of adaptive perception (fixed reasoning format), suggest avenues for future research. Enabling LVLMs to dynamically adjust their perceptual processes based on query complexity and task context could further enhance the framework's versatility and efficiency. Overall, PFlowNet contributes a method for improving the reliability, interpretability, and performance of LVLMs in complex visual reasoning tasks.
