# FEAST: A Flexible Mealtime-Assistance System Towards In-the-Wild Personalization

> [!summary] Summary
> Cornell University researchers and collaborators developed FEAST, a flexible mealtime-assistance system enabling in-the-wild personalization for individuals with physical disabilities. The system utilizes Large Language Models for natural language customization, achieved high skill success rates, and demonstrated adaptability, transparency, and safety during in-home evaluations.

## Problem

- Individuals with physical disabilities require mealtime assistance systems that can adapt to their diverse and dynamic personal needs, a capability lacking in existing robotic solutions.
- Current robotic mealtime assistance systems often rely on fixed, manually programmed actions, leading to limited adaptability, obtrusive motions, and restricted workspace, hindering widespread adoption.
- The inability to self-feed leads to negative emotional impacts on care recipients and significantly contributes to the workload of human caregivers.

## Method

- A flexible mealtime-assistance system, **FEAST**, was designed based on insights from a formative user study with 21 care recipients, prioritizing adaptability, transparency, and safety.
- The system integrates a modular hardware design with a novel utensil orientation and autonomous tool-changing capabilities for feeding, drinking, and mouth-wiping tasks.
- An LLM-powered software framework allows natural language-based skill adaptation and personalized gesture synthesis, incorporating structured safety checks and explanations for transparency.

## Results

- FEAST addressed 36 out of 46 identified personalization requests from users, substantially exceeding the capabilities of existing state-of-the-art systems.
- A five-day in-home study with two community researchers showed high skill success rates for bite acquisition (**89.27%**) and bite transfer (**93.07%**), alongside a low cognitive workload (NASA-TLX average 7.41-22.22).
- An independent Occupational Therapist evaluation reported a 5/5 personalization success rate and a preference for the personalized system over default settings in all 10 scenarios tested.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective in-the-wild personalization for assistive robots hinges on explicitly designing for adaptability, transparency, and safety, validated through extensive user co-design.
> - Large Language Models can be integrated safely into physical human-robot interaction, enabling intuitive natural language customization and dynamic generation of personalized gestures.
> - Providing a flexible, user-driven robotic system for mealtime assistance significantly enhances care recipient independence and control, often surpassing the perceived autonomy with human caregivers.
