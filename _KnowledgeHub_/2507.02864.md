# The Sound of Simulation: Learning Multimodal Sim-to-Real Robot Policies with Generative Audio

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of California, Berkeley researchers introduce MULTIGEN, a hybrid framework that integrates a physics simulator with a generative audio model to create realistic multimodal data for robot learning. This approach enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex tasks like robot pouring, yielding a 23.3% reduction in error for multimodal policies compared to vision-only methods.

## Problem

- Acquiring large-scale, diverse, and synchronized multimodal datasets (e.g., video, audio, actions) for real-world robot learning is prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive.
- Traditional physics-based simulators struggle to accurately model non-visual modalities like sound, limiting effective multimodal sim-to-real transfer.
- Robot policies trained primarily on visual data often lack robustness and adaptability in scenarios with visual ambiguities or occlusions, such as pouring opaque liquids.

## Method

- **MULTIGEN** integrates a ==physics-based simulator== (RoboVerse) for visual and kinematic data with a ==generative model== for difficult-to-simulate sensory signals (audio).
- A state-of-the-art ==video-to-audio diffusion model== (MMAudio) is finetuned on real-world pouring sounds and conditioned with ==semantic segmentation masks== (SAMv2) to generate high-fidelity audio.
- Policies are trained entirely on synthetically generated audiovisual trajectories from MULTIGEN, leveraging extensive ==domain randomization== to achieve zero-shot transfer to real-world pouring tasks.

## Results

- MULTIGEN-trained multimodal policies achieve strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with an average Normalized Mean Absolute Error (NMAE) of **0.46** for various pouring tasks.
- The multimodal (vision + audio) diffusion policy consistently outperforms the vision-only baseline, achieving a **23.3%** average reduction in NMAE, with up to a **29.4%** reduction for opaque containers.
- Generatively synthesized audio from MULTIGEN is both more diverse and accurate than traditional additive noise augmentation, leading to policies with stronger scaling properties and improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale generative models can effectively synthesize complex, difficult-to-model sensory modalities like audio, augmenting traditional physics simulations.
> - Multimodal policies incorporating audio significantly improve robot performance in tasks with visual ambiguities, such as pouring into opaque containers, demonstrating audio's crucial role.
> - Finetuning generative models on domain-specific data and conditioning them with semantic information is critical for producing high-quality synthetic data that enhances downstream robot policy learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is actively pursuing methods to enable robots to perform complex tasks with human-like adaptability and robustness. A significant hurdle in achieving this is the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and well-annotated multimodal datasets. While real-world data collection is prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive, simulation offers a scalable alternative. However, existing physics-based simulators excel primarily in visual modeling and rigid-body dynamics, leaving non-visual modalities—such as sound, touch, or smell—notoriously difficult to simulate with high fidelity.

This paper directly addresses a critical gap in the research landscape: the inability of current simulation paradigms to effectively generate realistic multimodal sensory data, particularly for auditory cues, thereby limiting the scope of sim-to-real transfer to largely vision-based tasks.

**Existing Approaches and Their Limitations:**

- **Multimodal Robot Learning:** Prior work has explored vision and touch extensively for tasks like grasping and fine motor control. Audio, however, has received comparatively less attention, mainly focusing on speech interaction, environment recognition, or sparse use of impact sounds for material classification or liquid quantity estimation. These approaches often rely on limited teleoperated real-world datasets, which do not scale well.
- **Traditional Sound Simulation:** Physics-based sound simulation (e.g., numerical wave propagation, ray tracing) can achieve high fidelity but is computationally expensive, requires detailed material properties, and is impractical for large-scale robot learning.
- **Generative Models in Simulation:** While generative models have been used to enhance simulation (e.g., LucidSim for visual domain randomization, Gen2Sim for 3D asset generation, Eureka for reward function generation), their application has primarily been to *augment existing* visual or procedural capabilities. They haven't been widely adopted to *introduce entirely new sensory streams* that are fundamentally hard to model with traditional physics engines.

**MULTIGEN's Unique Contribution:**

"The Sound of Simulation" introduces a novel paradigm by proposing **MULTIGEN**, a hybrid framework that integrates large-scale generative models directly into traditional physics simulators. This fundamentally changes how difficult-to-simulate modalities are handled. Instead of attempting to *physically model* every complex interaction (like liquid sounds), MULTIGEN *generatively synthesizes* these modalities conditioned on more easily simulated visual and kinematic information.

This work marks a significant departure by being the first to leverage generative audio models within a multimodal robot learning framework specifically for **sim-to-real transfer** of dynamic tasks. It directly tackles the challenge of generating rich, task-relevant auditory data at scale, thereby enabling the training of multimodal policies that can robustly transfer to the real world zero-shot. This pushes the boundaries of what is possible with simulation, moving beyond merely augmenting visual realism to enabling entirely new sensory capabilities in synthetic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to advance robot learning by enabling robots to effectively integrate multiple sensory modalities for robust and adaptive behavior, mirroring human perception. The authors identify two primary challenges that motivate their work:

1. **The Multimodal Data Acquisition Challenge:** Acquiring large-scale, diverse, and synchronized multimodal datasets (video, audio, tactile, actions) for robot learning in the real world is exceedingly difficult. It demands precise calibration, expensive hardware, and extensive manual labor, making current large-scale robot datasets often lacking in diverse sensory information.
2. **The Multimodal Simulation Gap:** While simulation is a promising alternative for data generation, it primarily excels in vision-based tasks. Simulating non-visual modalities, particularly sound, poses significant challenges due to the complex physics involved (wave dynamics, material interactions). This difficulty has largely prevented successful multimodal sim-to-real transfer.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of the paper are:

- To introduce **MULTIGEN**, a novel framework that augments traditional physics-based simulators with large-scale pretrained generative models. The aim is to enable the synthesis of realistic, task-relevant sensory signals (like audio) that are difficult to model physically.
- To demonstrate the effectiveness of MULTIGEN in facilitating **zero-shot sim-to-real transfer** for inherently multimodal robotic tasks, specifically robot pouring. This task is chosen because it strongly relies on auditory feedback due to visual ambiguities (occlusion, transparency) of liquids.
- To show that policies trained on these synthetically generated, rich audiovisual trajectories can generalize to novel real-world conditions (different containers, liquids) without any real robot data.
- To rigorously evaluate the quality and utility of the generatively synthesized audio compared to conventional data augmentation techniques.

Ultimately, the motivation is to close the "multimodal data gap" and pave the way for training more perceptually rich and adaptable robotic systems at scale, moving beyond the current limitations of primarily vision-based robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MULTIGEN proposes a hybrid pipeline that combines a traditional physics-based simulator with a generative model.

#### 3.1. MULTIGEN Framework Overview

The framework consists of:
1. **Physics-Based Simulation Engine:** Provides a controllable environment, modeling visual scenes, rigid-body dynamics, fluid interactions, and robot kinematics. It outputs visual (RGB) and proprioceptive (robot state) data. The authors utilize **RoboVerse**, a scalable platform known for high-fidelity rendering, efficient physics simulation, and parallelized execution. It offers diverse assets and robust Domain Randomization (DR) capabilities.
2. **Generative Model:** Conditions on information from the simulator (e.g., visual renders) to synthesize complementary sensory signals (e.g., audio) that are difficult for the physics engine to model accurately.

The core idea is that the physics engine handles the visually and kinematically consistent aspects, while the generative model fills in the perceptually rich details of hard-to-model modalities. The goal is to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with sufficiently powerful components.

#### 3.2. Instantiation for Robot Pouring

The authors instantiate MULTIGEN for the dynamic and multimodal task of robot pouring.

- **Generative Audio Model:**
 - They select **MMAudio**, a state-of-the-art video-to-audio diffusion model, as their base. MMAudio uses a multimodal transformer to process visual, audio, and textual features, generating sound from raw video frames.
 - **Finetuning:** Recognizing that MMAudio's zero-shot performance is insufficient (due to being trained on diverse but suboptimal, long-tail pouring data), they finetune it on a curated dataset of real-world pouring sounds. This dataset comprises clips from EPIC-Kitchens (first-person cooking videos) and a custom YouTube crawl, featuring various liquids and containers. This finetuning adapts the model to the nuances of pouring sounds.
 - **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Visual Gap:** To address the domain gap between real-world and simulated video (which could degrade MMAudio's performance), they introduce **semantic segmentation conditioning using SAMv2 (Segment Anything Model v2)**. For each video frame, segmentation masks of task-relevant objects (cup, liquid) are extracted and propagated across the sequence. MMAudio is then conditioned on both RGB frames and these segmentation masks, allowing it to focus on semantically meaningful regions and improve robustness to visual shifts.

- **Simulation Environment Setup:**
 - They construct a simulation environment in RoboVerse that coarsely mirrors real-world conditions, without requiring perfect digital twins.
 - **Domain Randomization (DR):** Crucially, DR is applied to a wide range of parameters: liquid properties (density, viscosity, color, transparency, friction), camera pose, lighting conditions, and background/table textures. This variability helps policies generalize to unseen real-world conditions and ensures robustness.

- **Generating Vision and Action:**
 - **Motion Planning:** Diverse and structured pouring trajectories are generated using a motion planning-based strategy. This involves randomizing initial liquid fill, grasp points, pour heights, and discrete pouring amounts (quarter, half, three-quarters, full), with deterministic tilt angles calculated for desired liquid transfer.
 - **Trajectory Execution:** The robot executes a sequence of keypoints: start, grasp, move above receiver, pour, untilt, and end.

- **Generating Audio:**
 - A **parallelized simulation pipeline with two threads** is used for synchronized multimodal data generation.
 - Thread 1: Executes the motion-planned pouring trajectory, recording RGB frames (from a real-world-aligned camera and a *dedicated virtual camera* positioned frontally), proprioception, and action sequences. The virtual camera's view is designed to be in-distribution with the real-world pouring videos used for MMAudio finetuning.
 - Thread 2: Leverages this virtual camera stream to process completed trajectories and generate the corresponding audio using the finetuned MMAudio model.

#### 3.3. Policy Learning and Evaluation

- **Policy Architecture:** They train a **Diffusion Policy**, a state-of-the-art denoising diffusion model that conditionally generates actions given state, vision, and audio inputs.
- **Training Data:** The policy is trained *entirely* on proprioception, vision, and audio data generated by MULTIGEN, with **no real-world robot data**.
- **Baselines:** A vision-only variant of the diffusion policy is also trained for comparison.
- **Evaluation:** Policies are evaluated zero-shot on a comprehensive real-world benchmark spanning diverse pouring conditions (e.g., opaque/transparent containers, various materials and liquid types, different occlusion levels).
- **Metric:** Normalized Mean Absolute Error (NMAE) of the poured liquid amount, averaged across multiple trials and language commands (quarter, half, three-quarters, full pour).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings demonstrating the efficacy of MULTIGEN.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer (Table 1)

- Policies trained with MULTIGEN achieve strong zero-shot transfer to challenging real-world pouring tasks. The average Normalized Mean Absolute Error (NMAE) across all conditions is **0.46**. This indicates that training solely on synthetic multimodal data, without any real robot sensory or action data, enables effective real-world task execution.
- **Impact of Multimodality (Vision + Audio):** The multimodal diffusion policy (Vision + Audio) consistently and significantly outperforms the vision-only baseline across all evaluated conditions. Including audio results in an average **23.3% reduction in NMAE**.
 - The benefit of audio is particularly pronounced for **opaque containers**, where visual feedback is limited. For these tasks, the NMAE reduction averages **29.4%**.
 - Even for **transparent containers**, where visual cues are more abundant, audio still provides a substantial advantage, leading to a **16.2% NMAE reduction**. This highlights audio's crucial role in estimating liquid flow rate and fill levels, even when visual information is partially available.

#### 4.2. Assessing Audio Generation Quality (Figure 4, Ablations)

The authors rigorously compare MULTIGEN's generated audio against standard data augmentation techniques (overlaying environment noise and robot motor sounds) and conduct ablations on the generative model itself.

- **Diversity and Fidelity (Figure 4a):**
 - MULTIGEN produces audio that is both more diverse (higher Log-Spectral Distance) and more accurate (higher Signal-to-Distortion Ratio) than traditional additive noise augmentation.
 - This indicates that MULTIGEN can synthesize a significantly broader range of audio samples while maintaining physical consistency and high fidelity to real-world pouring sounds. It avoids mode collapse and generates dynamically accurate audio even for samples far from the training distribution.
- **Usefulness for Policy Learning (Figure 4b):**
 - Policies trained with MULTIGEN's generated audiovisual data significantly outperform policies trained with augmentation-based audio.
 - Furthermore, MULTIGEN-trained policies exhibit stronger scaling properties, showing better performance gains with an increasing number of generated trajectories, compared to policies trained with naive augmentation. This confirms that the higher quality and diversity of MULTIGEN's synthesized audio directly translate to improved multimodal policy learning.
- **Generative Model Ablation (Zero-Shot vs. Finetuned MMAudio - Appendix B.2):**
 - A controlled ablation demonstrates that finetuning the MMAudio model on domain-specific pouring data, coupled with semantic segmentation conditioning, is essential.
 - The finetuned model achieves a **45% increase in Signal-to-Distortion Ratio (SDR)**, a **26.7% reduction in Log-Spectral Distance (LSD)**, and a **52.2% increase in Audio Diversity** compared to the zero-shot pretrained MMAudio.
 - Critically, this improved audio quality leads to a **26.0% reduction in policy NMAE**, confirming that task-specific adaptation of the generative model is crucial for downstream robotic task performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Sound of Simulation" paper presents a highly significant contribution to robot learning and artificial intelligence, with profound potential impacts:

1. **A New Paradigm for Multimodal Simulation:** MULTIGEN introduces a groundbreaking approach to synthesizing challenging sensory modalities (like audio) that defy traditional physics-based simulation. By integrating large-scale generative models, it fundamentally expands the capabilities of simulation beyond mere visual or rigid-body modeling. This could catalyze a shift in how synthetic data is generated for complex, perceptually rich robotic tasks.

2. **Closing the Multimodal Sim-to-Real Gap:** The work provides a viable and scalable solution to the long-standing challenge of learning multimodal robot policies without requiring extensive, costly, and labor-intensive real-world data collection for specific modalities. Demonstrating zero-shot transfer for a complex task like pouring signifies a critical step towards more general-purpose and adaptable robotic systems.

3. **Enhanced Robot Perception and Dexterity:** By enabling robots to leverage rich auditory feedback, especially in conditions of visual ambiguity or occlusion, MULTIGEN paves the way for robots to perform more dexterous and dynamic manipulation tasks. This is crucial for real-world deployment, where robots must operate in environments that are often noisy, cluttered, and where visual information alone is insufficient for precise control (e.g., pouring in low light, liquid transfer into opaque containers, tasks involving contact-rich interactions).

4. **Accelerated Robot Learning:** The ability to generate vast quantities of high-fidelity, multimodal synthetic data dramatically accelerates the iterative cycle of robot policy development, training, and evaluation. Researchers and practitioners can iterate on complex behaviors much faster, leading to more robust and capable robots.

5. **Foundation for Future Multimodal Generative Simulators:** This framework lays the groundwork for incorporating other difficult-to-simulate modalities (e.g., haptic feedback, subtle material interactions, even olfactory cues) into synthetic training environments. The core idea of using generative models to "fill in the blanks" where physics engines struggle can be generalized, potentially leading to truly human-like sensory perception in robotic agents.

6. **Reduced Barriers to Entry for Robotics Research:** By mitigating the need for specialized hardware and extensive real-world data collection, MULTIGEN could lower the barrier to entry for robotics research, allowing more researchers to experiment with and develop advanced multimodal robot policies.

In summary, MULTIGEN represents a significant leap forward in robot learning, demonstrating how the power of generative AI can be harnessed to overcome fundamental limitations of simulation, leading to more intelligent, robust, and perceptually aware robots.
