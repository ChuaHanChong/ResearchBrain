# ARM: Advantage Reward Modeling for Long-Horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Advantage Reward Modeling (ARM) introduces a framework for long-horizon robotic manipulation that estimates relative advantage between states, moving beyond absolute progress metrics to provide stable reward signals. This method achieved a 99.4% success rate on a bimanual towel-folding task while reducing human annotation time by 2.5 times and automated labeling speed by over 20 times.

## Problem

- Long-horizon robotic manipulation struggles with the "Reward Engineering Bottleneck," where defining scalable and stable reward functions, especially for absolute progress, is challenging.
- Existing dense reward methods suffer from VLM unreliability, coarse subtask partitions, and an inability to accurately characterize non-linear operational errors or regressive behaviors.
- Imitation learning from suboptimal human demonstrations often leads to policy convergence difficulties due to noisy data and a lack of effective mechanisms to prioritize high-value samples.

## Method

- A ==Tri-state Advantage Labeling Strategy== categorizes state transitions into Progressive, Regressive, or Stagnant, simplifying human annotation and enabling automated pseudo-labeling.
- An **ARM** (==Advantage Reward Model==), a multimodal ==Temporal Transformer==, predicts relative advantage gains from temporal video and proprioceptive states, integrating a ==task completion head== for global consistency.
- ==Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning== (AW-BC) extends Reward-Aligned Behavior Cloning by incorporating adaptive scaling coefficients to reweight samples based on predicted interval gains, filtering suboptimal data and prioritizing recovery trajectories.

## Results

- **ARM** demonstrated superior fidelity in dense progress estimation with an MSE of **0.0014**, outperforming SARM's **0.0059**, and achieved **100%** accuracy in identifying successful and failure terminal episodes.
- The tri-state labeling strategy significantly boosted annotation efficiency, achieving **2.5x** faster human throughput and over **20x** faster automated labeling compared to manual baselines.
- Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning (AW-BC) using **ARM** signals resulted in a **99.4%** success rate on a complex bimanual towel-folding task, surpassing the **62.1%** of standard BC and **78.5%** of Reward-Aware BC (GR00T+SARM).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Estimating relative advantage between states offers a more robust and task-agnostic approach to reward modeling for long-horizon tasks, accommodating non-monotonic behaviors and error recovery; the proposed Advantage Reward Model (ARM) reconstructs smooth, globally consistent dense progress curves by combining these localized relative advantage predictions with definitive task completion anchors.
> - Simplifying reward annotation to a categorical tri-state classification significantly reduces human cognitive load, increasing labeling throughput and quality for dense reward generation.
> - Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning (AW-BC) can effectively filter suboptimal demonstration data and prioritize learning from high-value recovery trajectories, leading to more robust policy refinement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which enable robots to perform general-purpose tasks. However, a significant portion of current VLA approaches relies on imitation learning (IL). This dependency necessitates the collection of extensive datasets, a process that is resource-intensive, requiring considerable human labor and physical infrastructure. Furthermore, human demonstrations, especially for complex, long-horizon tasks, frequently contain suboptimal behaviors and noise, which can impede the convergence and robustness of learned policies.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers an alternative for refining policies autonomously beyond initial demonstrations. Nevertheless, effective RL for long-horizon manipulation is contingent upon the availability of informative reward signals. Sparse rewards, such as binary success indicators, are simple to define but often prove insufficient for guiding learning in complex, multi-stage tasks due to the challenges of credit assignment. Consequently, there is a recognized need for high-quality dense rewards or informative value functions to provide continuous supervision and facilitate effective learning.

Existing frameworks attempting to leverage dense signals, often through advantage estimation or sample reweighting, are frequently limited by what the authors term the "Reward Engineering Bottleneck." This bottleneck arises from the difficulty and cost associated with designing reward functions that provide stable, high-frequency, and precise feedback. Specific limitations of current progress modeling paradigms include:
1. **Unreliability and Cost of Zero-shot VLMs:** These models often incur high inference overhead and provide low-precision annotations, lacking spatial-geometric grounding. This can lead to non-monotonic oscillations in reward signals, misrepresenting actual task progress.
2. **Quantization Ambiguity in Failure States:** Many methods assume strict monotonicity of task progress with time. This assumption struggles with real-world scenarios involving mistakes, retries, temporary regressions, or complex recovery maneuvers, leading to misspecified reward signals. Simple video rewinding, used to simulate regression, does not fully capture authentic, non-linear operational errors.
3. **Coarse Subtask Partitions:** Relying on broad subtask divisions often fails to capture the fine-grained intra-stage transitions, such as critical recovery or corrective maneuvers, which are essential for long-horizon tasks. This can result in misaligned reward signals and unstable policy updates.
4. **Temporal Monotonicity Assumption:** Related work that predicts interval gains often assumes a positive correlation between task progress and time, which does not naturally accommodate regressive or recovery behaviors.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing Advantage Reward Modeling (ARM), a framework that shifts the focus from quantifying absolute progress to estimating relative advantage. This approach is designed to provide a more intuitive, concise, and task-agnostic primitive for annotation, thereby accommodating non-monotonic behaviors and reducing the reward engineering bottleneck. It specifically improves upon Reward-Aligned Behavior Cloning (RA-BC) paradigms by eliminating the reliance on explicit environment rewards or labor-intensive manual language annotations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the reward engineering bottleneck, a significant challenge in applying reinforcement learning to complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. The authors identify several critical problems with existing reward generation methodologies that motivate their work:

1. **Sparse Reward Inadequacy:** In long-horizon tasks, simple sparse rewards (e.g., a binary success indicator) provide insufficient learning signals. This often leads to difficulties in credit assignment, where it is unclear which actions contributed to eventual success or failure, causing convergence issues for RL policies.
2. **Cost and Instability of Dense Rewards:** While dense rewards offer more continuous supervision, their engineering is costly and prone to instability. Designing a reward function that provides high-frequency, precise, and stable feedback across various task stages, including non-monotonic behaviors like backtracking or error recovery, is a formidable challenge.
3. **Limitations of Absolute Progress Metrics:** Current reward models frequently rely on quantifying "absolute progress," which requires highly specific, task-dependent heuristics. These heuristics are difficult to scale to diverse tasks and environments. Furthermore, methods based on absolute progress often assume strict monotonicity, meaning progress should always increase over time. This assumption is unrealistic for real-world robotic operations, where agents might need to regress or perform corrective actions to ultimately succeed.
4. **Unreliability of VLM-based Reward Generation:** Zero-shot Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have been explored for reward generation, but they suffer from high inference costs and low precision, particularly due to a lack of spatial-geometric grounding. This results in noisy and oscillating reward signals that can destabilize policy learning.
5. **Cognitive Load and Inconsistency in Manual Annotation:** Traditional dense reward labeling typically involves human annotators assigning a continuous scalar progress value to each frame of a video. This process imposes a high cognitive load, leading to inter-annotator inconsistency and substantial engineering overhead due to the subjective nature of "progress."

To address these motivations, the key objective is to develop a cost-effective, scalable, and robust framework for generating high-fidelity, informative reward signals for long-horizon manipulation. The core insight driving this objective is that defining "relative advantage" between states is more intuitive, concise, and task-agnostic than defining absolute progress. By decoupling progress rewards from global temporal anchors, such a system can naturally accommodate regressive behaviors and error recovery, providing a more accurate and stable learning signal for offline reinforcement learning and behavior cloning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Advantage Reward Modeling (ARM), redefines the approach to reward generation by shifting from absolute progress estimation to relative advantage. It consists of three integrated components: the Advantage Reward Model (ARM), Global Progress Reconstruction, and Policy Optimization via Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning (AW-BC).

#### 3.1. Advantage Reward Model (ARM)

The ARM is a Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) Temporal Transformer designed to estimate fine-grained relative advantages from multimodal observations. It contrasts with traditional "Multi-Input Single-Output" (MISO) models by processing temporal context to classify relative advantage instead of regressing a single scalar.

**MIMO Transformer Architecture:**
- **Temporal Context:** The model processes a sequence of historical observations `W_t = {o_{t-4k}..., o_t}` within a causal window. This window-based approach utilizes past frames for prediction, ensuring real-time inference and compatibility with both online and offline RL by avoiding reliance on future information.
- **Multimodal Fusion:** For each timestep `i` in the causal window, ARM integrates three signals:
 - CLIP-based visual features (`v_i`).
 - Robot proprioceptive states (`s_i`).
 - Task instructions (`g`).
 These inputs are projected into a unified latent space via Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to form a fused multimodal embedding `x_i = MLP(v_i) + MLP(s_i) + MLP(g)`.
- **Transformer Encoder:** The sequence of fused embeddings `{x_i}` is then fed into an 8-layer Transformer Encoder, which produces temporally enriched latent representations `{h_i}`. Each `h_i` encapsulates the historical evolution and kinematic state at that specific moment.
- **Dual-Head Learning Objective:** ARM is optimized with two synergistic output heads:
 - **Multi-frame Advantage Classification (Interval Head):** This head infers the relative advantage transitions (`Δŷ`) between consecutive hidden states (`h_i`, `h_{i+1}`). It is trained using a standard cross-entropy loss (`L_int`), supervised by the tri-state labels. Formulating this as a discrete classification task enhances robustness against non-linear noise and temporal stochasticity in demonstrations.
 - **Task Completion Prediction (Completion Head):** This head predicts the probability (`C_t`) that the current observation `s_t` represents a successful terminal state. This prediction serves to anchor relative advantage estimations to absolute task metrics and helps identify success points. To counteract severe class imbalance (successful terminal frames being rare), this head is optimized using Focal Loss (`L_succ`).
 - The total objective is a weighted sum: `L_ARM = λ_int L_int + λ_succ L_succ`. This joint training allows ARM to reconstruct continuous progress curves, accurately identify regressive behaviors, and pinpoint critical task completion moments.

**Lightweight Tri-state Auto Labeling Strategy:**
To address the cognitive load and inconsistency of traditional continuous reward labeling, ARM introduces a simplified tri-state categorical classification for relative advantage. For any observation pair (`s_t`, `s_{t+k}`), a label `y ∈ {-1, 0, +1}` is assigned:
- `+1` (Progressing): The state advances towards the task goal.
- `-1` (Regressing): The state deviates from the goal, encounters an error, or indicates failure.
- `0` (Stagnant): No substantial progress is made (e.g., waiting or idle).
This simplified paradigm facilitates efficient initial human annotation for cold-starting the model. Subsequently, the trained ARM can perform inference on large volumes of unannotated trajectories, generating pseudo-labeled data at scale.

#### 3.2. Global Progress Reconstruction

This automated pipeline synthesizes the discrete interval gains predicted by ARM into coherent, globally consistent dense progress curves.
- **Parallel Inference:** The MIMO architecture processes segments of lengthy episodic trajectories concurrently as parallel batches. This is achieved by partitioning videos into independent, non-overlapping segments, significantly accelerating inference compared to traditional sliding-window methods with redundant computations.
- **Sequence Alignment and Padding:** For video segments shorter than the model's specified window size, a tail-frame replication padding strategy is applied. During final aggregation, predictions from these synthetically padded regions are discarded.
- **Coherent Progress Generation:** The system integrates the model-predicted relative state transitions (`Δŷ`) with the absolute task completion signal (`C_t`). By treating `C_t` as a definitive progress anchor (e.g., `P_T = 1.0` at task completion), dense progress values for preceding frames are reconstructed by accumulating `Δŷ`. This process transforms local relative predictions into a dense, high-fidelity global progress signal.

#### 3.3. Policy Optimization via AW-BC (Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning)

AW-BC is designed to optimize policies by leveraging the reconstructed dense progress signals to prioritize learning from high-advantage transitions and suppress suboptimal behaviors.
- **Length-adaptive Gain Formulation:** To mitigate length bias in heterogeneous demonstrations, an adaptive scaling mechanism is introduced. For an action chunk with horizon `H`, the length-adaptive gain `ΔG_t` is formulated as `ΔG_t = (P_{t+H} - P_t) * (L_seq / L_bar)`, where `P_t` is the progress value, `L_seq` is the current episode length, and `L_bar` is the average episode length across the dataset. This normalization ensures the advantage reflects the relative efficiency of an action sequence, independent of its absolute duration.
- **Statistical Weighting and Objective:** Raw gains are converted into robust training weights using statistical normalization. Mean (`μ`) and standard deviation (`σ`) of `{ΔG_i}` are computed for the current batch, defining clipping bounds `b_lower = μ - 2σ` and `b_upper = μ + 2σ`. The importance weight `w_i` for each sample is clamped: `w_i = clamp((ΔG_i - b_lower) / (b_upper - b_lower + ϵ), 0, 1)`. This mechanism filters out regressive data (weights approaching 0) and caps outlier influence. The final AW-BC objective minimizes the weighted negative log-likelihood: `L_AW-BC(θ) = E_{(s,a)~D}[-w(s, a) log π_θ(a|s)]`.
- **Connection to Offline RL:** This formulation aligns with Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR), where ARM functions as a learned Critic. It provides advantage estimates (`ΔG_t`) that guide policy updates, effectively performing offline policy improvement by prioritizing transitions with high relative advantage from suboptimal demonstrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the ARM framework on a long-horizon bimanual towel-folding task, a complex 8-stage procedure. The dataset `D_all` comprised 972 episodes (20 hours), including expert demonstrations and DAgger-augmented error-correction episodes.

#### 4.1. Reward Model Performance

**Quantitative Evaluation (Table 1):**
- ARM demonstrated superior fidelity in dense progress estimation compared to SARM, achieving an MSE of 0.0014 versus SARM's 0.0059 when measured against Ground Truth (GT) progress.
- ARM achieved 100% success identification accuracy for both standard successful episodes (SE) and failure episodes (FE) on a validation set. SARM achieved 83.3% for SE and 91.6% for FE.

**Qualitative Analysis (Fig. 5 & Fig. 6):**
- SARM's progress curves were characterized by stepped transitions at subtask boundaries, failing to capture localized regressive movements.
- ARM, by leveraging relative advantage signals, generated smooth, dense progress curves that closely tracked the GT, even during non-monotonic robot adjustments and recovery behaviors.
- The tri-state labeling approach yielded smoother and more consistent dense progress signals compared to the stepped curves produced by manual segmentation and VLM methods.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Quality of Reward Labeling

**Labeling Throughput (Table 3):**
- The proposed human Tri-state labeling protocol achieved a throughput of 250 samples per 8-hour shift, representing a 2.5× speedup over the Human Baseline (Subtask Segmentation, 100 samples per 8-hour shift).
- The automated Tri-state pipeline demonstrated substantial scalability, processing over 400,000 samples per 8 hours on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU, indicating a >133× speedup over human baselines and significantly outperforming VLM-based labeling (∼3,000 samples per 8 hours).

#### 4.3. MIMO Architecture Efficiency Analysis

**Inference Throughput (Table 4):**
- ARM, with its MIMO design, achieved an inference speed of 14.1 iterations per second (it/s) (calculated as 2.82 × 5 outputs).
- This represents a 13.7× speedup compared to VLM-based labeling (MISO, 1.03 it/s) and a 3.6× improvement over the SARM baseline (SISO, 3.9 it/s). The efficiency gain is attributed to the MIMO architecture's ability to process multiple advantage predictions simultaneously.

#### 4.4. Policy Performance Analysis

Policy performance was evaluated using GR00T-N1.5-3B as the foundation model.

**Quantitative Comparison (Table 2):**
- **BC-Baseline (GR00T N1.5):** Achieved a 62.1% success rate, 18 episodes per hour task throughput, and a folding precision score of 2.2. This performance was attributed to the inability of standard Behavior Cloning to filter noise and suboptimal trajectories in the dataset.
- **RA-BC (GR00T + SARM):** Improved success rate to 78.5%, task throughput to 24 episodes per hour, and folding precision to 2.7. While an improvement, it was still limited by the lack of fine-grained advantage estimation for error-correction.
- **AW-BC (GR00T + ARM, Ours):** Demonstrated a 99.4% success rate, 32 episodes per hour task throughput, and the highest folding precision of 3.6. This indicates that the advantage-weighted objective effectively prioritizes high-quality, decisive movements, resulting in more agile and purposeful trajectories.

**Ablation Study (Table 5):**
- **Tri-state vs. Task Segmentation:** Comparing SARM (Task Segmentation + RA-BC) with ARM (Tri-state + RA-BC) showed that tri-state labeling alone improved the success rate from 78.5% to 92.3%, a gain of +13.8%. This highlights the superior annotation quality and efficiency of the tri-state approach.
- **AW-BC vs. RA-BC:** Comparing ARM (Tri-state + RA-BC) with ARM (Tri-state + AW-BC) demonstrated that the advantage-weighted training significantly increased the success rate from 92.3% to 99.4%, a +7.1% improvement. This finding underscores the effectiveness of dense advantage signals in policy optimization.
- The complete ARM framework achieved a +20.9% improvement over SARM, indicating a synergistic effect between tri-state labeling and AW-BC training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ARM framework presents a notable advancement in addressing the reward engineering bottleneck prevalent in long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. By conceptualizing reward as relative advantage rather than absolute progress, the approach offers several significant implications.

Firstly, the proposed tri-state labeling strategy substantially reduces the human cognitive load and annotation cost typically associated with generating dense reward signals. This simplification improves cross-annotator consistency and facilitates the automated generation of large-scale pseudo-labeled datasets, which is crucial for scaling robotic learning to more diverse and complex scenarios. The demonstrated efficiency gains in labeling throughput suggest a pathway toward significantly lowering data collection barriers for VLA models.

Secondly, ARM's ability to accurately reconstruct dense progress trajectories, even in the presence of non-monotonic behaviors such as backtracking and error recovery, marks an important improvement over methods that rely on strict monotonicity assumptions. This nuanced understanding of task progress provides higher-fidelity supervision, which is critical for learning robust policies capable of handling real-world task complexities and unexpected deviations.

Thirdly, the integration of ARM with Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning (AW-BC) demonstrates a robust mechanism for policy improvement from suboptimal demonstrations. By adaptively reweighting samples based on their predicted advantage, AW-BC effectively filters out suboptimal actions and prioritizes valuable recovery trajectories. The empirical results, particularly the 99.4% success rate on a challenging towel-folding task, indicate that this approach leads to more stable, efficient, and precise robotic manipulation policies compared to existing baselines.

The findings suggest that ARM provides a scalable and robust solution for training high-performance robotic policies with reduced human intervention during the policy training phase. This could accelerate the development and deployment of more general-purpose robot manipulation systems in unstructured environments, by making the generation of effective learning signals more accessible and reliable. The framework's capacity to handle the inherent noise and suboptimality of real-world demonstration data has broader implications for advancing offline reinforcement learning and robot learning from demonstration paradigms.
