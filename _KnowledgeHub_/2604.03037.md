# ARM: Advantage Reward Modeling for Long-Horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Advantage Reward Modeling (ARM) introduces a framework for long-horizon robotic manipulation that estimates relative advantage between states, moving beyond absolute progress metrics to provide stable reward signals. This method achieved a 99.4% success rate on a bimanual towel-folding task while reducing human annotation time by 2.5 times and automated labeling speed by over 20 times.

## Problem

- Long-horizon robotic manipulation struggles with the "Reward Engineering Bottleneck," where defining scalable and stable reward functions, especially for absolute progress, is challenging.
- Existing dense reward methods suffer from VLM unreliability, coarse subtask partitions, and an inability to accurately characterize non-linear operational errors or regressive behaviors.
- Imitation learning from suboptimal human demonstrations often leads to policy convergence difficulties due to noisy data and a lack of effective mechanisms to prioritize high-value samples.

## Method

- A ==Tri-state Advantage Labeling Strategy== categorizes state transitions into Progressive, Regressive, or Stagnant, simplifying human annotation and enabling automated pseudo-labeling.
- An **ARM** (==Advantage Reward Model==), a multimodal ==Temporal Transformer==, predicts relative advantage gains from temporal video and proprioceptive states, integrating a ==task completion head== for global consistency.
- ==Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning== (AW-BC) extends Reward-Aligned Behavior Cloning by incorporating adaptive scaling coefficients to reweight samples based on predicted interval gains, filtering suboptimal data and prioritizing recovery trajectories.

## Results

- **ARM** demonstrated superior fidelity in dense progress estimation with an MSE of **0.0014**, outperforming SARM's **0.0059**, and achieved **100%** accuracy in identifying successful and failure terminal episodes.
- The tri-state labeling strategy significantly boosted annotation efficiency, achieving **2.5x** faster human throughput and over **20x** faster automated labeling compared to manual baselines.
- Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning (AW-BC) using **ARM** signals resulted in a **99.4%** success rate on a complex bimanual towel-folding task, surpassing the **62.1%** of standard BC and **78.5%** of Reward-Aware BC (GR00T+SARM).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Estimating relative advantage between states offers a more robust and task-agnostic approach to reward modeling for long-horizon tasks, accommodating non-monotonic behaviors and error recovery.
> - Simplifying reward annotation to a categorical tri-state classification significantly reduces human cognitive load, increasing labeling throughput and quality for dense reward generation.
> - The proposed Advantage Reward Model (ARM) can reconstruct smooth, globally consistent dense progress curves by combining localized relative advantage predictions with definitive task completion anchors.
> - Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning (AW-BC) can effectively filter suboptimal demonstration data and prioritize learning from high-value recovery trajectories, leading to more robust policy refinement.
