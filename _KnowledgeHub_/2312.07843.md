# Foundation Models in Robotics: Applications, Challenges, and the Future

> [!summary] Summary
> This overview maps the current landscape of foundation models in robotics, demonstrating how large-scale models pre-trained on diverse data enhance robotic capabilities across perception, decision-making, and control. It highlights their application in enabling robots to understand natural language instructions and generalize to novel tasks without explicit re-training.

## Problem

- Robotics research traditionally relies on task-specific deep learning models that lack generalization to new environments or tasks.
- Robots face challenges operating in complex, unstructured environments requiring high adaptability and robustness.
- Difficulty in enabling robots to understand natural language instructions or adapt to novel objects without extensive re-training.

## Method

- Adapting large-scale foundation models (e.g., LLMs, ViTs, VLMs) from NLP and computer vision fields to robotics.
- Integrating these models for language-conditioned robot policy learning, high-level task planning, and open-vocabulary perception.
- Developing specialized transformer architectures like ==Robot Transformers== (RT-1, RT-2) for end-to-end multimodal input processing and action generation.

## Results

- Robots have demonstrated the ability to execute manipulation tasks from natural language instructions (e.g., "pick up the red cup") without explicit task-specific training.
- Robot Transformers (e.g., RT-2) enable robots to perform novel visual-language tasks, such as "place the red cube in the blue bowl," without prior direct training on that specific instruction.
- Foundation models enhance robot perception, allowing open-vocabulary object detection, semantic segmentation, and 3D scene understanding based on natural language descriptions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Foundation models offer generalization capabilities due to their vast pre-training data, allowing robots to adapt to diverse downstream tasks.
> - The integration of natural language understanding allows robots to interpret human instructions and perform complex high-level task planning.
> - Open-vocabulary perception and 3D scene representation enable robots to recognize and reason about novel objects and environments based on textual descriptions.
