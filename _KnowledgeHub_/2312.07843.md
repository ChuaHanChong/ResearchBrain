# Foundation Models in Robotics: Applications, Challenges, and the Future

> [!summary] Summary
> This overview maps the current landscape of foundation models in robotics, demonstrating how large-scale models pre-trained on diverse data enhance robotic capabilities across perception, decision-making, and control. It highlights their application in enabling robots to understand natural language instructions and generalize to novel tasks without explicit re-training.

## Problem

- Robotics research traditionally relies on task-specific deep learning models that lack generalization to new environments or tasks.
- Robots face challenges operating in complex, unstructured environments requiring high adaptability and robustness.
- Difficulty in enabling robots to understand natural language instructions or adapt to novel objects without extensive re-training.

## Method

- Adapting large-scale foundation models (e.g., LLMs, ViTs, VLMs) from NLP and computer vision fields to robotics.
- Integrating these models for language-conditioned robot policy learning, high-level task planning, and open-vocabulary perception.
- Developing specialized transformer architectures like ==Robot Transformers== (RT-1, RT-2) for end-to-end multimodal input processing and action generation.

## Results

- Robots have demonstrated the ability to execute manipulation tasks from natural language instructions (e.g., "pick up the red cup") without explicit task-specific training.
- Robot Transformers (e.g., RT-2) enable robots to perform novel visual-language tasks, such as "place the red cube in the blue bowl," without prior direct training on that specific instruction.
- Foundation models enhance robot perception, allowing open-vocabulary object detection, semantic segmentation, and 3D scene understanding based on natural language descriptions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Foundation models offer generalization capabilities due to their vast pre-training data, allowing robots to adapt to diverse downstream tasks.
> - The integration of natural language understanding allows robots to interpret human instructions and perform complex high-level task planning.
> - Open-vocabulary perception and 3D scene representation enable robots to recognize and reason about novel objects and environments based on textual descriptions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotics research has traditionally relied on task-specific deep learning models that are designed and trained for individual applications. While effective for specific scenarios, these models often struggle with generalization to new environments or tasks. This limitation has prompted researchers to explore the potential of foundation models—large-scale models pre-trained on vast amounts of data—to enhance robotic capabilities across perception, decision-making, and control.

Foundation models have already transformed fields like natural language processing (NLP) and computer vision, enabling impressive generalization capabilities. These models are now being adapted for robotics applications to address the significant challenges of operating in complex, unstructured environments that require adaptability and robustness.

#### 1.1. Background on Foundation Models

Foundation models represent a paradigm shift in artificial intelligence, characterized by their massive scale, extensive pre-training on diverse datasets, and adaptability across various downstream tasks. These models typically employ transformer architectures that enable efficient processing of sequential data through self-attention mechanisms.

The key types of foundation models being adapted for robotics include:

1. **Large Language Models (LLMs)**: Models like GPT-4 and PaLM can comprehend and generate human-like text, enabling robots to understand natural language instructions and engage in meaningful dialogue.

2. **Vision Transformers (ViTs)**: These models process visual information using transformer architectures, enabling more robust visual perception capabilities.

3. **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: By combining visual and textual understanding, models like CLIP enable robots to understand open-vocabulary commands about objects in their environment.

4. **Multimodal Models**: These integrate multiple sensory modalities (vision, language, audio) to build comprehensive representations of the world.

The mathematical foundation of transformer-based models can be expressed through the attention mechanism:

$$
\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
$$

Where Q, K, and V represent queries, keys, and values, respectively, and $d_k$ is the dimension of the keys.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

This overview presents the current landscape of foundation models in robotics, examining their applications, the challenges researchers face in implementing them, and the future directions this rapidly evolving field might take.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Foundation Models in Robot Policy Learning

Integrating foundation models into robot policy learning has spawned several innovative approaches:

#### 3.2. Language-Conditioned Imitation Learning

Language-conditioned imitation learning leverages natural language to specify tasks and guide robot behavior. Models like CLIPort, Play-LMP, and PerAct combine language understanding with visual processing to perform manipulation tasks based on verbal instructions. These approaches enable robots to execute tasks like "pick up the red cup" without needing task-specific training.

The general formulation involves learning a policy π that maps observations o and language instructions l to actions a:

$$
\pi(a|o,l) \rightarrow \text{Robot Action}
$$

#### 3.3. Language-Assisted Reinforcement Learning

These approaches use language models to enhance reinforcement learning by providing rewards, exploration strategies, or policy guidance. For example, the Adaptive Agent (AdA) framework leverages LLMs to dynamically adapt to new environments and tasks by generating exploratory behaviors based on natural language feedback.

#### 3.4. Language-Image Goal-Conditioned Value Learning

Models like R3M, SayCan, and VIP integrate language and vision to learn goal-conditioned policies. For instance, SayCan translates high-level language instructions into sequences of executable skills by combining LLM-based planning with learned affordance functions that ground language in the physical world.

#### 3.5. Foundation Models in Robot Task Planning

High-level task planning represents another area where foundation models are making significant contributions:

#### 3.6. High-Level Task Planning

Systems like NL2TL translate natural language instructions into formal task specifications that robots can execute. These approaches leverage the semantic understanding capabilities of LLMs to decompose complex instructions into manageable subtasks while maintaining logical coherence.

For example, an instruction like "make me a sandwich" might be decomposed into:
1. Navigate to refrigerator
2. Open refrigerator door
3. Locate bread and condiments
4. Place items on counter
5. Assemble sandwich
6. Clean workspace

#### 3.7. LLM-Based Code Generation

ProgPrompt, Code-as-Policy, and ChatGPT-Robotics represent approaches that use LLMs to generate executable code from natural language descriptions. These systems effectively turn LLMs into programmers that write code to control robots, bridging the gap between human intent and robot execution.

```python
# Example of LLM-generated code for a simple pick-and-place task
def pick_and_place(robot, object_name, destination):
    # Locate the object
    object_location = robot.vision_system.find_object(object_name)

    # Move to the object
    robot.arm.move_to(object_location)

    # Grasp the object
    robot.gripper.close()

    # Move to destination
    robot.arm.move_to(destination)

    # Release the object
    robot.gripper.open()
```

#### 3.8. Robot Transformers and Code Generation

Robot Transformers represent specialized architectures adapted for robotics applications:

#### 3.9. Robot Transformers

Models like RT-1, RT-2, and LATTE apply transformer architectures directly to robotics tasks. These models process multimodal inputs (images, sensor readings, task specifications) to predict appropriate robot actions. RT-2, for example, combines vision-language capabilities with action generation, enabling robots to respond to novel instructions like "place the red cube in the blue bowl" without explicit training on that specific task.

The transformer architecture allows these models to attend to relevant parts of the input data when determining actions:

$$
\text{RobotAction} = \text{TransformerDecoder}(\text{TransformerEncoder}([\text{VisualToken}, \text{LanguageToken}, \text{StateToken}]))
$$

#### 3.10. Foundation Models in Perception

Foundation models have significantly enhanced robot perception capabilities:

#### 3.11. Open-Vocabulary Object Detection and 3D Classification

Models like OWL-ViT, GLIP, and PointCLIP enable robots to recognize objects described in natural language without requiring extensive labeled training data for each object category. This "open-vocabulary" capability allows robots to identify novel objects based on textual descriptions.

#### 3.12. Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation

LSeg, Segment Anything, and MobileSAM provide pixel-level understanding of scenes, allowing robots to identify regions corresponding to arbitrary language descriptions. This enables more precise interaction with objects and navigation through complex environments.

#### 3.13. Open-Vocabulary 3D Scene Representation

CLIP-NERF, LERF, and DFF extend language-grounded understanding to 3D representations, allowing robots to reason about spatial relationships and objects from multiple viewpoints. These approaches enable more robust navigation and manipulation in 3D environments.

#### 3.14. Affordances

Affordance models like Affordance Diffusion and VRB learn to predict how objects can be interacted with, providing robots with an understanding of "what can be done" with various objects in their environment.

#### 3.15. Embodied AI and Simulation

Embodied AI represents the integration of multiple foundation models to create more general-purpose robotic systems:

#### 3.16. Generalist AI

Projects like EmbodiedGPT, MineDojo, and ELLM aim to develop generalist systems that can perceive, reason about, and act within physical or simulated environments. These systems integrate perception, planning, and control capabilities into unified models that can solve diverse tasks.

#### 3.17. Simulators

Simulated environments are crucial for robotics research, allowing for safer and more efficient training. Advanced simulators leverage foundation models to create more realistic and diverse training environments, accelerating the development of robust robotic systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Despite significant progress, several challenges hinder the broader adoption of foundation models in robotics:

#### 4.1. Data Scarcity

Unlike text or image datasets, high-quality robotics data is relatively scarce. This scarcity limits the ability to train large-scale models specifically for robotics tasks. Researchers are exploring data augmentation, synthetic data generation, and transfer learning to address this challenge.

#### 4.2. Real-Time Performance

Foundation models are computationally intensive, often making real-time execution on robotic hardware challenging. Researchers are developing model compression techniques, specialized hardware, and efficient inference methods to address these constraints.

#### 4.3. Multimodal Representation Limitations

Effectively combining inputs from different modalities (vision, language, tactile sensing) remains challenging, particularly in real-world settings where sensor data may be noisy or incomplete.

#### 4.4. Uncertainty Quantification

Robots operating in the real world need to understand the limitations of their knowledge. Developing methods to quantify uncertainty in foundation model outputs is crucial for safe deployment.

#### 4.5. Safety Evaluation

Ensuring that foundation model-powered robots operate safely presents significant challenges, particularly given their ability to generalize to novel situations that may not have been considered during development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Several promising research directions are emerging to address current limitations:

#### 5.1. Robot-Specific Foundation Models

Developing foundation models specifically designed for robotics, rather than adapting existing NLP or vision models, could yield significant performance improvements.

#### 5.2. Scaling Robot Learning

Collecting and leveraging large-scale, diverse robotics datasets will be crucial for training more capable foundation models. Approaches like robot play data collection, where robots explore their environment without specific task objectives, show promise.

#### 5.3. Robust Simulation-to-Real Transfer

Improving the transfer of skills learned in simulation to real-world environments will accelerate development while reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection.

#### 5.4. Integrated Perception-Action Systems

Moving beyond modular approaches to more tightly integrated perception and action systems could enable more robust and adaptive robot behavior.

#### 5.5. Safety-Critical Robotics

Developing rigorous safety guarantees for foundation model-powered robots will be essential for their deployment in safety-critical applications.

#### 5.6. Conclusion

Foundation models represent a transformative approach to robotics, offering unprecedented generalization capabilities and the potential to significantly accelerate progress in the field. By leveraging pre-trained knowledge from massive datasets, these models enable robots to understand natural language instructions, perceive their environment in an open-vocabulary manner, and adapt to novel situations.

While significant challenges remain—particularly in data collection, computational efficiency, multimodal integration, and safety—the rapid pace of advancement suggests that foundation models will play an increasingly important role in robotics research and applications. Continued collaboration between robotics, AI, and machine learning communities will be essential to realize the full potential of foundation models in creating more capable, adaptable, and useful robotic systems.

The future of robotics powered by foundation models promises systems that can operate effectively in complex, unstructured environments, understand and respond to human instructions naturally, and adapt to new tasks with minimal additional training—bringing us closer to the vision of general-purpose robots that can assist humans across a wide range of applications.
