# TopV-Nav: Unlocking the Top-View Spatial Reasoning Potential of MLLM for Zero-shot Object Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> TopV-Nav enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to directly reason on top-view maps for zero-shot object navigation, mitigating spatial information loss common in text-based approaches. This framework achieved a 35.2% success rate on the Matterport3D dataset and 52.0% on the Habitat-Matterport3D dataset, outperforming previous linguistic-reasoning methods.

## Problem

- Current LLM-based Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON) methods lose crucial spatial information when converting top-view maps to linguistic descriptions for reasoning.
- Existing frontier- and waypoint-based exploration paradigms limit the agent's action space, hindering flexible and globally informed navigation.
- LLM-based methods typically lack human-like predictive reasoning to infer potential target locations in unexplored areas, limiting foresight in exploration.

## Method

- Developed **TopV-Nav**, an MLLM-driven framework that directly interprets semantically rich top-view maps using ==Adaptive Visual Prompt Generation (AVPG)==.
- Introduced Dynamic Map Scaling (DMS) to prevent visual prompt occlusion on dense maps by allowing the MLLM to 'zoom in' on regions of interest.
- Integrated a Potential Target Driven (PTD) mechanism to enable human-like predictive reasoning for inferring target locations, even in unexplored areas, guiding navigation through probability fusion.

## Results

- TopV-Nav achieved a **35.2%** Success Rate (SR) and **16.4%** Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) on the Matterport3D (MP3D) dataset, surpassing the ESC method (SR **28.7%**, SPL **14.2%**).
- On the Habitat-Matterport3D (HM3D) dataset, TopV-Nav recorded an SR of **52.0%** and SPL of **28.6%**, outperforming VoroNav (SR **42.0%**, SPL **26.0%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of AVPG, DMS, and PTD, with AVPG increasing SR from **45.0%** to **49.0%**, DMS further to **50.0%**, and PTD to **52.0%** on a subset of HM3D.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can effectively perform direct spatial reasoning on visual top-view maps, preserving more information than linguistic descriptions for navigation tasks.
> - Dynamically scaling visual prompts and maps allows MLLMs to process dense information without occlusion, improving fine-grained decision-making.
> - Incorporating predictive reasoning, where MLLMs infer potential target locations, leads to more efficient and globally informed exploration strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI involves developing agents capable of perceiving and interacting with their environments. A fundamental task within this domain is Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON), which requires an agent to locate an instance of a previously unseen object category in an unfamiliar environment without category-specific training. This task emphasizes general reasoning and exploration abilities.

Historically, object-goal navigation methods have leveraged techniques such as reinforcement learning (RL) to train policies, often exploring visual representations, meta-learning, and semantic priors. Modular approaches have also been utilized, combining perception models to construct episodic maps that guide long-term goals and local policies.

More recently, the advent of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has introduced new avenues for improving agent reasoning. CLIP-based methods have enabled cross-domain zero-shot navigation by leveraging multimodal alignment. Subsequent works have integrated LLMs into ZSON agents, aiming to harness their extensive knowledge for tasks such as determining the next frontier or waypoint. These LLM-based approaches typically operate by converting visual observations, such as top-view maps, into natural language descriptions. The LLM then reasons in this linguistic domain to make navigation decisions.

This work addresses a significant limitation of these prevalent LLM-based approaches: the loss of crucial spatial information during the map-to-text conversion process. Spatial information, including room layouts, object relationships, and precise locations, is vital for effective navigation. While MLLMs have demonstrated capabilities in grasping spatial relationships within images for general image understanding tasks, their potential for direct spatial reasoning from top-view maps in navigation has remained largely underexplored. Previous MLLM-based navigation works have often focused on egocentric perspectives.

TopV-Nav distinguishes itself by pioneering the investigation into directly leveraging MLLM's image understanding capabilities to reason on top-view maps. By doing so, it aims to preserve and fully utilize the spatial information that is typically lost in linguistic conversions, thereby seeking to enhance an agent's reasoning and exploration in ZSON tasks. It moves beyond constrained local action spaces typical of frontier- or waypoint-based methods by attempting to create a global and semantically rich action space, and integrates a form of predictive reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON) capabilities by directly enabling Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to reason on top-view spatial maps, thereby overcoming limitations present in current LLM-based navigation methods.

The motivation for this work stems from several identified challenges and questions:

1. **Addressing Spatial Information Loss in LLM-based Methods:** Current LLM-based ZSON approaches convert visual observations, specifically top-view maps, into linguistic descriptions for reasoning. This "map-to-text" conversion inherently leads to a loss of vital spatial information, such as the precise layout of rooms and the spatial relationships between objects. For example, knowing that a fireplace is often opposite a sofa provides crucial spatial context that is difficult to convey purely textually without significant loss of detail. The core motivation is to prevent this loss and investigate if MLLMs, with their image understanding capabilities, can directly process and reason over visual map data. This leads to the central research question: "Can we leverage MLLM to reason directly on the image of a top-view map to produce executable moving decisions?"

2. **Expanding Beyond Constrained Local Action Spaces:** Existing frontier-based and waypoint-based exploration paradigms, often used with LLMs, restrict the agent's action space. Frontier-based methods limit actions to boundaries of unexplored regions, while waypoint-based methods confine actions to a predefined set of navigable points, which may lack semantic richness. These limitations can hinder effective exploration. A key motivation is to enable a more flexible and globally informed action space. This prompts the question: "Can we construct a global and semantic-rich action space?"

3. **Incorporating Human-like Predictive Reasoning:** When humans explore unfamiliar environments, they often infer the potential location of a target object based on partial observations, even if the target is in an currently unseen area. This predictive capability guides their subsequent movements. Current LLM-based methods, confined to known areas or predefined points, typically lack this foresight. The research is motivated by the desire to integrate such predictive reasoning into embodied agents. This leads to the question: "Can we leverage observations to infer the potential location of the target object to guide the current decision?"

To address these motivations, the authors propose a novel MLLM-driven paradigm named TopV-Nav. This method aims to fully unlock the MLLM's potential for top-view perception and spatial reasoning through several key innovations, detailed in the methodology.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TopV-Nav is an MLLM-driven framework designed to enable zero-shot object navigation by directly reasoning on top-view maps. The approach involves three main mechanisms: Adaptive Visual Prompt Generation (AVPG), Dynamic Map Scaling (DMS), and Potential Target Driven (PTD) mechanism, all integrated with a local policy for low-level actions.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The task is Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON). An agent, initialized at a random pose in an unseen continuous environment, must navigate to an instance of a specified object category (G). At each timestep, the agent receives egocentric RGB-D images (I_t) and its pose (p_t). The agent outputs a low-level action (e.g., move forward, turn left, stop). Success is defined by stopping within 1m of the target while the target is visible in the egocentric view.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Visual Prompt Generation (AVPG)

The AVPG module is designed to construct a semantically rich top-view map (M_t) that is directly interpretable by an MLLM for navigation decisions.
- **Map Construction:** Egocentric RGB-D images (I_t) are transformed into 3D point clouds using the agent’s pose (p_t). Points near the floor are classified as navigable areas, while points exceeding a height threshold are identified as obstacles. These points are projected onto M_t.
- **Object Integration:** An off-the-shelf detector identifies objects from the egocentric RGB images. These objects are then projected onto M_t. Text-boxes, serving as visual prompts, are drawn on the map to indicate each object's location and category.
- **Key Area Markers:** To facilitate MLLM interpretation, markers representing semantically significant regions are generated. A density-based spatial clustering algorithm is applied to group both frontiers (boundaries between explored/unexplored areas) and detected objects on the map. The centroids of these clusters form the key area markers {m_1..., m_Nm}, which adaptively change during navigation. An epsilon-neighborhood (ϵ) and minimum points (min_pts) parameter define the clustering process.
- **Spatial References:** A coordinate system and grid lines are overlaid on M_t to provide precise spatial references for the MLLM.

#### 3.3. Dynamic Map Scaling (DMS)

The DMS mechanism addresses the issue of overlapping visual prompts on dense maps, which can obscure crucial information. It enables the MLLM to "zoom in" on regions of interest.
- **Region Selection:** The MLLM interprets M_t and predicts the center coordinate (p_center) of a region requiring closer observation, guided by a textual prompt (P_DMS). A maximal edge-aligned rectangular sub-region (M_t,sub) is extracted with p_center as its center.
- **Scaling Factor Computation:** A layout-aware strategy dynamically calculates an appropriate scaling factor (f_scale). For each detected object (text-box) within M_t,sub, its nearest neighboring object is identified by Euclidean distance. The Intersection over Union (IoU) of their text-boxes quantifies occlusion. An individual scaling factor (f_i) is computed as 1 / (1 - IoU(box(o_i), box(o_j))), aiming to prevent overlap. The overall f_scale for M_t,sub is the maximum of all individual f_i values, ensuring all visual elements are fully displayed. The map M_t,sub is then scaled accordingly, and visual prompts are regenerated, maintaining standardized centering and cutting off regions beyond resolution limits.

#### 3.4. Potential Target Driven (PTD) Mechanism

The PTD mechanism is inspired by human ability to infer potential target locations, even in unexplored areas, to guide navigation.
- **Potential Target Prediction:** Given the top-view map (M_t), the MLLM predicts a potential coordinate (p_target) for the target object through spatial reasoning, using a textual prompt (P_tPTD). This p_target can lie in currently unexplored regions.
- **Probability Fusion:** To integrate p_target into navigation decisions, a Gaussian-based probability fusion strategy is employed. The MLLM is prompted to assign probability scores {α_1..., α_Nmk} to the key area markers generated by AVPG. A 2D Gaussian distribution is then constructed around each key area marker (m_i) with its assigned score (α_i) as a peak value, and another around the potential target location (p_target) with a hyper-parameter (β) as its peak. These distributions are superposed to create a fused Gaussian probability distribution P(x) map. P(x) indicates the likelihood of the target object being at each location x. The location with the highest probability on P(x) is selected as the agent’s actual moving location. The standard deviations (σ_i, σ_target) are dynamically calculated to ensure the probability map smoothly decreases.

#### 3.5. Local Policy

Once the PTD mechanism determines the agent's actual moving location, a local controller generates a series of low-level actions (e.g., move forward, turn left/right) to guide the agent towards this location. The task is successful if the agent discovers the target object and stops within a distance threshold; otherwise, a forced stop is triggered after an action step limit is reached.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TopV-Nav was conducted on two object navigation benchmarks: Matterport3D (MP3D) and Habitat-Matterport3D (HM3D), using Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) as key metrics.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Previous Methods

TopV-Nav demonstrated superior performance compared to prior state-of-the-art methods, particularly those operating in a training-free setting and relying on linguistic reasoning from LLMs.
- **MP3D Dataset:** TopV-Nav achieved an SR of 35.2% and an SPL of 16.4%. This performance surpassed methods like ESC (SR 28.7%, SPL 14.2%), which use LLMs for linguistic reasoning based on commonsense knowledge.
- **HM3D Dataset:** TopV-Nav recorded an SR of 52.0% and an SPL of 28.6%. This outperformed VoroNav (SR 42.0%, SPL 26.0%), another LLM-based method that selects optimal traversal paths from linguistic descriptions.

The improvements were attributed to TopV-Nav's ability to directly leverage MLLM reasoning on top-view maps, preserving comprehensive spatial information compared to the spatial cue loss inherent in map-to-text conversions used by previous LLM-based works.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Extensive ablation studies were conducted on a subset of the HM3D dataset to assess the contribution of each proposed component.
- **Adaptive Visual Prompt Generation (AVPG):** Introducing AVPG, which enabled MLLM to reason on the top-view map, significantly improved performance over a linguistic-only LLM baseline. The SR increased from 45.0% to 49.0%, and SPL from 25.44% to 28.07%, indicating the value of direct spatial reasoning.
- **Visual Prompt Components:** Ablations on AVPG's visual prompt elements showed that removing text-boxes representing objects or the coordinate grid led to notable performance drops. This highlights the importance of semantic object information and precise spatial references for MLLM's reasoning.
- **Dynamic Map Scaling (DMS):** The addition of the DMS module further improved SR from 49.0% to 50.0%. A slight decrease in SPL (0.91%) was observed, suggesting a trade-off where more fine-grained local exploration, while increasing target discovery likelihood, might lead to longer navigation paths.
- **Potential Target Driven (PTD) Mechanism:** Integrating the PTD mechanism resulted in additional gains, with SR increasing from 50.0% to 52.0% and SPL from 27.16% to 28.73%. This confirmed the effectiveness of predictive, human-like reasoning in guiding navigation decisions.
- **PTD Fusion Policy and β Parameter:** The Gaussian-based probability fusion strategy within PTD was found to outperform a direct "Max" selection policy. An analysis of the hyper-parameter β (the peak value assigned to the potential target location in the Gaussian fusion) indicated that β = 0.5 achieved the most balanced performance.
- **Analysis on Different MLLMs:** Comparative tests with various MLLMs showed that GPT-4o achieved the highest performance (SR 53.0%, SPL 29.78%). The open-source Qwen-2.5-VL, used as the primary MLLM in TopV-Nav, performed competitively, achieving an SR of 52.0% and SPL of 28.73%. LLaVA-NeXT also showed robust performance. This suggests that the method's performance can potentially scale with the advancement of MLLM technology.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative comparisons illustrated TopV-Nav's advantages. For instance, when searching for a "toilet," the MLLM interpreted the scene layout, identifying living and dining rooms, and inferred a hallway potentially leading to a bathroom, guiding the agent to the correct general area. In contrast, an LLM-based baseline, lacking spatial layout information, relied solely on object co-occurrence (e.g., chairs near toilets) and directed the agent incorrectly. Similarly, for a "bed" target, TopV-Nav correctly inferred potential bedroom locations based on the overall layout, while the LLM baseline was misled by local co-occurrences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TopV-Nav represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI, particularly in Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON), by introducing a novel paradigm for leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

**Advancement in Spatial Reasoning for Embodied AI:** The work demonstrates that MLLMs possess a substantial, largely untapped potential for direct spatial reasoning from top-view maps. By moving beyond linguistic descriptions, TopV-Nav effectively mitigates the problem of spatial information loss inherent in previous LLM-based navigation methods. This direct reasoning capability allows agents to interpret complex environmental layouts and relationships between objects, which is critical for robust navigation.

**Enhanced Navigation Performance:** The empirical results on MP3D and HM3D benchmarks consistently show that TopV-Nav outperforms existing state-of-the-art ZSON methods. This performance improvement highlights the practical benefits of preserving and directly utilizing spatial cues. The ablation studies further substantiate the individual contributions of each proposed component (AVPG, DMS, PTD), affirming their roles in enhancing spatial understanding, fine-grained exploration, and predictive capabilities.

**Human-like Exploration and Predictive Reasoning:** The Potential Target Driven (PTD) mechanism introduces a form of predictive reasoning, allowing agents to infer potential target locations even in unexplored areas. This mirrors human cognitive navigation strategies, where mental models and inferences guide exploration. This capability enables more efficient and globally informed exploration strategies, moving beyond limited, locally focused action spaces.

**Broader Implications for MLLM Applications:** The research validates the hypothesis that MLLMs can serve as effective spatial reasoners, not just linguistic interpreters of visual data. This expands the perceived utility of MLLMs in embodied AI beyond vision-language tasks to direct spatial decision-making based on visual maps. The finding that different MLLMs (e.g., Qwen-2.5-VL, GPT-4o) can be integrated, with performance scaling with model capability, suggests a promising future where advancements in foundation models can directly translate to improved embodied intelligence.

**Potential for Real-World Robotics:** The ability of an agent to reason directly from a top-view map, adapt its scale for detailed observation, and predict target locations could have substantial impact on real-world robotic applications. Robots operating in unfamiliar or dynamic environments, such as search and rescue, logistics, or service robotics, could benefit from more intelligent and autonomous navigation capabilities without extensive pre-training for specific object categories. The methods presented could contribute to more robust, adaptable, and human-like robotic exploration and interaction.

In summary, TopV-Nav establishes a new direction for ZSON by fundamentally shifting the reasoning paradigm from linguistic descriptions to direct MLLM-based spatial interpretation of top-view maps, offering a robust and adaptable framework for future embodied AI research.
