# See Less, Specify More: Visual Evidence Budgets for Generalizable VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> The S2 framework enhances VLA model generalization in robotic manipulation by refining the executor's conditioning interface. It combines goal-preserving local language guidance with a learned visual evidence budget, improving robustness against environmental distractors and task variations and raising mean real-robot subtask success from 54.2% to 79.0%.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models exhibit brittle generalization in robotic manipulation due to environmental distractors, object appearance shifts, and diverse task requirements.
- Low-level policies struggle with ambiguous high-level instructions, leading to underspecified supervision and a 'burden mismatch'.
- Training with unconstrained visual context encourages reliance on spurious correlations and irrelevant visual information, hindering robustness.

## Method

- The S2 framework introduces a 'cleaner' executor conditioning interface with 'Specify More' (language refinement) and 'See Less' (visual budgeting).
- 'Specify More' employs goal-preserving hierarchical relabeling using an off-the-shelf VLM to generate refined, trajectory-aware subtask instructions while maintaining the original high-level goal.
- 'See Less' implements an annotation-free, control-grounded visual evidence budgeting mechanism with learned gate heads, training the executor to operate from a smaller, task-sufficient visual subset.

## Results

- S2 demonstrated competitive performance on the LIBERO-PRO benchmark, achieving **94.0%** mean success on `libero_goal` and **95.5%** on `libero_object` (with GPT-5.4 nano planner).
- On eight real-robot tasks across TX-G2 and HSR platforms, S2 increased mean subtask success from **54.2%** to **79.0%** compared to a π0.5 baseline.
- The framework successfully maintained focus and completed tasks in real-world stress tests with unseen clutter and online human perturbations, showing enhanced robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly disentangling high-level planning from low-level action generation and optimizing their interface improves VLA generalization.
> - Reducing language ambiguity with goal-preserving local guidance is critical for preserving task identity and disambiguating local execution modes for the low-level policy.
> - Restricting visual input to task-sufficient evidence through learned, control-grounded visual budgeting enhances robustness without requiring manual annotations or external VLM supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of vision-language-action (VLA) models has demonstrated progress in robot manipulation, enabling robots to interpret open-ended goals and scenes for control. However, a significant challenge remains in generalizing these models to novel scenarios, particularly in the presence of distractors, appearance shifts, and semantically similar tasks requiring nuanced execution strategies. Existing VLA models often exhibit brittle performance under these conditions.

The broader research landscape addresses VLA generalization through several avenues. One prominent approach involves hierarchical control, where high-level planning is disentangled from low-level action generation. This decomposition allows larger vision-language models (VLMs) to handle complex instruction interpretation and in-context disambiguation, while dedicated low-level executors focus on precise continuous actions. This work builds upon this modular planner-executor paradigm, specifically focusing on improving the executor's learning and performance.

Another line of research explores enhanced language interfaces to improve low-level controllability. This includes using dense language grounding, subtask-specific commands, or enriching robot supervision with more informative language from pre-trained VLMs. The current work contributes to this area by developing a "goal-preserving local language interface" that refines coarse instructions without replacing the original high-level goal, aiming to reduce ambiguity for the executor.

Furthermore, efforts in selective perception aim to enable policies to focus on task-relevant visual evidence. Traditional attention mechanisms, object-centric methods, or token selection/pruning techniques are employed to filter out irrelevant visual information, thereby improving robustness and efficiency. This paper introduces a novel approach to selective perception through "explicit visual evidence budgeting," which is learned directly from the control objective without requiring manual annotations or external VLM supervision for region selection. Unlike efficiency-driven token pruning, the primary goal here is to reduce nuisance dependence and enhance generalization.

This research positions itself at the intersection of these trends, proposing a framework that simultaneously refines the linguistic input to the executor and constrains its visual input. By addressing both language ambiguity and perceptual distraction, the work seeks to reduce the burden on the low-level policy, thereby improving its generalization capabilities in a modular VLA system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of vision-language-action (VLA) models for robot manipulation. The authors identify generalization as a critical bottleneck, noting that current VLA policies often fail when confronted with distractors, appearance shifts, or tasks requiring fine-grained execution details inferred from coarse instructions.

The motivation stems from the observation that low-level VLA executors are often tasked with an overly complex learning problem. Specifically, the authors highlight two primary sources of difficulty:

- **Ambiguity in Coarse Language Instructions:** High-level, open-ended instructions can be ambiguous regarding local execution details. The same instruction might be compatible with multiple valid trajectories or execution modes (e.g., different approach angles, grasp points, or transport routes). When trained with such coarse instructions, the low-level policy must infer specific local behaviors, leading to "supervision aliasing" where distinct behaviors are mapped to the same linguistic input. This significantly enlarges the decision space and makes robust learning challenging from finite robot data.
- **Unconstrained Visual Context and Distraction:** Presenting the executor with the full visual observation, including irrelevant pixels, clutter, or distractors, encourages the policy to rely on spurious correlations that do not generalize. The executor must implicitly learn which parts of the image are relevant for the current control decision. This adds an unnecessary burden on perception, as not every pixel contributes to the immediate action.

The paper argues that expecting a single low-level policy to resolve both "what to do now" (from coarse language) and "what to look at" (from broad visual context) from weak supervision is an inefficient scaling strategy. Instead, the authors propose a "cleaner design" that explicitly disentangles high-level planning from low-level execution.

The key objectives are to:
1. **Reduce Linguistic Ambiguity for the Executor:** Provide the executor with more informative and context-specific local language guidance that clarifies the current execution mode, while maintaining the overall task identity. This aims to prevent supervision aliasing.
2. **Impose Explicit Visual Evidence Budgets:** Train the executor to operate from a smaller, task-sufficient subset of the visual observation, rather than relying on unconstrained visual context. This intends to reduce dependence on nuisance visual cues and improve robustness.
3. **Develop a Modular and Compatible Framework:** Design an executor-conditioning interface that seamlessly integrates with off-the-shelf VLM planners at deployment time through in-context learning, allowing different high-level planning modules to be swapped without retraining the executor.

By addressing these issues simultaneously, the research aims to "shrink the executor's learning problem" in both language and perception, thereby enabling it to focus on robustly executing intended behaviors rather than inferring them from weak or ambiguous supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named S2 (See Less, Specify More), addresses executor generalization in modular VLA systems by redefining the conditioning interface for the low-level policy. It comprises two main components: "Specify More" for language refinement and "See Less" for visual evidence budgeting. The framework is designed to be compatible with existing VLA backbones, exemplified by its instantiation with a $\pi_{0.5}$-based executor.

#### 3.1. Specify More: Goal-Preserving Hierarchical Relabeling

This component focuses on providing the executor with refined, local linguistic guidance while preserving the high-level task goal.
- **Trajectory-level Refinement:** For each demonstration trajectory $\tau$, the original coarse instruction $g$ is relabeled into a refined trajectory-level instruction $\tilde{g}$. This refinement is performed by an off-the-shelf VLM, conditioned on $g$ and sparse representative keyframes from the episode. The objective is to explicitly resolve execution details (e.g., approach direction, contact strategy, ordering) that might be omitted in $g$, without paraphrasing $g$ or introducing task drift. Contrastive refined instructions are used to guide the VLM, followed by candidate generation, grounded selection, and quality control.
- **Subtask-level Decomposition and Alignment:** The refined trajectory is further decomposed into an ordered sequence of subtasks $S(\tau) = \{(s_i, b_{i-1}, b_i)\}_{N}^{i=1}$. Each subtask instruction $s_i$ is intended to be directly executable and sufficiently specific to distinguish between different valid execution modes. A vision-language relabeling procedure, leveraging sparse trajectory context, is used to identify these subtasks and align them temporally to specific frame spans $[b_{i-1}, b_i)$.
- **Goal-Preserving Interface:** Crucially, "Specify More" does not replace the original instruction $g$. Instead, $g$ is retained as a persistent high-level goal, paired with the active refined subtask instruction $s_i$. This dual conditioning allows the policy to understand the overall task identity while receiving clear, local guidance on the current execution mode.

#### 3.2. See Less: Learned Visual Evidence Budgeting

This component aims to restrict the visual information available to the executor, encouraging it to act from task-sufficient evidence.
- **Explicit Evidence Bottleneck:** Unlike native attention mechanisms, which can be diffuse, "See Less" imposes an explicit visual evidence budget. This trains the executor to rely on a smaller subset of the image, rather than the entire visual context.
- **Annotation-Free Learning:** The visual evidence budget is learned directly from the control objective, without requiring manual region labels, mask annotations, or external VLM supervision to identify relevant visual regions. The executor implicitly discovers which evidence is necessary for successful subtask execution.
- **Task-Conditioned Visual Masks:** Lightweight learned gate heads within the executor produce soft visual evidence masks, $m_b^t \in^{P_b}$ and $m_w^t \in^{P_w}$, for base and wrist camera views, respectively. These gates operate on image tokens $z_v^{t,p}$ and are conditioned on a pooled summary of the goal-preserving language context $q_t$. The gate value $m_v^{t,p}$ is computed via a small learned gating network $h_v$ and a temperature-scaled sigmoid. A non-zero gate floor $\gamma \in [0, 1)$ ensures a minimal residual contribution from all tokens, preventing premature collapse.
- **Visual Budget Regularization:** Separate visual budgets, $\rho_b, \rho_w \in (0, 1]$, are defined for each view, controlling the average soft retention of evidence. These budgets can differ based on the information content of each view. A budget loss, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{budget}} = \sum_{v \in \{b,w\}} \lambda_v^{\text{budget}} \mathbb{E}_{\tau,t}[(\bar{m}_v^t - \rho_v)^2]$, penalizes the squared deviation between the mean pre-floor gate value ($\bar{m}_v^t$) and the desired soft budget ($\rho_v$). This term encourages sparsity while the task loss enforces control relevance.

#### 3.3. Policy Learning and Deployment

The S2 objective combines three components:
- **Ungated Path ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{full}}$):** A standard language-conditioned visuomotor training objective computed on the full (ungated) observation $o_t$ and language context $c_{\text{lang}}^t = (g, s_i)$. This path stabilizes optimization.
- **Gated Path ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{gated}}$):** The same visuomotor training objective, but computed on the observation $\tilde{o}_t$ after applying the learned visual evidence masks. This term ensures that the *gated* representation remains sufficient for action prediction.
- **Budget Regularizer ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{budget}}$):** The penalty described above to enforce the visual evidence budgets.
The overall training objective is $\mathcal{L}_{\text{S2}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{full}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{gated}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{budget}}$. Early gate collapse is prevented by a non-zero gate floor, the ungated loss, and an annealed soft-gating schedule that sharpens decisions over training.

At **deployment**, the framework maintains the modular planner-executor split. A user provides a coarse task instruction $g$. An off-the-shelf VLM acts as a planner, providing refined local subtask guidance $s_i$ through in-context learning. The low-level VLA executor then executes this detailed instruction, using its learned visual evidence budget to focus on relevant visual cues. This ensures that the executor operates under the same clean interface it was trained with.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the S2 framework across a range of benchmarks and real-robot tasks, focusing on executor generalization.

#### 4.1. LIBERO-PRO Benchmark Performance

S2 demonstrated improved performance on the LIBERO-PRO benchmark, which evaluates generalization under various perturbation categories (object position, semantic task, etc.).
- Across both GPT-5.4 nano and Kimi K2.5 planners, S2 showed robust performance, with notable gains on `libero_goal` and `libero_object` suites. For example, using Kimi K2.5, S2 achieved mean success rates of 70.1% on `libero_goal` and 73.1% on `libero_object`, compared to a $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline of 57.8% and 53.3% respectively (from Table 9). This suggests the cleaner executor interface helps policies identify intended local behavior and reduces reliance on task-irrelevant visual factors.

#### 4.2. LIBERO-PRO Object/Goal Ablation

An ablation study on `libero_goal` and `libero_object` suites investigated the contributions of the "Specify More" and "See Less" components:
- **Refined-only supervision:** Using only refined local instructions (replacing the original instruction) remained weak on `libero_goal` (37.8% mean success, Table 9), indicating that local text alone does not resolve the task-identity problem.
- **Original + VEB:** Combining original instructions with visual evidence budgeting (VEB) (62.5% on `libero_goal`, 61.8% on `libero_object`) outperformed the baseline (57.8% and 53.3%) but was still lower than the full S2.
- **Hybrid, no VEB:** Using goal-preserving hybrid language without learned VEB also underperformed the full S2.
- **Full S2 Interface:** The complete S2 framework, combining goal-preserving local guidance and learned visual evidence restriction, consistently achieved the strongest performance (70.1% on `libero_goal`, 73.1% on `libero_object`). This supports the claim that both components are necessary for comprehensive generalization.

#### 4.3. Visual Evidence Budget Sensitivity

- An analysis of the shared visual evidence budget $b$ on LIBERO-PRO indicated that the best mean performance occurred at $b=0.20$, with nearby budget values also yielding competitive results (Figure 3). This justified the default setting of $\rho_b = \rho_w = 0.2$ for the main experiments.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Results (TX-G2 and HSR)

S2 was evaluated on eight real-robot tasks on TX-G2 (bimanual) and HSR (mobile manipulation).
- **Overall Improvement:** S2 significantly improved mean subtask success across all reported tasks compared to the $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline. The mean subtask success rate across TX-G2 and HSR tasks increased from 54.2% for $\pi_{0.5}$ to 79.0% for S2.
- **Specific Task Gains:** On TX-G2, S2 increased mean subtask success from 5.0% to 15.0% on "Cutlery Transfer" and from 60.0% to 90.0% on "Dish Racking." On HSR, S2 achieved 100.0% subtask success on "Coffee" (up from 83.3% for $\pi_{0.5}$) and substantial gains on other tasks like "Bottles" (45.8% to 87.5%) and "Box" (41.7% to 91.7%) (Table 2).
- **End-to-End Success:** While subtask success improved, end-to-end success rates, which require all subtasks to succeed consecutively, also showed gains, particularly on tasks like TX-G2 Clothes Sorting (50.0% to 90.0%) and HSR Box Rearrangement (16.7% to 83.3%) (Tables 7 and 8).
- **Comparison to Other Methods:** VLA-Adapter consistently achieved 0.0% mean subtask success on both robots, suggesting potential issues with its fine-tuning process or compatibility. X-VLA showed some performance but was generally lower than $\pi_{0.5}$ and S2 on these real-robot tasks.

#### 4.5. Robustness under Unseen Clutter

- Stress tests on a deliberately cluttered TX-G2 clothes-sorting setup, featuring unseen distractors and online perturbations by a human operator, demonstrated S2's robustness. The policy successfully re-grounded to the current target and completed ordered sequences despite changing clutter layouts and interventions (Figure 5, Figure 11).

#### 4.6. Qualitative Evidence Selection

- Qualitative comparisons (Figure 6, Figure 9) illustrated that the learned visual evidence budget (VEB) masks were more concentrated on behavior-relevant objects, end-effectors, and destination contexts. In contrast, the backbone's native attention often remained more diffuse, allocating mass to broader or weakly task-related regions. This indicates that VEB effectively learns to select task-sufficient evidence without manual annotations, and the relevant evidence shifts with task phase.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The S2 framework demonstrates a method for improving the generalization capabilities of VLA models, particularly for the low-level executor in a modular planner-executor architecture. Its significance lies in addressing two critical challenges—linguistic ambiguity and perceptual distraction—simultaneously and with verifiable improvements.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

- **Improved Generalization for Robot Manipulation:** The consistent improvements across LIBERO-PRO benchmarks and real-robot tasks (TX-G2 and HSR) suggest that the proposed "cleaner interface" leads to more robust and generalizable robot control policies. This is crucial for deploying robots in varied, unstructured environments where task instructions can be imprecise and visual scenes complex.
- **Enhanced Executor Learning:** By providing goal-preserving local language and imposing a learned visual evidence budget, S2 simplifies the learning problem for the executor. This allows the policy to focus on precise action generation rather than inferring task details or sifting through irrelevant visual information, which can lead to better performance with finite robot data.
- **Novel Language Conditioning Strategy:** The "Specify More" component's approach of preserving the original high-level goal while adding refined local guidance offers a nuanced way to leverage the strengths of large language models for planning without sacrificing task identity. This reduces supervision aliasing and ensures that local instructions are consistent with the overall objective.
- **Annotation-Free Selective Perception:** The "See Less" component introduces an innovative method for learning task-conditioned visual evidence budgets without requiring manual region annotations or external VLM supervision. This self-supervised approach to selective perception allows the executor to automatically identify and focus on control-relevant visual cues, making the system more adaptable and less reliant on costly manual labeling.
- **Compatibility with Modular Architectures:** The design of S2 as an executor-conditioning interface means it is compatible with various off-the-shelf VLM planners. This modularity facilitates the integration of advanced planning capabilities without requiring changes to the low-level executor's training target, promoting flexibility and potential for future advancements in both planning and execution.
- **Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The demonstrated performance in cluttered real-robot scenes with unseen distractors and online perturbations highlights the framework's ability to create more robust robot policies that can re-ground and adapt to dynamic, unconstrained environments.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for the field of embodied AI and robotics. By enabling VLA models to generalize more effectively, S2 moves closer to the development of robots that can reliably execute complex tasks in diverse real-world settings. Future research could explore adaptive budget schedules, online learning of subtask boundaries, and the integration of even more sophisticated VLM planners, further extending the framework's capabilities.
