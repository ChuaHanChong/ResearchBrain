# Scaling Cross-Environment Failure Reasoning Data for Vision-Language Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an automated framework for generating diverse robotic planning and execution failure data, leading to FailCoT, a large-scale, cross-environment dataset of over 30,000 examples with explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. The developed Guardian Vision-Language Model, fine-tuned on FailCoT, achieved state-of-the-art failure detection on unseen real-world benchmarks and consistently boosted robotic task success rates in both simulated and real-world deployments.

## Problem

- Scarcity and limited diversity of high-quality failure data for training Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robotic manipulation, hindering generalization.
- Existing methods for failure data generation are either labor-intensive, unsafe, suffer from a sim-to-real gap, or lack diverse failure modes (e.g., high-level planning errors).
- Difficulty in developing robust robotic systems that can detect and recover from diverse planning and execution errors encountered in dynamic real-world environments.

## Method

- An automated framework for generating diverse robotic planning and execution failures across both simulated (==RLBench==) and real-world (==BridgeDataV2==) environments.
- Creation of **FailCoT**, a large-scale dataset of over **30,000** training examples with balanced success/failure samples, multi-view observations, and explicit ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== supervision.
- Development of **Guardian**, a multi-view reasoning VLM based on ==InternVL3-8B==, fine-tuned on FailCoT to detect both planning and execution failures, formulating verification as a ==visual question answering (VQA)== task.

## Results

- Guardian-8B achieved state-of-the-art overall performance on three unseen real-world benchmarks (RoboFail, UR5-Fail, RoboVQA), surpassing large closed-source VLMs like GPT-4o and specialized detectors.
- Integrating Guardian into a 3D-LOTUS++ manipulation policy boosted average task success rates in simulation from **0.45** to **0.54** on **10** unseen RLBench tasks.
- In real-world UR5 experiments, Guardian consistently improved success rates for three unseen tasks under both nominal and perturbed conditions, increasing success from **4/20** to **15/20** for a perturbed 'Put food' task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining data from both simulated and real-world environments during training is crucial for achieving robust cross-environment generalization in robotic failure detection.
> - Scaling the quantity of structured failure reasoning data directly improves VLM performance and generalization capabilities.
> - Integrating explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning during training significantly enhances detection accuracy, with a favorable speed-accuracy trade-off offered by CoT dropout strategies, and separate processing of multi-view images preserves fine-grained spatial details, improving performance.
