# Scaling Cross-Environment Failure Reasoning Data for Vision-Language Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an automated framework for generating diverse robotic planning and execution failure data, leading to FailCoT, a large-scale, cross-environment dataset of over 30,000 examples with explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. The developed Guardian Vision-Language Model, fine-tuned on FailCoT, achieved state-of-the-art failure detection on unseen real-world benchmarks and consistently boosted robotic task success rates in both simulated and real-world deployments.

## Problem

- Scarcity and limited diversity of high-quality failure data for training Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robotic manipulation, hindering generalization.
- Existing methods for failure data generation are either labor-intensive, unsafe, suffer from a sim-to-real gap, or lack diverse failure modes (e.g., high-level planning errors).
- Difficulty in developing robust robotic systems that can detect and recover from diverse planning and execution errors encountered in dynamic real-world environments.

## Method

- An automated framework for generating diverse robotic planning and execution failures across both simulated (==RLBench==) and real-world (==BridgeDataV2==) environments.
- Creation of **FailCoT**, a large-scale dataset of over **30,000** training examples with balanced success/failure samples, multi-view observations, and explicit ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== supervision.
- Development of **Guardian**, a multi-view reasoning VLM based on ==InternVL3-8B==, fine-tuned on FailCoT to detect both planning and execution failures, formulating verification as a ==visual question answering (VQA)== task.

## Results

- Guardian-8B achieved state-of-the-art overall performance on three unseen real-world benchmarks (RoboFail, UR5-Fail, RoboVQA), surpassing large closed-source VLMs like GPT-4o and specialized detectors.
- Integrating Guardian into a 3D-LOTUS++ manipulation policy boosted average task success rates in simulation from **0.45** to **0.54** on **10** unseen RLBench tasks.
- In real-world UR5 experiments, Guardian consistently improved success rates for three unseen tasks under both nominal and perturbed conditions, increasing success from **4/20** to **15/20** for a perturbed 'Put food' task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining data from both simulated and real-world environments during training is crucial for achieving robust cross-environment generalization in robotic failure detection.
> - Scaling the quantity of structured failure reasoning data directly improves VLM performance and generalization capabilities.
> - Integrating explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning during training significantly enhances detection accuracy, with a favorable speed-accuracy trade-off offered by CoT dropout strategies, and separate processing of multi-view images preserves fine-grained spatial details, improving performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have propelled progress in vision-language robotic manipulation, enabling more sophisticated control and interaction. Despite these developments, robotic systems often exhibit vulnerabilities to diverse failures, such as incorrect task decomposition, object misidentification, or unstable grasps. These failures can accumulate over extended task horizons, diminishing reliability in real-world applications. Consequently, the development of automatic failure detection and recovery mechanisms has gained increasing attention within the robotics community.

Current approaches to robot failure detection leverage the generalization capabilities of LLMs and VLMs. Some methods involve directly prompting pretrained foundation models to identify failures, occasionally augmented with chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning or multi-agent code generation. However, these techniques often face a significant domain gap; the visual and linguistic inputs encountered in robotic environments differ substantially from the web-scale data on which these models are typically pretrained. Accurate failure detection requires fine-grained, embodied reasoning that goes beyond generic visual understanding. This has led to a shift towards fine-tuning VLMs on specific robot failure datasets to bridge this domain disparity.

A fundamental challenge within this training-based paradigm is the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality failure data. Most existing robot learning datasets primarily comprise successful demonstrations, offering a limited number of failure examples. The manual collection of failures through policy rollouts is time-consuming, potentially unsafe, and often results in limited diversity. While simulated failure examples can be generated, they frequently suffer from a sim-to-real gap and may not encompass a comprehensive range of low-level execution errors or high-level planning failures. This research addresses this data bottleneck by proposing an automated framework for generating diverse, cross-environment failure data with structured reasoning annotations, thereby aiming to enhance the generalization capabilities of robotic failure detection models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized bottleneck in developing robust robotic manipulation systems: the scarcity and narrow coverage of high-quality failure data for training Vision-Language Models (VLMs). While VLMs demonstrate promise in robot failure detection, their ability to generalize to novel situations and real-world environments is significantly hindered by this data limitation. Existing approaches either rely on data that does not adequately represent the complexities of real-world failures, or they suffer from the sim-to-real gap when entirely synthetic data is used. Manual collection of diverse failures is impractical due to time, safety, and labor constraints, and current synthetic generation methods often lack diversity in failure modes (e.g., overlooking high-level planning errors).

Against this backdrop, the research establishes several key objectives:

1. **Develop an Automatic Failure Generation Framework**: The core objective is to create an automated system capable of synthesizing diverse robotic planning and execution failures. This framework is designed to operate across both simulated and real-world environments, departing from the limitations of purely simulated or labor-intensive manual data collection.
2. **Generate Realistic and Diverse Failures**: The framework aims to perturb successful manipulation trajectories to produce failures that accurately reflect the distribution of realistic failure modes encountered by robots. This includes generating both high-level planning errors (e.g., wrong object, incorrect order) and low-level execution errors (e.g., imprecise grasping, no gripper close).
3. **Produce Structured Reasoning Traces**: A key objective is to leverage VLMs to automatically generate structured, step-by-step reasoning traces for each synthesized failure example. This chain-of-thought (CoT) supervision is intended to improve the interpretability and performance of subsequent failure detection models.
4. **Construct a Large-Scale, Cross-Environment Dataset**: By applying the proposed generation framework, the research aims to build FailCoT, a comprehensive dataset comprising over 30,000 training examples. This dataset is designed to include a balanced mix of successful and failed samples, multi-view visual observations, and explicit CoT supervision for both plan and subtask-level verification, integrating data from both a simulator (RLBench) and a real-robot dataset (BridgeDataV2).
5. **Develop a Unified VLM for Failure Detection**: Based on the FailCoT dataset, the objective is to train Guardian, a multi-view reasoning VLM specifically designed for unified planning and execution failure detection. This model formulates verification as a visual question answering problem, incorporating explicit reasoning to enhance prediction accuracy.
6. **Introduce a New Real-Robot Benchmark**: To facilitate more realistic and comprehensive evaluation, a further objective is to introduce UR5-Fail, a new real-robot dataset constructed using the same failure generation framework. This benchmark aims to provide policy-driven, multi-view failure examples.
7. **Demonstrate Improved Generalization and Robustness**: Ultimately, the work seeks to demonstrate that scaling high-quality, structured failure reasoning data is critical for improving generalization in robotic failure detection. This includes showing state-of-the-art performance on unseen real-world benchmarks and, crucially, demonstrating that integrating Guardian into an LLM-based manipulation policy consistently boosts task success rates in both simulated and real-world deployments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces an automated framework for generating diverse robot planning and execution failures, subsequently utilizing this data to train a multi-view reasoning VLM called Guardian.

**A. Data Sources**
The framework integrates both simulated and real-world robot data to balance control and fidelity.
- **Simulated Data**: The RLBench simulator is used, drawing from 52 tasks across RLBench-18Task and GemBench benchmarks. Successful scripted trajectories are generated, with varied object placements, and then segmented into subtasks.
- **Real Robot Data**: BridgeDataV2, enhanced with ECoT annotations (providing fine-grained subtasks and object labels), serves as the real-world source. Automatic heuristics and Mistral-Small-3.1-24B are employed to clean annotations. Data augmentation is performed by reversing successful executions where appropriate (e.g., "open drawer" becomes "close drawer") to increase the number of successful demonstrations by approximately 20%.

**B. Automated Failure Data Generation**
Failures are categorized into planning and execution errors, drawing inspiration from established taxonomies.
- **Planning Failures**: These denote incorrect task decomposition into subplans (Figure 1, top row). Five types are generated:
 1. *Wrong object manipulated*: A subtask targets an incorrect object.
 2. *Wrong object state or placement*: A subtask selects an incorrect target location or state for the correct object.
 3. *Wrong order*: Subtasks are out of sequence, violating causal dependencies.
 4. *Missing subtask*: Essential subtasks are omitted from the plan.
 5. *Contradictory subtasks*: Subtasks conflict with one another.
 Types 1-3 are generated using an LLM (Mistral-Small-24B) for subtle alterations, while types 4-5 are created through rule-based perturbations. Each planning example includes the task instruction, the proposed plan, and an initial front-view image.
- **Execution Failures**: These reflect unsuccessful completion of a subplan (Figure 1, bottom row).
 - **Simulated (RLBench)**: Direct perturbation of subtask-level actions is possible. Four failure modes are introduced:
 1. *No gripper close*: Gripper is correctly positioned but fails to close.
 2. *Wrong object state or placement*: Correct object is manipulated but ends in an incorrect state/placement.
 3. *Wrong object manipulated*: The incorrect object is interacted with.
 4. *Imprecise grasping/pushing*: The gripper misses the target object due to inaccurate positioning.
 - **Real Robot (BridgeDataV2)**: Due to limitations in directly modifying real-world actions, perturbations are applied to the subtask text instruction associated with prerecorded trajectories. Two failure modes are used:
 1. *Task-execution semantic mismatch*: An LLM or rule-based preposition swap generates a semantically altered instruction while retaining the original visual segment.
 2. *Revert action*: The subtask instruction is unchanged, but the end image is replaced with the start image to indicate no progress.
Each execution example includes task and subtask descriptions, along with pre- and post-action multi-view images.

**C. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation**
To enhance interpretability and VLM performance, an automatic method generates step-by-step CoT reasoning traces. Object category, spatial location, and robot state (from RLBench or ECoT annotations), along with the failure reason, are collected. InternVL3-38B is then prompted to generate reasoning traces. For planning samples, the model sequentially verifies each subtask and analyzes the overall plan. For execution samples, it describes pre- and post-action images before assessing subtask completion.

**D. FailCoT Dataset**
The generated data forms FailCoT, a large-scale failure reasoning dataset comprising RLBench-Fail and BridgeDataV2-Fail. It contains over 30,000 training examples with balanced success/failure samples, multi-view observations, and explicit CoT supervision for both planning and execution verification.

**E. UR5-Fail Dataset**
For realistic evaluation, UR5-Fail is curated using a UR5 arm with three cameras. The 3D-LOTUS++ policy is run on 16 tasks, with initial and final multi-view images recorded for each subtask. Execution failures are manually labeled, while planning failures are generated using the automated method. UR5-Fail features policy-driven, multi-view failures, providing a more realistic benchmark compared to solely teleoperated datasets.

**F. Guardian Model**
- **Problem Formulation**: Failure detection is framed as a visual question answering (VQA) task. For planning, Guardian decides success (B_plan) given task, plan, and initial image. For execution, it decides success (B_exec) given task, subtask, and pre/post-action images.
- **Architecture (Figure 4, left)**: Built on InternVL3-8B, it comprises a text tokenizer, a visual encoder (InternViT-300M, processing each image independently), and a transformer-based LLM (Qwen2.5-7B). Crucially, multi-view images are processed separately to preserve fine-grained spatial details, and the model generates an explicit reasoning trace before concluding.
- **Training**: Fine-tuned on FailCoT using parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), with the visual encoder frozen. Training minimizes cross-entropy loss for next-token prediction. Three CoT strategies are explored: Vanilla (direct answer), Thinking (CoT then answer at train/test), and Dropout (CoT or direct answer at train, direct answer at test).

**G. Integration into Robotic Manipulation Framework**
Guardian functions as a plug-and-play verification layer within modular robotic manipulation pipelines, as depicted in Figure 4 (right). At each planning and subtask execution step, it detects failures and can trigger replanning or re-execution, leveraging its fine-grained failure reasoning as a hint for recovery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents comprehensive findings regarding the effectiveness of the proposed data generation framework, the Guardian model, and its impact on robotic task success rates.

**A. Evaluation of Synthetic Data Quality**
The analysis of failure mode distributions (Figure 3) indicates that the procedurally generated failures in FailCoT reflect the characteristics of real policy execution failures in RLBench. The distribution of synthetic planning failures (e.g., wrong object state/placement, missing subtasks, wrong order) and execution failures (e.g., wrong object state/placement, imprecise grasp/push, wrong object manipulated) aligns with those observed in actual policy rollouts, suggesting the realism and diversity of the synthesized data.

**B. Comparison with State of the Art (Table II)**
Guardian-8B achieves the highest overall performance across three unseen real-world benchmarks: RoboFail, UR5-Fail, and RoboVQA.
- On RoboFail, Guardian recorded 0.86 accuracy for execution and 0.70 for planning.
- On UR5-Fail, it achieved 0.77 for execution and 0.89 for planning.
- On RoboVQA, its execution accuracy was 0.85.
These results surpass large closed-source VLMs like GPT-4o and specialized robotic failure detectors such as RoboFAC, AHA, I-Fail-Sense, and Cosmos-Reason. Notably, fine-tuning other open-source models (I-Fail-Sense, Cosmos-Reason) on the FailCoT dataset consistently improved their performance, underscoring the importance of the curated, cross-environment data. Guardian's architectural design, which preserves multi-view spatial structure and produces explicit chain-of-thought reasoning, is identified as a contributing factor to its superior fine-grained failure detection.

**C. Failure Data Ablations**
- **Data Composition (Table III)**: Training Guardian solely on simulated data (RLBench-Fail) or real-world data (BridgeDataV2-Fail) yielded moderate performance and limited transfer to the other domain. However, combining both datasets in the training regimen (FailCoT full) consistently produced substantial gains across all real-world benchmarks. For instance, the average accuracy on RoboFail improved from 0.71 (BDV2-Fail only) to 0.78 (combined), on UR5-Fail from 0.68 to 0.83, and on RoboVQA from 0.77 to 0.85. This highlights the critical role of cross-environment data composition for generalization.
- **Dataset Scaling (Figure 5)**: Performance on both in-domain and unseen real-world datasets demonstrated a consistent upward trend with increasing amounts of FailCoT training data. This indicates that scaling the quantity of structured failure generation data directly translates to improved cross-environment generalization capabilities for the VLM.

**D. Failure Detection Method Ablations**
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Strategies (Table IV)**: Incorporating reasoning traces during training consistently improved detection accuracy. The "Thinking" strategy, which generates CoT and the answer at both training and inference time, achieved the highest average accuracy (0.83 for execution, 0.84 for planning). However, this came with a significant increase in inference time (4.3 seconds/sample compared to 0.68 seconds/sample for "Vanilla" and "Dropout"). The "Dropout" strategy, which trains with CoT but infers only the answer, offered a favorable speed-accuracy trade-off, retaining most of the accuracy gains without additional test-time computational cost (0.81 for execution, 0.83 for planning).
- **Image Representation (Table V)**: Processing multi-view inputs as separate, high-resolution images outperformed concatenating them into a single grid. For multi-view inputs, the "separated" format increased execution accuracy from 0.74 (concatenated) to 0.83, and planning accuracy from 0.82 to 0.84. Even with single-view inputs, separating the start and end images yielded a small gain in execution accuracy (from 0.69 to 0.72).

**E. Downstream Robotic Tasks**
- **Simulation (RLBench, Table VI)**: Integrating Guardian as a verification module into the 3D-LOTUS++ manipulation policy significantly boosted task success rates on 10 unseen RLBench tasks. The average success rate improved from 0.45 (without verifier) to 0.54 (with full Guardian). Gains were particularly noticeable on tasks characterized by long horizons or high error susceptibility, suggesting that structured failure reasoning provides effective recovery signals.
- **Real Robot (UR5, Table VII)**: In real-world experiments on a UR5 arm, Guardian deployed zero-shot consistently improved success rates for three unseen tasks under both nominal and perturbed conditions. For example, in the "Put food" task, the success rate under normal conditions improved from 15/20 to 18/20, and under perturbations, it dramatically increased from 4/20 to 15/20. These results demonstrate that structured failure supervision enhances the robustness of robotic manipulation systems against distribution shifts and execution noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robust robotic manipulation, primarily by addressing the critical bottleneck of data scarcity for training reliable failure detection models. The proposed automated framework for generating diverse planning and execution failures, complete with interpretable reasoning annotations, offers a scalable solution that spans both simulated and real-world environments. This capability is crucial, as manual data collection is often impractical and existing datasets are frequently limited in scope and diversity.

The creation of FailCoT, a large-scale dataset with over 30,000 training examples, substantially expands the coverage and quality of available failure data. By incorporating balanced success/failure samples, multi-view observations, and explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision, FailCoT provides a richer foundation for training sophisticated detection models. Furthermore, the introduction of UR5-Fail as a new, policy-driven, multi-view real-robot benchmark enhances the realism and rigor of future evaluations, allowing for better assessment of generalization to actual robotic systems.

The Guardian model, a VLM fine-tuned on FailCoT, demonstrates state-of-the-art generalization performance on three challenging real-world benchmarks (RoboFail, RoboVQA, and UR5-Fail). Its architectural design, leveraging multi-view image inputs and generating step-by-step reasoning, contributes to its superior ability to detect fine-grained failures. The ablation studies confirm the importance of cross-environment data composition and dataset scale for improving generalization, as well as the benefits of structured CoT reasoning and appropriate multi-view image representation.

A key practical impact of this work is Guardian's ability to seamlessly integrate as a plug-and-play verification module into existing LLM-based manipulation policies. This integration consistently boosts task success rates in both simulation and real-robot deployments, particularly under perturbed conditions. This suggests that Guardian can significantly enhance the reliability and robustness of autonomous robots in unstructured or dynamic environments, where unexpected failures are common. The explicit failure reasoning traces provided by Guardian offer valuable feedback that can be used to inform replanning or recovery strategies, moving beyond simple binary failure detection towards more intelligent fault diagnosis.

The research's implications extend to future work, specifically in exploring how rich failure feedback can be directly incorporated into policy learning algorithms to develop inherently more robust and adaptive robotic behaviors. By openly releasing the datasets, code, and models, the authors facilitate reproducibility and encourage further research and development in this critical area, ultimately contributing to the deployment of more reliable and general-purpose robotic systems.
