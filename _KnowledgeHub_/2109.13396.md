# Bridge Data: Boosting Generalization of Robotic Skills with Cross-Domain Datasets

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Bridge Data, a large-scale, multi-task, and multi-domain dataset designed to enhance the generalization capabilities of robotic skills. Integrating this dataset allows robots to learn new tasks in novel environments with a 2x improvement in performance compared to training on limited target data alone.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to broadly generalize learned skills, typically requiring extensive, task-specific, and environment-specific data for each new context, leading to high data collection costs.
- Unlike computer vision and natural language processing, robotics lacks a foundational, massive, and reusable dataset that enables effective pretraining and adaptation across diverse tasks and domains.
- Existing robotic datasets are often siloed, specific to particular tasks or platforms, and frequently lack the semantic meaningfulness needed for complex end-to-end imitation learning across varied environments.

## Method

- The authors created 'Bridge Data,' a novel dataset comprising 7,200 human teleoperated demonstrations across 71 distinct tasks in 11 diverse 'kitchen' environments (10 toy, 1 real) using a 6-DOF WidowX250s robot.
- They employed a task-conditioned behavioral cloning model, based on a ResNet visual encoder and spatial softmax, trained jointly on the comprehensive bridge data and small amounts of user-provided target domain demonstrations.
- A data rebalancing strategy was implemented during joint training to prevent the large bridge dataset from overwhelming the significantly smaller target-domain datasets, ensuring effective integration for transfer learning.

## Results

- For tasks present in both datasets, joint training with bridge data achieved an average success rate of 66%, outperforming baselines of target domain data alone (28%) and direct transfer (14%).
- In zero-shot transfer scenarios where only support tasks were provided in the target domain, the jointly trained policy achieved 44% success, enabling the 'import' of skills not specifically demonstrated in the new domain.
- For entirely new tasks (not in bridge data), joint training with bridge data achieved a 50% success rate, representing a 2x improvement over policies trained solely on limited target data (22%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large, diverse multi-task and multi-domain datasets can effectively serve as 'bridge data,' significantly boosting the generalization of robotic skills to previously unseen tasks and environments.
> - Jointly training a policy with bridge data and a small amount of target domain data consistently outperforms training with target data alone or direct transfer, acting as a powerful form of domain adaptation.
> - The accumulated diversity in bridge data can enable 'zero-shot' transfer of skills and substantially improve the learning and generalization of entirely *new* tasks not present in the original bridge dataset.
