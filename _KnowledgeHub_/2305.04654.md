# Synthesize Dexterous Nonprehensile Pregrasp for Ungraspable Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> A physics-based pipeline synthesizes nonprehensile pregrasp motions that turn initially ungraspable objects graspable by exploiting environment contacts. Exploiting that at most two finger contacts suffice, it builds a contact-state graph over object mesh triangles, scores nodes with a learned graspability metric, optimizes contact and object trajectories with sampling-based MPPI in simulation, then solves inverse kinematics. Grasp success reaches 0.73-0.93 across eight scenarios.

## Problem

- Dexterous grasp generators only produce grasps for unoccluded free-space objects, ignoring clutter where surrounding geometry blocks any direct firm grasp.
- Including environment contacts causes combinatorial blow-up in contact configurations, intractable for motion planners, and offers no obvious heuristic for trajectory optimization.
- Learning-based motion generators trade physical realism for speed, yielding interpenetration and telekinetic hand-object interaction under rich contact constraints.

## Method

- ==Contact-state graph==: object point cloud simplified to a 30-50 triangle mesh, nodes are thumb/index triangle pairs, edges permit only one finger relocating per transition.
- Learned ==graspability score function==, a ==PointNet++== plus MLP trained on synthetic feasible-grasp labels, prunes graph nodes and serves as the trajectory-optimization objective, consuming environment ==SDF== and shape.
- Double-loop optimization: outer loop ranks graph paths by accumulated score, inner loop runs ==MPPI== in PyBullet; a ==CVAE== grasp generator completes the remaining three fingertips, then reverse-order ==IK== animates the hand.

## Results

- Across eight cluttered scenarios grasp success rate ranged **0.733** to **0.933**, needing only **1.3** to **3.7** candidate paths on average, all solved within 20 minutes.
- Ablating the SDF input dropped plate success from **0.800** to **0.583** while paths attempted rose from **1.667** to **5.333**; removing pretraining fell to **0.400**.
- Object rescaling held up in the keyboard scene (**0.683-0.900**), while flattening to 0.02 thickness on the bookshelf halved success to **0.500** with occasional budget failures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A mechanical insight, that external contacts make two finger contacts sufficient for wrench closure, can collapse a combinatorial search space into a tractable graph.
> - Ill-defined task objectives like graspability can be learned from feasibility-labeled data rather than hand-designed, provided both local shape and environment SDF are encoded.
> - Solving sequential IK backwards from the most constrained final keyframe produces more consistent motion than the usual chronological initialization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly with dexterous multi-fingered hands, presents significant challenges, especially when objects are situated in complex, cluttered environments. Traditional robotic manipulation research often assumes objects are either in free space or can be grasped directly. However, in human-like environments, objects are frequently "ungraspable" in their initial state due to occlusion or difficult positioning. Humans routinely employ nonprehensile manipulation—moving objects without a firm grasp—to reconfigure them into a graspable state. This includes actions such as pushing, pivoting, or tilting objects by leveraging contacts with the environment.

Existing research in dexterous manipulation can be broadly categorized. One significant area focuses on **dexterous grasping** and **in-hand manipulation** without considering external environmental interactions. Works in dexterous grasping aim to generate stable hand and finger configurations for holding an object, often using analytical methods, motion data, or more recently, deep generative models. However, these methods typically produce grasps for unoccluded objects in free space. In-hand manipulation focuses on reorienting an object once it is already firmly held by the hand, with the complexity arising from hand-object interaction itself. Neither of these subfields addresses the initial problem of making an ungraspable object graspable within a cluttered scene.

Another branch of research, known as **nonprehensile manipulation** or **extrinsic dexterity**, explicitly utilizes external contact forces from the environment. This area has explored strategies like pushing, pivoting, and tilting, primarily for manipulating objects on a 2D plane or with simpler manipulators such as fixed-geometry manipulators, parallel-jaw grippers, or underactuated multi-finger hands. While these studies demonstrate the utility of environmental contacts, they often involve less complex motion planning or do not address the intricacies of fully actuated, multi-fingered dexterous hands. Integrating extrinsic dexterity into manipulation planning for dexterous hands introduces combinatorial complexity due to the vast number of possible contact configurations and the difficulty in defining appropriate heuristics for trajectory optimization. Many learning-based methods have emerged to address motion generation challenges, but producing physically realistic motions without interpenetration remains a hurdle.

This paper addresses a gap in the existing literature by focusing on synthesizing physically plausible nonprehensile pregrasp motions for *fully actuated multi-fingered dexterous hands* operating on *initially ungraspable objects* in *cluttered environments*. It aims to combine the benefits of learned policies (for assessing graspability) with traditional optimization (for physical realism and certified feasibility), thereby expanding the capabilities of dexterous robotic manipulation beyond free-space assumptions or simpler manipulators.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize physically realistic and diverse nonprehensile pregrasp motions that allow a dexterous multi-fingered hand to manipulate initially ungraspable objects in cluttered environments into a graspable state. This goal is driven by several key motivations and addresses specific challenges:

- **Addressing Ungraspable Objects in Contextual Environments:** Many objects encountered in daily life, such as a book on a packed bookshelf or a flat piece of paper, are not immediately graspable by a robotic hand. Humans instinctively perform a series of preparatory nonprehensile maneuvers, leveraging environmental contacts to make these objects accessible. Replicating this human-like proficiency is a core motivation for enabling robots to operate effectively in human-centric spaces.
- **Leveraging Environmental Contacts (Extrinsic Dexterity):** The ability to utilize surrounding objects or surfaces as additional contact points significantly expands the range of possible manipulation actions. This "extrinsic dexterity" is crucial for tasks that would otherwise be impossible with only hand-object interaction. The research aims to develop a method that automatically discovers and utilizes these environmental contacts for effective pregrasp strategies.
- **Overcoming Combinatorial Complexity:** Integrating environmental contacts into motion planning leads to a combinatorially complex problem space. Existing motion planners struggle with the sheer number of possible contact configurations. The research is motivated by the need to develop a method that can efficiently navigate this complexity. A key observation that at most two finger contacts are necessary for nonprehensile pregrasp, given external contacts, helps to reduce the search space and makes the problem tractable.
- **Defining "Graspability" in Context:** A significant challenge is to define a generalized and learnable metric for "graspability" during the nonprehensile pregrasp phase. This metric must implicitly account for the exposed surface of the object, the current state of the environment, and the configuration of the fingers to transition fluidly into a firm grasp. Relying on heuristics for this metric is difficult, thus motivating a learning-based approach trained on a dataset of dexterous grasps.
- **Ensuring Physical Realism and Kinematic Feasibility:** Generating manipulation motions that are not only effective but also physically realistic (avoiding interpenetration or telekinetic interactions) and kinematically achievable by a dexterous hand is a central motivation. The method aims to produce trajectories certified by physics simulation and validated against the hand's kinematic constraints.
- **Discovering Diverse Manipulation Strategies:** Humans employ a variety of strategies (pushing, pivoting, repositioning) for similar tasks. The research seeks a method capable of discovering diverse and effective pregrasp strategies rather than being limited to a single approach.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between dexterous manipulation in free space and real-world cluttered environments by synthesizing intelligent, physically-grounded nonprehensile pregrasp actions for advanced robotic hands, leveraging both learned knowledge and traditional optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method synthesizes nonprehensile manipulation by integrating graph search, optimal control, and a learning-based objective function. It operates in three main steps: constructing a contact state graph, optimizing dynamically feasible contact trajectories and object trajectories, and synthesizing the full hand animation. Two learned components, a grasp generator and a score function, are trained offline to support the online planning process.

#### 3.1. Grasp Generator and Score Function (Offline Training)

Two learning-based components are trained offline:
- **Grasp Generator ($g_{\phi}$):** This component is implemented as three Conditional Variational Autoencoders (CVAEs). It is designed to predict the contact locations for the middle, ring, and little fingers, conditioned on the known initial contact locations of the thumb and/or index finger, and the object's point cloud in its own coordinate frame.
 - **Training Data:** A synthetic dataset of feasible grasps is created using the YCB object set. For each object, a few "seed grasps" are manually defined. The dataset is then expanded by perturbing finger contact locations from these seeds and checking if the perturbed grasp remains both kinematically and dynamically feasible (satisfying wrench closure and reachability). This process iteratively generates a large set of diverse, feasible grasps.
- **Score Function ($f_{\theta}$):** This is a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) that evaluates the likelihood of a successful feasible grasp given the thumb and index finger contact locations, the environment's Signed Distance Function (SDF), and the object's point cloud.
 - **Architecture:** PointNet++ is used to encode the input point cloud and SDF. The resulting encoding is concatenated with the thumb and index finger contact locations before being fed into the MLP.
 - **Training Labels:** Labels are generated by randomly selecting one or two contact points on an object for the thumb and/or index finger. The corresponding grasp generator then samples its latent space multiple times (P times) to complete P full 5-finger grasps. The "label" for the training data point is the ratio of feasible grasps among these P samples. This provides a continuous score representing graspability.

#### 3.2. Contact State Graph Construction

A contact state graph G is constructed to represent possible hand-object-environment contact configurations. This graph allows for efficient exploration of contact behaviors.
- **Object Representation:** The object surface is approximated by fitting a mesh (30-50 triangles) to its point cloud.
- **Nodes (V):** Each node in G, $\mathbf{v} = (T_1, T_2)$, represents a pair of triangles on the object's mesh where the thumb ($T_1$) and index finger ($T_2$) are in contact.
- **Node Pruning:** To reduce the search space, nodes are pruned based on scores from the learned score function $f_{\theta}$:
 - **Contextual Score ($s_c$):** $f_{\theta}$ is queried with the full environment SDF and the object's point cloud (with occluded points removed) to approximate grasp success likelihood within the current environmental context.
 - **Non-contextual Score ($s_n$):** $f_{\theta}$ is queried without considering environmental obstacles (∅ for SDF) and the full object point cloud, indicating inherent graspability.
 - Nodes are included in G if either $s_c$ or $s_n$ is within the top M percentile. Nodes selected due to a high $s_c$ are put into a subset $V_c$, which serves as potential initial contact states.
- **Edges (E):** Edges connect two nodes if they share either $T_1$ or $T_2$. This constraint ensures that at most one finger changes its contact location between successive states, preventing unrealistic instantaneous jumps of both fingers. Each node also connects to itself, allowing for a finger to maintain its contact state.

#### 3.3. Trajectory Optimization

This is a double-loop iterative process to find optimal trajectories for the thumb, index finger, and the object's 6D pose, culminating in a successful 5-finger grasp.
- **Outer Loop (Discrete Path Planning):**
 - Enumerates possible paths (sequences of N nodes) on the constructed graph G. The first node of each path must be from $V_c$.
 - Each path is scored by accumulating contextual scores ($s_c$) for the first node and non-contextual scores ($s_n$) for subsequent nodes (Equation 2).
 - Paths are sorted from high to low based on their scores. The outer loop iterates through this sorted list.
- **Inner Loop (Continuous Optimal Control):**
 - For a given path, this loop uses a sampling-based optimizer, MPPI (Model Predictive Path Integral), to solve for continuous finger contact locations and object trajectories. MPPI is chosen for its ability to navigate contact-rich tasks where gradient-based methods often fail.
 - **State Variables ($\mathbf{x}_i$):** Represented by thumb and index finger positions ($p_1^i, p_2^i$) in the object coordinate frame and binary flags ($b_1^i, b_2^i$) indicating if a finger is in contact at contact stage $i$.
 - **Control Variables ($\mathbf{u}_t$):** Target locations for the thumb and index finger in the world coordinate frame. These change more frequently than contact stages ($K$ times per stage).
 - **Objective Function (L):** Maximizes the score function $f_{\theta}$ for each contact stage, considering the current finger positions, environment SDF ($S_i$), and visible object point cloud ($O'_i$) (Equation 3).
 - **Constraints:**
 - Contact points must remain within their assigned triangles, parameterized by barycentric coordinates.
 - A finger can only change its contact location if it was detached in the previous stage, preventing instantaneous jumps.
 - **Grasp Feasibility Check:** After an inner loop optimization, the object's final pose and the thumb/index finger locations are used to query the grasp generator $g_{\phi}$. The latent space of $g_{\phi}$ is sampled 20 times to generate multiple full 5-finger grasps. If any of these grasps are both dynamically and kinematically feasible, the optimization is considered successful, and the outer loop terminates.

#### 3.4. Hand Motion Synthesis

Once a successful trajectory (finger contacts and object motion) is found, an Inverse Kinematics (IK) problem is solved to generate the detailed hand pose for each frame of the animation.
- **IK Objective:** Minimize (1) discrepancy between actual and desired fingertip contact trajectories, (2) collisions between the hand and environment/self, and (3) joint movement for kinematic smoothness.
- **Solving Strategy:** A multi-layered, reverse chronological order strategy is employed.
 - First, IK is solved for $N+1$ "keyframes" (the first frame of each contact stage and the final frame).
 - Crucially, these keyframes are solved in *reverse chronological order*. This allows the optimization for an earlier keyframe to be initialized with the solution from a later, more constrained keyframe (e.g., the final 5-finger grasp), which typically has a stronger influence on the overall hand configuration.
 - After keyframes are solved, in-between frames are interpolated and then optimized.
- **Robustness:** If IK fails after multiple attempts, constraint tolerances (e.g., for collision avoidance) are slightly loosened to ensure a solution, prioritizing motion completion over strict optimality in challenging cases.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative evaluations of the proposed method, demonstrating its capabilities and robustness.

#### 4.1. Motion Quality and Diversity (Qualitative Evaluation)

The method was evaluated on eight diverse scenarios, including common household objects in challenging configurations (e.g., books on a shelf, a ruler on a table edge, a keyboard, a plate).
- **Effectiveness:** In all scenarios, the method successfully synthesized natural and physically plausible nonprehensile pregrasps that manipulated the objects into a graspable state.
- **Strategy Diversity:** Four distinct pregrasp strategies were observed:
 1. **Repositioning:** Moving the object to expose a previously occluded surface (e.g., ruler, cardboard).
 2. **Pivoting:** Rotating the object against its surroundings (e.g., marker, keyboard, book, spice bottle).
 3. **Finger Friction:** Utilizing friction between fingers and the object to manipulate it (e.g., food container).
 4. **Geometric Features:** Exploiting specific object geometry for manipulation (e.g., a plate's edge).
- **Multiple Strategies for Single Scenario:** The method demonstrated the ability to discover multiple, visually and functionally different pregrasp strategies for the same object, by exploring more paths on the contact graph. For instance, picking up cardboard could involve using the index finger to pull it out or using the thumb. Changes in the immediate environment (e.g., keyboard closer to a wall vs. shelf edge) also led to different adaptive strategies.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation of Algorithms

Two metrics were used for quantitative assessment:
- **Grasp Success Rate ($r_{suc}$):** The ratio of feasible 5-finger grasps among 20 samples from the grasp generator for the first successful path found.
- **Number of Paths Attempted ($N_{paths}$):** The count of candidate contact paths explored by the outer loop before a path leading to a feasible grasp was identified.
- **Results (Table 1):** For all eight scenarios, the method achieved high $r_{suc}$ values (ranging from 0.733 to 0.933), indicating that the grasp generator effectively predicted feasible full grasps. The $N_{paths}$ values were relatively low (1.333 to 3.667), suggesting reasonable efficiency in finding a successful pregrasp strategy within a computational budget of 10 paths. All tasks were completed within 20 minutes on a desktop PC.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Unseen Objects

- **Unseen Objects:** The method was tested on objects not present in the YCB training dataset. It successfully synthesized motions for objects that could be reasonably approximated by their convex hulls (e.g., a paper roll, a cookie jar).
- **Limitations for Non-convex Objects:** The current implementation exhibited limitations for highly non-convex objects. This was attributed to the requirements of the mesh reconstruction algorithm and the collision avoidance objective in Drake's IK solver, both of which implicitly assume object convexity.
- **Object Size Scaling (Table 2):** The method showed reasonable performance when object scales were varied. For the "Keyboard" environment, performance remained stable across different scales. In the "Bookshelf" environment, changing object scale sometimes led to performance fluctuations, potentially due to altered physics properties (e.g., center of mass) or changes in object accessibility in the confined space.

#### 4.4. Ablation on Score Function Design

An ablation study was conducted on the design of the score function ($f_{\theta}$) using "Plate" and "Food container" examples.
- **Variants Tested:**
 1. **"No SDF":** Only point positions on the object surface were used as input to the point cloud encoder.
 2. **"No Shape":** Only signed distance values (SDF) were used as input.
- **Results (Table 3):** The full model (using both SDF and object shape information) significantly outperformed both ablated variants. "No SDF" resulted in lower $r_{suc}$ (0.583 vs. 0.800 for Plate) and higher $N_{paths}$ (5.333 vs. 1.667). "No Shape" performed even worse, often failing to find feasible solutions within the computational budget. This indicates that both environment context (SDF) and object geometry are crucial for accurate graspability assessment.

#### 4.5. Ablation on Different Orders of Solving IK

Comparison of solving sequential IK in forward versus reverse chronological order showed that the reverse order yielded better consistency and smoother animations (Figure 7). Solving IK in a forward order occasionally led to abrupt palm movements because the solver lacked foresight regarding the upcoming, more constrained grasp poses.

#### 4.6. Comparison with ManipNet

The method was compared with ManipNet, a state-of-the-art kinematics-based method, in a scenario similar to the spice rack example.
- **Contextual Awareness:** While ManipNet assumes object and wrist trajectories as input and is trained on cylinder shapes, it does not inherently account for surrounding objects in its grasp generation. This resulted in significant finger-object interpenetration with environmental obstacles when ManipNet attempted to grasp the bottle.
- **Our Method's Advantage:** In contrast, the proposed method explicitly considers environmental contacts, executing a pull-out maneuver before grasping the bottle, thereby avoiding collisions and interpenetration (Figure 8). This highlights the advantage of integrating contextual environmental information into the planning process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks in complex, cluttered, and human-centric environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Manipulation of Ungraspable Objects:** The primary contribution is providing a robust method for robots to manipulate objects that are initially ungraspable due to environmental occlusion or unfavorable placement. This addresses a critical limitation of many existing manipulation systems that assume clear access to objects.
- **Integration of Extrinsic Dexterity:** The work effectively integrates the concept of extrinsic dexterity—leveraging environmental contacts—into the planning for fully actuated dexterous hands. This expands the operational capabilities of robots, allowing them to perform tasks that would be impossible with hand-only interactions.
- **Hybrid Planning Approach:** By combining discrete graph search (for contact sequencing), continuous optimal control (for trajectories), and learned objective functions (for graspability assessment), the method offers a novel hybrid approach that balances computational efficiency, physical realism, and adaptability. This methodological integration could inspire similar solutions for other complex robotic tasks.
- **Physically Realistic and Diverse Motions:** The method synthesizes motions that are certified to be physically feasible by simulation and kinematically achievable by the dexterous hand, avoiding common issues like interpenetration. Furthermore, its ability to discover diverse manipulation strategies demonstrates a level of intelligence and adaptability approaching human capabilities.
- **Context-Aware Grasping:** The learned "graspability" score function, which considers the environment's SDF and object visibility, represents a step towards more context-aware robotic manipulation. This allows robots to evaluate the manipulability of objects not just in isolation, but in relation to their surroundings.
- **Generalizability to Unseen Objects:** Demonstrating reasonable generalization to unseen objects and varying scales suggests that the learned components are capable of transferring knowledge across different instances, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy in Human Environments:** The ability to dexterously manipulate objects in cluttered settings directly contributes to the development of more autonomous and capable robotic systems for domestic, industrial, and service applications. This research helps robots transition from controlled lab environments to more unpredictable, real-world scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust pipeline for nonprehensile pregrasp. Future work could build upon this by optimizing contact stage durations, incorporating other contact points (e.g., palm, knuckles), or extending to deformable objects, further enhancing the dexterity and adaptability of robotic hands.
