# SVSR: A Self-Verification and Self-Rectification Paradigm for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> SVSR is a three-stage training paradigm that teaches vision-language models to interleave solving with explicit self-verification and self-rectification. It builds self-correction trajectories with a generator VLM, cold-starts supervised fine-tuning on them, then runs semi-online DPO against teacher-ranked model samples. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct it lifts average accuracy across six multimodal benchmarks from 57.9% to 62.5%.

## Problem

- Reproducing o1-style deep reasoning via Monte Carlo tree search or large-scale reinforcement learning demands huge datasets, compute and annotation cost, leaving smaller vision-language models underserved.
- Directly prompting models to self-verify at inference rarely works, and supervised fine-tuning on behavior data alone fails to instill reliable self-correction ability.
- Static offline preference datasets go stale as the policy evolves, so they stop reflecting the model's current weaknesses and actual failure modes.

## Method

- Stage 1 **self-correction trajectory construction**: GPT-4o emits solve/verify/rectify sequences under a ==rule-based state transition==, mixing ==direct derivation== with ==proof-by-contradiction== and varying correction cycles k in 1-4.
- Stage 2 **cold-start SFT** on 5K curated trajectories with ==autoregressive cross-entropy loss== plus a ==binary mask== flagging verify/rectify tokens, teaching the reflective output format.
- Stage 3 **semi-online DPO**: the current policy samples N trajectories per prompt, a teacher VLM ranks them into ==chosen/rejected pairs==, refreshed into a ==bounded preference buffer== against a fixed SFT reference.

## Results

- Average accuracy over six benchmarks rises **57.9 to 62.5**; MathVista **68.1 to 73.8**, DynaMath **21.0 to 29.2**, MathVerse **43.3 to 47.2**, MMVet **67.1 to 73.8**.
- Verification accuracy climbs **39.87 to 69.24** and error recall **48.18 to 69.15**, while successful incorrect-to-correct revisions rise from **10.15 to 23.10**.
- Harmful correct-to-incorrect revisions spike to **5.31** after cold start, then drop to **2.89** after reinforcement fine-tuning; correction trials fall on easy levels, stay high on hard ones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-verification and self-rectification act as transferable cognitive skills: training on math reasoning also lifts out-of-domain general multimodal benchmarks such as MMStar and MMVet.
> - Forward-derivation verification is biased toward confirming its own answers, while contradiction-based checking gives balanced judgments, so alternate both strategies when generating verification data.
> - Supervised format training buys the shape of reflection but also more harmful over-corrections; preference optimization is what tames them while keeping the beneficial ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A plausible first answer to a visual question can still rest on a mistaken reading or an incomplete argument. Consider a model that reads a food-web diagram, mistakes which way an arrow points, and names the wrong animal. SVSR trains a vision-language model to pause, check its own reasoning, and revise it before answering. In the paper's example, the model corrects its reading and identifies the rabbit as the organism getting energy from plants. This example illustrates the proposed behavior. It does not show that the model reliably catches its mistakes in general.

The paper reports that the full training pipeline improves Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct on most evaluated benchmarks. It also acknowledges that explicit checking increases inference time.

### 2. Solve, verify, rectify

SVSR adopts a three-stage training pipeline.

**Stage 1: build reasoning traces.** Each trace alternates between a proposed solution and a verification step. If verification finds an error, the trace adds a rectification and tries again. The authors describe forward reasoning and proof by contradiction as verification approaches. They vary the number of correction cycles according to initial accuracy and difficulty.

**Stage 2: cold-start supervised fine-tuning.** This stage teaches the model the structure of these solve–verify–rectify traces.

**Stage 3: semi-online DPO.** DPO is training that favors a preferred answer over a less preferred one. "Semi-online" means candidate data is refreshed during training rather than kept entirely fixed. In this stage, the current model generates several candidate traces. A teacher VLM judges them on answer correctness, logical coherence, and verification quality. Selected preferred and dispreferred pairs are then used to update the model. The teacher judges training candidates. At inference, the proposed behavior is for the model to check and revise its own reasoning.

The paper reports selecting 5,000 samples for cold start and a curated reinforcement-training set of approximately 20,000 examples.

The SVSR pipeline. Verification and rectification steps turn traces into alternating reasoning and checking steps. Supervised fine-tuning uses a binary mask distinguishing reasoning from verification steps. The SFT model then produces multiple candidate trajectories, a teacher selects preferred and rejected examples, and preference optimization feeds back into training.

SVSR is not the first work to teach self-verification or self-correction. [S²R: Teaching LLMs to Self-verify and Self-correct via Reinforcement Learning](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.12853) also trains models this way through an initialization stage followed by reinforcement learning. SVSR extends the idea to VLMs with a different training recipe.

### 3. What improved

The central comparison uses **MathVerse**, a math-reasoning benchmark, with Pass@1 as the metric. Pass@1 is the percentage of questions answered correctly on the first evaluated attempt. On MathVerse, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct scores:

- **47.2%** after SVSR reinforcement fine-tuning
- **43.3%** for the base model
- **43.9%** for the base model with CoT

This result goes beyond simply adding a chain of thought. It does not cleanly isolate the mechanism, though, because the final model has undergone both cold-start SFT and reinforcement fine-tuning.

Across the six reported benchmarks, the final model is above its base model on five. AI2D is the exception to a clean pattern: it gains only slightly over the base model and scores lower than after cold start. These results are evidence for the trained system's performance. They do not show that self-verification alone caused the gains. The experiment covers one 7B model, not VLMs generally.

Accuracy (bars) and average trial counts (lines) for SFT and SFT+RFT across five difficulty levels. A trial is a full correction loop: identify an incorrect answer, revise it, and re-validate. The caption reports fewer trials on Levels 1–2 and more verification on Levels 3–5.

Harder problems still require substantial or sometimes increased correction effort. The figure does not show that the method reduces total inference time.

### 4. The cost of checking

SVSR's contribution is a trained check-and-revise loop for visual-language reasoning. With the full pipeline, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct improves on five of six benchmarks. The checking step has a cost: the authors say explicit self-verification and self-rectification increase inference reasoning time.
