# Motion Dreamer: Boundary Conditional Motion Reasoning for Physically Coherent Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Motion Dreamer decouples motion reasoning from video synthesis for boundary-conditional generation: given a first frame and sparse user-drawn motion arrows for some objects, it predicts dense per-object instance flow, segmentation, and depth, then a second-stage decoder renders coherent RGB video. Outperforms Mofa-Video and Vista on Physion and driving benchmarks.

## Problem

- Text/image-to-video models offer no way to specify partial per-object motion, so they cannot simulate how surrounding entities react when one object moves in a defined way.
- End-to-end video generators rely only on implicit statistical priors without explicit motion constraints, producing visually plausible but physically inconsistent object motion.
- Motion-conditional methods like Mofa-Video need complete motion input for every object, which is rarely available in real autonomous-driving or embodied scenes.

## Method

- **Instance flow**: a ==sparse-to-dense motion representation== that averages per-object optical flow within instance masks, letting sparse user arrows expand into dense physically grounded motion fields.
- Two-stage **Motion Dreamer** pipeline built on ==CogVideoX==: Stage I reasons over intermediate motion representations (optical flow, segmentation, depth) via ==softmax-splatting conditioning==; Stage II decodes them into RGB video.
- ==Motion inpainting== training strategy randomly masks portions of instance flow plus a ==motion enhancement loss== weighted on high-motion regions, forcing the model to infer unseen object motion from partial cues.

## Results

- On Physion, Motion Dreamer reaches FVD **157.8** and FVMD **205.1**, beating Mofa-Video (**170.1**/**226.2**), a one-stage ablation (**173.0**/**224.3**), SG-I2V, and DragAnything.
- On the collected **9,000**-clip driving dataset, it achieves FVD **272.2** and FVMD **2913** versus Vista's **285.8**/**3557** and fine-tuned Mofa-Video's **309.7**/**7176**.
- Ablations show removing the segmentation map hurts most (FVD **243.7** vs **157.8**), and a **50**-participant user study gives Motion Dreamer **72%** preference versus **18%** for Mofa-Video.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling motion reasoning from visual synthesis resolves an optimization conflict where one network must jointly satisfy motion coherence and pixel fidelity.
> - Training with randomly masked motion cues (inpainting) teaches a model to infer unspecified object dynamics, a general recipe for reasoning from partial conditioning.
> - Explicit intermediate motion representations generalize better than implicit end-to-end priors but still fail on dense multi-agent physical interactions like chain collisions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video generation models have advanced significantly in recent years, demonstrating capabilities in creating future scenarios for applications such as entertainment, virtual reality, autonomous driving, and robotics. These models, often referred to as "world models," aim to predict plausible future states conditioned on initial observations. Existing approaches typically fall into categories like text-to-video or image-to-video generation, which often rely on implicit statistical priors (e.g., 3D convolutions or temporal attention mechanisms) to maintain consistency.

A notable limitation of many current methods is their inability to incorporate explicit, user-defined motion constraints for specific objects within a scene. This restricts their utility in interactive scenarios where the behavior of surrounding entities needs to respond to a particular object's movement, crucial for tasks like ego-centric planning in autonomous systems. Furthermore, while visually plausible, the generated motions often lack rigorous adherence to physical laws.

Conversely, some motion-conditional methods, such as Mofa-Video, can inherently satisfy physical constraints by explicitly encoding motion information. However, these methods typically demand comprehensive motion inputs for all objects in the scene, which is rarely practical in real-world applications where complete motion data is often unavailable.

This paper introduces "Boundary Conditional Motion Reasoning" as a critical capability to bridge the gap between visually plausible video generation and the practical demands of real-world planning and control. It addresses the need for models that can infer coherent and physically plausible motion trajectories for unknown entities based on partial, user-defined motion conditions and subsequently generate consistent future frames. By explicitly separating motion reasoning from visual synthesis, Motion Dreamer seeks to overcome the optimization conflicts prevalent in end-to-end approaches that struggle to balance motion coherence and visual fidelity simultaneously.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a video generation framework capable of "Boundary Conditional Motion Reasoning." This capability is defined as inferring coherent and physically plausible motion trajectories for unknown entities and generating consistent future frames, given an initial scene representation and partial motion conditions.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current video generation models:

1. **Lack of Explicit Motion Control:** Many state-of-the-art video generation models (e.g., text-to-video and image-to-video) do not provide mechanisms for users to explicitly specify initial motion conditions for particular objects. This hinders their application in interactive scenarios where the consequences of specific object movements need to be simulated, such as predicting reactions of surrounding entities in response to a controlled object's action. This limitation is particularly critical for decision-making in autonomous driving and embodied intelligence.
2. **Physical Inconsistency:** Current end-to-end approaches often rely on implicit statistical priors, which, despite producing visually plausible outputs, frequently fail to generate motions that are consistent with realistic physical behaviors (e.g., inertia, collision dynamics). Models have been shown to struggle with fundamental physical principles such as object permanence, conservation of mass, or Newtonian mechanics. This inconsistency limits their reliability in safety-critical applications.
3. **Impracticality of Complete Motion Inputs:** While some motion-conditional methods can ensure physical constraints, they often require comprehensive motion inputs for all objects in a scene. Such exhaustive data is seldom available in practical autonomous driving or embodied intelligence contexts, rendering these methods impractical for real-world deployment.

To address these challenges, the authors propose to decouple the tasks of motion reasoning and high-fidelity visual generation. This separation is intended to simplify the generation process, making it more feasible to achieve both objectives simultaneously: generating videos that are both visually appealing and maintain consistency with fundamental physical principles under user-defined partial motion conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Motion Dreamer is a novel two-stage video generation framework designed to explicitly separate motion reasoning from visual synthesis. This decoupling aims to resolve optimization conflicts often encountered in end-to-end methods that attempt to optimize both motion coherence and visual fidelity concurrently.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Intermediate Motion Representation

The framework aims to generate a sequence of future video frames $\{I_t\}_{t=1}^T$ by first generating future intermediate motion representations $\{R_t\}_{t=1}^T$, conditioned on an initial frame $I_0$ and user-provided motion prompts. The intermediate motion representation $R_t$ at any time step $t$ is a composite of three components:
- **Optical flow ($O_t \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times H \times W}$):** Captures the overall motion of the scene and camera movements.
- **Instance segmentation map ($S_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$):** Isolates and represents the movements of individual objects.
- **Depth map ($D_t \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times H \times W}$):** Encodes spatial relationships and distances between objects and the camera.
This unified representation $R_t = (O_t, S_t, D_t)$ provides a detailed understanding of both object-specific movements and their spatial interactions.

#### 3.2. Instance Flow

A core component of Motion Dreamer is "instance flow," a novel sparse-to-dense motion modality.

- **Training:** During training, where all intermediate motion representations are available, instance flow $F^{(i)}$ for each instance $i$ is calculated by averaging the optical flow vectors within that instance's mask $M^{(i)}$ over a temporal window $T$. The overall instance flow field $F$ is then constructed by aggregating these individual instance flows. For better model interpretability, the calculated instance flow is broadcast uniformly across each respective object's mask. During training, instance segmentation masks are obtained using models like YOLO-v11, and optical flow is computed using models such as UniMatch.
- **Inference:** During inference, users provide sparse motion cues, such as directional arrows, representing desired average motion vectors for specific objects. Instance segmentation masks for objects can be generated using models like Segment Anything Model (SAM). These inputs are combined to create a sparse instance flow $F_{user}$, where the user-specified motion vector is broadcast onto the object at the arrow's origin. This formulation allows for intuitive user guidance over instance-specific motion.

#### 3.3. Stage I: Reasoning Motion Generation (Motion Dreamer)

The first stage employs a diffusion-based video generation model built upon the pre-trained CogVideoX model. It is designed to generate the intermediate motion representations.

- **x_0 Prediction Parameterization:** The model adopts x_0 prediction parameterization to emphasize low-frequency motion representations and improve temporal coherence. The training objective minimizes the squared difference between the predicted and original intermediate motion representation, conditioned on noisy input, timestep, and instance flow $F$.
- **Conditions Incorporation:** To integrate the instance flow effectively, multi-scale versions of $F$ are prepared to align with the feature maps at different network scales (e.g., 8, 16, 32, 64). These scaled instance flows $F^{(s)}$ are used to warp the feature maps of the initial frame conditions (RGB image, instance segmentation, depth) at each scale using the Softmax Splatting function. The warped features are then added to the corresponding feature maps in the network's encoder, allowing the model to incorporate explicit motion cues across multiple scales, enhancing its ability to generate motion-coherent intermediate representations.
- **Motion Inpainting:** This strategy enhances the model’s reasoning capability from partial observations. During training, a random binary mask is applied to the instance flow field $F$ to create a partially observed flow $\tilde{F}$. The model is then trained to reconstruct the complete target intermediate motion representation using this masked instance flow. This forces the model to infer missing motion cues and reason about object interactions, improving its generalization to scenarios with incomplete motion information.
- **Motion Enhancement Loss:** To further improve consistency between generated results and initial instance flow, a motion enhancement loss $L_{motion}$ is introduced. A motion mask $M'(\tau)$ is defined for regions with significant motion (where flow vector magnitude exceeds a threshold $\tau$). The loss is computed as the squared difference between the predicted and ground-truth intermediate motion representations, masked by $M'(\tau)$. This loss term ensures that high-movement regions specified by the instance flow and segmentation maps are accurately reproduced, enhancing fidelity and coherence.
- **Camera Motion Incorporation:** Camera motion, represented as a vector, is encoded using a multi-layer perception (MLP) block and added to the encoder hidden states, which are repeated across frames. This encoded camera motion is then integrated into temporal attention layers to adjust attention weights, allowing the model to account for global camera movements affecting the entire sequence.

#### 3.4. Stage II: Video Decoder

In the second stage, the Video Decoder transforms the intermediate motion representations generated by Motion Dreamer into high-quality RGB video frames.

- **Conditioned Generation:** Building upon the pre-trained CogVideoX model, the Video Decoder performs conditioned image-to-video generation. It takes the initial frame $I_0$ and the generated intermediate motion representations $x_0 = \{R_t\}_{t=1}^T$ from Stage I as conditions to synthesize the final RGB video frames $\{I_t\}_{t=1}^T$.
- **v-parameterization:** While Stage I uses x_0-parametrization, Stage II adopts v-parametrization to enhance the generation of fine-grained video details. This two-stage approach leverages existing powerful image-to-video capabilities while enhancing them with the novel motion reasoning framework.

The models are fine-tuned using LoRA on 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs for approximately one week for each stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of Motion Dreamer was validated through extensive experiments on two datasets: Physion and a collected autonomous driving dataset.

#### 4.1. Comparisons with State-of-the-art Methods

- **Physical Coherent Video Generation (Physion Dataset):**
 - **Quantitative Results:** Motion Dreamer was compared against SG-I2V, DragAnything, Mofa-Video, and a simplified one-stage version of Motion Dreamer (One-stage*). The results, presented in Table 1, indicate that Motion Dreamer achieved superior performance with FVD of 157.8 and FVMD of 205.1. The one-stage version (FVD 173.0, FVMD 224.3) performed worse than the full two-stage pipeline, confirming the importance of generating intermediate motion representations. Motion Dreamer outperformed Mofa-Video (FVD 170.1, FVMD 226.2) and other baselines, demonstrating its ability to produce more physically plausible and temporally coherent results.
 - **Qualitative Results:** Figure 3 illustrates that Motion Dreamer successfully generated physically coherent results, such as varied object contact times and momentum, and different domino collapse outcomes based on initial arrow directions.

- **Real-world Driving Video Generation (Collected Driving Dataset):**
 - **Dataset:** A new, highly interactive driving dataset was collected from YouTube, comprising over 9,000 clips (200+ hours) covering diverse weather, geography, and interaction levels (Figure 5).
 - **Quantitative Results:** Motion Dreamer was compared against Mofa-Video (fine-tuned on the driving data) and Vista, a leading driving generation model. Table 2 shows Motion Dreamer achieving the lowest FVD (272.2) and FVMD (2913), outperforming Mofa-Video (FVD 309.7, FVMD 7176) and Vista (FVD 285.8, FVMD 3557).
 - **Qualitative Results:** Figure 4 and Figure 6 showcase Motion Dreamer's capability to generate realistic and reasonable outcomes in driving scenarios, such as controlling the forward/backward movements of a lead car, which appeared more plausible than Mofa-Video's outputs. It also demonstrated competitive high-quality and motion-consistent results for unconditional image-to-video generation compared to Vista.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study (Table 3) evaluated the contribution of each component within the framework:

- **Intermediate Motion Representation (IMR):**
 - Removing the segmentation map increased FVD to 243.7 and FVMD to 259.0, indicating its role in object isolation and movement representation.
 - Removing the depth map resulted in FVD 167.0 and FVMD 228.8, suggesting its contribution to spatial relationships.
 - Removing optical flow led to FVD 173.0 and FVMD 224.3, highlighting its importance for overall scene motion.
- **Functional Parts:**
 - Removing the motion enhancement loss increased FVD to 165.1 and FVMD to 210.1, confirming its role in reinforcing consistency for high-movement regions.
 - Replacing instance flow with sparse optical flow (as in Mofa-Video) resulted in FVD 169.1 and FVMD 217.5, demonstrating the superiority of the proposed instance flow representation for explicit motion guidance.

#### 4.3. User Study

A user study involving 50 participants on the Physion dataset further affirmed Motion Dreamer's effectiveness. Participants rated videos on a 5-point Likert scale across Video Quality, Motion Plausibility, Control Precision, and Physical Accuracy. Motion Dreamer consistently outperformed MOFA-Video, DragAnything, and SG-I2V in all metrics, achieving the highest user preference rate of 72%, which indicates a significant advantage in generating visually appealing, physically plausible, and accurately controllable video sequences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Motion Dreamer presents a significant advancement in video generation by explicitly addressing the problem of Boundary Conditional Motion Reasoning. The framework's core insight of decoupling motion reasoning from visual synthesis offers a principled approach to overcoming the inherent challenges in generating both motion-coherent and visually high-fidelity videos.

The introduction of instance flow as a sparse-to-dense motion representation facilitates intuitive user control through partial motion cues (e.g., directional arrows) while effectively translating them into dense, physically coherent motion trajectories. The motion inpainting strategy further enhances the model's ability to reason about object interactions and infer missing motion information from incomplete inputs, which is crucial for real-world applicability. The motion enhancement loss contributes to maintaining consistency between generated results and explicit motion guidance.

The demonstrated superior performance of Motion Dreamer on both physics-simulation (Physion) and real-world autonomous driving datasets, evidenced by quantitative metrics (FVD, FVMD) and qualitative comparisons, indicates its practical utility. Its ability to generate more physically plausible and temporally coherent videos, combined with fine-grained control, has several potential impacts:

- **Autonomous Driving:** By enabling more realistic and controllable simulations, Motion Dreamer could enhance the development and testing of autonomous driving systems. It can facilitate decision-making processes by predicting how surrounding entities might react to ego-vehicle actions under various boundary conditions, contributing to safer and more robust autonomous systems.
- **Embodied Intelligence and Robotics:** The framework provides a mechanism for robots to reason about their environment and the consequences of their actions. This could lead to more intelligent agents capable of planning and interacting in dynamic physical environments by predicting future states based on their actions and partial observations.
- **Interactive Content Creation:** The user-friendly control mechanism through instance flow could empower creators to generate complex, interactive video content with specific motion dynamics, finding applications in entertainment, virtual reality, and training simulations.
- **Foundational Research:** By explicitly defining and addressing Boundary Conditional Motion Reasoning, this work provides a framework for future research into world models that demand both visual realism and physical accuracy. The publicly released code and dataset will further support advancements in this domain.

While the model exhibits limitations in highly complex scenarios involving intricate physical interactions (e.g., multi-block tower collapse) or dense multi-agent environments with unpredictable non-motorized vehicles, it represents a substantial step towards bridging the gap between current video generation capabilities and the stringent demands of real-world intelligent systems.
