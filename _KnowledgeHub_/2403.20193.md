# Motion Inversion for Video Customization

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Motion Embeddings, a method to extract and apply motion from a reference video to new text-guided content within video diffusion models. It achieves superior motion fidelity, improved text alignment, and higher generated video quality compared to existing approaches.

## Problem

- Existing Text-to-Video (T2V) customization methods largely focus on appearance and lack effective ways to represent or customize motion.
- Prior attempts at motion customization often face limitations such as indirect motion injection, coupling of motion parameters with generative models, or inadequate temporal design.
- Disentangling motion from static appearance information in reference videos is challenging, hindering robust motion transfer to diverse subjects and scenes.

## Method

- A framework employing two types of learnable ==Motion Embeddings== (`M_QK` and `M_V`) is proposed to directly modulate ==temporal transformer== modules in T2V ==diffusion models==.
- The 1D Motion Query-Key Embedding (`M_QK`) captures global motion relationships and debiases appearance by design, excluding spatial dimensions.
- The 2D Motion Value Embedding (`M_V`) captures spatially varying local movements, with an additional differential operation applied during inference to remove static appearance information and improve generalization.

## Results

- The method achieved the highest motion fidelity (**0.9552**) and text similarity (**0.3113**) among evaluated baselines (DMT, VMC, Motion Director).
- It produced the lowest Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of **550.38**, indicating higher generated frame quality compared to all baselines.
- The approach garnered the highest user preference rate (**39.35%**) in a study assessing combined motion preservation and textual alignment in generated videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dedicated, temporally coherent motion embeddings can effectively disentangle motion representation from appearance in video generation.
> - Modulating both the Query-Key product (for global motion) and Value components (for local motion) within temporal self-attention layers provides fine-grained control over video dynamics.
> - An inference-time debiasing strategy, particularly a differential operation on value embeddings, is critical for filtering static appearance and ensuring robust motion transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative models has progressed significantly, with notable achievements in both image (Text-to-Image, T2I) and video (Text-to-Video, T2V) generation. A prominent area within T2I has been customization, allowing models to learn specific visual concepts (objects, styles) from user-provided images and integrate them into diverse generated outputs. However, extending these customization capabilities to T2V generation presents distinct challenges due to the spatiotemporal nature of video. Videos incorporate motion in addition to appearance, making motion representation and customization a critical yet underexplored aspect.

Existing customization methods for T2V models predominantly focus on appearance, often overlooking the dynamic components of motion. Prior attempts at motion customization have encountered several limitations:
1. **Lack of explicit motion representation**: Some methods, such as Diffusion Motion Transfer (DMT), inject motion indirectly through loss functions during inference, which introduces additional computational overhead.
2. **Coupling of motion parameters with generative models**: Approaches like Video Motion Customization (VMC) encode motion into the parameters of the T2V model. This coupling can compromise the diversity of the generative model after learning specific motions.
3. **Ineffective temporal design in separated representations**: While some methods, like Motion Director, utilize techniques such as low-rank adaptation (LoRA) to embed motion outside the core T2V model, they often lack a tailored temporal design. This limitation can hinder their effectiveness in capturing complex motion dynamics.

This research addresses these identified gaps by proposing a framework that focuses on developing explicit, temporally coherent motion embeddings. The aim is to disentangle motion representation from appearance, providing a more versatile and efficient solution for motion customization in video generation compared to existing methodologies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce a novel approach for motion customization in video generation. This overarching goal is driven by several specific objectives and motivations:

- **Address the gap in motion representation**: Recognize the prevalent lack of dedicated exploration into motion representation within video generative models, particularly when compared to the advancements in appearance customization.
- **Introduce Motion Embeddings**: Develop a set of explicit, temporally coherent embeddings, termed "Motion Embeddings," derived directly from a given reference video. These embeddings are designed to effectively capture and represent motion attributes.
- **Seamless integration with T2V architectures**: Ensure that the proposed Motion Embeddings can be integrated seamlessly with the temporal transformer modules commonly found in contemporary video diffusion models. This integration should modulate self-attention computations across frames without adversely affecting spatial integrity.
- **Efficient and compact motion solution**: Provide a concise and computationally efficient method for representing and transferring motion, thereby enhancing the practical applicability of video customization.
- **Debias appearance information**: Implement strategies to ensure that the Motion Embeddings focus predominantly on motion dynamics, actively filtering out or debiasing static appearance information from the reference video. This is crucial for enabling robust motion transfer to new subjects and scenes.
- **Demonstrate practical advantages**: Through extensive experimentation, validate the effectiveness and practical benefits of the proposed method across various motion types and integration scenarios with existing Text-to-Video frameworks.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the inherent complexity of video, which requires generative models to manage both appearance and dynamic motion simultaneously. Existing customization tools are largely insufficient for motion-specific tasks, particularly when adapting movements to different objects or characters with varying shapes and temporal deformations. By focusing on a dedicated and debiased motion representation, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of prior work, offering a more flexible and robust solution for customized video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces "Motion Embeddings" to enhance inter-frame dynamics in Text-to-Video (T2V) diffusion models. The approach is built upon the architecture of T2V models, which extend Text-to-Image (T2I) models by incorporating temporal transformer modules to handle inter-frame correlations via frame-level self-attention.

#### 3.1. Text-to-Video Diffusion Model Foundation

T2V models process an input spatiotemporal feature tensor, `F ∈ R^((H×W)×N×C)`, where `N` is the number of frames. The temporal attention (TA) mechanism within these models projects `F` into Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V) matrices, computing self-attention across the frame sequence using the formula: `TA(F) = softmax((QK^T)/sqrt(d_k))V`. This mechanism allows frames to exchange information, promoting temporal continuity.

#### 3.2. Proposed Motion Embeddings

The core of the method lies in two types of learnable Motion Embeddings, denoted `M = {M_QK, M_V}`, which directly modulate the self-attention computation:

1. **Motion Query-Key Embedding (M_QK)**:
 - **Structure**: Comprises `L` embeddings, `m_QK_i ∈ R^(1×N×C)`, where `L` is the number of temporal attention modules.
 - **Function**: Designed to capture global relationships between frames by influencing the temporal attention map (QK product). It modifies the query and key components before their projection by adding `m_QK_i` to `F`.
 - **Appearance Debiasing**: Critically, `m_QK_i` explicitly excludes spatial dimensions (H and W). This design choice is based on the observation that temporal attention maps inherently contain spatial details of objects. By keeping `m_QK_i` spatially invariant (1D), the embedding is prevented from capturing static appearance information from the reference video, thus ensuring it focuses solely on global motion.

2. **Motion Value Embedding (M_V)**:
 - **Structure**: Comprises `L` embeddings, `m_V_i ∈ R^((H×W)×N×C)`.
 - **Function**: Captures spatially varying movements across frames by modulating the attention values (V). The inclusion of spatial dimensions `(H×W)` allows `M_V` to represent local object movements and fine-grained motion details, complementing the global motion captured by `M_QK`.
 - **Appearance Debiasing**: To mitigate the risk of `M_V` capturing static appearance due to its spatial dimensions, a differential operation is applied during *inference*. For `j > 1`, `m_V_i[:, j, :]` is modified to `m_V_i[:, j, :] - m_V_i[:, j-1, :]`. This process isolates dynamic motion by modeling frame-to-frame changes, akin to optical flow, thereby removing static components and improving generalization. For the first frame (`j = 1`), `m_V_i[:, j, :]` is used directly.

#### 3.3. Integration into Temporal Attention

The Motion Embeddings are integrated into the self-attention computation as follows:
`TA_i(F) = softmax((W_q(F + m_QK_i))(W_k(F + m_QK_i))^T / sqrt(d_k)) (W_v(F + m_V_i))`

#### 3.4. Training Phase (Motion Inversion)

- Given a reference video `x_0^(1:N)`, all motion embeddings `M` are initialized to zero.
- The video diffusion model is then trained, with gradients backpropagated specifically to the motion embeddings.
- The objective is to minimize the difference between the actual noise `ϵ_t^(1:N)` and the noise predicted by the pre-trained diffusion model `ϵ_θ(x_t^(1:N), t, M)`. This process effectively "inverts" the motion from the reference video into the embeddings.
- The method supports existing loss formulations, including those from Jeong et al. (2023) and Zhao et al. (2023).

#### 3.5. Inference Phase

- During inference, the learned `M_QK` embeddings are used directly.
- The differential debiasing operation is applied to the learned `M_V` embeddings (Equation 5) to remove static appearance information.
- A user-provided text prompt then guides the generation of a new video sequence, where the learned motion attributes from the embeddings are applied to the new content described by the text.

This structured approach, with its dual embedding types and targeted debiasing strategies, aims to provide a robust and appearance-agnostic representation of motion for customized video generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed motion inversion method was evaluated through both qualitative and quantitative experiments, comparing it against established baselines: Diffusion Motion Transfer (DMT), Video Motion Customization (VMC), and Motion Director (MD). All methods were integrated with the ZeroScope T2V model for consistent comparison.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Evaluation

The qualitative results indicate that the proposed method demonstrates strong adaptability in capturing a diverse range of movements, from subtle gestures to complex dynamic actions, and in responding flexibly to various textual prompts. Key observations include:
- **Motion Preservation and Trajectory**: The method effectively preserves the motion trajectory and object poses from the source video. This was evidenced by the consistent positioning and posture of objects between the initial reference and the generated output.
- **Textual Alignment**: Generated visual features showed high congruence with textual descriptions. For example, a "walking duck" in a source video could be transformed into a "walking boy in a field" while maintaining the original walking motion trajectory. Similarly, a source video could be adapted to depict "a fox sitting in a snowy mountain" with high motion fidelity.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: The proposed method consistently outperformed baselines in maintaining motion integrity. While Motion Director could generate visually similar features, it was less effective in preserving the original video's motion.
- **Integration Flexibility**: The framework was shown to integrate seamlessly with various T2V models, including ZeroScope and AnimateDiff, demonstrating its versatility.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation

A comprehensive quantitative assessment was conducted using a set of 66 video-edit text pairs across multiple metrics:

- **Text Similarity (CLIP score, higher is better)**:
 - **Ours**: 0.3113
 - MD: 0.3042
 - DMT: 0.2883
 - VMC: 0.2707
 - The proposed method achieved the highest text similarity, indicating superior adherence to the input text prompts.

- **Motion Fidelity (score, higher is better)**:
 - **Ours**: 0.9552
 - MD: 0.9391
 - VMC: 0.9372
 - DMT: 0.7879
 - The method demonstrated the highest motion fidelity, confirming its effectiveness in faithfully retaining the input video's motion dynamics.

- **Temporal Consistency (CLIP cosine similarity, higher is better)**:
 - VMC: 0.9448
 - DMT: 0.9357
 - **Ours**: 0.9354
 - MD: 0.9330
 - The proposed method showed strong temporal consistency, comparable to DMT, though slightly lower than VMC, which the authors attribute to a lesser number of parameters.

- **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID, lower is better)**:
 - **Ours**: 550.38
 - MD: 614.07
 - DMT: 614.21
 - VMC: 695.97
 - The method achieved the lowest FID score, indicating higher quality of individual generated frames compared to all baselines. VMC was noted as the least effective in this aspect.

- **User Preference (percentage, higher is better)**:
 - **Ours**: 39.35%
 - MD: 27.27%
 - VMC: 17.18%
 - DMT: 16.19%
 - In a user study involving 121 participants, the proposed method garnered the highest preference rate, indicating its ability to generate videos that best combine the source video's motion with the textual description.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study further substantiated the design choices:
- **Motion Embedding Design**: The specific configuration of a 1D Motion Query-Key Embedding and a 2D Motion Value Embedding was shown to provide a balance between capturing original motion and generalizing to diverse text prompts, minimizing overfitting.
- **Inference Strategy**: The differential operation applied to the Motion Value Embedding during inference significantly improved text-to-video similarity by effectively debiasing appearance information. Without this strategy ("vanilla") or with a simpler normalization, performance declined.

#### 4.4. Limitations

The authors acknowledge two primary limitations:
1. **Reliance on T2V model priors**: Performance can be affected if the interplay between a target object and the input motion falls outside the T2V model's training distribution.
2. **Interfering motions**: The method learns overall video motion, which can be affected by multiple objects exhibiting different motions in the input video. Isolating instance-level motion is identified as an area for future work.

In summary, the quantitative and qualitative results consistently demonstrate that the proposed motion inversion framework surpasses existing methods in simultaneously preserving motion fidelity, aligning with textual descriptions, and producing high-quality, temporally consistent videos.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of video generation by effectively addressing the challenge of motion customization. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several aspects:

- **Addressing a core limitation in video generation**: The paper provides a dedicated solution for motion representation and customization, an area previously underdeveloped compared to appearance-focused methods in generative video models. This contributes to bridging the gap in control over dynamic video content.
- **Disentanglement of motion and appearance**: The introduction of explicit Motion Embeddings, coupled with specific debiasing strategies for both Query-Key and Value components, offers a method to disentangle motion from static appearance. This is a technical contribution that enhances the generalizability and transferability of learned motions to novel content.
- **Enhanced control and flexibility**: By enabling users to extract and apply specific motion attributes from a reference video to new textual descriptions, the method offers a higher degree of control over generated video content. This flexibility is valuable for adapting movements across diverse object categories and structural changes.
- **Improved quality and coherence**: The quantitative results, particularly in motion fidelity, text similarity, and FID scores, alongside strong user preferences, indicate that the method generates videos that are not only customized but also of higher quality and temporal coherence compared to existing approaches.
- **Efficiency and integration**: The proposed embeddings are designed to be compact and integrate seamlessly with existing temporal transformer modules of video diffusion models, making the approach practical for current T2V architectures.
- **Foundation for future research**: The identified limitations, such as the challenge of isolating instance-level motion in videos with multiple moving objects, suggest clear avenues for future research. This work provides a foundation upon which more fine-grained, object-centric motion control mechanisms can be developed.
- **Broad applicability**: The ability to customize video motion holds potential for various applications, including:
 - **Personalized content creation**: Generating videos where specific actions or camera movements are transferred to user-defined subjects.
 - **Animation and special effects**: Streamlining the process of animating characters or applying complex camera work by extracting and repurposing motion from existing footage.
 - **Creative industries**: Empowering artists and content creators with sophisticated tools for video editing and synthesis that go beyond simple appearance modifications.

In conclusion, this work offers a robust and effective framework for motion customization in video generation, addressing a critical need for explicit and disentangled motion control. Its contributions are likely to influence the development of more versatile and controllable video generative models in the future.
