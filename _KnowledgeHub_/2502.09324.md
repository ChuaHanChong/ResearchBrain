# Depth-Bounds for Neural Networks via the Braid Arrangement

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Max Planck Institute, University of Technology Nuremberg, and Freie Universität Berlin established the first conditional non-constant lower bound of 
($
\Omega(
\log
\log
d)
$) hidden layers for B

₀

d
-conforming ReLU networks, required to exactly represent the maximum of 
($d
$) numbers, and showed that a common upper bound for maxout networks' expressivity is not tight.

## Problem

- Determining the minimum number of hidden layers in ReLU networks for the exact representation of continuous piecewise linear (CPWL) functions.
- Establishing non-constant lower bounds on the required depth for general ReLU networks, as the best current lower bound is two hidden layers.
- Understanding the precise representational capacity of maxout networks and the tightness of existing upper bounds.

## Method

- Focus on B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU and ==maxout networks==, which restrict neuron breakpoints to specific hyperplanes related to the braid fan.
- Leverage a vector space isomorphism that maps CPWL functions compatible with the braid fan to set functions on Boolean lattices for combinatorial analysis.
- Analyze layer-wise function complexity by defining an operator 
($
\mathcal{A}(U)
$) that models the functions computable by a single B₀ᵈ-conforming rank-2-maxout layer, tracking how function complexity (k-value) evolves per layer.

## Results

- Derived a conditional 
($
\Omega(
\log
\log
d)
$) lower bound for B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU networks to exactly represent the function 
($x 
\mapsto 
\max\{0, x_1, \ldots, x_d\}
$).
- Combinatorially proved that 
($
\max\{0, x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4\}
$) requires more than two hidden layers in B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU networks, confirming prior computational findings.
- Demonstrated that the multiplicative upper bound for the expressive power of maxout networks (product of ranks) is not universally tight, showing a (3,2)-maxout network can compute functions corresponding to 
($
\mathcal{V}_{
\mathcal{B}_d}(7)
$) instead of the predicted 
($
\mathcal{V}_{
\mathcal{B}_d}(6)
$).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The isomorphism between B₀ᵈ-conforming CPWL functions and set functions on Boolean lattices provides a tractable combinatorial framework for analyzing network expressivity.
> - Each B₀ᵈ-conforming rank-2-maxout layer (equivalent to a ReLU layer in this context) increases the maximum 'k-value' of representable functions from 
($k
$) to 
($k^2+k
$).
> - The maximum function 
($x 
\mapsto 
\max\{0, x_1, \ldots, x_d\}
$) serves as a critical benchmark for proving depth lower bounds, directly linking network expressivity to fundamental combinatorial properties.
