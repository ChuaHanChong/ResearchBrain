# Depth-Bounds for Neural Networks via the Braid Arrangement

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Max Planck Institute, University of Technology Nuremberg, and Freie Universität Berlin established the first conditional non-constant lower bound of Ω(log log d) hidden layers for B₀d-conforming ReLU networks, required to exactly represent the maximum of d numbers, and showed that a common upper bound for maxout networks' expressivity is not tight.

## Problem

- Determining the minimum number of hidden layers in ReLU networks for the exact representation of continuous piecewise linear (CPWL) functions.
- Establishing non-constant lower bounds on the required depth for general ReLU networks, as the best current lower bound is two hidden layers.
- Understanding the precise representational capacity of maxout networks and the tightness of existing upper bounds.

## Method

- Focus on B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU and ==maxout networks==, which restrict neuron breakpoints to specific hyperplanes related to the braid fan.
- Leverage a vector space isomorphism that maps CPWL functions compatible with the braid fan to set functions on Boolean lattices for combinatorial analysis.
- Analyze layer-wise function complexity by defining an operator A(U) that models the functions computable by a single B₀ᵈ-conforming rank-2-maxout layer, tracking how function complexity (k-value) evolves per layer.

## Results

- Derived a conditional Ω(log log d) lower bound for B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU networks to exactly represent the function x ↦ max{0, x_1, …, x_d}.
- Combinatorially proved that max{0, x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4} requires more than two hidden layers in B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU networks, confirming prior computational findings.
- Demonstrated that the multiplicative upper bound for the expressive power of maxout networks (product of ranks) is not universally tight, showing a (3,2)-maxout network can compute functions corresponding to V_ B_d(7) instead of the predicted V_ B_d(6).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The isomorphism between B₀ᵈ-conforming CPWL functions and set functions on Boolean lattices provides a tractable combinatorial framework for analyzing network expressivity.
> - Each B₀ᵈ-conforming rank-2-maxout layer (equivalent to a ReLU layer in this context) increases the maximum 'k-value' of representable functions from k to k^2+k.
> - The maximum function x ↦ max{0, x_1, …, x_d} serves as a critical benchmark for proving depth lower bounds, directly linking network expressivity to fundamental combinatorial properties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The theoretical understanding of neural networks, particularly those employing Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation functions, involves characterizing their representational capacity. A foundational aspect of this characterization is determining the minimum number of hidden layers, referred to as depth, required to represent specific classes of functions. While universal approximation theorems indicate that a single hidden layer can approximate any continuous function with arbitrary precision, the question of *exact* representation for continuous piecewise linear (CPWL) functions remains an active area of research.

Exact representation is relevant for understanding the intrinsic piecewise linear structure of neural networks and establishing connections to fields such as tropical and polyhedral geometry. These connections, in turn, inform algorithmic tasks like neural network training and verification, where computational complexity is a significant concern.

Previous work by Arora et al. (2018) established that ReLU networks can exactly represent any CPWL function on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $\lceil \log_2(d+1) \rceil$ hidden layers, based on the ability to represent the maximum of $d+1$ affine terms. Recent improvements by Bakaev et al. (2025b) further reduced this upper bound to $\lceil \log_3(d-1) \rceil + 1$ hidden layers, refuting a prior conjecture that $\lceil \log_2(d+1) \rceil$ layers were necessary. A central open question in this domain is to establish non-constant lower bounds on the required depth for exact representation in general ReLU networks. Currently, the best known lower bound is 2 hidden layers, and it remains unresolved whether any CPWL function genuinely requires more than two hidden layers.

To approach lower bounds, researchers have explored two main strategies: restricting the breakpoints of neurons or restricting the network weights. Hertrich et al. (2023) introduced the concept of B₀ᵈ-conforming networks, where breakpoints are restricted to hyperplanes of the form $x_i = x_j$ or $x_i = 0$. This restriction aligns with the geometry of the braid fan. Other approaches involve restricting weights to N-ary fractions (Averkov et al., 2025) or to be non-negative (Bakaev et al., 2025a). These different restriction approaches are not directly comparable. This current work follows the breakpoint restriction approach initiated by Hertrich et al. (2023).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to contribute to resolving the open question regarding the minimum number of hidden layers required in ReLU networks to *exactly* represent all continuous and piecewise linear functions on $\mathbb{R}^d$. Specifically, the authors focus on networks that are compatible with certain polyhedral complexes, particularly the braid fan, referred to as B₀ᵈ-conforming networks.

The motivation behind this focus is multifaceted:
- **Advancing Depth Lower Bounds**: The general problem of establishing non-constant lower bounds for ReLU network depth remains challenging. By restricting the network to be B₀ᵈ-conforming, the authors aim to identify a tractable special case that allows for rigorous mathematical analysis and the derivation of the first conditional non-constant lower bound without restricting network weights. This provides a stepping stone towards understanding the expressivity of general networks.
- **Understanding the Max Function**: The representation of the maximum function, $x \mapsto \max\{0, x_1, \ldots, x_d\}$, is considered a critical component for representing general CPWL functions. Proving depth lower bounds for this function helps explain the power of elements like max-pooling layers in deep architectures.
- **Combinatorial Insights**: The B₀ᵈ-conforming assumption allows for the exploitation of specific combinatorial properties related to the braid arrangement and set functions. This approach leverages the isomorphism between compatible CPWL functions and functions on Boolean lattices, offering a framework for inductive analysis of layer-wise capabilities.
- **Re-evaluating Existing Results**: The work also aims to provide an alternative, combinatorial proof for a known result concerning the maximum of 5 numbers requiring three hidden layers in B₀ᵈ-conforming networks, which was previously verified through computational methods.
- **Investigating Maxout Networks**: As a generalization of ReLU networks, maxout networks are explored to assess the tightness of existing upper bounds on their representational capacity, specifically challenging a straightforward generalization of Arora et al.'s (2018) upper bound.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is primarily theoretical and combinatorial, leveraging insights from polyhedral geometry and lattice theory to analyze the expressivity of B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU and maxout neural networks.

**Core Concepts and Definitions:**
1. **Continuous Piecewise Linear (CPWL) Functions**: A function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is CPWL if its domain can be partitioned by a polyhedral complex $\mathcal{P}$ such that $f$ is affine on each full-dimensional polyhedron in $\mathcal{P}$.
2. **ReLU and Maxout Networks**:
 - A **ReLU network** applies a sequence of affine transformations interspersed with the component-wise ReLU activation $\sigma(x) = \max\{x, 0\}$.
 - A **rank-r-maxout layer** computes $x \mapsto (\max\{(T^{(1)}x)_j, \ldots, (T^{(r)}x)_j\})_j$, where $T^{(q)}$ are affine maps. A maxout network consists of a sequence of such layers followed by an affine output transformation.
3. **Braid Arrangement and Fan (B₀ᵈ)**: The braid arrangement in $\mathbb{R}^d$ consists of hyperplanes $x_i = x_j$. The braid fan $\mathcal{B}_d$ is the polyhedral fan induced by this arrangement. The fan $\mathcal{B}_0^d$ also includes hyperplanes $x_i = 0$. A neural network is $\mathcal{P}$-conforming (e.g., B₀ᵈ-conforming) if all functions computed by its neurons are compatible with the polyhedral complex $\mathcal{P}$.

**Key Methodological Steps:**

- **Isomorphism to Set Functions**: The paper establishes a vector space isomorphism $\Phi: \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d} \to \mathcal{F}_d$ between the space of CPWL functions compatible with the braid fan $\mathcal{B}_d$ ($\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}$) and the space of set functions on a Boolean lattice $\mathcal{F}_d = \mathbb{R}^{2^{[d]}}$. This isomorphism allows translating properties of CPWL functions into properties of set functions, which are often easier to analyze combinatorially. The inverse map $\Phi^{-1}$ is defined via the Möbius inversion formula.
- **Subspaces of Functions**: Specific subspaces $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(k) := \text{span}\{\sigma_M \mid M \subseteq [d], |M| \leq k\}$ are defined, where $\sigma_M(x) = \max_{i \in M} x_i$. The isomorphism maps these to $\mathcal{F}_d(k)$.
- **Layer-wise Analysis with $\mathcal{A}(U)$ Operator**:
 - The core of the depth analysis involves defining an operator $\mathcal{A}(U) = \text{span}\{\max\{f_1, f_2\} \mid f_1, f_2 \in U, \max\{f_1, f_2\} \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}\}$ which models the functions computable by a single B₀ᵈ-conforming rank-2-maxout layer (equivalent to a ReLU layer in terms of function class). This can be simplified to $\mathcal{A}(U) = \text{span}\{\max\{0, f\} \mid f \in U, \max\{0, f\} \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}\}$.
 - The key is to track how the complexity of functions (characterized by $k$ in $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(k)$ or $\mathcal{F}_d(k)$) evolves after applying $\mathcal{A}$. The main inductive step aims to prove $\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{F}_L(k)) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_L(k^2+k)$.
- **Conforming Tuples $\mathcal{C}_L^r$**: For maxout layers, the concept of "conforming tuples" $(F_1, \ldots, F_r) \in \mathcal{F}_L^r$ is introduced. A tuple is conforming if applying the maxout activation function to the corresponding CPWL functions results in a function that is still compatible with the braid fan. This ensures that $\Phi(\max\{f_1, \ldots, f_r\}) = \max\{\Phi(f_1), \ldots, \Phi(f_r)\}$.
- **Combinatorial Properties of Boolean Lattices**: The proofs heavily rely on properties of Boolean lattices.
 - Lemma 4.4 describes how elements $\alpha_{S,T}$ (linear functionals on $\mathcal{F}_L$) can be decomposed or related across sublattices, which is crucial for proving membership in $\mathcal{F}_L(k)$.
 - Lemma 4.5, "pushing elements down in the lattice," states that if a function $F \in \mathcal{F}_L(k) \cap \mathcal{C}_L$ is not entirely positive or negative, its positive and negative support must contain elements of relatively low rank.
- **Inductive Argument for Lower Bounds**: The general lower bound is derived by showing that each rank-2-maxout layer increases the "k-value" from $k$ to $k^2+k$. By starting with $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(1)$ (affine functions) and iteratively applying the $\mathcal{A}$ operator $\ell$ times, the maximum complexity that can be reached is $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(2^{2^{\ell-1}})$. This leads to the conclusion that if the target function requires a higher complexity (e.g., $\max\{0, x_1, \ldots, x_d\}$ for large $d$), it cannot be computed within $\ell$ layers.
- **Specific Case Analysis for $d=4$**: For dimension $d=4$ (max{0, x₁..., x₄}), a specialized combinatorial argument is provided. It refines the application of Lemma 4.5 by showing that under specific rank conditions (rank 5 lattice for $d=4$), elements in the support can be assumed to be of rank 1, simplifying the analysis to show $A(\mathcal{F}_L(2)) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_L(4)$.
- **Maxout Upper Bound Analysis**: The paper constructs specific functions to demonstrate that the general upper bound for maxout networks ($\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(\prod r_i)$) is not tight. This involves defining two complex functions $f_1, f_2 \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_7}(3)$ and showing that their maximum $ \max\{f_1, f_2\} $ belongs to $ \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_7}(7) \setminus \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_7}(6) $. This example implies that a (3,2)-maxout network can compute functions of higher complexity than expected from the direct product of ranks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several findings related to depth bounds for B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU and maxout networks:

1. **Doubly-Logarithmic Lower Bound for ReLU Networks (Theorem 4.8, Corollary 4.9)**:
 - For any number of layers $\ell \in \mathbb{N}$, B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU neural networks with $\ell$ hidden layers can only represent functions within the subspace $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(2^{2^{\ell-1}})$.
 - Consequently, the function $x \mapsto \max\{0, x_1, \ldots, x_d\}$ with $d = 2^{2^{\ell-1}}$ (or more generally $d \ge 2^{2^{\ell-1}}+1$) is *not* computable by a B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU neural network with $\ell$ hidden layers.
 - This result establishes a non-constant lower bound of $\Omega(\log \log d)$ hidden layers required for computing the maximum of $d$ numbers, given the B₀ᵈ-conforming constraint. This is presented as the first conditional non-constant lower bound without restricting network weights.

2. **Combinatorial Proof for Dimension Four (Theorem 5.2)**:
 - The paper provides a combinatorial proof that the function $x \mapsto \max\{0, x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4\}$ is *not* representable by a B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU neural network with two hidden layers. This result confirms and provides an alternative method for a previously computationally verified finding by Hertrich et al. (2023).
 - More generally, it is shown that B₀ᵈ-conforming networks with two hidden rank-2 maxout layers (equivalent to ReLU layers) can compute functions up to $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(4)$, implying that computing the maximum of 5 numbers requires more than 2 layers.

3. **Tightness of Maxout Upper Bounds (Proposition 6.1, Theorem 6.2)**:
 - The authors investigate the representational power of maxout networks. A straightforward generalization of an existing upper bound suggests that a B₀ᵈ-conforming maxout network with ranks $r_1, \ldots, r_\ell$ in its hidden layers can compute functions up to $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(\prod_{i=1}^\ell r_i)$.
 - The paper demonstrates that this general upper bound is *not tight*. Specifically, a maxout network with one rank-3 layer and one rank-2 layer (which would correspond to $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(3 \times 2 = 6)$ by the multiplicative rule) is sufficient to compute $x \mapsto \max\{0, x_1, \ldots, x_6\}$ (i.e., the maximum of 7 numbers), which corresponds to a function in $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}_d}(7)$. This finding suggests that certain maxout architectures can achieve higher "k-values" than the simple product of ranks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the theoretical understanding of neural network expressivity, particularly concerning the necessary depth for exact function representation.

- **Establishment of a Conditional Lower Bound**: The derivation of the $\Omega(\log \log d)$ lower bound for B₀ᵈ-conforming ReLU networks is a significant step. While conditional on the breakpoint structure, it represents the first non-constant lower bound without imposing restrictions on network weights. This bound provides a formal explanation for why specific structural properties (like the braid arrangement) can influence the required depth.
- **Foundation for Broader Generalizations**: The authors acknowledge that the B₀ᵈ-conforming assumption is a restriction, noting that other work (Bakaev et al., 2025b) shows that the maximum of 5 numbers *can* be computed with 2 layers in general ReLU networks. However, the combinatorial and polyhedral techniques developed in this paper, particularly the isomorphism to set functions and the layer-wise analysis through the $\mathcal{A}(U)$ operator, offer a foundation for extending these methods to more general network architectures or other underlying polyhedral fans. This may pave the way for proving unconditional lower bounds in the future.
- **Combinatorial Insights into Network Behavior**: The detailed analysis using Boolean lattices and the properties of conforming tuples provides a deeper combinatorial understanding of how ReLU and maxout activations interact with specific geometric structures. This connection to discrete mathematics offers a new perspective for analyzing neural network expressivity.
- **Refinement of Maxout Network Understanding**: The finding that the multiplicative upper bound for maxout networks is not tight is notable. It indicates that the expressive power of maxout layers, when combined, might be greater than what a simple product of ranks suggests, potentially implying more efficient deep architectures for certain tasks than previously theorized. This prompts further investigation into the precise expressive capacity of maxout networks.
- **Implications for Network Design and Verification**: Understanding depth bounds helps clarify the potential advantages of deep architectures over shallow ones, even if width is unrestricted. For practical applications, such theoretical insights can inform architectural choices and contribute to the development of more robust verification methods for neural networks, by characterizing the complexity of functions they can compute.

In summary, this work provides rigorous mathematical results on the depth requirements of a specific class of ReLU and maxout networks, offering a valuable theoretical framework and opening new avenues for research into the fundamental expressivity of deep learning models.
