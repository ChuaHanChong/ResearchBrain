# Spatial-MLLM: Boosting MLLM Capabilities in Visual-based Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University researchers develop Spatial-MLLM, a framework that enhances multimodal large language models' spatial reasoning from 2D videos by combining a standard visual encoder with a spatial encoder initialized from a visual geometry foundation model (VGGT), achieving state-of-the-art performance on VSI-Bench and substantially outperforming existing video MLLMs on ScanQA and SQA3D through a dual-encoder architecture, space-aware frame sampling strategy, and reinforcement learning training on their new Spatial-MLLM-120k dataset covering diverse spatial understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Existing video MLLMs rely on CLIP-based visual encoders trained on image-text pairs, which capture semantic content but lack spatial awareness needed for 3D reasoning
- Current 3D MLLMs require additional 3D or 2.5D data (point clouds, depth maps) that limit their applicability in scenarios with only 2D video available
- MLLMs struggle with visual-based spatial intelligence tasks including object counting, distance estimation, and spatial relationship understanding from monocular video

## Method

- **Spatial-MLLM** ==dual-encoder architecture== combining a 2D visual encoder for semantic features with a spatial encoder initialized from ==VGGT visual geometry foundation model== for 3D structural information
- ==Space-aware frame sampling strategy== that reformulates frame selection as a ==maximum coverage problem== over ==voxel grids== to identify spatially informative frames under input length constraints
- Two-stage training pipeline with ==SFT== on ==Spatial-MLLM-120k dataset== followed by ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== reinforcement learning for enhanced ==chain-of-thought spatial reasoning==

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on VSI-Bench, surpassing both proprietary and open-source MLLMs including models with significantly larger parameter counts
- Substantially outperforms video-input models on ScanQA and SQA3D benchmarks, even exceeding some task-specific models and approaches using 3D/2.5D input data
- Ablation studies confirm effectiveness of dual-encoder architecture, space-aware frame sampling, and GRPO training components for spatial reasoning enhancement

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual geometry foundation models pretrained on pixel-point pairs can effectively recover implicit 3D structural information from 2D video inputs when properly integrated with semantic encoders
> - Frame sampling based on spatial informativeness rather than temporal uniformity significantly improves performance by focusing computational resources on geometrically relevant content
> - Reinforcement learning with cold-start initialization and task-dependent reward modeling enhances long-chain spatial reasoning capabilities beyond supervised fine-tuning alone

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated advancements in processing diverse content modalities, particularly excelling in 2D visual tasks such as image and video understanding. Proprietary models like Gemini and GPT-4o, along with open-source initiatives such as the LLaVA series and Qwen-VL, have continuously improved the interpretation of visual and textual data. However, a significant challenge within this landscape is the limited capacity of these models to perceive, understand, and reason about 3D scenes, often referred to as 3D spatial intelligence.

Existing approaches to 3D MLLMs typically rely on supplementary 3D or 2.5D data, such as point clouds, camera parameters, or depth maps, to incorporate spatial awareness. This reliance restricts their applicability in real-world scenarios where only monocular 2D inputs (images or videos) are available. The ability to perform spatial understanding and reasoning purely from 2D observations is termed visual-based 3D spatial intelligence. A core difficulty in this area stems from the fact that each 2D frame offers only a partial observation, requiring the model to infer global spatial layouts and integrate these partial cues into a coherent internal 3D representation.

Current video MLLMs often employ visual encoders pre-trained on large image-text datasets (e.g., following the CLIP paradigm), which primarily optimize for high-level semantic content. While effective for semantic understanding, these encoders often lack the necessary structural and spatial information required for robust 3D spatial reasoning when only 2D video inputs are available. Consequently, their performance on spatial reasoning tasks generally lags behind their capabilities in temporal understanding or human performance. This research addresses this specific limitation by aiming to enhance 3D spatial intelligence from purely 2D visual inputs, without requiring explicit 3D or 2.5D data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly improve the visual-based 3D spatial intelligence of existing video MLLMs, enabling them to effectively understand and reason about 3D scenes using only 2D video inputs.

The motivation for this work arises from several identified limitations in the current state of MLLMs:

1. **Dependency on Auxiliary 3D/2.5D Data:** Many contemporary 3D MLLMs, while capable of spatial reasoning, necessitate additional 3D inputs (e.g., point clouds, 3D meshes) or 2.5D inputs (e.g., depth maps, camera poses). This requirement limits their practical utility in scenarios where such supplementary data is unavailable or difficult to acquire, as is common with standard monocular video footage. The research aims to overcome this dependency.

2. **Lack of Intrinsic 3D Spatial Awareness in Current Video MLLMs:** Existing video MLLMs are primarily designed and pre-trained on image-text or video-text datasets that emphasize 2D semantic understanding and temporal coherence. Their visual encoders, often based on CLIP, excel at extracting high-level semantic features but are not inherently optimized for inferring intricate 3D structural information from 2D projections. This results in suboptimal performance on tasks requiring detailed spatial perception, such as object localization, distance estimation, or navigational reasoning within a 3D environment.

3. **Challenges in Inferring Global Scene Layout from Partial 2D Observations:** A monocular video provides a sequence of partial 2D views of a scene. For MLLMs to perform 3D spatial reasoning, they must implicitly integrate these partial observations to construct a coherent global representation of the 3D environment. Current models often struggle with this internal integration, which demands robust spatial awareness beyond simple semantic recognition.

4. **Computational Constraints and Frame Sampling Limitations:** Processing long video sequences with MLLMs is computationally intensive due to GPU memory limitations and the transformer architecture's quadratic scaling with input length. This necessitates sampling a limited number of frames. Traditional uniform sampling methods, while adequate for general video understanding, may not capture the most spatially informative frames, thereby hindering precise 3D reasoning.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes Spatial-MLLM, a novel framework designed to leverage strong structural priors from visual geometry foundation models, integrate semantic and structural features, and employ intelligent frame sampling to enhance visual-based 3D spatial intelligence from purely 2D video inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Spatial-MLLM proposes a novel framework designed to enhance visual-based 3D spatial intelligence in MLLMs using purely 2D observations. The methodology comprises a dual-encoder architecture, a space-aware frame sampling strategy, and a two-stage training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Spatial-MLLM Architecture

The architecture is built upon Qwen2.5-VL-3B and VGGT, incorporating a dual-encoder system, a connector, and a Large Language Model (LLM) backbone.

- **Dual-Encoder:** This component is central to extracting both semantic and structural features.
 - **2D Encoder (E$_{2D}$):** This branch utilizes the visual encoder of a general-purpose video MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) to extract semantically rich 2D patch features from input frames. Its strength lies in capturing high-level semantic content from image-text pair pre-training.
 - **3D Spatial Encoder (E$_{Spatial}$):** This branch leverages the feature backbone of VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer), a model trained on pixel-point pairs. It processes input frames to recover implicit 3D structural information. The architecture of E$_{Spatial}$ involves patchifying input frames and extracting 3D features through alternating frame-wise self-attention and global self-attention, enabling aggregation of spatial information across different frames. The dense 3D features (e$_{3D}$) are primarily used in the feature fusion stage.

- **Connector:** This module is responsible for integrating the features from both the 2D and 3D encoders. It first aligns the 3D features (e$_{3D}$) with the 2D features (e$_{2D}$) in both spatial and temporal dimensions. This alignment involves rearranging the aggregated spatial and temporal information in e$_{3D}$ into the feature channel dimension. Subsequently, a lightweight MLP-based connector fuses these aligned semantic (e$_{2D}$) and structural (e'$_{3D}$) features into unified visual tokens, which are then passed to the LLM backbone.

#### 3.2. Space-Aware Frame Sampling

To address the challenge of limited GPU memory and the need for spatially informative frames from long video sequences, Spatial-MLLM introduces a space-aware frame sampling strategy at inference time.

- **Initial Subsampling:** From an original video sequence of N frames, a larger subset of N$_{m}$ frames (e.g., N$_{m}$=128) is uniformly subsampled as candidates for spatial analysis.
- **3D Feature and Geometry Extraction:** For these N$_{m}$ candidate frames, the 3D spatial encoder (E$_{Spatial}$) extracts 3D features (e$_{m3D}$) and camera features (e$_{mc}$). The pretrained camera head (f$_{c}$) and depth head (f$_{d}$) of the VGGT model decode camera parameters and depth maps, respectively.
- **Voxel Coverage Calculation:** From the depth maps and camera parameters, 3D point maps are reconstructed via depth reprojection. A 3D bounding box encompassing all valid scene points is established and discretized into voxels. Each frame's voxel coverage (V(f$_{m_i}$)) is then calculated based on its valid points within these voxels.
- **Maximum Coverage Problem:** The frame selection task is formulated as a maximum coverage problem: select N$_{k}$ frames (e.g., N$_{k}$=16) from the N$_{m}$ candidates such that the total number of unique covered voxels is maximized. This problem is solved using a greedy algorithm, which iteratively selects the frame that provides the maximum new coverage until N$_{k}$ frames are chosen. The 3D features for these selected frames are then reused from the precomputed set.

#### 3.3. Training

The model undergoes a two-stage training process after constructing a specialized dataset.

- **Training Data Construction:** A visual-based 3D spatial question-answering (QA) dataset of approximately 120k QA pairs is constructed. This dataset integrates data from ScanQA, SQA3D, and a self-created portion derived from ScanNet scenes. The self-created data involves converting ScanNet scenes into continuous video clips, extracting spatial and semantic metadata (room size, object bounding boxes, semantic labels, appearance order), and generating QA pairs covering tasks like object counting, object size, room size, absolute distance, appearance order, relative distance, and relative direction. Care is taken to prevent data leakage from VSI-Bench validation sets.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** In this stage, the pre-trained 2D (E$_{2D}$) and 3D spatial (E$_{Spatial}$) encoders are frozen to preserve their feature extraction capabilities. The connection module and the LLM backbone are jointly trained using the constructed QA dataset. The training objective is a standard cross-entropy loss between the model-generated answers and the ground-truth annotations.
- **RL Training (GRPO):** Following SFT, a brief cold start phase is implemented to help the model adapt to the correct reasoning format, using a small Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset generated by prompting Qwen2.5-VL-72B and filtering for correct reasoning-answer pairs. Subsequently, the model is trained using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enhance its long-CoT spatial reasoning capability. The GRPO objective maximizes a reward function that incorporates a formatting reward and task-dependent rewards. Task-specific rewards include exact match for multiple-choice, mean relative accuracy for numerical questions, and a normalized Levenshtein similarity score for verbal answers. A reasoning length reward is also included to encourage detailed thinking processes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that Spatial-MLLM achieves performance improvements across a range of visual-based spatial understanding and reasoning tasks.

#### 4.1. Comparisons on VSI-Bench

On the VSI-Bench benchmark, which assesses visual-spatial intelligence across configurational reasoning, measurement estimation, and spatiotemporal reasoning tasks, Spatial-MLLM (4.9B parameters) exhibited strong performance.

- **Overall Performance:** Spatial-MLLM achieved an average micro-average score of 48.4, outperforming all proprietary and open-source MLLMs, including larger models with substantially more parameters (e.g., 32B or 72B). For instance, it surpassed Gemini-1.5 Pro (average score 45.4), despite Gemini-1.5 Pro utilizing significantly more input frames (1 FPS sampling, averaging 85 frames per video) compared to Spatial-MLLM's 16 input frames. Among open-source models, Spatial-MLLM established state-of-the-art performance, achieving the top rank.
- **Task-Specific Performance:** Spatial-MLLM showed notable strengths in object counting (65.3), object size (63.1), and room size (45.1), which are numerical question types. For multiple-choice questions, it also demonstrated competitive scores, particularly in relative direction (46.2) and appearance order (46.3).

#### 4.2. Comparison on ScanQA and SQA3D

Evaluations on ScanQA (validation set) and SQA3D (test set) benchmarks, which focus on 3D question-answering and situated QA in 3D scenes, further supported Spatial-MLLM's capabilities.

- **Video-Input Models:** Spatial-MLLM significantly outperformed all other video-input MLLMs across all metrics on both ScanQA and SQA3D. For example, on ScanQA, Spatial-MLLM achieved an EM-1 of 26.3, a BLEU-1 of 44.4, and a CIDEr of 91.8, substantially exceeding Qwen2.5-VL-72B (EM-1: 24.0, BLEU-1: 26.8, CIDEr: 66.9) and LLaVA-Video-7B (BLEU-1: 39.7, CIDEr: 88.7).
- **Task-Specific Models:** Spatial-MLLM also surpassed specialized task-specific models like ScanQA and 3D-Vista.
- **3D/2.5D-Input Models:** While some models leveraging explicit 3D or 2.5D inputs (e.g., 3D-LLaVA and Video-3D LLM) achieved higher scores on certain metrics, Spatial-MLLM demonstrated competitive performance without relying on such additional data. It outperformed other 3D-dependent models such as 3D-LLM, LL3DA, and Chat-Scene. On SQA3D, Spatial-MLLM achieved an average EM-1 of 55.9 and EM-R1 of 58.7, which is comparable to or better than several 3D-input models.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study and Analysis

- **Effectiveness of Space-aware Frame Sampling:** The space-aware frame sampling strategy consistently outperformed uniform sampling across different input frame counts (8, 16, 32 frames). For instance, with 16 frames, space-aware sampling yielded an average score of 48.4 compared to 47.1 for uniform sampling. Visualizations confirmed that the space-aware strategy provided better spatial coverage and avoided redundant viewpoints compared to uniform sampling.
- **Ablation on Input Frame Number:** Performance for all models (Spatial-MLLM, Gemini-1.5 Pro, Qwen2.5-VL-3B) generally improved as the number of input frames increased, especially when the initial frame count was low.
- **Effectiveness of RL Training:** Even with limited-scale RL training (1,000 steps of GRPO), the model showed performance gains (from 47.1 to 48.4 on VSI-Bench average), indicating that Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) contributed to enhanced long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning capabilities crucial for spatial tasks.
- **Effectiveness of Architecture and Training Dataset:** A supervised fine-tuned version of Qwen2.5-VL-3B using the custom dataset showed improvements over the original Qwen2.5-VL-3B, validating the utility of the dataset. However, the supervised fine-tuned Spatial-MLLM (using its unique architecture) further outperformed these Qwen2.5-VL-3B variants (e.g., 48.4 vs. 44.9), confirming the effectiveness of the proposed dual-encoder architecture in integrating structural information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Spatial-MLLM represents a notable development in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly concerning their capability for visual-based 3D spatial intelligence.

The primary significance of this work lies in its demonstration of effective spatial understanding and reasoning from purely 2D visual inputs, without the reliance on auxiliary 3D or 2.5D data such as point clouds, depth maps, or explicit camera parameters. This capability addresses a fundamental limitation of many existing 3D-aware MLLMs, broadening the applicability of spatially intelligent AI in real-world scenarios where only monocular video footage is readily available.

Key impacts include:

- **Enhanced Accessibility for Spatial AI:** By eliminating the need for specialized 3D data acquisition, Spatial-MLLM can lower the barrier to entry for developing and deploying spatially aware AI systems. This could expand the use of such models in domains where 3D scanning or depth sensing equipment is impractical or cost-prohibitive.
- **Improvements in Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Accurate visual-based 3D spatial intelligence is fundamental for robots and autonomous agents to navigate complex environments, grasp objects, and execute precise actions. Spatial-MLLM's ability to infer 3D structure from standard video inputs could contribute to more robust perception systems for these applications.
- **Advanced Visual Content Understanding:** The enhanced spatial reasoning capability could improve how MLLMs interpret and interact with video content, leading to more nuanced responses in tasks like video question answering, scene description, and event comprehension that require an understanding of object relationships and spatial layouts.
- **Novel Architectural Contributions:** The dual-encoder architecture, which systematically integrates semantic features from a conventional 2D visual encoder with structural features derived from a visual geometry foundation model, offers a principled approach to combining different types of visual information. This design choice, along with the space-aware frame sampling strategy, provides a methodological blueprint for future MLLM developments aiming for comprehensive visual understanding.
- **Benchmarking Performance:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like VSI-Bench, ScanQA, and SQA3D without explicit 3D inputs sets a new baseline for visual-based spatial intelligence in MLLMs. This provides a clear reference point for subsequent research in the area.

While the work demonstrates advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the potential for further scaling the model size and training data. They also suggest future work could explore how integrating spatial structural information might benefit general video understanding and reasoning tasks beyond specialized spatial intelligence, implying a broader potential for these architectural innovations.
