# ISO: An RLVR-Native Optimization Stack

> [!summary] Summary
> Isospectral Optimization (ISO) provides an RLVR-native framework that addresses the distinct dynamics of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards by preserving the base model's weight spectra while adapting new behaviors through singular frame modifications. This approach improves training efficiency, achieving higher accuracy or faster convergence compared to conventional optimizers, and enables effective data-free merging of RLVR specialist models.

## Problem

- Existing RLVR optimization layers largely reuse techniques from pre-training, which are suboptimal for sparse, outcome-level reward feedback.
- A clear coordinate system is lacking to distinguish between stable and changing elements of LLM weights during RLVR adaptation.
- Efficient composition of multiple RLVR specialist models is challenging, often relying on data-intensive methods for merging.

## Method

- The research empirically and theoretically investigates RLVR weight-space dynamics using ==Singular Value Decomposition== to differentiate between spectral and singular frame changes.
- It introduces the **Isospectral Optimization (ISO)** stack, comprising **ISO-Merger** for offline expert composition and **ISO-Optimizer** for online RLVR training.
- ISO-Optimizer fixes the base model's weight spectra and optimizes only the singular frames via ==polar retraction==, while ISO-Merger combines learned frame changes from specialists by reusing shared base spectra.

## Results

- ISO-Optimizer consistently improved final accuracy and/or reduced training steps by up to **2.7 times** on models like Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base for math tasks compared to conventional optimizers.
- ISO-Merger achieved the highest aggregate performance among data-free merging methods on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (**63.80** vs. **62.88** for best baseline) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (**44.38** vs. **43.51**).
- Empirical analysis confirmed RLVR checkpoints remained near-isospectral (e.g., base-normalized spectral distance **~10⁻²%**), and restoring base spectra to learned frames preserved most performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RLVR adaptation exhibits "spectral inheritance," functionally reusing the base model's weight spectra while acquiring new behaviors by adapting its singular frames.
> - Unlike supervised fine-tuning, RLVR updates do not show a strong additional preference for spectrum-preserving motion, but rather demonstrate the functional reusability of base spectra.
> - Effective RLVR learning requires the adaptability of both singular frames, not just changes within existing spans or single subspaces, for low-residual reconstruction of learned endpoints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the optimization layer in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), a domain experiencing advancements in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of language models. While progress has been made in data, environments, learning objectives, and systems for RLVR, the optimization techniques used to translate reward feedback into weight-space updates largely derive from pre-training methodologies. This approach is noted as potentially suboptimal because pre-training involves dense token-level supervision, whereas RLVR adapts an already capable policy using comparatively sparse outcome-level rewards.

Existing research has begun to distinguish RLVR from pre-training and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) dynamics. At the policy level, RLVR has been characterized by reverse-KL and KL-proximal views. At the parameter level, RLVR updates have been reported as sparse, off-principal, and exhibiting strong overlap across independent runs initialized from the same base model. However, these observations have not yielded a clear coordinate system that distinguishes between elements of the model that are reused and those that undergo change during RLVR adaptation.

This work builds on prior analysis by the authors regarding RL optimization dynamics, specifically an observation of limited spectral drift in RLVR. It aims to develop a concrete RLVR-native optimization stack that leverages insights into the structural changes occurring during reward-driven adaptation. The work differentiates itself from other matrix-geometric optimization techniques by focusing on an evidence-to-design approach tailored specifically for RLVR, rather than applying general spectrum-preserving or manifold-constrained optimization principles. It also distinguishes itself from concurrent work that explores spectrum preservation for general LLM training by providing a functional characterization of spectral inheritance within RLVR and developing a framework that spans both online learning and offline expert composition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to understand and redesign the optimization layer for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The authors identify a "missing optimization layer" in RLVR, where current optimizers and parameterizations are largely inherited from pre-training, despite the distinct nature of reward-driven adaptation compared to dense token-level supervision.

The key motivations are:
1. **Understand RLVR Weight-Space Dynamics:** To uncover the structural properties of how RLVR modifies language model weights, specifically differentiating between what remains stable and what changes. This involves moving beyond Euclidean weight coordinates to a spectral view.
2. **Identify Functional Regularities:** To determine if observed spectral properties in RLVR are merely descriptive or functionally essential. This involves investigating whether small spectral changes are necessary for acquired behaviors and whether the base model's spectral structure can be effectively reused.
3. **Characterize Adaptability Requirements:** To identify which model components (e.g., singular frames, singular values) must remain adaptable to achieve significant learning, once a spectral reuse principle is established.
4. **Develop RLVR-Native Optimization Tools:** To operationalize the identified structural regularities into a practical optimization framework tailored for RLVR. This framework aims to offer a more efficient and effective approach to both online RLVR training and offline composition of RL specialists.
5. **Improve Efficiency and Performance:** By aligning optimization with the inherent dynamics of RLVR, the work aims to improve the accuracy of learned models and reduce the computational resources (e.g., training steps) required for adaptation.
6. **Facilitate Model Merging:** To provide a method for consolidating the capabilities of multiple RLVR specialists trained from a shared base without requiring additional data, rollouts, gradient updates, or on-policy distillation, addressing a challenge in modular RLVR workflows.

The central hypothesis guiding these objectives is that RLVR exhibits "spectral inheritance," meaning it can reuse the base model's weight spectra while acquiring new behavior by changing the associated input and output singular frames. The work seeks to validate this hypothesis and then translate it into a design principle: "inherit the spectrum, optimize the frames."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is divided into two main parts: an empirical and theoretical investigation of RLVR dynamics, and the subsequent operationalization of findings into the Isospectral Optimization (ISO) stack.

#### 3.1. Empirical and Theoretical Investigation

- **Spectral View of Weight Matrices:** The authors represent each weight matrix $W$ using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD): $W = U\Sigma V^\top$, where $\Sigma$ contains singular values (scales) and $(U, V)$ are the left and right singular frames (directions). This allows for separate analysis of spectral and frame changes.
- **Observation of Near-Isospectrality:** The study first quantifies "spectra stay" by measuring the Frobenius distance of learned RLVR checkpoints to the "fixed-spectrum family" $\mathcal{F}(W_0)$ of their base weights $W_0$. This family comprises all matrices sharing the singular values of $W_0$ but allowing arbitrary singular frames. Metrics like $\delta_{\Sigma}$ (spectral drift relative to base weight scale) and $\rho_{\Sigma}$ (relative spectral residual) are used.
- **Dimension-Aware Calibration:** To assess if near-isospectrality reflects a genuine preference for spectrum-preserving motion or is merely a high-dimensional artifact, a dimension-normalized spectrum-changing energy $\kappa_{\text{spec}}$ is introduced. This metric normalizes the energy in spectrum-changing coordinates by the ratio of their dimension to the total matrix dimension, with an isotropic displacement having an expected value of one.
- **Functional Regularity Tests (Spectral Inheritance):**
 - **Endpoint Intervention:** After unconstrained RLVR training, the base spectra are restored to RL-trained checkpoints while retaining the learned frames. Performance is then evaluated to see if acquired gains are preserved.
 - **Training-Time Intervention:** The base spectra are fixed from the beginning of training, and only the singular frames are allowed to update. This tests whether strong RLVR learning can occur without any spectral change. A control experiment freezes frames and updates only spectra.
- **Structural Characterization (Frame Adaptability):** To determine which components must remain adaptable, the authors compare four reconstruction classes for learned endpoints:
 1. **Remix (within incoming spans):** $P_j^{(r)} W_i^{(r)} Q_j^{(r)}$ – only remixes within the top-$r$ input and output spans of the incoming checkpoint $W_j$.
 2. **Retain Left Subspace:** $P_j^{(r)} W_i^{(r)}$ – retains the top-$r$ incoming output subspace.
 3. **Retain Right Subspace:** $W_i^{(r)} Q_j^{(r)}$ – retains the top-$r$ incoming input subspace.
 4. **Retain Incoming Spectrum, Adapt Both Frames:** $U_i^{(r)} \Sigma_j^{(r)} (V_i^{(r)})^\top$ – retains the incoming top-$r$ spectrum $\Sigma_j^{(r)}$ while allowing both singular frames to adapt.
 These are evaluated using "unexplained-update ratios" ($u_h$), which measure the Frobenius distance from the learned endpoint to the optimal reconstruction under each restriction, normalized by the total checkpoint displacement. This is tested across single and sequential RL stages with distinct objectives (e.g., vision-language-action pipeline).

#### 3.2. Isospectral Optimization (ISO) Framework

The findings from the investigation inform the design principle: "inherit the spectrum, optimize the frames." This is operationalized through ISO, an RLVR-native optimization stack with two instantiations:

- **ISO-Merger (Offline Expert Composition):**
 - **Principle:** Combines learned frame changes of shared-base specialists into a single fixed-spectrum model, reusing the shared base spectra ($\Sigma_0$).
 - **Mechanism:** For each expert $i$ and base $W_0 = U_0 \Sigma_0 V_0^\top$, the expert's SVD $W_i = U_i \Sigma_i V_i^\top$ is computed.
 - **Sign Canonicalization:** Individual singular-vector pairs $(u_k, v_k)$ are aligned against the base using a sign-canonicalization rule to avoid spurious displacements.
 - **Tangent Projection:** Frame displacements ($\Delta U_i = U_i - U_0$, $\Delta V_i = V_i - V_0$) are projected onto the Stiefel tangent spaces at $U_0$ and $V_0$.
 - **Mode Masking:** Trailing modes (columns associated with small singular values) are masked out (typically 10% smallest) to improve stability.
 - **Unit-Retention Target:** A Gram matrix $\Gamma$ of local first-order effect proxies $g_i$ (derived from masked tangent displacements) is formed. Coefficients $c^*$ for merging are determined by solving a ridge-stabilized system $\Gamma c = b$ (where $b$ targets unit self-retention for each expert) and clipping coefficients.
 - **Polar Retraction:** The aggregated frame displacements are projected onto the tangent spaces, and the resulting factors $(U_0 + \xi_{U,\star}, V_0 + \xi_{V,\star})$ are "retracted" using a polar decomposition to ensure they remain on the Stiefel manifold (i.e., have orthonormal columns).
 - **Reconstruction:** The merged weight matrix $W_\star = U_\star \Sigma_0 V_\star^\top$ is reconstructed using the base spectrum. One-dimensional parameters are averaged.

- **ISO-Optimizer (Online RLVR Training):**
 - **Principle:** Applies a chosen base optimizer (e.g., AdamW, Muon) to the singular-frame variables $(U, V)$ while keeping the base spectra ($\Sigma_0$) fixed.
 - **Mechanism:** The weight matrix is parameterized as $W(U, V) = U \Sigma_0 V^\top$.
 - **Factor Gradients:** Given the loss gradient with respect to $W$, $G_W = \nabla_W \mathcal{L}(W)$, the Euclidean gradients for the frame variables are derived via the chain rule: $G_U = G_W V \Sigma_0$ and $G_V = G_W^\top U \Sigma_0$.
 - **Tentative Updates:** The base optimizer (e.g., AdamW) applies its update rule independently to $U$ and $V$ using their respective gradients $G_U$ and $G_V$, producing tentative factors $(\bar{U}, \bar{V})$.
 - **Polar Retraction:** These tentative factors are then corrected using the polar retraction (similar to ISO-Merger) to ensure they remain orthonormal.
 - **Reconstruction:** The final weight matrix $W^+ = U^+ \Sigma_0 (V^+)^\top$ is reconstructed, preserving the base spectrum.
 - **Implementation:** The polar retraction is performed using FP64 SVD for numerical stability, with the cost distributed across multiple GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Spectral Inheritance in RLVR Dynamics

- **Near-Isospectrality:** Unconstrained RLVR checkpoints consistently remained close to the fixed-spectrum families of their base weights. For an illustrative RLVR run, the base-normalized spectral distance $\delta_{\Sigma}$ was approximately $10^{-2}\%$, and the relative spectral residual $\rho_{\Sigma}$ averaged approximately $3\%$ across analyzed matrices. This proximity was observed to persist throughout training trajectories, not just at endpoints.
- **Dimension-Aware Calibration:** While RLVR updates showed proximity to fixed-spectrum families, the dimension-normalized spectrum-changing energy $\kappa_{\text{spec}}$ was order-one (approximately 1.0–1.4). This suggests that RLVR does not exhibit a strong *additional* preference for spectrum-preserving motion beyond what high dimensionality naturally predicts. In contrast, SFT transitions exhibited $\kappa_{\text{spec}}$ values two to three orders of magnitude higher (89–1364), indicating a strong concentration of updates in spectrum-changing coordinates.
- **Functional Reusability:**
 - Restoring the base spectra of RLVR checkpoints while retaining their learned frames preserved most of the acquired performance gains.
 - Keeping the base spectra fixed throughout training and updating only the associated frames supported strong RLVR learning, sometimes outperforming the AdamW baseline. Conversely, a restricted "spectrum-only" control (freezing frames, updating singular values) yielded limited improvement. These results support "spectral inheritance" – RLVR can functionally reuse the base model’s spectral structure.
- **Frame Adaptability:** Among tested structural restrictions, low-residual reconstruction of learned RLVR endpoints required both singular frames to remain adaptable. Simpler transformations, such as remixing only within incoming input/output spans or retaining one singular subspace while changing the other, left substantially more of the checkpoint change unexplained (median 87% for remix, 45% for retaining output subspace, 42% for retaining input subspace). Retaining the incoming spectrum and adapting both frames left a median residual of only 1.8%. This pattern recurred across sequential RL stages with distinct objectives (e.g., spatial reasoning followed by embodied manipulation).

#### 4.2. ISO-Merger Performance (Offline Expert Composition)

- **Aggregate Performance:** ISO-Merger achieved the highest aggregate performance among the compared data-free merging methods in two settings:
 - On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with three RL experts (coding, tool use, memory), ISO-Merger attained an overall average of 63.80, surpassing the strongest baseline (RAM) at 62.88.
 - On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B with two RL experts (coding, math), ISO-Merger achieved 44.38, exceeding the strongest baseline (RAM) at 43.51.
- **Tail Performance:** ISO-Merger also demonstrated competitive performance in "best@4" metrics and improved "worst@4" metrics by 1.62 and 1.36 points, respectively, suggesting more consistent capability recovery across stochastic generations.
- **Data-Free Merging:** ISO-Merger successfully composed shared-base RLVR specialists directly from their checkpoints without requiring post-merge data, additional rollout generation, gradient updates, or on-policy distillation.

#### 4.3. ISO-Optimizer Performance (Online RLVR Training)

- **Improved Accuracy and Training Efficiency:** ISO-Optimizer consistently improved final accuracy and/or reduced training steps compared to conventional weight-space optimizers (AdamW, Muon).
 - **Qwen3-1.7B-Base (Math):** ISO-AdamW achieved the strongest aggregate score (28.74) compared to AdamW (28.35) and Muon (27.70).
 - **Qwen3-4B-Base (Math):** ISO-AdamW reached the strongest AdamW run’s final accuracy with approximately 2.2 times fewer training steps and continued to improve thereafter, achieving 43.46 vs. 41.69 for AdamW. ISO-Muon showed similar benefits over Muon.
 - **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (Coding):** ISO-AdamW improved over AdamW on LiveCodeBench splits and outperformed the strongest AdamW setting across most evaluation checkpoints within its training budget.
 - **Qwen3-8B-Base (Math):** ISO-AdamW reached an accuracy of 0.495 (AdamW's peak) in 100 steps, a 2.7x reduction in training steps, and further improved to 0.509 at 210 steps.
- **Overhead:** The SVD-based polar retraction in ISO-Optimizer increased optimizer-update time by approximately 86 seconds per step, but this accounted for only about 7% of the end-to-end RL step time in tested settings, as rollout generation remained the dominant factor.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work identifies "spectral inheritance" as a fundamental structural regularity in RLVR, which differentiates it from pre-training and SFT. This finding suggests that the spectral properties of a base model can be largely preserved, while the model adapts to new tasks by modifying its singular frames. This understanding contributes to the theoretical characterization of how large language models adapt under reinforcement learning.

The practical impact is manifested through the Isospectral Optimization (ISO) stack, which directly operationalizes this spectral inheritance principle.

- **Improved RLVR Training Efficiency:** ISO-Optimizer demonstrates that by aligning the optimization process with the natural dynamics of RLVR (i.e., optimizing frames under a fixed spectrum), training can become more efficient. This is evidenced by achieving comparable or higher accuracies with substantially fewer training steps. This could translate to reduced computational costs and faster iteration cycles in developing advanced RLVR-based models.
- **Enhanced Model Merging:** ISO-Merger provides a novel, data-free method for composing capabilities from multiple RLVR specialists. This capability is significant for modular RLVR workflows, potentially simplifying the process of combining diverse skills without the need for computationally intensive on-policy distillation or additional data. It offers a more structured approach to model consolidation that explicitly accounts for the underlying changes in RL-adapted models.
- **Guiding Future Optimization Design:** The "inherit the spectrum, optimize the frames" principle offers a new inductive bias for designing RLVR-native optimization algorithms and parameterizations. This may inspire further research into geometry-aware adaptation techniques, not only for full-model fine-tuning but also for parameter-efficient adaptation methods like adapters, where explicit management of singular frames could be beneficial.
- **Deeper Understanding of LLM Adaptation:** By providing a clear separation between reusable and adaptable components during RLVR, this research deepens the understanding of how LLMs acquire new behaviors post-pre-training. This insight could inform the design of future LLM architectures and training paradigms beyond RLVR.
- **Scalability:** The demonstrated benefits of ISO across models ranging from 1.5B to 8B parameters suggest its potential applicability to larger-scale models, although further investigation on very large models would be necessary. The analysis of computational overhead indicates that the additional costs are manageable within current RLVR system architectures.

In summary, this research moves beyond heuristic optimization choices inherited from pre-training by providing an evidence-based, RLVR-native optimization framework, potentially leading to more efficient, effective, and structured methods for developing and deploying advanced language models.
