# Discrete-WAM: Unified Discrete Vision-Action Token Editing for World-Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Discrete-WAM presents a unified framework for autonomous driving, integrating world modeling, action policy, and hierarchical decision-making within a shared discrete token space. This approach leverages discrete diffusion for token editing to enable structured causal reasoning about ego actions and future world states, achieving improved planning performance and robust visual generation.

## Problem

- End-to-end autonomous driving systems often rely on direct vision-to-action mappings, learning statistical correlations without explicitly modeling action-conditioned dynamics or exploring counterfactual scenarios.
- Traditional world models often treat world modeling and policy learning as separate, loosely coupled processes, leading to inconsistencies in causal correspondences between actions and future states.
- Continuous latent representations commonly used in existing systems often entangle features, hindering explicit compositional semantics and reliable action-conditioned reasoning.

## Method

- Introduces a unified latent vision-action world policy operating in a shared ==discrete token space==, where observations, actions, and future states are represented as aligned semantic units.
- Formulates world modeling, world-action policy, and hierarchical decision-enabled policy as unified generative tasks within a shared ==discrete diffusion== framework, allowing for bidirectional reasoning.
- Decomposes policy generation into a hierarchical process, predicting a high-level decision skeleton first, then performing discrete diffusion-based action-token editing conditioned on this decision.

## Results

- Achieved **90.4 EPDMS** on NAVSIM-v2, improving over WAM-Flow by +**2.7 EPDMS** and DreamerAD by +**3.1 EPDMS**, demonstrating enhanced safety and comfort in planning.
- Obtained an FID of 6.6 and FVD of 80.0 for world generation, outperforming existing generative driving world models like Vista and DrivingWorld, indicating high visual fidelity and effective capture of future dynamics.
- Ablation studies confirmed that LoRA-based adaptation preserved valuable world-policy knowledge (**90.0 EPDMS**), and reinforcement learning post-training further refined the policy to **90.4 EPDMS**, optimizing high-level decision selection and low-level token editing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligned discrete representations for observations and actions enable explicit compositional semantics and provide shared anchors for structured modeling of action-conditioned future evolution.
> - A unified generative approach, built on discrete diffusion for token editing, can effectively couple policy generation with world evolution, ensuring consistent causal correspondences.
> - Hierarchical policy modeling, by separating high-level maneuver intent from low-level action generation, improves the handling of multi-modal driving choices and temporal consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving is a complex physical AI problem that necessitates an agent's ability to reason about the impact of its actions on future world evolution, rather than merely reacting to instantaneous observations. The existing landscape of end-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving systems predominantly relies on direct vision-to-action mapping, often through behavior cloning. While effective in capturing statistical correlations, these methods typically lack explicit modeling of action-conditioned dynamics, limiting their generalization capabilities across diverse and counterfactual scenarios.

Prediction-augmented planning approaches attempt to incorporate future reasoning but frequently depend on predefined intermediate annotations or task-specific representations. This dependence can constrain their flexibility and ability to handle novel situations. Recent vision-language-action (VLA) models have sought to integrate semantic priors through language supervision; however, their reasoning often remains bounded by annotation granularity, offering a low-bandwidth interface for fine-grained spatio-temporal dynamics.

A notable bottleneck identified in current autonomous driving systems is the alignment of representations. Many systems operate in continuous latent spaces, which, despite offering smooth interpolation, can lead to highly entangled representations lacking explicit compositional semantics. This entanglement complicates reliable action-conditioned reasoning and comparison of alternative future trajectories. Consequently, visual observations, actions, and future states often remain weakly aligned, creating a disconnect between understanding future dynamics and generating optimal behaviors.

Moreover, while world models have demonstrated strong capabilities in future generation and action-conditioned world modeling, they often remain weakly aligned with downstream policy generation. Future prediction and policy learning are commonly optimized as separate modules or auxiliary objectives, leading to a gap between comprehending future dynamics and selecting appropriate actions. Driving policies inherently possess a hierarchical structure, where high-level decisions influence low-level trajectories, and future action tokens exhibit strong dependencies due to route constraints and vehicle dynamics. Fully parallel action generation, therefore, risks dependency mismatch.

Discrete representations, offering compositional semantic units and a shared token space, have emerged as a potential solution to these challenges, enabling unified sequence modeling with shared frameworks and token-level objectives for multimodal alignment. However, deterministic discretization error poses a challenge for continuous action control. This work aims to bridge these gaps by proposing a unified framework that aligns representations, integrates world and policy modeling, and incorporates hierarchical policy construction within a shared discrete token space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the Discrete-WAM research are to develop a unified framework for autonomous driving that addresses three identified key properties for reliable physical AI:

1. **Representation Alignment:** To establish a shared representation space that coherently links visual observations, ego actions, future states, and high-level decision variables. The motivation here is to overcome the limitations of continuous latent spaces, which often produce entangled representations that hinder action-conditioned reasoning and counterfactual analysis. By unifying these elements within a discrete token space, the framework aims to enable more explicit compositional semantics and facilitate tighter multi-modal alignment.
2. **World-Policy Alignment:** To integrate future prediction (world modeling) directly with action generation (policy learning) within the same representation and optimization space. The existing paradigm often treats these as separate or auxiliary objectives, leading to a gap where understanding future dynamics does not directly inform or optimize policy selection. The motivation is to foster bidirectional interaction between world dynamics and policy learning, allowing action-conditioned future prediction to directly support and be optimized alongside policy generation.
3. **Hierarchical Policy Construction:** To decompose policy generation into hierarchical components, linking high-level decisions with fine-grained low-level actions. Autonomous driving behavior is intrinsically hierarchical; high-level decisions (e.g., lane change intent) determine the feasible space for low-level trajectories. The motivation is to address the dependency mismatch inherent in fully parallel action generation by providing a structured approach that first predicts a high-level planning skeleton and then refines dense future actions efficiently, ensuring consistency and long-horizon behavioral structure.

In summary, the motivation stems from the perceived limitations of current end-to-end and world model-based autonomous driving systems, particularly their weak alignment between world understanding and policy generation, and the entanglement of representations in continuous latent spaces. Discrete-WAM aims to address these by introducing a unified discrete vision-action world-policy framework that processes observations, actions, decisions, and future evolution as coupled variables through a unified generative process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Discrete-WAM operates as a unified discrete vision-action world-policy framework. The approach formulates autonomous driving as sequence modeling over a shared discrete token space, where visual observations, ego actions, future states, and decision variables are jointly represented and modeled.

#### 3.1. Base Architecture

The architecture comprises four main components:
1. **Vision VQ Tokenizer:** Encodes continuous visual observations from cameras into compact discrete semantic tokens, aligning them with the Transformer's hidden dimension.
2. **Context Encoder:** Injects ego-state information (e.g., vehicle speed, position) and navigation commands into the latent sequences to provide situational awareness.
3. **Decoder-only Transformer Backbone:** A shared Transformer architecture that jointly models visual tokens, action tokens, and their future evolution in a unified token space. The model has 18 layers, 16 attention heads, and 8 grouped-query attention heads.
4. **Multi-task Prediction Heads:** Output modules for world modeling, policy generation, and world-action sequence generation, tailored to different training objectives.

#### 3.2. Tokenization

- **Vision Tokenization:** A pretrained VQ-VAE-based tokenizer (codebook size K_V = 16384) converts front-view camera images into discrete visual tokens. Each image is divided into non-overlapping patches (H_V x W_V = 16x16) and encoded into a sequence of tokens, which is applied independently to each input frame.
- **Action Tokenization:** Continuous future ego motion (trajectory over H steps) is converted into discrete action tokens representing 2D accelerations (longitudinal a_x, lateral a_y). This involves:
 1. Fitting the trajectory with a cubic spline for smoothness.
 2. Computing ego-centric 2D accelerations (a_x, a_y) using second-order finite differences.
 3. Constructing a uniformly distributed 2D acceleration vocabulary by partitioning a_x and a_y ranges into N_x x N_y bins (e.g., 60x60 = 3600 prototypes).
 4. Representing continuous accelerations with a "soft target" over their four neighboring vocabulary entries via bilinear interpolation. This technique is designed to mitigate deterministic hard-quantization error, allowing exact reconstruction of continuous accelerations if the soft target distribution is perfectly recovered. The action head is trained with cross-entropy against this soft target.
- **Decision Tokenization:** High-level driving decisions (D_t) are parameterized using a discrete vocabulary combining lateral path candidates (P) and longitudinal speed profiles (S), forming a decision token space D (e.g., 400 options). During pretraining, supervision assigns the "winner-take-all" decision based on the best EPDMS sub-scores among candidates. For finetuning, the decision space is restricted to top-D candidates.

#### 3.3. Unified World Policy Training

Discrete-WAM integrates three training modes, sharing the same discrete token interface and Transformer architecture but differing in conditioning inputs and prediction targets:
1. **World Modeling:** Predicts future visual tokens (V_t+1:t+H) given scene context (C_t) and teacher-forced future action tokens (A_t+1:t+H). This trains the model to understand action-conditioned scene evolution.
2. **World-Policy Modeling:** Jointly trains vision prediction and action prediction. Given C_t, it predicts an interleaved sequence of future action and visual tokens (Y_t+1:t+H = [A_t+1, V_t+1..., A_t+H, V_t+H]). This mode explicitly couples policy generation with world evolution.
3. **Policy Modeling:** Learns hierarchical decision-conditioned action generation. From C_t, the model first predicts a high-level decision (D_t), then, conditioned on C_t and D_t, predicts the future action sequence (A_t+1:t+H). This separates multi-modal choices from trajectory realization.

#### 3.4. Unified Pretraining Details

- **Token-Editing Objective:** A general objective where an input sequence of corrupted tokens (`~X`) is provided, and the model is trained to predict the clean target tokens (`X`). The loss is applied to all editable positions, encouraging recovery for corrupted tokens and identity mapping for clean ones (implicit stopping signal).
 - Visual token corruption: uniform sampling of positions.
 - Action token corruption: causal suffix pattern (first `h_γ` tokens clean, rest corrupted).
- **Training Objectives:** A weighted sum of multiple loss terms (Eq 9):
 - Cross-entropy for visual and action tokens (L_cls_v, L_cls_a).
 - Acceleration-level regression (L_acc).
 - Trajectory-level regression (L_traj), integrating predicted accelerations.
 - Auxiliary factorized position classification (L_cls_pos) for integrated x and y positions.
 - Losses for special tokens (L_cls_s) and decision classification (L_dec).
- **Mode-Aware Decoding:** To mitigate "decoding-induced mode averaging" for continuous motion losses, the predicted categorical action distribution is fitted with a multi-modal Gaussian mixture. A mode is sampled (e.g., top-p sampling), and expected acceleration is computed from the re-normalized distribution within that mode. This ensures continuous accelerations are physically plausible for multi-modal scenarios.
- **Multi-stage Training Schedule:**
 1. **Stage 1 (Visual Pretraining):** World-policy modeling and world modeling tasks are performed, focusing on vision prediction loss, with future actions as teacher-forced conditions.
 2. **Stage 2 (Joint Visual & Action Prediction):** World-policy modeling activates both vision and action prediction losses, strengthening policy-world coupling.
 3. **Stage 3 (Action Finetuning):** LoRA adapters are used to finetune the discrete diffusion policy model for future action generation and refinement, preserving learned visual-world representations.

#### 3.5. Post-Training

To enhance robustness to rare or safety-critical behaviors, a fine-tuning stage leverages model-based trajectory sampling and Grouped Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- **Policy Sampling:** For each scene context, Discrete-WAM generates a group of candidate trajectories by iterative token editing, which are then evaluated using an online reward function based on EPDMS metrics. This includes decision-level sampling across the full distribution of decision tokens.
- **Training Objectives (GRPO):** Computes per-token log-probabilities for actions and decisions, and uses an advantage function (R(τ_i) - avg(R(τ))) to optimize the policy, aiming to refine both high-level decision optimization and low-level token editing.

#### 3.6. Planning for Downstream Tasks

Discrete-WAM decomposes downstream planning into:
- **Hierarchical Decision Prediction:** The decision token provides a high-level planning skeleton.
- **Parallel Action-Token Editing:** Confidence-based scheduling refines dense future actions efficiently, prioritizing uncertain tokens for update and preserving stable predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Discrete-WAM was evaluated on large-scale autonomous-driving benchmarks (NAVSIM-v1 and NAVSIM-v2), demonstrating robust performance across planning, world generation, and detailed analyses.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Planning Performance:**
 - On NAVSIM-v2, Discrete-WAM achieved an EPDMS score of 90.4, outperforming state-of-the-art methods, including world-model-based (e.g., +6.2% relative improvement over Epona, +3.1% over DreamerAD) and reinforced cognitive planners (e.g., +4.1% over ReCogDrive).
 - On NAVSIM-v1, it achieved 92.2 PDMS, also demonstrating competitive results (e.g., +2.1 PDMS over WAM-Flow).
- **World Generation Performance:**
 - Discrete-WAM achieved an FID of 6.6 and FVD of 80.0, indicating high visual fidelity and outperforming existing generative driving world models (e.g., Vista, DrivingWorld) in terms of image and video generation quality for short horizons.

#### 4.2. Analysis

- **Effect of Unified Pretraining:** Training from scratch yielded strong performance, but full finetuning did not improve it, suggesting potential overwriting of useful pretrained representations. In contrast, LoRA-SFT (pretraining + LoRA finetuning) achieved the best EPDMS (90.0), indicating that lightweight adaptation effectively preserves pretrained world-policy knowledge while adapting to planning objectives.
- **Effect of Post-training:** RL post-training further improved EPDMS to 90.4 and EPDMS* to 87.0 (with gains in comfort and safety), building upon supervised finetuning (SFT-D_t: 90.0). This indicates that post-training helps explore better decision-trajectory combinations.
- **Effect of Decision Modeling:** Introducing the high-level decision token (D_t) during pretraining improved EPDMS from 84.7 ("Base") to 87.2 ("Base-D_t"), suggesting that decision-level guidance provides a strong behavioral prior that aligns high-level intent with low-level trajectory generation.
- **Effect of Scheduling Strategies:** Selective scheduling strategies (e.g., `replace_confidence`) improved or maintained L2 trajectory error with more editing rounds, unlike `full_replace` which degraded performance. This highlights the benefit of refining uncertain parts of the action sequence while preserving stable predictions. Confidence-based replacement was shown to gradually decrease token uncertainty and average normalized entropy across rounds, selectively updating uncertain tokens.
- **Inference Latency:** Discrete diffusion decoding demonstrated substantially lower online inference latency than an autoregressive action decoder for a moderate number of editing rounds due to its parallel token decoding mechanism.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- **Planning:** Predicted trajectories closely followed expert demonstrations, maintaining geometrical consistency with road topology, stable lane centering, accurate longitudinal progression, and smooth turning. Discrete-WAM handled complex urban scenes (e.g., lane-changing, nudging) without explicit rule-based constraints.
- **World Generation:** Generated temporally coherent future visual states, preserving scene layout, road geometry, surrounding vehicles, and ego-motion consistency across diverse urban driving conditions.
- **Attention Map Analysis:** The policy decoder attended to driving-relevant semantic regions (roads, lane markings, vehicles, traffic signs). A consistent high attention in sky regions was observed, hypothesized to function as implicit global anchors or attention sinks, providing global contextual cues while lower image regions supplied detailed local semantics. Ablation studies supported this hypothesis, showing attention redistribution when the sky region was masked and degraded performance when only the sky region was used for prediction.
- **Counterfactual Evaluation:** A strong negative correlation was observed between the surprise metric (KL divergence between factual and counterfactual world-model generations) and the planning quality (PDMS) when lateral actions were perturbed. This indicated that Discrete-WAM's world model captures action-conditioned scene dynamics and safety-critical outcomes (e.g., collisions, off-road driving). Surprise values increased significantly as driving quality deteriorated, demonstrating a causal understanding of scene dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Discrete-WAM represents a notable advancement in the field of autonomous driving and physical AI. Its primary significance lies in the validation of a comprehensive design paradigm characterized by:
1. **Discrete Representation Alignment:** By unifying visual observations, future states, driving decisions, and ego actions within a shared discrete token space, the framework establishes a foundation for more interpretable and compositionally rich representations compared to entangled continuous latent spaces. This alignment enables a common prior across world evolution and policy generation.
2. **Unified World-Policy Training:** The joint training of world modeling, world-policy modeling, and policy modeling ensures that future prediction is directly optimized to support policy-relevant action generation, rather than serving as an isolated auxiliary task. This approach fosters a deeper causal understanding of how actions influence future world states.
3. **Hierarchical Policy Construction with Parallel Token Editing:** Decomposing policy generation into high-level decision prediction and low-level action-token refinement, combined with confidence-based re-edit scheduling, provides an efficient and structured mechanism for generating temporally consistent future actions. This hierarchical approach reduces dependencies among dense action tokens and enhances the suitability of parallel discrete diffusion for planning.

The strong planning performance observed across large-scale benchmarks (NAVSIM-v1 and v2), coupled with capabilities such as controllable future generation, counterfactual evaluation, surprise-based world-model analysis, and efficient parallel policy decoding, indicates a potential shift from reactive imitation towards decision-oriented world modeling in physical AI systems.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Safety and Robustness:** The causal understanding demonstrated through counterfactual analysis and surprise metrics could lead to more robust and safety-aware autonomous driving systems, capable of anticipating and evaluating the consequences of diverse actions.
- **Improved Planning Efficiency:** The parallel token editing approach offers computational advantages for online inference, potentially leading to faster and more responsive planning systems.
- **Better Generalization:** The unified representation and training framework could enable better generalization to novel scenarios and environmental conditions by fostering a more fundamental understanding of world dynamics.
- **Foundation for Future AI Research:** The paradigm presented by Discrete-WAM could serve as a blueprint for developing more capable embodied AI systems, extending beyond autonomous driving to robotics and other physical domains.

Future work outlined by the authors includes incorporating language-based reasoning, exploring adaptive serial-parallel generation schedules, leveraging intrinsic value functions from world models for self-supervised reinforcement learning, and extending the paradigm to broader datasets and interactive field tests. These directions suggest that Discrete-WAM lays groundwork for addressing complex challenges in advanced autonomous systems.
