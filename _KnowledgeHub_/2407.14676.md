# On Learning Discriminative Features from Synthesized Data for Self-Supervised Fine-Grained Visual Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of California, Santa Barbara, and The University of Adelaide introduced a self-supervised learning strategy that enhances fine-grained visual recognition by generating synthetic data pairs from the latent feature space. This method improved top-1 classification accuracy by up to 5.51% on FGVC-Aircraft and consistently outperformed state-of-the-art self-supervised FGVR methods in both classification and image retrieval across multiple datasets.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods exhibit a "coarse-label bias," often overlooking subtle visual distinctions crucial for fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR).
- SSL models tend to be distracted by task-irrelevant features (e.g., background noise) when applied to FGVR, hindering their ability to capture discriminative patterns.
- There is a need to guide SSL models to selectively extract highly discriminative features and explicitly disregard non-informative ones for FGVR tasks.

## Method

- The method integrates into existing SSL frameworks (e.g., ==MoCo v2==, ==SimSiam==) by introducing a decoder and a ==proposed contrastive loss== applied to synthetically generated data pairs.
- Non-discriminative latent feature dimensions are identified using two criteria: ==Grad-CAM-based saliency== (for features less crucial to contrastive loss) and ==low dimension variance== (for features with poor discriminative power).
- Synthetic positive data pairs are created by perturbing identified non-discriminative feature dimensions, and images are reconstructed from these perturbed features, training the encoder to be invariant to these irrelevant variations.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently improved linear evaluation (classification) accuracy by an average of **3.31%** on CUB-200, **4.27%** on Stanford Cars, and **5.51%** on FGVC-Aircraft compared to baseline MoCo v2, especially in label-insufficient scenarios.
- It achieved state-of-the-art performance in both linear evaluation and image retrieval across various FGVR datasets (e.g., CUB-200, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft, GTSRB, ISIC2017), outperforming existing self-supervised FGVR techniques.
- Grad-CAM visualizations showed that the method enabled encoders to focus more precisely on pivotal objects and reduced attention to background distractions, confirming improved discriminative feature learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Perturbing non-discriminative features in the latent space and training with a reconstruction loss effectively guides the encoder to focus on fine-grained discriminative attributes.
> - Combining Grad-CAM-based saliency and dimension variance provides complementary mechanisms for robustly identifying and perturbing task-irrelevant features.
> - Generating diverse semantic variations directly from perturbed latent feature vectors offers a novel approach to data augmentation that is challenging to achieve with conventional image augmentations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) is a specialized area within computer vision focused on distinguishing subtle differences between subcategories, such as specific bird species or vehicle models. This task presents challenges due to the high similarity between classes, requiring models to capture minute visual patterns that differentiate them.

Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) has emerged as a prominent paradigm for learning visual representations without requiring extensive human-provided annotations. Methods based on contrastive learning, which aim to make representations of augmented views of the same image similar while dissimilar to views of other images, have achieved significant advancements in general visual recognition tasks. These SSL-trained encoders have demonstrated transferability to a wide array of downstream applications.

However, the application of SSL to FGVR tasks presents specific limitations. Research indicates that many existing SSL methods tend to prioritize general visual similarities, leading to a "coarse-label bias" where they may overlook the subtle, critical features necessary for fine-grained discrimination. Furthermore, these methods have been observed to be susceptible to distractions from task-irrelevant features, thus failing to capture the specific patterns pertinent to FGVR.

Prior efforts to address these limitations within SSL for FGVR have explored several directions:
- **Refining Data Augmentations:** Some approaches leverage attention-guided heatmaps to locate and crop key objects more effectively (e.g., SAGA, CAST, ContrastiveCrop) or synthesize new views by merging objects with different backgrounds (e.g., DiLo).
- **Employing Auxiliary Networks:** Other works train additional network components (e.g., LEWEL) or align encoder attention maps with external cues like segmentation annotations, saliency detectors, or Grad-CAM outputs (e.g., CVSA, LCR, SAM).
- **Data Selection Strategies:** SimCore focuses on selecting more relevant data from large datasets for pre-training.

This current work positions itself within this landscape by introducing a novel strategy that aims to directly enhance SSL's capacity for discriminative feature extraction. Unlike methods that modify input images through augmentation or rely on auxiliary networks to guide attention, this approach generates synthetic data pairs from the latent feature space. These pairs are specifically designed to guide the encoder to focus on discriminative features by introducing controlled perturbations to non-discriminative aspects of the feature vector, thereby addressing the issue of task-irrelevant feature learning in FGVR.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an innovative self-supervised learning strategy that improves the extraction of discriminative features essential for fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) while mitigating the influence of less informative or noisy features.

The motivation for this objective stems from observed shortcomings of existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods when applied to FGVR tasks:
1. **"Coarse-Label Bias" in SSL:** While SSL has been effective in learning robust general visual representations, it often focuses on broad similarities between images. This can lead to a "coarse-label bias," where the model does not adequately capture the subtle, minute visual distinctions that define subcategories in FGVR. Consequently, the learned representations may not be sufficiently discriminative for fine-grained tasks.
2. **Distraction by Task-Irrelevant Features:** Prior studies have indicated that current SSL methods can be distracted by features that are irrelevant to the specific discriminative task in FGVR. These task-irrelevant features, such as background elements or incidental details, can interfere with the model's ability to concentrate on the crucial patterns that differentiate fine-grained classes.
3. **Need for Targeted Feature Learning:** To overcome these issues, there is a motivation to guide the SSL model to selectively extract highly discriminative features and explicitly disregard non-informative ones. This guidance should lead the encoder to build representations that are invariant to variations in non-discriminative aspects while being sensitive to discriminative ones.

To achieve this, the authors propose a core mechanism centered on generating synthetic data pairs. This approach is motivated by the idea that by constructing positive pairs where non-discriminative features are systematically perturbed, the encoder can be trained to become robust to these irrelevant variations. Two specific criteria are motivated for identifying non-discriminative features:
- **Grad-CAM for Saliency:** Grad-CAM is a technique used to visualize salient regions. By applying Grad-CAM with respect to the SSL loss, the authors aim to identify feature dimensions that are less crucial for the current self-supervised contrastive task. Perturbing these dimensions is motivated by the expectation that the model should learn to be less reliant on features contributing little to discrimination.
- **Dimension Variance for Task-Relevance:** Empirical observations, as shown in the paper, indicate that in SSL-pre-trained encoders, certain feature dimensions exhibit low variance across the dataset and do not effectively separate data from different categories. These low-variance dimensions are considered task-irrelevant noise. The motivation here is to intentionally induce dimensional collapse in these specific dimensions, drawing inspiration from recent literature suggesting that collapsing task-irrelevant dimensions can enhance downstream performance. This helps the encoder disregard features that inherently offer poor discriminative power.

By combining these motivations, the overall objective is to enable the encoder to learn representations that are specifically tailored for FGVR, exhibiting strong discriminative power by focusing on subtle, relevant features and actively ignoring irrelevant variations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a novel self-supervised learning strategy designed to learn discriminative features for fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) by leveraging synthesized data. This approach is integrated into existing SSL frameworks, such as MoCo v2 and SimSiam, and comprises an encoder, a projection head, and a decoder.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The core idea involves generating synthetic data pairs by perturbing latent feature vectors and then using a decoder to reconstruct images from these vectors. The encoder is then trained on these generated pairs to learn invariance to non-discriminative features. The total training loss $L$ consists of three components:
1. **Conventional Contrastive Loss ($L_C$):** This standard SSL loss is applied to augmented views of original images ($x'$ and $x''$). The encoder $f_{\theta_e}$ maps images to a latent feature space $V \subset \mathbb{R}^n$, and a projection head $g_{\theta_p}$ projects these features to a lower-dimensional space $Z \subset \mathbb{R}^k$ where $L_C$ is computed.
 $L_C(z', z'') = -\log \frac{\exp(z' \cdot z'' / \tau)}{\sum_{z_i \in B_a, z_i \neq z', z_i \neq z''} \exp(z' \cdot z_i / \tau)}$
 where $z' = g_{\theta_p}(f_{\theta_e}(x'))$ and $z'' = g_{\theta_p}(f_{\theta_e}(x''))$.
2. **Reconstruction Loss ($L_R$):** A decoder $h_{\theta_d}$ is employed to reconstruct images from the encoder's latent feature vectors. This loss ensures the decoder learns to generate high-quality images from the latent space and tracks the encoder's evolution. It is calculated as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between an original non-augmented image $x$ and its reconstruction $\hat{x} = h_{\theta_d}(f_{\theta_e}(x))$.
 $L_R = \frac{1}{m} \|x - \hat{x}\|_2^2$
3. **Proposed Contrastive Loss ($L_{Cp}$):** This loss is applied to the synthetically generated data pairs, guiding the encoder to learn discriminative features.
 $L_{Cp}(\hat{z}, \hat{z}_p) = -\log \frac{\exp(\hat{z} \cdot \hat{z}_p / \tau)}{\sum_{\hat{z}_i \in B_r, \hat{z}_i \neq \hat{z}, \hat{z}_i \neq \hat{z}_p} \exp(\hat{z} \cdot \hat{z}_i / \tau)}$
 where $\hat{z} = g_{\theta_p}(f_{\theta_e}(\hat{x}))$ and $\hat{z}_p = g_{\theta_p}(f_{\theta_e}(\hat{x}_p))$.

The total loss is: $L = L_C + \alpha \cdot L_R + \nu \cdot L_{Cp}$, where $\alpha$ and $\nu$ are hyperparameters weighting the reconstruction and proposed contrastive losses, respectively.

#### 3.2. Non-Discriminative Feature Identification

Two methods are proposed to identify non-discriminative dimensions in the latent feature vector $v = f_{\theta_e}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

1. **Grad-CAM-based Perturbation ($\tilde{v}_g$):**
 - **Saliency Scoring:** Grad-CAM scores are computed for each dimension of the feature vector $v$. Specifically, the gradient of the contrastive loss $L_C(g_{\theta_p}(v), g_{\theta_p}(f_{\theta_e}(x'')))$ with respect to each element $v_i$ of $v$ is calculated. These gradients are multiplied by $v_i$ and passed through a ReLU function to obtain saliency scores $\eta_i$. Higher $\eta_i$ indicates higher contribution to contrastive discrimination.
 $\eta_i = \text{ReLU}\left(\frac{\partial L_C(g_{\theta_p}(v), g_{\theta_p}(v''))}{\partial v_i} \cdot v_i\right)$
 - **Noise Application:** The scores $\eta_i$ are min-max normalized to $\bar{\eta}_i \in$. Gaussian noise $\tilde{v}_{g,i}$ is then introduced to each dimension $i$ with a standard deviation inversely proportional to $\bar{\eta}_i$. This means dimensions with lower Grad-CAM scores (less discriminative) receive greater perturbation.
 $\tilde{v}_g = \{\tilde{v}_{g,i} \sim N(0, \epsilon_g \cdot (1 - \bar{\eta}_i))\}_{i=1}^n$
 where $\epsilon_g$ controls the noise intensity.

2. **Dimension Variance-based Perturbation ($\tilde{v}_{var}$):**
 - **Variance Estimation:** A memory bank $M \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times n}$ stores feature vectors from previous batches. The variance $s_i$ for each dimension $i$ across the dataset is approximated by calculating the variance of the $\ell_2$-normalized $i$-th column of $M$.
 - **Task-Relevance Thresholding:** Dimensions where $s_i < \kappa$ (a threshold hyperparameter) are deemed less discriminative due to low variance, suggesting they do not effectively separate data.
 - **Noise Application:** The variance scores $s_i$ are min-max normalized to $\bar{s}_i$. Gaussian noise $\tilde{v}_{var,i}$ is introduced only to dimensions where $s_i < \kappa$, with standard deviation inversely proportional to $\bar{s}_i$. Dimensions with high variance (i.e., $s_i \ge \kappa$) receive no noise.
 $\tilde{v}_{var,i} = \begin{cases} u_i \sim N(0, \epsilon_{var} \cdot (1 - \bar{s}_i)) & \text{if } s_i < \kappa \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$
 where $\epsilon_{var}$ controls the noise intensity.

#### 3.3. Learning from Reconstructed Data Pairs

The two perturbation vectors, $\tilde{v}_g$ and $\tilde{v}_{var}$, are combined and added to the original feature vector $v$ to create a perturbed feature vector $v_p$:
$v_p = v + \tilde{v}_g + \tilde{v}_{var}$
The decoder $h_{\theta_d}$ reconstructs an image $\hat{x}$ from $v$ and a perturbed image $\hat{x}_p$ from $v_p$. The pair $(\hat{x}, \hat{x}_p)$ forms a positive pair for the proposed contrastive loss $L_{Cp}$. This process encourages the encoder to learn to extract features that are invariant to the specific non-discriminative perturbations introduced, thereby focusing on the robust, discriminative aspects of the data.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Backbone and Initialization:** ResNet-50 is used as the encoder backbone, initialized with weights pre-trained on ImageNet-1k.
- **Decoder Pre-training:** Before main training, the decoder is pre-trained with a reconstruction loss while the encoder is frozen, aiming to ensure it can generate high-quality images.
- **Hyperparameters:** $\alpha=1$, $\nu=0.5$ for loss weighting. $\epsilon_g=0.1$, $\epsilon_{var}=0.05$ for noise intensity, and $\kappa=0.02$ for the variance threshold.
- **Evaluation Protocols:** Linear evaluation (training a linear classifier on frozen encoder features) and image retrieval (nearest neighbor search in latent space) are used. Attention maps are also visualized using Grad-CAM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method across various fine-grained visual recognition datasets.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Baseline SSL Methods

- **Performance Improvement:** The proposed method, particularly when integrated with MoCo v2, consistently outperformed the baseline MoCo v2 and SimSiam across all evaluated datasets: CUB-200, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft, German Traffic Sign Recognition Benchmark (GTSRB), and ISIC2017 (medical images).
- **Linear Evaluation (Classification):**
 - On CUB-200, Stanford Cars, and FGVC-Aircraft, the method achieved average top-1 accuracy improvements of 3.31%, 4.27%, and 5.51% over MoCo v2 when evaluated with 100%, 50%, and 20% of labels, respectively. This improvement was more pronounced in label-insufficient scenarios, indicating the learning of more generalizable features.
 - On GTSRB and ISIC2017, the proposed method also showed improved top-1 classification accuracy (e.g., 91.67% vs 88.25% for MoCo v2 on GTSRB).
- **Image Retrieval:** The method consistently outperformed MoCo v2 in rank-1, rank-5, and mean Average Precision (mAP) metrics across all datasets. This suggests that the learned feature space exhibits better semantic consistency, with images from the same category clustered more closely.
- **Grad-CAM Visualization:** Visual comparisons of Grad-CAM attention maps indicated that the proposed method enabled the encoder to focus more precisely on pivotal objects within images and reduced the influence of background distractions, unlike baseline MoCo v2 which sometimes attended to irrelevant regions.
- **Integration with SimSiam:** When integrated into SimSiam, the proposed framework ("SimSiam+Ours") also significantly outperformed the original SimSiam in both linear evaluation and image retrieval tasks, confirming its general applicability across different SSL architectures.

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Self-Supervised FGVR Methods

- The proposed method achieved the best overall performance in both linear evaluation (top-1 accuracy) and image retrieval (rank-1) when compared against several existing state-of-the-art self-supervised FGVR techniques (e.g., DiLo, CVSA, LEWEL, ContrastiveCrop, SAM-SSL-Bilinear, LCR). For example, on CUB-200, "Ours" achieved 66.17% classification accuracy and 42.06% rank-1 retrieval, surpassing LCR (65.24% and 41.26%) and SAM-SSL-Bilinear (64.94% and 40.08%).

#### 4.3. Analysis of Generated Data Pairs

- The visualization of generated data pairs showed that perturbed images largely retained the key objects of the original data. Modifications primarily appeared as subtle changes in background or less important regions (e.g., tree branches, vehicle light textures, airplane exterior finish).
- Notably, some perturbations resulted in entirely new object views (e.g., transforming a side view of a car into a front view, or an aircraft on the ground into one in flight) while maintaining the original object's identity. This demonstrates the capacity of the method to generate diverse semantic variations that are difficult to achieve with conventional image augmentations, effectively guiding the encoder in identifying and prioritizing crucial features.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Reconstruction Loss ($L_R$):** Experiments showed that including the reconstruction loss provided a modest but consistent improvement (less than 1%) to self-supervised FGVC performance, especially with appropriate weighting ($\alpha=0.5$ or $1.0$). This validates its role in ensuring the decoder evolves with the encoder and aids feature learning.
- **Effectiveness of Feature Perturbation Techniques:**
 - Both the Grad-CAM-based perturbation ("Ours - Grad-CAM") and the dimension variance-based perturbation ("Ours - low-var") individually contributed to performance improvements over the MoCo v2 baseline.
 - Combining both techniques yielded the best overall performance, indicating their complementary nature in identifying and perturbing non-discriminative features.
 - A baseline experiment with random Gaussian noise applied to all feature dimensions ("Ours - Random") performed worse than targeted perturbation, highlighting the importance of the proposed identification criteria.
- **Impact of Perturbation Intensity:** The intensity of the introduced Gaussian noise, controlled by $\epsilon_g$ and $\epsilon_{var}$, was critical. Both too little and too much perturbation led to reduced performance. Minimal perturbation provided insufficient new information, while excessive perturbation risked distorting image identity and introducing noise. Optimal values for $\epsilon_g$ and $\epsilon_{var}$ were identified.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The choice of $\nu$ (weight for $L_{Cp}$) and $\kappa$ (threshold for low-variance dimensions) impacted performance, with specific optimal values identified through experimentation.

#### 4.5. Performance on Non-Fine-Grained Datasets

- Experiments on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 (non-fine-grained datasets) showed that the proposed method also provided a modest boost to SSL performance (e.g., 63.26% vs 61.79% on CIFAR-100 for MoCo v2 baseline). This indicates general applicability, although the gains were substantially more pronounced in FGVR contexts, confirming its specialized effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel approach to address a key limitation of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) in the context of Fine-grained Visual Recognition (FGVR). By explicitly guiding the model to focus on discriminative features and disregard non-critical ones, the work contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in self-supervised FGVR.

The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing the "Coarse-Label Bias" in SSL:** The study directly confronts the tendency of conventional SSL methods to prioritize general visual similarities, which can hinder their performance in FGVR tasks. By learning from synthetically generated data pairs that emphasize fine-grained distinctions, the proposed method enables SSL to overcome this limitation, leading to representations that are more suitable for distinguishing subtle differences between subcategories.
2. **Enhanced Discriminative Feature Learning:** The core innovation lies in the systematic identification and perturbation of non-discriminative features within the latent space. By combining Grad-CAM-based saliency and dimension variance-based relevance, the method provides a robust mechanism for guiding the encoder to selectively extract truly discriminative patterns. This ensures that the learned representations are not distracted by irrelevant noise, leading to higher accuracy in classification and retrieval tasks.
3. **Novel Data Generation Paradigm:** Unlike prior approaches that primarily rely on modifying input images through traditional augmentations or external attention mechanisms, this work introduces a method of generating realistic and semantically varied data pairs directly from the perturbed latent feature space. This capability allows for the creation of positive pairs where non-critical attributes are altered while identity-defining features are preserved or subtly transformed, which is challenging with conventional augmentation techniques. This paradigm could inspire new directions in data augmentation and representation learning.
4. **Improved Performance in Critical Domains:** The demonstrated superior performance across various FGVR datasets, including natural images (birds, cars, aircraft), traffic signs, and medical images, highlights the practical utility of the proposed method. FGVR is crucial in many real-world applications, such as biological species identification, autonomous vehicle navigation, quality control, and medical diagnostics. Enhancing SSL's capability in these areas without relying on extensive manual annotation can accelerate deployment and reduce development costs.
5. **Flexibility and Generalizability:** The method's ability to integrate with different existing SSL frameworks (demonstrated with MoCo v2 and SimSiam) indicates its modularity and potential for broad applicability. The observed, albeit more modest, performance improvements on general visual recognition datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100) further suggest its foundational principles may extend beyond fine-grained tasks.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The work identifies the strong gains in FGVR contexts compared to general datasets and suggests the refinement of the methodology for large-scale general datasets as an avenue for future research. This provides a clear direction for further exploration into how targeted feature learning from synthetic data can be optimized for broader applications.

In summary, this research presents a method that directly addresses the challenges of applying SSL to fine-grained tasks, offering a robust and adaptable solution that has the potential to impact various fields relying on precise visual recognition.
