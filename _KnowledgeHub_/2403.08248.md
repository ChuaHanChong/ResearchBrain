# CoPa: General Robotic Manipulation through Spatial Constraints of Parts with Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The CoPa framework employs foundation vision-language models to enable precise 6-DoF robotic manipulation in unstructured environments, generating fine-grained spatial constraints between object parts for tasks. It achieved a 63% success rate across ten diverse real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming baseline approaches.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation in unstructured environments faces challenges beyond controlled industrial settings.
- Existing methods often require extensive task-specific training or offer only coarse spatial understanding, limiting precise 6-DoF manipulation.
- Enabling robots to understand and interact with their environment at a fine-grained physical level remains difficult.

## Method

- **CoPa** decomposes robotic manipulation into ==Task-Oriented Grasping== and ==Task-Aware Motion Planning== phases.
- It leverages foundation ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== like GPT-4V to understand object properties and spatial relationships, generating ==fine-grained spatial constraints== between object parts.
- A ==visual prompting== technique (==Set-of-Marks==) grounds natural language instructions to specific objects and their parts for precise ==6-DoF manipulation==, solved as a ==constrained optimization== problem.

## Results

- CoPa achieved a **63%** success rate across **ten** diverse real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming the VoxPoser baseline.
- The framework demonstrated effective control of all six degrees of freedom for the robot's end-effector with minimal prompt engineering.
- It successfully executed long-horizon, multi-step tasks such as making pour-over coffee, when integrated with high-level planning methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging common-sense knowledge embedded in foundation Vision-Language Models can provide fine-grained physical understanding for robotic tasks.
> - Decomposing complex manipulation into task-oriented grasping and task-aware motion planning simplifies the problem.
> - Generating explicit spatial constraints between object parts from VLM outputs enables precise 6-DoF control and reduces the need for task-specific training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation in unstructured environments remains a significant challenge in the field of robotics. While robots excel in controlled industrial settings, they struggle with everyday tasks that humans find intuitive. Recent advances in foundation models—particularly large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs)—have shown promise in enabling robots to understand and interact with their environment more effectively.

The CoPa (Constraints of Parts) framework represents a novel approach to robotic manipulation that leverages foundation models to understand the physical world at a fine-grained level. Unlike previous methods that rely heavily on task-specific training or simplistic spatial understanding, CoPa enables robots to perform complex manipulation tasks by generating precise spatial constraints between object parts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

CoPa addresses the challenge of robotic manipulation by decomposing it into two distinct phases:

1. **Task-Oriented Grasping**: Determining the optimal way to grasp an object based on the intended manipulation task.
2. **Task-Aware Motion Planning**: Planning the sequence of movements needed to accomplish the task once the object is grasped.

This separation allows CoPa to handle complex manipulation tasks by focusing first on how to appropriately grasp an object, and then on how to move it to complete the task. The framework relies on the common-sense knowledge embedded within foundation vision-language models (VLMs) like GPT-4V to understand both the physical properties of objects and their spatial relationships.

What distinguishes CoPa from previous approaches is its ability to generate fine-grained spatial constraints between object parts, enabling precise 6-DoF (degrees of freedom) manipulation without requiring additional task-specific training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The first phase of CoPa is task-oriented grasping, which determines the most appropriate way to grasp an object based on the intended task. This process involves three key steps:

1. **Grasp Pose Proposals**: CoPa uses a pre-trained grasping model (GraspNet) to generate a set of 6-DoF grasp pose candidates from RGB-D images of the scene.

2. **Grasping Part Grounding**: The system employs a coarse-to-fine grounding mechanism to identify the appropriate part of the object to grasp.

3. **Pose Filtering**: Finally, CoPa filters the grasp pose candidates based on the identified grasping part and the graspness scores from GraspNet to select the optimal grasp pose.

A key innovation in this process is the use of a visual prompting technique called Set-of-Marks (SoM) in conjunction with GPT-4V. This combination allows the system to ground natural language instructions to specific objects and parts in the scene. For example, when given the instruction "Hammer the nail," the system can identify both the hammer (coarse-grained object grounding) and its handle (fine-grained part grounding) as the appropriate grasping location.

#### 3.1. Task-Aware Motion Planning

After successfully grasping an object, CoPa transitions to the task-aware motion planning phase, which determines how to move the grasped object to accomplish the task. This phase consists of three primary components:

1. **Task-Relevant Part Grounding**: Similar to the grasping phase, CoPa identifies task-relevant parts within the scene (e.g., the head of the hammer and the nail when hammering).

2. **Manipulation Constraints Generation**: The system leverages VLMs to generate spatial geometry constraints that task-relevant parts must meet to accomplish the task. These constraints are expressed as relationships between geometric elements (points, vectors, surfaces) that represent the object parts.

3. **Target Pose Planning**: CoPa employs a constraint solver to determine the robot's poses based on the generated spatial constraints. This is formulated as a constrained optimization problem, and traditional motion planning algorithms ensure smooth transitions between adjacent poses.

For example, in the "Hammer the nail" task, the system might generate constraints such as "Vector 2 and Vector 3 are colinear" (aligning the hammer head with the nail) and "Vector 2 points downward" (orienting the hammer for striking). These spatial constraints are then used to determine a sequence of robot poses that satisfy the constraints.

The use of spatial constraints allows CoPa to handle a wide range of manipulation tasks without requiring task-specific training. Instead, it relies on the common-sense knowledge embedded in foundation models to understand the spatial relationships between object parts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CoPa demonstrates impressive performance across a diverse set of manipulation tasks. In experiments conducted on ten different real-world manipulation tasks, CoPa achieved a success rate of 63%, significantly outperforming the VoxPoser baseline and various ablation variants.

The tasks ranged from simple actions like "Find scissors" and "Press button" to more complex manipulations such as "Pour water," "Put spoon into cup," and "Sweep nuts." CoPa's ability to handle this diverse set of tasks demonstrates its generalizability and adaptability.

Key performance characteristics include:

1. **Fine-Grained Physical Understanding**: CoPa exhibits a detailed understanding of the physical properties of objects and their parts, enabling precise manipulation.

2. **Minimal Prompt Engineering**: Unlike many foundation model-based approaches, CoPa requires minimal prompt engineering, needing only a few examples to guide the VLMs.

3. **Effective Handling of Rotation DoF**: CoPa can effectively control all six degrees of freedom of the robot's end-effector, which is essential for many manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Existing Approaches

To evaluate CoPa's effectiveness, the researchers compared it with VoxPoser, another foundation model-based approach to robotic manipulation. The comparison revealed several key advantages of CoPa:

1. **Precision and Accuracy**: CoPa demonstrated higher precision and accuracy in tasks requiring fine manipulation, such as inserting a spoon into a cup or aligning a hammer with a nail.

2. **Rotational Control**: While VoxPoser struggled with controlling rotational degrees of freedom, CoPa handled these effectively, enabling more complex manipulations.

3. **Adaptability**: CoPa showed greater adaptability to different task requirements, adjusting its approach based on the specific constraints of each task.

The researchers also conducted an ablation study to validate the importance of various components of the CoPa framework. This study confirmed the significance of foundation models, the coarse-to-fine grounding mechanism, and constraint generation in achieving high performance.

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Task Execution

One of the most impressive demonstrations of CoPa's capabilities is its ability to execute long-horizon tasks when integrated with high-level planning methods. The researchers showed that CoPa could be seamlessly combined with a high-level planner to accomplish complex tasks like making pour-over coffee and setting up a romantic table.

These tasks required the robot to perform a sequence of manipulations, each with its own spatial constraints. For example, the pour-over coffee task involved scooping beans into a container, putting a funnel onto a carafe, pouring powder into the funnel, and pouring water into the funnel—all of which CoPa executed successfully.

This capability represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, as it enables robots to perform complex, multi-step tasks that more closely resemble the kinds of activities humans perform in everyday life.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Despite its impressive performance, CoPa has several limitations that present opportunities for future research:

1. **Perception Challenges**: CoPa relies on accurate perception of the environment, which can be challenging in cluttered or poorly lit environments. Improving the system's ability to handle perceptual uncertainties would enhance its robustness.

2. **Dynamic Environments**: The current implementation of CoPa is primarily designed for static environments. Extending the framework to handle dynamic environments, where objects may move or change during task execution, is an important direction for future work.

3. **Generalization to New Tasks**: While CoPa demonstrates good generalization across the tested tasks, further research is needed to evaluate its performance on a broader range of manipulation tasks and to develop methods for improving generalization to entirely new tasks.

4. **Real-Time Performance**: The computational requirements of foundation models can be substantial, potentially limiting the real-time performance of the system. Optimizing the framework for faster execution would enhance its practicality for real-world applications.

#### 5.1. Conclusion

CoPa represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, offering a framework that leverages foundation models to enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks with high precision and adaptability. By decomposing manipulation into task-oriented grasping and task-aware motion planning, and by generating fine-grained spatial constraints between object parts, CoPa provides a robust approach to general-purpose robotic manipulation.

The framework's ability to handle a diverse range of tasks with minimal prompt engineering, its effective control of all six degrees of freedom, and its seamless integration with high-level planning for long-horizon tasks all contribute to its effectiveness. These capabilities bring us closer to the goal of general-purpose robots capable of performing a wide range of tasks in diverse environments.

As foundation models continue to advance and as researchers address the limitations of the current approach, we can expect even more capable robotic manipulation systems that can assist humans in an increasingly wide range of tasks, from household chores to industrial applications and beyond.
