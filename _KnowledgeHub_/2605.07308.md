# AT-VLA: Adaptive Tactile Injection for Enhanced Feedback Reaction in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The AT-VLA framework enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling robots to perform contact-rich manipulation tasks with greater precision by adaptively injecting tactile feedback and ensuring real-time responsiveness. This approach yielded superior manipulation performance and maintained robustness even when tactile input was absent during inference.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with contact-rich manipulation tasks due to the lack of tactile feedback in large-scale pretraining datasets.
- Directly incorporating new tactile modalities during finetuning can disrupt pretrained VLA capabilities like visual perception and object grounding.
- The inherent latency of large VLA models limits their ability to react swiftly and in real-time to high-frequency tactile feedback, which is crucial for closed-loop control.

## Method

- An ==Adaptive Tactile Injection== mechanism dynamically controls the timing and location of tactile feedback, using a tactile gating network to activate tactile integration only during contact phases, thus preserving pretrained VLA visual-language capabilities.
- A ==Tactile Reaction Dual-Stream== mechanism decouples sensory processing into a slow stream for visual/language input and a fast stream for high-frequency tactile feedback, enabling rapid action adjustments during contact.
- The framework extends a pretrained VLA backbone (GO-1) with a lightweight tactile encoder and integrates these mechanisms within the Action Expert's cross-attention module.

## Results

- AT-VLA achieved superior manipulation performance in contact-rich tasks (e.g., Unzip Bag, Stamp, Wipe Vase), outperforming state-of-the-art VLA baselines and other tactile-based policies.
- The framework maintained comparable performance to vanilla VLAs in non-contact-rich tasks and exhibited robustness, performing reliably even when tactile input was unavailable during inference.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Adaptive Tactile Injection provided a **17%** improvement by preserving pretrained knowledge, and the Tactile Reaction Dual-Stream added an additional **11%** improvement by enabling rapid responses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically gating tactile information is critical to prevent interference with a VLA model's pretrained visual-language understanding, especially during non-contact phases of manipulation.
> - Decoupling the processing frequency of visual/language inputs from tactile feedback allows for rapid, real-time responses essential for dynamic and precise contact-rich tasks.
> - An adaptive integration strategy allows VLA models to leverage the benefits of tactile input for fine-grained control while maintaining robust performance in general tasks and even when tactile sensing is unavailable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant advancement in the development of generalist robotic agents. These models, leveraging large-scale manipulation datasets and foundation models, enable robots to interpret linguistic instructions within visual contexts and execute diverse tasks. However, a prevalent challenge for current VLA models lies in contact-rich manipulation scenarios. These scenarios often demand precise physical interactions and real-time adjustments that require rich interaction feedback, such as tactile signals, which are typically absent in the large-scale pretraining datasets used for VLAs.

Previous research has attempted to integrate tactile information during the finetuning of pretrained VLAs, often employing multimodal alignment strategies or chain-of-thought reasoning to enable interpretation of these novel sensory inputs. While these approaches aim to enhance a model's understanding of tactile feedback, they introduce several limitations. Directly incorporating new modalities, which are fundamentally different from the visual and linguistic data prevalent during pretraining, can disrupt the model's existing pretrained capabilities, such as visual perception or object grounding. Furthermore, the inherent latency of VLA models hampers their ability to react swiftly to high-frequency tactile feedback, diminishing the effectiveness of tactile input in closed-loop manipulation tasks where timely adjustments are crucial for precision and safety.

The AT-VLA framework addresses these limitations by introducing mechanisms designed to balance the preservation of pretrained VLA capabilities with the effective integration of tactile feedback. It seeks to provide rapid and accurate reactions in contact-rich tasks, positioning itself as an advancement in multimodal robotic control that aims to overcome the trade-offs observed in prior tactile integration approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in contact-rich manipulation tasks by effectively incorporating tactile feedback. This overarching goal is driven by two specific challenges inherent in current VLA frameworks when applied to such scenarios:

1. **Minimizing Interference with Pretrained VLA Capabilities:** Current VLA models are pretrained on extensive datasets that typically lack tactile information. When tactile signals are introduced during downstream finetuning, the model's existing, robust visual perception and object grounding capabilities, acquired during pretraining, can be disrupted. This disruption can lead to performance degradation, particularly in initial manipulation phases like grasping, where visual context is paramount.
 - **Motivation:** The paper presents an empirical observation where directly incorporating tactile feedback into a vanilla VLA model not only failed to improve performance but also led to noticeable inaccuracies in grasp localization, with attention shifting away from target objects. This highlighted the need for a mechanism to adaptively manage the introduction of tactile information, ensuring it complements rather than compromises existing pretrained knowledge. The complementary nature of vision (for contextual localization) and tactile sensing (for precise contact feedback) further motivated the idea of leveraging each modality when it is most relevant, such as preserving VLA behavior during non-contact phases and integrating tactile information only upon contact.

2. **Enabling Real-time Responsiveness to High-Frequency Tactile Feedback:** Contact-rich manipulation tasks often require immediate and precise adjustments based on sensory input to ensure safety and successful task completion. The inherently slow inference speed of large VLA models limits their ability to process and react to high-frequency tactile feedback in real-time. This latency reduces the efficacy of tactile signals for closed-loop control, making it difficult for robots to execute dynamic and intricate physical interactions.
 - **Motivation:** Tasks like unzipping a bag or wiping a curved surface necessitate continuous, swift adjustments to avoid sticking, jamming, or collisions. Traditional VLA architectures, processing all modalities at a uniform, slower frequency, cannot provide the rapid action updates required for these dynamic interactions. This limitation motivated the development of a mechanism that could decouple sensory processing, allowing tactile feedback to influence actions at a much higher frequency than visual-language reasoning, thereby enabling prompt and accurate action adjustments.

To address these challenges, the research sets forth two specific objectives:
- To develop an **Adaptive Tactile Injection** mechanism that dynamically determines when and where to incorporate tactile signals, thereby balancing the preservation of pretrained knowledge with the integration of new tactile representations.
- To design a **Tactile Reaction Dual-Stream** mechanism that ensures rapid and accurate action adjustments based on high-frequency tactile feedback, enabling real-time closed-loop control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AT-VLA framework is designed to be modular and is instantiated using a pretrained GO-1 model as its vanilla VLA backbone. GO-1, trained on the AgiBot world dataset, utilizes Intern-VL-2B as its base Vision-Language Model (VLM) and a DiT module as its action expert. The AT-VLA extends this architecture by adding a lightweight tactile encoder, composed of several MLP layers, to process tactile signals efficiently. The policy takes image observations (from head and wrist cameras), language instructions, tactile feedback (resultant force with 3D normal and tangential components), and robot proprioceptive state as input, generating a 14-DoF end-effector pose action chunk.

The core of the AT-VLA methodology is built upon two novel mechanisms:

#### 3.1. Adaptive Tactile Injection

This mechanism aims to dynamically control the timing and location of tactile feedback injection, thereby balancing the preservation of pretrained visual-language knowledge with the learning of new tactile representations.

- **Tactile Gating:** A learnable Tactile Gating Network, comprising MLP layers, is introduced to determine when tactile feedback should be incorporated. It processes tactile tokens (z_T) from the tactile encoder to classify whether the robot is currently in contact with an object. This network outputs a score, and if it exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., 0.5), the tactile gate is activated. The gate is supervised using a binary cross-entropy gating loss (L_g), with training data manually annotated for contact/non-contact frames. This gating mechanism serves to both discriminate contact stages and encourage the model to interpret tactile signals effectively.

- **Adaptive Cross Attention:** To accommodate the varying states of the tactile gate, an Adaptive Attention mechanism is integrated into the Action Expert's cross-attention module. In the vanilla VLA, image (z_I) and text (z_L) tokens serve as key and value, while the state token (z_S) is the query. The Adaptive Cross Attention modifies this behavior conditionally:
 - **Inactive Gate (Non-Contact):** The state token (z_S) is used as the query, maintaining consistency with the vanilla VLA and preserving its pretrained representations, particularly for visual perception and localization.
 - **Active Gate (Contact):** The tactile token (z_T) replaces the state token as the query. This allows the model to condition action generation on tactile feedback during contact without altering the fundamental model structure or feature dimensionality, thereby minimizing the impact on the model's robust visual-based action generation capabilities.

#### 3.2. Tactile Reaction Dual-Stream

This mechanism is designed to ensure real-time responsiveness to high-frequency tactile inputs, enabling rapid and accurate action adjustments during contact. It decouples sensory processing into two streams operating at different frequencies:

- **Slow Stream:** Visual and language inputs are processed through the large Vision-Language Model (VLM) at a lower inference frequency. This stream is responsible for high-level perceptual reasoning, task understanding, and visual perception. Its output provides comprehensive latent features that serve as the key and value for the Action Expert's cross-attention module, guiding action generation over a future action chunk horizon (H steps).

- **Fast Stream:** Tactile feedback is continuously fed into the Action Expert at a high inference frequency. This stream is dedicated to understanding high-frequency physical interactions and enabling closed-loop real-time responses. When the tactile gate is active, the tactile token from this stream serves as the cross-attention query.

- **Asynchronous Processing:** The Action Expert integrates features from both streams. At each time step, it leverages the most recent tactile feedback from the fast stream (t_n+k, where 0 < k < H) while conditioning on the periodically updated output from the slow stream (at time t_n). During training, the frequency ratio between the fast and slow streams is randomly set to h:1 (where 1 < h < H). In inference, a fixed ratio of 3:1 is adopted, meaning the fast stream infers three times for every single inference of the slow stream, enabling a closed-loop control with an inference speed of 0.04s.

#### 3.3. Training Objectives and Inference Pipeline

The AT-VLA is trained end-to-end with a combined loss function: L = L_a + λ_1 \* L_g, where L_a is the action loss and L_g is the gating loss (with λ_1 set to 0.01 for loss balancing).

During inference, if the tactile gate is inactive, the model operates identically to the vanilla VLA, with synchronous fast and slow streams and the state token as the attention query. If the tactile gate is active, the asynchronous frequency (3:1 ratio) is initiated, and tactile tokens become the attention queries in the Action Expert.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on an AgiBot Genie1 hardware platform, featuring dual 7-DoF arms and wrist-mounted cameras, and a gripper equipped with Xense Robotics tactile sensors. The model was evaluated on four contact-rich tasks (Unzip Bag, Stamp, Wipe Vase, Unscrew Lid) and two non-contact-rich tasks (Pick and Place, Open Drawer), with 30-50 demonstrations and 15 trials per task.

#### 4.1. Contact-rich Task Evaluation

AT-VLA demonstrated superior manipulation performance across the evaluated contact-rich tasks compared to baseline methods.

- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art VLAs (GO-1, π0.5):** AT-VLA exhibited comparable performance during pre-contact manipulation phases, indicating effective preservation of pretrained knowledge such as visual grounding and semantic reasoning required for object grasping. In the contact-rich stages, AT-VLA achieved significant improvements over GO-1 and π0.5. These baselines frequently encountered failures, such as the zipper getting stuck or excessive downward stamping, highlighting the necessity of tactile signals for complex contact-rich tasks.

- **Comparison with Tactile-Based Policies (VTLA, RDP):** AT-VLA generally outperformed VTLA and RDP in the contact-rich phases. An exception was the "Unscrew Lid" task, where AT-VLA performed marginally worse. This was attributed to the experimental setup for baselines, which involved manually placing the robot in an ideal grasping pose to ensure stable rotation, whereas AT-VLA, despite being able to grasp, did not always guarantee a sufficiently firm grip, leading to occasional slipping during unscrewing. The overall superior performance of AT-VLA in these comparisons validated its ability to adjust actions promptly and accurately based on tactile feedback.

#### 4.2. Modality-agnostic Evaluation

The study assessed AT-VLA's robustness to missing tactile signals during inference.

- **Performance without Tactile Input:** When compared to GO-1 and π0.5 (which do not use tactile input), AT-VLA inferred without tactile feedback ("AT-VLA w/o.") achieved comparable performance in non-contact-rich tasks like Pick and Place and Open Drawer. This indicates that the Adaptive Tactile Injection mechanism enables the model to generate reliable actions even in the absence of tactile feedback, demonstrating robustness to missing sensory modalities.
- **Implicit Learning in Contact-Rich Tasks:** In the "Stamp" task, a representative contact-rich example, AT-VLA (w/o tactile) showed a slight performance improvement over its vanilla counterpart, GO-1. This was attributed to the model's training with tactile input, implicitly learning richer contact dynamics and cross-modal correlations, which allowed it to infer approximate tactile cues from visual features during inference.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study investigated the contribution of each proposed component and the impact of different tactile formats.

- **Contribution of Adaptive Tactile Injection:**
 - **Direct Tactile Incorporation (Ex1 vs Ex0 - Vanilla VLA):** Directly incorporating tactile input by conditioning the Action Expert's queries on tactile features throughout manipulation resulted in a 9% decrease in success rate compared to the vanilla VLA. Failures primarily occurred during object grasping, confirming that unmanaged tactile injection can disrupt pretrained visual grounding.
 - **With Tactile Gate (Ex2 vs Ex1):** Adding the Tactile Gate and Adaptive Cross Attention (but without dual-stream) yielded a 17% improvement over the vanilla VLA. This demonstrated the gate's effectiveness in preserving pretrained knowledge by only introducing tactile feedback when relevant.
- **Contribution of Tactile Reaction Dual-Stream:**
 - **With Reaction Dual-Stream (Ex3 - Ours vs Ex2):** Integrating the Tactile Reaction Dual-Stream, which enabled different processing frequencies for visual and tactile modalities, resulted in an additional 11% improvement. This highlighted the necessity of rapid, high-frequency tactile responses for dynamic tasks.
- **Different Tactile Formats:**
 - **Direct Incorporation with Various Formats (Ex1, Ex4, Ex6 vs Ex0):** Directly introducing tactile information in any format (6D force, 2D marker, visual-tactile images) consistently led to significant performance degradation when not managed adaptively.
 - **Robustness of Framework across Formats (Ex5, Ex7 vs Ex4, Ex6):** Applying the proposed framework substantially improved performance across different tactile formats (e.g., Ex5 outperformed Ex4 by 27%, Ex7 surpassed Ex6 by 38%).
 - **Optimal Tactile Format:** 6D force achieved the best performance, followed by 2D marker, and then visual-tactile images. The paper hypothesized that higher-dimensional tactile inputs might excessively perturb the pretrained representation space if not carefully managed, underscoring the importance of balancing new modality influence with pretrained model integrity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AT-VLA framework represents a significant development in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring precise physical interaction. Its contributions have several implications for the future of robotic systems:

- **Enhanced Robotic Capabilities in Contact-Rich Tasks:** By effectively integrating tactile sensing, AT-VLA enables robots to perform complex contact-rich manipulation tasks with greater precision and reliability. This addresses a critical limitation of previous VLA models, which often struggled in scenarios demanding intricate physical interactions. The ability to unzipper a bag, stamp, wipe a curved surface, or unscrew a lid with adaptive feedback marks an advancement in robotic dexterity.

- **Improved Modality Integration Strategies:** The Adaptive Tactile Injection mechanism provides a robust solution to the challenge of integrating new sensory modalities into large, pretrained foundation models without compromising their existing capabilities. This method of dynamically determining *when* and *where* to inject tactile information can serve as a template for incorporating other specialized sensors into generalist robot models, preserving broad task performance while enhancing specific interaction skills.

- **Advancements in Real-time Closed-Loop Control:** The Tactile Reaction Dual-Stream mechanism offers a novel approach to addressing the latency issues of large VLA models. By decoupling slow perceptual reasoning from fast physical control, it enables real-time, high-frequency responses to dynamic sensory feedback. This architectural innovation, achieving closed-loop reactions within 0.04 seconds, is crucial for tasks requiring immediate adjustments and contributes to safer and more agile human-robot and robot-environment interactions.

- **Increased Robustness and Generalizability:** AT-VLA's demonstrated modality-agnostic robustness is a practical advantage for real-world deployment. The ability to maintain strong performance even when tactile sensors are unavailable or unstable enhances the reliability of robotic systems in diverse and unpredictable environments. Furthermore, the implicit learning of contact dynamics from tactile training data, which can inform actions even without explicit tactile input at inference, suggests a deeper and more transferable understanding of physical interactions.

- **Foundation for Future Multimodal Robotics:** This research establishes a framework for balancing high-level, language-grounded reasoning with low-level, high-frequency sensorimotor control. Such an approach could inspire further development in multimodal perception, action generation, and the design of hybrid control architectures for complex robotic systems, moving towards more intelligent, adaptable, and generalizable robots capable of operating in unstructured and dynamic environments.
