# Adaptive Surrogate Gradients for Sequential Reinforcement Learning in Spiking Neural Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Delft University of Technology and Harvard University developed adaptive surrogate gradient methods and a novel reinforcement learning algorithm (TD3BC+JSRL) to enable effective training of Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) for sequential control tasks. This approach successfully trained an SNN quadrotor controller in simulation, achieving robust real-world flight with estimated low energy consumption.

## Problem

- The optimization properties of surrogate gradients in deep Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) are not well understood, affecting learning stability and efficiency.
- Training stateful SNNs for sequential reinforcement learning (RL) is challenging due to short, unstable trajectories during early training, which prevents the network from properly 'warming up' and leveraging its temporal dynamics.
- Existing workarounds such as frame-stacking to inject temporal context are inefficient for SNNs, which are already inherently temporal, wasting their intrinsic capacity for sequential processing.

## Method

- Proposed adaptive scheduling for surrogate gradient slopes to balance gradient magnitude and alignment, improving training stability and efficiency.
- Introduced an online ==reinforcement learning== algorithm, TD3BC+JSRL, which uses a pre-trained guiding ANN actor to 'jump-start' SNN learning and ensure sufficiently long experience sequences for warm-up.
- Integrated a decaying Behavioral Cloning (BC) term into the objective function to stabilize early SNN training and explicitly handled SNN warm-up periods by zeroing out Q-value terms.

## Results

- Adaptive surrogate gradient slope scheduling improved SNN-RL training efficiency by a factor of **4.5** compared to a fixed steep slope.
- The TD3BC+JSRL method achieved an average return of 400 points, outperforming baseline methods by over 600 points on a quadrotor position control task with a reward curriculum.
- The sim-trained SNN controller successfully transferred to a real Crazyflie quadrotor, executing complex maneuvers with an estimated energy consumption of **9.7** × 10^-5 mJ per inference, comparable to a performance-matched ANN.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shallower surrogate gradient slopes can improve online RL performance in SNNs by enhancing exploration, despite introducing more directional noise compared to steeper slopes.
> - Both a jump-start mechanism (guiding policy) and a behavioral cloning term are essential for efficient and successful SNN-RL training in environments prone to short, unstable rollouts.
> - Spiking Neural Networks intrinsically capture temporal dependencies, allowing successful control of a quadrotor without explicit action history augmentation, a capability not matched by a similarly unaugmented ANN.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) represent a class of bio-inspired algorithms poised to leverage neuromorphic computing systems for energy-efficient, temporal processing, particularly in robotics and other energy-constrained applications. Despite their potential, training SNNs for complex control tasks, especially with reinforcement learning (RL), presents significant challenges.

The existing research landscape for SNN-based reinforcement learning has explored various approaches. Early efforts utilized biologically plausible learning rules like Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP) for tasks such as source seeking and maze navigation. While demonstrating SNN potential, these methods often lack the scalability and efficiency of modern deep RL. To bridge this gap, surrogate gradients have been employed, allowing end-to-end training of SNNs with standard RL algorithms. Algorithms like Deep Spiking Q-Network (DSQN) adapt DQN to the spiking domain, showing energy savings but often processing single transitions and resetting network states, thereby losing temporal context. This limitation restricts their application to discrete action spaces and prevents them from fully exploiting SNNs' inherent temporal dynamics. Recurrent value-based methods like R2D2, while designed for sequential data, assume agents can gather long experience sequences, which is often not feasible in high-risk robotic environments where early policy failures lead to truncated episodes.

Policy-based approaches, such as SNN-PPO, have been applied to continuous control tasks using entire trajectory sequences, but they are typically on-policy, leading to high sample costs and offering no explicit solution for the warm-up period issue. Off-policy methods like PopSAN, while leveraging asymmetric actor-critic setups for stability and energy efficiency, also often reset hidden states between transitions, limiting temporal dependency capture.

This work addresses two critical, inter-related challenges that restrict SNNs from fully realizing their potential in sequential RL: (1) the uncharacterized optimization properties of surrogate gradients in deep SNNs, and (2) the difficulty of training stateful SNNs on sequences in environments where poor initial policies prevent the collection of sufficiently long trajectories for network warm-up. By systematically analyzing surrogate gradients and proposing a novel training approach, this research aims to advance both the theoretical understanding and practical deployment of SNNs for robust, energy-efficient sequential control in real-world robotic systems, going beyond the limitations of prior work in handling temporal dependencies and early training instability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the effective and robust training of Spiking Neural Networks for complex, sequential control tasks, particularly in the context of energy-constrained robotics. This overarching goal is motivated by two critical challenges inherent to SNNs in reinforcement learning:

1. **Challenge of Surrogate Gradients in Deep SNNs:**
 - **Motivation:** Spiking neurons are non-differentiable, making gradient-based optimization challenging. Surrogate gradients are a common solution, but their optimization properties, particularly in deep or stacked SNN architectures, are not well understood. Previous studies indicate that the choice of surrogate gradient can significantly alter the estimated gradient from its true value. The research is motivated by the need to understand how these deviations affect learning in deep networks and to identify optimal or adaptive strategies for surrogate gradient settings.
 - **Objective:** To systematically analyze the effect of surrogate gradient slope settings on the gradient magnitude and its alignment with a more precise gradient estimate across different layers of deep SNNs. Furthermore, the work aims to investigate how these settings influence learning performance across various regimes, from supervised learning (Behavioral Cloning) to online reinforcement learning (TD3), and to propose adaptive slope scheduling methods that balance exploration and exploitation.

2. **Challenge of Sequential Training and Warm-up Periods in RL:**
 - **Motivation:** SNNs inherently process temporal information and maintain internal states, requiring training on sequences rather than isolated transitions. A crucial aspect of training recurrent or stateful networks is a "warm-up period," during which hidden states stabilize before gradient updates are applied. In real-world robotic control tasks (e.g., drone flight), sub-optimal initial policies frequently lead to early episode termination (e.g., crashes). These short, incomplete sequences prevent the network from accumulating enough data to bridge its warm-up period, thereby hindering learning and improvement. Existing work-arounds like frame-stacking are inefficient for SNNs that are naturally temporal.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a novel reinforcement learning algorithm specifically designed for continuous control with SNNs that explicitly leverages their inherent temporal dynamics without relying on inefficient methods like frame stacking. This approach is motivated to overcome the challenge of short sequences by bootstrapping the learning process, enabling the SNN to gather sufficient experience to bridge its warm-up period and facilitate stable learning in high-risk, sequential environments.

As a case study, the research aims to demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed approach by training a low-level spiking neural controller for a Crazyflie quadrotor. This includes training the controller entirely in simulation and successfully transferring it to the real-world platform, bridging the reality gap without the need for observation or action history augmentation, which is a practical validation of the theoretical advancements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the identified challenges through a multi-faceted approach encompassing a systematic analysis of surrogate gradients and the development of a novel reinforcement learning algorithm.

#### 3.1. Surrogate Gradient Analysis and Adaptive Scheduling

The authors investigate the impact of surrogate gradient slope settings on learning in deep SNNs. They adopt the gradient of a fast sigmoid function (`d/dx fs(k*x) * 1/k = 1/(1+k*|x|)^2`) for the backward pass, where `k` represents the slope.
- **Slope Analysis:** They analyze slope configurations ranging from shallow (k=1) to steep (k=100) on a 4-layer SNN.
 - Shallower slopes (`k=1`) provide non-zero gradients over a broader input range compared to steeper slopes (`k=100`).
 - This leads to increased average gradient magnitude, particularly in deeper layers, which helps mitigate vanishing gradients (Figure 1b).
 - However, shallower slopes also reduce the cosine similarity between the surrogate gradient and a more precise gradient estimate (approximated by k=100), indicating increased directional noise in deeper layers (Figure 1c).
- **Adaptive Slope Scheduling:** Based on the observed trade-off between gradient magnitude and alignment, the authors propose adaptive scheduling for the surrogate gradient slope:
 - **Fixed:** The slope `k` remains constant throughout training.
 - **Interval:** The slope `k` is made steeper according to a predefined time schedule.
 - **Adaptive:** The slope `k` is dynamically adjusted based on the achieved reward (`k_t = 1/10 * sum(0.5*r_{t-i} + 0.5*r'_{t-i})`, clamped between 1 and 100). This aims to balance exploration (noisy, shallow gradients when performance is low) and exploitation (sharper, precise gradients when performance stabilizes).

#### 3.2. Online TD3BC with Jump-Starting Privileged Actor (TD3BC+JSRL)

To address the challenge of sequential SNN training in environments with short, unstable rollouts, the authors adapt the Jump-Start Reinforcement Learning (JSRL) framework, combining it with TD3BC.
- **Guiding Policy:** A privileged, non-spiking ANN actor, initially pre-trained for a few epochs using TD3, is employed. This policy receives privileged information (e.g., action histories) and is designed to ensure the agent can perform stable actions for at least the SNN's warm-up period, thus "jump-starting" the learning process.
- **Hybrid Experience Collection:** A replay buffer is continuously populated with experience generated by both:
 - The **guiding actor:** During the initial segment of rollouts (`500 - N` timesteps), ensuring long enough sequences are collected. The duration `N` linearly increases over training, gradually reducing the guide's influence.
 - The **spiking policy:** During the subsequent `N` timesteps, facilitating online interaction and learning.
- **Behavioral Cloning (BC) Integration:** The actor's objective function incorporates a Behavioral Cloning (BC) term, similar to TD3BC:
 `J_pi = E_tau~D [ sum_i=0^n_seq-1 ( 1_{i>=n_warm-up} * Q_phi1(s_tau,i, pi_theta(s_tau,i|h_tau,i)) - lambda * ||pi_theta(s_tau,i|h_tau,i) - a_tau,i||^2 ) ]`
 - The BC weight `λ` starts at 0.2 and decays exponentially (`λ ← 0.99λ`) after each epoch, shifting the training emphasis from imitation to reward-based optimization. This BC term helps stabilize training and leverages the collected demonstration data.
- **SNN Warm-up Handling:** A warm-up period of `n_warm-up = 50` steps is implemented. During this period, the Q-value term in the objective is zeroed out (`1_{i>=n_warm-up}`). Hidden states are zero-initialized. The critic network, being an ANN, does not require a warm-up period.

#### 3.3. Spiking Neural Network Architecture

The SNN controller is a feedforward network with:
- **Input Layer:** 18 neurons (for state vector).
- **Hidden Layers:** Two layers of 256 and 128 Leaky Integrate-and-Fire neurons with a soft-reset mechanism.
- **Output Layer:** 4 neurons (for motor commands).
- **Encoding/Decoding:** Population-based encoding translates continuous input values into currents for the first layer, and the final layer decodes output spikes into continuous motor commands.

#### 3.4. Asymmetric Actor-Critic

The system employs an asymmetric actor-critic setup:
- The **actor** is the spiking neural network.
- The **critic** is a conventional feedforward ANN that receives observed states and an action history of 32 timesteps. This ANN critic is used to stabilize training but is not deployed on the drone. The jump-starting actor is also an ANN with privileged inputs.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup: Quadrotor Position Control

The approach is validated on a low-level quadrotor position control task using a simulated Crazyflie 2.1 quadrotor, with subsequent sim-to-real transfer.
- **State Space:** 18-dimensional vector (position, velocity, orientation, angular velocity).
- **Action Space:** 4 motor commands at 100Hz.
- **Episode Termination:** After 500 timesteps or a crash.
- **Reward Structure:** A dense reward function `r_t = C_rs - C_rp ||p_t - p_des||^2 - C_rv ||v_t - v_des||^2 - C_rq ||q_t - q_des||^2 - C_ra ||a_t - C_rab||^2` (Equation 8) is used.
- **Curriculum Learning:** Penalty coefficients for position (`C_rp`), velocity (`C_rv`), and action magnitude (`C_ra`) are linearly increased from lenient to strict values throughout training to progressively increase task difficulty.
- **Baselines:** The proposed TD3BC+JSRL is compared against Behavioral Cloning (BC), TD3BC (offline), and vanilla TD3 (online).

#### 3.6. Computational Efficiency Evaluation

Computational efficiency is quantitatively evaluated using NeuroBench, comparing the trained SNN to a performance-matched feedforward ANN. Energy consumption estimations are performed based on established neuromorphic hardware methodologies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding surrogate gradients, sequential SNN training, and practical application in robotics:

#### 4.1. Surrogate Gradients During Training

- **Supervised Learning (Behavioral Cloning):** The choice of surrogate gradient slope had minimal effect on convergence speed or final performance. Shallower slopes' broader gradient propagation appears to compensate for any increased noise in updates.
- **Online Reinforcement Learning (TD3):** In contrast, RL performance was significantly influenced by slope settings. Shallower slopes, or scheduled slopes, led to a 2.1x improvement in both training speed and final deployed performance. This is attributed to the gradient noise introduced by shallower slopes, which enhances exploration similar to parameter noise, although it also increases training variability.
- **Scheduled Slopes:** Both interval and adaptive scheduling strategies for the surrogate gradient slope achieved competitive convergence speeds and final performance, often approaching that of optimally tuned fixed shallow slopes. Crucially, scheduling improved training stability and reduced the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning for `k`. Adaptive slope scheduling specifically improved training efficiency by a factor of 4.5 compared to a fixed slope of 100.

#### 4.2. Training on Sequences with Reward Curriculum

When evaluated under a reward curriculum designed to gradually increase task difficulty and requiring sequence-based learning:
- **TD3BC+JSRL (Ours):** The proposed method achieved an average return of 400 points, demonstrating its ability to robustly adapt to the evolving curriculum and successfully learn stable control.
- **Offline Methods (BC and TD3BC):** These approaches, trained on static datasets, struggled significantly, failing to exceed -200 points. They were unable to generalize to the increasing difficulty of the curriculum due to the lack of online interaction.
- **Vanilla TD3 (Online without Jump-Start):** This method also failed to surpass -200 points. Without the jump-starting mechanism, it struggled to collect sufficiently long and meaningful sequences early in training, leading to premature episode terminations and preventing the SNN from bridging its warm-up period.
- The results demonstrate a 600-point performance gap between TD3BC+JSRL and other methods, validating the effectiveness of combining demonstrations for bootstrapping with online adaptation for curriculum learning.

#### 4.3. Bridging the Reality Gap (Sim-to-Real Transfer and Efficiency)

- **Real-World Deployment:** The SNN controller, trained entirely in simulation using TD3BC+JSRL, successfully transferred to the real Crazyflie quadrotor. It was capable of executing complex maneuvers (circular flight, figure-eight, square trajectories) without failure, albeit exhibiting slightly oscillatory behavior.
- **Computational Efficiency (NeuroBench):** The temporally-trained SNN (256-128 neuron architecture) achieved a performance comparable to a feedforward ANN (64-64 neuron architecture) that explicitly required action history (SNN reward 446 vs. ANN reward 447). Despite a higher memory footprint and dense operation count, the SNN benefited from significant activation sparsity (0.79) and primarily utilized energy-efficient accumulates (ACs) over multiply-accumulates (MACs). The estimated energy consumption per inference for the SNN was 9.7 × 10^-5 mJ.
- **Temporal Processing Advantage:** While ANNs requiring an explicit action history achieved smoother control, an ANN without action history failed to control the drone at all. This highlights the SNN's intrinsic ability to capture temporal dependencies internally, making explicit action history augmentation unnecessary for successful control.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study was performed to assess the contribution of each component of the TD3BC+JSRL algorithm:
- **Removing the BC-term:** The SNN eventually showed performance improvements, but convergence was substantially slower (approximately 15 times more training steps to reach a reward of 100), and the final reward was lower (334 vs. 412).
- **Eliminating the Jump-Start Period:** Removing the jump-starting actor entirely prevented the SNN from collecting sequences long enough to bridge its warm-up period, resulting in the network failing to learn stable flight altogether (final reward of ~32).
- **Removing both terms:** Led to similar failure, confirming the critical role of both components.
The study concluded that both the jump-start mechanism and the BC term are essential for efficient and successful SNN-RL training in this challenging domain, with the BC term significantly improving training efficiency when the warm-up period is successfully bridged.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes significant contributions to the fields of neuromorphic computing, spiking neural networks, and reinforcement learning, with several potential impacts:

- **Advancement in SNN Training Methodologies:** The research provides a deeper theoretical understanding of surrogate gradient dynamics in deep SNNs, particularly highlighting the trade-off between gradient magnitude, alignment, and their effect in supervised vs. reinforcement learning. The proposed adaptive slope scheduling offers a practical solution to stabilize and optimize SNN training, reducing the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning.
- **Enabling SNNs for Complex Sequential Tasks:** By introducing the TD3BC+JSRL framework, the paper successfully addresses a critical bottleneck in SNN-based RL: the inability to learn in environments where initial poor policies lead to short trajectories, preventing effective warm-up and stateful processing. This opens up SNNs to a wider array of complex, real-world sequential control tasks in robotics and beyond.
- **Path Towards Energy-Efficient Robotics:** The successful deployment and robust performance of an SNN controller on a real quadrotor, without requiring explicit action history, demonstrates the practical feasibility of neuromorphic controllers. This is a crucial step towards developing significantly more energy-efficient embodied agents, as SNNs, especially on specialized neuromorphic hardware, promise orders-of-magnitude efficiency gains compared to conventional ANNs. The NeuroBench evaluation and energy estimations further support this potential.
- **Intrinsic Temporal Processing in Robotics:** The finding that an SNN can effectively control a drone without explicit action history, outperforming an ANN similarly deprived of history, underscores the intrinsic temporal processing capabilities of SNNs. This capability reduces observation requirements and simplifies system design for dynamic control tasks.
- **Broader Impact on Neuromorphic AI:** The insights into surrogate gradient optimization and the sequential training framework are generalizable and can inform the development of other SNN-based algorithms for various applications, accelerating the progress of neuromorphic artificial intelligence.

While the method relies on the availability of a guiding policy, which might not always be trivial to obtain, its ability to bootstrap learning and achieve robust performance with SNNs for real-world control tasks represents a substantial step forward for energy-efficient, neuromorphic robotics.
