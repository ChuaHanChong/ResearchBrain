# SiT: Self-supervised vIsion Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> The SiT (Self-supervised vIsion Transformer) framework, developed at the University of Surrey, introduces Group Masked Model Learning (GMML) for pretraining vision transformers using unlabeled data. This method, combining masked image modeling and contrastive learning, consistently outperforms supervised pretraining on small and medium-scale downstream classification tasks (e.g., +11.8% on CUB) and achieves competitive performance on large-scale datasets and multi-label or video instance segmentation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) typically require pretraining on extremely large supervised datasets (e.g., ImageNet-21K) or rely on distillation from CNNs, making them 'data-hungry' and computationally expensive.
- Effectively applying self-supervised learning (SSL) to ViTs, which lack CNNs' inductive biases, remained a challenge for learning local context and consistently outperforming supervised pretraining.
- Prior pretext tasks in computer vision, mainly explored with CNNs, often struggled to capture both local and global context seamlessly with transformers.

## Method

- Proposed ==Group Masked Model Learning (GMML)==, a self-supervised pretraining framework that adapts ==masked image modeling== for vision transformers, similar to BERT's Masked Language Modeling.
- GMML involves perturbing groups of connected image patches (using random noise or replacement from other images) and training the transformer to reconstruct the original image.
- Developed a ==multi-task learning== approach that jointly optimizes both the image reconstruction loss (from GMML) and a contrastive learning loss, leveraging the transformer's architectural flexibility with a lightweight decoder.

## Results

- SiT consistently outperforms random initialization and state-of-the-art self-supervised methods by substantial margins (e.g., **+11.8%** on CUB, **+11.7%** on Pets) when pretraining and finetuning solely on small and medium target datasets.
- When pretrained on ImageNet-1K, SiT achieves a **1.1%** improvement on ImageNet-1K itself and performs on par with or slightly better than concurrent and post-art self-supervised methods on various downstream multi-class datasets.
- SiT pretraining leads to significant improvements in multi-label classification (e.g., **+40.6** absolute mAP on PASCAL VOC from scratch) and outperforms other SSL methods on video instance segmentation (DAVIS-2017) benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Masked Image Modeling (MIM), as implemented through GMML, is highly effective for learning rich visual representations in vision transformers, paralleling its success in NLP.
> - Self-supervised pretraining (SSP) can consistently outperform supervised pretraining (SP) for vision transformers on downstream classification tasks, especially with small and medium-sized datasets.
> - Vision transformers can function as efficient autoencoders with significantly lightweight decoders (e.g., a 2-layer perceptron), simplifying the reconstruction task compared to CNN-based autoencoders.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "SiT: Self-supervised vIsion Transformer" positions itself at a critical intersection of recent advancements in deep learning: **Self-supervised Learning (SSL)** and **Transformer architectures**, specifically adapted for **Computer Vision (CV)**.

In Natural Language Processing (NLP), SSL combined with transformers (e.g., BERT, GPT) has been tremendously successful, establishing them as the methods of choice due to their ability to learn rich, contextual representations from vast amounts of unlabeled text data. This success has naturally inspired similar explorations in computer vision.

However, the authors point out a significant challenge in vision: while Vision Transformers (ViT) have shown promise, they typically require pretraining on extremely large *supervised* datasets (e.g., ImageNet-21K with hundreds of millions of labeled samples) or rely on co-supervision techniques like distillation from Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). This makes ViTs "data-hungry" and computationally expensive, limiting their adoption and generalizability, particularly when compared to CNNs which inherently possess inductive biases (like locality and translation equivariance) that make them more efficient with smaller datasets. The manual annotation of such massive datasets is also a significant bottleneck.

Self-supervised learning in computer vision aims to address this by learning meaningful representations from unlabeled data, reducing the reliance on costly annotations and potentially leading to more generalizable models, much like the development of the visual cortex is believed to be influenced by visual experiences. Prior SSL methods in vision broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Generative Approaches:** Model data distribution but are often computationally expensive.
2. **Discriminative Approaches:** More common, using either contrastive learning (e.g., SimCLR, MoCo) or pretext tasks (e.g., inpainting, colorization, jigsaw puzzles). While contrastive methods achieve impressive results, they often focus on global transformations, disregard local context, and require careful implementation details to avoid representation collapse. Pretext tasks, explored mostly with CNNs, also have their limitations.

**SiT's primary contribution to this landscape is its pioneering role in establishing Masked Image Modeling (MIM) for vision transformers.** The paper claims SiT is the "first masked image modelling work for vision transformers." This directly parallels the success of Masked Language Modeling (MLM) in BERT, where masked words are predicted from context. By introducing **Group Masked Model Learning (GMML)**, SiT adapts this powerful idea to the visual domain, leveraging the transformer's ability to model both local and global context without the inductive bias assumptions of CNNs.

Crucially, SiT also makes a bold claim that at the time of its conception (early 2021), it was the *first work to demonstrate that Self-supervised Pretraining (SSP) can consistently outperform Supervised Pretraining (SP) for downstream vision classification tasks using transformers*, especially on small and medium datasets without external data. This directly challenges the prevailing notion that ViTs are inherently data-hungry and need massive supervised pretraining.

The paper's "Impact" statement further solidifies its position as a foundational work. It explicitly states that GMML, as proposed in SiT, was widely adopted and extended by later influential studies such as SIMMIM and MAE towards the end of 2021. These subsequent works, which used huge vision transformers on large-scale datasets, built upon the masked autoencoder framework introduced by SiT. This confirms SiT as a critical precursor, enabling the computer vision community to achieve similar SSL successes with transformers as seen in NLP with BERT.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the "SiT: Self-supervised vIsion Transformer" paper is to develop and validate a self-supervised learning (SSL) framework that enables vision transformers (ViTs) to learn robust and generalizable visual representations from *unlabeled data*, particularly overcoming the common limitation of ViTs requiring massive *supervised* pretraining datasets.

The key objectives and underlying motivations can be broken down as follows:

1. **Overcome the "Data-Hungry" Nature of Vision Transformers:**
 - **Motivation:** ViTs, adopted from NLP, lack the inherent inductive biases (like locality) present in CNNs. This typically makes them highly dependent on huge amounts of supervised pretraining data (hundreds of millions of labeled samples) or distillation techniques from CNNs to perform well. This reliance on large labeled datasets is expensive and limits their accessibility for researchers with constrained resources.
 - **Objective:** To enable ViTs to learn useful local inductive bias and perform on par with (or outperform) CNNs, even on small and medium datasets, without requiring external supervised data.

2. **Establish Self-supervised Pretraining (SSP) as Superior to Supervised Pretraining (SP) for ViTs:**
 - **Motivation:** At the time, supervised pretraining was the norm for achieving high performance with ViTs. While SSL was gaining traction, a consistent performance gap often existed. The authors also argue that single-label supervised learning might lead to "sub-expressive features" as it ignores the rich context and multiple concepts within natural images, potentially limiting generalization.
 - **Objective:** To demonstrate for the first time that SSP can consistently outperform SP for downstream vision classification tasks when using transformers, thereby establishing SSP as a more suitable and effective pretraining choice.

3. **Introduce and Validate Masked Image Modeling (MIM) for Vision Transformers:**
 - **Motivation:** Inspired by the tremendous success of Masked Language Modeling (MLM) in NLP (e.g., BERT), which learns contextual representations by predicting masked words, the authors sought to apply a similar principle to the visual domain. Previous pretext tasks in computer vision (e.g., inpainting) were mainly explored with CNNs and often struggled to capture both local and global context seamlessly.
 - **Objective:** To propose **Group Masked Model Learning (GMML)**, a simple masked autoencoder framework, as the first effective masked image modeling approach for vision transformers. This aims to learn rich representations by recovering large amounts of missing visual information based on context.

4. **Leverage Architectural Flexibility of Transformers for Multi-task Self-supervision:**
 - **Motivation:** Transformers' architecture, particularly their ability to process sequences of tokens and handle various inputs/outputs, offers flexibility for integrating multiple learning objectives.
 - **Objective:** To develop a strong self-supervised framework that jointly optimizes both reconstruction (GMML) and contrastive learning losses, leveraging their complementary strengths to learn both useful and discriminative representations. The goal is also to show that the transformer can function as an efficient autoencoder with a lightweight decoder.

5. **Achieve Strong Performance Across Various Downstream Tasks and Dataset Scales:**
 - **Motivation:** A robust SSL method should generalize well across different applications and dataset sizes.
 - **Objective:** To illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework on standard benchmarks, including multi-class classification, multi-label classification, and video instance segmentation, using different evaluation protocols like domain transfer and finetuning.

In essence, SiT aims to redefine the landscape of vision transformer pretraining by offering an effective, resource-efficient, and highly performant self-supervised alternative that leverages the unique strengths of the transformer architecture to learn from the intrinsic structure of unlabeled visual data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of SiT revolves around **Group Masked Model Learning (GMML)**, a novel self-supervised pretraining framework for vision transformers that draws inspiration from masked language modeling in NLP and denoising autoencoders in computer vision. The approach leverages the architectural flexibility of transformers to seamlessly integrate multiple self-supervised tasks.

#### 3.1. Vision Transformer as an Autoencoder

The fundamental idea is to use a vision transformer (specifically, a modified ViT-S/16 architecture) as an autoencoder. Unlike CNN-based autoencoders that require complex encoder-decoder structures with many layers and skip connections, SiT demonstrates that a transformer-based autoencoder can use a very *lightweight decoder*, implemented as a 2-layer perceptron. This is possible because transformers, unlike CNNs, do not discard information through strided convolutions or max-pooling in the encoder, thus simplifying the reconstruction task.

#### 3.2. Group Masked Model Learning (GMML)

GMML is the heart of SiT's self-supervised strategy for image reconstruction. It is inspired by the principle of recovering masked content from context, akin to BERT's Masked Language Modeling (MLM), denoising autoencoders, and context encoders. The key principles guiding GMML are:
1. **Random Data Token Perturbation:** Instead of masking individual tokens (like words in NLP), GMML applies transformations to *groups of connected tokens* (patches) in the 2D image. This reflects the semantic meaning often residing in spatially contiguous regions in images.
2. **Recovery/Reconstruction:** The network is trained to recover these transformed local parts from the uncorrupted visible tokens, implicitly learning semantic concepts and "visual integrity." This process encourages the network to understand the relationships between different parts of an image.
3. **Modeling of All Semantic Concepts:** The corruption can be applied to both foreground objects and background, treating every part of the image as a semantic concept. This helps SiT generalize better to various downstream tasks, whether object-centric or scene-centric.

#### 3.3. Multi-Task Self-Supervised Learning

SiT combines two primary self-supervised tasks:

- **Task #1: Image Reconstruction (GMML)**
 - **Corruption Strategy:** Two types of image inpainting are employed:
 - **Random Dropping:** Randomly replacing groups of neighboring patches with random noise.
 - **Random Replacement:** Randomly replacing groups of patches with patches from another image.
 - **Objective:** Restore the original image from the corrupted version. The `L1-loss` is used as the reconstruction loss, chosen over `L2-loss` for its ability to produce sharper edges in image restoration.
 - **Decoder:** A light decoder consisting of two fully connected layers followed by a transposed convolution reconstructs the image from the output data tokens of the vision transformer backbone.

- **Task #2: Contrastive Learning**
 - **Motivation:** Inspired by SimCLR, this task aims to learn image embeddings that are invariant to different augmented views of the same image (positive pairs) while being discriminative among different images (negative pairs).
 - **Mechanism:** Uses a contrastive token (similar to a classification token) whose embedding is passed through a contrastive head (two FC layers with batch norm and GeLU, followed by a linear layer). The normalized temperature-scaled softmax similarity (NT-Xent loss), is employed to maximize similarity for positive pairs and minimize for negative pairs.
 - **Momentum Encoder:** To generate representations for clean images for the contrastive loss, a momentum encoder (an exponentially moving average of the main encoder's weights) is used, which was found to yield slightly better performance than a siamese network without momentum.

#### 3.4. End-to-End Training and Finetuning

- **Combined Loss:** The overall loss function is a weighted sum of the reconstruction loss and the contrastive loss: `L_total = α × L_recons + L_contr`. The scaling factor `α` is set higher (α=5) for small datasets to emphasize local inductive bias learning and lower (α=1) for large datasets (ImageNet-1K).
- **Data Augmentation:** Simple augmentations like cropping, horizontal flipping, color jittering, solarization, and Gaussian blurring are applied. Aggressive augmentations (e.g., MixUp, AutoAugment) were found to be detrimental.
- **Optimization:** Models are trained using the Adam optimizer, with specified batch sizes, momentum, weight decay, and learning rates, for 800 epochs (ImageNet-1K) or 3000 epochs (small datasets).
- **Finetuning:** After self-supervised pretraining, the reconstruction and contrastive heads are removed, and a new linear classification head is appended to the class token for specific downstream tasks.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The authors primarily use a **ViT-S/16** (Small Vision Transformer with 16x16 patch size) architecture with 224x224 input images. This model has 21 million parameters. The percentage of corrupted patches varies from 5% to 25% of the image size, with an overall corruption rate up to 70% for noise replacement and 30% for replacement from other images.

This multi-faceted approach allows SiT to effectively train vision transformers from unlabeled data, learning robust visual representations that capture both local semantic details and global contextual information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results presented in the paper highlight the significant advantages of SiT, particularly its ability to enable self-supervised pretraining (SSP) to outperform supervised pretraining (SP) for vision transformers and its strong performance across various datasets and tasks.

#### 4.1. Outperformance on Small and Medium Scale Datasets (Standalone Pretraining)

- **Key Discovery:** SiT consistently and significantly outperforms both randomly initialized models and state-of-the-art concurrent and post-art self-supervised methods (MoCo-v3, DINO, MAE) when pretraining and finetuning are performed *only on the target dataset* (e.g., Flowers, Pets, CUB, Aircraft, STL10, Cars, CIFAR10, CIFAR100).
- **Magnitude of Improvement:** SiT achieved substantial improvements, such as +11.8% on CUB, +11.7% on Pets, and +8.8% on Aircraft compared to the best prior art (MoCo-v3). Even for datasets like CIFAR10/100 and STL10, where other methods were already strong, SiT still showed notable gains (+0.8% to +1.8%).
- **Addressing Data-Hunger:** This finding is crucial as it demonstrates SiT's ability to overcome the "data-hungry" nature of vision transformers, making them effective even with limited data without relying on external pretraining or distillation. The poor performance of SimMIM on small datasets (not converging) further underscores SiT's robustness in this challenging scenario.

#### 4.2. Multi-class Classification on Large-scale Datasets (ImageNet-1K Pretraining)

- **Superiority over Supervised Pretraining:** When pretrained on ImageNet-1K and finetuned on various downstream multi-class datasets, SiT outperforms supervised pretraining in most cases. For instance, with ViT-S/16, SiT achieves a 1.1% improvement on ImageNet-1K itself.
- **On-par or Better than SOTA:** SiT performs on par with or slightly better than concurrent and post-art methods (MoCo-v3, DINO, SimMIM, MAE) across most small datasets when pretrained on ImageNet-1K. For instance, with ViT-S/16, SiT achieves the highest accuracy on Flowers, Pets, CUB, Aircraft, STL10, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100 among the compared methods. With ViT-B/16, SiT achieves highly competitive results, on par with or slightly better than MAE and SimMIM.

#### 4.3. Multi-Label Classification

- **Significant Gains from Local Pretraining:** When trained from scratch on multi-label datasets (PASCAL VOC, MS-COCO, Visual-Genome), SiT pretraining leads to massive improvements (e.g., +40.6 absolute mAP on PASCAL VOC, +27.1 on MS-COCO) compared to random initialization.
- **Outperformance with ImageNet-1K Pretraining:** Pretraining with SiT on ImageNet-1K also outperforms other SSL SOTA frameworks (MoCo-v3, DINO) on MS-COCO (+0.5 mAP) and Visual-Genome (+0.6 mAP).

#### 4.4. Instance Segmentation

- **Strong Generalization of Features:** On the DAVIS-2017 video instance segmentation benchmark, SiT (pretrained on ImageNet-1K) significantly outperforms state-of-the-art supervised (DeiT) and self-supervised methods (MoCo-v3, DINO, MAE) in terms of mean region similarity (Jm) and mean contour-based accuracy (Fm).
- **MAE Limitations:** Notably, MAE shows poor performance in this task, which the authors attribute to its representations being less linearly separable than contrastive learning-based methods, highlighting SiT's advantage.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Individual Components:** The reconstruction loss (GMML) alone provides a highly effective self-supervision mechanism, even outperforming contrastive learning with momentum. Combining both reconstruction and contrastive losses offers marginal further improvements.
- **Longer Pretraining:** Performance consistently increases with longer pretraining epochs, showing no saturation even after 800 epochs.
- **Corruption Type:** Replacing patches with content from *another image* proved to be the most effective single corruption type. Combining "replace" with "noise" yielded the best overall performance, slightly better than "replace with zeros."
- **Corruption Percentage:** The optimal corruption rate was found to be between 50% and 70%.
- **Qualitative Insights:** t-SNE visualizations reveal that SiT effectively clusters data into distinct classes even without labels, and this discriminative ability improves with more unlabeled pretraining data. Attention maps demonstrate that SiT learns to focus on salient regions of images after pretraining.

In summary, the results firmly establish SiT as a highly effective and pioneering self-supervised pretraining framework for vision transformers. It demonstrates that SSP can not only match but *outperform* SP, especially for resource-constrained scenarios with smaller datasets, while also achieving state-of-the-art or on-par performance on large-scale datasets and diverse downstream tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "SiT: Self-supervised vIsion Transformer" paper represents a highly significant contribution to the field of computer vision and deep learning, with several profound potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Vision Transformer Pretraining (Pioneering MIM):** SiT is a pioneering work that introduces and validates the concept of **Masked Image Modeling (MIM)** for vision transformers through its **Group Masked Model Learning (GMML)** framework. This directly mirrors the revolutionary success of Masked Language Modeling (MLM) in NLP with models like BERT. By demonstrating the effectiveness of MIM in vision, SiT paved the way for a new wave of highly successful self-supervised vision models, as acknowledged by its "Impact" section and the subsequent emergence of influential works like SIMMIM and MAE which extended SiT's core ideas. This establishes MIM/GMML as a leading SSL framework across multiple application areas.

2. **Overcoming the "Data-Hungry" Nature of Vision Transformers:** Perhaps the most impactful finding is SiT's demonstration that self-supervised pretraining (SSP) can enable vision transformers to perform strongly on **small and medium-scale datasets without any external data**. More critically, SiT consistently *outperforms supervised pretraining* in these scenarios, a claim the authors assert has not been made before. This directly addresses one of the major limitations of ViTs – their reliance on massive, expensive *labeled* datasets – and democratizes access to these powerful architectures for researchers and practitioners with limited data and computational resources. This can significantly accelerate research and application development in domains where large labeled datasets are scarce (e.g., specialized medical imaging, niche industrial applications).

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By learning from the intrinsic structure and contextual information within unlabeled data, SiT produces more robust and generalizable visual representations than traditional supervised methods. The ability to recover corrupted image patches forces the model to learn a deeper understanding of visual integrity and semantic concepts, leading to features that transfer exceptionally well to diverse downstream tasks like multi-class classification, multi-label classification, and video instance segmentation.

4. **Architectural Efficiency and Multi-task Learning:** The paper effectively demonstrates the architectural flexibility of transformers, enabling them to function as efficient autoencoders with significantly lighter decoders (a 2-layer perceptron) compared to CNN-based counterparts. This architectural advantage, combined with the seamless integration of multiple self-supervised tasks (GMML for reconstruction and contrastive learning), showcases a powerful and versatile framework for representation learning.

5. **Foundational Impact on Future Research:** As explicitly stated in its "Impact" section, SiT introduced the GMML framework at the beginning of 2021, and its principles were later extended by major models like SIMMIM and MAE. This positions SiT not just as a high-performing model but as a foundational contribution that catalyzed subsequent advancements in the field. The success of MIM, enabled by SiT, allows the computer vision community to achieve breakthroughs in SSL similar to those enjoyed by NLP with BERT.

In conclusion, SiT is a landmark paper that challenges conventional wisdom about vision transformer pretraining. By pioneering masked image modeling and demonstrating the superiority of self-supervised learning over supervised alternatives, especially in resource-constrained settings, SiT has significantly advanced the state of the art and will continue to influence the development of more efficient, powerful, and accessible vision models.
