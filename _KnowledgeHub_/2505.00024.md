# Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1: Exploring Tool-Using Language Models with Reinforced Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA, Pennsylvania State University, and the University of Washington introduce Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1, which applies rule-based reinforcement learning (R1-style RL) to train large language models for tool-calling. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on various tool-use benchmarks, outperforming models like GPT-4o, and demonstrates that pure RL training can be more effective than traditional supervised fine-tuning or SFT-then-RL pipelines.

## Problem

- Existing tool-augmented Large Language Models primarily rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which often leads to imitative reasoning rather than robust, generalized understanding.
- SFT-trained models frequently struggle to generalize to novel or out-of-distribution tool-use scenarios due to a lack of explicit reasoning supervision.
- SFT's rigid next-token prediction penalizes semantically equivalent but syntactically different tool-call outputs, limiting the model's flexibility.

## Method

- The paper applies a ==rule-based (R1-style) reinforcement learning paradigm== using the GRPO algorithm to train LLMs for general-purpose tool-calling.
- A ==simple binary reward function== is employed, granting a reward of 1 only if the output adheres to a specified structured format and the predicted tool call(s) exactly match the ground-truth functionally.
- A lightweight =='thinking template'== guides the LLM to produce internal reasoning within XML tags followed by tool invocations as JSON, ensuring structured output while allowing flexible reasoning.

## Results

- Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 models achieved state-of-the-art performance on tool-calling benchmarks, with Tool-N1-14B improving approximately **2%** over GPT-4o on BFCL and surpassing GPT-4o by **5.03%** on APIBank.
- Pure R1-style RL consistently outperformed both Reason-SFT and No-Reason SFT under equal data budgets, demonstrating the efficacy of the RL-centric approach.
- The training method scaled effectively with model size (**7B** and **14B** models showing significant gains) and generalized well across different LLM backbones like Qwen2.5-Instruct and LLaMA series.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The commonly adopted SFT-then-RL paradigm does not necessarily outperform pure RL for tool-calling tasks under equal data budgets, challenging established RLHF assumptions.
> - A simple binary reward scheme (all-or-nothing for format and tool-call correctness) is more effective than fine-grained partial rewards, preventing 'reward hacking' and encouraging full functional accuracy.
> - Explicitly guiding the model to output structured reasoning within specific tags, even without supervising the reasoning content, is crucial for performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has rapidly advanced, moving beyond pure text generation to incorporating external tools. This integration is crucial for extending LLMs' capabilities to interact with the real world, perform complex calculations, access up-to-date information, and execute multi-step tasks.

**Current Landscape of Tool-Augmented LLMs:**
Existing research on enabling LLMs with tool-calling capabilities has predominantly relied on **supervised fine-tuning (SFT)**. This typically involves:
- **Synthesizing large volumes of tool-use trajectories:** Often using advanced LLMs to generate demonstrations of how tools should be used.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Training smaller "student" models on these synthetic datasets to mimic the tool-calling behavior.

**Limitations of Prior SFT-based Approaches:**
This paper highlights several critical shortcomings of the SFT paradigm for tool-calling:
- **Imitative Reasoning:** Models trained via SFT often learn to mimic surface-level patterns without truly internalizing the underlying decision-making process. This leads to "pseudo reasoning," where the model reproduces observed behaviors rather than developing robust, generalizable reasoning.
- **Lack of Explicit Reasoning Supervision:** Many synthetic datasets focus only on tool calls, omitting explicit intermediate reasoning steps. While some approaches distill reasoning trajectories from stronger models, this can still result in mere imitation rather than genuine understanding.
- **Limited Generalization:** SFT models tend to overfit to the specific training trajectories, struggling to generalize to novel or out-of-distribution (OOD) tool-use scenarios.
- **Rigid Supervision:** SFT typically uses next-token prediction, penalizing even semantically equivalent but structurally different outputs (e.g., reordered arguments in a function call).

**Emergence of R1-style Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
In parallel, a new trend has emerged in LLM reasoning research: **rule-based (or R1-style) reinforcement learning**. Pioneered by works like DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025), this paradigm assigns rewards based on the correctness of the *final answer* and the *output format*, rather than step-by-step supervision of intermediate reasoning. This allows the model to implicitly learn its own reasoning steps. R1-style RL has shown success in various domains like mathematics, coding, and vision tasks, proving that explicit reasoning supervision isn't always necessary for effective learning.

**This Work's Contribution to the Landscape:**
Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 directly addresses the limitations of SFT by applying and systematically studying R1-style reinforcement learning for general-purpose tool-calling. It shifts the focus from "imitating reasoning traces" to "learning from functional correctness." Key aspects of its contribution include:
- **Bridging R1-style RL and Tool-Calling:** It extends the successful R1 paradigm from domains like mathematics or coding (often involving a single, general-purpose tool) to the more complex, multi-tool, and function-calling environments.
- **Reasoning-Trace Free Training:** It demonstrates that high-quality tool-calling models can be trained without needing to distill or supervise explicit reasoning trajectories, simplifying the data pipeline and reducing reliance on potentially unstable distillation processes.
- **Flexible Supervision:** By rewarding dictionary-based functional correctness rather than rigid string matching, it allows for more semantic understanding and better generalization.
- **Systematic Comparison:** It provides a crucial empirical analysis comparing SFT, pure RL, and the common SFT-then-RL pipeline, offering counter-intuitive findings that challenge established best practices in RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) for LLMs.

In essence, Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 proposes a more efficient, robust, and semantically grounded method for training tool-using LLMs, pushing the research frontier beyond imitation towards intrinsic reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 stems from the perceived limitations of current approaches to training tool-calling Large Language Models, predominantly supervised fine-tuning (SFT). The authors identify that SFT, while widely adopted, often leads to models that merely imitate predefined tool-use trajectories rather than developing a deeper, generalized understanding of *when* and *how* to use tools effectively. This imitative behavior results in limited generalization, especially when faced with novel or complex scenarios not explicitly present in the training data.

Based on this critique, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Validate the Efficacy of Rule-Based Reinforcement Learning (R1-style RL) for Tool-Calling:** The primary objective is to investigate whether the R1-style RL paradigm, which has shown success in enhancing LLM reasoning without explicit step-by-step supervision, can be effectively applied to improve tool-calling capabilities. The hypothesis is that by providing rewards based on the *correctness of the final tool invocation* and its *format*, models can learn the intermediate reasoning steps implicitly.

2. **Develop Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1: A Series of Reinforced Tool-Using LLMs:** To demonstrate the practical applicability of their approach, the authors aim to develop and release a series of tool-calling reasoning models (Tool-N1-7B and Tool-N1-14B) that incorporate the proposed RL training strategy.

3. **Systematically Design and Analyze the RL Pipeline for Tool-Call Training:** Beyond just applying RL, the research seeks to conduct a thorough study into the optimal design choices for an R1-style RL pipeline for tool-calling. This includes aspects like data preparation, prompt templating, and, crucially, the reward modeling strategy.

4. **Investigate the Roles of SFT, Pure RL, and SFT-then-RL Paradigms:** A significant objective is to empirically compare the effectiveness of different training recipes:
 - Pure SFT (with and without reasoning traces).
 - Pure RL.
 - The widely adopted SFT-then-RL pipeline.
 The goal is to determine which approach yields the best performance for tool-using LLMs with reasoning capabilities, particularly challenging the assumption that SFT pre-training is always beneficial before RL.

5. **Promote Robust Generalization and Intrinsic Reasoning:** The ultimate underlying motivation is to enable LLMs to develop more robust, intrinsic reasoning skills for tool usage, moving beyond rote memorization. This is achieved by allowing the model more flexibility in its internal reasoning process while enforcing only the correctness of the final action and its structured format.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1's methodology is the application of a **rule-based (R1-style) reinforcement learning** paradigm, specifically using the **GRPO (Generalized Policy Optimization)** algorithm, to train LLMs for tool-calling. This approach significantly deviates from the conventional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods by focusing on lightweight, outcome-based supervision rather than detailed imitation of reasoning traces.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key components:

1. **Problem Formulation:**
 - The LLM interacts with a set of external tools, each defined by a name, description, and input parameters.
 - The model receives historical context (previous tool-call and observation pairs) and available tools.
 - Its objective is to generate an action (one or more tool calls with parameters) that effectively addresses the user query.
 - A critical aspect is the structured output: the model is guided to produce textual reasoning followed by the tool invocation.

2. **GRPO Algorithm for Policy Optimization:**
 - The policy model ($\pi_\theta$) generates a set of candidate responses, each comprising textual reasoning and an associated action.
 - These responses are evaluated by a reward function, yielding a reward for each.
 - GRPO optimizes the policy by maximizing the advantage of better-rewarded responses while constraining the divergence from the previous policy (KL divergence term). This helps stabilize training.

3. **Data Preparation:**
 - The training data is sourced from existing tool-calling datasets: **xLAM** (single-turn) and a subset of **ToolACE** (single-turn and multi-turn).
 - **Crucial Preprocessing:** Since these datasets often come from LLMs and can be inconsistent, a rigorous filtering process is applied:
 - Invalid tool calls (e.g., tools not in the candidate list) are removed.
 - Tool specifications are parsed into structured dictionary formats (e.g., JSON) to enable robust verification.
 - Instances failing JSON parsing or containing formatting inconsistencies are discarded.
 - **Multi-turn to Single-step:** Multi-turn trajectories from ToolACE are segmented into single-step prediction instances, where each instance has a target tool call and preceding steps as context. This allows the R1-style training to focus on single-step decision-making within a larger context.

4. **Thinking Template (Prompting Strategy):**
 - A lightweight, structured prompt (see Figure 2 in the paper) is used to guide the LLM's output.
 - It explicitly instructs the model to:
 1. Produce internal reasoning within `<think>...</think>` XML tags.
 2. Provide the tool invocation(s) as a JSON object within `<tool_call>...</tool_call>` XML tags.
 3. Ensure both reasoning and tool calls are in a single reply.
 - The design emphasizes flexibility, allowing the model freedom in its reasoning expression while ensuring structural adherence for evaluation. This template encourages explicit reasoning without imposing rigid content.

5. **Reward Modeling (Binary Reward Function):**
 - This is a cornerstone of the methodology, defining the feedback signal for RL. The reward is *binary* (0 or 1).
 - **Format Correctness:** The model receives a reward of 1 only if its output adheres to the specified XML tag format (`<think>...</think>` followed by `<tool_call>...</tool_call>`). This encourages structured reasoning.
 - **Tool-Calling Correctness:** The predicted tool call(s) must *exactly match* the ground-truth call(s) in both:
 - **Tool Name:** The invoked tool name must be correct.
 - **Argument Key-Value Pairs:** All required arguments and their values must be accurate.
 - **Key Distinction:** Unlike SFT's next-token prediction, this checking is done at a *semantic/functional* level by parsing tool calls as dictionaries. This allows flexibility (e.g., reordered arguments are still correct) and prevents penalizing minor syntactic variations, encouraging focus on functional accuracy rather than rigid string memorization.
 - **Binary Nature:** A reward of 1 is given *only if both* format and tool call conditions are met; otherwise, it's 0. This "all-or-nothing" reward is found to be more effective than fine-grained partial rewards in the ablation studies.

6. **Training and Evaluation Details:**
 - **Backbone Models:** Qwen2.5-7B/14B-Instruct models are primarily used, with additional experiments on LLaMA series for generalizability.
 - **RL Library:** Open-source Verl library is utilized for training.
 - **Benchmarks:** Performance is evaluated on BFCL (Berkeley Function Call Leaderboard), API-Bank, and ACEBench, focusing on single-turn scenarios and various complexities (simple, multiple, parallel tool calls).

This systematic approach, by combining rigorous data preprocessing, a flexible reasoning template, and a precise binary reward function within a GRPO framework, enables Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 to learn robust tool-calling capabilities without explicit supervision over complex reasoning trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 yield several significant findings that challenge conventional wisdom in tool-augmented LLM training.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Tool-Calling:**
 - Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 models (7B and 14B) consistently outperform strong baselines, including state-of-the-art closed-source models like **GPT-4o** and specialized SFT-tuned models (e.g., ToolACE-8B, Hammer2.1-7B) across multiple benchmarks.
 - On BFCL, Tool-N1-14B achieves approximately **2%** improvement over GPT-4o, and Tool-N1-7B surpasses GPT-4o-mini by **0.85%** and Hammer2.1-7B by **2.97%**.
 - On APIBank and ACEBench, Tool-N1-7B shows substantial absolute improvements (over 20% on APIBank, 30% on ACEBench) compared to base models, and Tool-N1-14B surpasses GPT-4o by **5.03%** on APIBank.
 - These results robustly demonstrate the superiority of the R1-style reinforcement learning paradigm for improving LLMs' tool-calling capabilities over traditional SFT methods.

2. **Effective Scaling with Model Size:**
 - The R1-style training method scales effectively with increasing model size. Larger models (7B, 14B) from the Qwen2.5-Instruct series show significant performance gains after post-training compared to their original instruction-tuned variants.
 - Smaller models (0.5B, 1.5B) show limited improvements, suggesting that the benefits of this training method are more pronounced with larger, more capable base models.

3. **Generalizability Across Backbones:**
 - The proposed method generalizes well to different LLM backbones. While Qwen2.5-Instruct models consistently outperform LLaMA3/3.1 variants at comparable scales, this indicates that a stronger base model (like Qwen, known for its reasoning capabilities) allows the R1-style training paradigm to elicit even better performance.

4. **SFT vs. RL: A Deep Investigation Reveals RL's Advantage:**
 - This is one of the most critical findings: The commonly adopted **"SFT-then-RL" paradigm does *not* necessarily outperform pure RL** for tool-calling tasks. Under equal data budgets, the combined approach (Reason-SFT+RL) performs marginally worse than pure RL (83.17% vs. 83.24%). This contradicts a widely held belief in RLHF that SFT provides an essential initialization.
 - **Pure RL consistently outperforms both Reason-SFT** (SFT on reasoning trajectories) and **No-Reason SFT** (SFT without reasoning traces) when using the same data budget.
 - Interestingly, providing explicit reasoning traces during SFT offers only limited additional benefit compared to SFT without such traces, suggesting that SFT struggles to internalize reasoning effectively.

5. **Binary Rewards are Superior to Fine-Grained Rewards:**
 - An ablation study on reward design showed that a **simple binary reward scheme** (all-or-nothing: 1 for perfectly correct format AND tool call, 0 otherwise) consistently yields better performance than fine-grained rewards that give partial credit for correct reasoning format or partial tool-call matches.
 - This effect is particularly pronounced on "Live" (more realistic) datasets, indicating that binary rewards reduce "reward hacking" and encourage the model to achieve full functional correctness rather than superficial adherence.

6. **Crucial Role of Structured Reasoning Format:**
 - Within the binary reward setup, removing the requirement for the model to output intermediate reasoning within `<think>` tags significantly degrades performance. This underscores the critical importance of explicitly guiding the model to perform structured reasoning, even if that reasoning isn't directly supervised.

7. **Training Data Composition Matters:**
 - Both xLAM and ToolACE datasets contribute positively to the model's performance, with ToolACE data yielding particularly strong improvements, especially in the challenging "Live" settings. This suggests that data diversity and quality remain important.
 - R1-style training on these datasets significantly outperforms models trained solely with SFT on the same data sources (e.g., Tool-N1-7B on xLAM beats xLAM-8B SFT model by 6.36%).

8. **Learning Dynamics Insights:**
 - Analysis of learning curves shows stable improvement in KL divergence and validation accuracy.
 - Notably, the **average response length does not significantly increase** throughout training, unlike some other R1-style reasoning works. This implies that longer reasoning chains are not necessarily beneficial for tool-use tasks; there might be an optimal, concise reasoning length.

In summary, the findings establish Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 as a new benchmark in tool-calling LLMs, advocating for a shift towards a more efficient and effective RL-centric training paradigm, unburdened by the need for explicit reasoning supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1" carries significant implications for the development and deployment of advanced Large Language Models, particularly in their ability to interact with the real world via tools.

1. **A Paradigm Shift in Tool-Learning:** The most profound impact of this research is its challenge to the prevailing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) paradigm for tool-calling. By demonstrating that simple, rule-based reinforcement learning (R1-style RL) can not only match but *outperform* SFT-based methods, even without supervising explicit reasoning traces, the paper offers a more efficient and effective approach. This is a significant methodological contribution that could redirect future research efforts in tool-augmented LLMs.

2. **Enhanced LLM Capabilities for Real-World Applications:** Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 achieves state-of-the-art performance, surpassing even powerful closed-source models like GPT-4o on major benchmarks. This means more capable and reliable open-source tool-using LLMs can be developed. Such models are crucial for building autonomous agents, intelligent assistants, and complex enterprise solutions that can leverage external APIs, databases, and computational resources to solve problems beyond their core knowledge.

3. **Cost-Effectiveness and Accessibility of Training:** The R1-style RL approach eliminates the need for expensive and complex reasoning trace distillation processes or sophisticated reward models. By relying on a simple binary reward function and readily available SFT-style tool-calling data, the training pipeline becomes significantly more streamlined, cost-effective, and accessible. This democratizes the ability to train high-performance tool-using LLMs, lowering the barrier for researchers and developers.

4. **Promotion of Intrinsic Reasoning:** By forcing the model to infer the best reasoning steps from a binary outcome reward (and a structured output format), the approach encourages the development of more intrinsic and robust reasoning capabilities. This is vital for improving generalization, especially when models encounter novel tool-use scenarios or out-of-distribution inputs, reducing the reliance on rote memorization of training examples. The finding that enforcing a *structured reasoning format* (via `<think>` tags) is crucial, even without supervising its content, highlights a powerful technique for guiding model behavior.

5. **Valuable Insights for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):** The counter-intuitive finding that "SFT-then-RL" does not necessarily outperform pure RL, and that fine-grained rewards can lead to "reward hacking," provides critical insights for the broader RLHF community. These findings suggest that simpler reward structures and a direct RL approach might be more effective in certain domains, prompting a re-evaluation of current RLHF best practices.

6. **Open-Source Contribution:** The authors' commitment to releasing the code (`https://github.com/NVlabs/Tool-N1`) will further accelerate research and development in this area. An open-source, high-performing tool-calling model serves as a strong baseline and a practical foundation for future innovations, fostering collaboration and competition in the AI community.

In conclusion, Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 represents a significant step forward in making LLMs more functional and intelligent. By refining the training methodology through rule-based reinforcement learning, it paves the way for more robust, generalized, and cost-effective development of AI agents capable of truly "thinking" and "acting" with external tools.
