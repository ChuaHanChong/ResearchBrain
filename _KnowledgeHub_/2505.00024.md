# Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1: Exploring Tool-Using Language Models with Reinforced Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA, Pennsylvania State University, and the University of Washington introduce Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1, which applies rule-based reinforcement learning (R1-style RL) to train large language models for tool-calling. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on various tool-use benchmarks, outperforming models like GPT-4o, and demonstrates that pure RL training can be more effective than traditional supervised fine-tuning or SFT-then-RL pipelines.

## Problem

- Existing tool-augmented Large Language Models primarily rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which often leads to imitative reasoning rather than robust, generalized understanding.
- SFT-trained models frequently struggle to generalize to novel or out-of-distribution tool-use scenarios due to a lack of explicit reasoning supervision.
- SFT's rigid next-token prediction penalizes semantically equivalent but syntactically different tool-call outputs, limiting the model's flexibility.

## Method

- The paper applies a ==rule-based (R1-style) reinforcement learning paradigm== using the GRPO algorithm to train LLMs for general-purpose tool-calling.
- A ==simple binary reward function== is employed, granting a reward of 1 only if the output adheres to a specified structured format and the predicted tool call(s) exactly match the ground-truth functionally.
- A lightweight =='thinking template'== guides the LLM to produce internal reasoning within XML tags followed by tool invocations as JSON, ensuring structured output while allowing flexible reasoning.

## Results

- Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 models achieved state-of-the-art performance on tool-calling benchmarks, with Tool-N1-14B improving approximately **2%** over GPT-4o on BFCL and surpassing GPT-4o by **5.03%** on APIBank.
- Pure R1-style RL consistently outperformed both Reason-SFT and No-Reason SFT under equal data budgets, demonstrating the efficacy of the RL-centric approach.
- The training method scaled effectively with model size (**7B** and **14B** models showing significant gains) and generalized well across different LLM backbones like Qwen2.5-Instruct and LLaMA series.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The commonly adopted SFT-then-RL paradigm does not necessarily outperform pure RL for tool-calling tasks under equal data budgets, challenging established RLHF assumptions.
> - A simple binary reward scheme (all-or-nothing for format and tool-call correctness) is more effective than fine-grained partial rewards, preventing 'reward hacking' and encouraging full functional accuracy.
> - Explicitly guiding the model to output structured reasoning within specific tags, even without supervising the reasoning content, is crucial for performance.
