# TWIST2: Scalable, Portable, and Holistic Humanoid Data Collection System

> [!summary] Summary
> TWIST2 provides a scalable and portable system for collecting holistic humanoid data, leveraging affordable VR and a custom robot neck for whole-body teleoperation with active egocentric vision. This approach allows efficient generation of diverse human demonstrations, exemplified by collecting 100 bimanual pick & place demonstrations in 18.5 minutes, and enables training real-world autonomous policies.

## Problem

- Humanoid robotics research faces a scarcity of high-quality, large-scale demonstration data, limiting the application of data-driven learning methods.
- Existing full whole-body teleoperation systems for humanoids depend on expensive, non-portable motion capture setups, restricting data collection to specialized lab environments.
- Developing general visuomotor policies for humanoids is challenging due to the need for coordinated whole-body control and integrated egocentric vision for complex tasks.

## Method

- Developed TWIST2, a portable, low-cost human data source that replaces traditional MoCap by using a PICO 4U VR headset and ankle trackers for real-time full human body pose estimation.
- Designed and integrated a custom, low-cost 2-DoF neck module for the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, equipped with a ZED Mini stereo camera to provide active egocentric stereo vision.
- Implemented a two-level hierarchical control framework comprising a low-level reinforcement learning-trained motion tracker and a high-level command generator, either from human teleoperation or a visuomotor policy.

## Results

- TWIST2 successfully demonstrated teleoperation of complex, long-horizon whole-body dexterous tasks, such as folding towels and mobile object transport through doors.
- The system achieved high data collection efficiency, with an expert teleoperator collecting approximately 100 bimanual pick & place demonstrations in 18.5 minutes (11 seconds/episode) with a 100% success rate.
- Autonomous policies, trained on data collected via TWIST2, successfully performed real-world tasks like whole-body dexterous pick & place and kicking a T-shaped box to a target, demonstrating robust autonomous capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining consumer-grade VR hardware with a custom active vision module enables highly effective, portable, and scalable whole-body teleoperation for humanoid robots.
> - Egocentric active perception and smooth whole-body tracking are crucial for allowing humanoids to execute natural, long-horizon, and mobile manipulation tasks efficiently.
> - Hierarchical visuomotor policies, trained on holistically collected data, can achieve vision-based autonomous control for diverse and complex whole-body humanoid tasks in real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is undergoing a significant transformation, heavily influenced by the data-driven paradigms that have revolutionized other areas of artificial intelligence, such as natural language processing (e.g., GPT-4) and computer vision. Large-scale datasets and robust data collection infrastructures have been pivotal in enabling advanced capabilities in areas like bimanual manipulation, as evidenced by models such as π0 and π0.5. However, the paper critically identifies a significant gap: this data-driven revolution has largely bypassed humanoid robotics. Humanoid robots, with their complex high-dimensional bodies capable of both locomotion and dexterous manipulation, represent a formidable challenge for data collection and control.

Existing humanoid teleoperation systems, crucial for generating expert demonstrations, are categorized by the authors into three groups:
1. **Decoupled Control:** Systems like HOMIE control the lower and upper body separately, hindering natural whole-body coordination.
2. **Partial Whole-Body Control:** Approaches such as AMO and CLONE coordinate selected body segments (arms, torso) while legs track simple velocity commands, limiting dynamic whole-body skills. These often leverage VR for practicality but fall short in capturing intricate human motions.
3. **Full Whole-Body Control:** Systems like HumanPlus and the predecessor TWIST aim to track human body poses across all joints, including legs, arms, and torso, offering the greatest potential for versatility. However, these often rely on expensive, non-portable motion capture (MoCap) systems, confining them to laboratory settings and making scalable data collection impractical.

TWIST2 directly addresses this critical void by proposing a solution that combines the comprehensive control of full whole-body systems with the portability and scalability previously lacking. By moving away from expensive MoCap setups towards lightweight, consumer-grade VR devices (PICO 4U) and introducing an innovative, low-cost robot neck, TWIST2 democratizes high-quality humanoid data collection. This is a significant step towards enabling the "data-driven revolution" for humanoid robots, similar to how large datasets have fueled progress in other robotic domains.

Furthermore, the work contributes to the landscape of visuomotor control for humanoids. Previous visual control methods often relied on LiDAR for locomotion or were limited to simpler tasks and static interactions. TWIST2's hierarchical visuomotor policy, trained on its uniquely collected whole-body data, moves beyond simplified commands like root velocity, enabling full humanoid body control based on egocentric vision. This positions TWIST2 as a foundational system for developing more general and versatile autonomous humanoid behaviors in complex, interactive environments. The paper's emphasis on open-sourcing aligns with a growing community effort to standardize hardware and share data, accelerating collective progress in humanoid research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of TWIST2 stem directly from the identified limitations in current humanoid robotics research, particularly concerning data collection and autonomous control:

1. **Enable Scalable and Holistic Data Collection for Humanoids:** The overarching goal is to overcome the current scarcity of high-quality, large-scale demonstration data for humanoid robots. The authors note that while data has driven breakthroughs in other ML fields and even bimanual manipulation, humanoid robotics lacks comparable data collection frameworks. TWIST2 aims to provide a system that can collect hundreds of successful demonstrations efficiently and consistently.
2. **Achieve Full Whole-Body Control without Expensive MoCap:** Current full whole-body teleoperation systems, while promising, are tethered to costly and non-portable motion capture facilities. A key objective of TWIST2 is to retain the advantages of full whole-body control – allowing for natural and versatile human-like movements, including dexterous manipulation and dynamic locomotion – but liberate it from the constraints of traditional MoCap setups, making it accessible and deployable "in the wild."
3. **Ensure Portability and Fast Setup:** A core motivation is to make the data collection system portable and easy to set up, moving away from complex, time-consuming calibrations. The goal is to allow quick deployment and operation, exemplified by the "1 minute from walk-in to finish setup" claim.
4. **Integrate Egocentric Vision for Enhanced Teleoperation:** The authors recognize that egocentric vision is crucial for human-like task execution and fine-grained manipulation. An explicit objective is to integrate active egocentric stereo vision into the teleoperation framework, addressing the limitations of fixed or third-person views for complex, long-horizon tasks. This led to the design of a custom robot neck.
5. **Develop a Single-Operator System for Efficiency:** Many existing teleoperation systems require multiple operators (e.g., one for upper body, one for lower body, or one for system control). A key objective for TWIST2 is to streamline the operation to a single user, from initiating tasks to recording data, thus improving efficiency and practical usability.
6. **Pioneer Hierarchical Visuomotor Policy Learning for Full Humanoids:** Beyond data collection, TWIST2 aims to demonstrate the utility of the collected data by training sophisticated autonomous policies. The motivation is to develop a hierarchical framework that enables vision-based control of the *entire* humanoid body, going beyond simpler approaches that only control root velocity or specific segments. This seeks to unlock truly versatile and autonomous humanoid behaviors.
7. **Open-Source the System, Data, and Models:** A strong motivation is to contribute back to the research community by making the entire system, collected dataset, and trained models fully reproducible and accessible. This aims to standardize humanoid hardware usage (e.g., Unitree G1) and democratize data collection and research in this challenging field.

In essence, TWIST2 is motivated by the desire to accelerate progress in humanoid robotics by making the process of collecting high-quality, holistic human demonstrations dramatically more accessible, efficient, and impactful, ultimately enabling a new generation of autonomous, data-driven humanoid skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TWIST2 employs a multi-faceted approach, integrating hardware innovation, refined motion processing, and advanced policy learning techniques to achieve its objectives. The system is designed as a two-level hierarchical control framework, comprising a low-level motion tracker and a high-level command generator (either human teleoperator or visuomotor policy).

**A. Humanoid Robot with Active Vision:**
- **Robot Platform:** The system utilizes the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, equipped with 29 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) for its body (3 DoF waist, two 6 DoF legs, two 7 DoF arms) and two 7 DoF Dex31 hands.
- **TWIST2 Neck (Add-on Low-Cost Neck):** A critical innovation is the custom-designed, low-cost (approximately $250) 2-DoF (yaw and pitch) neck module. This neck, built using two Dynamixel XC330-T288 motors and 3D-printed parts, attaches seamlessly to the Unitree G1 without modifying its original head. It hosts a ZED Mini stereo camera (an additional $400 cost) to provide active egocentric stereo vision, deemed essential for effective teleoperation. A MuJoCo simulation model of the neck is also provided for standardization.

**B. Portable MoCap-Free Whole-Body Human Data Source:**
- **VR-based Motion Capture:** To replace traditional MoCap, TWIST2 leverages the PICO 4U VR headset ($1000) combined with two PICO Motion Trackers, which are bound to the human's ankles. This setup provides real-time full human body poses (global translations and rotations for each body part).
- **Simplified Setup:** A key advantage is its portability and rapid setup time (around 1 minute) and minimal calibration compared to optical MoCap systems. The XRoboToolkit is used for streaming motion data from PICO at 100Hz.

**C. Holistic Human-to-Humanoid Retargeting:**
- **Body Retargeting:** An adapted version of GMR, (General Motion Retargeting) is used. This modification addresses PICO's less accurate global pose estimation by adjusting the two-stage optimization. For the lower body, both position and rotation constraints are optimized, ensuring less foot sliding. For the upper body, only rotation constraints are optimized, allowing for smoother "teleportation" experiences without artifacts from global-pose jumps. All human positions are measured in a pelvis-centric frame.
- **Hand Retargeting:** Given the Unitree Dex31 hand's limited 3-finger DoF, it's simplified to a gripper-like functionality. Hand control is via PICO handheld controller buttons, defining an open and close pose (q_open, q_close). A scalar grasp command interpolates between these.
- **Neck Retargeting:** The robot neck's yaw and pitch angles are directly derived from the relative rotation between the human head and spine, allowing the robot to mimic human head movements naturally.

**D. Training General Motion Trackers for Low-Level Control:**
- **Task-Agnostic Controller:** A low-level motion tracking controller (π_low) is trained to translate high-level reference motion commands (p_cmd) into desired joint positions (q_tgt) for the robot.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** This controller is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) on a curated dataset of approximately 20k motion clips. This dataset combines retargeted GMR data, original TWIST data (AMASS, OMOMO, in-house MoCap), and crucially, a small set (73 motions) collected via PICO to bridge the domain gap.
- **Reward Formulation:** The reward function includes a tracking term (exponential decay based on command-actual state difference) and regularization terms (e.g., action change penalty). The actor network uses a convolutional history encoder and an MLP backbone for efficient learning.

**E. Scalable Humanoid Data Collection:**
- **Egocentric Whole-Body Teleoperation:** Human motions from PICO are mapped to robot commands (p_cmd) via retargeting and sent to the low-level controller (π_low). Stereoscopic vision from ZED Mini is streamed to the PICO VR headset via GStreamer, providing depth perception.
- **Single-Operator Design:** PICO's handheld controllers are programmed to act as a control center, enabling the demonstrator to start/pause/terminate teleoperation and record data without external assistance, enhancing efficiency.
- **Safe Control:** Features like motion interpolation during pauses and transitions ensure smooth and safe operation, preventing sudden robot movements.
- **Low System Delay:** All system modules stream above 50Hz, ensuring an overall delay lower than 0.1s, a significant improvement over prior work.
- **Data Filtering:** A post-processing GUI allows segmentation of long trajectories into task-specific episodes and removal of idle actions or failure cases.

**F. Whole-Body Visuomotor Policy Learning:**
- **Hierarchical Framework:** Building on the collected data, a high-level visuomotor policy (π_auto_high) is trained to generate p_cmd based on egocentric vision and proprioception, which then feeds into the low-level motion tracker.
- **Observation & Action Space:** Visual input consists of 224x224 RGB images from ZED Mini. Proprioception uses historical command sequences (p_cmd) instead of raw robot states to decouple the high-level policy from the low-level controller and mitigate error accumulation. The action space is the same p_cmd vector used in teleoperation.
- **Network Architecture:** Diffusion Policy is employed for policy learning, using 1D convolutional blocks for temporal modeling. A ResNet-18 backbone pre-trained with R3M encodes visual observations.
- **Data Augmentation & Regularization:** Gaussian noise is injected into proprioceptive inputs (10%) and comprehensive visual augmentations (cropping, rotation, color jittering) are applied to improve robustness and generalization.
- **Deployment:** The trained Diffusion Policy is converted to ONNX for efficient real-time inference (20Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 4090).

This systematic methodology, from hardware design to data-driven policy learning, provides a comprehensive solution for addressing the challenges of humanoid robot control and data collection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with TWIST2 yield several significant findings across teleoperation, data collection efficiency, and autonomous policy execution.

**A. Long-Horizon Teleoperation Capabilities:**
- **Complex Dexterous Tasks:** TWIST2 successfully demonstrates teleoperation of complex, long-horizon whole-body dexterous tasks, such as folding towels (continuous folding of 3 randomly placed towels) and transporting objects through a door (picking up baskets, pushing a door open, walking through, and placing objects on a shelf). These tasks require fine-grained control of wrists, hands, active vision, and coordinated whole-body reaching and locomotion.
- **Importance of Egocentric Active Perception:** The authors highlight that egocentric active perception and smooth whole-body tracking (as opposed to decoupled control) are crucial for enabling such natural, smooth, long-horizon, and mobile manipulation tasks.

**B. Efficient Data Collection:**
- **Scalable Data Generation:** TWIST2 proves highly effective for collecting imitation learning data. An expert teleoperator can collect approximately 100 successful bimanual pick & place demonstrations in 18.5 minutes (average 11 seconds per episode) or around 50 successful mobile pick & place demonstrations in 19.5 minutes (average 25 seconds per episode), all with a 100% success rate. This demonstrates significant efficiency and scalability compared to traditional methods.
- **Impact of System Components (User Study):** A user study with novice and expert users quantifies the importance of TWIST2's design choices:
 - **Stereo Vision:** Crucial for accurate depth estimation, without which users tend to grasp above objects, leading to high failure rates. TWIST2 achieved an average of 67.8 seconds to collect 10 demos with stereo, compared to 98.3 seconds without.
 - **Neck Module:** Essential for perceiving objects beyond a fixed field of view. Without the neck, teleoperation became extremely challenging (average 112.3 seconds for 10 demos, compared to 67.8 seconds with TWIST2).
 - **Egocentric View:** While an expert could collect data remarkably fast (43 seconds for 10 demos) using a third-person view *while standing next to the robot*, this is not feasible for remote or long-horizon mobile manipulation. Egocentric vision enables versatile remote control.

**C. Whole-Body Policy Learning Results (Autonomous Tasks):**
- **Whole-Body Dexterous Pick & Place (WB-Dex):** The autonomous policy, trained on 170 human demonstrations, allows the robot to pick up a cup from a shelf and place it into a box on the ground. The policy reliably reaches for the cup; however, grasping success is sensitive to precise control due to the cup's lightness, leading to some grasp failures.
- **Kick T-shaped Box to Target (Kick-T):** Trained on 50 demonstrations, the policy enables the robot to use its foot to kick a T-shaped box towards a target region. The policy successfully transports the box in 6 out of 7 trials, demonstrating robust kicking behavior and balance maintenance (e.g., kicking with the left foot, then stepping forward with the right). Currently, it is limited to forward kicks.

These results collectively demonstrate that TWIST2 not only provides a highly effective and practical platform for collecting diverse, high-quality human demonstrations but also enables the successful training of vision-based autonomous policies for complex whole-body humanoid tasks in the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TWIST2 represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics, with the potential to fundamentally shift how researchers approach data collection and policy learning for these complex platforms. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Democratization of Humanoid Data Collection:** By replacing expensive, non-portable motion capture systems with affordable, lightweight VR devices (PICO 4U, custom neck), TWIST2 significantly lowers the barrier to entry for high-quality humanoid data collection. This "democratization" means that more labs and researchers, especially those without access to specialized MoCap studios, can engage in advanced humanoid research, potentially leading to a broader array of innovations.
2. **Enabling Scalable, High-Quality Datasets:** The demonstrated ability to collect hundreds of successful demonstrations in a short timeframe, coupled with holistic whole-body control and egocentric vision, paves the way for truly large-scale, diverse, and high-fidelity humanoid datasets. Such datasets are crucial for leveraging modern data-driven machine learning techniques, mirroring the success seen in LLMs and VLA models.
3. **Foundation for General-Purpose Humanoid AI:** The hierarchical visuomotor policy, trained on TWIST2 data, is a crucial step towards developing general-purpose humanoid AI. By allowing vision-based autonomous control of the *full* humanoid body (not just simplified commands), it unlocks possibilities for robots to perform a vast range of tasks requiring coordinated loco-manipulation, interaction with complex environments, and dynamic skills. This moves humanoids closer to real-world applicability beyond structured lab settings.
4. **Advancement in Teleoperation Systems:** TWIST2 establishes new benchmarks for teleoperation systems in terms of portability, single-operator efficiency, low latency, and the integration of essential features like active egocentric stereo vision. The user study clearly quantifies the critical role of these design choices in successful and efficient human-robot interaction.
5. **Standardization and Open Science:** The explicit commitment to open-sourcing the entire system (hardware designs, software), the collected data, and trained models is a profound contribution. This fosters reproducibility, encourages community collaboration, and helps standardize research on platforms like the Unitree G1. Standardized hardware and shared datasets are vital for cumulative progress in robotics.
6. **Addressing Key Limitations in Humanoid Research:** The paper directly tackles the critical "missing piece" in humanoid development: the lack of effective data collection frameworks. By providing a tangible, accessible solution, TWIST2 can accelerate research into areas like robust sim-to-real transfer, dexterous manipulation in unstructured environments, and complex human-robot collaboration.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including challenges with highly dynamic movements (e.g., sprinting) for the general motion tracker and the PICO system's pose estimation being less accurate than high-cost MoCap for specific joints (elbows, knees). These areas suggest future work for improving motion tracking robustness and VR-based pose estimation accuracy.

Despite these, TWIST2's holistic approach, from hardware innovation to policy learning and open-sourcing, positions it as a pivotal contribution. It is not merely an incremental improvement but a system designed to catalyze the next generation of data-driven, versatile humanoid robots. Its impact will likely be felt across academic research and potentially in the development of practical humanoid applications.
