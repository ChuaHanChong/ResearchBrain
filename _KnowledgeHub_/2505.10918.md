# Unleashing Humanoid Reaching Potential via Real-world-Ready Skill Space

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Real-world-Ready Skill Space (R²S²), a framework that enables humanoid robots to achieve a large reachable workspace for complex manipulation tasks by learning a unified, continuous skill representation. R²S² demonstrated 100% success rates in simulation across challenging reaching tasks and robust real-world transferability with up to 100% success on a Unitree H1 humanoid.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with complex whole-body control (WBC) tasks requiring a large reachable space due to optimization difficulties in end-to-end learning from scratch.
- Poor sim2real transferability for multi-skill WBC is a significant challenge, often requiring extensive reward engineering, curriculum design, or iterative real-world deployments.
- Existing hierarchical RL methods often rely on complex reward shaping, while animation-focused skill spaces face a 'cross-embodiment gap' when applied to robots, leading to stability issues.

## Method

- A structural skill prior, Real-world-==Ready Skill Space== (R²S²), is proposed, composed of a library of individually pre-trained, real-world-ready primitive skills (locomotion, body-pose-adjustment, hand-reaching).
- A heterogeneous skill ensembling stage learns a unified neural skill representation via a conditional variational information bottleneck (CVAE), using a dynamic blend of Imitation Learning and Reinforcement Learning.
- A high-level planner is trained via RL in this continuous latent skill space, leveraging the frozen CVAE decoder to generate low-level actions for task-specific objectives.

## Results

- R²S² achieved **100%** success rates and low distance errors (**0.02**-0.04m) across four challenging reaching tasks (Single/Dual-point Touch, Shelf Touch, Box Pickup) in simulation.
- The framework demonstrated robust real-world transferability on Unitree G1 and H1 humanoids, maintaining **100%** success for point and shelf touch, and **90%** for box pickup tasks (**0.03**-0.08m DE).
- Ablation studies confirmed that all components (primitive skills, skill ensembling, latent space) are critical, with ablated versions showing significantly degraded performance (0-**56%** SR) and lacking sim2real transferability compared to the full R²S².

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex whole-body tasks into individually pre-trained, real-world-ready primitive skills significantly reduces optimization difficulty and enhances stability for sim2real transfer.
> - Dynamically combining imitation learning with reinforcement learning during skill ensembling effectively expands coordination and transition capabilities while preventing catastrophic forgetting.
> - A unified, continuous latent skill space (learned via CVAE) provides an efficient and structural action space for high-level planning, outperforming direct command space control or discrete skill indicators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are envisioned to assist humans in diverse daily tasks, many of which involve interacting with objects across a wide range of heights and distances. This necessitates a large and versatile reachable space, posing a complex challenge in whole-body control (WBC). The current research landscape for humanoid WBC presents several hurdles that this work aims to address.

Traditional model-based control methods for humanoids often face limitations due to inherent imperfections in system modeling and environmental disturbances, which can compromise stability and performance in unstructured environments. Recent advancements in end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) have shown promise in humanoid WBC, enabling new capabilities such as locomotion and whole-body manipulation. However, these learning-from-scratch approaches, especially for complex, multi-skill tasks like achieving a large reachable space, typically encounter significant optimization difficulties. The coupling between different skills, as well as between planning and control in end-to-end policies, makes diagnosing and mitigating the sim2real gap particularly challenging, often requiring intricate reward engineering, curriculum design, or trajectory optimization (e.g., in works like AMO and HOMIE). Such reliance on specialized engineering limits generalizability and scalability to new or more complex skills.

Another related area is skill space learning, which has been extensively explored in physics-based character animation (e.g., PULSE). These methods often learn latent skill spaces from human motion capture datasets to reuse motion priors. However, directly applying these techniques to humanoid robots is problematic due to the "cross-embodiment gap," where retargeting human motions to diverse robot morphologies often requires substantial manual effort and can introduce artifacts. Furthermore, skill priors learned directly from human motions, while diverse, can be difficult to constrain and regularize for stable real-world robotic deployment, leading to poor sim2real transferability for certain challenging motions.

This work positions itself to overcome these limitations by proposing a structural skill prior, "Real-world-Ready Skill Space (R²S²)," for humanoid WBC. Unlike existing RL methods that struggle with optimization and sim2real transfer for multi-skill learning, R²S² inherits knowledge from individually pre-trained, real-world-ready primitive skills. This decoupling simplifies training and sim2real evaluation. It further expands these skills into a unified neural representation that addresses the coordination and transition issues inherent in separate primitive skills and the problem of mismatched command spaces. In contrast to animation-focused skill spaces, R²S² derives its priors from robot-specific primitive skills, guaranteeing real-world transferability. The research aims to provide a more efficient and robust framework for autonomous humanoid WBC tasks, requiring minimal reward engineering and facilitating broader applications, including large-workspace teleoperation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to effectively utilize a large reachable space in the 3D world, allowing them to interact with objects at various heights and distances, similar to human capabilities. This large workspace is considered essential for humanoids to perform a wide array of daily assistance tasks, ranging from accessing overhead shelves to picking up items from the floor.

The motivation for developing the Real-world-Ready Skill Space (R²S²) framework stems from several detailed challenges identified in the current state of humanoid whole-body control (WBC):

- **Complexity of Whole-Body Control:** Achieving a large reachable space is inherently a complex WBC problem. It demands the mastery and intelligent coordination of multiple distinct motor skills, including base positioning and reorientation, dynamic height and body posture adjustments, and precise end-effector (hand) pose control. All of this must occur within a dynamically unstable bipedal system.
- **Optimization Difficulty for Learning from Scratch:** When attempting to train an end-to-end policy for such complex, multi-skill WBC tasks from scratch using reinforcement learning, significant optimization difficulties are encountered. The vast action space and the intricate interplay between various body parts make it challenging for RL algorithms to converge to effective and stable behaviors.
- **Poor Sim2Real Transferability:** Policies learned in simulation often exhibit poor transferability to the real world, a phenomenon known as the "sim2real gap." For complex WBC problems involving multiple coupled skills (e.g., locomotion combined with manipulation), diagnosing the source of discrepancies between simulated and real-world behavior and designing effective constraints to mitigate them becomes exceptionally challenging. Existing methods often require iterative sim2real deployments, which are time-consuming and labor-intensive.
- **Limitations of Existing Hierarchical RL Approaches:** Current hierarchical humanoid control frameworks, such as AMO and HOMIE, often alleviate some complexity by employing hierarchical structures. However, they frequently rely on highly intricate reward engineering, complex curriculum designs to balance rewards, or extensive trajectory optimization to guide learning. This complexity limits their scalability to new skills and their adaptability to diverse scenarios. Moreover, these methods typically operate in the primary command space of low-level controllers, which can be discontinuous and less amenable to efficient high-level planning.
- **Need for a Structural Skill Prior:** To address the optimization and sim2real difficulties, the authors are motivated to design a form of prior knowledge. This prior should assist WBC tasks by reducing the learning burden, enhancing stability, and ensuring real-world readiness.
- **Bridging Primitive Skills and Complex Tasks:** While pre-training individual "primitive skills" (e.g., pure locomotion, pure body posture adjustment) can yield real-world-ready behaviors, these isolated skills are insufficient for complex WBC tasks. They lack seamless coordination and smooth transitions between each other, leading to "out-of-distribution" scenarios during multi-skill execution. Additionally, their command spaces are often mismatched, preventing a unified representation for high-level planning.
- **Expanding Teleoperation Capabilities:** Beyond autonomous execution, a secondary motivation is to leverage the learned skill space to support whole-body teleoperation. The goal is to enable teleoperation across the humanoid's full reachable workspace, moving beyond the limitations of simple table-top pick-and-place tasks often targeted by existing teleoperation systems.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop a principled, efficient, and sim2real transferable method that enables humanoid robots to robustly perform complex whole-body tasks requiring a large interaction space, by systematically structuring and unifying diverse motor skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Real-world-Ready Skill Space (R²S²), is a structural skill prior designed to facilitate efficient and sim2real transferable autonomous whole-body control (WBC) task execution. The framework consists of three sequential stages: Primitive Skill Library construction, Heterogeneous Skill Ensembling, and High-Level Planning in the learned skill space. All policies are trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm within the Isaac Gym simulation environment. Sim-to-real transfer is enhanced by applying random Gaussian noise to observations, randomizing dynamics parameters, and introducing random external force disturbances during training.

**A. Primitive Skill Library**
The initial stage involves creating a library of individually pre-trained primitive skills. These skills are designed to be task-agnostic and generalizable, each capable of robust real-world performance. The authors selected three categories of skills essential for large-space reaching: locomotion, body-pose-adjustment (for changing body height and bending), and hand-reaching. This decomposition is intended to prevent performance degradation often observed in multi-skill learning from scratch.

Each primitive skill is implemented as a goal-conditioned RL policy, $\pi_{\text{prim}}: \mathcal{G}_{\text{prim}} \times \mathcal{S}_{\text{prim}} \to \mathcal{A}_{\text{prim}}$.
- $\mathcal{G}_{\text{prim}}$ represents goal commands specific to the skill (e.g., desired linear/angular velocities for locomotion, target body height/pitch for body-pose-adjustment, 6D end-effector pose for hand-reaching).
- $\mathcal{S}_{\text{prim}}$ includes the robot’s proprioceptive observations (angular velocity, projected gravity, body-part DOF positions, body-part DOF velocities) and the previous low-level action. Importantly, each skill policy observes only information relevant to its controlled body parts.
- $\mathcal{A}_{\text{prim}}$ consists of PD targets for the robot's specific body-part joints, which are then fed into a PD controller to compute torques. Other joints not associated with the active skill are held fixed.

The training reward for primitive skills, $r_{\text{prim}}$, is composed of three terms:
- $r_{\text{command}}$: Encourages tracking the specified skill target goals (e.g., velocity tracking, pose tracking).
- $r_{\text{behavior}}$: Skill-specific constraints crucial for sim2real stability. For locomotion, this includes rewards for gait velocity and force tracking, structured around alternating swing and stance phases using Von Mises distributions. For body-pose-adjustment, it incorporates rewards for kinematic symmetry (base roll error, leg position symmetry) and dynamic balance (leg torque symmetry, contact with ground). Hand-reaching does not require specific $r_{\text{behavior}}$ due to its comparative ease of sim2real transfer.
- $r_{\text{regularization}}$: Skill-agnostic regularization terms, such as penalties for high action acceleration/rate, collision, and deviation from default joint positions.

**B. Heterogeneous Skill Ensembling**
The core stage addresses the limitations of isolated primitive skills: their inability to seamlessly coordinate and transition, and their mismatched command spaces. This stage aims to learn a unified neural skill representation by training an ensemble student policy, $\pi_{\text{ensem}}(a_t|s_t, g_t)$, utilizing a conditional variational information bottleneck (CVAE).

- **Expanding Coordination and Transition Capability:** A heterogeneous skill training environment is designed to generate scenarios requiring both skill coordination (e.g., walking while simultaneously reaching with an arm) and skill transition (e.g., switching from locomotion to body-pose-adjustment for the lower body). At each timestep, two pre-trained primitive skills (one for the lower body, one for the upper body) act as teacher policies. The student policy's goal input $g_t$ includes a skill indicator for the active teacher.
 To learn new coordination and transition behaviors while retaining real-world transferability, a dynamic combination of Imitation Learning (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) is employed. The loss function is $L_{\text{Ensem}} = \lambda_1 L_{\text{DAgger}} + \lambda_2 L_{\text{PPO}}$.
 - $L_{\text{DAgger}}$ is an imitation loss (mean squared error) that encourages the student policy to mimic the combined actions of the teacher policies ($a_t^*$). The weight $\lambda_1$ gradually decreases from 0.95 to 0.05.
 - $L_{\text{PPO}}$ is an RL loss using a reward function that combines the $r_{\text{command}}$ and $r_{\text{behavior}}$ terms from the primitive skill training, along with $r_{\text{regularization}}$. The weight $\lambda_2$ inversely adjusts to $\lambda_1$. This dynamic weighting encourages initial imitation followed by exploration, and maintaining IL supervision throughout training is designed to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
- **Learning a Unified Neural Skill Representation:** To overcome the issue of mismatched command spaces for high-level planning, the student policy is structured as a CVAE. This maps heterogeneous skills into a continuous, unified latent skill space $z$.
 - An encoder $E(z_t|s_t, g_t)$ models the latent codes, conditioned on the current state and goal.
 - A decoder $D(a_t|s_t, z_t)$ maps the sampled latent code $z_t$ back to per-joint actions $a_t$, conditioned on the state.
 - A learnable conditional prior $P(z_t|s_t)$ is incorporated to capture state-based action distributions.
 The total loss for training $\pi_{\text{ensem}}$ includes $L_{\text{Ensem}}$ plus two regularization terms:
 - $L_{\text{Regu}} = \left\Vert \mu_e(s_t, g_t) - \mu_e(s_{t+1}, g_{t+1}) \right\Vert$ encourages temporal consistency between consecutive latent codes, structuring the skill space for smoother transitions.
 - $L_{\text{KL}} = D_{\text{KL}}(E(z_t|s_t, g_t) \Vert P(z_t|s_t))$ regularizes the latent code distribution to be close to the learnable prior.

**C. High-Level Planning in Real-World-Ready Skill Space**
In the final stage, the learned R²S² is leveraged for autonomous WBC tasks. A task-specific high-level planner $\pi_{\text{plan}}(z_t|s_{\text{plan}_t}, g_{\text{plan}_t})$ is trained using RL. The planner's action space is now the continuous latent space $z_t$. The sampled $z_t$ is then fed into the *frozen* decoder $D$ from the ensembling stage to generate low-level per-joint actions $a_t$.

The reward for the high-level planner, $r_{\text{plan}}$, is defined as $r_{\text{task}} + r_{\text{regularization}}$.
- $r_{\text{task}}$ specifies the task execution objective (e.g., single-point touch, dual-point touch, shelf touch, box pickup).
- $r_{\text{regularization}}$ is reused from the primitive skill stage to maintain motion stability.
A key advantage here is that only $r_{\text{task}}$ needs to be defined, as the underlying skill complexity and sim2real transferability are encoded within R²S².

**Teleoperation System**
As a beneficial side effect, the ensemble student policy $\pi_{\text{ensem}}$ can also be used to build a whole-body teleoperation system. This system integrates a stereo RGB camera on the robot head for ego-centric observation streaming to a user's VR device. The user's hand pose, captured by the VR device, is retargeted to the humanoid's hands using Inverse Kinematics. Split joysticks allow independent control of lower-body movement and hand gripping, without interfering with the hand pose commands.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validates R²S² through extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world, utilizing high-DOF Unitree G1 (29 DOFs) and full-sized Unitree H1 (1.8 meters tall) humanoid platforms. The findings address two primary questions: R²S²'s performance compared to baselines, and the contribution of its individual components.

**A. Performance of R²S² on Whole-Body Control Tasks**
Four challenging goal-reaching WBC tasks were selected to evaluate the system's ability to unleash humanoid reaching potential: Single-point Touch, Dual-point Touch, Shelf Touch (involving obstacle avoidance), and Box Pickup (requiring lifting to a target height). Performance was measured by Success Rate (SR, %) and Distance Error (DE, m).

- **Comparison with Baselines (Table III):**
 - **Vanilla PPO and Vanilla DreamerV3:** These model-free and model-based RL methods, without manually designed priors, exhibited poor performance in simulation, with low success rates (0-30%) and high distance errors (0.15-0.65m). They failed to achieve sim2real transferability.
 - **HumanoidBench:** This hierarchical RL framework, while achieving 100% SR and low DE (0.04m) for simpler point-touch tasks in simulation (due to auxiliary rewards), showed significant degradation for more complex tasks like Shelf Touch (47.69% SR, 0.13m DE) and failed completely at Box Pickup (0% SR, 0.44m DE). Its performance in complex scenarios was hampered by heavy vibration, precluding sim2real transfer.
 - **Our Method (R²S²):** Achieved 100% success rate and low distance errors (0.02-0.04m) across all four tasks in simulation. Furthermore, R²S² demonstrated robust sim2real transferability. In real-world experiments, it maintained 100% SR for point-touch and shelf touch (0.03m DE) and a 90% SR for Box Pickup (0.08m DE).
- **Motor Behavior (Figure 3):** Visual comparisons indicated that R²S² produced more stable motor behaviors compared to the baselines, contributing to its superior sim2real transferability.

**B. Evaluation of Real-world-Ready Skill Space Components**
Ablation studies were conducted in simulation to quantify the contribution of each major design component of R²S² (Table IV).

- **R²S² w/o PS (Primitive Skills):** Training a multi-skill controller from scratch, without pre-trained individual primitive skills, resulted in poor task performance (SR 30-50%, DE 0.12-0.26m) and lacked sim2real transferability. This was attributed to the difficulty in learning low-level skills from scratch, leading to unstable motor behaviors and low skill command tracking accuracy (Figure 4).
- **R²S² w/o SE (Skill Expansion):** When the heterogeneous skill ensembling stage used only imitation learning (IL) without reinforcement learning (RL) exploration, the performance was significantly degraded (SR 22-30%, DE 0.15-0.33m), and sim2real transferability was lost. This variant struggled with coordination and transition between skills, often leading to unsafe behaviors and falls during skill switching, indicating the importance of explicit skill expansion.
- **R²S² w/ Seq-ILRL (Sequential IL and RL):** A sequential training strategy, where IL pre-training was followed by exclusive RL fine-tuning, also yielded poor results (SR 29-54%, DE 0.09-0.24m) and failed to transfer to the real world. This was attributed to "catastrophic forgetting," where the policy lost knowledge acquired during IL once RL fine-tuning began.
- **R²S² w/o LS (Latent Space):** Replacing the CVAE-based student policy with an MLP-based one, where the high-level planner directly outputted skill indicators and mismatched primary command spaces, showed moderate performance (SR 44-56%, DE 0.07-0.19m). While the low-level skills could be learned and transferred to the real world, the planning policy struggled with inefficient RL exploration due to the non-structural, discontinuous action space. This highlights the effectiveness of the continuous latent skill space in facilitating high-level planning.
- **Our Method (R²S²):** The full R²S² framework consistently outperformed all ablated versions, achieving 100% SR and low DE across all tasks.

These results indicate that each component of R²S²—the initial decomposition into real-world-ready primitive skills, the dynamic combination of IL and RL for skill expansion during ensembling, and the unified continuous latent skill representation—contributes significantly to the overall system's superior performance and robust sim2real transferability for complex WBC tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by introducing a structured and efficient approach to address the challenge of achieving a large and versatile reachable space for humanoid robots. The proposed Real-world-Ready Skill Space (R²S²) framework has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Humanoid Autonomy and Interaction Capabilities:** By enabling humanoids to robustly perform complex whole-body control tasks across a wide reachable space, R²S² moves these robots closer to assisting humans in practical daily scenarios. This capability is fundamental for humanoids to interact effectively with their environment, manipulate objects at varying elevations and distances, and navigate cluttered spaces, thereby broadening their potential applications in service robotics, logistics, and hazardous environments.
- **Improved Efficiency and Robustness in Skill Acquisition:** The methodology significantly reduces the complexity associated with training multi-skill policies for humanoid WBC. By decoupling the acquisition of primitive skills and ensuring their real-world readiness, the framework provides a robust foundation. The subsequent heterogeneous skill ensembling stage, combining imitation learning with reinforcement learning, efficiently unifies these skills and expands their coordination and transition capabilities, mitigating the need for extensive manual reward engineering or intricate curriculum designs often required by end-to-end RL systems.
- **Stronger Sim2Real Transferability:** A critical contribution of R²S² is its demonstrated ability to achieve reliable sim2real transfer. By grounding skill priors in real-world-ready primitive behaviors and incorporating specific behavioral regularization terms, the framework addresses one of the most persistent challenges in robot learning. This robust transferability means that policies trained in simulation can be confidently deployed on physical robots, accelerating research and development cycles.
- **Novel Skill Representation for Planning:** The introduction of a unified, continuous latent skill space, learned via a CVAE, represents a novel approach to skill representation for humanoid control. This continuous space facilitates more efficient high-level planning compared to discrete skill indicators or mismatched command spaces. It provides a structured action space for RL planners, enabling them to explore and exploit behaviors more effectively, which is crucial for complex, multi-modal tasks.
- **Generalizability Across Platforms and Tasks:** The validation of R²S² across different humanoid platforms (Unitree G1 and H1) and a variety of goal-reaching tasks (including obstacle avoidance and object manipulation) indicates the framework's generalizability. This suggests that the approach could be adapted to other humanoid robots and extended to an even broader range of complex interaction scenarios.
- **Facilitating Advanced Teleoperation Systems:** The framework's ability to support whole-body teleoperation with a large reachable space is a valuable side effect. This can lead to more intuitive and capable telepresence and telemanipulation systems, where human operators can leverage the humanoid's full physical potential without being constrained to limited workspaces. This has implications for remote operation in dangerous or inaccessible environments.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the current number of control interfaces and primitive skills, and the reliance on motion capture for interaction targets (suggesting a need for visual modules), the proposed R²S² framework lays a significant foundation. It offers a principled methodology for developing more autonomous, capable, and physically robust humanoid robots, paving the way for future advancements in human-robot collaboration and intelligent robotic assistance.
