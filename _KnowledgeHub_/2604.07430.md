# HY-Embodied-0.5: Embodied Foundation Models for Real-World Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Tencent Robotics X and HY Vision Team introduced HY-Embodied-0.5, a family of foundation models designed for real-world embodied agents that enhance fine-grained visual perception and complex embodied reasoning. The models achieved leading performance on 16 out of 22 embodied benchmarks and demonstrated robust control in real-world robotic tasks like precision plug-in packing (85% success) and mug hanging (75% success).

## Problem

- General Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lack the fine-grained visual perception necessary for robust physical interaction and decision-making in the real world.
- Mainstream VLMs are not inherently optimized for embodied prediction, interaction, and planning in dynamic physical environments.
- A significant functional gap exists between the broad capabilities of general VLMs and the specialized requirements of embodied intelligence for real-world agents.

## Method

- Developed **HY-Embodied-0.5**, a family of foundation models with architectural innovations like **HY-ViT 2.0** for efficient visual encoding and ==Modality-Adaptive Computing== with ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)==.
- Compiled a diverse, high-quality corpus of over **100 million** vision-language training samples, including visual perception, embodied-centric, and spatial-centric data.
- Employed a ==multi-stage training pipeline== (pre-training, mid-training, post-training with Supervised Fine-tuning, Reinforcement Learning, ==iterative self-evolving training==, and ==large-to-small on-policy distillation==).

## Results

- The efficient **HY-Embodied-0.5 MoT-2B** model achieved an average score of **58.0%** across 22 embodied-relevant benchmarks, leading on **16** tasks.
- The powerful **HY-Embodied-0.5 MoE-A32B** model achieved an average score of **67.0%**, surpassing frontier models like Gemini 3.0 Pro (**63.6%**) and Seed 2.0 (**66.2%**).
- In real-world robot control tasks, the VLA model (based on MoT-2B) achieved **85%** success for precision plug-in packing, **80%** for tableware stacking, and **75%** for mug hanging.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modality-adaptive architectures like Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) can efficiently integrate visual and linguistic processing while maintaining performance for embodied tasks.
> - A comprehensive data strategy, combining diverse visual perception, embodied, and spatial understanding data, is crucial for developing robust embodied intelligence.
> - Iterative self-evolving post-training strategies and on-policy distillation effectively enhance complex reasoning abilities and enable knowledge transfer to compact models suitable for edge deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent agents has emerged as a significant paradigm in problem-solving and workflow automation, largely driven by advancements in large language models (LLMs). These agents are increasingly applied across diverse fields, including coding, personal assistance, and scientific research. Extending the capabilities of these agents into physical environments represents a frontier in artificial intelligence.

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have made substantial progress in general-purpose scenarios, excelling in tasks predominantly based on static, web-scale datasets. However, their application to real-world physical and embodied environments presents specific challenges. Current VLMs exhibit limitations in two key areas relevant to embodied agents:
1. **Fine-Grained Visual Perception:** Robust interaction with the physical world necessitates precise, granular visual understanding to inform decisions and actions. Existing VLMs often lack the detailed perceptual capabilities required for such physical grounding.
2. **Embodied Prediction, Interaction, and Planning:** Mainstream VLMs are not inherently optimized for dynamic, action-oriented tasks within physical environments. They frequently lack the capabilities essential for prediction, interaction, and planning in real-time embodied scenarios.

This work introduces HY-Embodied-0.5, a family of foundation models specifically developed to address these deficiencies. By designing models for real-world embodied agents, the research aims to bridge the functional gap between general VLMs and the specific demands of physical interaction. The authors position embodied VLMs as a mechanism to integrate the broad knowledge of large language and multimodal models with the practical requirements of real-world tasks, thereby enabling the transition of digital intelligence into physical applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop foundation models purpose-built for real-world embodied agents, thereby translating digital intelligence into the physical domain. The authors identify a significant gap between general Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the specialized requirements of embodied intelligence, which serves as the central motivation for their work.

Specifically, the key objectives include:

- **Enhancing Core Capabilities for Embodied Intelligence:** The models are designed to significantly improve foundational capabilities crucial for agents operating in physical environments. These capabilities encompass:
 - **Spatial and Temporal Visual Perception:** This is considered a fundamental prerequisite for understanding the physical world and making informed decisions for specific actions. The motivation is to overcome existing VLM deficiencies in capturing granular visual details necessary for physical grounding.
 - **Advanced Embodied Reasoning:** This involves capabilities for prediction, interaction, and planning within dynamic physical environments. The motivation stems from the observation that mainstream VLMs, primarily trained on static datasets, are not adequately optimized for action-oriented tasks in embodied settings.
- **Developing a Versatile Model Suite:** The project aims to create a family of models that cater to different deployment needs and computational complexities. This suite comprises two main variants:
 - An efficient model with 2 billion activated parameters, optimized for real-time responsiveness and deployment on edge devices.
 - A powerful model with 32 billion activated parameters, engineered to handle more complex visual perception and embodied reasoning tasks.
- **Innovating Across Multiple Dimensions:** The research is motivated by a systematic approach to enhance model capabilities. This involves innovations in:
 - **Model Architecture:** Developing new architectural components to improve visual perception and modality-adaptive computation.
 - **Data Curation:** Constructing extensive and high-quality datasets specifically tailored for perception and embodied tasks, including real robot data and reasoning-focused data.
 - **Training Strategies:** Designing advanced training paradigms, such as iterative self-evolving post-training and knowledge distillation, to refine reasoning abilities and transfer competencies between models.
- **Validating Practical Effectiveness:** A core motivation is to demonstrate the practical utility of these models. This is pursued through extensive evaluation across a comprehensive set of benchmarks and, crucially, through real-world physical evaluations in downstream robotic control tasks. The goal is to establish that the developed VLM foundation can support effective Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for physical agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing HY-Embodied-0.5 encompasses innovations in model architecture, data curation, and multi-stage training strategies. The approach aims to specifically enhance visual perception and embodied reasoning for real-world agents.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The HY-Embodied-0.5 models are built upon a standard Vision-Language Model (VLM) framework, comprising a vision encoder and a large language model. Several architectural improvements are introduced, particularly for the edge-optimized variant (HY-Embodied-0.5-MoT-2B).

- **HY-ViT 2.0: Efficient Native-Resolution Visual Encoder:** This component serves as the visual encoder, projecting visual inputs into the language embedding space. HY-ViT 2.0 is an upgraded Vision Transformer (ViT) designed to support arbitrary-resolution inputs. It is trained with a larger scale of pre-training data, incorporates a small LLM for language supervision signals, and employs visual reconstruction supervision to minimize information loss. For efficiency on edge devices, a 400M-parameter ViT is used, trained via knowledge distillation from a larger internal ViT. A larger ViT variant is also used to generate discrete visual representations (with a codebook size of 2,000, compressing each 8x8 image patch into a single code) which supervise the model's visual tokens.

- **Modality-Adaptive Computing with Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT):** The MoT architecture is integrated to provide a balance between computational efficiency and performance, particularly for smaller models. It introduces non-shared parameters for language and vision tokens by duplicating the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) and QKV (Query, Key, Value) parameters of the pre-trained language model. Visual tokens are processed using these duplicated parameters, while text tokens use the original text-specific parameters. This effectively doubles the parameter count for modality-specific processing with minimal overhead.
 - **Distinct Attention Mechanisms:** Bidirectional attention masks are designed for visual tokens, recognizing the non-unidirectional nature of visual data, while causal attention is maintained for text tokens.
 - **Visual Next-Code Prediction Task:** To further optimize the vision branch, a visual next-code prediction task is introduced. This involves applying a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) module to the LLM output features of the vision branch to predict the discrete code of the next patch, supervised by the discrete visual representations from the larger ViT using a cross-entropy loss.

- **Visual Latent Tokens Connecting Vision and Language:** Learnable visual latent tokens are appended to the end of each visual element (e.g., image or video frame). During pre-training, these tokens are supervised using the global CLS (Class) features from a large ViT via a negative cosine similarity loss. This design aims to improve the model's overall perceptual capacity and facilitate stronger connections between visual and textual content.

#### 3.2. Pre-training

The training pipeline builds upon the Hunyuan-1.8B large language model and integrates physical world understanding from early stages.

- **Pre-training Data:** A diverse, high-quality corpus of vision-language data, totaling over 100 million training samples, is compiled. This includes:
 - **Visual Perception Data:** Omni-Detection (62M samples for 2D/3D grounding and object recognition), Depth Estimation (36M samples for absolute and relative depth), Segmentation (5M samples for fine-grained visual perception), and Pointing and Counting (11M samples for precise point-level perception). Data sources include OpenImages, Objects365, RefCOCO, SA-1B, Pixmo-Points, and automated labeling pipelines leveraging VLMs and SAM.
 - **Embodied-Centric Data:** Aggregated from open-source data and ego-view robotics manipulation sequences, organized into embodied perception, semantic understanding, and high-level planning/manipulation. This includes Grounding (point/bbox localization, referring expressions), Affordance (visual grounding with instructions), Trajectory (motion prediction), Understanding (multi-level VLM capabilities), Planning (current state assessment, action prediction), and Reasoning (complex, in-house long-horizon reasoning).
 - **Spatial-Centric Data:** Focuses on 3D environment understanding, categorized into Correspondence (cross-frame/2D-3D mapping), Geometry (depth estimation/comparison), Configuration (object arrangement/relationships), Measurement (metric estimation), and Dynamics (motion and temporal changes). Sources include ScanNet, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes, and self-collected data.
 - **General Understanding Data:** A substantial volume of in-house general VLM data is incorporated to establish foundational reasoning across general semantics, STEM, fine-grained visual parsing, complex problem-solving, and agentic operations. These datasets are partitioned between pre-training and mid-training stages.

- **Training Recipe:** The training is structured into two stages:
 - **Large-scale Pre-training:** Utilizes a multimodal corpus of over 600 billion tokens (389B general, 236B embodied/perception). Training parameters include a base learning rate of 5e-5, ViT learning rate of 5e-6, weight decay of 1e-4, and a global batch size of 256. All ViT, MoT, and latent visual token parameters are trainable.
 - **Embodied-Spatial Mid-training:** Involves approximately 30 million instances of higher-quality and more complex embodied and spatial data. Data is mixed at a ratio of 12:5:3 (general:embodied:spatial). For the MoT-2B model, a mixture of long and short reasoning chains is used, while the MoE-32B model uses short-chain data exclusively. ViT parameters are frozen, and only HY-Embodied-0.5 modules are updated.

- **Training Strategy (Loss Functions):** During large-scale pre-training, the model is jointly optimized using three objectives:
 - `L_llm`: Standard autoregressive language loss.
 - `L_vision`: Cross-entropy loss for the visual next-code prediction task.
 - `L_global`: Negative cosine similarity loss to align the visual latent token with global image semantics.
 For mid-training and fine-tuning stages, only `L_llm` is used.

#### 3.3. Post-training

The post-training pipeline is designed to enhance reasoning capabilities and optimize knowledge transfer.

- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):**
 - **Data Construction:** Focuses on long-chain reasoning by sampling high-complexity, multi-step problems from spatial, embodied, and general sources. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories are constructed through human-model collaboration and evaluated by an LLM for quality and correctness. This yields ~100k cold-start CoT instances.
 - **Training Recipe:** Uses standard cross-entropy loss without sequence packing, processing each sample individually. Base learning rate is 5e-5.

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Data Construction:** Training data is dynamically constructed for each RL round based on the current model's capabilities. A candidate pool is maintained, and samples with partial success (at the model's capability frontier) are selected. Data is balanced across perception, prediction, interaction, and planning, with 50K samples per RL stage.
 - **Reward Designs:** A task-aware reward system (r = R_t(y, y*) ∈) is implemented, utilizing deterministic and structure-aware rewards for specific task types (e.g., IoU for grounding, relative error for regression, DTW/Fréchet distance for trajectory). For open-ended reasoning, an LLM-based judge provides fallback rewards.
 - **Training Recipe:** Employs a GRPO-based objective. Group-relative advantages are computed by normalizing rewards within sampled response groups. The policy is updated with a clipped policy-ratio objective. Practical controls like masking zero-variance groups, quality control for responses, and asymmetric clipping are used to stabilize training.

- **Evolving Deep Thinking with Iterative Training:** This paradigm alternates between RL and Rejection Sampling Fine-tuning (RFT). RL explores and improves behaviors, while RFT consolidates high-quality reasoning traces by selecting partially successful rollouts and using a stronger teacher model to score and retain high-quality reasoning traces for SFT. This process iteratively expands and refines the model's reasoning patterns.

- **Large-to-Small On-Policy Distillation (OPD):** To transfer the advanced reasoning capabilities from the large HY-Embodied-0.5 model to the compact variant, an on-policy distillation strategy is used. The student model generates its own response, and the teacher model then provides supervision by minimizing the KL divergence between its own next-token distribution and the student's, conditional on the student-generated prefixes. This aims to transfer rich, token-level reasoning signals and reduce the training-inference mismatch.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HY-Embodied-0.5 demonstrated its performance across a range of benchmarks and real-world robot control tasks.

#### 4.1. Results of HY-Embodied-0.5 MoT-2B

The efficient HY-Embodied-0.5 MoT-2B model was evaluated on 22 benchmarks covering visual perception, embodied understanding, and spatial understanding, in addition to general visual understanding tasks.

- **Performance on 22 Embodied-Relevant Benchmarks:** The MoT-2B model achieved the best performance on 16 out of 22 benchmarks and ranked second on 4 others among compared generalist and specialist embodied VLMs of similar sizes. It attained an average score of 58.0% across all 22 benchmarks. This performance exceeded that of Qwen3-VL-4B (a generalist VLM with larger activated parameters) by 10.2% and RoboBrain2.5-4B (a specialist embodied VLM with larger activated parameters) by 8.6%. The model demonstrated strong visual perception capabilities, competitive embodied understanding, and a significant advantage in spatial understanding tasks, indicating effective fine-grained spatial reasoning. These results suggest that the model's performance gains are attributed to its embodied-centric architectural design, data construction, and post-training strategies, rather than scale alone.
- **Performance on General Benchmarks:** On a suite of general visual understanding benchmarks (including RealWorldQA, Hallusion-Bench, BLINK, CharXiv-RQ, DocVQA, OCRBench, and TextVQA), HY-Embodied-0.5 MoT-2B achieved performance comparable to size-matched general VLMs such as Qwen3-VL-2B-Thinking and InternVL 3.5-2B. This indicates that while optimized for embodied and spatial reasoning, the model retains robust general visual understanding capabilities.

#### 4.2. Results of HY-Embodied-0.5 MoE-A32B

The more powerful HY-Embodied-0.5 MoE-A32B model was also evaluated on the same 22 benchmarks against state-of-the-art visual agents.

- **Performance on 22 Embodied-Relevant Benchmarks:** The MoE-A32B model achieved an average score of 67.0% across the 22 benchmarks. It secured first place in 7 tasks (32%) and second place in 6 tasks (27%). This performance surpassed several frontier models, including Gemini 3.0 Pro (63.6%), Seed 2.0 (66.2%), Qwen 3.5 A17B (66.1%), and Kimi K2.5 (61.1%).

#### 4.3. Analysis

- **Qualitative Results on Visual Perception Tasks:** The model demonstrated proficiency in foundational visual tasks such as depth estimation, object detection, and complex counting. Predictions for depth estimation were closer to ground truth compared to baseline models. In visual grounding, it achieved high precision in bounding box detection, point-level localization, and region-level captioning. For complex counting tasks, the model employed a visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) process, systematically identifying and spatially localizing objects to deduce the final count.
- **Qualitative Results on Embodied Tasks:** The model exhibited comprehensive performance across embodied perception (grounding), scene understanding, and task planning. It provided precise localization for target objects, accurately parsed complex 3D spatial relationships, and logically deduced next actions in multi-step planning scenarios.
- **Illustration of Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Both the MoT-2B and A32B variants showed strong long-chain reasoning capabilities. The CoT process involved systematic analysis of spatial relationships, affordances, and sequential decision-making. The models also exhibited self-reflection and error-correction during the thinking phase. This was observed in tasks requiring evaluation of robot trajectories, identification of interaction points, complex perspective-taking, sequential navigation planning, and 3D geometric deduction.
- **Efficiency for HY-Embodied-0.5 MoT:** The Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture demonstrated faster convergence and lower final loss during training compared to standard transformers. During inference, with fixed input image tokens and generated output tokens, the MoT architecture exhibited comparable speed to a Dense-2B baseline, introducing negligible overhead, particularly in the decode stage which dominates total inference time.
- **Attention Visualizations for Visual Latent Tokens:** Visualizations indicated that visual attention maps accurately localized salient objects, specific object parts, and key spatial regions. Concurrently, language attention focused on corresponding semantic entities, state descriptions, spatial relationships, and action instructions. This suggests that the visual latent tokens effectively bridge modalities by extracting fine-grained visual features and aligning them with linguistic concepts, enhancing cross-modal understanding.

### 5. Robot Control Results

The HY-Embodied-0.5 MoT-2B base model was extended with an Action Expert module, forming a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for real-world robot control. The VLA model underwent two stages of fine-tuning: initially with 5,000 hours of UMI (Universal Manipulation Interface) data, followed by supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with real-robot data specific to three tasks (300 to 700 episodes per task). Baselines (π0 and π0.5) were fine-tuned under identical conditions.

The VLA model demonstrated robust performance across the evaluated real-world tasks:

- **Precision Plug-in Packing:** Achieved an 85% success rate, matching π0.5 and surpassing π0 (80%).
- **Tableware Stacking:** Achieved an 80% success rate, improving significantly over π0 (60%) and remaining competitive with π0.5 (85%).
- **Mug Hanging:** Achieved a 75% success rate, representing a notable improvement over both π0 (45%) and π0.5 (50%), suggesting superior control capabilities in this more challenging task.

These results indicate that the UMI data fine-tuning, combined with the MoT architecture, equipped the VLA model with generalizable representations that effectively transferred to complex, embodiment-specific manipulation tasks after targeted SFT.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

HY-Embodied-0.5 represents a development in the field of embodied AI by specifically targeting the integration of vision-language models with real-world agent capabilities. The research addresses the identified limitations of general VLMs in handling the spatial, temporal, and action-oriented demands of physical environments.

The architectural innovations, including the modality-adaptive Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) and the incorporation of visual latent tokens, contribute to enhancing fine-grained spatial and visual perception, which is critical for physical grounding. The multi-stage embodied post-training pipeline, featuring iterative self-evolving training and on-policy distillation, is designed to refine complex reasoning abilities and efficiently transfer these competencies, even to compact models suitable for edge deployment.

The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across 22 diverse benchmarks and compelling results in real-world robotic manipulation tasks suggest that HY-Embodied-0.5 can translate broad digital intelligence into practical, physical-world competence. This work contributes to the development of robust and versatile foundation models for real-world agents.

The potential impact of this research lies in enabling more capable and adaptable autonomous agents that can seamlessly perceive, reason, and act within dynamic physical environments. The development of efficient yet powerful variants could facilitate wider deployment of embodied AI in various applications, from industrial robotics to personal assistance. Future directions for this work include further bridging the gap between language and action models to train more sophisticated "real-world brains" for complex applications.
