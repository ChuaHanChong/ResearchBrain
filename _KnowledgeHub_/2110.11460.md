# MUGL: Large Scale Multi Person Conditional Action Generation with Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> MUGL generates pose-based human action sequences for single and two-person actions across 120 NTU-RGBD categories. A Conditional Gaussian Mixture VAE supplies intra/inter-category diversity, decoupled local-pose and global-trajectory branches add locomotion, and a duration-aware indexed-sequence encoding yields variable-length output. With 922K parameters it beats larger baselines, cutting MMD-A to 0.34 versus 0.57 for the strongest competitor.

## Problem

- Prior generative models cover only 10-13 action categories and single-person actions, leaving most large-scale activity repertoires and interaction dynamics unmodelled.
- Existing methods omit global-frame locomotion, so walking, running, and multi-person actions like kicking cannot be represented with correct relative displacement.
- Fixed-length generation makes short actions such as throw or salute look unnatural, and raw Kinect joint data is noisy and temporally incoherent.

## Method

- ==Conditional Gaussian Mixture VAE== (**C-GMVAE**) replaces the unimodal Gaussian prior, conditioning encoder, latent sample, and decoder on action class and a transformed viewpoint.
- Decoupled representation: a local branch encodes joint rotations in continuous ==6-D== form with ==forward kinematics== for fixed bone length, a 1-D CNN branch encodes global root trajectory plus relative displacements.
- ==Sequence Length encoder-decoder== maps a normalized non-decreasing indexed sequence, reconstructed via ReLU-cumulative-sum-sigmoid and thresholded, plus a hybrid VIBE-video and Kinect-trajectory dataset of 114,480 samples.

## Results

- On 94 single-person NTU-VIBE classes **MUGL** reaches MMD-A **0.34** and MMD-S **0.17**, versus **0.57**/**0.52** for action2motion and **0.68**/**0.43** for SA-GCN.
- MUGL uses **922K** parameters with **0.02s** inference, against **14502K** and **3.31s** for SA-GCN, while extending conditioning from 10-13 classes to **120** with two-person support.
- Ablations confirm each component: removing the variable-length module raises MMD-A to **1.77**, transpose-conv decoding to **0.65**, dropping latent viewpoint conditioning to **0.79**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mixture priors beat unimodal Gaussians when latent data contains many attribute-driven clusters, giving capacity for large category counts without model growth.
> - Decoupling local articulation from global trajectory, plus continuous 6-D rotations with forward kinematics, keeps bone lengths valid while permitting genuine locomotion.
> - Classifier-feature scores like FID or diversity are unreliable here, since standard skeleton preprocessing removes the very rotation and translation dynamics being evaluated.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human motion synthesis is a field with applications in animation, robotics, and human-object interaction. Historically, approaches have included physics-based simulation, key-framing, and data-driven methods. The availability of motion capture systems has further enabled pose-based human action synthesis, which offers advantages over pixel-based video data by decoupling action-centric aspects from appearance or background details.

However, human action synthesis presents challenges due to the spatiotemporal correlations in body joint movements and the inter/intra-category variability of actions. This complexity is amplified when considering multiple actors. Prior research has often been limited to synthesizing small sets of fixed-duration, simple, single-person actions, partly due to the scarcity of large-scale datasets.

The NTU-RGBD dataset, which contains 120 diverse single and multi-person activities, represents a significant development. However, the raw Kinect-based 3D joint data within this dataset can suffer from temporal incoherence and noise, which can lead to suboptimal learning in generative models. Recent advances in 3D human pose estimation from RGB videos offer a potential solution to this data quality issue.

Existing generative models for human motion have demonstrated limitations:
- **Scale and Diversity:** Many approaches do not extend beyond a small number of categories or single-person actions.
- **Locomotion:** The absence of global frame locomotion limits applicability to actions like walking or running, and inadequately models multi-person interactions involving movement.
- **Fixed-Length Sequences:** Generating sequences of fixed duration can result in unnatural-looking actions for activities that inherently vary in length.
- **Representation:** While 3D joint positions are common, they can lead to unnatural motion due to unconstrained bone lengths. Quaternion representations for joint rotations address bone length but can be discontinuous. Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) for spatiotemporal graphs are computationally expensive for large action sets.
- **Controllability:** Some methods allow conditioning on initial sequences or latent vectors but may lack high-level control over action categories or stochasticity.
- **Evaluation:** Standard feature-based metrics (FID, Diversity, Multimodality scores) often rely on pre-trained classifiers that use preprocessing steps, potentially distorting action dynamics and making evaluation unreliable for direct motion synthesis.

MUGL positions itself within this landscape by addressing these shortcomings. It aims to provide a unified, controllable model for large-scale, diverse, variable-length, single and multi-person pose-based action generation, including locomotion. It introduces a hybrid pose sequence representation to mitigate data quality issues in existing datasets and proposes a method that is computationally efficient while achieving higher generation quality compared to several strong baselines. The work also contributes to the discussion on appropriate evaluation metrics for generative models of human motion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel deep neural model, termed MUGL, for generating large-scale, diverse, single and multi-person pose-based action sequences that include locomotion and are of variable length. The model aims to achieve controllable generation across over 100 action categories within a single, unified framework.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and limitations identified in the field of human action synthesis:

- **Complexity of Human Motion:** Human actions involve intricate spatiotemporal correlations among body joints, coupled with significant intra- and inter-category variability. This inherent complexity makes realistic synthesis difficult.
- **Multi-Person Interactions:** The challenge is further compounded when multiple actors are involved, requiring the modeling of relative global displacements and interactions.
- **Dataset Limitations:** While large-scale datasets like NTU-RGBD-120 exist, the raw Kinect-based 3D joint data often suffers from temporal incoherence and noise. Naïve use of such data can lead to generative models learning suboptimal representations and producing unrealistic outputs. There is a need for high-quality, large-scale pose data.
- **Restricted Generative Capabilities of Existing Models:**
 - Many contemporary approaches focus on small action sets, fixed-duration sequences, and simple single-person actions, which limits their practical utility.
 - The absence of locomotion modeling in existing methods renders them unsuitable for actions involving global movement, such as walking or running, and complex multi-person interactions (e.g., kicking).
 - Generating fixed-length sequences can result in unnatural or truncated actions for activities that naturally vary in duration (e.g., 'throw,' 'salute').
- **Computational Efficiency:** Some graph-convolution-based methods, while capable of modeling spatiotemporal structures, are computationally expensive for a large number of action classes.
- **Controllability:** The ability to customize generated sequences by action category, and to ensure diversity within and between categories, is a desirable characteristic for practical applications.

To address these motivations, MUGL proposes solutions such as decoupling local pose and global trajectory components, incorporating duration-aware feature representations, and utilizing a hybrid pose sequence representation derived from both video and Kinect data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed model, MUGL, is a deep neural architecture designed for controllable, large-scale, and diverse generation of variable-length, single and multi-person human action sequences with locomotion. It is based on a Conditional Gaussian Mixture Variational Autoencoder (C-GMVAE) framework.

#### 3.1. Action Sequence Representation

To accommodate actions with and without locomotion, MUGL decouples the action sequence X into a global component (Xg) and a local component (Xl).
- **Global Component (Xg):** This models the global trajectory of the root node for the first person, G^(1), and relative displacements, D^(j), for other individuals. The global trajectory for any person j is G^(j) = G^(1) + D^(j).
- **Local Component (Xl):** This comprises per-person kinematic pose tree sequences where root nodes are implicitly at the global origin. Instead of 3D joint positions, which can lead to unnatural bone lengths, a continuous 6-D representation for joint rotation matrices is used, based on the method by Zhou et al.. This representation helps maintain bone length consistency and bypasses orthogonalization requirements.

#### 3.2. MUGL Architecture

MUGL consists of encoder and decoder modules for the local component, global component, and sequence length, structured around a C-GMVAE.

- **Conditional Gaussian Mixture VAE (C-GMVAE):** The model extends a standard VAE to a Gaussian Mixture VAE, where the latent prior is a Mixture of Gaussians. This addresses the unimodality bottleneck of standard VAEs, particularly for data with diverse clusters like action categories. It further incorporates conditioning information (action class 'c' and viewpoint 'v') into both the encoder and decoder, modeling q_phi(y, z|X, a) and p_theta(X|y, z, a) respectively, where 'y' denotes component weights.

- **Encoder Modules:**
 - **Local Pose Encoder:** Processes the local component Xl (T × 6 × (J × P)). It involves a Spatial Pose Encoder (2D residual CNN for per-timestep features) followed by a Temporal Encoder (multiple 2D residual CNNs to downsample timesteps). The output is flattened into spatio-temporal local features f_l.
 - **Global Trajectory Encoder:** Processes the global component Xg (T × 3 × P). It uses a series of 1D convolutions to downsample timesteps, followed by a linear layer to produce flattened feature representation f_g.
 - **Sequence Length Encoder:** Encodes the length 't_s' of a training sequence by defining an indexed sequence t[n] (normalized, non-decreasing) of maximum length T. This sequence is transformed into f_s via 1D convolutions.
 - **Encoder Representation:** The concatenated features (f_l, f_g, f_s) are conditionally modulated with the action class label 'c' and a transformed viewpoint 'v'. This combined representation is mapped to the parametric representations of the C-GMVAE's variational approximation distribution q_phi(y, z|X, a).

- **Latent Representation:** A latent vector 'z' is sampled from the mixture of K Gaussian components. This vector 'z' is then conditioned on the class label and transformed viewpoint by concatenation, forming z_cv, which is further processed by a linear layer. During inference, the viewpoint is set to a fixed default.

- **Decoder Modules:**
 - **Sequence Decoder:** Complementary to the encoder, it performs spatiotemporal upsampling from z_cv. The local pose components are generated as 6-D rotation representations. The Spatial Decoder employs a multi-layer perceptron instead of transpose convolutions for improved sequence quality.
 - **Forward Kinematics:** This module converts the 6-D rotation representation from the Temporal Decoder into 3x3 rotation matrices for each joint. It then uses a fixed rest pose and the kinematic tree to compute the 3D positions of joints, ensuring consistent bone lengths. This differentiable process generates local pose structure sequences (e_Xe).
 - **Global Trajectory Decoder:** Upsamples the conditioned latent sample z_cv via 1D CNN layers to generate the global trajectory for the first person and relative displacements for others (e_Xg).
 - **Final Sequence Assembly:** The local pose sequences (e_Xe) are offset using information from the global trajectory decoder (e_Xg) to produce the final generated sequence (e_X). For single-person actions, only the first person's sequence is considered.
 - **Sequence Length Decoder:** Transforms z_cv via a linear layer and 1D convolutions into a T × 1 sequence. After ReLU activation, cumulative sum, and sigmoid activation, this sequence (d_t[n]) is used to determine the effective sequence length (b_ts) by finding the first element exceeding a threshold (theta_s).

#### 3.3. Optimization

The overall VAE-based framework is optimized using a combined loss function:
L = (L_rec_local + λ_global * L_rec_global) + λ_len * L_rec_len + λ_KL * L_KL
- **L_rec_local:** Local motion reconstruction loss, comprising Mean Squared Error (MSE) for 6-D rotation representation (L_6D) and 3-D joint coordinates (L_3D), weighted by λ_rot (L_rec_local = λ_rot * L_6D + L_3D).
- **L_rec_global:** Global trajectory MSE loss.
- **L_rec_len:** Sequence length MSE loss.
- **L_KL:** KL-divergence loss of the GMVAE.
Hyperparameters (λ_rot, λ_global, λ_len, λ_KL) control the balance of these components, with λ_KL using a cyclic annealing schedule. Losses are computed only for the non-padded extent of sequences.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

MUGL is trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate scheduler. The maximum sequence length (T) is 256 timesteps. The original 33.33 fps NTU-RGBD sequences are subsampled to 8.25 fps for training. During inference, K (number of Gaussian components) is set to the number of action classes (|C|=120), and the latent vector is conditioned on a one-hot representation of the desired action class. Output sequence resolution is increased using bicubic interpolation. Class distribution-based oversampling is applied for minibatches to address class imbalance.

#### 3.5. Hybrid Pose Sequence Representation

To overcome the noise and temporal incoherence of raw Kinect data in NTU-RGBD, the authors construct a hybrid dataset:
- **Local Pose:** 3D pose estimates are sourced from RGB videos of NTU-RGBD-120 using VIBE, a state-of-the-art RGB-based 3D pose estimator. VIBE provides more reliable local pose estimates with consistent bone lengths.
- **Global Trajectory:** Corresponding global trajectories for each subject are obtained from the original NTU-120 Kinect sequences. For multi-person activities, subjects are associated based on orientation similarity.
This hybrid dataset spans 120 action classes with 114,480 samples, preprocessed to a uniform length of 256 timesteps. Training and evaluation follow the cross-setup protocol of NTU-RGBD-120.

#### 3.6. Baselines and Evaluation Metrics

MUGL is compared against four generative models: SA-GCN, action2motion, CS-GCN, and VAE-LSTM. Where necessary, baselines are extended to include action class conditioning.
Generation quality is measured using two variants of Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) with an RBF kernel:
- **MMD-A:** Measures similarity between generated and ground-truth sample distributions on a per-timestep basis (K(g_t, g_t) + K(e_t, e_t) - 2K(g_t, e_t)).
- **MMD-S:** Measures similarity on the whole sequence, treating flattened sequences as vectors (K(g, g) + K(e, e) - 2K(g, e)).
The authors note that feature-based metrics like Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Diversity Score (DS), and Multimodality Score (MS) are not used due to issues with preprocessing steps that distort action dynamics and render feature representations unreliable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the performance of MUGL.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance (Table 3)

- **Comparison with Baselines:** MUGL exhibits lower MMD-A and MMD-S scores compared to the baselines (SA-GCN, action2motion, CS-GCN, VAE-LSTM) on the NTU-VIBE-94 dataset (single-person classes). For instance, MUGL achieved MMD-A of 0.34 ±0.12 and MMD-S of 0.17 ±0.01, whereas the best performing baseline, action2motion, showed MMD-A of 0.57 ±0.11 and MMD-S of 0.52 ±0.03.
- **Multi-person Generation:** For multi-person action generation on the full NTU-VIBE-120 dataset, MUGL reports MMD-A of 0.45 ±0.15 and MMD-S of 0.36 ±0.03. While not directly comparable to the single-person variant, these scores indicate similar performance ranges.
- **Computational Efficiency (Table 1):** MUGL has a parameter count of 922K, which is lower than SA-GCN (14502K) and CS-GCN (7744K), and comparable to VAE-LSTM (1468K) and action2motion (503K). It demonstrates a faster inference time of 0.02 seconds, outperforming all baselines (ranging from 0.03 to 3.31 seconds). Training time for MUGL is 18.92 hours.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Performance (Figures 1 and 3)

- Visual examples (Figure 1 and 3) illustrate the model's capacity to generate diverse, variable-length, single and multi-person human pose action sequences.
- The generated sequences depict a range of actions across 120 categories, including "Kicking object," "Wipe face," "Yawning," "Drink Water," "Hush," "Pick Up," "Reading," "Walk Apart," "Giving Object," "Punch Slap," "Hopping," "Walk Towards," "Cheer Up," and "Eat Meal."
- For multi-person actions, the changing length of the dotted line connecting kinematic tree root joints indicates changes in relative global positions.
- Variable sequence length is visibly demonstrated in the generated examples.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 4)

An ablation study on MUGL's components, trained on all 120 NTU-120 classes, revealed the following:
- **C-GMVAE vs. VAE:** Using a Gaussian Mixture Model (K > 1) for the latent space (C-GMVAE) generally results in better MMD scores compared to a vanilla VAE with a unimodal Gaussian prior (K=1), particularly for MMD-S.
- **Architectural Choices:** Replacing the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) in the Spatial Decoder with a 2D Transpose Convolution leads to a noticeable increase in MMD scores (e.g., MMD-A from 0.45 to 0.65), indicating the MLP's contribution to quality.
- **Conditioning:** Removing class and viewpoint conditioning in the Sequence Encoder, or removing viewpoint conditioning entirely (especially in the latent space), negatively impacts performance.
- **Pose Representation:** Using raw 3D joint representation as input/output instead of the 6-D rotation representation leads to higher MMD scores, supporting the choice of 6-D representation.
- **Variable Sequence Length:** Removing the variable sequence length encoder-decoder considerably degrades performance (MMD-A increases from 0.45 to 1.77, MMD-S from 0.36 to 0.79), underscoring its importance.
- **Loss Components:** Removing either the 3D loss or the Rotation Loss from the local reconstruction loss also results in higher MMD scores, suggesting the benefit of their combined use.

These findings collectively demonstrate that MUGL achieves superior generative quality and efficiency compared to several established baselines, attributed to its specific architectural design choices, representation scheme, and optimization strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MUGL represents a contribution to the field of human motion synthesis by addressing several limitations of previous approaches. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Advancement in Generative Capabilities:** MUGL enables the generation of human action sequences on a larger scale (120 categories), with greater diversity, and critically, supports both single and multi-person actions that include locomotion and variable durations. This capability extends beyond the scope of many existing models that typically focus on smaller action sets, fixed lengths, or single actors.
- **Enhanced Realism and Controllability:** The model's ability to generate variable-length sequences makes actions appear more natural, as it can adapt to the inherent duration variability of different activities. The explicit conditioning on action categories provides a higher degree of control for users or applications.
- **Improved Data Utility:** The creation of a hybrid pose sequence representation (NTU-VIBE), by combining 3D pose estimates from RGB videos (VIBE) with global trajectories from Kinect data, addresses the problem of noisy and temporally incoherent raw sensor data. This approach yields a more reliable and curated dataset, which can benefit future research in human activity understanding and generation.
- **Computational Efficiency:** MUGL's design, particularly its use of CNNs and a relatively lower parameter count compared to some GCN-based models, results in faster inference times. This efficiency makes it more practical for real-time applications or large-scale content generation.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper's critical evaluation of feature-based generative quality measures (FID, DS, MS) highlights a potential flaw in common evaluation practices due to preprocessing distortions. This discussion can inform the development of more appropriate and robust metrics for human motion generation.
- **Applications:** The generated sequences can serve as input for various applications, such as:
 - **Animation:** Providing realistic, diverse, and controllable motion for virtual characters in games, films, or simulations (e.g., by integrating with models like SMPL for rendering).
 - **Robotics:** Generating diverse human-like motions for robots, particularly in human-robot interaction scenarios or for learning tasks.
 - **Human-Object Interaction:** Synthesizing actions involving objects, which can be valuable for training or testing perception systems.
 - **Data Augmentation:** Creating synthetic motion data to augment existing datasets, potentially improving the robustness of downstream tasks like action recognition or prediction.

In summary, MUGL contributes a robust and efficient generative framework that pushes the boundaries of large-scale, controllable, and diverse human action synthesis, with a direct impact on the realism and utility of synthetic motion data across several domains.
