# Marco-o1: Towards Open Reasoning Models for Open-Ended Solutions

> [!summary] Summary
> The Marco-o1 project from Alibaba International Digital Commerce explores a hybrid approach for Large Reasoning Models to address open-ended problem-solving. It demonstrates improved mathematical reasoning on MGSM datasets through a combination of Supervised Fine-Tuning, Monte Carlo Tree Search, and a reflection mechanism, and shows enhanced ability to translate nuanced colloquial language.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to generalize advanced reasoning capabilities, exemplified by models like o1, from well-defined problems (e.g., math, coding with clear answers) to complex, open-ended real-world scenarios.
- Developing robust Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) for tasks where clear standards are absent and rewards are challenging to quantify presents an unclear technical roadmap.
- Existing machine translation tools often lack the ability to accurately translate colloquial expressions and slang, which require deeper contextual and cultural understanding.

## Method

- The base model, Qwen2-7B-Instruct, is fine-tuned using ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== with a curated dataset including filtered Open-O1 CoT, synthetically generated Marco-o1 CoT via MCTS, and instruction-following data.
- ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== is integrated with the LLM, where nodes represent reasoning states and actions are LLM outputs; paths are evaluated by a ==confidence score based on token log probabilities==.
- A ==reflection mechanism== is incorporated by prompting the model to self-critique and re-evaluate its reasoning, along with exploring different action granularities (step vs. mini-step) within the MCTS.

## Results

- Marco-o1-MCTS (step) achieved **90.40%** accuracy on MGSM-En, a **6.4%** improvement over Marco-o1-CoT, and Marco-o1-MCTS (mini-step) reached **82.40%** on MGSM-Zh, an **11.2%** improvement over Marco-o1-CoT.
- The reflection mechanism helped solve about half of the difficult problems that the model initially failed, demonstrating its effectiveness in self-correction.
- A qualitative case study showed Marco-o1 provided more accurate and natural translations of Chinese colloquialisms into English compared to Google Translate, excelling in understanding context and nuances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with Large Language Models effectively expands the solution space, increasing the probability of finding correct answers, particularly evident in improved Test@1 scores.
> - A self-reflection mechanism, which prompts the model to re-evaluate its reasoning, helps solve approximately half of previously failed challenging problems.
> - Applying Large Reasoning Models to machine translation, especially for colloquialisms and slang, shows promise in handling nuanced linguistic and cultural contexts, an area where traditional systems struggle.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broad Research Landscape and Motivation

This work is positioned within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs) and, more specifically, Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). The paper explicitly states its inspiration from OpenAI’s recently introduced "o1" model, which has garnered significant attention for its exceptional reasoning capabilities, particularly in domains with standard, quantifiable answers such as mathematics (e.g., AIME) and coding (e.g., CodeForces). These domains are well-suited for reinforcement learning (RL) because the correctness of solutions can be objectively verified and assigned clear rewards.

The broader research landscape for LLMs is currently grappling with how to extend their impressive language generation abilities to robust, multi-step reasoning. While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting has shown promise in eliciting reasoning pathways, truly complex, open-ended problem-solving remains a significant challenge. The motivation behind Marco-o1 stems precisely from this gap: the authors aim to address the critical question of whether o1-like reasoning models can effectively generalize to broader, real-world domains where "clear standards are absent and rewards are challenging to quantify." This shift from "closed-ended" problems (with single, verifiable answers) to "open-ended" ones represents a crucial frontier in AI research, as many real-world challenges inherently lack definitive solutions or clear-cut evaluation metrics.

By focusing on open-ended resolutions, Marco-o1 seeks to push the boundaries of LLM reasoning beyond traditional benchmarks. The paper acknowledges the "unclear technical roadmap" for LRMs, positioning its work as an exploration into potential avenues for developing more generalized and robust reasoning capabilities. This also implies a broader ambition to enhance LLMs' ability to tackle complex, real-world problem-solving tasks, which often require nuanced understanding, iterative refinement, and adaptive strategies rather than simple factual recall or rule application. The exploration into multilingual applications further broadens the scope, indicating an interest in how reasoning capabilities translate across linguistic barriers, particularly in handling culturally specific nuances like slang.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Marco-o1 project is to develop and explore approaches for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) that can effectively generalize their reasoning capabilities beyond well-defined tasks (like mathematics or coding) to more complex, open-ended real-world problems. This goal is directly motivated by the recent advancements exemplified by OpenAI's o1 model, which showcases strong reasoning in domains where answers are often standard and suitable for reinforcement learning.

The core motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **Generalizability to Open-Ended Domains:** The central question driving Marco-o1 is: "Can the o1 model effectively generalize to broader domains where clear standards are absent and rewards are challenging to quantify?" This highlights a desire to move beyond benchmarks that provide explicit success/failure signals and address real-world scenarios that are inherently ambiguous.
- **Enhancing Reasoning for Complex Real-World Challenges:** The team aims to augment the reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to tackle problems that are characteristic of the real world, which often require nuanced understanding, adaptive strategies, and iterative refinement.
- **Exploring the LRM Technical Roadmap:** Recognizing that the technical pathway for developing truly advanced LRMs remains largely undefined, Marco-o1 seeks to explore potential architectural and methodological approaches that could contribute to this roadmap. This includes integrating various state-of-the-art techniques.
- **Investigating Multilingual Reasoning and Translation:** A unique motivation is the exploration of LRM capabilities in multilingual contexts, particularly in machine translation tasks involving colloquialisms and slang, an area where standard translation tools often struggle. This suggests an interest in how reasoning models can grasp cultural and linguistic nuances.

In essence, Marco-o1 seeks to build upon the momentum of advanced reasoning models by making them more adaptable and robust for the complexities and ambiguities inherent in practical, real-world applications, while also contributing to the foundational understanding of how such models can be constructed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Marco-o1 integrates several advanced techniques to enhance the reasoning capabilities of its base Large Language Model (LLM), Qwen2-7B-Instruct. The methodology is primarily built upon Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) combined with sophisticated inference-time strategies.

#### 3.1. Base Model and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The foundational model for Marco-o1 is **Qwen2-7B-Instruct**, a powerful instruction-tuned LLM. To imbue it with enhanced reasoning capabilities, the authors employ an SFT strategy using a carefully curated set of reasoning datasets.

#### 3.2. Marco Reasoning Datasets

The training data for SFT consists of three main components, totaling 60,266 samples:
- **Open-O1 CoT Dataset (Filtered):** A refined version of an existing open-source Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset, enhanced through heuristic and quality filtering to ensure structured reasoning patterns.
- **Marco-o1 CoT Dataset (Synthetic):** This dataset is uniquely generated using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), a core component of their methodology. By leveraging MCTS, complex reasoning pathways are formulated and then used as training data, creating a self-reinforcing loop for reasoning capabilities.
- **Marco Instruction Dataset:** A collection of instruction-following data designed to ensure the model's general effectiveness and robust adherence to various task instructions, critical for complex problem-solving.

#### 3.3. Solution Space Expansion via Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)

A cornerstone of Marco-o1's approach is the integration of LLMs with MCTS, a technique renowned for its success in game playing (e.g., AlphaGo). This integration aims to expand the solution space and systematically explore multiple reasoning paths:
- **Nodes as Reasoning States:** Each node in the MCTS tree represents a specific state in the problem-solving process.
- **Actions as LLM Outputs:** Possible actions from a node are the outputs generated by the LLM, representing potential steps or "mini-steps" in the reasoning chain.
- **Rollout and Reward Calculation:** During the rollout phase, the LLM continues the reasoning process to a terminal state. The quality of a path is evaluated using a confidence score. For each token `t_i` generated during the rollout, its confidence `c_i` is calculated by applying the softmax function to its log probability relative to the log probabilities of the top 5 alternative tokens. The overall reward score `v` for a rollout path is then the average confidence score across all tokens in the sequence. A higher `v` indicates a more confident and likely accurate reasoning path, guiding the MCTS search.

#### 3.4. Reasoning Action Strategy

To optimize the MCTS search, the authors explore different granularities for actions:
- **Step as Action:** The model generates complete reasoning steps as actions. This provides efficient exploration but may overlook nuanced paths.
- **Mini-step as Action:** To enable finer-grained exploration, steps are divided into smaller units of 32 or 64 tokens. This strategy aims to expand the solution space in greater detail, potentially allowing the model to find correct answers that coarser granularities might miss. The authors note that while token-level search offers maximum flexibility, it is currently impractical due to computational costs and reward model design challenges.

#### 3.5. Reflection Mechanism

To enable self-correction and refinement, Marco-o1 incorporates a reflection mechanism. By appending the phrase "Wait! Maybe I made some mistakes! I need to rethink from scratch." to the end of each thought process, the model is prompted to self-reflect and reevaluate its reasoning steps. This acts as an internal feedback loop, encouraging the model to act as its own critic, detect inconsistencies, and refine its output without external intervention, drawing parallels to self-critic approaches in LLM literature.

In summary, Marco-o1 leverages a synergistic combination of supervised fine-tuning with tailored datasets, MCTS for systematic exploration of reasoning paths guided by internal confidence, and innovative action granularities, all complemented by a self-reflection mechanism to achieve enhanced reasoning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on Marco-o1 highlight its enhanced reasoning capabilities across mathematical reasoning and demonstrate its unique strength in multilingual translation, particularly for colloquial expressions.

#### 4.1. Performance on MGSM Datasets

The model's performance was evaluated on the English (MGSM-En) and Chinese (MGSM-Zh) subsets of the MGSM dataset, which tests mathematical reasoning.
- **Marco-o1-CoT vs. Base Model:** Marco-o1-CoT (fine-tuned with CoT data) showed an accuracy improvement of +1.6% (from 84.00% to 85.60%) on MGSM-En compared to the base Qwen2-7B-Instruct. However, it exhibited a decrease in performance on MGSM-Zh (from 76.80% to 71.20%), which the authors attribute to the CoT data primarily being in English, indicating a potential language transfer issue.
- **MCTS-Enhanced Models:** All MCTS-enhanced models consistently demonstrated improvements over Marco-o1-CoT, affirming that integrating MCTS expands the solution space and increases the probability of correct answers.
 - On MGSM-En, Marco-o1-MCTS (step) achieved the highest accuracy at 90.40%, a significant jump of +6.4% over Marco-o1-CoT.
 - On MGSM-Zh, Marco-o1-MCTS (mini-step of 32 tokens) performed best at 82.40%, improving by +11.2% over Marco-o1-CoT and +5.6% over the base Qwen2-7B-Instruct.
- **Action Granularity:** The results for action granularity (step vs. mini-step) were mixed, with no single strategy definitively superior across both English and Chinese datasets. This suggests that the optimal granularity might be problem-dependent, and the authors believe that more accurate reward models will further unlock the potential of MCTS's expanded solution space.

#### 4.2. Test@N Scores

The study also reported Test@N scores, which measure the percentage of problems solved correctly at least once when the model is allowed to make N separate guesses.
- MCTS-enhanced models showed an advantage at Test@1 (the initial guess), indicating their potential to find correct answers more directly and efficiently. For example, on MGSM-En, Marco-o1-MCTS (step) achieved 90.40% at Test@1, compared to 85.60% for Marco-o1-CoT. This reinforces the MCTS's ability to explore promising paths early on.

#### 4.3. Impact of Reflection Mechanism

The introduction of the reflection mechanism ("Wait! Maybe I made some mistakes! I need to rethink from scratch.") yielded significant improvements, particularly on challenging problems that the original model initially failed. Approximately half of these difficult problems were subsequently solved correctly with the addition of self-reflection, underscoring its effectiveness in fostering self-correction and refined thought processes.

#### 4.4. Case Study: Multilingual Translation Tasks

A notable finding is Marco-o1's superior performance in translating colloquial and slang expressions from Chinese to English, as demonstrated through a qualitative case study comparing it with Google Translate.
- The model excelled in understanding context and nuances, producing more accurate and natural translations of idiomatic phrases (e.g., translating "This shoe offers a stepping-on-poop sensation" to "This shoe has a comfortable sole"). This highlights the model's advanced reasoning and grasp of colloquial subtleties, a challenging area for traditional translation systems. The paper claims this is the "first to investigate LRM on Machine Translation tasks."

#### 4.5. Notable Observations

An intriguing observation was made with the classic "How many ‘r’s are in ‘strawberry’" question. While Marco-o1 provided the correct answer, its reasoning process sometimes omitted explicitly addressing the final letter ‘y’. This behavior, observed across multiple MCTS search paths, suggests that the model might be confident enough to skip seemingly obvious steps, akin to human reasoning where implicit understanding allows for shortcuts.

In summary, Marco-o1 demonstrates measurable improvements in mathematical reasoning through the combined power of CoT fine-tuning, MCTS, and action granularity. The self-reflection mechanism significantly bolsters problem-solving on difficult tasks. Furthermore, its unique prowess in translating nuanced, colloquial language marks a significant step towards more sophisticated multilingual AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Marco-o1 project represents a significant step towards advancing Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), particularly by pushing their capabilities beyond well-defined, easily quantifiable problems into the realm of open-ended, real-world challenges.

#### 5.1. Contribution to LRM Development

The paper directly addresses the "unclear technical roadmap" for LRMs, contributing by exploring a powerful hybrid approach. By integrating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) fine-tuning with Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and innovative reasoning action strategies, Marco-o1 demonstrates a viable pathway for enhancing the reasoning depth and breadth of LLMs. The use of confidence scores to guide MCTS is a clever mechanism for integrating LLM uncertainty into a tree search framework, offering a systematic way to explore solution spaces that might otherwise be missed. The finding that MCTS provides an advantage at "Test@1" suggests that this approach helps the model arrive at correct answers more directly, which is crucial for practical applications.

#### 5.2. Generalizability to Real-World Scenarios

Perhaps the most significant impact lies in the explicit focus on "open-ended resolutions." Many real-world problems—ranging from complex decision-making in business to creative problem-solving and nuanced human interaction—lack single, definitive answers or easily quantifiable rewards. Marco-o1's attempt to generalize to these domains is vital. The self-reflection mechanism, which allows the model to "rethink from scratch," is a crucial development in this context, mimicking human metacognition and enabling self-correction, which is essential for robustness in uncertain environments. This capability moves LLMs closer to becoming reliable assistants in complex, ambiguous tasks where iterative refinement is key.

#### 5.3. Advancements in Multilingual and Translation AI

The pioneering application of LRMs to Machine Translation tasks, particularly with a focus on colloquial and slang expressions, showcases a novel and highly impactful use case. Traditional translation systems often struggle with the cultural and contextual nuances embedded in informal language. Marco-o1's ability to accurately translate such expressions signifies a leap towards more human-like, context-aware translation, which has immense commercial value for global digital commerce platforms. This opens new avenues for research into how reasoning capabilities can improve cross-cultural communication in AI systems.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Continued Optimization

The "Work in Progress" nature of the paper, coupled with the explicit mention of future work—such as refining reward signals through Outcome Reward Modeling (ORM) and Process Reward Modeling (PRM), and exploring reinforcement learning techniques—indicates a commitment to continuous improvement. This foresight suggests that the Marco-o1 framework has significant potential for further development, addressing the current limitations (e.g., randomness in tree search results due to confidence-based rewards) and moving closer to a "fully realized o1 model." The continued optimization could pave the way for LRMs that are not only powerful reasoners but also highly adaptable and reliable across a vast spectrum of complex, real-world challenges.

In conclusion, Marco-o1 is a noteworthy contribution that pushes the boundaries of large reasoning models by tackling the difficult problem of open-ended solutions. Its innovative integration of CoT, MCTS, and self-reflection, coupled with compelling results in nuanced multilingual translation, positions it as an important exploratory step in defining the future roadmap for highly capable and generalized AI.
