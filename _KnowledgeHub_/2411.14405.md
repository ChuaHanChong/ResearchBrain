# Marco-o1: Towards Open Reasoning Models for Open-Ended Solutions

> [!summary] Summary
> The Marco-o1 project from Alibaba International Digital Commerce explores a hybrid approach for Large Reasoning Models to address open-ended problem-solving. It demonstrates improved mathematical reasoning on MGSM datasets through a combination of Supervised Fine-Tuning, Monte Carlo Tree Search, and a reflection mechanism, and shows enhanced ability to translate nuanced colloquial language.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to generalize advanced reasoning capabilities, exemplified by models like o1, from well-defined problems (e.g., math, coding with clear answers) to complex, open-ended real-world scenarios.
- Developing robust Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) for tasks where clear standards are absent and rewards are challenging to quantify presents an unclear technical roadmap.
- Existing machine translation tools often lack the ability to accurately translate colloquial expressions and slang, which require deeper contextual and cultural understanding.

## Method

- The base model, Qwen2-7B-Instruct, is fine-tuned using ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== with a curated dataset including filtered Open-O1 CoT, synthetically generated Marco-o1 CoT via MCTS, and instruction-following data.
- ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== is integrated with the LLM, where nodes represent reasoning states and actions are LLM outputs; paths are evaluated by a ==confidence score based on token log probabilities==.
- A ==reflection mechanism== is incorporated by prompting the model to self-critique and re-evaluate its reasoning, along with exploring different action granularities (step vs. mini-step) within the MCTS.

## Results

- Marco-o1-MCTS (step) achieved **90.40%** accuracy on MGSM-En, a **6.4%** improvement over Marco-o1-CoT, and Marco-o1-MCTS (mini-step) reached **82.40%** on MGSM-Zh, an **11.2%** improvement over Marco-o1-CoT.
- The reflection mechanism helped solve about half of the difficult problems that the model initially failed, demonstrating its effectiveness in self-correction.
- A qualitative case study showed Marco-o1 provided more accurate and natural translations of Chinese colloquialisms into English compared to Google Translate, excelling in understanding context and nuances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with Large Language Models effectively expands the solution space, increasing the probability of finding correct answers, particularly evident in improved Test@1 scores.
> - A self-reflection mechanism, which prompts the model to re-evaluate its reasoning, helps solve approximately half of previously failed challenging problems.
> - Applying Large Reasoning Models to machine translation, especially for colloquialisms and slang, shows promise in handling nuanced linguistic and cultural contexts, an area where traditional systems struggle.
