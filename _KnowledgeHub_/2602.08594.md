# MOSAIC: Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap in Generalist Humanoid Motion Tracking and Teleoperation with Rapid Residual Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> The MOSAIC system offers a generalist approach to humanoid motion tracking and teleoperation, effectively bridging the simulation-to-real-world performance gap through a novel rapid residual adaptation mechanism. This enables stable, minute-scale operation on physical humanoid robots, even with varied and imperfect teleoperation interfaces.

## Problem

- Despite high simulation performance, generalist humanoid motion policies often fail when deployed on real hardware due to a "metric-deployment gap."
- Real-world teleoperation introduces challenging interface-specific errors, including latency, noise, and retargeting biases from diverse motion capture systems, leading to instability and catastrophic failures.
- Existing generalist trackers struggle with long-horizon locomotion and sustained teleoperation on physical robots, particularly with heterogeneous motion data.

## Method

![[2602.08594-method.png]]
*MOSAIC System Overview. MOSAIC consists of a unified training–deployment pipeline for humanoid motion tracking and teleoperation. Training/Simulation aggregates heterogeneous multi-source motions, two-level adaptive resampling, policy training process, yielding a deployable policy that preserves generality while improving real-robot robustness. Deployment/Real Robot supports both offline motion replay and online teleoperation. Finally, RobotBridge provides a modular interface that enables consistent evaluation and portable deployment across platforms.*

- A teleoperation-oriented general motion tracker is learned using reinforcement learning on a multi-source motion corpus, employing adaptive resampling and world-frame-centric reward design for global consistency.
- Rapid residual adaptation is introduced via an additive residual policy (π_RES) trained with dual-teacher behavior cloning on minimal interface-specific data, allowing corrections without degrading the frozen generalist policy (π_GMT).
- A modular, open-source deployment framework called RobotBridge is provided to standardize interfaces, enabling consistent evaluation and portability from simulation to various real humanoid robots.

## Results

- The MOSAIC general tracker outperformed baselines like GMT and TWIST in simulation, demonstrating superior global position tracking and robustness for dynamic motions.
- Residual adaptation significantly improved real-robot teleoperation stability and accuracy, outperforming fine-tuning and continual learning with as little as 15-30 minutes of interface-specific data.
- The Residual Adapter successfully corrected for interface-induced errors on real robots while preserving the general tracker's broad motion coverage across out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving robustness in generalist motion tracking requires diverse data sources and explicit world-frame rewards to ensure global motion consistency for teleoperation.
> - The primary bottleneck for real-robot performance, once simulation metrics are high, shifts from motion data diversity to specific interface- and dynamics-induced errors (e.g., latency, noise).
> - Rapid residual adaptation is the most effective and data-efficient strategy for bridging sim-to-real interface gaps, preserving the generalist policy's breadth while quickly adapting to new hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid motion imitation and control has seen rapid advancements, evolving from simple single-clip tracking to complex generalist policies capable of replicating diverse human behaviors. Early reinforcement learning (RL) systems like DeepMimic demonstrated the potential of learning physics-based character skills. Subsequent work extended this to more dynamic and contact-rich behaviors, with a recent shift towards "general motion tracking" policies trained on vast motion corpora, such as GMT, Any2Track, UniTracker, and KungfuBot2. Parallel to this, dedicated teleoperation systems (e.g., ExBody, OmniH2O, HumanPlus, CLONE, TWIST, SONIC) have emerged to facilitate scalable whole-body data collection and remote mobile manipulation.

MOSAIC integrates and extends these two convergent trajectories. The paper identifies a critical "metric-deployment gap" where high simulation tracking metrics often fail to translate to reliable hardware performance, especially for long-horizon locomotion and sustained teleoperation. This gap is exacerbated by heterogeneous motion data and diverse teleoperation interfaces, each introducing distinct latency, noise, and retargeting biases. Small, interface-induced errors can compound into catastrophic failures on real robots.

MOSAIC directly addresses this crucial sim-to-real challenge. Unlike previous generalist trackers that primarily optimize tracking metrics or disturbance adaptation in isolation, MOSAIC emphasizes a "teleoperation-ready system perspective" by focusing on stable, minute-scale tracking on a real humanoid. It distinguishes itself from other teleoperation systems by unifying offline motion replay and online IMU teleoperation under a single motion tracker, explicitly tackling long-horizon stability and dataset heterogeneity through its multi-source pipeline and adaptive resampling.

Furthermore, the work builds upon the concept of residual learning, seen in works like ResMimic and RMA, where an existing controller is refined with an additive corrective module. MOSAIC adapts this paradigm to the specific challenge of interface shifts in teleoperation, demonstrating its effectiveness for system stability and generality under out-of-distribution (OOD) settings. The project also contributes to the broader trend of leveraging large-scale motion corpora (like AMASS and GENMO) and "Behavior Foundation Models" for pretraining and distillation, but with a practical focus on translating imperfect and heterogeneous data sources into a *stable, deployable* real-world controller. In essence, MOSAIC represents a significant step towards practical, general-purpose humanoid teleoperation by systematically closing the sim-to-real gap that has historically plagued advanced robot control policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a deployable, robust, and generalist motion tracking and teleoperation system for humanoid robots that effectively bridges the persistent sim-to-real gap.

The motivation stems from a critical observation: despite rapid advancements in reinforcement learning-based humanoid motion imitation, policies that achieve state-of-the-art performance in simulation often exhibit brittle and unreliable behavior when deployed on physical hardware, particularly during long-horizon locomotion and sustained teleoperation. This "metric-deployment gap" is attributed to several interface- and dynamics-induced errors:
- **System Latency:** End-to-end delays across sensing, transmission, processing, and control execution create temporal mismatches between human intent and robot state, leading to instability.
- **Noise and Jitter:** Real-world teleoperation streams suffer from packet loss and sensor noise, introducing non-smooth perturbations that can trigger safety limits or destabilize contact transitions.
- **Retargeting Biases:** Different teleoperation interfaces (e.g., optical MoCap suits, inertial MoCap suits, VR trackers) have distinct kinematic and dynamic biases when retargeting human motion to a robot, leading to variations in commanded gait patterns.
- **Heterogeneous Data:** The very scalability of generalist trackers, built on fragmented and diverse motion datasets, introduces challenges in maintaining robustness across all scenarios on hardware.

The authors highlight that for mobile teleoperation, "accurate tracking" in simulation is insufficient; what is needed is "usable teleoperation" on a real robot, which implies long-term stability and fidelity in the world frame.

To address these motivations, MOSAIC sets out with several key objectives:
1. **Develop a Teleoperation-Oriented General Motion Tracker:** Learn a single, general policy via reinforcement learning (RL) on a multi-source motion bank, employing adaptive resampling and a reward design that explicitly emphasizes global (world-frame) motion consistency, critical for mobile teleoperation.
2. **Introduce Rapid Residual Adaptation:** Create an efficient mechanism to bridge the sim-to-real interface gap for new teleoperation devices. This involves training an interface-specific policy with minimal data and distilling its corrections into the general tracker through an additive residual module, without compromising the tracker's generality.
3. **Provide a Modular Deployment Framework:** Develop "RobotBridge," a robust, open-source framework to standardize interfaces across motion references, policy execution, simulators, robot backends, and low-level controllers, ensuring consistent evaluation and portability.
4. **Open-Source Resources:** Release a comprehensive suite of resources, including the multi-source motion dataset, training and deployment pipelines, and trained checkpoints, to foster reproducibility and accelerate community research.
5. **Validate with Rigorous Experiments:** Conduct systematic ablations, out-of-distribution benchmarking, and real-robot experiments to demonstrate robust offline motion replay and online long-horizon teleoperation under realistic latency and noise conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MOSAIC is presented as a full-stack system comprising two core components: a teleoperation-oriented general motion tracker and a rapid residual adaptation mechanism, supported by a robust deployment framework.

#### 3.1. Teleoperation-Oriented General Motion Tracker Learning

The first core component involves training a general motion tracking policy (π_GMT) capable of handling a wide array of human motions.
- **Reinforcement Learning Formulation:** The task is framed as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), optimized using the asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. The actor receives proprioceptive observations (with noise and history), while the critic has access to privileged state information.
- **Multi-Source Data Corpus:** MOSAIC is trained on an extensive, heterogeneous corpus of approximately 64 hours of motion data. This includes:
 - In-house optical MoCap recordings (3.1 hours).
 - In-house inertial MoCap recordings (7.0 hours), notably collected with a *different* commercial suit (IO-AI.Tech) than the one used for final teleoperation testing (Noitom.Tech), creating an intentional device mismatch to validate the adaptation module.
 - Public motion datasets (AMASS, OMOMO – 51.0 hours).
 - GENMO-generated synthetic motions (2.2 hours).
 - A small amount of interface-specific teleoperation data (1.0 hour) later used for adaptation.
 All data is uniformly converted to a robot-space representation at 50 Hz.
- **Adaptive Resampling:** To manage the heterogeneity and uneven lengths/difficulties of the multi-source data, a two-level adaptive resampling strategy is employed:
 - **Within-motion sampling:** Follows BeyondMimic, dividing each motion into coarse time bins and sampling from a failure-aware distribution to focus on unstable segments.
 - **Motion-level sampling:** Periodically assigns environments to motions based on a mixture of difficulty (higher failure rates), novelty (less-assigned motions), and a uniform term to ensure broad coverage.
- **Reward Design:** The reward function extends BeyondMimic's tracking objectives by explicitly incorporating world-frame teleoperation tracking rewards. These emphasize global motion consistency (e.g., tracking body positions and world-frame motion) alongside kinematic fidelity, which is crucial for stable mobile teleoperation. Regularization penalties for undesired contacts, action rate, joint limits, acceleration, and torque are also included.
- **Domain Randomization:** Standard domain randomization techniques (e.g., body friction, torso CoM offset, base velocity perturbation) are applied during training to enhance sim-to-real transfer.

#### 3.2. Rapid Residual Adaptation

The second core component, Residual Adaptation, addresses the specific sim-to-real interface gap without sacrificing the generalist capabilities of π_GMT.
- **Adaptation Policy (π_ADAPT):** A separate adaptation policy is trained on a small, interface-specific dataset (e.g., ~30 minutes of VR teleoperation data) collected directly from the target teleoperation device. This policy learns the intrinsic noise characteristics and device-specific gait variations.
- **Residual Adapter (π_RES):** Instead of fine-tuning π_GMT (which can lead to catastrophic forgetting), an additive residual module, π_RES, is introduced. The student policy (π_S) is defined as π_S(o_t) = π_GMT(o_t) + π_RES(o_t), where π_GMT remains frozen.
- **Zero-biased Initialization:** The final layer of π_RES is initialized with near-zero weights and zero bias, ensuring its initial output is close to zero. This makes early training updates conservative and prevents degradation of the base policy.
- **Dual-Teacher Behavior Cloning:** π_RES is trained through a multi-teacher distillation framework. For teleoperation adaptation data (D_adapt), π_ADAPT serves as the teacher, providing expert demonstrations of interface-specific corrections. For general heterogeneous data (D_gmt), π_GMT serves as the teacher to preserve general proficiency. This strategy allows π_RES to learn to compensate for domain gaps while retaining the foundational skills of π_GMT.

#### 3.3. RobotBridge and Open Resources

- **RobotBridge:** A modular deployment framework that standardizes interfaces among motion references, policy execution, simulator/robot backends, and low-level controllers. It enables consistent evaluation from simulation to real robots (Unitree G1, H1, PND Adam) with minimal changes, promoting reproducibility and fair comparisons.
- **Open-Source Release:** The project includes a comprehensive open-source release of training code, deployment code (RobotBridge), trained checkpoints, collected motion data, and data collection pipelines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation provides key insights into robust general motion tracking and effective sim-to-real adaptation.

#### 4.1. Design Principles for Robust General Motion Tracking

- **Data Source Diversity is Crucial:** Ablation studies showed that increasing the diversity of motion data sources (from 1-Source to 5-Sources) consistently improved both tracking fidelity and robustness (success rate, episode length) on an out-of-distribution (OOD) test set (Motion-X-Sub). This highlights the importance of heterogeneous data for generalization.
- **World-Frame Rewards are Essential for Teleoperation:** Augmenting ego-centric (robot-frame) tracking rewards with world-frame objectives (global pose and motion consistency) significantly reduced long-horizon drift and improved stability. This demonstrates that explicit incentives for maintaining global trajectories are vital for robust mobile teleoperation, preventing falls and stumbles.
- **Pure RL Outperforms DAgger-style Distillation:** In this specific setting, direct reinforcement learning consistently yielded superior tracking fidelity and robustness compared to DAgger-style distillation from a teacher policy. The authors attribute this to the ability of pure RL to directly optimize long-horizon objectives with sufficient data, bypassing the complexities of transferring fine-grained behaviors through distillation.
- **Superior Performance over Baselines:** The final MOSAIC general tracker (trained with multi-source data, world-frame rewards, and pure RL) outperformed prior representative methods like GMT and TWIST in simulation, demonstrating notable gains in global position tracking and robustness, especially for high-dynamic motions (e.g., substantial ground clearance during jumps).

#### 4.2. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap via Rapid Adaptation

- **Interface Gap is the Bottleneck:** A critical finding was that despite high simulation metrics and excellent generalization to OOD motions in simulation, the general tracker's performance significantly degraded on a real robot. This degradation was attributed primarily to teleoperation interface factors (system latency, noise, and hardware execution artifacts) rather than insufficient motion diversity, confirming the central problem MOSAIC aims to solve.
- **Residual Adaptation is the Most Effective Strategy:**
 - **Preserving Generality:** Unlike *fine-tuning* (which caused catastrophic forgetting and reduced overall robustness) or *continual learning* (which yielded negligible improvement due to gradient dilution), the Residual Adapter successfully injected interface-specific corrections *without degrading* the general tracker's broad motion coverage on OOD benchmarks.
 - **Real-Robot Performance:** On a 10-minute held-out real-robot teleoperation test set (using VR data), the Residual Adapter (particularly with world-frame rewards, "Adapter (W)") consistently and substantially outperformed the base model, fine-tuning, and continual learning in terms of position tracking accuracy and success rate.
 - **Data Efficiency:** Meaningful improvements with the Residual Adapter were observed with as little as 15 minutes of adaptation data, with further gains at 30 minutes, highlighting its sample efficiency.
 - **Limited Impact of FLD Augmentation:** Fourier Latent Dynamics (FLD) augmentation, while improving interpolation within periodic motion manifolds, provided only marginal gains for real-robot teleoperation robustness, as it did not directly address interface-induced system errors. This reinforces the finding that interface-level adaptation is more critical than simply expanding periodic locomotion data.

**Overall Conclusion from Findings:** The research definitively shows that after simulation tracking metrics saturate, real-robot failures are mainly driven by interface and dynamics gaps. Residual adaptation emerges as the most stable and sample-efficient method to bridge these gaps, allowing for rapid, data-efficient correction on a frozen generalist policy, thereby enabling stable, minute-scale teleoperation while preserving broad motion generality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MOSAIC represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics, offering a practical and principled approach to developing deployable generalist motion controllers. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

- **Enabling Robust Humanoid Teleoperation:** By explicitly addressing the sim-to-real gap, MOSAIC provides a foundation for reliable and sustained whole-body teleoperation of humanoid robots. This is critical for tasks requiring human supervision, remote operation in hazardous environments, and efficient data collection for further learning. The ability to faithfully mirror real-time human motion streams, even for challenging contact-rich and high-agility behaviors, is a game-changer for practical applications.
- **Accelerating Real-World Robot Deployment:** The rapid residual adaptation mechanism offers a powerful tool for quickly deploying generalist policies to new hardware interfaces with minimal additional data. This greatly reduces the time and effort traditionally required for sim-to-real transfer and calibration, making advanced humanoid capabilities more accessible and adaptable to diverse real-world scenarios. This flexibility is essential for the widespread adoption of humanoid robots.
- **Advancing Generalist Robot Control:** MOSAIC's approach to learning a teleoperation-oriented general motion tracker, coupled with robust adaptive resampling and world-frame reward design, pushes the boundaries of how generalist policies can be trained for real-world efficacy. It provides actionable insights into balancing data diversity with deployment-specific stability requirements.
- **Fostering Open Science and Reproducibility:** The commitment to an open-source release of the full-stack system (MOSAIC), the modular deployment framework (RobotBridge), extensive motion datasets, training pipelines, and trained checkpoints is invaluable. This will lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, enabling faster progress, direct comparisons, and more reproducible results across the field of humanoid robotics. RobotBridge, in particular, promotes portability across various humanoid platforms, fostering a collaborative ecosystem.
- **Guiding Future Research Priorities:** The paper's conclusion that interface- and dynamics-induced errors (like latency and noise) become the primary bottleneck for real-robot performance, rather than insufficient motion diversity (once simulation metrics saturate), offers crucial guidance for future research. This suggests that resources should increasingly focus on mitigating these specific real-world effects rather than solely on scaling motion data or model capacity.
- **Laying Foundation for Mobile Manipulation:** Stable and general whole-body teleoperation is a foundational capability for more complex mobile manipulation tasks. By providing a robust base, MOSAIC indirectly accelerates research in areas like mobile manipulation, human-robot interaction, and large-scale imitation learning for humanoids.

In summary, MOSAIC moves beyond merely achieving high simulation scores to deliver a truly deployable and adaptable humanoid control system. Its practical contributions, coupled with a strong emphasis on open-source development, are set to significantly impact the trajectory of humanoid robotics research and its real-world application.
