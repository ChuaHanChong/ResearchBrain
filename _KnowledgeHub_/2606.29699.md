# Early Warning Signals for OpenVLA Failure under Visual Distribution Shift

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper tests whether a frozen OpenVLA policy's own internal activations warn of impending failure under visual distribution shift. One MLP down-projection state per LIBERO-10 step feeds two linear monitors: a mean-difference direction and a logistic probe. Occlusion cuts success from 57% to 17%; a layer-16 probe reaches AUROC 0.972 on failed matched-reset episodes yet fires 3.32 times per clean episode.

## Problem

- Runtime monitors built on deep ensembles or external vision-language success detectors need extra trained predictors, and predictive uncertainty itself degrades under dataset shift.
- Prior feature-based detectors target overall task failure or unseen-task transfer, leaving untested whether a temporally localized pre-failure window is linearly accessible under controlled visual shift.
- Conformal and out-of-distribution methods assume exchangeability and treat novelty as the target, so they fit closed-loop rollouts poorly where familiar observations still precede failure.

## Method

- A forward pre-hook on the language model's ==mlp.down_proj== records one activation per environment step, retaining the final autoregressive action-token pass from a frozen ==OpenVLA==-7B LIBERO-10 checkpoint.
- Two linear monitors: a unit ==mean-difference direction== between failed and successful episode-mean activations, and a step-level ==logistic probe== on standardized activations fit to pre-failure-window labels.
- A labeling protocol sets failure time from wrong-object, drop, or stalled-progress heuristics, marks steps within K as positive, omits the ambiguous band, and thresholds warnings at the clean 95th percentile.

## Results

- Occlusion drops task success from **57/100** to **17/100** episodes; on failed occluded rollouts the layer-16 logistic probe reaches AUROC **0.972** and AUPRC **0.352**.
- The mean-difference direction reaches only **0.654** AUROC at that layer and action disagreement **0.496**, near chance; logistic AUROC is non-monotone across layers 8, 10, 16 (**0.952**, **0.801**, **0.972**).
- Without refitting, the probe transfers to camera jitter at AUROC **0.689**; color shift produced zero failures, and clean-run thresholding still yields **3.32** onsets per episode, **2.63%** warned steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strong retrospective ranking does not imply operationally quiet warning behavior: the same thresholded monitor can interrupt clean episodes several times per rollout.
> - Reusing tasks, resets and seeds between fitting and evaluation measures separability, not prediction; step-level metrics amplify that dependence and episode grouping cannot fix it.
> - Cross-shift transfer rules out only the narrowest shortcut story; a probe may still read generic image degradation or task progress rather than impending failure.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot policy may keep moving plausibly after a visual change, while an operator has little external evidence that it is drifting toward failure. In this paper, the authors leave the policy frozen and read a simple warning score from one of its internal states. When a black rectangle is added to the camera image, OpenVLA succeeds less often. A readout of its hidden state nevertheless distinguishes near-failure steps in the recorded rollouts far better than action disagreement. Clean behavior still triggers warnings, though. The paper defines "early warning" only as association with steps in a fixed window before a logged failure. The score might instead respond to a visual shift, a familiar reset, or neighboring frames.

Scores for OpenVLA MLP down-projection layers 8, 10, and 16. AUROC is on the left and AUPRC on the right, on separate scales. Blue circles show the mean-direction monitor and orange squares the logistic regression probe. Gray segments connect the two monitor scores and are not uncertainty intervals. The dashed AUROC line marks the action-disagreement baseline at 0.496. These are point estimates with matched task, reset, and seed. They support ranking on the stated split, not independent prediction.

### 2. A readout, not a robot intervention

A vision-language-action (VLA) policy maps camera observations and a language instruction to robot actions. The researchers froze OpenVLA and changed only rendered pixels. At the instant of perturbation, the simulator state, instruction, dynamics, and success rule stayed unchanged. A hook recorded the input to an MLP down projection at a selected model layer once per environment step, retaining the final autoregressive action-token pass.

The paper tests two monitors, and neither changes the policy:

- One projects activation vectors onto a direction formed from successful-versus-failed episode means.
- The other fits step-level logistic regression to labeled near-failure steps.

For failed episodes, the logger uses the first wrong-object, drop, or stalled-progress event, or terminal timeout if no heuristic fires. All steps in successful episodes are negative. A positive step is one within the evaluation window of the logged failure. These events are unaudited heuristics.

### 3. The hidden-state signal and its tests

The paper tests the OpenVLA checkpoint fine-tuned on LIBERO-10, using task IDs 0–4, seed 7, and 20 trials per task per condition (100 episodes). In this collection, clean success was 57/100 and occlusion success was 17/100. These are not average LIBERO-10 performance estimates.

Two metrics are reported. AUROC measures how well a score ranks positive versus negative labeled steps. AUPRC is more sensitive to the rare positive class, and random-ranking AUPRC equals positive-step prevalence.

The strongest recorded result comes from pre-failure-window steps within failed occluded trajectories. There, the layer-16 logistic probe reaches AUROC 0.972 and AUPRC 0.352, compared with AUROC 0.496 for action disagreement. The layer-16 mean-direction monitor reaches AUROC 0.654 and AUPRC 0.057. These results come with three conditions:

- The probe was fit with a 20-step horizon and evaluated against a 15-step horizon.
- Fit and evaluation reused task, reset, and seed. The result is therefore retrospective separability under the recorded protocol, not an independent-episode prediction result.
- Layer 16 was not independently selected. Only layers 8, 10, and 16 were tested, without a documented prespecified selection rule.

The action-disagreement baseline perturbs one image slightly, samples actions, and scores variance across action dimensions. The comparison establishes that this particular action-instability score did not supply the same ranking signal. It does not show that activations add information unavailable from pixels or other action-level signals.

The paper also tests transfer to a second perturbation. On failed camera-jitter episodes, the occlusion-trained layer-16 probe reached AUROC 0.689 without refitting. This weakens the narrow explanation that the probe detects only the occlusion artifact, but a shared image-degradation or task-progress feature remains possible. A color shift at the tested strength caused no failures, so it cannot test detection.

### 4. Ranking is not quiet warning behavior

AUROC and AUPRC assess ranking, not how often a warning interrupts clean behavior. The warning threshold is the 95th percentile of clean calibration scores. The reported clean warning counts reuse those calibration runs, so they are calibration checks, not held-out false-alarm rates. The layer-16 logistic probe has the lowest active-warning fraction among the listed logistic layers, 2.63% of clean steps, but still produces 3.32 warning onsets per clean episode.

Clean-run warning onsets per episode and active percentage of warned steps for the direction and logistic monitors at layers 8, 10, and 16. The layer-16 logistic monitor has the lowest active percentage, 2.63%, and still has 3.32 onsets per episode. The threshold uses these same rollouts, so these are calibration checks.

### 5. A signal, not a safety system

The strong matched-reset ranking and the frequent clean warnings together make internal state a candidate monitoring signal. A deployment claim would still require independent resets and seeds, audited failure times, separately calibrated thresholds, and a tested supervisor.
