# RoboSeg: Online Part-Level Semantic Reconstruction for Robotic Manipulation via a Single Eye-in-Hand Camera

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboSeg builds a part-level semantic 3D map online from a single eye-in-hand RGB-D camera. A vision-language model names one to three functional parts, an asynchronous geometry thread does RGB-D odometry and TSDF fusion while keyframes trigger SAM3 part masks fused by voxel voting, and part labels index AnyGrasp candidates. It reaches 83.4% mean part IoU and 21/24 combined task successes.

## Problem

- Offline 3D part segmentation assumes a CAD model, mesh, or complete point cloud is available beforehand, and never closes the loop to actual robot manipulation.
- Open-vocabulary 2D segmenters split objects by texture, shadows, seams, and highlights rather than by manipulation-relevant functional structure, producing cluttered grasp targets.
- Open-vocabulary semantic mapping systems target room or object scale, leaving part-level granularity, eye-in-hand viewpoint drift, and partial observation unaddressed for tabletop manipulation.

## Method

- ==VLM-driven part discovery==: ==Qwen3-VL== reads the first RGB frame and returns object name plus one to three self-contained part prompts that constrain the ==SAM3== vocabulary.
- ==Asynchronous online reconstruction==: a high-frequency thread runs ==kinematics-regularized RGB-D odometry== and ==TSDF fusion== while a keyframe-triggered semantic thread projects ==SAM3== masks into shared voxels.
- ==Temporal consensus by voxel voting==: each occupied voxel keeps a top-3 instance-ID vote tensor, with labels from the argmax vote, suppressing frame-to-frame flicker; ==AnyGrasp== candidates are then indexed by dominant part.

## Results

- Part accuracy: **83.4%** mIoU over five annotated objects (Hammer **99.8%**, KitchenKnife **99.3%**, BigPot **85.8%**, WateringCan **70.1%**, DoubleHandlePot **61.9%**) under a **5 mm** surface cutoff.
- On four aligned objects RoboSeg reaches **86.7%** mIoU versus PartField's **83.2%**, despite PartField receiving the complete vertex-aligned mesh; geometry accuracy is **4.6-4.9 mm** with F1@10 of **87-89%**.
- Physical pilot: **21/24** physical, **24/24** task-part, **21/24** combined successes; semantic filtering hits the target part in **8/8** conditions versus **4/8** for unfiltered top-1, labeling **792/883** (**89.7%**) proposals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining a promptable segmenter with a compact VLM-generated part vocabulary before segmentation beats filtering exhaustive masks afterward, shrinking both the semantic search space and fusion cost.
> - Decoupling fast geometry fusion from slow semantic inference, plus per-voxel temporal voting, converts unstable per-frame 2D masks into persistent 3D labels without stalling tracking.
> - A semantic map is best positioned as an indexing layer over an existing grasp proposer, not a replacement; residual failures were mechanical lift instability, not semantic routing errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Effective robotic manipulation often necessitates understanding objects at a functional part level, rather than merely identifying entire object categories or amorphous point clouds. For instance, a robot might need to interact specifically with a mug handle for lifting or a watering-can spout for pouring. This requirement distinguishes manipulation tasks from broader object recognition or scene understanding, where object-level semantics might suffice.

Existing approaches to 3D part-level segmentation, such as PartSLIP, PartSTAD, and ZeroPS, typically operate under the assumption of known or precomputed complete object geometry, like a CAD model or a dense mesh. These methods primarily focus on optimizing part labels given a static, complete geometric input, without explicitly integrating the perception process into an online manipulation loop. A robot, however, must build and utilize such representations dynamically from raw sensor data while observing an unknown object in its workspace.

The advent of 2D vision foundation models, including Segment Anything Model (SAM) and Grounding DINO, has enabled open-vocabulary segmentation of arbitrary visual regions. However, their direct application in an online robotic manipulation context presents challenges. Firstly, these models may segment based on visual cues such as texture or color, producing masks that do not necessarily correspond to manipulation-relevant functional parts. Secondly, projecting these 2D masks into a stable 3D representation requires robust handling of camera motion, pose drift, and partial observations inherent in an online scanning process.

Furthermore, open-vocabulary semantic mapping systems, such as ConceptFusion, ConceptGraphs, and SplaTAM, have demonstrated the integration of language-aligned visual features into 3D maps, typically for room-scale or object-level scene understanding. While these systems successfully create semantic maps, their utility for fine-grained, part-level manipulation, specifically in guiding task-relevant grasp selection, is not their primary focus.

Learning-based grasp detection systems, like AnyGrasp, provide dense 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) grasp candidates. Some research incorporates language or semantic information into grasping through methods like semantic grasp generation or task-aware grasp ranking. RoboSeg differentiates itself by focusing on the "engineering bridge" between online part-level semantic mapping and task-conditioned grasp selection. It aims to construct and stabilize a persistent 3D part map as a precursor to downstream grasp proposal and selection, rather than solely reformulating task-oriented grasping as a point-cloud semantic segmentation or direct grasp ranking problem.

RoboSeg addresses these identified gaps by integrating VLM-guided part discovery, asynchronous online 3D semantic mapping, and task-conditioned grasp candidate selection into a unified pipeline. It aims to create a system that can discover and map manipulation-relevant parts from real-time RGB-D observations and use this map to inform grasp selection for specific tasks, without requiring pre-existing object models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a perception system, named RoboSeg, that enables robotic manipulation by identifying functional parts of objects in an online, interactive setting. The system aims to link VLM-based functional-part discovery, asynchronous online RGB-D semantic reconstruction, and task-oriented grasp generation.

The core motivation stems from two coupled questions critical for effective robotic manipulation:
1. **Which regions of an object afford stable contact?** This involves identifying geometrically stable points or surfaces suitable for grasping.
2. **How does the selected contact region support the intended task?** This requires semantic understanding, linking specific parts to their functions (e.g., a handle for lifting, a spout for pouring).

Traditional approaches often struggle with this dual requirement because the operationally meaningful unit for manipulation is frequently a functional part rather than the entire object category. Current 3D part-level segmentation methods are typically offline and assume complete object geometry, making them unsuitable for dynamic robotic interaction with unknown objects. Meanwhile, general 2D segmentation models, while powerful, may segment based on visual appearance (texture, color) rather than functional relevance, and their outputs are not inherently stable in 3D. Existing open-vocabulary semantic mapping systems generally target larger scales or object-level understanding, lacking the part-level granularity needed for fine manipulation.

RoboSeg is motivated to bridge these gaps by:
- Enabling a robot to discover manipulation-relevant parts from raw, online RGB-D observations captured by an eye-in-hand camera.
- Building a 3D model with part-level semantics concurrently with observation.
- Generating a persistent and stable part-labeled 3D map despite noisy and partial observations.
- Using this map to assign and select grasp candidates based on task-relevant part labels, thus closing the perception-to-manipulation loop without relying on CAD models or pre-scanned meshes.

The specific contributions outlined are:
1. Presenting an online perception-to-grasp-candidate interface that connects VLM-guided functional part discovery, asynchronous 3D semantic reconstruction, and AnyGrasp-based part-indexed candidate selection.
2. Evaluating this interface through three questions:
 - Whether discovered parts match manipulation-relevant annotations and are comparable to offline methods.
 - Whether the geometric backbone is accurate enough for candidate indexing.
 - Whether the resulting map can guide real task-part grasp attempts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboSeg is structured into three main modules: VLM-guided functional part discovery, asynchronous online semantic reconstruction, and task-oriented grasping. The system operates with a UR5 manipulator equipped with an Intel RealSense D435i eye-in-hand camera.

**A. VLM-Driven Part Discovery:**
This module aims to constrain generic 2D segmentation to focus on manipulation-relevant functional parts.
1. **Initial Observation:** The robot captures an initial RGB frame of the object.
2. **VLM Query:** The Qwen3-VL model is queried with a prompt to name the object and list one to three functional parts relevant for manipulation. The output format specifies the object name, a list of part names (e.g., `watering can handle`, `screwdriver shaft`), and a rationale.
3. **Prompt Generation:** Only the compact, self-contained part names are forwarded as prompts to the SAM3 segmentation model. This design reduces the semantic search space, focusing on functional components rather than arbitrary visual regions (which might arise from textures or shadows), and limits the number of masks requiring 3D fusion.

**B. Asynchronous Online Semantic Reconstruction:**
This module converts the eye-in-hand RGB-D scan into a stable, part-labeled 3D point cloud. Its core design principle is the asynchronous decoupling of metric reconstruction from semantic inference.
1. **Two Asynchronous Streams:**
 - **High-Frequency Geometry Thread:** This thread continuously processes incoming RGB-D frames. It estimates camera poses (optionally regularized by robot forward kinematics) and integrates every depth image into a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) volume. This ensures responsive odometry and geometry fusion.
 - **Lower-Frequency Semantic Thread:** This thread is triggered at a configurable keyframe interval. It runs the SAM3 model using the VLM-generated part prompts to produce 2D semantic masks for the keyframe. These masks are then back-projected into the current TSDF voxel coordinates.
2. **Temporal Consensus via Voxel Voting:** To mitigate the temporal instability of per-frame 2D masks (e.g., due to viewpoint changes, boundary shifts, or flickering labels), RoboSeg uses a voxel-voting mechanism.
 - Each occupied voxel in the TSDF volume is augmented with a compact voting tensor `S(v)`. This tensor stores the top-K (where K=3) candidate instance IDs and their corresponding vote counts.
 - When a new mask from the semantic thread projects onto a set of voxels, the system first attempts to find an existing instance ID of the same class within those voxels. If no match is found, a new instance ID is allocated.
 - The selected instance ID receives one vote in every voxel covered by the mask.
 - At query time, the semantic label for a voxel is determined by the instance ID with the maximum votes, `ˆi(v) = arg max_k s_k(v)`. This temporal accumulation stabilizes the 3D semantic labels, suppressing transient 2D errors.

**C. Task-Conditioned Grasp Candidate Selection:**
After the scanning process is complete, this module utilizes the reconstructed part-labeled 3D map for task-oriented grasping.
1. **Map Export:** The system exports the reconstructed point cloud, along with per-point semantic instance IDs and an instance table mapping IDs to part names.
2. **Grasp Proposal:** AnyGrasp, a pre-trained grasp detector, is applied to the reconstructed geometry to generate dense 6-DoF grasp candidates.
3. **Semantic Assignment:** For each generated grasp candidate, the system queries the points within its gripper closing region and assigns the candidate to the dominant semantic instance detected in that region.
4. **Task-Conditioned Selection:** Given a natural-language task (e.g., "grasp the handle to lift the mug"), task-level reasoning maps this instruction to one or more relevant part labels in the instance table. The system then filters the AnyGrasp candidates, retaining only those whose assigned semantic label matches the requested part. These filtered candidates are then ranked by their original AnyGrasp score, and the highest-ranked one is selected for execution.
This approach leverages the semantic map as an actionable interface for downstream manipulation, rather than merely for visualization or direct grasp generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RoboSeg focused on three main questions: the quality of discovered manipulation-relevant parts, the accuracy of the geometric backbone, and the system's ability to guide physical task-part grasping. Experiments utilized a UR5 robot with an eye-in-hand Intel RealSense D435i camera, evaluating household and workshop objects with relevant parts.

**A. Comparison with Offline Part Segmentation Methods:**
The first experiment compared RoboSeg's online part segmentation with offline 3D part segmentation methods (PartSLIP, PartField) under conditions favorable to the baselines (operating on complete object meshes or processed geometry). RoboSeg, in contrast, built its representation online from RGB-D observations.

- **Qualitative Comparison (Fig. 3):** RoboSeg's functional part structure was observed to be more aligned with manipulation tasks. Offline methods, despite receiving complete geometry, sometimes absorbed critical parts (handles, spouts) into larger regions or partitioned objects based on broad geometric components rather than functional relevance. RoboSeg directly separated task-critical regions in the provided examples.
- **Quantitative Comparison (mIoU):** Functional parts were manually annotated for five RoboSeg reconstructions. RoboSeg achieved an 83.4% mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) across these objects (BigPot: 85.8%, DoubleHandlePot: 61.9%, Hammer: 99.8%, KitchenKnife: 99.3%, WateringCan: 70.1%). Lower scores were associated with thin or protruding parts.
 - On four aligned objects (BigPot, DoubleHandlePot, Hammer, KitchenKnife) where a direct metric comparison with PartField under identical merged material labels was possible, RoboSeg achieved 86.7% mIoU compared to PartField's 83.2%. This comparison was made with different input conditions: RoboSeg on online-scan points projected to ground truth (GT) surface with a 5 mm cutoff, and PartField on the complete vertex-aligned mesh. The results indicate that RoboSeg's online approach yields comparable part segmentation quality to offline methods on restricted common labels, despite not using complete mesh input.

**B. Geometric Accuracy of RGB-D Odometry:**
This experiment assessed the accuracy of the geometric backbone (RGB-D odometry and TSDF fusion) against aligned CAD or dataset geometry for representative objects. Two pose sources were compared: robot forward kinematics and visually estimated RGB-D odometry. Metrics included Chamfer distance, accuracy, completeness, and F1 scores at 5 mm and 10 mm.

- **Aggregate Metrics (Table I):** Across five paired objects, RGB-D odometry showed slightly better performance on Chamfer distance (10.7mm vs 11.1mm), accuracy (4.6mm vs 4.9mm), completeness (6.2mm vs 6.3mm), and F1@5 (75.0% vs 74.3%) compared to kinematic poses. However, F1@10 was marginally lower (87.2% vs 88.9%). The results were mixed, with the "Hammer" object being a significant source of variance and "KitchenKnife" proving challenging due to its thin geometry. The metrics were computed on the semantic instance cloud, which excludes the support plane.

**C. Pilot Physical Task-Part Grasp Validation:**
This experiment evaluated RoboSeg's capability to serve as an execution interface for task-conditioned grasping on a physical robot. It focused on the semantic routing of AnyGrasp proposals rather than on generating new grasp types.

- **Experimental Setup:** 24 physical trials were conducted across eight object-task conditions, with each condition repeated three times. Tasks included "Lift the pot by the main body," "Hold the red handle to drive a nail," and "Lift the watering can by the spout." For each trial, RoboSeg performed a single scan, resolved the task to a target semantic part, generated AnyGrasp candidates on the reconstructed cloud, and executed the highest-ranked candidate whose contact point lay on the specified part.
- **Success Metrics (Table II):**
 - **Physical Success:** The robot successfully grasped and lifted the object stably.
 - **Task-Part Success:** The executed grasp contacted the intended task-relevant part, regardless of physical stability.
 - **Combined Success:** Both physical and task-part conditions were met.
- **Results:**
 - **Task-Part Success:** Achieved in 24/24 trials, indicating consistent and correct semantic routing to the requested functional part by the system.
 - **Physical Success:** Achieved in 21/24 trials.
 - **Combined Success:** Achieved in 21/24 trials. The three failures were attributed to issues with grasp execution (lack of stability during lift) rather than incorrect semantic selection, as they still contacted the correct part.
- **Grasp Proposal Analysis:** When re-ranking recorded AnyGrasp proposals, RoboSeg's semantic filtering selected a target-part candidate in 8/8 conditions, whereas the unfiltered AnyGrasp top-1 candidate matched the target part in only 4/8 conditions. This suggests improved task alignment through semantic filtering.
- **Candidate-Indexing Coverage:** Across 21 reconstructed scenes, RoboSeg’s candidate-to-part assignment successfully labeled 792 of 883 AnyGrasp proposals (89.7%) with a known functional part, indicating broad coverage of generated grasp candidates by the reconstructed map.

These results collectively support the claim that RoboSeg provides a functional semantic indexing interface for task-conditioned grasp execution, effectively integrating part-level semantic understanding into robotic manipulation. The pilot nature of the physical trials suggests feasibility rather than a definitive performance rate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboSeg represents an advancement in robotic manipulation by providing an online, closed-loop perception-to-grasp system that addresses several limitations of existing methods. Its primary significance lies in the integration of diverse AI components to enable robots to understand and interact with objects at a functional part level without prior knowledge of object geometry.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Online Functional Part Discovery and Reconstruction:** RoboSeg facilitates the real-time discovery of manipulation-relevant functional parts from raw RGB-D observations using an eye-in-hand camera. This capability is crucial for robots operating in unstructured or novel environments where CAD models or pre-scanned meshes are unavailable. This directly contrasts with traditional 3D part segmentation methods that rely on complete, offline geometric inputs.
- **VLM-Guided Semantic Focusing:** By leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like Qwen3-VL, RoboSeg constrains general-purpose 2D segmentation models (like SAM3) to output masks corresponding to functional, manipulation-relevant parts. This addresses the common issue of unconstrained 2D segmentation producing visually-driven but functionally irrelevant partitions, reducing semantic clutter.
- **Asynchronous and Stable 3D Semantic Mapping:** The asynchronous architecture, decoupling high-frequency geometry tracking from lower-frequency semantic inference, allows for responsive system operation. The voxel-voting mechanism for fusing 2D semantic masks into a 3D volume effectively stabilizes part labels against temporal noise, viewpoint changes, and partial observations, leading to a persistent and reliable part-labeled 3D map.
- **Task-Conditioned Grasp Selection:** RoboSeg provides a practical interface that links the semantically rich 3D map to task-oriented grasp selection. By assigning pre-trained grasp candidates (from AnyGrasp) to specific semantic parts and filtering them based on natural-language task instructions, the system enables more intelligent and context-aware manipulation. This transforms a general grasp proposal into a task-specific, part-indexed choice, enhancing the robot's ability to execute commands like "grasp the handle."

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** The ability to autonomously discover and segment functional parts, combined with task-conditioned grasping, could significantly enhance the autonomy of robots in diverse applications, from household chores and elderly care to industrial assembly and logistics.
- **Reduced Manual Effort and Data Dependency:** By reducing the reliance on pre-annotated datasets, CAD models, or manual programming for specific objects, RoboSeg could lower the entry barrier for deploying robots in varied environments and with novel objects.
- **Foundation for More Complex Manipulation Tasks:** The part-level semantic map serves as a fundamental building block for more complex manipulation behaviors, such as multi-part interaction, tool use, and dexterous manipulation, where specific parts of objects must be identified and manipulated precisely according to their function.
- **Advancement in Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability to interpret natural language instructions like "grasp the red handle" and map them to physical actions on specific object parts could improve the naturalness and effectiveness of human-robot collaboration.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, including the small scale of physical trials (24 trials with manual reset and standardized initial views), the limited set of objects with compatible manual annotations, challenges in complete metric-frame alignment for all offline baselines, and the absence of component-level runtime profiling.

Future work aims to address these limitations by:
- Expanding physical trials to a larger scale and variety of objects.
- Evaluating robustness under broader object poses and occlusions.
- Extending semantic evaluation with reliably aligned baselines.
- Profiling runtime at the component level to understand computational bottlenecks.
- Comparing against additional robot-executed selection policies to further validate the system's performance.

Overall, RoboSeg establishes a functional framework for online, part-level semantic reconstruction that directly supports task-conditioned robotic manipulation, positioning it as a foundational semantic indexing layer for advanced robotic capabilities.
