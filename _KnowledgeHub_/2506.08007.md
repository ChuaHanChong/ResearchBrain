# Reinforcement Pre-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Reinforcement Pre-Training (RPT) introduces a novel paradigm for pre-training large language models by reframing next-token prediction as a reasoning task, utilizing on-policy reinforcement learning with verifiable, rule-based rewards. This approach enhances next-token prediction accuracy and improves zero-shot performance on diverse downstream tasks by fostering deeper reasoning capabilities from the foundational training stage.

## Problem

- Standard self-supervised next-token prediction in LLM pre-training primarily optimizes for statistical likelihood, which may not foster deeper reasoning or robust understanding.
- Current reinforcement learning methods for LLMs (like RLHF and RLVR) are mostly confined to post-training fine-tuning due to their reliance on costly human preference data or limited domain-specific annotations.
- A significant gap exists in applying scalable and general reinforcement learning techniques to the foundational, general-purpose pre-training phase of LLMs using vast amounts of unannotated text.

## Method

- Reinforcement Pre-Training (RPT) reframes the ubiquitous next-token prediction task as a =='next-token reasoning process'==, requiring the model to generate a chain-of-thought before predicting the final token.
- The approach employs an ==on-policy reinforcement learning framework==, where the language model performs multiple 'thinking trajectories' for each context.
- A ==deterministic prefix matching reward== (1 for exact prefix match, 0 otherwise) is used to verify the correctness of the predicted next token, leveraging unannotated text data for verifiable intrinsic rewards.

## Results

- Reinforcement Pre-Training (RPT)-14B achieved higher next-token prediction accuracy across all difficulty levels on OmniMATH, reaching **23.75%** accuracy on 'Hard' tokens, outperforming a 14B baseline and matching a 32B baseline.
- Models pre-trained with RPT provided a stronger foundation for subsequent reinforcement fine-tuning, reaching **58.3%** accuracy after RLVR on Skywork-OR1, compared to **52.7%** for the baseline.
- RPT-14B showed enhanced zero-shot performance on general downstream tasks, with approximately **7 points** gain on SuperGPQA and **22 points** gain on MMLU-Pro over a 32B standard next-token prediction baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing pre-training as an active reasoning task, where the model explicitly generates a chain-of-thought to predict the next token, enables LLMs to develop deeper understanding and reasoning capabilities directly.
> - Leveraging objective, rule-based rewards derived from the correctness of next-token predictions on abundant unannotated text provides a scalable and general approach for applying reinforcement learning during pre-training, bypassing data scarcity and reward hacking issues.
> - Incentivizing reasoning during pre-training not only fosters complex abilities and generalizable intelligence but also inherently improves fundamental next-token prediction accuracy, suggesting a deeper understanding enhances core language modeling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models has been primarily driven by the **scaling paradigm** of self-supervised next-token prediction on massive text corpora. This approach, exemplified by models like GPT-4 (`AAA+23`), has proven incredibly effective for general-purpose pre-training, enabling LLMs to acquire vast linguistic knowledge and emergent capabilities.

Concurrently, **reinforcement learning (RL)** has emerged as a powerful technique in the post-training phase of LLMs, primarily for aligning models with human preferences or enhancing specific skills. This includes:
- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)** (`OWJ+22`), which fine-tunes pre-trained models using costly human preference data. While highly effective for alignment, RLHF suffers from scalability issues due to data annotation costs and susceptibility to "reward hacking" (where models exploit flaws in learned reward models).
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** (`LMP+25`), which utilizes objective, rule-based rewards, often derived from explicit question-answer pairs. RLVR mitigates reward hacking but is limited by the scarcity of annotated data, restricting its application primarily to domain-specific fine-tuning rather than general-purpose pre-training.

The broader research landscape is also intensely focused on enhancing the **reasoning capabilities** of LLMs. This has manifested in "test-time scaling" approaches (e.g., chain-of-thought prompting, self-correction), which involve extending inference compute to improve reasoning (`ZLS+25a`), and "training-time scaling," which focuses on increasing model parameters and training data (`KMH+20`, `HBM+22a`).

"Reinforcement Pre-Training" (RPT) directly addresses a critical gap in this landscape: **how to apply the power and objective-driven nature of reinforcement learning to the foundational, general-purpose pre-training phase of LLMs, in a scalable manner.** Current RL methods are largely confined to fine-tuning, struggling with the scale and generality required for pre-training. RPT proposes a novel integration, reframing the ubiquitous next-token prediction task itself as a reasoning problem trained with RL.

This work distinguishes itself from other related efforts. While some research (e.g., `ZHS+24` on Quiet-STAR) explores using rationales for next-token prediction with RL, RPT uniquely leverages *next-token prediction correctness* as the direct, rule-based reward signal. This distinction is crucial for minimizing the risk of reward hacking, which can arise when learned reward models are used for rewarding the "helpfulness" of generated rationales. By focusing on verifiable, intrinsic rewards from the corpus, RPT offers a more robust and scalable approach to embedding reasoning capabilities directly into the LLM's pre-training process, conceptually integrating training-time and test-time scaling principles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **Reinforcement Pre-Training (RPT)** as a novel and scalable paradigm for pre-training large language models and advancing reinforcement learning for general AI.

The core motivations underpinning RPT stem from the limitations of existing LLM training methodologies:
- **Scalability of Next-Token Prediction:** While the self-supervised next-token prediction objective has been incredibly effective in scaling LLMs, it fundamentally optimizes for statistical next-token likelihood, not necessarily deeper reasoning or understanding.
- **Scalability and Generality Challenges of RL for LLMs:** Despite RL's prowess in fine-tuning LLMs for specific tasks and alignment, its application has been hampered by:
 - The prohibitive cost and limited availability of high-quality human preference data (in RLHF).
 - The domain-specific nature and data scarcity inherent in RLVR, which relies on annotated question-answer pairs. These limitations prevent current RL methods from being applied broadly to the vast, unannotated text corpora used for general pre-training.
- **Incentivizing Deeper Understanding:** The authors hypothesize that merely predicting the next token, while effective, might not foster the deepest possible understanding or robust reasoning capabilities. They propose that by *incentivizing* the model to actively "reason" about the next token, it can develop more profound internal representations and generalization abilities. This reframing shifts the pre-training objective from simple pattern matching to a form of active inferential processing.

Therefore, the key motivation is to **bridge the gap** between the scalability of self-supervised pre-training and the power of reinforcement learning. RPT aims to transform the ubiquitous next-token prediction into a verifiable reasoning task, thereby enabling RL to leverage the vast amounts of unannotated text data for general-purpose pre-training. This approach seeks to cultivate more robust, reasoning-capable LLMs directly from their foundational training, rather than relying solely on post-training fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RPT reframes the fundamental next-token prediction task into a **next-token reasoning process**, which is then trained using on-policy reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Pre-Training Task: Next-Token Reasoning

For any given context $x_{<t}$ from a pre-training corpus, the model is required to generate a `chain-of-thought` reasoning sequence ($c_t$) before outputting its predicted next token ($y_t$). The complete model response is $o_t = (c_t, y_t)$. This chain-of-thought (illustrated in Figure 2) encourages deliberative processes like brainstorming, self-critique, and self-correction, fundamentally shifting pre-training from superficial token correlations to understanding the underlying knowledge.

#### 3.2. Pre-Training with Reinforcement Learning

RPT employs an on-policy reinforcement learning framework.
- **Rollout Process:** Given a context $x_{<t}$, the language model $\pi_\theta$ performs $G$ "thinking trajectories" or rollouts. Each trajectory $o^i_t = (c^i_t, y^i_t)$ consists of a reasoning sequence and a final predicted token.
- **Reward Function:** A **prefix matching reward** is introduced for verification. For each prediction $y^i_t$, a deterministic verifier assigns a reward $r^i_t$. The reward is 1 if $y^i_t$ is an exact prefix of the ground-truth completion $x_{\ge t}$ and its length matches a valid token boundary; otherwise, the reward is 0. This rule-based reward is crucial as it minimizes the risk of reward hacking often associated with learned reward models, providing a direct, verifiable signal of correctness.
- **Optimization Objective:** The model is trained to maximize the expected reward across all sampled trajectories: $J_{RPT}(\theta) = E_{(x_{<t},x_{\ge t}) \sim D,\{o^i_t\}_{i=1}^G \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x_{<t})} [r^i_t]$. This objective directly incentivizes the model to generate reasoning processes that lead to correct next-token predictions.

#### 3.3. Pre-Training Setup

- **Dataset:** The **OmniMATH** dataset (`GSY+24`), comprising 4,428 competition-level mathematical problems and solutions, was chosen. This dataset is suitable because many of its tokens require deep reasoning to predict correctly.
- **Data Filtering:** To ensure training focuses on challenging predictions, an entropy-based filtering mechanism was applied. A smaller proxy model (Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) was used to calculate the entropy of next tokens. Positions with low entropy were filtered out, prioritizing high-entropy (challenging) tokens for RPT training.
- **Base Model:** **Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B** (`GYZ+25`) served as the base model, chosen for its existing foundational reasoning capabilities, making it a suitable starting point for an RL-based reasoning pre-training.
- **RL Algorithm and Implementation:** The **GRPO algorithm** (`GYZ+25`) was used for training, implemented within the `verl` library (`SZY+24`). Inference employed `vllm`.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key settings included an 8k training length, a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-6}$, zero KL penalty, a batch size of 256 questions (with $G=8$ responses sampled per question), and a temperature of 0.8 for rollouts. Dynamic sampling (`YZZ+25`) was used after 500 steps to boost efficiency. The main experiment ran for 1,000 total training steps.
- **Prediction Extraction:** The final next-token prediction was consistently extracted from within the last `\boxed{}` after the `</think>` special token in the generated output.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Methods

The effectiveness of RPT was evaluated across multiple dimensions:
- **Language Modeling Performance:** Measured by next-token prediction accuracy on a held-out OmniMATH validation set, categorized into "easy," "medium," and "hard" splits based on entropy.
- **Scaling Properties:** Investigated how next-token prediction accuracy scales with increased training compute, modeling the relationship with a power-law form.
- **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning Capability:** Assessed whether RPT provides a superior pre-trained foundation for subsequent RL fine-tuning, using verifiable answers from Skywork-OR1 (`HLL+25`).
- **Zero-Shot Performance on Downstream Tasks:** Evaluated the generalizability of RPT models on unseen tasks using widely recognized benchmarks: MMLU-Pro (`HBB+20`) and SuperGPQA (`DYM+25`).
- **Reasoning Pattern Analysis:** Qualitatively and quantitatively analyzed the unique reasoning patterns elicited by RPT compared to standard problem-solving approaches, using keyword-based detection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several compelling results demonstrating the efficacy and potential of Reinforcement Pre-Training:

- **Significant Improvement in Next-Token Prediction Accuracy:**
 - RPT-14B consistently achieved higher next-token prediction accuracy across all difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard) on the OmniMATH validation set (Table 1). For instance, on "Hard" tokens, RPT-14B reached 23.75% accuracy, outperforming R1-Distill-Qwen-14B (20.43% standard, 1.41% reasoning-mode) and even matching the performance of a significantly larger model, R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (Figure 4). This indicates that incentivizing reasoning leads to better fundamental language modeling.

- **Favorable Scaling Properties:**
 - The study showed that next-token prediction accuracy under the RPT framework consistently improved with increased training compute across all data difficulty levels (Figure 5). The high coefficients of determination ($R^2$ values > 0.989) confirmed that the performance trends accurately followed a power-law relationship, positioning RPT as a promising and sustainable scaling strategy for future LLMs.

- **Stronger Foundation for Reinforcement Fine-Tuning:**
 - Models pre-trained with RPT exhibited a higher "upper bound" when subsequently fine-tuned with RLVR on verifiable Q&A data from Skywork-OR1 (Table 2). RPT-14B reached 58.3% accuracy after RL fine-tuning, compared to only 52.7% for the baseline R1-Distill-Qwen-14B. This suggests that RPT minimizes the objective gap between pre-training and post-training RL, allowing for more efficient transfer of learned reasoning patterns and quicker performance improvements on end tasks with limited data.

- **Enhanced Zero-Shot Performance on General End Tasks:**
 - RPT-14B, when evaluated in reasoning mode, consistently outperformed the R1-Distill-Qwen-14B baseline (both standard and reasoning modes) and even the larger R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (standard mode) on general-domain benchmarks like SuperGPQA and MMLU-Pro (Table 3). Notable gains included approximately 7 points on SuperGPQA and 22 points on MMLU-Pro over R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (standard NTP). This demonstrates that the reasoning capabilities fostered during RPT pre-training generalize effectively to diverse downstream tasks without explicit fine-tuning.

- **Qualitative Shift in Reasoning Patterns:**
 - An analysis of reasoning patterns (Figure 6) revealed a qualitative difference in how RPT-14B processes information compared to standard problem-solving models. RPT-14B showed a 161.8% greater use of "hypothesis" patterns and 26.2% greater use of "deduction" patterns. In contrast, standard problem-solving models relied more on "breakdown" patterns. This suggests RPT encourages a more inferential and exploratory thought process, involving hypothesis generation, alternative consideration, and self-reflection, as illustrated in the provided case studies (Table 4, Table 11).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Reinforcement Pre-Training (RPT) represents a significant advancement in the field of large language models and reinforcement learning, offering a novel paradigm with profound implications:

- **Fundamental Re-thinking of Pre-training:** RPT moves beyond the traditional passive next-token prediction objective to an active "next-token reasoning" task. This fundamental shift during pre-training allows LLMs to leverage extended computation to build deeper understanding and stronger foundational reasoning capabilities directly, rather than acquiring them primarily through fine-tuning or emergent properties.
- **Scalability and Generality of RL:** By transforming the abundant, unannotated text data into a source for verifiable, intrinsic rewards, RPT offers a truly scalable and general-purpose approach to applying reinforcement learning. This overcomes the data scarcity and domain-specificity limitations of current RL methods like RLHF and RLVR, opening up avenues for RL to influence the very foundations of AI models.
- **Inherent Reasoning Capabilities:** The explicit incentivization of reasoning patterns during pre-training means that RPT models are inherently "smarter" from the outset. This translates into improved performance on complex reasoning tasks in a zero-shot setting and provides a more robust foundation for subsequent specialized fine-tuning, reducing the effort needed in later stages to instill complex behaviors.
- **Improved Language Modeling Performance:** Counter-intuitively, by encouraging a deeper reasoning process, RPT also improves the basic accuracy of next-token prediction. This suggests that "understanding" or "reasoning" is not just an added layer but can actually enhance the core language modeling task itself, leading to more accurate and coherent generations.
- **Mitigation of Reward Hacking:** The reliance on objective, rule-based rewards derived directly from the correctness of next-token predictions significantly minimizes the risk of reward hacking, a common pitfall when using complex, learned reward models. This makes the training process more stable and reliable.
- **New Scaling Law Frontier:** The observed power-law scaling of RPT accuracy with increased training compute indicates that this paradigm is sustainable for developing even larger and more capable models. This sets the stage for discovering new scaling laws specific to reinforcement pre-training, which could guide future research and development efforts.

**Potential Impact:** RPT has the potential to accelerate the development of more capable, robust, and generally intelligent AI systems. By embedding reasoning as a core capability during pre-training, it could lead to models that not only generate human-like text but also genuinely "think before they speak," leading to more reliable and trustworthy AI applications across various domains, from complex problem-solving to scientific discovery.

**Future Work:** The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the current focus on a 14B parameter model and primarily mathematical pre-training data. Future directions include scaling RPT to larger models and more diverse, general-domain web corpora, establishing formal scaling laws for reinforcement pre-training, and exploring integration with adaptive thinking mechanisms (like "hybrid thinking") to allow models to dynamically decide when to engage in more extensive reasoning. This line of research promises to fundamentally reshape how LLMs are built and how they acquire intelligence.
