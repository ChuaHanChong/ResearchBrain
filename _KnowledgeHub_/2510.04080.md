# PoLi-RL: A Point-to-List Reinforcement Learning Framework for Conditional Semantic Textual Similarity

> [!summary] Summary
> The POLI-RL framework from Tsinghua University and Tencent Youtu Lab introduces a two-stage reinforcement learning approach for Conditional Semantic Textual Similarity (C-STS), leveraging an LLM-based cross-encoder. It achieves a new state-of-the-art Spearman correlation of 48.18 on the C-STS benchmark, outperforming previous cross-encoder methods by 4.35 points and surpassing proprietary models like GPT-4o in few-shot settings.

## Problem

- Conditional Semantic Textual Similarity (C-STS) requires sophisticated, scenario-based reasoning, which traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often struggles to adequately guide in large language models (LLMs).
- Directly optimizing non-differentiable ranking metrics like Spearman correlation with conventional SFT methods is challenging, as differentiable loss functions only indirectly approximate the true objective.
- Naive direct application of listwise Reinforcement Learning (RL) with batch-level ranking rewards to C-STS leads to training instability due to complexity for untrained models and overly coarse reward signals.

## Method

- POLI-RL proposes a two-stage Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework built on an LLM-based ==cross-encoder== architecture to optimize for C-STS.
- Stage I focuses on Foundational Skill Acquisition using a weighted sum of pointwise accuracy, binary judgment, and format rewards to establish basic scoring capabilities.
- Stage II refines fine-grained semantic distinction using a hybrid reward signal (pointwise, pairwise, listwise) enabled by the Parallel Slice Ranking Reward (PSRR) mechanism, which computes rewards within independent slices of predicted scores.

## Results

- POLI-RL with the Qwen3-**8B** model achieved a new state-of-the-art Spearman correlation of 48.18 on the official C-STS benchmark, improving upon the previous cross-encoder SOTA by 4.35 points.
- The framework consistently provided substantial gains over standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) (e.g., 8.75 points for Qwen3-**0.6B**) and outperformed prominent proprietary models like GPT-4o by 3.95 points (48.18 vs. 44.23 Spearman) in few-shot settings.
- The Parallel Slice Ranking Reward (PSRR) mechanism demonstrated generalizability by showing a 3.43-point gain over the SFT baseline on the distinct WMT-QE 2020 translation quality estimation task, confirming its transferability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A progressive, two-stage reward curriculum effectively addresses the challenges of applying RL to complex ranking tasks, first establishing basic scoring before refining nuanced distinctions.
> - The Parallel Slice Ranking Reward (PSRR) mechanism successfully overcomes coarse-grained reward issues in listwise RL by providing precise, differentiated learning signals within independent slices of predictions, allowing for direct optimization of ranking metrics.
> - Specialized RL-based alignment can enable smaller, purpose-built LLMs to achieve superior performance on complex conditional ranking tasks compared to larger, general-purpose proprietary models in few-shot settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Semantic Textual Similarity (STS) is a foundational task in Computational Linguistics with wide applications across various Natural Language Processing (NLP) domains. Traditional STS tasks, however, frequently suffer from inherent ambiguity, as similarity judgments can be subjective and context-dependent. To address this limitation, Conditional Semantic Textual Similarity (C-STS) was introduced. C-STS involves measuring semantic proximity between text segments under an explicit natural language condition, aiming for more precise and objective similarity assessments. This approach places higher demands on a model's reasoning capabilities due to the need for contextual interpretation.

Existing research on C-STS has primarily utilized three architectural paradigms: Bi-encoders, Tri-encoders, and Cross-encoders. Bi-encoders typically employ Siamese networks, while Tri-encoders process texts and conditions separately before aggregation. Cross-encoders, which process the text pair and condition as a single input, allow for deep token-level interactions. Despite the theoretical advantages of cross-encoders for incorporating contextual information, previous discriminative models in this category did not consistently achieve superior performance. Methods like SEAVER (Li et al., 2024) sought to mitigate this by optimizing internal attention to focus on salient information.

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has influenced C-STS research, with current applications largely limited to few-shot inference or using LLMs as feature extractors. These approaches often fall short of achieving satisfactory results, especially in few-shot settings. A notable gap in the existing literature is the lack of an end-to-end LLM-based cross-encoder for C-STS, and critically, the absence of advanced training techniques such as Reinforcement Learning (RL) in this domain. This paper identifies and aims to address this gap, proposing the first successful application of RL to an LLM-based cross-encoder architecture for the C-STS task. It positions itself as an advancement that leverages LLMs' generative capabilities and RL's direct optimization of non-differentiable ranking metrics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate an end-to-end, LLM-based cross-encoder framework for the Conditional Semantic Textual Similarity (C-STS) task, specifically integrating reinforcement learning (RL) into its training paradigm.

This objective is motivated by several considerations:

1. **Demand for Sophisticated Reasoning:** C-STS requires models to perform sophisticated, scenario-based reasoning. For instance, determining similarity under a specific condition often necessitates transcending surface-level semantics to identify underlying relationships and abstractions. Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods, while effective for pattern recognition, may not adequately guide LLMs in complex inference processes. The authors argue that RL, through explicit reward signals, can more effectively guide the reasoning process of LLMs by rewarding correct reasoning paths and outcomes in a scenario-dependent manner.

2. **Direct Optimization of Non-Differentiable Metrics:** The primary evaluation metric for C-STS is the Spearman correlation coefficient, which is a non-differentiable measure of ranking quality. Conventional SFT methods typically rely on differentiable loss functions, such as Mean Squared Error (MSE), which only indirectly and approximately optimize this ranking objective. This misalignment between the training objective and the evaluation metric can limit performance. RL, in contrast, allows for the direct optimization of reward functions designed to correlate strongly with rank-based metrics, thereby enabling a more precise alignment of the training process with the task's true evaluation criteria.

3. **Limitations of Naive RL Application:** Preliminary experiments conducted by the authors indicated that a direct, naive application of listwise RL with a batch-level ranking reward (e.g., Spearman's correlation) did not yield significant improvements over few-shot baselines. This failure was attributed to two main problems:
 - **Complexity for Untrained Models:** A complex ranking objective proves too challenging for models that have not yet established fundamental scoring capabilities, often leading to training instability or collapse.
 - **Coarse-Grained Reward Signals:** A single reward computed across an entire batch provides an overly coarse signal, hindering precise credit assignment to individual completions. This can result in unfair penalization of good completions due to a few poor ones.

To overcome these challenges, the authors were motivated to design a novel RL framework that progressively introduces complexity and provides granular reward signals. This led to the development of PoLi-RL, a two-stage Point-to-List Reinforcement Learning framework with an innovative Parallel Slice Ranking Reward (PSRR) mechanism, aimed at establishing foundational scoring abilities before refining fine-grained semantic distinctions through more nuanced, yet precisely attributed, ranking objectives.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces PoLi-RL, a two-stage Point-to-List Reinforcement Learning framework, built upon an LLM-based cross-encoder architecture, designed to optimize for conditional semantic textual similarity.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The C-STS task requires learning a scoring function, $\pi_\theta$, that estimates the semantic similarity between two text segments ($t_1, t_2$) given a natural language condition ($c$). Each data sample is represented as $x = (t_1, t_2, c)$, with a human-annotated similarity label $y \in$ (Likert scale). The scores denote different levels of equivalence, from "completely dissimilar" (1) to "completely equivalent" (5), demanding fine-grained reasoning. The dataset possesses a paired structure where adjacent samples share $(t_1, t_2)$ but have different conditions, with an ordinal relationship between their labels ($y_{\text{high}} \ge y_{\text{low}}$). The model takes a unified prompt $p = [I, E, x]$ (instruction, few-shot demonstrations, query) to generate an output sequence $o$, from which a predicted score $\tilde{y}$ is parsed. The training objective is to maximize the ranking consistency between predicted scores and ground-truth scores, primarily quantified by Spearman's correlation coefficient.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning for C-STS

The C-STS task is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where the LLM policy $\pi_\theta$ acts as the agent.
- **State ($s_t$):** The sequence of tokens generated up to time $t$, conditioned on the initial prompt.
- **Action ($a_t$):** The selection of the next token from the model's vocabulary.
- **Transition Function:** Deterministic, appending the selected token to the current state.
- **Reward (R):** A terminal reward $R_T = R(x, y, o)$ is given only after the entire output sequence $o$ is generated.
- **Discount Factor ($\gamma$):** Set to 1 to backpropagate the terminal reward without decay.

The objective is to maximize the expected reward: $\theta^* = \arg \max_\theta E_{(x,y) \sim D, o \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|p)}[R(x, y, o)]$.
For optimization, Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO) is employed. For each sample, the policy generates $G$ completions $\{o_i\}_{i=1}^G$. A scalar reward $r_i$ is computed for each completion, and the advantage $\hat{A}_i$ is calculated by Z-score normalization against the group rewards: $\hat{A}_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(\{r_i\}_{i=1}^G)}{\text{std}(\{r_i\}_{i=1}^G) + \epsilon}$. The model parameters $\theta$ are updated using the DAPO objective function (Equation 3 in the paper), which ensures gradient flow only through the numerator.

#### 3.3. PoLi-RL: A Two-Stage Reinforcement Learning Framework

PoLi-RL utilizes a two-stage progressive reward curriculum:

**Stage I: Foundational Skill Acquisition**
The objective of this stage is to establish the model's basic understanding of the C-STS scoring rules. The total reward $R_{S1}$ is a weighted sum of three components:
1. **Pointwise Accuracy Reward ($R_{\text{pointwise}}$):** Measures the normalized distance between the predicted score $\tilde{y}_j$ and the ground-truth $y_j$: $1 - \frac{|\tilde{y}_j - y_j|}{\text{max}(Y) - \text{min}(Y)}$.
2. **Binary Judgment Reward ($R_{\text{binary}}$):** Encourages the model to correctly classify similarity (scores $\ge 3$) or dissimilarity (scores $\le 2$). It grants 1 for correct binary judgment and 0 otherwise. This is crucial for preventing convergence to intermediate scores.
3. **Format Reward ($R_{\text{format}}$):** A simple reward to ensure the output adheres to the specified "yes/no(score)" format.

**Stage II: Fine-Grained Semantic Distinction**
Building on Stage I, this stage refines the model's ability to discern subtle semantic differences using a hybrid reward signal that integrates pointwise, pairwise, and listwise ranking objectives, enabled by the Parallel Slice Ranking Reward (PSRR) mechanism.

**Parallel Slice Ranking Reward (PSRR) Mechanism:**
This mechanism addresses the challenge of coarse-grained reward signals. For a batch of $N$ samples, the policy generates $G$ completions $\{o_{i,1}, \ldots, o_{i,G}\}$ for each sample. Predicted scores $\tilde{y}_{i,j}$ are parsed. Instead of a single list, these $N \times G$ scores are organized into $G$ "parallel slices." Each slice $Y_j^{\text{pred}} = \{\tilde{y}_{1,j}, \tilde{y}_{2,j}, \ldots, \tilde{y}_{N,j}\}$ consists of the $j$-th predicted score from all $N$ samples in the batch. Reward computation occurs within each independent slice, providing a specific learning signal for each completion. Gradient accumulation is used to handle large slice sizes (N) under memory constraints.

Within each parallel slice, the following ranking rewards are computed:
1. **Pairwise Ranking Reward ($R_{\text{pairwise}}$):** Leverages the paired structure of the C-STS dataset. It compares the predicted difference $\Delta_{\text{pred}} = \tilde{y}_{i,j} - \tilde{y}_{i+1,j}$ with the true difference $\Delta_{\text{true}} = y_i - y_{i+1}$ for adjacent, valid pairs.
 - If $\Delta_{\text{true}} = 0$ (tied scores), the reward minimizes the absolute prediction difference.
 - If $\Delta_{\text{true}} \ne 0$ (ordered scores), a base reward $R_{\text{base}}$ is given for correct ordinality, with an additional component penalizing the magnitude of prediction error.
2. **Listwise Ranking Reward ($R_{\text{listwise}}$):** Provides a global ranking perspective within each slice. It is calculated as $1 - \frac{|\text{Rank}(\tilde{y}_{i,j}, Y_j^{\text{pred}}) - \text{Rank}(y_i, Y_{\text{true}})|}{N-1}$, where $Y_{\text{true}}$ is the set of ground-truth labels for the batch. This normalizes the rank error between 0 and 1.

The final reward for Stage II, $R_{S2}$, is a weighted combination of the Pointwise Accuracy Reward (from Stage I, serving as an anchor), the Pairwise Ranking Reward, and the Listwise Ranking Reward: $R_{S2} = \mu_1 R_{\text{pointwise}} + \mu_2 R_{\text{pairwise}} + \mu_3 R_{\text{listwise}}$.

The models are built upon the Qwen3 family (0.6B, 4B, 8B) using the ms-swift framework for RL training. Experiments are conducted on the official C-STS dataset, with Spearman correlation as the primary metric.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents PoLi-RL as a framework that achieves notable performance in Conditional Semantic Textual Similarity (C-STS), demonstrating improvements over existing methods and establishing new benchmarks.

**Overall Performance and SOTA Establishment:**
PoLi-RL, particularly with the Qwen3-8B backbone, achieved a Spearman correlation coefficient of 48.18 on the official C-STS benchmark, setting a new state-of-the-art (SOTA) for the cross-encoder architecture. The corresponding Pearson correlation was 48.27.

**Superiority over Baselines:**
- **Previous Cross-encoder SOTA:** PoLi-RL significantly surpassed SEAVER (Li et al., 2024), the previous SOTA in the cross-encoder setting, by 4.35 points (48.18 vs. 43.83 Spearman).
- **Standard SFT:** Across all Qwen3 model sizes, PoLi-RL consistently yielded substantial gains over standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). For the Qwen3-0.6B model, PoLi-RL achieved an 8.75-point improvement over SFT (44.34 vs. 35.59 Spearman) and a 19.09-point improvement over few-shot inference (44.34 vs. 25.25 Spearman).
- **Differentiable Ranking Objectives:** When compared against a strong cross-encoder regression baseline trained with Pcc Loss (a state-of-the-art differentiable proxy for Spearman), PoLi-RL still maintained a clear advantage of 1.74 points (48.18 vs. 46.44 Spearman). This suggests that direct RL optimization of the reasoning process provides benefits beyond surrogate loss functions.

**Efficacy Across Model Scales:**
The framework demonstrated effectiveness across different model sizes. The Qwen3-0.6B model, powered by PoLi-RL, achieved a Spearman correlation of 44.34, outperforming the proprietary GPT-4 (43.6) and the prior cross-encoder SOTA SEAVER (43.83). This indicates that the performance gains are attributable to the alignment of the reasoning process with ranking objectives rather than solely to model scale.

**Advantage over Proprietary Models:**
The PoLi-RL (Qwen3-8B) model surpassed prominent proprietary reasoning models in few-shot settings, outperforming GPT-4o by 3.95 points (48.18 vs. 44.23 Spearman) and DeepSeek-R1 by 5.33 points (48.18 vs. 42.85 Spearman). This suggests that specialized RL-based alignment can allow smaller, purpose-built models to exceed the performance of larger, general-purpose LLMs on complex conditional ranking tasks.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effectiveness of Two-Stage Curriculum and Reward Components:**
 - Naive RL (listwise objective only) showed negligible improvement over the few-shot baseline (38.19 vs. 37.90 Spearman), highlighting the need for the progressive curriculum.
 - PoLi-RL Stage I (pointwise + binary rewards) significantly improved performance by 6.87 points over few-shot. Ablating the binary reward from Stage I resulted in a 0.58-point drop, confirming its role in basic judgment grounding.
 - The full PoLi-RL model (Stage II, adding pairwise and listwise) further boosted performance by 3.41 points. Removing the listwise reward incurred the most significant penalty (1.47 points), while removing the pairwise reward also reduced performance (0.58 points), confirming the necessity of both ranking signals in Stage II, with the listwise signal being critical for refinement.
- **Sensitivity to Hyperparameters:**
 - Stage II reward weights showed robustness, with optimal performance achieved by a moderate increase in the pairwise weight (μ2=1.5). The framework demonstrated stable convergence across various weight configurations.
 - The optimal parallel slice size (N) for the PSRR mechanism was found to be 24, with performance degrading for smaller or larger sizes, indicating a need for a balanced, localized ranking signal.
 - Analyses on generation multiplicity (G) and pairwise base reward (R_base) confirmed the robustness of the chosen default configurations.

**Generalizability of PSRR Mechanism:**
The PSRR mechanism's generalizability was tested on the WMT-QE 2020 task, a multilingual translation quality estimation task with different domains, continuous label scales, and independent sample structures. A simplified PoLi-RL (pointwise + PSRR listwise rewards only) achieved a Spearman correlation of 54.33, a 3.43-point gain over the SFT baseline (50.90). This validates PSRR as a transferable solution for aligning LLMs with listwise ranking objectives across diverse NLP tasks.

**Analysis of Prediction Error and Reasoning:**
- **Prediction Error Distribution:** PoLi-RL exhibited a more favorable error distribution compared to few-shot and SFT baselines. It achieved the highest density of perfect predictions (error=0) and, more importantly, effectively suppressed the probability of high-error predictions (errors $\ge 2$), indicating increased precision against severe misjudgments.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** A case study demonstrated PoLi-RL's ability to perform nuanced contextual inference. Unlike baselines that exhibited literal interpretation or lacked reasoning depth, PoLi-RL successfully applied world knowledge to infer the correct conditional similarity by identifying core states (e.g., "not connected to the ground") and deeming differing actions leading to that state as unimportant details.

**Robustness to Data Quality:**
Evaluation on a re-annotated C-STS dataset (addressing potential label noise) confirmed PoLi-RL's robustness, maintaining a SOTA Spearman correlation of 76.08 and outperforming the SFT baseline by nearly 4 points. This indicates that the observed improvements stem from genuine reasoning enhancements rather than overfitting to data artifacts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces PoLi-RL, a novel two-stage reinforcement learning framework, representing the first successful application of reinforcement learning (RL) to an end-to-end, LLM-based cross-encoder for the Conditional Semantic Textual Similarity (C-STS) task. This work offers several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Advancement in C-STS Performance:** PoLi-RL establishes a new state-of-the-art for the cross-encoder architecture on the official C-STS benchmark. This indicates that the proposed RL-based approach significantly improves the model's ability to assess semantic similarity under complex conditions compared to previous discriminative models and traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods.

2. **Addressing Challenges in Ranking Optimization:** The paper successfully overcomes the inherent difficulties of directly optimizing non-differentiable ranking metrics (like Spearman correlation) with RL. The proposed two-stage curriculum, progressing from foundational pointwise scoring to nuanced hybrid (pointwise, pairwise, listwise) rewards, and the innovative Parallel Slice Ranking Reward (PSRR) mechanism, provide precise and differentiated learning signals. This approach mitigates the problems of complex, coarse-grained reward signals and credit assignment that typically hinder naive listwise RL applications.

3. **Enhanced LLM Alignment for Complex Reasoning:** The study demonstrates that explicit RL-based alignment can guide LLMs to perform sophisticated, scenario-based reasoning required by C-STS. The qualitative analysis and error distribution findings suggest that PoLi-RL cultivates a deeper contextual inference ability, leading to more accurate and reliable conditional judgments, even allowing smaller models to outperform much larger, general-purpose proprietary LLMs in this specific task. This impact suggests a powerful paradigm for fine-tuning LLMs on specialized, reasoning-intensive tasks.

4. **Generalizability of the PSRR Mechanism:** The empirical validation of the PSRR mechanism on the WMT-QE 2020 task, a distinct ranking task across different domains and label scales, highlights its broad applicability. This indicates that PSRR is not merely a task-specific optimization for C-STS but a transferable solution for aligning LLMs with listwise ranking objectives in other domains involving multiple candidate generations. This can accelerate research and development in various ranking-related NLP applications.

5. **Robustness and Reliability:** The validation of PoLi-RL's performance on a re-annotated C-STS dataset, designed to address potential label noise, confirms the robustness and genuine nature of the reported improvements. This adds credibility to the framework's effectiveness and its capacity to generalize beyond potential data quality issues.

In summary, PoLi-RL introduces a foundational framework for applying reinforcement learning to conditional textual tasks, demonstrating a method to bridge the gap between LLMs' general reasoning capabilities and their alignment with specific, non-differentiable, ranking-based objectives. Its architectural innovations and empirical successes suggest a path for developing more precise and contextually aware NLP models for a range of complex conditional judgment tasks.
