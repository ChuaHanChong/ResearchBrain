# Environmental Understanding Vision-Language Model for Embodied Agent

> [!summary] Summary
> The Environmental Understanding Embodied Agent (EUEA) framework integrates four core skills (object perception, task planning, action understanding, goal recognition) into a single VLM to improve instruction-following for embodied agents. This approach yields a 10.96% increase in task success rate on ALFRED benchmarks compared to a behavior cloning baseline, along with robust self-correction via a sampling-based recovery mechanism.

## Problem

- Existing embodied agents often rely on complex modular pipelines or environment metadata, limiting generalization and autonomy.
- End-to-end Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently lack explicit environmental interpretation, leading to hard-to-diagnose execution errors and task failures.
- Current recovery methods for agent failures often depend on external environmental feedback, predefined failure types, or textual feedback without sufficient visual grounding, restricting effective self-correction.

## Method

- Proposing the **EUEA** framework, which explicitly fine-tunes a single VLM with ==four fundamental environmental understanding skills==.
- Integrating ==Object Perception==, ==Task Planning==, ==Action Understanding==, and ==Goal Recognition== skills directly into the VLM architecture, formulated as reward-free ==POMDP== sub-skills.
- Implementing a ==sampling-based recovery step== for self-correction by proposing alternative actions upon failure and a ==GRPO== stage to refine inconsistent VLM decisions.

## Results

- EUEA achieved **+8.86%** average task success rate on ALFRED after SFT, **+3.03%** from GRPO, totaling **+10.96%** over behavior cloning baseline.
- The sampling-based recovery step achieved **86.48%** task success rate, surpassing external feedback methods.
- Skill-level accuracy: **99.40%** Main Goal Recognition, **98.60%** Subgoal Recognition on ALFRED, while zero-shot VLMs showed major weakness in action understanding and object perception.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly fine-tuning a VLM on core environmental understanding skills is crucial for robust performance and interpretability in embodied agent tasks, moving beyond implicit learning.
> - A sampling-based self-correction mechanism, leveraging learned skills, can effectively recover from interaction failures without requiring external environmental feedback, enhancing agent autonomy.
> - Despite strong reasoning abilities, state-of-the-art zero-shot VLMs exhibit significant limitations in specific environmental understanding capabilities like action success prediction and object grounding, necessitating dedicated skill training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied agents, which involves artificial intelligence systems that can perceive, interpret, and interact with physical or simulated environments, has seen advancements with the emergence of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). These models have demonstrated capabilities in perception, reasoning, and instruction-following. However, existing approaches to embodied agents often encounter limitations related to environmental understanding.

Prior work has explored various strategies to enhance environmental understanding. Some methods rely on complex modular pipelines, where different components handle specific tasks, which can increase system complexity and hinder generalization. Other approaches depend on environment metadata, such as object IDs or masks, which are not always available in real-world scenarios, thereby limiting autonomy. End-to-end models, while offering a unified architecture, frequently lack explicit mechanisms for interpreting the environment, leading to execution errors and task failures that are difficult to diagnose or correct.

Current recovery methods for these failures typically involve relying on environmental feedback for validation, using predefined failure types, or leveraging textual feedback without sufficient visual grounding. Such dependencies can restrict the agent's ability to self-correct effectively in dynamic and unconstrained environments.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that aims to integrate explicit skill modeling directly into a VLM, fostering a more robust and end-to-end environmental understanding. By defining and training a VLM on core skills, the work seeks to enable the agent to interpret its surroundings, anticipate action outcomes, and recover from failures without heavy reliance on external metadata or predefined error categories, thereby contributing to the development of more generalizable and autonomous embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in achieving comprehensive environmental understanding for instruction-following embodied agents. Despite their strong perceptual and reasoning abilities, existing VLMs frequently exhibit shortcomings in interacting with environments, leading to task failures. These failures often arise from a lack of explicit environmental interpretation, a reliance on environment metadata, or an inability to effectively recover from execution errors.

The key objectives of this study were:

1. **Enhance Environmental Understanding in VLMs:** To develop a novel framework, named Environmental Understanding Embodied Agent (EUEA), that explicitly fine-tunes a VLM with a set of core skills essential for interacting with and understanding an environment. This aims to move beyond implicit learning to instill explicit environmental interpretation capabilities within a single, end-to-end VLM architecture, without requiring complex modular components.

2. **Define and Integrate Core Skills:** To identify and operationalize four fundamental skills crucial for embodied agents:
 - **Object Perception:** Enabling the agent to identify relevant objects and localize them within the visual scene.
 - **Task Planning:** Facilitating the generation of sequential subgoals from a main goal and determining appropriate actions for each subgoal.
 - **Action Understanding:** Allowing the agent to anticipate the likelihood of action success or failure and to describe the resulting changes in the environment.
 - **Goal Recognition:** Empowering the agent to determine the completion status of both intermediate subgoals and the overarching main goal.
 The objective was to fine-tune these skills within a unified VLM architecture, providing both performance improvements and enhanced interpretability of the agent's decision-making process.

3. **Implement Robust Error Recovery Mechanisms:** To introduce a sampling-based recovery step that allows the agent to self-correct failed interactions by proposing alternative actions without requiring additional training. Additionally, to integrate a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) stage to refine inconsistent skill predictions, thereby improving interaction performance and stabilizing the agent's decision-making.

4. **Develop Comprehensive Skill Datasets and Evaluation Benchmarks:** To construct new skill datasets by leveraging existing instruction-following benchmarks (ALFRED and LangR) and design prompt templates for each core skill. This objective included creating a skill evaluation benchmark to systematically assess the environmental understanding capabilities of both the proposed framework and existing closed- and open-source VLMs.

5. **Analyze and Identify VLM Limitations:** To conduct a detailed analysis of the performance of existing VLMs on the proposed skill benchmarks, aiming to identify specific areas where they lack environmental understanding and to highlight the capabilities necessary for effective agent-environment interaction in instruction-following tasks.

By addressing these objectives, the research aimed to develop more reliable, interpretable, and self-correcting embodied agents capable of functioning effectively in dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Environmental Understanding Embodied Agent (EUEA), is designed to enhance a Vision-Language Model's (VLM) environmental understanding for instruction-following tasks. The core approach involves fine-tuning a single VLM with four fundamental skills based on a reward-free Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) formulation. The framework also incorporates a recovery step and a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) stage.

#### 3.1. Environmental Understanding Skills

The VLM receives an image frame and accumulated past memory at each time step. It infers task-relevant information to select an action, an interaction target object, and a bounding box to achieve a main goal. The entire interaction, including the action result and subgoal, is recorded as memory. The instruction, denoted `I_SKILL`, is tailored for each of the eight distinct sub-skills:

- **Object Perception:**
 - **Object Recognition (OR):** Identifies objects present in the observed image. Formulated as `v_t = π_θ(I_OR, f_t)`.
 - **Object Detection (OD):** Detects a bounding box for an object relevant to a given current goal. Formulated as `b_t = π_θ(I_OD, sg_t, a_t, o_t, f_t)`. These skills provide low-level control interaction.

- **Task Planning:**
 - **Subgoal Task Planning (STP):** Generates subgoals to achieve a given main goal. Formulated as `sg_n = π_θ(I_STP, M)`.
 - **Step-by-Step Action Planning (SAP):** Generates an action (`a_t, o_t`) to achieve a given current subgoal, utilizing `k` past memory steps. Formulated as `a_t, o_t = π_θ(I_SAP, f_t, v_t, p_t, sg_t, m_t-k:t-1)`. These skills enable subgoal identification and action generation.

- **Action Understanding:** Designed to allow the VLM to anticipate action outcomes and describe environmental state changes.
 - **Action Success Prediction (ASP):** Predicts whether an action will succeed or fail. Formulated as `ASP_t = π_θ(I_ASP, a_t, o_t, b_t, f_t)`.
 - **Future Situation Captioning (FSC):** An extension of ASP, describing expected changes from a given action. Formulated as `FSC_t = π_θ(I_FSC, a_t, o_t, b_t, f_t)`.
 - **Action Grounding (AG):** Predicts which action was executed between two given images. Formulated as `a_t-1, o_t-1, b_t-1 = π_θ(I_AG, f_t-1:t)`. To generate data for ASP and FSC, which often lack failure examples in expert demonstrations, random exploration is used to generate diverse successful and failed interactions. FSC data also involves generating captions explaining image differences.

- **Goal Recognition:**
 - **Main Goal Recognition (GR_main):** Determines if the main goal has been achieved. Formulated as `GR_main = π_θ(I_GR_main, M)`.
 - **Subgoal Recognition (GR_sub):** Determines if a subgoal has been achieved based on recent actions and frames. Formulated as `GR_sub = π_θ(I_GR_sub, sg_t, a_t-n:t, f_t-n:t)`. These skills enhance overall task understanding and progress monitoring.

#### 3.2. Skill Dataset Construction

Skill datasets were constructed using two instruction-following benchmarks: ALFRED (AI2-THOR) and LangR (Habitat 2.0). Environmental observations, image-action pairs, and annotations were collected. Prompt templates were designed for each of the eight sub-skills. For generalization assessment, a subset of training scenes from both ALFRED and LangR were sampled. The final datasets include 382k training, 858k validation, and 5.3k evaluation samples for ALFRED, and 906k training, 2.8M validation, and 4.4k evaluation samples for LangR, with specific distributions across the four core skills.

#### 3.3. Recovery Step via Sampling

When an interaction failure occurs, a "Recovery Step" is triggered. The VLM agent samples `n` alternative actions using its SAP skill. These alternative actions are selected based on their negative log-likelihood score (`s_t,i = -log π_θ(a_t,i, o_t,i | I_SAP, m_t-k:t)`) from the SAP skill, interpreted as goal-achievement confidence. If all sampled action-object pairs are identical to the failed one, OD is performed `n` times on the previously failed action-object pair. The agent then selects the action-object pair or bounding box with the lowest score for re-execution. This step allows for self-correction without additional training.

#### 3.4. GRPO Refinement Stage

To mitigate inconsistent VLM decisions post-supervised fine-tuning (SFT), a GRPO refinement stage is introduced. This stage uses rule-based reward functions defined by task-specific correctness metrics (e.g., IoU for bounding boxes, Jaccard index for object recognition, action-sequence order for planning). A total reward function (`R_total = R_OP + R_TP + R_AU + R_GR`) sums rewards from individual skills. For dataset construction for GRPO, eight response samples are collected for each validation instance, and instances with a normalized standard deviation of rewards exceeding a threshold `τ` (indicating uncertainty) are selected. This creates a compact dataset (around 10k instances on ALFRED) for refining ambiguous responses.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Training:** InternVL3-8B was used as the primary VLM backbone, with InternVL2.5-series and Qwen2.5-VL-series also used for analysis. The SFT stage involved full fine-tuning for one epoch, freezing the vision encoder while tuning MLP and LLM components. The GRPO stage used LoRA for fine-tuning over 5-10 epochs.
- **Task Evaluation:** Models were evaluated on ALFRED, utilizing 134 instruction-following tasks and 429 augmented free-form variants categorized into six types (e.g., Pick&Place, Clean&Place). LangR was excluded from task evaluation due to potential incompatibilities with non-visible object interactions. An instruction-following pipeline was implemented, where the VLM takes instructions and observed images, storing intermediate outputs in memory. The process distinguishes between Navigation (using PDDL expert actions and OR) and Interaction phases (using SAP, OD, and GR_sub). An action failure is defined as no image change. `k` was set to 4 for memory input, and `n` to 10 for the recovery step.
- **Skill Evaluation:** An evaluation benchmark was proposed with metrics for each skill: Object Grounding (count, inclusion accuracy), Object Detection (IoU), Planning (BERT-based cosine similarity for subgoals), Step-by-Step (action and object match), Action Prediction (Yes/No accuracy), Action Grounding (action, object, bounding box correctness), and Goal Recognition (Yes/No accuracy). Both open-source and closed-source VLMs were evaluated on ALFRED and LangR skill datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings regarding the Environmental Understanding Embodied Agent (EUEA) framework and the capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in embodied tasks.

#### 4.1. Task Evaluation

- **Improved Task Success:** The EUEA framework demonstrated substantial improvements in task success rates on ALFRED tasks compared to a behavior cloning (BC) baseline using InternVL3-8B. The supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage achieved an 8.86% improvement in average task success rate.
- **Further Refinement by GRPO:** The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) stage provided an additional 3.03% gain, leading to a total 10.96% improvement over the BC baseline. This indicates that GRPO effectively refines inconsistent skill predictions, enhancing the VLM's environmental understanding.
- **Performance Comparison:** EUEA (GRPO) reached an average task success rate of 85.78%, significantly outperforming the BC baseline (74.59%). While EMMA (67.83%) relies on high-level interactions and environment metadata, EUEA operates solely on visual observations and low-level actions guided by subgoals, achieving strong fine-grained performance in a more demanding setting.

#### 4.2. Comparison of Recovery Methods

- **Effectiveness of Recovery Step:** The proposed sampling-based recovery step achieved the highest task success rate for both the SFT and GRPO stages, outperforming a baseline recovery method that relies on external environment feedback. Specifically, it achieved 86.48% success in the GRPO stage, surpassing the 85.78% of the GRPO stage with external environmental feedback. This suggests that the agent's learned environmental understanding, when leveraged through sampling, can enable effective self-correction without external guidance.

#### 4.3. Skill Evaluation

- **Zero-Shot VLM Limitations:** Evaluation of closed- and open-source VLMs on the ALFRED and LangR skill benchmarks revealed that, despite strong reasoning abilities (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro achieving high scores in goal recognition and planning), most zero-shot VLMs still exhibit limited environmental understanding in areas such as action success prediction, action grounding, and object perception. For instance, Gemini-2.5-Pro showed an 85.76% accuracy in step-by-step action planning on ALFRED, implying a nearly 14% failure rate for individual actions, which can accumulate to task failures.
- **EUEA Skill Enhancement:** The EUEA fine-tuned model (InternVL3-8B) significantly improved performance across all evaluated skills on both ALFRED and LangR datasets. It achieved 99.40% accuracy in Main Goal Recognition, 98.60% in Subgoal Recognition, 96.80% in Action Prediction, and 98.20% in Step-by-Step planning on ALFRED. This demonstrates that explicit fine-tuning on environmental understanding skills is crucial for robust performance in embodied tasks.

#### 4.4. Analyses

- **Impact of Skill Ablation:** An ablation study showed that removing any single proposed skill from the EUEA framework resulted in a drop in task success rate. Action Understanding had the largest impact, leading to a 9.32% decrease in task success, followed by Action Grounding (4.9%) and Subgoal Task Planning (2.8%). This indicates that each skill contributes meaningfully to the overall task performance by enhancing the agent's understanding of environmental changes and actions.
- **Performance Across VLM Backbones:** The EUEA approach demonstrated robustness across different VLM backbones, including InternVL2.5-series and Qwen2.5-VL-series, and even with models having fewer parameters (e.g., InternVL2.5-4B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B). Significant performance gains (12.84% and 37.53%, respectively) were observed over their BC baselines, indicating the generalizability of the proposed method.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Skill Evaluation:** An ALFRED-tuned EUEA model, when evaluated on the LangR skill benchmark, performed substantially lower than a LangR-tuned model due to distribution shift. However, it still outperformed the zero-shot InternVL3-8B on most skills except object grounding. This result suggests promising OOD generalization capabilities of EUEA skills, even under significant environmental differences.
- **Qualitative Recovery Cases:** Qualitative examples illustrated the recovery step's ability to correct incorrect bounding box detections and generate alternative actions after execution failures, contributing to task completion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces the Environmental Understanding Embodied Agent (EUEA) framework, which aims to enhance the environmental understanding of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for instruction-following embodied agents. The study presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field:

**Advancement in Embodied AI:** The work directly addresses a critical limitation of current VLMs in embodied tasks: their struggle with environmental understanding, which often leads to task failures or reliance on external metadata. By integrating and fine-tuning four core skills—object perception, task planning, action understanding, and goal recognition—into a single VLM, EUEA enables a more robust, end-to-end learning approach for agent-environment interaction. This unified framework represents a step toward more capable and autonomous embodied agents that can interpret, plan, and act within dynamic environments.

**Enhanced Reliability and Interpretability:** The explicit skill-level supervision within EUEA not only improves the VLM's ability to perform visual perception and decision-making but also offers a degree of interpretability regarding its capabilities. This interpretability can be crucial for diagnosing failures and understanding an agent's reasoning process, which is often a challenge with opaque end-to-end neural networks. The significant improvements in task success rates, particularly the 8.86% gain from supervised fine-tuning and the additional 3.03% from the GRPO stage, indicate a substantial increase in the reliability of embodied agents.

**Robust Error Recovery Mechanisms:** The introduction of a sampling-based recovery step, which leverages the learned EUEA skills to propose alternative actions upon failure, provides a powerful self-correction mechanism. This contrasts with prior recovery methods that often depend on external feedback or predefined failure types. The finding that this self-correction mechanism can achieve comparable or even superior performance to relying on external environmental feedback highlights a potential path toward more self-sufficient agents. The GRPO refinement stage further solidifies this by reducing inconsistent skill predictions, thereby improving interaction robustness.

**Contribution of Resources and Benchmarking:** The creation and release of large skill datasets (1.24M and 3.7M total samples) and a skill evaluation benchmark based on ALFRED and LangR are valuable contributions to the research community. These resources provide standardized tools for future research, enabling direct comparison and facilitating the development of new methods for environmental understanding. The evaluation of both open- and closed-source VLMs on this benchmark elucidates their current limitations, specifically in environmental understanding, underscoring the necessity for dedicated skill learning.

**Foundation for Future Research:** The findings highlight that even state-of-the-art VLMs, without specific environmental skill fine-tuning, demonstrate limited environmental understanding for embodied tasks. This suggests a clear direction for future research in tailoring foundation models for embodied intelligence. The identified limitations also point toward areas for future work, such as extending the approach to continuous environments, incorporating video-based inputs for better state transition tracking, addressing low-level navigation, and integrating explicit reasoning mechanisms to move beyond intuitive predictions.

In summary, this research provides a comprehensive framework and empirical evidence for enhancing environmental understanding in embodied agents through explicit skill-based VLM fine-tuning, offering a significant step toward developing more reliable, autonomous, and interpretable intelligent systems.
