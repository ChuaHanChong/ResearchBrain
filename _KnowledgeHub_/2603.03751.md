# Interaction-Aware Whole-Body Control for Compliant Object Transport

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Texas at Arlington and Carnegie Mellon University developed Interaction-Oriented Whole-Body Control (IO-WBC), a hierarchical framework enabling humanoid robots to perform compliant object transport under strong, time-varying interaction forces and heavy payloads. The approach achieved an 80% success rate for an 18 kg tire carrying task and successfully pushed a 65 kg crate, outperforming existing whole-body control baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional whole-body control (WBC) methods struggle with strong, non-conservative interaction forces from heavy payloads, often leading to unstable or aggressive robot behaviors.
- Precise velocity tracking, a common objective for WBC, can become physically infeasible in heavy-load collaborative tasks, causing control degradation.
- Existing deep reinforcement learning (DRL) approaches for humanoid control may lack explicit mechanisms to functionally decouple upper-body interaction from lower-body support, and can be vulnerable to out-of-distribution interaction forces.

## Method

- Developed a cascaded, hierarchical IO-WBC architecture that integrates a trajectory-optimized Reference Generator (RG) with an interaction-aware ==reinforcement learning== (RL) policy.
- Structurally separated upper-body interaction execution, handled by local inverse kinematics, from lower-body support control, managed by the RL policy and RG prior, to coordinate flexible force exchange with dynamic balance.
- Employed an asymmetric teacher-student distillation framework during training, allowing the student policy to implicitly infer interaction dynamics (e.g., object mass, external disturbances) from proprioceptive history without explicit force-torque sensors.

## Results

- IO-WBC achieved an **80%** success rate (4 out of 5 trials) in real-world cooperative carrying of an 18 kg tire, where a state-of-the-art WBC baseline consistently failed (**0%** success rate).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Reference Generator (RG) and asymmetric distillation were critical for performance; the `w/o RG` variant showed 2-4 times higher errors, and the `w/o distillation` variant experienced catastrophic collapse under heavy loads.
- The framework demonstrated superior postural resilience under incremental loads, maintaining stable pitch and height errors up to 8 kg where baselines failed, and successfully pushed a 60 kg crate by prioritizing postural invariance over locomotion speed to maintain stability near actuator limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A bio-inspired hierarchical control architecture that functionally separates upper-body interaction from lower-body support effectively manages complex whole-body dynamics under heavy-load interactions.
> - Implicitly inferring interaction dynamics from proprioceptive history via asymmetric teacher-student distillation enables robust performance in real-world scenarios without reliance on explicit force/torque sensing.
> - Integrating a trajectory-optimized kinematic reference generator as a prior significantly reduces the complexity for the reinforcement learning policy, enhancing stability and compliance for heavy-payload tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of humanoid robots in human-centric environments, particularly for tasks involving physical human-robot collaboration (HRC) and cooperative object transport, presents a significant challenge for whole-body control (WBC) frameworks. Traditional WBC methods often rely on accurate posture or trajectory tracking, employing techniques like inverse kinematics or quadratic programming. While these approaches offer theoretical guarantees for stability under multi-contact conditions, they typically operate under assumptions of relatively weak physical interaction or nominal robot dynamics. Consequently, they often struggle with strong, time-varying, and non-conservative interaction forces encountered during heavy-load collaborative tasks, where precise velocity tracking may become physically infeasible. Enforcing strict tracking objectives in such high-pressure scenarios can lead to unstable or overly aggressive robot behaviors.

Recent advancements in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) have shown promise in enabling agile motor skill acquisition for humanoid robots, providing adaptability in high-dimensional, nonlinear systems. However, extending DRL capabilities to physical HRC, especially for tasks like cooperative transport of heavy payloads, introduces additional complexities. Humanoid control frameworks incorporating DRL, such as adaptive motion optimization (AMO), often focus on single-agent performance and may lack explicit mechanisms to functionally decouple upper-body interaction from lower-body support while maintaining their coupling for absorbing and redirecting interaction forces through the whole body. Furthermore, DRL-based methods can be vulnerable to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios when encountering unforeseen interaction forces. Existing motion synthesis and kinematic prior methods, while providing structured starting points for policy exploration, frequently do not explicitly link these kinematic references with interaction-aware execution, leading to performance degradation under complex, coupled collaborative behaviors.

This research addresses these limitations by investigating a WBC paradigm that prioritizes stable force application and coupled motion consistency over exact tracking accuracy under heavy-load interactions. It proposes a bio-inspired, hierarchical architecture that structurally separates upper-body interaction execution from lower-body support control, aiming to enable the robot to maintain balance while adaptively shaping force exchange within a tightly coupled robot-object system. By focusing on interaction-awareness and leveraging reinforcement learning with asymmetric distillation, this work contributes to the development of more robust and compliant humanoid control systems for demanding physical HRC tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and versatile whole-body control framework, termed Interaction-Oriented Whole-Body Control (IO-WBC), that enables assistive humanoid robots to perform compliant object transport in unstructured environments, particularly under conditions involving strong, time-varying interaction forces and heavy payloads.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in existing whole-body control strategies:
- **Reliability under strong interaction forces:** Traditional tracking-centric WBC approaches become unreliable when confronted with significant and non-conservative physical interaction forces, which are characteristic of tasks like cooperative object transport.
- **Limitations of existing WBC frameworks:** While frameworks like ALBERT, AMO, and optimization-based methods have advanced humanoid motion control, they primarily emphasize accurate posture or trajectory tracking under assumptions of weak interaction. These methods often degrade under high-pressure physical interactions, such as those encountered when transporting heavy payloads with human partners, where object mass and contact forces induce substantial disturbances.
- **Physical infeasibility of precise velocity tracking:** In heavy-load scenarios, enforcing strict velocity tracking objectives can lead to unstable or overly aggressive robot behaviors, as the precise tracking may be physically infeasible given the system dynamics and actuator limits.
- **Need for active compliance and balance:** Collaborative tasks require the robot to apply sustained forces to an external payload while simultaneously maintaining balance. This necessitates a high degree of active compliance to safely absorb human-induced disturbances and a mechanism to coordinate flexible upper-body interaction with lower-body dynamic support.
- **Implicit inference of interaction dynamics:** To achieve robust collaboration without reliance on explicit and potentially fragile force/torque sensors, the robot needs a mechanism to implicitly infer interaction dynamics from readily available proprioceptive feedback.

To address these motivations, the research establishes the following key objectives:
1. **Develop a hierarchical whole-body control architecture:** This architecture should integrate a trajectory-optimized reference generator (RG) with an interaction-aware reinforcement learning (RL) policy. The RG is intended to provide kinematic priors from high-level HRC commands, while the RL policy is designed to regulate whole-body stability and force application under strong interaction coupling.
2. **Enable structural separation of control responsibilities:** The framework aims to structurally separate upper-body interaction execution from lower-body support control. This separation is intended to allow the robot to maintain balance while effectively shaping force exchange within a tightly coupled robot-object system.
3. **Facilitate implicit inference of interaction dynamics:** The research aims to develop a physics-aware training environment that models heavy payload dynamics, external perturbations, and contact uncertainties. Within this environment, an asymmetric teacher-student distillation scheme is proposed to enable the RL policy to implicitly infer interaction dynamics from proprioceptive histories at runtime, without requiring privileged sensing.
4. **Demonstrate stable and compliant object transport:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate through extensive experiments that the proposed IO-WBC can maintain stable whole-body behavior and physical interaction, enabling compliant object transport across a range of scenarios, even when precise velocity tracking becomes infeasible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Interaction-Oriented Whole-Body Control (IO-WBC) framework employs a cascaded, hierarchical architecture designed to manage motion coordination and postural stability for humanoid robots during dynamic collaborative carrying tasks. This framework translates tactical decisions from a skill-level policy into low-level driving torques, utilizing kinematic priors and local task-space resolution.

**A. System Modeling and Cascaded Control Architecture**

The humanoid robot is modeled as a branched dynamic system. The generalized coordinate vector `q` includes the floating base position (`x_b`) and rotation (`R_b`), upper-body interaction joint sequence (`θ_I`), and lower-body/waist support joint sequence (`θ_S`). This architectural separation of `I` and `S` branches distributes control responsibilities to handle non-conservative interaction forces, despite dynamic coupling across the full body.

The task execution follows a three-tier cascaded structure:
1. **Skill Policy Layer (HRC):** An abstract multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) policy generates an 11-dimensional command set `C`. This set includes collaborative system movement velocity (`v_coll`), target center-of-mass (CoM) height and torso pitch angle (`σ_base`), and 3D spatial offsets of the bilateral wrists relative to the base (`∆p`). This layer abstracts multi-agent task coordination into reachable task-space objectives.
2. **Posture Generation Layer (RG):** This layer receives commands from the HRC layer and resolves the kinematic reference posture for the support branch (`θ_ref_S`). A pre-trained mapping (`φ_RG`) ensures that the lower body can perform predictive adjustments kinematically. The RG functions as a feasibility prior, aiming to pre-shape the support branch so that the CoM projection remains within the geometric stability boundary, thereby reducing the search space for the subsequent RL policy.
3. **Interaction Execution Layer (IO-WBC):** The core of this layer is an interaction-aware RL policy (`π_low`). This policy maps high-level commands `C`, the kinematic prior `θ_ref_S`, and proprioceptive history `H_t` to optimal joint-space adaptations `a_t`. Unlike traditional tracking controllers, this RL policy is trained to implicitly infer interaction dynamics, generating real-time residual corrections that compensate for heavy-load perturbations and non-conservative contact forces. These learned increments `a_t` are superposed onto the RG prior and converted into driving torques `τ` via low-level PD controllers (`τ_S = K_p(θ_ref_S + a_t - θ_S) - K_dθ_S`).

**B. Kinematic Mapping and Reference Generator**

- **Upper-body Reference:** The upper-body reference configuration (`θ_ref_I`) is generated based on wrist spatial offsets (`∆p`) from the HRC layer. This is formulated as a local inverse kinematics (IK) resolution process, calculating `∆θ_I` using the Jacobian pseudo-inverse `J†_I`. This ensures end-effector precision while allowing IO-WBC to compensate for dynamic deviations using whole-body redundancy.
- **Lower-body Reference (RG):** To enable the lower body to compensate for CoM shifts caused by upper-body postures, the RG module is constructed using physics-model-driven trajectory optimization. A pre-training dataset is generated from a multi-contact optimal control problem (Equation 4) that minimizes CoM tracking error, joint torques, and velocities, subject to full-body dynamics. This pre-trained RG module, which encodes kinematic limits and static stability boundaries, is then frozen during real-time RL training, providing a robust lower-body reference.

**C. Learning Strategy and Asymmetric Distillation**

The interaction-aware policy is formulated as a Markov decision process (MDP). The system leverages the coupling between object geometric states and proprioceptive feedback to construct a closed-loop control law, implicitly perceiving external interaction forces through measurable deviations in joint tracking and base acceleration.

- **Observation and Action Space:** The observation vector `s_t` for the low-level policy includes the HRC command `c_t`, base Euler angles (`Φ_b`) and angular velocities (`ω_b`), whole-body joint positions (`q`) and velocities (`˙q`), gait phase signal (`ϕ_t`), previous action (`a_t-1`), support reference (`θ_ref_S`), and critically, the object's egocentric geometry (`x_obj`) and its derivative (`˙x_obj`). A proprioceptive history buffer `H_t` (`k` past observations) captures time-series responses, allowing the policy to differentiate between internal and external resistance. The action space `a_t` comprises 15 joint position increments (12 leg, 3 waist motors) for the support branch. Final driving torques `τ_S` are generated via a PD scheme using these corrective residuals.
- **Asymmetric Teacher-Student Distillation:** To enhance sim-to-real transferability and enable practical perception, an asymmetric distillation framework is employed:
 - **Privileged Teacher Policy (π_T):** During training, the teacher has access to privileged information (`s_priv`), including exact object mass, CoM offset, and ground-truth external disturbances. It establishes a high-performance baseline by directly mapping specific physical parameters to stabilizing joint actions.
 - **Adaptive Student Policy (π_S):** The student policy relies solely on the proprioceptive history buffer `H_t`. Knowledge transfer occurs by minimizing the KL divergence between the policy distributions of the teacher and student. This process enables the student to infer interaction dynamics from joint-error histories and base vibrations, eliminating the need for explicit force-torque sensors or mass-estimation modules at runtime.

**D. Interaction Robustness and Multi-Objective Reward**

A rigorous randomization and reward scheme is implemented in a massively parallel simulation environment to ensure IO-WBC's stability under non-stationary interactions.

- **Perturbation and Domain Randomization:** Random impulsive forces are periodically applied to the object's CoM. Domain randomization is applied to object mass, friction coefficients, and sensor latencies to improve generalization across heterogeneous payloads and bridge the simulation-to-real gap.
- **Reward Function Design:** The total reward `R` is decomposed into four components:
 - `r_sync`: Coupled motion consistency reward, penalizing deviations of the support branch from the RG prior and the robot-object system velocity from HRC commands.
 - `r_obj`: Object equilibrium reward, penalizing 3D pose deviations and vertical fluctuations of the object.
 - `r_smooth`: Whole-body smoothness reward, penalizing joint accelerations, action jitters, and mechanical power consumption.
 - `r_gate`: Gait constraint term, providing positive guidance for foot swing height and contact timing.
- **Curriculum Learning Schedule:** A progressive curriculum exposes the policy to increasing robot-object coupling, starting from static balance under varied loads, progressing to dynamic locomotion with perturbations, and concluding with extreme commands where translational motion may be partially or fully infeasible.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the IO-WBC framework included both simulations and physical experiments using a Unitree G1 robot collaborating with a human partner, monitored by a motion-capture (MoCap) system. The high-level tactical commands were generated by a multi-agent reinforcement learning-based skill policy. The evaluation focused on the lower-limb policy's ability to translate HRC commands into robust locomotion and balanced postures under physical constraints.

**A. Experimental Setup and Baselines**
Training was conducted in Isaac Lab, with domain randomization applied to link inertia, joint friction, and motor damping for sim-to-real transferability. Task scenarios included cooperative lifting (7 kg box, 18 kg rubber tire) and cooperative pushing (65 kg caster-mounted crate).

Three baselines were used for comparison:
1. **WBC (SOTA):** An adaptive motion optimization (AMO-style) whole-body controller, representing a model-based approach without explicit coupling terms for heavy payloads or inherent load-awareness.
2. **w/o RG:** A variant lacking the trajectory-optimized reference generator prior.
3. **w/o distillation:** A variant trained without the teacher-student asymmetric distillation.

Performance was quantified using tracking error metrics `E_i`, defined as the mean absolute deviation between reference commands and actual states for linear velocity (`E_v`), base yaw (`E_ψ`), torso pitch (`E_α`), and CoM height (`E_h`).

**B. Performance Benchmark and Ablation Study**
Real-world HRC trials and simulation benchmarks were conducted over five independent runs.
- **Impact of RG:** The `w/o RG` variant exhibited `E_h` and `E_ψ` errors 2-4 times higher than the full IO-WBC even under nominal loads. This indicates that the kinematic priors from the RG are essential for reducing the complexity of the policy's task, preventing it from having to solve global balance and local interaction simultaneously.
- **Impact of Distillation:** The `w/o distillation` variant experienced catastrophic collapse during heavy loading trials. This finding supports the conclusion that the implicit latent representation of payload properties, learned through history-based distillation, is a prerequisite for stabilizing high-inertia interactions without explicit force sensing.
- **Real-world Success Rates:** In the critical 18 kg tire carrying task, IO-WBC achieved an 80% success rate (4 out of 5 trials). In contrast, the WBC baseline consistently failed (0% success rate) due to its inability to adapt its lower-limb stiffness or CoM projection to compensate for the significant mass.

**C. Postural Resilience in Lifting Tasks**
An incremental load-stress test (0 kg to 8 kg with 1 kg resolution) demonstrated IO-WBC's superior linear stability.
- **Performance at Varying Loads:** While IO-WBC and WBC showed comparable performance at 0 kg, their trajectories diverged significantly as mass increased. At 4 kg, the WBC baseline's pitch error (`E_α`) began to climb non-linearly.
- **Breakdown Point:** As the load approached 8 kg, WBC and all ablation variants consistently failed due to divergent CoM oscillations. IO-WBC, however, maintained structural integrity with its pitch and height errors remaining nearly constant. This indicates that IO-WBC implicitly internalizes the payload's gravity as a learned prior, demonstrating a deterministic compensation strategy for high-inertia coupling. The low variance across trials confirmed the robustness of IO-WBC.

**D. Effectiveness and Compliance in Heavy-Duty Pushing**
The 60 kg pushing task highlighted a trade-off between command following and physical survival under extreme resistance.
- **Trade-off in Performance:** IO-WBC's velocity error (`E_v`) increased significantly (to approximately 0.486 m/s) as the load reached 60 kg. This was interpreted not as a failure, but as a strategic prioritization of postural invariance (`E_α`, `E_h`) over locomotion speed. The robot entered a compliant-stagnation regime, reducing forward progression to maintain its center-of-mass within the friction cone, rather than saturating joint torques or inducing foot slippage.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** The WBC baseline failed at 50 kg due to kinematic constraint violations in all trials. IO-WBC remained operational up to 60 kg by actively modulating its impedance. While baseline errors exhibited exponential growth beyond 40 kg, IO-WBC's error norm grew linearly and remained bounded, demonstrating robustness near physical actuator limits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an interaction-oriented whole-body control framework (IO-WBC) that addresses a critical challenge in assistive robotics: enabling humanoid robots to maintain stable force application and postural integrity during heavy-load human-robot collaborative transport. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Compliance:** The primary contribution is a WBC framework that demonstrates significantly improved robustness and compliance when confronted with strong, non-conservative interaction forces and heavy payloads. Unlike traditional tracking-centric methods that degrade under such conditions, IO-WBC prioritizes stable force application and coupled motion consistency, allowing robots to operate reliably in physically demanding scenarios where precise velocity tracking is infeasible. This directly broadens the operational envelope for humanoid robots in real-world HRC tasks.
- **Bio-inspired Hierarchical Control:** The bio-inspired hierarchical architecture, functionally separating upper-body interaction from lower-body support, provides a structured approach to manage complex whole-body dynamics. This mirrors biological motor control principles, contributing to a deeper understanding and practical implementation of human-like compliance and stability in robotic systems. The "artificial cerebellum" analogy highlights its role in translating high-level commands into stable physical behavior under contact.
- **Implicit Interaction Dynamics Inference:** The novel use of asymmetric teacher-student distillation for policy learning is significant. By enabling the student policy to infer complex interaction dynamics (e.g., object mass, CoM offset, external disturbances) solely from proprioceptive history, the framework eliminates the reliance on explicit force-torque sensors or external perception modules during deployment. This simplifies hardware requirements, reduces system complexity, and enhances the practical applicability and cost-effectiveness of humanoid robots in dynamic, unstructured environments.
- **Effective Integration of Kinematic Priors and Reinforcement Learning:** The synergy between a trajectory-optimized reference generator (RG) and an interaction-aware residual RL policy is a notable methodological advance. The RG provides a kinematically feasible prior, reducing the search space for the RL policy and ensuring a stable foundation, while the RL policy learns real-time, adaptive corrections for dynamic uncertainties. This combined approach bridges the gap between traditional model-based control (for kinematic consistency) and modern learning-based control (for dynamic adaptation), leading to more robust and versatile control solutions.
- **Advancement in Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC):** The demonstrated capability of IO-WBC to handle cooperative lifting of an 18 kg tire and pushing a 65 kg crate, where state-of-the-art baselines failed, represents a substantial step forward for HRC. This work enables humanoids to become more capable and reliable partners in physical tasks, potentially expanding their roles in logistics, manufacturing, elderly care, and disaster relief, where collaborative object manipulation is common.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a robust foundation for future research in several areas, including integrating more sophisticated high-level HRC planning within the MARL framework, exploring real-time adaptation of physical properties (e.g., stiffness modulation), and extending the approach to other complex multi-contact and manipulation tasks in highly dynamic environments. The successful sim-to-real transferability also underscores its potential for rapid development and deployment of advanced robotic skills.
