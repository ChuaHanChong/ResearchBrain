# Interaction-Aware Whole-Body Control for Compliant Object Transport

> [!summary] Summary
> The Interaction-Oriented Whole-Body Control (IO-WBC) framework enables assistive humanoid robots to perform stable and compliant object transport under heavy loads in unstructured environments. This system, which integrates a hierarchical control architecture with an interaction-aware reinforcement learning policy, successfully transported an 18 kg payload with an 80% success rate and maintained stability under pushing loads up to 60 kg, outperforming existing model-based controllers.

## Problem

- Current whole-body control (WBC) frameworks struggle with strong, time-varying interaction forces and heavy payloads, leading to instability in human-robot collaboration (HRC) tasks.
- Existing learning-based control methods often lack explicit mechanisms to functionally decouple upper-body interaction from lower-body support, crucial for stable force application during collaborative tasks.
- Reliable deployment of humanoid robots in HRC tasks for heavy-load conditions is challenged by the need to apply sustained forces while maintaining balance without relying on explicit force-torque sensors.

## Method

The interaction-oriented WBC policy is developed through teacher-student distillation, where a privileged teacher guides a student policy to decode interaction dynamics from proprioceptive history.*

- A three-tier cascaded ==Interaction-Oriented Whole-Body Control (IO-WBC)== framework is proposed, structurally separating upper-body interaction execution from lower-body support control.
- It integrates a trajectory-optimized ==reference generator (RG)== for kinematic priors with an interaction-aware ==reinforcement learning (RL) policy== that provides residual corrections for dynamic uncertainties.
- An ==asymmetric teacher-student distillation== scheme allows the student RL policy to implicitly infer interaction dynamics and payload properties from proprioceptive feedback without explicit force-torque sensors at runtime.

## Results

- IO-WBC achieved an **80%** success rate in transporting an **18 kg** tire, while a state-of-the-art WBC baseline consistently failed due to inability to compensate for significant mass.
- The framework maintained structural integrity, with pitch and height errors remaining nearly constant during incremental load-stress tests up to **8 kg**, in contrast to baseline controllers which suffered catastrophic failure beyond **4 kg**.
- In heavy-duty pushing tasks (**60 kg**), IO-WBC entered a 'compliant-stagnation' regime, maintaining postural invariance by actively modulating impedance, successfully remaining operational where the WBC baseline failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical control architecture that decouples interaction from support significantly enhances postural resilience and robust execution during heavy-load physical interactions, mimicking biological systems.
> - Implicit identification of interaction dynamics through proprioceptive history and asymmetric distillation enables effective compensation for heavy payloads without explicit force-torque sensing or complex mass estimation modules.
> - Prioritizing 'physical survival' and compliance over strict trajectory tracking allows humanoid robots to remain stable and operational even when traditional tracking becomes physically infeasible under extreme loads.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of humanoid robots in complex, human-centric environments necessitates advanced control systems capable of managing significant physical interactions. Current whole-body control (WBC) frameworks, particularly those focused on trajectory tracking, often exhibit limitations when faced with strong, time-varying interaction forces, heavy payloads, or external disturbances. This is especially true in close-contact human-robot collaboration (HRC) tasks such as cooperative object transport, where the robot must apply sustained forces to an external object while simultaneously maintaining its own balance.

Traditional WBC approaches, utilizing inverse kinematics or quadratic programming, offer theoretical guarantees but can struggle with the non-linear, high-frequency disturbances inherent in heavy-load physical interactions. Recent advancements in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) have enhanced robot agility and motor skill acquisition. However, many DRL-based methods, while robust in single-agent performance, frequently lack explicit mechanisms to functionally decouple upper-body interaction from lower-body support, a capability observed in biological systems that is crucial for maintaining effective force application during collaborative tasks. Existing learning-based control frameworks, such as ALBERT and AMO, typically emphasize accurate posture or trajectory tracking under assumptions of relatively weak interaction, leading to degradation or instability under high-pressure physical interactions where precise velocity tracking becomes physically unfeasible. The challenge of out-of-distribution (OOD) vulnerability for DRL policies when encountering unforeseen interaction forces also remains.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing an interaction-oriented whole-body control paradigm. It seeks to develop a control framework that prioritizes stable force application and coupled motion consistency over strict tracking accuracy in scenarios involving heavy-load interactions. The research draws inspiration from biological principles by introducing a hierarchical structure that decouples upper-body interaction from lower-body support, aiming to enhance postural resilience and robust execution under significant external payloads. This approach aims to bridge the gap between high-level task planning and low-level physical execution in dynamic, interaction-rich environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and versatile whole-body control (WBC) framework, termed Interaction-Oriented Whole-Body Control (IO-WBC), that enables assistive humanoid robots to perform cooperative object transport in unstructured environments. This framework is designed to function as an "artificial cerebellum," translating high-level task commands into stable and physically consistent whole-body behavior, even under continuous and strong physical interactions.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges in humanoid robotics:
- **Reliability in HRC:** Deploying humanoid robots in human-centric environments for tasks like human-robot collaboration (HRC) requires managing bidirectional physical interaction effectively. Existing tracking-centric WBC methods often prove unreliable in these scenarios due to strong, time-varying interaction forces, particularly in close-contact support tasks.
- **Heavy-Load Conditions:** Cooperative object transport involves applying sustained forces to external payloads while maintaining robot balance. This creates a tightly coupled robot-object dynamic system. Under such heavy-load conditions, robot control effectiveness is determined less by precise tracking accuracy and more by the ability to maintain stability, compliance, and responsiveness to non-conservative interaction forces.
- **Biological Inspiration:** Humans naturally decouple upper-body interaction from lower-body support, allowing interaction forces to be absorbed and redirected through the entire body while maintaining balance. The research is motivated by replicating this principle in a hierarchical WBC architecture to prevent conflicts between interaction objectives and balance maintenance.
- **Limitations of Current WBC:** Current state-of-the-art WBC frameworks primarily focus on accurate posture or trajectory tracking under assumptions of weak interaction. When integrated with learning-based components, these methods still tend to degrade under high-pressure physical interactions (e.g., carrying heavy payloads), where enforcing strict tracking can lead to unstable or overly aggressive behaviors.
- **Implicit Interaction Sensing:** The motivation also includes developing a system that can implicitly infer interaction dynamics and payload properties without relying on explicit force-torque sensors or complex mass estimation modules at runtime, relying instead on proprioceptive feedback.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives are:
1. To propose a hierarchical whole-body control architecture that structurally separates upper-body interaction execution from lower-body support control, thereby enabling stable force exchange and balance.
2. To integrate a trajectory-optimized reference generator (RG) that provides kinematic priors, alongside a reinforcement learning (RL) policy governing body responses under heavy-load interactions and disturbances.
3. To develop a physics-aware training environment with randomization and an asymmetric teacher-student distillation scheme, allowing the RL policy to infer interaction dynamics from proprioceptive histories without privileged sensing during deployment.
4. To demonstrate the system's ability to maintain stable whole-body behavior and physical interaction, enabling compliant object transport across diverse scenarios, even when precise velocity tracking becomes physically infeasible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves an Interaction-Oriented Whole-Body Control (IO-WBC) framework structured as a cascaded, three-tier architecture (Figure 2), designed to bridge skill-level commands with low-level execution for dynamic collaborative tasks.

### 4. A. System Modeling and Cascaded Control Architecture

The humanoid robot is modeled as a branched dynamic system with generalized coordinates `q = [x_b^T, R_b^T, θ_I^T, θ_S^T]^T`, where `x_b`, `R_b` define the floating base, `θ_I` represents upper-body interaction joints, and `θ_S` represents lower-body and waist support joints. The control responsibilities are distributed across these branches.

The three-tier cascaded structure includes:
1. **Skill Policy Layer (HRC):** An MARL policy generates 11-dimensional commands `C = {v_coll, σ_base, Δp}`. These include collaborative system velocity and yaw (`v_coll`), target center-of-mass (CoM) height and torso pitch (`σ_base`), and 3D bilateral wrist spatial offsets (`Δp`). This layer provides abstract task-space objectives.
2. **Reference Generation Layer (RG):** This layer receives commands from the HRC layer. It first resolves the upper-body joint reference `θ_ref_I` using a differential inverse kinematics (IK) model based on `Δp`. Subsequently, it computes a kinematic reference posture `θ_ref_S` for the support branch using a pre-trained mapping `ϕ_RG`. The RG is pre-trained via supervised learning using physics-model-driven trajectory optimization, formulated as a multi-contact optimal control problem (Equation 4). This module functions as a feasibility prior, ensuring the lower body adjusts predictively to keep the CoM projection within stability boundaries, thereby narrowing the search space for the subsequent RL policy.
3. **Interaction Execution Layer (IO-WBC):** At this core layer, an interaction-aware RL policy (`π_low`) maps high-level commands `C`, the kinematic prior `θ_ref_S`, and a proprioceptive history `H_t` to optimal joint-space adaptations `a_t`. Unlike traditional tracking controllers, this policy is trained to implicitly infer interaction dynamics. It generates real-time residual corrections `a_t` that compensate for heavy-load perturbations and non-conservative contact forces. These learned increments are then superposed onto the RG prior, and final driving torques `τ_S` are calculated via low-level PD controllers: `τ_S = K_p(θ_ref_S + a_t - θ_S) - K_d * d(θ_S)/dt`.

### 5. B. Learning Strategy and Asymmetric Distillation

The execution of the interaction-aware policy is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **Observation and Action Space:** The policy's observation vector `s_t` includes HRC commands, base Euler angles and angular velocities, whole-body joint positions and velocities, gait phase, previous action, the support reference `θ_ref_S` from RG, and, crucially, the object's egocentric geometry `x_obj` and its velocity `d(x_obj)/dt`. The inclusion of object states allows the policy to implicitly perceive interaction trends. A proprioceptive history buffer `H_t` (`s_(t-k), ..., s_t`) captures time-series responses, enabling differentiation between internal friction and external resistance. The action space `a_t` consists of 15 joint position increments for the support branch (12 leg, 3 waist joints).
- **Asymmetric Teacher-Student Distillation:** To facilitate sim-to-real transfer without requiring external force sensors, an asymmetric distillation framework is employed:
 - **Privileged Teacher Policy (`π_T`):** During training, the teacher has access to privileged information (`s_priv`), including exact object mass, CoM offset, and ground-truth external disturbances. It establishes a high-performance baseline by directly mapping specific physical parameters to stabilizing joint actions.
 - **Adaptive Student Policy (`π_S`):** At runtime, the student policy relies only on the proprioceptive history `H_t`. Knowledge transfer occurs by minimizing the KL divergence between the teacher's and student's policy distributions (Equation 7). This process enables the student to infer interaction dynamics and generate compensatory actions from observed joint errors and base vibrations, thus negating the need for explicit force/torque sensors or mass estimation modules during deployment.

### 6. C. Interaction Robustness and Multi-Objective Reward

Robustness is ensured through a rigorous randomization and reward scheme in a massively parallel simulation environment:
- **Perturbation and Domain Randomization:** Random impulsive forces are periodically applied to the object's CoM. Domain randomization is applied to object mass, friction coefficients (ground, object, surface), and sensor latencies to enhance generalization.
- **Reward Function Design:** The total reward `R` is decomposed into four components:
 - **Coupled motion consistency reward (`r_sync`):** Encourages the support branch to align with the RG prior and the system velocity with HRC commands (Equation 8).
 - **Object equilibrium reward (`r_obj`):** Penalizes object pose deviations and vertical fluctuations relative to the base.
 - **Whole-body smoothness reward (`r_smooth`):** Penalizes joint accelerations, action jitters, and mechanical power consumption.
 - **Gait constraint term (`r_gait`):** Provides positive guidance for foot swing height and contact timing based on the phase signal.
- **Curriculum Learning:** A progressive curriculum exposes the policy to increasing levels of robot-object coupling, starting from static balance under varied loads, moving to dynamic locomotion with impulsive perturbations, and culminating in scenarios with extreme commands where full translational motion may be infeasible.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The IO-WBC framework was evaluated through extensive simulations in Isaac Lab and physical experiments on a Unitree G1 robot, collaborating with a human partner and utilizing a motion-capture (MoCap) system. The high-level tactical commands were generated by a separate multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) based skill policy. Performance was quantified using mean absolute deviation tracking errors (`E_v` for linear velocity, `E_ψ` for base yaw, `E_α` for torso pitch, `E_h` for CoM height) and success rates.

### 8. A. Experimental Setup and Baselines

Task scenarios included cooperative lifting of payloads (7 kg cardboard box and an 18 kg rubber tire) and cooperative pushing of a 65 kg crate equipped with omni-directional wheels.
Three baseline variants were used for comparison:
1. **WBC (SOTA):** A full adaptive motion optimization (AMO-style) whole-body controller, representing a model-based approach that is load-agnostic and lacks explicit coupling terms for heavy payloads.
2. **w/o RG:** The IO-WBC framework without its trajectory-optimized reference generator prior.
3. **w/o distillation:** The IO-WBC framework trained without the asymmetric teacher-student distillation process.

### 9. B. Performance Benchmark and Ablation Study

- **Ablation Studies (Fig. 3):** The `w/o RG` variant exhibited significantly higher `E_h` and `E_ψ` errors (2–4 times greater than the full IO-WBC) even under nominal loads. This indicated that without kinematic priors, the policy struggled to simultaneously solve for global balance and local interaction. The `w/o distillation` variant experienced catastrophic collapse during heavy loading trials, confirming that the implicit understanding of payload dynamics, facilitated by history-based distillation, is critical for stabilizing high-inertia interactions without explicit force sensing.
- **Real-World HRC Trials (Fig. 3c):** In the 18 kg tire carrying task, IO-WBC achieved an 80% success rate (4 out of 5 trials). In contrast, the WBC baseline consistently failed (0% success rate) due to its inability to proactively modulate lower-limb stiffness or appropriately shift its CoM projection to compensate for the significant mass.

### 10. C. Postural Resilience in Lifting Tasks (Fig. 6, Table II)

An incremental load-stress test (0 kg to 8 kg) revealed that IO-WBC maintained superior linear stability. While IO-WBC and WBC showed similar performance at 0 kg, their trajectories diverged as mass increased. Beyond 4 kg, the WBC baseline's pitch error (`E_α`) began to climb non-linearly. At the 8 kg critical limit, the WBC and all ablation variants consistently suffered from divergent CoM oscillations, leading to catastrophic failure in 100% of trials. IO-WBC, however, maintained structural integrity, with its pitch and height errors remaining nearly constant. This indicated that IO-WBC effectively internalized the payload's gravity as a learned prior, demonstrating a deterministic compensation strategy for high-inertia coupling with low variance across trials.

### 11. D. Effectiveness and Compliance in Heavy-Duty Pushing (Fig. 7, Table III)

In the 60 kg pushing task, IO-WBC's linear velocity error (`E_v`) significantly increased, reaching approximately 0.486 m/s. This was interpreted not as a failure, but as a strategic prioritization of postural invariance (`E_α`, `E_h`) over locomotion speed. The robot entered a "compliant-stagnation" regime, where it reduced forward progression to preserve its center-of-mass within the friction cone, rather than saturating joint torques or inducing foot slippage, which caused the WBC baseline to fail in all 5 trials at this load. IO-WBC successfully remained operational up to 60 kg, actively modulating its impedance, with its error norm growing linearly and remaining bounded, demonstrating robustness at the physical actuator limits.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a critical challenge in humanoid robotics: stable and compliant physical interaction under heavy loads in unstructured environments. The development of Interaction-Oriented Whole-Body Control (IO-WBC) offers several significant contributions and potential impacts.

The primary significance lies in the demonstration that humanoid robots can maintain stable force application and postural integrity during heavy-load human-robot collaborative transport. This capability is crucial for advancing the utility of assistive humanoids in real-world scenarios, which often involve unknown and varying physical interactions. By prioritizing "physical survival" and compliance over rigid trajectory tracking under extreme conditions, the IO-WBC framework offers a paradigm shift for robot control in interaction-rich tasks.

Specifically, the work demonstrates the effectiveness of:
- **Hierarchical Control Architecture:** The proposed architecture, which synergizes a trajectory-optimized reference generator (RG) with an interaction-aware residual policy, effectively bridges kinematic feasibility and dynamic uncertainty. This design enables robust execution of skill-level commands even under high-inertia coupling, where traditional analytical models often fail. This hierarchical approach offers a structured pathway for integrating high-level task planning with low-level reactive control.
- **Implicit Interaction Identification:** The physics-aware learning approach coupled with asymmetric teacher-student distillation facilitates the implicit identification of interaction dynamics solely from proprioceptive feedback. This eliminates the need for explicit, often expensive and fragile, force-torque sensors or complex mass estimation modules at runtime, simplifying hardware requirements and enhancing robot autonomy in unknown environments. This is a crucial step towards robust sim-to-real transfer for highly interactive tasks.
- **Robustness under Extreme Conditions:** The extensive experimental validation, including ablation studies and comparisons with a state-of-the-art WBC baseline, confirms significant improvements in robustness. IO-WBC successfully managed to transport an 18 kg payload (80% success rate) and remained stable under pushing loads up to 60 kg, tasks that caused baseline controllers to fail. This capability for sustained operation at the edge of physical actuator limits underscores the framework's practical applicability.

The potential impact of this work extends to various domains where physical human-robot collaboration is envisioned. It could enable more reliable and safer human-robot collaboration in logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, and assistive robotics, where tasks often involve co-carrying or manipulating heavy or awkwardly shaped objects. The ability of robots to adapt compliantly to human intent and unforeseen disturbances without external force sensing can foster more intuitive and effective collaboration. Furthermore, the bio-inspired structural separation of upper-body interaction and lower-body support could inform future designs of humanoid control systems, moving towards more adaptable and resilient robotic platforms in unpredictable, dynamic environments.
