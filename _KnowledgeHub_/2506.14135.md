# GAF: Gaussian Action Field as a 4D Representation for Dynamic World Modeling in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Beijing Normal University developed Gaussian Action Field (GAF), a 4D representation leveraging motion-augmented 3D Gaussian Splatting, for dynamic scene modeling and action generation in robotic manipulation. GAF achieved an average success rate of 60.4% across 9 simulated RLBench tasks, outperforming the Diffusion Policy baseline by 15.7% and Act3D by 7.3%, and demonstrated successful deployment on a real-world robot.

## Problem

- Existing vision-based robotic manipulation methods (V-A and V-3D-A) struggle with dynamic scenes, treating environments as static entities despite inherent object and robot movement.
- V-A paradigms rely on implicit scene understanding, which limits precise 3D spatial reasoning essential for high-precision manipulation tasks.
- V-3D-A methods, while using 3D representations, often neglect temporal evolution and face challenges in explicitly encoding dynamic information or acquiring sufficient 3D datasets, leading to actions lacking physical feasibility.

## Method

- Introduced **GAF** (Gaussian Action Field), a unified ==spatiotemporal representation== that extends ==3D Gaussian Splatting== by associating each Gaussian primitive with ==learnable motion dynamics (∆μ)== for high-fidelity 4D scene modeling.
- Developed a ==geometry-agnostic and pose-free== GAF architecture that reconstructs motion-augmented Gaussians directly from sparse, unposed multi-view RGB inputs using a ==Vision Transformer== and decoupled prediction heads.
- Implemented an ==action-vision-aligned denoising== framework, utilizing a ==diffusion model== conditioned on "Actionable Multiview RGB Guidance," to refine initial vision-driven actions from GAF, ensuring physical feasibility and accurate execution.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **60.4%** across **9** RLBench tasks, outperforming the Diffusion Policy baseline by **15.7%**, Act3D by **7.3%**, and ManiGaussian by **10.3%**.
- Demonstrated superior scene reconstruction and future state prediction, with GAF showing average improvements of **+11.5385 dB** PSNR for current states and **+10.5311 dB** PSNR for future states compared to ManiGaussian.
- Successfully deployed on a Franka Emika Panda robot for **5** real-world tasks, completing tasks like "Push Button" with **10/10** success, and exhibiting robust spatial generalization and multi-tasking capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling scene dynamics with learnable motion vectors (∆μ) directly within a 3D Gaussian representation enables high-fidelity 4D world modeling and more accurate understanding of evolving manipulation environments.
> - Deriving initial actions by matching gripper-related Gaussians between current and predicted future states provides a strong, physically grounded, and vision-driven prior for robotic manipulation.
> - Refining these initial, purely vision-driven actions through an action-vision-aligned diffusion model is critical to ensure physical feasibility and precision, bridging the gap between perceived dynamics and executable robotic movements.
