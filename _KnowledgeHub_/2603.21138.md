# Incentivizing Generative Zero-Shot Learning via Outcome-Reward Reinforcement Learning with Visual Cues

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and Stanford University developed RLVC, an outcome-reward reinforcement learning framework with visual cues that enhances generative Zero-Shot Learning. This approach yielded state-of-the-art CZSL accuracies of 90.1% on CUB, 77.7% on SUN, and 84.0% on AWA2, and GZSL harmonic means of 81.2% on CUB, 57.6% on SUN, and 80.4% on AWA2.

## Problem

- Existing generative Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) models often optimize feature generators in isolation from downstream classifiers, leading to "task-agnostic" features that lack optimal discriminative power.
- Many generative ZSL methods rely solely on semantic conditions, which are insufficient for distinguishing between semantically similar but visually distinct classes, resulting in overlapping feature distributions and misclassifications.

## Method

- An ==outcome-reward== reinforcement learning framework is introduced, treating the generator as a policy model and optimizing it to maximize a classification-oriented reward for task-relevant feature synthesis.
- Class-wise visual cues, derived from fine-tuned visual features, are incorporated via a prototype-distillation loss to provide richer supervision and align synthesized features with real data distributions.
- A diffusion-based adversarial generative framework is employed with a cold-start training strategy and alternating updates to stabilize the integration of reinforcement learning and mitigate gradient conflicts.

## Results

- RLVC achieved state-of-the-art Conventional Zero-Shot Learning (CZSL) accuracies of **90.1%** on CUB, **77.7%** on SUN, and **84.0%** on AWA2, surpassing previous methods by up to **3.1%**.
- In Generalized Zero-Shot Learning (GZSL), the framework yielded the highest harmonic means of **81.2%** on CUB, **57.6%** on SUN, and **80.4%** on AWA2, reflecting average gains of **4.7%** over prior art.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the outcome-reward reinforcement learning component and visual cues are critical for performance, with their removal leading to significant drops in GZSL harmonic mean (e.g., from **80.4%** to **73.9%** on AWA2 without RL).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing generative ZSL models with an outcome-reward reinforcement learning mechanism directly aligns the feature generation process with the classification objective, producing more discriminative features.
> - Integrating visual cues through a prototype-distillation loss improves the fidelity and distinctiveness of synthesized features, especially for fine-grained categories with high semantic similarity.
> - A carefully designed training strategy, including adversarial pre-training and alternating updates, is crucial for stably and effectively combining reinforcement learning with generative adversarial models in ZSL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) addresses the challenge of recognizing objects from classes not encountered during training by leveraging semantic information (e.g., attribute vectors or word embeddings). Historically, ZSL methods have largely fallen into two paradigms: embedding methods, which project visual features into a semantic space for classification, and generative methods, which synthesize visual features or images for unseen classes.

Recent advancements in generative ZSL have shown promise by utilizing models such as variational autoencoders (VAEs), generative adversarial networks (GANs), and diffusion models. These models synthesize visual features conditioned on semantic prototypes, enabling the training of classifiers on augmented data. This approach offers an expanded representation space for modeling data distributions, aiming to mitigate the data scarcity issue for unseen classes.

However, existing generative ZSL solutions exhibit two primary limitations. First, the generative models are often optimized in isolation from the downstream classifier. This separation can lead to the synthesis of "task-agnostic" features, meaning the generated data, while plausible, may not be optimally discriminative for classification. Second, many methods rely exclusively on semantic conditions, which can be insufficient for distinguishing between classes that are semantically similar but visually distinct (e.g., different species of birds like "Indigo Bunting" and "Painted Bunting"). This limitation can result in overlapping inter-class feature distributions and subsequent misclassifications.

This paper introduces Reinforcement Learning with Visual Cues (RLVC) as a method to address these limitations within the generative ZSL paradigm. By integrating an outcome-reward reinforcement learning framework, the authors aim to align the feature generation process more directly with the downstream classification objective. The inclusion of class-wise visual cues is intended to provide richer supervision, thereby improving inter-class separability and the fidelity of synthesized features. This work represents an exploration into applying reinforcement learning to generative ZSL, a domain primarily dominated by adversarial and variational approaches, aiming to enhance the task-relevance and discriminative power of generated features.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address the limitations inherent in existing generative Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) approaches and advance the performance of ZSL systems. The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Overcome Task-Agnostic Feature Synthesis:** Current generative ZSL models typically optimize the generator independently of the downstream classifier. This optimization often prioritizes generating visually plausible features that align with semantic prototypes but may not necessarily be maximally discriminative or "task-relevant" for accurate classification. The paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing an explicit mechanism to incentivize the generator to produce features that are more useful for the classification task.

2. **Enhance Discriminability for Semantically Similar, Visually Distinct Classes:** Existing methods frequently rely solely on semantic prototypes (e.g., attribute vectors or word embeddings) to condition feature generation. While semantic prototypes provide valuable information, they can be insufficient for differentiating between classes that share high semantic similarity but exhibit clear visual distinctions. This can lead to synthesized features for such classes having overlapping distributions, resulting in increased misclassification rates. The research seeks to incorporate additional visual guidance to enable the generative model to infer more faithful and distinct data distributions, even for fine-grained categories.

3. **Introduce Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Generative Model Optimization:** The authors are motivated to explore the application of Reinforcement Learning, specifically an outcome-reward RL framework, to generative ZSL. The rationale is that RL's trial-and-error learning paradigm, driven by outcome-based rewards, can enable the generative model to "self-evolve." This approach is posited to allow the generator to learn how to produce features that maximize a classification-oriented reward, thereby directly aligning the generation process with the ultimate classification objective. This contrasts with traditional adversarial losses that may not fully capture downstream task relevance.

4. **Incorporate Class-wise Visual Cues for Richer Supervision:** To address the limitations of semantic-only conditions and to stabilize RL training, the paper aims to introduce "class-wise visual cues." These cues are derived from fine-tuned visual features of seen classes and serve as "visual prototypes." The objective is to use these visual prototypes to impose a direct alignment constraint on the synthesized features, guiding them towards real data distributions and providing a robust form of supervision during the training process, particularly for RL-based updates.

5. **Develop a Stable Training Strategy for RL in Generative ZSL:** Integrating RL into generative models can introduce training instability. The research intends to develop a novel "cold-start" training strategy and an alternating update mechanism. This approach aims to first stabilize the generative model using conventional adversarial losses before activating RL, and then manage potential gradient conflicts by separating the adversarial and RL optimization steps.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond mere plausible feature generation to **task-relevant and discriminative feature synthesis** for ZSL, particularly for unseen classes, by leveraging the self-evolutionary capabilities of outcome-reward reinforcement learning, augmented with rich visual supervision and a robust training methodology.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named RLVC (Reinforcement Learning with Visual Cues), is an outcome-reward reinforcement learning framework designed to improve generative Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL). It extends a diffusion-based adversarial generative framework by incorporating explicit task-relevant optimization through RL and richer visual supervision via visual cues.

#### 3.1. Problem Statement

The work addresses both Conventional Zero-Shot Learning (CZSL) and Generalized Zero-Shot Learning (GZSL).
- **CZSL:** The goal is to classify samples from unseen classes ($Y_u$) using knowledge acquired from seen classes ($Y_s$). The test set consists solely of unseen class samples.
- **GZSL:** The goal is to classify samples from both seen and unseen classes ($Y_s \cup Y_u$). The test set contains samples from both categories.
Training data ($D_{tr}$) are available only for seen classes, comprising visual features ($x_s$), labels ($y_s$), and semantic prototypes ($z_c$). Semantic prototypes ($z_c$) are available for all classes (seen and unseen).

#### 3.2. Diffusion-based Generative Framework

RLVC builds upon a diffusion-based adversarial framework, which includes a generator $G_\theta$ and two discriminators, $D_{x_0}$ and $D_{x_t}$.
- **Generator ($G_\theta$):** Takes Gaussian noise ($\epsilon$), a class semantic prototype ($z_c$), a diffusion state ($x_t$), and a timestep ($t$) as input to synthesize a visual feature ($\tilde{x_0}$).
- **Discriminator $D_{x_0}$:** Distinguishes between real clean features ($x_0$) and synthesized clean features ($\tilde{x_0}$), conditioned on $z_c$.
- **Discriminator $D_{x_t}$:** Distinguishes between real diffusion transitions ($(x_t, x_{t+1})$) and synthesized transitions ($( \tilde{x_t}, x_{t+1})$), conditioned on $z_c$ and $t$.
The framework uses standard adversarial losses for the discriminators ($L_D$) and the generator ($L_{adv_G}$), including a gradient penalty ($\lambda_{gp}GP$). During training, $L_D$ and $L_{adv_G}$ are updated alternately.

#### 3.3. Outcome-Reward Reinforcement Learning

To make synthesized features task-relevant, RLVC treats the generator $G_\theta$ as a policy model and optimizes it using an outcome-based reward.
- **Reward Model ($R$):** A frozen classifier, implemented as a linear layer, is used to calculate the reward. Given a synthesized feature $\tilde{x_0}$, $R$ outputs class logits, which are converted into probabilities $p(y | \tilde{x_0})$ via softmax.
- **Outcome Reward ($r$):** The reward is defined as the log-probability of the ground-truth class for the synthesized feature, $r = \log p(y | \tilde{x_0})$. A higher confidence from the reward model on the correct class results in a larger reward.
- **Advantage Calculation:** To stabilize RL training, an exponential moving average (EMA) baseline ($b$) of rewards is maintained. The advantage $\hat{r}_i$ is computed as the difference between the current reward $r_i$ and the baseline $b$ ($\hat{r}_i = r_i - b$). A stop-gradient operator is applied to $\hat{r}_i$ to treat it as a constant during policy optimization.
- **RL Objective ($L_{RL}$):** The policy model (generator) is optimized to maximize the expected advantage, formulated as minimizing $L_{RL} = - \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^{B} \text{sg}[\hat{r}_i] \log p(y_i | \tilde{x}_{0,i})$. This objective propagates gradients through the reward model $R$ and softmax to $G_\theta$, guiding it to produce more discriminative features.

#### 3.4. Visual Cues with Prototype-Distillation Loss

To enhance the fidelity of feature distributions and provide richer supervision beyond semantic prototypes, class-wise visual cues are introduced.
- **Visual Encoder Fine-tuning:** A visual encoder (e.g., ViT) is fine-tuned to extract visual features. This process helps alleviate cross-dataset bias and injects dataset-specific priors.
- **Visual Prototypes:** For each seen class $c$, a visual prototype $v_c$ is computed as the mean of all fine-tuned visual features belonging to that class.
- **Prototype-Distillation Loss ($L_{PD}$):** A cosine similarity-based loss is applied to align synthesized features $\tilde{x_0}$ with their corresponding visual prototypes $v_c$. Specifically, $L_{PD} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^{B} (1 - \frac{\tilde{x}_{0,i}^\top v_{c_i}}{\|\tilde{x}_{0,i}\|_2 \|v_{c_i}\|_2})$. This loss pulls generated features closer to the real data distribution and contributes to RL update stability.
- **Total Generator Objective ($L_{total_G}$):** The generator is optimized by minimizing a combined loss: $L_{total_G} = L_{adv_G} + \lambda_{PD} L_{PD}$, where $\lambda_{PD}$ is a weighting coefficient.

#### 3.5. Cold-Start Training Procedure

To ensure stable optimization, RLVC employs a cold-start strategy:
- **Initial Adversarial Training:** The model is first trained for a specified number of epochs ($E_{RL}$) using only the generative adversarial objective ($L_{adv_G}$ and $L_D$). This phase allows the generator to develop basic class separability before RL is introduced.
- **RL Activation and Alternating Updates:** After the cold-start threshold, RL training is activated. To mitigate gradient conflicts between the adversarial and RL objectives, the policy model $G_\theta$ is updated alternately within each iteration. It is first updated using $L_{total_G}$ (adversarial and prototype distillation) and then separately updated using $L_{RL}$ (RL objective).

#### 3.6. Inference for CZSL and GZSL

- **Feature Synthesis:** The trained generator $G_\theta$ is frozen and used to synthesize visual features for all unseen classes based on their semantic prototypes.
- **Classifier Training:**
 - **CZSL:** A standard softmax classifier is trained exclusively on the synthesized unseen features.
 - **GZSL:** The classifier is trained on a combined dataset consisting of the fine-tuned real visual features of seen classes and the synthesized visual features of unseen classes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper conducted comprehensive experiments on three ZSL benchmarks (CUB, SUN, AWA2) to evaluate RLVC's effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Core Quantitative Results (Table 1)

RLVC demonstrated improved performance compared to existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) embedding and generative ZSL methods.
- **CZSL Accuracy (Acc):** RLVC achieved the highest CZSL accuracy across all three datasets: 90.1% on CUB, 77.7% on SUN, and 84.0% on AWA2. These figures represent gains of 3.1%, 1.4%, and 1.5% over the previously leading method, VADS.
- **GZSL Harmonic Mean (H):** In the GZSL setting, RLVC yielded the highest harmonic mean on all datasets: 81.2% on CUB, 57.6% on SUN, and 80.4% on AWA2. These H-scores reflect improvements of 5.5% (over VSPCN) on CUB, 1.5% (over VADS) on SUN, and 0.6% (over PSVMA+) on AWA2. While RLVC did not consistently achieve the highest individual seen (S) or unseen (U) accuracies, it maintained a balanced performance, leading to the highest harmonic mean, which is the standard metric for GZSL.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness with Different Semantic Prototypes (Table 2)

To assess robustness, RLVC was evaluated with two types of semantic prototypes: word embeddings from CLIP and expert-annotated attribute vectors.
- RLVC consistently improved both CZSL (Acc) and GZSL (H) scores across all datasets and prototype types, with gains ranging from 0.1% to 8.3%.
- Notably, RLVC showed significant improvements when using attribute vectors, demonstrating H-score gains of 6.1% on CUB, 2.6% on SUN, and 7.6% on AWA2 over a vanilla generative model without RL and visual cues. This indicates that RLVC effectively enhances feature synthesis even with challenging semantic conditions like word embeddings, and further benefits from richer attribute information.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study and Analysis (Table 3)

An ablation study was performed to quantify the contribution of each component of RLVC.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Removing the RL component (i.e., Eq. (11)) resulted in a significant performance drop, particularly on AWA2 (H-score decreased from 80.4% to 73.9%). This indicates that the outcome-reward RL mechanism plays a critical role in enhancing the model's ability to learn task-relevant distributions.
- **Visual Cues (Prototype-Distillation Loss):** Excluding visual cues (i.e., Eq. (13)) also degraded performance across all benchmarks (e.g., H-score dropped from 81.2% to 79.2% on CUB). This suggests that aligning synthesized features with visual prototypes is beneficial for consolidating feature distributions and stabilizing training.
- **Visual Encoder Fine-tuning:** Without fine-tuning the visual encoder, GZSL performance declined (e.g., H-score decreased from 81.2% to 77.5% on CUB). This highlights the importance of incorporating dataset-specific priors through fine-tuned visual features.
- **EMA Advantage Smoothing:** Removing the EMA-based reward smoothing (i.e., Eq. (9)) led to lower performance (e.g., H-score dropped from 81.2% to 79.7% on CUB), indicating that stable advantage calculation contributes to optimization effectiveness.

#### 4.4. Ablation on Prototype-Distillation Losses (Table 4)

The proposed prototype-distillation loss ($L_{PD}$) was compared against KL divergence and $L_1$ loss.
- $L_{PD}$ generally outperformed KL and $L_1$ losses in most settings, with an exception in the GZSL setting on SUN. The authors attribute this to $L_{PD}$ being better suited for clustering and distilling prototype information.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Evaluation (Figure 4)

t-SNE visualizations of feature distributions on CUB illustrated the impact of RLVC:
- A vanilla model (without RL and visual cues) showed significant overlap between seen and unseen classes, especially for semantically similar categories.
- The model without visual cues showed improved boundaries, but the full RLVC demonstrated more compact clustering for each class, indicating a more accurate and task-relevant data distribution.

#### 4.6. Hyperparameter Analysis (Figure 5)

Sensitivity analyses on CUB validated the chosen hyperparameter settings:
- The RL cold-start epoch of 30 was found to balance stable initial training with optimal performance.
- A visual loss coefficient of 20 resulted in peak performance, indicating an optimal balance between generative and prototype-distillation objectives.
- The number of synthetic unseen samples (400 for CUB) impacted the balance between seen and unseen accuracy, with the chosen value yielding the best harmonic mean.

These findings collectively indicate that RLVC's components, particularly outcome-reward reinforcement learning and visual cues, contribute to its ability to synthesize more discriminative and task-relevant features, leading to consistent performance improvements in both CZSL and GZSL settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RLVC represents a methodological advancement in generative Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) by addressing persistent limitations related to task-agnostic feature synthesis and inter-class feature overlap. The paper's primary significance lies in its novel application of an outcome-reward reinforcement learning framework to incentivize the generative model, directly aligning feature generation with the downstream classification objective. This differs from prior generative ZSL methods that often optimize the generator and classifier separately.

The potential impacts of this work include:

1. **Improved ZSL Performance:** By demonstrating consistent state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks (CUB, SUN, AWA2) in both conventional and generalized ZSL settings, RLVC contributes to raising the performance ceiling for this field. The average gain of 4.7% reported over previous methods suggests a tangible improvement in the ability to recognize unseen classes.

2. **Enhanced Feature Discriminability:** The methodology, particularly the outcome-reward mechanism and visual cues, is shown to yield more discriminative and task-relevant synthesized features. This is critical for real-world applications where distinguishing between fine-grained, semantically similar categories is essential. The t-SNE visualizations provide qualitative evidence of improved class separability and more compact feature clusters.

3. **New Direction for Generative ZSL:** This research positions reinforcement learning as a viable and effective paradigm for optimizing generative models in ZSL. As an initial exploration, it opens avenues for future research into more sophisticated RL algorithms, reward designs, and policy optimization strategies tailored for visual synthesis tasks within resource-constrained learning scenarios. The success of RL in large language models suggests a broader potential for self-evolving models in vision.

4. **Robustness to Semantic Conditions:** The empirical validation across different types of semantic prototypes (word embeddings and attribute vectors) demonstrates the robustness of RLVC. This implies that the framework can effectively enhance performance even when semantic information is less rich or highly similar, making it more broadly applicable.

5. **Stable Training Mechanisms:** The proposed cold-start training strategy and alternating update mechanism offer practical solutions for stabilizing the integration of RL into generative adversarial frameworks. These contributions are relevant not only for ZSL but potentially for other generative tasks where RL is used for fine-tuning or aligning generation with specific objectives.

In conclusion, RLVC contributes to the ZSL landscape by introducing a principled way to guide generative models towards synthesizing features that are explicitly optimized for classification. This work provides a foundation for further exploring the intersection of reinforcement learning and generative models in computer vision, potentially influencing the development of more adaptive and task-aware feature synthesis techniques.
