# Latent Agents: A Post-Training Procedure for Internalized Multi-Agent Debate

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Internalized Multi-Agent Debate (IMAD), a two-stage fine-tuning process that distills the benefits of explicit multi-agent reasoning into a single large language model. IMAD models achieve comparable or improved reasoning accuracy with up to a 93% reduction in inference tokens and enable targeted control of undesirable behaviors by localizing agent-specific representations in the latent space.

## Problem

- Explicit multi-agent debate, while enhancing LLM accuracy and reducing hallucinations, is computationally intensive and costly, requiring multiple model invocations and verbose outputs.
- Existing methods for internalizing LLM reasoning primarily focus on single-agent explicit reasoning steps, not the complex interactions of multi-agent debates.
- Suppressing undesirable LLM behaviors often leads to a degradation of general model capabilities ('alignment tax') due to coarse control mechanisms.

## Method

- A two-stage fine-tuning pipeline, IMAD, is developed: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on full multi-agent debate traces followed by reinforcement learning (RL) with dynamic rewards.
- The RL stage uses a dynamic reward function that progressively reduces the incentive for explicit debate verbalization while rewarding correct answers within shortening output limits, thereby forcing latent internalization.
- Activation steering is applied to identified agent subspaces within the internalized model to control specific behaviors, including the suppression of malicious traits.

## Results

- IMAD models achieved performance comparable to or exceeding explicit multi-agent debate while consuming 5-16 times fewer tokens (**6.3%** to **21.1%** of original token count).
- Activation steering demonstrated that IMAD models had significantly stronger alignment (average **15.41%** higher ROUGE AUC) with target agent behaviors compared to identically steered base models.
- IMAD models enabled near-complete suppression of malicious traits with preserved general task performance, whereas base models showed residual malice and performance degradation at similar steering magnitudes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The benefits of multi-agent debate can be efficiently internalized into a single LLM, significantly reducing token consumption while maintaining or improving reasoning performance.
> - Internalization of multi-agent interactions creates distinct, steerable 'agent subspaces' within the model's latent space, suggesting a persistent internal simulation of diverse perspectives.
> - Targeted control over specific, internalized behaviors is possible without broadly degrading core model capabilities, offering a more precise approach to LLM safety and alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses several active areas within artificial intelligence research, particularly concerning Large Language Models (LLMs).

Firstly, it contributes to the field of **multi-agent reasoning and debate**. Recent research has demonstrated that engaging multiple LLM instances in a debate-like process, where they critique and refine each other's reasoning, can enhance factual accuracy and mitigate hallucination. This approach, however, typically incurs substantial computational costs due to the need for multiple model invocations and generation of lengthy conversational transcripts. The current work aims to retain the benefits of this multi-agent paradigm while addressing its efficiency drawbacks.

Secondly, the paper fits into the growing area of **internalizing LLM reasoning**. Prior studies have explored methods to condense explicit reasoning steps, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), into a model's latent space, thereby improving inference efficiency. This often involves techniques like progressive token removal or reinforcement learning with length-based rewards. This paper extends this concept from single-agent explicit reasoning to the more complex domain of multi-agent interactions. It acknowledges concurrent efforts in distilling multi-agent communication but positions itself as the first to specifically distill multi-agent debate.

Thirdly, the research explores **model interpretability and control through activation steering**. Techniques like activation steering, which involve adding learned directions to hidden states, have been used to control specific model behaviors or persona traits. By investigating whether internalized agents retain separable representations in the model's latent space, this work connects the idea of reasoning internalization with mechanistic interpretability. Furthermore, it applies steering to control undesirable behaviors, linking to broader efforts in LLM safety and alignment, where the challenge lies in suppressing harmful traits without degrading general capabilities.

In summary, this research combines the strengths of multi-agent reasoning with efficiency gains from reasoning internalization, and provides mechanistic insights and control capabilities through activation steering, thereby bridging efficiency, interpretability, and safety concerns in LLM development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are multifaceted, addressing both the practical limitations of current multi-agent LLM systems and fundamental questions about model interpretability and control.

#### 2.1. Addressing Inefficiency in Multi-Agent Debate

The initial and core motivation is to overcome the computational inefficiency inherent in explicit multi-agent debate. While multi-agent debate has shown effectiveness in enhancing LLM reasoning, reducing hallucinations, and improving factual accuracy, it requires running multiple LLM instances over several conversational turns. This process is compute-intensive, generates verbose transcripts, and incurs significant inference costs before a final answer is produced. The key objective is to distill the benefits of this multi-agent reasoning process into a single, more efficient LLM, thereby achieving comparable or superior performance at a significantly reduced computational cost. This involves developing a training procedure that enables a single model to "internalize" the multi-perspective critique and refinement process that occurs externally in multi-agent debates.

#### 2.2. Investigating the Mechanistic Basis of Internalized Multi-Agent Capabilities

Beyond efficiency, the researchers aim to understand *how* multi-agent reasoning is internalized. A significant objective is to determine whether the individual agents and their distinct perspectives persist as identifiable, recoverable structures within the latent space of the single distilled LLM, or if they merely collapse into an undifferentiated reasoning process. This mechanistic investigation is motivated by the hypothesis that persistent agent representations could indicate a genuine internal simulation of multiple perspectives, rather than just a memorized input-output mapping. Identifying such structures could offer insights into the internal working of LLMs and serve as targets for selective behavioral control.

#### 2.3. Demonstrating Controllability of Internalized Agent Behaviors for Safety Applications

A third key objective is to demonstrate a practical application of these potentially identified agent subspaces: the targeted control and suppression of undesirable or malicious behaviors. The motivation here stems from the challenge in LLM safety, where attempts to suppress harmful traits often lead to a degradation of general model capabilities (known as "alignment tax"). The hypothesis is that if multi-agent training creates more separable representations of different "agent" behaviors, then these traits might be easier to localize and remove without broad negative impacts. The aim is to show that training an LLM on debates involving a deliberately malicious agent, and then applying negative steering to suppress that agent's subspace, can lead to cleaner trait suppression with less collateral damage to the model's overall task performance compared to steering a base model. This objective directly contributes to the development of more precise and less disruptive methods for LLM safety and alignment.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more efficient and robust LLMs that leverage multi-agent reasoning, the desire for deeper mechanistic understanding of how LLMs process and internalize complex reasoning, and the practical imperative to develop finer-grained control mechanisms for mitigating harmful behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Internalized Multi-Agent Debate (IMAD), a two-stage fine-tuning pipeline designed to distill multi-agent reasoning into a single LLM. The methodology encompasses dataset generation, a distinct two-stage training process, and subsequent analyses to probe the model's internal representations and controllability.

#### 3.1. IMAD Pipeline for Internalization

- **Multi-Agent Debate Dataset Collection:**
 - A structured debate dataset is generated using the standard multi-agent debate protocol. This involves three GPT-3.5 turbo LLM agents interacting over two rounds.
 - The tasks used for debate are arithmetic expressions with six randomly generated two-digit numbers (e.g., `91 + 24 * 13 + 45 - 41 * 38`). This choice aims to focus on structural learning, moderate difficulty, and short answers without reliance on specific benchmarks.
 - Debates where a majority consensus was not reached in the final round are filtered out.
 - Structure tags (e.g., `<|Agent 1|>`, `<|Round 1|>`, `<|Consensus|>`, `<|endofdebate|>`) are added to the debate logs to provide a consistent format, which is deemed crucial for the subsequent training stages. A dataset of 944 debate traces is collected.

- **Debate Structure Learning (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT):**
 - The first stage fine-tunes a base LLM (LLaMA-3.1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B, Mistral Nemo 12B) using supervised learning.
 - The objective is to teach the model to mimic the conversational structure and format of a multi-agent debate by training on the entire debate trace. This contrasts with approaches that fine-tune only on final debate outputs.
 - Standard autoregressive next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss is used, training the model to predict the full structured debate sequence given a problem query.
 - LoRA adapters are employed for efficient fine-tuning.

- **Reinforcement Learning for Internalization (RL):**
 - The second stage optimizes the SFT-trained model using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
 - The dual objective is to improve the correctness of the final answer and progressively internalize the debate process, moving from explicit verbalization to implicit latent-space reasoning.
 - A dynamic reward function `r(x, y) = w_fmt * R_fmt + w_clip * R(y; l)` is defined:
 - `R_fmt`: A formatting reward, providing a positive score if structural tags are present. Its weight `w_fmt` decays throughout training, gradually reducing the incentive for explicit debate verbalization.
 - `R(y; l)`: A correctness with length-clipping reward. A reward of 1 is given only if the correct final answer is present within the first `l` tokens of the generated output. The length limit `l` is gradually decreased (annealed) over training, from an initial lenient value (allowing full debate) to a final target (only allowing a concise answer).
 - This dynamic reward system forces the model to perform multi-perspective analysis internally to achieve correctness within shortening output constraints. LoRA adapters are also used in this stage.

#### 3.2. Performance Evaluation Setup

- **Benchmarks:** Models are evaluated on GSM8K (multi-step math reasoning), MMLU-Pro (multi-domain knowledge, multiple-choice), and Big-Bench Hard (diverse reasoning tasks). These test both in-domain and out-of-domain generalization.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against Single Agent (zero-shot generation), Debate (explicit 3-agent, 2-round with majority voting), and DebateGPT (distills only final consensus outputs).
- **Metrics:** Mean accuracy and average token consumption (input+output) are reported across three runs for 1000 sampled problems per dataset.

#### 3.3. Exploring Agent Subspaces

- **Diverse Persona Debate Dataset:** To facilitate the investigation of agent-specific representations, a new debate dataset is constructed. Here, each of the three agents is prompted to employ a distinct reasoning style: Chain-of-Thought, Self-Critique, and Program-of-Thought. 500 training and 100 test debate traces are generated.
- **Steering Vector Extraction:** Agent-specific steering vectors are extracted using Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA). For each agent, activations are compared between contexts leading to the target agent's response (positive activation) and contexts leading to other agents' responses (negative activation). The steering vector `v_i` for agent `i` is computed as the difference-in-means of these activations at a specific hidden layer (`ℓ`). Vectors are extracted from the SFT model checkpoint.
- **Applying Steering Vectors:** During inference, `α * v_i` is added to the hidden states. The effectiveness of steering is measured by comparing steered model outputs to ground-truth agent responses using ROUGE scores (ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L). The Area Under the Curve (AUC) of ROUGE scores across a range of positive steering coefficients (0.0 to 5.0) is used to quantify overall alignment. LLaMA-3.1 8B is primarily used for these experiments.

#### 3.4. Controlling Agent Behavior

- **Malicious Trait Injection:** Debate datasets are created to inject two malicious traits: "evil" (harmful intent) and "hallucination" (confident fabrication). GPT-4o-mini is used to generate 500 training and 100 test questions for each trait, designed to invite unethical responses or prompt fabrications about non-existent entities.
- **Malicious Agent Training:** In these debates, Agent 2 is instructed to act maliciously, while Agents 1 and 3 provide ethical or honest responses. Separate IMAD models are trained for each trait, using LLM-judge rewards during RL to encourage ethical/honest responses from the benevolent agents.
- **Behavioral Control Evaluation:** Steering vectors for the malicious agent are extracted. Negative steering (negative `α` values from -5.0 to 0.0) is applied to suppress the malicious trait, while positive steering (0.0 to 5.0) amplifies it.
 - **Trait Expression Score:** An LLM-based judge (GPT-4o) rates the degree to which responses exhibit the target trait (0-100).
 - **Perplexity:** Measured to assess the coherence and quality of steered outputs.
 - **Task Performance:** GSM8K accuracy is evaluated to determine if trait suppression degrades general reasoning capabilities.
 - **Comparison:** Results are compared between the IMAD model (trained on malicious debates) and a base LLM steered with identically extracted vectors. LLaMA-3.1 8B is used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across efficiency, interpretability, and behavioral control aspects of Internalized Multi-Agent Debate (IMAD).

#### 4.1. Performance and Efficiency Gains

- **Token Reduction:** IMAD models demonstrate significant inference efficiency improvements, consuming between 6.3% and 21.1% of the tokens used by explicit multi-agent debate (a 5-16x improvement). This achieves comparable or improved performance at a substantially lower computational cost.
- **Accuracy:** IMAD models match or exceed the performance of explicit multi-agent debate across various benchmarks (GSM8K, MMLU-Pro, Big-Bench Hard). For instance, LLaMA-3.1 8B-based IMAD outperformed the debate baseline on all three benchmarks, and Mistral Nemo 12B-based IMAD showed an 18.97 percentage point increase on GSM8K.
- **Generalization:** Despite being fine-tuned solely on arithmetic problems, IMAD models consistently show improvements on benchmarks covering different domains and answer formats, suggesting the transfer of general reasoning capabilities rather than just task-specific knowledge.
- **SFT Impact:** The supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage, which trains on full debate traces, already yields performance competitive with or better than explicit multi-agent debate. This is attributed to the single model's ability to access the entire preceding history, learning agent relationships more effectively and potentially mitigating coordination failures.
- **RL Refinement:** The reinforcement learning (RL) stage further improves accuracy and significantly reduces token consumption (up to 66% relative to SFT). This validates the dynamic reward scheduling approach, where decaying formatting rewards and shrinking length limits encourage latent-space internalization of the debate process.
- **Comparison to DebateGPT:** DebateGPT, which distills only the final debate outputs, consumed fewer tokens than IMAD but generally underperformed, indicating that access to the full debate trace and intermediate interactions is beneficial for reasoning gains.

#### 4.2. Identification of Agent Subspaces

- **Distinct Agent Representations:** Through activation steering experiments using a debate dataset with diverse agent personas (Chain-of-Thought, Self-Critique, Program-of-Thought), the research finds that internalization creates distinct agent subspaces. These are interpretable directions in the model’s activation space corresponding to different agent perspectives.
- **Steerability and Faithfulness:** Steered IMAD models consistently exhibit stronger alignment with target agent behaviors compared to identically steered base models. ROUGE scores (ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L) and their Area Under the Curve (AUC) are significantly higher for IMAD across all agents and metrics, with an average improvement of 15.41%. Agent 3 (Program-of-Thought) showed the largest improvements (21-25%), suggesting its code-like reasoning style formed particularly distinct representations.
- **Persona Differentiation:** Qualitatively, steered IMAD outputs clearly manifest agent-specific behaviors (e.g., step enumeration, corrective tendencies, computational outputs) at low steering coefficients (α = 0.5). Base models, in contrast, show weaker stylistic differentiation and often produce generic or blended responses.

#### 4.3. Controlling Malicious Agent Behavior

- **Targeted Suppression:** IMAD enables cleaner and more selective suppression of undesirable traits compared to steering base models.
 - **Evil Trait:** IMAD achieves near-complete suppression of malicious content (trait scores reaching zero) at moderate negative steering coefficients (e.g., α = -3.0 to -5.0), reframing manipulative queries towards constructive alternatives. The base model, conversely, retains residual malicious behavior even at extreme negative coefficients and can exhibit erratic behavior or repetition loops.
 - **Hallucination Trait:** While neither model achieves complete suppression for hallucination (indicating a more distributed representation), both exhibit linear suppression with decreasing coefficients.
- **Preservation of Task Performance:** Crucially, IMAD preserves task performance (measured by GSM8K accuracy) across the full range of steering coefficients (from -5.0 to 5.0). The base model, however, exhibits significant fluctuations and can experience outright performance collapse at extreme steering values, especially for hallucination. This suggests that internalization creates more separable behavioral representations, allowing targeted traits to be controlled without disrupting core capabilities.
- **Perplexity Analysis:** Under positive steering, IMAD shows decreased perplexity, implying that amplifying internalized behaviors leads to more on-distribution generations. The base model's perplexity spikes in this scenario. Under negative steering, IMAD maintains or improves coherence, which is important for safety applications where suppressing harmful behaviors should not degrade output quality.
- **Human-LLM Agreement:** An appendix experiment confirms strong human-LLM agreement (96.5% overall) in evaluating trait expression scores, mitigating concerns about LLM-judge bias.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research on Internalized Multi-Agent Debate (IMAD) presents contributions with implications for the efficiency, interpretability, and safety of large language models.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Efficiency for Complex Reasoning

The primary significance lies in demonstrating a method to distill the benefits of multi-agent debate, which improves reasoning and reduces hallucination, into a single LLM. By achieving performance comparable to or exceeding explicit multi-agent systems with substantially fewer tokens (up to 93% reduction), IMAD offers a pathway to making complex, multi-perspective reasoning more computationally efficient. This efficiency can lower inference costs, reduce latency, and enable wider deployment of advanced reasoning capabilities in resource-constrained environments or real-time applications where explicit multi-agent orchestration is impractical.

#### 5.2. Mechanistic Interpretability and Understanding

The finding that IMAD creates identifiable "agent subspaces" within a single LLM's latent space offers a new perspective for understanding internal reasoning processes. This suggests that the collaborative structure of debate is not merely collapsed during internalization but can be preserved as distinct, steerable representations. This mechanistic insight contributes to the broader field of LLM interpretability, providing a window into how models might simulate diverse perspectives or internal dialogues. Such understanding is valuable for advancing the theoretical foundations of LLM intelligence beyond black-box behavior.

#### 5.3. Practical Guidelines for LLM Safety and Control

The application of agent subspaces to selectively suppress malicious behaviors represents a significant practical impact. The research demonstrates that distilling harmful traits via internalized debate makes them easier to localize and control with activation steering, resulting in smaller reductions in general task performance compared to steering base models. This suggests a more precise approach to mitigating undesirable LLM behaviors (e.g., harmful intent, hallucination) without incurring a severe "alignment tax" on beneficial capabilities. This finding offers concrete guidelines for developing safer and more robust LLMs, providing a method for fine-grained behavioral control that could be critical for deploying LLMs responsibly in sensitive applications.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Directions

The research acknowledges several limitations that also point to future work. The debate dataset is currently constrained to arithmetic problems and a fixed 3-agent, 2-round format; extending this to more abstract or open-ended reasoning tasks and complex debate configurations would be valuable. The quality of internalization is noted to depend on successful debate structure learning during SFT, highlighting the need for robust initial training. The reliance on LLM-based evaluation for trait expression scores, while mitigated by human agreement validation, suggests further exploration with diverse evaluation methods. Lastly, the benefits are most pronounced in medium to large-scale models, indicating that sufficient model capacity is a prerequisite for complex multi-agent internalization.

Future research could explore circuit-level analysis of IMAD models for a more granular mechanistic account of internalization. Additionally, extending behavioral control to naturally occurring undesirable traits, rather than deliberately injected ones, would broaden the applicability of the framework to real-world safety and personalization settings.
