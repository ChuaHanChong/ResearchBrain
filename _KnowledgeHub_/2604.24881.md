# Latent Agents: A Post-Training Procedure for Internalized Multi-Agent Debate

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Internalized Multi-Agent Debate (IMAD), a two-stage fine-tuning process that distills the benefits of explicit multi-agent reasoning into a single large language model. IMAD models achieve comparable or improved reasoning accuracy with up to a 93% reduction in inference tokens and enable targeted control of undesirable behaviors by localizing agent-specific representations in the latent space.

## Problem

- Explicit multi-agent debate, while enhancing LLM accuracy and reducing hallucinations, is computationally intensive and costly, requiring multiple model invocations and verbose outputs.
- Existing methods for internalizing LLM reasoning primarily focus on single-agent explicit reasoning steps, not the complex interactions of multi-agent debates.
- Suppressing undesirable LLM behaviors often leads to a degradation of general model capabilities ('alignment tax') due to coarse control mechanisms.

## Method

- A two-stage fine-tuning pipeline, IMAD, is developed: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on full multi-agent debate traces followed by reinforcement learning (RL) with dynamic rewards.
- The RL stage uses a dynamic reward function that progressively reduces the incentive for explicit debate verbalization while rewarding correct answers within shortening output limits, thereby forcing latent internalization.
- Activation steering is applied to identified agent subspaces within the internalized model to control specific behaviors, including the suppression of malicious traits.

## Results

- IMAD models achieved performance comparable to or exceeding explicit multi-agent debate while consuming 5-16 times fewer tokens (**6.3%** to **21.1%** of original token count).
- Activation steering demonstrated that IMAD models had significantly stronger alignment (average **15.41%** higher ROUGE AUC) with target agent behaviors compared to identically steered base models.
- IMAD models enabled near-complete suppression of malicious traits with preserved general task performance, whereas base models showed residual malice and performance degradation at similar steering magnitudes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The benefits of multi-agent debate can be efficiently internalized into a single LLM, significantly reducing token consumption while maintaining or improving reasoning performance.
> - Internalization of multi-agent interactions creates distinct, steerable 'agent subspaces' within the model's latent space, suggesting a persistent internal simulation of diverse perspectives.
> - Targeted control over specific, internalized behaviors is possible without broadly degrading core model capabilities, offering a more precise approach to LLM safety and alignment.
