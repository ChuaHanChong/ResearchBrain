# HiVLA: A Visual-Grounded-Centric Hierarchical Embodied Manipulation System

> [!summary] Summary
> HiVLA introduces a hierarchical embodied manipulation system that decouples high-level VLM planning from low-level motor control using visual-grounded plans, achieving an 83.3% average success rate across 9 manipulation tasks in simulation and demonstrating robust real-world generalization while preserving VLM reasoning capabilities.

## Problem

- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often suffer from catastrophic forgetting, degrading a VLM's broad reasoning when fine-tuned on limited robotic manipulation data.
- Existing visual-grounded-centric VLA designs struggle to effectively translate grounded information into physical actions, often losing either absolute spatial context or fine-grained visual details.
- Low-level action policies lack a robust mechanism to fully leverage comprehensive grounded plans that combine high-resolution local appearance, precise global spatial awareness, and explicit skill-level subtask directives.

## Method

- **HiVLA** proposes a ==hierarchical system== with a ==High-Level VLM Planner Agent== for semantic planning and a ==DiT Action Expert== for low-level motor control, operating ==asynchronously==.
- The VLM Planner generates ==structured JSON plans==, including semantic subtask descriptions and high-resolution ==bounding boxes==, which are then used to extract ==object-centric image crops==.
- The DiT Action Expert uses a ==conditional Diffusion Transformer== with a ==cascaded cross-attention== mechanism to integrate global visual context, ==position-aware local features== from high-resolution crops, and subtask language guidance to generate robot actions.

## Results

- Achieved an **83.3%** total average success rate across **9 manipulation tasks** in RoboTwin simulation, representing a **17.7%** improvement over the H-RDT baseline and **42.7%** over π0.
- Demonstrated an emergent error-correction property where the VLM Planner, acting as a semantic supervisor, re-issues commands for failed executions, enhancing system resilience.
- Successfully generalized to complex, cluttered real-world robotic environments where baselines often failed, owing to its ability to map limited real-world data to precise local visual-language conditions for accurate sub-skill execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling high-level VLM planning from low-level execution effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting, allowing VLMs to retain their broad semantic understanding and zero-shot generalization.
> - A comprehensive visual-grounded plan, combining high-resolution local image crops with absolute positional embeddings and semantic subtask language, is critical for bridging abstract VLM reasoning and precise physical action.
> - The 'Coarse-to-Fine' guidance injection strategy within the DiT Action Expert (Global Context → Local Crop → Language Skill) is optimal for progressively narrowing attention and ensuring precise action generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic systems capable of human-like perception, reasoning, and execution is a central objective in embodied artificial intelligence. The emergence of web-scale, pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has provided new opportunities for robotic manipulation, leading to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

Current VLA research largely employs end-to-end architectures, categorized into single-system (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) or dual-system (e.g., π0, GR00T-N1.5) approaches. These integrated paradigms tightly couple visual reasoning with low-level action generation. However, a significant limitation of these approaches is that fine-tuning VLMs on relatively sparse, domain-specific manipulation data often leads to "catastrophic forgetting," compromising their original, broad reasoning capabilities inherited from web-scale pre-training. This degradation limits the extent to which advanced VLM cognitive power can be leveraged for robotic tasks.

Hierarchical systems offer an alternative by explicitly decoupling high-level semantic planning from low-level motor control. In this paradigm, a VLM can function purely as a high-level planner, preserving its reasoning abilities, while a specialized action expert handles execution. The success of such decoupled designs, however, depends on the efficacy of the intermediate representation bridging the two modules. Visual grounding, inspired by the "thinking with images" approach in VLM agents, is considered a suitable candidate for this interface, as it involves explicitly localizing relevant target regions in high-resolution images.

Despite the conceptual advantages of visual-grounded VLA systems, existing designs often face challenges in effectively translating grounded information into physical actions. Current methods frequently involve a compromise between retaining global spatial context and preserving high-resolution visual fidelity. For instance, extracting local image crops can inadvertently remove absolute spatial coordinate information, while applying object masks to down-sampled global images can discard the detailed visual cues necessary for fine-grained manipulation. Previous explicit hierarchical grounding methods, such as DexGraspVLA and RoboGround, have also exhibited limitations, including reliance on external expert models for segmentation masks, use of less performant transformer policies, or loss of detail due to down-sampling.

HiVLA positions itself within this landscape by addressing the aforementioned trade-offs and limitations. It proposes a hierarchical framework centered on robust visual-grounded plan generation and utilization. By explicitly decoupling planning from control and introducing a novel mechanism for processing grounded visual information, HiVLA aims to preserve VLM reasoning capabilities while enabling precise and robust low-level action execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind HiVLA is to resolve a fundamental trade-off observed in current end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This trade-off involves the degradation of Vision-Language Model (VLM) reasoning capabilities when these models are fine-tuned on limited, domain-specific robotic control data, a phenomenon commonly referred to as catastrophic forgetting. The researchers identified that this degradation prevents VLA systems from fully leveraging the sophisticated cognitive power of advanced VLMs.

To address this, HiVLA's key objectives are:

1. **Decouple High-Level Planning from Low-Level Control**: The core objective is to establish a hierarchical framework that explicitly separates the semantic reasoning and task decomposition functions from the precise motor control aspects of robotic manipulation. This decoupling aims to allow the VLM to operate primarily as a high-level planner, thereby preserving its inherent zero-shot generalization and deep semantic understanding.

2. **Facilitate Effective Visual Grounding for Action Generation**: A critical challenge is to design an intermediate representation that can effectively bridge the VLM planner and the action execution module. The motivation is to leverage visual grounding—explicit localization of target objects—as this interface. However, existing methods struggle to fully exploit grounded information, often compromising between global spatial context and high-resolution local appearance. HiVLA aims to develop a policy capable of leveraging a grounded plan that includes high-resolution local appearance, precise global spatial awareness, and explicit skill-level subtask directives without such compromises.

3. **Develop a Robust Action Expert**: The system needs a dedicated low-level action expert capable of translating the visually-grounded plans into accurate physical actions. This expert must be designed to effectively integrate global scene context, fine-grained object-centric details, and specific subtask instructions to ensure robust and precise manipulation, especially in complex and cluttered environments.

4. **Enhance Performance in Complex Manipulation Tasks**: The motivation extends to significantly improving performance in challenging scenarios, such as long-horizon skill composition and fine-grained manipulation of small objects in cluttered scenes, where current end-to-end and existing hierarchical VLA models tend to underperform.

5. **Enable Independent Improvement of Components**: By decoupling the VLM planner and the action expert, the system aims to allow for independent development, optimization, and scaling of each component, fostering greater flexibility and extensibility.

Overall, the research is motivated by the goal of building more robust, generalizable, and scalable robotic manipulation systems that can effectively integrate the advanced cognitive capabilities of VLMs with precise physical execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HiVLA proposes a visual-grounded-centric hierarchical manipulation system that explicitly decouples high-level semantic planning from low-level motor control. The framework comprises two main components: a High-Level VLM Planner Agent and a DiT Action Expert.

**High-Level VLM Planner Agent:**
This module acts as the cognitive core, interpreting high-level language instructions (`L`) in the context of current visual observations (`O_t`) to determine the next action. At each decision step, the VLM Planner is provided with the overall goal, the robot's gripper status, the previously executed subtask, and a visual history (scene before and after the last action). Based on these multi-modal inputs, the VLM reasons about task progress and decomposes complex instructions into a sequence of subtasks.

The output of the VLM Planner is a structured plan, presented as a JSON object. This plan includes:
- A semantic subtask description (`L_sub,t`), indicating the required skill (e.g., ‘pick’, ‘place’) and the target object's name.
- An action type and target object name.
- A normalized bounding box (`B_t = [ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]`) localizing the target object within the current high-resolution scene image (1920x1080).

This design allows the VLM to leverage its pre-trained reasoning capabilities without direct fine-tuning on low-level control data, thereby avoiding catastrophic forgetting. The bounding box `B_t` acts as a directive for an "Image Crop tool" to extract a high-resolution, object-centric patch (`I_local,t`) from the original camera observation. This structured plan, including `L_sub,t` and `I_local,t`, is then passed to the DiT Action Expert as conditional guidance.

**DiT Action Expert:**
This module serves as the manipulation-focused execution unit, translating the high-level plans into precise low-level motor commands. It is based on a conditional Diffusion Transformer (DiT), designed to model the conditional probability distribution `p(A_t | S_0:t, L_sub,t, B_t)`. The architecture utilizes a LLaMA-style transformer backbone with RMSNorm and SwiGLU activation functions.

1. **Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) for Action Generation**: The DiT Action Expert employs Conditional Flow Matching to learn a deterministic mapping from a simple noise distribution to the complex data distribution of action sequences.
 - A linear interpolation path `x_τ = τ A_t + (1 - τ)z` is defined, where `A_t` is the ground truth action sequence, `z` is a sample from a standard Gaussian prior, and `τ ∈` is a continuous time variable.
 - The neural network `v_θ` (the DiT architecture) is trained to predict the vector field `u = A_t - z` that defines the "flow" from noise to data, minimizing the L2 distance between its prediction and the target vector field.
 - During inference, action sequences are generated by solving an ordinary differential equation (ODE) defined by `dx/dτ = v_θ(x_τ, τ, C_t)`, starting from an initial noise sample.

2. **Hierarchical Transformer Architecture (Cascaded Cross-Attention Mechanism)**: The core innovation of the action expert lies in its cascaded cross-attention mechanism within each DiT block. This mechanism sequentially integrates three distinct forms of guidance:
 - **Global Visual Context**: Multi-view visual inputs (`O_t`) are processed by a pre-trained vision encoder (DINOv2 + SigLIP) to generate global feature tokens (`C_global`). A first cross-attention layer allows the state-action tokens to attend to `C_global`, providing a holistic understanding of the scene and enabling coarse-grained reasoning.
 - **Position-Aware Local Features**: A high-resolution local image patch (`I_local,t`), cropped using the VLM's bounding box `B_t` from the original 1920x1080 image, is processed by the same vision encoder to obtain local feature tokens (`C_local`). Crucially, absolute sinusoidal positional embeddings (`PE(p)`) are added to these local tokens based on their central coordinates `p` within the original camera frame. This `C_local-pos` is then fed into a second specialized cross-attention layer, providing a magnified, detailed view of the target object with explicit global spatial awareness.
 - **Subtask Language Guidance**: The subtask description (`L_sub,t`) from the VLM's plan is encoded into language embeddings (`C_lang`). A third cross-attention layer integrates these embeddings, conditioning the generated motion on the precise semantics of the required skill (e.g., ‘pick’, ‘place’).

After processing through all transformer blocks, an MLP-based Action Decoder maps the hidden states back to the robot's action space. This cascaded conditioning strategy allows the DiT Action Expert to leverage every component of the VLM’s high-level reasoning, grounding abstract plans into precise physical execution.

**Asynchronous Inference**: The system addresses the frequency mismatch between the slow VLM Planner (1.9s per step) and the fast DiT Action Policy (0.162s for a 16-step action chunk) by executing them in parallel. This approach, combined with temporal consistency mechanisms, achieves an effective control frequency of 8Hz for real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of HiVLA's visual-grounded hierarchical design was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world robotic manipulation settings.

**VLM Planner Performance:**
- A specialized 210K-instance dialogue dataset, HiVLA-HD, was curated and used to fine-tune the Qwen3-VL 8B model as the VLM Planner.
- The fine-tuned Qwen3-VL 8B model achieved a bounding box grounding accuracy (mIoU) of 90.37% and a strict exact-match subtask prediction accuracy of 98.57%. This significantly outperformed zero-shot performance of various VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B zero-shot: 12.68% mIoU, 35.71% subtask accuracy; GPT-4o zero-shot: 3.45% mIoU, 42.85% subtask accuracy).
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of visual history, as removing historical frames led to a drop in subtask accuracy from 98.57% to 95.24%.

**RoboTwin Simulation Evaluation:**
- Experiments were conducted on the RoboTwin2.0 platform under a ‘domain randomization’ setting, using the HiVLA-HD dataset.
- HiVLA achieved a total average success rate of 83.3% across 9 diverse tasks (4 easy, 5 hard), demonstrating superior performance over state-of-the-art baselines.
 - It outperformed H-RDT by 12.7% (70.6% success rate) and π0 by 37.7% (45.6% success rate).
- **Easy Tasks**: HiVLA achieved an average success rate of 96.0%, showing benefits for tasks involving small targets due to the use of high-resolution, object-centric crops that preserve critical visual features.
- **Hard Tasks**: The performance gap was more pronounced, with HiVLA achieving a 73.2% average success rate—an 18.6% absolute improvement over H-RDT (54.6%). This highlights HiVLA's effectiveness in long-horizon skill composition and complex spatial reasoning (e.g., 'Stack 3 Blocks', 'Click 3 Bells').
- **Skill Decomposition and Error Correction**: An ablation (Ours w/o Skill) where specific sub-task skills were replaced with the global instruction on Hard tasks resulted in an 8.8% performance drop (73.2% to 64.4%). This indicated that decomposed, "one-to-one" language conditions reduce the cognitive load on the diffusion policy. The VLM Planner also exhibited an emergent error-correction property, re-issuing commands for incomplete subtasks, a capability absent in coupled VLA models.
- **Robustness to Planner Errors**:
 - The Action Expert demonstrated resilience to spatial noise, maintaining a 57.0% success rate even with 100% shifting in the target bounding box, leveraging global image features for self-correction.
 - However, performance degraded proportionally with noise injected into language instructions, confirming the policy's strict semantic compliance.

**Real-World Evaluation:**
- HiVLA was deployed on an Aloha-Agilex-1.0 bimanual robot for 7 object categories and 16 distinct sub-type scenarios designed to test strong cross-environmental generalization and precise instruction following in cluttered multi-object scenes.
- HiVLA significantly outperformed H-RDT in these challenging real-world tasks. For instance, in multi-object cluttered scenarios like 'Pick & Place 3 Cups' and 'Pick & Place 3 Blocks', H-RDT's success rate dropped to 0/30, whereas HiVLA achieved 6/30 and 7/30 respectively.
- This demonstrated HiVLA's ability to navigate complex scenes and execute sub-skills accurately by effectively mapping limited real-world data to precise local visual-language conditions, contrasting with H-RDT's struggle due to its reliance on global visual features.

**Ablation Study on DiT Action Expert:**
- **Guidance Injection Strategy**: The optimal "Coarse-to-Fine" injection strategy (Global Context → Local Crop → Language Skill) yielded an 83.3% average success rate. Other orders, such as Local→Text (70.4%), were less effective, confirming the benefit of progressively narrowing attention.
- **Visual-Grounding Components**:
 - Cropping from low-resolution images (640x360 instead of 1080p) significantly degraded performance, especially on fine-grained tasks (e.g., 'Lift Pot'), indicating the importance of high-definition crops.
 - Removing absolute sinusoidal positional encoding for cropped image tokens (w/o Abs. PE) reduced accuracy (76.8% vs. 83.3%), proving its indispensability for disambiguating identical objects and providing explicit spatial guidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HiVLA presents a hierarchical visual-grounded-centric manipulation system that addresses a key challenge in embodied artificial intelligence: the trade-off between leveraging the broad reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and achieving precise, low-level robotic control. By explicitly decoupling high-level semantic planning from low-level motor control, the system successfully mitigates catastrophic forgetting, a common issue in end-to-end VLA models where fine-tuning on limited robotic data degrades VLM's inherent reasoning.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Preservation of VLM Reasoning**: The decoupled architecture allows VLMs to function purely as high-level planners, preserving their zero-shot generalization and deep semantic understanding. This ensures that the system can leverage the full cognitive power of state-of-the-art foundation models without compromising their pre-trained knowledge.
2. **Enhanced Manipulation Performance**: HiVLA demonstrates significant performance improvements over existing state-of-the-art end-to-end and global-vision-based hierarchical baselines in both simulation and real-world environments. Its particular strength in long-horizon skill composition and fine-grained manipulation of small objects in cluttered scenes showcases its ability to handle complex and challenging robotic tasks.
3. **Robustness and Interpretability**: The system exhibits resilience to spatial grounding errors while strictly adhering to semantic commands, indicating a balanced and robust control policy. Furthermore, by generating explicit intermediate plans (subtask instructions and bounding boxes), HiVLA offers greater interpretability into the system's decision-making process, which is valuable for debugging and understanding agent behavior.
4. **Modular and Scalable Architecture**: The hierarchical design enables independent development and improvement of both the VLM planner and the DiT action expert. This modularity facilitates future advancements, allowing the system to seamlessly integrate more powerful VLMs or more sophisticated action control policies as they emerge.
5. **Effective Visual Grounding**: The novel cascaded cross-attention mechanism within the DiT action expert effectively integrates global context, position-aware high-resolution local crops, and subtask language guidance. This mechanism provides a robust solution to the long-standing challenge of translating grounded visual information into precise physical actions, overcoming limitations of previous approaches that often compromised between spatial context and visual fidelity.
6. **Practical Real-World Applicability**: Despite the inherent latency differences between VLM reasoning and high-frequency motor control, HiVLA's asynchronous inference strategy achieves a practical control frequency for real-world deployment. Its demonstrated robust performance in complex real-world cluttered scenarios further underlines its potential for practical applications beyond controlled laboratory settings.

In conclusion, HiVLA establishes a robust and scalable foundation for advancing complex robotic manipulation systems. By effectively integrating high-level cognitive reasoning with precise physical execution through a visual-grounded-centric hierarchical framework, this work contributes to the development of more capable, general-purpose, and interpretable embodied AI agents.
