# MARCH: Model-Assisted Reinforcement Learning for the Perceptive Control of Humanoids over Sparse Footholds

> [!summary] Summary
> MARCH introduces a model-assisted reinforcement learning framework for perceptive humanoid control, enabling robust bipedal locomotion over sparse footholds with lateral constraints through vision-based policies. The method achieves improved sample efficiency and smoother movements in simulation, successfully transferring to a Unitree G1 robot for real-world navigation of stepping stones.

## Problem

- Model-based bipedal locomotion methods are often brittle in real-world scenarios due to reliance on precise system dynamics and complete environmental knowledge, especially with high-dimensional visual input.
- Model-free reinforcement learning methods struggle to discover the precise, constrained motions required for safety-critical tasks like navigating sparse footholds, often demanding extensive computational resources and complex training curricula.
- Previous model-assisted RL (CLF-RL) approaches had not adequately addressed the challenge of simultaneous forward and lateral foothold constraints, which are common in complex stepping-stone tasks.

## Method

- The **MARCH** framework generates safe ==reference trajectories== using simplified dynamics (single integrator for foot, HLIP for CoM) to define desirable robot motions.
- A privileged teacher policy is trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), employing Control Lyapunov Function (CLF)-inspired rewards to guide learning towards safe reference tracking and convergence.
- A vision-based student policy is distilled from the teacher, utilizing a CNN for depth image processing, a transformer for multi-modal fusion, and a Mixture Density Network (MDN) output to enable perceptive control on hardware.

## Results

- Model-assisted training achieved comparable performance to model-free baselines with approximately **50%** fewer teacher training episodes (10,000 vs. 20,000), demonstrating improved sample efficiency.
- The learned policy exhibited at least **12%** less mean absolute joint torque and **39%** less mean absolute angular jerk compared to the model-free baseline, indicating smoother and more efficient locomotion.
- The distilled student policy successfully enabled a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to navigate a sequence of physical stepping stones with both forward and lateral constraints using only onboard depth camera and proprioception.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating simplified dynamic models to generate safe reference trajectories and using CLF-inspired reward shaping significantly improves RL sample efficiency and locomotion quality for safety-critical tasks.
> - A specialized student policy architecture featuring a CNN for depth perception, a transformer for multi-modal fusion, and a Mixture Density Network (MDN) output enables robust, vision-based control capable of disambiguating action choices in complex terrains.
> - Domain randomization during simulation training, combined with a model-assisted approach, facilitates effective sim-to-real transfer for complex bipedal locomotion policies on humanoid hardware.
