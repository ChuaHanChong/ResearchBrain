# Learning Reactive Dexterous Grasping via Hierarchical Task-Space RL Planning and Joint-Space QP Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical control framework for reactive dexterous grasping combines a multi-agent Reinforcement Learning planner with a GPU-parallelized Quadratic Programming controller, achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and improved training efficiency. The system demonstrated an 81.4% success rate for a 5F hand in simulation and successfully grasped 22 out of 26 unseen objects on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Monolithic Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies for dexterous grasping are burdened with simultaneously learning high-level task reasoning, low-level kinematic coordination, and safety, leading to slow training, poor scalability, and unpredictable behavior.
- Traditional model-based approaches and existing RL methods often lack robust reactivity to dynamic disturbances and provide limited explicit safety guarantees for multi-DoF hands in unstructured environments.
- Learned RL policies typically exhibit limited steerability or adaptability to unforeseen environmental changes without extensive retraining, hindering their practical deployment.

## Method

- A hybrid hierarchical control framework is proposed, explicitly decoupling high-level task-space planning via ==multi-agent Reinforcement Learning== from low-level joint-space execution via a physics-based ==Quadratic Programming== (**QP**) controller.
- A multi-agent **RL** planner, with specialized arm and hand agents trained using ==centralized training with decentralized execution== (**CTDE**), generates desired task-space velocity commands for strategic manipulation.
- A GPU-parallelized **QP** controller translates these high-level commands into safe and feasible joint velocity commands, strictly enforcing collision avoidance, joint position, and velocity limits.

## Results

- The framework achieved an **81.4%** average success rate for the 20-DoF five-finger hand in simulation, significantly outperforming end-to-end RL baselines that reached only **13.2%** success.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, successfully grasping and lifting 22 out of 26 diverse, unseen everyday objects on physical hardware without fine-tuning.
- Exhibited reactive recovery capabilities on hardware, autonomously adjusting trajectories and re-grasping objects after external physical disturbances.
- The low-level QP controller effectively enforced physical constraints, ensuring the RL policy operated within kinematically feasible and safe regions, with tracking errors primarily arising from the QP clamping infeasible desired velocities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the control problem into hierarchical task-space planning (RL) and joint-space execution (QP) significantly reduces the learning burden on the RL agent, improving training efficiency and performance for high-DoF dexterous manipulation.
> - Integrating a physics-based QP controller provides explicit and guaranteed safety enforcement against collisions and kinematic limits, enhancing the predictability and reliability of robotic systems in real-world scenarios.
> - The modular architecture enables "zero-shot steerability," allowing dynamic, post-training adjustments to operational parameters (e.g., obstacle avoidance, speed-safety trade-offs) without requiring costly policy retraining.
