# Rethinking Entropy Regularization in Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces SIREN (SelectIve entRopy rEgularizatioN), a method designed to mitigate entropy collapse in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for large reasoning models. It significantly improved mathematical reasoning abilities, achieving an average maj@k of 54.6 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B across five benchmarks and promoting stable, effective exploration.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) suffers from entropy collapse and premature convergence, limiting exploration and performance.
- Naive entropy regularization fails in LRMs due to their vast action space (diffusing probability mass) and long trajectories (propagating uncertainty, leading to global entropy explosion).
- Traditional regularization exhibits high hyperparameter sensitivity, offering minimal gains with small coefficients or rapid, uncontrollable entropy explosions with large ones.

## Method

- Proposed ==SIREN (SelectIve entRopy rEgularizatioN)==, built upon Dr.GRPO, to selectively apply and stabilize entropy regularization in LRMs.
- Implemented a two-step entropy masking mechanism: a ==top-p mask== within a token (action space level) to focus on meaningful tokens, and a ==peak-entropy mask== for a trajectory (trajectory level) to target logically critical positions.
- Introduced ==Self-Anchored Regularization==, transforming the objective to maintain entropy at an appropriate, stable level by using an 'entropy anchor' initialized from the pre-trained policy, rather than simply maximizing it.

## Results

- SIREN achieved an average maj@k of **54.6** and avg@k of **46.1** on Qwen2.5-Math-7B across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including a **+6.6** maj@k improvement on AIME24/25 over baselines.
- The method demonstrated generalizability, consistently outperforming baselines on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and LLaMa3.1-8B models, while increasing perplexity in generated responses, indicating greater diversity.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three components (top-p mask, peak-entropy mask, and self-anchored regularization) are crucial, with self-anchored regularization being particularly critical for stabilizing training and preventing excessive entropy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive entropy regularization is ineffective for LRMs because their vast action space and long autoregressive trajectories lead to either diluted, uninformative entropy or an explosive, incoherent state.
> - Effective exploration in LRMs requires targeted regularization, focusing entropy control on semantically meaningful tokens and logically critical positions within reasoning trajectories.
> - Stabilizing the regularization objective by anchoring the target entropy to the model's initial state prevents extreme fluctuations, ensuring both sufficient exploration and robust training without premature convergence or uncontrolled entropy explosion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically focusing on enhancing the **reasoning abilities of LLMs** through **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. RLVR is a relatively new and promising paradigm that replaces the traditional learned reward model (as in RLHF) with an automated verification function that directly checks the correctness of a model's output against a ground truth. This approach has gained significant traction in domains where correctness can be objectively verified, such as mathematical problem-solving and code generation, exemplified by projects like DeepSeek-R1 (2025) and efforts by Kimi Team (2025) and OpenAI (Jaech et al., 2024).

However, the paper identifies a critical practical limitation of current RLVR methods for LRMs: **entropy collapse and premature convergence**. This issue causes the policy to become overly deterministic early in training, leading to a lack of exploration and a degradation in the diversity and quality of generated responses, ultimately hindering performance and training efficiency.

In traditional RL, entropy regularization is a standard technique to encourage exploration and prevent premature convergence. The paper highlights that **naive application of entropy regularization fails in the context of LRMs**. The reasons for this failure, as articulated in the paper, are the *vast action space* (hundreds of thousands of tokens) and *long trajectories* (thousands of steps) inherent to LRMs. These characteristics, distinct from traditional RL tasks with smaller, continuous action spaces and shorter trajectories, lead to either minimal gains with small regularization coefficients or rapid "global entropy explosion" with large coefficients. An entropy explosion means the model indiscriminately explores all possible actions and states, diffusing probability mass across many low-utility tokens and propagating uncertainty through autoregressive generation, leading to incoherent outputs.

The paper distinguishes itself from existing works that also leverage entropy in RLVR:
- Some approaches (e.g., Cui et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025) investigate the correlation between entropy and performance, using entropy indirectly as a heuristic signal for advantage shaping, rollout enhancement, or loss masking.
- In contrast, this work directly addresses the fundamental failure of naive entropy regularization and proposes **SIREN (SelectIve entRopy rEgularizatioN)** as a more explicit and effective mechanism to control entropy. SIREN's novelty lies in its targeted approach, confining exploration to a meaningful subset of actions and states, rather than treating entropy as a general, undifferentiated signal across the entire model's output space.

By providing a principled and empirically validated solution to a recognized challenge in RLVR for LRMs, this work contributes to the advancement of more robust and effective fine-tuning strategies for complex reasoning tasks, paving the way for more capable and reliable AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a critical practical issue observed in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) applied to Large Reasoning Models (LRMs): **entropy collapse and premature convergence**. Despite the promise of RLVR in enhancing reasoning abilities, this problem causes the model's policy to become overly deterministic too early in training. This results in the generation of nearly identical responses, severely limiting exploration, reducing training efficiency, and ultimately capping overall performance.

The authors identify that conventional solutions, specifically **naive entropy regularization**, which is a staple for encouraging exploration in traditional RL, fail to adequately address this issue in the LRM context. Their preliminary analysis reveals the root causes of this failure:
1. **Vast Action Space:** LRMs operate with an extremely large vocabulary (action space). Naive regularization attempts to flatten the probability distribution across this entire vocabulary, causing probability mass to diffuse into many semantically meaningless tokens. This yields disproportionately large, yet uninformative, entropy gains, biasing the model towards incoherent exploration.
2. **Long Trajectories:** The autoregressive nature of LRMs, generating long sequences of tokens, means that initial uncertainties or "entropy spikes" propagate and accumulate exponentially. This "chain reaction" leads to a global entropy explosion where almost every token position exhibits uniformly high entropy, resulting in meaningless output sequences.
3. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Naive entropy regularization is highly sensitive to its coefficient; a small value yields negligible improvement, while a large value quickly triggers an uncontrollable entropy explosion.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this research are to:
- **Deeply analyze the limitations** of naive entropy regularization in the specific context of RLVR for LRMs, explaining why the vast action space and long trajectories make it problematic.
- **Propose a novel and effective method**, SIREN (SelectIve entRopy rEgularizatioN), that rethinks how entropy regularization is applied to LRMs.
- **Confine exploration to meaningful subsets** of actions (tokens) and states (positions within a trajectory) to prevent indiscriminate exploration and global entropy explosion.
- **Stabilize the training process** by appropriately calibrating the regularization strength, preventing both premature convergence and excessive entropy.
- **Demonstrate superior performance** of SIREN over existing entropy-related RLVR approaches on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks, thereby significantly enhancing the reasoning abilities of LRMs.
- **Promote greater response diversity** and maintain entropy at an appropriate level throughout training, effectively mitigating the premature convergence problem.

In essence, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between traditional RL exploration strategies and the unique characteristics of LRMs, enabling controlled and productive exploration for complex reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **SIREN (SelectIve entRopy rEgularizatioN)**, addresses the limitations of naive entropy regularization in LRMs by selectively applying regularization and stabilizing its strength. SIREN is built upon the **Dr.GRPO** (Liu et al., 2025) framework, a variant of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO, Shao et al., 2024), which is an RLVR method that models the entire response as a single action and uses group-normalized advantages.

SIREN introduces three key innovations:

#### 3.1. Two-Step Entropy Masking Mechanism

This mechanism is designed to precisely identify and target the "meaningful subset" of actions (tokens) and "critical positions" within trajectories for exploration.

1. **Top-p Mask within a Token (Action Space Level):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** The vast vocabulary of LRMs causes naive regularization to distribute probability mass to many semantically meaningless tokens, leading to incoherent outputs.
 - **Approach:** SIREN introduces the concept of a "policy nucleus" – a subset of the vocabulary containing the most semantically important tokens. This is inspired by Top-p sampling (Holtzman et al., 2019).
 - **Mechanism:** For each token position, a mask `M_top-p_j(v)` is created, retaining only tokens `v` whose cumulative probability reaches a predefined threshold `p`. Entropy is then recomputed exclusively within this `V(p)_j` (policy nucleus), effectively restricting exploration to meaningful tokens.

2. **Peak-Entropy Mask for a Trajectory (Trajectory Level):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Uncertainty accumulates exponentially along long trajectories in LRMs, leading to a global entropy explosion where most tokens exhibit uniformly high, yet uninformative, entropy.
 - **Approach:** Recognizing that only a small fraction of tokens play a disproportionately important logical role (e.g., connectors, sentence heads) and tend to have higher entropy, SIREN focuses regularization on these "critical tokens."
 - **Mechanism:** For each trajectory, tokens whose recomputed entropy (from the top-p masked distribution) falls within the top `τ`-quantile are selected. A mask `m_peak_j` is applied to only these tokens, ensuring that entropy and gradients are computed and applied solely at these logically significant points in the sequence. The overall entropy `H` for regularization is then aggregated from these masked, recomputed token entropies across the batch.

#### 3.2. Self-Anchored Regularization

- **Problem Addressed:** The naive objective of entropy regularization (maximizing entropy) can easily swing between two extremes in LRMs: either too high (leading to global explosion) or too low (failing to mitigate premature convergence). This makes regularization hyperparameter-sensitive and unstable.
- **Approach:** Instead of simply maximizing entropy, SIREN transforms the objective into a self-anchored form, aiming to maintain the policy's entropy at an appropriate, stable level.
- **Mechanism:** The regularization loss `L_sa` is defined as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the aggregated entropy `H` (from Eq. 10) and an "entropy anchor" `H_a`. Crucially, `H_a` is not an external hyperparameter but is **initialized once** using the aggregated entropy of the pre-trained policy at the initial training step. This self-anchoring ensures that the target entropy level is model- and dataset-specific, reflecting the inherent uncertainty of the base model before RL fine-tuning and providing an exploration-friendly starting point. The overall training objective becomes `J' = J_PO - βL_sa`.

By combining these selective masking mechanisms with a stabilizing self-anchored regularization, SIREN aims to achieve targeted, effective, and stable exploration within the challenging environment of LRMs, preventing both uninformative entropy explosions and detrimental premature convergence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate SIREN's performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks yielded several significant findings:

- **Superior Performance on Qwen2.5-Math-7B:** SIREN demonstrated substantial gains over the base Qwen2.5-Math-7B model and outperformed strong RL baselines. It achieved an average maj@k of **54.6** and an average avg@k of **46.1** across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC22, MATH500, OlympiadBench). This represents a notable improvement, for instance, a **+6.6 maj@k improvement** on the challenging AIME24/25 benchmarks over the best-performing baseline.
- **Generalizability Across Model Scales:** The method's effectiveness was validated on different backbone models. SIREN consistently achieved the best maj@k and avg@k on the smaller **Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B** and the comparatively weaker **LLaMa3.1-8B** models. This demonstrates its robustness and applicability beyond a single model architecture or size.
- **Promotion of Effective Exploration:**
 - **Pass@k Analysis:** SIREN's performance in pass@k, an indicator of the upper bound of reasoning ability, showed that its advantage increased with higher `k` values, particularly on easier benchmarks. On the hardest benchmark (AIME25), SIREN achieved the best pass@k at the maximum `k`. This indicates that SIREN not only explores a wider range of possible responses but also effectively balances exploration with exploitation, leading to higher-quality and more consistent answers.
 - **Perplexity (PPL) Analysis:** Responses generated by SIREN consistently exhibited higher perplexity compared to baseline RL methods. Higher PPL suggests greater response diversity and indicates that SIREN successfully mitigates the "collapse" of responses into a smaller, more deterministic subset, thereby fostering broader exploration.
- **Stable and Progressive Exploration during Training:** Analysis of training dynamics (entropy and validation pass@16) revealed that SIREN (with an appropriate coefficient) maintains a substantial level of entropy during early training stages, encouraging diverse response generation. This entropy level then gradually converges and stabilizes, remaining higher than other baselines. Crucially, this sustained exploration is effectively converted into more correct responses, leading to an improved and sustained validation pass@k throughout training, preventing the premature convergence observed in other methods. In contrast, naive regularization often leads to either premature convergence or rapid entropy explosion and performance collapse.
- **Ablation Study Confirms Component Efficacy:** An ablation study demonstrated that all three components of SIREN—the top-p mask, the peak-entropy mask, and the self-anchored regularization—are crucial for its overall performance.
 - Removing any single component led to noticeable performance drops in both maj@k and avg@k.
 - The **self-anchored regularization** showed the most severe degradation upon removal (e.g., -10.3 maj@k and -15.5 avg@k), underscoring its critical role in stabilizing training and balancing performance with exploration by preventing entropy from rising excessively.
 - Ablation with *only* self-anchored regularization showed improvements over naive entropy regularization, confirming its stabilizing power, but still underperformed the full SIREN, highlighting the combined impact of all components.
- **Qualitative Validation of Masking Strategy:** A word cloud generated from tokens selected by SIREN's masking mechanism revealed a concentration on core terms related to mathematical problem statements and reasoning (e.g., "Given," "The," "To," "Answer," "Consider," along with mathematical concepts like "product," "sum," "value," "equation"). This qualitatively validates that the masking strategies effectively focus regularization on logically critical and semantically meaningful tokens.

In summary, the main findings strongly support SIREN's efficacy in addressing the challenges of entropy regularization in LRMs, leading to improved reasoning capabilities through controlled, effective, and stable exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "RETHINKING ENTROPY REGULARIZATION IN LARGE REASONING MODELS" carries significant implications for the field of large language models and reinforcement learning, particularly in their application to complex reasoning tasks.

### 6. A Principled Solution to a Critical Problem in RLVR

The paper pinpoints and offers a robust solution to the pervasive issue of entropy collapse and premature convergence in RLVR for LRMs. This problem has been a significant bottleneck, preventing LLMs from fully leveraging RL's potential for sophisticated reasoning. By identifying the unique challenges posed by LRMs' vast action spaces and long trajectories to traditional entropy regularization, the authors provide a foundational understanding and a targeted fix. This is a critical step towards making RLVR a more reliable and effective fine-tuning paradigm for reasoning-intensive applications.

### 7. Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities for LLMs

The demonstrated superior performance across multiple mathematical benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC22, MATH500, OlympiadBench) signifies a tangible improvement in the reasoning abilities of LLMs. Mathematical reasoning is a strong proxy for general logical and problem-solving capabilities. By enabling more effective exploration, SIREN allows models to discover better reasoning paths and produce more diverse, yet accurate, solutions. This directly contributes to the development of more intelligent and capable AI assistants for complex analytical tasks.

### 8. Advancing the Understanding of Exploration in Large Models

The research deepens our understanding of how exploration should be managed in the context of LLMs. It moves beyond generic entropy maximization to advocate for "targeted and effective exploration," acknowledging the unique architectural and operational characteristics of these models. The proposed two-step masking mechanism (top-p and peak-entropy) and self-anchored regularization offer novel conceptual tools for controlling policy diversity in high-dimensional, sequential decision-making environments. These concepts could inspire future research in other areas where LLMs face similar exploration challenges.

### 9. Improved Training Stability and Efficiency

The self-anchored regularization component significantly enhances training stability by maintaining entropy at an appropriate level, preventing both uninformative explosion and detrimental collapse. This not only leads to better final performance but also likely makes the training process more robust and less sensitive to hyperparameter tuning, which is a major practical concern in LLM fine-tuning. More stable training means more efficient resource utilization and a smoother development cycle for future models.

### 10. Broader Applicability and Future Research Directions

While validated on mathematical reasoning, the principles behind SIREN—managing exploration in vast action spaces and long trajectories—are highly transferable. The paper explicitly mentions potential future applications in other RLVR scenarios such as code generation and scientific reasoning, where correctness can be verified, and outputs are sequential and complex. This opens avenues for extending SIREN's impact across diverse domains requiring high-quality, verifiable outputs from LLMs. Future work may also explore more dynamic entropy control (e.g., annealing) or richer signals for token selection, building upon the strong foundation laid by SIREN.

In essence, SIREN provides a crucial methodological advancement for fine-tuning large reasoning models, offering both a theoretical understanding of previous failures and a practical, empirically validated solution. Its impact could be far-reaching, enabling the development of more robust, diverse, and ultimately more intelligent AI systems capable of tackling increasingly complex reasoning challenges. The open-sourcing of their code further facilitates adoption and future development within the research community.
