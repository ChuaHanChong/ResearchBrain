# Rethinking Entropy Regularization in Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces SIREN (SelectIve entRopy rEgularizatioN), a method designed to mitigate entropy collapse in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for large reasoning models. It significantly improved mathematical reasoning abilities, achieving an average maj@k of 54.6 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B across five benchmarks and promoting stable, effective exploration.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) suffers from entropy collapse and premature convergence, limiting exploration and performance.
- Naive entropy regularization fails in LRMs due to their vast action space (diffusing probability mass) and long trajectories (propagating uncertainty, leading to global entropy explosion).
- Traditional regularization exhibits high hyperparameter sensitivity, offering minimal gains with small coefficients or rapid, uncontrollable entropy explosions with large ones.

## Method

- Proposed ==SIREN (SelectIve entRopy rEgularizatioN)==, built upon Dr.GRPO, to selectively apply and stabilize entropy regularization in LRMs.
- Implemented a two-step entropy masking mechanism: a ==top-p mask== within a token (action space level) to focus on meaningful tokens, and a ==peak-entropy mask== for a trajectory (trajectory level) to target logically critical positions.
- Introduced ==Self-Anchored Regularization==, transforming the objective to maintain entropy at an appropriate, stable level by using an 'entropy anchor' initialized from the pre-trained policy, rather than simply maximizing it.

## Results

- SIREN achieved an average maj@k of **54.6** and avg@k of **46.1** on Qwen2.5-Math-7B across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including a **+6.6** maj@k improvement on AIME24/25 over baselines.
- The method demonstrated generalizability, consistently outperforming baselines on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and LLaMa3.1-8B models, while increasing perplexity in generated responses, indicating greater diversity.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three components (top-p mask, peak-entropy mask, and self-anchored regularization) are crucial, with self-anchored regularization being particularly critical for stabilizing training and preventing excessive entropy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive entropy regularization is ineffective for LRMs because their vast action space and long autoregressive trajectories lead to either diluted, uninformative entropy or an explosive, incoherent state.
> - Effective exploration in LRMs requires targeted regularization, focusing entropy control on semantically meaningful tokens and logically critical positions within reasoning trajectories.
> - Stabilizing the regularization objective by anchoring the target entropy to the model's initial state prevents extreme fluctuations, ensuring both sufficient exploration and robust training without premature convergence or uncontrolled entropy explosion.
