# Ro-SLM: Onboard Small Language Models for Robot Task Planning and Operation Code Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UMass Dartmouth and CSU San Marcos introduce Ro-SLM, a framework that distills Large Language Model reasoning into Small Language Models (SLMs) for robot task planning and operation code generation. The fine-tuned SLM (Llama-3.1-8B) achieved up to 97.7% task success rate on basic UAV tasks and 75% on advanced tasks, demonstrating effective onboard autonomy for resource-constrained robots.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are impractical for onboard robot deployment due to their high computational demands, requiring either substantial hardware or stable cloud connectivity.
- Small Language Models (SLMs), while deployable on resource-constrained robots, inherently lack the robust reasoning and code generation capabilities of LLMs.
- A significant performance gap exists between LLMs and SLMs, limiting SLMs' ability to reliably interpret complex human instructions and generalize to novel robotic tasks.

## Method

- The **Ro-SLM** framework employs a two-stage methodology: LLM-driven dataset synthesis and SLM fine-tuning with LLM feedback, designed to transfer LLM knowledge to SLMs.
- Dataset synthesis involves using LLMs (GPT-5.1, o4-mini) to generate diverse task instructions, ground truth robot operation code, and augmented real-world application scenarios, with human assistance for complex code verification.
- SLM fine-tuning uses Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with ==LoRA== on the synthesized dataset, followed by optimization with Group Relative Policy Optimization (==GRPO==) where an LLM (GPT-5.1) acts as a binary reward function for code correctness.

## Results

- Ro-SLM enabled Llama-3.1-8B to achieve a **97.7%** task success rate and **98.9%** completeness on basic UAV tasks, and **70.0%** SR and **83.7%** completeness on advanced UAV tasks, significantly outperforming the untuned SLM (approx. 5-**10%** SR).
- The framework demonstrated robust performance on real-world mapped advanced tasks, achieving **75%** SR and **87%** completeness, even outperforming some LLM baselines on 'Very High' complexity tasks (**50%** SR vs. **25%** SR).
- Ro-SLM showed generalization capability by achieving **75%** SR and **81.1%** completeness on an unseen ground vehicle platform, approaching the performance of state-of-the-art LLM methods for that domain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Removing comments from the code in the training dataset significantly improves SLM performance, increasing success rate on advanced tasks by 20%, indicating that comments introduce noise during training.
> - Dataset augmentation with diverse, naturalistic language enhances the SLM's robustness and generalization to various instruction styles, improving performance on real-world mapped tasks.
> - Training with a diverse range of task complexities is crucial for robust SLM performance, as SLMs tend to overfit to specific task distributions, failing to generalize to different complexity levels.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have significantly impacted robotics by providing contextual reasoning abilities, enabling robots to interpret human instructions and generate corresponding operation code for tasks such as control, planning, and navigation. LLMs, trained on vast datasets and possessing billions of parameters, demonstrate strong semantic understanding and code generation capabilities, leading to efforts in designing prompting strategies to enhance their utility in robotics.

However, the scale and architecture of LLMs present significant deployment challenges for many robotic platforms. Cloud-based LLMs, such as OpenAI GPT models, necessitate stable internet connectivity, which is often unavailable in remote or dynamic environments (e.g., mountainous regions, disaster zones). Conversely, locally deploying LLMs (e.g., Llama-3.1-405B) demands substantial computational resources (GPU memory, processing power) and energy, making them impractical for resource-constrained robots like Small Ground Vehicles (SGVs) and Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) with limited payload and battery capacity.

Small Language Models (SLMs) offer a potential solution for onboard deployment on these resource-limited platforms due to their reduced parameter count and computational footprint. Despite this advantage, SLMs typically exhibit weaker reasoning capabilities compared to their larger counterparts, limiting their ability to extract relevant information from prompts, comprehend complex instructions, and generalize to novel robotic tasks as effectively as LLMs.

This research addresses the gap between the strong reasoning capabilities of LLMs and the deployment constraints of resource-limited robots. It aims to bridge the performance disparity between LLMs and SLMs by distilling the knowledge and reasoning abilities of LLMs into SLMs. The work builds upon existing research in LLM-driven robot code generation (which often relies on cloud-based LLMs and prompt engineering), synthetic dataset generation using LLMs (e.g., LLM-Trainer, PRISM), and SLM fine-tuning techniques (e.g., LoRA, RLHF/GRPO). Ro-SLM contributes by presenting a comprehensive framework for creating high-quality, domain-specific datasets and optimizing SLMs using LLM feedback, specifically tailored for robust robot task planning and operation code generation in resource-constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the Ro-SLM framework is to overcome the deployment limitations of Large Language Models (LLMs) on resource-constrained robotic platforms. While LLMs offer advanced reasoning and code generation capabilities crucial for interpreting complex human instructions, their reliance on either stable internet connectivity (for cloud-based inference) or high-performance computing infrastructure (for local deployment) renders them unsuitable for many real-world robotic applications. Robots operating in environments with unreliable internet access (e.g., remote areas, disaster sites) or those with strict power and payload limitations (e.g., UAVs, small ground vehicles) cannot effectively leverage current LLM solutions.

Small Language Models (SLMs), with their smaller parameter sizes, are inherently more amenable to onboard deployment on such platforms. However, they typically suffer from weaker reasoning capabilities, which hinders their ability to reliably understand complex task instructions, generate accurate robot operation code, and generalize to diverse scenarios. This performance gap between SLMs and LLMs presents a significant barrier to enabling truly autonomous, language-driven robot operations in resource-constrained settings.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Enable Reliable SLM-Driven Robot Operations**: To develop a framework that allows SLMs to drive robot task planning and operation code generation effectively, despite their inherent limitations, thus facilitating onboard deployment.
2. **Distill LLM Knowledge and Reasoning into SLMs**: To transfer the advanced contextual reasoning and code generation capabilities of LLMs to SLMs. This involves leveraging LLMs as a powerful resource for both data generation and model optimization.
3. **Develop a Robust Dataset Synthesis Methodology**: To create a high-quality, diverse, and domain-specific dataset of robot task instructions and corresponding executable code. This dataset is crucial for fine-tuning SLMs and must reflect varying task complexities and real-world application scenarios, while minimizing human effort.
4. **Implement an LLM-Guided Fine-Tuning and Optimization Process**: To train SLMs effectively using the synthesized dataset, incorporating LLM feedback to refine the model's performance, particularly in terms of step-level reasoning and code correctness.
5. **Demonstrate Performance Comparable to LLMs on Resource-Constrained Platforms**: To experimentally validate that the fine-tuned SLMs, within the Ro-SLM framework, can achieve a level of performance for robotic tasks that approaches that of LLMs, making them practical for onboard deployment without requiring extensive external resources.
6. **Assess Generalization Across Task Complexities and Robotic Platforms**: To evaluate the framework's ability to handle tasks of varying difficulty and to adapt to different types of robots with distinct APIs and operational constraints.

In essence, the motivation is to bring the intelligence of large language models to small, mobile robots where computational and connectivity resources are severely limited, thereby expanding the reach and autonomy of robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Ro-SLM framework is structured into two main stages: Dataset Synthesis and SLM Fine-tuning, designed to transfer the reasoning and knowledge from Large Language Models (LLMs) to Small Language Models (SLMs) for robot task planning and operation code generation.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Dataset Synthesis

This stage focuses on constructing a high-quality, domain-specific dataset crucial for effectively fine-tuning the SLM. It leverages LLMs to automate much of the data generation process, requiring minimal human intervention.

- **Task Instruction Set Generation (Function F₁: (P, D) → I)**:
 - An LLM agent (specifically, OpenAI GPT-5.1) is configured using a structured system prompt. This prompt defines the LLM's role, provides a set of robot operational policies (P) detailing the robot's capabilities and constraints, and includes demonstrations (D) of task instructions.
 - The policies ensure the generated instructions are executable, concise, and clear, preventing ambiguities that could lead to misinterpretation. Demonstrations help the LLM learn and replicate standard instruction formats and patterns.
 - To ensure diversity, multiple system prompt configurations are used to generate tasks of varying complexity, from simple one- or two-action tasks requiring basic spatial reasoning to multi-stage tasks involving five or more actions and complex reasoning about robot dynamics. This broad distribution aims to equip the SLM with robust and generalizable reasoning capabilities.

- **Ground Truth Code Generation (Function F₂: I → C)**:
 - LLM agents (OpenAI o4-mini) are employed to translate each task instruction (Iᵢ) into its corresponding ground truth robot operation code (Cᵢ).
 - The process adopts a corrective code generation framework, which identifies and iteratively refines errors in the generated code to ensure correctness.
 - To maintain high fidelity, a hybrid code generation strategy is implemented: for simple tasks, code generation is fully automated by the LLM. However, for complex tasks involving five or more action steps, human experts review and correct the generated code. This human oversight is crucial to prevent the SLM from being trained on erroneous supervision, especially for tasks requiring intricate spatial reasoning or long-horizon planning where LLM performance might degrade.

- **Augmentation (Function F₃: (I, C) → (Î, C))**:
 - This step bridges the gap between the concise, high-level task instructions generated earlier and the more natural, human-like descriptions often encountered in real-world scenarios.
 - An LLM agent (GPT-5.1) leverages its world knowledge to rephrase the original task instructions (Iᵢ) into descriptions of realistic robotic application scenarios (Îᵢ), while ensuring the underlying robot operations remain unchanged.
 - Augmentation quality control is enforced via a system prompt that mandates rules such as avoiding the introduction of additional robot actions, ensuring real-world feasibility, using common vocabulary, limiting descriptions to a single paragraph, and maintaining a human-like tone. This ensures that the augmented instructions remain faithful to the original task's execution plan.

- **Dataset Construction**:
 - The final fine-tuning dataset, S, consists of paired instructions and code, encompassing both the original and augmented instructions (S = (I ∪ Î, C)).
 - A critical data preprocessing step involves removing all comments from the generated code. This strategy aims to prevent the SLM from overfitting to linguistic patterns within the comments, ensuring it focuses on learning the actual code logic and generating operational code accurately.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: SLM Fine-tune

This stage involves training the chosen SLM using the synthesized dataset and an LLM-aided optimization process.

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**:
 - The SLM (Llama-3.1-8B) is initially trained on the constructed dataset S. This supervised training phase aims to align the SLM with the desired code generation style, enabling it to learn specific robot actions (APIs) and their mappings from task instructions to executable code.
 - Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is utilized during SFT to improve fine-tuning efficiency and reduce memory consumption, making the process more practical.

- **Optimization with LLM Feedback**:
 - Following SFT, the SLM's performance is further optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
 - An LLM (GPT-5.1) serves as a reward function. Unlike conventional text generation tasks where semantic similarity might suffice, robotic code generation requires precise operational equivalence. The LLM is configured to interpret the implied robot behavior of the SLM-generated code and compare it against the ground truth code's behavior.
 - A binary reward (1 for a full match in operational outcome, 0 otherwise) is assigned. This reward signal guides GRPO to refine the SLM's weights, encouraging it to produce code that accurately executes the same robot actions as the ground truth, thereby enhancing step-level reasoning, especially for long-horizon tasks.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that Ro-SLM effectively leverages the advanced capabilities of LLMs to overcome the data scarcity challenge in robotics and improve the reasoning and code generation performance of SLMs for resource-constrained platforms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate Ro-SLM focused on UAV operation tasks and also included a generalization test on a ground vehicle.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Implementation**: OpenAI GPT-5.1 was used for task instruction generation and augmentation, while o4-mini was employed for ground truth code generation. The SLM chosen for fine-tuning was Llama-3.1-8B, with GPT-5.1 acting as the reward function during optimization.
- **Dataset Size**: The synthetic training set comprised 203 original task instructions. Of these, 150 were automatically grounded into code by LLMs, and 53 required human assistance for grounding due to their complexity. Dataset augmentation expanded this to 598 instruction-code pairs, which were then split into 492 for training and 106 for evaluation.
- **Simulation Environment**: The "block" environment of the AirSim simulator was used for UAV tasks. Results were averaged over three runs to account for randomness in LLM generation.
- **Baselines**: Three methods were used for comparison:
 - **Llama-3.1-8B**: The original, un-fine-tuned SLM configured with basic prompting strategies.
 - **GSCE (Wang et al., 2025a)**: An LLM-based method using a structured system prompt for UAV operation code generation (with o4-mini).
 - **Corrective GSCE (Wang et al., 2025b)**: A state-of-the-art LLM-based method that iteratively refines generated code (with o4-mini LLMs).
- **Test Datasets**:
 - **Basic Dataset**: 44 intuitive UAV operation tasks, each involving one or two actions, assessing basic planning and logical reasoning.
 - **Advanced Dataset**: 20 long-horizon UAV operation tasks with four complexity levels (Low, Medium, High, Very High), requiring complex reasoning about UAV dynamics.
 - Both datasets were manually validated. Additionally, extended versions with real-world application scenarios were created to assess robustness to naturalistic instructions.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Completeness**: Measures the proportion of correctly executed actions within a given task, providing insight into intermediate reasoning.
 - **Success Rate (SR)**: A task-level metric indicating whether the entire action sequence was executed without error (Completeness = 1).

#### 4.2. Overall Results

Tables 1 and 2 summarize the performance across different methods and datasets.

- **Performance of Llama-3.1-8B (untuned)**: The original Llama-3.1-8B, when provided with the same prompting as GSCE, exhibited very low performance. On Basic tasks, it achieved 9.1% SR and 9.1% Completeness, and on Advanced tasks, 5% SR and 9% Completeness. This indicates its inherent inability to support robotic task planning and code generation without specialized fine-tuning. For real-world mapped tasks, its performance was similarly poor (11.4% SR Basic, 0% SR Advanced).
- **Ro-SLM on Original Datasets (Table 1)**:
 - **Basic Tasks**: Ro-SLM achieved 97.7% SR and 98.9% Completeness. This performance is close to the LLM-based GSCE and Corrective GSCE, both of which achieved 100% SR and Completeness on these tasks.
 - **Advanced Tasks**: Ro-SLM attained 70.0% SR and 83.7% Completeness. While lower than Corrective GSCE (90.0% SR, 97.8% Completeness), it represents a significant improvement over the untuned SLM. The paper notes that Corrective GSCE employs three LLMs, suggesting higher computational requirements.
- **Ro-SLM on Real-World Mapped Datasets (Table 2)**:
 - **Basic Tasks**: Ro-SLM achieved 93.2% SR and 95.5% Completeness.
 - **Advanced Tasks**: Ro-SLM demonstrated improved performance compared to the original advanced tasks, achieving 75% SR and 87% Completeness. This improvement is attributed to the dataset augmentation process, which exposed the SLM to diverse real-world phrasing.

Overall, the results demonstrate that Ro-SLM effectively elevates the performance of a Small Language Model (Llama-3.1-8B) from being largely incapable to approaching the capabilities of Large Language Models in robotic task planning and code generation. This suggests that the distillation of LLM reasoning and knowledge into SLMs is successful.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study investigated the contribution of individual components of the Ro-SLM framework (Tables 1 and 2):

- **Comments**: Removing code comments from the dataset (Ro-SLM vs. Ro-SLM with Comments) improved SR on Advanced tasks by 20% (from 50% to 70%). This indicates that SLMs are sensitive to syntactic patterns in comments, and their presence can introduce noise, leading to degraded performance, particularly for longer code sequences.
- **Augmentation**: Omitting the dataset augmentation process (Ro-SLM vs. Ro-SLM without Augmentation) resulted in a decrease of at least 2% in SR and Completeness on Basic tasks and more than 5% on Advanced tasks (both original and real-world mapped). This highlights that exposing the SLM to diverse, naturalistic task instructions enhances its robustness and generalization across varying instruction styles.
- **Task Diversity**: Training the SLM exclusively on simple tasks (Ro-SLM Simple Task Only) led to good performance on Basic tasks but near-zero performance on Advanced tasks (0% SR, 1.9% Completeness). Conversely, training only on complex tasks (Ro-SLM Complex Task Only) resulted in poor performance on Basic tasks (0% SR, 2.3% Completeness) but good performance on Advanced tasks (75% SR, 83.8% Completeness). This confirms that SLMs tend to overfit to the distribution of their training data, underscoring the necessity of diverse task complexity in the synthesized dataset for robust generalization.
- **GRPO Optimization**: Removing the GRPO optimization step (Ro-SLM without GRPO Optimization) did not significantly affect Basic tasks but reduced Completeness on Advanced tasks by 0.6%. This suggests that GRPO, guided by the LLM reward function, refines the SLM's step-level reasoning, improving intermediate decision-making, particularly for long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.4. Analysis over Task Complexity

Performance was analyzed across the four complexity levels of the Advanced tasks (Table 3):

- **Success Rate**: As task complexity increased, the SR of the LLM-based GSCE generally decreased. Interestingly, Ro-SLM showed comparable or sometimes superior SR on higher complexity levels. For example, at the "Very High" level, Ro-SLM achieved 50.0% SR compared to GSCE's 25.0%. This is attributed to the accurate human-assisted ground-truth code provided for complex tasks during dataset construction, allowing the SLM to learn challenging long-horizon behaviors more effectively.
- **Completeness**: Ro-SLM's Completeness approached that of GSCE across Low, Medium, and High complexity levels. At the "Very High" level, while its SR was higher, its Completeness remained similar to GSCE. This indicates that Ro-SLM could handle critical steps in highly complex tasks more accurately, contributing to higher task success, even if minor errors might occur in other steps.

#### 4.5. Experiment on Ground Vehicle

To assess generalization, Ro-SLM was applied to a ground vehicle platform using a new dataset of 8 ground vehicle operation tasks (Table 4). The framework successfully synthesized relevant instructions and code for the ground vehicle's distinct APIs and constraints. The fine-tuned SLM achieved 75% SR and 81.1% Completeness, approaching the performance of Corrective GSCE (87.5% SR, 97.2% Completeness) on this new platform. This result suggests that Ro-SLM captures general principles of robot task planning rather than overfitting to specific platform APIs, demonstrating its potential for generalization across heterogeneous robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Ro-SLM framework represents a significant step towards enabling advanced language-driven control on resource-constrained robotic platforms. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that the powerful reasoning and knowledge encoded in Large Language Models (LLMs) can be effectively distilled into Small Language Models (SLMs). This distillation process allows SLMs to achieve a level of performance for robot task planning and operation code generation that approaches that of LLMs, thereby overcoming critical deployment barriers.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Autonomy in Resource-Constrained Environments**: Ro-SLM can enable autonomous robots, such as UAVs and small ground vehicles, to operate intelligently in environments lacking reliable internet connectivity or possessing limited onboard computational and energy resources. This expands the operational reach of advanced robotic systems to remote areas, disaster zones, or missions requiring prolonged operation away from robust infrastructure.
- **Wider Adoption of Language-Driven Robotics**: By making sophisticated language understanding and code generation capabilities accessible to a broader range of robotic hardware, Ro-SLM facilitates the development of more intuitive and adaptable human-robot interaction paradigms. This could accelerate the adoption of robots in various fields, from environmental monitoring and logistics to search and rescue.
- **Reduced Reliance on Cloud Infrastructure**: The framework lessens the dependency on cloud-based LLM services for complex robot decision-making. This improves operational reliability, reduces latency, and enhances data privacy and security by keeping critical processing local to the robot.
- **Foundation for Future SLM Development**: The methodology for dataset synthesis and LLM-aided optimization provides a template for developing and fine-tuning specialized SLMs for other domain-specific tasks, not limited to robotics, where large models are impractical.

**Limitations Identified by the Authors:**

Despite its promise, the authors acknowledge several limitations that could be addressed in future research:

- **Generalization and Real-World Deployment**: The dataset synthesis process is currently task-specific. Transferring the framework across vastly different domains (e.g., from aerial to underwater robotics, or industrial manipulation) remains a challenge. Moreover, while demonstrated in simulation, the robustness of Ro-SLM in complex, unpredictable real-world environments requires further validation.
- **Computational Scalability**: Although Ro-SLM significantly improves Llama-3.1-8B's performance for onboard use, the model still requires approximately 16GB of memory. This restricts deployment on extremely compact edge devices with very limited memory capacities. Investigating the performance with even smaller SLMs (e.g., 1B or 3B parameters) within the Ro-SLM framework is an area for future work.
- **Human Involvement in Data Generation**: While LLMs automate much of the data synthesis, minimal human assistance is still required to verify and correct ground-truth code for high-complexity tasks. This introduces manual overhead, demands domain expertise, and limits the scalability of dataset construction for entirely new or significantly more complex domains.
- **Lack of Iterative Correction**: Unlike some LLM-based approaches (e.g., Corrective GSCE) that employ multi-round refinement during inference, Ro-SLM relies on a single-pass generation. While this design choice prioritizes efficiency and deployability, it limits the model's ability to self-correct intermediate reasoning errors, particularly in intricate multi-step tasks.

**Ethical Considerations:**

The paper also highlights ethical considerations related to the deployment of language model-driven robotic systems. The generated code directly controls physical robot operations, inherently carrying risks of physical harm if execution is improper or erroneous. Potential issues include collisions, property damage, or injury to humans. The authors emphasize the necessity of integrating additional safeguards, such as emergency stop mechanisms, when transferring Ro-SLM to real-world robotic platforms to mitigate these risks.
