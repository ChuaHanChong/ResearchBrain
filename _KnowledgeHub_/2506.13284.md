# AceReason-Nemotron 1.1: Advancing Math and Code Reasoning through SFT and RL Synergy

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers developed AceReason-Nemotron 1.1, a Qwen2.5-7B based model, by systematically investigating the synergy between supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance math and code reasoning. The model achieved new state-of-the-art performance for its size on challenging benchmarks like AIME25 (64.8%) and LiveCodeBench v6 (52.1%).

## Problem

- Prior research lacked a systematic study on the synergy between supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) in developing reasoning models.
- Technical reports from frontier models often omitted detailed insights into how SFT and RL post-training techniques interact and influence each other's effectiveness.
- Optimizing training pipelines required a better understanding of SFT data scaling strategies, RL hyperparameter tuning, and stage-wise curriculum design.

## Method

- NVIDIA researchers developed a comprehensive, ==stage-wise training pipeline== combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and ==GRPO-based Reinforcement Learning (RL)==, building upon a Qwen2.5-Math-7B base model.
- They systematically investigated ==SFT data scaling== by constructing various datasets to analyze the impact of unique prompts versus responses per prompt on performance.
- Extensive RL ablation studies were conducted on factors such as initial SFT model strength, sampling temperature, strategies for handling overlong responses, and the necessity and duration of an initial ==warm-up RL stage==.

## Results

- AceReason-Nemotron-1.1-7B achieved new state-of-the-art pass@1 performance among Qwen2.5-7B-based models, including **64.8%** on AIME25 and **52.1%** on LiveCodeBench v6.
- The model demonstrated absolute performance gains of **10.6%** on AIME24, **16.4%** on AIME25, **8.4%** on LiveCodeBench v5, and **8.3%** on LiveCodeBench v6 over its strong SFT starting model.
- RL training enabled the model to solve a 'long tail' of harder problems that the initial SFT model struggled with, particularly evident on LiveCodeBench where it solved over **ten** additional problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling the number of unique prompts in supervised fine-tuning (SFT) had a more significant impact on reasoning performance than increasing the number of generated responses per prompt, as indicated by higher regression coefficients.
> - Reinforcement learning (RL) consistently improved performance even when starting from strong SFT baselines, and while a stronger SFT initialization led to better final results, RL training effectively narrowed initial performance gaps between different SFT models.
> - An initial 'Math-only Stage-1' in the RL curriculum, despite a temporary performance dip, was crucial for learning concise reasoning and enabling higher gains in subsequent stages, facilitating a smoother transition from SFT to RL.
