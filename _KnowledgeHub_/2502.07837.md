# RoboBERT: An End-to-end Multimodal Robotic Manipulation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed RoboBERT, an end-to-end multimodal robotic manipulation model, employing a two-stage training paradigm and systematic data augmentation. It achieved state-of-the-art performance on CALVIN benchmarks with a 208M parameter architecture and demonstrated robust success rates of 80-92% on individual and 80-87% on sequential real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- High computational and time costs associated with fine-tuning large foundation models on robotic data, exacerbated by data scarcity and robot platform heterogeneity.
- Existing robotic models often exhibit instability and confusion when confronted with diverse natural language instructions.
- Lack of robustness to visual perturbations and redundant information in raw image observations, which can degrade model performance.

## Method

- A lightweight, end-to-end **RoboBERT** model (208M parameters) integrates BERT for language, ==CLIP ViT== for vision, and a CNN-based diffusion model as the action head.
- A two-stage training paradigm separates policy learning using consistent language from linguistic generalization with diverse natural language variants.
- Systematic data augmentation techniques, including color jitter, salt-and-pepper noise, and Robotic Mixup, are applied to enhance visual robustness and data efficiency.

## Results

- RoboBERT achieved a mean episode length of **4.52** ± **0.03** on CALVIN ABCD → D and **3.79** ± **0.03** on ABC → D, outperforming higher-parameter or data-intensive baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the two-stage training paradigm significantly improved performance (e.g., from **3.39** to **4.52** on ABCD→D), and identified color jitter as the most impactful augmentation (+**0.65** average length on ABC→D).
- Real-robot evaluations on a REALMAN RM65B arm showed high success rates (80-**92%** for individual tasks and 80-**87%** for sequential tasks), surpassing MT-ACT and RT-1 baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The two-stage training strategy significantly improves performance by first establishing a stable policy with consistent language, then aligning diverse instructions, thereby facilitating cross-modal learning.
> - Strategic data augmentation, especially color jitter, effectively enhances visual robustness by making the model less reliant on low-frequency visual attributes and more focused on task-relevant features.
> - Competitive or superior robotic manipulation performance can be achieved with a comparatively lightweight architecture and efficient training, reducing reliance on massive pre-training datasets or billions of parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is witnessing rapid advancements, particularly with the integration of multimodal language models (MLLMs) and foundation models pretrained on vast internet-scale datasets. These models aim to equip robots with enhanced generalization capabilities, enabling them to execute complex tasks in varied and novel scenarios. A specialized subset of MLLMs relevant to robotics are Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models integrate visual perception, linguistic understanding, and motor control into a unified, end-to-end decision-making framework. Examples include RT-2, RT2-X, and OpenVLA, which fine-tune VLMs on robotic trajectory data and large-scale vision-language datasets to improve generalization across commands and scenes.

Despite these developments, a significant challenge in robot learning remains the practical implementation of LLM-style pre-training or fine-tuning for most robotic manipulation tasks. This is primarily due to the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality action modality datasets and the inherent heterogeneity of different robot platforms. Even when such data is available, the associated computational and time costs for training and fine-tuning billions of parameters can be prohibitive for many research and development settings. Furthermore, current robot-based models frequently exhibit instability and can be susceptible to confusion when presented with diverse natural language instructions.

The research presented in this paper, RoboBERT, addresses these challenges by focusing on maximizing policy performance through a combination of network design and efficient utilization of limited robot data. Unlike some contemporary VLA models that rely on extensive pre-training on large robotics datasets or leverage billions of parameters, RoboBERT aims to achieve competitive or superior performance with a comparatively lightweight architecture and a data-efficient training paradigm. This work differentiates itself by proposing a two-stage training strategy that reduces the need for large-scale robotics data fine-tuning and by emphasizing the role of systematic data augmentation to enhance robustness and generalization from limited expert demonstrations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to develop an end-to-end multimodal robotic manipulation model that addresses key limitations in current VLA systems, specifically:

1. **Reduce dependence on massive fine-tuning:** A core motivation stems from the high computational and time costs associated with fine-tuning large foundation models on robotic data. The authors aimed to mitigate this by designing a system that can achieve robust performance without requiring extensive additional robotic datasets for fine-tuning.
2. **Enhance stability and generalization with diverse language instructions:** Existing models often struggle with the variability of natural language commands, leading to unreliable performance. A key objective was to develop a training strategy that ensures the model can stably learn a policy while effectively adapting to and interpreting a wide range of natural language instructions.
3. **Improve robustness to visual perturbations:** End-to-end models directly process raw image observations, which can contain redundant information or be subject to quality degradation. The research sought to make the model more resilient to these visual variations, ensuring that critical task-relevant information is consistently extracted.
4. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance with a lightweight architecture:** The authors aimed to demonstrate that it is possible to achieve competitive or superior performance on established benchmarks using a more parameter-efficient model, contrasting with trends towards increasingly larger models.
5. **Validate real-world applicability:** Beyond simulation, a critical objective was to confirm the model's effectiveness and reliability on physical robot hardware, assessing its success rates in practical manipulation tasks.

To achieve these objectives, the authors proposed a novel two-stage training paradigm to separate policy formation from linguistic generalization and emphasized the application of systematic data augmentation techniques to improve robustness and data efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboBERT model integrates vision, language, and action through a diffusion-based policy network. The methodology encompasses a specific problem formulation, a modular model structure, a two-stage training strategy, and systematic data augmentation techniques.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work frames the problem as an end-to-end, language-conditioned robotic manipulation task. The model, denoted as $M$, accepts natural language inputs ($L \in R^l$) and raw observation sequences ($V \in R^{t \times c \times h \times w}$). Through a designated training method, the model adjusts its internal parameters ($\theta$) to predict an action ($\hat{A} \in R^a$) that closely approximates the expected action ($A \in R^a$). This process is divided into three interconnected steps:
1. **Feature Extraction ($M_{ext-\theta}$):** Raw inputs are encoded from their respective modalities.
2. **Modality Fusion ($M_{fusion-\theta}$):** The encoded observations are integrated.
3. **Policy Generation ($M_{policy-\theta}$):** A probability distribution of corresponding actions is provided by analyzing the fused results.
The overall model can be represented as: $M_\theta(L, V) = M_{policy-\theta}(M_{fusion-\theta}(M_{ext-L\theta}(L), M_{ext-V\theta}(V)))$.

#### 3.2. Model Structure

RoboBERT consists of three main components:

- **Feature Extractor ($M_{ext-\theta}$):**
 - **Language Processing:** A BERT encoder (110M parameters) is utilized for processing natural language instructions. To adapt BERT's output for subsequent stages, a "Language Connector," implemented as a perceiver resampler-based fine-tuning head, further processes linguistic information to extract task-relevant features.
 - **Image Processing:** The Vision Transformer (ViT) from the CLIP model (87M parameters) is employed for image inputs. CLIP's pre-training with a text-image contrastive method provides a strong initialization, aligning language with visual observations.

- **Modality Fusion ($M_{fusion-\theta}$):** After feature extraction, the model converts various modality inputs into tokens. These tokens are integrated using a transformer decoder, which operates without a causal mask. Similar to OpenFlamingo, language features serve as queries, while visual observations act as keys and values. Fusion is achieved through multiple layers of cross-attention and self-attention, culminating in a set of latent semantic tokens. These tokens are then compressed via max-pooling to represent the multimodal observation.

- **Action Head ($M_{policy-\theta}$):** A CNN-based diffusion model is used as the action head. It takes a noise vector and the latent representations from the modality fusion module as inputs. These inputs serve as seeds for generating and denoising conditions, respectively. The action head iteratively transforms the noise seed into action vectors for a future interval, with the most recent predictions selected as the final output.

#### 3.3. Training Method

The training approach focuses on Behavioral Cloning (BC) to mimic expert demonstrations. The objective is to minimize the difference between the model's predicted action and the expert's action. In the context of a diffusion model, the action head predicts Gaussian noise ($\epsilon_k$) conditioned on the fused multimodal representation, rather than directly predicting the action.

- **Two-stage Training:** To manage the complexity of training with diverse natural language inputs, a two-stage strategy is implemented:
 - **Stage 1: Stable Policy Learning:** During this stage, training uses a single, consistent "standard language" instruction phrasing. This allows the model to prioritize learning a stable policy without the additional complexity of varied linguistic expressions. To accelerate training and leverage pre-trained weights, the vision encoder (CLIP ViT) is mostly frozen, with only its final layer being trainable. The trainable parameters at this stage include the language feature extractor, modality fusion module, and the action head.
 - **Stage 2: Linguistic Generalization:** After the first stage establishes a stable policy, all model modules are unfrozen. Diverse natural language variants are then injected into the training data. This stage aims to rapidly align the varied instructions to the already-learned policy, minimizing the risk of destabilizing the performance established in Stage 1. All parameters, including the full vision encoder, are fine-tuned during this phase.

#### 3.4. Data Augmentation

Recognizing that raw image observations often contain redundant information and are susceptible to quality variations, the authors apply several systematic data augmentation techniques to enhance visual robustness:

- **Salt-and-pepper Noise:** Randomly replaces pixels with black or white to simulate impulse interference, encouraging the model to learn holistic image features rather than relying on individual pixels.
- **Affine Transformation:** Applies random translations, rotations, scaling, or distortions to image inputs. This simulates real-world viewpoint variations due to camera or robot movement, fostering the learning of spatially invariant features.
- **Color Jitter:** Applies HSV color jittering to reduce the model's reliance on low-frequency attributes like color or illumination, forcing it to focus on high-frequency features (edges, contours, shapes, textures) which are often more task-relevant. This also contributes to robustness against real-world lighting and camera variations.
- **Robotic Mixup:** Generates new training examples by linearly interpolating pairs of RGB frames, language tokens, and corresponding action vectors from different demonstrations. This technique increases data diversity and promotes generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of RoboBERT was evaluated through extensive experiments on both simulated benchmarks and real-robot setups, addressing specific research questions regarding its performance, the impact of the two-stage training, and the effect of data augmentation.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation (CALVIN Benchmark)

The CALVIN dataset, known for its language-conditioned long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks, was used for simulation. Performance was assessed on the ABCD → D and ABC → D subsets, with tests comprising 1000 groups of 5 subtasks guided by natural language instructions.

- **CALVIN ABCD → D Performance:** RoboBERT achieved a mean episode length of 4.52 ± 0.03, outperforming all baseline models. Notably, this was accomplished with a comparatively lightweight architecture (208M parameters) and without relying on auxiliary datasets or extensive unlabeled data, unlike several higher-parameter or data-intensive baselines such as RoboFlamingo (1000M parameters, pre-trained), DeeR (1000M parameters, pre-trained), GR-1 (130M parameters, pre-trained with 2.7TB data), and MoDE (436M parameters, pre-trained). This result indicates that RoboBERT can achieve state-of-the-art performance with a more efficient resource footprint.

- **CALVIN ABC → D Performance:** On the ABC → D benchmark, which involves adaptation to an unfamiliar object 'D' not present during training, RoboBERT achieved a mean episode length of 3.79 ± 0.03. This result demonstrates RoboBERT's strong adaptivity and generalization capabilities to novel environments, again surpassing models that utilize larger datasets, more parameters, or additional observation inputs like proprioception and depth (e.g., 3D Diffuser Actor, MoDE without pre-training).

These simulation results collectively confirmed that RoboBERT achieved comparable or superior performance to existing state-of-the-art models, even with a lighter architecture and without reliance on auxiliary datasets or extensive fine-tuning data.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Ablation on Training Method:** A comparison between direct training with natural language instructions ("NL. directly") and the proposed two-stage training method was conducted.
 - On ABCD → D, direct training resulted in an average episode length of 3.39 ± 0.05, whereas two-stage training yielded 4.52 ± 0.03.
 - On ABC → D, direct training achieved 1.25 ± 0.04, compared to 3.79 ± 0.03 with two-stage training.
 These results indicate that the two-stage paradigm significantly improved performance by first stabilizing policy learning with consistent language and then effectively aligning diverse natural language to the established policy, suggesting it facilitates learning cross-modal correspondences.

- **Ablation on Data Augmentation:** Various augmentation techniques were individually and collectively tested on the ABC → D dataset.
 - "No Augmentation" served as the baseline with an average length of 3.00 ± 0.02.
 - **Color Jitter** provided the most significant performance boost, increasing the average length to 3.65 ± 0.02 (+0.65). This is attributed to its ability to decouple operations from color, focusing the model on shape and enhancing robustness to illumination and camera variations.
 - **Salt-and-pepper Noise** and **Robotic Mixup** provided moderate gains, with average lengths of 3.22 ± 0.03 (+0.22) and 3.23 ± 0.04 (+0.23), respectively.
 - **Affine Transformation** unexpectedly *reduced* performance (2.75 ± 0.03, -0.25). The authors suggest this might be due to spatial ambiguities introduced by perspective changes, making it difficult for the model to discern actual object movement from augmentation-induced shifts.
 - Combining all augmentations resulted in 3.52 ± 0.02 (+0.52). However, removing the detrimental Affine Transformation from the combination ("Combining All w/o Aff.") further improved performance to 3.79 ± 0.03 (+0.79), indicating the importance of selecting effective augmentations.

#### 4.3. Real Robot Evaluation

RoboBERT's performance was validated on a physical REALMAN RM65B 6-DOF robotic arm. The evaluation included both individual and sequential tasks, with 25-30 teleoperated trajectories collected per task and GPT-generated varied language labels.

- **Individual Tasks:** On tasks like stacking cubes, returning a pen to its holder, and opening a cabinet door, RoboBERT achieved success rates ranging from 80% to 92%. This demonstrated superior performance compared to baseline methods like MT-ACT (72-80%) and RT-1 (60-72%), even when test environments differed from training conditions.
- **Sequential Tasks:** For long-horizon sequential tasks involving object transfers and operations on articulated objects (e.g., opening/closing drawers), RoboBERT maintained high success rates (80-87%). This indicates a strong potential for handling complex, multi-step instructions, again outperforming MT-ACT (68-78%) and RT-1 (56-72%).

These real-robot trials corroborated the simulation findings, confirming RoboBERT's effectiveness and practical applicability in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on RoboBERT presents several significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation and embodied intelligence:

1. **Efficiency and Scalability:** The introduction of a novel two-stage training paradigm, combined with a relatively lightweight architecture (208M parameters), demonstrates that state-of-the-art performance can be achieved in multimodal robotic manipulation without the reliance on massive fine-tuning datasets or billions of parameters typical of many recent foundation models. This reduces the computational and hardware costs, making advanced robotic learning more accessible for researchers and smaller development teams.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The two-stage training strategy effectively separates stable policy formation from linguistic generalization, leading to a more robust model that can accurately interpret and respond to diverse natural language instructions. Furthermore, the systematic application of data augmentation techniques, particularly color jitter, significantly improves visual robustness against real-world perturbations like varying illumination and backgrounds.
3. **Demonstrated Real-World Applicability:** The successful validation of RoboBERT on a physical 6-DOF manipulator, achieving higher success rates than comparable methods in both individual and complex sequential tasks, confirms its practical utility beyond simulated environments. This is crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap and advancing the deployment of intelligent robotic systems.
4. **Inspiration for Data-Efficient Designs:** By achieving strong results with limited language-labeled expert demonstrations and without auxiliary datasets, RoboBERT offers a beneficial inspiration for designing other end-to-end robotic manipulation models. It highlights the potential of thoughtful architectural design and training methodologies to maximize performance from constrained data resources.
5. **Advancement in Embodied Intelligence:** This work contributes to the overarching goal of embodied intelligence by demonstrating a more efficient and effective way to integrate vision, language, and action capabilities into a cohesive robotic system. Its ability to generalize across tasks and adapt to novel environments with a streamlined approach represents a step towards more capable and autonomous robots.

In summary, RoboBERT offers a method for developing efficient, scalable, and robust multimodal robotic manipulation systems, which could influence future research directions focused on resource-constrained robot learning and practical deployment in diverse real-world settings.
