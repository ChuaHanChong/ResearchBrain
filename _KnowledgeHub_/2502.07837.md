# RoboBERT: An End-to-end Multimodal Robotic Manipulation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed RoboBERT, an end-to-end multimodal robotic manipulation model, employing a two-stage training paradigm and systematic data augmentation. It achieved state-of-the-art performance on CALVIN benchmarks with a 208M parameter architecture and demonstrated robust success rates of 80-92% on individual and 80-87% on sequential real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- High computational and time costs associated with fine-tuning large foundation models on robotic data, exacerbated by data scarcity and robot platform heterogeneity.
- Existing robotic models often exhibit instability and confusion when confronted with diverse natural language instructions.
- Lack of robustness to visual perturbations and redundant information in raw image observations, which can degrade model performance.

## Method

- A lightweight, end-to-end **RoboBERT** model (208M parameters) integrates BERT for language, ==CLIP ViT== for vision, and a CNN-based diffusion model as the action head.
- A two-stage training paradigm separates policy learning using consistent language from linguistic generalization with diverse natural language variants.
- Systematic data augmentation techniques, including color jitter, salt-and-pepper noise, and Robotic Mixup, are applied to enhance visual robustness and data efficiency.

## Results

- RoboBERT achieved a mean episode length of **4.52** ± **0.03** on CALVIN ABCD → D and **3.79** ± **0.03** on ABC → D, outperforming higher-parameter or data-intensive baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the two-stage training paradigm significantly improved performance (e.g., from **3.39** to **4.52** on ABCD→D), and identified color jitter as the most impactful augmentation (+**0.65** average length on ABC→D).
- Real-robot evaluations on a REALMAN RM65B arm showed high success rates (80-**92%** for individual tasks and 80-**87%** for sequential tasks), surpassing MT-ACT and RT-1 baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The two-stage training strategy significantly improves performance by first establishing a stable policy with consistent language, then aligning diverse instructions, thereby facilitating cross-modal learning.
> - Strategic data augmentation, especially color jitter, effectively enhances visual robustness by making the model less reliant on low-frequency visual attributes and more focused on task-relevant features.
> - Competitive or superior robotic manipulation performance can be achieved with a comparatively lightweight architecture and efficient training, reducing reliance on massive pre-training datasets or billions of parameters.
