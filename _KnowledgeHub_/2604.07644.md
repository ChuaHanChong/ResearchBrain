# Safe Large-Scale Robust Nonlinear MPC in Milliseconds via Reachability-Constrained System Level Synthesis on the GPU

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed GPU-SLS, a GPU-parallelized framework for safe, robust nonlinear model predictive control, integrating inequality-constrained NMPC with System Level Synthesis. This method achieved millisecond-scale solve times for high-dimensional robotic systems, demonstrated by a 50 Hz whole-body control on a physical quadruped robot and 100% empirical safety in obstacle avoidance under disturbances.

## Problem

- Traditional Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) is often too slow for real-time applications on high-dimensional robotic systems due to unfavorable scaling with system dimension and prediction horizon.
- Ensuring robust safety under disturbance typically relies on reachability analysis, which is computationally intractable for systems exceeding a few states, and data-driven methods lack formal guarantees.
- Existing GPU-accelerated NMPC solutions often lack native support for inequality constraints or fail to incorporate real-time reachability analysis for formal safety guarantees.

## Method

- A GPU-parallelized framework, GPU-SLS, unifies inequality-constrained NMPC with System Level Synthesis (SLS) within a Real-Time Iteration (RTI) scheme.
- A novel GPU ADMM QP solver, leveraging parallel associative scans and factorization caching, efficiently solves the nominal trajectory optimization with logarithmic complexity.
- GPU-parallelized SLS computes robust disturbance-feedback controllers and closed-loop reachable sets, using these to tighten nominal constraints and ensure robust feasibility under uncertainty.

## Results

- GPU-SLS reduced solve times by up to **99.8%** compared to CPU solvers (OSQP, HPIPM) and **71.8%** compared to existing GPU solvers for NMPC benchmarks, demonstrating superior scaling with problem size.
- Achieved **100%** empirical safety (zero collisions) in obstacle avoidance tasks for a Dubins car under adversarial disturbances, outperforming learning-based reachability and classical MPC.
- Successfully deployed on a physical Unitree Go2 EDU quadruped robot, performing whole-body constrained NMPC at 50 Hz with an average solve time of 16 ms (for a 25-step horizon), enabling safe navigation through obstacle fields.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving logarithmic-depth computational complexity for both the equality-constrained LQR primal update and SLS controller synthesis by using parallel associative scans on the GPU.
> - Employing ADMM for inequality-constrained QP enables efficient GPU parallelization and improved numerical robustness compared to augmented Lagrangian methods.
> - Integrating SLS with NMPC allows for the co-design of nominal trajectories and robust feedback controllers, providing verifiable safety guarantees against disturbances in real-time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of safe, real-time control methods is a prerequisite for sustained autonomous operation of robotic systems. This necessity has driven research into two primary areas: trajectory optimization and reachability analysis. Model Predictive Control (MPC), particularly Nonlinear MPC (NMPC), is a widely adopted trajectory optimization method for synthesizing dynamically feasible paths and control inputs that adhere to task and safety constraints. Concurrently, reachability analysis, which approximates the set of all states reachable by a system from a given initial state under all admissible inputs and disturbances, provides a mechanism for verifying the robustness of closed-loop behavior against uncertainties.

Despite their individual successes, constrained trajectory optimization and reachability methods face significant challenges in scaling to large, high-dimensional problems. For complex robotic systems, such as legged robots with over 60 states, NMPC often operates too slowly for real-time applications. Traditional nonlinear reachability analysis methods, such as Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability, generally become computationally intractable for systems exceeding approximately 10 states. While data-driven reachability approaches have demonstrated improved scalability, their estimates can be imprecise, potentially compromising safety.

System Level Synthesis (SLS) has emerged as a scalable alternative that facilitates both reachability analysis and the co-design of robust control policies. SLS offers a convex parameterization of closed-loop responses for linear time-varying (LTV) systems, which can be extended to nonlinear dynamics through linearization, enabling robust constraint satisfaction under disturbance. However, existing SLS implementations have historically been too slow for real-time control of high-dimensional robotic systems.

Recent efforts have focused on leveraging GPU acceleration to enhance the performance of NMPC, achieving real-time operation for certain applications. Nevertheless, many of these GPU-accelerated methods either neglect inequality constraints or do not incorporate reachability analysis, thereby lacking formal guarantees of safety under uncertainty. This research addresses these limitations by integrating GPU-accelerated NMPC with SLS to enable real-time, robust, and safe control for high-dimensional, uncertain nonlinear robotic systems, while explicitly handling inequality constraints and providing verifiable safety assurances.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a GPU-parallelized framework, termed GPU-SLS, for safe, robust nonlinear model predictive control that is scalable to high-dimensional uncertain robotic systems and long planning horizons, operating in real-time within millisecond timescales. This overarching goal is motivated by several critical gaps in existing control methodologies for complex robotic applications:

1. **Computational Bottleneck in NMPC for High-Dimensional Systems:** Traditional NMPC methods, particularly those involving sequential quadratic programming (SQP) and interior-point solvers, exhibit computational costs that scale unfavorably with system dimension and prediction horizon. This often renders them unsuitable for real-time control of robots with many degrees of freedom (e.g., legged robots, humanoids) that require high control update rates (e.g., 50 Hz or faster). The objective is to significantly reduce the solve times for nominal trajectory optimization.

2. **Intractability of Robustness Guarantees:** Ensuring safety under disturbance for nonlinear systems typically involves reachability analysis, which traditionally suffers from severe scalability issues, becoming intractable for systems with more than a few states. While data-driven approaches offer some scalability, they often lack formal safety guarantees and can fail in practice. The motivation is to integrate a scalable and robust verification method that provides formal guarantees for closed-loop behavior under uncertainty.

3. **Lack of Real-Time Integrated Safe and Robust Control:** Existing GPU-accelerated NMPC solutions often lack native support for inequality constraints or do not incorporate reachability analysis. Conversely, scalable robust control methods like SLS have not achieved real-time performance on GPUs for high-dimensional systems. This work aims to bridge this gap by creating a unified framework that jointly optimizes dynamically-feasible nominal trajectories, robust tracking controllers, and closed-loop reachable sets under disturbance, all within real-time constraints and on GPU hardware.

To achieve these objectives, the research sets forth several specific aims:

- **Develop a GPU-accelerated inequality-constrained linear quadratic regulator (LQR) solver:** This solver is based on the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) and leverages factorization caching and parallel associative scans to efficiently handle the quadratic programs arising from SQP.
- **Integrate the LQR solver into a real-time NMPC method:** The goal is to enable nonlinear inequality-constrained trajectory optimization with a computational complexity of O(log N log^2 n_x + log^2 n_u) per SQP iteration.
- **Formulate a unified GPU-parallelized method for real-time reachability, trajectory optimization, and disturbance-feedback synthesis via SLS:** This component aims for similar logarithmic-depth complexity, enabling robust constraint satisfaction by tightening nominal constraints based on calculated reachable sets.
- **Validate the approach on large-scale, complex nonlinear systems:** This includes demonstrating performance on humanoid and quadruped robots with a large number of states and control inputs, long planning horizons, and a high number of decision variables and constraints, while achieving high empirical safety rates and fast solve speeds compared to existing baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed GPU-SLS method integrates several computational techniques to achieve real-time, safe, and robust nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) for high-dimensional systems. The core approach revolves around a GPU-parallelized framework that iteratively optimizes nominal trajectories and robust controllers/reachable sets.

**Overall Architecture:**
The method employs a Real-Time Iteration (RTI) scheme, where at each control step, a single Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) iteration is performed. This iteration involves linearizing the nonlinear dynamics and constraints around a nominal trajectory and solving a resulting Linear Time-Varying Quadratic Program (LTV-QP). The solution to this QP yields an update to the nominal trajectory. Concurrently, a robust controller is synthesized using System Level Synthesis (SLS), which also computes closed-loop reachable sets. These reachable sets are then used to tighten the constraints for the subsequent nominal trajectory optimization, ensuring robust feasibility under disturbance.

**A. GPU ADMM QP Solver for Nominal Trajectory Optimization**

1. **Problem Formulation:** The NMPC problem is cast as a sequence of constrained optimal control problems with linear time-varying (LTV) dynamics, solved as QPs within an SQP loop. The QP objective minimizes a quadratic cost, subject to linearized dynamics, initial state constraints, and linearized inequality constraints.
2. **ADMM Reformulation:** To efficiently handle the inequality constraints on the GPU, the QP is reformulated using the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM). A split variable `z` is introduced to represent the left-hand side of the inequality constraints, and an indicator function is used to enforce `z` within a convex set. The augmented Lagrangian incorporates these split variables and a quadratic penalty term.
3. **ADMM Update Steps:**
 - **Primal Update (δx, δu):** This step involves solving an equality-constrained LQR problem, where the cost function is augmented with terms derived from the ADMM penalty and dual variables.
 - **Split Variable Update (z):** This update reduces to a component-wise projection of the current state and input (mapped through the constraint matrices) onto the convex set defined by the inequality constraints and the dual variables.
 - **Dual Variable Update (λ):** A simple gradient ascent step updates the Lagrange multipliers based on the difference between the primal and split variables.
 The `z`- and `λ`-updates are fully parallelizable across time and constraints. The primary computational bottleneck resides in the primal update.
4. **Efficiently Solving the Primal Update via Associative Scans:**
 - The equality-constrained LQR problem (primal update) is traditionally solved using sequential Riccati recursions. To enable GPU parallelization, the method utilizes reverse parallel associative scan operations.
 - **Conditional Value Function (CVF):** The problem's value function is expressed in a form (CVF) that allows for associative combination across time steps. This enables computing the Riccati terms (P, p) via a logarithmic-depth parallel associative scan. Each step of this scan requires matrix inversions (O(n_x^2)) and matrix multiplications, leading to an O(log N log^2 n_x) complexity for the scan.
 - **Conditional Optimal Trajectory (COT):** Once the Riccati terms are computed, a forward parallel associative scan, using a Conditional Optimal Trajectory (COT) formulation, parallelizes the recovery of the nominal state (δx) and control (δu) updates.
 - The total complexity for solving the LQR problem is O(log N log^2 n_x + log^2 n_u) on parallel hardware, a significant improvement over sequential methods.
5. **Caching for Accelerated Associative Scans:** To further optimize ADMM performance when the penalty parameter `ρ` is kept fixed for multiple iterations, the method caches the iteration-invariant matrix factorizations (e.g., `P_cache`, `C_cache`, `Υ_cache`, `Ψ_cache`). This avoids recomputing expensive matrix inversions in subsequent ADMM iterations, reducing the complexity for those iterations to O(log N log n_x + log n_u).

**B. Robust Nonlinear MPC via System Level Synthesis (SLS)**

1. **Problem Formulation:** To achieve robust constraint satisfaction under disturbance, the NMPC is unified with SLS. This involves optimizing over causal disturbance feedback controllers. The SLS problem minimizes a quadratic cost on system-level responses (Φ matrices, which capture disturbance influence) subject to linearized dynamics and disturbance propagation equations. The core constraints are tightened by `h_k(Φ)` and `h_f(Φ)` terms, which quantify conservative margins induced by disturbance propagation.
2. **FastSLS Decomposition:** Similar to FastSLS, the robust policy optimization is decomposed into an iterative procedure that alternates between optimizing the nominal trajectory (already described) and optimizing the robust controller.
3. **GPU Parallelization for SLS Controller Update:** The SLS controller update problem involves solving N independent Riccati recursions and N independent forward propagations, one for each disturbance injection time `j`.
 - **Reverse Parallel Associative Scan for Riccati Terms:** For each disturbance `j`, a separate Conditional Value Function (CVF) is defined for the Riccati recursions, enabling a parallel associative scan to compute the Riccati terms (P_j) and feedback gains (K_j) in O(log N log^2 n_x + log^2 n_u) time.
 - **Forward Parallel Associative Scan for Disturbance Propagation:** A Conditional Optimal Propagation (COP) formulation is used with a forward parallel associative scan to compute the system-level response matrices (Φx_k,j, Φu_k,j) in O(log N log^2 n_x + log^2 n_u) time.
 - **Dual Variables (τ):** The dual variables (`τ`) from the nominal trajectory optimization (specifically from the Lagrange multipliers `λ`) are used to inform the SLS objective, selectively penalizing disturbance propagation for constraints that are currently active.

**C. Real-Time Iteration (RTI)**

The method adopts an RTI framework for online operation. In this scheme, only one SQP iteration is performed per control step. The controller update is warm-started using the `τ` variables from the previous iteration. The resulting constraint tightenings (`h_k`, `h_f`) are then used to solve the tightened nominal trajectory optimization for the current control step. This approach prioritizes speed, with linearization errors being corrected by subsequent MPC updates as the robot executes its trajectory and relinearizes around the new state. The total solve time for one RTI update consists of one linearization, one controller update, and one tightened nominal trajectory update.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GPU-SLS focused on computational efficiency, safety guarantees, and practical applicability across various robotic systems, including hardware validation.

**A. Constrained Nonlinear MPC Benchmarks:**
- **Performance against CPU Solvers:** On a torque-constrained 10-link inverted pendulum task, GPU-SLS substantially outperformed CPU-based solvers. It reduced solve times by 99.8% relative to OSQP and 98.9% relative to HPIPM in long-horizon scenarios.
- **Performance against GPU Solvers:** GPU-SLS also demonstrated superior performance compared to existing GPU-accelerated methods, achieving a 71.8% reduction in solve times compared to primal-dual iLQR AL. It also outperformed cparcon-IPM and cparcon-ADMM.
- **Scaling:** The method's logarithmic scaling with respect to horizon length, state, control, and constraint dimensions enabled solving problems with up to 1.35 × 10^5 decision variables within 73 milliseconds (for N=3000 on an n-link pendulum family), outperforming baselines that exhibit cubic scaling.
- **Numerical Robustness:** The ADMM-based approach demonstrated greater robustness to ill-conditioned problems and reduced GPU precision (FP32) compared to augmented Lagrangian methods, which can diverge under such conditions.

**B. SLS Benchmarks (Safety and Speed):**
- **Empirical Safety:** In an obstacle avoidance task for a Dubins car subject to adversarial and random disturbances, GPU-SLS achieved 100% empirical safety, with no obstacle collisions across all rollouts. This contrasts with a learning-based reachability baseline (DeepReach), which failed to certify safety in 6 out of 31 rollouts.
- **Robustness over Classical MPC:** For a 6D planar quadrotor, GPU-SLS consistently avoided all obstacles under disturbance, whereas classical closed-loop MPC failed in 6 out of 31 executions. The robustness procedure introduced minimal computational overhead, with the controller solve accounting for approximately 3% of the total computation time.
- **Speedup over FastSLS:** GPU-SLS achieved significant speedups over FastSLS, the state-of-the-art CPU solver for SLS, with a maximum speedup of 237.5× for the longest prediction horizons. This performance gain is attributed to GPU-SLS's logarithmic scaling versus FastSLS's cubic scaling.
- **Large-Scale Environment Handling:** The method successfully generated robust trajectories for a Dubins car navigating through a dense field of 30 obstacles over a long horizon (20m), consistently keeping the system within calculated robust tubes.

**C. Legged Experiments (Simulation and Hardware):**
- **Humanoid Control (75D):** In MuJoCo simulation, a 75-state, 19-control Unitree H1 humanoid robot successfully navigated around obstacles. The synthesized robust controller ensured the humanoid's state remained within computed robust tubes even under external disturbances. Robust policies were calculated in approximately 22 milliseconds on average.
- **Quadruped Simulation (61D):** For a 61-state quadruped, GPU-SLS outperformed the CPU-based OSQP solver by 95% at the longest horizon, requiring fewer ADMM iterations for convergence.
- **Quadruped Hardware Validation (Unitree Go2 EDU):** The RTI GPU-SLS algorithm was deployed on a physical Unitree Go2 EDU quadruped (61 states, 12 controls). The robot executed whole-body constrained NMPC at 50 Hz, with an average solve time of 16 ms for a prediction horizon of 25 steps. It successfully navigated through obstacle fields in a collision-free manner (Fig. 1). Real-time reachability analysis on hardware demonstrated that the robot consistently remained within its robust tubes while navigating around an obstacle, confirming the practicality of the approach for high-dimensional nonlinear systems in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GPU-SLS framework represents a notable advancement in the field of robotic control, addressing several long-standing challenges in deploying advanced control strategies on complex physical systems.

**Significance:**
The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to enable real-time, safe, and robust nonlinear model predictive control for high-dimensional robotic systems, a capability that has been computationally prohibitive with prior methods. By unifying inequality-constrained NMPC with System Level Synthesis (SLS) and leveraging GPU parallelism through novel ADMM and associative scan implementations, the authors have developed a solver that achieves logarithmic-depth scaling. This scaling effectively removes a critical computational bottleneck that previously limited the application of robust, formally-verifiable control to simpler systems or offline planning. The empirical validation, including hardware deployment on a quadruped and extensive simulation with humanoids, supports the practical viability and efficacy of the approach.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Enabling Advanced Robotic Autonomy:** The millisecond-scale solution times and robust safety guarantees provided by GPU-SLS can facilitate the deployment of more sophisticated and intelligent behaviors on complex robotic platforms, such as legged robots, humanoids, and manipulators, in dynamic and uncertain environments. This can lead to increased autonomy, agility, and task performance in applications ranging from logistics and industrial automation to search and rescue.
2. **Enhancing Safety in Human-Robot Interaction:** The capability for real-time reachability analysis and robust constraint satisfaction under disturbance is crucial for safety-critical applications, particularly in environments shared with humans. By providing verifiable guarantees that a robot will remain within safe operational bounds despite uncertainties, GPU-SLS can increase trust and accelerate the adoption of robots in diverse settings.
3. **Broadening the Scope of Model-Based Control:** By making formally robust nonlinear control computationally tractable for large-scale systems, this work can rejuvenate interest and research in model-based control approaches. It provides a powerful tool that combines the predictive power of MPC with the rigorous safety assurances of reachability, without the accuracy limitations often associated with purely data-driven methods.
4. **Facilitating Research and Development:** The open-source availability of the GPU-SLS implementation has the potential to accelerate further research and development in robust control, motion planning, and robot learning. Researchers can build upon this efficient backend to explore more complex control objectives, integrate with perception systems, or develop adaptive disturbance models.
5. **Addressing "Sim-to-Real" Gaps:** The robust tube-based approach, which accounts for disturbances and model uncertainties, can help mitigate the "sim-to-real" gap, where controllers developed in simulation often fail to perform robustly on physical hardware due to unmodeled dynamics and external perturbations. The explicit computation and use of reachable sets provide a buffer against such discrepancies.
