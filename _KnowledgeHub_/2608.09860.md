# Entanglement-Free Trajectory Planning for Tethered Mobile Robots with a Slack Tether

> [!summary] Summary
> A three-stage motion planner for tethered mobile robots with a slack tether. It builds a simplicial-complex model of the entanglement-free configuration space by checking an entanglement predicate only at simplex vertices, enumerates homotopically distinct candidate paths on that model, then solves a homotopy-constrained mixed-integer trajectory problem coupling robot and tether dynamics. It builds models orders of magnitude faster than homotopy-augmented graphs and plans trajectories in about 0.72 s.

## Problem

- Most tethered-robot planners assume a taut tether, reducing entanglement to pure geometry, which demands a tether-management winch and drags the cable against obstacles.
- Slack-tether methods judge entanglement on the tautened shortest path and hard-code one tether model and one entanglement definition, so they generalize poorly.
- Homotopy-augmented graphs are costly to build, only discretely approximate configuration space, and obstacle-induced disconnected homotopy classes trap trajectory optimizers in local minima.

## Method

- Algorithm 1 lifts a ==constrained Delaunay triangulation== into the ==universal covering space==, admitting a triangle only when tether length and the entanglement indicator hold at its three vertices, building the **entanglement-free complex**.
- Path planning runs on the complex's primal and dual graphs, enumerating ==homotopically distinct== entanglement-free paths and, from the length-reachable complex, disentangling paths out of an already-entangled state.
- Trajectory generation solves a ==mixed-integer program== whose ==big-M constraints== confine the robot to the triangles crossed by the candidate path, coupled to ==lumped-mass tether dynamics==; a ==MIQP relaxation== warm-starts the ==MINLP==.

## Results

- On the hardest environment (**15** obstacles, tether **15** units), building the entanglement-free complex took **57.18 s** versus **540.98 s** and **10,479.63 s** for the entanglement-checked homotopy-augmented graph at resolutions **0.5** and **0.25**.
- Path-planning queries averaged **0.001 s** on the complex; on the largest map they ran **0.0041 s** versus **0.0220 s** and **0.1004 s** at the two graph resolutions.
- The MIQP relaxation returned entanglement-free trajectories in **0.35** to **1.71 s** (mean **0.72** plus-minus **0.46 s**), never violating the constraint; parallel homotopy classes gave costs **5.50**, **7.89**, **11.08**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verifying a constraint only at simplex vertices, when a convexity argument makes it invariant inside, turns a continuous feasibility check into finitely many tests.
> - Decoupling homotopy-class selection from in-class trajectory optimization keeps each subproblem connected, and optimizing several classes in parallel trades compute for closer-to-global solutions.
> - Caution: strict entanglement definitions can shrink feasible area below 2%, and all evidence is 2D simulation with a relaxation that ignores tether dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tethered mobile robots are utilized across various applications, including search and rescue, infrastructure inspection, and underwater exploration, due to their capacity to provide continuous power and reliable communication via a physical cable. However, a significant operational challenge for these robots is the risk of tether entanglement. Entanglement, where the tether winds around obstacles or forms knots, can lead to task failure, suboptimal trajectories, or necessitate external intervention.

The existing literature on motion planning for tethered robots largely simplifies the problem by assuming a "taut" tether configuration. A taut tether, typically managed by a dedicated system, always forms a locally shortest path, allowing its configuration and entanglement state to be determined solely from environmental geometry and the robot's path. While this simplifies planning and entanglement evaluation, it necessitates a tether management system, introduces tension forces on the robot, and increases the risk of tether damage or snagging around obstacles.

In contrast, planning for "slack" tethers, where a dedicated management system may be absent and the tether's shape is influenced by its dynamics, robot trajectory, and exogenous forces, presents a more complex problem. Current approaches for slack tethers often simplify entanglement definitions to geometric criteria or evaluate entanglement on a "tautened" version of the tether. These methods tend to be tailored to specific tether dynamics and lack generality. The direct optimization of trajectories considering both robot and slack tether dynamics, along with general entanglement definitions, is computationally challenging due to high dimensionality, nonlinearity, non-convexity, and the presence of multiple homotopy classes that trap solvers in local minima.

This work addresses these limitations by developing a comprehensive motion planning framework specifically designed for tethered mobile robots with slack tethers. It aims to integrate a general definition of entanglement into all stages of the planning process, compute dynamically feasible trajectories, and systematically handle the topological complexities arising from obstacles, thereby offering a more robust and general solution compared to previous approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel motion planning algorithm that computes dynamically feasible entanglement-free trajectories for tethered mobile robots operating with a slack tether in environments with static obstacles. This overarching objective is driven by several detailed motivations:

- **Address Limitations of Taut Tether Models:** The majority of existing work relies on the assumption of a taut tether, which simplifies planning but has practical drawbacks. These include the necessity of a dedicated tether management system, the constant tension exerted on the robot, and the increased susceptibility of the tether to damage or snagging when bending around obstacles. The authors aim to move beyond this simplification to account for the more complex and realistic scenario of a slack tether.

- **Overcome Computational Challenges of Slack Tether Dynamics:** When a tether is slack, its shape and entanglement state are not purely geometric but depend on the robot's trajectory and the tether's dynamics, which can be highly complex, nonlinear, and involve interactions with obstacles. Directly optimizing trajectories under such conditions is computationally expensive and difficult. The research is motivated by the need for a tractable approach to handle these dynamics and general entanglement definitions.

- **Manage Topological Complexity and Homotopy Classes:** Obstacles in the environment create multiple topologically distinct paths, or homotopy classes, between two points. In motion planning, this leads to a solution space that is disconnected, making optimization difficult as solvers can get trapped in local minima. The paper aims to explicitly account for and efficiently navigate these homotopy classes to ensure that potential entanglement-free solutions across different topological configurations are explored. This is particularly relevant for tethered robots, where homotopy constraints naturally arise.

- **Integrate Entanglement Avoidance Holistically:** Current methods often evaluate entanglement post-planning or rely on simplified geometric criteria. A key motivation here is to consider the entanglement state throughout the entire planning pipeline—from initial configuration space modeling to path and trajectory generation—to compute inherently safer trajectories that prevent entanglement during robot motion.

- **Enable Disentanglement Planning:** Beyond avoiding initial entanglement, the research seeks to provide a mechanism for disentanglement. If a robot finds itself in an entangled state, the planning algorithm should be capable of computing a trajectory to guide it back to a non-entangled configuration, enhancing the robot's operational resilience.

- **Promote Generality and Flexibility:** The authors intend for the proposed pipeline to be flexible and adaptable to different robot types (e.g., aerial, underwater, space), various tether dynamic models, and a broad range of entanglement definitions. This aims to create a versatile framework that is not constrained by specific application domains or simplified assumptions about the tether.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed motion planning algorithm is structured as a three-step pipeline designed to compute dynamically feasible, entanglement-free trajectories for tethered mobile robots with slack tethers.

#### 3.1. Step 1: Construction of a Topological Model of the Entanglement-Free Configuration Space

The first step involves building a topological model of the entanglement-free configuration space, denoted as $\mathcal{W}$, and the length-constrained configuration space, $\mathcal{R}$.
- **Reduced Configuration Space:** The configuration of a tethered robot is an infinite-dimensional path. To make this tractable, the concept of a "reduced configuration" is introduced, defined by the robot's end-effector position and the homotopy class of the tether path connecting it to a fixed anchor point. This reduced configuration lives in $\mathcal{R} = \mathcal{W}_{\text{free}} \times \mathbb{F}_m$, where $\mathbb{F}_m$ is the free group representing homotopy classes induced by $m$ obstacles.
- **Simplicial Complex Model:** The authors adopt a simplicial complex model, specifically a triangulation, to represent $\mathcal{R}$ and $\mathcal{W}$. A 2D polygonal workspace is triangulated (constrained Delaunay triangulation), and this triangulation is then "lifted" to the universal covering space of the workspace. Each "lifted" triangle corresponds to a specific region in the workspace reachable via a specific homotopy class.
- **Efficient Constraint Verification:** A crucial aspect of this step is the efficient verification of both tether length and entanglement avoidance constraints during the construction of the simplicial complex.
 - **Tether Length Constraint:** This ensures that only configurations reachable within the maximum tether length $l$ are included in $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}$.
 - **Entanglement Avoidance Constraint:** The paper leverages two key propositions for efficient entanglement checking:
 - **Proposition 1 (Entanglement state of shortest path):** For a broad set of binary entanglement definitions, if the shortest path in a given homotopy class is entangled, then all paths in that homotopy class are entangled. This reduces the check for an entire homotopy class to evaluating only its shortest path.
 - **Proposition 3 (Entanglement-free triangle):** If all vertices of a $k$-simplex (e.g., a triangle) can be reached by an entanglement-free shortest path (in a specific homotopy class), then all points within that simplex can also be reached by an entanglement-free path in the same homotopy class. This allows for efficient verification at the level of simplices, reducing the number of points at which the entanglement definition must be evaluated.
- **Algorithm 1:** This algorithm incrementally builds the simplicial complex models $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}$ (length-constrained) and $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$ (entanglement-free). It starts from the anchor point and iteratively adds adjacent triangles to the complex, checking tether length and entanglement constraints for the vertices of each triangle. If a triangle's vertices (in a given homotopy class) satisfy both constraints, the triangle is added to $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$; if only the length constraint is met, it's added to $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}$. This process prunes homotopy classes and regions that do not admit feasible entanglement-free tether configurations.
- **Conservativeness Reduction (Appendix A):** The model can be conservative, as an entire triangle is excluded if even one vertex violates a constraint. A strategy is proposed to mitigate this by adding new triangles at the boundaries of the complex, effectively "filling in" parts of the configuration space that are genuinely feasible but were initially excluded.

#### 3.2. Step 2: Entanglement-Free Path Planning

Once the simplicial complex model $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$ is constructed, it is used for path planning.
- **Path Planning on $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$:** Since $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$ is a simply connected manifold where every point is guaranteed to be reachable via an entanglement-free tether configuration, standard graph search algorithms (e.g., Dijkstra, A*) or sampling-based planners (e.g., RRT, RRT*) can be applied to its underlying primal or dual graphs. This yields paths for the robot that remain in entanglement-free regions of the configuration space.
- **Enumeration of Multiple Paths:** The approach allows for the enumeration of multiple homotopically distinct entanglement-free paths between the start and goal. These paths serve as candidate initial solutions for the subsequent trajectory optimization phase.
- **Disentanglement Path Planning:** For scenarios where the robot's initial tether configuration is entangled, the algorithm uses the larger $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}$ model. A path planning query is run on $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}$ from the robot's current location to any point that is also within $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$. This provides a path for the robot to move from an entangled state to a non-entangled one.

#### 3.3. Step 3: Computation of Dynamically Feasible Entanglement-Free Trajectories

The candidate paths obtained from Step 2 are then used to compute dynamically feasible trajectories.
- **Homotopy-Constrained Trajectory Generation:** This is formulated as a mixed-integer optimization problem. The key is to constrain the robot's trajectory to lie within the same homotopy class as a selected candidate path $\alpha$. This is achieved by defining a "polygonal state constraint" as the union of all triangles in the base triangulation of $\mathcal{W}_{\text{free}}$ that are traversed by $\alpha$. Mixed-integer constraints (using Big-M formulation) enforce that the robot's position at each time step must be within one of these specific triangles.
- **Dynamic Models:** The optimization problem incorporates:
 - **Robot Dynamics:** A discrete-time dynamic model of the robot (e.g., a double integrator for an omnidirectional robot) relates control inputs to robot states (position and velocity).
 - **Tether Dynamics:** A low-tension lumped-mass or finite-element model is used to simulate the tether's behavior, including collision detection and resolution with obstacles. The robot's position is a boundary condition for the tether dynamics, alongside the fixed anchor point.
- **Objective Function:** The objective function typically minimizes control effort (e.g., quadratic cost on control inputs) and a term related to the distance to the goal region.
- **Entanglement Constraint in Optimization:** A non-entanglement constraint (based on the chosen definition $\delta$) is included for all tether configurations throughout the trajectory, ensuring dynamic entanglement avoidance.
- **Computational Efficiency Strategy:** Solving the full Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Program (MINLP) can be computationally intensive. A two-step approach is proposed:
 1. First, a relaxed Mixed-Integer Quadratic Program (MIQP) is solved by temporarily dropping the complex tether dynamics and related entanglement constraints. This yields a dynamically feasible robot trajectory that respects the homotopy class.
 2. Second, the tether trajectory is simulated using the obtained robot trajectory, and the entanglement constraint is verified. If satisfied, the MIQP solution is accepted. If not, the MIQP solution serves as an initial guess for the full MINLP, which is then solved. This often allows for fast solutions in the first step.
- **Parallel Trajectory Optimization:** Given multiple candidate paths from Step 2, multiple trajectory optimization problems can be run in parallel, each yielding a homotopically distinct, dynamically feasible, entanglement-free trajectory. These can then be compared (e.g., by cost) to select the optimal one for execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings validated through numerical simulations across various environments and entanglement definitions.

#### 4.1. Entanglement-Free Simplicial Complex Model

- **Computational Efficiency:** The proposed Algorithm 1 for constructing the simplicial complex models ($\tilde{\mathcal{R}}$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$) demonstrates significant computational advantages over traditional homotopy-augmented graph models. For instance, the simplicial complex models could be built in fractions of a second (e.g., 0.01s to 33.40s for larger environments and tether lengths), whereas homotopy-augmented graphs (even with coarser resolutions) required orders of magnitude longer (e.g., 0.35s to over 7660s). This suggests potential for real-time planning.
- **Memory Footprint:** The simplicial complex models also exhibited a substantially smaller memory footprint (number of 2-simplices) compared to the number of nodes in homotopy-augmented graphs, despite being continuous models of the configuration space.
- **Configuration Space Reduction:** The introduction of non-entanglement constraints during model construction resulted in a considerable reduction in the size of the feasible configuration space. Depending on the environment and tether length, the area covered by the entanglement-free model $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$ could be as low as 0.31% of the length-reachable space $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}$, highlighting how entanglement severely limits maneuverability. The impact varied with the chosen entanglement definition, with Definition 6 (Obstacle-free Convex Hull) generally imposing stricter constraints than Definition 9 (Local Visibility Homotopy).
- **Conservativeness Reduction:** The proposed method in Appendix A for reducing the conservativeness of the simplicial complex model successfully increased the covered feasible area, narrowing the gap with the area covered by homotopy-augmented graphs. In some complex environments, the reduced-conservativeness simplicial complex even covered a larger area than the homotopy-augmented graphs, partly attributed to the latter's use of Manhattan distance heuristics.

#### 4.2. Path Planning and Disentangling Path Planning

- **Efficient Path Discovery:** Path planning queries on the $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$ models were solved rapidly, with average computation times around 0.001s for entanglement-free paths, enabling fast decision-making.
- **Entanglement-Free Paths:** The algorithm successfully identified paths that ensured the tether remained in a non-entangled state throughout the motion. It demonstrated scenarios where a path chosen from an unconstrained model ($\tilde{\mathcal{R}}$) would lead to entanglement, while the path from $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$ remained entanglement-free.
- **Enumeration of Alternatives:** The method effectively enumerated multiple homotopically distinct entanglement-free paths, providing the planner with several viable options to reach a goal.
- **Disentanglement Capability:** The approach was validated for disentanglement planning, where the robot started from an entangled configuration. The algorithm computed paths on the length-constrained model $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}$ to reach a region within $\tilde{\mathcal{W}}$ (where entanglement-free paths exist), demonstrating the ability to guide the robot to a safe, non-entangled state under different entanglement definitions.

#### 4.3. Entanglement-Free Trajectory Generation

- **Dynamically Feasible Trajectories:** The pipeline successfully computed dynamically feasible trajectories that guided the robot to its goal while maintaining the slack tether in an entanglement-free configuration. This was demonstrated in various scenarios, including an environment with water current acting on the tether and a disentanglement scenario.
- **Computational Tractability for Trajectories:** By employing a two-step optimization strategy (MIQP relaxation followed by full MINLP if needed), the trajectory generation problem achieved practical solution times, ranging from 0.35s to 1.71s (average 0.72s). In the simulations, the MIQP relaxation consistently produced entanglement-free trajectories, suggesting its practical effectiveness for this type of problem.
- **Parallel Optimization and Selection:** The framework demonstrated the ability to compute and compare multiple homotopically distinct trajectories in parallel. By evaluating these trajectories based on a cost function, the system could select the most efficient entanglement-free path, showcasing a complete and effective planning process. For instance, three distinct trajectories had costs of 5.50, 7.89, and 11.08, allowing selection of the optimal one.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of motion planning for tethered mobile robots, particularly by addressing the complex challenges associated with slack tethers and general entanglement definitions.

- **Enhanced Safety and Reliability:** By integrating entanglement avoidance into all stages of the planning pipeline, the proposed method can generate inherently safer and more reliable trajectories. This is crucial for long-duration missions or operations in hazardous environments where tether entanglement poses a high risk to mission success and robot integrity. The ability to perform disentanglement planning further enhances operational safety by providing recovery strategies.
- **Increased Operational Autonomy:** The framework's capacity to handle slack tethers, which are more common in practical applications than strictly taut ones, expands the operational envelopes of tethered robots. It reduces reliance on complex tether management systems and external human intervention, promoting greater autonomy.
- **Generality and Flexibility:** The approach's flexibility across various robot dynamics, tether models, and entanglement definitions makes it broadly applicable to different tethered robotic platforms (aerial, underwater, space) and diverse operational scenarios. This adaptability future-proofs the methodology against evolving robot designs and mission requirements.
- **Computational Efficiency for Topological Planning:** The use of a simplicial complex model for the entanglement-free configuration space offers a computationally efficient and memory-light alternative to existing graph-based methods. This efficiency is critical for enabling global motion planning within practical time constraints, potentially paving the way for online adaptation in certain applications.
- **Systematic Homotopy Handling:** Explicitly considering and navigating different homotopy classes, both in path generation and trajectory optimization, is a robust way to explore the full solution space. This mitigates the risk of local minima and ensures that globally optimal or highly performant entanglement-free trajectories can be discovered.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a robust foundation for future research, particularly in extending the methodology to 3D environments, further optimizing the trajectory generation phase, and validating the approach in real-world experiments. The identified areas for future work directly address key challenges in advancing tethered robot capabilities.

In summary, this work contributes a comprehensive and robust framework for entanglement-free trajectory planning, significantly advancing the state of the art for slack tethered mobile robots and enabling their safer and more effective deployment in complex environments.
