# MLA: A Multisensory Language-Action Model for Multimodal Understanding and Forecasting in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, CUHK, and X-Humanoid developed MLA, a multisensory language-action model that integrates 2D images, 3D point clouds, and tactile signals using an encoder-free alignment and multisensory future state forecasting. The model demonstrated an average 12% increase in success rate over state-of-the-art 2D VLA models in complex real-world robotic manipulation tasks and achieved 81% average success in RLBench simulations.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models predominantly use 2D image inputs, which are insufficient for capturing complex spatial dependencies and physical dynamics in real-world robotic manipulation.
- Integrating new sensory inputs into VLAs typically requires inefficient modality-specific encoders that often lack robotic-specific pre-training and struggle to align with the LLM backbone.
- Existing VLA approaches for predicting future states are often limited to 2D visual information, failing to provide comprehensive understanding of 3D geometric structures or tactile interactions crucial for complex physical dynamics.

## Method

- Introduces **MLA**, a model that repurposes a ==Large Language Model== (LLaMA-2 7B) as a unified perception-and-reasoning policy, directly tokenizing 2D images, 3D point clouds, and tactile signals.
- Proposes an encoder-free multimodal alignment mechanism utilizing token-level contrastive learning on shallow LLM layers to effectively integrate diverse sensory inputs without dedicated encoders.
- Implements a future multisensory generation strategy during post-training, enabling forecasting of future images, point clouds, and tactile states to enhance physical dynamics reasoning and action generation.

## Results

- MLA achieved an average success rate increase of **12%** over the state-of-the-art 2D VLA model (π_0) and **24%** over the state-of-the-art 3D VLA model (SpatialVLA) on six real-world robotic manipulation tasks.
- Ablation studies indicated that token-level contrastive alignment improved accuracy by **7%** compared to image-level alignment, and that comprehensive multisensory future generation across images, point clouds, and tactile signals significantly enhanced action robustness.
- The model demonstrated strong generalization, experiencing less performance degradation than baselines in real-world scenarios with unseen manipulated objects and complex background environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Repurposing initial LLM transformer blocks with token-level contrastive learning effectively aligns diverse multisensory inputs, demonstrating superior performance and efficiency compared to separate modality-specific encoders.
> - Optimally, multimodal feature alignment occurs in shallower LLM layers, allowing subsequent layers to specialize in predicting future states and generating actions more effectively.
> - Generating comprehensive future multisensory states (image, point cloud, tactile) is critical for robust physical dynamics reasoning and action generation, as shown by accuracy decreases when any future modality prediction is omitted.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements, particularly with the emergence of imitation learning policies trained on expert demonstrations and the integration of large vision-language models (VLMs) for common-sense reasoning. This has led to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which extend VLMs by incorporating robot demonstrations for action prediction, thereby achieving generalization and precise manipulation by mapping human instructions and visual observations to robot control signals.

However, existing VLA models primarily rely on 2D image inputs. While effective for some tasks, 2D vision is fundamentally limited in capturing complete spatial dependencies and modeling physical dynamics within the real world. Robots must perceive spatial environments, infer semantic relationships, and interact with dynamic physical configurations, necessitating a more comprehensive understanding beyond visual cues.

Previous research has attempted to address these limitations by augmenting VLAs with richer multimodal observations. Some approaches integrate 3D inputs, such as point clouds, to enhance geometric scene understanding, while others introduce tactile signals to capture interaction feedback from manipulated objects. Despite these efforts, several challenges persist:
- **Inefficiency and Alignment Issues**: Many existing multimodal VLA models require modality-specific encoders to process new sensory inputs. These encoders often lack pre-training on robotic-specific multisensory data, leading to suboptimal alignment with the Large Language Model (LLM) backbone and hindering overall efficiency. Pre-training new encoders is computationally intensive.
- **Limited Physical Dynamics Modeling**: While some VLA studies explore predicting future states (e.g., subgoal images, depth maps) to reason about physical dynamics, these predictions are often confined to 2D visual information. They typically do not extend to comprehensive point cloud structures or tactile interaction information, which are critical for understanding complex, contact-rich scenes and for effective motion planning in intricate robotic manipulation tasks.

This work positions itself to bridge these identified gaps by proposing a methodology that not only integrates diverse robotic-specific multisensory information (2D images, 3D point clouds, tactile signals) into a unified representation but also enhances physical world modeling through comprehensive multisensory future state forecasting. The objective is to enable VLA models to reason more robustly about semantic, geometric, and interaction information within the physical environment, particularly in contact-rich scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a multisensory language–action (MLA) model that enhances the physical-world understanding and action generation capabilities of robots by collaboratively perceiving diverse sensory inputs and predicting their corresponding future states.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent limitations of current VLA models when applied to real-world robotic manipulation:
- **Inadequate 2D Perception for Physical Interaction**: Existing VLA models predominantly rely on 2D image input. This is insufficient for capturing the intricate spatial relationships and physical dynamics crucial for robots to effectively perceive and interact within the three-dimensional physical world, especially in tasks involving contact.
- **Need for Comprehensive Multisensory Integration**: Robots require rich information spanning visual (semantic), geometric (spatial), and haptic (interaction) dimensions. A model that can process 2D images, 3D point clouds, and tactile feedback in a unified manner is essential for complex manipulation tasks that involve precise contact, object recognition, and environmental awareness.
- **Addressing Encoder Bottlenecks**: Incorporating additional sensory modalities often involves adding modality-specific encoders. This approach can be inefficient, as these encoders may not be pre-trained on robotic data and struggle to align their representations with the LLM backbone, requiring extensive and costly pre-training. A more streamlined, encoder-free integration method is needed.
- **Improving Physical Dynamics Reasoning through Forecasting**: While some VLA models attempt to predict future states, these are typically limited to 2D images or depth maps. A comprehensive understanding of physical dynamics requires forecasting not only visual changes but also future 3D geometric structures and tactile interaction feedback. Such multisensory forecasting can provide more robust conditions for generating precise and context-aware actions.

To address these motivations, the authors propose MLA with two key innovations: an encoder-free multimodal alignment mechanism to directly integrate diverse sensory cues within the LLM, and a future multisensory generation strategy to enable detailed reasoning about physical dynamics. The aim is to create a robot policy capable of robustly handling complex, contact-rich tasks with strong generalization abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Multisensory Language–Action (MLA) model is built upon a Large Language Model (LLM) backbone, specifically LLaMA-2 7B, initialized from the Prismatic VLM. The methodology involves a novel architecture and a three-stage training paradigm designed to integrate multisensory perception and future state forecasting efficiently.

**A. MLA Architecture**

The MLA architecture fundamentally repurposes the LLM itself as a unified perception-and-reasoning policy, avoiding separate modality-specific encoders.
- **Lightweight Tokenizers**: Raw multisensory inputs are converted into a shared token sequence.
 - **Image Tokenizer**: Divides a 2D image (I ∈ R^(H×W×3)) into non-overlapping 14x14 patches, yielding a sequence of N_img = 256 tokens.
 - **3D Point Cloud Tokenizer**: Processes raw point clouds (P ∈ R^(B×1024×3)) by partitioning points into local groups, using farthest point sampling (FPS) for downsampling, k-nearest neighbors (KNN) for local aggregation, and a linear layer for feature encoding, resulting in N_pc = 256 tokens.
 - **Tactile Tokenizer**: An MLP-based tokenizer embeds low-dimensional tactile signals (e.g., normal force, tangential force, and direction from two gripper sensors) into a single tactile token.
- **LLM Backbone**: Tokens from images, point clouds, tactile signals, and language instructions are projected into a shared embedding space and jointly processed by the LLM. Noise tokens for the diffusion-based action head are appended, with diffusion noise and timesteps embedded via MLP projectors. This design leverages the pre-trained LLM for direct interpretation of robotic-specific multisensory cues.
- **Future Prediction Decoders**: Transformer-based decoders are attached to the LLM's final hidden states to predict future multimodal observations. Each decoder maps the unified multimodal embeddings to its target space (image, point cloud, tactile) and consists of four stacked self-attention and feed-forward layers.

**B. Encoder-Free Multimodal Alignment**

This mechanism addresses the challenge of aligning heterogeneous sensory modalities without introducing new, unaligned encoders. It repurposes the initial transformer blocks of the LLM as a unified perception module through token-level contrastive learning. The embedding features from the 8th transformer block are used for this alignment.
- **Formulation of Positive and Negative Pairs**: Positional indicators are leveraged to establish cross-multisensory positional mappings.
 - 3D points and the 3D positions of tactile grippers are projected onto 2D image planes using camera parameters.
 - For each 3D point cloud token (aggregated from a set of 3D points), its center point is unprojected to 2D image coordinates.
 - For the tactile token, the robot state is used to project the tactile gripper's 3D position onto the 2D image plane.
 - The 2D image patch corresponding to these projected locations is identified.
 - **Positive Pairs**: The 3D token and tactile token are aligned with their corresponding 2D image token.
 - **Negative Pairs**: All other unmatched tokens serve as negative pairs.
- **Contrastive Losses**:
 - **Image–Point Cloud Alignment**: An InfoNCE loss is applied at the token level (N_img = N_pc = 256) to pull positive pairs together and push negative pairs apart.
 - **Tactile–Image and Point Cloud Alignment**: A unidirectional contrastive loss is applied, as tactile embeddings consist of a single token, linking it to its corresponding image and point cloud tokens.
- **Overall Objective**: L_contrastive = L_img_pc + L_tac_img + L_tac_pc. This process integrates semantic and spatial information within the LLM's embedding space.

**C. Future Multisensory Generation**

This post-training strategy enables MLA to reason about physical dynamics by jointly forecasting the future states of images, point clouds, and tactile modalities. These predictions are supervised against future keyframes, identified by changes in robot joint velocity and action transitions.
- **Image Prediction**: A transformer-based decoder predicts the next keyframe image, supervised by an MSE loss. Background pixels are removed using depth maps to focus prediction on foreground regions.
- **Point Cloud Prediction**: Another transformer-based decoder reconstructs the next-keyframe point cloud. The ground-truth point cloud is partitioned into local patches. The decoder outputs predicted coordinates, supervised with Chamfer Distance, enhancing stability by aligning coarse center points before refining local neighbor points.
- **Tactile Prediction**: The decoder outputs a low-dimensional tactile embedding, supervised by an MSE loss against the ground truth.

These future-state prediction losses (L_future) are applied only during post-training, meaning they do not impact inference efficiency.

**D. Overall Training Recipe**

MLA employs a progressive, three-stage training pipeline:
1. **Large-Scale Pretraining**: The LLM is pre-trained on a large dataset of over 570K trajectories (e.g., Open-X-Embodiment, RoboMIND) consisting primarily of image-language-action pairs. Token positions for 3D point clouds and tactile signals are reserved as empty tokens. A standard DDPM objective (L_diff) is used.
2. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: The pre-trained model is fine-tuned on high-quality, task-specific datasets that include all multisensory modalities (image, point cloud, tactile, language). The encoder-free multimodal alignment mechanism is introduced, incorporating L_contrastive. The total objective is L_sft = L_diff + L_contrastive.
3. **Post-Training**: This final stage builds upon the SFT model, using the same data and input modalities. It integrates the future multisensory generation supervision (L_future), enabling the model to capture physical dynamics. The overall objective is L_post = L_diff + L_contrastive + L_future. During inference, DDIM is employed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MLA involved comprehensive experiments in both real-world and simulation environments, coupled with ablation studies and generalization tests.

**A. Real-World Experiment Performance**
MLA was evaluated on six complex, contact-rich real-world robotic tasks, including four single-arm and two dual-arm manipulation tasks (e.g., pressing a stamp, wiping a whiteboard, scooping popcorn). Each task involved 15 rollouts.
- MLA achieved superior performance across all six tasks, demonstrating an average success rate increase of 12% over the state-of-the-art 2D VLA model (π_0) and 24% over the state-of-the-art 3D VLA model (SpatialVLA).
- For tasks such as "Wiping a Whiteboard," MLA effectively utilized tactile sensing to regulate end-effector movements, contributing to its performance.
- The improved performance was attributed to MLA's enhanced ability to align and interpret robotic multisensory inputs and its capability to generate future multisensory states, which provided a more robust understanding of physical dynamics.

**B. Ablation Study**
Ablation studies were conducted on two real-world tasks ("Pressing a Stamp onto Paper" and "Placing an Egg on Bread") to ascertain the contribution of each proposed component.
- **Impact of Input Modalities and Alignment Strategies**:
 - Progressively adding 3D point clouds and tactile signals to 2D images, even with simple token concatenation, showed a gradual increase in task success, indicating the importance of all three modalities for contact-rich manipulation.
 - The proposed token-level contrastive alignment significantly outperformed simple token concatenation and image-level contrastive alignment, yielding a 7% accuracy gain over the latter. This highlighted the effectiveness of token-level, position-guided consistency constraints in strengthening multimodal representations for physical-world perception.
- **Impact of Contrastive Loss Position**: Applying the contrastive loss at the 8th transformer layer of the LLaMA-2 backbone yielded the best performance. This suggested that aligning features in relatively shallow layers allowed subsequent transformer blocks to focus more effectively on future state prediction and action generation. Applying the loss at deeper layers (e.g., 32nd) provided limited gains, as final hidden states were already optimized for multiple objectives.
- **Impact of Multimodal Data Encoding Methods**: Directly repurposing the LLM for perception, as in MLA, proved more effective than injecting features from additional 2D or 3D encoders. The latter approach not only decreased performance by 7% but also reduced inference efficiency.
- **Impact of Different Generation Modalities in Future State Generation**: Removing any single future modality prediction (image, point cloud, or tactile) from the post-training stage resulted in a decrease in accuracy. This finding reaffirmed that generating comprehensive semantic, spatial, and interactive information provided more robust feature conditions for action generation. Furthermore, predicting future keyframes (70% success) outperformed predicting adjacent frames (64% success), as predicting adjacent frames introduced redundancy with limited benefits for motion planning.

**C. Generalization Experiment**
Generalization capabilities were tested on the "Placing an Egg on Bread with a Spatula" task under two scenarios: unseen manipulated objects and unseen complex backgrounds.
- **Unseen Objects**: When the egg was replaced with lettuce and the plate color changed, MLA exhibited almost no decrease in success rate for the initial subtask.
- **Unseen Backgrounds**: In cluttered scenes with novel background objects, MLA maintained a 40% success rate for the entire task.
- Compared to π_0, MLA showed less performance degradation (e.g., -15% vs -26% for unseen objects, and -25% vs -47% for unseen backgrounds). This robustness was attributed to its strong multimodal perception and ability to anticipate future states of manipulated objects.

**D. Simulation Experiment**
MLA was benchmarked on 10 tasks in the RLBench simulator, using image and point cloud modalities (tactile signals were omitted due to realism concerns in simulation).
- MLA achieved an average success rate of 81% across 10 tasks, significantly outperforming several state-of-the-art baselines including π_0 (65%) and SpatialVLA (46%).
- Improvements were particularly evident in challenging tasks such as "Place Wine at Rack Location" and "Water plants."
- Attention heatmaps showed that MLA learned more effective feature representations, demonstrating a clearer focus on both the robot and manipulated objects. These results validate the efficacy of the multimodal alignment and future multisensory generation, even without tactile input.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a Multisensory Language-Action (MLA) model that addresses critical limitations in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby advancing the capabilities of robotic manipulation in complex, physical environments. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Physical World Understanding**: By integrating 2D visual, 3D geometric (point cloud), and tactile cues, MLA provides a more comprehensive perception of the robot's operating environment. This multisensory integration is crucial for tasks requiring fine motor control, precise contact, and detailed spatial reasoning, which are common in real-world scenarios. The ability to reason about semantic, geometric, and interaction information allows for a richer understanding of physical dynamics.

- **Efficient Multimodal Integration**: The proposed encoder-free multimodal alignment mechanism represents a methodological advancement. By repurposing the initial transformer blocks of a large language model (LLM) as a perception module and using token-level contrastive learning with positional correspondence, MLA avoids the need for additional, potentially misaligned, modality-specific encoders. This approach offers computational efficiency and improves feature alignment within the LLM's unified embedding space, reducing the barriers to incorporating new modalities.

- **Improved Action Robustness through Forecasting**: The future multisensory generation post-training strategy enables MLA to predict future states across multiple modalities (images, point clouds, tactile signals). This predictive capability allows the model to anticipate environmental changes and object interactions, providing more robust conditions for action generation. This is particularly impactful for planning and executing complex, long-horizon tasks, where understanding future states is paramount for successful manipulation.

- **Strong Generalization Capabilities**: The demonstrated ability of MLA to generalize to unseen objects and complex background configurations in real-world tasks indicates a higher degree of robustness compared to existing VLA models. This generalization capacity is essential for deploying robots in varied and unstructured environments, reducing the need for extensive retraining on new scenarios.

- **New Foundation Model Paradigm**: This work proposes a "new multimodal foundation model paradigm" for the robotics community. By leveraging and adapting large language models to directly process and integrate diverse robotic-specific sensory inputs, MLA contributes to the development of more versatile and capable robot learning systems. This paradigm could inspire further research into how pre-trained general-purpose models can be efficiently specialized for embodied intelligence tasks.

- **Reproducibility and Benchmarking**: The evaluation across both real-world and well-established simulation benchmarks (RLBench) with competitive results strengthens the credibility and reproducibility of the proposed methods. The open-source nature of the project (via its website) further facilitates community engagement and future research.

In summary, MLA advances the state of the art in robot manipulation by offering a more holistic approach to perception and reasoning. Its contributions pave the way for more capable, adaptable, and generalized robotic systems that can operate effectively in complex physical environments.
