# MLA: A Multisensory Language-Action Model for Multimodal Understanding and Forecasting in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, CUHK, and X-Humanoid developed MLA, a multisensory language-action model that integrates 2D images, 3D point clouds, and tactile signals using an encoder-free alignment and multisensory future state forecasting. The model demonstrated an average 12% increase in success rate over state-of-the-art 2D VLA models in complex real-world robotic manipulation tasks and achieved 81% average success in RLBench simulations.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models predominantly use 2D image inputs, which are insufficient for capturing complex spatial dependencies and physical dynamics in real-world robotic manipulation.
- Integrating new sensory inputs into VLAs typically requires inefficient modality-specific encoders that often lack robotic-specific pre-training and struggle to align with the LLM backbone.
- Existing VLA approaches for predicting future states are often limited to 2D visual information, failing to provide comprehensive understanding of 3D geometric structures or tactile interactions crucial for complex physical dynamics.

## Method

- Introduces **MLA**, a model that repurposes a ==Large Language Model== (LLaMA-2 7B) as a unified perception-and-reasoning policy, directly tokenizing 2D images, 3D point clouds, and tactile signals.
- Proposes an encoder-free multimodal alignment mechanism utilizing token-level contrastive learning on shallow LLM layers to effectively integrate diverse sensory inputs without dedicated encoders.
- Implements a future multisensory generation strategy during post-training, enabling forecasting of future images, point clouds, and tactile states to enhance physical dynamics reasoning and action generation.

## Results

- MLA achieved an average success rate increase of **12%** over the state-of-the-art 2D VLA model (π_0) and **24%** over the state-of-the-art 3D VLA model (SpatialVLA) on six real-world robotic manipulation tasks.
- Ablation studies indicated that token-level contrastive alignment improved accuracy by **7%** compared to image-level alignment, and that comprehensive multisensory future generation across images, point clouds, and tactile signals significantly enhanced action robustness.
- The model demonstrated strong generalization, experiencing less performance degradation than baselines in real-world scenarios with unseen manipulated objects and complex background environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Repurposing initial LLM transformer blocks with token-level contrastive learning effectively aligns diverse multisensory inputs, demonstrating superior performance and efficiency compared to separate modality-specific encoders.
> - Optimally, multimodal feature alignment occurs in shallower LLM layers, allowing subsequent layers to specialize in predicting future states and generating actions more effectively.
> - Generating comprehensive future multisensory states (image, point cloud, tactile) is critical for robust physical dynamics reasoning and action generation, as shown by accuracy decreases when any future modality prediction is omitted.
