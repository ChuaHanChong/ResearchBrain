# Self-Evolving Curriculum for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Mila, ServiceNow AI Research, and other institutions developed the Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC), an automated framework that fine-tunes Large Language Models by dynamically selecting training problems to maximize learning gain. This method improves LLM reasoning, particularly generalization to out-of-distribution problems, achieving up to 33% relative accuracy gains on specific challenging math benchmarks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning for Large Language Models (LLMs) is highly dependent on the quality and sequencing of training data, often limited by suboptimal curricula.
- Existing curriculum strategies are either manually designed (labor-intensive, non-adaptive), random (suboptimal), or rely on computationally expensive online filtering methods.
- Poor curriculum design hinders learning efficiency, limits generalization to novel problems, and prevents the emergence of complex reasoning behaviors in LLMs.

## Method

- Formulates curriculum selection as a ==non-stationary Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB)== problem, where problem categories are arms whose expected learning gains evolve over time.
- Introduces the =='absolute advantage'== from policy gradient methods as an efficient, locally measurable proxy reward for immediate learning gain, which prioritizes problems in the model's Zone of Proximal Development.
- Implements an ==interleaved training process== that updates the LLM policy using sampled problems and simultaneously updates the curriculum policy using TD(0) and Boltzmann exploration to adapt problem selection.

## Results

- Achieved consistent performance improvements across various reasoning domains (planning, inductive reasoning, mathematics), with up to **33%** relative gain on challenging AIME24 mathematics problems (OOD) for the Qwen2.5-3B model.
- Demonstrated adaptive curriculum adjustment, progressively introducing more challenging problems as the model's capabilities improved and adapting to different model capacities.
- Maintained stable and robust performance in multi-task learning settings (SEC-2D variant), preventing performance collapse, and showed broad compatibility with alternative RL algorithms (PPO, RLOO) and automatically inferred curriculum categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maximizing the batch-wise expectation of the absolute advantage inherently guides the curriculum to prioritize problems with an approximate 0.5 success rate, optimizing learning efficiency.
> - An automatic and computationally efficient curriculum can be achieved by framing it as a non-stationary MAB, eliminating the need for manual design or expensive online filtering.
> - Dynamically adapting the training curriculum based on the model's evolving capabilities is crucial for enhancing LLM generalization to out-of-distribution problems and maintaining stable performance in multi-task learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of two rapidly advancing fields: **Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning of Large Language Models (LLMs)** and **Curriculum Learning**.

**RL Fine-tuning of LLMs:**
RL has emerged as a powerful technique for enhancing LLM capabilities, particularly for tasks requiring complex reasoning, such as mathematics, code generation, and logical puzzles. Methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) have shown remarkable success in aligning LLMs with desired behaviors or optimizing for verifiable outcomes. However, the effectiveness of RL fine-tuning is highly dependent on the quality and sequencing of the training data. The paper acknowledges this by highlighting that aligning the curriculum with the model's learning progress is crucial for maximizing learning outcomes and enabling complex behaviors like self-correction.

**Curriculum Learning in RL:**
Curriculum learning, first formally introduced by Bengio et al. (2009), posits that organizing training examples from easy to hard can smooth optimization landscapes and improve generalization. In the context of RL, curriculum learning aims to mitigate issues like sparse rewards and challenging exploration, by gradually exposing agents to increasing complexity. Existing approaches generally fall into a few categories:
- **Manually Designed Curricula:** These rely on human heuristics to pre-define the order of tasks, which can be effective but are labor-intensive, non-adaptive, and often suboptimal for new models or tasks. The paper demonstrates this limitation with its "Difficulty-Ordered Curriculum" baseline.
- **Online Filtering Methods:** These dynamically select training samples based on on-policy metrics, but often incur substantial computational overhead due to continuous generation and evaluation of additional samples.
- **Adaptive Curriculum Learning (ACL) Frameworks:** More sophisticated methods, such as Teacher-Student Curriculum Learning (Matiisen et al., 2020), have explored non-stationary Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) formulations to maximize learning progress.

**Positioning of SEC:**
The Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC) directly addresses the limitations of existing RL fine-tuning curricula. It aims to provide an **automatic, adaptive, and computationally efficient** solution that bypasses the inflexibility of manual methods and the computational burden of online filtering. SEC builds upon the MAB framework previously explored in ACL but introduces a novel and efficient proxy for "immediate learning gain" tailored for policy gradient methods in LLM fine-tuning.

Specifically, by leveraging the *absolute advantage* as a reward signal within a non-stationary MAB, SEC efficiently guides the curriculum toward problems that are neither too easy nor too hard, aligning with the educational concept of the **Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)**. This approach advances the state-of-the-art by offering a principled and practical way to optimize the training trajectory of LLMs, making RL fine-tuning more effective and broadly applicable across diverse reasoning domains. The work contributes significantly to the growing body of literature on making LLM training more robust, efficient, and capable of achieving stronger generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing the Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC) stems from the recognition that the training curriculum is a **critical, yet often suboptimal, factor** influencing the success of Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning for Large Language Models (LLMs). Despite RL's proven effectiveness in enhancing LLM reasoning, the choice of curriculum—the order in which training problems are presented—can significantly impact learning efficiency, generalization, and the emergence of complex behaviors.

The authors identify several key limitations with existing curriculum strategies:
1. **Suboptimal Random Curricula:** While a common baseline, drawing problems uniformly at random does not actively guide the learning process, often leading to slower progress and weaker generalization. The paper illustrates this with a controlled experiment (Figure 1), where a random curriculum performs better than a deliberately poor one, but still leaves room for significant improvement.
2. **Heuristic-driven Manual Curricula:** Manually designed curricula, though sometimes effective, rely heavily on human intuition and heuristics. They are costly to develop, require human intervention for new tasks or models, and lack the dynamic adaptability to the LLM's evolving capabilities. The "Difficulty-Ordered Curriculum" baseline often performs poorly, underscoring this limitation.
3. **Computationally Prohibitive Online Filtering:** Dynamic online methods that filter problems based on on-policy samples offer adaptiveness but demand substantial computational resources due to the continuous generation and evaluation of additional data. This makes them impractical for large-scale LLM fine-tuning.

In response to these challenges, the **key objectives** of the Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC) are to:
- **Automate Curriculum Learning:** Develop an automatic method that learns an optimal curriculum policy concurrently with the RL fine-tuning process, reducing human effort and improving efficiency.
- **Enhance Reasoning Capabilities and Generalization:** Significantly improve LLMs' reasoning abilities, with a particular focus on enabling better generalization to harder, out-of-distribution (OOD) test problems.
- **Achieve Skill Balance:** Ensure that when fine-tuning on multiple reasoning domains simultaneously, the model can effectively balance learning and maintain robust performance across all tasks.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Provide an adaptive curriculum strategy that avoids the high computational overhead of generating additional on-policy samples for online filtering.

The overarching motivation is to create a more intelligent and efficient RL fine-tuning paradigm for LLMs, where the training data is always optimally aligned with the model's current learning state, thereby maximizing the learning gain and fostering robust, adaptable reasoning agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC) proposes an automatic curriculum learning framework that operates in tandem with the RL fine-tuning of LLMs. Its core approach involves formulating curriculum selection as a **non-stationary Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) problem**.

**Curriculum Selection as Multi-Armed Bandit:**
1. **Categorization of Training Data:** The first step involves partitioning the entire training dataset into *N* distinct categories. These categories can be naturally defined (e.g., by different reasoning domains like mathematics, planning, coding) or constructed (e.g., by explicit difficulty levels, or inferred difficulty levels through empirical success rates or expert LLM prompting). Each of these problem categories is treated as an "arm" in the MAB framework.
2. **Expected Return (Q-values):** The objective is to learn an expected return, Q\_t(c), for each category *c* at training step *t*. This Q-value represents the expected learning gain associated with selecting that category. The MAB is considered "non-stationary" because the optimal category to select changes as the LLM policy evolves during training.
3. **Updating Q-values with TD(0):** To adapt to the non-stationary nature of the MAB, the Q-values are updated iteratively using the classic TD(0) method (Exponential Moving Average):
 `Q_t+1(c) = α * r_t(c) + (1 - α) * Q_t(c)`
 where `α` is the learning rate, and `r_t(c)` is the immediate reward obtained from category `c` at step `t`.

**Measuring Learning Outcomes with Absolute Advantage:**
1. **Proxy for Learning Gain:** Directly measuring the LLM's final performance after each curriculum selection is computationally infeasible. Therefore, SEC introduces a locally measurable proxy objective: the immediate learning gain derived from a training batch.
2. **Leveraging Policy Gradient Norm:** In policy gradient methods, the magnitude of parameter updates (gradient norm) is a good indicator of learning gain. The authors observe that the gradient norm of the policy gradient loss function is weighted by the absolute value of the advantage function (`|A_hat_t|`).
3. **Reward Function:** Consequently, the reward `r(c)` for a curriculum category `c` at each training step is defined as the batch-wise expectation of the absolute advantage across all rollouts associated with problems drawn from that category:
 `r(c) = E(|A_hat_t|)`
 Crucially, for common RL fine-tuning settings with binary rewards (0 for incorrect, 1 for correct), the paper formally demonstrates (Appendix A) that maximizing this expected absolute advantage is equivalent to prioritizing problems with an approximate success rate of 0.5. This aligns with the "Zone of Proximal Development," where the highest learning occurs when problems are neither too easy nor too hard for the learner.

**Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC) Algorithm Flow (Algorithm 1):**
1. **Initialization:** All Q-values for curriculum categories are initialized to zero.
2. **Curriculum Selection:** At each RL training step *t*:
 - Categories are sampled according to a **Boltzmann distribution** defined by their current Q-values: `p(c) = exp(Q_t(c)/τ) / Σ exp(Q_t(c_i)/τ)`, where `τ` is a temperature parameter controlling the exploration-exploitation trade-off.
 - Once categories are selected, problems are uniformly sampled from these categories until the desired batch size is reached.
3. **LLM Policy Update:** The sampled batch of problems is used to update the LLM policy `π_θ` using a chosen RL algorithm (e.g., GRPO, PPO, RLOO). Rewards are computed, and advantages are estimated.
4. **Curriculum Policy Update:** For each category `c` that was sampled in the current batch, the reward `r_t(c)` (average absolute advantage) is calculated. The corresponding `Q_t(c)` value is then updated using the TD(0) rule.
5. **Iteration:** This interleaved process of curriculum selection, LLM fine-tuning, and curriculum policy update continues for a predefined number of training steps.

This methodology provides a robust and adaptive mechanism to continuously adjust the training curriculum, ensuring that the LLM is consistently exposed to problems that maximize its learning potential throughout the fine-tuning process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors across various reasoning domains consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of the Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC) in improving LLM reasoning capabilities and generalization.

#### 4.1. Performance Across Reasoning Tasks (Table 1)

- **Consistent Improvements:** SEC consistently outperforms or remains highly competitive with baseline curricula (Random/No Curriculum and Difficulty-Ordered Curriculum) across three distinct reasoning domains: planning (Countdown, Zebra Puzzles), inductive reasoning (ARC-1D), and mathematics (MATH, AMC, AIME).
- **Enhanced Generalization to OOD Problems:** A key strength of SEC is its ability to significantly improve generalization to harder, out-of-distribution (OOD) test problems.
 - For the smaller **Qwen2.5-3B model**: SEC achieved substantial relative improvements, such as 13% on Countdown OOD, 21% on Zebra OOD, and a remarkable 33% on the challenging AIME24 mathematics dataset compared to the random baseline. The difficulty-ordered curriculum often performed poorly, highlighting its limitations.
 - For the larger **Qwen2.5-7B model**: While showing competitive performance on some tasks like Countdown and ARC (where stronger base models might already handle a broader range of problems), SEC still delivered clear gains on more challenging domains. For instance, Zebra OOD accuracy improved by approximately 11% relative, and AIME24 performance saw a 27% relative gain.
- **Adaptive for Harder Tasks:** When Qwen2.5-7B was tested on an even harder Countdown variant, SEC recovered clear gains over the random curriculum on both in-distribution (ID) and OOD splits (Table 2), confirming that curriculum benefits persist for larger models when task difficulty increases.

#### 4.2. Adaptive Curriculum Analysis (Figure 3)

- **Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment:** Visualizations of sampled average difficulty over training steps revealed that SEC adaptively adjusts the training curriculum. It typically starts with easier problems to facilitate early-stage learning, then progressively introduces more challenging problems as the model's capabilities improve.
- **Model-Specific Adaptation:** SEC also demonstrated the ability to adapt to different model capacities, selecting harder problems earlier for the stronger Qwen2.5-7B model compared to the Qwen2.5-3B model. This showcases SEC's capacity to align problem complexity with the model's current learning state.

#### 4.3. SEC with Multiple Curriculum Categories (Figure 4)

- **Multi-Task Learning and Skill Balance:** SEC seamlessly extends to scenarios involving multiple curriculum categories, such as combining problem type and difficulty level (SEC-2D). When fine-tuning on a mixed dataset of Countdown, Zebra, and ARC tasks, SEC-2D consistently achieved higher accuracy and improved generalization across all tasks compared to a random curriculum.
- **Stability in Multi-Task Settings:** The learning curve for Countdown OOD accuracy illustrated that while a random curriculum experienced a significant performance collapse midway through training in the multi-task setting, SEC-2D maintained stable and robust performance, underscoring its ability to effectively balance multiple learning objectives.

#### 4.4. Compatibility with Alternative RL Algorithms (Table 3)

- **Broad Applicability:** SEC's effectiveness was not limited to the GRPO algorithm used in main experiments. When evaluated with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and REINFORCE Leave-One-Out (RLOO) on the Countdown task, SEC consistently improved performance on both ID and OOD splits, demonstrating its generalizability across different RL algorithms.

#### 4.5. SEC with Automatically Inferred Curriculum Categories (Table 4)

- **Reduced Reliance on Manual Labels:** The study showed that SEC remains effective even when curriculum categories are derived automatically from continuous difficulty signals (e.g., empirical success rates binned into `k` categories), rather than relying on predefined metadata. This enhances the practicality and scalability of the approach.

In summary, the main findings highlight SEC as a robust, adaptive, and efficient curriculum learning strategy that significantly improves LLM reasoning, particularly for generalization to novel and harder problems, and offers strong performance balance in multi-task learning environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC) represents a significant advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning for Large Language Models (LLMs), with considerable potential impact on how these powerful models are trained and deployed.

**Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Automation and Efficiency in Curriculum Learning:** SEC provides a fully automatic and computationally efficient solution to curriculum learning for LLM fine-tuning. By framing curriculum selection as a non-stationary Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) problem and leveraging the absolute advantage as an immediate learning gain signal, SEC eliminates the need for manual curriculum design and avoids the high computational overhead of prior online filtering methods. This automation streamlines the training process, making it more accessible and scalable for diverse tasks and model architectures.

2. **Enhanced LLM Reasoning and Generalization:** A primary impact of SEC is its proven ability to substantially improve LLMs' reasoning capabilities, particularly their generalization to challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) problems. This is crucial for developing robust and intelligent AI systems that can adapt to novel situations and problems, rather than merely memorizing training patterns. The consistent gains observed across planning, inductive reasoning, and mathematics tasks underscore this fundamental improvement.

3. **Theoretical Grounding with Practical Relevance:** The paper's conceptual link between maximizing absolute advantage and the "Zone of Proximal Development" (where problems are neither too easy nor too hard) provides a strong theoretical underpinning. This insight explains *why* SEC is effective, moving beyond purely empirical heuristics and connecting it to established principles of optimal learning in educational psychology. This makes SEC a principled approach, not just an effective one.

4. **Robustness and Versatility:**
 - **Across Model Sizes and Task Difficulties:** SEC demonstrated adaptability to different LLM scales (3B vs. 7B models) and its continued effectiveness as tasks become increasingly difficult, confirming its broad applicability.
 - **Multi-Task/Multi-Domain Learning:** The ability of SEC to balance learning across multiple reasoning domains (SEC-2D variant) is a critical feature for developing more generalist LLMs capable of handling diverse problem types without catastrophic forgetting or performance degradation on specific tasks.
 - **Compatibility with Diverse RL Algorithms:** Its effectiveness across GRPO, PPO, and RLOO shows that SEC is largely agnostic to the underlying RL algorithm, making it a flexible plug-in component for various RL fine-tuning pipelines.
 - **Automated Category Inference:** The demonstration that SEC can work with automatically inferred curriculum categories reduces reliance on expert-annotated metadata, further increasing its practicality.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated LLM Development:** By making RL fine-tuning more efficient and effective, SEC can accelerate the development of specialized and highly capable LLMs for various applications, from scientific discovery to complex problem-solving.
- **More Capable AI Agents:** The improved generalization to OOD problems means LLMs fine-tuned with SEC are likely to be more robust and reliable when facing real-world scenarios that often present novel or unseen challenges.
- **Advancement of RLVR Paradigm:** SEC makes the promising Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm more practical and impactful by optimizing the training data flow, leading to better task-specific performance.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** SEC opens new avenues for research into more sophisticated curriculum reward functions (e.g., incorporating uncertainty measures), flexible problem categorization techniques (e.g., embedding-based clustering), and lightweight models for real-time reward estimation.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The authors acknowledge certain limitations, such as the introduction of extra hyperparameters (temperature `τ`, learning rate `α`) that require tuning, a common challenge in many machine learning methods. Future work could explore adaptive hyperparameter tuning or more robust default settings. Additionally, while SEC supports diverse curriculum definitions, more flexible and dynamic curriculum definitions, such as clustering problems based on embeddings or using lightweight models to directly estimate curriculum rewards, could further enhance its adaptability and reduce reliance on predefined categorizations.

Despite these minor limitations, SEC stands as a promising strategy that moves LLM fine-tuning towards a more intelligent, autonomous, and effective paradigm, paving the way for more capable and generalizable AI systems.
