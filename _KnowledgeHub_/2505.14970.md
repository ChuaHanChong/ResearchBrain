# Self-Evolving Curriculum for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Mila, ServiceNow AI Research, and other institutions developed the Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC), an automated framework that fine-tunes Large Language Models by dynamically selecting training problems to maximize learning gain. This method improves LLM reasoning, particularly generalization to out-of-distribution problems, achieving up to 33% relative accuracy gains on specific challenging math benchmarks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning for Large Language Models (LLMs) is highly dependent on the quality and sequencing of training data, often limited by suboptimal curricula.
- Existing curriculum strategies are either manually designed (labor-intensive, non-adaptive), random (suboptimal), or rely on computationally expensive online filtering methods.
- Poor curriculum design hinders learning efficiency, limits generalization to novel problems, and prevents the emergence of complex reasoning behaviors in LLMs.

## Method

- Formulates curriculum selection as a ==non-stationary Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB)== problem, where problem categories are arms whose expected learning gains evolve over time.
- Introduces the =='absolute advantage'== from policy gradient methods as an efficient, locally measurable proxy reward for immediate learning gain, which prioritizes problems in the model's Zone of Proximal Development.
- Implements an ==interleaved training process== that updates the LLM policy using sampled problems and simultaneously updates the curriculum policy using TD(0) and Boltzmann exploration to adapt problem selection.

## Results

- Achieved consistent performance improvements across various reasoning domains (planning, inductive reasoning, mathematics), with up to **33%** relative gain on challenging AIME24 mathematics problems (OOD) for the Qwen2.5-3B model.
- Demonstrated adaptive curriculum adjustment, progressively introducing more challenging problems as the model's capabilities improved and adapting to different model capacities.
- Maintained stable and robust performance in multi-task learning settings (SEC-2D variant), preventing performance collapse, and showed broad compatibility with alternative RL algorithms (PPO, RLOO) and automatically inferred curriculum categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maximizing the batch-wise expectation of the absolute advantage inherently guides the curriculum to prioritize problems with an approximate 0.5 success rate, optimizing learning efficiency.
> - An automatic and computationally efficient curriculum can be achieved by framing it as a non-stationary MAB, eliminating the need for manual design or expensive online filtering.
> - Dynamically adapting the training curriculum based on the model's evolving capabilities is crucial for enhancing LLM generalization to out-of-distribution problems and maintaining stable performance in multi-task learning.
