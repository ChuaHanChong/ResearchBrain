# AcL: Action Learner for Fault-Tolerant Quadruped Locomotion Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Singapore developed the Action Learner (AcL) framework, enabling quadrupedal robots to autonomously adapt locomotion under multiple simultaneous joint faults, including up to four faulty joints across two legs. This framework employs a novel teacher-student reinforcement learning approach with an encoder-decoder policy, allowing for smooth, bidirectional gait transitions and robust performance on a physical Unitree Go2 robot.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning methods for quadruped locomotion are vulnerable to internal joint failures, particularly in complex scenarios involving multiple simultaneous faults.
- Prior fault-tolerant approaches mainly address single-motor failures, often leading to abrupt gait transitions and neglecting the critical recovery phase to a normal gait.
- Training separate policies for each fault scenario is resource-intensive and lacks the ability to generalize or seamlessly switch between diverse fault conditions.

## Method

- **AcL** introduces a two-stage teacher-student ==reinforcement learning== framework, where multiple specialized teacher policies are trained for specific fault cases.
- A unified student policy, utilizing an encoder-decoder architecture, learns to autonomously identify fault types from proprioceptive observations and generate appropriate control actions.
- Student policy training is guided by 'style rewards' from teacher policies (promoting action amplitude and direction similarity) and 'regularization rewards' for overall robustness and smooth gait transitions across diverse terrains.

## Results

- The AcL framework enabled a Unitree Go2 robot to maintain stable locomotion with up to four simultaneous faulty joints across one or two legs in real-world experiments.
- The robot demonstrated autonomous, smooth, and bidirectional transitions between normal, various limping, and recovery gaits upon fault introduction or resolution.
- Ablation studies confirmed the essential role of style rewards for learning effective gaits, regularization rewards for robustness and adaptability, and the encoder for autonomous fault detection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling learning into fault-specific teacher policies and a general student policy, guided by 'style rewards,' allows for distilling diverse gaits into a single network capable of generalizing to complex and unseen fault conditions.
> - An encoder-decoder policy architecture enables autonomous fault detection and classification from proprioceptive data, facilitating adaptive gait selection without external state estimators.
> - Combining style rewards for specific behavior imitation with regularization rewards for stability and smooth transitions is crucial for consolidating multiple adaptive behaviors and achieving robust, bidirectional gait changes.
