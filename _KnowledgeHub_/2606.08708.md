# PRPO: Perception-Reinforced Policy Optimization via Token-Level Dynamic Advantage Reshaping

> [!summary] Summary
> PRPO (Perception-Reinforced Policy Optimization) introduces a token-level reinforcement learning framework for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) that effectively solves the "Perceptual Credit Assignment Problem." This method consistently boosted LVLM performance by over 20% on average compared to base models, and by 1.7-1.9% over leading RL methods across various multimodal reasoning benchmarks, concurrently enhancing training efficiency.

## Problem

- Current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LVLMs apply a coarse, single trajectory-level reward to the final response, distributing an identical optimization signal across all generated tokens.
- This uniform credit assignment leads to the "Perceptual Credit Assignment Problem," where sparse, pivotal perceptual tokens receive weak supervision and are overshadowed by abundant linguistic priors, resulting in gradient dilution.
- LVLM reasoning failures are predominantly caused by perceptual bottlenecks, such as misinterpreting local visual evidence or extracting erroneous numerical cues at intermediate steps, rather than pure reasoning deficiencies.

## Method

- Introduced Perception-Reinforced Policy Optimization (**PRPO**), a ==token-level== RL framework with two core components: Robust Visual Dependency (RVD) and Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR).
- RVD identifies pivotal perceptual tokens by combining Visual Dependency (KL divergence from strongly perturbed images) with Visual Inconsistency (KL divergence from weakly perturbed images), robustly filtering out brittle, noise-sensitive visual dependencies.
- PAR dynamically reshapes trajectory-level advantages using a synaptic S-shaped function to amplify learning signals for identified robust perceptual tokens, while providing a lower suppression baseline for non-perceptual tokens, integrated into a clipped surrogate objective.

## Results

- PRPO achieved average performance gains of **23.3%** for **3B** models (from **30.7%** to **54.0%**) and **21.1%** for **7B** models (from **41.4%** to **62.5%**) compared to baseline Qwen2.5-VL models across seven multimodal benchmarks.
- PRPO consistently surpassed state-of-the-art multimodal RL algorithms, demonstrating improvements of **1.9%** (**3B** models) and **1.7%** (**7B** models) over the strongest baseline (VPPO).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Robust Visual Dependency (RVD) for filtering brittle signals and Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR) for dynamic credit assignment are critical for PRPO's performance, effectively mitigating hallucination and representation collapse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective multimodal reasoning in LVLMs requires a fine-grained, token-level credit assignment mechanism, as uniform trajectory-level rewards fail to adequately reinforce sparse but critical visually grounded tokens.
> - Distinguishing between robustly grounded visual dependencies and brittle, noise-sensitive visual cues is crucial for stable multimodal learning, preventing hallucination and ensuring robust semantic grounding.
> - Dynamically amplifying advantages for genuinely perceptual tokens while maintaining stable gradients for other tokens (e.g., linguistic priors, reasoning templates) leads to more efficient and effective learning for LVLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the application of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). RLVR has demonstrated efficacy in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in text-based tasks. Inspired by these advancements, researchers have explored adapting RLVR for LVLMs, with existing efforts primarily focusing on improving training data quality, refining reward modeling and shaping, or modifying optimization procedures.

However, the paper identifies a limitation in current multimodal RLVR methods: they largely adopt principles from language-only settings, applying a single trajectory-level reward to the final response. This approach broadcasts an identical optimization signal across all generated tokens. The authors contend that this coarse-grained credit assignment is mismatched for multimodal reasoning. In such contexts, only a sparse subset of generated tokens is causally grounded in visual evidence (referred to as "pivotal perceptual tokens"). These critical tokens receive weak supervision and are susceptible to being overshadowed by more abundant linguistic priors or reasoning-template tokens.

An error analysis conducted on the MathVerse benchmark using a Qwen2.5-VL-7B model supports this claim, indicating that a majority of LVLM reasoning failures stem from perceptual bottlenecks rather than insufficient reasoning capabilities. Specifically, issues include grounding on incorrect visual regions, misinterpreting local visual evidence, or extracting erroneous numerical cues at intermediate steps, leading to either isolated or cascading perception errors. Pure reasoning errors were observed to be comparatively less frequent.

This observation leads to the identification of the "Perceptual Credit Assignment Problem," characterized by two key issues: the inability to explicitly identify and reinforce sparse perceptual signals from trajectory-level rewards, and gradient dilution resulting from uniform advantage assignment. Prior work in multimodal RL, such as Perception-Aware Policy Optimization (PAPO), has accounted for modality differences but still broadcasts uniform learning signals. Methods for identifying pivotal tokens, like VPPO, use KL Divergence-based visual dependency to reweight trajectory-level advantages, but risk amplifying noise-sensitive tokens due to reliance on a single metric and the coarse nature of trajectory-level reshaping.

This research aims to advance the field by introducing a dynamic, token-level reinforcement learning framework that explicitly addresses these fine-grained credit assignment challenges, focusing on robustly identifying and amplifying the learning signals for pivotal perceptual tokens.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to resolve the "Perceptual Credit Assignment Problem" within multimodal reinforcement learning, particularly for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). This overarching goal is pursued through several specific objectives:

1. **Explicit Identification of Pivotal Perceptual Tokens**: To develop a principled method that can accurately pinpoint tokens within long multimodal reasoning trajectories that are genuinely grounded in visual evidence and simultaneously robust to minor visual perturbations. This involves distinguishing truly informative visual cues from brittle or noisy visual signals.
2. **Effective Reinforcement of Perceptual Signals**: To design a mechanism that can selectively amplify the learning signals directed towards these identified pivotal perceptual tokens. The aim is to ensure that these critical, visually grounded tokens receive stronger and more focused supervision during the reinforcement learning process.
3. **Preservation of Stable Optimization for Non-Perceptual Tokens**: To implement this amplification in a manner that maintains stable optimization gradients for the majority of tokens that are driven by language priors or serve as reasoning templates. This objective seeks to prevent phenomena such as gradient collapse or explosion, which could compromise the model's overall reasoning capabilities.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several observations regarding the limitations of current RLVR methods when applied to LVLMs:

- **Mismatch with Multimodal Reasoning**: Existing RLVR frameworks, largely adapted from text-only models, apply a uniform, trajectory-level reward signal to all generated tokens. This design is fundamentally mismatched to multimodal reasoning, where only a sparse subset of tokens directly depends on visual evidence.
- **Dominance of Perceptual Errors**: Diagnostic analysis on the MathVerse benchmark indicated that a significant portion of LVLM failures arises from errors in visual perception at intermediate steps of reasoning (e.g., misidentifying visual anchors, misreading local evidence, incorrect numerical extraction). Errors purely attributable to reasoning logic were found to be less common. This highlights visual grounding as a primary bottleneck.
- **Sparse but Influential Perceptual Signals**: Key perceptual tokens (such as coordinates, numerical values, or geometric indicators) are sparse within long reasoning chains but exert a disproportionate influence on the correctness of the final answer. Under uniform reward schemes, these critical tokens receive the same diluted supervision as more abundant, less critical linguistic tokens, leading to inefficient learning.
- **Gradient Dilution**: The uniform assignment of advantages across all tokens results in gradient dilution. This phenomenon causes critical, sparse perceptual signals to be overwhelmed by the plentiful reasoning-template tokens, thereby hindering the model's ability to develop robust visually grounded decision-making.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Earlier attempts at token-level or modality-aware reinforcement learning, such as VPPO, often rely on single visual dependency metrics which can be susceptible to noise and may not differentiate between semantically grounded perception and numerically brittle responses. Furthermore, trajectory-level reward reshaping in these methods is considered insufficient for precise reinforcement of visually important tokens.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for a more granular, robust, and dynamically adaptive credit assignment mechanism that can effectively leverage the unique characteristics of multimodal data to enhance the reasoning capabilities of LVLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **Perception-Reinforced Policy Optimization (PRPO)**, is a token-level reinforcement learning method designed to address the perceptual credit assignment problem in multimodal reasoning. PRPO comprises two core components: Robust Visual Dependency (RVD) and Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR).

**A. Robust Visual Dependency (RVD)**

RVD aims to identify pivotal perceptual tokens within multimodal reasoning trajectories that are both genuinely grounded in visual evidence and robust to perturbations. The method uses Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence as a metric, interpreted from both an information-theoretic and an information-geometric perspective.

1. **Visual Dependency (S_t)**: This metric quantifies the policy's reliance on visual information for generating a specific output token *o_t*. It is defined as the KL divergence between the policy's token distribution given the original image and its distribution given a severely corrupted image:
 `S_t = D_KL(π_θ(· | I, q, o_<t) || π_θ(· | P_str(I), q, o_<t))`
 Here, *I* is the original visual input, *q* is the textual query, *o_<t* represents preceding tokens, and *P_str* is a "strong perturbation operator" designed to drastically corrupt visual information. The paper uses "Random Patch Masking" with a 60% patch dropout probability as *P_str*. A high *S_t* value indicates that token *o_t* is strongly dependent on the visual input, distinguishing it from tokens primarily driven by language priors.

2. **Visual Inconsistency (S'_t)**: This metric assesses the policy's sensitivity to numerical noise for *o_t*, probing the local curvature of the policy distribution on the perceptual manifold. It is defined as the KL divergence between the policy's token distribution given the original image and its distribution given a weakly perturbed image:
 `S'_t = D_KL(π_θ(· | I, q, o_<t) || π_θ(· | P_wk(I), q, o_<t))`
 *P_wk* is a "weak perturbation operator" that introduces infinitesimal stochastic pixel jitter (low-variance Gaussian noise) while preserving visual semantics. A high *S'_t* suggests numerical instability or reliance on brittle, high-frequency features, whereas a low *S'_t* implies stability and grounding in robust semantic features.

3. **Robust Visual Dependency (RVD_t)**: The RVD index combines *S_t* and *S'_t* to filter out brittle visual dependencies. It is formulated as a non-linear information filter:
 `RVD_t = S_t · ReLU(1 − (S'_t / (S_t + η)))`
 Where *η* is a small constant for numerical stability. This formulation effectively suppresses tokens that exhibit high visual dependency (*S_t*) but also high sensitivity to noise (*S'_t ≥ S_t*), which are termed "brittle anchors." Concurrently, it preserves the magnitude of "pivotal perceptual tokens" (where *S'_t ≪ S_t*), which are both semantically grounded and numerically robust. This dual-metric approach addresses the limitation of single-metric methods that cannot distinguish meaningful visual grounding from noise-sensitive responses, as corroborated by lexical analysis showing overlap in vocabulary between brittle and pivotal tokens.

**B. Token-Level Dynamic Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR)**

PAR is designed to transform the potentially skewed distribution of RVD values into bounded, stable token-level weights for credit assignment, mitigating the issues of long-tailed RVD distributions and potential gradient collapse.

1. **Synaptic S-shaped Reshaping**: PAR employs a differentiable, non-linear mapping based on a generalized logistic function:
 `T(x) = m + (M − m) / (1 + exp(−k · (x − μ)))`
 Here, *x* represents *RVD_t*. The function is governed by four parameters:
 - `μ` (activation threshold): Determines the point beyond which RVD signals are significantly amplified. Ablation studies indicated that setting `μ` to the 25th percentile of RVD yielded optimal performance.
 - `M` (upper saturation bound): Sets a ceiling for the reshaped advantage, preventing gradient explosion. A value of 4.0 was found to be optimal.
 - `m` (lower suppression baseline): Ensures a non-zero, albeit minimal, gradient even for tokens with low RVD, preventing "optimization dead zones." A value of 0.1 was found to be effective.
 - `k` (activation sharpness): Controls the steepness of the S-shaped curve, regulating the phase transition of the gating mechanism. A value of 2.0 was selected.
 This S-shaped curve suppresses low-signal tokens, amplifies informative perceptual tokens above `μ`, and provides smooth saturation.

2. **Perceptual Advantage Reshaping**: The final token-level reshaped advantage (`ˆA_i,PAR_t`) is computed by multiplying the standard trajectory-level advantage (`ˆA_i`) by the output of the synaptic response function:
 `ˆA_i,PAR_t = ˆA_i · T(RVD_i_t)`
 This mechanism redistributes learning signals dynamically, amplifying advantages for perceptually relevant tokens while softly discounting perceptual noise. The positive lower bound `m` ensures continuous, non-zero gradients across the sequence.

**C. Training Objectives**

PRPO integrates PAR into a clipped surrogate objective, building upon frameworks like DAPO (Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling), which refines the optimization landscape with asymmetric clipping and dynamic sampling. The final policy objective is:
`J_PRPO(θ) = E[ (1 / Σ|o_i|) Σ_i Σ_t min(r_i_t(θ) ˆA_i,PAR_t, clip(r_i_t(θ), 1−ϵ_low, 1+ϵ_high) ˆA_i,PAR_t) ]`
This objective bounds the dynamically reshaped advantages within an asymmetric trust region, aiming for precise multimodal alignment without overfitting to visual noise or compromising language priors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of PRPO was evaluated through extensive experiments across various multimodal reasoning benchmarks, utilizing Qwen2.5-VL models as the base architecture.

**A. Experimental Setup**
- **Base Models**: Qwen2.5-VL series at 3B and 7B parameter scales.
- **Training Data**: ViRL39K dataset.
- **Baselines**: Nine prominent training-based methods, including GRPO, DAPO, PAPO (PAPO-G, PAPO-D), VPPO, MM-Eureka-7B, VL-Rethinker-7B, NoisyRollout-7B, and R1-ShareVL-7B.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks**: Seven widely adopted benchmarks for mathematical reasoning and general multimodal understanding: Geo3K, MathVista, We-Math, MMK12, MathVerse, LogicVista, and MMMU-Pro. MathVerse_V denotes a vision-centric subset of MathVerse.
- **Training Parameters**: 2 epochs, learning rate of 1e-6, rollout batch size of 384, maximum response length of 2048 tokens.
- **Compute Resources**: All experiments were conducted using 8 NVIDIA A100 80GB GPUs.

**B. Main Results**
- **Performance and Consistency**: PRPO achieved average performance levels exceeding all other tested multimodal RL algorithms across both model scales.
 - For 3B models, PRPO delivered an average gain of 23.3% (from 30.7% to 54.0%) compared to the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-3B.
 - For 7B models, PRPO achieved an average gain of 21.1% (from 41.4% to 62.5%) over the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
 - Compared to the strongest baseline (VPPO), PRPO demonstrated improvements of 1.9% and 1.7% for 3B and 7B models, respectively. These gains were consistently observed across all evaluated tasks.
- **Dynamic Token-level Credit Assignment**: The observed performance improvements relative to baselines that primarily utilize trajectory-level reward signals (e.g., PAPO, VPPO) indicate that PRPO's RVD and PAR mechanisms effectively identify perceptual pivotal tokens and amplify their learning signals while suppressing irrelevant noise.
- **Training Efficiency**: PRPO exhibited higher training efficiency and faster convergence compared to GRPO and DAPO baselines. An equal-time budget analysis revealed that PRPO achieved a substantial 11.7-point average gain over DAPO within the same computational time (38.2 hours), whereas DAPO's performance stagnated even with extended training. This suggests that the gains from PRPO are attributable to superior credit assignment rather than merely increased computational expenditure.

**C. Ablation Studies**
- **Core Components**:
 - The Visual Inconsistency (VI) penalty was found to be critical. Removing VI ("PRPO w/o VI") resulted in a decrease in average performance to 61.2%, indicating that VI is essential for filtering out brittle visual dependencies and mitigating hallucination.
 - The Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR) mechanism was also shown to be indispensable. Removing PAR ("PRPO w/o PAR") led to a notable performance degradation to 60.2%. This suggests that without the bounded non-linear gating, the large gradients from perceptual tokens can overpower linguistic priors, potentially causing representation collapse.
 - Even the ablated PRPO variants significantly outperformed the trajectory-level DAPO baseline (50.6%), supporting the premise that fine-grained token-level routing is a more effective paradigm for multimodal RL.
- **PAR Curve Parameters**:
 - **Upper Saturation Bound (M)**: An `M` value of 4.0 was identified as optimal, balancing signal amplification and gradient regularization. Values of 2.0 were insufficient, and 6.0 led to reward dilution.
 - **Lower Suppression Baseline (m)**: An `m` value of 0.1 was optimal. `m` = 0.05 starved gradient flow, while `m` = 0.15 hindered noise filtering. This parameter is crucial for preventing optimization dead zones.
 - **Activation Sharpness (k)**: A `k` value of 2.0 was optimal, ensuring a smooth and robust optimization landscape. `k` = 1.5 homogenized advantage distribution, and `k` = 2.5 destabilized optimization near the threshold.
- **Activation Threshold (μ)**: Setting `μ` to the 25th percentile of RVD yielded the best performance. This implies that reinforcing the top 25% of pivotal perceptual tokens is sufficient for effective perceptual optimization, while maintaining lower gradients for the remaining tokens aids in filtering linguistic noise and stabilizing foundational reasoning.
- **Heuristic Metrics for Visual Dependency**: A comparison of RVD with other single-metric heuristics (Jensen-Shannon Divergence, Top-1 Probability Drop, standard KL Divergence) demonstrated RVD's superior performance, achieving a 60.2% average score compared to others ranging from 52.8% to 59.2%.

**D. Case Study**
A qualitative case study on the MathVista benchmark illustrated PRPO's ability to correct perceptual errors made by the baseline model. In a geometry problem, PRPO's output demonstrated accurate visual grounding, leading to a correct answer and a significantly more concise reasoning trajectory (314 tokens vs. 515 tokens for the baseline).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, PRPO, that provides a fine-grained, token-level approach to credit assignment in multimodal reinforcement learning, specifically addressing the Perceptual Credit Assignment Problem in LVLMs. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Addressing a Fundamental Bottleneck**: By identifying and providing a solution to the "Perceptual Credit Assignment Problem," the work addresses a core limitation in how current RLVR methods manage multimodal information. This problem, characterized by coarse-grained rewards and gradient dilution, has been shown to impede LVLMs from effectively learning truly visually grounded reasoning.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities**: The consistent and substantial performance gains observed across a diverse array of multimodal reasoning benchmarks (exceeding 20% average improvement over base models and 1-2% over prior state-of-the-art RL methods) indicate a significant advancement in LVLM capabilities. This suggests that PRPO enables models to more accurately interpret and integrate visual evidence into their reasoning processes.
- **Improved Robustness and Reduced Hallucination**: The introduction of Robust Visual Dependency (RVD) via a dual-metric approach (Visual Dependency and Visual Inconsistency) contributes to the development of more robust LVLMs. By actively filtering out tokens that are sensitive to numerical noise, PRPO helps models learn from stable visual cues, which can lead to reduced visual hallucination and increased reliability in their outputs.
- **Greater Training Efficiency and Scalability**: The empirical evidence demonstrating PRPO's superior training efficiency and faster convergence, even under equivalent computational budgets, suggests that it offers a more effective and scalable optimization objective. This could translate into reduced computational resources and time required for training high-performing multimodal models, making advanced LVLMs more accessible.
- **Conceptual Advancement in RL for Multimodal Models**: The research highlights the critical importance of token-level, fine-grained credit assignment in multimodal reinforcement learning. It shifts the paradigm from uniform trajectory-level rewards to a more nuanced approach that differentiates between the perceptual and linguistic contributions of individual tokens, offering valuable insights into how LVLMs learn and reason.
- **Broadened Application Scope**: The improved visual reasoning capabilities fostered by PRPO could have a positive impact on various applications demanding accurate visual interpretation combined with complex reasoning. This includes areas such as advanced scientific discovery (e.g., analysis of complex diagrams and data visualizations), robotics (e.g., nuanced visual navigation and interaction), educational technologies (e.g., intelligent tutors that interpret visual problems), and potentially medical imaging analysis.
- **Direction for Future Research**: PRPO establishes a new avenue for future research into dynamic weighting schemes, more sophisticated credit assignment mechanisms, and advanced robustness measures for visual grounding within multimodal learning systems.

In summary, this work represents a notable step toward developing LVLMs that can perceive and reason with visual information in a manner that is more accurate, robust, and aligned with the intricate nature of human-like multimodal cognition.
