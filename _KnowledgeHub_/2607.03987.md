# Fast Asymptotically Optimal Kinodynamic Planning via Vectorization

> [!summary] Summary
> PAKR (Parallel Asymptotically Optimal Kinodynamic RRT) provides a massively parallel kinodynamic motion planner, leveraging JAX and XLA for GPU acceleration and the AO-x meta-algorithm to achieve rapid execution with asymptotic optimality. The method achieves initial solutions orders of magnitude faster than CPU-based asymptotically optimal planners and competitive runtimes compared to existing GPU-accelerated methods across complex dynamic systems and physics simulations.

## Problem

- Sampling-based motion planners for kinodynamic systems are often too slow (hundreds of milliseconds to tens of seconds) for real-time reactive robotic applications.
- Existing parallel motion planners typically compromise solution quality (optimality) for speed, facing a 'staleness problem' in batched execution that complicates maintaining optimality.
- Achieving GPU acceleration for planning usually requires specialized programming (e.g., CUDA), which limits accessibility and portability.

## Method

- Introduces **PAKR**, which combines a massively parallel ==kinodynamic RRT== (based on forward propagation) with the ==AO-x meta-algorithm== for iterative refinement towards asymptotic optimality.
- Implements the entire planning loop in ==JAX== and compiles it into a single GPU kernel using the ==XLA compiler==, eliminating CPU-GPU transfer overhead and enabling high-performance GPU acceleration via Python.
- Employs optimizations like a ==tiered nearest neighbor strategy== and a ==branching factor== to efficiently balance exploration and nearest neighbor search overhead in a parallel context.

## Results

- PAKR achieved initial solution times orders of magnitude faster than CPU-based asymptotically optimal planners (e.g., **1.3ms** vs. **254ms** for Unicycle 1), often with better or competitive final costs.
- Demonstrated runtimes comparable to or superior to state-of-the-art GPU-accelerated planners like Kino-PAX, while consistently producing smaller search trees (e.g., **9k** nodes vs **164k** for 6D Double Integrator in Env A).
- Successfully scaled to complex dynamics and physics simulations (e.g., 12D Quadcopter, MuJoCo-XLA for Cartpole/Block Push, soft vine robot), achieving planning times typically in tens to hundreds of milliseconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The AO-x meta-algorithm effectively reconciles parallelization speed with asymptotic optimality by allowing fast, potentially suboptimal, inner planning loops to iteratively refine solutions towards optimality.
> - Utilizing JAX and XLA enables high-performance GPU acceleration of complex planning algorithms without requiring specialized low-level GPU programming, significantly improving accessibility.
> - Vectorized operations, a static-graph architecture, and fused GPU kernels are crucial for achieving millisecond-level planning times in kinodynamic systems, even with complex, high-dimensional dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Motion planning for robotic systems in dynamic environments necessitates algorithms that are both fast and capable of handling complex constraints, including kinodynamic constraints (i.e., those governing the robot's dynamics). Sampling-based motion planners (SBMPs) have demonstrated effectiveness in navigating high-dimensional state spaces and complex nonlinear dynamics. However, these planners often exhibit high computational costs, leading to solution times that can range from hundreds of milliseconds to tens of seconds, which is insufficient for real-time reactive applications.

Recent advancements in parallel computing have been explored to address this computational bottleneck. Approaches include GPU and CPU parallelization for operations such as collision checking, entire expansion iterations, or running multiple independent planner instances. For kinodynamic systems specifically, techniques like Kino-PAX have achieved substantial speedups, enabling solutions in milliseconds through GPU-accelerated tree expansion.

A recognized challenge in parallel motion planning, however, is the potential compromise on solution quality. Many asymptotically optimal (AO) planners, such as RRT* and Stable-Sparse RRT*, rely on sequential ordering mechanisms like rewiring or maintaining best-cost nodes within witness regions to ensure convergence to optimal solutions. In a batched parallel execution context, samples within the same batch operate without mutual visibility, leading to a "staleness problem." This means a node might connect to a suboptimal parent because a more optimal parent is still being processed, which can result in redundant exploration and suboptimal decisions, thereby complicating the maintenance of theoretical convergence guarantees without introducing significant synchronization overhead.

This work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome the trade-off between planning speed and solution quality in kinodynamic planning, particularly in parallel settings. It aims to harness the computational power of GPUs for speed while ensuring asymptotic optimality, and critically, to make such high-performance planning more accessible by reducing the need for specialized GPU programming. The paper addresses the staleness problem inherent in parallel AO planners by adopting an iterative replanning meta-algorithm, which redefines the approach to achieving optimality in a parallel context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a massively parallel kinodynamic motion planner that achieves both rapid execution and high solution quality. The authors aim to overcome the limitations of existing parallel planners, which often sacrifice optimality for speed, and traditional asymptotically optimal planners, which are typically slow.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing the Need for Real-Time Reactivity:** Robotic systems in dynamic environments require fast, reactive motion planning to account for complex kinodynamic constraints. Current sampling-based planners, while effective for complex dynamics, are often too slow for real-time applications, requiring hundreds of milliseconds to tens of seconds per plan.
- **Reconciling Speed and Solution Quality in Parallel Planning:** Existing parallel motion planning algorithms often compromise on solution quality. Asymptotically optimal planners rely on sequential operations (e.g., rewiring, maintaining best-cost nodes) that are difficult to implement efficiently in a batched parallel context due to the "staleness problem" where concurrently processed nodes lack up-to-date information about the tree's global state. The authors seek a method to maintain or achieve asymptotic optimality without incurring significant synchronization overheads that would negate the benefits of parallelization.
- **Improving Accessibility of GPU-Accelerated Planning:** While GPU-accelerated planners have demonstrated significant speedups, they frequently require specialized programming, such as CUDA, which can limit accessibility and portability for researchers and developers. A motivation is to enable GPU acceleration through standard, high-level programming tools like Python and JAX, thereby lowering the barrier to entry for developing high-performance planning solutions.
- **Leveraging Iterative Refinement for Optimality:** The authors propose that the AO-x meta-algorithm is particularly well-suited for parallelization. Rather than attempting to maintain optimality within a single, complex, and highly synchronized parallel tree expansion, they embrace fast, potentially suboptimal, planning in inner loops. The motivation is that if these inner loops are fast enough, numerous rapid replanning cycles can effectively converge towards optimal solutions, thereby sidestepping the synchronization issues that plague other parallel AO approaches.
- **Demonstrating Scalability to Complex Dynamics:** The research aims to show that the proposed approach can scale effectively to systems with complex, high-dimensional dynamics and heavy physics simulations, extending its applicability beyond simpler models.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to create a fast, high-quality, and accessible kinodynamic planner that can meet the demands of advanced robotic applications by innovatively combining parallel computation with an iterative optimality-achieving meta-algorithm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Parallel Asymptotically Optimal Kinodynamic RRT (PAKR), combines a massively parallel kinodynamic RRT (based on forward propagation) with the AO-x meta-algorithm for achieving asymptotic optimality. The core strategy is to leverage fast, sub-optimal parallel planning cycles, which are then refined iteratively by the AO-x framework.

**A. Core Algorithm (PAKR)**

PAKR operates with an outer loop and an inner loop, as described in Algorithm 1:

- **Outer Loop (AO-x Meta-algorithm):** This loop iteratively refines solutions. It maintains a `c_best` variable, which stores the cost of the best trajectory found so far. Each time a goal is reached by the inner planner, `c_best` is updated to the new, lower cost, and the inner planner restarts with this tighter cost bound. This iterative reduction of the cost threshold forces the planner to find increasingly efficient trajectories over time, leading to asymptotic optimality.
- **Inner Loop (Parallel Kinodynamic RRT):** This loop performs tree expansion. In each iteration:
 - `B/A` target states (`X_rand`) are sampled.
 - Their nearest neighbors (`X_near`) in the current tree are identified.
 - `A` random controls are sampled for each of these `B/A` parent nodes, resulting in `B` total state-action pairs.
 - All `B` state-action pairs are propagated in parallel to generate new states (`X_new`) and their associated costs (`C_new`).
 - For each new state, a cost-bounded pruning step is applied: the node is only considered for insertion if its estimated total cost (cost-to-come `c_new` + Euclidean heuristic `h(x_new)`) is less than the current `c_best`. This focuses exploration on regions likely to improve the current best solution.
 - Collision checking is performed for the path segment leading to `x_new`.
 - If collision-free and within the cost bound, the new node is added to the tree.
 - If a new node reaches the goal region, `c_best` is updated, and the inner loop breaks, allowing the outer loop to restart with the tighter bound.

**B. Implementation Details**

To maximize performance and accessibility, PAKR is implemented using JAX and the XLA compiler, leveraging a static-graph architecture optimized for GPU execution:

- **JAX and XLA Compilation:** The entire planning loop is fused into a single GPU kernel. This eliminates CPU-GPU device transfer overhead, which is a common bottleneck in GPU-accelerated applications. JAX provides a Python interface for XLA, allowing high-performance GPU code generation without requiring specialized CUDA programming.
- **Static Shape Constraint:** For efficient compilation into a monolithic kernel, the maximum tree size (`N`) and parallel batch size (`B`) must be defined at compile-time.
- **Tree Representation:** The search tree is stored as a structure of arrays (SoA), using contiguous buffers for states, actions, parent indices, and costs. Node insertion uses indexed slice updates to write batches of new nodes, and a scalar counter tracks valid nodes for masked operations.
- **Nearest Neighbor (NN) Search Optimization:**
 - NN distance calculations are vectorized to scale efficiently with GPU core counts.
 - A **Branching Factor (A)** is introduced. Instead of performing an NN search for every candidate expansion, `K = B/A` parent nodes are selected, and `A` random controls are sampled per parent. This balances exploration efficiency with NN search overhead.
 - A **tiered nearest neighbor strategy** is employed to mitigate the performance penalty of padding in a growing tree. Specialized kernels are used for different tree size tiers (e.g., 4^), processing only the active portion of the tree.
- **Modular Propagator Functions:** The framework supports arbitrary dynamics through modular propagator functions. This allows direct interfacing with physics simulators like MuJoCo-XLA for complex systems. Forward propagation uses fourth-order Runge-Kutta integration, which is vectorized and compiled efficiently. Collision validity is checked at each timestep during propagation using batched queries.

**C. Theoretical Analysis**

The authors provide a theoretical analysis establishing two key properties:

- **Probabilistic Completeness:** The parallel kinodynamic RRT (inner planner) is shown to inherit probabilistic completeness from sequential kinodynamic RRT. The introduction of batching only delays the discoverability of nodes by at most one batch, without altering the sample distribution or the fundamental probability of finding a path. The branching factor (A > 1) also preserves probabilistic completeness by augmenting each parent with additional control samples, thus not compromising expansion opportunities.
- **Asymptotic Optimality:** Given that the inner planner is probabilistically complete and the `c_best` cost threshold monotonically decreases across AO-x iterations, the conditions for the AO-x meta-algorithm are satisfied. Therefore, under Lipschitz continuous dynamics and cost functions, the sequence of solutions produced by PAKR converges almost surely to the optimal cost, confirming its asymptotic optimality. The fixed-step propagator (∆t) is consistent with discrete-time kinodynamic planners, provided ∆t is sufficiently small.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PAKR involved comparisons with state-of-the-art GPU-accelerated and CPU-based kinodynamic planners across various dynamical systems and environments.

**A. Comparison with Kino-PAX**

- **Setup:** PAKR (without the AO-x meta-algorithm for a fair, non-AO comparison) was benchmarked against Kino-PAX, a GPU-accelerated planner implemented in CUDA C. Three dynamics models were used: 6D Double Integrator (DI), 6D Dubins Airplane (DA), and 12D Quadcopter (QC) across three environments (Tree, Narrow, House).
- **Results (Table I):**
 - PAKR achieved comparable or superior runtimes to Kino-PAX across all tested dynamics and environments. For the 6D DI, PAKR was approximately 2x faster (e.g., 2.5ms vs 5.0ms in Env A). For 12D QC, PAKR showed slightly faster or competitive performance (e.g., 14.9ms vs 17.8ms in Env A).
 - PAKR consistently produced smaller tree sizes upon finding a solution, suggesting more efficient exploration of the state space. For instance, in 6D DI (Env A), PAKR generated 9k nodes compared to Kino-PAX's 164k nodes.
 - Success rates were 100% for all trials for both planners.

**B. AO Convergence**

- **Setup:** PAKR, utilizing the AO-x meta-algorithm, was evaluated for its convergence properties on the same Kino-PAX environments and dynamics. The cost criterion was distance traveled.
- **Results (Table II, Fig. 2):**
 - PAKR demonstrated rapid convergence, significantly reducing the cost of solution trajectories within tens of milliseconds.
 - For the 6D DI in Environment A, the median initial solution cost was 1.76 (found in 2.3ms), which was refined to a final cost of 1.51 in 28.0ms. Similar improvements were observed across other dynamics and environments.
 - Figure 2 visually illustrated this convergence for the 6D DI in Environment A, showing a monotonic decrease in cost with increasing planning time/iterations.

**C. Comparison with iDb-A* and SST***

- **Setup:** PAKR (with AO-x) was compared against the asymptotically optimal CPU-based planners iDb-A* and SST* (from OMPL). Four dynamical systems from DynoBench were used: Unicycle 1 (3D), Unicycle 2 (5D), Acrobot (4D), and Quadcopter (12D). Planners were given a 300-second budget. Solution duration was used as the cost.
- **Results (Table III):**
 - PAKR achieved orders of magnitude speed-up for initial solution time. For example, on Unicycle 1, PAKR found an initial solution in 1.3ms compared to iDb-A*'s 254ms and SST*'s 600ms.
 - PAKR demonstrated better or competitive initial solution costs in 3 out of 4 problems compared to iDb-A* and SST*. For Unicycle 1, PAKR's initial cost was 4.62s vs. 5.80s (iDb-A*) and 8.34s (SST*).
 - PAKR achieved better or competitive final solution costs. For Acrobot, PAKR's final cost was 3.22s, outperforming iDb-A* (4.96s) and slightly better than SST* (3.39s). For Unicycle 2, PAKR's final cost was 4.75s, significantly better than iDb-A* (8.70s) and SST* (6.95s).
 - iDb-A* exhibited a particularly low success rate (12%) on Unicycle 2, attributed to its reliance on trajectory optimization, which struggled with complex dynamics and cluttered environments. PAKR and SST* achieved 100% success on Unicycle 1 and 2.

**D. Effects of Batch Size and Branching Factor**

- **Setup:** The impact of varying batch sizes (4k, 8k, 16k, 32k) and branching factors (A=2, 16, 64, 128) on PAKR's performance was evaluated using the 12D quadcopter in Environment A.
- **Results (Table IV):**
 - Moderate branching factors (e.g., A=16) generally yielded the best runtimes. For a batch size of 8k, A=16 resulted in 13.1ms, which was the overall best runtime.
 - For the high-dimensional quadcopter, very large branching factors (e.g., A=128) were detrimental to exploration, leading to decreased success rates and increased runtimes.
 - Increased batch sizes generally decreased the number of iterations required to find a solution, but could increase runtime due to larger computation per batch.
 - Both batch size and branching factor influenced exploration efficiency, as indicated by varying tree sizes.

**E. Scalability to Heavy Simulation**

- **Setup:** PAKR was tested on problems using physics simulators: Cartpole and Block Push with MuJoCo-XLA, and a soft growing vine robot problem with a specialized soft robot simulator.
- **Results (Fig. 4, Fig. 5):**
 - PAKR successfully generated trajectories for these complex, physically simulated systems.
 - Median solution times increased with the complexity of the simulation: Cartpole (22.82 ms), Block Push (283.65 ms), and Vine (360.25 ms). This increase was attributed to heavier computation during propagation when using physics simulators compared to analytical functions. The Vine robot problem, which previously took seconds, was solved in milliseconds.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents PAKR, a parallel kinodynamic motion planner that integrates rapid execution with the capability for high solution quality. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the Gap between Speed and Optimality:** PAKR addresses a long-standing challenge in motion planning by effectively combining the speed benefits of parallelization with the solution quality guarantees of asymptotic optimality. By leveraging the AO-x meta-algorithm in conjunction with a fast, parallel RRT, the framework demonstrates that fast, sub-optimal planning can be iteratively refined to achieve near-optimal solutions in a practical timeframe. This provides a valuable paradigm for developing high-performance, high-quality planners.
- **Enhanced Accessibility to GPU Acceleration:** A key contribution is the use of JAX and the XLA compiler, enabling GPU acceleration through standard Python tooling. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners who may not have specialized CUDA programming expertise, making high-speed kinodynamic planning more accessible and potentially fostering broader adoption and development in the field.
- **Competitive Performance with State-of-the-Art:** The experimental results demonstrate that PAKR achieves runtimes comparable to or superior to other state-of-the-art GPU-accelerated planners like Kino-PAX, while also producing smaller search trees, indicating more efficient exploration. Furthermore, against established asymptotically optimal CPU-based planners like iDb-A* and SST*, PAKR exhibits orders of magnitude speed-up for initial solutions and often achieves better or competitive final solution costs. This establishes PAKR as a high-performance alternative for complex planning problems.
- **Scalability to Complex Dynamics and Physics Simulations:** The successful application of PAKR to systems like the 12D Quadcopter, Cartpole, Block Push, and soft growing vine robots (using MuJoCo-XLA and other physics simulators) highlights its scalability to high-dimensional, complex, and physically realistic domains. This expands the practical applicability of fast, optimal kinodynamic planning to challenging robotic manipulation and control tasks where accurate physics modeling is crucial.
- **Theoretical Foundations for Parallel AO Planning:** The theoretical analysis confirming probabilistic completeness and asymptotic optimality for PAKR underpins the reliability and robustness of the approach. This provides a strong foundation for future research in parallel asymptotically optimal motion planning.

In terms of potential impact, PAKR could enable more sophisticated real-time reactive behaviors in autonomous systems such as drones, mobile manipulators, and soft robots operating in dynamic and uncertain environments. Its accessibility through Python and JAX could accelerate research and development in areas like reinforcement learning for control, where differentiable physics simulators and rapid planning are increasingly important. Future work includes extending the planner to even higher-dimensional manipulation problems, integrating learned action proposals to further enhance efficiency, and evaluating against newer near-optimal parallel planners.
