# ConsisVLA-4D: Advancing Spatiotemporal Consistency in Efficient 3D-Perception and 4D-Reasoning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework from Harbin Institute of Technology, ConsisVLA-4D, enhances spatiotemporal consistency for robotic manipulation by integrating efficient 3D perception and 4D reasoning from 2D observations. It attains a 98.1% average success rate on LIBERO benchmarks and reduces inference latency by 2.31x while decreasing training costs by 1.36x compared to prior VLA models.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with inefficient and inconsistent 3D spatial understanding when relying primarily on 2D visual observations, leading to issues like projection bias and high computational overhead for explicit 3D methods.
- Current VLA models exhibit underdeveloped 4D spatiotemporal reasoning in dynamic scenes, often failing to consistently correlate current spatial states with predicted future scenes or understand evolving object identities and geometric relationships.

## Method

- The ==Cross-View Aligner== (**CV-Aligner**) efficiently constructs semantically consistent 3D representations by selecting instruction-relevant object tokens from multi-view 2D inputs and fusing them with 3D features.
- The ==Cross-Object Fuser== (**CO-Fuser**) resolves geometric ambiguity by implicitly aggregating spatial geometric relationships across multiple viewpoints into compact latent representations.
- The ==Cross-Scene Thinker== (**CS-Thinker**) employs ==Spatiotemporal Consistency Attention== to learn implicit local object dynamics and global depth knowledge, enabling efficient 4D visual reasoning for stable action generation without explicit intermediate predictions during inference.

## Results

- ConsisVLA-4D achieved an average success rate of **98.1%** across LIBERO simulation benchmarks, outperforming SpatialVLA by **20%** and CoT-VLA by **14.2%**, and demonstrated robust performance in ManiSkill2 and RoboTwin 2.0.
- In real-world tasks, the framework attained an average success rate of **70.0%** on long-horizon bimanual tasks, showing a substantial improvement over OpenVLA (**28.5%**) and OpenVLA-OFT (**51.8%**) with strong sim-to-real transfer.
- The model demonstrated significant efficiency gains, including a **2.31x** speedup in inference latency (0.110 seconds) and a **1.36x** reduction in training cost compared to OpenVLA, alongside a FLOPs reduction from 8.48 T to 4.59 T.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instruction-guided semantic object selection and cross-view fusion significantly reduce visual redundancy (e.g., 1/8 of original visual inputs) while maintaining robust 3D semantic consistency.
> - Implicit aggregation of geometric relations across views using learnable tokens provides an efficient method to resolve 3D spatial ambiguities and capture complex object layouts without redundant information.
> - Learning implicit knowledge of dynamic objects and global depth within a Spatiotemporal Consistency Attention mechanism enables effective 4D reasoning, leading to stable action prediction without requiring explicit future scene generation at inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotics that integrates visual perception, language instructions, and action generation to enable robots to perform complex tasks. Recent advancements in VLA models, such as RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, and π-series, have demonstrated capabilities in bridging 2D perception with spatial manipulation across various benchmarks. These models typically operate by mapping 2D visual observations to robot actions, often leveraging large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

However, current VLA models encounter limitations in robustly handling complex scenarios characterized by intricate object layouts or dynamic scene changes induced by robot actions. These limitations stem from two primary areas:
1. **3D Spatial Understanding**: Many existing VLA models primarily process 2D visual inputs, leading to challenges in capturing the full structural complexity of the 3D world. While some approaches attempt basic 2D-to-3D mapping, they can suffer from projection bias, geometric inconsistencies, and occlusion errors. Alternatively, methods that incorporate explicit 3D representations (e.g., point clouds, depth maps) often rely on additional sensors, thereby introducing substantial computational overhead and limiting deployment in resource-constrained environments.
2. **4D Spatiotemporal Reasoning**: The dynamic evolution of spatial scenes during action execution presents a challenge for VLA models. Existing models frequently exhibit deficiencies in establishing consistent correlations between current spatial states and predicted future scenes due to an insufficient understanding of evolving scene dynamics. This absence of spatiotemporal consistency can result in impaired visual reasoning and unstable action generation. World model-based VLA approaches aim to predict future visual states and actions, but primarily focus on image prediction rather than comprehensive 3D spatial understanding or 4D reasoning.

ConsisVLA-4D positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified challenges. It proposes a unified and efficient framework designed to enhance spatiotemporal consistency in both 3D-Perception and 4D-Reasoning. The work aims to achieve robust 3D representation from 2D observations with reduced computational cost and to improve temporal stability in action prediction through explicit 4D visual reasoning, moving beyond future-frame prediction. This approach is motivated by human capabilities in maintaining consistent spatial perception and performing visual reasoning in dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations observed in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models regarding spatiotemporal perception and reasoning for robotic manipulation. The authors identify two core challenges that impede the effectiveness of VLA models, particularly in real-world, dynamic scenarios:

1. **Inefficient and Inconsistent 3D Spatial Understanding from 2D Observations**: Current VLA models predominantly rely on 2D visual inputs. While some methods attempt to infer 3D structures from these 2D observations, they often struggle to capture the complete structural complexity of the 3D environment, leading to issues such as object misalignment and ambiguous spatial relationships. Methods that explicitly incorporate 3D data, such as point clouds or depth maps, necessitate specialized sensors and incur significant computational costs, thereby limiting their practicality in diverse robotic applications. The objective is to efficiently generate accurate 3D representations from standard 2D observations without introducing prohibitive computational overhead, addressing the need for robust spatial awareness.

2. **Underdeveloped 4D Spatiotemporal Reasoning for Dynamic Scenes**: Robotic manipulation tasks often involve dynamic scene changes as actions unfold. Existing VLA models typically exhibit limitations in comprehensive 4D spatiotemporal reasoning. They often focus on future-frame prediction rather than understanding how object identities and geometric relationships evolve over time in alignment with task instructions. This deficiency leads to a lack of spatiotemporal consistency, where the model struggles to maintain a coherent understanding of the scene's state and predict consistent future states. Consequently, this can compromise visual reasoning and result in unstable or unreliable action generation. The objective is to enhance spatiotemporal consistency by developing a mechanism that reasons about dynamic spatial scenes, thereby optimizing action prediction and ensuring temporal stability throughout task execution.

Inspired by human cognitive abilities, which involve maintaining accurate and consistent spatial perception across multiple viewpoints and performing visual reasoning to predict future states based on this stable perception, the authors aim to develop a VLA framework that emulates these capabilities. The key questions guiding this research are:
- How can 3D representations be efficiently generated from 2D observations without excessive computational overhead?
- How can spatiotemporal consistency be enhanced to optimize action prediction through 4D visual reasoning?

ConsisVLA-4D is proposed as a unified and efficient framework to address these questions, aiming to achieve stable and coherent understanding of dynamic scenes and maintain spatiotemporal consistency throughout action generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ConsisVLA-4D is presented as a unified and efficient framework designed to enhance spatiotemporal consistency in 3D-Perception and 4D-Reasoning for robotic manipulation. The framework comprises three core components: Cross-View Aligner (CV-Aligner), Cross-Object Fuser (CO-Fuser), and Cross-Scene Thinker (CS-Thinker), which are tightly integrated.

**Efficient 3D-Perception Phase:** This phase focuses on constructing spatially consistent 3D representations from multi-view 2D inputs, serving as the foundation for spatiotemporal consistency.

- **1. Cross-View Aligner (CV-Aligner):**
 - **Explicit Semantic Object Selection (ES-Selection):** Given multi-view RGB observations (Main, Left, Right) and a language instruction, SigLIP encoders extract semantic representations (z\_sem). The instruction embedding then modulates these visual tokens using FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) layer-wise to enhance semantic correlation. A cosine similarity measure is used to quantify the relevance of each visual token to the instruction. To remove visual redundancy, only the Top-K (default K=32) instruction-relevant tokens are retained for each view, resulting in selected object tokens (z\_obj). This process reduces visual input significantly (e.g., from 256 to 32 tokens per view, about 1/8 of original).
 - **Single-Fusion:** The selected object tokens (z\_obj) are then fused with 3D features (z\_3D) extracted by VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer) for each view. This frame-wise fusion is performed via a 4-layer Transformer, where z\_obj acts as the query, and z\_3D as key and value. This module injects 3D information into object tokens and establishes associations between objects of the same identity across different viewpoints, ensuring cross-view object semantic consistency.

- **2. Cross-Object Fuser (CO-Fuser):**
 - **Group-Fusion:** This module addresses geometric ambiguity from single-view depth estimation by jointly processing multiple viewpoints (Main, Left, Right). It leverages the structural similarity between DINOv2 (for geometric features, z\_geo) and VGGT (for 3D features, z\_3D) to perform deep, block-level feature fusion. For each encoder block, the DINOv2 features (z\_geo) are element-wise aggregated with a cosine-decayed weighted VGGT 3D representation (z\_3D). The weighting factor (α\_l) for z\_3D decreases with layer depth, allowing the model to initially absorb prior geometric information and then gradually shift focus to learned geometric features.
 - **Implicit Geometric Relation Aggregation (IG-Aggregation):** To abstract spatial geometric relationships between objects across views, a set of learnable aggregation tokens (z\_agg-3D) is initialized and concatenated with the fused geometric features (z\_geo-3D). These tokens are progressively learned through subsequent blocks using a block-wise causal self-attention mechanism. Causal attention is applied between z\_geo-3D and z\_agg-3D, while bidirectional attention is used within each set. This process efficiently aggregates spatial geometric information into compact latent representations (e.g., 64 tokens, 1/12 to 1/8 of original visual tokens), thereby maintaining cross-object spatial geometric consistency without redundancy with semantic tokens. The final layer's aggregation tokens (z\_agg-3D\_L') are used.

**Efficient 4D-Reasoning Phase:** This phase extends 3D-Perception into the spatiotemporal domain to enhance temporal stability of action generation.

- **3. Cross-Scene Thinker (CS-Thinker):** This component utilizes Spatiotemporal Consistency Attention (SC-Attn) to learn implicit semantic and geometric knowledge for efficient visual reasoning during inference.
 - **Multi-View Objects to Single-View Dynamic Objects:** For each viewpoint, a set of learnable dynamic tokens (0\_dyn-4D) is initialized. These tokens are guided by their corresponding object representations (z\_obj-3D) and the instruction embedding. During training, the model predicts the dynamic object (ˆz\_dyn-4D) for a fixed viewpoint after an action, supervised by CoTracker. This focuses reasoning on objects that change, establishing cross-view identity consistency over time.
 - **Abstract Relation to Concrete Global Depths:** A set of learnable depth tokens (0\_dep-4D) is initialized and guided by the aggregated geometric relation tokens (z\_agg-3D\_L') and the instruction. These tokens are decoded into global depth features (ˆz\_dep-4D) for all views, supervised by Depth-Anything. This infers future global depth through implicit spatial relations.
 - **Parallel Decoding of Action Chunks in SC-Attn:** Both ˆz\_dyn-4D and ˆz\_dep-4D are learned as intermediate visual reasoning within the same contextual window as action prediction. During inference, the CS-Thinker operates without explicitly generating dynamic objects or global depth, relying on the learned implicit knowledge for efficient and stable action generation. Initialized action tokens (0\_A) are decoded in parallel via SC-Attn to produce the action output (ˆA).

The overall training objective combines losses for action prediction (L\_action), dynamic objects (L\_dyn-4D), and global depth (L\_dep-4D). This methodology aims to achieve spatiotemporal consistency by reducing visual redundancy, aligning object identities across views, resolving geometric ambiguities, and reasoning about dynamic scenes, ultimately leading to robust and efficient robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the performance and efficiency of ConsisVLA-4D across simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

**Overall Performance:**

- **Simulation Benchmarks (LIBERO):** ConsisVLA-4D exhibited an average success rate of 98.1% across the four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), outperforming all compared methods. Specifically, it achieved 98.8% in Spatial and 99.8% in Object suites, indicating strong spatial perception and object recognition. The average success rate surpassed SpatialVLA by 20% and CoT-VLA by 14.2%.
- **Simulation Benchmarks (ManiSkill2 & RoboTwin 2.0):** The model demonstrated stable superiority in ManiSkill2, achieving an average success rate of 94.3% across PickCube, StackCube, and PushCube tasks. In RoboTwin 2.0, it showed robust performance across seven diverse bimanual tasks.
- **Real-World Tasks:** On the Galaxea R1 Lite and AgileX Cobot Magic platforms, ConsisVLA-4D achieved phased and final success rates across four long-horizon bimanual tasks (Microwave Operation, Banana Peeling, Drawer Arrangement, T-shirt Folding). For example, on Galaxea R1 Lite, it reached an average success rate of 70.0%, significantly higher than OpenVLA (28.5%) and OpenVLA-OFT (51.8%). The performance was consistently maintained across different deployment platforms (±1.7%), and its real-world results were nearly consistent with RoboTwin 2.0, indicating robust sim-to-real transfer.

**Efficiency Optimization:**

- **Simulation Efficiency:** Despite the addition of approximately 2B parameters (primarily from VGGT), ConsisVLA-4D demonstrated an inference latency of 0.110 seconds and a throughput of 72.7 Hz. This represents a 2.31× speedup in latency and a 1.36× speedup in training cost compared to OpenVLA (base 7B baseline), and 1.25× speedup in latency and 1.43× speedup in training cost compared to OpenVLA-OFT (efficient 7B baseline). The FLOPs were reduced to 4.59 T from OpenVLA's 8.48 T and OpenVLA-OFT's 8.45 T.
- **Real-World Efficiency:** In real-world settings (with an additional viewpoint, larger action chunk size, and smaller batch size), ConsisVLA-4D maintained similar efficiency gains. It achieved 0.231 seconds latency and 108.2 Hz throughput, a significant improvement enabling real-time operation of large VLA models.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **CV-Aligner and CO-Fuser:** Removing components of CV-Aligner (ES-Selection and Single-Fusion) resulted in significant success rate drops (e.g., 7.0% in LIBERO and 10.0% in real-world for both combined). Similarly, removing components of CO-Fuser (Group-Fusion and IG-Aggregation) led to drops (e.g., 8.2% in LIBERO and 13.3% in real-world for both combined). This indicates the crucial role of consistency modeling in these modules for 3D scene understanding.
- **CS-Thinker:** Ablating dynamic objects (Dyn. O.) or global depth (Glob. D.) within CS-Thinker led to success rate drops of 2.7%-4.8% in simulation and 5.7%-11.6% in the real world. This highlights the importance of local object dynamics and global depth information in 4D-Reasoning and action prediction. The designed Spatiotemporal Consistency Attention (SC-Attn) also outperformed simpler causal and bidirectional attention mechanisms.
- **Sparsification Ratio:** An optimal sparsification ratio of approximately 1/8 (reducing visual tokens to 64 for object features and 64 for geometric features, plus 18 4D tokens) was identified as providing a balance between performance and efficiency. This setting significantly outperformed other general sparsification methods like FastV and SliME at the same compression level.

**Qualitative Analysis:**

- Visualizations of real-world task execution (Fig. 6, 9) illustrate the model's ability to perform fine-grained gripper operations and execute long-horizon tasks smoothly and accurately.
- Attention heatmaps (Fig. 7, 10) show that CV-Aligner effectively filters instruction-relevant objects by identifying corresponding regions across main and wrist views.
- CO-Fuser attention heatmaps (Fig. 11) demonstrate its ability to capture global geometric layouts and spatial relationships between multiple objects across views, providing complementary scene understanding.

In summary, ConsisVLA-4D demonstrates substantial performance improvements in robotic manipulation tasks through its efficient spatiotemporal consistency design, coupled with significant gains in inference speed and reduced training costs compared to existing VLA models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ConsisVLA-4D framework introduces a method for enhancing spatiotemporal consistency in VLA models, which has implications for the field of robotic manipulation. By integrating efficient 3D perception and 4D visual reasoning, the work addresses identified limitations in existing VLA models regarding robust 3D understanding and coherent reasoning in dynamic environments.

The development of the Cross-View Aligner (CV-Aligner) and Cross-Object Fuser (CO-Fuser) contributes to more efficient and accurate 3D spatial perception from 2D inputs. CV-Aligner's explicit semantic object selection and single-fusion strategy allow for the filtering of redundant visual information and alignment of object identities across multiple viewpoints, which can lead to reduced computational overhead (e.g., 1/8 of original visual inputs) while maintaining semantic consistency. CO-Fuser's approach to implicitly aggregating geometric relations through multi-view fusion helps to mitigate spatial ambiguities, resulting in compact latent representations and cross-object spatial geometric consistency. These components together aim to improve the model's foundational understanding of complex 3D scenes.

The Cross-Scene Thinker (CS-Thinker) with its Spatiotemporal Consistency Attention (SC-Attn) represents an effort to extend spatial consistency into the temporal domain. By learning implicit knowledge of local object dynamics and global depth, the framework aims to enable more effective 4D visual reasoning. This capability can contribute to generating consistent and reliable actions in dynamic environments where scenes continuously change as tasks unfold. The design of CS-Thinker to operate without explicitly generating dynamic objects or global depth during inference suggests a pathway toward efficient deployment of such reasoning capabilities.

The empirical results, including improved success rates on benchmarks like LIBERO, ManiSkill2, and RoboTwin 2.0, as well as real-world robotic tasks, indicate the potential of this framework to enhance task performance. Furthermore, the demonstrated improvements in inference speed and reduced training costs suggest that the proposed methods can contribute to more efficient and deployable robotic systems. The robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities noted in the experiments may facilitate the transition of VLA research from simulated environments to practical applications.

The work may provide insights for future research in embodied AI, particularly in developing VLA models that can perceive and reason about the world with a higher degree of spatiotemporal coherence and computational efficiency. This could contribute to the development of more generalist robot policies capable of handling diverse and dynamic manipulation tasks.
