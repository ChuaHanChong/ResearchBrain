# Diffusion Reward: Learning Rewards via Conditional Video Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> The Diffusion Reward framework learns dense reward functions from action-free expert videos by leveraging conditional video diffusion models, enabling significant improvements in sample efficiency and performance for complex visual robotic manipulation tasks, including zero-shot generalization.

## Problem

- Manually designing dense reward functions for complex real-world reinforcement learning tasks is labor-intensive and often infeasible without privileged state information.
- Sparse rewards, while easier to specify, lead to poor sample efficiency and prohibitive data collection costs in reinforcement learning.
- Prior video-based reward learning methods, such as GANs and Video Prediction models, struggle to fully capture temporal information, suffer from training instability, or inaccurately model complex expert video distributions.

## Method

- A ==VQ-GAN== first compresses high-dimensional visual observations into lower-dimensional discrete latent codes for video representation.
- A ==VQ-Diffusion== model is trained on these latent video sequences, conditioned on historical latent frames to effectively capture temporal context and predict future frames.
- **Diffusion Reward**'s reward function is defined as the ==negative conditional entropy== of the diffusion model's predicted future frames, estimated by generating denoised samples conditioned on the agent's historical trajectory.

## Results

- Achieved significant performance improvements over baselines, with **38%** higher success rates on MetaWorld tasks and **35%** on Adroit tasks.
- Demonstrated zero-shot generalization, successfully guiding reinforcement learning agents in **5** unseen MetaWorld tasks without additional training or tuning.
- Validated on real robots, assigning higher rewards to human expert demonstrations and improving offline reinforcement learning performance for button pressing at unseen positions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditional video diffusion models excel at modeling complex expert video distributions, providing a more robust foundation for reward learning compared to previous generative approaches.
> - Lower generative diversity from a diffusion model, when conditioned on expert-like trajectories, serves as an effective and discriminative reward signal for guiding reinforcement learning agents.
> - Leveraging temporal information by conditioning the diffusion process on historical frames is crucial for accurately discerning and rewarding expert-like behavior patterns in sequential data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a fundamental and persistent challenge in reinforcement learning (RL): reward specification. Manually designing dense reward functions is notoriously difficult, labor-intensive, and often infeasible for complex real-world tasks, such as intricate robotic manipulation, where privileged state information is hard to obtain. While sparse rewards alleviate manual effort, they severely hinder sample efficiency, making data collection prohibitively expensive.

Learning reward functions directly from expert videos has emerged as a promising alternative, offering a lower-effort approach to gather task-execution information. Previous attempts in this domain have utilized generative models:
- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs):** Early approaches used GANs to train a discriminator that distinguishes between agent and expert behaviors, serving as a reward. However, these methods often underutilized crucial temporal information inherent in videos and suffered from the instability common in adversarial training.
- **Video Prediction Models (e.g., VideoGPT):** More recent work leveraged models like VideoGPT to encode temporal information and use predicted log-likelihood as a reward signal (e.g., VIPER). Yet, these models struggle to accurately capture complex expert video distributions, particularly in tasks with intricate dynamics, leading to sub-optimal rewards for out-of-distribution but still optimal behaviors.

Concurrently, **video diffusion models** have revolutionized computer vision, demonstrating unparalleled performance in tasks like text-to-video generation and video editing due to their remarkable ability to model complex data distributions. Their potential has also been explored in RL, for instance, as generalizable planners or for improving policy expressiveness and experience augmentation.

"Diffusion Reward" strategically positions itself at the intersection of these advancements. It acknowledges the limitations of prior video-based reward learning methods and the immense capabilities of video diffusion models. The key gap it addresses is the **under-explored area of extracting informative reward signals directly from video diffusion models** for online RL training. Unlike previous works that use diffusion models for policy learning or planning, this paper specifically focuses on *reward learning* to accelerate *online RL*, especially for vision-based robotic manipulation. It distinguishes itself from other diffusion-for-RL methods by conditioning on historical frames to infer rewards, rather than learning policies or augmenting experiences directly. Furthermore, it aims to learn these rewards solely from action-free expert videos, circumventing the need for additional reward labels, action labels, or task-agnostic data often required by other pretraining schemes in RL. By doing so, Diffusion Reward seeks to harness the generative power of diffusion models to provide a more robust and generalizable dense reward signal, thereby making RL more accessible and efficient for complex visual tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **Diffusion Reward**, a novel framework designed to learn dense reward functions from expert videos using conditional video diffusion models. This framework aims to effectively solve complex visual reinforcement learning (RL) problems, particularly in robotic manipulation.

The motivation behind Diffusion Reward stems from several critical observations and challenges:
1. **Limitations of Manual Reward Engineering:** Manually designing dense reward functions for complex real-world tasks is extremely labor-intensive, often requires privileged state information unavailable in practice, and can be practically infeasible.
2. **Inadequacies of Sparse Rewards:** While easier to specify, sparse rewards provide insufficient supervision, leading to poor sample efficiency in RL, which translates to prohibitive data collection costs, especially in real-robot scenarios.
3. **Shortcomings of Prior Video-Based Reward Learning:**
 - **Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) methods:** These approaches often fail to fully leverage temporal information in videos and are prone to instability during adversarial training.
 - **Video Prediction Models (e.g., VIPER with VideoGPT):** While addressing temporal information, these models struggle to accurately capture the intricate distributions of complex expert videos. This results in learned rewards that degrade significantly when encountering optimal behaviors that are slightly out-of-distribution from the training data, as illustrated in Figure 2 of the paper.
4. **Leveraging the Power of Video Diffusion Models:** Recent advancements have demonstrated the remarkable capability of video diffusion models in capturing complex video distributions and generating high-quality, intricate content. The authors hypothesize that this generative power can be effectively harnessed to model expert behaviors more robustly.
5. **Core Insight – Generative Diversity as a Reward Signal:** The central motivation is a key insight: diffusion models conditioned on *expert-like* trajectories exhibit *lower generative diversity* (i.e., they are more predictable and consistent with expert demonstrations) compared to those conditioned on *unexpert-like* trajectories. This difference in generative diversity can serve as a natural and discriminative signal to guide RL exploration. By encouraging the RL agent to seek behaviors that result in low generative diversity from the diffusion model, the agent is implicitly incentivized to mimic expert-like actions.

In essence, the authors are motivated to create a more robust, sample-efficient, and generalizable reward learning mechanism for visual RL by exploiting the unique capabilities of conditional video diffusion models to discern and reward expert-like behavior patterns effectively, even in challenging and unseen scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Diffusion Reward framework comprises two main phases: pretraining a reward model from expert videos and then using this model to provide dense rewards for online RL training.

#### 3.1. Expert Video Modeling via Latent Conditional Video Diffusion

1. **Latent Diffusion Process:**
 - **Observation Compression:** High-dimensional visual observations (RGB images) are first compressed into lower-dimensional, discrete latent codes. This is achieved using a **VQ-GAN (Vector Quantized Generative Adversarial Network)**. A VQ-GAN consists of an encoder (E) that maps images to a continuous latent space, followed by an element-wise quantizer (Q) that maps these continuous latents to discrete codes from a learned codebook. A decoder reconstructs images from these codes, and a discriminator ensures perceptual quality. This process transforms a video frame `x` into a latent code `z = Q(E(x))`.
 - **Video Representation:** An entire video sequence is represented as a sequence of these latent variables: `τ = {z_0, z_1, ..., z_{K-1}}`.
 - **Diffusion Model Training:** A **VQ-Diffusion model** is trained on these latent video sequences. Diffusion models work by gradually adding noise to data (forward process) and then learning to reverse this process (denoising) to generate data. The VQ-Diffusion model predicts the noise added at each timestep `t` (`ϵ_θ(z_t)`) during the forward process. The reverse process then iteratively denoises a random initial distribution to generate clean latent codes.

2. **Historical Frames as Condition:**
 - To capture and leverage temporal information effectively, the reverse diffusion process is *conditioned* on a sequence of historical latent frames (`z_c`). This `z_c` is a concatenation of `ℓ` past latent frames (`[z_{k-ℓ}, ..., z_{k-1}]`). This conditioning helps the model predict future frames in the context of recent history, effectively matching agent and expert trajectory distributions.
 - The VQ-Diffusion model tokenizes these conditional latent codes and embeds them, feeding them into the decoder's transformer blocks via cross-attention to provide context for video generation. The paper uses `ℓ=2` historical frames.

#### 3.2. Conditional Entropy as Rewards

1. **Key Insight Formalization:** The core idea is that the diffusion model will exhibit *lower generative diversity* when conditioned on trajectories that are expert-like, and higher diversity for unexpert-like ones. This means expert-like behaviors should correspond to lower *conditional entropy* of the predicted future frames, given the historical context.
2. **Reward Definition:** The reward is formalized as the *negative* of the conditional entropy: `-H(p_θ(·|z_c))`. A lower entropy (more predictable, less diverse generation) implies a higher reward.
3. **Entropy Estimation:** Direct computation of entropy is intractable. Therefore, the authors estimate a variational bound of the conditional log-likelihood (Eq. 2), which can be further simplified and estimated using the noise predictor `ϵ_θ` or closed-form distributions within the diffusion model (Eq. 6).
 - To estimate the entropy, `M` denoised samples (`˜z`) are generated by running the inverse diffusion process starting from randomly sampled noise, conditioned on `z_c`. The conditional entropy term `r_ce` is then calculated by averaging the variational bound of log-likelihood across these `M` samples. The paper notes that `M=1` is used for high inference speed.
 - **Standardization:** The raw conditional entropy reward `r_ce` is standardized using the empirical mean and standard deviation from a dataset of expert videos (`¯r_ce`) to mitigate scale variations across tasks and domains.
4. **Composite Reward Function:** The final reward function (`r_diff`) for the RL agent is a combination of three components:
 - **Standardized Conditional Entropy Reward (`¯r_ce`):** The primary expert-guided signal.
 - **Novelty-Seeking Reward (`r_rnd`):** From Random Network Distillation (RND), encouraging exploration.
 - **Sparse Environmental Reward (`r_spar`):** The binary 0/1 reward from the environment, indicating task completion.
 - The composite reward is given by: `r_diff = (1 - α) · ¯r_ce + α · r_rnd + β · r_spar`.
 - `α` (e.g., 0.95) balances the entropy reward and RND.
 - `β` (set to 1) weights the sparse environmental reward.

#### 3.3. Training Details

- **Pretraining:** VQ-GAN is trained first, followed by VQ-Diffusion on the collected expert videos.
- **Reward Inference:** During online RL, for each state `x_k`, its latent `z_k` is derived. `z_c` (historical frames) is formed, and the diffusion model generates `M=1` denoised sample to estimate `¯r_ce`.
- **Downstream RL:** DrQv2 is used as the backbone RL algorithm, receiving `r_diff` as the dense reward signal.
- **Efficiency:** A DDIM-like sampling strategy with 10 denoising steps is used, along with `M=1` for fast reward inference.

This comprehensive approach leverages the advanced generative capabilities of diffusion models to extract nuanced, temporally-aware, and informative reward signals from expert videos, significantly enhancing the efficiency and generalizability of online visual RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling evidence for the effectiveness and generalizability of Diffusion Reward across various visual robotic manipulation tasks, both in simulation and the real world.

1. **Superior Performance in Simulation:**
 - **MetaWorld and Adroit Tasks:** Diffusion Reward consistently and significantly outperforms all baseline methods across 10 challenging visual robotic manipulation tasks (7 from MetaWorld, 3 from Adroit). It achieves performance improvements of 38% over the best-performing baselines in MetaWorld tasks and 35% in Adroit tasks.
 - **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Raw Sparse Reward:** Performs poorly on complex tasks, highlighting the necessity of dense rewards.
 - **RND (novelty-seeking):** Improves exploration, especially in moderately complex tasks, but struggles with the vast configuration spaces of dexterous manipulation.
 - **AMP (Adversarial Motion Priors):** Generally better than RND in simpler tasks but less effective in complex ones, suggesting limitations in encoding temporal information or generating discriminative rewards for intricate dynamics.
 - **VIPER (VideoGPT log-likelihood):** Unexpectedly underperforms RND and AMP. This is attributed to VideoGPT's struggle in modeling complex video distributions and the unstandardized log-likelihood rewards. VIPER-std (standardized VIPER) shows improvement but still lags behind Diffusion Reward.
 - **Confirmation of Hypotheses:** These results empirically validate the paper's core motivations: the powerful modeling capabilities of diffusion models are crucial, and conditional entropy provides a more effective and generalizable reward signal than log-likelihood.

2. **Zero-Shot Reward Generalization to Unseen Tasks:**
 - **Robust Discrimination:** Even for unseen MetaWorld tasks, the pretrained Diffusion Reward model demonstrates a remarkable ability to assign relatively high learned returns to expert-like behaviors, despite less pronounced distinctions than in seen tasks.
 - **Effective RL Guidance:** When applied directly to 5 unseen MetaWorld tasks without any additional tuning, Diffusion Reward effectively guides RL exploration, largely outperforming RND and VIPER. This suggests its capacity to constrain the exploration space effectively due to its retained ability to discriminate between expert-like and unexpert-like behaviors across different tasks.
 - **Impact:** This zero-shot generalization capability is a significant finding, pointing towards the potential for learning universal reward functions for diverse robotic tasks.

3. **Real Robot Evaluation:**
 - **Reward Analysis:** On a real robot task (picking up a bowl with an Allegro hand), Diffusion Reward successfully assigns higher rewards to human-operated expert videos compared to random ones, indicating its applicability in real-world scenarios.
 - **Offline RL Performance:** In a real-world Button Press task using offline RL (DrQ+BC), Diffusion Reward significantly outperforms VIPER, yielding more generalizable policies that can press buttons at unseen positions. This highlights its practical utility for deploying learned behaviors in novel real-world contexts.

4. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Conditional Entropy vs. Log-Likelihood with Diffusion:** Both the use of a diffusion model (over VideoGPT) and conditional entropy (over log-likelihood) are critical for robust performance, confirming the design choices.
 - **Denoising Steps:** An intermediate number of denoising steps (around 10) balances generative quality, diversity, and inference speed effectively.
 - **Sampling Noise:** The inherent stochasticity (randomness) in the diffusion process contributes positively to RL exploration, akin to maximum entropy RL, suggesting that deterministic rewards are less effective.
 - **Reward Coefficient `α`:** An optimal `α` (0.95) for balancing `¯r_ce` and `r_rnd` is essential, indicating that both expert guidance and novelty-seeking are important; too much or too little of either can hinder performance.
 - **Context Length:** Using 1 or 2 historical frames for conditioning is sufficient, with longer contexts sometimes leading to marginal performance decline, possibly due to overfitting to specific expert trajectories.
 - **Sparse Environmental Reward:** The inclusion of sparse environmental reward is crucial, as its complete absence dramatically reduces performance by failing to provide task completion signals.
 - **Data Efficiency:** The method shows favorable performance even with a limited number of expert demonstrations (e.g., 10 per task for Adroit), thanks to the robust modeling ability of diffusion models.
 - **Comparison with Visual Representation Pretraining:** Directly pretraining rewards with Diffusion Reward is shown to be superior to merely pretraining visual encoders, emphasizing the importance of the reward function itself.

In summary, the main findings strongly support Diffusion Reward as a highly effective, robust, and generalizable framework for learning dense reward functions from expert videos, enabling significant progress in complex visual reinforcement learning for robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Diffusion Reward" framework makes several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact on the fields of reinforcement learning, robotics, and artificial intelligence:

1. **Advancement in Reward Learning:** The paper introduces a powerful and innovative approach to tackle the long-standing challenge of reward specification in RL. By leveraging the advanced generative capabilities of conditional video diffusion models, it provides a robust method to extract dense, informative reward signals from readily available action-free expert videos. This significantly reduces the manual effort and domain expertise traditionally required for reward engineering.

2. **Novel Application of Diffusion Models:** This work demonstrates a novel and impactful application of video diffusion models beyond their typical uses in video generation or planning. It showcases their utility as sophisticated "expert behavior discriminators" that can translate subtle patterns in expert demonstrations into effective reward functions for online RL. This opens new research avenues for how these powerful generative models can be integrated into broader AI systems.

3. **Enhanced RL Efficiency and Performance:** By providing highly discriminative and temporally-aware dense rewards, Diffusion Reward dramatically improves the sample efficiency and overall performance of RL agents in complex visual robotic manipulation tasks. The reported improvements of 38% and 35% on MetaWorld and Adroit tasks, respectively, are substantial and signify a step forward in making RL more practical for real-world robotics.

4. **Breakthrough in Generalization:** The most impactful finding is the demonstration of **zero-shot generalization** of the learned reward function to entirely unseen tasks. This capability implies that the diffusion model can learn fundamental aspects of "expertness" or task progress that transcend specific task instances. Such a generalized reward function could be a critical component towards developing truly intelligent and versatile robotic agents capable of adapting to novel scenarios without extensive re-training or re-specification.

5. **Real-World Applicability:** The successful validation of Diffusion Reward on real robot tasks (both in assigning meaningful rewards to human demonstrations and in improving offline RL performance for a physical robot) is crucial. It bridges the gap between theoretical advancements and practical deployment, indicating that this method can be a viable tool for developing robust robot policies in complex real-world environments with less human intervention.

6. **Reduced Data Requirements:** The ablation studies showing favorable performance with relatively few expert demonstrations highlight the data-efficient nature of the approach. This is particularly valuable in robotics, where collecting large, high-quality demonstration datasets can be costly and time-consuming.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Democratization of RL for Robotics:** By simplifying reward specification, Diffusion Reward could make sophisticated RL techniques more accessible to a wider range of robotic applications and researchers, particularly those without extensive expertise in control engineering or reward design.
- **Foundation for Universal Robotics:** The generalization capabilities lay groundwork for creating "universal reward models" that, akin to large language models, could guide robots across a vast array of tasks with minimal task-specific engineering.
- **Safer and More Reliable AI Systems:** More precise and robust reward functions lead to behaviors that are better aligned with human intent, potentially contributing to safer and more reliable autonomous systems.
- **New Directions in Research:** The work prompts further exploration into optimizing diffusion models for reward learning, integrating multimodal inputs (e.g., language for task specification), and combining this approach with other advanced RL techniques to push the boundaries of robotic intelligence.

In conclusion, Diffusion Reward represents a significant leap forward in learning meaningful reward signals from visual demonstrations. Its strong performance, impressive generalization, and real-world applicability position it as a foundational contribution with the potential to profoundly impact the development of advanced, adaptable, and autonomous robotic systems.
