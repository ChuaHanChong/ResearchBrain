# Seek in the Dark: Reasoning via Test-Time Instance-Level Policy Gradient in Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NLCo Lab at BIGAI and collaborators developed LATENTSEEK, a framework enhancing LLM reasoning by applying test-time policy gradient optimization in the continuous latent space. It achieves average gains of 15.23 points over Best-of-N on GSM8K and demonstrates a novel axis for test-time scaling without parameter updates.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with tasks requiring structured, meticulous, step-by-step reasoning.
- Existing methods for enhancing LLM reasoning are either limited in expressiveness (e.g., prompt engineering) or are computationally expensive and data-intensive (e.g., training-based RL).
- A need exists for efficient test-time scaling methods that extend beyond discrete token generation to explore continuous latent spaces for more effective and adaptive reasoning.

## Method

- LATENTSEEK proposes optimizing ==initial latent representations== in the LLM's continuous latent space using the ==REINFORCE policy gradient method== at test time, without updating model parameters.
- It employs ==self-generated reward signals== from the LLM itself to guide iterative updates of a fraction of initial latent representations.
- The framework performs ==instance-level adaptation== by decoding optimized latents into reasoning sequences and iteratively refines them for a problem-specific solution.

## Results

- LATENTSEEK consistently outperforms all tested baselines, achieving average gains of **15.23 points** over Best-of-N on GSM8K and improving over Self-Reflection on MATH-500 and Chain-of-Thought on AIME2024.
- The method demonstrates robustness across various LLM architectures (Qwen2, Qwen2.5, LLaMA3.1) and sizes (from **1.5B** to **14B** parameters).
- Under a Perfect Sparse Reward Model, a small Qwen2.5-1.5B model achieves significant performance, surpassing GPT-4o on AIME2024 by **14 points** and nearing o1-preview on MATH-500, indicating high latent space expressivity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active, gradient-guided optimization in the LLM's continuous latent space at test time offers a powerful, lightweight alternative for enhancing reasoning capabilities.
> - The optimal reasoning paths for LLMs in latent space may not always align with human-interpretable linguistic coherence, suggesting a distinct mode of effective AI reasoning.
> - The concept of 'test-time scaling' can be extended beyond searching in discrete token space to iterative optimization within latent representations, showing performance gains with increased iterations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in the development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI): enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). While LLMs have achieved remarkable success in many tasks, structured, step-by-step reasoning remains a significant hurdle. The current landscape of LLM enhancement can be broadly categorized into:

- **Training-based methods:** These involve updating model parameters through supervised fine-tuning (SFT), reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), or various forms of process/self-supervision. While powerful, these methods are computationally expensive, require vast amounts of data, and can suffer from issues like catastrophic forgetting. Examples include PPO, GRPO, and SFT on reasoning datasets.
- **Test-time adaptation methods:** These aim to improve LLM performance *without* modifying model parameters, typically by increasing computation during inference. This paradigm is gaining traction as an alternative to continuous retraining.
 - **Prompt Engineering:** Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and its variants (Few-Shot CoT, Self-Reflection, Self-Consistency) guide LLMs through explicit textual instructions or by generating multiple outputs and selecting the best one (Best-of-N, majority voting). These are widely adopted but suffer from limited expressiveness and reliance on human-designed prompts.
 - **Latent Space Exploration (Training-Dependent):** Some prior works (e.g., iCoT by Deng et al., 2024; Hao et al., 2024) have explored reasoning in the continuous latent space of LLMs, but these typically require extensive fine-tuning to train the model to internalize reasoning steps within these latent representations. Their performance has, at times, been shown to be inferior to CoT.
 - **Test-Time Training (TTT):** Frameworks that adapt models online using self-supervised objectives, still involving parameter updates, albeit localized and instance-specific.

"Seek in the Dark" introduces **LATENTSEEK**, a novel framework that uniquely positions itself within the test-time adaptation paradigm. It distinguishes itself by being the *first attempt* to perform instance-level reasoning by actively *seeking* or optimizing in the LLM's **latent space** at test time, crucially **without updating any model parameters**. Unlike prompt engineering, it optimizes continuous representations, offering greater expressiveness. Unlike prior latent CoT methods, it avoids the need for dedicated training or fine-tuning, operating purely at inference time on a per-instance basis.

The work leverages policy gradient methods, commonly used in reinforcement learning to update model *parameters*, but applies them here to iteratively refine *latent representations* guided by self-generated reward signals. This approach bypasses the training-related challenges while unlocking the expressive power of the latent space for reasoning, thus proposing a novel axis for "test-time scaling" beyond just generating more tokens. It also implicitly touches upon the debate of whether LLM reasoning *needs* to mirror human-interpretable thought processes, given its findings of effective but "unintelligible" intermediate steps.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind LATENTSEEK can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Current LLM Reasoning:** Despite advancements, LLMs struggle with tasks requiring structured, meticulous, step-by-step reasoning. Existing methods often fall short, either due to their inherent expressiveness limits (prompt engineering) or high computational and data costs (training-based RL).
2. **Explore Test-Time Scaling beyond Token Space:** The authors observe that model performance has improved under the training scaling law, but novel training data is scarce, and training algorithms face challenges like catastrophic forgetting. Test-time scaling, which increases computation during inference without parameter updates, offers an alternative. However, prior test-time methods primarily operate in the discrete token space (e.g., generating multiple CoTs). A key motivation is to explore whether leveraging the *continuous latent space* could offer a more effective and natively aligned paradigm for test-time reasoning.
3. **Enhance Reasoning without Parameter Updates:** Training-based methods, while effective, incur substantial computational costs and introduce risks such as catastrophic forgetting of general competencies. The authors seek a lightweight and efficient solution that operates solely during the testing phase, adapting the model's internal representations to specific problem instances without altering its learned parameters.
4. **Leverage the Semantic Richness of Latent Space:** Previous work has shown that reasoning capabilities can be internalized within the latent space through fine-tuning. This motivated the authors to investigate if this inherent semantic richness could be directly optimized at test-time to guide reasoning. They hypothesize that latent representations, acting as a "planning or control mechanism," could steer the pre-trained model towards better reasoning paths for each specific problem.
5. **Develop a Scalable and Efficient Framework:** The goal is to create a method that is not only effective but also computationally efficient, converging quickly for most problems, and demonstrating a clear benefit from increased test-time computation (test-time scaling law).

In essence, the paper aims to introduce a novel paradigm for enhancing LLM reasoning by performing instance-level, gradient-guided optimization directly within the model's latent space, thereby offering a lightweight, scalable, and effective alternative to traditional training and explicit search methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LATENTSEEK proposes a novel framework to enhance LLM reasoning through Test-Time Instance-level Adaptation (TTIA) within the model’s latent space. Its methodology can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Problem Formulation:** The task is framed as finding an optimal reasoning sequence `x*` that maximizes a reward function `R(x, c)` for a given context prompt `c`. The core innovation is reformulating this objective as optimizing a sequence of latent representations `z = (z1, z2..., zN)` associated with initial tokens, rather than directly searching for tokens. The objective becomes `z* = arg max_z E_{x~π(x|z,c)} [R(x, c)]`.
2. **Latent Representation and Decoding:** In this work, the "latent space" refers to the transformers' output space *before* the final language model (LM) head. `zt` is the latent representation for token `xt`. To sample a full sequence `x` from `z`, the initial `N` latents (`z1` to `zN`) are first decoded into tokens (`x1` to `xN`) using `π_LM-head(xt | zt)`. The remaining sequence (`x_{N+1}` to `xT`) is then generated autoregressively by the frozen LLM based on the preceding tokens.
3. **Policy Gradient Optimization:** LATENTSEEK employs the REINFORCE policy gradient method (Williams, 1992) to iteratively update the latent representations `z`. The update rule is `z ← z + η∇z J(z)`, where `η` is the learning rate. The gradient `∇z J(z)` is derived as `E_{x~π(x|z,c)} [R(x, c)∇z log π(x | z, c)]`.
 - **Independence Assumption:** A crucial aspect of this approach is the assumption of independence among the latent representations `zt` during the gradient calculation (`[∇z J(z)]t = E_{x~π(x|z,c)} [R(x, c)∇zt log π(xt | zt)]`). This is justified for two reasons: (1) it substantially enlarges the exploration space by decoupling token optimizations, preventing the autoregressive structure from collapsing the search onto the first token, and (2) theoretical analysis (in the appendix) draws an analogy to Multi-Prover Interactive Proofs (MIP), suggesting that this assumption can be mitigated by the base model's generation and reward model's evaluation capabilities.
4. **Self-Generated Reward Signals:** The reward function `R(x, c)` is self-generated by the LLM itself (`R(x, c) ~ π(· | x, c, prompt_self-reward)`). This mechanism relies solely on the model's internal capabilities, without external information or ground truth, making it practical for test-time application. The reward prompts are designed to evaluate aspects like answer correctness, problem comprehension, calculation accuracy, and completeness. The reward is typically scaled between -1 and 0.
5. **Iterative Refinement (LATENTSEEK Algorithm):**
 - **Initialization:** The process begins by generating an initial reasoning sequence `x` and corresponding latent representations `z` using standard Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Equation 1). This leverages existing reasoning capabilities as a strong starting point.
 - **Fractional Sequence Optimization:** Instead of optimizing the entire sequence of latents, only a fraction `ρT` of the initial latent representations (`z1` to `z_ρT`) are selected for optimization. This balances representational capacity for exploration with limiting potentially incoherent modifications, as excessive changes to latents can compromise reward function reliability.
 - **Loop:** For a predefined number of iterations `K` (typically small, e.g., 10) or until a reward threshold `τ` is met:
 - Latent representations `z` are updated using the policy gradient (Equation 5).
 - A new reasoning sequence `x` is sampled by decoding the updated `z` and continuing autoregressive generation (Equation 4).
 - The self-reward `R(x, c)` is computed for the new sequence.
 - **Return:** The final generated sequence `x` (or the one with the highest reward) is returned.
6. **Greedy Decoding:** For sampling `x` from the latents `z` during optimization, greedy decoding is preferred over stochastic sampling, as it is found to be more computationally efficient and robust, reducing approximation errors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LATENTSEEK demonstrates significant and consistent improvements across a range of mathematical reasoning benchmarks and LLM architectures.

- **State-of-the-Art Test-Time Reasoning Performance:**
 - **Overall Superiority:** LATENTSEEK consistently outperforms all tested baselines across 30 experimental settings on GSM8K, MATH-500, and AIME2024.
 - **Quantifiable Gains:** It achieves average gains of 15.23 points over Best-of-N (BoN) on GSM8K, 4.72 points over Self-Reflection on MATH-500, and 6.67 points over Chain-of-Thought (CoT) on AIME2024.
 - **Strong Baselines Beaten:** On LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct, LATENTSEEK surpasses advanced RL methods like Genius (+5.4 points on GSM8K, +20.0 points on MATH-500) and even verifiable-feedback RL methods like SimpleRL-Zoo (+18.1 points average across datasets).
- **Robustness Across Backbones:** The method exhibits strong generalizability, yielding superior results across different model families (Qwen2, Qwen2.5, LLaMA3.1) and sizes (from 1.5B to 14B parameters), demonstrating its broad applicability.
- **Performance on Challenging Problems (AIME2024):** Even on the difficult AIME2024 dataset, LATENTSEEK consistently outperforms all baselines, with an average gain of 6.34 points over CoT, highlighting its effectiveness for complex reasoning.
- **Ideal Experiment with Perfect Sparse Reward Model (PSRM):** Using a PSRM (which returns 0 for correct, -1 for incorrect answers), LATENTSEEK-PSRM shows an average gain of 10.67 points over the self-reward variant. This crucial finding indicates that the latent space offers substantial potential for exploration, even under minimal, sharp feedback, suggesting that exploration alone in this space can yield meaningful improvements.
 - **Extreme Scaling:** With PSRM and increased iterations (up to 256), a small Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct model achieves remarkable performance, even surpassing GPT-4o on AIME2024 by 14 points and nearing o1-preview on MATH-500, indicating the high expressivity of latent space and efficiency of LATENTSEEK.
- **Test-Time Scaling Law:** Performance generally improves with an increasing number of update iterations, both for self-reward and PSRM. This validates the concept of test-time scaling in the latent space as a complementary axis to parameter scaling.
- **Gradient Guidance Effectiveness:** Comparison with unguided stochastic exploration in latent space shows LATENTSEEK outperforms it by an average of 13.66 points, confirming that the gains come from effective gradient guidance, not just random perturbations.
- **Self-Reward Mechanism Reliability:** The self-reward mechanism is found to be reliable in approximately 80% of cases on GSM8K. An ablation study replacing it with a constant negative reward leads to a significant average performance drop of 14.21 points, underscoring the necessity and effectiveness of the self-reward guidance.
- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - **Low Iteration Count:** For average-level problems on GSM8K and MATH-500, the algorithm typically converges in fewer than two iterations, demonstrating rapid convergence.
 - **Token Efficiency:** LATENTSEEK does not rely on generating excessively long outputs; the average answer token length is comparable to or slightly longer than CoT, avoiding overly verbose generation. FLOPs analysis shows it to be significantly more efficient than fine-tuning based RL methods like Genius for typical inference loads.
- **Qualitative Analysis and Unintelligible Reasoning:** Case studies reveal that LATENTSEEK often arrives at correct answers even when its intermediate reasoning steps are linguistically "unintelligible" or semantically anomalous to human readers (e.g., "total downloads of downloads"). This suggests that the optimal reasoning paths for LLMs in latent space might fundamentally differ from human cognitive strategies, operating in a more native, less human-interpretable manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LATENTSEEK represents a significant advancement in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **A New Paradigm for LLM Reasoning Enhancement:** By demonstrating the effectiveness of instance-level policy gradient optimization directly in the latent space at test time, LATENTSEEK introduces a novel and powerful alternative to existing methods. It circumvents the need for computationally expensive and data-intensive parameter updates associated with fine-tuning or traditional reinforcement learning, offering a "lightweight, scalable, and effective solution."
2. **Unlocking Latent Space for Active Reasoning:** The work highlights the rich, yet often unexplored, potential of LLM latent spaces for active, dynamic reasoning. Instead of being merely passive representations of input tokens, these continuous spaces can be actively manipulated to steer the model's thought process towards more accurate conclusions. The "Perfect Sparse Reward Model" experiment strongly reinforces this, showing that exploration in latent space alone can yield substantial performance gains.
3. **New Axis for Test-Time Scaling:** LATENTSEEK proposes a novel axis for test-time scaling, where increasing the number of optimization iterations in the latent space—rather than solely generating more discrete tokens—leads to improved performance. This is crucial for developing more powerful yet efficient inference strategies for LLMs.
4. **Challenging Anthropocentric Views of AI Reasoning:** The qualitative findings, particularly the success despite "unintelligible" intermediate reasoning steps, are highly significant. They suggest that optimal reasoning trajectories for LLMs may not mirror human cognitive processes or linguistic coherence. This can lead to a re-evaluation of how we assess and design reasoning capabilities in AI, potentially freeing future research from the constraint of human interpretability when machine performance is the primary goal.
5. **Practical Efficiency for Deployment:** The method's rapid convergence (typically fewer than two iterations for average problems) and efficiency compared to training-based methods make it highly practical for real-world deployment where computational resources and latency are critical considerations. It allows LLMs to adapt to specific problems dynamically without the overhead of retraining or managing multiple specialized models.
6. **Future Research Directions:** This work opens several promising avenues for future research:
 - **Improved Reward Models:** The primary limitation identified is the reliance on self-reward. Developing more robust, generalizable, and principled outcome-based reward models (ORMs) or more effective process-based reward models is a critical next step to further unlock the potential of latent space optimization.
 - **Advanced Latent Optimization Algorithms:** Exploring more sophisticated reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g., PPO adaptations for continuous latent spaces) or latent-space-specific optimization techniques could yield even greater performance.
 - **Scaling to Larger Models:** Testing LATENTSEEK on even larger base models (beyond 14B parameters) could reveal further benefits and scalability properties.
 - **Understanding Latent Space Dynamics:** Deeper theoretical and empirical analysis of how information and reasoning unfold within the latent space, and how to best manipulate it, will be crucial.

In conclusion, LATENTSEEK represents a paradigm shift in how we might approach LLM reasoning, emphasizing efficient, dynamic adaptation within the model's internal representations. Its findings challenge conventional notions of interpretability in AI reasoning and pave the way for a new generation of more capable and resource-efficient language models.
