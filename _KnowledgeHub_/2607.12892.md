# UR-VC: Unsupervised Robotic Value Correction for Time-Derived Progress Proxies

> [!summary] Summary
> UR-VC introduces an unsupervised, training-free method to correct noisy time-derived progress proxies in robot learning by leveraging cross-episode state recurrences. This approach generates more accurate advantage labels, which improved real-robot task success rates by 6.1% in bimanual cloth manipulation.

## Problem

- Robot learning systems for complex, long-horizon tasks frequently rely on normalized time as a proxy for physical task progress, which introduces noise.
- This time-derived proxy is problematic due to non-monotonic physical progress within episodes (e.g., slips) and inconsistencies in timing for similar states across different demonstrations.
- Existing methods that learn progress estimators often inherit these biases, leading to suboptimal downstream policy learning.

## Method

- **UR-VC** identifies semantically similar states across various demonstration episodes using L2-normalized ==SigLIP-2 visual embeddings==.
- It corrects the original ==time-derived progress labels== by aggregating scores from episode-balanced, semantically similar states, applying a ==temporal locality constraint== and ==similarity thresholding==.
- The corrected progress estimates are then used to calculate enhanced ==advantage labels== for conditioning downstream policy training without requiring additional model training or human annotations.

## Results

- The method achieved high cross-episode coverage, with **98%** of frames finding at least one semantically similar neighbor (cosine similarity **>= 0.90**) in other episodes.
- UR-VC successfully recovered non-monotone progress, identifying local regressions in **13.4%** of frames that would otherwise show monotonic increases with raw time labels.
- Integrating UR-VC-derived advantage labels into an advantage-conditioned VLA pipeline improved real-robot task success rates from **72.8%** to **78.9%** in bimanual cloth flatten-and-fold tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large robot demonstration datasets contain sufficient redundancy for effective cross-episode matching of semantically similar states, even with moderate dataset sizes.
> - Averaging time-derived labels from independently collected, semantically similar states across episodes can effectively reduce episode-specific timing errors and enable the system to capture non-monotonic physical progress.
> - Directly improving the quality of the fundamental supervision signal before it is used for learning can lead to more robust and capable robot policies without altering existing policy architectures or training pipelines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern robot learning systems, particularly those addressing long-horizon and complex manipulation tasks, increasingly rely on dense intermediate task signals such as progress, value, or advantage. These signals are crucial for evaluating intermediate states, guiding policy learning, and detecting task completion. The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like RT-2, OpenVLA, and the π family further amplifies the importance of high-quality dense signals as they are trained on increasingly large and diverse robot datasets.

A common and scalable approach to obtain these dense signals is to use normalized time within a successful demonstration as a proxy, where later frames are considered indicative of higher progress. However, the paper identifies this time-derived label as a noisy substitute for actual physical task progress. This noise stems from two primary issues:
1. **Non-monotonicity:** In contact-rich manipulation, especially with deformable objects, physical progress can regress (e.g., due to slips or failed grasps) while the time-derived label monotonically increases.
2. **Cross-episode timing distortions:** Similar physical states can occur at different normalized times across demonstrations due to varying operator speeds, pauses, or recovery paths, leading to inconsistent labels for semantically similar states.

Existing research often addresses the need for dense signals by learning progress estimators or value models from data. These methods utilize various techniques such as temporal contrast, language-image objectives, value pretraining, optimal transport, or generative modeling. Pretrained models have also been explored for eliciting value estimates. However, the paper highlights a limitation in these approaches: if the supervision target itself (the time-derived proxy) is systematically biased, learned estimators may inherit these biases, smoothing over local regressions or encoding execution speed as progress.

UR-VC differentiates itself by proposing a complementary approach. Instead of learning an estimator, it aims to *correct the noisy time-derived proxy labels directly* before they are used as supervision for any estimator or policy. This positions UR-VC as a preprocessing step that enhances the quality of the fundamental supervision signal.

The work also relates to the field of learning with noisy labels and graph/nearest-neighbor label propagation. Unlike these methods, which often propagate sparse human labels, UR-VC exploits the inherent redundancy of repeated robot states across independently collected demonstrations, treating each timestamp as a noisy measurement of latent physical progress. The use of semantic encoders, specifically SigLIP-2, for cross-episode state matching further connects this work to advancements in vision-language models for understanding complex visual information in robotic contexts, particularly relevant for deformable object manipulation tasks like cloth folding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central problem addressed by this research is the inherent limitation of using normalized time as a proxy for physical task progress in robot learning systems. While normalized time offers a scalable way to generate dense supervision signals, it suffers from significant inaccuracies that hinder the effectiveness of downstream learning.

The key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Problem Formulation:** The paper formalizes normalized time ($g^{(e)}_t = t/T_e$) as a noisy proxy for an unobserved, true task progress ($p(o)$). The core issue is that this proxy assumes progress increases monotonically and uniformly with time, which is often not true in real-world, contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- **Addressing Within-Episode Non-Monotonicity:** A primary motivation is to correct for instances where a robot's physical progress regresses within an episode (e.g., due to slips, failed grasps, or reintroduction of wrinkles in cloth manipulation). The time-derived label, by construction, continues to increase monotonically, thus misrepresenting the actual state. This mismatch leads to a degenerate advantage signal: for a fixed action horizon H, the raw time proxy's difference ($g_{t+H} - g_t = H/T_e$) is constant within an episode, providing the same "ranking signal" regardless of whether an action improved or worsened the physical state. The objective is to enable the supervision signal to capture these local regressions.
- **Mitigating Across-Episode Timing Distortions:** The second significant motivation is to address the variability in execution speed and recovery behaviors across different demonstrations. Similar physical states can appear at different normalized times simply because operators move at different paces or handle failures differently. This variation introduces noise into the proxy labels, potentially causing models to learn incidental cues (like execution speed or appearance) instead of genuine physical task progress. The objective is to provide a more consistent progress label for similar physical states, independent of the specific demonstration's temporal dynamics.
- **Improving Downstream Learning without Additional Overhead:** The ultimate goal is to improve the quality of supervision signals for robot learning without introducing further complexities. Existing methods that learn progress estimators often inherit the biases of the time-derived proxy they are trained on. UR-VC aims to provide a *pre-correction* step that does not require manual progress labels, reward annotations, online rollouts, or training an additional value model. This makes it a training-free, offline method that can be seamlessly integrated into existing advantage-conditioned policy learning pipelines.
- **Validating Correction Conditions:** Given that true physical progress is not directly observable, a key objective is to empirically validate the underlying conditions that make the correction method useful. This includes demonstrating that cross-episode state recurrences are sufficiently dense, retrieved frames indeed correspond to the same semantic task state (not just superficial appearance), and the corrected labels can express non-monotone progress.

In summary, the motivation behind UR-VC is to bridge the gap between easily obtainable but noisy time-derived progress proxies and the true latent physical progress of a robotic task, thereby furnishing more accurate and robust supervision signals for advanced robot learning systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UR-VC (Unsupervised Robotic Value Correction) is designed as an offline, training-free method to correct time-derived progress labels. Its core approach is to leverage the redundancy of similar physical states recurring across different demonstrations.

**A. Time-derived progress as a noisy proxy**

The method first formalizes the problem. For an episode $e$ with $T_e$ frames, the normalized time proxy is defined as $g^{(e)}_t = t/T_e$. This is assumed to be a noisy observation of an unobserved true task progress $p(o) \in$ specific to a physical state $o$.

The noise manifests in two ways:
1. **Within-episode:** $g$ is monotonically increasing, while $p$ can be non-monotone (e.g., due to a grasp slip). This means raw time labels cannot differentiate between actions that improve or degrade the physical state. When using a fixed action horizon $H$, the advantage signal $g_{t+H} - g_t = H/T_e$ becomes constant within an episode, failing to provide state-dependent feedback.
2. **Across-episodes:** Identical physical states can occur at different normalized times in different episodes due to variations in execution speed or recovery behaviors. This introduces episode-specific timing distortion ($g^{(e)} = p + \epsilon_e$).

**B. UR-VC: Episode-Balanced Cross-Episode Correction**

UR-VC addresses these issues by aggregating time-derived labels from semantically similar states found in different episodes. The underlying premise is that averaging proxy scores from independent episodes can reduce the episode-specific timing error $\epsilon_e$.

The correction procedure for a query frame $i$ from an episode works as follows:
1. **Visual Embedding:** Each frame's visual information is converted into an L2-normalized semantic embedding using SigLIP-2. Cosine similarity ($f_i^\top f_j$) is used to measure semantic similarity between embeddings.
2. **In-band Candidate Set Creation:** For every other episode $e$ in the dataset, a candidate set $C_{i,e}$ is formed. This set includes frames $j$ from episode $e$ whose time-derived label $g_j$ is temporally close to the query frame's time-derived label $g_i$, specifically $|g_j - g_i| \le \tau$. The parameter $\tau$ imposes a locality constraint on the correction, bounding its magnitude.
3. **Episode-Balanced Representative Selection:** From each candidate set $C_{i,e}$, only the single most semantically similar frame $j^{*}_{i,e}$ to the query frame $i$ is selected: $j^{*}_{i,e} = \arg\max_{j \in C_{i,e}} f_i^\top f_j$. This "episode-balanced" approach ensures that each episode contributes at most one proxy score, preventing over-weighting of temporally adjacent frames from a single trajectory and promoting aggregation across distinct demonstrations.
4. **Similarity Thresholding:** Any selected representative $j^{*}_{i,e}$ with a cosine similarity below a threshold $\rho$ to the query frame $i$ is discarded. This acts as a conservative filter, ensuring that only high-quality, semantically similar matches contribute to the correction.
5. **Aggregated Progress Estimate:** The final corrected progress estimate $\hat{g}_i$ for the query frame $i$ is calculated by averaging the time-derived labels $g_{j^{*}_{i,e}}$ of all remaining valid episode representatives $M_i$: $\hat{g}_i = \frac{1}{|M_i|} \sum_{e \in M_i} g_{j^{*}_{i,e}}$. If no valid representatives are found for a query frame, its original time-derived label $g_i$ is retained.
6. **Implementation Efficiency:** The process is designed for offline execution. After computing visual embeddings, it primarily involves similarity searches, per-episode maximum selection, and scalar averaging. The authors state that the per-episode argmax can be efficiently computed using a masked scatter-max over the offline corpus, reducing the correction to a few matrix operations.

**C. Advantage Labels from Corrected Progress**

Once the time-derived proxies are corrected to $\hat{g}$, these estimates are used to construct advantage labels for policy training. This serves as a downstream application, showcasing how the improved supervision signal can be integrated.
1. **Advantage Calculation:** For an action chunk starting at frame $i$ with horizon $H$, a scalar corrected advantage proxy $r_i$ is defined as $r_i = \hat{g}_{i+H} - \hat{g}_i$.
2. **Episode End Handling:** For frames near the end of an episode, the difference to the final frame is calculated and rescaled to the horizon rate: $r_i = \frac{H}{T_e - i} (\hat{g}_{T_e} - \hat{g}_i)$.
3. **Policy Conditioning:** Frames are ranked by their $r_i$ values. The top 20% are labeled as "positive-advantage examples." In policy training, specifically for VLA models, these frames receive a plain-text prompt suffix (e.g., "..., advantage: positive"). The policy architecture, training data, and optimization schedule remain unchanged from standard VLA training, meaning UR-VC acts solely as a preprocessor for the supervision signal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of UR-VC was conducted on real bimanual cloth flatten-and-fold data, a long-horizon deformable object manipulation task prone to local regressions and timing variations. Since true physical progress is unobservable, the evaluation focused on validating the conditions for effective correction and demonstrating a downstream impact.

**A. Cross-Episode Coverage and Scaling**
The study found that cross-episode matches are abundant:
- In the primary evaluation set of 150 episodes, 98% of frames had at least one semantically similar neighbor (cosine similarity $\ge 0.90$) in a different episode. This coverage was 69.9% for a stricter threshold of $\ge 0.955$.
- As the retrieval pool increased to 10,000 episodes, coverage further improved, reaching 99.9% at $\ge 0.90$ similarity and 90.4% at $\ge 0.955$.
These results indicate that sufficient redundancy exists in demonstration datasets, even at moderate sizes, to support the cross-episode aggregation mechanism of UR-VC.

**B. Semantic Consistency of Retrieved States**
Visual inspection of retrieved frames demonstrated that SigLIP-2 embeddings effectively capture the folding state of the garment rather than superficial appearance features like color or texture. For query frames at different fold stages, the nearest neighbors retrieved from other episodes consistently exhibited the same underlying task state despite variations in garment appearance. This supports the assumption that visual similarity, as measured by the encoder, corresponds to meaningful task progress.

**C. Recovering Non-Monotone Progress**
UR-VC successfully addresses the monotonicity limitation of time-derived proxies:
- **Local Regressions:** In a real cloth flatten-and-fold episode, the corrected progress estimate decreased when a grasp slip re-introduced wrinkles and the physical state visibly worsened, unlike the raw time-derived label which continued to increase.
- **Quantification of Regressions:** Across the primary evaluation set, 13.4% of frames received a negative horizon advantage under UR-VC, indicating the method's ability to identify and quantify local regressions in progress.
- **Global Trend Preservation:** Despite capturing local regressions, the corrected estimate maintained a high correlation (0.98) with normalized time overall, indicating that the method preserves the general progression of the task.
- **Stability with Scale:** The roughness of the corrected estimate, measured by mean $| \Delta^2 \hat{g}_t |$, decreased by approximately one third when the retrieval pool grew from 50 to 10,000 episodes. This demonstrates that larger retrieval sets lead to smoother and more stable progress estimates.

**D. Downstream Real-Robot Evaluation**
UR-VC's utility was tested in a downstream application: advantage-conditioned VLA training using a π$_{0.5}$ backbone on an AgileX bimanual robot performing flatten-then-fold.
- **Training Setup:** Policies were trained with a mix of 5,700 flatten-and-fold demonstrations and 1,795 recovery demonstrations. The UR-VC variant used corrected progress to identify the top 20% positive-advantage frames for policy conditioning. All other training parameters (model architecture, data, schedule) were identical across methods.
- **Performance:** On a test set of six table conditions (6 garments, 5 repetitions each, totaling 180 trials per method), UR-VC improved the average task success rate from 0.728 (131/180) for the baseline to 0.789 (142/180). UR-VC demonstrated higher success rates in 5 out of 6 table conditions.
- **Conclusion:** These results provide evidence that UR-VC-derived advantage labels, when integrated into a standard VLA pipeline, can lead to improved real-robot policy performance, attributing the gains to the enhanced quality of the supervision signal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UR-VC presents a significant contribution to robot learning by addressing a fundamental limitation in the acquisition of dense supervision signals. The reliance on normalized time as a proxy for physical task progress, while scalable, introduces systemic biases and noise that can hinder policy learning, especially in complex, contact-rich, and deformable object manipulation tasks.

The significance of UR-VC lies in several key aspects:

- **Improved Supervision Quality:** UR-VC directly corrects the noisy time-derived proxy labels, producing a more accurate representation of latent physical task progress. This allows robot learning systems to distinguish between actions that truly advance the task and those that cause regressions or merely represent temporal progression without physical change.
- **Unsupervised and Training-Free Nature:** A major advantage is that UR-VC requires no manual progress labels, reward annotations, or the training of an additional value model. It operates offline as a pre-processing step, making it a low-overhead solution that can be easily integrated into existing pipelines without significant architectural or computational changes. This democratic approach broadens its applicability for researchers and practitioners without extensive labeling budgets.
- **Leveraging Data Redundancy:** The method effectively exploits the inherent redundancy in large-scale robot demonstration datasets. By aggregating evidence from semantically similar states across multiple, independently collected episodes, UR-VC makes the supervision signal more robust to individual demonstration quirks, timing distortions, and minor errors.
- **Complementary Approach:** UR-VC is complementary to existing research that focuses on *learning* value or progress estimators. Instead of training yet another model to predict progress, it improves the *target supervision* itself. This means it can potentially enhance the performance of any downstream method that consumes progress or value signals, including sophisticated VLA models.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Impact:** The empirical validation on real bimanual cloth manipulation, showing improved task success rates for an advantage-conditioned VLA, provides strong evidence of its practical utility. This suggests that better supervision signals, even from an unsupervised correction method, can translate into more capable and robust robot policies.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** By highlighting and providing a solution for the "time is not progress" problem, UR-VC opens avenues for further research into robust proxy label generation and correction techniques. It encourages a more critical examination of default supervision signals and their true alignment with underlying task objectives.

While the current evaluation focuses on a specific deformable object manipulation task, the principles of UR-VC, particularly leveraging cross-episode state recurrence via semantic embeddings, could be applicable to a broader range of robotic tasks where intermediate states are visually meaningful and demonstrations exhibit temporal variability. The method's limitations, such as the assumption that visual similarity equates to task progress and the scope of its downstream evaluation, suggest areas for future exploration to further solidify its impact. Overall, UR-VC offers a practical and principled approach to enhance the quality of supervision in robot learning, contributing to the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
