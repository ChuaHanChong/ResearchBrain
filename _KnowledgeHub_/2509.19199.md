# Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Implicit Step Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> iStar introduces a framework for agentic Large Language Models that uses implicit step rewards derived from a trajectory-based Direct Preference Optimization objective. This approach resolves the credit assignment problem in complex, multi-step environments, leading to state-of-the-art performance, enhanced sample efficiency, and improved training stability for LLM agents across various interactive tasks.

## Problem

- Sparse and delayed rewards in complex LLM agent tasks hinder effective credit assignment over long horizons.
- Existing dense feedback methods, such as token-level rewards or process supervision, are either costly, prone to bias, or introduce high variance, destabilizing training.
- Current reinforcement learning approaches struggle with generalizability and robustness in open-ended, non-stationary environments with potentially unverifiable rewards.

## Method

- **iStar** employs an alternating optimization loop between an ==implicit Process Reward== Model (PRM) and the LLM agent's policy model.
- It derives implicit step rewards for each action by measuring how much more probable an action is under the freshly learned PRM compared to a dynamic previous policy snapshot, using a trajectory-based Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) objective.
- Policy updates combine these implicit step rewards with episode-level outcome rewards, forming a weighted advantage to balance global task success and local action quality during PPO-like optimization.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art success rates on benchmarks like WebShop (**86.5%** success) and VisualSokoban (**91.7%** success), outperforming frontier LLMs and strong RL baselines.
- Demonstrated superior performance in open-ended social interactions within SOTOPIA, increasing goal completion by up to **48%** when interacting with GPT-4o, even in environments with unverifiable rewards.
- Showed significantly improved sample efficiency, reaching the performance of vanilla RLOO twice as fast, and maintained enhanced training stability compared to methods utilizing overly fine-grained rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit step rewards, formulated as a log-ratio of the PRM and a dynamic policy snapshot, offer a stable and dense credit signal for multi-turn interactions, avoiding the noise of overly fine-grained feedback.
> - Training the PRM with a trajectory-based DPO objective on positive-negative pairs effectively learns a step-wise reward function that is theoretically equivalent to a Bradley-Terry model, aligning individual step contributions with overall task success without explicit labels.
> - Combining episode-level advantages with implicit step-level advantages is crucial for robust credit assignment, preventing speculative reward exploitation and achieving optimal learning in long-horizon tasks.
