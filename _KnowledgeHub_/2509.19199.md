# Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Implicit Step Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> iStar introduces a framework for agentic Large Language Models that uses implicit step rewards derived from a trajectory-based Direct Preference Optimization objective. This approach resolves the credit assignment problem in complex, multi-step environments, leading to state-of-the-art performance, enhanced sample efficiency, and improved training stability for LLM agents across various interactive tasks.

## Problem

- Sparse and delayed rewards in complex LLM agent tasks hinder effective credit assignment over long horizons.
- Existing dense feedback methods, such as token-level rewards or process supervision, are either costly, prone to bias, or introduce high variance, destabilizing training.
- Current reinforcement learning approaches struggle with generalizability and robustness in open-ended, non-stationary environments with potentially unverifiable rewards.

## Method

- **iStar** employs an alternating optimization loop between an ==implicit Process Reward== Model (PRM) and the LLM agent's policy model.
- It derives implicit step rewards for each action by measuring how much more probable an action is under the freshly learned PRM compared to a dynamic previous policy snapshot, using a trajectory-based Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) objective.
- Policy updates combine these implicit step rewards with episode-level outcome rewards, forming a weighted advantage to balance global task success and local action quality during PPO-like optimization.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art success rates on benchmarks like WebShop (**86.5%** success) and VisualSokoban (**91.7%** success), outperforming frontier LLMs and strong RL baselines.
- Demonstrated superior performance in open-ended social interactions within SOTOPIA, increasing goal completion by up to **48%** when interacting with GPT-4o, even in environments with unverifiable rewards.
- Showed significantly improved sample efficiency, reaching the performance of vanilla RLOO twice as fast, and maintained enhanced training stability compared to methods utilizing overly fine-grained rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit step rewards, formulated as a log-ratio of the PRM and a dynamic policy snapshot, offer a stable and dense credit signal for multi-turn interactions, avoiding the noise of overly fine-grained feedback.
> - Training the PRM with a trajectory-based DPO objective on positive-negative pairs effectively learns a step-wise reward function that is theoretically equivalent to a Bradley-Terry model, aligning individual step contributions with overall task success without explicit labels.
> - Combining episode-level advantages with implicit step-level advantages is crucial for robust credit assignment, preventing speculative reward exploitation and achieving optimal learning in long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper directly addresses a critical and evolving challenge in the field of artificial intelligence, specifically at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), often referred to as "agentic RL."

Traditionally, LLMs have excelled as passive generators, producing text based on prompts. However, a significant trend is their development into autonomous agents capable of reasoning, acting, and adapting in interactive environments. These "LLM agents" are envisioned for tasks like web navigation, software engineering, and social interaction, demanding sequential decision-making over long horizons.

The broader research landscape of agentic LLMs is currently grappling with several key challenges:
- **Sparse and Delayed Rewards:** In complex, multi-step tasks, explicit feedback (rewards) is often only available at the end of a long sequence of actions, making it difficult to assign credit or blame to intermediate steps. This "credit assignment problem" is a foundational challenge in RL.
- **Long and Non-Markovian Trajectories:** LLM agents often operate with "chain-of-thought" (CoT) reasoning, meaning each "step" is a complex sequence of tokens. Applying rewards at the individual token level can lead to excessively fine-grained feedback, increasing variance and destabilizing training.
- **Open-ended and Non-stationary Environments:** Real-world environments are dynamic, open-ended, and may involve interaction with other agents, leading to unverifiable or subjective rewards (e.g., in social interactions). Traditional RL methods often assume stationary environments and clear reward signals.

Prior attempts to address these challenges include:
- **Process Supervision:** Providing denser feedback at intermediate steps, often through handcrafted labels or human annotation. This is costly, prone to bias, and vulnerable to "reward hacking."
- **Generative Reward Models (GRMs):** Using LLMs as "judges" to predict the quality of intermediate steps. These can be noisy and inconsistent.
- **Implicit Process Reward Models (PRMs):** Learning rewards implicitly, often at the token level. While reducing annotation overhead, token-level rewards can be too fine-grained for multi-turn interactions, leading to high variance.
- **State-Grouping Approaches:** Computing step-level advantages by grouping identical states. This fails in open-ended environments where state overlap is rare.

"AGENTIC REINFORCEMENT LEARNING WITH IMPLICIT STEP REWARDS" (iStar) positions itself as a novel solution that seeks to overcome the limitations of these existing approaches. It provides a general credit-assignment strategy that is label-efficient (no explicit step labels), stable, scalable to multi-turn interactions, and robust to unverifiable rewards in open-ended environments. By introducing *implicit step rewards* derived from a *trajectory-based DPO objective*, iStar offers a more balanced and effective feedback mechanism, sitting between overly sparse trajectory-level rewards and excessively noisy token-level rewards. This work contributes significantly to making LLM agents more robust, efficient, and capable in complex, interactive settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the iStar framework stems from the inherent difficulties in applying standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques to train Large Language Model (LLM) agents in complex, interactive environments. The authors identify several critical problems that current approaches fail to adequately address:

1. **Credit Assignment in Sparse Reward Settings:** LLM agents often operate in environments where positive feedback (rewards) is only received at the end of a long sequence of actions, if at all. This makes it challenging to determine which specific intermediate actions contributed to the final success or failure, a problem known as sparse and delayed rewards. Standard trajectory-level optimization in such scenarios leads to high-variance policy learning, brittle exploration, and limited performance gains.
2. **Variance from Overly Fine-Grained Rewards:** While process supervision and token-level implicit reward models attempt to provide denser feedback, they often suffer from significant drawbacks. Handcrafted step labels are expensive, biased, and can be "hacked." Generative reward models can be noisy. Crucially, token-level process rewards, as used in some prior work, are often too fine-grained for multi-turn agentic tasks, which involve complex "chain-of-thought" (CoT) reasoning before each action. This excessive granularity amplifies variance and destabilizes training as trajectories become longer and more intricate.
3. **Limitations in Open-Ended and Non-Stationary Environments:** Many real-world agent tasks, particularly social interactions, involve non-stationary environments and partners, and may come with "unverifiable rewards" (i.e., subjective or complex to quantify). Methods relying on state-overlap for advantage computation, for instance, fail in such open-ended language environments where identical states are rare.
4. **Need for Generalizability and Stability:** A desirable credit-assignment strategy for LLM agents must be robust and generalizable across diverse domains, including those with subjective or unverifiable outcomes, while maintaining training stability and sample efficiency.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of this research are to design and validate a credit-assignment strategy that is:
- **Label-efficient:** Does not rely on expensive, handcrafted step labels.
- **Stable:** Provides sufficiently coarse feedback to avoid high variance in multi-turn RL.
- **Scalable:** Applicable to long-horizon, multi-turn interactions.
- **Robust and Generalizable:** Works effectively with (un)verifiable rewards in open-ended and non-stationary environments.

By achieving these objectives, iStar aims to enable LLMs to evolve into more capable, autonomous, and efficiently trainable agents for a wide array of complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Implicit Step Rewards for Agentic Reinforcement Learning (iStar)**, introduces a novel credit-assignment strategy that addresses the limitations of prior work by integrating implicit, step-level rewards into standard RL algorithms. iStar operates through an alternating optimization scheme between an implicit Process Reward Model (PRM) and the LLM agent's policy model, creating a self-reinforcing training loop.

#### 3.1. Overview of the iStar Pipeline

The iStar framework consists of three main components that interact iteratively:
1. **Multi-Step Rollout Generation:** The LLM agent (policy model) interacts with the environment to generate trajectories (rollouts).
2. **Implicit PRM Optimization:** These rollouts are then used to train an implicit PRM.
3. **Policy Learning with Combined Advantages:** The updated PRM generates implicit step rewards, which, along with episode-level outcome rewards, are used to compute advantages for updating the LLM agent's policy.

#### 3.2. Implicit Step Rewards

The core idea is to define an implicit step reward, $r_\phi(o_{1:t}, a_t)$, for each action $a_t$ taken at step $t$ in a trajectory. This reward is formulated as:
$r_\phi(o_{1:t}, a_t) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\phi(a_t|o_{1:t}, x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t|o_{1:t}, x)}$

- $\pi_\phi$: Represents the implicit PRM.
- $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$: Refers to a previous snapshot of the LLM agent's policy.
- $\beta$: A temperature hyperparameter that scales the reward.

This formulation measures how much more probable the current action $a_t$ is under the freshly learned PRM ($\pi_\phi$) compared to the previous policy snapshot ($\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$). Positive values indicate actions that the PRM deems responsible for improvements, while negative values suggest actions to be discouraged. This provides dense, step-level feedback.

#### 3.3. Optimizing the Implicit PRM via Trajectory-Based DPO

The implicit PRM $\pi_\phi$ is trained online using positive-negative trajectory pairs. These pairs are generated by the policy model and subsequently ranked by an *outcome reward verifier* (or model). The optimization objective for the PRM is a trajectory-based variant of the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) loss:

$J_{\text{PRM}}(\phi) = -E_{(\tau^+, \tau^-) \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}; x \sim p(X)} \left[ \log \sigma \left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_\phi(\tau^+|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\tau^+|x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\phi(\tau^-|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\tau^-|x)} \right) \right]$

Key distinctions from original DPO:
- **Dynamic Reference Model:** $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$ (the previous policy snapshot) is used as the reference model, whose parameters evolve during training, unlike a frozen initial policy.
- **Trajectory-Level Preferences:** Preferences are learned from *pairwise trajectories* ($\tau^+, \tau^-$) rather than single-shot responses. $\tau^+$ is a trajectory preferred over $\tau^-$, as labeled by the outcome reward verifier (e.g., success rate above zero, or a goal completion score above a threshold).

**Theoretical Justification:** The authors provide theoretical analysis (building on prior work) demonstrating that this learning objective for the implicit PRM is equivalent to a Bradley-Terry (BT) model with a *step-wise reward function*. This ensures that the learned implicit reward genuinely reflects the preferences for individual steps within a trajectory.

#### 3.4. Policy Learning with Implicit Step Rewards

The LLM agent's policy model $\pi_\theta$ is updated using a combination of two types of advantages:

1. **Episode-Level Advantages ($A_E$):** These are derived from the overall *outcome rewards* $r_o(\tau)$ for complete trajectories. For a group of $N$ trajectories, $A_E(\tau_i)$ is computed by centering and normalizing the outcome reward of trajectory $i$ relative to the group's mean and standard deviation. This captures global task success.
2. **Step-Level Advantages ($A_S$):** These are computed using the *implicit step rewards* $r_\phi(a_t)$ generated by the latest PRM. Similar to episode-level advantages, $A_S(a_t)$ is obtained by centering and normalizing the implicit step reward of action $a_t$ relative to the collected step rewards. This provides dense feedback on individual action quality.

The final advantage $A(a_t)$ used for policy updates is a weighted combination of these two:
$A(a_t) = A_E(\tau) + \alpha A_S(a_t)$
where $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter balancing the two advantage types. This combination allows the policy to learn from both global task success and the contribution of individual actions, providing a more robust credit assignment mechanism for long-horizon tasks.

The policy model $\pi_\theta$ is then optimized using a PPO-like surrogate objective, which incorporates the importance sampling ratio at the step-level to ensure low-variance training with multi-step rollouts, without a KL-divergence penalty.

#### 3.5. Self-Reinforcing Training Loop

The alternating optimization between the implicit PRM and the policy model forms a self-reinforcing loop. As the policy improves, it generates better preference data, which in turn refines the implicit PRM. A more accurate PRM then delivers more precise implicit step rewards, further guiding and enhancing the policy. This continuous feedback loop helps maintain data distribution consistency, minimize off-policy bias, and accelerate convergence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of iStar demonstrates its superior performance, efficiency, and generalizability across diverse and challenging agentic tasks, significantly outperforming existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on Benchmarks

- **WebShop & VisualSokoban:** iStar achieves state-of-the-art results.
 - On WebShop, iStar obtains an average 86.5% success rate and 93.6% score (using RLOO as the base RL algorithm). This surpasses frontier LLMs like GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and Claude-Sonnet-4-Thinking, as well as strong RL baselines (PPO, GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++) and recent agentic RL methods like GiGPO (Feng et al., 2025) and single-turn RL PRIME (Cui et al., 2025).
 - On VisualSokoban, iStar achieves 91.7% success, showing notable gains, especially crucial in this domain where irreversible mistakes and limited foresight challenge RL algorithms.
- **SOTOPIA (Open-ended Social Interactions):** In this environment with unverifiable rewards, where GiGPO and PRIME are inapplicable, iStar still achieves state-of-the-art performance.
 - **Self-Chat:** Increases goal completion in hard social scenarios by 14% (from 7.92 to 8.06) compared to vanilla GRPO.
 - **GPT-4o-as-Partner:** Achieves an even more significant increase in goal completion, up to 48% (from 6.68 to 7.16) when interacting with GPT-4o, highlighting its robustness in dynamic, multi-agent settings.

#### 4.2. Generalizability and Compatibility with Existing RL Algorithms

- iStar is shown to be compatible with and consistently improve the performance of various vanilla RL algorithms, including RLOO, REINFORCE++, and GRPO. For instance, iStar with RLOO yields substantial gains of 6.3% in success rate on both WebShop and VisualSokoban, demonstrating its robustness across diverse RL backbones and environments.

#### 4.3. High Sample Efficiency and Training Stability

- **Faster Improvement:** iStar achieves faster improvement in validation metrics during RL training. On WebShop, it reaches the performance of vanilla RLOO in just 105 steps (approximately 2x improvement in training efficiency) and attains its highest score of 94.7% by 165 steps.
- **Increased Stability:** Unlike PRIME (a token-level PRM approach) which shows early gains but then stagnates and fluctuates due to overly fine-grained rewards, iStar maintains consistent improvement. This indicates that its implicit step rewards provide dense feedback while being sufficiently coarse to reduce variance and ensure stable training in multi-turn interactions.

#### 4.4. Efficient Exploration

- **Reward Dynamics:** Visualization of training dynamics shows that implicit step rewards improve early in training, followed by an increase in episode rewards. This suggests that iStar effectively captures good local action heuristics that are then composed into higher-return trajectories.
- **Reduced Episode Length:** iStar leads to shorter episode lengths (fewer steps to achieve a goal) without compromising task success, signifying more efficient exploration and decision-making.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies Confirm Core Components' Importance

- **Implicit Step Rewards vs. Raw Penalties:** Using raw step penalties (e.g., from VisualSokoban) yielded limited improvement over vanilla RL, underscoring that iStar's *learned* implicit step rewards are superior credit signals.
- **Combining Advantages is Crucial:** Directly merging implicit step rewards into episode rewards (instead of combining advantages) showed modest gains. This indicates that gating credit by final task success in addition to rewarding intermediate actions is crucial to prevent speculative reward exploitation and achieve optimal credit assignment in long horizons.
- **Step-Level vs. Token-Level Rewards:** Learning token-level process rewards was suboptimal, further confirming that overly fine-grained rewards introduce noise and difficulty in multi-turn RL.

In summary, the results strongly validate iStar as a highly effective, efficient, and robust solution for the challenging problem of credit assignment in agentic reinforcement learning with LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The iStar framework represents a significant advancement in the training and development of autonomous Large Language Model (LLM) agents, addressing one of the most persistent challenges in reinforcement learning: effective credit assignment in complex, multi-step environments with sparse or unverifiable rewards. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling More Capable LLM Agents:** By providing a robust and label-efficient mechanism for dense, step-level feedback, iStar empowers LLM agents to learn more effectively in challenging interactive environments like web navigation, puzzle-solving, and open-ended social interactions. This moves LLMs beyond passive generation towards truly autonomous, reasoning, and acting entities, accelerating their deployment in real-world applications where multi-turn decision-making is critical.

2. **Addressing the Credit Assignment Problem:** iStar offers a practical and theoretically grounded solution to the credit assignment problem in agentic RL. It elegantly balances the need for dense feedback (via implicit step rewards) with the risks of high variance (by keeping rewards at the action-step level, not token-level, and combining with episode-level advantages). The theoretical justification of its DPO objective mapping to a step-wise reward function adds strong scientific backing.

3. **Label-Efficiency and Scalability:** A major hurdle for training advanced agents is the cost and bias of human annotation for process supervision. iStar's implicit reward modeling, derived from trajectory preferences, significantly reduces the reliance on explicit step labels. This makes the training process more scalable, cost-effective, and less prone to human biases, thereby accelerating research and development in agentic AI.

4. **Robustness to Diverse Environments and Reward Structures:** The method's demonstrated success across varied benchmarks—from structured environments with verifiable outcomes (WebShop, VisualSokoban) to open-ended social interactions with unverifiable rewards (SOTOPIA)—highlights its remarkable robustness and generalizability. This versatility is crucial for developing agents that can operate effectively in the messy and unpredictable real world.

5. **Improved Training Dynamics:** The observed improvements in sample efficiency and training stability are critical for practical LLM agent development. Faster convergence and more stable learning curves mean less computational cost and more reliable training, which are vital considerations given the resource-intensive nature of LLM fine-tuning. The self-reinforcing training loop between the PRM and policy further contributes to this stability and efficiency.

6. **Compatibility and Modularity:** iStar's compatibility with various existing RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++) makes it a modular and easily integratable enhancement. This allows researchers and practitioners to leverage iStar's benefits within their preferred RL frameworks without significant architectural overhaul.

**Potential Future Impact:**
- **Broader Applications:** The principles of iStar could be extended to other complex tasks requiring long-horizon reasoning, such as advanced mathematical problem-solving (where CoT steps are critical) or sophisticated code generation and debugging.
- **Unified Agent Architectures:** The paper points to future work on unifying the implicit PRM and the policy model, which could lead to more memory-efficient and integrated agent architectures, potentially improving representation sharing and overall performance.
- **Multi-Objective Learning:** Extending the implicit PRM to predict multi-objective preferences (e.g., in SOTOPIA, beyond just goal completion) could lead to agents with more nuanced and human-aligned behaviors.
- **Test-Time Guidance:** The learned implicit step rewards could also serve as powerful signals for test-time search and reranking, guiding agents to better actions without further training.

In conclusion, iStar offers a foundational step towards building truly autonomous, intelligent, and adaptable LLM agents. By solving the vexing credit assignment problem in a practical and generalizable manner, it paves the way for a new generation of AI assistants and autonomous systems capable of tackling increasingly complex real-world challenges.
