# IntPhys 2: Benchmarking Intuitive Physics Understanding In Complex Synthetic Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meta's FAIR laboratory developed IntPhys 2, a benchmark to evaluate intuitive physics understanding in AI systems. The benchmark features photorealistic environments, complex occlusion scenarios, and a "violation of expectation" framework, revealing that state-of-the-art AI models achieve near-chance performance (52-58% accuracy) while humans achieve 96% accuracy.

## Problem

- Existing intuitive physics benchmarks like IntPhys 1 are saturated, with state-of-the-art AI models achieving near-perfect scores, thus failing to differentiate model capabilities.
- Previous benchmarks featured simplistic environments and lacked the visual complexity and real-world variations necessary to robustly test AI systems.
- Many evaluations did not adequately test a model's ability to maintain understanding of objects during extended occlusions, a crucial aspect of real-world physics.

## Method

- Researchers at Meta's **FAIR** laboratory introduced **IntPhys** 2, a comprehensive benchmark for intuitive physics understanding in AI, built using Unreal Engine for photorealistic environments.
- The benchmark employs a "violation of expectation" (VOE) framework with quadruplet video structures (two possible, two impossible outcomes) focusing exclusively on challenging occlusion scenarios.
- **IntPhys** 2 evaluates four core physical principles (Object Permanence, Immutability, Spatio-Temporal Continuity, Solidity) across diverse environmental backgrounds and camera perspectives to test memory and reasoning.

## Results

- Humans achieved 96.44% overall accuracy on IntPhys 2, including 92.44% on the challenging Held-Out set, demonstrating strong intuitive physics understanding.
- State-of-the-art MLLMs like Gemini 2.5 Flash achieved only 55.63% accuracy, while predictive models such as V-JEPA 2 reached 57.51% accuracy, both barely exceeding random chance.
- Performance of AI models often decreased when given more input frames, indicating difficulty processing extended temporal contexts and maintaining consistent object representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial gap exists between human and state-of-the-art AI performance in intuitive physics, with AI models performing near chance levels (50-58%) on IntPhys 2 compared to human accuracy (96%).
> - Current AI architectures, including MLLMs and predictive models, struggle with maintaining object representations during extended occlusions and with temporal reasoning in visually complex environments.
> - The significant performance drop from previous benchmarks suggests that current models may rely on superficial visual patterns rather than developing a genuine understanding of physical principles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Understanding intuitive physics—the ability to predict how objects move and interact in the physical world—is fundamental to human cognition. From infancy, humans demonstrate remarkable capabilities in understanding physical principles like object permanence, solidity, and continuity. However, despite significant advances in artificial intelligence, current models still struggle to match human-level performance in this domain.

This paper introduces IntPhys 2, a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate intuitive physics understanding in AI systems. Developed by researchers at Meta's FAIR laboratory, this work addresses critical limitations in existing benchmarks by creating more challenging and realistic scenarios that better reflect the complexity of real-world physics understanding.

### 2. The Challenge of Evaluating Intuitive Physics

Current artificial intelligence models, including state-of-the-art multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and predictive video models, consistently fall short of human capabilities when it comes to understanding basic physical principles. While previous benchmarks like the original IntPhys have been valuable, they suffer from several limitations:

**Benchmark Saturation**: Advanced models like V-JEPA have achieved near-perfect performance (98.30%) on the original IntPhys benchmark, suggesting that these evaluations no longer provide meaningful differentiation between models.

**Simplistic Environments**: Earlier benchmarks often featured basic geometric shapes in simple environments, lacking the visual complexity and variations present in real-world scenarios.

**Limited Memory Requirements**: Many existing benchmarks don't adequately test a model's ability to maintain understanding of objects during extended occlusions, a crucial aspect of real-world physics understanding.

IntPhys 2 addresses these limitations by focusing on four core physical principles inspired by developmental psychology research:

1. **Object Permanence**: Objects continue to exist even when not visible
2. **Object Immutability**: Objects maintain their shape and structure over time
3. **Spatio-Temporal Continuity**: Objects move smoothly through space without teleporting
4. **Solidity**: Objects cannot pass through one another

### 3. IntPhys 2 Design and Methodology

The benchmark employs a sophisticated "violation of expectation" (VOE) framework, a methodology extensively used in developmental psychology to study infant cognition. This approach presents scenarios where objects either behave according to physical laws (possible events) or violate them (impossible events).

**Quadruplet Video Structure**: Each scenario consists of four videos—two possible and two impossible outcomes. This design is crucial for preventing models from relying on low-level visual cues rather than understanding high-level physical principles. The possible video from one scenario can serve as the impossible video in another, forcing models to reason about temporal dependencies.

**Enhanced Realism**: IntPhys 2 leverages Unreal Engine to create photorealistic environments with:
- Dynamic shadows and realistic lighting
- Complex textures and materials
- Diverse environmental backgrounds
- Both fixed and moving camera perspectives

**Focus on Occlusions**: Unlike previous benchmarks that might include various scenario types, IntPhys 2 exclusively focuses on occlusion scenarios. This targeted approach allows for deeper evaluation of how models handle objects that temporarily disappear from view—a common occurrence in real-world settings.

**Data Organization**: The benchmark includes three carefully designed splits:
- **Debug Set**: 60 videos with static cameras and minimal environmental variations for model calibration
- **Main Set**: 1,012 videos divided into Easy, Medium, and Hard difficulty levels based on visual complexity
- **Held-Out Set**: 344 videos for testing generalization without access to ground truth labels

### 4. Evaluation Framework

IntPhys 2 implements distinct evaluation protocols for different types of AI models:

**Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**: The evaluation tests models like Gemini, GPT-4o, and Qwen-VL by asking them to judge the plausibility of video sequences. The protocol includes careful prompt engineering and testing with different numbers of input frames to understand model limitations.

**Predictive Models**: For models like V-JEPA and VideoMAE, the evaluation measures "surprise"—the prediction error when unexpected events occur. Videos are divided into context segments (for making predictions) and target segments (what gets predicted). The protocol tests whether models show higher surprise for physically impossible events.

### 5. Experimental Results

The evaluation reveals a striking and consistent pattern: current AI models perform significantly below human levels on IntPhys 2, often barely exceeding random chance performance.

**Human Performance**: Humans achieve near-perfect accuracy (96.44% overall, 92.44% on the most challenging Held-Out set), demonstrating robust understanding across all physical principles and camera conditions.

**MLLM Performance**:
- Best performer: Gemini 2.5 Flash at 55.63% overall accuracy
- Most models hover around 52-54%, only marginally above chance (50%)
- Performance often decreases when given more input frames, suggesting difficulty in processing extended temporal contexts
- Significant sensitivity to prompt phrasing and output format requirements

**Predictive Model Performance**:
- V-JEPA 2 (best performer): 57.51% overall accuracy
- Most models perform at or near chance level (50%)
- Stark contrast with original IntPhys performance (V-JEPA achieved 98.30% on IntPhys 1)
- No consistent advantage for either fixed or moving camera conditions
- Single video classification proves even more challenging than pairwise comparisons

The results demonstrate that current AI architectures struggle with the enhanced memory requirements and visual complexity of IntPhys 2. Qualitative analysis suggests that while models can detect initial object motion, they fail to maintain robust representations during extended occlusions and don't consistently show higher surprise for physically impossible events.

### 6. Significance and Implications

IntPhys 2 represents a significant advancement in benchmarking intuitive physics understanding and reveals critical gaps in current AI capabilities. The consistent near-chance performance of state-of-the-art models, contrasted with near-perfect human performance, highlights several important implications:

**Diagnostic Value**: The benchmark successfully identifies specific weaknesses in current AI architectures, particularly in short-term memory, temporal reasoning, and handling of complex visual environments. This diagnostic capability is crucial for guiding future research directions.

**Beyond Pattern Matching**: The results suggest that current models may be relying on superficial visual patterns rather than developing genuine understanding of physical principles. The drop in performance from IntPhys 1 to IntPhys 2 indicates that increased complexity reveals the limitations of pattern-matching approaches.

**Research Directions**: The findings point toward several critical areas for future investigation:
- Development of architectures with enhanced visual memory capabilities
- Integration of explicit physical reasoning mechanisms
- Self-supervised learning objectives that encourage understanding of physical causality
- Hybrid approaches combining data-driven learning with symbolic physical knowledge

**Real-World Applications**: Intuitive physics understanding is fundamental for AI systems that must operate in physical environments, including robotics, autonomous vehicles, and general-purpose AI agents. The persistent human-AI gap revealed by IntPhys 2 underscores the work needed before AI systems can safely and effectively navigate complex real-world scenarios.

The benchmark also strengthens connections between AI research and cognitive science, drawing on developmental psychology insights to create more principled evaluation frameworks. This interdisciplinary approach may prove essential for achieving human-level artificial intelligence.

IntPhys 2's open release, including code and evaluation protocols, provides the research community with a robust tool for measuring progress in this fundamental aspect of intelligence. As models continue to advance, this benchmark will serve as a critical test of whether improvements in scale and architecture translate to genuine understanding of the physical world that surrounds us.
