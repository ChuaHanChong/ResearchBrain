# IntPhys 2: Benchmarking Intuitive Physics Understanding In Complex Synthetic Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meta's FAIR laboratory developed IntPhys 2, a benchmark to evaluate intuitive physics understanding in AI systems. The benchmark features photorealistic environments, complex occlusion scenarios, and a "violation of expectation" framework, revealing that state-of-the-art AI models achieve near-chance performance (52-58% accuracy) while humans achieve 96% accuracy.

## Problem

- Existing intuitive physics benchmarks like IntPhys 1 are saturated, with state-of-the-art AI models achieving near-perfect scores, thus failing to differentiate model capabilities.
- Previous benchmarks featured simplistic environments and lacked the visual complexity and real-world variations necessary to robustly test AI systems.
- Many evaluations did not adequately test a model's ability to maintain understanding of objects during extended occlusions, a crucial aspect of real-world physics.

## Method

- Researchers at Meta's **FAIR** laboratory introduced **IntPhys** 2, a comprehensive benchmark for intuitive physics understanding in AI, built using Unreal Engine for photorealistic environments.
- The benchmark employs a "violation of expectation" (VOE) framework with quadruplet video structures (two possible, two impossible outcomes) focusing exclusively on challenging occlusion scenarios.
- **IntPhys** 2 evaluates four core physical principles (Object Permanence, Immutability, Spatio-Temporal Continuity, Solidity) across diverse environmental backgrounds and camera perspectives to test memory and reasoning.

## Results

- Humans achieved 96.44% overall accuracy on IntPhys 2, including 92.44% on the challenging Held-Out set, demonstrating strong intuitive physics understanding.
- State-of-the-art MLLMs like Gemini 2.5 Flash achieved only 55.63% accuracy, while predictive models such as V-JEPA 2 reached 57.51% accuracy, both barely exceeding random chance.
- Performance of AI models often decreased when given more input frames, indicating difficulty processing extended temporal contexts and maintaining consistent object representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial gap exists between human and state-of-the-art AI performance in intuitive physics, with AI models performing near chance levels (50-58%) on IntPhys 2 compared to human accuracy (96%).
> - Current AI architectures, including MLLMs and predictive models, struggle with maintaining object representations during extended occlusions and with temporal reasoning in visually complex environments.
> - The significant performance drop from previous benchmarks suggests that current models may rely on superficial visual patterns rather than developing a genuine understanding of physical principles.
