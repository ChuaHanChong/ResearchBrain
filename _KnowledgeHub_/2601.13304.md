# CausalSpatial: A Benchmark for Object-Centric Causal Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new diagnostic benchmark, CausalSpatial, evaluates Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) capacity for causal spatial reasoning, revealing their tendency for spatially ungrounded hallucinations when predicting object interactions. The CAUSAL OBJECT WORLD MODEL (COW) framework is also introduced, providing explicit visual simulation to ground MLLM reasoning in physical reality and improving performance on causal spatial tasks.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with causal spatial reasoning, failing to predict dynamic consequences of actions in 3D environments.
- MLLMs over-rely on linguistic priors, leading to 'spatially ungrounded hallucinations' when attempting causal reasoning tasks.
- Existing benchmarks do not adequately assess models' ability to reason about dynamic object interactions and causal prediction at an object level.

## Method

- Developed ==CausalSpatial==, a synthetic, object-centric diagnostic benchmark featuring four causal anticipation tasks (e.g., Collision, Occlusion, Trajectory) across two difficulty levels, using physically simulated 3D scenes.
- Created the ==CAUSAL OBJECT WORLD MODEL (COW)== framework, which externalizes 4D trajectory simulation and generates controllable videos to provide MLLMs with explicit visual evidence of causality.
- Evaluated MLLMs using ==True Positive Rate (TPR) and Not Sure Rate (NSR)== on multiple-choice questions with 'Not Sure' options to diagnose hallucination bias and epistemic uncertainty.

## Results

- Humans achieved **84.49%** overall accuracy on the CausalSpatial benchmark, while the top-performing MLLM, GPT-5, scored **54.17%**.
- Collision Anticipation proved most challenging for MLLMs, with many open-source models performing near random guessing on Level 2, whereas Trajectory Anticipation showed the narrowest performance gap (GPT-5 at **64.96%** on L2 vs. human **62.50%**).
- Incorporating three initial frames from COW-generated simulation videos as visual cues led to performance gains for MLLMs, with Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct improving by **2.40%** on Collision and **2.20%** on Trajectory tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a substantial performance gap compared to humans in causal spatial reasoning, with GPT-5 scoring 54.17% versus human 84.49%.
> - MLLMs often display increased confidence (lower Not Sure Rate) without a proportional increase in accuracy, indicating overconfidence in incorrect, ungrounded predictions and a tendency for textual hallucinations.
> - Explicit visual simulation provided by the COW framework consistently boosts MLLM performance on CausalSpatial tasks, validating that grounding reasoning in dynamic physical reality helps suppress hallucinations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical gap in the capabilities of current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): their struggle with **causal spatial reasoning**. While MLLMs have made remarkable progress in 2D image understanding and even 3D static spatial perception (e.g., identifying objects, their attributes, and their relationships in a static scene), they largely fall short when asked "what-if" questions that require anticipating the dynamic consequences of actions in a 3D environment. This limitation stems from their tendency to over-rely on linguistic priors, leading to "spatially ungrounded hallucinations" during reasoning processes.

The broader research landscape of visual reasoning benchmarks has traditionally focused on:
- **2D Perception**: Tasks like object recognition and attribute identification (e.g., VQA, CLEVR).
- **3D Static Relations**: Understanding spatial relationships between objects in a fixed 3D scene (e.g., GQA, Spatial457).
- **Temporal Dynamics (Passive)**: Observing and describing pre-existing motion or events (e.g., STI-Bench, VSI-Super) but not predicting outcomes of hypothetical interventions.

The authors argue that true "4D causal spatial reasoning" requires models to:
1. Extract 3D spatial relationships from static images.
2. Mentally simulate hypothetical or counterfactual interactions with temporal consistency.
3. Infer causal consequences of these interactions.

This paper positions CausalSpatial as the first **object-centric diagnostic benchmark** specifically designed to evaluate this advanced form of reasoning. It differentiates itself from existing benchmarks like CausalVQA (Foss et al., 2025) by focusing on **object-level manipulation** and the prediction of *causal outcomes* in realistic 3D environments, rather than just descriptive tasks or passive motion tracking.

Furthermore, the paper connects to the growing interest in **World Models (WMs)**. While WMs have shown promise in capturing causal outcomes and temporally consistent dynamics, existing approaches often focus on global scene exploration or ego-motion, lacking the **object-level spatial consistency** crucial for fine-grained causal reasoning about specific object interactions. The proposed CAUSAL OBJECT WORLD MODEL (COW) aims to bridge this by providing a framework for controlled, object-centric video generation that can serve as a visual grounding mechanism for MLLMs. This work is a significant step towards enabling MLLMs to move from static scene understanding to dynamic, interactive, and causally-aware intelligence, crucial for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and advanced human-computer interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold:
1. **To expose and diagnose the fundamental limitations of current MLLMs** in performing causal spatial reasoning, particularly their inability to anticipate the consequences of object movements in a 3D environment.
2. **To propose and demonstrate a novel framework**, CAUSAL OBJECT WORLD MODEL (COW), that leverages externalized visual simulation to provide explicit causal cues, thereby helping MLLMs overcome these limitations and ground their reasoning in physical reality.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that despite their impressive linguistic and visual understanding capabilities, MLLMs fundamentally lack the ability that humans possess naturally: to observe a static scene and predict "what happens next" if an object were to move. This "Causal Spatial Reasoning" is essential for understanding the physical world and performing real-world tasks.

Specifically, the authors identify several key problems motivating their work:
- **Static vs. Dynamic Reasoning**: Current MLLMs are largely restricted to static spatial perception, unable to answer dynamic "what-if" questions about object interactions.
- **Hallucination in Reasoning**: When MLLMs attempt causal reasoning, they tend to over-rely on textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT), which often drifts from visual evidence, leading to fluent but spatially ungrounded and inaccurate predictions. They generate "spatial illusion" rather than physically plausible outcomes.
- **Lack of Diagnostic Benchmarks**: Existing benchmarks do not adequately assess models' ability to reason about how objects dynamically affect each other in given spatial contexts, especially regarding causal prediction and object-level manipulation. There's a need for a comprehensive, object-centric diagnostic tool.
- **Need for Visual Grounding**: There is a recognized need for mechanisms that can anchor MLLM reasoning in concrete visual and physical realities, rather than abstract linguistic patterns.

By introducing CausalSpatial and COW, the researchers aim to not only pinpoint these deficiencies but also to offer a promising direction for developing more robust and causally intelligent MLLMs capable of better interacting with and understanding the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-pronged approach: first, the creation of a diagnostic benchmark, CausalSpatial, and second, the development of a framework, COW, to address the identified MLLM shortcomings.

#### 3.1. CausalSpatial Benchmark Design

CausalSpatial is a synthetic, object-centric benchmark designed to diagnose MLLMs' causal spatial reasoning capabilities.
- **Question Formulation**: Each query consists of an initial image ($I_0$) of a 3D scene and a textual motion context ($T$) describing a hypothetical motion. The model must predict the future spatial state ($S_{future}$) as if the object moved. This requires approximating a transition function $f: S_0 | I_0, m \rightarrow S_{future}$.
- **Object-level Design**: The focus is on how a single target object's hypothetical motion affects other objects, demanding precise 3D spatial attribute perception, mental simulation, and causal consequence reasoning.
- **Taxonomy of Tasks**: Four distinct causal anticipation tasks are defined:
 - **Collision Anticipation**: Predict if a target object will collide if it moves, and how removing another object might change the outcome. Requires reasoning about object arrangement and conflicts.
 - **Occlusion Anticipation**: Predict how moving a target object affects the visibility of other objects, requiring understanding camera perspective and depth relationships.
 - **Trajectory Anticipation**: Predict object motion under basic physical laws (gravity, bouncing, momentum transfer) and interactions.
 - **Compatibility Anticipation**: Assess the feasibility of containment (e.g., fitting an object into a container) considering geometric constraints (overflow, overlap, penetration, stability).
- **Difficulty Levels**: Each task has two levels:
 - **L1 (One-to-one reasoning)**: Focuses on interactions between a single pair of objects.
 - **L2 (One-to-multiple reasoning)**: Requires comprehending simultaneous interactions with multiple objects.
- **Data Collection and Annotation**:
 - Utilizes publicly available realistic 3D scenes and assets (over 50 object categories) with refined textures to ensure visual realism.
 - Scenes are accompanied by annotations like RGB images, depth maps, and oriented bounding boxes.
 - Action prompts define hypothetical motions, which are then **physically simulated and rendered using Blender's physics engine** to ensure adherence to real-world dynamics and physical plausibility.
 - Each instance undergoes human verification for causal correctness and quality.
- **Evaluation Setup**:
 - Evaluates state-of-the-art open-source (e.g., Qwen-VL, LLaVA) and closed-source MLLMs (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini, Claude), plus human performance.
 - **Metrics**: Employs True Positive Rate (TPR) for accuracy and **Not Sure Rate (NSR)** to quantify abstention when uncertain, aiming to mitigate hallucination bias and distinguish confident errors from epistemic uncertainty.
 - Questions are multiple-choice with a "Not Sure" option, with randomized answer ordering and explicit Chain-of-Thought prompting.

#### 3.2. CAUSAL OBJECT WORLD MODEL (COW) Framework

COW is proposed as an object-centric framework to externalize the simulation process and provide visual grounding for MLLMs.
- **4D Trajectory Simulation**:
 - Extends spatial state to 4D: $Obj_i = \{x, y, z, v_x, v_y, v_z\}$.
 - Initial position (bounding box) and velocity vector (orientation/direction indicator) are estimated using prompting MLLMs.
 - Synthetic trajectory $\tau$ is computed by iteratively updating object position, modulated by physical factors like gravity (using Blender's physics engine).
- **Controllable Video Generation**:
 - Uses ATI (Wang et al., 2025a), a diffusion-based video generation model, as the world model.
 - Predicted trajectories ($\tau_i$) and motion descriptions ($m_i$) act as control signals to synthesize coherent future frames ($F_{later} = W_\theta(F_{init} | \tau_t^i, d_i)$). This bridges analytical physical modeling with data-driven imagination.
- **Assist MLLMs in Reasoning**:
 - The MLLM jointly interprets the simulated video (e.g., three initial frames) and the text query.
 - This provides explicit visual evidence of causality, allowing the MLLM to ground its reasoning in dynamic physical reality rather than relying solely on linguistic priors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several critical findings regarding the current state of MLLMs in causal spatial reasoning:

- **Severe Performance Gap with Humans**: Humans achieve high accuracy (84.49% overall, with 85.44% on Level 1 and 83.54% on Level 2). In stark contrast, even the state-of-the-art GPT-5 scores only 54.17%, indicating a profound gap between machine and human capabilities in this domain.
- **Varied Performance Across Subtasks**:
 - **Collision Anticipation** is the most challenging task for MLLMs, with many open-source models barely surpassing random guessing on Level 2. Humans, however, maintain high accuracy.
 - **Occlusion Anticipation** also poses significant difficulties for open-source models (20-30% on L2), while closed-source models like GPT-5 show better robustness (62.96% on L2), suggesting stronger internal 3D spatial representations.
 - **Trajectory Anticipation** exhibits the narrowest performance gap. MLLMs show surprising competence (GPT-5 at 64.96% on L2 vs. human 62.50%), suggesting they are relatively effective at recognizing or mapping common trajectory patterns, even if they lack deeper physical understanding. This implies MLLMs might be better at identifying plausible *paths* than understanding complex *interactions*.
- **Limited Returns from Model Scaling**: Within the Qwen3-VL series, scaling model parameters (e.g., from 4B to 30B-A3B) yields limited proportional improvement in causal spatial reasoning accuracy. The performance plateaus, suggesting that "more compute" alone is not sufficient.
- **Decoupling of Confidence and Accuracy**: Larger models, especially with "thinking" modes, exhibit dramatically reduced "Not Sure Rate" (NSR), indicating higher confidence and decisiveness. However, this increased confidence is *not* accompanied by a corresponding leap in accuracy, implying MLLMs are often overconfident in their incorrect, ungrounded predictions.
- **Failure Analysis: Textual Hallucinations**: MLLMs frequently produce lengthy and seemingly coherent textual Chain-of-Thought reasoning that **drifts from visual evidence**. They misinterpret or dilute visual cues, relying on generic linguistic patterns rather than specific geometric constraints. This leads to plausible-sounding but spatially inaccurate causal conclusions (e.g., ignoring direct object blockages or misjudging relative sizes).
- **Effectiveness of CAUSAL OBJECT WORLD MODEL (COW)**: Introducing just three initial frames from COW-generated simulation videos as additional visual cues leads to a **consistent performance boost** in CausalSpatial. For instance, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct shows gains of 2.40% on Collision and 2.20% on Trajectory. This validates the hypothesis that explicit visual simulation helps ground MLLMs' reasoning in actual physical dynamics, thereby suppressing hallucinations. The slight decrease in Occlusion performance suggests a need for longer-term simulations in tasks with continuous or evolving occlusion dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CausalSpatial benchmark and the proposed COW framework represent significant contributions with substantial potential impact on the development of more intelligent and robust AI systems:

- **Advancing the Frontier of Visual Reasoning**: CausalSpatial elevates the definition of visual intelligence beyond static perception to dynamic, causal reasoning. It provides a first-of-its-kind diagnostic testbed, rigorously defining and evaluating a crucial cognitive ability that is currently lacking in MLLMs. By explicitly focusing on "what-if" questions and object-centric manipulation in realistic 3D scenes, it pushes the field towards more human-like understanding of the physical world.
- **Highlighting MLLM Limitations**: The benchmark starkly reveals a critical weakness in state-of-the-art MLLMs: their tendency to generate "spatially ungrounded hallucinations" due to over-reliance on linguistic priors. This finding is crucial for directing future research, demonstrating that simply scaling up models or refining textual reasoning alone is insufficient for achieving true causal spatial intelligence. The decoupling of confidence and accuracy, where models become more decisive without being more correct, points to a fundamental issue in their epistemic uncertainty modeling.
- **Proposing a Solution for Visual Grounding**: The CAUSAL OBJECT WORLD MODEL (COW) offers a promising conceptual and practical approach to mitigate MLLM hallucinations. By externalizing the physical simulation process into visually explicit video sequences, COW provides the concrete, temporally consistent visual cues that MLLMs currently struggle to generate internally. This framework demonstrates the power of integrating physically plausible world models with MLLMs to anchor reasoning in reality.
- **Paving the Way for Embodied AI and Robotics**: Causal spatial reasoning is foundational for embodied AI agents and robots to effectively plan, interact with, and navigate complex physical environments. A robot needs to predict the consequences of moving an object to avoid collisions or achieve a goal. CausalSpatial provides a benchmark for developing and evaluating models that can perform such predictions, while COW offers a methodology for implementing this capability.
- **Driving Future Research**: The paper opens several avenues for future work:
 - **Improving World Models**: Further enhancing the physical plausibility and geometric consistency of generated videos, particularly for complex attributes like volume, shape deformation, and intricate inter-object collision dynamics.
 - **Integrating World Models with MLLMs**: Exploring more seamless and efficient ways to integrate external simulations into MLLM architectures, perhaps through tighter feedback loops or more advanced multi-modal fusion mechanisms.
 - **Combating Hallucinations**: Developing MLLMs that are inherently more robust to visual ambiguities and less prone to linguistic over-generalization, perhaps through stronger visual grounding at earlier stages of reasoning or better uncertainty quantification.

In conclusion, "CausalSpatial" is a landmark paper that not only provides a robust diagnostic tool for a critical aspect of AI intelligence but also offers a concrete, forward-looking solution pathway, effectively bridging the gap between abstract linguistic reasoning and the concrete dynamics of the physical world.
