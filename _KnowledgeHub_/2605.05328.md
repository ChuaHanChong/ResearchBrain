# Query2Uncertainty: Robust Uncertainty Quantification and Calibration for 3D Object Detection under Distribution Shift

> [!summary] Summary
> Query2Uncertainty, a density-aware calibration framework, enhances the robustness of uncertainty estimates for 3D object detection by dynamically adjusting both classification and bounding box regression uncertainties based on the density of latent object queries. This method consistently outperformed non-density-aware baselines and other uncertainty quantification techniques, achieving improved calibration quality across in-distribution and corrupted environments on nuScenes and MultiCorrupt datasets.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems require reliable 3D object detection with accurate uncertainty estimates, but current deep learning detectors often produce overconfident predictions, posing safety risks.
- Existing uncertainty quantification (UQ) and post-hoc calibration methods often fail or become brittle when encountering distribution shifts (e.g., adverse weather) and do not jointly calibrate classification and regression uncertainty.
- A lack of a unified benchmark hinders comprehensive evaluation of uncertainty in 3D object detection, particularly for joint classification and regression tasks.

## Method

- Developed **Query2Uncertainty**, a ==density-aware post-hoc calibration== framework for DETR-style 3D object detectors, employing ==RealNVP normalizing flows== to estimate the feature density of latent object queries.
- Integrated the ==normalized query density score== into existing post-hoc calibrators (e.g., ==Temperature Scaling==, ==Isotonic Regression==) to dynamically modulate both classification confidences and regression variances.
- Established a comprehensive evaluation framework, extending the nuScenes benchmark, to jointly assess classification uncertainty (==D-ECE==, ==LaECE==) and regression uncertainty (==MCA==, ==KS==) under various distribution shifts.

## Results

- Density-aware calibrators (DA-TS, DA-PS, DA-IR) consistently outperformed non-density-aware baselines for both in-distribution and distribution-shifted scenarios on nuScenes and MultiCorrupt datasets.
- For classification under distribution shifts, DA-IR (Cls.) achieved D-ECE values of **7.211%** for PETR and **6.895%** for SECOND, improving upon IR (Cls.) at **7.449%** and **7.076%**.
- For regression under distribution shifts, DA-TS (Cls.) yielded MCA_xyz of **3.546%** for PETR and **4.037%** for SECOND, which was better than TS (Cls.) at **3.780%** and **5.264%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The density of latent object queries serves as a robust, adaptable signal for identifying distribution shifts and dynamically modulating calibration parameters in 3D object detection.
> - Density-aware calibration significantly improves the robustness of uncertainty estimates for both classification and regression, particularly when detectors encounter out-of-distribution data.
> - Post-hoc, class-wise density-aware calibration offers an efficient and effective approach for 3D object detection, outperforming global calibration and computationally intensive sampling-based methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of autonomous systems, such as vehicles and robots, necessitates reliable environmental perception, which includes accurate object detection and robust uncertainty estimation. Current 3D object detectors, often based on multi-view cameras and LiDAR point clouds, have advanced in accuracy but frequently produce overconfident predictions. This miscalibration poses safety risks, particularly when these systems encounter novel or "distribution-shifted" scenarios (e.g., adverse weather conditions or different geographical locations) that differ from their training data.

Existing uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods for deep neural networks include sampling-based techniques like Monte-Carlo Dropout (MCD) and Deep Ensembles (DE), or parametric approaches that directly model variance. While these methods aim to estimate uncertainty, they are often computationally intensive or focus on either classification or regression uncertainty in isolation. Post-hoc calibration methods, such such as Temperature Scaling (TS), Platt Scaling (PS), and Isotonic Regression (IR), have demonstrated effectiveness in correcting miscalibration for in-distribution data, particularly for classification tasks in 2D detection. However, these traditional post-hoc methods are often brittle under distribution shifts because they apply a static correction irrespective of the input data's resemblance to the training distribution.

A more recent line of research explores density-aware calibration, which leverages feature density to adjust confidence scores dynamically, particularly for image classification in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. This approach has not been extensively applied to the more complex domain of 3D object detection, nor has it been explored for jointly calibrating both classification and bounding box regression uncertainties within a unified framework. This paper addresses this gap by extending density-aware calibration to 3D object detection, aiming to provide robust and calibrated uncertainty estimates that adapt to distribution shifts, while also establishing a comprehensive evaluation framework for 3D UQ.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for robust uncertainty quantification and calibration in 3D object detection, specifically addressing the challenges posed by distribution shifts. The motivation stems from several critical needs in autonomous systems:

- **Safety and Reliability:** Autonomous vehicles and robots must operate safely in diverse and unpredictable real-world environments. Unreliable or miscalibrated uncertainty estimates from 3D object detectors can lead to incorrect decisions in downstream tasks like object tracking and collision avoidance, thereby compromising safety. Current deep learning models tend to be overconfident, especially when facing data outside their training distribution, which exacerbates this problem.
- **Robustness to Distribution Shift:** While modern detectors perform well on in-distribution data, their performance and confidence calibration often degrade significantly when encountering domain shifts (e.g., changes in lighting, weather, or sensor noise). Existing post-hoc calibration methods, designed for in-distribution performance, typically fail to adapt in such scenarios. A method that can dynamically adjust uncertainties based on how "familiar" the input data is to the model's training experience is therefore highly motivated.
- **Joint Calibration of Classification and Regression:** 3D object detection involves both classifying objects and regressing their 3D bounding box parameters (position, size, orientation). Traditional UQ and calibration often treat these aspects separately. This work aims to jointly recalibrate both classification confidences and bounding box regression uncertainties, which is crucial for holistic and reliable perception.
- **Leveraging Query-Based Detectors:** The paper specifically targets DETR-style 3D object detectors, which utilize "object queries" as a compact, location- and class-aware feature representation. The authors hypothesize that the density of these latent object queries can serve as a robust signal for identifying distribution shifts and subsequently modulating calibration.
- **Standardized Evaluation:** A lack of a unified benchmark for comprehensively evaluating uncertainty in 3D object detection hinders systematic progress. A key objective is to establish such a framework, building upon existing benchmarks like nuScenes, to facilitate rigorous comparison of UQ methods.

In summary, the core motivation is to enhance the trustworthiness of 3D object detection in safety-critical autonomous applications by developing a lightweight, adaptive, and robust calibration method that works across diverse operating conditions, alongside a standardized evaluation toolkit.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Query2Uncertainty, introduces a density-aware calibration framework for DETR-style 3D object detectors. It couples a feature density estimator with traditional post-hoc calibrators to refine both classification and regression uncertainty estimates, particularly under distribution shifts.

#### 3.1. Method Overview

The pipeline begins with a standard DETR-style 3D object detector. This detector processes 3D sensor data (multi-view camera or LiDAR) through a backbone to extract features. These features are then fed as tokens into a transformer decoder, where they interact with learnable object queries. The refined queries from the last decoder layer are subsequently processed by detection heads to predict class scores, mean box parameters, and associated variances (e.g., $\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z}, \hat{\ell}, \hat{w}, \hat{h}, \hat{\theta}, \hat{v}_x, \hat{v}_y, \sigma^2_x, \sigma^2_y, \sigma^2_z, \sigma^2_{\ell}, \sigma^2_w, \sigma^2_h, \kappa_{\theta}$). Simultaneously, an epistemic density estimation module models the feature density of these final object queries. At inference time, the computed query density is used to dynamically calibrate both classification and regression uncertainties via a density-aware post-hoc calibration module. The authors used PETR (camera-based) and a SECOND-based (LiDAR-based) detector as base architectures.

#### 3.2. Uncertainty Evaluation Framework

A comprehensive evaluation framework, extending the nuScenes detection benchmark, was developed to assess both classification and regression uncertainties.

- **Classification Uncertainty:**
 - **Detection Expected Calibration Error (D-ECE):** Measures the discrepancy between predicted confidence and empirical precision across confidence bins, adapting the ECE for object detection.
 - **Location-Aware Expected Calibration Error (LaECE):** Similar to D-ECE, but the accuracy term for each bin is replaced by an average localization quality, which penalizes confident yet poorly localized detections. False Positives receive a localization quality of 0.
- **Regression Uncertainty:**
 - **Miscalibration Area (MCA):** Quantifies Gaussian predictive uncertainty by measuring the area between the nominal and empirical coverage of centered prediction intervals. It is reported separately for centroid (MCA$_{xyz}$), size (MCA$_{lwh}$), and orientation (MCA$_\phi$).
 - **Uncertainty Realism Criterion (KS$_{xyz}$):** (Described in Appendix) Assesses if predicted Mahalanobis distances of ground truth from the predictive Gaussian distribution follow a $\chi^2$ distribution, using a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test statistic.

To account for class imbalance in nuScenes, all metrics are reported as class-wise averages.

#### 3.3. Probabilistic Transformer Detector

The base detector predicts 3D boxes along with associated uncertainties.
- **Regression Uncertainty Head:** A separate uncertainty head is supervised using heteroscedastic losses derived from the KL divergence between the predicted Gaussian (for position and size) or von-Mises (for yaw angle) distributions and a ground-truth Dirac distribution. This includes terms for squared error normalized by variance and a log-variance regularization term, with numerical stability improvements for the von-Mises concentration.

#### 3.4. Query-Density Estimator Training and Prediction

- **Training:** A RealNVP normalizing flow is trained on the latent query vectors of True Positive matches from the training set, separately for each semantic class. This flow learns the class-conditional feature distribution. The objective is to minimize the negative log-likelihood, and empirical priors for each class (based on frequency) are also stored. Quantiles of log-densities are cached for normalization.
- **Prediction:** At inference, for a given query vector, class-conditional log-densities are computed using the trained flows. These are then aggregated into a marginal log-density, weighted by the empirical class priors. The resulting log-density is normalized to a range using the cached quantiles, yielding a scalar density score (log $q(z)'$) that indicates how closely the query resembles the feature distribution of training objects. High values indicate in-distribution queries, while values approaching zero indicate samples drifting from the manifold.

#### 3.5. Density-aware Post-hoc Calibration

The normalized query density (log $q(z)'$) is incorporated into existing post-hoc calibrators. First, a standardized log-density deviation, $z_{dens}(z)$, is computed using the mean and standard deviation of log $q(z)'$ from the calibration set.

- **Classification:**
 - **Density-aware Temperature Scaling (DA-TS):** Modulates the single temperature $T$ per instance using $z_{dens}(z)$ via a learned scaling parameter and a bounded gain function.
 - **Density-aware Platt Scaling (DA-PS):** Applies the same principle to both the scale and shift parameters of Platt Scaling.
 - **Density-aware Isotonic Regression (DA-IR):** Combines the confidence score and $z_{dens}(z)$ into a new feature, which is then used as input for an isotonic regressor.
All parameters for these calibrators (original and density-specific) are jointly optimized by minimizing the negative log-likelihood on the calibration set.

- **Regression:**
 - **Density-aware Temperature Scaling (DA-TS):** For each predicted variance and yaw concentration, an affine correction is first applied, followed by a modulation using $z_{dens}(z)$ and a bounded gain function. Coefficients are learned by minimizing the MCA on the calibration set.

#### 3.6. Baseline Methods

- **Sample-based:** Monte-Carlo Dropout (MCD) and Deep Ensembles (DE), with detections clustered and uncertainties derived from sample statistics.
- **Train-time Calibration:** CalDETR and TCD (for classification), and Energy-based Score (ES) or KL-divergence loss for regression variances.
- **Post-hoc Calibration:** Traditional Temperature Scaling (TS), Platt Scaling (PS), Isotonic Regression (IR) for classification, and Depth-aware or standard Temperature Scaling (TS) for regression variances.

Both global (treating all classes uniformly) and class-wise (training separate calibrators per class) variants are evaluated for post-hoc methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the nuScenes dataset for in-distribution evaluation and the MultiCorrupt dataset for distribution-shifted scenarios, utilizing both camera-based (PETR) and LiDAR-based (SECOND) detectors. All post-hoc calibrators were tuned on a calibration split of the validation set.

#### 4.1. In-Distribution Evaluation (Tables 1 and 2)

- **General Performance:** Sample-based methods (MCD, DE) and train-time calibration methods (CalDETR, TCD) exhibited poor performance, often performing worse than the uncalibrated baseline in terms of both calibration error (D-ECE, LaECE, MCA) and accuracy (mAP, NDS). This indicates challenges in adapting these methods to complex 3D detection tasks with object-dense scenes and multiple classes, compared to prior work on simpler datasets.
- **Post-hoc Calibration:** Traditional post-hoc methods (TS, PS, IR) generally improved calibration over uncalibrated and sampling/train-time methods. Class-wise calibration (Cls.) consistently outperformed global calibration (Glb.) for these methods, highlighting the importance of class-specific adjustments on imbalanced datasets like nuScenes.
- **Density-aware Calibration (Ours):** The proposed density-aware calibrators (DA-TS, DA-PS, DA-IR) consistently outperformed their non-density-aware counterparts across both PETR and SECOND detectors, and for both classification and regression tasks. For instance, DA-IR (Cls.) achieved D-ECE values of 2.955% for PETR and 1.839% for SECOND, lower than IR (Cls.) at 2.999% and 1.867%, respectively. Similarly, DA-TS (Cls.) for regression yielded MCA$_{xyz}$ of 1.538% (PETR) and 1.518% (SECOND), improving upon TS (Cls.) at 1.692% and 1.533%. This suggests that even within in-distribution data, density deviations provide valuable signals for refining calibration.

#### 4.2. Distribution-Shift Evaluation (Tables 3 and 4)

- **Baseline Performance Under Shift:** Standard post-hoc calibration methods showed a significant drop in calibration quality under distribution shifts (e.g., various corruptions from MultiCorrupt), with errors often approaching those of the uncalibrated model. This confirms their vulnerability to out-of-distribution data.
- **Density-aware Robustness:** In contrast, the density-aware calibrators consistently outperformed their non-density-aware baselines for all evaluated corruptions and severity levels. For classification (Table 3), DA-IR (Cls.) achieved D-ECE of 7.211% for PETR and 6.895% for SECOND, compared to IR (Cls.) at 7.449% and 7.076%. For regression (Table 4), DA-TS (Cls.) yielded MCA$_{xyz}$ of 3.546% for PETR and 4.037% for SECOND, better than TS (Cls.) at 3.780% and 5.264%. This demonstrates the effectiveness of integrating feature density to adapt calibration dynamically, making the uncertainty estimates more robust to adverse scenarios.

#### 4.3. Semantic Shift (Tables 5 and 6)

- A transfer experiment from Boston (training/calibration) to Singapore (testing) scenes of nuScenes revealed that density-aware methods consistently improved classification calibration. For regression, where geometric features are relatively invariant, the method also maintained robust performance.

#### 4.4. Density Estimator Comparison (Table 7)

- Comparing Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) and Normalizing Flows (NF) as density estimators for the query features, NF generally exhibited slightly superior calibration performance (e.g., D-ECE of 7.211% for PETR with NF vs. 7.430% with GMM). This indicates that NFs are better at capturing complex latent feature distributions, although at the cost of increased parameters and higher inference latency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of uncertainty quantification and calibration for 3D object detection, with several key implications:

- **Enhanced Safety for Autonomous Systems:** By providing more reliable and calibrated uncertainty estimates, Query2Uncertainty can contribute to safer deployment of autonomous vehicles and robots. Downstream modules like object tracking and motion planning can leverage these improved uncertainties to make more informed and cautious decisions, particularly in ambiguous or novel situations. This directly addresses a critical requirement for regulatory approval and public acceptance of autonomous technologies.
- **Robustness in Diverse Environments:** The demonstrated effectiveness under distribution shifts is particularly significant. Autonomous systems operate in highly dynamic and unpredictable environments, encountering varied weather, lighting, and geographical conditions. The ability of Query2Uncertainty to adapt calibration dynamically to these shifts, without requiring retraining, enhances the robustness and trustworthiness of perception systems in real-world scenarios.
- **Practical Applicability:** The proposed method is a post-hoc approach, meaning it does not require costly re-training of the entire 3D detector. This makes it a lightweight and practical solution for existing deployed models, allowing for improved calibration with minimal computational overhead during inference, especially with the GMM-based density estimator.
- **Unified Uncertainty Quantification:** The joint calibration of both classification and bounding box regression uncertainties addresses a comprehensive need in 3D object detection, providing a more holistic understanding of model confidence across all critical prediction aspects.
- **Standardized Evaluation Framework:** The establishment of a comprehensive uncertainty quantification benchmark for 3D object detection, extending the widely used nuScenes dataset, is a valuable contribution to the research community. This framework provides a standardized and rigorous means to evaluate and compare future UQ methods, fostering further advancements in the field.

The findings suggest that the density of latent object queries serves as a powerful and adaptable signal for improving uncertainty calibration in 3D object detection, paving the way for more reliable and resilient autonomous perception systems.
