# Depth Anything: Unleashing the Power of Large-Scale Unlabeled Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Depth Anything is a foundation model for monocular relative depth estimation built by scaling data rather than architecture. A DINOv2-initialized DPT teacher trained on 1.5M labeled images pseudo-labels 62M unlabeled images; a student then learns them under strong color and CutMix perturbations plus a margin-tolerant feature-alignment loss to a frozen DINOv2 encoder, yielding state-of-the-art zero-shot depth and strong segmentation transfer.

## Problem

- MiDaS-style models train only on mixed labeled depth datasets, so limited data coverage causes disastrous failures on scenes outside those distributions.
- Depth labels come from sensors, stereo matching, or structure-from-motion, making datasets with tens of millions of depth labels costly, slow, or intractable to build.
- Semi-supervised methods assume only limited images are available and rarely address the realistic regime of sufficient labels alongside far larger unlabeled collections.

## Method

- Data engine: a ==DINOv2==-initialized ==DPT== teacher trained on 1.5M labeled images with an ==affine-invariant loss== auto-annotates 62M unlabeled images from eight datasets, pseudo-labels feeding a re-initialized student.
- Harder optimization target: strong ==color jitter==, ==Gaussian blur==, and ==CutMix== perturb unlabeled inputs; the unlabeled loss combines region-wise affine-invariant terms weighted by CutMix mask areas.
- Semantic preservation: a ==cosine feature-alignment loss== to a frozen DINOv2 encoder, with tolerance margin 0.85 so already-aligned pixels are dropped, replacing discrete auxiliary segmentation supervision.

## Results

- Zero-shot ViT-L beats MiDaS v3.1 on all six unseen sets: KITTI AbsRel **0.076** versus **0.127** and delta1 **0.947** versus **0.850**; DDAD AbsRel **0.230** versus **0.251**.
- Fine-tuned metric depth sets records: NYUv2 delta1 rises **0.964** to **0.984**, AbsRel falls **0.069** to **0.056** versus VPD; the encoder reaches **86.2** mIoU on Cityscapes, **59.4** on ADE20K.
- Ablation: adding pseudo-labels alone leaves KITTI AbsRel at **0.085**; strong perturbations drop it to **0.081**, feature alignment to **0.076**; removing the tolerance margin worsens mean AbsRel **0.175** to **0.188**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-training only helps when the student faces a harder target than the teacher; identical inputs and shared pretraining make pseudo-labels informationally redundant.
> - Continuous feature alignment transfers richer auxiliary supervision than discrete label spaces, but needs a tolerance margin so task-specific within-object distinctions survive.
> - Data diversity outweighs volume: the 20K-image HRWSI generalized best among labeled sets, and auxiliary semantic supervision helps noisy pseudo-labels while adding nothing on clean labels.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within computer vision, primarily focusing on monocular depth estimation (MDE) within the paradigm of "foundation models" and leveraging large-scale unlabeled data.

**Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE):** MDE, the task of inferring 3D depth information from a single 2D image, is a fundamental problem in computer vision with extensive applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and virtual reality. Historically, MDE has evolved from methods relying on handcrafted features and traditional computer vision techniques to deep learning-based approaches that learn representations from meticulously annotated datasets. Despite significant progress in accuracy, these deep learning models often struggle with generalization to unseen domains due to the diversity and scale limitations of labeled datasets.

**Foundation Models:** The paper explicitly aligns itself with the "revolution" of foundation models seen in computer vision and natural language processing (e.g., large language models and vision transformers). These models, characterized by their immense scale, pre-training on vast amounts of data, and strong zero-shot or few-shot generalization capabilities, represent a paradigm shift. The paper identifies a gap: MDE has largely lacked a robust foundation model due to the prohibitive cost and difficulty of acquiring labeled depth data at the scale required for such models.

**Zero-Shot Depth Estimation:** A direct lineage of this work is in zero-shot MDE, where the goal is to train a model that can predict depth for any given image without specific fine-tuning for that domain. MiDaS is acknowledged as a pioneering effort in this direction, demonstrating "certain level of zero-shot ability" by training on a mixed collection of labeled datasets. However, MiDaS's performance is noted to "suffer disastrous performance in some scenarios" due to insufficient data coverage. "Depth Anything" aims to overcome this limitation by significantly scaling up the training data and improving generalization.

**Leveraging Unlabeled Data (Semi-Supervised Learning at Scale):** This is the core novelty of the paper's approach. While semi-supervised learning has been a long-standing area of research, typically focusing on scenarios with limited labeled data, Depth Anything tackles a more challenging yet realistic scenario: "when there are already sufficient labeled images but also larger-scale unlabeled images." The paper demonstrates that simply adding pseudo-labeled data in a naive self-training loop does not guarantee improvement when a strong baseline from labeled data already exists. This highlights a critical challenge in scaling semi-supervised learning that the authors address through novel strategies. The paper's contribution is showing *how* to effectively leverage massive, cheap, and diverse unlabeled monocular images, which are far easier to acquire and pseudo-label than traditional depth data (stereo, SfM, LiDAR).

**Semantic-Assisted Perception:** Prior research has explored using auxiliary semantic segmentation tasks to improve MDE performance, aiming to equip models with better high-level scene understanding. However, the authors observe that these approaches often fall short when the MDE model is already powerful, possibly due to information loss when decoding into discrete class spaces. "Depth Anything" proposes an alternative: inheriting rich semantic priors directly from powerful pre-trained encoders (like DINOv2) using a feature alignment loss. This novel approach allows for the transfer of high-dimensional, continuous semantic information, leading to a multi-task encoder capable of both mid-level (depth) and high-level (semantic segmentation) perception tasks.

In summary, "Depth Anything" builds upon the zero-shot MDE capabilities pioneered by MiDaS, but critically differentiates itself by focusing on the principled and effective utilization of truly *massive* unlabeled data. It contributes to the broader foundation model movement by demonstrating how to build a robust general-purpose MDE model, and it pushes the boundaries of semi-supervised learning by providing concrete strategies for data scaling in a challenging data regime.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **highly practical and robust foundation model for monocular depth estimation (MDE)**. The authors explicitly state their ambition: to create a model capable of generating "high-quality depth information for any images under any circumstances."

The motivation stems from several critical observations and challenges in the field of MDE:

- **Absence of a True MDE Foundation Model:** Despite the emergence of "foundation models" in other computer vision and NLP domains (e.g., for language understanding or image segmentation like Segment Anything), MDE has lagged. This is primarily because building massive, diverse datasets with tens of millions of *labeled* depth maps is prohibitively costly, time-consuming, and often intractable with traditional sensor-based methods (LiDAR, stereo cameras) or Structure-from-Motion (SfM).
- **Limitations of Existing Zero-Shot MDE Models:** While models like MiDaS made pioneering strides in zero-shot MDE by aggregating existing labeled datasets, they are "limited by its data coverage, thus suffering disastrous performance in some scenarios." This highlights the need for a model with superior generalization ability across a wider range of real-world conditions.
- **Underutilization of Unlabeled Data:** The authors identify a vast, untapped resource: readily available monocular unlabeled images. These images are cheap and easy to acquire (ubiquitous, no specialized devices), highly diverse (covering a broader range of scenes crucial for generalization), and "easy to annotate" using existing pre-trained MDE models to generate pseudo-labels. The core motivation is to effectively harness this massive, yet largely ignored, data source to overcome the data bottleneck.
- **Challenges in Naive Self-Training:** A significant motivation for the specific methodological innovations is the empirical observation that a straightforward self-training approach (combining labeled and pseudo-labeled data) fails to improve performance when "already sufficient labeled images and strong pre-training models" are available. This suggests that simply adding more data, even pseudo-labeled, is not enough; the learning process needs to be challenged.
- **Improving Semantic Understanding for Depth:** While semantic information is known to be beneficial for depth estimation, previous attempts using discrete semantic segmentation masks as auxiliary supervision often did not yield substantial gains for already powerful MDE models. This motivated the search for a more informative and continuous semantic signal to enhance the model's scene understanding, leveraging the rich priors available in advanced vision models like DINOv2.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond the limitations of purely supervised MDE by systematically exploiting the abundance of unlabeled visual data. Their objective is not just to collect more data, but to design a robust learning framework that can effectively "unleash the power" of this data to achieve unprecedented generalization and accuracy in monocular depth estimation, thereby establishing a true foundation model for the task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Depth Anything employs a sophisticated semi-supervised learning pipeline that leverages both manually labeled and vast quantities of automatically pseudo-labeled data. The core methodology revolves around a two-stage training process combined with two novel strategies to effectively utilize unlabeled images. The architecture is based on a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder and a DPT decoder, similar to MiDaS.

#### 3.1. Two-Stage Training Pipeline

1. **Teacher Model Training (Stage 1: Learning Labeled Images):**
 - An initial "teacher model" (T) is trained on a collection of 1.5 million manually labeled images sourced from six diverse public datasets (BlendedMVS, DIML, HRWSI, IRS, MegaDepth, TartanAir). Notably, common MDE evaluation datasets like NYUv2 and KITTI are *excluded* from this training to ensure a true zero-shot evaluation for the final model.
 - The training uses an **affine-invariant loss** (similar to MiDaS) to normalize depth predictions to a consistent scale and shift for multi-dataset joint training. This is crucial as different datasets have varying depth scales.
 - The encoder is initialized with **DINOv2** pre-trained weights, providing a strong visual representation backbone.
 - An additional refinement involves detecting sky regions using a pre-trained semantic segmentation model and setting their disparity values to zero (farthest), which helps in better handling open-world scenes.

2. **Student Model Training (Stage 2: Unleashing the Power of Unlabeled Images):**
 - The trained teacher model (T) is then used to generate **pseudo depth labels** for a massive collection of 62 million *unlabeled* images from eight large-scale public datasets (e.g., SA-1B, Open Images, ImageNet-21K). This process is highly efficient, requiring only a forward pass.
 - A "student model" (S), which shares the same architecture as the teacher but is re-initialized (not fine-tuned from the teacher) for better performance, is then trained on a combined dataset of the original 1.5M labeled images and the 62M pseudo-labeled unlabeled images. The ratio of labeled to unlabeled images in each batch is 1:2.

#### 3.2. Key Strategies for Effective Unlabeled Data Utilization

The authors identify that simply adding pseudo-labeled data is insufficient when the labeled dataset is already substantial. To address this, they propose two simple yet effective strategies:

1. **Challenging Optimization Target for the Student Model:**
 - During the student training phase, **strong perturbations** are applied to the unlabeled images. This forces the student model to learn more robust and invariant representations by actively seeking extra visual knowledge.
 - Two types of perturbations are used:
 - **Strong color distortions:** Including color jittering and Gaussian blurring.
 - **Strong spatial distortion:** Specifically, **CutMix**. CutMix spatially interpolates two unlabeled images (u_a, u_b) and their corresponding pseudo-labels (T(u_a), T(u_b)) using a binary mask (M). The loss is computed across the mixed image (u_ab) against the appropriate pseudo-labels for each masked region. This technique helps the model handle occlusions and learn from mixed contexts. Crucially, the teacher model receives *clean* unlabeled images for pseudo-labeling, while the student trains on *distorted* ones.

2. **Semantic-Assisted Perception (Inheriting Rich Semantic Priors):**
 - Recognizing the limitations of auxiliary semantic segmentation tasks (which decode images into discrete, potentially information-losing class spaces), the authors propose to transfer richer, high-dimensional semantic information.
 - They introduce an **auxiliary feature alignment loss (L_feat)**. This loss encourages the features extracted by the student model's encoder (f) to be similar to the features extracted by a **frozen DINOv2 encoder (f')**. DINOv2 is chosen for its "excellent performance in semantic-related tasks."
 - A crucial detail is the use of a **tolerance margin (α)** in the feature alignment loss. If the cosine similarity between f and f' already surpasses α (set to 0.85), that pixel is excluded from the loss calculation. This prevents the model from exhaustively aligning all features, allowing it to maintain part-level discriminative representations essential for depth, while still benefiting from DINOv2's semantic awareness.
 - This semantic constraint is primarily applied to *unlabeled data*, as it helps combat potential noise in pseudo-labels and equips the model with semantic capability without interfering with the higher-quality labeled data.

#### 3.3. Overall Loss Function

The total loss for the student model is an average combination of three components:
- `L_l`: Affine-invariant loss for labeled images.
- `L_u`: Affine-invariant loss for pseudo-labeled unlabeled images (with perturbations).
- `L_feat`: Feature alignment loss for unlabeled images (with a tolerance margin).

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Encoder/Decoder:** DINOv2 encoder and DPT decoder.
- **Input Resolution:** Images are resized to 518 on the shorter side and cropped to 518x518 during training. Inference is performed at original resolution, ensuring sides are multiples of 14 (DINOv2 patch size).
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with a linear learning rate schedule. Encoder learning rate is 5e-6, decoder is 10x larger.
- **Data Augmentation:** Horizontal flipping for labeled images.

This comprehensive methodology allows Depth Anything to effectively utilize unprecedented amounts of data, leading to a highly generalized and robust depth estimation model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The results presented in "Depth Anything" unequivocally demonstrate its superior performance across various benchmarks and tasks, solidifying its claim as a highly practical foundation model for MDE.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Relative Depth Estimation (Table 2)

- **Superior Generalization:** Depth Anything significantly outperforms MiDaS v3.1, the previous state-of-the-art in zero-shot MDE, across all six evaluated unseen datasets (KITTI, NYUv2, Sintel, DDAD, ETH3D, DIODE). For instance, on KITTI, Depth Anything (ViT-L) achieves an AbsRel of 0.076 and a δ1 of 0.947, compared to MiDaS v3.1's 0.127 and 0.850. Similar improvements are observed across all datasets.
- **Efficiency at Scale:** Even smaller Depth Anything models (ViT-B and ViT-S) consistently surpass the larger MiDaS-ViT-L model on several datasets. The ViT-S model, less than 1/10th the size of MiDaS's ViT-L, still outperforms it on Sintel, DDAD, and ETH3D, highlighting the efficiency and strong generalization of the proposed training approach.
- **True Zero-Shot Advantage:** Critically, Depth Anything maintains its superiority even on datasets like KITTI and NYUv2, where MiDaS v3.1 *used* training images, whereas Depth Anything did not. This reinforces its true zero-shot capability.

#### 4.2. Fine-tuned to Metric Depth Estimation (Tables 3, 4, 5)

- **In-Domain SOTA:** When fine-tuned with metric depth information on specific datasets, Depth Anything sets new state-of-the-art results. On NYUv2 (Table 3), it achieves a δ1 of 0.984 and AbsRel of 0.056, remarkably outperforming previous best methods like VPD (0.964 δ1, 0.069 AbsRel). Similar advancements are seen on KITTI (Table 4), with a δ1 of 0.982 and AbsRel of 0.046, surpassing methods like NDDepth and GEDepth.
- **Zero-Shot Metric Generalization:** Depth Anything also demonstrates superior zero-shot metric depth estimation compared to ZoeDepth (Table 5). When trained on NYUv2 (for indoor scenes) or KITTI (for outdoor scenes) and evaluated on various unseen indoor (SUN RGB-D, iBims-1, HyperSim) and outdoor (Virtual KITTI 2, DIODE Outdoor) datasets, Depth Anything consistently yields better AbsRel and δ1 scores. This indicates its pre-trained encoder serves as a more robust initialization for metric depth tasks.

#### 4.3. Fine-tuned to Semantic Segmentation (Tables 7, 8)

- **Multi-Task Encoder Capability:** A significant finding is the strong semantic capability of the Depth Anything encoder, a byproduct of its MDE training with semantic feature alignment. When fine-tuned for semantic segmentation on Cityscapes (Table 7) and ADE20K (Table 8), its ViT-L encoder achieves superior mIoU scores (86.2 on Cityscapes, 59.4 on ADE20K) compared to other prominent encoders pre-trained on large-scale ImageNet-21K (e.g., Swin-L, ConvNeXt-XL, BEiT-L). This suggests the MDE training framework, especially the semantic constraint, cultivates a powerful and versatile visual encoder.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Tables 9, 12, 13)

- **Effectiveness of Strategies:**
 - **Strong Perturbations (S):** Table 9 clearly shows that simply adding unlabeled images does not improve performance ("✓ ✓" row vs. "✓" row). However, applying strong perturbations ("✓ ✓ ✓" row) significantly boosts generalization across all datasets, confirming the necessity of challenging the student model.
 - **Semantic Constraint (L_feat):** Further adding the semantic feature alignment loss ("✓ ✓ ✓ ✓" row) provides additional performance gains, amplifying the benefits of unlabeled data.
 - **Tolerance Margin (α):** Table 12 demonstrates that using a tolerance margin (α=0.85 or 0.70) in L_feat is crucial for performance, outperforming a strict alignment (α=1.00).
 - **L_feat Application:** Applying L_feat only to unlabeled data is more effective than applying it to both labeled and unlabeled data (Table 13), suggesting it helps combat noise in pseudo-labels without interfering with high-quality manual labels.

#### 4.5. Encoder Comparisons (Tables 10, 11)

- **Superiority over MiDaS Encoder:** The Depth Anything encoder consistently outperforms the MiDaS v3.1 encoder when fine-tuned for both downstream depth estimation and semantic segmentation tasks (Table 10).
- **Enhancement of DINOv2:** Even starting from the robust DINOv2 initialization, Depth Anything's large-scale MDE training further enhances the encoder's performance for downstream tasks, surpassing the original DINOv2 encoder (Table 11).

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results (Figures 1, 3, 4, 5-10)

- Visualizations showcase Depth Anything's impressive robustness and accuracy in diverse and challenging scenes, including low-light, complex, foggy, and ultra-remote environments. It provides visually superior depth maps compared to MiDaS.
- The improved depth maps also lead to better quality image synthesis when used with depth-conditioned ControlNet, demonstrating direct utility in generative AI applications.

Overall, the findings strongly support the authors' claim of creating a robust, generalizable, and practical foundation model for MDE, validating their proposed strategies for leveraging large-scale unlabeled data and semantic priors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Depth Anything" holds significant implications for the field of computer vision and its applications, promising a substantial impact across research and industry.

#### 5.1. Establishing a Foundation Model for Monocular Depth Estimation

The most direct impact is the creation of a powerful and highly generalized foundation model for MDE. By demonstrating exceptional zero-shot capabilities and state-of-the-art performance upon fine-tuning, "Depth Anything" fills a critical gap in the computer vision landscape. This model can serve as a robust pre-trained backbone for various MDE-related tasks, significantly reducing the need for extensive task-specific data collection and training. It sets a new benchmark and paves the way for future research to build upon a truly generalizable depth perception system.

#### 5.2. Revolutionizing Data Utilization for Dense Prediction Tasks

The paper's core contribution lies in its innovative and effective strategies for leveraging massive, cheap, and diverse unlabeled monocular images. Unlike traditional semi-supervised learning that focuses on scarcity of labeled data, "Depth Anything" shows *how* to extract value from unlabeled data even when ample labeled data already exists. The methods of "challenging optimization target" (strong perturbations like CutMix) and "semantic-assisted perception" (feature alignment with frozen powerful encoders) provide a blueprint for future large-scale semi-supervised learning for dense prediction tasks. This approach bypasses the prohibitive costs of manual depth annotation, making it scalable to unprecedented levels.

#### 5.3. Development of a Versatile Multi-Task Encoder

A remarkable finding is that the encoder trained with "Depth Anything"'s MDE framework demonstrates superior performance not only for depth estimation but also for high-level semantic segmentation tasks. This indicates that the proposed training methodology, particularly the integration of rich semantic priors from DINOv2, yields a "universal multi-task encoder for both middle-level and high-level perception tasks." Such a versatile encoder could become a foundational component for various vision systems, unifying feature extraction for diverse downstream applications and promoting more holistic scene understanding.

#### 5.4. Enhanced Real-World Applications

The "highly practical solution" translates directly into improved performance for numerous real-world applications that rely on accurate and robust depth information:
- **Robotics and Autonomous Driving:** More reliable depth maps in diverse and challenging conditions (low-light, complex scenes, fog, remote distances) are crucial for safe navigation, obstacle avoidance, and 3D scene reconstruction.
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** High-quality depth is essential for realistic 3D content creation, scene understanding, and human-computer interaction in immersive environments.
- **Image and Video Editing/Generation:** As demonstrated with ControlNet, more accurate depth maps provide superior control signals for generative AI models, leading to higher fidelity image synthesis and advanced video editing capabilities. This opens new creative avenues for content creators.

#### 5.5. Paving the Way for Future Research

The limitations identified by the authors themselves (model size and training resolution) suggest clear avenues for future research. Scaling up to larger models (e.g., ViT-Giant) and higher resolutions promises even greater accuracy and robustness. The success of using pseudo-labels from a strong teacher to train a student model also points towards potential self-distillation or knowledge distillation techniques on an even grander scale. This work acts as a catalyst, encouraging the community to further explore and refine large-scale, data-centric approaches to fundamental computer vision problems.

In conclusion, "Depth Anything" is a pivotal contribution that not only pushes the boundaries of monocular depth estimation but also redefines how massive unlabeled datasets can be effectively leveraged in the era of foundation models, with far-reaching implications for both academic research and practical applications.
