# GeoDrive: 3D Geometry-Informed Driving World Model with Precise Action Control

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoDrive is a driving world model that reconstructs a 3D point cloud from a single reference frame via MonST3R, renders it along a user-specified trajectory with a dynamic vehicle-editing module, then refines the rendering with a dual-branch video diffusion transformer. It cuts trajectory-following error 42% versus Vista while using 99.7% less training data.

## Problem

- Existing driving world models optimize in 2D pixel space, causing structural incoherence across novel views and physically implausible object interactions in dense traffic.
- Methods conditioning on dense annotations like HD-map sequences and 3D bounding-box tracks only reproduce prescribed motions without modeling underlying vehicle dynamics.
- Approaches that inject raw numerical camera/action vectors into the generative backbone produce weak, ungrounded control signals, causing unstable control and requiring large training datasets.

## Method

- ==MonST3R-based 3D reconstruction== extracts a metric-scale point cloud and camera pose from a single reference frame using a ==static/dynamic decoupler== to filter moving-object matches.
- ==Dynamic editing module== renders projective views along user trajectories while repositioning vehicle bounding boxes across frames to simulate motion, not just a static scene.
- ==Dual-branch conditioning== fuses rendered-video latents into a frozen ==CogVideo-5B-I2V== ==DiT== backbone through a lightweight ==zero-initialized condition encoder== covering only 6% of parameters.

## Results

- On nuScenes, GeoDrive reduces ADE from **2.77** to **1.62** and FDE from **5.28** to **3.1** versus Vista, while using only **5h** of training data versus Vista's **1740h**.
- GeoDrive achieves best FID (**4.1**) and FVD (**61.6**) on the nuScenes full validation set, beating GEM, Vista, GenAD, and Drive-WM across the board.
- Ablations show removing dynamic editing raises ADE to **3.68**, and removing the dual-branch design raises ADE to **3.45**, versus **1.62** for the full model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rendering action commands as explicit visual conditions aligned with the generative latent space gives far stronger, more stable control than injecting raw numerical vectors into the backbone.
> - Grounding a world model in explicit 3D geometry lets it generalize zero-shot to out-of-distribution trajectories, like reverse motion, that implicit action-conditioned models fail on.
> - Freezing a pretrained backbone and training only a small conditioning branch can outperform larger data-hungry baselines, showing geometric structure substitutes for scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "world models" has emerged as a critical paradigm in artificial intelligence, enabling agents to predict future states of dynamic environments and evaluate potential actions. In autonomous driving, world models are invaluable for tasks such as anticipating road user behavior, performing risk-aware planning, accelerating training in simulation, and adapting to novel scenarios, all of which contribute to enhanced safety and reliability.

Existing driving world models can be broadly categorized by their representational choices (point clouds, occupancy grids, images) and their approach to control.
- **Geometric-based models (point clouds, occupancy grids):** These methods, such as those leveraging LiDAR data, offer detailed geometric information and are strong in spatial modeling (e.g.,). However, they can be computationally intensive and may struggle with the rich visual details necessary for human-like perception.
- **Image-based generative models:** These models, often based on powerful generative diffusion models, are gaining prominence due to their flexibility with sensor data and scalability. They excel at synthesizing visually rich, trajectory-faithful sequences, crucial for perception and planning. However, many current image-based approaches suffer from several key limitations that GeoDrive aims to overcome:
 - **Lack of 3D Geometric Awareness:** Most existing methods optimize in 2D space, leading to structural incoherence across novel views and physically implausible object interactions, which is highly detrimental for safety-critical applications like collision avoidance. GeoDrive explicitly addresses this by integrating robust 3D geometry.
 - **Reliance on Dense Annotations for Control:** Models like DriveDreamer and DrivingDiffusion require extensive, dense annotations (e.g., HD-map sequences, 3D bounding box tracks) to achieve scene control. This limits their flexibility, as they primarily reproduce prescribed motions rather than inferring dynamic priors from sparse input and conditioning on desired ego-trajectories.
 - **Weak Action Control:** Recent action-conditioned generative models such as Vista, Terra, and GAIA 1&2 attempt to infer dynamic priors from single images and condition on desired ego-trajectories by injecting raw numerical control vectors into their generative backbones. However, this numerical conditioning is often weakly grounded in the visual latent space, resulting in unstable control, reduced consistency, and a demand for larger training datasets. GeoDrive tackles this by rendering action commands as *visual conditioning inputs*, providing a substantially stronger and more stable control signal.

GeoDrive positions itself at the intersection of these challenges by proposing a hybrid neural-geometric framework. It leverages advancements in 3D reconstruction (MonST3R) to establish a robust 3D structural prior and combines it with the generative power of latent video diffusion models for photorealistic synthesis. By explicitly enforcing 3D geometric consistency and introducing a novel "dynamic editing" module for agents, GeoDrive aims to deliver a driving world model that offers superior spatio-temporal coherence, precise 3D controllability over ego-vehicle trajectories, and kinematically constrained motion patterns for non-ego agents, pushing the boundaries of reliable and adaptable scene modeling for autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the GeoDrive research stem from addressing critical shortcomings in existing driving world models, particularly concerning 3D geometric consistency and precise action controllability.

The authors identify the following key problems that current approaches exhibit:
1. **Deficiencies in 3D Geometric Consistency:** Many existing world models, especially those relying heavily on 2D space optimization, struggle to maintain robust 3D geometric consistency. This leads to issues such as structural incoherence when generating novel views and the accumulation of artifacts, particularly during occlusion handling. For safety-critical autonomous driving tasks, accurate spatial understanding is paramount, and these inconsistencies can compromise reliable safety assessment.
2. **Lack of Precise Action Controllability:** While generative models have advanced, achieving precise control over the ego-vehicle's trajectory and the dynamics of other agents remains a challenge. Existing methods often rely on dense annotations for control, which limits them to reproducing predefined motions. More flexible approaches that condition on single images and desired ego-trajectories (e.g., Vista, Terra) typically inject raw numerical control vectors. This results in weak action signals that are not well-grounded in the visual latent space, leading to unstable control and demanding extensive training data.
3. **Inability to Model Dynamic Objects Plausibly:** World models often treat scenes as static or struggle to realistically simulate the independent motion of dynamic agents (e.g., other vehicles). This limits their utility in complex, real-world driving scenarios where interactions between multiple moving entities are crucial.

To overcome these limitations, GeoDrive sets out with three core criteria for a reliable driving world model:
1. **Rigid Spatio-Temporal Coherence:** Ensuring consistency across static infrastructure and dynamically moving agents.
2. **3D Controllability over Ego-Vehicle Trajectories:** Allowing precise and stable control of the ego-car's path in 3D space.
3. **Kinematically Constrained Motion Patterns for Non-Ego Agents:** Enabling plausible and controllable movements for other vehicles in the scene.

The overarching motivation is to develop a driving world model that significantly enhances spatial understanding and action controllability, thereby leading to more realistic, adaptable, and reliable scene modeling for safer and more effective autonomous driving systems. This includes enabling applications such as accurate trajectory following, novel view synthesis, and interactive scene editing (object insertion, replacement, and motion control).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoDrive introduces a hybrid neural-geometric framework that explicitly enforces 3D geometry consistency and provides robust action control. The methodology comprises three main stages: extracting 3D representations, rendering 3D videos with dynamic editing, and employing a dual-branch control mechanism for spatio-temporal consistency.

#### 3.1. Extracting 3D Representations from a Reference Image

The first step involves constructing a 3D representation from a single input reference image ($I_0$). GeoDrive utilizes **MonST3R**, an off-the-shelf dense stereo model. MonST3R is chosen for its ability to simultaneously predict 3D geometry (per-pixel 3D coordinates) and camera poses from video sequences. For a single input image, it is duplicated to meet MonST3R’s cross-view matching requirements.
Crucially, MonST3R disentangles static scene geometry from moving objects using a transformer-based decoupler. This allows for a more robust camera pose estimation by primarily considering static features, reducing pose errors in dynamic urban scenes. The output is a colored point cloud ($P_0$), which serves as the foundational geometric scaffold for subsequent steps.

#### 3.2. Rendering 3D Videos with Dynamic Editing

To guide the video generation process and achieve precise trajectory following, GeoDrive renders a video sequence based on the extracted 3D representation and user-specified camera trajectories.
- **Initial Rendering:** The point cloud $P_0$ is projected through each user-provided camera configuration ($C_t = (R_t, T_t, f_t)$) using standard projective geometry and z-buffering for occlusion handling. This produces an initial rendered view ($\tilde{I}_t$) for each camera position.
- **Limitations of Static Rendering:** A key challenge is that since $P_0$ is derived from a single first frame, the initial rendered scene is static. This contradicts the dynamic nature of autonomous driving environments where vehicles are constantly in motion.
- **Dynamic Editing Module:** To overcome this, GeoDrive proposes a novel **dynamic editing module**. When 2D bounding box information for moving vehicles is provided (during training, optional during inference), the module dynamically adjusts the positions of these vehicles within the rendering. This creates the illusion of motion, making the rendered video more aligned with real-world dynamics. This dynamic rendering serves as a powerful visual guide, not only for the ego-vehicle's trajectory but also for controlling the movement of other vehicles, a capability lacking in previous methods like Vista and GAIA.

#### 3.3. Dual-Branch Control for Spatio-Temporal Consistency

The point cloud-based renderings, while geometrically accurate, often suffer from visual quality issues such as occlusions, missing areas, and reduced fidelity. To refine these projections into photorealistic videos while preserving 3D structural fidelity, GeoDrive adapts a latent video diffusion model.
- **Latent Video Diffusion Backbone:** The core of this stage is a pre-trained latent video diffusion model (specifically, CogVideo-5B-I2V), which operates on compressed latent representations of videos. This backbone is **frozen** during GeoDrive's training to leverage its existing photorealism capabilities.
- **Condition Encoder:** A lightweight condition encoder is introduced. This encoder processes two inputs: the noisy latent representation ($z_t$) at a given timestep $t$ and the latent representation of the dynamic renderings ($z_R$) obtained from the VAE encoder. The dynamic renderings provide essential temporal and contextual nuances.
- **Feature Integration:** The features extracted by the condition encoder are selectively fused into the frozen Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone. This integration is formulated such that relevant contextual information from the renderings guides the denoising process without requiring extensive retraining of the large backbone model. A learnable linear transformation, initialized to zero, ensures stable training.
- **Refinement and Decoding:** By optimizing only the parameters of this lightweight condition encoder (approximately 6% of total parameters), the model efficiently learns to refine the geometrically consistent rendered views into high-quality, photorealistic video sequences that faithfully follow the specified ego-trajectory. The final video sequence ($\hat{I}_t$) is then reconstructed using a frozen VAE decoder.

This dual-branch control mechanism, with a strong visual conditioning signal derived from 3D geometry and dynamic editing, allows GeoDrive to achieve precise camera control and maintain spatio-temporal coherence, generalizing well to various trajectories while ensuring photorealistic output.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GeoDrive demonstrates significant advancements over existing driving world models across several key metrics and applications, particularly excelling in action accuracy, 3D spatial awareness, and generation quality.

#### 4.1. Trajectory Following

- **Quantitative Results:** On the nuScenes validation subset, GeoDrive significantly outperforms baselines like Vista and Terra.
 - **Prediction Quality:** GeoDrive achieves superior scores across all visual quality metrics: lower LPIPS (0.303 vs 0.351 for Vista), higher PSNR (21.979 vs 20.086), higher SSIM (0.6535 vs 0.621), lower FID (7.17 vs 8.35), and lower FVD (85.22 vs 163.7).
 - **Action Fidelity:** GeoDrive drastically improves trajectory following accuracy, with significantly lower Average Displacement Error (ADE) (1.62 vs 2.77 for Vista) and Final Displacement Error (FDE) (3.1 vs 5.28 for Vista).
 - **Data Efficiency:** A notable finding is that GeoDrive achieves this superior performance using significantly less training data (5 hours) compared to Vista and Terra (1740 hours), demonstrating remarkable data efficiency (99.7% less data). In a broader comparison across various driving world models (Table 2), GeoDrive also achieves the lowest FID (4.1) and FVD (61.6) scores with only 5 hours of training data.
- **Qualitative Results:** GeoDrive's generated agents adhere more precisely to specified paths, unlike baselines that often deviate (Figure 4). The generated videos are noticeably sharper, contain fewer artifacts, and exhibit a stronger understanding of scene dynamics (Figure 5), such as anticipating that a car should wait for an obstacle rather than causing a collision. GeoDrive also successfully generalizes to unseen trajectories, such as reverse trajectories (Figure 10), where Vista fails to perform.

#### 4.2. Novel View Synthesis

- **Quantitative Results:** Evaluated on the Waymo validation set for novel view synthesis (horizontally shifted trajectories), GeoDrive demonstrates competitive performance compared to the scene reconstruction method StreetGaussians. While StreetGaussians achieves slightly lower FID on some shifts, GeoDrive consistently achieves lower FVD scores, indicating better temporal consistency and video quality for unseen camera paths (e.g., FVD of 1245.23 vs 1438.89 for StreetGS on Left@3m shift).
- **Qualitative Results:** GeoDrive maintains trajectory adherence and preserves photorealistic rendering for deviated trajectories in a zero-shot manner (Figure 6). In contrast, StreetGaussians, a reconstruction-based method, exhibits severe geometric distortions and significant artifacts under viewpoint shifts, highlighting its limitations in resolving occlusion boundaries and handling sparse observational data.

#### 4.3. Applications

GeoDrive's robust capabilities enable several practical applications:
- **Object Trajectory Control:** By providing sequences of 2D bounding boxes, users can precisely control the movement directions and trajectories of non-ego vehicles within the scene (Figure 7).
- **Object Editing:** The model supports intuitive scene editing, allowing users to remove or replace objects in the scene using off-the-shelf image editing models, facilitating seamless future scenario predictions (Figure 8).
- **Assistance for VLA Planning:** GeoDrive can serve as an interactive simulation environment to assist Visual Language Action (VLA) models in decision-making. By simulating planned trajectories, GeoDrive helps VLA systems foresee hazards and assess action safety, enabling iterative refinement of decisions for safer and more effective trajectory planning (Figure 9).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirm the importance of key architectural components (Table 4):
- **Dynamic Editing (D.E.):** Removing the dynamic editing module leads to worse FID (7.01 vs 7.17), FVD (88.68 vs 85.22), and significantly higher ADE (3.68 vs 1.62), underscoring its critical role in improving action fidelity and reducing the disparity between static renders and dynamic real-world scenarios.
- **Dual-branch Architecture:** Training a single-branch architecture (adjusting input channels to adapt renders and fine-tuning the backbone) results in inferior performance (FID 8.83, FVD 74.76, ADE 3.45) compared to GeoDrive's dual-branch approach, indicating the effectiveness of leveraging a frozen pre-trained backbone with a lightweight condition encoder for efficiency and performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GeoDrive represents a significant step forward in the development of driving world models, offering substantial significance and potential impact across several dimensions of autonomous driving research and development:

1. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability for Autonomous Systems:** By explicitly integrating robust 3D geometry and achieving precise action control, GeoDrive directly addresses critical deficiencies in existing models that lead to structural incoherence and physically implausible interactions. This improved spatial understanding and control are paramount for safety-critical tasks like collision avoidance and risk-aware planning, leading to more reliable and trustworthy autonomous driving systems.

2. **More Realistic and Adaptable Scene Modeling:** The dynamic editing module and the ability to generalize to novel trajectories and views enable the creation of highly realistic and diverse driving scenarios. This is crucial for training and testing autonomous vehicles in a wide range of conditions, including rare or hazardous events that are difficult to capture in real-world data.

3. **Efficiency and Scalability:** GeoDrive's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance with significantly less training data (5 hours compared to 1740 hours for baselines) is a major practical advantage. This dramatically reduces the computational resources and data collection efforts required, making the development of advanced world models more accessible and scalable. The use of a lightweight condition encoder with a frozen pre-trained backbone also contributes to this efficiency.

4. **Flexible and Interactive Scenario Generation:** The demonstrated applications of object trajectory control and scene editing empower developers and researchers with unprecedented flexibility. They can create customized training data, analyze the impact of specific objects or configurations, and design targeted test cases for specific driving challenges. This interactive capability facilitates faster iteration and validation cycles in the development pipeline.

5. **Assistance for High-Level Planning (VLA Models):** GeoDrive's integration as an interactive simulation environment for Visual Language Action (VLA) models showcases its potential to enhance high-level decision-making. By allowing VLA systems to simulate and verify planned trajectories, GeoDrive provides a crucial feedback loop that can refine driving decisions, ensuring they are contextually appropriate and prioritize safety. This represents a tangible step towards more intelligent and human-like autonomous reasoning.

6. **Setting a New Benchmark:** The superior quantitative and qualitative results across multiple metrics and tasks establish GeoDrive as a new benchmark in controllable driving world models. Its innovative approach of turning numerical action commands into visually grounded conditioning signals sets a new standard for robust action fidelity and spatial consistency.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge that GeoDrive's performance is dependent on the accuracy of depth and pose estimation from the underlying MonST3R model. Furthermore, relying solely on image and trajectory input for world prediction can be challenging. Future work aims to incorporate richer contextual information through text conditions and deeper integration with VLA understanding, which could further enhance realism, consistency, and the overall intelligence of the world model. Despite these limitations, GeoDrive presents a compelling framework that significantly advances the capabilities of world models for autonomous driving.
