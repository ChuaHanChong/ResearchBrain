# Efficient Action Robust Reinforcement Learning with Probabilistic Policy Execution Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> ARRLC is a model-based episodic RL algorithm for action-robust MDPs where the agent's chosen action is replaced by an adversarial one with probability rho. The paper proves perfect duality and an action-robust Bellman optimality equation with a deterministic optimal policy, then uses paired optimistic/pessimistic value estimates as policy certificates to attain minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity, beating non-robust and robust-TD baselines under action perturbation.

## Problem

- Standard RL policies are not robust to actuator noise, power limits, or failures, where the executed action differs from the action the learned policy specifies.
- Prior probabilistic robust policy iteration needs an MDP solver for each side of the game, so it cannot handle unknown reward functions or unknown transition probabilities.
- Robust temporal-difference extensions converge to the robust Q-function but supply no regret or sample-complexity guarantee, and Markov-game solvers are suboptimal by a factor of A or H squared.

## Method

- Formalizes a ==probabilistic policy execution uncertainty set== mixing the agent policy with any adversarial policy at weight rho, and proves perfect duality plus an ==action-robust Bellman optimality equation== solvable by backward induction.
- **ARRLC** alternates trajectory sampling under simulated adversarial actions with adversarial planning on the empirical model, jointly computing agent and adversary policies via ==Bernstein-style bonuses== and ==optimistic-pessimistic value iteration==.
- Optimistic and pessimistic value functions serve as ==policy certificates== bounding the returned policy's robust return and suboptimality; a model-free variant, **AR-UCBH**, uses Q-learning with UCB-Hoeffding updates.

## Results

- ARRLC attains regret order **square-root of SAH-cubed-K plus S-squared-A-H-cubed** and sample complexity **SAH-cubed over epsilon-squared**, matching known lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.
- The model-free AR-UCBH variant achieves regret order **square-root of SAH-to-the-fifth-K plus SAH-squared**, trading an H-squared factor for avoiding model storage and planning.
- On Cliff Walking and MuJoCo Inverted Pendulum with training rho of **0.2** and test perturbation probabilities **0.1** and **0.2**, ARRLC outscores non-robust ORLC and converges faster than robust TD across fixed and random adversaries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting the adversary into the sampling policy at the assumed perturbation rate lets a single clean-environment training run yield policies that hold up under deployment-time execution noise.
> - Maintaining both optimistic and pessimistic value bounds gives free certificates on worst-case return, turning robustness from an untestable claim into a per-episode measurable quantity.
> - Robustness need not cost statistical efficiency: with rho known, action-robust learning matches the non-robust minimax rates, so the price is adversary planning cost, not sample count.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in various applications such as recommendation systems, autonomous driving, finance, and business management. However, a significant challenge in deploying standard RL solutions in real-world scenarios is their inherent lack of robustness to uncertainties, perturbations, or structural changes within the environment. These discrepancies between idealized training conditions and practical deployment environments frequently lead to performance degradation.

Robust Reinforcement Learning (Robust RL) is an area of research dedicated to developing policies that optimize worst-case performance under various forms of uncertainty. These uncertainties can manifest in different components of a Markov Decision Process (MDP), including observations/states, actions, transitions, and rewards. This paper specifically focuses on "action robust RL," which addresses discrepancies between the action prescribed by the RL agent's policy and the action actually executed by the environment or actuator. This type of uncertainty is referred to as "policy execution uncertainty." Examples include actuator noise or failure in robotic control, or patient non-compliance in healthcare advice.

A common approach in Robust RL, derived from methods in supervised learning, is adversarial training. This framework often models uncertainties as adversarial attacks, formulating the learning problem as a zero-sum game between the RL agent and an adversary. Previous work, such as that by Tessler et al., introduced the Probabilistic Action Robust MDP (PR-MDP) where an alternative adversarial action is taken with a certain probability (ρ). They proposed the Probabilistic Robust Policy Iteration (PR-PI) algorithm, which converges to an optimal value but requires known MDP dynamics (reward functions and transition probabilities) for an MDP solver, limiting its applicability in unknown environments. Another related work by Klima et al. extended temporal difference (TD) learning with a robust operator, demonstrating convergence to an optimal robust Q-function, but lacked theoretical guarantees on sample complexity or regret.

This current research addresses a gap in the existing literature by providing a sample-efficient algorithm for action robust RL with probabilistic policy execution uncertainty, offering strong theoretical guarantees (minimax optimality) for both model-based and model-free settings, which were largely absent in prior work focusing on this specific uncertainty model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop efficient algorithms for action robust reinforcement learning when faced with probabilistic policy execution uncertainty, accompanied by rigorous theoretical guarantees. The motivation stems from the observed fragility of standard RL algorithms in real-world deployments and the limitations of existing robust RL methods, particularly concerning sample efficiency and theoretical optimality for the probabilistic action uncertainty model.

Specifically, the key objectives articulated in the paper are:

- **Formalize the Problem Setting**: To model episodic RL in the context of a probabilistic policy execution uncertain set. This involves establishing the existence of an optimal robust policy within this framework and deriving the action robust Bellman equation and Bellman optimality equation necessary for its solution. This theoretical groundwork is crucial for understanding the properties of optimal policies under such uncertainties.
- **Develop a Minimax Optimal Model-Based Algorithm**: To design a novel algorithm, named Action Robust Reinforcement Learning with Certificates (ARRLC), for episodic action robust MDPs. A critical goal for ARRLC is to achieve minimax order optimal regret and sample complexity, thereby providing strong theoretical guarantees on its learning efficiency.
- **Develop a Model-Free Algorithm with Theoretical Guarantees**: To extend the approach to a model-free setting by developing an algorithm called Action Robust Q-learning with UCB-Hoeffding (AR-UCBH). This algorithm aims to provide theoretical bounds on its regret and sample complexity, addressing scenarios where explicit model estimation might be impractical or computationally expensive.
- **Empirical Validation**: To conduct numerical experiments to validate the robustness and efficiency of the proposed algorithms. This objective includes demonstrating that ARRLC outperforms non-robust RL algorithms when subjected to action perturbations and that it exhibits faster convergence compared to existing robust TD algorithms. These experiments aim to bridge the gap between theoretical guarantees and practical performance.

These objectives collectively aim to advance the field of robust RL by offering a comprehensive solution that is both theoretically grounded and practically effective for a prevalent type of real-world uncertainty.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a rigorous theoretical framework combined with algorithm development and empirical validation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated within the context of a tabular episodic Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by $M = (S, A, H, P, R)$. Here, $S$ is the state space, $A$ is the action space, $H$ is the horizon (number of steps per episode), $P$ represents the state transition probabilities, and $R$ is the reward function. The agent interacts with the MDP over episodes, observing states, choosing actions, and receiving rewards and next states. A policy $\pi$ maps states to actions (or probability distributions over actions).

The core of the "action robust" aspect is the **Probabilistic Policy Execution Uncertain Set**, denoted $\Pi_ρ(\pi)$. This set models the scenario where, for a given policy $\pi$ that specifies an action, the agent actually takes that action with probability $(1 - ρ)$ and an *alternative adversarial action* with probability $ρ$. The adversarial action is chosen to minimize the cumulative reward. The goal of action robust RL is to find an optimal robust policy $\pi^*$ that maximizes the worst-case accumulated reward under this uncertainty. This involves defining action robust value functions ($V^\pi_h(s)$) and Q-functions ($Q^\pi_h(s,a)$) that account for the minimizing adversary.

#### 3.2. Existence of Optimal Robust Policy

The paper establishes the existence of a deterministic optimal robust policy for action robust MDPs with this specific probabilistic uncertainty model. It proves a "perfect duality," showing that $\max_{\pi} \min_{e\pi \in \Pi_ρ(\pi)} E[\dots] = \min_{e\pi \in \Pi_ρ(\pi)} \max_{\pi} E[\dots]$. This duality implies that the problem can be framed as a zero-sum game. Furthermore, the research derives the **action robust Bellman equation** and the **action robust Bellman optimality equation**, which are crucial for solving for the optimal policy via backward induction, similar to standard MDPs but adapted for the adversarial component.

#### 3.3. Action Robust Reinforcement Learning with Certificates (ARRLC) Algorithm (Model-Based)

ARRLC (Algorithm 1) is a model-based algorithm designed for episodic action robust MDPs. It operates in two main phases within each episode:
1. **Sample Trajectory and Update Model Estimate (Lines 3-11)**: The algorithm simulates the action robust MDP. The agent executes a "behavior policy" $e\pi_h^k = (1 - ρ)\pi_h^k + ρ\pi_h^k'$, where $\pi_h^k$ is the current agent policy and $\pi_h^k'$ is the current adversarial policy. Samples ($s, a, r, s'$) are collected, and empirical estimates of the reward function ($\hat{r}$) and transition probabilities ($\hat{P}$) are updated.
2. **Adversarial Planning from the Estimated Model (Lines 16-25)**: This phase involves value iteration on the estimated model. The algorithm computes optimistic estimates of the robust Q-function ($Q_h$) and pessimistic estimates ($Q_h$). These estimates are derived using bonus terms ($\theta_h(s,a)$) that incorporate uncertainty from estimation errors, ensuring optimism for exploration. Crucially, the algorithm simultaneously plans both the agent's optimal policy ($\pi_h^{k+1}(s) = \arg\max_a Q_h(s,a)$) and the corresponding adversarial policy ($\pi_h^{k+1'}(s) = \arg\min_a Q_h(s,a)$) using the action robust Bellman optimality equation. The optimistic ($V_h$) and pessimistic ($V_h$) robust value functions are then updated based on these Q-values. The policy certificates ($V_1(s_1), V_1(s_1)$) provide bounds on the return of the learned policy.

The design of ARRLC addresses two main challenges: (1) planning both agent and adversarial policies under estimation errors, which is handled by carefully designed bonus terms to maintain optimism for the agent and pessimism for the adversary, ensuring $V_h \ge V_h^* \ge V_h^\pi \ge V_h$; and (2) bounding the variance of estimated value functions when the behavior policy is non-deterministic due to adversarial actions.

#### 3.4. Action Robust Q-learning with UCB-Hoeffding (AR-UCBH) Algorithm (Model-Free)

AR-UCBH (Algorithm 2) provides a model-free alternative. Instead of explicitly estimating transition probabilities and rewards, it directly updates optimistic ($Q_h$) and pessimistic ($Q_h$) Q-values using Q-learning-style updates based on observed rewards and the value of the next state. It uses a learning rate $\alpha_t$ and a bonus term $b_t$ derived from Hoeffding's inequality for exploration. Similar to ARRLC, it simultaneously determines the agent and adversarial policies based on these Q-values. A cautious policy update rule is implemented (lines 14-16) to ensure the output policy is only updated if its lower bound on the value function improves, contributing to stability in the model-free setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical guarantees for the proposed algorithms and empirical validation of their performance.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Guarantees for ARRLC (Model-Based)

Theorem 2 establishes the theoretical performance of the ARRLC algorithm for episodic action robust MDPs:
- **Sample Complexity**: To achieve an $\epsilon$-suboptimal policy (i.e., $V_1^*(s_1) - V_1^{\pi_{\text{out}}}(s_1) \le \epsilon$), the algorithm requires a number of episodes $K \ge \Omega(SAH^3\iota^2/\epsilon^2 + S^2AH^3\iota^2/\epsilon)$. For small $\epsilon \le H/S$, this simplifies to $O(SAH^3\iota^2/\epsilon^2)$.
- **Regret Bound**: The cumulative regret over $K$ episodes, defined as $\sum_{k=1}^K (V_1^*(s_1^k) - V_1^{\pi_k}(s_1^k))$, is bounded by $O(\sqrt{SAH^3K\iota} + S^2AH^3\iota^2)$. Here, $\iota = \log(2SAHK/\delta)$ accounts for logarithmic factors and the failure probability $\delta$.

These bounds demonstrate that ARRLC achieves *minimax order optimal* regret and sample complexity. This optimality is derived by comparing the bounds to information-theoretic lower bounds for standard MDPs (when $ρ=0$), which are $\Omega(SAH^3/\epsilon^2)$ for sample complexity and $\Omega(\sqrt{SAH^3K})$ for regret. The ARRLC algorithm matches these lower bounds up to logarithmic factors, indicating its efficiency.

#### 4.2. Theoretical Guarantees for AR-UCBH (Model-Free)

Theorem 3 provides the theoretical guarantees for the model-free AR-UCBH algorithm:
- **Sample Complexity**: To achieve an $\epsilon$-suboptimal policy, the number of episodes required is $K \ge \Omega(SAH^5\iota/\epsilon^2 + SAH^2/\epsilon)$.
- **Regret Bound**: The cumulative regret over $K$ episodes is bounded by $O(\sqrt{SAH^5K\iota} + SAH^2)$.

These bounds indicate that the model-free AR-UCBH algorithm is also sample-efficient, although its dependency on the horizon $H$ is higher ($H^5$) compared to the model-based ARRLC ($H^3$). This is generally expected for model-free methods, which often require more samples to learn optimal policies without explicit model information.

#### 4.3. Numerical Experiments

Numerical experiments were conducted using the OpenAI Gym framework on two environments: Cliff Walking (a toy text environment) and Inverted Pendulum (a continuous control environment simulated with MuJoCo). The experiments focused on demonstrating robustness and efficiency.
- **Robustness Demonstration**: ARRLC was compared against ORLC (Optimistic Reinforcement Learning with Certificates), a non-robust RL algorithm. Policies were trained in a clean environment but tested under action perturbations, where an adversarial action was taken with probability $p$. The results showed that ARRLC consistently achieved a *much higher accumulated reward* than ORLC across various perturbation probabilities ($p=0.1, 0.2$) and perturbation types (fixed adversarial policy or random actions). This validates ARRLC's ability to maintain performance under policy execution uncertainty.
- **Efficiency Demonstration**: ARRLC was compared against the robust TD algorithm proposed by Klima et al., which lacks theoretical guarantees. The results indicated that ARRLC *converged much faster* than the robust TD algorithm in the presence of action perturbations. This demonstrates the efficiency of ARRLC in learning optimal robust policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by addressing a critical challenge concerning the robustness of RL agents in uncertain real-world environments. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Rigorous Theoretical Foundation**: The establishment of the action robust Bellman equation and Bellman optimality equation, along with the proof of existence for a deterministic optimal robust policy, provides a solid theoretical foundation for addressing probabilistic policy execution uncertainty. This generalizes key theoretical tools of standard MDPs to a robust setting.
- **Minimax Optimal Algorithms**: The development of ARRLC, a model-based algorithm with minimax optimal regret and sample complexity, marks a significant advancement. This provides strong theoretical guarantees that were largely absent in prior work on this specific form of action robustness. Such guarantees are crucial for understanding the fundamental limits of learning in adversarial environments and for building confidence in the algorithm's performance.
- **Practical Applicability (Model-Based and Model-Free)**: By providing both model-based (ARRLC) and model-free (AR-UCBH) algorithms, the research offers flexible solutions for different practical scenarios. Model-based approaches are suitable when environmental dynamics can be estimated or approximated, while model-free methods are critical when direct model learning is infeasible or too complex.
- **Enhanced Trustworthiness and Deployability**: The empirical results demonstrate that ARRLC significantly outperforms non-robust algorithms under action perturbations and converges faster than existing robust TD methods. This practical validation, combined with theoretical guarantees, indicates that the proposed methods can lead to more robust and reliable RL agents. This is particularly relevant for high-stakes applications like autonomous systems, robotics, and healthcare, where even small execution uncertainties can have substantial consequences. The ability to learn policies that inherently account for and mitigate these uncertainties is a key step towards making RL trustworthy for real-world deployment.
- **Addressing a Gap in Robust RL**: The paper specifically fills a gap in the literature by focusing on probabilistic policy execution uncertainty with strong theoretical performance guarantees. This distinct uncertainty model, where an adversary probabilistically intervenes in action execution, is highly relevant to many physical and human-in-the-loop systems.

In summary, this work provides a comprehensive theoretical and algorithmic framework for efficient action robust reinforcement learning, offering provable performance guarantees and demonstrating practical effectiveness. It advances the state-of-the-art in robust RL, moving towards the development of more reliable and deployable autonomous agents.
