# Doe-1: Closed-Loop Autonomous Driving with Large World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Doe-1 is a closed-loop driving world model that casts perception, prediction, and planning as next-token generation over interleaved observation, description, and action tokens with a single Chameleon-based autoregressive transformer, achieving unified vision QA, controllable video generation, and motion planning on nuScenes without task-specific finetuning.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end pipelines rely on manually designed BEV/occupancy scene representations that discard fine-grained cues, capping the performance ceiling when scaling up data and model size.
- World models predict future frames without conditioning on the planned ego action, ignoring that other agents' behavior depends on what the ego vehicle actually does next.
- Pipelines plan several future steps at once yet execute only the first, wasting computation on redundant multi-step deduction instead of instant next-frame decisions.

## Method

- **Doe-1**'s unified multimodal token sequence interleaves observation, description, and action tokens per frame, modeled autoregressively by a single ==Chameleon==-derived transformer with ==next-token prediction== as the sole training objective.
- ==Position-aware action tokenizer== applies ==sinusoidal positional encoding== to discretized floating-point displacements and yaw angles so metric closeness in Euclidean space is preserved after tokenization.
- ==Action masking scheme== retains only the first predicted motion of a multi-frame action while masking the remaining p-1 future motions during generation, preventing error accumulation across autoregressive steps.

## Results

- On OmniDrive-nuScenes VQA using only a front camera, Doe-1 reaches **54.5%** AP and **72.8** CIDEr, matching or beating surround-view OmniDrive-3D/2D/BEV baselines on several metrics.
- Action-conditioned video generation attains FID **15.9** on nuScenes, comparable to Drive-WM (**15.8**) and GenAD (**15.4**) while being the first autoregressive rather than diffusion-based driving world model.
- End-to-end motion planning with only QA supervision achieves **0.70m** average L2 error and **0.21%** average collision rate (VAD-style metric), and ablation shows removing the action mask raises average L2 error to **2.83m** and collision rate to **2.72%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing perception, prediction, and planning as transitions between token modalities lets one autoregressive model serve multiple driving tasks purely by changing the prompt, without separate heads or finetuning.
> - Masking future predicted actions during training is a simple but critical guard against compounding errors in long-horizon autoregressive control, generalizable beyond driving.
> - Directly generating future observations in raw RGB space avoids the information bottleneck of hand-crafted intermediate representations, trading interpretability for higher representational ceiling as data scales.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Autonomous driving technology has been advancing rapidly, evolving from rule-based systems to sophisticated artificial intelligence models. A significant challenge in this field has been creating systems that can effectively perceive their environment, predict future states, and plan appropriate actions—all while maintaining a continuous feedback loop between these components. The Doe-1 framework, developed by researchers at Tsinghua University, represents a breakthrough approach to this problem.

As shown in the figure above, Doe-1 introduces a novel closed-loop autonomous driving framework that unifies perception, prediction, and planning within a single large world model. Unlike traditional approaches that handle these tasks as separate modules, Doe-1 integrates them into a cohesive system that processes information in a continuous cycle, allowing the vehicle to respond dynamically to changes in its environment.

### 2. The Evolution of Autonomous Driving Systems

Autonomous driving systems have traditionally followed a modular architecture, with separate components for perception (understanding the environment), prediction (anticipating what will happen next), and planning (deciding what actions to take). This approach, while intuitive, has several limitations:

1. **Information loss** between modules can accumulate, degrading overall performance
2. **Limited scalability** as each module requires separate design and optimization
3. **Weak interactions** between components prevent holistic reasoning about complex scenarios

As illustrated in the figure above, autonomous driving models have evolved through several generations:

- **Modular End-to-End Models** (Panel a) separate perception, prediction, and planning but train them jointly
- **Direct End-to-End Models** (Panel b) map observations directly to actions
- **LLM/VLM-based Models** (Panel c) leverage language models to interpret scenes and determine actions
- **Driving World Models** (Panel d, Doe-1) create a closed-loop system where all components interact continuously

Recent approaches have begun incorporating Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to improve scene understanding and decision-making. However, these models often operate in an open-loop fashion, meaning they don't account for how the vehicle's actions affect future perceptions of the environment.

### 3. Understanding the Doe-1 Framework

The key insight behind Doe-1 is approaching autonomous driving as a closed-loop system, where perception, prediction, and planning are tightly interconnected:

As shown in the figure, there are three main approaches to autonomous driving:

1. **End-to-End Autonomous Driving** (Panel a): Converts observations to descriptions and then to actions
2. **World Model for Autonomous Driving** (Panel b): Predicts future observations based on actions
3. **Closed-Loop Autonomous Driving** (Panel c): Combines both approaches, creating a complete cycle

Doe-1 implements the closed-loop approach (Panel c), which offers several advantages:

- **Complete feedback loop**: Actions influence future observations
- **Unified representation**: All components share the same underlying model
- **Temporal consistency**: The system maintains coherence across time steps

This approach allows Doe-1 to handle complex scenarios that require understanding the long-term consequences of actions.

### 4. The Closed-Loop World Model

At the core of Doe-1 is a large driving world model based on an autoregressive transformer architecture. This model treats autonomous driving as a next-token prediction problem, where the system learns to generate the appropriate next element in a sequence of multimodal tokens.

The figure illustrates how driving data (including sensor data, trajectories, and perception information) is reorganized into a sequence that feeds into the Doe-1 world model. The model then performs next-token prediction across different modalities (image, text, and action) over time.

Unlike traditional approaches that require manually designed representations of the environment, Doe-1 directly processes raw sensor data and generates free-form text descriptions, enabling more flexible and detailed representations of complex driving scenarios.

### 5. Multimodal Token Approach

Doe-1 represents driving scenes using three types of tokens:

1. **Observation Tokens (O)**: Represent RGB images captured by the vehicle's camera. These are processed using an image tokenizer (specifically, a vector-quantized variational autoencoder or VQ-VAE) that converts visual information into a format the model can process.

2. **Description Tokens (D)**: Represent free-form text descriptions of the scene. These might include information about road conditions, other vehicles, pedestrians, traffic signs, and potential hazards. The text tokenizer converts these descriptions into tokens.

3. **Action Tokens (A)**: Represent the vehicle's planned actions, encoded as displacements in a bird's-eye view (BEV) space. The action tokenizer converts trajectory points into a sequence that the model can generate.

This multimodal approach allows Doe-1 to learn rich representations of driving scenarios while maintaining the ability to generate interpretable outputs that humans can understand and verify.

### 6. Training and Implementation

The Doe-1 model is trained on the nuScenes dataset, a large-scale dataset for autonomous driving that includes annotated sensor data from real-world driving scenarios. The training process involves teaching the model to predict the next token in sequences that include observation, description, and action tokens.

The model uses different prompting strategies depending on the task:

For different autonomous driving tasks, Doe-1 uses various combinations of input and generated tokens:

- **Visual Question-Answering**: Generates description tokens (D) based on observation tokens (O)
- **Action-Conditioned Video Generation**: Predicts future observation tokens (O) based on current observations (O) and actions (A)
- **End-to-End Motion Planning**: Generates action tokens (A) based on observation tokens (O) and descriptions (D)

The implementation of Doe-1 is based on the Lumina-mGPT 7B model, which is fine-tuned for autonomous driving using the nuScenes dataset. The model architecture includes a tokenizer for each modality (image, text, and action) and a transformer backbone that processes the tokenized sequences.

### 7. Key Capabilities and Results

Doe-1 demonstrates impressive capabilities across various autonomous driving tasks:

1. **Visual Question-Answering**: The model can accurately answer questions about the driving scene, providing detailed descriptions of the environment, identifying objects, and reasoning about appropriate driving behaviors.

As shown in the figure, Doe-1 can answer questions about vehicles in the vicinity, the presence of pedestrians, and appropriate driving behavior. It can also generate detailed scene descriptions and assess the safety of potential trajectories.

2. **Action-Conditioned Video Generation**: The model can predict how the environment will change in response to specific driving actions, generating future frames that maintain visual consistency with the vehicle's motion.

3. **End-to-End Motion Planning**: Doe-1 can generate safe and efficient trajectories for the vehicle to follow, with a low collision rate within the critical 1-second horizon.

Experimental results on the nuScenes dataset show that Doe-1 achieves competitive or superior performance compared to existing methods, particularly in scenarios requiring understanding of complex interactions between the vehicle and its environment.

### 8. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive capabilities, Doe-1 has several limitations that point to directions for future research:

1. **Computational Complexity**: As a large autoregressive model, Doe-1 requires significant computational resources, which may limit its real-time application in autonomous vehicles.

2. **Dataset Limitations**: The model is trained on the nuScenes dataset, which, while comprehensive, cannot cover all possible driving scenarios. This may affect the model's generalization to rare or unseen situations.

3. **Safety Guarantees**: Like most deep learning approaches, Doe-1 doesn't provide formal safety guarantees, which are crucial for deployment in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving.

Future work could address these limitations by:

- Developing more efficient model architectures and inference methods
- Incorporating additional data sources and simulation environments
- Integrating formal verification techniques to provide safety guarantees
- Exploring hybrid approaches that combine the flexibility of large models with the reliability of rule-based systems

### 9. Conclusion

Doe-1 represents a significant advancement in autonomous driving technology by introducing a closed-loop framework that unifies perception, prediction, and planning within a single large world model. By framing autonomous driving as a next-token prediction problem, Doe-1 overcomes many limitations of existing approaches while providing a more scalable and interpretable solution.

The multimodal token approach allows the model to process different types of information—visual observations, textual descriptions, and driving actions—in a unified manner, enabling more coherent and context-aware decision-making. The closed-loop nature of the system ensures that the vehicle's actions influence its future perceptions, creating a more realistic model of the driving process.

While challenges remain, particularly in terms of computational efficiency and safety guarantees, Doe-1 demonstrates the potential of large world models to revolutionize autonomous driving. By continuing to refine this approach and address its limitations, researchers can move closer to developing autonomous vehicles that can navigate complex, dynamic environments with human-like understanding and adaptability.
