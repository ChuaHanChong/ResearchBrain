# GC-VLN: Instruction as Graph Constraints for Training-free Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A training-free framework for Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) leverages linguistic instructions as explicit graph constraints to guide robot movement. The approach establishes new state-of-the-art performance among training-free methods in simulated benchmarks and achieves successful real-world deployment on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) methods struggle with generalization to novel environments and instructions due to their reliance on extensive training data.
- A persistent "sim-to-real gap" hinders the practical deployment of navigation agents trained in simulators onto physical robots, even for many zero-shot approaches.
- The high cost and effort of data collection and model training remain significant barriers for developing robust, deployable embodied AI systems.

## Method

- Decomposes natural language instructions into a multi-stage ==Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)== with waypoint and object nodes and explicit spatial constraints, using a Large Language Model.
- Leverages a pre-defined constraint library to formalize diverse spatial relationships (angle and distance) and solves for waypoint and object coordinates via nonlinear constrained optimization.
- Incorporates a Navigation Tree and backtracking mechanism to systematically explore potential paths and recover from navigation failures or ambiguities in constraint solutions.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance among training-free methods on the R2R-CE benchmark, demonstrating a **2%** increase in Success Rate compared to previous approaches.
- Outperformed all other reported zero-shot methods on the more challenging RxR-CE dataset, showing robust generalization to complex instructions and environments.
- Successfully deployed on a physical robot in real-world environments without any adaptation, validating the framework's ability to overcome the sim-to-real gap.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Natural language instructions can be effectively translated into explicit, mathematically formalized graph constraints, guiding robot navigation without task-specific training.
> - A training-free framework, by completely avoiding simulator-based learning, can overcome the sim-to-real gap and enable direct deployment on physical robots.
> - Combining Large Language Models for linguistic decomposition and Vision-Language Models for real-time object perception provides a potent, interpretable navigation strategy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) stands as a foundational challenge in embodied AI, requiring robots to comprehend natural language instructions and navigate novel environments. The research landscape for VLN has evolved considerably, grappling with issues of linguistic understanding, environmental perception, and spatial reasoning.

Initially, VLN research was dominated by **data-driven methods**. These approaches relied on extensive annotated datasets and supervised learning within simulated environments. While achieving notable performance within their trained domains, they exhibited two critical limitations:
1. **Poor Generalization:** These models struggled significantly when encountering unseen environments or novel instructions, a consequence of their dependence on the specific data distributions they were trained on.
2. **Sim-to-Real Gap:** The discrepancy between simulated training environments (often discrete, with predefined navigation graphs) and the complexities of real-world physics and perception made direct deployment onto physical robots largely impractical.

To address these shortcomings, **zero-shot VLN methods** emerged, aiming to enable navigation in entirely novel scenarios without task-specific training data. Early zero-shot efforts, exemplified by DiscussNav and MapGPT, primarily functioned within discrete simulated environments. Although innovative, their confinement to discrete spaces still imposed a significant sim-to-real gap, limiting their applicability to real-world continuous navigation.

The introduction of **Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE)** marked a crucial step towards greater realism. VLN-CE allows robots to move freely and predict low-level actions (e.g., precise turns and movements), more accurately mirroring real-world robot operation. However, even within VLN-CE, most existing zero-shot methods, such as A²Nav, still involve some form of unsupervised or self-supervised training within simulators. While not requiring human-labeled data, this simulator-based training means these models implicitly learn biases from synthetic environments, thus maintaining a residual sim-to-real gap that impedes seamless deployment on real-world robots.

GC-VLN directly targets this persistent challenge by proposing a **truly training-free framework for VLN-CE**. By completely eliminating any reliance on simulator-based training (supervised or unsupervised), GC-VLN aims to eradicate the sim-to-real gap at its source. This allows for direct and effective deployment on physical robots in real-world scenarios without prior adaptation. This work represents a significant paradigm shift, moving away from data-intensive training and towards a more symbolic, constraint-driven approach augmented by powerful foundation models. It positions itself as a pioneering effort to develop highly robust, autonomous systems capable of interpreting complex human instructions and navigating continuous physical spaces with unprecedented generalization and real-world applicability. The integration of LLMs for linguistic decomposition and pre-trained vision models (like Grounded-SAM-2) for object perception also situates GC-VLN firmly within the contemporary trend of leveraging powerful foundation models for complex embodied AI tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the GC-VLN research is to develop a **training-free framework for Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE)**. This central aim is to empower robots to interpret and execute intricate natural language instructions for navigation in novel, continuous real-world environments, without requiring any prior supervised or unsupervised training specific to the navigation task or environment.

Underpinning this primary goal are several crucial secondary objectives:
- To achieve **robust generalization** across a wide spectrum of unseen environments and diverse linguistic instruction sets.
- To entirely **eliminate the sim-to-real gap**, a persistent bottleneck that has traditionally hindered the practical deployment of navigation agents trained in simulators onto physical robots.
- To reformulate navigation guidance as an **explicit graph constraint optimization problem**, thereby moving away from opaque, data-driven predictive models.
- To systematically **decompose complex human instructions** into well-defined, actionable spatial constraints that a robot can directly process and solve.
- To ensure **fault tolerance** and robustness during navigation by effectively handling uncertainties in constraint satisfaction, specifically addressing scenarios where no feasible solution exists or multiple solutions are possible.

The motivations driving this ambitious research are deeply rooted in the limitations of current VLN methodologies:
- **Lack of Generalization in Data-Driven VLN:** Existing data-driven VLN methods, despite their performance in familiar settings, exhibit poor generalization when faced with novel scenes or instructions. Their heavy reliance on specific annotated datasets makes them brittle to out-of-distribution data, limiting their utility in dynamic real-world environments.
- **Persistent Sim-to-Real Gap:** Even with the advent of zero-shot VLN, the challenge of seamlessly transferring learned behaviors from simulation to reality remains. While discrete-environment zero-shot methods offer insights, they are fundamentally impractical for continuous real-world movement. Furthermore, VLN-CE approaches that employ self-supervised training in simulators still introduce a sim-to-real gap, as their learning is biased by the synthetic data, leading to performance degradation in physical deployment.
- **Imperative for Robust Real-World Autonomy:** For embodied AI to achieve widespread adoption, robots must demonstrate truly autonomous navigation capabilities driven by natural language commands in unpredictable real-world settings. A framework that is completely training-free circumvents the laborious process of data collection and annotation, as well as the biases of specific training environments, making it ideal for developing highly adaptable and easily deployable robotic systems.
- **Desire for Interpretable Decision-Making:** Many contemporary deep learning models function as "black boxes," making it difficult to understand their decision-making processes. A constraint-driven paradigm offers a more interpretable approach, where a robot's actions are a direct consequence of solving explicit spatial relationships, which greatly aids in debugging, analysis, and building trust in autonomous agents.

In essence, GC-VLN aims to deliver a highly practical and dependable navigation system that can effortlessly translate human linguistic directives into precise robotic actions within continuous, unfamiliar real-world environments, all while circumventing the significant overhead and inherent limitations associated with data collection, model training, and simulator-to-real transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GC-VLN introduces an innovative, training-free framework for VLN-CE, meticulously structured around two principal modules: Graph Constraint Construction and Constrained Optimization.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline (Figure 2)

The navigation process begins with a robot initialized in an unknown environment, receiving a linguistic instruction $I$. This instruction is first fed into the **Graph Constraint Construction module**, which translates it into a structured graph constraint $K$. This $K$ then serves as the guiding blueprint for the **Constrained Optimization module**, which iteratively determines waypoint coordinates and generates low-level actions for the robot. A crucial component, the **Navigation Tree** coupled with a backtracking mechanism, is employed to robustly manage any uncertainties arising from constraint solutions.

#### 3.2. Graph Constraint Construction

This module's primary function is to transform the typically verbose and potentially ambiguous natural language instruction into a precise, actionable set of spatial relationships.
- **Instruction Decomposition:** A Large Language Model (LLM), specifically DeepSeek-R1, is prompted to parse the instruction $I$ into a multi-stage Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), denoted as $G = (V, E)$.
 - **Nodes ($V$):** The graph comprises **waypoint nodes ($V_w$)**, representing sequential points along the navigation path, and **object nodes ($V_o$)**, corresponding to landmarks or objects explicitly mentioned in the instruction. Each object is uniquely assigned to a specific navigation stage.
 - **Edges ($E$):** Edges connect waypoints sequentially ($E_w$) and establish spatial relationships between waypoints and objects ($E_o$).
 - **Attributes:** Each navigation stage is characterized by a high-level navigation direction (e.g., "front," "right," "left," "back," "unknown"). Each object node is associated with a specific spatial relationship to the navigation path (e.g., "right," "left," "through," "pass," "near"). The decomposition process is designed to preserve all information from $I$, explicitly represent all relevant objects, and provide clear navigational cues and spatial relationships.

- **Constraint Library ($\mathcal{L}$):** A pre-defined library encapsulates six fundamental types of spatial constraints (Figure 3). These types are designed to cover the full spectrum of spatial relationships commonly encountered in VLN instructions.
 - **Categorization:** Constraints are classified based on the presence of known/unknown angular and distance information, and whether they are "unary" (involving two nodes, e.g., "move forward 3 meters") or "multi-constraints" (involving three or four nodes, e.g., "move through a door" or "walk between two chairs").
 - **Formalization:** Each constraint $c(v|u)$ (or $c(v|u_1, u_2)$, etc.) is mathematically formalized using angle ($c_a$) and distance ($c_d$) sub-constraints. These sub-constraints quantitatively assess how well a candidate position $v$ adheres to the specified spatial relationship with a known reference position $u$ (or multiple $u$'s), incorporating baseline values ($\phi, d$) and tolerance parameters ($\Delta\phi, \Delta d$).

- **Graph Constraint ($\mathcal{K}$):** By querying the constraint library $\mathcal{L}$ with the edges $e \in E$ derived from the decomposed graph $G$, the specific types of constraints are retrieved. The collection of all nodes $V$ and these instantiated constraints $C$ forms the complete graph constraint $\mathcal{K} = (V, C)$. The directed nature of constraints in $C$ implies causality: the coordinates of a child node can only be determined once the coordinates of its parent node(s) are known.

#### 3.3. Constrained Optimization for Node Coordinates

This module leverages $\mathcal{K}$ to direct the robot's movement by sequentially solving for the coordinates of waypoint nodes in the environment.
- **Topological Sort (TS):** To ensure a well-ordered and logical sequence for coordinate resolution, a topological sort is applied to $\mathcal{K}$. This guarantees that for any constraint $c(v|u)$, the parent node $u$ is always processed and its coordinates determined before its child node $v$.
- **Object Node Coordinate Determination:** For object nodes ($v_o_{ij}$), a pre-trained vision model, Grounded-SAM-2, is employed to perceive objects from the robot's real-time RGB-D observations. These perceived objects are then projected onto a Bird's-Eye View (BEV) map. From the pool of detected objects, those whose coordinates satisfy their corresponding constraint conditions (derived from $\mathcal{K}$) are selected as potential solutions for $v_o_{ij}$.
- **Waypoint Node Coordinate Determination:** The coordinates for waypoint nodes ($v_w_i$) are determined by solving a **nonlinear constrained optimization problem (P1)**:
 - **Objective Function:** The optimizer aims to maximize the aggregate satisfaction, represented by `sum(c)`, across all constraints $c \in C_{v_w_i}$ that apply to the current waypoint $v_w_i$.
 - **Constraint Conditions:** Crucially, all individual sub-constraints must be satisfied (i.e., `min(c) >= 0`). Additionally, to encourage exploration of alternative paths, any new solution for $v_w_i$ must be sufficiently distinct from previously found solutions for that same node. This solver identifies the most optimal coordinates that best adhere to the specified spatial relationships.
- **Navigation Tree ($\mathcal{T}$) and Backtracking:** The constraint solver may produce an uncertain number of solutions (zero, one, or multiple) for each node's coordinates. The navigation tree $\mathcal{T}$ is designed to systematically manage this uncertainty and enhance the system's fault tolerance.
 - **Tree Structure:** The topological sort order is extended into $\mathcal{T}$. Each level `i` in $\mathcal{T}$ corresponds to the `i`-th node $v_i$ in the TS sequence. Branches at level `i+1` represent the multiple coordinate solutions found for node $v_{i+1}$, conditional on the chosen parent node solution at level `i`. A unique path from the root to a leaf in $\mathcal{T}$ defines a specific candidate navigation path in the environment.
 - **Backtracking Mechanism:** If the constraint solver fails to find any feasible solution for a given node (resulting in no branches at that level in $\mathcal{T}$), the robot initiates a backtracking procedure. It reverts up the tree $\mathcal{T}$ to the most recent branch point that has unexplored sibling branches. From this point, the robot selects an alternative path to explore. This mechanism is vital for recovering from incorrect initial interpretations or local navigation failures, significantly enhancing the system's robustness.
 - Navigation successfully terminates when the robot reaches the leaf node corresponding to the final destination waypoint $v_{|V|}$.
- **Local Policy:** Once the coordinates of the next intermediate waypoint are determined, the Fast Marching Method (FMM) is utilized to generate the precise low-level actions (e.g., specific turns and movements) required for the robot to navigate to that target point.

This meticulously designed approach seamlessly integrates sophisticated linguistic parsing, formal semantic constraint modeling, real-time visual perception, and robust optimization with an advanced fault-tolerance mechanism, culminating in a truly training-free navigation system for continuous environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GC-VLN framework demonstrates substantial progress in training-free Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE), rigorously validated through comprehensive experiments in both simulated and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance in Simulators

GC-VLN was benchmarked on the widely recognized R2R-CE and RxR-CE datasets, specifically utilizing their challenging `validation-unseen` splits to evaluate generalization to novel environments.
- **R2R-CE Benchmark:** GC-VLN achieved state-of-the-art performance among training-free methods. It notably surpassed InstructNav, the previous leading training-free approach, by a significant **2% increase in Success Rate (SR)**. This highlights its superior capability in successfully completing navigation tasks in previously unseen R2R-CE environments.
- **RxR-CE Benchmark:** On the RxR-CE dataset, which is recognized as more challenging due to its longer paths and multilingual instructions, GC-VLN demonstrably **outperformed all other reported zero-shot methods**. This robust performance on a more complex dataset underscores its exceptional ability to handle increased environmental and linguistic complexities.
- **Comparison to Supervised Methods:** Remarkably, GC-VLN's training-free performance was competitive with, and in some cases even surpassed, that of certain *supervised* VLN-CE methods (e.g., NaVid on RxR-CE). This outcome profoundly illustrates the efficacy and efficiency of its constraint-driven approach, especially considering it operates without the benefit of extensive labeled data or simulator-specific training.
- **Consistent Metric Improvements:** The observed improvements were consistently reflected across all key evaluation metrics, including Success Rate (SR), Success Rate weighted by Path Length (SPL), Navigation Error (NE), and Oracle Success Rate (OSR), reinforcing the comprehensive superiority of the GC-VLN framework.

#### 4.2. Strong Generalization and Robustness

The ability of GC-VLN to sustain high performance on RxR-CE, a dataset considerably more difficult than R2R-CE, is a strong indicator of its exceptional generalization capacity. This suggests that the constraint-driven paradigm effectively distills navigational knowledge in a manner that remains robust and effective across diverse instruction lengths, varying linguistic complexities, and unfamiliar environmental layouts.

#### 4.3. Successful Real-World Deployment

A pivotal validation of GC-VLN's core claim of being training-free is its successful deployment on a physical robot. Using a Hexmove TRIGGER platform equipped with an Orbbec Gemini 336L camera and RealSense T265 tracking camera, qualitative experiments (Figure 5) showcased that the framework effectively generalizes to new, real-world environments and instruction sets without any need for adaptation or fine-tuning. This remarkable achievement directly demonstrates its ability to effectively overcome the persistent sim-to-real gap, a long-standing and critical challenge in embodied AI.

#### 4.4. Comprehensive Ablation Studies

A detailed ablation study conducted on the R2R-CE benchmark meticulously analyzed the contribution of each module within GC-VLN (Table 2):
- **Effectiveness of Graph Constraints:** Relaxing graph constraints (e.g., removing angle constraints, simplifying distance constraints) or entirely removing specific constraint types (such as waypoint, object, unary, or multi-constraints) consistently resulted in significant drops in both SR and SPL. This outcome rigorously confirms the critical importance of the comprehensive constraint library and the precise mathematical formalization of spatial relationships in guiding accurate and successful navigation.
- **Effectiveness of Constraint Solver and Navigation Tree:** Manipulating the topological sort order, disorganizing the solution ordering, replacing the optimization-based constraint solver with random sampling, simplifying the navigation tree (by discarding unexplored branches), or eliminating the backtracking mechanism entirely, all led to notable performance degradations. These results definitively validate the crucial roles played by the structured approach to constraint solving, the systematic exploration of the solution space, and the fault-tolerance mechanisms provided by the navigation tree and backtracking.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Additional ablation experiments investigating the impact of hyperparameters such as angle tolerance (∆ϕ) and distance baseline (d) (Table 3) confirmed that the chosen default values are indeed optimal for maximizing the system's performance.

In conclusion, GC-VLN not only establishes new benchmarks for training-free VLN-CE but also fundamentally demonstrates the viability of a constraint-driven, non-training approach for highly generalizable and practical robot navigation. This is supported by compelling empirical evidence from both extensive simulations and successful real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GC-VLN framework represents a substantial advancement in the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation, offering a novel paradigm with profound implications for embodied AI and robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**1. Pioneering True Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The most impactful contribution of GC-VLN is its effective eradication of the sim-to-real gap for VLN in continuous environments. By being entirely training-free, it completely bypasses any reliance on simulator-based training, which inherently introduces discrepancies between virtual and physical environments. This allows for the direct deployment of robots into new, real-world settings using human instructions, marking a critical breakthrough towards truly practical and flexible autonomous systems.

**2. Unprecedented Real-World Generalization and Robustness:** The constraint-driven approach, complemented by the robust navigation tree and backtracking mechanism, endows GC-VLN with exceptional generalization capabilities. Its demonstrated ability to perform effectively in unseen environments and interpret complex, multi-lingual instructions (as shown on RxR-CE) without task-specific training signifies a highly robust and adaptable navigation agent. This level of adaptability is indispensable for robots operating in dynamic and unpredictable human environments, where exhaustive pre-training on every conceivable scenario is simply unfeasible.

**3. Enhanced Interpretability and Explainability:** Unlike many "black-box" deep learning models, GC-VLN's navigation decisions are directly derived from explicitly defined spatial constraints and an optimization process. This provides a significantly higher degree of interpretability, as the robot's "reasoning" — how it interprets instructions like "turn right at the door" or "pass through two chairs" — is transparently encoded in the graph constraints and their solutions. This inherent explainability is invaluable for debugging, analyzing failures, and fostering greater trust in autonomous systems.

**4. Synergistic Integration of Foundation Models:** GC-VLN effectively showcases how powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can be integrated not just for semantic understanding, but for concrete, actionable generation in robotics. By leveraging DeepSeek-R1 for linguistic instruction decomposition and Grounded-SAM-2 for robust object perception, the framework demonstrates a potent synergy where high-level semantic reasoning from LLMs and accurate visual grounding from VLMs are seamlessly channeled into a structured, executable navigation strategy. This approach offers a compelling template for the broader application of foundation models in complex embodied AI tasks.

**5. Reduced Computational Overhead and Data Dependency:** The training-free nature of GC-VLN dramatically minimizes the computational resources, time, and human effort traditionally associated with data collection, annotation, and model training. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying advanced navigation capabilities, potentially democratizing access to complex robotic behaviors for a wider range of researchers and commercial entities.

**6. Paving the Way for Future Autonomous Systems:** GC-VLN lays a strong theoretical and empirical foundation for future advancements in autonomous navigation. Its success suggests that a more symbolic, constraint-based approach, powerfully augmented by modern foundation models, can serve as a compelling alternative or complement to purely end-to-end learning methods. This could open new avenues for research in symbolic AI, cognitive robotics, and the intelligent fusion of symbolic and sub-symbolic methods, ultimately leading to more intelligent, adaptable, and deployable embodied agents that can truly understand and act within human environments.
