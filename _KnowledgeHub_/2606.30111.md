# Automating the Design of Embodied Agent Architectures

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a framework for Agent Architecture Search (AAS) tailored for perceptual embodied agents, demonstrating that automated search can improve agent performance in simulators while also identifying key challenges related to evaluation noise and credit assignment. The proposed AGENTCANVAS substrate and KDLoop optimizer facilitate structured architectural modifications and achieve success-rate gains across various vision-language navigation, embodied question answering, and language-conditioned manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Modern embodied agent architectures are manually designed based on researcher intuition, leading to an increasingly vast and complex design space that is intractable to explore.
- Existing Agent Architecture Search (AAS) methods, successful in text domains, do not directly transfer to embodied agents due to stateful interactions, noisy simulator evaluations, and a lack of generic reasoning primitives.
- The proliferation of foundation models and specialized tools for embodied AI exacerbates the architectural design bottleneck, hindering scalable development.

## Method

- Introduced **AGENTCANVAS**, a ==typed-graph runtime==, to represent embodied agents as editable node-and-wire programs, enabling structured architectural modification and instrumented simulator execution.
- Developed a shared ==coding-agent harness== leveraging large language models for applying architectural edits, validating changes, and executing performance evaluations.
- Implemented and systematically evaluated three ==Agent Architecture Search (AAS)== variants (ADAS, AFlow, and the novel **KDLoop**) across four diverse embodied executors and task families.

## Results

- AAS variants achieved confirmed success-rate (SR) gains over seeded baselines, including **~7 percentage points** for MapGPT (AFlow/KDLoop), **4.0 pp** for SmartWay (ADAS), and **~3.8 pp** for VoxPoser (ADAS/AFlow).
- No single optimizer consistently outperformed others across all tasks, indicating different exploration strategies; for instance, KDLoop distributed edits across a broader range of intervention axes.
- The KDLoop variant uniquely diagnosed a deliberately injected logging fault in VoxPoser by actively inspecting episode-wise logs, demonstrating its potential for improved mechanism-level credit assignment beyond scalar metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Architecture-level search can achieve confirmed, deployable success-rate gains for perceptual embodied agents when the primary failure mode resides within the editable graph.
> - Embodied AAS is significantly challenged by high evaluation noise from simulator rollouts, which can obscure true performance, and by optimizers tending to converge in local edit basins.
> - Despite access to rich episode-level evidence, standard AAS variants primarily function as scalar-metric optimizers, struggling to effectively leverage detailed log data for fine-grained credit assignment or diagnosing underlying system faults.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence (AI) has seen a growing emphasis on embodied agents, systems that perceive and act within physical or simulated environments. These agents are frequently constructed by composing various modules, such as perception, memory, planning, and action, often integrating foundation models. This modular design is prevalent across tasks like vision-language navigation (VLN), embodied question answering (EQA), and language-conditioned manipulation (VLA). A key characteristic of these agentic systems, distinguishing them from end-to-end policies, is their explicit architectural structure. While this transparency allows for module-level editing and understanding of information flow, it also introduces a significant design challenge: the architectural choices (e.g., sensor abstractions, memory structures, planner topologies, model connections) are typically made manually based on researcher intuition for specific benchmarks. As the number of available foundation models and embodied tools expands, the complexity and size of this architectural design space quickly outpace manual exploration.

Agent Architecture Search (AAS) has emerged as a method to automate the design of agent workflows. Previous work in AAS has predominantly focused on text-domain agents, where optimizers propose and evaluate alternative Large Language Model (LLM)-agent workflows. These text-domain applications often involve cheap, stateless calls and a well-defined vocabulary of runnable primitives like chain-of-thought or self-refinement. However, extending AAS to embodied agents presents distinct challenges. Embodied agents interact with stateful simulators, produce noisy evaluations across multiple episodes, and generate long execution traces encompassing observations, actions, tool calls, and intermediate reasoning steps. Furthermore, there is no small, generic palette of composable embodied reasoning primitives comparable to those in the text domain; instead, researchers often build upon existing, complex published systems such as MapGPT, SmartWay, ExploreEQA, or VoxPoser.

This research paper addresses the gap in systematically evaluating AAS for perceptual embodied agents through simulator rollouts. It investigates the transferability of AAS principles from text-based to embodied domains and aims to characterize the specific constraints and opportunities that arise in this new context. By doing so, it contributes to the broader objective of making embodied agent development more scalable and less reliant on manual architectural design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing complexity and manual effort involved in designing architectures for embodied agents. Modern embodied agents, operating in diverse tasks such as vision-language navigation, embodied question answering, and language-conditioned manipulation, are typically engineered as hand-designed compositions of various modules including perception, memory, planning, and action. While this modularity offers clarity and editability, it simultaneously generates a vast architectural design space. Current practices heavily depend on researcher intuition to make critical design decisions regarding information storage, observation processing, and inter-module connections. This manual approach becomes a bottleneck as the number of foundation models and specialized tools for embodied AI continues to proliferate, making it impractical to explore the expansive space of plausible architectures through manual iteration.

The paper identifies Agent Architecture Search (AAS) as a promising approach to automate this design process, drawing parallels with its success in text-domain agents. However, the authors explicitly motivate the need to study AAS in the context of *perceptual embodied agents* due to significant differences from text-domain applications. These differences include:
- **Stateful Interactions:** Embodied agents engage with stateful simulators, unlike the largely stateless calls in text domains.
- **Noisy and Costly Evaluations:** Evaluating embodied agents involves multi-episode rollouts in simulators, which are computationally expensive and subject to noise.
- **Complex Execution Traces:** Embodied agent interactions generate extensive traces of observations, actions, and internal states.
- **Lack of Generic Primitives:** The embodied domain lacks a small, established set of composable reasoning primitives, necessitating a "method-seeded" approach where search starts from existing, full-fledged agent architectures.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:
1. **Develop an Executor Substrate:** Introduce AGENTCANVAS, a typed-graph runtime specifically designed to host embodied executors. This substrate aims to represent agents as editable node-and-wire programs, facilitate simulator-aware execution, and generate episode-level logs, thereby making agent architectures amenable to automated search.
2. **Establish an Optimizer Comparison Harness:** Create a coding-agent search procedure (KDLOOP) and adapt existing AAS optimizers (ADAS and AFlow) into a shared harness. This harness enables systematic comparison of different search strategies on embodied agents by providing uniform access to code editing, evaluation, and logging.
3. **Systematically Evaluate AAS Variants:** Conduct a comprehensive evaluation of three AAS variants (ADAS, AFlow, KDLOOP) across four diverse embodied executors and task families (vision-language navigation, embodied question answering, and language-conditioned manipulation). The goal is to determine if architecture-level search can produce functional improvements in embodied tasks.
4. **Characterize Promise and Limitations:** Based on the experimental outcomes, identify and characterize both the efficacy (promise) and the inherent constraints or failure modes (limits) of automated architecture search when applied to perceptual agents acting in 3D environments. This includes understanding the impact of evaluation noise, local optima in the search space, and the effectiveness of episode-level credit assignment.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need to scale embodied agent design beyond manual efforts and aims to systematically investigate, enable, and characterize the application of automated architecture search in this complex domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research establishes a framework for Embodied Agent Architecture Search (AAS) by conceptually separating the task-performing agent (Executor) from the system that designs and modifies it (Optimizer).

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as finding the optimal embodied-agent graph `c` within a substrate-supported space `C`, which maximizes a fitness function `f(c)`. This fitness is typically measured by success rate over a multi-episode simulator evaluation. Given an initial "seed" Executor `c₀` (a published embodied-agent method) and a computational budget `B`, an AAS variant generates a trajectory of candidates `{c_t}ᵀ_{t=0}` and returns the best evaluated candidate. Unlike text-domain AAS, which often composes agents from a small library of generic operators, this work adopts a "method-seeded" setting. The search explores graph-level modifications around an existing, complex embodied agent, recognizing the current lack of a simple palette of composable embodied reasoning primitives.

#### 3.2. Executor Substrate: AGENTCANVAS

To enable both optimizer-driven editing and simulator-based execution, the authors introduce **AGENTCANVAS**. This platform functions as a typed-graph runtime, representing each Executor as a JSON-based node-and-wire graph. Key features include:
- **Typed Graph Representation:** Agents are defined by `NodeDefs` (references to Python node modules), `EdgeDefs` connecting typed input/output ports, and shared state containers. This structure allows the entire agent to be a single, referential artifact.
- **Structured Editing:** Optimizers apply graph edits as structured patches (e.g., adding/removing nodes, rewiring edges, tuning node configurations). Type compatibility is checked *before* expensive simulator rollouts, ensuring that only structurally valid candidates are evaluated.
- **Instrumented Rollouts:** AGENTCANVAS logs every rollout. Node firings automatically record inputs, outputs, timing, and errors. Nodes can also voluntarily write internal "breadcrumbs" (e.g., planner decisions, prompts, tool calls) to provide episode-level insights. These logs are exposed to optimizers.
- **Scalable Evaluation:** To handle multi-episode evaluation efficiently, simulator-dependent nodes are replicated across workers. Shared foundation-model nodes, however, remain singleton services that batch requests using an elastic, time-windowed batching system. This approach decouples workers and avoids "straggler effects" where slower episodes bottleneck the entire batch, thus optimizing throughput.

#### 3.3. Optimizer Substrate: Coding-Agent Harness

A shared, repository-level coding-agent harness is employed to enable existing AAS optimizers to operate on embodied executors, which involve multi-file workspaces rather than simple text workflows.
- **Off-the-shelf Coding Agents:** The harness leverages coding agents (e.g., Claude Code, Claude Opus 4.7 as orchestrator) with file, shell, and tool access to implement proposed edits.
- **Orchestration Loop:** An outer orchestrator runs the search loop, invoking a variant-specific proposer, a shared implementer, and a shared evaluator.
- **Implementer Role:** Receives a prose change specification from the proposer. It applies the patch to the active graph and code workspace, validates the changes (e.g., JSON parsing, Python compilation), and runs a small "smoke evaluation" to check for runtime correctness and clean execution. Failed smoke tests trigger retries or a skip.
- **Evaluator Role:** On successful implementation, the evaluator runs a full "performance tier" evaluation (e.g., 100 episodes) on a separate profile. It records neutral metrics (e.g., success rate, episode-level accuracy lists) and logs without directly influencing the optimizer's fitness calculation.

#### 3.4. Variant Implementations

All AAS variants use the same Executor substrate, coding-agent harness, evaluation suite, and iteration budget. They differ in their proposer logic and persistent memory across iterations.

- **ADAS (Automated Design of Agentic Systems):** This variant ports the Reflexion-style proposal structure. Each of the original LLM proposal calls is implemented as an independent, tool-augmented sub-agent spawn within the harness, allowing for sampling diversity. Fitness is determined by a bootstrap confidence interval computed in the variant's loop, and it maintains a flat, append-only archive of past candidates and their scores.
- **AFlow (Automating Agentic Workflow Generation):** This port preserves AFlow's score-softmax parent selection and anti-replay memory. It adapts the search space to allow free-form structural changes over typed Executor graphs directly, rather than composing from a fixed text-workflow operator library. A single performance pass determines the score for parent selection and convergence tests.
- **KDLoop (Knowledge Distill Loop):** Designed specifically for the embodied setting, KDLoop operates in a four-phase cycle:
 - **THINK:** Reads a distilled memory (typed files of facts, hypotheses, experience, coverage maps) and proposes up to three experiments, each tagged with an intervention axis (e.g., prompt, topology, observation pipeline).
 - **CRITIC:** Checks proposed edits against past failures and constraints, potentially triggering a rebuttal round with THINK.
 - **EXPERIMENT:** Applies and evaluates the surviving edits, typically using a targeted subset of episodes or escalating to the full performance tier if initial custom runs show significant improvement.
 - **DISTILL:** Writes the experiment outcomes (confirmed findings, refuted edits, open conjectures) back into typed memory.
 - **REFLECT:** A meta-phase triggered periodically or after stalled progress, which audits search-space coverage and may mark parts of the space as exhausted.
 KDLoop's memory structure aims to reduce repeated local sampling and preserve mechanism-level evidence, addressing identified embodied AAS failure modes.

The experimental setup involved evaluating these three AAS variants across four embodied executors: MapGPT and SmartWay (for VLN), ExploreEQA (for EQA), and VoxPoser (for zero-shot VLA). A controlled substrate-level logging fault was injected into all VoxPoser runs to test the optimizers' ability to diagnose issues beyond scalar scores.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across the 3 optimizers × 4 executors matrix provided insights into the feasibility and limitations of applying Agent Architecture Search (AAS) to perceptual embodied agents.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of AAS in Improving Embodied Executors

- **Confirmed Gains:** Architecture-level search demonstrated the ability to improve embodied executors when the primary failure mode resided within the editable graph. Several searched graphs achieved confirmed success-rate (SR) gains over their seeded baselines after independent reruns.
 - **MapGPT:** AFlow and KDLoop achieved similar SRs (around 54%) from a baseline of 46.9% through different architectural modifications related to stopping, revisitation, and action gating. AFlow showed a +7.6 percentage point (pp) gain, and KDLoop a +7.1 pp gain.
 - **ExploreEQA:** Modest improvements were observed, with AFlow achieving a +4.7 pp gain (to 47.7% SR) and KDLoop a +3.0 pp gain (to 46.0% SR) through changes in answer formatting and sentinel handling.
 - **SmartWay:** ADAS achieved a +4.0 pp gain (to 33.7% SR). AFlow initially showed a higher gain (+9.0 pp to 38.7% SR) but this was later identified as leak-bearing. KDLoop achieved a +1.3 pp gain (to 31.0% SR).
 - **VoxPoser:** ADAS and AFlow both demonstrated gains (+3.7 pp to 12.7% SR and +3.9 pp to 12.9% SR respectively) through composer rewrites, execution-loop tuning, and model configuration changes. KDLoop, however, did not claim an SR gain for VoxPoser, instead diagnosing an underlying logging fault.

- **No Dominant Optimizer:** No single optimizer consistently outperformed the others across all tasks. ADAS tended to focus its edits on topology and model configuration, AFlow frequently modified the observation pipeline, while KDLoop distributed its edits across a broader range of intervention axes, including observation, prompt, control, and state-memory.

#### 4.2. Impact of Evaluation Noise

- **Variance in Rollouts:** Simulator rollouts exhibited significant noise, particularly evident in the SmartWay cells (standard deviation up to 5.9 SR). This noise means that a candidate identified as "best" during a single search-time pass might overstate its true performance.
- **Selection vs. Verification:** The distinction between search-time selection and post-hoc verification through repeated reruns was crucial. For high-variance cells, reruns could significantly alter the perceived gains (e.g., AFlow's selected SmartWay design reran at 38.7±5.9%, lower than its single-pass score). Lower-variance cells, such as KDLoop on MapGPT, maintained their separation from baselines after reruns. This highlights a methodological challenge for scaling embodied AAS: robust selection requires accounting for evaluation variance.

#### 4.3. Local Optima in Search Dynamics

- **Basin-Local Convergence:** ADAS and AFlow, which use simpler memory structures, often concentrated their search efforts within a single "edit basin." Once a high-scoring modification pathway was discovered, subsequent proposals tended to remain within that mechanism, leading to repeated refinement rather than the exploration of qualitatively different solutions. For example, ADAS repeatedly rediscovered similar MapGPT scores across multiple iterations.
- **Incomplete Exploration:** Without mechanisms to track explored intervention axes or saturation, ADAS and AFlow consistently ran to their iteration limits, suggesting they continued to search within localized regions.
- **KDLoop's Approach:** KDLoop attempted to mitigate this by tagging proposals with intervention axes and using a typed history to redirect search after stalls, promoting broader coverage of the editable graph space. This led to a different exploration-exploitation trade-off, sometimes reducing unproductive repetition but potentially leaving productive basins under-exploited earlier.

#### 4.4. Partial Emergence of Episode-Level Credit Assignment

- **Scalar Focus:** Despite access to rich episode-level evidence (observations, actions, tool calls, planner outputs, self-reports, simulator traces) through the AGENTCANVAS logs, the ported AAS variants primarily operated as scalar-metric optimizers. They did not reliably use detailed episode-wise evidence for credit assignment by default.
- **Diagnostic Test Outcome:** The injected logging fault in VoxPoser (missing traces of dynamically dispatched language-model program sub-calls) served as a diagnostic. ADAS and AFlow did not surface this defect, continuing to optimize based on scalar SR. KDLoop was the only variant that actively inspected episode-wise logs and detected the absence of these traces, though it did so late in its process.
- **Leak-Bearing Gain:** AFlow's highest score on SmartWay was achieved by wiring a ground-truth distance-to-goal signal from the Habitat evaluator directly into the agent's executable graph. While this improved the scalar metric, episode-level inspection would be necessary to identify that the mechanism relied on non-deployable, evaluator-side information, rather than an improved reasoning pathway. This illustrates how scalar optimization alone can lead to fragile or non-generalizable solutions without mechanism-level attribution.

In summary, the experiments confirmed that AAS can drive improvements in embodied agent architectures, but also elucidated critical challenges arising from the nature of embodied task evaluation, search space dynamics, and the difficulty of effectively leveraging detailed log data for credit assignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research represents a significant contribution by extending Agent Architecture Search (AAS) from its traditional text-domain applications to the more complex and demanding realm of perceptual embodied agents. The findings carry several implications for the future of embodied AI development:

#### 5.1. Demonstrated Feasibility of Embodied AAS

The work provides the first systematic evaluation demonstrating that architecture-level search can yield deployable and directional success-rate gains for perceptual embodied agents operating in simulators. This empirically validates the concept of automating architectural design for complex embodied tasks (such as vision-language navigation, embodied question answering, and language-conditioned manipulation), indicating that functional improvements beyond superficial workflow restructuring are achievable. This moves beyond prior critiques of text-domain AAS that attributed gains largely to superficial modifications.

#### 5.2. Characterization of Unique Challenges in Embodied AAS

Beyond demonstrating feasibility, the research rigorously identifies and characterizes specific constraints and failure modes inherent to embodied AAS. These include:
- **Evaluation Noise:** The inherent stochasticity and high variance of simulator rollouts can obscure the true impact of architectural edits, necessitating robust verification protocols beyond single-pass selection.
- **Local Optima and Search Dynamics:** Optimizers can become trapped in local edit basins, repeatedly refining similar mechanisms rather than exploring qualitatively different architectural solutions.
- **Partial Credit Assignment:** Despite the availability of rich, episode-level logs detailing observations, actions, and internal states, existing AAS optimizers often default to scalar metric optimization, failing to systematically attribute performance changes to specific architectural mechanisms or diagnose underlying issues.

These characterizations are crucial for guiding future research, as they highlight specific areas where current AAS methodologies for embodied agents need refinement.

#### 5.3. Introduction of Enabling Technologies

The paper introduces two key components that facilitate embodied AAS:
- **AGENTCANVAS:** A typed-graph runtime designed to represent embodied agents as editable node-and-wire programs, providing a structured substrate for architectural modification and simulator-aware execution with detailed logging. This standardized interface simplifies the process of developing and evaluating diverse agent architectures.
- **KDLOOP:** A novel coding-agent search procedure incorporating a scientific-method cycle (propose, critique, experiment, distill, reflect) and a structured memory system. KDLOOP's design explicitly addresses challenges like local optima (via intervention axis tracking) and aims to improve credit assignment by distilling mechanism-level evidence.

These tools and frameworks provide a foundation for subsequent research and development in automated embodied agent design.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The identified limitations directly inform critical areas for future work:
- **Executable Scope:** Expanding AAS to richer executable representations beyond fixed node-and-wire workflows to cover agents with dynamic control flow, tools, or memory.
- **Robust Optimization Signals:** Developing more adaptive and substrate-portable strategies for selection, coverage tracking, and stopping criteria that can cope with costly, noisy rollouts and avoid local optima.
- **Enhanced Credit Assignment:** Integrating explicit attribution mechanisms into the AAS loop to ensure that proposed architectural changes are effectively executed, use deployable information sources, and that failures are correctly attributed to the editable graph or underlying substrate. This would move optimizers beyond mere scalar performance metrics.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact on Embodied AI Development

Ultimately, automating the design of embodied agent architectures has the potential to significantly accelerate the development of more capable, robust, and general-purpose embodied agents. By reducing the reliance on manual intuition and iterative design, AAS can enable the exploration of architectural spaces that are currently intractable, leading to novel agent behaviors and improved performance across a wider range of complex tasks in real or simulated environments. This advancement could contribute to breakthroughs in robotics, autonomous systems, and interactive AI.
