# Compliant Residual DAgger: Improving Real-World Contact-Rich Manipulation with Human Corrections

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Columbia University's REALab developed Compliant Residual DAgger (CR-DAgger), a framework that leverages a compliant human-robot interface to collect precise force-aware corrections for improving robot policies in contact-rich manipulation tasks. This system enables pre-trained policies to achieve significantly higher success rates, such as boosting book flipping by 60% and belt assembly by 50%, with minimal human intervention data.

## Problem

- Existing Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) methods struggle to collect high-quality human corrections in real-world robotics, often leading to non-smooth interventions or failure to capture policy-induced failures.
- Updating pre-trained robot policies for contact-rich tasks is challenging due to the need for efficient learning from small correction datasets and the effective integration of new modalities like force feedback.
- Many real-world tasks require precise force control and adaptation, capabilities that traditional position-focused imitation learning policies frequently lack.

## Method

- A Compliant Intervention Interface uses admittance control to allow humans to apply on-policy delta corrections, smoothly guiding the robot and capturing human intent, including 6D force information.
- A lightweight Compliant Residual Policy is trained to predict small pose and force corrections on top of a frozen base policy, integrating visual, proprioceptive, and force feedback to acquire force-awareness.
- The residual policy is trained with strategies such as prioritizing data immediately after human intervention and including 'no correction' samples, enabling sample-efficient and stable updates from minimal data.

## Results

- CR-DAgger significantly improved base policy success rates on challenging contact-rich tasks, for instance, increasing book flipping success by **60%** and belt assembly by **50%**.
- The force-aware compliant residual policy outperformed position-only baselines by 45-**53%** on tasks requiring force control, demonstrating the critical role of force modality.
- On-policy delta correction data collection resulted in a **45%** higher success rate compared to take-over corrections due to smoother trajectories and a more accurate reflection of human intent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A compliant human-robot interaction interface that allows on-policy delta corrections through kinesthetic teaching substantially improves the quality and informativeness of correction data, leading to more stable policy updates.
> - Learning a force-aware residual policy is crucial for mastering contact-rich tasks, enabling position-based base policies to acquire robust force control capabilities with a limited number of human corrections.
> - Sample-efficient learning (50-100 episodes) of residual corrections proves effective for real-world DAgger, providing a practical and stable alternative to computationally intensive retraining or unstable finetuning of base policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several critical areas in robotics and artificial intelligence: Imitation Learning (IL), Dataset Aggregation (DAgger), Human-in-the-Loop (HIL) systems, Contact-Rich Manipulation, and Compliance Control.

**Imitation Learning** and **DAgger**: Learning from human demonstrations has proven highly effective for robotic tasks. However, initial demonstrations alone often fall short of robust performance, especially in diverse or challenging real-world scenarios. DAgger (Ross et al., 2011) addresses this by iteratively improving a policy: the robot executes, failures are observed, a human corrects, and the policy is updated with this new "correction data." While theoretically sound, practical DAgger implementations in robotics face significant challenges.

**Human-in-the-Loop (HIL) Robotics**: The efficiency and quality of human intervention are paramount in DAgger. Existing HIL methods for correction often involve either offline data collection (observing failures then providing new demonstrations) or human "take-over" of robot control during execution. The paper highlights critical drawbacks of these: offline corrections may not capture policy-induced failure states, while take-over corrections suffer from non-smooth transitions, force discontinuities, and potential deviation from the original policy's state-action distribution. This work seeks to improve the HIL interface to make corrections more precise, natural, and informative.

**Contact-Rich Manipulation**: Many real-world robotic tasks, such as assembly, grasping, and precise insertion, involve intricate physical interactions with the environment. These "contact-rich" tasks require not only accurate position control but also sophisticated force control and adaptation. Traditional imitation learning often focuses on position trajectories, struggling with the dynamic and often visually ambiguous nature of contact. Policies trained purely on position data often fail when unexpected forces arise.

**Compliance Control**: This technique is vital for robust interaction with the environment, allowing a robot to "give way" or exert controlled force in response to external contact. While compliance control has been widely used for safe and robust task execution (e.g., impedance or admittance control), this paper uniquely integrates it into the *data collection* process itself, enabling a novel form of kinesthetic teaching that directly captures human force intent.

**Residual Policies**: A common strategy to improve a pre-trained policy without retraining from scratch is to learn a "residual" policy that predicts small corrections on top of the base policy's actions. Prior work on residual policies has often relied on reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation, which faces sim-to-real transfer challenges, or large amounts of real-world imitation data. This paper aims to make residual policy learning more sample-efficient and capable of incorporating new modalities like force feedback in real-world settings.

**The CR-DAgger Contribution**: This work positions itself as a significant advancement by tackling the limitations of existing DAgger methods in real-world contact-rich manipulation. It introduces a novel framework that bridges the gap between intuitive human correction and effective policy learning, particularly by addressing the critical need for force awareness. By proposing a compliant interface for data collection and a force-aware residual policy, it aims to make DAgger more practical, robust, and generalizable for complex physical interaction tasks, pushing the boundaries of what can be achieved with limited human intervention.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly improve the performance of robot policies on precise, real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks using minimal human correction data, by enhancing both the collection of informative human corrections and the effective updating of policies with this new data.**

The authors identify two key challenges inherent in applying Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) to real-world robotic problems, which directly motivate their proposed solution:

1. **How to collect informative human correction data?**
 - **Motivation**: Traditional methods fall short. Offline demonstrations, where humans observe failures and then recollect new trajectories, often fail to capture the specific policy-induced failure scenarios and can lead to demonstrations that deviate significantly from the robot's typical behavior. Human "take-over" methods, while allowing on-policy intervention, introduce abrupt transitions in control authority and often lack the ability to capture subtle human intent, especially regarding forces. There's a critical need for an interface that allows precise, instantaneous, and smooth human intervention without disrupting the ongoing robot policy, and that can record the full spectrum of human corrective intent, including forces.

2. **How to effectively update the policy with new data?**
 - **Motivation**: Standard policy update strategies have limitations. Retraining a policy from scratch with aggregated data can be computationally expensive and requires access to the original, often vast, training dataset, which may not always be available (e.g., for large pre-trained foundation models). Finetuning a pre-trained policy with only the new, small correction dataset is often unstable due to distribution shifts and sensitivity to data quality. Training a separate residual policy, while promising, typically requires large numbers of samples for Reinforcement Learning or Imitation Learning, often relying on simulation with its inherent sim-to-real challenges. The authors aim to develop an efficient update mechanism that can leverage small correction datasets, remain stable, and importantly, incorporate new sensory modalities (like force) that might be crucial for contact-rich tasks but absent in the base policy.

In essence, the motivation stems from the practical difficulties of translating the theoretical benefits of DAgger into robust real-world robotic systems, particularly for tasks demanding fine motor control and intelligent force interaction. The authors seek a system that is intuitive for human demonstrators, captures rich and precise corrective information, and enables efficient, stable, and modality-aware policy improvements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is **Compliant Residual DAgger (CR-DAgger)**, a system composed of two novel, integrated components: a Compliant Intervention Interface for data collection and a Compliant Residual Policy for policy updating.

#### 3.1. Compliant Intervention Interface

This component addresses the challenge of collecting high-quality, on-policy human correction data.
- **Delta Correction, Not Take-over**: Unlike traditional "take-over" methods, CR-DAgger proposes an on-policy delta correction approach. The robot's base policy continues to execute, while a human demonstrator applies forces to the robot's end-effector (via a handle). This results in "delta actions" that are added on top of the base policy's output. This design allows the human to continuously sense the base policy's intention through haptic feedback and smoothly control the magnitude of their intervention, avoiding abrupt transitions and large deviations from the base policy's behavior.
- **Compliance Control for Intervention**: To enable safe and intuitive force application, the interface leverages a **compliance controller**, specifically **admittance control**. This creates a virtual spring-mass-damper system around the base policy's commanded position ($q_{ref}$). When the human applies force ($F$), the robot's end-effector ($q$) deviates from $q_{ref}$ proportionally to a virtual stiffness, inertia, and damping. This allows the human to "push" the robot into desired positions and feel the resistance. A constant stiffness of ~1000 N/m is used for easy intervention and accurate tracking. The controller is implemented at a high frequency (500 Hz).
 - The compliance control law is given by: $M \ddot{q} = K(q_{ref} - q) - D \dot{q} + F$ (Equation 1).
- **Correction Recording with Force Sensor**: The system includes an ATI 6-D force sensor to directly measure interaction forces (including human correction forces). A single-key keyboard on the handle allows the demonstrator to precisely mark the start and end of their corrections. Both the base policy's original commands, the human's delta corrections, and the force sensor readings are recorded. This captures comprehensive information about the human's intent in both motion and force.
- **Addressing Intention Misinterpretation (Improvements from Conference Version)**: A key enhancement from the conference version addresses a phenomenon where small human corrections could be misinterpreted if the robot's tracking error was larger than the intended delta motion. To mitigate this:
 1. A **velocity tracking term** is added to the compliance control law: $M \ddot{q} = K(q_{ref} - q) + D(\dot{q}_{ref} - \dot{q}) + F$ (Equation 2). This reduces spatial tracking error.
 2. A **look-ahead time ($\Delta t$)** is incorporated when computing the delta correction motion: $q_{delta}[t] = q[t] - q_{ref}[t - \Delta t]$ (Equation 3). This compensates for hardware latency, reducing temporal tracking error. These combined efforts reduced the largest tracking error from 30 mm to below 5 mm.

#### 3.2. Compliant Residual Policy

This component addresses how to efficiently and effectively update the policy with the collected correction data.
- **Residual Policy Formulation**: Instead of retraining the entire base policy or finetuning it, a light-weight **compliant residual policy** is trained to predict corrections on top of the frozen base policy. This residual policy is specifically designed to incorporate the rich force information from the Compliant Intervention Interface.
- **Inputs and Outputs**:
 - **Inputs**: The residual policy takes the same visual and proprioceptive feedback as the base policy (but with a shorter horizon) *plus* an additional **force modality** (the 6D wrench from the force sensor).
 - **Outputs**: It predicts 15-dimensional actions: 9 dimensions for an SE3 delta pose (residual motion relative to the base policy's action) and 6 dimensions for an expected wrench (target forces and torques). These combined poses and target forces are sent to the admittance controller for execution.
 - The residual policy runs at a higher frequency (50 Hz) than the base policy (1 Hz), allowing for more reactive corrective behaviors, crucial for contact tasks.
- **Architecture**: The policy uses the base policy's frozen image encoder, a temporal convolution network to encode force vectors, followed by fully-connected layers to decode actions. This architecture is lightweight (~2MB trainable weights).
- **Advantages**:
 - **Sample-Efficient Learning**: The small network size and focused learning on corrections enable effective training with only 50-100 demonstrations.
 - **New Sensor Modality Integration**: It allows any position-based base policy to become force-aware by simply learning from force-inclusive correction data, without modifying the base policy's architecture.
 - **High-Frequency Inference**: Its lightweight nature permits faster execution, enabling quick reactions to contact events.
- **Training Strategy**:
 - **Ensuring Coverage**: To prevent the residual policy from extrapolating poorly in states without human correction, "no correction" data is included in training, labeled as zero residual actions. Additionally, some trajectories are collected where the human demonstrator continuously holds the handle, marking the entire trajectory as correction, even for small or zero interventions.
 - **Prioritizing Correction Data**: During training, data samples are drawn more frequently from periods immediately after a human intervention begins. This emphasizes learning from critical moments where the base policy failed and human corrections were applied, leading to more reactive and accurate corrections.

The CR-DAgger system, by combining a sophisticated human-robot interaction interface with a specialized, force-aware learning architecture, provides a comprehensive solution for efficient and effective iterative policy refinement in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of CR-DAgger was thoroughly evaluated through extensive real-world experiments on four challenging contact-rich manipulation tasks: Book Flipping, Belt Assembly, Cable Routing, and Gear Insertion. A diffusion policy (Chi et al., 2023) served as the base policy for all tasks.

#### 4.1. Contact-Rich Manipulation Tasks and Base Policy Performance

- **Book Flipping**: Required precise finger insertion and forceful pushing. Base policy success rate: 40%. Common failures: missed insertion, incomplete flipping.
- **Belt Assembly**: Demanded high position and force accuracy for threading a soft belt onto pulleys. Base policy success rate: 15.6% (improved to 43.8% with force input). Common failures: missed slotting, belt slippage.
- **Cable Routing**: Involved guiding a USB cable around clips, challenging due to variations in cable stiffness and appearance. Base policy success rate: 70%. Common failure: missing a clip.
- **Gear Insertion**: Required sub-millimeter accuracy for mating gears, with small-magnitude human corrections. Base policy success rate: 5%. Common failure: misalignment.

#### 4.2. Comparisons and Key Findings

The CR-DAgger method was compared against various baselines across different correction data collection methods and policy update strategies.

- **Finding 1: Compliant Residual Policy Significantly Improves Base Policy Performance.**
 - CR-DAgger, specifically the [Compliant Residual] policy trained with [On-Policy Delta] data, dramatically improved base policy success rates. For example, it boosted book flipping success by 60% and belt assembly by 50% from their initial base policy rates (Fig. 9). This demonstrates its ability to learn effective corrective strategies from limited human data.

- **Finding 2: Force Modality is Crucial for Contact-Rich Tasks.**
 - The [Compliant Residual] policy, which incorporates force input and predicts target forces, outperformed position-only baselines by a large margin (45% higher success on book task, 53% on belt task). This highlights the importance of force awareness. For instance, in book flipping, it learned to apply strong pushing forces to stabilize books, achieving 100% success at that stage, whereas the position-only residual policy dropped to 35%. In belt assembly, it learned delicate height adjustments using contact forces.

- **Finding 3: On-Policy Delta Data Collection Leads to Stable Residual Policies.**
 - Training with [On-Policy Delta] data resulted in a 45% higher success rate compared to [Take-over-Correction] data on the book flipping task. [Take-over-Correction] often led to noisy, unstable motions due to abrupt control transitions. [On-Policy Delta] produced smoother action trajectories that better reflected human intent, introducing less distribution shift from the base policy's behavior (Fig. 10, 11). This smoothness and fidelity to human intent are attributed to the continuous haptic feedback and gentle correction mechanism.

- **Finding 4: Retraining is Stable but Inefficient for Learning Corrections.**
 - Retraining the base policy from scratch with combined original and correction data resulted in stable motions but showed only marginal improvements over the base policy (e.g., a 1.67% *drop* on book task, 18.8% improvement on belt task). This is because the small amount of correction data is diluted by the dominant volume of initial training data, making it hard for the policy to specifically learn corrective behaviors.

- **Finding 5: Finetuning Base Policies is Unstable.**
 - Finetuning the base policy with only correction data performed the worst, often underperforming the base policy itself (30% success rate drop on book task, 15.6% drop on belt task). This instability is attributed to distribution mismatch between the original and correction data, leading to noisy and out-of-distribution actions. While KL-regularization helped stabilize finetuning, overall performance remained inferior.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Training Frequency and Batch Size**: Large batch sizes (e.g., 50 episodes) for DAgger updates were found to be more stable than small batch sizes (e.g., 10 episodes), preventing catastrophic forgetting. The study also revealed that simply increasing the number of batches does not guarantee monotonic performance improvement; for the belt assembly task, two batches yielded better results than three, suggesting potential distribution shift issues with too much sequential correction data.
- **Sampling Strategy During Training**: Denser sampling of data *immediately after* a human intervention starts proved most effective. This strategy focuses learning on the critical moments of error correction. Denser sampling *before* intervention starts (which often contains "negative" data of observed failures) was found to decrease policy success rates.
- **Intention Misinterpretation and Tracking Error**: The proposed fixes (velocity tracking and look-ahead time) successfully addressed the intention misinterpretation problem, particularly evident in the gear insertion task where small-magnitude corrections are common. Before the fixes, the residual policy sometimes learned to move the gear away from the pole; with fixes, it correctly learned to guide it towards the target, significantly improving success rates.

Overall, the results clearly demonstrate that CR-DAgger provides a superior and more practical approach for improving robot manipulation policies, especially for tasks requiring precise contact and force control, by leveraging high-quality human corrections collected through a compliant interface.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Compliant Residual DAgger (CR-DAgger) system represents a significant advancement in real-world robot learning, particularly for contact-rich manipulation. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

#### 5.1. Scientific Contributions

- **Novelty in Human-in-the-Loop Data Collection**: The introduction of the **Compliant Intervention Interface** is a pioneering contribution. By enabling on-policy delta corrections through kinesthetic teaching and compliance control, it offers a fundamentally new way to gather human corrections. This method addresses long-standing issues of non-smooth transitions, force discontinuities, and distribution shifts present in traditional "take-over" or offline correction methods. The ability to capture human intention in both motion and force, combined with a smooth intervention experience, sets a new standard for human-robot interaction in learning.
- **Force-Aware Policy Learning**: The **Compliant Residual Policy** formulation is a crucial innovation. It demonstrates how to effectively integrate a new sensor modality (force feedback) into a learning pipeline for pre-trained policies, without necessitating retraining the entire base model. This allows position-only base policies to gain critical force-awareness, a necessity for robust contact-rich tasks, through minimal correction data.
- **Sample-Efficient and Stable DAgger**: CR-DAgger proves that effective DAgger is possible with very small datasets (50-100 episodes) in real-world scenarios, which is a major bottleneck for many robot learning approaches. Its lightweight residual policy architecture and carefully designed training strategies (e.g., prioritizing correction data, using larger batch sizes) contribute to its stability and efficiency.
- **Practical Guidance for Real-World DAgger**: Beyond the core methods, the paper offers invaluable empirical insights and practical guidance for implementing DAgger, including optimal batch sizes, data sampling strategies, and crucial fixes for subtle issues like "intention misinterpretation" due to robot tracking errors. These detailed findings provide a roadmap for future research and applications.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact and Practical Implications

- **Democratizing Robot Skill Acquisition**: CR-DAgger offers a practical and accessible pathway for researchers and practitioners to enhance robot capabilities for specific, challenging tasks. It lowers the barrier to entry for improving pre-trained policies, making advanced robot manipulation more achievable without requiring vast computational resources or extensive expertise in complex reinforcement learning setups.
- **Bridging the Gap for Generalist Robot Policies**: With the rise of large-scale pre-trained generalist robot policies (e.g., VLAs like RT-X, Octo), a critical challenge is adapting them to specific, high-precision, or contact-rich tasks in novel environments. CR-DAgger provides a powerful mechanism for "specializing" these generalist policies with minimal, targeted human intervention, effectively adding specific contact-rich skills without altering the broad capabilities of the base model.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability**: For industrial applications and service robotics, where robustness to minor environmental variations and precise interaction is key, CR-DAgger offers a method to quickly adapt existing robot programs to new scenarios or unforeseen disturbances. The ability to incorporate force feedback makes robots more resilient to uncertainty and more capable of handling tasks that require subtle physical interaction.
- **Advancing Human-Robot Collaboration**: The compliant interface promotes a more intuitive and symbiotic relationship between human and robot during the learning process. By allowing humans to gently guide and correct, it fosters trust and reduces the cognitive load on the demonstrator, paving the way for more natural and effective human-robot collaboration in task learning.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors thoughtfully discuss several limitations that also point to exciting future research directions:

- **Base/Residual Trade-off**: Determining the optimal balance between improving the base policy and training the residual policy remains an open question, especially given a new challenging task. Developing theoretical guidelines for this resource allocation would be highly beneficial.
- **Incorporating More Data for Extreme Robustness**: While sample-efficient, the current residual policy might still lack extreme robustness under very large variations. Future work could explore how to integrate more data or knowledge (e.g., from world models or dynamics models) to handle novel perturbations more effectively.
- **Policy Architecture Exploration**: While a simple MLP proved sufficient for the current tasks, more expressive policy formulations (e.g., probabilistic MLPs, Flow Matching Transformers) could be beneficial for tasks exhibiting high action multi-modality.
- **Flexible Data Collection Interfaces**: Expanding the compliant intervention concept to more intuitive and less labor-intensive interfaces (e.g., advanced teleoperation systems) or to manipulate higher degrees-of-freedom systems (e.g., bi-manual or dexterous hands) would further broaden the applicability of CR-DAgger.

In conclusion, CR-DAgger makes substantial contributions to the field of robot learning by addressing critical practical challenges in DAgger for real-world, contact-rich manipulation. Its innovative approach to human intervention and policy refinement promises to significantly impact the development of more capable, adaptable, and robust robotic systems.
