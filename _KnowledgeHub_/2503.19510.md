# RoboFlamingo-Plus: Fusion of Depth and RGB Perception with Vision-Language Models for Enhanced Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboFlamingo-Plus integrates depth data with RGB visual inputs into Vision-Language Models for robotic control, significantly improving zero-shot generalization capabilities on the CALVIN benchmark with a 10-20% performance lead over RGB-only methods.

## Problem

- Most Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based robotic control systems rely primarily on RGB visual data, neglecting crucial depth information.
- This reliance limits robustness and stability, particularly when dealing with varying lighting, occlusions, or novel 3D environments.
- Prior VLM frameworks like RoboFlamingo faced challenges in executing long-term tasks and generalizing to unseen environments due to their RGB-only perception.

## Method

- **RoboFlamingo-Plus** integrates pre-processed depth data alongside RGB inputs into the ==RoboFlamingo VLM== architecture for language-conditioned robotic control.
- It utilizes a frozen Vision Transformer (ViT) for encoding both RGB and depth information, followed by a fine-tuned Perceiver Resampler to compress and fuse these multimodal features.
- The solution employs a feature fusion decoder with fine-tuned gated cross-attention layers to align linguistic instructions with the combined visual-depth embeddings, outputting actions via an LSTM and MLP policy head.

## Results

- RoboFlamingo-Plus showed slight performance gains in imitation learning in familiar environments compared to the original RoboFlamingo.
- The model achieved a substantial 10-**20%** performance lead over RoboFlamingo in zero-shot generalization across novel visual-depth environments and enriched language instructions on the CALVIN benchmark.
- Ablation studies revealed that normalizing depth values based on the default dataset's range improved task success rates compared to a broader normalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating depth information significantly enhances a robot's 3D spatial understanding and robustness, especially in novel or dynamic environments.
> - Effectively leveraging large pre-trained VLMs for robotics can be achieved by selectively fine-tuning specific components like the Perceiver Resampler and cross-attention mechanisms, rather than retraining the entire backbone.
> - The normalization strategy for depth data, particularly using statistics from a specific dataset range, is crucial for maintaining the fidelity and utility of spatial cues for the model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into robotic systems represents a rapidly evolving and critical area within artificial intelligence and robotics. The broader research landscape is characterized by a significant shift towards end-to-end learning for robotic control, leveraging the powerful capabilities of VLMs to process complex visual and linguistic information for task execution.

Historically, robotic control often relied on explicit programming or task-specific models. However, the advent of large-scale pre-trained VLMs, such as Flamingo and OpenFlamingo, has opened new avenues for more intuitive and generalized human-robot interaction. These models excel at aligning visual and linguistic information, enabling robots to understand natural language instructions and adapt to diverse visual scenes.

Initial efforts in applying VLMs to robotics explored combining vision and language encoders for robotic manipulation. Subsequent advancements introduced the use of pre-trained large language models (LLMs) like T5 to encode multimodal prompts, as seen in VIMA. A significant trend involved fine-tuning these models on specific robotic datasets, exemplified by HULC and HULC++ using the CALVIN dataset.

More recently, the field has seen the development of large-scale robotic transformer models, such as RT-1 and RT-2, which integrate vision, language, and action (VLA) by leveraging vast real-world manipulation datasets and internet-scale pre-training (e.g., PaLM-E, PaLI-X). While these approaches demonstrate immense potential for low-level robotic control and knowledge transfer from web data, they often come with high computational costs and reliance on extensive, proprietary datasets.

The original RoboFlamingo project emerged as a response to the limitations of these high-cost, data-intensive strategies. By leveraging OpenFlamingo's pre-trained parameters (derived from vast web multimodal data) and fine-tuning them with robotic-specific datasets, RoboFlamingo offered a more cost-effective solution while achieving competitive performance on benchmarks like CALVIN. It specifically enhanced critical components such as cross-attention, the Perceiver Resampler, and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units.

Despite these advancements, a notable gap in the existing VLM-robotics landscape is the underutilization of depth information. Most current robot models primarily rely on RGB visual data for action prediction. This reliance can lead to instability and reduced robustness, particularly in unknown or dynamically changing environments where precise 3D spatial understanding is crucial.

RoboFlamingo-Plus directly addresses this critical gap. It builds upon the successful foundation of RoboFlamingo by meticulously integrating depth data into the VLM framework. This work positions itself as a significant step towards enabling robots to operate with a more comprehensive understanding of their 3D surroundings, enhancing their robustness and task execution capabilities beyond what is achievable with RGB data alone. By demonstrating how depth information can be effectively introduced into existing large models, RoboFlamingo-Plus contributes to making VLM-powered robots more reliable and adaptable in real-world complex scenarios, paving the way for more generalized and robust embodied intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind RoboFlamingo-Plus stems from the persistent challenges in robotic manipulation, specifically concerning the robust fusion of multimodal sensor data and the precise execution of language-guided tasks in complex 3D environments. While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown exceptional promise in aligning visual and linguistic information, their application in robotics has largely been confined to RGB data, leaving the rich potential of depth information largely untapped.

The existing RoboFlamingo framework, while cost-effective and high-performing on the CALVIN benchmark, still faced limitations. Specifically, it exhibited room for improvement in executing long-term language tasks in unknown environments and adapting to diverse environmental configurations (such. as the ABCD-D environment split in CALVIN). These limitations largely arise from the inherent instability of solely relying on RGB information, which can be sensitive to lighting variations, occlusions, and lack precise spatial depth cues.

In response to these challenges, the authors set forth the following key objectives for RoboFlamingo-Plus:

1. **Multimodal Input Adaptation:** The primary objective was to enable the model to seamlessly process both visual (RGB) and depth data simultaneously. This fusion aims to provide the robot with a more profound and accurate understanding of the environment's 3D structure, moving beyond the two-dimensional understanding offered by RGB alone.

2. **Utilizing Pre-trained Resampler Tuning for Feature Fusion:** A core technical objective was to effectively integrate depth features extracted by a Vision Transformer (ViT) with RGB features. This was planned by leveraging and fine-tuning a pre-trained Perceiver Resampler, which is capable of compressing and aligning features from different modalities, ensuring an optimal fusion of RGB and depth information.

3. **Enhanced Feature Integration with Cross-Attention:** The goal was to refine the model's ability to combine linguistic cues with the newly fused visual and depth features. This involves employing sophisticated cross-attention mechanisms to intricately weave together information from all three modalities (language, RGB, and depth), leading to more precise multimodal processing and, consequently, enhancing the model's overall performance in language-guided tasks.

By achieving these objectives, RoboFlamingo-Plus aims to overcome the input instability associated with fluctuations in RGB information, particularly in novel environments. The addition of depth data is intended to bolster the model's robustness and facilitate the execution of complex, long-term language tasks, which are critical for robots operating in dynamic and unstructured real-world settings. Ultimately, the motivation is to advance the state-of-the-art in robotic manipulation by building more capable, depth-aware, and language-guided robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboFlamingo-Plus enhances the existing RoboFlamingo framework by meticulously integrating depth data alongside RGB inputs within a Vision-Language Model (VLM) architecture for language-conditioned robotic control. The core methodology revolves around an imitation learning paradigm, where the robot learns to replicate manipulation strategies from expert demonstration data labeled with linguistic instructions.

**Problem Formulation:** The problem is framed as a goal-conditioned partially observable Markov decision process. The robot's policy $\pi_\theta(a|o,l)$ predicts actions ($a$) based on observations ($o$) and a length-M language instruction ($l$). Observations $o_t$ at timestep $t$ consist of two RGB images ($I_t^{rgb}, G_t^{rgb}$) and two depth images ($I_t^{depth}, G_t^{depth}$), captured from a third-perspective camera and an end-effector camera, respectively. The action $a$ typically includes the desired relative pose of the gripper and its open/close status.

**Architecture (RoboFlamingo-Plus Framework):**
The architecture is composed of a backbone based on Flamingo ($f_\theta$) and a policy head ($p_\theta$).

1. **Vision and Depth Encoder:**
 - **Vision Encoder:** A pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) is used to process the RGB observations ($I_t^{rgb}, G_t^{rgb}$) into a sequence of visual tokens ($X_t^v$). The ViT parameters are frozen during training to prevent catastrophic forgetting, a common issue when fine-tuning large pre-trained models on new datasets.
 - **Depth Encoder:** This is a novel addition. For each depth map, it undergoes a specific pre-processing pipeline before ViT encoding:
 - **Replication:** The single-channel depth map is replicated three times to create a three-channel input, making it compatible with the ViT's expected input dimensions (which are typically designed for RGB images).
 - **Normalization:** Each pixel value ($d_{ij}$) is normalized based on the global maximum ($D_{max}$) and minimum ($D_{min}$) depth values identified across the *entire default dataset*. This normalizes values to a range.
 - **Standardization (Regularization):** The normalized depth values are further standardized using the mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$) calculated across the *default dataset*. This ensures consistent mean and variance, which is crucial for uniform interpretation by the model.
 - **ViT Encoding:** The pre-processed, regularized depth images ($I_t^{depth}, G_t^{depth}$) are then fed into the *same frozen ViT* used for RGB, generating a sequence of depth tokens ($X_t^{de}$).

2. **Perceiver Resampler:**
 - Recognizing that the frozen ViT, trained on web datasets, may not optimally extract robotic-specific features, a Perceiver Resampler is employed.
 - This resampler, which leverages an attention mechanism and learnable parameters, serves to fine-tune and compress the high-dimensional visual ($X_t^v$) and depth ($X_t^{de}$) tokens from the ViT. It reduces the sequence length from $N$ to a smaller $K$, effectively minimizing data while retaining crucial feature information.
 - The paper explores two configurations for the resampler: (1) a single resampler processing both modalities, and (2) separate resamplers for RGB and depth (investigated in ablation studies). The outputs from the resampler(s) for RGB ($X_t^v$) and depth ($X_t^{de}$) are then concatenated to form a unified visual-depth feature set ($X_t^{v-de}$).

3. **Feature Fusion Decoder:**
 - The concatenated visual-depth tokens ($X_t^{v-de}$) are fed into the feature fusion decoder, which is adapted from OpenFlamingo.
 - This decoder, comprising L layers, integrates the language instruction with the encoded vision and depth features. It uses a pre-trained language model (like MPT) for its transformer layers, which remain fixed during training.
 - Crucially, a gated cross-attention layer within each decoder layer is fine-tuned. In this layer, the tokenized language instruction ($X$) acts as the query (Q), while the unified visual-depth tokens ($X_t^{v-de}$) serve as the keys (K) and values (V). This mechanism allows for intricate interaction and alignment between linguistic cues and multimodal visual-depth information. A learnable gate parameter ($\alpha$) is introduced for stability.
 - The output of the decoder, $X_t^L$, is a rich vision-language joint embedding.

4. **Policy Head:**
 - The joint embedding $X_t^L$ from the feature fusion decoder is then transformed into low-level robotic control commands.
 - A max-pooling operation is first applied across the token dimension of $X_t^L$ to obtain an aggregated embedding ($X_t$).
 - This aggregated embedding is then fed into an LSTM network. The LSTM is crucial for processing historical information ($h_{t-1}$) and capturing temporal dynamics essential for long-horizon tasks.
 - Finally, a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) takes the LSTM's output ($h_t$) to predict the 7 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) end-effector pose ($a_t^{pose}$) and the gripper's open/close state ($a_t^{gripper}$).

**Training Objective:**
The model is trained using maximum likelihood objectives for imitation learning. The loss function combines:
- Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss for predicting the continuous end-effector pose ($a_t^{pose}$).
- Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss for classifying the discrete gripper state ($a_t^{gripper}$).
During training, only the parameters of the resampler, the gated cross-attention modules in each decoder layer, and the policy head are fine-tuned, while the rest of the VLM backbone remains frozen.

**Experimental Setup:**
The CALVIN benchmark is used, which provides an open-source testbed for language-conditioned, long-horizon robot manipulation tasks using a Franka Emika Panda robotic arm. The dataset includes diverse tasks and over 2 million trajectories. Experiments were conducted on eight A-800 GPUs, with training durations of 3-5 days, and results were validated on 200 test sequences.

This methodological framework provides a comprehensive approach to integrating depth information into VLMs, aiming to leverage its benefits for enhanced robustness and improved task execution in robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RoboFlamingo-Plus, conducted on the CALVIN benchmark, demonstrates significant advancements over its predecessor, RoboFlamingo, particularly in areas of zero-shot generalization and robustness.

**Imitation Performance:**
When trained and tested within the same environment splits (ABCD to D), RoboFlamingo-Plus shows slight improvements over the original RoboFlamingo (Table 1: RoboFlamingo Avg 3.94 vs Ours Avg 4.02 for ABCD D). This indicates that the RGB modality in the original model was already well-optimized for imitation learning in familiar settings. Crucially, the addition of depth features in RoboFlamingo-Plus *does not compromise* its performance, affirming that depth sensing effectively strengthens the model's robustness in known environments without diminishing its existing imitation learning capabilities.

**Zero-Shot Generalization:**
This is where RoboFlamingo-Plus truly shines, showcasing its enhanced adaptability to novel scenarios:

1. **Vision-Depth Generalization (ABC → D):** When models are trained on environmental splits A, B, and C, and then tested on the distinct visual context of split D, RoboFlamingo-Plus significantly outperforms the baseline. For tasks 1-5, the average success rate for RoboFlamingo was 2.062, while RoboFlamingo-Plus achieved an average of 3.00 (Table 1: ABC D row). This represents a substantial improvement in generalizing to unseen visual and depth environments, highlighting the benefit of depth integration in handling novel spatial configurations.

2. **Language Generalization (Enriched):** To assess robustness to linguistic variations, an "Enriched" setting was created using GPT-4 to generate 50 synonymous instructions for each task. In this challenging scenario, RoboFlamingo-Plus consistently maintained a 10-20% performance lead over RoboFlamingo. The average success rate for RoboFlamingo was 0.83, whereas RoboFlamingo-Plus reached 1.42 (Table 1: ABC D(Enriched) row). This demonstrates that the model's enhanced multimodal understanding, facilitated by depth data, allows it to better interpret and execute tasks even with diverse linguistic inputs.

**Ablation Studies:**
The paper includes ablation studies to dissect the contributions of specific architectural choices:

1. **Separate Resampler (Sep_resampler):** The investigation into using separate resamplers for RGB and depth modalities (instead of a single shared one) aimed to understand if dedicated processing would enhance performance. While the text notes that a single resampler *could adequately* process combined RGB and depth, Figure 3(c) suggests a comparison between a shared resampler and separate ones. Although specific numbers for the "separate" resampler are not listed in Table 1 or directly compared in the text beyond the observation, Figure 3(a) visually confirms the superior performance of "Ours" (RoboFlamingo-Plus, which implicitly uses the single resampler described in the main architecture) over RoboFlamingo across tasks, supporting the overall design choice. The detailed quantitative comparison for separate resamplers would be in Fig 3, but the text does not elaborate on the specific outcome.

2. **Depth Extremes (Normalization Range):** The study on depth value normalization revealed that the range used for normalization significantly impacts task success rates. The experiments showed that normalizing depth values using the mean and variance from the *default dataset* (which has a narrower, more moderate range of depth extremes) resulted in better performance compared to using the wider range from the *comprehensive ABC dataset*. This finding suggests that a too-wide normalization range can reduce the differentiation of depth information, making it harder for the model to discern precise spatial cues. Figure 3(b) visually depicts the impact of this choice on task success rates over epochs, and Figure 5 illustrates the difference in depth sensitivity.

In summary, RoboFlamingo-Plus consistently boosts robotic manipulation performance by 10-20% over prior methods, primarily due to its robust integration of depth information, which enhances both imitation learning in familiar settings and, more critically, zero-shot generalization to new visual environments and varied language instructions. The ablation studies further reinforce the importance of careful depth data processing and fusion within the VLM framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RoboFlamingo-Plus marks a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, primarily through its effective integration of depth information with RGB data within Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This work addresses a critical limitation of previous VLM-based robotic systems that predominantly relied on RGB perception, offering a more comprehensive and robust approach to environmental understanding and task execution.

The significance of RoboFlamingo-Plus can be understood through several key aspects:

1. **Enhanced Environmental Perception:** By successfully fusing depth cues with traditional RGB inputs, RoboFlamingo-Plus moves beyond a two-dimensional understanding of the environment. This enriched perception allows robots to interpret spatial relationships and physical interactions with greater accuracy, leading to a more nuanced understanding of their 3D surroundings. This is paramount for tasks requiring precise grasping, collision avoidance, and navigation in cluttered or unstructured environments.

2. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** A major breakthrough demonstrated by RoboFlamingo-Plus is its enhanced robustness, particularly in unknown or novel environments (zero-shot generalization). RGB-only systems can be susceptible to variations in lighting, texture, and occlusions. The addition of stable depth information provides an invaluable geometric context that mitigates these sensitivities, allowing the robot to perform reliably even in unseen conditions. The 10-20% performance improvement over existing methods, especially in vision-depth and language generalization, underscores this enhanced reliability.

3. **Advancement in Language-Guided Robotics:** The ability of RoboFlamingo-Plus to effectively combine linguistic instructions with fused visual-depth data represents a crucial step towards more intuitive and flexible human-robot interaction. Robots can now better understand and execute complex, long-term language tasks, even when instructions are varied or paraphrased, leading to more versatile and user-friendly robotic systems. This capability is vital for deploying robots in settings where non-expert users need to direct them through natural language.

4. **Optimized VLM Utilization for Robotics:** The methodology highlights an efficient way to leverage powerful pre-trained VLMs by selectively fine-tuning specific components (like the Perceiver Resampler and cross-attention modules) while keeping the large core backbone (ViT, language model) frozen. This approach minimizes the risk of catastrophic forgetting and reduces the computational cost of training, making advanced VLM capabilities more accessible for robotic applications.

5. **Opening New Avenues for Multimodal Robotics:** The success of RoboFlamingo-Plus underscores the pivotal role of multimodal data fusion in developing more capable robotic systems. It paves the way for future research to explore the integration of even more sensory modalities (e.g., tactile, force, audio) with VLMs to further enhance robotic intelligence and dexterity.

**Potential Impact:**
The potential impact of RoboFlamingo-Plus is substantial, spanning both research and practical applications:

- **Accelerated Robotic Deployment:** By enabling robots to operate more reliably and effectively in complex, dynamic, and unknown real-world scenarios, RoboFlamingo-Plus could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robotic systems in various industries, including manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and service robotics.
- **More Intuitive Human-Robot Collaboration:** The improved language understanding and environmental awareness fostered by depth integration can lead to more seamless and natural collaboration between humans and robots, particularly in tasks requiring precise manipulation and shared workspaces.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong foundation for future research in several directions, including exploring different depth sensor modalities, more sophisticated fusion techniques, and crucially, bridging the sim-to-real gap, which is a stated future work direction for the authors. Transferring models trained in simulation to real-world environments robustly remains a significant challenge, and the depth-aware capabilities of RoboFlamingo-Plus could prove instrumental in this endeavor.

In conclusion, RoboFlamingo-Plus signifies a vital leap forward in bringing advanced VLM capabilities closer to practical robotic applications. By comprehensively addressing the limitations of RGB-only perception through intelligent depth integration, it enhances robot robustness, understanding, and performance, charting a clear path toward more intelligent, adaptable, and deployable robotic systems.
