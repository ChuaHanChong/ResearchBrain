# HOLO-MPPI: Multi-Scenario Motion Planning via Hierarchical Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> HOLO-MPPI, a hierarchical motion planning framework, combines offline reinforcement learning of a high-level policy with online Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control for real-time refinement. This approach improves robustness and generalization across diverse scenarios, achieving an overall success rate of 68.0% compared to 57.8% for vanilla MPPI and 56.2% for end-to-end RL in a multi-scenario highway driving benchmark.

## Problem

- Robotic systems need robust motion planning that adapts to diverse real-world scenarios without requiring extensive re-tuning.
- End-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies often lack robustness due to issues like long-horizon credit assignment and sensitivity to distribution shifts, especially when learning in low-level action spaces.
- Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control relies heavily on a high-quality sampling prior, which is difficult to manually design or scale for varied multi-scenario deployments.

## Method

- A hierarchical framework (**HOLO-MPPI**) is proposed, integrating high-level offline policy learning with low-level online MPPI optimization.
- A high-level policy is trained via Deep Q-Networks (DQN) in a compact, abstract action space (e.g., semantic commands) across all scenarios, using a ==learned world model== for rollouts.
- During deployment, the learned high-level policy and world model generate a nominal low-level control sequence as a prior for MPPI, which then refines it in real-time using a comprehensive task cost function.

## Results

- HOLO-MPPI achieved an overall success rate of **68.0%** in a multi-scenario highway driving benchmark, surpassing vanilla MPPI (**57.8%**) and end-to-end SAC (**56.2%**).
- The framework demonstrated improved generalization, maintaining success rates above **50%** in challenging scenarios like Intersection (**68.3%**) and Roundabout (**50.7%**), where end-to-end RL policies significantly degraded (e.g., **22.9%** in Intersection).
- HOLO-MPPI maintained real-time capability with a planning time of 169 ± 28 ms, suitable for 5Hz control, while delivering competitive control smoothness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning high-level, abstract actions allows policies to generalize more robustly across diverse scenarios compared to direct low-level control learning.
> - Using a learned policy to parameterize MPPI's sampling distribution provides a data-driven, scenario-robust prior, overcoming the limitations of handcrafted or scenario-specific priors.
> - Decoupling the RL reward function (for learning) from the MPPI cost function (for online optimization) enables each component to optimize its specific objective more effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic systems deployed in real-world environments require motion planning capabilities that can adapt to a wide range of scenarios without requiring extensive re-tuning for each specific situation. This challenge, known as multi-scenario motion planning, is particularly pronounced due to the variability in environmental layouts, agent dynamics, task objectives, and the potential for unexpected disturbances.

Existing approaches to motion planning in multi-scenario settings exhibit distinct limitations:
- **End-to-End Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: RL offers the potential to learn policies directly from data, enabling generalization across diverse environments. However, end-to-end RL policies often struggle with robustness due to issues such as long-horizon credit assignment, reward misspecification, sensitivity to distribution shifts, and stochastic dynamics. These challenges are amplified when learning directly in low-level action spaces, which can lead to high policy-gradient variance and brittle performance.
- **Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) Control**: MPPI is a sampling-based optimizer effective for online policy refinement, capable of handling nonlinear dynamics and leveraging parallel sampling for real-time operation. A critical dependency for MPPI's performance, however, is the quality of its sampling prior—the distribution from which candidate control sequences are drawn. Manually designing such priors or relying on scenario-specific trajectory libraries is often impractical and does not scale to diverse multi-scenario deployments.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a hierarchical framework that seeks to combine the generalization capabilities of learning-based methods with the real-time robustness of online stochastic optimal control. It positions itself in the landscape of approaches that integrate learning with Model Predictive Control (MPC), specifically by using learned policies to inform the sampling distribution of MPPI, and by operating in a high-level action space to enhance learning robustness across scenarios. The work also contributes to the field of hierarchical control, where a high-level learned component guides a low-level model-based controller, but differentiates itself by using the learned policy to parameterize a sampling distribution rather than to provide a deterministic reference trajectory.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a motion planning framework capable of robustly and safely operating across a diverse spectrum of scenarios in real-time, without requiring per-scenario re-tuning or handcrafted priors.

The motivations behind the HOLO-MPPI framework stem from the identified shortcomings of existing multi-scenario motion planning solutions:
- **Addressing RL's brittleness**: End-to-end RL policies, while promising for generalization, frequently demonstrate instability and poor performance when faced with distribution shifts or complex stochastic interactions, especially when learning in low-level action spaces. The authors aim to mitigate this by decoupling the learning process from direct low-level control, allowing RL to focus on higher-level decision-making.
- **Improving MPPI's scalability**: MPPI's reliance on a well-shaped sampling prior is a major bottleneck for multi-scenario deployment, as manually designing effective priors for varied and complex situations is not scalable. The research is motivated by the need for a data-driven, scenario-robust method to generate these priors automatically.
- **Leveraging hierarchical decomposition**: The core hypothesis is that a complementary decomposition, where learning provides scenario-robust, data-driven priors and online stochastic optimal control provides precise, constraint-aware refinement, can overcome the individual limitations of each approach. This hierarchical structure allows the learning component to focus on broader task intent, which is hypothesized to be more stable across scenarios, while the online optimizer handles the fine-grained, real-time adaptation.
- **Optimizing in abstract action spaces**: The authors hypothesize that learning policies in a high-level, abstract action space (e.g., semantic commands or subgoals) can facilitate more robust and generalizable learning across multiple complex scenarios. This approach allows the policy to capture decisions shared across scenarios, simplifying the learning problem by reducing the need to account for fine-grained dynamics and highly variable local interactions at the policy learning stage.
- **Decoupling RL reward and MPC cost**: The framework is motivated by the understanding that the objectives best suited for RL (often involving reward shaping) may not align with the precise, constraint-aware costs required for MPC. By using the RL policy solely to shape MPPI's sampling distribution, the two components can optimize their respective objectives independently, leading to better overall performance.

In summary, the key objective is to create a robust, generalized, and real-time motion planner for complex multi-scenario environments by hierarchically combining a high-level learned policy that generates scenario-robust priors in an abstract action space with a low-level MPPI optimizer for online refinement, thereby overcoming the limitations of both direct RL and vanilla MPPI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HOLO-MPPI (High-level Offline, Low-level Online MPPI) is a hierarchical motion planning framework designed to address multi-scenario challenges by integrating offline policy learning with online stochastic optimal control.

The methodology involves two main phases:

#### 3.1. High-Level Offline Policy Learning

This phase involves training two core components:
- **High-level Policy ($\pi_\theta$)**: A policy is learned using reinforcement learning to produce abstract, scenario-robust plans. This policy operates in a compact high-level action space ($U_{high}$) designed to capture task intent shared across scenarios. The policy takes the current state ($\mathbf{x}$) and observation ($\mathbf{o}$) as input and outputs a high-level action ($\mathbf{u}_{high}$).
- **World Model ($F_\phi$)**: A learned world model is trained to predict future observations given the current state, observation, and action. This model is crucial for online rollout and evaluating the high-level policy's plans.

The high-level policy is trained via Deep Q-Networks (DQN) across all driving scenarios, using a dense reward function that encourages fast goal-reaching while penalizing collisions, off-road events, lane deviation, and control effort. The policy network architecture consists of:
- A permutation-invariant attention module for vehicle states.
- A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) images.
- A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for goal position.
These embeddings are concatenated and fed into an MLP head to output high-level actions.

The world model is a hybrid design:
- **Ego Kinematics**: Propagated analytically using a kinematic bicycle model.
- **Neighboring Vehicles**: Follow a constant-velocity model in the world frame.
- **BEV Image Prediction**: A learned component predicts the residual correction to the BEV image after an ego-translation shift, using an encoder-decoder architecture with skip connections. The training loss for the image predictor is a weighted pixel-wise reconstruction objective over multi-step rollouts, with higher weights on foreground and edge pixels.

#### 3.2. Low-Level Online Policy Optimization

At each planning step during deployment, HOLO-MPPI refines the high-level plan in the original low-level action space ($U$) using MPPI.
1. **Prior Generation**: The learned high-level policy ($\pi_\theta$) and world model ($F_\phi$) are rolled out over the planning horizon to generate a nominal low-level control sequence ($\tilde{\mathbf{U}}$).
 - First, the high-level policy proposes a sequence of abstract actions.
 - These abstract actions are converted into low-level acceleration and steering commands using a deterministic, known conversion function ($g$).
 - The conversion function maps semantic driving commands (e.g., IDLE, FASTER, SLOWER, LANE LEFT, LANE RIGHT) to specific acceleration and steering values. Longitudinal control uses a proportional controller to track target speeds derived from high-level actions, while lateral control uses a cascaded controller to track target lanes selected based on high-level actions and the local lane graph.
 - The world model is used to predict observations needed for subsequent high-level policy queries during this rollout.
2. **MPPI Refinement**: MPPI then performs real-time stochastic optimization around this learned prior.
 - MPPI samples $K$ candidate control sequences ($\mathbf{U}^k$) from a Gaussian distribution centered around the nominal prior ($\tilde{\mathbf{U}}$).
 - Each sampled sequence is evaluated under a comprehensive task cost function, which includes penalties for velocity deviation, control effort (acceleration and steering), lateral offset from lane centerline, off-road excursions, and collisions. This cost function is distinct from the RL reward and is designed for precise, constraint-aware optimization.
 - The ego vehicle's dynamics are propagated using a kinematic bicycle model. Neighboring vehicles' kinematics are propagated with a constant-velocity model. For MPPI evaluation, only kinematic branches are rolled out, skipping image prediction for speed.
 - Collision checking is performed using the separating axis theorem (SAT) for convex polygons (vehicles are modeled as oriented rectangles).
 - Importance weights are computed for each sampled trajectory based on its cost.
 - The nominal control sequence is updated by an importance-weighted average of the sampled control sequences.
 - Only the first action of the optimized sequence is executed, and the process repeats in a receding-horizon fashion.

#### 3.3. Autonomous Driving Instantiation

The framework is instantiated in a multi-scenario highway driving benchmark built on the Highway-Env simulator. The scenarios include Straight, Merge, Roundabout, and Intersection, with randomized initial positions, goal locations, and traffic behaviors. The planner receives observations including kinematic states of surrounding vehicles, a BEV image, a goal position, and local lane graph information. Low-level actions are defined as longitudinal acceleration and steering angle.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HOLO-MPPI was conducted across 400 episodes (100 per scenario) in a multi-scenario highway-driving benchmark.

#### 4.1. Reinforcement Learning of High-Level Priors

- **Learning Dynamics**: The high-level policy (Prior) exhibited more gradual improvement during training compared to the low-level end-to-end SAC policy, which improved more rapidly in early stages by exploiting easier scenario modes.
- **Generalization vs. Specialization**: SAC showed strong performance on simpler scenarios (Straight and Merge) but demonstrated significantly weaker and unstable performance on complex scenarios (Intersection and Roundabout). In contrast, the high-level policy achieved a higher overall reward and average success rate, with a more balanced and consistent performance profile across all scenario types, including the more challenging ones. This suggests that learning in an abstract action space fosters better generalization.
- **Prior Quality**: The research confirmed that broad competence across scenarios in the learned policy is valuable, as it provides a more robust warm start for MPPI, reducing the likelihood of starting from poor solution modes.

#### 4.2. Overall Performance and Comparison with Baselines

HOLO-MPPI was compared against several baselines:
- **MPPI**: Generic MPPI with a prior based on the previous plan.
- **SAC**: End-to-end RL policy trained via SAC directly in the low-level action space.
- **Prior**: The high-level policy alone, without MPPI refinement.
- **SAC + MPPI**: MPPI with a prior generated by the SAC policy.
- **FHP**: Filtered high-level policy instances (to account for collapsed training runs).

Key quantitative results (Table I):
- **Success Rate**: HOLO-MPPI achieved the highest overall success rate (68.0% ± 7.2) across all scenarios, outperforming vanilla MPPI (57.8%), SAC (56.2% ± 7.6), and even its own prior (Prior: 64.9% ± 8.0).
- **Control Effort and Comfort**: HOLO-MPPI maintained competitive control smoothness (Ctrl Effort: 1.27 ± 1.94, Ctrl Comfort: 2.20 ± 3.74). Vanilla MPPI had the lowest control effort but also the lowest success rate, indicating conservative behavior that failed in complex situations. SAC-based methods resulted in substantially higher control effort and discomfort, despite high success rates in simple scenarios.
- **Scenario-Specific Performance**:
 - On simpler scenarios (Straight, Merge), SAC and SAC + MPPI achieved the highest success rates but with much harsher control.
 - On challenging scenarios (Intersection, Roundabout), SAC and SAC + MPPI performance significantly degraded (e.g., 22.9% for SAC in Intersection), while HOLO-MPPI maintained success rates above 50% (68.3% in Intersection, 50.7% in Roundabout). This highlights HOLO-MPPI's superior generalization and robustness to scenario complexity.

#### 4.3. Impact of High-Level Policy Training and Terminal Costs

- **Prior Maturity**: The benefits of HOLO-MPPI increased with the maturity of the high-level policy. With a partially trained prior (3.5M steps), HOLO-MPPI was competitive but not uniformly dominant. As the prior improved to 7M steps, HOLO-MPPI significantly outperformed all baselines and showed robustness to different terminal cost settings (Table II).

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

- **Trajectory Comparison**: In an intersection scenario (Figure 5), vanilla MPPI and SAC-based variants selected poor turning modes, leading to off-road excursions. The learned high-level policy captured the correct maneuver, which HOLO-MPPI then refined into a smoother, lower-curvature trajectory, staying closer to the lane geometry and reaching the goal more efficiently.
- **World Model Quality**: The learned world model, despite visible drift over longer rollouts, preserved the coarse scene structure adequately to support rollout-based evaluation for nominal prior generation (Figure 6).

#### 4.5. Real-Time Capability

- **Planning Time**: HOLO-MPPI, including prior generation and action conversion, maintained a planning time of 169 ± 28 ms (Table III), which is compatible with 5Hz real-time control, demonstrating practical deployability. End-to-end RL methods were faster but less robust, while vanilla MPPI was also real-time capable but less performant.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HOLO-MPPI framework represents an advancement in multi-scenario motion planning for robotic systems, particularly in complex domains like autonomous driving. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: The core contribution is the demonstration of a method that effectively combines the strengths of learning and model-based control to achieve robust performance across a diverse range of scenarios. By learning a high-level policy in an abstract action space, the system acquires a generalized understanding of task intent, which is less susceptible to the brittleness and distribution shifts that often plague end-to-end reinforcement learning approaches. This improved generalization is critical for deploying robots in varied real-world environments without extensive re-tuning.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency for MPPI**: HOLO-MPPI addresses a fundamental limitation of MPPI by providing a data-driven, scenario-robust sampling prior. This eliminates the need for manual prior design or scenario-specific trajectory libraries, significantly improving MPPI's sample efficiency and allowing it to explore promising regions of the control space more effectively. This makes MPPI a more scalable solution for complex, multi-scenario problems.
- **Decoupled Optimization Objectives**: The framework's design decouples the reward function used for high-level RL from the cost function used for low-level MPPI. This allows each component to be optimized with objectives best suited for its purpose—RL can leverage reward shaping for efficient learning, while MPPI can utilize precise, constraint-aware costs for safety and performance, leading to more robust and reliable control.
- **Practical Real-Time Deployment**: Despite its hierarchical complexity, HOLO-MPPI maintains real-time control capabilities, as demonstrated by its planning time. This aspect is crucial for applications such as autonomous driving, where immediate and continuous decision-making is required.
- **Foundation for Future Hybrid Control Systems**: HOLO-MPPI establishes a principled blueprint for integrating learned decision-making with model-based control. This hierarchical structure can serve as a foundation for developing more sophisticated hybrid control systems, particularly in domains where high-level strategic reasoning needs to be combined with low-level dynamic execution.

Potential future impacts include:
- **Autonomous Driving Advancements**: The framework's success in diverse highway driving scenarios suggests its applicability to more complex urban environments, potentially contributing to safer and more adaptable autonomous vehicles.
- **Broader Robotic Applications**: The hierarchical design is generalizable and could be applied to other robotic domains beyond autonomous driving, such as manipulation, aerial robotics, or legged locomotion, where complex, multi-scenario planning under uncertainty is a challenge.
- **Research in Learning for Control**: HOLO-MPPI provides a concrete example of how learning can be effectively leveraged to enhance model-based control, inspiring further research into differentiable control, adaptive world modeling, and even more sophisticated high-level action spaces.

In conclusion, HOLO-MPPI offers a practical and robust solution for multi-scenario motion planning, advancing the state-of-the-art by judiciously combining the strengths of learned policies and online optimization, with significant implications for the development of autonomous systems.
