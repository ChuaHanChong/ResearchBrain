# VLA-Arena: An Open-Source Framework for Benchmarking Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-Arena introduces an open-source diagnostic benchmark to systematically evaluate Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic control. Evaluations reveal that current VLA models primarily rely on memorization, struggle with out-of-distribution challenges, lack safety integration, and cannot compose skills for long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Existing simulation benchmarks use static task designs, which hinder fine-grained analysis of VLA model performance and precise identification of capability boundaries.
- Prior works often overlook crucial safety constraints, a non-negotiable aspect for real-world robotic deployment, by situating tasks in idealized environments.
- Evaluations typically concentrate on measuring robustness to perceptual or linguistic noise on known tasks, neglecting the more challenging ability of skill extrapolation to structurally complex or novel tasks.

## Method

- A structured task design quantifies difficulty along three orthogonal axes: ==Task Structure== (using ==Constrained BDDL== with dynamic objects and safety constraints), ==Language Command== (principled semantic perturbations), and ==Visual Observation== (cumulative visual perturbations).
- Introduces ==170 distinct tasks==, grouped into ==11 suites== across four dimensions (Safety, Distractor, Extrapolation, Long Horizon), each with multiple difficulty levels (==L0-L2==) to comprehensively cover various aspects of robotic intelligence.
- Provides a complete, open-source framework including an end-to-end toolchain, a formal task definition language (==CBDDL==), and curated datasets (**VLA-Arena-S/M/L**) to foster reproducible research.

## Results

- State-of-the-art **VLA models** show catastrophic performance drops to **near-zero success rates** on **L1 and L2** tasks, indicating memorization over true generalization and an inability to adapt to structurally novel challenges.
- Models are highly susceptible to static distractors, with a **sharp collapse** in performance, and exhibit limited generalization to abstract semantic concepts, novel skill workflows, and entirely new object categories.
- Long-horizon capabilities are absent, with performance collapsing to **near-zero** on simple multi-step **L1** tasks and **uniformly 0%** on more complex **L2** tasks, demonstrating an inability to chain atomic skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models exhibit a strong tendency to overfit to in-distribution tasks, leading to significant performance degradation on near- and far-distribution challenges across all task dimensions.
> - Models demonstrate asymmetric robustness: relatively high but often insensitive robustness to language perturbations, yet generally higher sensitivity to visual perturbations, especially viewpoint shifts and sensor noise.
> - A critical safety-performance trade-off exists where current VLAs largely fail to integrate safety constraints, often exhibiting unsafe behaviors or achieving task success at the expense of high cumulative safety costs in novel scenarios.
