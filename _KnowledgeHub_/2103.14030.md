# Swin Transformer: Hierarchical Vision Transformer using Shifted Windows

> [!summary] Summary
> The Swin Transformer introduces a hierarchical Vision Transformer architecture that employs a novel shifted window-based self-attention mechanism, addressing the quadratic complexity and lack of multi-scale representation in previous Vision Transformers. This design enables the model to achieve state-of-the-art performance across image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision Transformers (ViTs) processed image patches at a single, fixed resolution, lacking multi-scale feature representations crucial for tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation.
- The global self-attention mechanism in standard ViTs incurred quadratic computational complexity with respect to image resolution, making them computationally prohibitive for high-resolution images and dense prediction tasks.

## Method

- The **Swin Transformer** constructs a ==hierarchical feature representation== through ==patch merging== layers that progressively reduce resolution and increase channel dimensions, similar to CNNs.
- A novel ==Shifted Window-based Self-Attention (SW-MSA)== mechanism computes self-attention within non-overlapping windows for ==linear complexity==, with alternating shifted window partitions to enable cross-window connections.
- A learnable ==relative position bias== is incorporated into each attention head to enhance modeling capabilities for spatial relationships.

## Results

- Achieved **87.3% top-1 accuracy** on ImageNet-1K with ImageNet-22K pre-training using Swin-L, surpassing previous ViT models with similar throughput.
- Obtained **58.7 box AP** and **51.1 mask AP** on the COCO test-dev set using Swin-L with HTC++, demonstrating substantial improvements over previous state-of-the-art models.
- Attained **53.5 mIoU** on the ADE20K validation set with Swin-L, outperforming the prior best model (SETR) by **+3.2 mIoU**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The shifted window mechanism is crucial for performance, enabling cross-window connections while maintaining linear computational complexity relative to image size.
> - Learnable relative position bias significantly improves model performance across various tasks (e.g., +1.2% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K), proving superior to absolute position embeddings.
> - The hierarchical design and shifted window approach are generalizable, demonstrating improved performance even when applied to all-MLP architectures like MLP-Mixer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of the Swin Transformer is situated at a pivotal moment in deep learning, where the dominance of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in computer vision is being challenged by the remarkable success of Transformer architectures from Natural Language Processing (NLP).

#### 1.1. Dominance of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)

For over a decade, CNNs have reigned supreme as the standard backbone networks in computer vision. Their hierarchical processing, local receptive fields, and weight sharing properties proved highly effective for tasks ranging from image classification to object detection and semantic segmentation. Landmark architectures like AlexNet, VGG, ResNet, DenseNet, and EfficientNet have progressively pushed performance boundaries, establishing CNNs as the de facto standard for visual feature extraction. The inherent inductive biases of CNNs, such as translation equivariance and locality, have been considered well-suited for processing visual data.

#### 1.2. Rise of Transformers in Natural Language Processing (NLP)

Concurrently, in NLP, the Transformer architecture, introduced by Vaswani et al. in "Attention Is All You Need" (2017), revolutionized sequence modeling. Its core mechanism, self-attention, enabled powerful modeling of long-range dependencies, leading to significant breakthroughs in tasks like machine translation, text generation, and language understanding. The success of large pre-trained Transformer models (e.g., BERT, GPT) transformed the NLP landscape.

#### 1.3. Initial Adaptations of Transformers to Vision (Vision Transformers - ViT)

Inspired by NLP's progress, researchers began exploring the adaptation of Transformers to computer vision. The pioneering Vision Transformer (ViT) by Dosovitskiy et al. (2020) directly applied a standard Transformer to sequences of image patches for image classification. ViT demonstrated that, with sufficient training data (e.g., JFT-300M), Transformers could achieve competitive or even superior performance to CNNs on image classification. Subsequent work like DeiT (2020) improved training strategies, making ViT effective even on smaller datasets like ImageNet-1K.

However, these early vision Transformers, including ViT and DeiT, faced significant limitations when considered as general-purpose backbones for diverse computer vision tasks:
- **Fixed-scale tokens:** Unlike CNNs which naturally produce hierarchical feature maps at various scales, ViT processes image patches at a single, fixed resolution. This makes them less suitable for tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation, where visual entities vary greatly in scale and require multi-scale feature representations.
- **Quadratic computational complexity:** The global self-attention mechanism in standard Transformers computes relationships between every pair of tokens. For an image with `H x W` patches, the complexity is `O((H*W)^2)`. This quadratic dependence on image resolution becomes computationally prohibitive for high-resolution images and dense prediction tasks that require fine-grained analysis.

#### 1.4. Addressing ViT's Limitations: The Niche of Swin Transformer

The Swin Transformer directly addresses these critical limitations of previous Vision Transformers. It aims to bridge the gap between the power of Transformers and the requirements of diverse computer vision tasks, effectively positioning itself as a universal backbone, much like ResNet for CNNs. By introducing a hierarchical architecture and an efficient window-based self-attention mechanism, Swin Transformer seeks to overcome the scale variability and computational complexity hurdles, thereby enabling Transformers to compete with and surpass CNNs across a broad spectrum of vision applications. It fits into the landscape by being a key enabler for the widespread adoption of Transformers as core components in computer vision systems, moving beyond just image classification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Swin Transformer paper is to develop a new vision Transformer architecture that can serve as a **general-purpose backbone for computer vision**, analogous to how Transformers function in NLP or CNNs in vision.

This overarching goal is motivated by two fundamental challenges encountered when directly applying standard Transformer architectures (like ViT) to visual data:

1. **Large Variations in Visual Entity Scale:** Unlike the relatively uniform "word tokens" in language, visual information encompasses objects and features that exist at vastly different scales within an image. Tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation critically rely on the ability to perceive and process information at multiple scales (e.g., detecting both a small bird and a large car in the same image). Existing Vision Transformers, which operate on fixed-scale patches, inherently lack this multi-scale modeling capability, making them unsuitable for dense prediction tasks.

2. **High Resolution of Image Pixels and Quadratic Computational Complexity:** Images typically have a much higher resolution (many more "pixels" or "patches") compared to the number of words in a text sequence. The global self-attention mechanism in standard Transformers, where each token attends to all other tokens, leads to a computational complexity that is **quadratic** with respect to the number of tokens (i.e., image size). For high-resolution images or tasks requiring dense, pixel-level predictions, this quadratic complexity quickly becomes intractable, rendering such models impractical.

To overcome these issues, the authors propose a Transformer architecture designed with two core principles:
- **Hierarchical Representation:** To mirror the multi-scale feature maps produced by CNNs, crucial for various vision tasks.
- **Linear Computational Complexity:** To make the model efficient and scalable for high-resolution images and dense prediction tasks.

By addressing these limitations, the Swin Transformer aims to unlock the full potential of Transformer-based models for a broader range of real-world computer vision applications, solidifying their role as a foundational architecture in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Swin Transformer's methodology revolves around two core innovations: building a hierarchical feature representation and introducing a novel, efficient self-attention mechanism called "Shifted Window-based Multi-head Self-Attention" (SW-MSA).

#### 3.1. Overall Swin Transformer Architecture

The Swin Transformer architecture is designed to produce hierarchical feature maps, similar to traditional CNNs, making it compatible with various downstream vision tasks that rely on such multi-scale representations (e.g., Feature Pyramid Networks for detection, U-Net for segmentation).

1. **Patch Partition:** The input RGB image is first divided into non-overlapping patches. For example, a 224x224 image is divided into 4x4 pixel patches. Each patch is treated as a "token," and its raw RGB pixel values (4x4x3 = 48 dimensions) are flattened.
2. **Linear Embedding:** A linear layer projects the raw patch features to an arbitrary dimension, denoted as `C`. This initial processing forms "Stage 1" of the network, maintaining the original patch resolution (e.g., `H/4 x W/4` tokens).
3. **Hierarchical Design via Patch Merging:** As the network deepens, a "Patch Merging" layer is introduced between Transformer stages. This layer reduces the number of tokens and increases the feature dimension, creating a hierarchical representation:
 - It concatenates the features of 2x2 neighboring patches (effectively downsampling the resolution by 2x).
 - A linear layer is applied to the concatenated 4C-dimensional features, projecting them to `2C` dimensions.
 - This process is repeated across subsequent stages (Stage 2, Stage 3, Stage 4), progressively reducing the spatial resolution (e.g., `H/8 x W/8`, `H/16 x W/16`, `H/32 x W/32`) while increasing the channel dimension. This hierarchical structure enables the model to capture features at various scales.
4. **Swin Transformer Block:** The core building block of the Swin Transformer. It replaces the standard multi-head self-attention (MSA) module found in a typical Transformer block with the proposed Shifted Window-based Multi-head Self-Attention (SW-MSA). Each Swin Transformer block also includes a 2-layer MLP with GELU non-linearity, Layer Normalization (LN) before MSA/MLP, and residual connections after each module.

#### 3.2. Shifted Window-based Self-Attention (SW-MSA)

This is the most critical innovation for efficiency and cross-window connection.

1. **Window-based Self-Attention (W-MSA):** To address the quadratic computational complexity, self-attention is computed **locally within non-overlapping windows**. The image is partitioned into `M x M` non-overlapping windows (e.g., 7x7 patches). Since attention is computed only within these fixed-size windows, the computational complexity becomes linear with respect to the number of image patches (`H*W`), instead of quadratic. This makes the model scalable to high-resolution images.
2. **Shifted Window Partitioning:** While local window-based attention is efficient, it limits connections between different windows, potentially hindering modeling power. To introduce cross-window connections, the Swin Transformer alternates between two window partitioning configurations in successive Swin Transformer blocks:
 - **Regular Window Partitioning (W-MSA):** In one layer, windows are partitioned regularly from the top-left corner.
 - **Shifted Window Partitioning (SW-MSA):** In the next layer, the window partitioning is shifted by `(M/2, M/2)` pixels (e.g., 3.5 patches for M=7). This shifted partitioning creates new windows that cross the boundaries of the windows from the previous layer, thereby facilitating information flow and connections among previously isolated windows.
3. **Efficient Batch Computation for Shifted Configuration:** A naive implementation of shifted windows would result in a variable number of windows and some smaller windows at the image boundaries, leading to computational inefficiency due to padding. The authors propose an efficient batch computation approach using **cyclic-shifting** of the feature map towards the top-left direction. After the shift, a masking mechanism is applied within the self-attention computation to ensure that attention is only computed within the *original* sub-windows, preserving the shifted connections while maintaining a fixed number of batched windows and thus high efficiency.

#### 3.3. Relative Position Bias

To further enhance the modeling capabilities, the Swin Transformer incorporates a **learnable relative position bias** to each attention head. This bias is added to the similarity score (`Q K^T / sqrt(d)`) before the softmax operation. This is in contrast to some other Vision Transformers that use absolute position embeddings or no position encoding. The authors found that relative position bias significantly improves performance across all tasks and is superior to absolute position embedding, which even slightly degrades performance for dense prediction tasks. This suggests that incorporating inductive biases related to spatial relationships is still beneficial for visual modeling.

#### 3.4. Architecture Variants

The paper defines four architecture variants to cater to different computational budgets and performance requirements:
- **Swin-T (Tiny):** Smallest model, comparable to ResNet-50.
- **Swin-S (Small):** Intermediate size, comparable to ResNet-101.
- **Swin-B (Base):** Base model, comparable to ViT-B/DeiT-B.
- **Swin-L (Large):** Largest model.

These variants differ in their channel dimensions (`C`) and the number of Swin Transformer blocks in each stage, allowing for a comprehensive evaluation of the architecture's scalability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Swin Transformer achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across three fundamental computer vision benchmarks, demonstrating its efficacy as a general-purpose backbone.

#### 4.1. Image Classification on ImageNet-1K

- **Comparison with DeiT:** Swin Transformers noticeably surpassed their DeiT counterparts with similar computational complexities. For instance, Swin-T achieved 81.3% top-1 accuracy (at 224x224 input), outperforming DeiT-S (79.8%) by 1.5%. Swin-B also showed significant gains over DeiT-B.
- **Comparison with ConvNets:** Swin Transformers achieved a slightly better speed-accuracy trade-off compared to SOTA ConvNets like RegNet and EfficientNet, despite being a more nascent architecture.
- **ImageNet-22K Pre-training:** Leveraging ImageNet-22K pre-training, Swin-B obtained 86.4% top-1 accuracy, 2.4% higher than ViT-B with similar inference throughput. The larger Swin-L model achieved an impressive 87.3% top-1 accuracy, showcasing strong scalability.

#### 4.2. Object Detection on COCO

- **Comparison to ResNe(X)t:** Swin-T consistently brought significant gains (e.g., +3.4 to +4.2 box AP) over ResNet-50 across various object detection frameworks (Cascade Mask R-CNN, ATSS, RepPoints v2, Sparse RCNN), with slightly higher model size and FLOPs. Swin-B achieved 51.9 box AP and 45.0 mask AP using Cascade Mask R-CNN, a gain of +3.6 box AP and +3.3 mask AP over ResNeXt101-64x4d, which has similar complexity.
- **Comparison to DeiT:** Swin-T significantly outperformed DeiT-S (+2.5 box AP and +2.3 mask AP) while achieving substantially higher inference speed (15.3 FPS vs. 10.4 FPS), highlighting the efficiency benefits of its design.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The best Swin Transformer model (Swin-L with HTC++) achieved 58.7 box AP and 51.1 mask AP on the COCO test-dev set. This represents a substantial improvement over previous state-of-the-art models, including Copy-paste (+2.7 box AP) and DetectoRS (+2.6 mask AP).

#### 4.3. Semantic Segmentation on ADE20K

- **Comparison to DeiT and ConvNets:** Swin-S achieved 49.3 mIoU, which is +5.3 mIoU higher than DeiT-S and +4.4 mIoU higher than ResNet-101.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Swin-L, pre-trained on ImageNet-22K, obtained 53.5 mIoU on the ADE20K validation set, surpassing the previous best model (SETR) by a notable +3.2 mIoU.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Shifted Windows:** This key component proved crucial for performance. Swin-T with shifted windows outperformed its counterpart without shifting by +1.1% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, +2.8 box AP/+2.2 mask AP on COCO, and +2.8 mIoU on ADE20K, confirming the importance of cross-window connections.
- **Relative Position Bias:** The inclusion of relative position bias yielded significant improvements: +1.2% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, +1.3 box AP/+1.1 mask AP on COCO, and +2.3 mIoU on ADE20K, compared to models without position encoding. Interestingly, absolute position embedding slightly harmed performance on dense prediction tasks.
- **Efficiency:** The cyclic-shift implementation for shifted windows was shown to be significantly more hardware efficient than naive padding, resulting in notable speed-ups. The shifted window approach also demonstrated superior efficiency (up to 40.8x faster) compared to sliding window self-attention methods, while maintaining similar accuracy. It was also shown to be slightly faster than Performer, another efficient Transformer architecture, while achieving higher accuracy.
- **Generalizability to MLP-Mixer:** The hierarchical design and shifted window approach were successfully applied to MLP-Mixer architectures, resulting in "Swin-Mixer," which significantly outperformed the original MLP-Mixer (81.3% vs. 76.4% top-1 accuracy) with a slightly smaller computational budget, demonstrating the generalizability of the proposed design principles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Swin Transformer paper represents a significant leap forward in the field of computer vision, establishing Transformers as truly general-purpose backbones, capable of excelling across a wide array of tasks.

1. **Bridging the Gap: Transformers as Universal Vision Backbones:** Swin Transformer successfully addresses the fundamental limitations that hindered earlier Vision Transformers (ViTs) from becoming general-purpose backbones. By incorporating hierarchical feature maps and efficient, linear-complexity self-attention, it overcomes the challenges of scale variability and high-resolution image processing. This makes it a direct competitor and, in many cases, a superior alternative to traditional CNN backbones for diverse applications.
2. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The consistent and substantial improvements over previous state-of-the-art models on leading benchmarks like ImageNet-1K (image classification), COCO (object detection and instance segmentation), and ADE20K (semantic segmentation) clearly demonstrate its effectiveness. The margins of improvement are often significant, highlighting the power of the architecture.
3. **Efficiency and Scalability:** The "shifted window" mechanism is a crucial innovation that enables linear computational complexity with respect to image size, making the Swin Transformer practical for high-resolution images and real-world dense prediction tasks where quadratic complexity is prohibitive. This efficiency also translates to competitive or superior inference speeds compared to other Transformer variants and even highly optimized CNNs.
4. **Novel Attention Mechanism:** The shifted window-based self-attention is a novel and effective way to balance computational efficiency with the need for cross-window information flow, a problem that plagued earlier local attention mechanisms. Its generalizability, even to all-MLP architectures, indicates its broader applicability beyond pure Transformers.
5. **Catalyst for Unified Vision and Language Modeling:** The authors explicitly state their belief that a unified architecture across computer vision and natural language processing could benefit both fields, facilitating joint modeling of visual and textual signals. Swin Transformer's strong performance positions it as a key enabler for this grander vision, encouraging more research into multi-modal AI systems.
6. **Open-Source Contribution:** The public availability of the code and pre-trained models on GitHub has a massive impact, fostering widespread adoption, benchmarking, and further research within the computer vision community. This accelerates the development and deployment of Transformer-based models.

In conclusion, the Swin Transformer has fundamentally altered the landscape of deep learning in computer vision. It has moved Transformers from being specialized image classifiers to versatile, high-performing backbones suitable for the most complex vision tasks, thereby potentially ushering in a new era where Transformer architectures become the dominant paradigm across both language and vision.
