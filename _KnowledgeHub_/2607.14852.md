# Towards Human-like Physical Intelligence: LifelongVision-Language-Action Learning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called LifelongVLA allows robotic manipulators to continually learn new skills while retaining old ones, achieving an 83.2% average success rate and significantly reducing forgetting rates compared to existing methods. This is accomplished through a dual-timescale adaptation and cache-efficient replay, making lifelong Vision-Language-Action learning practical and memory-efficient.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies rely on static, offline training, limiting their adaptability to new tasks, objects, and environments.
- Sequentially acquiring new manipulation skills in VLA models often leads to catastrophic forgetting of previously learned tasks or incurs prohibitive memory and computational costs due to full replay.
- The fundamental plasticity-stability dilemma in continual learning for VLA models, balancing rapid adaptation to new tasks with the retention of old skills, remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- A ==Dual-Timescale LoRA Gating== module decomposes parameter adaptation into short-term (plasticity) and long-term (stability) pathways, dynamically controlled by a shared ==task-aware gate==.
- A ==Cache-Efficient Stochastic Replay== module stores only compact skill-level prefix tokens and recomputes suffix tokens during replay, significantly reducing memory and computational overhead compared to full trajectory storage.
- The framework integrates current task learning with old skill retention through a ==masked new-replay objective==, combining current-task loss, replay loss, and a ==distillation loss== from a detached teacher model.

## Results

- LifelongVLA achieved an **83.2%** average success rate and **11.4%** average forgetting rate across **10** sequential tasks, surpassing the strongest baseline (ER) by **13.0 percentage points** in success rate and reducing forgetting by **8.2 percentage points**.
- The cache-efficient replay reduced memory cost by **91.92 MiB** per task compared to storing full raw trajectories, demonstrating significant memory savings while maintaining performance.
- Real-robot evaluations on an xArm for five sequential tasks demonstrated success rates above **80%** on every task, confirming the framework's practical utility and robustness in real-world scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly disentangling new skill learning from old skill consolidation via dual-timescale adaptation effectively manages the plasticity-stability trade-off, enabling both rapid learning and robust retention.
> - Recomputing suffix tokens during replay using compact cached prefixes dramatically reduces memory and computational overhead for lifelong VLA while providing diverse and fresh retention signals.
> - Weight-level composition of short-term and long-term adaptation pathways allows for dynamic control over plasticity and stability with a single inference pass during deployment, enhancing efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements through Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. These policies bridge high-level semantic reasoning from natural language instructions with low-level motor control, enabling robots to perform complex tasks based on visual observations and verbal commands. Modern VLA systems, such as RT-style models, Octo, and π-series models (e.g., π0, π0.5, π0.7), leverage large robot datasets and pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) to enhance multimodal understanding and action generation. These systems often employ transformer architectures, scaling their capabilities from passive perception to active physical interaction.

However, a predominant characteristic of most existing VLA policies is their reliance on a static, offline training paradigm. They are typically trained once on a fixed set of pre-collected skills and subsequently deployed without further updates. This approach presents a limitation in dynamic, open-world environments where robots are expected to encounter new objects, instructions, scene layouts, and embodiment-specific action distributions continuously. For humans, the ability to sequentially acquire new tasks is a fundamental aspect of intelligence. Mimicking this "lifelong learning" or "continual learning" capability in robots is crucial for their adaptability and autonomy in real-world scenarios.

Introducing new skills sequentially into a pre-trained VLA policy, however, often leads to two primary challenges: catastrophic forgetting and high resource costs. Direct sequential learning can cause previously acquired knowledge and behaviors to be overwritten, leading to a significant degradation in performance on old tasks. An alternative, retaining all past task data and repeatedly retraining the model on the full historical dataset, can mitigate forgetting but incurs prohibitive memory and computational costs as the number of learned tasks increases. Existing continual learning methods for VLA models often rely on replay buffers, architectural expansion, or parameter isolation strategies. While these methods aim to alleviate the problem, they frequently struggle with the fundamental plasticity-stability dilemma. This dilemma refers to the trade-off between a model's capacity to rapidly adapt and learn new tasks (plasticity) and its ability to retain proficiency in previously learned tasks (stability). High plasticity can lead to forgetting, while strong stability can hinder the acquisition of new skills. Furthermore, methods involving full-trajectory replay, common in VLA policies, are particularly resource-intensive, requiring the storage of large, image-rich episodes and repeated re-encoding of old observations, thereby increasing backbone computation and inference latency.

This work positions itself as an exploration into lifelong VLA learning, specifically targeting the unresolved plasticity-stability trade-off and the high computational and memory costs associated with current approaches for large VLA models. It aims to develop an efficient framework that allows robots to continuously learn new manipulation skills while preserving their existing capabilities, thus moving towards more human-like physical intelligence in robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to acquire new manipulation skills continually in open-world environments, similar to human learning, while efficiently addressing the inherent challenges of lifelong learning in the context of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The work is motivated by several detailed challenges:

1. **Addressing the Plasticity-Stability Trade-off:** A core motivation is to overcome the fundamental dilemma in continual learning for VLA models. Existing methods often prioritize either effective learning of current tasks (plasticity) or maintaining high accuracy on previous tasks (stability), but struggle to achieve both simultaneously. The researchers aim to develop an explicit mechanism that can effectively balance this trade-off, allowing for rapid adaptation to new skills without catastrophically forgetting old ones. This is crucial for robots deployed in dynamic settings where new experiences are constantly encountered.

2. **Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting:** Direct sequential learning, where a model is fine-tuned on new tasks without explicit mechanisms to preserve old knowledge, leads to catastrophic forgetting. The objective is to design a system that can absorb incoming manipulation skills while preventing the erosion of previously learned behaviors and knowledge.

3. **Reducing Computational and Memory Overhead for Large VLA Models:** Existing solutions for continual learning, particularly those involving replay, often incur prohibitive memory and computational costs, especially with large VLA backbones. Full-trajectory replay requires storing extensive image-rich episodes and re-encoding old observations, which is resource-intensive. The researchers are motivated to develop a cache-efficient design that significantly reduces the memory footprint and computational overhead associated with replay, making lifelong learning practical for robotic deployment. This includes minimizing storage requirements and reducing the need for repeated backbone computations.

4. **Enabling Low-Cost Robotic Deployment:** The ultimate goal is to facilitate the cost-effective deployment of VLA-powered robots in real-world scenarios. By achieving a better balance between plasticity and stability and developing a cache-efficient learning mechanism, the research aims to reduce the reliance on repeated retraining from scratch, which is often expensive and time-consuming. This directly translates to more practical and scalable robotic systems.

In summary, the key objectives are to introduce lifelong learning capabilities to VLA-based robotic manipulation by proposing a framework that explicitly manages the plasticity-stability trade-off and significantly lowers the memory and computational costs associated with continuous skill acquisition, thereby making human-like sequential learning feasible for robots in open-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, LifelongVLA, is designed to enable lifelong Vision-Language-Action learning for robotic manipulation. It operates on a frozen VLA backbone and introduces two main modules to address the plasticity-stability trade-off and reduce replay overhead: Dual-Timescale LoRA Gating and Cache-Efficient Stochastic Replay.

#### 3.1. Dual-Timescale LoRA Gating

This module aims to disentangle the learning of new skills from the consolidation of old skills by decomposing parameter adaptation into two distinct pathways with different temporal scales. This design enables explicit control over plasticity and stability.

- **Short-term and Long-term LoRA Pathways:** For each adapted linear layer in the VLA backbone, two Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) pathways are defined: a short-term pathway (∆W_sh,t) and a long-term pathway (∆W_lg,t). Both are implemented as low-rank matrices (Aℓt Bℓt).
 - The **short-term pathway** is primarily updated using the current-task denoising loss (L_new) with a higher learning rate (η_sh). This pathway is designed for rapid adaptation and high plasticity to quickly learn newly arriving manipulation skills.
 - The **long-term pathway** is updated with replay loss (L_rep) on cached old-skill samples and a teacher distillation loss (L_dist), using a lower learning rate (η_lg). This pathway is responsible for consolidating previously learned skills, promoting stability and preventing forgetting.
- **Shared Task-Aware Gate:** To dynamically control the contribution of each pathway, a shared gate is introduced. This gate computes a context (c) from frozen prefix features (extracted by E_pre from the VLA input u) before any LoRA adaptations are applied. This prevents a circular dependency where the gate's output influences its input. The gate then predicts a scalar αℓt(c) ∈ for each layer, representing the contribution of the long-term pathway.
- **Weight-Level Composition:** The outputs of the two LoRA pathways are combined directly in the weight space to form the composite LoRA weight (∆_c W_t(c)). This composition is formulated as: ∆_c W_t(c) = (1 - α_t(c))∆W_sh,t + α_t(c)∆W_lg,t. This means that when α_t(c) is close to 0, the short-term pathway dominates, emphasizing plasticity. Conversely, when α_t(c) is close to 1, the long-term pathway dominates, emphasizing stability. A key advantage of this weight-level composition is that it only requires a single forward pass through the adapted VLA model during inference, avoiding the computational overhead of multiple passes.

#### 3.2. Cache-Efficient Stochastic Replay

This module addresses the memory and computational costs associated with traditional full-trajectory replay, which is particularly challenging for large VLA models.

- **Skill-Level Prefix Cache:** Instead of storing entire image-rich episodes, language tokens, or detailed diffusion information, LifelongVLA maintains a compact cache. For each new task Tt, a small subset of samples R_t is randomly selected. For each selected sample, the replay buffer stores only the stop-gradient prefix token (h_pre) and compact state-action supervision (s, y). This significantly reduces storage and I/O overhead compared to storing raw images or full trajectories.
- **Stochastic Replay with Recomputed Suffix:** During replay, for a sample (h_pre, s, y) retrieved from the cache, the diffusion variables (τ' and ε') are freshly re-sampled. A noised action (y_rep) is constructed based on these new diffusion variables. The crucial aspect is that the suffix token (h_suf,rep) is recomputed by the *current* model (b_Θ_t(c_rep)) using the cached state (s), the re-sampled noised action (y_rep), and the new diffusion variables (τ'). This approach offers several benefits:
 - **Diversity:** Re-sampling diffusion variables for each replay step allows a single cached prefix to generate multiple stochastic replay instances, providing diverse retention signals.
 - **Freshness:** Recomputing the suffix token with the current model ensures that the representations are aligned with the model's current state, preventing "staleness" that could occur if fixed suffix features were cached.
- **Masked New-Replay Objective:** To integrate current task learning with old skill retention, current and replay samples are combined and shuffled into a single mini-batch. A replay mask differentiates between current and replay samples. The final objective function combines three losses:
 - **Current-task loss (L_new):** Calculated for current task samples.
 - **Replay loss (L_rep):** Calculated for replay samples, ensuring retention of old skills.
 - **Distillation loss (L_dist):** For replay samples, a detached model snapshot from the previous task (F_t-1) acts as a teacher to provide stable old-skill predictions, minimizing the difference between the current model's prediction and the teacher's prediction for old tasks.
 - Separate normalization of L_new and L_rep prevents one from dominating the objective due to batch size differences, maintaining a balanced retention signal even with a small replay budget. The total loss is L_t = L_new + λL_rep + βL_dist, where λ and β control the weighting of replay and distillation components.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of LifelongVLA was conducted on a sequence of 10 manipulation tasks selected from the LIBERO benchmark. The policy was trained incrementally, with access only to the current task data and a bounded replay cache from previous tasks. Evaluation measured performance on all tasks seen up to that point. A frozen PaliGemma backbone (Gemma 2B) and a Gemma 300M continuous action decoder were used.

#### 4.1. Main Comparison and Process-Level Analysis

LifelongVLA was compared against several representative lifelong learning and VLA adaptation baselines:
- **Sequential Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Direct fine-tuning on new tasks.
- **Learning without Forgetting (LwF-LoRA):** Uses distillation with LoRA.
- **Experience Replay (ER):** Employs episodic replay.
- **Info-VLA:** Uses information-guided regularization.
- **AtomicVLA:** Utilizes skill-level expert specialization.
- **Joint Training:** An upper bound representing training on all task data simultaneously.

The key performance metrics were:
- **Average Success Rate (SR):** Mean success rate across all seen tasks after completing all tasks.
- **Average Forgetting Rate (FOR):** Quantifies the degradation in performance on previously learned tasks.
- **Process-level Incremental Accuracy (ACC):** Average success rate on all tasks learned up to a given incremental step.

#### 4.2. Results Summary

- **Overall Performance:** LifelongVLA achieved an average success rate of 83.2% and an average forgetting rate of 11.4%. This performance surpassed the strongest baseline, ER, by 13.0 percentage points in SR and reduced FOR by 8.2 percentage points.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - SFT and LwF-LoRA exhibited low average SR (7.8%) and severe early-task forgetting, indicating that simple sequential fine-tuning or distillation alone is insufficient for this complex setting.
 - Info-VLA and AtomicVLA showed some task-specific improvements but had an average SR of 28.6%, suggesting that their regularization or expert specialization approaches did not fully resolve the plasticity-stability trade-off.
- **Task-Level Performance:** LifelongVLA demonstrated strong performance across most individual skills, achieving 96% on task 5, 98% on task 9, and 96% on task 10. Notably, task 9 achieved a FOR of 0, indicating no forgetting after sequential learning.
- **Process-Level Stability:** The incremental accuracy curve for LifelongVLA remained stable as more tasks were introduced, and its position in the stability-plasticity plot (Figure 3) was closer to the ideal upper-left region, indicating an effective balance.

These results indicate that the combination of dual-timescale adaptation and stochastic prefix replay in LifelongVLA supports efficient skill expansion while effectively mitigating catastrophic forgetting throughout the continual learning process.

#### 4.3. Complexity and Ablation

An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of the two core modules and to analyze the framework's complexity.
- **Cache-Efficient Stochastic Replay (Memory Cost):**
 - "Ours (raw data replay)," which stores full trajectories, had a memory cost of 167.62 MiB/task.
 - "Ours (latent replay + dual LoRA)," which uses the proposed cache-efficient method, reduced memory cost to 95.70 MiB/task, while maintaining comparable SR (83.2%) and lower FOR (11.4%). This demonstrates the effectiveness of compact cached prefixes with stochastic suffix recomputation in retaining old skills without full trajectory storage.
- **Dual-Timescale LoRA Gating (Plasticity-Stability Trade-off):**
 - "Ours (single LoRA)," which replaced dual LoRA with a single adapter, achieved an average SR of 74.6% and FOR of 21.78%.
 - "Ours (latent replay + dual LoRA)" significantly improved these metrics to 83.2% SR and 11.4% FOR, confirming that separating short-term plastic adaptation from long-term stable consolidation is beneficial.
- **Trainable Parameters:** While dual LoRA increased trainable parameters to 99.97M (compared to 49.99M for single LoRA), LifelongVLA remained considerably more compact than AtomicVLA, which required 226.49M trainable parameters and 452.98 MiB/task memory.
- **Efficiency Balance:** Compared to LwF-LoRA and Info-VLA, LifelongVLA maintained comparable or lower memory usage while achieving substantially higher SR and lower FOR. This indicates a more effective balance between computational efficiency and continual-learning performance.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Evaluation

LifelongVLA was further evaluated on a real xArm robot for a stream of five sequential tasks.
- **Real-World Performance:** After learning all five tasks, LifelongVLA achieved success rates above 80% on every task. This suggests that the proposed retention mechanisms are robust and effective beyond simulated environments.
- **Qualitative Results:** Qualitative rollouts demonstrated successful start-to-final-state executions in both real-world and simulator settings, confirming the model's ability to perform language-conditioned manipulation based on visual observations without task identity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical challenges in enabling lifelong learning for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The proposed LifelongVLA framework offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Advancing Human-like Physical Intelligence:** The ability to continually acquire new skills without forgetting old ones is fundamental to human intelligence. By providing a framework that allows robots to sequentially learn manipulation tasks in open-world settings, this research moves closer to endowing robots with human-like adaptability and intelligence. This is crucial for robots operating in dynamic, unstructured environments where they must constantly adapt to new objects, tasks, and scenarios.

2. **Addressing the Plasticity-Stability Trade-off:** The core innovation of the Dual-Timescale LoRA Gating module provides an explicit and effective mechanism to manage the long-standing plasticity-stability dilemma in continual learning. By decoupling short-term, plastic adaptation for new skills from long-term, stable consolidation of old skills, the framework offers a principled approach to achieve both rapid learning and robust retention. This design, particularly the weight-level gate, allows for dynamic control of this trade-off in a single forward pass, enhancing both performance and efficiency.

3. **Enhancing Practicality and Deployment Efficiency:** The Cache-Efficient Stochastic Replay module significantly reduces the memory and computational overhead typically associated with replay-based continual learning, especially for large VLA models. By storing only compact prefix tokens and dynamically recomputing suffix tokens, the method drastically cuts down storage requirements (e.g., 91.92 MiB per task reduction compared to full replay). This efficiency makes lifelong learning more feasible for resource-constrained robotic platforms and enables prolonged deployment without the prohibitive costs of storing massive datasets or frequent, expensive retraining.

4. **Improved Performance in Lifelong Robotic Manipulation:** The experimental results demonstrate that LifelongVLA consistently outperforms existing baselines in terms of both new-skill acquisition (higher average success rate) and retention of old skills (lower average forgetting rate). This empirical superiority validates the effectiveness of the proposed mechanisms, showcasing that efficient skill expansion and robust behavior retention can be achieved concurrently.

5. **Robustness in Real-World Scenarios:** The successful evaluation on a real xArm robot stream, achieving over 80% success rates on all sequential tasks, underscores the method's practical utility and robustness beyond simulated environments. This real-world validation is critical for demonstrating the viability of the approach for actual robotic applications.

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge limitations that also suggest avenues for future research:
- **Task Scale and Diversity:** The current evaluation is limited to a relatively constrained set of tasks. Future work could involve longer and more heterogeneous task sequences, more diverse object categories, richer scene layouts, and randomized task orders to assess long-term stability and robustness under more challenging conditions.
- **Language Robustness:** The current system relies on clear language instructions. Real-world users may provide ambiguous, conversational, or context-dependent commands. Future research could explore incorporating paraphrased instructions, conversational commands, and mechanisms like language augmentation or instruction rewriting to improve the model's ability to handle diverse user expressions and contextual dependencies in lifelong VLA learning.

In summary, this research provides a significant step towards enabling robots to learn continuously and adaptively in complex environments, mitigating key bottlenecks related to forgetting and resource consumption, and thus paving the way for more autonomous and intelligent robotic systems.
