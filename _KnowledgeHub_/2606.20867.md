# FOCA: Future-Oriented Conditioning for Data-Efficient Vision-Language-Action Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> FOCA is a plug-in conditioning framework for diffusion-based VLA policies that predicts task-grounded future interaction embeddings (explicit) and aligns current representations to future goal observations via contrastive learning (implicit), both in latent space, enabling action-free co-training on synthetic video. It reaches 95.7% success with only 20 demonstrations on LIBERO, plus 7-12% RoboCasa and up to 26% real-robot gains.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art VLA models are evaluated with far more than a handful of trajectories, so their reliability under severely limited demonstration budgets (10-30 shots) is largely untested.
- Standard behavioral-cloning fine-tuning treats each demonstration as independent observation-action pairs, discarding the rich long-horizon structure visible in future frames.
- Prior future-prediction approaches for VLA reconstruct pixel-level future frames, which is computationally redundant and hard to optimize in low-data regimes.

## Method

- **Explicit future latent prediction**: learnable interaction tokens are decoded by a small transformer head to predict future gripper-object interaction region embeddings, grounded via an off-the-shelf detector's bounding boxes.
- **Implicit future alignment**: task-grounded implicit tokens are contrastively aligned (==InfoNCE==) to geometrically-sampled future goal-frame embeddings against task-mismatched negatives, framed as ==goal-conditioned discounted occupancy estimation==.
- **Structured token-isolation attention mask** decouples explicit and implicit token streams while co-training three losses jointly, and the implicit objective alone supports action-free training on ==DreamGen==-generated synthetic rollouts.

## Results

- FOCA reaches **96.6%** average LIBERO success at full data and **85.3%** at 10% demos versus **77.6%** for the pi-0 baseline, beating PEFT baselines LoRA, DoRA, and Control-VLA which plateau near **78%**.
- Combining FOCA's implicit objective with DreamGen synthetic rollouts hits **95.7%** success using only **40%** real demonstrations, surpassing pi-0 trained on 100% of the data (**94.6%**).
- On RoboCasa FOCA improves pi-0 by **4.4-12%** and GR00T-N1.5 by **7.2-10.4%** across five tasks, and on real Aloha and humanoid robots delivers up to **26** percentage points absolute gain (e.g. Open Bag **20%** to **45%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Few-shot VLA adaptation is bottlenecked more by the learning signal extracted per demonstration than by raw model capacity or parameter-efficient tuning tricks.
> - Latent, object-centric future prediction restricted to interaction regions generalizes better than dense pixel-level or full-image future prediction, especially under scarce data.
> - Framing future alignment as goal-conditioned discounted occupancy lets an auxiliary objective be action-free, so it can absorb supervision cheaply from generative video world models rather than only real trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control has advanced significantly with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, which extend large vision-language models, enable general-purpose robotic control by generating actions conditioned on visual observations and language instructions. Current VLA systems leverage large-scale multimodal pretraining, transferring knowledge from web-based sources to robotics, and developing open generalist robot policies capable of impressive generalization across diverse manipulation settings and platforms. Examples include autoregressive VLAs like RT-1/2 and diffusion-based VLAs such as π0 and Octo.

Despite these advancements, a recognized limitation within the broader landscape is the data efficiency of VLA models during task adaptation, particularly in few-shot imitation learning scenarios. Collecting task-specific robot demonstrations is resource-intensive, and real-world deployments frequently encounter distribution shifts (e.g., new objects, viewpoints) that make extensive demonstration budgets impractical. Existing evaluations often involve substantial demonstration quantities, leaving the few-shot adaptability of pretrained VLA representations largely unaddressed. Prior adaptation strategies often treat demonstrations as independent observation-action pairs, underutilizing the rich long-horizon structural information available in future observations.

This work contributes to the landscape by addressing this data efficiency gap. Instead of proposing a new VLA architecture, FOCA introduces a framework that augments existing VLA adaptation strategies by incorporating future-oriented learning signals during fine-tuning. This approach seeks to improve data efficiency by extracting more learning signal from each demonstration, including leveraging action-free synthetic videos, and thereby enhancing few-shot transfer capabilities across tasks and robot embodiments. It diverges from prior work on pixel-level future frame prediction by operating in latent space and focusing on task-grounded interaction regions, aiming for computational efficiency and relevance to control. It also builds upon contrastive learning in robotics but adapts it for large, pretrained VLA models by using compact, representative tokens.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the data efficiency of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in few-shot imitation learning settings. The authors aim to improve the performance of VLA models when adapting to new tasks with limited demonstrations, specifically in the range of 10 to 30 trajectories.

The motivation for this objective stems from several practical challenges in deploying VLA models for robotic control:
1. **Costly Data Collection:** Gathering task-specific robot demonstrations is a time-consuming and expensive process. This cost makes it impractical to collect large datasets for every new task or variation encountered in real-world applications.
2. **Limited Few-Shot Performance:** The authors identify a significant weakness in current state-of-the-art VLA models: their performance degrades sharply as the number of available demonstrations for adaptation decreases. A systematic stress test revealed substantial performance degradation when demonstration budgets are severely limited, indicating that existing adaptation strategies do not effectively utilize scarce data.
3. **Underutilized Information in Demonstrations:** Standard behavioral cloning approaches typically use one supervised action target per time step. However, each demonstration inherently contains rich long-horizon information in the form of future observations, which illustrate the successful progression of a task. Existing methods largely fail to explicitly leverage this temporal structure.
4. **Need for Long-Horizon Reasoning:** Effective robotic manipulation, especially for complex tasks, requires reasoning beyond immediate actions, anticipating future states, and understanding the overall task progression. Incorporating long-horizon supervision can facilitate this.
5. **Potential for Action-Free Learning:** The ability to leverage synthetic videos from world models for adaptation, even without explicit action labels, could significantly expand the available training data and reduce reliance on costly real-world demonstrations.

To address these motivations, the authors propose FOCA (Future-Oriented Conditioning for Data-Efficient VLA Adaptation). The core idea is to augment VLA adaptation by explicitly exploiting future observations to enrich learning signals, thereby shaping representations and improving long-horizon decision-making, particularly in data-scarce environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FOCA is introduced as a plug-and-play framework designed to augment diffusion-based VLA policies for data-efficient few-shot imitation learning. It integrates two distinct future-oriented conditioning objectives: explicit future latent prediction and implicit future alignment, both operating within the latent embedding space of a VLA model.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

FOCA augments an existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) $F_\theta(\cdot)$ and a diffusion-based action policy $A_\phi(\cdot)$. Given an instruction $l$ and observation $o_t$, the VLM typically produces a hidden representation $h_t = F_\theta(l, o_t)$. FOCA introduces two auxiliary token sets: explicit future tokens $r^\text{exp}_t$ and implicit future tokens $r^\text{imp}_t$. These tokens are concatenated with $(l, o_t)$ and jointly processed by the VLM, yielding $h_t, \tilde{r}^\text{exp}_t, \tilde{r}^\text{imp}_t = F_\theta(l, o_t, r^\text{exp}_t, r^\text{imp}_t)$. Lightweight heads ($P^\text{exp}_\omega(\cdot)$ and $P^\text{imp}_\psi(\cdot)$) then map these token outputs to specific prediction spaces. The action generation policy $A_\phi(\cdot)$ is conditioned on the robot state $s_t$, a noised action chunk $\tilde{a}_{t,\rho}$, and the combined future-aware representations $\{h_t, \tilde{r}^\text{exp}_t, \tilde{r}^\text{imp}_t\}$.

#### 3.2. Explicit Future Latent Prediction

This module aims to forecast future representations of object-centric interaction regions.
- **Object-Centric Region Construction:** For a future observation $I^i_{t^+}$ at time $t^+ > t$ in the same episode, an off-the-shelf grounding model $G(\cdot)$ localizes task-relevant objects and the robot gripper based on the instruction $l$. Bounding boxes for these elements are obtained, and a minimal enclosing box $B^i_{t^+}$ is defined as the single object-centric interaction region. This region captures the spatial extent of robot-object interactions relevant to the task.
- **Latent-space Future Interaction Prediction:** At each timestep $t$, a set of learnable explicit prediction tokens $r^\text{exp}_t$ is initialized. These tokens, along with visual tokens from $o_t$ and language tokens from $l$, are fed into the VLM. The VLM's output tokens $\tilde{r}^\text{exp}_t$ are then mapped by a lightweight predictor $P^\text{exp}_\omega(\cdot)$ (e.g., a small Transformer) to produce a predicted future region embedding $\hat{y}^i_{t^+}$ for view $i$ at time $t^+$. Positional encoding of the bounding box $B^i_{t^+}$ is incorporated.
- **Prediction Target and Loss:** The prediction target $\tilde{y}^i_{t^+}$ is formed by extracting object-centric patch features from $I^i_{t^+}$ restricted to $B^i_{t^+}$ using a frozen vision encoder $V(\cdot)$ within the VLM. This ensures the target captures only interaction-relevant content, avoiding dense scene reconstruction. The loss $L_\text{exp} = E_{t,t^+,i}[\|\hat{y}^i_{t^+} - \tilde{y}^i_{t^+}\|^2_2]$ minimizes the squared error between the predicted embedding and the target.

#### 3.3. Implicit Future Alignment

This module provides a complementary signal by capturing the long-horizon task semantics and goal reachability at a representation level.
- **Token Initialization and Projection:** Implicit tokens $r^\text{imp}_t$ are initialized by pooling features from the frozen vision encoder $V(\cdot)$ across multiple views of $o_t$, followed by a shared learnable projection layer. The VLM processes these tokens, and their outputs $\tilde{r}^\text{imp}_t$ are mapped to a contrastive embedding space via a projection head $P^\text{imp}_\psi(\cdot)$, yielding $\hat{z}_t^i$.
- **Future Goal Embeddings and Negatives:** Future goal embeddings $y^i_{t^+}$ are extracted by pooling global vision embeddings from $I^i_{t^+}$ restricted to the object-centric region $B^i_{t^+}$. Negative samples $N_{\neg\text{task}}$ are constructed from other mini-batch episodes with different task descriptions, ensuring they are semantically similar but task-mismatched.
- **InfoNCE Loss:** An InfoNCE-style loss $L_\text{imp}$ is optimized to align the current task-grounded interaction tokens with future goal observations while contrasting them against negative samples. The future offset $k = t^+ - t$ is sampled from a truncated geometric distribution. This objective is theoretically interpreted as estimating goal-conditioned discounted occupancies, a value-like notion of reachability.

#### 3.4. Co-training with Denoising Diffusion Transformer

- **Structured Token Isolation:** To ensure functional disentanglement, direct information exchange between $r^\text{exp}_t$ and $r^\text{imp}_t$ is blocked within the VLM's attention layers, while both can attend to shared vision and language tokens.
- **Overall Training Objective:** The total training loss $L_\text{total} = L_\text{fm} + L_\text{exp} + L_\text{imp}$ combines the flow-matching loss $L_\text{fm}$ (for action supervision) with the explicit and implicit future-conditioning losses. For action-free videos (e.g., synthetic rollouts), $L_\text{fm}$ is omitted, and only $L_\text{exp}$ and $L_\text{imp}$ are optimized.

#### 3.5. Action-Free Learning from Synthetic Rollouts

A key advantage is the implicit objective's ability to leverage synthetic videos generated by video world models (e.g., DreamGen) without requiring pseudo-action labels. This involves a two-stage training strategy:
1. Fine-tuning a video world model on few-shot demonstrations, then generating prompt-conditioned synthetic rollouts to optimize $L_\text{imp}$.
2. Co-training on real robot demonstrations by optimizing the full objective $L_\text{total}$. This curriculum allows FOCA to absorb future-structured knowledge from action-free video while retaining precise control through explicit prediction and action supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate FOCA's effectiveness across various settings, including data efficiency, generalization capabilities, and its ability to leverage synthetic data.

**Q1: Data efficiency of FOCA for VLA model adaptation.**
- **Performance under Few-Shot Imitation:** FOCA consistently improved data efficiency across varying demonstration budgets on the LIBERO benchmark. With 40% of the demonstrations, FOCA achieved 94.0% success, comparable to π0 trained with 100% data (94.6%). In the 10% data regime, FOCA increased success from 77.6% (baseline) to 85.3%, significantly outperforming parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods (which plateaued around 78%).
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Under full supervision, FOCA also achieved a success rate of 96.6%, establishing a new state-of-the-art on LIBERO compared to a wide range of existing generalized robot policies and VLA systems. This suggests that the quality of supervision, rather than just model capacity, is a bottleneck in few-shot VLA adaptation.

**Q2: Leveraging synthetic videos without action supervision.**
- **Comparison with Pseudo-Action Methods:** Inverse Generative Modeling (IGM), which infers pseudo-actions from synthetic videos, did not consistently improve performance over π0 and degraded under limited supervision.
- **FOCA with Synthetic Data:** FOCA, specifically its implicit alignment objective, demonstrated robustness and consistent performance gains across all data regimes when incorporating synthetic videos from DreamGen. Even with implicit alignment alone, FOCA outperformed both π0 and IGM. When pre-trained with DreamGen-generated videos, FOCA + DreamGen (95.7% at 40% data) surpassed π0 trained with 100% real data (94.6%). This indicates that FOCA effectively leverages synthetic videos as representation-level supervision, remaining robust even with imperfect synthetic rollouts, unlike brittle pseudo-action inference.

**Q3: Generalization across different VLA architectures and real robotic embodiments.**
- **VLA Architecture Generalization:** FOCA consistently improved performance on the ROBOCASA benchmark across two architecturally distinct VLA models: π0 and GR00T-N1.5. Gains ranged from 4.4% to 12% on π0 and 7.2% to 10.4% on GR00T-N1.5 across challenging manipulation tasks.
- **Real-World Robot Generalization:** FOCA was evaluated on three real-robot setups, including a bimanual ALOHA robot and a simulated humanoid platform (VinR-H3). On the simulated humanoid, FOCA boosted GR00T-N1.5 from 58% to 84% success on a Place Part task. On the Aloha bimanual robot, FOCA showed substantial gains on household tasks, doubling success on Open Bag under limited data and reaching 95% success on the Tie Shoelaces task. Evaluations on a UR5 platform also showed consistent improvements across tasks like Dispense Napkin, Handle Bulk Material, and Short Chemistry Test Tube. These results collectively demonstrate FOCA's robust generalization across diverse robot embodiments, task types, and real-world deployment conditions.

#### 4.1. Ablation Study

- **Contribution of Explicit and Implicit Components:** Both explicit and implicit modules contribute complementary signals. Full FOCA consistently outperformed either component alone and the base π0, with the performance gap widening in low-data settings (e.g., 85.3% for full FOCA vs. lower for individual components at 10% data).
- **Explicit Ablations:** Operating in a latent, object-centric space consistently outperformed pixel-level prediction, suggesting that structured representations provide more robust supervision. Performance was sensitive to the number of representative tokens, with moderate counts yielding the best results.
- **Implicit Ablations:** Focusing on single, well-aligned future frames with object-centric representations yielded strong performance. Extending alignment to multiple future frames achieved comparable results, while alternative contrastive objectives were less effective.
- **Token Initialization Strategies:** Dynamic (adaptive) initialization for both explicit and implicit tokens consistently improved performance over static (random) initialization, validating the importance of grounding both token types in visual features.
- **Comparison with Other Future-Oriented Baselines:** FOCA consistently achieved the best overall performance compared to re-implemented DreamVLA-style and FLARE baselines across different policy backbones and fine-tuning settings (e.g., +1.0 average score over DreamVLA-style on LIBERO at 100% data, and 34.4% average success rate vs. 28.4% for FLARE on RoboCasa with GR00T-N1.5).
- **Contribution of DreamGen on Real Robot:** Incorporating DreamGen with FOCA yielded the largest gains on real-robot table setup tasks with the bimanual Aloha robot, further confirming the benefits of action-free synthetic data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents FOCA as a framework that significantly enhances the data efficiency of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in few-shot imitation learning scenarios. This work addresses a critical practical challenge in robotics by enabling VLA models to adapt to new tasks with substantially fewer demonstrations, which are costly and time-consuming to collect in real-world environments.

The key significance of FOCA lies in its novel combination of explicit and implicit future-oriented conditioning in latent space. By explicitly predicting task-grounded future interaction embeddings and implicitly aligning current observations with future goal observations, FOCA extracts richer learning signals from limited demonstrations. This approach leads to more robust and generalizable policies, even in the presence of distribution shifts that commonly occur in robotic deployments.

The ability of FOCA to leverage action-free synthetic videos from world models is a notable contribution. This allows for augmenting training data without the need for pseudo-action labels or inverse dynamics modeling, offering a scalable method to improve representation learning and potentially bridge the gap between simulation and the real world. This capability could democratize access to advanced robot learning by reducing the reliance on extensive real-world data collection.

Empirical results demonstrate consistent performance gains across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa) and real-robot platforms (ALOHA, UR5, simulated humanoid). FOCA established new state-of-the-art performance in few-shot VLA adaptation, generalizing effectively across different VLA architectures (π0, GR00T-N1.5) and robotic embodiments. This broad applicability suggests FOCA's potential as a widely adoptable fine-tuning mechanism for existing and future VLA systems.

The theoretical interpretation of FOCA's implicit objective as learning a goal-conditioned discounted occupancy provides a principled understanding of how future-oriented conditioning aids long-horizon reasoning. This connection to value-like reachability in representation space highlights the depth of the approach.

In summary, FOCA's advancements in data-efficient VLA adaptation contribute to making general-purpose robotic control more practical and accessible, potentially accelerating the deployment of intelligent robots in various domains by mitigating the bottleneck of data acquisition.
