# LLaVA-MORE: A Comparative Study of LLMs and Visual Backbones for Enhanced Visual Instruction Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaVA-MORE introduces a systematic comparative study of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), investigating the impact of various Large Language Models and visual backbones under a unified training and evaluation protocol. This empirical analysis provides insights into component contributions and architectural trade-offs, establishing new performance baselines for MLLMs.

## Problem

- Most state-of-the-art MLLMs converge on a narrow technical approach, largely relying on LLaMA-derived LLMs and CLIP-based visual encoders, limiting architectural exploration.
- Inconsistent training data, datasets, and evaluation protocols across rapidly introduced MLLM models hinder fair comparisons and a clear understanding of optimal design choices.
- There is a need to systematically understand the trade-offs between MLLM component size, architecture, and performance, rather than just pursuing parameter scaling.

## Method

- **LLaVA-MORE** systematically pairs diverse small-to-medium scale LLMs (e.g., Gemma, Phi, LLaMA, DeepSeek) with various ==visual backbones== (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP, SigLIP2) under a ==unified framework==.
- A consistent ==two-stage training protocol== (==visual-linguistic alignment== and ==instruction tuning==) and a comprehensive set of evaluation benchmarks are applied to all model configurations.
- The study also investigates the effects of increased image resolution using ==Scaling on Scales (S2)== and different pre-training datasets for the visual-linguistic alignment stage.

## Results

- LLaVA-MORE models consistently surpassed existing LLaVA baselines across both small and medium scales, with Gemma-2-9B emerging as a top performer, particularly in VQA benchmarks.
- SigLIP-based visual backbones demonstrated substantial improvements over CLIP, establishing a new performance frontier due to their larger-scale image-text pre-training.
- Qualitative results showed LLaVA-MORE models generated more detailed and accurate image descriptions while significantly reducing object hallucinations compared to the LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Architectural choices and fine-tuning strategies can enable smaller LLMs (e.g., Phi-4-3.8B) to outperform larger baselines on specific benchmarks, challenging the assumption that only larger models perform better.
> - Visual backbones pre-trained with image-text data (e.g., SigLIP family) consistently outperform self-supervised backbones (DINOv2), suggesting that inherent text alignment simplifies multimodal integration.
> - Optimal MLLM configurations are highly task-dependent, with the benefits of increased image resolution and specific pre-training datasets varying based on model scale and the visual task at hand.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, largely driven by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-4 and Gemini. MLLMs integrate visual perception with linguistic understanding, enabling sophisticated reasoning across modalities. A significant portion of this progress has been spearheaded by the LLaVA (Large Language and Vision Assistant) model family, which introduced visual instruction tuning as a foundational paradigm.

However, the authors point out a critical trend within the MLLM landscape: a convergence around a somewhat narrow technical approach. Most state-of-the-art MLLMs tend to rely on LLaMA-derived LLMs and visual encoders based on CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) and its derivatives. While these choices have proven effective in aligning image and text embeddings, they represent a limited exploration of the vast architectural space. Furthermore, the rapid introduction of new models often comes with specially curated datasets and inconsistent evaluation protocols, hindering fair and direct comparisons across different architectures and training strategies. This makes it challenging for researchers to derive optimal design choices and understand the fundamental trade-offs between model size, architecture, and performance.

LLaVA-MORE directly addresses these gaps by providing a much-needed systematic and comparative study. It moves beyond the common practice of simply scaling models to billions of parameters, instead focusing on empirically understanding the contributions of diverse LLMs and visual backbones. By adhering to a unified training protocol and evaluation framework, LLaVA-MORE offers a principled way to assess different components, thereby enriching the understanding of MLLM design principles and paving the way for more efficient and effective models. The work challenges the assumption that only larger models or specific types of visual encoders (e.g., CLIP) are optimal, opening up the exploration of alternative architectures and training data strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives of the LLaVA-MORE research are multi-faceted:

- **Systematic Exploration of MLLM Components:** The primary goal is to conduct a comprehensive empirical study that systematically pairs diverse LLMs (from small to medium scale) with various visual backbones. This aims to uncover the strengths and limitations of different vision-language integration strategies.
- **Addressing Under-explored Trade-offs:** The authors highlight that while prior work focused on scaling MLLM components, the trade-offs between model size, architecture, and performance remain largely unexamined. LLaVA-MORE seeks to fill this gap by analyzing these relationships.
- **Ensuring Fair Comparisons:** A major motivation is to overcome the inconsistencies in training data and evaluation protocols prevalent in current MLLM research. By introducing a unified training protocol applied consistently across all architectures, the study enables direct and fair comparisons, facilitating reliable insights.
- **Challenging Conventional Assumptions:** The work aims to challenge existing assumptions about the default choices for visual (e.g., CLIP-based) and textual (e.g., LLaMA-derived) backbones, as well as the optimal dataset requirements for pre-training.
- **Guiding Optimal Design Choices:** Ultimately, the research seeks to provide practical guidance for developing more efficient and effective MLLMs, offering insights into optimal design configurations rather than merely pursuing scale.
- **Reproducible Evaluation Framework:** The authors are motivated to establish a reproducible evaluation framework, contributing to the open-science ethos by making their source code and trained models publicly available.

In essence, LLaVA-MORE is driven by the need for a more rigorous, empirical, and comparative understanding of MLLM architecture and training dynamics, moving beyond anecdotal successes to establish foundational principles.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LLaVA-MORE builds upon the standard LLaVA architecture, which comprises three main components: an LLM backbone, one or more visual encoders, and a vision-to-language adapter (a two-layer MLP in this work) to bridge modalities. The training process follows the established two-stage paradigm:

- **Stage 1 – Pre-Training (Visual-Linguistic Alignment):** In this initial phase, only the vision-to-language adapter's weights are optimized. The goal is to align image features with the text embedding space. Training data consists of image-caption pairs following an "alt-text" structure from web-scale multimodal datasets. The models are trained for one epoch on 558k samples from sources like LAION, CC3M, and SBU.
- **Stage 2 – Instruction Tuning (Multimodal Conversational Capabilities):** This stage fine-tunes the MLLM on high-quality visual instruction-following data. During this phase, both the multimodal adapter and the LLM parameters are updated to enhance conversational abilities and multimodal reasoning. Next token prediction is used as the loss function in both stages.

A crucial aspect of LLaVA-MORE's methodology is the **consistent training and evaluation strategy** applied across all models to ensure fairness in comparisons.

**Architectural Components Investigated:**

- **Large Language Models (LLMs):**
 - **Small-scale:** Gemma-2 2B and Phi-4-Mini (3.8B parameters).
 - **Medium-scale:** LLaMA-3.1 8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMA-8B, and Gemma-2 9B.
- **Visual Backbones:** All visual encoders tested use the ViT-L/14 architecture.
 - **CLIP-based:** Standard CLIP ViT-L/14@336.
 - **Self-supervised:** DINOv2 (with and without visual register tokens).
 - **Contrastive (Enhanced):** SigLIP and SigLIP2.
 - **Resolution Enhancement:** Scaling on Scales (S$^2$) was applied to CLIP and SigLIP2 to investigate the impact of increased image resolution (2x and 3x standard resolution, chunked into 4 and 9 squared images, respectively, resulting in 14 images per sample with concatenated channels).

**Evaluation Benchmarks:**
The models were evaluated on a diverse set of benchmarks covering both task-oriented and instruction-following capabilities:

- **VQA Benchmarks (Visual Question Answering):** GQA, ScienceQA, TextVQA, AI2D. These assess the model's ability to answer questions based on visual input.
- **MLLM Benchmarks (Broader Multimodal Understanding):** POPE (object hallucination), MME (diverse communication modalities), MMBench (multiple-choice questions across domains), SEED-Bench (12 dimensions including scene understanding, OCR), MMMU (massive multi-discipline college-level tasks).

**Additional Experiments:**
Beyond varying LLMs and visual backbones, the study conducted additional experiments to analyze:
- The effects of **increased image resolution** using the S$^2$ scheme.
- The impact of different **pre-training datasets** for the first stage:
 - Original LLaVA pre-training (LAION, CC3M, SBU mixture).
 - Exclusively LAION.
 - Recap-DataComp-1B (MLLM-generated dense captions).
 - A balanced combination of LAION+Recap.

All experiments were performed using consistent hyperparameters as LLaVA-1.5, with a global batch size of 256 for pre-training and 128 for instruction tuning, on a multi-GPU, multi-node configuration with 16 A100 64GB NVIDIA GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The LLaVA-MORE study yielded several key findings, systematically investigating various components of MLLMs:

#### 4.1. Assessing the Optimal LLM Choice (Table 1)

- **Small-Scale LLMs:** LLaVA-MORE models (Gemma-2-2B, Phi-4-3.8B) consistently outperformed existing small-scale LLaVA baselines (LLaVA-Phi-2.7B). Notably, Phi-4-3.8B showed superior reasoning and generalization, particularly excelling in MMMU (5.4% higher than Gemma-2-2B) and SEED benchmarks.
- **Medium-Scale LLMs:** LLaVA-MORE models (LLaMA-3.1-8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMA-8B, Gemma-2-9B) surpassed stronger baselines like LLaVA-1.5-7B and LLaVA-1.5-LLaMA3-8B. Gemma-2-9B emerged as the top performer, especially in VQA benchmarks (highest on GQA, AI2D). LLaMA-3.1-8B performed strongly on MMBench.
- **Scaling vs. Architecture:** A significant observation was that some small-scale LLaVA-MORE models (e.g., Phi-4-3.8B) could outperform certain medium-scale baselines (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B) on benchmarks like Science-QA and AI2D. This indicates that architectural choices and fine-tuning strategies can significantly impact effectiveness, challenging the notion that "scaling up is the only path to better performance." The models also exhibited less sensitivity to object hallucinations (POPE) and superior performance on SEED.

#### 4.2. Changing the Visual Backbone (Table 2)

- **Image-Text vs. Self-supervised Pre-training:** Visual backbones pre-trained with image-text data (CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2) consistently outperformed self-supervised DINOv2 backbones across both small and medium scales. This suggests that features readily aligned with text, due to contrastive pre-training, simplify the adapter's role in connecting to the LLM. The addition of register tokens in DINOv2 did not significantly close this gap, except for MME-C.
- **SigLIP Family Performance:** SigLIP showed substantial improvement over CLIP at both scales. SigLIP2, despite a more complex training recipe, performed on par with the original SigLIP at 3.8B scale and recorded an average 0.4% gain at 9B scale. The superior performance of SigLIP-based backbones is attributed to the massive billion-scale image-text pre-training using sigmoid loss, compared to CLIP's 400M pairs. SigLIP-based visual backbones established a new performance frontier.

#### 4.3. Changing the Image Resolution (Table 3)

- **Impact of S$^2$ (Scaling on Scales):** Applying S$^2$ (multi-scale visual processing) generally improved LLaVA-MORE-3.8B performance with both CLIP and SigLIP2.
- **Diminishing Returns with Scale:** However, for LLaVA-MORE-9B, the benefits of S$^2$ diminished or even appeared detrimental on some benchmarks (e.g., Science-QA, AI2D). This indicates that higher resolution is highly beneficial for smaller MLLMs, but its positive impact can decrease as the LLM's size increases.
- **Task-Dependent Benefits:** S$^2$ consistently improved performance on tasks heavily relying on fine-grained visual information, such as TextVQA (OCR capabilities) and GQA (in-depth scene understanding). Interestingly, it showed substantial gains on the Chinese version of MMBench (MMB-Cn).

#### 4.4. Dataset Contribution During Pre-Training (Table 4)

- **Small-Scale Sensitivity:** For LLaVA-MORE-3.8B, exclusively training on LAION often yielded the best results (winning 8 out of 13 benchmarks), outperforming the original LLaVA mixture. This suggests that the noisy, web-sourced nature of LAION pairs might align well with the SigLIP2 visual encoder's pre-training.
- **Medium-Scale Robustness:** LLaVA-MORE-9B demonstrated more robust performance across different pre-training data configurations, being less sensitive to the specific data source. Adding Recap samples (MLLM-generated dense captions) boosted Chinese fluency (MMB-Cn) and improved other MLLM results for the 9B model.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results (Figure 3)

- **Improved Description Accuracy:** LLaVA-MORE versions (3.8B and 9B) generated more detailed and accurate image descriptions compared to the LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline.
- **Reduced Hallucinations:** LLaVA-MORE models were more effective at avoiding object hallucinations (e.g., correctly identifying a snowboarder instead of a skier, or a teddy bear instead of a cat), highlighting better visual understanding and alignment with textual output.

Overall, the findings emphasize that there is **no single optimal configuration** that universally outperforms others. Performance is highly dependent on the specific task, model scale, and the interplay between the chosen LLM, visual backbone, image resolution, and pre-training data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LLaVA-MORE study makes significant contributions to the MLLM research landscape with substantial potential impact:

- **Empirical Foundation for MLLM Design:** By conducting the first systematic, comparative analysis of different LLMs and visual backbones under consistent experimental settings, this work provides a much-needed empirical foundation for MLLM architecture design. It moves beyond anecdotal evidence and broad scaling trends to offer concrete insights into component contributions.
- **Challenging Conventional Wisdom:** The results challenge several prevailing assumptions in the field. For instance, the finding that smaller LLMs (like Phi-4-3.8B) can outperform larger baselines, and that certain visual backbones (SigLIP/SigLIP2) consistently surpass the commonly used CLIP, encourages researchers to explore a broader spectrum of architectural choices beyond simple parameter scaling or default selections.
- **Guiding Efficient MLLM Development:** The insights into trade-offs between model size, architectural choices, and performance are invaluable. They can guide researchers and developers in creating more efficient MLLMs, especially when computational resources are constrained. The demonstration that input resolution and pre-training data sources have a task-dependent impact further refines design strategies.
- **Reproducibility and Open Science:** The commitment to releasing source code and trained models is a critical contribution to open science. This ensures that the community can reproduce the results, build upon the LLaVA-MORE framework, and conduct further experiments with new configurations, accelerating innovation.
- **Advancing Multimodal Reasoning:** The qualitative results, demonstrating LLaVA-MORE's superior ability to generate accurate descriptions and reduce object hallucinations compared to baselines, indicate a step forward in robust multimodal reasoning and visual understanding.
- **Roadmap for Future Research:** The paper explicitly outlines open problems, such as improving multimodal reasoning through specifically designed training strategies, and presents preliminary results on integrating text-only reasoning models into multimodal tasks. This acts as a clear roadmap for future research directions.

In summary, LLaVA-MORE is a pivotal study that provides comprehensive empirical evidence for designing more effective and efficient MLLMs. Its rigorous methodology, systematic comparisons, and reproducible framework are crucial for advancing the understanding and development of the next generation of multimodal AI systems. The potential impact lies in fostering more informed design decisions, driving innovation towards more capable yet efficient MLLMs, and strengthening the foundation for cross-modal understanding. The limitations acknowledged, such as the snapshot nature of current best configurations in a rapidly evolving field, underscore the dynamic nature of MLLM research but do not diminish the foundational value of this systematic comparison.
