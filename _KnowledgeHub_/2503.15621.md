# LLaVA-MORE: A Comparative Study of LLMs and Visual Backbones for Enhanced Visual Instruction Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaVA-MORE introduces a systematic comparative study of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), investigating the impact of various Large Language Models and visual backbones under a unified training and evaluation protocol. This empirical analysis provides insights into component contributions and architectural trade-offs, establishing new performance baselines for MLLMs.

## Problem

- Most state-of-the-art MLLMs converge on a narrow technical approach, largely relying on LLaMA-derived LLMs and CLIP-based visual encoders, limiting architectural exploration.
- Inconsistent training data, datasets, and evaluation protocols across rapidly introduced MLLM models hinder fair comparisons and a clear understanding of optimal design choices.
- There is a need to systematically understand the trade-offs between MLLM component size, architecture, and performance, rather than just pursuing parameter scaling.

## Method

- **LLaVA-MORE** systematically pairs diverse small-to-medium scale LLMs (e.g., Gemma, Phi, LLaMA, DeepSeek) with various ==visual backbones== (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP, SigLIP2) under a ==unified framework==.
- A consistent ==two-stage training protocol== (==visual-linguistic alignment== and ==instruction tuning==) and a comprehensive set of evaluation benchmarks are applied to all model configurations.
- The study also investigates the effects of increased image resolution using ==Scaling on Scales (S2)== and different pre-training datasets for the visual-linguistic alignment stage.

## Results

- LLaVA-MORE models consistently surpassed existing LLaVA baselines across both small and medium scales, with Gemma-2-9B emerging as a top performer, particularly in VQA benchmarks.
- SigLIP-based visual backbones demonstrated substantial improvements over CLIP, establishing a new performance frontier due to their larger-scale image-text pre-training.
- Qualitative results showed LLaVA-MORE models generated more detailed and accurate image descriptions while significantly reducing object hallucinations compared to the LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Architectural choices and fine-tuning strategies can enable smaller LLMs (e.g., Phi-4-3.8B) to outperform larger baselines on specific benchmarks, challenging the assumption that only larger models perform better.
> - Visual backbones pre-trained with image-text data (e.g., SigLIP family) consistently outperform self-supervised backbones (DINOv2), suggesting that inherent text alignment simplifies multimodal integration.
> - Optimal MLLM configurations are highly task-dependent, with the benefits of increased image resolution and specific pre-training datasets varying based on model scale and the visual task at hand.
