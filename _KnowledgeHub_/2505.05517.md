# Web2Grasp: Learning Functional Grasps from Web Images of Hand-Object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> Web2Grasp trains dexterous multi-finger grasping by reconstructing 3D hand-object interaction meshes from web images, then learning an interaction-centric DRO model on this noisy data, filtered geometrically and refined via simulation disturbance testing. Achieves 75.8% sim success and 77.5% real-world success across 12 objects with LEAP and Inspire hands, generalizing to unseen functional grasps.

## Problem

- Prior functional grasping work mostly targets power grasps that hold objects rigidly, ignoring diverse poses needed for actual object use like triggers or handles.
- High-quality human-collected functional grasp datasets exist but curating accurate contact annotations across object categories is costly and does not scale.
- Learning affordances directly from raw images or object shapes is challenging and typically still requires additional robot demonstrations to fine-tune the learned prior.

## Method

- **Web2Grasp** reconstructs robot hand-object interaction from web images via a pretrained ==HOI reconstruction model==, aligning noisy object meshes to text-to-3D generated shapes via ==ICP== and retargeting ==MANO== poses to robot hand configurations.
- An ==interaction-centric DRO model==, a ==CVAE== predicting dense point-to-point hand-object distance matrices, is trained on reconstructed data instead of robot-centric joint regression, making it robust to retargeting noise.
- A ==two-stage dataset refinement pipeline== applies ==geometry-based filtering== for penetration and contact thresholds, then ==simulation-based disturbance testing== to retain and expand grasps that resist external forces.

## Results

- Model trained on filtered web data alone achieves **75.8%** average grasp success rate across **10** seen ShadowHand object categories in IsaacGym simulation.
- On **9** unseen objects, Web2Grasp reaches **46.3%** success rate and **7.91%** functional contact ratio, outperforming GenDexGrasp, DexGraspNet, and DRO baselines in both success and human-rated functionality score of **47.7**.
- Simulation-augmented retraining raises average success rate from **61.8%** to **83.4%** across **19** objects, and real-world tests on LEAP and Inspire hands yield **77.5%** success across **12** physical objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Noisy, uncurated web-scale reconstructions can substitute for costly human-collected contact datasets when paired with an interaction-centric representation robust to geometric noise.
> - Combining cheap geometric filtering with physics-based simulation validation is an effective general recipe for cleaning and expanding weakly-supervised robotic manipulation data.
> - Functional grasp quality metrics like penetration and contact ratio can be misleading in isolation, since interpenetration artificially inflates contact coverage, so complementary human evaluation is needed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic grasping is an active area of research, with a primary goal of enabling robots to interact with objects in diverse and effective ways. Within this field, a distinction exists between "power grasps," which prioritize stable holding, and "functional grasps," which focus on enabling an object's intended use. Prior work in dexterous robotic grasping has largely concentrated on achieving stable power grasps, often through methods such as force-closure estimation or reinforcement learning in simulated environments. While these approaches have demonstrated progress, they frequently result in generic grasps that may not support the object's specific functionality.

Learning functional affordances from visual data like images and object shapes has been explored, but training grasp poses from such high-dimensional data remains challenging, often requiring supplementary robot demonstrations for refinement. Furthermore, the creation of high-quality, human-collected datasets that incorporate functional constraints is resource-intensive and costly.

Concurrently, advancements in 3D vision have enabled the reconstruction of 3D human hand-object interactions (HOI) from RGB images. This progress offers a potential alternative for acquiring functional grasp information from readily available web images. However, the 3D data reconstructed from web images is typically noisy, exhibiting issues such as mutual occlusions, inaccurate hand-object contact, and imprecise object geometries, which make it difficult to directly use for training accurate robot grasping models. Some previous works have leveraged reconstructed 3D data in simulators to improve grasping, but these often focus on parallel grippers or power grasps, leaving functional multi-fingered grasping largely unaddressed.

This research aims to bridge the gap between the availability of functional human interaction data in web images and the practical challenges of using such noisy data to train dexterous robot hands for functional grasping. It proposes a methodology to extract, refine, and utilize this information to enable robots to learn human-like functional grasps, thereby advancing the capabilities of dexterous manipulation beyond simple object holding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable dexterous multi-finger robot hands to learn "functional grasping," defined as the ability to grasp an object in a manner that supports its intended purpose during manipulation. This contrasts with traditional "power grasps," which merely focus on stably holding an object without necessarily enabling its operation.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in the existing landscape of robotic grasping:

1. **Limitations of Current Grasping Approaches:** Many existing dexterous grasping methods prioritize stable holding (power grasps) and may apply similar grasps across objects that actually require diverse poses for effective use. This often prevents robots from performing complex in-hand manipulation tasks that rely on specific functional contact points (e.g., pressing a trigger on a power drill).
2. **Cost and Scarcity of Functional Data:** High-quality, human-collected datasets that provide functional grasp annotations are costly and time-consuming to curate. This limits the scalability and diversity of objects for which robots can learn functional grasps. While learning affordances from images and shapes has been explored, direct learning of grasp poses from high-dimensional visual data remains challenging and often necessitates additional robot demonstrations for fine-tuning.
3. **Untapped Potential of Web Images:** Web images inherently depict humans interacting with objects in natural, functional ways. Recent advancements in 3D vision, particularly in hand-object pose estimation and reconstruction from RGB images, offer a pathway to extract this rich source of functional information.
4. **Challenges with Noisy Reconstructed Data:** Despite the promise of web images, 3D reconstructions of hand-object interactions (HOI) from these images are often inaccurate due to factors like mutual occlusions, lack of annotated training data, and limitations of vision models. These inaccuracies can lead to physically implausible or "noisy" grasps (e.g., hand-object interpenetration, improper contact) that are difficult to directly use for training robust robot grasping models.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of Web2Grasp are:

- To develop an approach that leverages human grasp information extracted from web images to train a functional grasping model for dexterous robot hands.
- To overcome the inherent noise and inaccuracies present in 3D reconstructed HOI data from web images. This involves employing robust modeling techniques and data refinement strategies.
- To train a model capable of learning functional interaction patterns between the hand and the object, rather than merely predicting robot joint configurations directly.
- To validate the effectiveness of the proposed method in both simulated environments (using IsaacGym with a ShadowHand) and real-world scenarios (using LEAP and Inspire dexterous hands), demonstrating high success rates and superior functional quality, including generalization to unseen objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Web2Grasp methodology is designed to collect functional training grasps from web images, refine them, and then use them to train a robust grasping model for dexterous robot hands. The approach comprises four main steps: HOI reconstruction, interaction-centric model training, data refinement, and model retraining.

**A. Reconstructing Robotic Hand-Object Interaction (Sec. III-A)**

1. **Web Image Collection:** The process begins by crawling web images depicting humans grasping various objects.
2. **3D HOI Reconstruction:** For each collected image, a pretrained 3D reconstruction model (specifically, the method by Ye et al. leveraging FrankMocap) is used to predict the MANO hand pose (a low-dimensional representation of the human hand) and infer the shape of the interacting object using a signed-distance function (SDF) conditioned on the hand pose.
3. **Object Mesh Alignment:** The reconstructed object meshes are often of low quality. To address this, they are aligned with accurate 3D meshes from the same category obtained from text-to-3D mesh generators like MeshyAI and Genie. The Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm is employed for this alignment, aiming to preserve key interaction cues rather than achieving pixel-perfect reconstruction.
4. **Robot Hand Retargeting:** The reconstructed MANO hand poses are then transferred to a robot hand configuration (q). This is achieved through position-based optimization, minimizing the 3D position error between keypoints on the robot’s links and corresponding points on the MANO hand. The final robot HOI representation includes the object mesh centered at the origin, with the robot hand configuration encoding the wrist’s relative position and orientation, alongside finger joint configurations.

**B. Interaction-Centric Grasping Model Training (Sec. III-B)**

1. **Addressing Data Noise:** Grasps obtained through direct retargeting from human HOI are often physically implausible due to the embodiment gap between human and robot hands, inaccuracies in hand detection, and mismatches between web-image objects and aligned target objects.
2. **Model Selection:** Instead of robot-centric or object-centric grasping models, Web2Grasp adopts the interaction-centric model DRO (D(R,O) Grasp). This model is chosen for its robustness to noise in HOI grasps as it predicts dense point-to-point distances between the robot hand and the object, rather than directly predicting specific robot joint values or requiring precise contact maps.
3. **Model Architecture and Training:** The DRO model utilizes a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) to learn and predict a point-to-point distance matrix D(R, O) between initial robot and object point clouds. The training objective minimizes the L1 difference between the predicted and ground-truth distance matrices.
4. **Grasp Generation:** After prediction of the distance matrix, the target robot grasp pose P_R_target is computed using a multilateration method, and the final joint configuration is derived through optimization.

**C. Reconstructed HOI Dataset Refinement (Sec. III-C)**

To improve the quality and physical plausibility of the reconstructed HOI data for training, a two-stage filtering and augmentation process is applied:

1. **Geometry-based Filtering:** Initial reconstructed HOI grasps are filtered based on geometric criteria. This involves thresholds for low hand-object penetration, low disjoint distance (average distance from fingers to object surface), and high contact ratio between hand and object meshes. This step removes overtly infeasible grasps without requiring simulation.
2. **Simulation-based Evaluation and Augmentation:**
 - An initial DRO model is trained on the *geometry-filtered* dataset.
 - This model is then used to generate a large number of grasps for diverse objects.
 - These generated grasps are evaluated in the IsaacGym physical simulator. External force disturbances are applied to each grasp, and only grasps that successfully withstand these disturbances (i.e., securely hold the object) are retained. This process expands the dataset with physically plausible and robust grasps.
3. **Retraining:** The DRO model is then retrained using the augmented dataset, which combines the geometry-filtered reconstructed grasps and the successful, simulation-validated grasps. This sequential data augmentation and retraining procedure ensures that the final model learns from a dataset that is both functionally representative of human behavior and physically plausible.

**D. Experimental Setup and Evaluation (Sec. IV)**

The methodology is evaluated in three contexts:

- **HOI Reconstruction Quality:** Quantitative metrics (Penetration Score, Disjoint Distance, Contact Region Ratio) are used to assess the quality of the reconstructed HOI dataset compared to synthesized and human-collected datasets.
- **Simulation Grasp Experiment:** Using the ShadowHand in IsaacGym, the model's performance is assessed via:
 - **Grasp Success Rate:** Defined by object displacement remaining below 2 cm under external force disturbances.
 - **Grasp Functionality:** Measured by a Functional Contact Ratio (FCR) on annotated functional regions of objects and a human study evaluating overall grasp suitability.
- **Real-world Experiment:** The learned models are deployed on LEAP and Inspire dexterous robot hands mounted on 7-DoF arms. Grasp success is determined by the robot's ability to lift and hold objects against gravity. Object pose estimation is handled by FoundationPose.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings across HOI reconstruction quality, simulation experiments, and real-world robotic grasping tasks.

**A. HOI Reconstruction Results (Sec. IV-A)**

- **Quantitative Evaluation:** The *full web dataset* initially exhibited high penetration scores and high contact ratios, which were often inflated due to hand-object interpenetration. The *geometry-based filtering* process significantly improved these metrics, yielding a *filtered web dataset* with lower penetration depth (from 1.11 cm to 0.59 cm) and volume (from 9.79 cm³ to 4.85 cm³), and a lower disjoint distance (from 0.18 cm to 0.08 cm), indicating better contact without severe interpenetration. The *simulation-augmented dataset* (sim data) showed minimal penetration (0.01 cm depth, 0.13 cm³ volume) due to physical engine constraints, along with a small disjoint distance, though a lower contact ratio was observed compared to the initial noisy web data, as penetration artificially inflates this metric. The human-collected OakInk dataset demonstrated the best balance of low penetration and high contact.
- **Qualitative Evaluation:** Many reconstructed grasps successfully preserved the functional interaction patterns observed in the original web images for objects like power drills, pens, spray bottles, wine glasses, syringes, and tongs. However, the reconstruction process encountered difficulties with objects having complex geometries or unique interaction modes, such as buckets (where thin handles were often missed) and scissors (due to the requirement of finger insertion into holes).

**B. Simulation Grasp Experiment Results (Sec. IV-C)**

The simulation experiments, using the ShadowHand in IsaacGym, addressed three main questions:

- **Q1: Effectiveness of Reconstructed Web Data for Training:** The model trained solely on the *filtered web dataset* (10 object categories) achieved a 75.8% average success rate on these seen objects (Table II, "Web data" row for Power drill to Sword). This demonstrates that functional grasping models can be effectively trained using 3D reconstructed HOI from web data without requiring additional robot demonstrations. The grasps also exhibited human-like functional behaviors, as illustrated in Fig. 4.
- **Q2: Generalization to Unseen Objects:** When evaluated on 9 unseen test object categories, the Web2Grasp model, trained on the *filtered web dataset*, achieved an average success rate of 46.3%. This outperformed baseline methods: GenDexGrasp (20.6%), DexGraspNet (39.6%), and DRO (33.4%) (Table III). Furthermore, Web2Grasp demonstrated superior functionality, achieving the highest Functional Contact Ratio (FCR) of 7.91% and the highest human evaluation score of 47.7%, compared to baselines. This suggests that the model effectively learns nuanced, functional interactions from web data and can generalize these patterns to novel, but functionally related, objects (e.g., from Pen to Writing Brush, Spray Bottle to Soap Bottle). Baselines often failed to capture functional strategies (e.g., DRO grasping a Soap Bottle from the side instead of placing a finger on the trigger, resulting in low FCR, Fig. 5).
- **Q3: Impact of Simulation Augmentation:** Retraining the model with the *simulation-augmented dataset* (combining the filtered web data with 3,800 successful grasps collected in IsaacGym) significantly improved the average success rate across all 19 objects (seen and unseen) from 61.8% (web data only) to 83.4% (sim data augmented) (Table II, "Average" column). This augmentation was particularly effective for objects where initial grasps were less stable (e.g., Sword, improving from 24% to 55% success) or for unseen objects (e.g., Teapot, Whip, Writing Brush). The FCR remained comparable (8.37% for web data vs. 7.42% for sim-augmented data), indicating that the simulation refinement primarily enhanced grasp stability while largely preserving the functional grasping behaviors learned from the web data.

**C. Real-World Experiment Results (Sec. IV-D)**

Real-world experiments were conducted using the LEAP hand and Inspire hand. The models were trained solely on web-image-based HOI data for specific target categories.

- **Overall Performance:** Across 12 objects and two dexterous hands, Web2Grasp achieved an average real-world success rate of 77.5%.
- **LEAP Hand:** Achieved an average success rate of 83.75% across eight object categories (Wine Glass, Tong, Syringe, Phone, Power Drill, Microphone, Spray Bottle, Mug). The method performed effectively on objects requiring form-closure functional grasps (e.g., Power Drill, Microphone) and demonstrated functional grasps like placing fingers on the spray head of a Spray Bottle or the handle of a Mug (Fig. 6).
- **Inspire Hand:** Achieved success rates of 10/10 for Bowl, 8/10 for Plate, 3/10 for Knife, and 5/10 for Fork. The soft finger layers of the Inspire Hand facilitated secure grasping of smaller objects like the Fork. However, small objects like the Fork and Knife remained challenging, with grasp failures occasionally attributed to minor pose estimation inaccuracies (Fig. 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Web2Grasp, a method that addresses a notable challenge in robotic manipulation: enabling dexterous hands to perform functional grasps without relying on costly human demonstrations or precisely curated datasets. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

1. **Reduced Data Collection Burden:** The primary contribution is demonstrating that functional dexterous grasping models can be effectively trained using readily available, uncurated web images of human-object interactions. This significantly reduces the reliance on laborious and expensive human-collected grasp datasets, making functional grasp learning more accessible and scalable.
2. **Robustness to Noisy Data:** The work presents effective strategies—an interaction-centric model (DRO) combined with geometry-based filtering and simulation-based data augmentation—to leverage inherently noisy 3D reconstructed data. This approach successfully mitigates issues like hand-object interpenetration and inaccurate contact that typically plague reconstructions from single RGB images, thereby enabling the use of a rich, yet imperfect, data source.
3. **Generalization and Functional Quality:** Web2Grasp not only achieves competitive grasp success rates in both simulation and the real world but also demonstrates superior functional quality and generalization capabilities compared to existing baselines, particularly on unseen objects. This indicates that the method learns the underlying functional interaction patterns from human behavior rather than merely fitting to specific object geometries.
4. **Advancing Dexterous Manipulation:** By enabling robots to learn human-like functional grasps, this research contributes to advancing the capabilities of dexterous robotic manipulation. Robots equipped with these skills can move beyond simple object holding to perform more nuanced, task-oriented interactions, such as operating tools, pouring liquids, or interacting with everyday items in a functionally appropriate manner.
5. **Foundation for Future Work:** The method establishes a foundation for future research in several directions. Improvements in 3D reconstruction techniques could directly benefit Web2Grasp, further enhancing data quality. The integration of language conditioning could enable the generation of diverse, task-specific grasps beyond a single typical functional pattern. Moreover, exploring advanced optimization methods for refining initially failed reconstructed grasps could maximize the utility of the collected data.

In summary, Web2Grasp represents a step towards making functional dexterous grasping more practical and scalable by innovatively utilizing ambient web data, thereby fostering the development of more versatile and intelligent robotic systems.
