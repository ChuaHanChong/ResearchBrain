# OptiWorld: Optimal Control for Video World Generation under Physical Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> OptiWorld inserts classical optimal control into video generation at inference time: it extracts a compact 3D world state, plans a physically preferable trajectory on a Riemannian manifold under goal, safety, smoothness, and efficiency constraints, then renders video conditioned on that plan, beating baselines on goal error and safety violations.

## Problem

- Video generation models act as implicit world models but only produce plausible motion, without explicitly controlling or optimizing the underlying dynamics they render.
- Physics-aware video generation methods enforce plausibility priors but never optimize trajectories jointly for safety, smoothness, and efficiency under task constraints.
- 3D-trajectory-conditioned video generators already render motion well but depend on a predefined input trajectory, leaving proactive planning as the unaddressed missing stage.

## Method

- Zero-shot understanding module builds a compact world state from ==metric depth==, ==promptable segmentation==, and ==VLM== reasoning to assign object roles like obstacle, buffer, hazard, and target.
- Planning formulates trajectory optimization as a geometric shortest-path problem on a ==Riemannian manifold==, where a task-induced metric and potential encode safety, goal, and efficiency as one continuous geometry. This is **OptiWorld**'s core.
- A practical solver converts the world state into ==3D cost fields==, searches a ==voxel graph== for a global seed path, then refines it into a continuous geodesic-like curve resampled as generator guidance.

## Results

- On goal-conditioned image-to-video, OptiWorld achieves goal error **0.310** and safety-violation rate **0.260**, both best among HunyuanVideo-1.5, Wan2.2, Cosmos-Predict2.5, and VLIPP baselines.
- OptiWorld cuts motion energy to **0.0126** versus **0.0576**, **0.0860**, **0.0398**, and **0.0419** for the four compared video generators, while also raising VBench motion smoothness to **0.997**.
- On video dynamics editing, OptiWorld reduces track deviation to **0.113**, acceleration to **0.0107**, jerk to **0.0181**, and path length to **0.524** versus Wan2.1 FLF2V and the source video.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating world understanding, optimal planning, and rendering into explicit stages lets a pretrained video generator inherit better dynamics without any retraining.
> - Encoding heterogeneous physical constraints as one Riemannian metric and potential avoids the brittleness of hand-designed rule stacks and non-convex multi-penalty objectives.
> - A visually strong video generator cannot compensate for a poor guidance trajectory, so the trajectory-planning stage is the primary lever for physically preferable dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The work presented in "OptiWorld" is situated within the evolving field of generative AI, specifically at the intersection of video generation models and world models. Recent advancements in video generation, particularly those leveraging diffusion models and large transformers, have enabled the synthesis of visually realistic future frames from initial observations and conditions. These models are increasingly viewed as a scalable, pixel-space form of "world models," which are computational systems that predict future world states from current observations and control inputs. This capability has facilitated applications in areas such as robot learning, generative gaming, and general world dynamics simulation.

However, a recognized limitation of many existing video generation models, even those considered world models, is their primary focus on generating "plausible" or "realistic" motion rather than "optimal" or "physically consistent" motion. These models often learn appearance-level dynamics from data, which can result in generated trajectories that are unsafe, unsmooth, inefficient, or physically inaccurate, especially in scenarios requiring precise control or in out-of-distribution contexts. While some prior research has incorporated physical priors to enhance plausibility or introduced additional motion-related conditions (e.g., camera poses, optical flow) to improve controllability, these approaches typically rely on predefined input trajectories and do not explicitly optimize the underlying dynamics under a comprehensive set of physical constraints.

OptiWorld addresses this gap by integrating classical optimal control principles into the video generation pipeline. It departs from methods that only aim for physical plausibility or rely on external, unoptimized control signals. Instead, it proposes a system that actively understands the scene and plans an optimal, physically constrained trajectory *before* video rendering. This positions OptiWorld as a development aiming to advance video world models beyond merely predicting plausible futures to proactively shaping and optimizing those futures based on physical laws and explicit objectives. The paper argues that for video generation models to function effectively as true world models, particularly for applications requiring precise physical interaction or decision-making (like robotics), they require an explicit decision-making layer that optimizes motion under specific goals and constraints.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce classical optimal control into the video generation process at inference time, thereby enabling the generation of videos that depict physically preferable dynamics. This involves moving beyond the current capability of video generative models to produce merely plausible motion towards generating motion that is proactively controlled and optimized under a set of explicit physical constraints.

The motivation for OptiWorld stems from several identified shortcomings of existing video generation models when considered as world models:

1. **Lack of Physical Consistency and Controllability:** Current video generation models, while achieving high visual fidelity, frequently produce motions that violate fundamental physical principles. This can lead to unsafe, unsmooth, or inefficient trajectories for objects within the generated video. For instance, an object might collide with obstacles, follow a jagged path, or take an unnecessarily long route to a destination.
2. **Absence of an Optimization Layer:** Most video generation models are designed to generate a *plausible* future given an initial state and conditions. They lack an inherent "decision layer" that allows them to optimize *which* future should occur based on criteria like safety, smoothness, energy efficiency, or precise goal-reaching. This limits their utility in applications demanding high degrees of control or adherence to specific physical requirements.
3. **Reliance on Predefined Trajectories:** While 3D-aware video generation models exhibit strong rendering capabilities once a motion signal is provided, this motion is typically predefined or derived from external inputs. The core missing component, as identified by the authors, is the ability for the model to proactively *understand* the world and *plan* an optimal trajectory independently before rendering.
4. **Challenges in Open Visual Scenes:** Implementing optimal control for video generation in open visual scenes is complex. It requires a system that can jointly model continuous 3D geometry and a variety of task-dependent physical constraints (e.g., goal-reaching, safety, smoothness, efficiency). Traditional rule-based planners often struggle with the generality required for diverse visual scenes, and directly optimizing these heterogeneous constraints as independent penalties can lead to brittle and highly non-convex problems.

To address these motivations, OptiWorld aims to fill the gap by developing a framework that:
- Extracts a compact, task-relevant world state from initial visual observations.
- Plans an optimal trajectory based on this world state, explicitly considering physical constraints.
- Conditions a video generation model on this optimized trajectory to render the final video.

The ultimate goal is to enable video world models to not only predict how the world *might* evolve but to actively *choose* and depict how it *should* evolve to achieve specified objectives while respecting physical realities.

### 3. Methodology and approach

OptiWorld proposes a three-stage pipeline: Understanding, Planning, and Generation. This modular design aims to integrate classical optimal control into the inference time of video generation models. The approach is designed to be generalizable, leveraging existing foundation models for the understanding and generation stages while introducing a novel planning component.

#### 3.1. Overview

The overall workflow begins with an initial frame and a goal (specified by a point and language instruction). The **understanding** stage processes these inputs to construct a task-relevant 3D world state. The **planning** stage then takes this world state and optimizes a 3D path, considering constraints such as goal-reaching, safety, smoothness, and efficiency. Finally, the **generation** module renders a photorealistic video, using the optimized 3D path as motion guidance.

#### 3.2. Understanding: Zero-Shot 3D World State Construction

This initial stage translates raw pixel data and high-level goals into a structured and compact 3D world state, denoted as W = {P, S, A, T}. This state is designed to be directly usable by the planner, capturing essential geometric, semantic, and task-level information without requiring per-task training. The construction proceeds in a zero-shot, agentic manner using several generic vision foundation models:

- **Metric Geometry Model (MoGe2):** Estimates dense depth maps and 3D point maps from the input frame, providing the metric 3D point world (P).
- **Promptable Segmentation Model (SAM3):** Localizes task-relevant objects and regions, providing fine-grained masks for scene pixels and extending vocabulary to open-world settings (part of S).
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM) (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct):** Reasons about the goal and assigns task-specific roles to segmented objects or regions. This includes identifying manipulable objects, support surfaces, obstacles, safety buffers, safety hazards, and targets (A, task attributes).
- **3D Tracking (SpatialTrackerV2):** For video dynamics editing tasks, dense 3D point tracks from the source video are extracted and incorporated (T). These tracks serve as editable variables for the optimization process while anchoring the motion to the source video's intent.

The outputs (object centroids, goal positions, semantic point clouds, signed-distance fields, hazard fields, and task weights) are lifted into 3D space. This structured representation allows the planning module to perform explicit geometric reasoning and apply task-dependent constraints.

#### 3.3. Planning: Optimal Control on a Continuous Manifold

This is the central optimal control component. It decides the object's motion before video rendering, aiming to produce trajectories that are safe, smooth, and efficient. The challenge lies in integrating continuous 3D geometry with diverse, task-dependent physical constraints.

- **Geometric Problem Formulation:** Instead of traditional rule-based or brittle penalty-based optimization, planning is formulated as a geometric shortest-path problem on a Riemannian manifold. This approach converts world information into continuous geometry.
 - A **positive-definite metric G(q)** is defined at each object state `q`, specifying the local squared travel cost: $ds^2_G = dq^T G(q) dq$. Regions that are unsafe or inefficient are assigned a large metric, making paths through them "long" even if they are short in Euclidean distance.
 - A **scalar potential V(q)** reshapes the scene into a landscape: goals become low-potential "valleys," while risky or inefficient regions become high-potential "hills."
- **Planning Objective:** The optimal path $\gamma^\ast$ minimizes an objective function that combines Riemannian length, scalar potential, and a dynamics regularization term:
 $\gamma^\ast = \arg\min_\gamma \int_0^1 \left( \frac{1}{2} \dot{\gamma}(s)^T G(\gamma(s))\dot{\gamma}(s) + V(\gamma(s)) \right) ds + \lambda_{dyn} \int_0^1 \| \nabla_{\dot{\gamma}} \dot{\gamma}(s) \|^2 ds$
 Here, the term with $G(\gamma(s))$ promotes short paths in the induced geometry, $V(\gamma(s))$ pulls the path towards goals and away from hazards, and $\lambda_{dyn} \int_0^1 \| \nabla_{\dot{\gamma}} \dot{\gamma}(s) \|^2 ds$ penalizes high acceleration and sharp turns for smoothness.
- **Constraint-induced Geometry:** All physical constraints are encoded into G(q) and V(q) using three main planning fields:
 - **Goal Reaching:** The potential $V(q)$ includes a term $\lambda_{goal} d(q, G(g))^2$, creating a low-potential basin at the target $G(g)$.
 - **Safety:** The metric $G(q)$ and potential $V(q)$ include terms related to $\phi_{safe}(q)$, which combines geometric obstacles (from signed distance fields) and semantic hazards (from VLM-inferred risk weights). High safety costs stretch the metric and raise potential in unsafe regions.
 - **Efficiency:** $G(q)$ and $V(q)$ incorporate terms related to $\phi_{eff}(q)$, which favors short, low-energy motion and discourages unnecessary upward movement (positive lift) or detours.
 - The combined metric is $G(q) = (1 + \lambda_{safe} \phi_{safe}(q) + \lambda_{eff} \phi_{eff}(q))I + \lambda_{up} \phi_{eff}(q)aa^T$, and the potential $V(q) = \lambda_{goal} d(q, G(g))^2 + \lambda_{safe} \phi_{safe}(q) + \lambda_{eff} \phi_{eff}(q)$.
- **Practical Solver:** The planning process is executed in three steps:
 1. Convert the 3D world state W into cost fields for goal, safety, and efficiency.
 2. Perform an A* graph search on a voxelized representation of the 3D world to find a globally feasible *seed path*. The edge cost for the search incorporates the metric G(q) and potential V(q).
 3. Refine this discrete seed path into a continuous curve by optimizing a small set of control points using a gradient-based solver, minimizing the objective function E(τ) which includes terms for metric (E_G), potential (E_V), dynamics/smoothness (E_dyn), and anchors (E_anchor). For video dynamics editing, E_anchor also incorporates source 3D tracks.
 The optimized curve is then resampled into a frame-level trajectory $\tau^\ast$.

#### 3.4. Generation: 3D-Aware Video Rendering

The final stage takes the first frame, a text prompt, and the planned frame-level tracking controls (the optimized trajectory $\tau^\ast$) to render a photorealistic video. OptiWorld is generator-agnostic, meaning it can interface with any suitable 3D-trajectory-controlled image-to-video model. For this work, Diffusion as Shader (DaS) is used, adapted to accept metric 3D points as control signals.

- For goal-conditioned I2V, the planned 3D path for the selected object is used. Points within the object mask are translated according to the path, while background points remain static. This creates a dense, camera-aware, and depth-aware tracking video used to condition DaS.
- For video dynamics editing, the optimized 3D tracks (which combine autonomous planning with source motion cues) replace the raw foreground motion.

This three-stage pipeline ensures that the generated video benefits from both strong visual generation capabilities and an explicit, physically-aware decision-making layer.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research evaluates OptiWorld across two main tasks: goal-conditioned image-to-video (I2V) generation and video dynamics editing, using custom benchmarks (OptiBench-I2V and OptiBench-V2V). Evaluation includes both quantitative metrics for motion and video quality, and qualitative visual comparisons.

#### 4.1. Goal-Conditioned Image-to-Video Generation

- **Quantitative Results (Table 2):** OptiWorld demonstrates superior performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines including HunyuanVideo-1.5, Wan2.2, Cosmos-Predict2.5, and VLIPP. It achieves the lowest values for:
 - **Goal Error:** 0.310 (compared to 0.331 for VLIPP, the next best, and much higher for others).
 - **Violations@Success:** 0.260 (significantly lower than 0.286 for Cosmos-Predict2.5, and over 0.5 for others), indicating fewer safety breaches among successful goal-reaching instances.
 - **Acceleration and Jerk:** 0.0070 and 0.0116 respectively, reflecting smoother trajectories.
 - **Energy:** 0.0126, indicating more efficient motion.
 - **VBench Video Quality:** OptiWorld also reports the highest scores for motion smoothness (0.997), background consistency (0.982), and flicker (0.996), suggesting that the planned optimal control does not degrade visual quality and may even improve it due to smoother motion.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 3):** Visual comparisons illustrate that baselines often produce plausible but unsafe (e.g., cutting through outlets/hazards), inefficient, or less accurate motions. OptiWorld, by planning a 3D path under explicit constraints, generates videos with more direct goal reaching, fewer safety violations, and noticeably smoother trajectories.

#### 4.2. Video Dynamics Editing

- **Quantitative Results (Table 3):** OptiWorld effectively refines existing video dynamics:
 - **Track Deviation:** 0.113, showing controlled deviation from the source track for improvement while preserving overall intent. This is slightly better than Wan2.1 FLF2V (0.123).
 - **Acceleration and Jerk:** Reduces these metrics to 0.0107 and 0.0181 respectively, indicating smoother object motion compared to the source video (0.0124, 0.0205) and Wan2.1 FLF2V (0.0157, 0.0266).
 - **Path Length and Energy:** Reduces path length to 0.524 and energy to 0.0236, demonstrating more efficient trajectories than the source video (0.647, 0.0269) and Wan2.1 FLF2V (0.733, 0.0387).
 - **VBench Video Quality:** Maintains high video quality, with scores comparable to or slightly better than baselines, suggesting that dynamic improvements do not compromise visual integrity.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 4):** Visual examples confirm that OptiWorld improves source trajectories by making them smoother and more efficient, while maintaining the scene identity and task instruction.

#### 4.3. Counterfactual Physics Generation

OptiWorld demonstrates the ability to generate counterfactual motions by altering planner parameters. Changing the goal or adjusting constraint weights (e.g., setting safety constraint weight to zero) results in different, quantitatively distinct trajectories for the same scene. This capability allows for creating multi-physics videos that can be valuable for training or evaluating robot policies under controlled physical variations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirms the contribution of each stage and component of the OptiWorld pipeline (Table 4, Figure 8):
- **Understanding (w/o VLM):** Removing VLM reasoning degrades motion regularity, highlighting the importance of task-dependent semantic constraint inference.
- **Planning (w/o Manifold, w/o Safe, w/o Eff., w/o Smooth):**
 - Replacing the constraint-induced manifold with a flatter Euclidean objective ("w/o Man.") significantly increases safety violations.
 - Removing safety, efficiency, or smoothness terms individually leads to increases in safety violations, energy, or acceleration/jerk respectively.
- **Generation (2D guidance):** Using 2D optical-flow guidance instead of 3D trajectory guidance worsens final video metrics across goal error, safety violations, and energy.

These results collectively support the integrated pipeline design, confirming that each module contributes to achieving physically optimal and high-quality video generation.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

OptiWorld represents a notable advancement in the field of video generation and world modeling by explicitly integrating optimal control principles. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift from Plausibility to Optimality:** The primary significance of OptiWorld is its ability to transition video generation models beyond merely predicting "plausible" future states to actively *optimizing* for "preferable" dynamics. This framework demonstrates that video world models can be engineered to *choose* futures that adhere to specific physical constraints and objectives (safety, smoothness, efficiency) rather than just reproducing patterns observed in training data. This moves towards a more proactive and decision-making capability in generative AI.

- **Enhanced Controllability and Physical Consistency:** By introducing an explicit optimal control layer, OptiWorld significantly improves the controllability and physical consistency of generated video content. The generated object motions are not only visually realistic but also safer, smoother, more efficient, and physically more accurate. This addresses a critical limitation of many current video generative models.

- **Methodological Innovation for Constraint Handling:** The novel approach of formulating optimal planning as a geometric shortest-path problem on a continuous Riemannian manifold is a key methodological contribution. This allows for the unified modeling of heterogeneous physical constraints—including 3D geometry, goal-reaching, safety, smoothness, and efficiency—within a single, continuous geometric framework. This provides a robust and generalizable solution for motion planning in open visual scenes, overcoming the brittleness of traditional rule-based planners or direct, non-convex optimization of separate penalties.

- **Versatile Applications:** OptiWorld demonstrates its effectiveness across multiple practical applications:
 - **Goal-Conditioned Image-to-Video Generation:** Users can specify a goal, and the system generates a video showing an object moving towards it optimally. This is valuable for content creation, interactive simulations, and robotics.
 - **Video Dynamics Editing:** The ability to take an existing video and refine the motion of objects within it to be smoother, safer, or more efficient has implications for post-production, animation, and creating physically corrected simulations.
 - **Counterfactual Physics Generation:** The capacity to generate different physical outcomes by simply changing planning parameters (e.g., goal, safety constraints) opens avenues for creating diverse simulation data. This is particularly impactful for training and evaluating robot policies, allowing controlled variations of physics and constraints in a generated world.

- **Leveraging Foundation Models:** OptiWorld's modular architecture, which leverages strong zero-shot vision foundation models (for geometry estimation, segmentation, and VLM reasoning), makes it highly generalizable. It does not require retraining for every new object category, scene type, or task setting, which is a significant practical advantage.

- **Future Directions for World Models:** The work highlights a crucial direction for the development of future "world models"—that they should not merely forecast plausible events but also possess the intelligence to reason about, optimize, and *choose* physically sound and desirable outcomes. This could pave the way for more intelligent AI systems capable of planning and interacting with the physical world.

**Limitations and Broader Impacts:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including that the final video quality remains dependent on the underlying renderer, which can introduce artifacts, deformation, or imperfect texture. The current system is also designed as an offline inference pipeline, suggesting a need for real-time integration for interactive applications.

Regarding broader impacts, while OptiWorld enables high-quality content generation and educational videos, the capability to produce highly convincing AI-generated videos necessitates clear content markers to mitigate the risk of spreading misinformation.
