# Review of Hallucination Understanding in Large Language and Vision Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This review from Nanyang Technological University introduces the Model–Observer–World–Input (MOWI) framework to systematically define and categorize hallucinations in Large Language, Vision-Language, and Text-to-Vision Models. It maps these phenomena to their underlying causes across the entire model lifecycle, aiming to provide a unified understanding beyond reactive mitigation.

## Problem

- Existing research largely focuses on mitigating AI hallucinations, lacking a principled understanding of their underlying causes.
- The field suffers from fragmented conceptualizations of hallucinations, often using narrow or task-specific definitions.
- This fragmentation impedes the development of generalizable and robust solutions for generative AI risks.

## Method

- Proposes the ==Model-Observer-World-Input (MOWI) framework==, a general, multi-level definition for characterizing both image and text hallucinations across diverse applications.
- Systematically surveys hallucination causes by tracing them across ==five distinct stages== of a model's operational lifecycle: ==training data==, ==architectural limitations==, ==inference mechanisms==, ==loss and optimization==, and ==misleading evaluations==.
- Unifies the four levels of the MOWI framework into a ==formal probabilistic definition== to quantify the ==divergence== between learned, real, and observer-perceived distributions, accounting for world uncertainty and input quality.

## Results

- Models tend to hallucinate when operating outside their training data distributions, exacerbated by data scarcity, limited diversity, structural misalignment, or memorization over generalization.
- Hallucinations are significantly driven by inherited biases present in training data, evaluation processes, and model interactions, including effects from self-consumption and multi-agent debates.
- Fine-grained internal dynamics, such as architectural flaws (e.g., attention glitches, autoregressive constraints) and optimization trajectories (e.g., oscillatory knowledge acquisition, reward hacking), critically shape hallucination patterns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hallucinations are systematic, predictable outcomes deeply rooted in the entire lifecycle of generative AI models, from data and architecture to training and evaluation.
> - Current evaluation practices are often misleading, propagating a false sense of progress and failing to detect or address hallucinatory tendencies effectively.
> - Addressing hallucinations requires a shift from reactive symptom management to proactive, principle-driven prevention grounded in a unified understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The proliferation and widespread adoption of Large Language Models (LLMs), Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), and Text-to-Vision Models (TVMs) in real-world applications have brought to the forefront the critical issue of "hallucinations." These refer to instances where AI models generate outputs that are incorrect, inconsistent, or nonsensical, leading to significant risks such as misinformation, flawed code, or unreliable media. While extensive research has been dedicated to *mitigating* these hallucinations, the authors highlight a crucial gap: a comprehensive and unified *understanding* of their underlying causes.

This paper positions itself as a critical synthesis effort within this landscape. Existing surveys (e.g., Ji et al., Lin et al., Huang et al. for language hallucinations; Bai et al., Sahoo et al., Kamali et al. for multimodal hallucinations) are critiqued for largely focusing on mitigation strategies, evaluation metrics, or for adopting "narrow or anecdotal definitions tailored to specific tasks or modalities." Such fragmentation, the authors argue, impedes the development of generalizable and robust solutions, as efforts might only address surface symptoms rather than fundamental mechanisms.

By proposing a "unified, multi-level framework" and tracing hallucination causes to "identifiable mechanisms within a model’s lifecycle" across various modalities (text and vision), this work aims to provide a more holistic and principled understanding. It moves beyond descriptive categorization of errors to an analytical investigation of their genesis, identifying shared failure patterns rather than focusing on task-specific anomalies. In doing so, it seeks to systematize the fragmented knowledge base, offering a foundational conceptual framework that can guide future research towards more effective and proactive solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to address two critical gaps in the current understanding of AI hallucinations:
1. **Limited Understanding:** Much of the existing research on hallucinations is reactive, focusing on mitigation strategies in response to observed failures rather than being grounded in a principled understanding of *why* they occur. This limits the effectiveness and generalizability of proposed solutions.
2. **Fragmentation and Insufficient Systematization:** The field lacks a coherent and unified conceptualization of hallucinations. Different studies often use narrow, task-specific, or anecdotal definitions, making it difficult to identify common underlying causes across diverse models and modalities.

To tackle these gaps, the authors set forth three key objectives:
1. **Introduce a Unified Framework:** To reduce conceptual fragmentation, they propose a general, multi-level framework for characterizing both image and text hallucinations across diverse applications. This framework is designed to be modality- and task-agnostic, improving coverage and applicability.
2. **Comprehensive Survey of Causes:** To provide a deeper understanding, the paper aims to present an exhaustive survey of hallucination causes across LLMs, LVLMs, and TVMs. This review is structured in a modality-interleaved fashion, deliberately avoiding rigid task delineations to identify shared failure patterns. Crucially, this survey grounds the causes in identifiable mechanisms within a model's lifecycle.
3. **Consolidate Insights and Propose Future Directions:** The final objective is to synthesize the findings from the survey, identify recurring themes, and suggest concrete future research directions that leverage a more integrated understanding of hallucinations to develop generalizable and effective solutions.

The ultimate goal is to move from reactive symptom management to proactive, principle-driven prevention, thereby reducing the risks posed by generative AI systems in real-world deployments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a systematic review methodology, structured around a novel conceptual framework and a lifecycle-based analysis of hallucination mechanisms.

The core of their approach is the **Model–Observer–World–Input (MOWI) framework**, which offers a general, four-level definition of hallucinations:
- **Model Level:** Hallucinations arise from density estimation errors where the model's learned distribution ($P_\theta(x|c)$) diverges from the true data distribution ($P_{real}(x|c)$). This includes interpolation errors (sampling within high-probability regions but missing variations) and extrapolation errors (sampling from regions with no real density).
- **Observer Level:** Hallucinations occur when a model's output ($x \sim P_\theta(x|c)$) diverges from an observer’s viewpoint ($P_{O_i}(x|c)$), reflecting individual epistemic frameworks. This accounts for subjectively perceived inconsistencies or unhelpfulness.
- **World Level:** Hallucinations stem from epistemic (model lacks data to approximate $P_{real}(x|c,t)$) and aleatoric (inherent randomness in $P_{real}(x|c,t)$) uncertainty. This encompasses limitations due to inaccessible, esoteric, or time-sensitive knowledge.
- **Input Level:** Hallucinations are triggered when the conditioning variable $c$ is sparse, contradictory, or out-of-distribution, forcing the model to operate beyond its learned priors, especially in interactive, evolving contexts.

These four levels are then unified into a formal probabilistic definition of hallucination, $P_{Hal}(x|c, O_i)$, which quantifies the divergence between learned, real, and observer-perceived distributions, along with the influence of world uncertainty and input quality. This framework is then applied to various challenging scenarios across different modalities (LLMs, LVLMs, TVMs) to demonstrate its broad applicability.

Following the MOWI framework, the authors systematically investigate the root causes and mechanisms of hallucinations by tracing them across **five distinct stages of a model's operational lifecycle**:
1. **Training Data Factors:** Examines how the quality, frequency, diversity, structural alignment, memorization tendencies, self-consumption (training on synthetic data), and directional asymmetries in pretraining data contribute to hallucinations.
2. **Architectural Limitations:** Discusses inherent weaknesses in model architectures, such as attention glitches (e.g., U-shaped attention, attention sinks), autoregressive constraints (limiting bidirectional understanding, causal reasoning), incorrect positional encoding (affecting long-context performance), and various inductive biases (e.g., harmonic priors in TVMs, permutation symmetry in transformers).
3. **Inference Mechanisms:** Analyzes how failures emerge during model deployment, including issues related to few-shot prompting quality (sensitivity to noisy or repetitive examples), multi-agent debates (amplifying biases, poor meta-reasoning, uncooperative behavior), and exposure bias (error accumulation from conditioning on self-generated outputs).
4. **Loss and Optimisation:** Explores how optimization dynamics during pretraining and post-training stages (instruction tuning, RLHF, domain finetuning) can introduce vulnerabilities. This includes oscillatory knowledge acquisition, catastrophic forgetting, instruction overfitting, reward hacking, shortcut learning (simplicity bias), and heterogeneous preferences (single-reward models failing to capture diverse human viewpoints).
5. **Misleading Evaluations:** Investigates how flaws in evaluation practices can mask or even exacerbate hallucinations. This covers metric blind spots (e.g., FID, perplexity failing to capture true quality), biased judges (model-as-a-judge preferences for verbosity, self-similarity; human cognitive biases), and test contamination (leakage of test data into training sets, leading to illusory performance gains).

By structuring their review in this comprehensive, lifecycle-interleaved manner, the authors aim to reveal systemic connections between seemingly disparate hallucination phenomena and their underlying mechanisms across different generative AI models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive survey and the proposed MOWI framework lead to several critical insights, consolidating into four broad themes that explain hallucinations not as sporadic errors, but as structural and predictable outcomes of model design, training, and usage:

1. **Hallucinations Stem from Operating Outside Training Distributions:** A pervasive finding is that models tend to hallucinate when pushed beyond the statistical boundaries of their training data. This manifests in various ways:
 - **Data Scarcity and Rarity:** Low-frequency concepts or rare tasks in the pretraining data lead to increased hallucinations (Model-level).
 - **Limited Diversity:** Insufficient diversity in training tasks or compositional patterns hinders generalization, making models prone to failure on novel but related tasks (Model-level).
 - **Structural Misalignment:** When input structures or task demands diverge from what was learned during pretraining, models struggle, often resorting to interpolative "uncanny artifacts" (e.g., in TVMs for disjoint modes) (Model-level, Input-level).
 - **Memorization Over Generalization:** High data specificity or uniqueness in training data increases the likelihood of memorization, which can impede generalization to novel compositions or reasoning chains (Model-level).

2. **Hallucinations Are Driven by Inherited Biases:** Models internalize and amplify biases present in their data, training incentives, and interactions, leading to predictable failure patterns:
 - **Directional Asymmetries:** LLMs and LVLMs exhibit biases in understanding relationships more reliably in one direction than the other, often reflecting inherent biases in natural language (Model-level).
 - **Evaluation Biases:** Human annotators, model-as-a-judge systems, and reward models in RLHF introduce biases (e.g., favoring verbosity, self-similarity, or superficial cues), which can inadvertently reinforce ungrounded or idiosyncratic outputs during alignment (Observer-level).
 - **Self-Consumption:** Training on synthetic data (model's own output) can lead to a loss of alignment with real-world distributions and a decline in diversity, promoting new forms of hallucinations (Model-level).
 - **Multi-Agent Debates:** While intended for improvement, multi-LLM debates can amplify shared misconceptions, demonstrate poor meta-reasoning, and even lead to uncooperative behaviors, converging to erroneous consensus (Observer-level, Input-level).

3. **Internal Dynamics Shape Hallucinations:** Fine-grained internal dynamics during architectural design and optimization stages, often overlooked, critically influence whether models achieve robust generalization or systemic hallucinations:
 - **Architectural Flaws:** Attention mechanisms can suffer from "glitches" (e.g., U-shaped attention, attention sinks), leading to positional sensitivity or inadequate focus. Autoregressive constraints hinder bidirectional understanding. Misconfigured positional encodings impair long-context comprehension. Implicit inductive biases (e.g., toward permutation symmetry in transformers, harmonic structures in TVMs) can lead to suboptimal learning for tasks misaligned with these priors (Model-level, Input-level).
 - **Optimization Trajectories:** Pretraining dynamics can lead to oscillatory knowledge acquisition (acquire-forget cycles for factual knowledge) or competition between task recognition and task learning. Post-training phases like instruction tuning can result in overfitting to stylistic patterns, and RLHF can be susceptible to "reward hacking" due to aggressive optimization, sparse preferences, or human biases in reward signals. Shortcut learning, where models learn "easy" but brittle solutions, further compromises generalization (Model-level).

4. **Evaluation Reform is Essential:** Current evaluation practices are often misleading, propagating a false sense of progress and failing to detect or address hallucinatory tendencies:
 - **Metric Blind Spots:** Widely used metrics like FID for images or perplexity for language models often do not align with human perceptual judgment or real-world task performance, particularly in long-context or complex reasoning scenarios (Observer-level).
 - **Biased Judges:** Both model-as-a-judge systems and human annotators are susceptible to various cognitive and systemic biases (e.g., verbosity preference, self-similarity bias, framing effects, conflating quality dimensions), which can distort feedback signals during development (Observer-level).
 - **Test Contamination:** The unintentional leakage of test data into training sets undermines benchmark credibility, leading to illusory performance gains and masking true generalization failures (Model-level).

In essence, the findings reveal that hallucinations are not random anomalies but rather systematic and predictable artifacts, deeply rooted in the entire lifecycle of generative AI models, from their data and architecture to their training and evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive review carries significant implications for the future development and deployment of generative AI systems:

1. **Unified Conceptualization:** The MOWI framework provides a much-needed, modality-agnostic lens for diagnosing and categorizing hallucinations. This unified understanding can foster more coherent discourse across diverse research communities (NLP, CV, ML Theory), reducing fragmentation and allowing for the identification of common underlying mechanisms across different model types and tasks. This is crucial for collaborative and interdisciplinary efforts to tackle hallucination effectively.

2. **Foundation for Robust Solutions:** By systematically tracing hallucinations to their root causes across the model lifecycle, the paper lays a principled foundation for developing more robust, generalizable, and proactive mitigation strategies. Instead of reacting to symptoms, researchers can now target fundamental issues in data curation, architectural design, optimization objectives, and evaluation methodologies. For instance, understanding architectural inductive biases or the dynamics of catastrophic forgetting can lead to more targeted interventions during model training.

3. **Improved Model Reliability and Trust:** The paper directly addresses the "urgent need to address hallucinations" in real-world applications. By deepening understanding, it contributes to building more reliable AI systems that are less prone to propagating misinformation, generating flawed content, or exhibiting unpredictable failures. This will be critical for increasing public trust and enabling the safe and ethical deployment of generative AI in sensitive domains (e.g., healthcare, finance, legal).

4. **Guiding Future Research:** The "Future Directions" section is particularly impactful, outlining several promising avenues:
 - **Data Distribution Adaptation:** Leveraging machine teaching and test-time adaptation to ensure models remain grounded within their intended data distributions, particularly for rare or expert tasks.
 - **Deeper Mechanistic Understanding:** Advocating for mechanistic interpretability to uncover the computational anatomy of intelligence in neural networks, alongside formalizing how abstractions arise in both human and machine cognition. This includes exploring hybrid neuro-symbolic approaches for tasks where parametric models prove brittle.
 - **Evaluation Reform:** Emphasizing the adoption of proactive red-teaming strategies, reframing hallucinations as part of a Pareto trade-off, and integrating Social Choice Theory to account for diverse observer-perceived hallucinations. This pushes for a shift towards evaluation paradigms that genuinely reflect real-world performance and user expectations.

5. **Highlighting Unexplored Modalities:** The acknowledgement of limitations, particularly the current focus on text and the need for deeper exploration of visual and multimodal dimensions, as well as less common modalities like audio, serves as a clear call to action for future research.

In summary, this survey moves the field beyond a superficial cataloging of errors to a foundational understanding of *why* generative AI models hallucinate. Its impact lies in providing a robust conceptual and analytical framework that will guide the development of the next generation of more reliable, transparent, and trustworthy AI systems.
