# Do You Need Proprioceptive States in Visuomotor Policies?

> [!summary] Summary
> Robotic visuomotor policies achieve dramatically improved spatial generalization by removing proprioceptive state inputs. This approach leverages a relative end-effector action space and comprehensive egocentric vision to enable robust task performance across varied spatial configurations, while also enhancing data efficiency and cross-embodiment adaptation.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor policies, reliant on proprioceptive states, show limited spatial generalization, struggling with variations in object placement or robot starting positions.
- Proprioceptive states encourage policies to overfit by memorizing specific training trajectories, preventing the learning of a generalizable visual understanding.
- Addressing generalization issues in state-based policies typically requires extensive and costly data collection across numerous spatial configurations.

## Method

- Introduces "==State-free Policies==" by completely omitting ==proprioceptive state inputs== from the visuomotor policy.
- Employs a ==relative end-effector (EEF) action space==, allowing actions to be invariant to the robot's absolute pose and dependent only on visual observations.
- Utilizes ==dual wide-angle wrist-cameras== to ensure full, egocentric visual observation of the task environment, critical for policies relying solely on visual cues.

## Results

- State-free Policies achieved up to **98%** height generalization and **58%** horizontal generalization success rates for manipulation tasks, compared to **0%** for state-based policies.
- The method consistently improved spatial generalization across **5 real-world tasks**, multiple robot embodiments, and various policy architectures (π0, ACT, Diffusion Policy).
- Demonstrated enhanced data efficiency for in-domain tasks and faster cross-embodiment adaptation, requiring less fine-tuning data for new robot platforms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprioceptive states, while seemingly beneficial, can act as a "shortcut" that limits a policy's ability to generalize spatially.
> - A relative end-effector action space is crucial for enabling spatial invariance in vision-only policies, ensuring consistent actions despite absolute pose changes.
> - Comprehensive egocentric vision from wide-angle wrist cameras can be more effective for spatial generalization than traditional world-centric overhead cameras, especially in out-of-domain scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements through imitation learning (IL)-based visuomotor policies. These policies typically leverage large-scale demonstration datasets and powerful pre-trained models to achieve remarkable performance across various real-world tasks. A common practice in this domain is to feed both visual observations (camera images) and proprioceptive states (e.g., end-effector poses, joint angles) into the policy for precise and reliable control.

This paper directly challenges a fundamental assumption within this established practice: the necessity of proprioceptive states for robust generalization. The authors argue that while state inputs offer compact and accurate information about the robot's configuration, they inadvertently create a "shortcut" for the policy. Instead of developing a deep understanding of the visual environment, the policy becomes overly reliant on memorizing specific training trajectories tied to these absolute proprioceptive states. This leads to severe limitations in *spatial generalization*, meaning the robot struggles when task-relevant objects or the robot's starting configuration are varied in space—a critical bottleneck for real-world deployment where perfect consistency is rare.

The broader research landscape has explored several avenues to address this spatial generalization problem:
- **Data-driven approaches:** Collecting vast and diverse demonstration data across many spatial configurations. This is often prohibitively expensive and time-consuming in real-world settings.
- **Simulation-based methods:** Training policies in simulation with domain randomization to bridge the sim-to-real gap. While promising, challenges remain in achieving perfect transfer to the physical world.
- **Representation-driven approaches:** Developing object-centric features to isolate task-relevant information. These often require complex perception pipelines.
- **Architectural approaches:** Introducing physical symmetries into models to promote invariance. However, these assumptions can sometimes break down in messy real-world conditions.
- **Regularization techniques:** Penalizing absolute position encoding to encourage relational reasoning. These add training complexity with often limited gains.

Against this backdrop, Zhao et al. propose a *simpler and more practical solution*: completely removing proprioceptive state inputs. This work distinguishes itself by directly tackling the perceived root cause of generalization issues (over-reliance on state) rather than adding complexity or requiring extensive data. By demonstrating that vision-only policies, when designed correctly, can outperform state-based counterparts in generalization, this paper prompts a re-evaluation of fundamental architectural choices in visuomotor policy design. It pushes the frontier towards truly vision-centric robotic control, paving the way for more adaptable and scalable robot learning systems that depend less on precise absolute state information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to significantly enhance the **spatial generalization ability** of visuomotor policies in robot manipulation. The authors aim to enable robots to reliably perform tasks even when the spatial configuration of task-relevant objects or the robot's initial pose differs from the training data.

The core motivation stems from a critical observation regarding current visuomotor policies:
- **Overfitting to Proprioceptive States:** The common practice of incorporating proprioceptive state inputs (e.g., end-effector poses, joint angles) alongside visual observations, while intended for precise control, makes policies prone to overfitting. The policy tends to memorize specific training trajectories and directly associate absolute states with expert actions, rather than learning a robust understanding of the visual cues in the task environment.
- **Limited Spatial Generalization:** This overfitting severely restricts the policy's ability to generalize to new spatial layouts or variations in object locations, leading to poor performance in out-of-domain scenarios (e.g., different table heights or horizontal shifts).
- **High Data Collection Costs:** To counteract this overfitting, state-based policies require highly diverse demonstration datasets that cover a wide range of spatial configurations. Collecting such data is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming in the real world, posing a major bottleneck for practical robot deployment.

Driven by these challenges, the paper proposes a radical solution: completely removing proprioceptive state inputs, leading to "State-free Policies." The specific objectives, therefore, include:
1. **Validate the "Shortcut" Hypothesis:** Empirically demonstrate that proprioceptive state inputs are indeed a primary factor limiting spatial generalization, even showing that simple hacks or noise augmentation on states can improve performance.
2. **Develop Enabling Conditions:** Identify and implement the necessary conditions for State-free Policies to function effectively, specifically the use of a relative end-effector (EEF) action space and ensuring full task-relevant visual observation.
3. **Demonstrate Superior Spatial Generalization:** Show that State-free Policies, under these conditions, achieve significantly stronger height and horizontal generalization compared to state-based policies across diverse real-world tasks and robot embodiments.
4. **Highlight Practical Benefits:** Demonstrate that State-free Policies also offer advantages in data efficiency (requiring less data for fine-tuning) and cross-embodiment adaptation (easier transfer to different robots).
5. **Rethink Sensor Design:** Investigate the role of traditional sensing components like overhead cameras and potentially propose more efficient and effective sensor configurations for future visuomotor systems.

In essence, the motivation is to simplify the input to visuomotor policies by removing potentially misleading information (absolute states), thereby forcing the policy to learn a more fundamental, generalizable understanding of the task based purely on visual cues, ultimately leading to more robust and deployable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this research revolves around the development and empirical validation of "State-free Policies," which fundamentally diverge from conventional visuomotor policy design by *omitting proprioceptive state inputs*. This approach is built upon two critical conditions:

**A. Foundation of State-free Policies:**

1. **Relative End-Effector (EEF) Action Space:** The policy is designed to predict relative displacements of the end-effector (∆x, ∆q) rather than absolute poses or joint angle changes.
 - **Rationale:** The authors argue that among various action spaces, the relative EEF action space naturally supports spatial generalization. An action (∆p) depends solely on the current visual observation, not on the robot's absolute pose. This invariance ensures that identical visual observations result in the same desired relative motion, regardless of the robot's absolute position, which is crucial for adapting to different spatial configurations.
 - **Comparison:** Experiments explicitly compare this to absolute EEF, relative joint-angle, and absolute joint-angle action spaces, demonstrating the catastrophic failure of other representations in generalization tasks. Relative joint-angle actions, for instance, lead to different end-effector displacements for the same joint changes if the current joint configuration varies, hindering spatial invariance.

2. **Full Task Observation:** Since State-free Policies rely solely on visual inputs, they demand comprehensive visual information about the task environment.
 - **Rationale:** Without state inputs acting as shortcuts, the policy must make decisions entirely from visual cues. Insufficient visual information would lead to ambiguity and failure.
 - **Implementation:** To achieve "full task observation," the primary camera setup employs **dual wide-angle wrist-cameras** (approx. 120° × 120° field of view) mounted on the top and bottom of the end-effector. This configuration significantly expands the field of view, ensuring that all task-relevant objects and the immediate workspace are visible, even in complex manipulation scenarios or when objects are directly beneath the end-effector. The paper also evaluates simpler camera setups (single normal wrist-cam, overhead cam only) to show the importance of full observation.

**B. Experimental Design and Evaluation:**

1. **Tasks and Robot Embodiments:**
 - **Real-world:** A diverse suite of 5 real-world tasks, including "Pick Pen," "Pick Bottle," "Put Lid" (Pick & Place type), "Fold Shirt" (challenging deformable object manipulation), and "Fetch Bottle (whole-body)" (complex task involving a 26 DoF robot with torso/waist motions).
 - **Robot Platforms:** Tasks span multiple robot embodiments: a 2x8 DoF human-like dual-arm robot, a 2x7 DoF dual-arm Arx5 system, and a 26 DoF whole-body robot.
 - **Simulation:** Evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark to assess performance in a controlled virtual environment.

2. **Data Collection Strategy:**
 - **Real-world data:** Collected using teleoperation by professional data collectors. Crucially, during training data collection, the table height was fixed, and task-relevant object locations were restricted to a narrow 2D range. This intentional constraint ensures that any observed spatial generalization ability is due to the policy's design, not merely diverse training data.
 - **Data Volume:** 300 trajectory episodes for "Pick & Place" tasks, 10,000 episodes for "Fold Shirt" and "Fetch Bottle (whole-body)."

3. **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Spatial Generalization:**
 - **Height Generalization:** Evaluated at out-of-domain table heights (e.g., 72 cm and 90 cm when trained at 80 cm).
 - **Horizontal Generalization:** Assessed by shifting target objects (e.g., pen holder, teacup) by 5 cm, 10 cm, or 15 cm laterally. For "Fold Shirt," this involved shifting arms.
 - **Success Rate:** Measured over 30 trials per condition, requiring the completion of the entire trajectory.
 - **In-domain Performance:** Also assessed to ensure that removing states does not compromise performance on familiar tasks.

4. **Policy Architectures:**
 - The primary policy architecture used is **π0**, a state-of-the-art visuomotor policy, leveraging its fine-tuning recipe.
 - To demonstrate the generality of the findings, additional experiments were conducted with **ACT (Action-conditioned Transformer)** and **Diffusion Policy**.

**C. Detailed Analysis and Further Investigations:**

- **Action Representation Analysis:** Comparative study of relative EEF, absolute EEF, and joint-angle (relative and absolute) action spaces.
- **Observation Level Analysis:** Evaluation of State-free Policies with varying camera setups (e.g., overhead only, single/dual normal wrist-cam, single/dual wide-angle wrist-cam, with/without overhead) to quantify the impact of "full task observation."
- **Policy Architecture Generality:** Testing State-free Policies across π
 0, ACT, and Diffusion Policy to confirm the universality of the conclusions.
- **Data Efficiency Study:** Experiments varying the amount of fine-tuning data for both state-based and State-free Policies to assess learning curves and robustness.
- **Cross-Embodiment Adaptation Study:** Transferring policies trained on one robot embodiment (Arx5) to another (human-like dual-arm robot) with limited fine-tuning to evaluate adaptation capabilities.
- **Rethinking Overhead Camera:** Specific experiments to investigate whether the overhead camera, despite being common, could be detrimental to generalization in challenging out-of-domain scenarios, especially when dual wide-angle wrist-cameras provide sufficient egocentric vision.

This comprehensive methodology, combining rigorous experimental controls, diverse tasks, and detailed analyses, allows the authors to thoroughly investigate their hypothesis and demonstrate the robust benefits of State-free Policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several significant findings that strongly support the efficacy and benefits of State-free Policies:

1. **Dramatic Improvement in Spatial Generalization:**
 - **Real-world "Pick & Place" Tasks:** State-free Policies exhibited profoundly superior spatial generalization. For instance, in the "Pick Pen" task, the average success rate increased from 0% to 98% in height generalization and from 0% to 58% in horizontal generalization (with dual wide-angle wrist cameras). Across three "Pick & Place" tasks, the average success rate improved from 0% to 85% in height generalization and from 6% to 64% in horizontal generalization.
 - **Challenging Real-world Tasks:** Even in complex tasks like "Fold Shirt" and "Fetch Bottle (whole-body)," State-free Policies (with normal wrist cameras) showed significantly stronger horizontal generalization, with success rates improving from 0.183 to 0.834 for "Fold Shirt" and 0.117 to 0.784 for "Fetch Bottle," respectively. This indicates the approach's robustness across different task complexities.

2. **Validation of Proprioceptive State as a Bottleneck:**
 - Initial experiments (Table I) confirmed that state-based policies completely failed in height generalization. Simple "hacks" like manually shifting states or adding noise significantly improved performance, directly indicating that state input was the critical factor limiting spatial generalization. This motivated the complete removal of state input.
 - Further analysis (Appendix Table VIII) showed that various optimization strategies for state-based policies (e.g., random noise augmentation, diverse data collection, task-mixed fine-tuning, LoRA) yielded only limited or no fundamental improvement in generalization, reinforcing that state inputs inherently act as a bottleneck.

3. **Crucial Role of Relative EEF Action Space and Full Task Observation:**
 - **Action Representation:** The relative EEF action space was found to be indispensable. Alternative action representations (absolute EEF, relative joint-angle, absolute joint-angle) resulted in disastrous spatial generalization performance (0% success rate), even with State-free Policies. This confirms that coupling vision-only input with actions invariant to absolute robot pose is vital.
 - **Full Task Observation:** The spatial generalization ability of State-free Policies directly correlated with the comprehensiveness of visual observation. Performance steadily improved as the camera's field of view expanded, culminating in the best results with dual wide-angle wrist-cameras (Table IV). This confirms that without state input, the policy critically relies on seeing the entire task-relevant scene.

4. **Generality Across Policy Architectures:**
 - The benefits of State-free Policies were consistently observed across different powerful visuomotor policy architectures, including π
 0, ACT, and Diffusion Policy (Table V). In all cases, State-free Policies exhibited much stronger spatial generalization than their state-based counterparts, demonstrating that the effectiveness of the approach is a general principle, not tied to a specific model implementation.

5. **Enhanced Data Efficiency:**
 - State-free Policies demonstrated significantly higher data efficiency for in-domain tasks. When fine-tuned with fewer demonstration episodes (e.g., 50 or 100), State-free Policies maintained substantially higher success rates compared to state-based policies, which quickly overfit and failed (Figure 6). This highlights their practicality for real-world deployment where data collection is costly.

6. **Improved Cross-Embodiment Adaptation:**
 - State-free Policies adapted much faster and achieved substantially higher success rates when fine-tuned from one robot embodiment (Arx5) to another (human-like dual-arm robot) with limited fine-tuning steps (Table VI). This is attributed to avoiding the need to align with new state spaces.

7. **Rethinking the Overhead Camera:**
 - A surprising finding was that the overhead camera, often considered a standard sensor, could be detrimental to spatial generalization, especially in challenging out-of-domain scenarios (e.g., extremely high table, shifted pen holder). State-free Policies without the overhead camera, relying solely on dual wide-angle wrist-cameras, achieved perfect or much higher success rates in these challenging scenarios (Table VII). This suggests that ego-centric wrist cameras, when comprehensive, provide sufficient and more consistent observations, while world-centric overhead cameras can introduce harmful distribution shifts.

In summary, the main findings unequivocally demonstrate that removing proprioceptive states, when combined with a relative EEF action space and full task observation via wide-angle wrist cameras, dramatically improves the spatial generalization of visuomotor policies without sacrificing in-domain performance. Furthermore, this approach offers significant advantages in data efficiency and cross-embodiment adaptation, leading to a profound re-evaluation of current practices in robot learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "Do You Need Proprioceptive States in Visuomotor Policies?" hold profound significance and potential impact across several dimensions of robotic learning and deployment:

1. **Challenging a Fundamental Paradigm:** The most significant contribution is the direct challenge to the widely accepted practice of integrating proprioceptive states into visuomotor policies for precise control. By rigorously demonstrating that these states can actually *hinder* generalization, the paper proposes a paradigm shift towards truly vision-centric robotic control. This forces the research community to re-evaluate what information is truly essential and what might be a detrimental "shortcut" in learned policies.

2. **Breaking the Generalization Bottleneck:** Spatial generalization has been a critical barrier to deploying robots in unstructured, real-world environments. This work offers a powerful, yet conceptually simple, solution to this problem. By enabling robots to reliably perform tasks across varying object locations and robot configurations, State-free Policies pave the way for more robust and versatile robot manipulation systems. This is crucial for applications ranging from manufacturing and logistics to service robotics and assistive technologies.

3. **Reducing Real-world Costs:**
 - **Data Collection:** The ability of State-free Policies to achieve strong generalization with less diverse training data directly addresses the prohibitive cost of real-world data collection. This reduces the time, labor, and resources needed to develop and fine-tune robotic skills, making advanced robot capabilities more accessible.
 - **Deployment & Adaptation:** Enhanced cross-embodiment adaptation means that policies trained on one robot can be transferred to different hardware with significantly less re-training. This streamlines the deployment process, reduces engineering effort, and lowers the cost of maintaining and upgrading robot fleets.

4. **Simplifying and Robustifying Policy Architectures:** By showing that complex regularization techniques or elaborate data augmentation strategies previously employed to improve generalization might be less necessary, the paper advocates for a more fundamental and robust approach. This could lead to simpler, more efficient, and easier-to-understand policy architectures, as the underlying learning mechanism is forced to rely on more generalizable visual reasoning.

5. **Informing Sensor Design and Robot Hardware:** The counter-intuitive finding that overhead cameras can be detrimental to generalization, while comprehensive egocentric (wrist) cameras are highly effective, inspires a rethinking of sensor suite design. Future robot designs might prioritize multiple wide-angle wrist cameras, potentially simplifying hardware, reducing costs, and improving the consistency of visual observations for learned policies. This could lead to more compact and agile robot systems.

6. **Foundation for Future Generalizable Systems:** The State-free Policy framework lays a crucial foundation for building truly general-purpose robotic learning systems. As robot learning moves towards foundational models and large behavior models, ensuring strong generalization is paramount. This work provides a core principle that can guide the development of such scalable and adaptable robot intelligence.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as sensitivity to background changes, these are identifiable areas for future research and do not diminish the profound implications of its core findings. Overall, this research is a pivotal step towards developing more intelligent, adaptable, and deployable robots, potentially accelerating their integration into a wider array of real-world applications.
