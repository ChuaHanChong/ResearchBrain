# C2-Evo: Co-Evolving Multimodal Data and Model for Self-Improving Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The C
2
-Evo framework introduces an automatic, closed-loop approach for jointly evolving multimodal data and model capabilities, specifically improving complex geometric reasoning in MLLMs. It achieves notable performance improvements, increasing geometric reasoning accuracy on Geo-Sub-Aux from 40.4% to 54.9% while utilizing less than 1% of the training data compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- High demand for costly, manually curated, and scale-challenged high-quality multimodal datasets for MLLM training, especially for complex reasoning tasks.
- Current self-improvement methods for MLLMs often result in misaligned complexity between visual and textual components of generated data.
- A static or unadaptive difficulty progression in existing self-improvement frameworks leads to inefficient training due to a mismatch between model capabilities and task complexity.

## Method

- An automatic, ==closed-loop C2-Evo framework== with interleaved co-evolution loops for multimodal data and model capabilities.
- A ==Cross-Modal Data Evolution Loop== utilizes advanced LLMs (GPT-4o, Doubao, DeepSeek-R1) and an image editing tool to generate visually and textually complex, aligned geometric reasoning problems.
- A ==Data and Model Co-evolution Loop== refines MLLMs through SFT to standardize reasoning and GRPO with accuracy/format rewards, applying ==adaptive error-based filtering== (error rate ">= 0.3") to select optimal training data.

## Results

- The Qwen2-VL-7B model, enhanced by C2-Evo, improved geometric reasoning accuracy on the Geo-Sub-Aux benchmark from **40.4%** to **54.9%** across **three** iterations.
- C2-Evo achieved strong performance using less than **1%** of the training data typically required by many prior reasoning models.
- Qualitative assessments confirmed that C2-Evo models generate more structured and logically sound step-by-step reasoning traces, reducing inaccuracies seen in base models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synchronous complexification of both visual and textual data components is vital for fostering robust, integrated multimodal reasoning in MLLMs.
> - Aligning training data difficulty with the model's current capability through adaptive error-based filtering is key for efficient and effective self-improvement.
> - Cumulative learning is maximized by iteratively refining the model (SFT followed by RL) and building upon the previously evolved model rather than restarting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated substantial capabilities in various reasoning tasks, including mathematics and coding. Strategies such as chain-of-thought prompting and tool-augmented reasoning have contributed to these developments, with reinforcement learning (RL) playing a role in aligning model outputs with desired behaviors through structured reward signals. Prominent MLLMs like BLIP-2, LLaVA, and MiniGPT-4 have shown strong generalization across tasks such as visual question answering and image-text reasoning, often leveraging high-quality visual instruction data.

However, the continued enhancement of MLLMs, particularly for complex reasoning, is limited by the availability of large-scale, high-quality datasets with carefully curated task complexities. Creating such datasets is resource-intensive and challenging to scale. This limitation has led to increased interest in self-improving paradigms, where models iteratively enhance their capabilities by generating and refining synthetic data and reasoning traces.

Existing self-improving approaches, such as OpenVLThinker, have explored alternating supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and RL for vision-language tasks. However, these methods often encounter two primary challenges:
1. **Mismatched Evolution of Modalities:** Visual and textual data components are frequently augmented in isolation, leading to discrepancies where, for instance, oversimplified diagrams are paired with complex textual descriptions, or vice versa. This can hinder the model's ability to develop integrated cross-modal reasoning.
2. **Discrepancy Between Model Capability and Task Difficulty:** Current frameworks typically rely on static or pre-defined difficulty schedules for training data, which do not adapt to the model's evolving capabilities. This can result in inefficient training, as the model may be under-challenged or overwhelmed by tasks with mismatched difficulty levels.

SyncLoop is positioned within this landscape as a self-improving framework designed to address these specific limitations by jointly evolving both training data and model capabilities in a closed-loop manner, with a focus on geometric reasoning. It aims to provide a more aligned and adaptive learning process compared to prior approaches that evolved modalities or difficulties separately.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an automatic, closed-loop self-improving framework, named SyncLoop, that jointly evolves both training data and model capabilities to enhance multimodal mathematical reasoning, particularly in geometric tasks.

The motivation for SyncLoop stems from two core challenges observed in existing self-improving MLLM paradigms:

1. **Addressing Mismatched Complexity in Multimodal Data:**
 - **Problem:** Current self-improvement methods often augment visual or textual data components independently. This leads to a disparity in complexity between the visual elements (e.g., diagrams) and the textual components (e.g., problem descriptions and reasoning steps). For instance, a visually simple diagram might be accompanied by an overly complex textual reasoning task, or a complex visual scene might lack a correspondingly intricate textual problem.
 - **Consequence:** This mismatch restricts an MLLM's ability to learn and apply integrated cross-modal reasoning strategies effectively. The model may struggle to connect nuanced visual information with abstract textual concepts if their complexity levels are not harmonized.
 - **SyncLoop's Motivation:** To overcome this, SyncLoop is motivated to create a framework that ensures the joint evolution of visual and textual complexity, ensuring that diagrams and textual problems are semantically aligned and progressively challenging in a synchronized manner.

2. **Resolving the Discrepancy Between Model Capability and Task Difficulty:**
 - **Problem:** As an MLLM undergoes iterative training and its capabilities improve, its optimal learning trajectory requires exposure to progressively more challenging tasks. However, existing self-improvement approaches typically employ static or manually defined difficulty schedules for their training data. These schedules do not dynamically adapt to the model's real-time performance or evolving skill set.
 - **Consequence:** This misalignment can lead to inefficient training scenarios. If the tasks are too easy, the model's learning stagnates. If they are excessively difficult, the model becomes overwhelmed, potentially hindering learning and generalization.
 - **SyncLoop's Motivation:** The framework is motivated to implement an adaptive mechanism that assesses the model's current performance and selects training data with an appropriate level of difficulty. This ensures that the model is consistently challenged at its "edge of competence," optimizing the learning process and leading to continuous improvement.

By addressing these two intertwined challenges, SyncLoop aims to facilitate more effective and efficient self-improvement in MLLMs for complex mathematical reasoning by creating a continuously refining cycle of data generation and model training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SyncLoop is designed as an automatic, closed-loop self-improving framework that jointly evolves both training data and model capabilities. It operates through two core interleaved loops: a cross-modal data evolution loop and a data-model evolution loop.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

The framework aims to improve a pre-trained MLLM, denoted as $\pi_\theta$, by optimizing its parameters $\theta$ on a dataset $D$ comprising image-question-answer triplets $(I_n, Q_n, G_n)$ over $T$ iterations. It assumes the availability of external tools and oracle models for code generation and image editing.

#### 3.2. Co-Evolving Multimodal Data Loop

This loop addresses the discrepancy between visual and textual data complexity by jointly synthesizing complex visual-textual problems.

- **Action and Reasoning Generation:**
 - Given an existing triplet $D^t_n = (I^t_n, Q^t_n, G^t_n)$, key points and coordinates are extracted from the image $I^t_n$ using OCR and vision-based techniques.
 - GPT-4o is prompted to determine if auxiliary image augmentations (e.g., drawing parallel, perpendicular, or connecting lines) are necessary to facilitate problem-solving.
 - If augmentations are needed, GPT-4o generates `Thought` (decision for augmentation type) and a corresponding Python code segment ($C^t_n$).
 - This code is executed using an external image editing tool (e.g., Jupyter) to produce a more complex image $I^{t+1}_n$ with the auxiliary augmentations.
 - Concurrently, GPT-4o is fed the pair ($I^t_n$, $Q^t_n$) to generate a detailed, step-by-step natural language reasoning trace $R^t_n$.

- **Challenging Question Generation:**
 - The newly augmented image $I^{t+1}_n$ is processed by the Doubao model to generate a formal description $F^t_n$, which provides precise geometric information.
 - A set of guiding principles (P) is defined to promote diversity and complexity in generated problems:
 1. **Geometric Constraints:** Extracting properties like perpendicularity or equality from $F^t_n$.
 2. **New Theorems and Concepts:** Incorporating relevant mathematical theorems (e.g., Pythagorean theorem based on right triangles) related to $F^t_n$.
 3. **Backward Reasoning:** Leveraging sub-steps from the inference trace $R^t_n$ (e.g., the Tangent-Secant theorem).
 - DeepSeek-R1 is then prompted with ($F^t_n, R^t_n, P$) to generate a set of $m$ diverse sub-problems ($q_1, \ldots, q_m$, typically 4-10).
 - Using a "role-playing strategy" (from R1-Onevision) and analyzing sub-problem commonalities/differences, DeepSeek-R1 composes these into a more challenging, integrated question $Q^{t+1}_n$.
 - To obtain answers ($G^{t+1}_n$), DeepSeek-R1 is run three times with ($Q^{t+1}_n, F^t_n$), generating step-by-step reasoning traces and final answers. Only samples where all three answers are consistent are retained.
 - GPT-4o performs a final filtering step to remove questions inconsistent with the original image constraints.
 - The resulting new triplet $D^{t+1}_n = (I^{t+1}_n, Q^{t+1}_n, G^{t+1}_n)$ is added to the dataset, characterized by increased reasoning length, indicating escalating difficulty.

#### 3.3. Co-Evolving Data and Model Loop

This loop adaptively aligns task difficulty with the model's capabilities through iterative training and filtering.

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - The model is initially fine-tuned using SFT on the data $D^{t+1}$ generated by the cross-modal data evolution loop.
 - The objective is to teach the model to generate reasoning traces and answers in a standardized format (`<think></think><answer></answer>`), essential for subsequent RL.
 - The SFT loss is $L_{SFT} = -\mathbb{E}_{(I,Q,R)\sim D} [\log(\pi_\theta(R|I, Q)]$.

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
 - Following SFT, the model $\pi_{\theta,SFT}$ is further refined using GRPO.
 - Two types of reward rules are employed:
 1. **Accuracy Reward:** Based on the correctness of the final answer $A$ compared to the ground truth $G$.
 2. **Format Reward:** Penalizes deviations from the predefined structured format (`<think>...</think>`) and enforces sequential consistency in reasoning steps.
 - The GRPO objective is $L_{RL} = \mathbb{E} [\min (r_t(\theta)A_t, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)A_t) - \beta D_{KL}[\pi_\theta \| \pi_{old}]]$.

- **Iterative Refinements and Filtering:**
 - At each iteration $t$, SFT and RL training update the model to $\pi^{t+1}_{\theta,RL}$.
 - **Error-Based Filtering:** To ensure data difficulty aligns with the evolving model capability, the model $\pi^{t}_{\theta,RL}$ (from the previous iteration) is used to evaluate the newly generated data $D^{t+1}$. Each sample is run 32 times, and an "error rate" (proportion of wrong answers) is calculated.
 - Only samples with an error rate $\geq 0.3$ are selected to form the training set for the *next* iteration. This criterion ensures that the selected problems are neither too easy (error rate < 0.3) nor likely beyond the model's current grasp, providing a meaningful challenge that promotes continuous learning.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

Experiments utilize Qwen2-VL-2B and Qwen2-VL-7B as base MLLMs. LLaMA-Factory is used for SFT (batch size 128, learning rate 1e-5), and VLM-R1 for RL (32 rollouts initially, then 8, temperature 0.9, KL coefficient $\beta=0$). All experiments are conducted on 32 NVIDIA V100-32GB GPUs. The framework is evaluated on Geo-Sub from Geometry3k, MathVista, and MathVerse, with a specialized Geo-Sub-Aux benchmark created by adding auxiliary lines to Geo-Sub images.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SyncLoop demonstrates performance improvements across multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including Geo-Sub from Geometry3k, MathVista (specifically on geometric reasoning (GEO), algebraic reasoning (ARI), and geometry problem solving (GPS) sub-tasks), and MathVerse.

- **Overall Performance Improvement:** SyncLoop consistently shows performance gains over the baseline Qwen2-VL-7B and Qwen2-VL-2B models across three iterations. For instance, the geometric reasoning accuracy (Geo-Sub-Aux for Qwen2-VL-7B) increased from a baseline of 40.4% to 52.4% after three iterations of SyncLoop training. On MathVista (ALL), Qwen2-VL-7B improved from 60.0% to 63.2%.
- **Data Efficiency:** The approach achieves performance levels comparable to closed-source models like GPT-4o while utilizing less than 1% of the training data that larger reasoning models often employ.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** A comparison of solutions for a parallelogram geometry problem indicated that SyncLoop generates more structured, logically coherent, and step-by-step reasoning processes than the Qwen2-VL-7B baseline. The baseline exhibited inaccuracies in formula application and computation, leading to an incorrect answer, while SyncLoop provided a correct and well-explained solution.
- **Influence of Iteration Strategies:**
 - Experiments (Table 3) showed that progressively refining the model using reinforcement learning in subsequent iterations yields improved results. However, applying additional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) *after* an initial SFT+RL phase within the same iteration, especially with an already RL-refined model, can lead to performance degradation. This suggests that the RL phase effectively aligns the model, and subsequent SFT might disrupt this alignment.
 - Comparing warm-starting from a previous iteration's refined model versus starting from the initial model (Table 4) indicated that using the progressively updated model (e.g., $\pi^1_\theta$ for the second iteration) leads to superior performance.
- **Influence of Error-Rate Filtering:** The error-based filtering method, which selects samples with an error rate $\geq 0.3$ (challenging but within the model's grasp), proved effective (Table 5). Using the full generated dataset (without filtering) or selecting samples with very high error rates (indicating excessive difficulty) did not optimize the model's reasoning capabilities as effectively as the targeted error rate range.
- **Influence of Training Data Complexity:** Training with complexified data, involving both complex geometric diagrams (with auxiliary lines) and complex textual reasoning, led to better performance compared to training with original (simpler) images paired with complex text (Table 6). This finding highlights the importance of jointly evolving both visual and textual complexities.
- **Data Difficulty Evolution:** Analysis of reasoning length (Figure 3) demonstrated that the generated problems progressively increased in difficulty over iterations. Evaluation of model performance on data from previous iterations (Figure A.3) also indicated that while current models solve older, simpler data more easily, there are signs of catastrophic forgetting on some older samples, underscoring the dynamic nature of learning and the need for adaptive data selection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SyncLoop presents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal large language models and automated reasoning by addressing critical limitations in their training and self-improvement processes.

- **Resolution of Key Challenges:** The framework offers a direct solution to the two major bottlenecks in MLLM development: the high cost and difficulty of acquiring high-quality, complexity-aligned multimodal datasets, and the misalignment between a model's evolving capabilities and the difficulty of its training tasks. By automating the joint evolution of data and model, SyncLoop reduces reliance on manual curation and static difficulty schedules.
- **Enhanced Cross-Modal Reasoning:** The cross-modal data evolution loop, which synthesizes complex geometric diagrams and corresponding challenging textual problems, promotes a more integrated form of vision-language reasoning. This approach ensures that models learn to correlate intricate visual information with complex logical steps, potentially leading to more robust and generalized reasoning abilities in multimodal contexts.
- **Efficient and Adaptive Learning:** The data-model co-evolution loop, particularly its error-based filtering mechanism, allows the model to continuously train on "just-right" difficulty problems. This adaptive learning strategy optimizes the training process by ensuring the model is consistently challenged without being overwhelmed, leading to more efficient and effective skill acquisition. The demonstrated performance gains with less than 1% of the data used by other models highlight its data efficiency.
- **Foundation for Future Self-Improvement Frameworks:** The insights gained from SyncLoop's experimental results, such as the effectiveness of different iteration strategies and data curation approaches, provide a foundation for designing more effective self-improving paradigms. The framework itself can serve as a template for developing similar systems in other domains.
- **Potential for Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated in geometric reasoning, the core principles of jointly evolving multimodal data complexity and adaptively aligning task difficulty with model capabilities could be extended to other challenging domains. This includes other areas of mathematical understanding (e.g., function analysis) and broader vision-language tasks, paving the way for more generally capable MLLMs.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By automating the creation of high-quality, complex training data, SyncLoop offers a method to mitigate the substantial resources typically required for MLLM development, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible for research and application.
