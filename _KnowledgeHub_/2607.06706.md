# Vision Language Action (VLA) Models for Unmanned Aerial Robotics and Bimanual Manipulation: A Review

> [!summary] Summary
> A review investigates Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models, analyzing their application in bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics (UAVs) to establish a unified analytical framework and demonstrate the transferability of coordination strategies and training methodologies between these robotic domains. The analysis synthesizes 183 contributions to identify shared machinery and future research directions.

## Problem

- Existing literature lacked a unified treatment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models across diverse robotic embodiments.
- Prior surveys failed to explicitly connect bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics as manifestations of the same VLA problem, hindering cross-domain insights.
- Both bimanual manipulation and aerial robotics present complex challenges in coordinating multiple degrees of freedom and actuators under visual observation and language instructions.

## Method

- A comprehensive literature review of 183 contributions (2017-2026) on ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== models was conducted.
- The review established a unified taxonomy for VLA models, categorizing ==architectural foundations==, ==training recipes==, and ==action representations== across bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics.
- A cross-domain analysis was performed to connect coordination strategies and advancements between bimanual systems and aerial robotics, highlighting shared challenges and transferable solutions.

## Results

- Flow matching-based VLAs demonstrate superior efficiency and performance in generating continuous action chunks for bimanual tasks.
- Reinforcement Learning from Autonomous Practice (RECAP) leads to substantial performance gains, such as increasing laundry folding success from **60% to over 90%** in bimanual settings.
- VLA architectures and training strategies show promise for unmanned aerial robotics, with emerging end-to-end drone VLA systems like CognitiveDrone achieving **77.2%** task success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Flow matching emerges as an effective and efficient action generation mechanism for VLA models in bimanual tasks, enabling continuous and high-dimensional action chunking.
> - Pre-training VLAs on internet-scale image-text data and utilizing reinforcement learning from autonomous practice (RECAP) are crucial for achieving semantic grounding and self-improvement beyond initial demonstrations.
> - Coordination strategies and architectural insights from bimanual manipulation are directly transferable and applicable to unmanned aerial systems, demonstrating a cross-embodiment nature of VLA principles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper presents a comprehensive review of Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models, positioning itself within the rapidly evolving field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics. VLA models represent a convergence of visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation within a single foundational model, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute corresponding actions based on camera input. This review specifically addresses the application of VLA models in two complex and distinct domains: bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics (UAVs or drones).

The authors identify a gap in existing literature, noting that previous surveys on foundation models for robotics, diffusion-based imitation learning, and multi-arm systems often focus on high-level planning or specific architectural aspects without providing a unified treatment of VLA across different embodiments. Critically, these prior works do not generally analyze bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics as two instances of the same underlying VLA problem.

This review aims to bridge this gap by offering a cross-domain analysis. It posits that coordination strategies, training methodologies, and action representations developed for bimanual manipulation systems are transferable and applicable to unmanned aerial systems. By synthesizing 183 contributions published between 2017 and 2026, the paper seeks to establish a common analytical framework for VLA models, highlighting the shared machinery and transferability of insights between these seemingly disparate robotic platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this review is to unify the understanding of Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models by analyzing their application across two distinct yet structurally similar robotic domains: bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics. The authors aim to provide a detailed, comprehensive overview of the architectural, training, and operational aspects of VLAs within this integrated perspective.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations regarding the current state and trajectory of VLA research:

1. **Emergence of VLA Models as a Dominant Framework:** VLA models, by leveraging internet-scale pre-training, have become a leading framework for learning-based robot control. They enable robots to follow natural language instructions directly from visual input, offering broad applicability in various settings such as homes, factories, and disaster response.

2. **Bimanual Manipulation as a Challenging Testbed:** Bimanual coordination, involving two multi-degree-of-freedom arms working in concert to perform tasks like folding or assembly, represents a demanding challenge for VLA models. The significant research effort concentrated on bimanual tasks has led to a deeper understanding of VLA architectures, training strategies, and action representations in this context.

3. **Structural Similarities with Unmanned Aerial Robotics:** The authors identify a structural analogy between bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics. Drones must coordinate thrust, attitude, and increasingly, gripper commands from visual observations under strict latency and payload constraints. This coordination challenge is analogous to that of orchestrating two robot arms or multiple agents from shared observations. The authors observe that VLA concepts developed for manipulation are being adopted in the aerial domain.

4. **Gap in Existing Literature:** As noted in the broader research landscape section, existing surveys do not provide a unified treatment of bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics as two manifestations of the same VLA problem. There is a need for a review that explicitly connects the insights and advancements across these two embodiment families.

To address these motivations, the review outlines three specific contributions:

- **Unified Taxonomy:** The establishment of a comprehensive taxonomy for VLA models, encompassing architectures, training methodologies, action representations, and their application to both bimanual manipulation and UAVs. This includes comparison tables for over 30 methods.
- **Cross-Domain Analysis:** The presentation of the first cross-domain analysis that connects coordination strategies from bimanual systems to multi-drone and aerial manipulation systems, demonstrating the transferability of knowledge between these robotic embodiments.
- **Identification of Research Directions:** The identification of fourteen specific research directions, highlighting open challenges and promising avenues for future inquiry that span both bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics, ranging from real-time control and safety to end-to-end drone VLAs and bridging the research-to-production gap.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors conducted a comprehensive literature review spanning 183 contributions published from 2017 to early 2026. The methodology is structured to first define the core concepts of VLA models, then to systematically categorize and analyze existing work across key dimensions, and finally, to synthesize findings and identify future research directions.

The review is organized along five major dimensions, as presented in the paper's taxonomy (Figure 1):
1. **Architectural Foundations:** Examination of autoregressive, flow-based, diffusion-based, and hybrid VLA architectures.
2. **Training Recipes:** Analysis of pre-training, post-training (fine-tuning), and reinforcement learning (RL) strategies.
3. **Action Representations:** Investigation of discrete tokenization and continuous generation methods for robot actions.
4. **Bimanual-Specific Concerns:** Review of coordination strategies and task types pertinent to bimanual manipulation.
5. **Unmanned Aerial Robotics (UAVs):** Coverage of navigation, aerial manipulation, and multi-agent unmanned systems.

The methodological approach involves several key steps:

- **Problem Formalization:** Section 2 formalizes core VLA concepts, defining the VLA policy, action chunking, flow matching for action generation, and bimanual coordination. Key notation is introduced to ensure consistency throughout the review.
- **Background Establishment:** Section 3 provides foundational context by reviewing Vision-Language Models (VLMs), imitation learning, generative modeling techniques for action generation (autoregressive, diffusion, flow matching), bimanual robotic systems, and aerial robotic systems. This sets the stage for understanding the building blocks of VLAs.
- **Data and Evaluation Infrastructure:** Section 4 details the datasets, benchmarks (e.g., OXE, DROID, BridgeData V2 for datasets; LIBERO, SIMPLER for simulation benchmarks), and evaluation metrics (e.g., task success rate, inference latency, generalization ratio) used in VLA research for both manipulation and aerial domains.
- **Systematic Architectural Analysis:** Section 5 categorizes VLA architectures into autoregressive, flow-based, diffusion-based, and hybrid models. Representative methods (e.g., RT-2, π0, RDT-1B, HybridVLA) are described, and their design choices, parameter counts, and performance characteristics are compared using tabular summaries.
- **Training and Data Strategy Review:** Section 6 examines the three-stage VLA training pipeline: VLM pre-training, robot data pre-training (on diverse cross-embodiment datasets), and post-training (fine-tuning and reinforcement learning). It highlights data collection strategies for bimanual manipulation and the impact of data scaling laws.
- **Action Representation Focus:** Section 7 delves into discrete and continuous action representations, action chunking strategies, and real-time execution techniques like Real-Time Chunking (RTC) and Bidirectional Decoding (BID), emphasizing the latency implications for bimanual and aerial control.
- **Domain-Specific Application Analysis:** Sections 8 and 9 constitute the core cross-domain analysis. Section 8 extensively reviews bimanual manipulation, detailing coordination strategies (joint action space, independent policies, leader-follower), task types (contact-rich, deformable object, long-horizon, mobile bimanual), and hardware platforms. Section 9 parallels this analysis for unmanned aerial robotics, covering navigation, end-to-end flight control, aerial manipulation, language-guided missions, multi-agent systems, UAV-UGV collaboration, and sim-to-real transfer. Explicit parallels are drawn between bimanual and aerial challenges and solutions.
- **Cross-Cutting Concerns:** Section 10 discusses language grounding, hierarchical reasoning, open-ended instruction following, and cross-embodiment generalization. Section 11 addresses other critical aspects such as visual representation learning, safety, sim-to-real transfer, world models and future state prediction, human-robot interaction, and scalability for deployment.
- **Synthesis and Future Directions:** Section 12 synthesizes the findings, characterizes the state-of-the-art performance (including a timeline of progress), discusses the emergence of industrial VLA deployment patterns, and proposes fourteen specific research directions to guide future work in the field.

Throughout the review, the authors employ comparative tables to summarize key features, performance metrics, and design choices of various VLA methods, enabling a structured and direct comparison across different approaches and domains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The review synthesizes a broad range of findings across VLA architectures, training methodologies, action representations, and their applications in bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics. The core results highlight convergence on specific technical approaches and identify key drivers of progress, as well as persistent challenges.

#### 4.1. Core VLA Design and Performance

- **Action Generation Mechanism:** Flow matching is identified as the most effective action generation mechanism for bimanual VLAs. It facilitates continuous, high-dimensional, and temporally correlated action chunks with efficient sampling (e.g., 10 steps), outperforming autoregressive methods in bimanual tasks by avoiding quantization bottlenecks and diffusion models in inference efficiency.
- **VLM Pre-training:** VLM pre-training on internet-scale image-text data is critical for providing semantic grounding. VLAs leveraging pre-trained VLMs (e.g., PaLIGemma, Prismatic) consistently surpass models trained solely on robot data, benefiting from inherent understanding of objects, spatial relationships, and task semantics.
- **Action Chunking:** Action chunking, typically with a horizon of H=50 steps (1 second of motion at 50 Hz), is found to be essential for capturing coordinated, multi-arm motion patterns, enabling smooth and coherent execution of bimanual primitives.
- **Efficiency and Latency:** While large VLAs face latency challenges, efficient architectures (e.g., TinyVLA, MiniVLA) achieve 85–90% of the performance of larger models with 3–10x reduced inference costs. Flow-based models offer the best latency-quality tradeoff.

#### 4.2. Training and Data Strategies

- **Reinforcement Learning from Autonomous Practice:** RECAP (Reinforcement Learning from Autonomous CAPability), as demonstrated by π ∗0, is presented as a highly impactful advance. It enables VLAs to self-improve beyond demonstration quality, yielding 10–40% absolute improvement on bimanual tasks (e.g., laundry folding success rising from 60% to over 90%).
- **Hierarchical Architectures:** Hierarchical VLAs (e.g., π0.5, Hi Robot) are crucial for long-horizon bimanual tasks, decomposing complex instructions into subgoals and extending planning horizons from seconds to minutes.
- **Data Diversity over Quantity:** Pre-training on diverse, cross-embodiment data (e.g., OXE) promotes better generalization than large volumes of homogeneous data. However, task-specific fine-tuning (50–200 demonstrations) remains necessary for high precision on specific bimanual tasks.
- **Bimanual Coordination:** VLAs that predict both arms' actions jointly within a single action chunk implicitly learn complex coordination patterns without explicit mechanisms, particularly with flow-based and diffusion-based models.

#### 4.3. Cross-Embodiment Transfer and Aerial Robotics

- **Cross-Embodiment Nature:** VLA architectures exhibit cross-embodiment transferability. Techniques proven in bimanual manipulation are being adapted for unmanned aerial robotics.
- **Aerial VLA Progress:** The aerial VLA field is approximately two years behind manipulation, but is rapidly developing. Integrated end-to-end VLA systems for physical drones (e.g., CognitiveDrone, DroneVLA) are emerging, building upon earlier successes in RL-based flight control and VLN benchmarks. CognitiveDrone achieved 77.2% success in cognitive aerial tasks with VLM reasoning.
- **Bridging Domains:** Direct parallels exist between bimanual coordination strategies and multi-drone systems, as well as between bimanual manipulation and aerial manipulation (e.g., Flying Hand uses action chunking for a hexarotor arm, and aerial harvesting systems employ dual-arm coordination).

#### 4.4. Advanced Capabilities and Deployment

- **Memory-Augmented VLAs:** Multi-scale memory mechanisms (e.g., MEM) enable VLAs to handle long-horizon tasks (up to 15 minutes) by combining short-term visual memory with long-term language-based semantic memory.
- **World Models:** World model-powered VLAs (e.g., GigaBrain family) demonstrate the ability to predict future states and generate synthetic training data, leading to up to 30% performance improvement on bimanual tasks. Video prediction models are also emerging as alternatives to direct action regression, leveraging vast web video data.
- **Industrial Deployment Patterns:** Product-level VLA systems are converging on dual-system architectures, separating slow reasoning modules (e.g., VLM at 7-10 Hz) from fast action modules (e.g., diffusion Transformer at 100-200 Hz) to balance capability and real-time control. Companies like Google (Gemini Robotics), NVIDIA (GR00T N1), and Figure AI (Helix) adopt this approach.
- **Production Reliability:** Industrial systems (e.g., Dyna's DYNA-1 achieving 99.4% success over 24+ hours) demonstrate that continuous self-improvement through RL loops and autonomous data generation is crucial for achieving high reliability in commercial deployment, validating the RECAP paradigm at scale.

#### 4.5. Identified Limitations and Gaps

- **Bimanual Benchmarks:** The lack of standardized simulation and real-world benchmarks for bimanual manipulation significantly hinders systematic comparison and progress tracking.
- **Latency-Reactivity Tradeoff:** Large VLA models still face challenges in achieving truly reactive control (>100 Hz) due to inference latency, despite mitigation techniques.
- **Distribution Shift:** VLA policies can degrade when encountering out-of-distribution observations, particularly in highly variable bimanual tasks.
- **Contact Modeling:** Current position-space VLAs do not explicitly address precise force control during bimanual contact, limiting their capability in force-sensitive tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This review paper holds significance for the field of embodied AI and robotics by providing a structured and forward-looking analysis of Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models. Its potential impact extends across several dimensions:

#### 5.1. Intellectual Contribution and Research Direction

- **Unified Conceptual Framework:** By explicitly connecting bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics under a single VLA framework, the review fosters cross-pollination of ideas and methodologies between previously distinct research communities. This unified perspective can lead to more generalizable robotic solutions.
- **Roadmap for Future Research:** The identification of fourteen specific research directions provides a clear and actionable roadmap for addressing the most critical challenges in VLA development. These directions span fundamental issues like standardized evaluation, dexterous control, real-time reactivity, data efficiency, and safety, guiding future efforts towards high-impact areas.
- **Acceleration of Progress:** The detailed synthesis of architectural choices, training strategies, and action representations, coupled with comparative analyses (e.g., flow matching's efficiency, RECAP's impact), can inform researchers and accelerate the development of more capable and robust VLA models.

#### 5.2. Advancing Robotic Capabilities

- **Enhanced Bimanual Manipulation:** Progress in bimanual VLAs has enabled robots to perform complex tasks such as laundry folding, box assembly, and table clearing with high success rates. This advances the potential for robotic assistance in both domestic and industrial settings, where multi-arm coordination is essential for intricate tasks.
- **Sophisticated Aerial Robotics:** The application of VLA principles to drones promises more autonomous and intelligent unmanned aerial systems. This includes language-guided navigation, agile flight control, and complex aerial manipulation tasks like grasping, transport, and inspection. Such capabilities can revolutionize applications in logistics, infrastructure maintenance, disaster response, and agriculture.
- **General-Purpose Robotics:** The demonstrated cross-embodiment transferability of VLA models signifies a step towards truly general-purpose robots. Policies trained on diverse robot platforms can be adapted to new morphologies and tasks with reduced data, lowering development costs and expanding the applicability of robotic systems across various industries.

#### 5.3. Enabling Real-World Deployment and Commercialization

- **Increased Reliability and Robustness:** The emphasis on reinforcement learning from autonomous practice and continuous self-improvement pathways (e.g., RECAP and industrial systems like DYNA-1) highlights a shift towards developing robots that can learn and adapt in deployment. This is crucial for achieving the high levels of reliability required for commercial and human-proximate operation.
- **Addressing Practical Constraints:** The analysis of latency, efficiency (e.g., TinyVLA, MiniVLA), and the emergence of dual-system architectures in industry (e.g., Gemini Robotics, GR00T N1, Helix) underscore the practical considerations for deploying VLA models on real hardware. These developments are critical for overcoming computational bottlenecks and enabling real-time control on embedded or distributed systems.
- **Safety and Trust:** The identified need for safety certification, interpretability, and trustworthy human-robot interaction for bimanual VLAs operating in human environments is fundamental. Addressing these concerns is a prerequisite for widespread societal adoption and regulatory approval.
- **Scalable Data Collection:** Innovations in data collection, such as zero robot data training (Sunday Robotics' ACT-1) and world model-generated data (GigaBrain), promise to alleviate the data scarcity bottleneck, making VLA development more accessible and scalable.

In conclusion, this review provides a timely and integrated perspective on VLA models, demonstrating their transformative potential for advanced robotics. By synthesizing diverse research and highlighting the shared challenges and solutions across bimanual manipulation and unmanned aerial robotics, it serves as a foundational resource for guiding future research and development towards the realization of more capable, versatile, and safely deployable autonomous systems.
