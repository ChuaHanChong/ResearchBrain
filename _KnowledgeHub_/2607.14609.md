# Representation-Aligned Tactile Grounding for Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The research from Fudan University and Lenovo CTO Organization developed "representation-aligned tactile grounding," a method that identifies and leverages an optimal intermediate representation within VLA policies to predict future tactile states. This approach improves robotic performance in contact-rich manipulation, achieving a 74% average success rate on five real-world tasks using a SmolVLA backbone by focusing learning on control-relevant contact dynamics without inference-time overhead.

## Problem

- VLM-driven embodied agents struggle with contact-rich manipulation tasks because crucial interaction states (e.g., slip, pressure) are often visually ambiguous or hidden, creating a "contact-observability gap."
- Existing tactile integration methods often treat touch as a passive input or apply future tactile prediction without systematically determining the optimal internal policy representation for this supervision.
- Directly predicting raw, high-dimensional, and noisy tactile signals can force policies to model sensor artifacts rather than control-relevant physical contact dynamics.

## Method

- Introduced a "representation-aligned diagnosis" methodology using ==frozen linear probes== to identify the intermediate ==action-expert representation== that is most predictive of future tactile states within a VLA policy.
- Developed a lightweight **Latent Tactile Predictor (LTP)** that attaches to this identified optimal intermediate interface, predicting compact ==future tactile latents== rather than raw signals.
- The **LTP** operates only during training, providing an ==auxiliary loss== to ground action-conditioned contact dynamics, and is removed during inference to ensure zero computational overhead.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an average success rate of **74%** (SmolVLA) and **73%** (π0) across **five** real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, surpassing all baselines.
- This performance represented an improvement of up to **16 percentage points** over misaligned VLM-level prediction and **12 percentage points** over final-action-state prediction using the SmolVLA backbone.
- Ablation studies confirmed that predicting compact latent tactile embeddings (**80%** success for Plug Insertion) was more effective than raw tactile prediction (**55%** success), and single, aligned grounding outperformed multi-interface approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The most effective internal representation for grounding future tactile information within a VLA policy is consistently an intermediate layer of the action expert, not the VLM-side or final action state.
> - Aligning the future tactile prediction objective with this specific intermediate representation is critical, as misaligned supervision at other layers can be suboptimal or even detrimental.
> - Predicting compact latent tactile embeddings significantly outperforms predicting raw tactile signals, as latents focus the grounding objective on control-relevant physical contact dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work situates itself within the evolving field of robotic manipulation, particularly at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and tactile sensing. Recent advancements in VLA models have enabled robots to perform complex tasks by translating visual observations and language instructions into executable actions. These models leverage the semantic and perceptual capabilities of pre-trained vision-language models, improving scalability, generality, and deployability of robot policies.

However, a significant challenge for VLA models remains contact-rich manipulation tasks, such as insertion, alignment, and force-sensitive handling. In these scenarios, critical interaction states (e.g., slip, pressure, local resistance, precise alignment) are often visually ambiguous or entirely hidden from standard camera-based perception. This "contact-observability gap" can lead to failures even with sophisticated VLA policies.

To address this limitation, researchers have explored tactile-enhanced VLA methods, which integrate touch information into the policy. Existing approaches primarily focus on incorporating tactile signals as input, either through tactile tokens, embeddings, multimodal fusion, or adapter mechanisms. While these methods improve the policy's ability to condition on tactile observations, they often treat touch as a passive input.

A complementary direction involves using future tactile prediction, where the policy is explicitly supervised to model how its actions will change subsequent contact states. This approach transforms tactile outcomes into an active supervisory signal that encourages action-conditioned representations to encode contact dynamics. This paper specifically targets a gap in this area: it investigates *where* within the internal architecture of a VLA policy this future tactile prediction supervision should be applied, addressing what the authors term the "representation-alignment problem." This differentiates it from prior work that typically uses tactile prediction as a generic auxiliary loss without detailed consideration of the specific internal representation it is intended to shape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to effectively and optimally integrate future tactile prediction into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies to enhance performance in contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks. The work specifically aims to identify and leverage the most suitable internal representation within a VLA policy for applying future tactile supervision.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key observations and challenges in current robotic manipulation:

- **Limitations of Vision-Language-Action Models in Contact-Rich Tasks:** Despite their successes, VLA models often struggle with tasks requiring precise physical interaction. These tasks—such as inserting a plug, wiping a whiteboard with consistent pressure, or grasping deformable objects—rely on subtle contact dynamics that are frequently not observable through vision alone. The success of these manipulations hinges on how the robot applies force, slides, or corrects its pose, information often hidden from visual perception.
- **The Promise of Tactile Sensing:** Tactile sensors provide direct, localized information about robot-object-environment interactions, offering a window into these otherwise hidden contact states. This makes tactile sensing a valuable modality for addressing the contact-observability gap.
- **Active Use of Tactile Information through Prediction:** Beyond simply providing tactile observations as input, predicting future tactile states conditioned on actions offers a more proactive way to utilize touch. By requiring the policy to forecast contact outcomes, this prediction acts as a supervisory signal, compelling the policy's internal representations to explicitly learn and encode the underlying contact dynamics induced by its actions.
- **The Representation-Alignment Problem:** A central motivation is the ambiguity regarding *where* within a VLA policy's architecture this future tactile supervision should be applied. VLA policies are composed of various computational stages, from perceptual encoding (vision-language backbone) to motor command generation (action expert layers and final action state). Each stage processes information differently and serves a distinct functional role.
 - Applying tactile prediction to the **final action representation** might be intuitive due to the tight coupling of touch and motor control. However, this representation is already specialized for immediate motor decoding and may have compressed away crucial information relevant for modeling future contact consequences.
 - Conversely, applying it to the **vision-language backbone features** might keep the objective too focused on semantic perception, too far removed from the action-conditioned dynamics that give tactile signals their control relevance.
 - This uncertainty highlights a "representation-alignment problem": simply adding an auxiliary tactile prediction loss anywhere might introduce mismatched regularization, dilute the learning signal, or force representations to learn details unrelated to their primary function, thereby hindering effective grounding of action-conditioned contact dynamics.
- **Challenges of Raw Tactile Prediction:** Directly predicting raw tactile signals presents additional difficulties. Tactile measurements are typically high-dimensional, sensor-specific, and prone to noise, calibration biases, and local contact artifacts. Supervising raw signals might inadvertently force the policy to model these sensing artifacts rather than the control-relevant physical contact dynamics, diminishing the quality of the grounding signal.

In summary, the key motivation is to move beyond generic tactile integration in VLA models by systematically determining the optimal internal policy representation for future tactile prediction, thereby ensuring that this powerful supervisory signal grounds action-conditioned contact dynamics effectively and efficiently, without adding inference-time overhead or introducing detrimental noise.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research addresses the representation-alignment problem by first diagnosing the optimal internal representation for tactile grounding and then developing a lightweight prediction mechanism tailored to that interface.

#### 3.1. Overview of Approach

The proposed method, "representation-aligned tactile grounding," involves two main steps:
1. **Representation-Aligned Diagnosis:** Utilize frozen linear probes to systematically identify which internal representation along the VLA action pathway is most predictive of future tactile states.
2. **Latent Tactile Predictor (LTP):** Based on the diagnosis, introduce a lightweight Latent Tactile Predictor that attaches to the identified optimal interface, predicting compact future tactile latents during training without modifying the inference pathway.

#### 3.2. Diagnosing the Tactile Grounding Interface

To identify the most suitable internal representation, a diagnostic procedure was implemented:
- **Base Policy Training and Freezing:** A base VLA policy (specifically SmolVLA in initial experiments) was trained using its native action objective. All policy parameters were then frozen to isolate the diagnostic analysis from policy learning.
- **Candidate Representation Extraction:** For each trajectory segment at time *t*, various candidate internal representations were extracted:
 - The VLM-side output ($h_{vlm\_t}$).
 - Features from each layer of the action expert ($\{h_{\ell_t}\}_{L_{\ell=1}}$).
- **Linear Probe Analysis:** For each candidate representation $h$, an independent, lightweight linear probe $g_h$ was trained. The objective of this probe was to predict the raw future tactile sequence ($r_{t+1:t+H}$). The loss function used was a Mean Squared Error (MSE): $L_{probe} = ||\hat{r}_{t+1:t+H} - r_{t+1:t+H}||^2_2$. The use of raw future tactile measurements as the target ensured the diagnosis was independent of any subsequent latent tactile encoder.
- **Predictability Metric:** Lower probe error (MSE) indicated that future tactile information was more linearly accessible from the corresponding representation. For flow-based action experts, probes were evaluated at different normalized action-generation times ($T \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$) to observe trends across the action generation process.
- **Findings of Diagnosis:** The diagnostic results consistently showed a non-monotonic trend in future tactile predictability. The VLM-side representation exhibited high prediction error, suggesting it was too perception-oriented. Prediction error decreased significantly within the action expert layers, reaching a minimum at an *intermediate layer*. It then increased again towards the final action state, which was specialized for immediate motor decoding. This pattern held true whether tactile observations were included as input or when a larger VLA backbone ($\pi_0$) was used. These findings indicated that the intermediate action-expert representation best encoded action-conditioned interaction features before excessive compression for motor commands.

#### 3.3. Latent Tactile Predictor (LTP)

Based on the diagnostic findings, the Latent Tactile Predictor was designed:
- **Problem with Raw Tactile Prediction:** Recognizing that directly predicting high-dimensional, noisy, and sensor-specific raw tactile signals could lead to the policy modeling artifacts rather than control-relevant dynamics, the authors opted for latent space prediction.
- **Tactile Encoder ($E_{tac}$):** A tactile encoder was introduced to map the raw future tactile sequence ($r_{t+1:t+H}$) into a more compact and meaningful latent tactile sequence ($z_{t+1:t+H}$). This ensures the auxiliary objective focuses on contact-relevant structure.
- **LTP Architecture and Connection:** Let $h_{mid_t}$ denote the intermediate action-expert representation identified by the probe analysis. The Latent Tactile Predictor ($P_{\theta}$) uses a set of learnable tactile queries ($Q = \{q_1, \ldots, q_H\}$) to predict the future tactile latent sequence ($\hat{z}_{t+1:t+H}$) from $h_{mid_t}$. This prediction head is lightweight and query-based.
- **Training Objective:** The LTP is trained with a latent tactile prediction loss: $L_{tac} = ||\hat{z}_{t+1:t+H} - \text{sg}(z_{t+1:t+H})||^2_2$. The `sg()` (stop-gradient) operation is crucial as it prevents gradients from flowing through the tactile target, ensuring the target remains stable and the predictor learns to map the policy's intermediate representation to the contact outcomes, rather than co-adapting the tactile encoder.
- **Combined Loss:** The final training objective combines this grounding loss with the native VLA action objective: $L = L_{act} + \lambda_{tac}L_{tac}$.
- **Inference-Time Removal:** A key design aspect is that the LTP branch is used *only during training*. At inference time, it is removed, meaning it adds no computational overhead to the policy's execution.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** Real-world single-arm ARX R5 robot equipped with PaXini tactile sensors.
- **Sensory Input:** Two RGB camera streams (third-person and wrist-mounted), robot proprioception, and current tactile readings.
- **Tasks:** Five contact-rich manipulation tasks chosen to test various physical interaction challenges: Plug Insertion, USB Drive Insertion, Whiteboard Wiping, Light Bulb Unscrewing, and Deformable Object Grasping.
- **Data Collection:** 50 expert demonstrations per task via leader-follower teleoperation, recording synchronized RGB, robot states, actions, and tactile readings.
- **VLA Backbones:** SmolVLA and $\pi_0$ (fine-tuned using LoRA).
- **Baselines:**
 - Standard VLA policies (SmolVLA, $\pi_0$) without tactile grounding.
 - Tactile-conditioned variants (receiving tactile input but without future tactile prediction).
 - exUMI: A baseline using external predictive tactile representation learning.
 - Interface-controlled variants: Attaching the same tactile prediction loss to the VLM-side output, the final action state, or the diagnosed intermediate action-expert feature.
 - Multi-interface grounding variants: Applying tactile losses to multiple layers simultaneously.
- **Evaluation:** 20 real-world trials per task, with task success rate as the primary metric, judged by task-specific criteria.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the effectiveness of representation-aligned tactile grounding across various settings and baselines, providing evidence for the proposed approach.

#### 4.1. Linear Probe Diagnosis and Downstream Validation

- **Intermediate Layer Predictability:** The linear probe analysis consistently revealed that future tactile information was most predictable from intermediate action-expert representations. This pattern was observed across conditions, including with and without current tactile input, and with different VLA model scales (SmolVLA and $\pi_0$). The VLM-side representations showed higher prediction errors, while the final action states also exhibited increased error, indicating that the information had become specialized for motor decoding.
- **Alignment with Policy Performance:** The diagnostic findings directly correlated with downstream task success rates. In the USB Drive Insertion task, policies grounded at the VLM-side performed weakest, final-action-state grounding showed improvement but remained suboptimal, and grounding at the intermediate action-expert representation achieved the highest success. This established a link between the diagnostic predictability and actual control effectiveness.

#### 4.2. Representation-Aligned Tactile Grounding Performance

- **SmolVLA Backbone:** Using the SmolVLA backbone, representation-aligned tactile grounding achieved an average success rate of 74% across the five contact-rich manipulation tasks. This outperformed:
 - Input-level tactile conditioning (which was not explicitly reported in the `SmolVLA +Tactile` baseline, but implied by the comparison to `SmolVLA` in Fig 5).
 - External predictive tactile representation learning (exUMI, 62% average success).
 - Less aligned tactile prediction interfaces: VLM-level prediction (58% average success) and final-action-state prediction (62% average success).
 - This represented a 16 percentage point improvement over VLM-level prediction and a 12 percentage point improvement over final-action-state prediction. The improvement was consistent across most tasks, with the method being best on four tasks and tying for best on Deformable Object Grasping.
- **$\pi_0$ Backbone Generalization:** The effectiveness extended to the $\pi_0$ backbone, a different VLA architecture employing LoRA fine-tuning. Here, representation-aligned grounding achieved an average success rate of 73%. This again significantly surpassed:
 - The standard $\pi_0$ policy (40% average success).
 - Tactile-conditioned $\pi_0$ (54% average success).
 - VLM-side prediction (58% average success).
 - Final-action prediction (59% average success).
 This result demonstrated that the benefit of intermediate-layer grounding was not specific to a single backbone architecture.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Latent vs. Raw Tactile Prediction:** An ablation study on the form of the future tactile target showed that predicting compact tactile latents was superior to directly predicting raw tactile signals. For the Plug Insertion task, using latent prediction resulted in 80% success, whereas raw tactile prediction yielded only 55% success. This supports the hypothesis that compact latent targets focus the grounding objective on control-relevant contact structure, rather than noisy sensor specifics.
- **Effect of Supervision Scope (Multi-Interface Grounding):** The study also investigated whether simply applying tactile prediction losses broadly across multiple layers could achieve similar benefits. Comparing to a baseline of SmolVLA with tactile input but no future tactile prediction (41% average success):
 - Grounding all action-expert features (Multi-A) resulted in 38% success.
 - Grounding every two action-expert layers (Multi-B) achieved 48% success.
 - Grounding multiple middle layers (Multi-C) reached 60% success.
 In contrast, the proposed single, representation-aligned intermediate interface achieved 74% average success. These results indicated that performance gains were not due to an accumulation of losses, but rather the strategic alignment of the tactile supervision with the appropriate internal representation. Applying the same dynamic target to representations with mismatched roles was shown to be suboptimal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by providing a methodical framework for effectively integrating tactile information into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Conceptual Reframing of Tactile Supervision:** The work reframes future tactile prediction from a generic auxiliary loss into a "representation-alignment problem." This conceptual shift emphasizes that the effectiveness of tactile supervision is critically dependent on *where* it is applied within a complex neural policy. This moves beyond treating multi-modal auxiliary tasks as interchangeable additions and highlights the importance of understanding the functional roles of different internal representations.
- **Diagnostic Methodology for Policy Design:** The introduction and validation of the linear probe analysis as a diagnostic tool is a significant methodological contribution. This approach provides a data-driven, systematic way to identify the optimal grounding interface within a VLA policy's action pathway. Such a diagnostic framework can inform the design of future multi-modal robot learning architectures, potentially extending beyond tactile sensing to other sensory modalities or auxiliary tasks.
- **Enhanced Performance in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The consistent and substantial performance gains demonstrated across a suite of real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, using two different VLA backbones (SmolVLA and $\pi_0$), directly address a key limitation of existing VLA models. Tasks like precise insertion, stable wiping, and pressure-sensitive grasping, which are challenging for vision-only systems, show improved reliability. This has practical implications for deploying robots in environments requiring dexterous, interactive capabilities.
- **Efficient Tactile Grounding:** The Latent Tactile Predictor (LTP) provides an efficient mechanism for tactile grounding. By predicting compact latent tactile embeddings rather than noisy raw signals, it focuses the learning objective on control-relevant information. Furthermore, its removal during inference ensures that the benefits of tactile grounding are realized without introducing additional computational overhead or latency into the operational policy, making it suitable for real-time robotic applications.
- **Informing Future Multi-Modal Robot Learning:** The findings suggest that careful consideration of the information flow and representational specialization within deep learning architectures is crucial for integrating diverse sensory inputs effectively. This work could guide future research in multi-modal learning for robotics, prompting deeper investigation into how different sensory cues (e.g., force, proprioception, audio) should be aligned with specific internal representations to maximize their utility for action generation.
- **Foundation for More Capable Robotic Systems:** By improving a robot's ability to reason about and act upon physical contact dynamics, this research lays groundwork for more robust, adaptive, and dexterous robotic systems. This could enable robots to perform a broader range of complex manipulation tasks in unstructured environments, contributing to advancements in manufacturing, logistics, domestic assistance, and other fields.
