# Interpretable Zero-Shot Learning with Locally-Aligned Vision-Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A training-free framework, LaZSL, developed by researchers from MBZUAI and collaborators, enhances interpretable zero-shot learning by employing Optimal Transport to align local visual regions with LLM-generated semantic attributes. This approach achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across diverse benchmarks, including 69.7% on average with a ViT-B/16 backbone, while providing human-understandable explanations for predictions.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP offer limited interpretability in Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL), providing global similarity scores without explaining specific visual cues for predictions.
- Global image-text alignment struggles with fine-grained classification and shows poor generalization across domain shifts, leading to suboptimal accuracy for challenging distinctions.
- Existing interpretable ZSL methods typically align entire images with attributes, failing to establish fine-grained local correspondences between visual regions and specific semantic attributes, particularly with frozen pre-trained VLM encoders.

## Method

- Employs Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate sets of ==discrete semantic attributes== for each class, providing fine-grained textual descriptions.
- Constructs a diverse ==region vision set== from multi-scale cropped image patches to capture local visual information.
- Leverages ==Optimal Transport (OT)== to establish fine-grained alignment between local visual features and semantic attributes, minimizing the cost of transforming visual distributions to semantic ones, enhanced by a vision selection mechanism and a region-global hybrid cost.

## Results

- LaZSL achieved state-of-the-art average accuracy on five diverse image classification benchmarks, demonstrating a **69.7%** average accuracy with a ViT-B/16 backbone and outperforming direct interpretable baselines like DCLIP by up to **3.8%**.
- The method showcased superior domain generalization capabilities on ImageNet variants (V2, R, Sketch, A), achieving an average accuracy of **60.9%** with ViT-B/16, surpassing both training-required and training-free baselines.
- Provides explicit, human-understandable interpretations for predictions by aligning specific local visual regions with discrete semantic attributes, illustrating *why* a particular class was chosen.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Establishing fine-grained, local visual-semantic alignment is essential for truly interpretable Zero-Shot Learning, moving beyond black-box global similarity scores.
> - Optimal Transport offers an effective training-free mechanism to achieve precise local alignment between visual regions and discrete semantic attributes derived from LLMs.
> - A balanced integration of local visual information, LLM-generated attributes, and global VLM context is key to boosting accuracy and domain generalization in ZSL while maintaining interpretability.
