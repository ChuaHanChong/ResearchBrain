# Video Generation with Predictive Latents

> [!summary] Summary
> Predictive Video VAE (PV-VAE) is a video compression framework that enhances the temporal coherence and motion awareness of latent spaces by integrating a predictive reconstruction objective. This design yields superior video generation quality and efficiency, demonstrating robust performance across various video understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Optimizing video VAEs solely for reconstruction fidelity does not consistently lead to improved generative performance, indicating a lack of "diffusability" in conventional latent spaces.
- Existing video VAEs often extend image VAEs without explicitly structuring latent spaces to capture robust temporal dynamics and motion awareness essential for high-quality video generation.
- Conventional video VAE latent spaces can struggle to effectively encode the underlying temporal dynamics and motion structures, limiting the quality and consistency of generated videos.

## Method

- A ==Predictive Video VAE== (**PV-VAE**) is proposed, incorporating a novel ==predictive reconstruction== objective that unifies predictive learning with standard video reconstruction within the VAE framework.
- The encoder is trained by observing only a partial, past portion of a video, while the decoder is tasked with reconstructing the complete video sequence, including unobserved future frames.
- A ==multi-stage training== regimen is employed, including image pretraining, video training with predictive reconstruction, and a decoder fine-tuning stage, to balance reconstruction fidelity and latent space diffusability.

## Results

- PV-VAE achieved superior video generation quality, outperforming contemporary video VAEs with up to a 34.42 FVD improvement on UCF101 and achieving 33.34 FVD on RealEstate10K.
- The model demonstrated significantly improved training speed (**2.68x** faster) and memory reduction (**62%**) compared to baselines, alongside **52%** faster convergence.
- Latent space analysis revealed strong correlation between latent activations and video motion, with improved performance in optical flow estimation (**12.5%** EPE reduction), next-frame prediction (**8.0%** MSE reduction), and point tracking (**8.5%** AUC improvement).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating predictive learning into video VAE training compels the latent space to inherently encode temporally predictive structures and motion awareness, making it more "diffusable" for generative models.
> - Stronger predictive regularization through higher dropping ratios of future frames during training leads to more robust latent representations and consistently higher generative quality.
> - The learned latent space, enriched with temporal and motion awareness through predictive reconstruction, yields consistent performance gains in downstream video understanding tasks, highlighting its broad utility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video generation has advanced significantly, with Latent Video Diffusion Models (LVDMs) and Video Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) playing a central role. LVDMs generate videos in a compact spatiotemporal latent space, which is typically created by a video VAE. Video VAEs compress high-dimensional video data into a lower-dimensional latent representation, thereby improving the efficiency and stability of subsequent generative modeling processes.

The conventional approach to developing video VAEs often involves extending image VAEs by inflating 2D convolutions into 3D causal convolutions and then training them on video datasets. While existing video VAEs have achieved high reconstruction quality, the paper identifies a challenge: optimizing reconstruction fidelity does not consistently lead to improved generative performance. This issue relates to the "diffusability" of latent spaces—their suitability for the diffusion process. The broader landscape of video VAE research has focused on various aspects, including hybrid designs to leverage pre-trained image VAEs, efficiency improvements through lightweight architectures or wavelet-based methods, and decoupling motion dynamics from static content. However, the explicit structuring of latent spaces to directly benefit video generation remains an area requiring further investigation.

This work positions itself within this context by exploring how predictive learning, a paradigm rooted in "predictive world modeling," can enhance the temporal structure and motion awareness of video VAE latent spaces. By doing so, it aims to create latent representations that are inherently more "diffusable" and thus more effective for high-quality video generation. The paper draws inspiration from the success of predictive learning in various representation learning tasks, particularly its ability to acquire temporal and causal structures from sequences, to address the challenge of creating temporally coherent and motion-aware video latents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the "diffusability" of video latent spaces within the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) framework, thereby improving the quality and efficiency of subsequent video generation processes. The authors observe that while current video VAEs can achieve high reconstruction fidelity, this does not consistently translate into superior generative performance. This discrepancy motivates the need for a latent space that not only compresses visual information but also effectively encodes the underlying temporal dynamics and motion structures of videos.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives are:

- **Address the "Diffusability" Challenge**: The paper aims to resolve the issue where optimizing video VAEs for reconstruction does not guarantee improved generative capabilities. This suggests that the latent spaces generated by conventional VAEs may lack the specific structured properties beneficial for diffusion models.
- **Integrate Predictive Learning into Video VAEs**: Inspired by the principles of predictive world modeling, which posits that predicting future states helps models acquire a deeper understanding of temporal and causal structures, the authors seek to integrate predictive learning into the video VAE training objective. The hypothesis is that forcing the VAE to predict future frames will compel its latent space to capture robust motion priors and temporal coherence.
- **Develop a Predictive Reconstruction Objective**: A core objective is to design a novel training objective that unifies standard video reconstruction with predictive learning. This involves a mechanism where the encoder processes only a partial, past observation of a video, while the decoder is tasked with reconstructing the complete video sequence, including the unobserved future frames.
- **Enhance Latent Space with Temporal and Motion Awareness**: By employing the predictive reconstruction objective, the goal is to encourage the latent space to inherently encode temporally predictive structures and develop a more coherent understanding of video dynamics. This structured latent space is expected to yield better generation quality and exhibit properties useful for video understanding tasks.
- **Improve Training Efficiency and Scalability**: The proposed method also aims to achieve faster convergence during training and demonstrate favorable scalability, meaning that generative performance improves consistently with larger training datasets.
- **Demonstrate Broader Utility of Learned Latents**: Beyond video generation, the research aims to show that the learned latent space, enriched with temporal and motion awareness, also yields consistent gains in downstream video understanding tasks such as optical flow estimation, next-frame prediction, and point tracking. This would underscore the quality and utility of the learned representations.

In summary, the motivation stems from the perceived inadequacy of reconstruction-focused video VAEs for generative tasks, leading to the objective of infusing predictive capabilities into the VAE's latent space to create more structured, temporally coherent, and "diffusable" representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, termed Predictive Video VAE (PV-VAE), involves introducing a predictive reconstruction objective that unifies predictive learning with traditional video reconstruction within the VAE framework.

#### 3.1. Framework: Integrating Predictive Learning into Reconstruction

- **Partial-to-Complete Reconstruction Task**: The central mechanism is to train the VAE to reconstruct an entire video clip (x) while only providing a partial, past portion of it (x\_obs) to the encoder.
- **Frame Dropping Mechanism**: At each training step, a video clip of `1+T` frames is partitioned into `G = 1 + T/p_t` groups, where `p_t` is the temporal compression ratio. The first group always contains the first frame, and subsequent groups contain `p_t` frames. A number of future groups (`k`) are randomly sampled to be "dropped" from the observation, where `k` is uniformly sampled between `0` and `⌊(G-1) * r⌋` (`r` being the maximum dropping ratio, set to 100% in experiments). This means the encoder only observes `x_obs`.
- **Latent Space Encoding and Padding**: The observed frames `x_obs` are fed into the encoder to produce `z_obs`. To enable the decoder to reconstruct the full `1+T` frames, the `z_obs` sequence is temporally padded with `k` padding vectors (`z_pad`) sampled from an uninformative prior (e.g., standard Gaussian distribution or learnable tokens). This complete latent sequence (`z_obs` + `z_pad`) is then passed to the decoder.
- **Joint Supervision**: This design compels the model to infer the evolution of the video from past observations and encode this predictive information into its latent spaces. The decoder receives both reconstructive supervision (for `x_obs`) and predictive supervision (for `x_drop`, the discarded future frames), fostering a deeper understanding of temporal dynamics and motion awareness.

#### 3.2. Model Design

- PV-VAE employs a CNN-based architecture with **3D causal convolutions**.
- It uses **16x spatial and 4x temporal downsampling**, resulting in a latent channel dimension of 64.
- **Encoder Structure**: Performs two stages of spatiotemporal downsampling (reducing temporal and spatial dimensions by a factor of 4), followed by two additional spatial downsampling operations (keeping temporal length fixed), achieving a total 16x spatial reduction.
- **Decoder Structure**: Symmetric to the encoder, it first performs two stages of spatial upsampling, followed by two stages of spatiotemporal upsampling.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Multi-Stage Training**:
 1. **Image Pretraining**: PV-VAE is initially pretrained on multi-resolution image data (256x256, 384x384, 512x512) for 300K steps.
 2. **Video Training with Predictive Reconstruction**: Subsequently, it is trained on video data (256x256, 512x512) for 50K steps using the proposed predictive reconstruction objective.
 3. **Decoder Fine-tuning**: To mitigate the training-inference gap (where inference requires a full-length latent sequence but training involves partial inputs), an additional fine-tuning stage is introduced. The encoder is frozen, random frame-dropping is disabled, and only the decoder is trained for another 50K steps to perform standard video reconstruction from full-length latents. This step aims to improve reconstruction quality without altering the learned latent space structure.
- **Loss Functions**: A combination of standard VAE losses is used:
 - **Mean Squared Error (MSE) Loss**: For pixel-level reconstruction.
 - **Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) Loss**: To capture perceptual similarity.
 - **Adversarial (GAN) Loss**: Activated from step 5,000 and throughout decoder fine-tuning, to improve visual realism.
 - **KL Regularization Term**: To regularize the latent space.
 - **Motion-aware Objective**: An additional term is included to prevent "copy-shortcut" for static regions. The model is required to reconstruct not only raw pixels but also the temporal differences between adjacent frames. This encourages learning of structural motion.
 The total loss is a weighted sum of these components.
- **Optimizer and Learning Rate**: AdamW optimizer with a base learning rate of 5e-5, warmed up linearly and decayed using a cosine schedule.
- **Padding Strategy**: Learnable tokens are used for padding the dropped latent segments, outperforming sampling from a Gaussian distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of PV-VAE across video generation, reconstruction, and various analyses of its latent space properties.

#### 4.1. Comparison on Generation Performance

- **Superior Generative Quality**: PV-VAE achieved the best overall performance compared to several contemporary video VAEs (CogX-VAE, IV-VAE, WF-VAE-L, Hunyuan-VAE, Wan2.1/2.2 VAE, SSVAE) on both class-conditional (UCF101) and unconditional (RealEstate10K) video generation tasks.
 - On UCF101, PV-VAE achieved 72.50 FVD, 4.06 KVD, and 4.40 IS. This represents a 34.42 FVD improvement over Wan2.2 VAE and 22.31 FVD over SSVAE.
 - On RealEstate10K, PV-VAE achieved 33.34 FVD.
- **Improved Efficiency**: Compared to models with similar or even lower compression rates, PV-VAE demonstrated substantial improvements in training speed (TSpeed) and memory consumption (TMem). For example, it achieved a 2.68x speedup and 62% memory reduction compared to Hunyuan-VAE on UCF101.
- **Faster Convergence**: The model demonstrated 52% faster convergence during training.

#### 4.2. Comparison on Reconstruction Performance

- **Comparable Reconstruction Fidelity**: On the Kinetics-400 dataset, PV-VAE exhibited reconstruction performance comparable to existing 4x16x16 compression models. It slightly underperformed Wan2.2 VAE but consistently outperformed SSVAE across rFVD, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics.
- **Inference Efficiency**: PV-VAE achieved 38% faster inference than Hunyuan-VAE and 19% faster than Wan2.2 VAE, while reducing memory consumption by 64% and 15%, respectively.
- **Qualitative Reconstruction**: PV-VAE maintained competitive reconstruction fidelity, though it showed limitations in reconstructing dense text, which the authors attribute to data distribution.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Latent Space Properties

- **PCA Analysis**: Visualizations of Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the latent space revealed a clear correspondence between latent activations and underlying video motion. Activation patterns in PV-VAE were strongly aligned with optical flow, indicating that the model effectively concentrates spatiotemporal saliency in dynamic regions and maintains smoother representations for static backgrounds.
- **Correlation Study**: A direct correlation was observed between improved predictive accuracy (how well the model predicts future frames) and superior generative performance (lower FVD), validating the core motivation of the predictive reconstruction objective.
- **Scalability**: PV-VAE exhibited consistent performance gains as training data scaled, a trend not observed with a pure reconstruction objective, suggesting superior scalability of the predictive reconstruction paradigm.
- **Latent Temporal Coherence**:
 - **Short-term Smoothness**: PV-VAE showed a lower median and a sharper histogram peak in adjacent-frame Latent Temporal Distance (LTD), suggesting smoother temporal transitions.
 - **Long-term Dynamics**: As frame intervals increased, PV-VAE demonstrated a consistent monotonic increase in normalized LTD, indicating a smoothly evolving latent trajectory that captures continuous video dynamics effectively.
- **Probing Video Understanding in Latent Space**: Diffusion features extracted from PV-VAE's latent space exhibited stronger performance across several downstream video understanding tasks:
 - **Optical Flow Estimation**: Achieved 12.5% reduction in Average End-Point Error (EPE) on Sintel.
 - **Next-Frame Prediction**: Achieved 8.0% reduction in Mean Squared Error (MSE) on Kinetics-400.
 - **Point Tracking**: Achieved 8.5% improvement in Area Under the Curve (AUC) on TAP-Vid-DAVIS.
 These gains suggest that PV-VAE's latent space encodes superior video dynamics and motion-aware representations.
- **Predictive Reconstruction Visualization**: Qualitative results showed that PV-VAE could generate plausible future frames aligned with underlying temporal evolutions, accurately predicting relative spatial shifts and action progression from partial observations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Incremental Ablation**:
 - Introducing Predictive Reconstruction (PR) significantly improved generation performance (gFVD from 174.81 to 156.33).
 - Adding the Motion-aware Objective further improved gFVD to 150.10.
 - Decoder Fine-tuning primarily boosted reconstruction quality (rFVD from 5.79 to 3.45) while also providing a slight benefit to generation (gFVD to 146.37).
- **Maximum Dropping Ratio (MDR)**: Generative performance consistently improved with higher perturbation levels (e.g., gFVD from 159.82 at 50% MDR to 146.37 at 100% MDR), indicating that stronger predictive regularization leads to more robust representations.
- **Padding Strategies**: Using learnable tokens for padding dropped latents yielded slightly better generation quality compared to sampling from a standard Gaussian distribution.
- **Transformer-based VAEs**: An exploratory comparison with a Transformer-based PV-VAE variant showed comparable reconstruction but limited generative capability compared to the CNN-based PV-VAE. However, the Transformer variant achieved 87% faster inference despite a larger parameter count, suggesting potential for future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Predictive Video VAE (PV-VAE) contributes to the field of video generation by introducing a method to enhance the "diffusability" of latent spaces, which is a critical factor for high-quality generative models. The work demonstrates that explicitly training a VAE with a predictive reconstruction objective, where future frames must be inferred from partial past observations, leads to a latent space that is more structured, temporally coherent, and motion-aware.

**Key Significance Points:**

- **Improved Generative Performance**: PV-VAE achieves substantial improvements in video generation quality, evidenced by lower FVD scores and faster convergence compared to existing video VAEs. This indicates that by focusing on temporal dynamics during latent space formation, the subsequent diffusion process benefits significantly.
- **Enhanced Latent Space Properties**: The research establishes a clear link between predictive accuracy and generative quality. The in-depth analyses, including PCA and Latent Temporal Distance (LTD), confirm that PV-VAE's latent space effectively captures structural motion, exhibits smoother temporal transitions, and follows a monotonic trajectory across varying frame intervals. This represents a shift from latent spaces that are "mere pixel containers" to those that are "motion-aware."
- **Broader Utility for Video Understanding**: The demonstration that PV-VAE's latent features lead to consistent gains in downstream video understanding tasks (optical flow, next-frame prediction, point tracking) underscores the robustness and richness of the learned representations. This suggests that representations optimized for video generation through predictive learning also hold value for analytical tasks, potentially reducing the need for separate representation learning schemes.
- **Scalability and Efficiency**: The improved scalability with increasing data and the enhanced training/inference efficiency highlight the practical benefits of the proposed methodology, making it suitable for larger datasets and deployment.
- **Strategic Training Regimen**: The multi-stage training, including decoder fine-tuning, is identified as an effective strategy ("free lunch") for balancing reconstruction fidelity with latent diffusability without compromising the encoder's learned temporal priors. This can accelerate development workflows by allowing parallel training of the VAE decoder and diffusion backbone.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancement of Video Generative Models**: The principles introduced by PV-VAE could guide the design of future video VAEs, pushing them beyond simple data compression towards learning intrinsically structured and dynamically rich latent spaces. This could lead to more realistic, temporally consistent, and controllable video generation.
- **Foundation for World Models**: By leveraging predictive learning, PV-VAE aligns with the broader research direction of "world models" and joint embedding predictive architectures. The findings reinforce the idea that predicting future states is a powerful mechanism for acquiring robust visual representations, opening avenues for agents that can better understand and reason about dynamic environments.
- **Exploration of Masked Modeling Paradigms**: The authors suggest generalizing this predictive philosophy to other masked modeling tasks, such as frame infilling or joint spatiotemporal prediction, which could further bolster the robustness and versatility of video latent spaces.
- **Future of Transformer-based Video VAEs**: The exploratory analysis on Transformer-based VAEs, despite current generative gaps, highlights their potential for computational efficiency and representational flexibility. PV-VAE's approach could be integrated into such architectures to unlock their full potential, aligning video VAEs with modern advancements in vision Transformers.

In conclusion, PV-VAE provides meaningful insights into structuring video latent spaces for enhanced generative performance and offers a promising direction for future research in video VAEs and generative modeling.
