# Boosting MLLM Reasoning with Text-Debiased Hint-GRPO

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Text-Debiased Hint-GRPO, a method that improves Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) reasoning by addressing low data utilization and text-bias in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). It significantly boosts performance on challenging multimodal tasks, particularly mathematical geometry reasoning, by adaptively providing hints during training and calibrating for image neglect at inference time.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) trained with Outcome Reward Methods (ORM) like GRPO suffer from low data utilization, as difficult questions often yield no correct answers, resulting in ineffective training samples.
- GRPO-trained MLLMs exhibit a 'text-bias,' tending to ignore crucial image information and rely solely on text context during reasoning, leading to errors in multimodal tasks.
- Current MLLM ORM methods perform well on simple visual tasks but struggle with complex multimodal reasoning, such as mathematical geometry, which requires deep integration of visual and textual cues.

## Method

- **Hint-GRPO** adaptively provides reasoning ==hints as the beginning of the model's output sequence== (`I_answer`) during training, ensuring even difficult samples yield at least one correct answer and increasing data utilization.
- A novel ==adaptive hint level strategy== dynamically selects the minimum necessary hint for each sample, ensuring sufficient guidance without over-hinting easier questions.
- **Text-Bias Calibration**, inspired by ==Classifier-Free Guidance==, is applied at test-time to amplify the image condition's influence, mitigating the MLLM's tendency to neglect visual information.

## Results

- Text-Debiased Hint-GRPO achieved significantly superior performance across **seven geometry reasoning datasets**, with an average accuracy increase from **36.83%** (GRPO) to **41.95%** on Qwen2.5-VL-3B.
- Ablation studies confirmed that converting multi-choice to fill-in-the-blank questions (preventing guessing) and using the adaptive hint strategy with `I_answer` injection are crucial for performance gains.
- Text-bias calibration further improved reasoning accuracy, with an optimal **`γ=0.8`**, demonstrating the method's ability to ensure MLLMs genuinely integrate visual context for correct answers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive hint injection as an output prefix effectively guides MLLMs to utilize reasoning steps during training while maintaining consistency between training and inference.
> - The 'text-bias' in MLLMs trained with ORM is a significant impediment to genuine multimodal reasoning and can be effectively counteracted by a test-time calibration mechanism.
> - Robust ORM training for MLLMs requires careful consideration of data sample validity and balanced multimodal input integration to achieve strong generalization on complex tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, particularly in their problem-solving capabilities, with models like OpenAI's o1 (though not explicitly cited, often a reference point for leading-edge models) pushing the boundaries. This paper specifically targets the improvement of MLLM reasoning, which is a critical aspect of their intelligence.

Current MLLM reasoning methods broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Process Reward Methods (PRM):** These methods supervise the intermediate reasoning steps, aiming to teach the model *how* to think. Historically, PRM approaches, often involving techniques like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) applied to reasoning chains, have been considered superior. Examples include LLaVA-o1, AtomThink, Mulberry, M-STAR, AR-MCTS, LLaVA-Reasoner-DPO, Virgo, and LlamaV-o1. However, PRM can suffer from challenges in accurately evaluating intermediate steps and is susceptible to "reward hacking."
2. **Outcome Reward Methods (ORM):** These methods supervise only the final reasoning results, evaluating the correctness of the ultimate answer. Recently, the landscape shifted dramatically with the emergence of **DeepSeek-R1**, which demonstrated that an ORM approach, specifically using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, could achieve strong generalization performance by inducing self-reflection in models without explicit supervision of intermediate steps. This finding challenged the conventional view and spurred significant research into ORM/GRPO for both LLMs and MLLMs, leading to models like Open-R1, simpleRL-reason, R1-V, and R1-Multimodal-Journey.

This work squarely situates itself within the post-DeepSeek-R1 era, acknowledging the power of ORM/GRPO. However, it identifies a crucial gap: while current MLLM ORM methods perform well on relatively simple visual tasks (e.g., counting objects), they still struggle with more challenging and complex multimodal reasoning tasks, such as mathematical reasoning, which often requires deep integration of visual and textual information to follow logical steps.

The paper, therefore, aims to bridge this gap by enhancing the robustness and effectiveness of GRPO specifically for MLLMs tackling complex tasks. It moves beyond simply applying GRPO to MLLMs by identifying and systematically addressing two fundamental problems that impede its performance in this context: low data utilization and text-bias. By tackling these specific limitations, the authors aim to push the state-of-the-art in MLLM reasoning further, making ORM a more viable and powerful paradigm for complex multimodal intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to significantly boost the reasoning capability of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly for challenging and complex tasks like mathematical reasoning, which demand intricate multimodal understanding.

The authors identify two critical problems that currently impede the performance of Outcome Reward Method (ORM) algorithms, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), when applied to MLLMs:

1. **Low Data Utilization:**
 - **Motivation:** GRPO relies on generating multiple predictions for a given question and assigning rewards (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) based on their accuracy. To effectively update the model, there needs to be a variation in rewards within a group of generated answers (i.e., `std(r)` must be non-zero).
 - **Problem:** For difficult questions, the initial MLLM often generates *all incorrect* answers. In this scenario, all rewards are zero, leading to zero advantages (`ˆA_i,t = 0`) for all tokens. This renders the training sample "invalid" because it provides no effective gradient for model updates related to reasoning accuracy. As shown in Figure 3(a), the original GRPO can have a data utilization rate as low as 40-50%, meaning a significant portion of training data is wasted.
 - **Objective:** To devise a method that increases the likelihood of the MLLM generating at least one correct answer for difficult samples, thereby ensuring positive rewards and improving the data utilization rate during GRPO training.

2. **Text-Bias:**
 - **Motivation:** MLLMs are designed to integrate information from both image and text inputs. However, observations suggest a detrimental phenomenon emerging after GRPO training.
 - **Problem:** The MLLM, post-GRPO training, exhibits a tendency to bypass the image condition and instead rely solely on the text condition to infer the final answer (as depicted in Figure 2). Figure 5(a) supports this, showing MLLM accuracy *without* an image increases as GRPO training progresses. This "text-bias" can lead to reasoning errors when the query text alone is insufficient to fully describe the question, necessitating crucial visual context.
 - **Objective:** To develop a mechanism that mitigates this text-bias, ensuring the MLLM effectively incorporates and weighs the image condition during its reasoning process, especially at inference time.

In summary, the key objectives are to thoroughly diagnose these two problems and propose practical, effective solutions—Hint-GRPO for low data utilization and text-bias calibration for text-bias—to significantly enhance the overall reasoning capabilities of MLLMs across a spectrum of multimodal tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose two primary methodological contributions, Hint-GRPO and Text-Bias Calibration, to address the identified problems within the GRPO framework for MLLM reasoning.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

The work builds upon GRPO, an Outcome Reward Method (ORM) that optimizes a model by sampling a group of outputs (`{o_1, ..., o_G}`) from an old policy (`π_θ_old`), assigning a reward (`r_i`) to each output based on its final correctness, and then calculating normalized advantages (`ˆA_i,t`) for each token. A KL divergence regularization term keeps the new policy (`π_θ`) close to a reference model (`π_ref`). The core challenge GRPO faces, as highlighted, is when all `r_i` are zero for a given sample, leading to `ˆA_i,t = 0` and thus ineffective gradient updates for the reasoning component.

#### 3.2. Hint-GRPO: Addressing Low Data Utilization

To overcome the problem of low data utilization, where MLLMs fail to generate any correct answers for difficult questions, Hint-GRPO adaptively provides reasoning hints (`h`) during training. The implementation involves three key components:

1. **Dataset Construction:**
 - **Base Dataset:** LLaVA-CoT, a high-quality MLLM reasoning dataset.
 - **Modifications:**
 - **Structured Reasoning Steps:** GPT-4o is used to split the original, long reasoning steps into multiple structured, granular steps. This allows for precise control over the hint level by providing a specific number of initial steps.
 - **Fill-in-the-Blank Conversion:** Multi-choice questions are converted into fill-in-the-blank format by removing options. This prevents the MLLM from achieving correct answers through mere random guessing, forcing it to engage in genuine reasoning.

2. **Hint Injection Method:**
 - **Problem with Naive Injection (`I_query`):** Simply appending hints to the query text (e.g., "Solve following hint: {Hint}") often results in the MLLM ignoring the hint or creating inconsistencies between training (with hint in query) and testing (no hint in query).
 - **Proposed `I_answer` (Hint Injection in Answer):** The hint is provided as the *beginning* of the model's output sequence (`<BoS> + Hint + ...`). This ensures the MLLM "faithfully" continues reasoning from the given steps. Critically, the original query text remains unchanged in both training and testing, maintaining consistency and improving performance.

3. **Hint Adaptation Strategy:**
 - **Goal:** Provide an optimal hint level (`α`, representing the ratio of correct reasoning steps provided) for samples of varying difficulty, avoiding both insufficient hints (leading to zero rewards) and excessive hints (preventing the MLLM from learning to reason independently).
 - **Explored Strategies:**
 - **Fixed Hint Level:** A constant `α` for all samples. Suboptimal as it doesn't account for varying difficulty.
 - **Random Hint Level:** `α` is randomly sampled from Uniform(0,1) for each sample. Improves data utilization but still not optimal for individual sample difficulty.
 - **Adaptive Hint Level (Proposed):** This is the core strategy. For each training sample, `M` different hint levels are generated, corresponding to hint ratios `α_i = (i-1)/M` (e.g., for M=3, α=0, 0.33, 0.66). The MLLM generates outputs for each of these `M` groups. The strategy then **selects the first group (starting from the lowest hint level, i.e., group 1 with α=0) that contains at least one correct answer** for training. This ensures that:
 - Difficult samples receive enough hints to yield at least one correct output, making them "valid" for GRPO.
 - Easier samples are not over-hinted, maximizing their potential for independent reasoning training.
 - **Efficiency:** Although multiple groups are generated, only one group is used for training. Coupled with `vLLM` for accelerated generation, this strategy introduces only a moderate increase in training time (e.g., 20.5% for M=2).

#### 3.3. Text-Bias Calibration: Mitigating Image Neglect

To counteract the tendency of GRPO-trained MLLMs to ignore image conditions, text-bias calibration is applied during **test-time**:

- **Inspiration:** Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) from image generation, which enhances the impact of conditioning inputs.
- **Mechanism:**
 1. The MLLM generates token prediction logits in two modes:
 - `ˆπ_θ(o_t|q_img)`: With both image (`q_img`) and text (`q_text`) conditions.
 - `ˆπ_θ(o_t)`: With only text (`q_text`) condition (effectively, an "unconditional" prediction relative to the image).
 2. The final calibrated logit `ˆπ_calibrated_θ(o_t|q_img)` is computed as:
 `ˆπ_calibrated_θ(o_t|q_img) = ˆπ_θ(o_t|q_img) + γ * (ˆπ_θ(o_t|q_img) - ˆπ_θ(o_t))`
 - **Hyper-parameter:** `γ` controls the intensity of image conditioning.
- **Intuition:** This formula amplifies the difference between the image-conditioned and text-only predictions, effectively pushing the model's output away from what it would predict based on text alone and closer to what it predicts when fully considering the image. This directly alleviates the text-bias by making the image condition more influential in the final token selection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments to validate their proposed methods across various MLLMs and datasets, demonstrating significant performance improvements.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Base MLLMs:** Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B (for geometry reasoning); Llama-3.2-11B-Vision (for universal multimodal reasoning).
- **Training:** AdamW optimizer, learning rate 5e-5, 2 epochs, batch size 1 per GPU. DeepSpeed with ZeRO-3 optimization and vLLM for accelerated generation were employed.
- **Prompting:** A structured system prompt ` <think>{Reasoning Steps}</think><answer>{Reasoning Result}</answer>` was used.
- **Hyper-parameters:** Adaptive hint group number `M=3`, text-bias calibration strength `γ=0.8`.
- **Training Datasets:** Geometry samples from LLaVA-CoT (7840 samples) for geometry tasks; full LLaVA-CoT (100,000 samples) for universal tasks.
- **Test Benchmarks:**
 - **Geometry Reasoning:** Geo170K, MathVista (Geometry), MMStar (Geometry), MathVerse (Geometry), Math-Vision (Geometry), MM-Math (Geometry), WeMath.
 - **Universal Multimodal Reasoning:** MMStar, MMBench, MMVet, MathVista, AI2D, Hallusion.
- **Baselines:** Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT), PRM methods (Mulberry), and existing GRPO methods (Open-R1-Multimodal, R1-V, and GRPO on their own dataset).

#### 4.2. Comparison Analysis

- **Mathematical (Geometry) Reasoning (Table 1):**
 - **SFT Baseline:** Showed reduced performance on out-of-domain data, indicating rote memorization rather than generalized reasoning.
 - **PRM (Mulberry):** Outperformed SFT, suggesting some benefit from supervising intermediate steps.
 - **GRPO Methods:** Open-R1-Multimodal, R1-V, and GRPO on their dataset consistently demonstrated superior performance over both SFT and PRM, reinforcing the efficacy of ORM in fostering independent reasoning.
 - **Debiased Hint-GRPO:** The proposed method achieved **significantly superior performance** across all seven geometry datasets and for both Qwen2-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B base models. For instance, on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, the average accuracy increased from 36.83% (GRPO) to 41.95% (Debiased Hint-GRPO), underscoring the effectiveness of addressing low data utilization and text-bias.
- **Universal Multimodal Reasoning (Table 2):**
 - **Debiased Hint-GRPO:** Outperformed the LLaVA-o1 (SFT) baseline on Llama-3.2-11B-Vision across six universal benchmarks.
 - **Observation:** The performance improvement in universal multimodal reasoning was less pronounced compared to geometry reasoning. The authors hypothesize this is due to limitations in "accuracy estimation" for diverse universal tasks (e.g., bounding box localization requiring exact matches, leading to many "incorrect" labels even for nearly correct answers). They suggest using more robust metrics like IoU for such tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Experiments

- **Dataset Construction:** Converting multi-choice questions to fill-in-the-blank significantly improved GRPO performance (e.g., +4.19% average for Qwen2-VL-7B), validating the prevention of shortcut learning (guessing).
- **Hint Injection Method:** `I_answer` (hint as output prefix) dramatically outperformed `I_query` (hint in query) and the original GRPO, confirming its ability to ensure MLLMs use hints effectively and maintain train-test consistency.
- **Hint Adaptation Strategy:**
 - Low fixed hint ratios improved performance, but high ratios degraded it (over-hinting).
 - Random hint levels showed improvement over fixed.
 - **Adaptive hint level achieved the best results**, confirming its effectiveness in dynamically providing the most appropriate hint level for each sample's difficulty. `M=3` was found to be an optimal balance between performance and efficiency.
- **Text-Bias Calibration:** The calibration step further improved performance over Hint-GRPO. Ablations on `γ` showed that an optimal value (0.8) enhanced results, while excessively high `γ` led to performance degradation due to over-correction.

#### 4.4. Visualization Analysis

- **Hint-GRPO Impact (Figure 7):** Visual examples illustrated that after Hint-GRPO training, MLLMs (Qwen2-VL-7B) produced more detailed and correct reasoning chains for complex geometry problems. Similar improvements were observed for universal tasks (Llama-3.2-11B-Vision).
- **Text-Bias Calibration Impact (Figure 8):** A crucial visualization demonstrated that before calibration, the MLLM would ignore the real image and generate an answer based on an "imagined image" derived from the text, leading to an incorrect result. After applying text-bias calibration, the MLLM successfully focused on the actual image, leading to the correct reasoning and answer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) reasoning, particularly in enhancing the robustness and efficacy of Outcome Reward Method (ORM) paradigms like GRPO. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Deep Understanding and Diagnosis of MLLM Challenges:** The paper meticulously identifies and thoroughly analyzes two previously unaddressed, fundamental problems that hinder GRPO's performance in MLLMs: **low data utilization** and **text-bias**. This diagnostic work is crucial for the community, providing clear insights into the limitations of current state-of-the-art training methods.
- **Novel and Effective Solutions:**
 - **Hint-GRPO:** By introducing adaptive hinting during training, Hint-GRPO directly tackles the low data utilization problem. This innovative approach ensures that even difficult samples contribute meaningfully to model learning, preventing wasted computational resources and accelerating the acquisition of complex reasoning skills. The adaptive strategy (selecting the minimum hint for a correct answer) is particularly clever in optimizing learning efficiency and avoiding over-reliance on hints.
 - **Text-Bias Calibration:** The proposed test-time calibration method, inspired by Classifier-Free Guidance, offers an elegant solution to the text-bias phenomenon. By actively emphasizing the image condition, it ensures that MLLMs genuinely integrate visual information, leading to more accurate and contextually grounded reasoning, especially when visual cues are critical.
- **Advancing State-of-the-Art in MLLM Reasoning:** The experimental results demonstrate that the combined "Text-Debiased Hint-GRPO" significantly outperforms original MLLMs, PRM methods, and existing GRPO baselines across a wide array of challenging multimodal reasoning tasks, especially mathematical geometry problems. This establishes a new state-of-the-art for MLLM reasoning in these domains.
- **Enhanced MLLM Capabilities for Complex Tasks:** By making GRPO more robust, this work paves the way for MLLMs to tackle increasingly complex real-world problems that require deep logical inference and careful integration of visual and textual information. This is particularly relevant for applications in science, engineering, and education, where understanding diagrams and visual contexts is paramount.
- **Contributions to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Generalization:** The findings contribute to the broader understanding of RLHF-based training for large models. The insight that ORM can induce self-reflection and generalization (as shown by DeepSeek-R1) is further strengthened by this work, which demonstrates how to make such methods more robust in multimodal settings. The issue of 'reward hacking' often seen in PRM methods is inherently avoided by ORM's outcome-based supervision, which this work makes more effective.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also opens up new avenues for research, particularly the call for more robust accuracy estimation methods for GRPO in diverse universal multimodal tasks (e.g., using IoU for bounding box tasks). This suggests that while GRPO is powerful, its effectiveness is still tied to the quality and granularity of reward signals, prompting further innovation in multimodal evaluation.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The commitment to making the code publicly available (`https://github.com/hqhQAQ/Hint-GRPO`) is a valuable contribution to the research community, enabling reproducibility, further development, and wider adoption of these innovative techniques.

In conclusion, this paper provides a principled approach to overcoming key bottlenecks in MLLM reasoning, offering both theoretical insights and practical solutions that push the boundaries of multimodal AI capabilities.
