# Representation Calibration and Uncertainty Guidance for Class-Incremental Learning based on Vision Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sun Yat-sen University and HKUST developed a VLM-based class-incremental learning framework that mitigates cross-task class confusion and enables robust inference in exemplar-free settings. The method, employing a Mixture of Projectors for representation calibration and uncertainty guidance during inference, consistently surpassed existing methods on benchmarks like CIFAR100 and ImageNet-R, improving Last-A on CIFAR100 by 6.62% over memory-based methods.

## Problem

- Deep learning models struggle with catastrophic forgetting when acquiring knowledge about new classes sequentially in Class-Incremental Learning (CIL).
- Task-wise fine-tuning of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often creates disparate feature spaces, leading to cross-task class confusion and image-text misalignment during inference.
- Selecting the most appropriate task-specific feature representation for a test image is challenging when its originating task is unknown, particularly in exemplar-free CIL settings where no old data is retained.

## Method

- A two-stage training framework utilizing ==task-specific adapters== to learn new knowledge for each incremental task while keeping the pre-trained CLIP VLM frozen.
- Cross-task representation calibration is performed using a ==Mixture of Projectors (MoP)==, which maps features from diverse task-specific spaces into a unified, task-shared feature space.
- An ==uncertainty-guided inference strategy== leverages prediction entropy to select the most appropriate calibrated feature from multiple task-specific branches for final classification.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks including CIFAR100, ImageNet-R, Cars196, and Skin40, consistently outperforming both memory-based and exemplar-free baselines.
- On CIFAR100 in a 10-task setting, the method improved Last-A by **6.62%** and **7.09%** over memory-based PROOF and PR-CLIP, respectively, and by over **10%** Last-M on ImageNet-R (20-task) compared to prompt-based methods.
- The framework demonstrated robustness to varying hyperparameters and generalized effectively to different VLM backbones (OpenCLIP, SigLip), maintaining efficiency with a moderate number of trainable parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task-specific adapters, while efficient for individual tasks, necessitate a subsequent calibration step to unify their disparate feature spaces and alleviate cross-task confusion.
> - Pseudo-features, sampled from approximated Gaussian distributions of past class embeddings, can effectively substitute real data for training a shared representation module (MoP) in exemplar-free CIL.
> - Prediction uncertainty, quantified by entropy, serves as a reliable mechanism to guide the selection of the most suitable task-specific model output during inference in multi-task continual learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the critical challenge of **Class-Incremental Learning (CIL)**, a subfield of continual learning (CL). CIL aims to enable deep learning models to acquire knowledge about new classes sequentially over time without forgetting previously learned information (known as "catastrophic forgetting"). Unlike humans, traditional deep learning models struggle to adapt to new data streams without significant retraining or performance degradation on old tasks.

Historically, CIL methods have fallen into several categories:
- **Parameter regularization-based approaches:** These methods add constraints to model parameters to prevent significant shifts that would erase old knowledge.
- **Knowledge distillation-based approaches:** These use the outputs of a previous model version to guide the learning of a new model, preserving knowledge.
- **Replay-based approaches:** These store a small subset of old data (exemplars) in a memory buffer and replay them alongside new data during training, which is effective but raises privacy concerns and storage overhead.
- **Structure-based approaches:** These expand the model architecture by adding new modules for each task, aiming to avoid parameter overwriting.

More recently, the advent of powerful **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** like CLIP has revolutionized CIL. VLMs, pre-trained on vast amounts of image-text pairs, possess strong generalization abilities and rich semantic understanding. This allows them to leverage textual information as supervision, significantly enhancing resilience to catastrophic forgetting in image classification tasks. VLM-based CIL approaches can be broadly categorized into:
- **Fine-tuning the pre-trained image encoder:** These methods often employ Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) techniques but still face challenges like parameter overwriting or prompt selection.
- **Freezing the pre-trained image encoder:** These methods enhance image representations with trainable modules *following* the frozen encoder. While avoiding forgetting of the core VLM, the frozen features can limit performance, especially when domain gaps exist between pre-training and downstream data.

This paper specifically targets a common problem within **task-wise fine-tuning** of VLMs, where each CL task is associated with a task-specific learnable light module (e.g., adapters). While efficient, a key challenge arises: how to correctly select or fuse these task-specific modules or features during inference, especially when the test image's originating task is unknown. Existing task-specific fine-tuning often leads to a "cross-task image-text misalignment issue," where a test image from one task might be misclassified by an encoder adapted for another task due to spurious alignments. Furthermore, the paper operates in the challenging **exemplar-free CIL setting**, meaning no old class samples are available during the learning of new tasks, increasing the difficulty compared to replay-based methods.

By proposing a novel framework that combines task-specific adapters with a cross-task representation calibration strategy and an uncertainty-guided inference mechanism, this work directly addresses these fundamental limitations in the VLM-based CIL landscape. It aims to unify diverse task-specific feature spaces and improve inference accuracy without relying on memory buffers, pushing the boundaries of exemplar-free continual learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a novel VLM-based continual learning framework that effectively addresses the challenges of **differentiating classes across learning tasks** in an exemplar-free CIL setting. The authors identify two primary motivations stemming from the limitations of current state-of-the-art VLM-based CIL methods:

1. **Alleviating Cross-Task Class Confusion and Image-Text Misalignment:** Existing VLM-based CIL methods that employ task-wise fine-tuning (e.g., with task-specific adapters) often lead to independent, disparate feature spaces for each task. While an adapter *f_i* may perform well on its corresponding *i*-th task, it can inadvertently align an image from task *j* (where *j ≠ i*) with a class from task *i*. This "cross-task image-text misalignment" causes incorrect class predictions because the model needs to classify images from *all* learned tasks, not just the current one. The primary motivation is to unify these disparate task-specific feature spaces into a single, cohesive "task-shared feature space" where classes from all tasks are well-separated and comparable, thus enhancing differentiation across tasks.

2. **Developing an Accurate Inference Strategy for Task-Specific Features:** Following the learning of multiple tasks with task-specific modules, a critical problem emerges during inference: how to select the "most appropriate" task-specific feature representation for a given test image when its originating task is unknown. Simply taking the highest similarity score across all task-specific predictions can lead to errors due to the misalignment issue. The motivation here is to design an intelligent inference mechanism that can reliably identify which task's adapted encoder generates the most suitable feature for prediction, ensuring accuracy even when multiple task-specific modules are involved. The authors hypothesize that prediction uncertainty could serve as a reliable guide for this selection process.

In essence, the paper seeks to improve both the *representation quality* (by unifying task-specific features) and the *decision-making process* (by guiding inference with uncertainty) in VLM-based class-incremental learning, particularly in the challenging scenario where no old data is retained.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for exemplar-free class-incremental learning is a novel VLM-based system structured around a two-stage model training process, complemented by a prediction uncertainty-guided inference strategy. The backbone is the pre-trained and frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 model.

**A. Two-stage Model Training**

1. **Task-specific Adapters for CIL (Training Stage-I):**
 - **Objective:** To enable the pre-trained VLM's image encoder to learn new knowledge specific to each incoming task without extensive modification of the original model.
 - **Mechanism:** For each new task *t* with *c_t* classes, light-weight adapters (denoted *A_t*) are added to each Transformer block of the frozen CLIP image encoder. Only the parameters of these newly added task-specific adapters are optimized.
 - **Loss Function:** Standard cross-entropy loss (*L_ce*) is used, with class probabilities derived from image-text similarity.
 - **Outcome:** After learning *T* tasks, *T* distinct groups of adapters are obtained, each specializing in its respective task.
 - **Identified Problem:** While effective for individual tasks, this approach leads to independent feature spaces. During inference, feeding an image through an encoder *f_i* adapted for task *i* might yield high similarity scores with classes from task *i* even if the image truly belongs to task *j ≠ i*, causing cross-task misalignment.

2. **Cross-Task Representation Calibration (Training Stage-II):**
 - **Objective:** To alleviate the cross-task image-text misalignment issue by mapping image embeddings from diverse task-specific spaces into a unified, task-shared feature space where classes are better separated.
 - **Mechanism:** This is achieved through a novel application of a **Mixture of Projectors (MoP)** module. MoP consists of:
 - **Multiple Projectors ({P_m | m=1...,M}):** Each projector is a light-weight sub-network (e.g., a two-layer perceptron) that transforms an input feature.
 - **Gating Network (G):** A linear layer followed by a softmax operator. For an input image *x* processed by a task-specific encoder *f_t*, the gating network *G(f_t(x))* produces weights *g_t* that determine the contribution of each projector.
 - **Adaptive Mapping:** The calibrated feature *z_t* from an adapted encoder *f_t(x)* is obtained by *z_t = f_t(x) + Σ_{m=1}^{M} g_{t,m} P_m(f_t(x))*. This allows MoP to adaptively adjust the feature representation based on which task-specific encoder generated it, unifying all features into a shared space.
 - **Training MoP (Exemplar-Free Challenge):** Since old class samples are unavailable in the exemplar-free setting, training data from *all* learned tasks are required to train the task-shared MoP. The authors address this by approximating the feature distribution of each class (from any task *t* and class *c*) as a multivariate Gaussian distribution *N(μ_{t,c}, Σ_{t,c})*. The mean (*μ_{t,c}*) and covariance (*Σ_{t,c}*) are computed and saved after Stage-I training for each class.
 - **Pseudo-Feature Sampling:** *N_p* pseudo image embeddings are sampled from these approximated Gaussian distributions for all learned classes across all tasks. These pseudo-features are then used to train the MoP with cross-entropy loss (*L_ce*), ensuring that the MoP can effectively separate classes across tasks in the unified feature space.

**B. Inference Strategy with Prediction Uncertainty**

- **Challenge:** Given a test image, its true task is unknown. Simply passing it through all *T* adapted encoders and MoPs yields *T* calibrated features {*z_t* | *t*=1...,*T*}. The key is to select the *most appropriate* *z_t* for final prediction.
- **Proposed Strategy:** The authors leverage **prediction uncertainty**, hypothesizing that a model provides lower uncertainty for in-distribution samples. They propose that if a test image comes from task *t*, the feature *z_t* (calibrated from *f_t(x)*) should result in lower prediction uncertainty compared to features calibrated from *f_j(x)* (where *j ≠ t*).
- **Uncertainty Measurement:** Entropy is used to quantify prediction uncertainty. For each calibrated feature *z_t*, its similarities with all *C* class text embeddings are calculated to obtain a probability distribution *p*. The entropy *E_t(p) = - Σ_{c=1}^{C} p_c log p_c* is computed.
- **Selection and Prediction:** The calibrated feature *z_t* that exhibits the **lowest entropy** (least uncertainty) is selected as the most appropriate. This selected feature is then used for final classification by calculating similarities with text embeddings of all *C* classes, and the class with the highest similarity is the final prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experimental evaluations demonstrating the superiority of their proposed method across various datasets and settings.

**A. Effectiveness Evaluation (State-of-the-Art Performance):**
- **Datasets:** Evaluated on CIFAR100, ImageNet-R, Cars196 (natural scenarios), and Skin40 (medical applications), and Mini-ImageNet100 (in appendix). These cover diverse image domains and fine-grained classification challenges.
- **Settings:** Experiments conducted under 5-task, 10-task, and 20-task settings to assess performance under varying numbers of incremental tasks.
- **Baselines:** Compared against a comprehensive set of CIL methods, including:
 - Memory-based: PROOF, PR-CLIP (using a fixed memory buffer for fair comparison).
 - Prompt-based (exemplar-free): L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt.
 - Adapter/Structure-based (exemplar-free): EASE, SLCA, MoE-Adapters, AttriCLIP, Lang-CL.
- **Metrics:** Last Accuracy (Last-A) and Average Accuracy (Avg-A) for class-balanced datasets; Last Mean Class Recall (Last-M) and Average Mean Class Recall (Avg-M) for class-imbalanced datasets.

**Key Performance Highlights:**
- **Overall SOTA:** The proposed method consistently achieves state-of-the-art results across almost all evaluated benchmarks and task settings.
- **CIFAR100:** In the 10-task setting, it improved Last-A by 6.62% and 7.09% over memory-based methods PROOF and PR-CLIP, respectively, and by substantial margins over other exemplar-free methods.
- **ImageNet-R:** For the challenging 20-task setting, the method outperformed typical prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) by at least 10% in Last-M.
- **Fine-Grained Datasets (Cars196, Skin40):** Demonstrated strong performance on fine-grained and domain-specific datasets. On Cars196 (10-task), it surpassed EASE and Lang-CL by 15.64% and 3.45%, respectively. Its performance on Skin40 highlights its superior generalization ability to medical image data.
- **Mini-ImageNet100 (Appendix):** Achieved the best results, even though performance gaps were generally smaller due to high CLIP zero-shot performance.
- **Learning Curves (Appendix):** Figure 5 shows consistent outperformance across almost every incremental task, indicating sustained effectiveness throughout the continual learning process.

**B. Ablation Study:**
- The study confirmed the effectiveness of each proposed component:
 - **Task-Specific Adapters (TSA):** Adding TSAs significantly improved performance (over 5% Last-A/M) compared to a CLIP zero-shot baseline, validating their role in task adaptation.
 - **Mixture of Projectors (MoP) for Calibration:** Training MoP with pseudo-features alone (without TSAs) still outperformed the baseline, confirming the efficacy of cross-task representation calibration.
 - **Uncertainty-Guided Inference (Entropy):** The combination of TSA, MoP, and entropy-guided inference achieved the best performance, highlighting the synergistic effect of the entire two-stage framework.

**C. Sensitivity Analysis:**
- **Number of Projectors (M) in MoP:** The method showed robustness when *M* was set between 2 and 8. Performance slightly declined only for *M > 5*, attributed to potential overfitting from increased parameters.
- **Number of Pseudo-Features (N_p) per Class:** The method was robust to varying *N_p*. Performance dropped only for very small *N_p* (insufficient diversity) or very large *N_p* (potential overfitting).

**D. Further Analysis:**
- **MoP vs. Single Projector:** MoP significantly outperformed a single projector (over 1% improvement), demonstrating the advantage of its greater capacity and the gating network's ability to provide dynamic mapping combinations for cross-task feature calibration.
- **Different Inference Strategies:** The proposed entropy-based inference strategy consistently outperformed other approaches, including simply selecting the maximum probability across tasks ("Max") and an energy-based approach ("Energy"). The "Energy" approach performed poorly due to the minimal energy differences between in-distribution and out-of-distribution samples in CLIP's cosine similarity space.

**E. Generalizability Study (Appendix):**
- The method was tested with different pre-trained VLM weights (OpenCLIP and SigLip) to assess generalizability.
- It significantly outperformed replay-based methods (PROOF, PR-CLIP) on both SigLip and OpenCLIP, especially on SigLip (e.g., 47.44% Last-M improvement over PROOF on ImageNet-R), confirming its robust generalization capabilities across different VLM variants.

**F. Comparison in Trainable Parameters and Inference Time (Appendix):**
- **Trainable Parameters:** The method achieved SOTA performance with a moderate number of trainable parameters (11M, or 3.9% of total parameters), indicating efficiency and not relying on excessively large models.
- **Inference Time:** While slightly slower than some baselines due to evaluating multiple branches, the inference time remained acceptable (avg. 50 images/second). Crucially, the multi-branch architecture allows for **multi-process parallelization**, significantly reducing inference time and improving scalability with an increasing number of tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of exemplar-free Class-Incremental Learning (CIL), particularly for Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based systems. Its contributions have several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Breakthrough in Exemplar-Free CIL:** By achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple challenging benchmarks without retaining any old class samples, the proposed framework addresses a critical limitation of many CIL methods. This is particularly impactful for applications where data privacy is paramount, storage is limited, or legal/ethical constraints prevent storing past data. It enables more practical and scalable continual learning systems.

2. **Effective Resolution of Cross-Task Confusion:** The core innovation of using a Mixture of Projectors (MoP) for cross-task representation calibration directly tackles the pervasive problem of feature misalignment and class confusion that plagues task-specific fine-tuning approaches. By unifying disparate task-specific feature spaces into a single, well-separated shared space, the method significantly improves the model's ability to distinguish between classes learned at different times.

3. **Novel and Robust Inference Mechanism:** The uncertainty-guided inference strategy, based on entropy, provides a simple yet highly effective way to select the most appropriate task-specific feature during prediction. This is crucial for models that learn multiple, specialized modules over time, ensuring accurate decision-making even when the origin of the test data is unknown. Its superiority over other common inference strategies (Max, Energy) highlights its robustness.

4. **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across a diverse range of datasets (natural images, fine-grained, medical) and different VLM backbones (OpenCLIP, SigLip) underscores the strong generalization capabilities of the proposed method. This suggests that the framework is not narrowly tailored but offers a robust solution applicable to various real-world scenarios and future VLM developments.

5. **Parameter Efficiency and Scalability:** Achieving SOTA results with a moderate number of trainable parameters (3.9% of total) is important for deploying continual learning systems in resource-constrained environments. Furthermore, the inherent parallelizability of the inference process in their multi-branch architecture offers a practical solution to manage increasing inference times as more tasks are learned, enhancing scalability.

6. **Future Research Directions:** This work opens avenues for future research into more sophisticated methods for synthesizing pseudo-features, exploring other forms of uncertainty quantification in VLMs, and applying similar representation calibration techniques to other continual learning paradigms (e.g., domain-incremental learning or task-incremental learning with different task boundaries). The success with pseudo-features might also inspire methods to synthesize more diverse or complex "replay" data without explicit storage.

In conclusion, this paper presents a highly significant contribution to continual learning, offering practical, effective, and theoretically sound solutions to long-standing problems in VLM-based CIL. Its impact is likely to be felt in various applications requiring adaptive AI systems, from medical diagnostics to autonomous systems, where models must continually learn and evolve in dynamic environments.
