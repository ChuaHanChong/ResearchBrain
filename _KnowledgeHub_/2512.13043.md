# GTR-Turbo: Merged Checkpoint is Secretly a Free Teacher for Agentic VLM Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed GTR-Turbo, a framework that enables Vision-Language Models to train as autonomous agents by generating an internal teacher from merged checkpoints of their own learning process. This approach notably reduces training time and computational costs while achieving performance competitive with methods reliant on external, expensive teacher models on multi-turn reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Training Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents for multi-turn decision-making is challenging due to sparse rewards and difficulties in long-horizon credit assignment.
- Existing process-guided reinforcement learning methods depend on costly, proprietary, or inaccessible external teacher models, leading to high computational expenses and extended training times.
- VLM agents often suffer from "thought collapse," where their reasoning becomes repetitive or inconsistent, without adequate step-level guidance.

## Method

- **GTR-Turbo** introduces a self-contained framework where an internal "==free teacher==" is dynamically generated by merging historical checkpoints of the agent during ongoing reinforcement learning.
- The framework employs TIES-merging to combine model checkpoints, which effectively mitigates parameter interference and sign disagreement, enhancing the quality of the merged teacher.
- Guidance from the internal teacher is provided either through SFT-based distillation of reference thoughts or via an efficient soft logit distillation using reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence on thought tokens.

## Results

- GTR-Turbo (KL) achieved a **53.5%** success rate on the Points24 task, outperforming the original GTR (**44.5%** SR) and significantly surpassing larger commercial API models.
- The framework reduced wall-clock training time by up to **52%** and computational cost by 31-**60%** across various tasks compared to methods relying on external API teachers.
- GTR-Turbo effectively mitigated "thought collapse" and enabled fine-tuned models to achieve performance beyond the `pass@k` success rate ceiling of their base capabilities on complex visual agentic benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An evolving internal teacher, created by aggregating the agent's own past experiences through checkpoint merging, can effectively provide process guidance without external dependencies.
> - Sophisticated merging techniques like TIES are crucial for producing a consistent and high-performing internal teacher by resolving conflicts among diverse model parameters.
> - Soft logit distillation using clipped reverse KL divergence offers an efficient and effective method for guiding the agent's thought process, promoting alignment with desirable reasoning patterns.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in training Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as agents for complex, multi-turn decision-making tasks using reinforcement learning (RL). Historically, VLMs have evolved from static question-answering systems to autonomous agents capable of perceiving, reasoning, and acting in interactive environments. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Outcome Rewards (RLVR) is a paradigm that fine-tunes these models directly with environmental reward signals, showing promise in domains like mathematics and code generation.

However, vanilla RL methods for multi-turn agentic tasks face several hurdles: sparse rewards, long-horizon credit assignment, and noisy environments. These issues can lead to "thought collapse," where an agent's reasoning becomes repetitive, inconsistent, or lacks diversity, thereby degrading performance.

To counteract sparse rewards and thought collapse, prior research has explored "process guidance," which involves providing step-level feedback. Methods such as Guided Thought Reinforcement (GTR) and On-Policy Distillation exemplify this by utilizing a larger, more capable "teacher" model to offer step- or token-level supervision, regulating the agent's reasoning process. While effective in enhancing performance, these teacher-dependent paradigms introduce significant practical limitations, including high computational costs, extended training times, and reliance on expensive, often proprietary, or inaccessible models (e.g., GPT, Gemini).

In parallel, model merging techniques have gained prominence as an efficient method for model adaptation. These techniques combine the weights of multiple models to improve performance, enable continual learning, or escape local optima. Approaches range from simple averaging to more sophisticated methods like Fisher Merging, Task Arithmetic, and TIES-merging, which specifically address parameter interference.

GTR-Turbo integrates these two areas by proposing a self-contained process guidance mechanism. It aims to circumvent the need for external teachers by internally generating one through the merging of checkpoints produced during the ongoing RL training. This positions GTR-Turbo as an evolution of process-guided RL that emphasizes efficiency and autonomy, addressing the scalability and accessibility constraints of previous teacher-dependent approaches while aiming to maintain or improve performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a highly efficient and scalable framework, named GTR-Turbo, for training multi-turn agentic Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This framework aims to achieve performance comparable to or surpassing existing process-guided reinforcement learning (RL) methods, specifically Guided Thought Reinforcement (GTR), without incurring the substantial costs and dependencies associated with external teacher models.

The motivation behind GTR-Turbo stems from several challenges inherent in the current landscape of VLM agent training:

1. **Addressing Sparse Rewards and Long-Horizon Credit Assignment:** Multi-turn agentic tasks typically feature sparse rewards, meaning feedback is received only at the end of long sequences of actions. This makes it difficult for RL agents to attribute credit to individual actions or intermediate reasoning steps, leading to slow and unstable learning. While methods like GTR offer dense, step-level feedback, their implementation presents other difficulties.

2. **Eliminating Reliance on Expensive and Inaccessible External Teacher Models:** Existing process-guided methods, such as GTR, achieve dense reward signals by querying a powerful, often proprietary, external VLM as a teacher or corrector. This reliance introduces several significant drawbacks:
 - **High Computational Cost:** API calls to large, sophisticated models like GPT-4o are expensive, accumulating substantial costs over the many steps of RL training.
 - **Extended Training Time:** Network latency and the sequential nature of API calls significantly prolong the wall-clock training time.
 - **Model Inaccessibility:** Cutting-edge VLM teachers may be proprietary or not publicly accessible, limiting reproducibility and widespread adoption of these training paradigms.
 - **Data Privacy Concerns:** Sending sensitive intermediate reasoning steps to external API models can raise privacy and security issues.

3. **Mitigating "Thought Collapse" and Enhancing Training Stability:** In the absence of adequate guidance, VLM agents in multi-turn RL can suffer from "thought collapse" or "entropy collapse," where their generated reasoning becomes repetitive, illogical, or inconsistent, hindering effective exploration and learning. A key motivation is to maintain the benefits of process guidance in preventing this phenomenon, but through an internal mechanism.

4. **Improving Scalability and Practicality:** By removing the dependency on external teachers, the researchers aim to create a fully self-contained, self-evolving training system. This would allow VLM agents to learn and improve autonomously from their own experiences and internal knowledge aggregation, making the training process more scalable, practical, and adaptable for various downstream tasks and environments, especially where external expertise or strong teachers are unavailable.

In summary, GTR-Turbo seeks to provide a cost-effective, time-efficient, and autonomous solution for robustly training VLM agents in complex interactive environments, while maintaining or improving their reasoning and decision-making capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GTR-Turbo builds upon the Guided Thought Reinforcement (GTR) framework, modifying it to eliminate the dependency on external teacher models. The core innovation lies in generating an internal, "free" teacher by merging checkpoints produced during the agent's ongoing reinforcement learning (RL) training.

**Revisiting Guided Thought Reinforcement (GTR):**
The original GTR framework trains a VLM agent for multi-turn decision-making tasks by jointly optimizing an RL objective for action tokens and a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) objective for reasoning tokens. An external VLM, acting as a "corrector," evaluates and refines the agent's reasoning (thoughts) at each RL step, providing the supervision for the SFT loss. This mechanism helps prevent "thought collapse" and stabilizes training. GTR also incorporates Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) to mitigate distribution shifts.

**Core Innovation: Merged Checkpoints as the Teacher:**
GTR-Turbo introduces a buffer that stores historical model checkpoints generated throughout the RL training process. At each update epoch, these checkpoints are merged to create a new "merged teacher" model, $\pi^{(k)}_{\text{merged}}$. This merged model is designed to aggregate prior experiences and consistently outperform the current training agent, thus serving as an internal teacher without requiring additional training or external model dependency. A proof-of-concept shows that the merged model achieves more stable and better performance than individual checkpoints.

**Model Merging Technique (TIES-merging):**
To avoid harmful parameter interference when merging checkpoints, GTR-Turbo employs the Trim, Elect Sign, and Merge (TIES) method. TIES consists of three steps:
1. **Trimming:** Only parameter changes with magnitudes in the top-k% are retained, filtering out redundant or minor perturbations.
2. **Sign Election:** For each parameter, a majority vote determines a consolidated sign vector across all models, based on the total magnitude of positive and negative values.
3. **Selective Averaging:** Only parameters whose signs align with the elected sign are included in the averaging process, ensuring that merging aligns contributions rather than canceling them out.

**Weight Adjustment Variants for Merging:**
The research explores two strategies for assigning weights to checkpoints during merging:
- **Simple Moving Average (SMA):** All historical checkpoints are given equal weight, and their parameters are arithmetically averaged.
- **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** More recent checkpoints are prioritized by applying exponentially decayed weights, controlled by a smoothing factor $\alpha$.

**Thought Guidance Mechanisms:**
GTR-Turbo offers two mechanisms for the merged teacher to provide guidance:

1. **SFT-based Thought Guidance (GTR-Turbo (SFT)):**
 - This approach directly replaces the external corrector in the original GTR framework with the internally generated merged teacher.
 - At each RL step, after the agent generates its thought and action, the merged teacher generates a "reference thought" based on the same context.
 - This reference thought is stored in a replay buffer.
 - During PPO updates, an SFT loss is computed between the agent's thought and the teacher's reference thought, which is then added to the PPO loss for backpropagation.
 - This variant continues to incorporate format rewards and DAgger techniques for training stability.

2. **Soft Logit Distillation via Minimizing Reverse KL Divergence (GTR-Turbo (KL)):**
 - This is presented as a more efficient alternative to SFT guidance.
 - Instead of requiring autoregressive generation from the teacher, which is computationally intensive, this method uses a single forward pass to compute logits.
 - A negative reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence is calculated between the agent's and the merged teacher's token-level output distributions for the thought tokens.
 - This KL divergence term is then incorporated as an auxiliary reward for PPO updates.
 - The use of KL divergence provides a more relaxed constraint than one-hot SFT labels, captures probability information across all candidate tokens, and is computationally efficient.
 - To ensure the validity of the reward signal, negative parts of the sentence-level KL estimation are clipped.

**Overall Training Flow:**
The GTR-Turbo framework operates in an iterative loop:
1. An agent interacts with the environment, generating thoughts and actions.
2. The merged teacher (or its logits) provides guidance based on the agent's context.
3. An RL update is performed, combining environmental rewards with the teacher's guidance (either SFT loss or KL-based auxiliary reward).
4. The updated agent model's weights are saved as a new checkpoint.
5. This new checkpoint is added to the buffer, and the buffer is used to create a new merged teacher for the next training iteration.

This self-evolving design allows the agent to continuously improve without external assistance, leveraging its own aggregated past experiences. The system is implemented using the Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B models on diverse visual agentic tasks like Points24 and ALFWorld.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance, efficiency, and design choices of the GTR-Turbo framework across various visual agentic tasks.

**Effectiveness of GTR-Turbo Framework:**

- **Points24 Task:**
 - GTR-Turbo (KL) achieved the highest success rate (SR) of 53.5% and an episode return (ER) of 2.39. This surpassed both the original GTR (44.5% SR, 0.53 ER) and other baselines, including much larger commercial API models (e.g., GPT-4o at 2.5% SR).
 - GTR-Turbo (SFT) also outperformed GTR, achieving an SR of 48.0% and an ER of 1.32.
 - RL4VLM, a baseline without process guidance, exhibited "thought collapse," leading to declining performance.
 - The results indicate that GTR-Turbo not only mitigates thought collapse but also enables a smaller, fine-tuned model to surpass larger general-purpose models on specialized tasks.
- **ALFWorld Environment:**
 - In the complex ALFWorld environment, GTR-Turbo (KL) achieved a peak average success rate of 15%, which is comparable to GTR (16%). This is notable given that GTR relied on powerful external teachers (GPT-4o) while GTR-Turbo learned solely from its own exploration and merged teacher.
 - RL4VLM again showed model collapse.
 - The performance demonstrates GTR-Turbo's capability to learn effectively in challenging tasks, even when lacking external knowledge or a pre-trained strong teacher.

**Computational Efficiency and Cost:**

- GTR-Turbo significantly reduced both wall-clock training time and computational cost compared to GTR.
- For Points24, GTR-Turbo (KL) reduced training time by approximately 50% (89 hours vs. GTR's 191 hours) and computational cost by about 60% (~$114.81 vs. GTR's ~$307.78).
- For ALFWorld, GTR-Turbo (KL) reduced training time by approximately 52% (78 hours vs. GTR's 164 hours) and cost by about 31% (~$100.62 vs. GTR's ~$145.76).
- GTR-Turbo eliminated the need for online API calls, thereby removing network latency, API costs, and data privacy concerns associated with external teacher models.

**Ablation Studies and Discussions:**

- **Comparison with Self-Improvement Baselines:**
 - Using a static initial checkpoint as a KL reference failed to achieve stable improvement, demonstrating the necessity of dynamic model merging for the teacher.
 - Rejection Sampling, an SFT-based self-improvement method, struggled with challenging tasks like Points24, highlighting the importance of RL exploration for generating effective trajectories.
- **Range of Guidance:** Guiding only the "thought" (reasoning) part of the agent's response, rather than both "thought" and "action" simultaneously, proved more effective. Guiding actions or imposing stronger SFT constraints limited the agent's exploratory freedom, which is crucial for self-evolution in GTR-Turbo.
- **Effectiveness of TIES Merging:** TIES merging consistently boosted performance compared to traditional linear averaging of parameters. This suggests that TIES effectively mitigates parameter interference and sign disagreement, enhancing the quality of the merged teacher model.
- **Different KL Estimation Methods:**
 - All non-negative KL estimators (clipping, absolute value, K3, forward KL) led to performance improvements.
 - Clipping the negative part of the reverse KL divergence (as used in GTR-Turbo (KL)) yielded the best results. This is attributed to its ability to control the magnitude of KL values, leading to finer-grained updates and improved stability.
 - Reverse KL generally outperformed forward KL, consistent with its "mode-seeking" characteristic, which helps the agent align with specific desirable behaviors of the teacher.
- **Different Weights Assignment Methods:** Simple Moving Average (SMA) showed strong performance. Exponential Moving Average (EMA) required a balanced smoothing factor ($\alpha \approx 0.5$) to be effective. Extreme $\alpha$ values either diminished the influence of historical checkpoints too quickly or introduced bias in early stages, degrading performance.

**Additional Results (from Appendix):**

- **Compatibility with Stronger Models:** GTR-Turbo demonstrated compatibility and improved performance when applied to the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model on ALFWorld, even surpassing larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B models.
- **Performance on Open-Ended Tasks:** In the Android-in-the-Wild (AitW) GUI benchmark, GTR-Turbo outperformed baselines like DigiRL and PPO, and achieved higher reasoning scores.
- **Beyond Base Model Capabilities:** GTR-Turbo enabled the agent to surpass the `pass@k` success rate ceiling of the base model, indicating that it acquired capabilities beyond its original distribution.
- **Reasoning Score Evaluation:** Evaluation using GPT-5.2 as an LLM-as-a-judge confirmed that GTR-Turbo effectively mitigated "thought collapse," leading to higher quality reasoning traces compared to baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GTR-Turbo presents a significant contribution to the field of agentic Vision-Language Model (VLM) training, primarily by addressing critical limitations of existing process-guided reinforcement learning (RL) methods. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Enhanced Scalability and Practicality:** The most immediate impact of GTR-Turbo is the elimination of reliance on expensive, proprietary, or inaccessible external teacher models (e.g., GPT, Gemini). This directly translates to substantial reductions in computational cost and wall-clock training time. By making advanced VLM agent training more affordable and faster, GTR-Turbo increases the practicality and accessibility of these techniques for a wider range of researchers and applications, particularly for those without access to vast computational resources or commercial API budgets.

2. **Autonomous and Self-Evolving Agents:** GTR-Turbo introduces a fully self-contained, self-evolving training paradigm. Agents can improve their decision-making and reasoning capabilities solely through environmental feedback and the aggregation of their own past experiences. This autonomy is crucial for developing agents that can adapt and learn in dynamic, open-ended environments without continuous external intervention or supervision.

3. **Robustness and Performance Maintenance:** The framework demonstrates that it can achieve comparable or even superior performance to methods relying on powerful external teachers. This indicates that the internal "free teacher" generated through merged checkpoints is effective in providing high-quality guidance, mitigating common issues like "thought collapse" without sacrificing performance. The robustness across diverse tasks (Points24, ALFWorld, Android-in-the-Wild) and different model sizes further attests to its general applicability.

4. **Mitigation of "Thought Collapse":** "Thought collapse," where VLM agents produce repetitive or incoherent reasoning, is a pervasive problem in multi-turn RL. GTR-Turbo effectively counters this by consistently providing a stable and improving internal teacher. This leads to more logical, correct, and coherent reasoning traces, which are fundamental for reliable decision-making in complex tasks.

5. **Data Privacy and Security:** By eliminating the need to send intermediate reasoning data to external API models, GTR-Turbo offers a solution for training agents in scenarios with strict data privacy requirements. This can expand the adoption of VLM agents in sensitive domains where data confidentiality is paramount.

6. **Customization and Specialization:** The ability to fine-tune VLMs efficiently and autonomously enables greater customization. Developers can train specialized VLM agents for niche tasks or domain-specific applications without needing to rely on general-purpose models that might lack the required domain knowledge or be prohibitively expensive to query. This could lead to a proliferation of highly effective, tailored agents across various industries.

7. **Future Research Directions:** GTR-Turbo's success in bootstrapped learning opens new avenues for research into self-improvement mechanisms, the dynamics of internal teacher evolution, and the optimal balance between exploration and guidance in self-learning systems. It challenges the assumption that external, superior models are indispensable for advanced process supervision, pushing the boundaries of what can be achieved with self-generated knowledge.

In conclusion, GTR-Turbo represents a significant step towards more practical, scalable, and autonomous training of VLM agents, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and efficient for broader scientific and industrial applications.
