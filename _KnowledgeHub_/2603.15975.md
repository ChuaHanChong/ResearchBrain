# UMO: Unified In-Context Learning Unlocks Motion Foundation Model Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> The UMO framework unifies diverse 3D human motion generation tasks by adapting a pretrained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) foundation model through a novel in-context learning formulation. It achieved state-of-the-art performance across various tasks like temporal inpainting and instruction-based editing, demonstrating emergent capabilities in geometric reasoning and multi-person interaction from single-person pretraining.

## Problem

- The 3D human motion generation field was fragmented, relying on task-specific architectures that hindered knowledge sharing and cross-task generalization.
- Powerful large-scale motion foundation models (LFMs), primarily trained for text-to-motion (T2M), had their rich generative priors largely unutilized for diverse downstream applications.
- Prior attempts to adapt pretrained generative models to individual downstream tasks often required substantial architectural modifications or complex conditioning modules, leading to inefficiencies.

## Method

- **UMO** introduces a unified formulation that casts diverse motion generation tasks as compositions of atomic per-frame operations using learnable ==`[preserve]`==, ==`[generate]`==, and ==`[edit]`== meta-operation embeddings.
- It efficiently adapts a pretrained ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== backbone, specifically **HY-Motion**, through a lightweight ==in-context learning== mechanism that injects task-aware input and unified language conditions.
- All task conditions are expressed as text and processed by the same pretrained LLM encoder, removing the need for task-specific conditioning modules and ensuring extensibility.

## Results

- UMO-Unified achieved a state-of-the-art **FID of 9.460** on HumanML3D for text-to-motion generation, outperforming dedicated T2M models and demonstrating enhanced performance through joint task training.
- For geometric-constrained generation, UMO matched the trajectory error of optimization-based methods (**18.78 cm vs. 17.89 cm** for OmniControl) while being approximately **90 times faster** (0.759s vs. 68.10s).
- The framework successfully generalized to dual-identity reaction generation from single-person pretraining data, achieving the best **FID (2.055)** among all methods on the InterHuman benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale T2M pretraining implicitly embeds highly transferable motion priors, allowing a single model to generalize robustly to tasks like geometric reasoning and multi-person interaction despite not being explicitly trained for them.
> - The LLM encoder, when activated through fine-tuning, can understand and enforce complex geometric constraints (e.g., parameterized trajectories, obstacle avoidance) purely through structured language.
> - Explicit meta-operation embeddings (`[preserve]`, `[generate]`, `[edit]`) are essential for providing granular, frame-level control over motion content, enabling effective editing and preservation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D human motion generation has seen progress across various tasks, but it has historically been characterized by fragmentation, with specialized architectures developed for each specific task. This fragmentation hinders knowledge sharing and cross-task generalization. In other generative domains, such as text and image synthesis, the unification of diverse tasks under shared formulations has been shown to effectively leverage powerful generative priors from large foundation models (LFMs). However, a comprehensive unified framework has largely been absent in the motion domain.

Recent advancements in motion LFMs, such as Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) and decoder-only transformers, have significantly scaled model capacity and training data, learning general generative priors for human motion. These models, exemplified by HY-Motion, primarily focus on basic text-to-motion (T2M) generation. Consequently, the generative priors embedded within these T2M models remain largely unutilized for a broader range of applications. Prior work typically adapts pretrained generative models to individual downstream tasks in a task-specific manner, often requiring dedicated architectures or conditioning modules for each application.

This research addresses the challenge of effectively and efficiently leveraging these single-purpose T2M motion LFMs for a wider spectrum of cross-modal and in-context motion generation tasks. It aims to bridge the gap between the established capabilities of T2M models and the diverse requirements of downstream applications like temporal inpainting, text-guided motion editing, geometric constraint application, and multi-identity reaction generation. The work is positioned to explore whether the rich, general motion priors learned during large-scale T2M pretraining can be unlocked and transferred to these varied tasks within a single, unified framework, thereby moving away from task-specific solutions towards a more generalized approach.

Motion generation tasks can be broadly categorized. Cross-modal generation (e.g., from audio, 3D environments, or spatial constraints) involves mapping external signals to motion sequences. In-context generation requires balancing fidelity to a source motion with responsiveness to conditions, further divided into homogeneous tasks (single motion entity, e.g., editing, completion) and heterogeneous tasks (multiple entities, e.g., reaction generation). Existing efforts to unify multiple tasks often face data insufficiency or fail to surpass specialist models. UMO's approach of lightweight supervised fine-tuning of large-scale pretrained T2M models aims to overcome these limitations by activating existing priors rather than learning from scratch.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified and efficient framework, named UMO (Unified In-Context Learning), that can unlock the generative priors of pretrained large-scale motion foundation models (LFMs) to support a broad spectrum of diverse downstream motion generation tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and existing limitations in the field of 3D human motion generation:

1. **Underutilization of Pretrained Motion LFM Priors:** Recent motion LFMs have demonstrated strong generative capabilities in text-to-motion (T2M) synthesis by learning from massive 3D human motion datasets. However, these models are typically trained for a single purpose (T2M) and their rich generative priors, which could potentially benefit a wider array of applications, remain largely untapped for more diverse cross-modal and in-context motion generation tasks.
2. **Fragmentation of Solutions:** The prevalent approach in motion generation involves developing task-specific architectures and dedicated training pipelines for each downstream task. This fragmentation leads to isolated solutions that cannot share knowledge or generalize across different applications, resulting in redundant development efforts and inefficiencies in data utilization.
3. **Lack of a Unified Framework in Motion:** While other generative domains (e.g., text, images) have successfully adopted unified frameworks to leverage LFMs for diverse tasks, such a framework has been largely absent in the motion domain. This absence prevents the consolidation of scarce per-task data and limits the potential for improved generalization that a unified approach could offer.
4. **Inefficiency of Task-Specific Adaptations:** Prior attempts to adapt pretrained generative priors to individual downstream tasks often require substantial architectural modifications or the addition of complex conditioning modules. This process can be computationally intensive and may not fully exploit the latent capabilities of the original foundation model.
5. **Desire for Broad Task Coverage within a Single Model:** The researchers aim to enable a single model, originally limited to T2M generation, to handle diverse previously unsupported tasks. These tasks include temporal inpainting (e.g., prediction, backcasting, in-betweening, keyframe control), text-guided motion editing (via fine-grained instructions or high-level style prompts), text-serialized geometric constraints, and multi-identity reaction generation. The goal is to achieve this without significant architectural modifications to the pretrained backbone.

By addressing these motivations, UMO seeks to provide a generalizable, efficient, and extensible solution for motion generation, maximizing the utility of powerful motion LFMs and fostering cross-task knowledge transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The UMO framework proposes a unified formulation that casts diverse downstream motion generation tasks into compositions of atomic per-frame operations. This approach enables in-context adaptation to unlock the generative priors of pretrained Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based motion LFMs, specifically using HY-Motion as its backbone.

#### 3.1. Preliminary

- **Flow Matching:** UMO adopts the rectified flow formulation for motion generation. This involves training a neural network `v_θ` to regress the target velocity `v_t = x_1 - x_0` between Gaussian noise `x_0` and clean motion `x_1` along an interpolation path `x_t = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1`. Motion is generated by solving an ODE using `v_θ`.
- **HY-Motion Backbone:** HY-Motion is employed as the pretrained motion LFM. It is a DiT-based T2M model trained on over 3,000 hours of motion data, utilizing a multimodal DiT (MMDiT) architecture and text conditioning via a Qwen3-8B LLM encoder and a CLIP encoder.
- **Motion Representation:** Consistent with HY-Motion, each motion frame is represented as a 201-dimensional vector `f ∈ R^201`, comprising global root translation (3D), root orientation (6D), 21 local joint rotations (each 6D), and 22 local joint positions (each 3D). Motions are resampled to 30 fps and normalized. A sequence of T frames is denoted `x ∈ R^(T ×201)`.

#### 3.2. Unified In-Context Motion Generation Formulation

The core insight of UMO is that any target motion frame can be related to its source motion context in one of three mutually exclusive ways:

- **Frame-Level Meta-Operation Embeddings:** Three learnable frame-level meta-operation embeddings—`[preserve]` (P), `[generate]` (G), and `[edit]` (E), each `R^201`—are introduced. These embeddings specify per-frame intent:
 - `[preserve]`: Retain the frame unchanged.
 - `[generate]`: Synthesize the frame from scratch, without direct reference to existing content.
 - `[edit]`: Modify the source motion content based on a condition.
 For a T-frame sequence, each frame `i` is assigned `τ_i ∈ {P, G, E}` and paired with a source motion `s_i ∈ R^201` (original motion `m_i` for P and E, or `0` for G).

- **Unified Language Conditioning Design:** All task conditions are expressed as text and processed by the same pretrained LLM encoder, eliminating the need for task-specific conditioning modules. Four prompt templates are designed:
 - **Description:** Standard semantic motion descriptions.
 - **Editing Instruction:** Natural language instructions for motion modification.
 - **Parameterized Trajectory:** Structured language (JSON-like format) for 3D trajectory specifications (e.g., lines, arcs, Bézier curves), including start/end points, control points, curvature, etc.
 - **Spatial Constraint:** Structured language describing scene-level 3D constraints, such as start/goal positions and obstacles (center coordinates, safety radius).
 This design is extensible, allowing new constraint types via new prompt templates without architectural changes.

- **Task Instantiation via Composition:** Each downstream task is fully specified by a per-frame `(s, τ)` configuration (source motion and meta-operation embedding) combined with a language condition. The three meta-operations form a complete and minimal basis for frame-level intent, and the language conditioning provides an open-ended condition space.

#### 3.3. In-Context Conditioning Architecture

The encoded in-context motion signal `(s, τ)` is injected into the DiT backbone.

- **In-Context Motion Encoder:** The meta-operation embedding `Emb(τ)` is added element-wise to the source motion `s` to form a task-aware input `s̃ = s + Emb(τ)`. This `s̃` is then encoded by an MLP encoder `E_ctx`, which is initialized from HY-Motion's pretrained input encoder `E_in` and finetuned.

- **Architecture Alternatives (for injecting `E_ctx(s̃)`):** Four injection architectures were considered and ablated:
 - **(a) Temporal Fusion:** `E_ctx(s̃)` is added element-wise to `E_in(x_t)` (the input embedding of the noisy motion `x_t`) before the DiT blocks. This is a lightweight approach with minimal parameter and runtime overhead, preserving per-frame granularity.
 - **(b) Sequential Concatenation:** `E_ctx(s̃)` is concatenated with `E_in(x_t)` along the sequence dimension, resulting in `2T` tokens. An asymmetric attention mask ensures `s̃` acts as a read-only conditioning signal. This increases computational cost significantly.
 - **(c) AdaLN:** `E_ctx(s̃)` is compressed into a global vector via attention pooling and injected alongside the timestep embedding through adaptive layer normalization. This provides layer-wise modulation but sacrifices per-frame granularity.
 - **(d) ControlNet:** The backbone is frozen, and its blocks are cloned into a parallel trainable branch. `E_ctx(s̃)` is injected through zero-initialized residual connections. This preserves original backbone weights but introduces substantial parameter overhead.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** HumanML3D (T2M, temporal inpainting), MotionFix (instruction-based editing), Inter-X and InterHuman (reaction generation). Custom datasets (2,000 sequences each) were constructed for parameterized trajectory control and obstacle avoidance, using TLControl to synthesize motions based on generated curves and obstacle configurations.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Standard metrics were used: FID, R-Precision (R@1/2/3), Multimodal Distance (MM-Dist) for generation quality and semantic alignment; Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and [P]-MPJPE (for preserved frames) for temporal inpainting; Generated-to-Target retrieval R@1/2/3 and average rank (AvgR) for editing; Trajectory Error, inference Latency, and obstacle avoidance Success Rate for geometric constraints.
- **Implementation:** HY-Motion-Lite (460M parameters) was used. Models were trained on 4 NVIDIA B200 GPUs with a batch size of 256. The unified model was trained jointly for 100k steps across all tasks, while expert models were trained for 6k steps on their respective tasks. Inference used a 50-step Euler ODE solver with a classifier-free guidance scale of 2.0.
- **Baselines:** UMO was compared against state-of-the-art methods for each task, including a training-free inversion baseline for HY-Motion in temporal inpainting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of the UMO framework in unlocking and transferring motion priors from a pretrained T2M foundation model to diverse downstream tasks.

#### 4.1. Architecture Ablation

An ablation study on keyframe infilling (Table 3) revealed that **Temporal Fusion** achieved the most favorable balance of performance and efficiency. It yielded the best [P]-MPJPE (0.95 cm) and FID (0.476) with minimal additional parameters (0.207M) and latency (0.01s). AdaLN, despite achieving high R-Precision, showed poor [P]-MPJPE (11.1 cm), indicating its inability to respect per-frame constraints due to collapsing source motion into a global vector. Sequential Concatenation and ControlNet introduced higher overhead (up to 234.2M parameters and 0.89s latency) without superior performance. Temporal Fusion was thus selected as the default architecture for subsequent experiments.

#### 4.2. In-Domain Task Adaptation

- **Text-to-Motion Generation (HumanML3D):** Direct evaluation of HY-Motion on HumanML3D showed a high FID (61.035), suggesting a distribution gap. With lightweight adaptation, UMO-Expert significantly closed this gap (FID 17.04). **UMO-Unified** further improved all metrics, achieving a state-of-the-art FID of 9.460, surpassing dedicated T2M models (Table 4). This indicates that joint training across diverse tasks provides complementary supervision, enhancing performance on individual tasks rather than diluting it.

#### 4.3. Out-of-Domain Tasks Adaptation

- **Temporal Inpainting (HumanML3D):** UMO was evaluated on prediction, backcasting, in-betweening, and keyframe infilling (Table 5). A training-free HY-Motion baseline failed across all subtasks. Both UMO-Expert and **UMO-Unified** consistently outperformed CondMDI and MotionLab in generation quality (FID, R-Precision, MM-Dist). UMO also achieved competitive [P]-MPJPE, indicating its ability to respect conditioning frames through learned `[preserve]` embeddings, which is a different mechanism from hard replacement methods.
- **Instruction-Based Motion Editing (MotionFix):** UMO-Expert and **UMO-Unified** achieved near-perfect batch-level retrieval (R@3=100.0%) and substantially outperformed all prior task-specific methods. On the more challenging full-set protocol, UMO-Unified slightly surpassed UMO-Expert in R-Precision (Table 6). Qualitative results (Fig. 5) illustrated UMO's ability to apply precise, instruction-aligned edits while preserving unmodified semantics, validating the `[edit]` embedding. This demonstrated the repurposing of general motion priors for instruction following, despite never being pretrained on editing instructions.

#### 4.4. Stress Test: Ability Emergence

- **Geometric-Constrained Generation:** UMO was tested on trajectory following and obstacle avoidance, where constraints are encoded purely as structured language (Table 7). Zero-shot HY-Motion failed (Traj. Err=325.1cm). **UMO-Unified** matched optimization-based methods like OmniControl in trajectory error (18.78 cm vs. 17.89 cm) while being approximately 90 times faster (0.759s vs. 68.10s). Optimization-based methods achieved higher precision but incurred significantly greater latency. These results suggest that the LLM encoder, through its pretraining on diverse corpora, possesses a latent capacity for structured geometric understanding that fine-tuning can activate and align with the DiT's generative space (Fig. 6).
- **Dual Identity Reaction Generation (InterHuman):** UMO was evaluated on generating two-person interactions, a setting entirely absent from its single-person pretraining data (Table 8). **UMO-Unified** achieved the best FID (2.055) among all methods, though InterMask had a slightly higher R@1. This emergence of high-quality multi-person reactions from a single-person motion LFM suggests that core motion priors (e.g., body dynamics, temporal coherence) are transferable across single-person and multi-person scenarios, and UMO's in-context formulation can effectively activate them.

#### 4.5. Necessity of Meta-Operation Embeddings (Appendix C)

An ablation confirmed the necessity of all three meta-operation embeddings ([preserve], [generate], [edit]). Collapsing `[edit]` into `[generate]` led to degraded performance in both temporal inpainting and instruction-based editing (Table 11). This indicated that `[generate]` cannot effectively handle both unconditional synthesis and source-aware modification simultaneously, and an explicit `[edit]` signal is crucial for fine-grained editing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UMO framework represents a significant step towards unifying diverse tasks in 3D human motion generation and has several implications:

- **Unified and Generalizable Framework:** UMO introduces a novel, simple, yet general unified formulation that casts diverse motion tasks into compositions of atomic per-frame operations. This addresses the long-standing fragmentation in the field by enabling a single model to handle tasks ranging from temporal inpainting and text-guided editing to geometric constraints and multi-identity interactions.
- **Efficient Unlocking of Foundation Model Priors:** The research demonstrates that the powerful, single-purpose generative priors of large-scale T2M foundation models (like HY-Motion) can be effectively and efficiently unlocked for a broad spectrum of previously unsupported tasks. This is achieved through a lightweight in-context adaptation mechanism, involving only three learnable meta-operation embeddings and a temporal fusion module, with negligible runtime overhead compared to the base model.
- **Evidence of Broad Prior Transferability:** A notable finding is the robust transferability and generalization of motion priors learned from large-scale T2M pretraining. This is exemplified by:
 - **Geometric Reasoning:** The ability to encode complex geometric constraints (e.g., parameterized trajectories, obstacle avoidance) purely in language space, achieving performance comparable to dedicated spatial conditioning methods without specialized modules. This suggests that large language models (LLMs) used for conditioning may implicitly encode structured spatial understanding.
 - **Multi-Person Interaction:** The successful generalization of single-person motion priors to dual-identity reaction generation, producing high-quality interactions despite never being trained on multi-person data. This indicates that fundamental motion dynamics learned from individual motions are transferable to interaction scenarios.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** UMO consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance across a wide range of benchmarks, outperforming task-specific and training-free baselines with a single, unified model. The lightweight conditioning design (Temporal Fusion) ensures high efficiency.
- **Extensibility and Scalability:** The proposed meta-operation and unified language conditioning design is inherently extensible. Supporting new constraint types or task variations primarily requires defining new prompt templates and `(s, τ)` patterns, rather than significant architectural modifications, suggesting a scalable path for future development in motion generation.
- **Implications for Foundation Models:** The work provides evidence that large-scale pretraining in the motion domain, much like in other domains, can implicitly embed latent competencies that extend beyond the initial training objective. These competencies can be activated and leveraged through appropriate in-context interfaces and targeted fine-tuning, maximizing the return on investment in large-scale model pretraining.

Potential limitations, such as the whole-body nature of meta-operations and the current focus solely on language conditioning, suggest avenues for future research into finer-grained part-level control and multimodal (e.g., audio) integration. Nevertheless, UMO lays a foundation for more versatile and unified approaches to human motion synthesis.
