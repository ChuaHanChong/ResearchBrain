# UMO: Unified In-Context Learning Unlocks Motion Foundation Model Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> The UMO framework unifies diverse 3D human motion generation tasks by adapting a pretrained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) foundation model through a novel in-context learning formulation. It achieved state-of-the-art performance across various tasks like temporal inpainting and instruction-based editing, demonstrating emergent capabilities in geometric reasoning and multi-person interaction from single-person pretraining.

## Problem

- The 3D human motion generation field was fragmented, relying on task-specific architectures that hindered knowledge sharing and cross-task generalization.
- Powerful large-scale motion foundation models (LFMs), primarily trained for text-to-motion (T2M), had their rich generative priors largely unutilized for diverse downstream applications.
- Prior attempts to adapt pretrained generative models to individual downstream tasks often required substantial architectural modifications or complex conditioning modules, leading to inefficiencies.

## Method

- **UMO** introduces a unified formulation that casts diverse motion generation tasks as compositions of atomic per-frame operations using learnable ==`[preserve]`==, ==`[generate]`==, and ==`[edit]`== meta-operation embeddings.
- It efficiently adapts a pretrained ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== backbone, specifically **HY-Motion**, through a lightweight ==in-context learning== mechanism that injects task-aware input and unified language conditions.
- All task conditions are expressed as text and processed by the same pretrained LLM encoder, removing the need for task-specific conditioning modules and ensuring extensibility.

## Results

- UMO-Unified achieved a state-of-the-art **FID of 9.460** on HumanML3D for text-to-motion generation, outperforming dedicated T2M models and demonstrating enhanced performance through joint task training.
- For geometric-constrained generation, UMO matched the trajectory error of optimization-based methods (**18.78 cm vs. 17.89 cm** for OmniControl) while being approximately **90 times faster** (0.759s vs. 68.10s).
- The framework successfully generalized to dual-identity reaction generation from single-person pretraining data, achieving the best **FID (2.055)** among all methods on the InterHuman benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale T2M pretraining implicitly embeds highly transferable motion priors, allowing a single model to generalize robustly to tasks like geometric reasoning and multi-person interaction despite not being explicitly trained for them.
> - The LLM encoder, when activated through fine-tuning, can understand and enforce complex geometric constraints (e.g., parameterized trajectories, obstacle avoidance) purely through structured language.
> - Explicit meta-operation embeddings (`[preserve]`, `[generate]`, `[edit]`) are essential for providing granular, frame-level control over motion content, enabling effective editing and preservation.
