# SegDiff: Segmented Trajectory Diffusion for Consistent and Adaptive Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SegDiff presents a closed-loop visuomotor policy for robotic manipulation that integrates segmented trajectory diffusion with dynamic temporal ensembling. This system achieved an 86.7% average success rate on RLBench, surpassing baselines by at least 11.1%, and showed enhanced adaptability in real-world tasks involving multi-modal actions and dynamic environments.

## Problem

- Existing continuous action prediction methods for imitation learning are susceptible to compounding errors over long task horizons.
- Current approaches struggle with multi-modal action distributions, where a single observation might correspond to several valid actions, leading to ambiguous or unsafe behaviors.
- Keypose-based methods, while mitigating compounding errors, often rely on open-loop external planners, limiting real-time adaptability to dynamic environments.

## Method

- The **Segmented Trajectory Modeling (STM)** component reframes prediction to continuous trajectories from the current state to the next ==keypose==, leveraging a ==diffusion model== to learn conditional distributions of these interpolated segments.
- **Dynamic Temporal Ensembling (DTE)** uses ==DDIM inversion== to generate prior-informed predictions, enabling intelligent blending of current policy predictions with an action buffer based on mode-level consistency and buffer validity metrics.
- A closed-loop ==visuomotor policy== is formed by integrating **STM** within a ==Receding Horizon Control (RHC)== scheme, using **DTE** to maintain action consistency and responsiveness during inference.

## Results

- SegDiff achieved an **86.7%** average success rate on 10 RLBench tasks, outperforming all baselines by at least **11.1%**, and showed a **34%** improvement over Diffusion Policy on the full RLBench-60 benchmark.
- In real-world experiments, SegDiff achieved **100%** success on the 'Package' task (multi-modal actions) by preventing mode switching and demonstrated faster recovery and adaptation in the dynamically perturbed 'Banana' task.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Segmented Trajectory Modeling (STM) improved convergence and performance, and Dynamic Temporal Ensembling (DTE) was crucial for handling multi-modal actions and dynamic perturbations in real-world scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing trajectory prediction to segments anchored by keyposes effectively transforms long-term compounding error accumulation into a series of bounded, short-horizon regulation problems.
> - DDIM inversion facilitates the regeneration of consistent trajectories from a previously sampled mode, which is crucial for preventing mode-switching issues in multi-modal action distributions.
> - A conditional blending mechanism, informed by buffer validity and mode discrepancy, enables dynamic adaptation to environmental changes while maintaining temporal consistency of actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is continuously advancing, with imitation learning (IL) emerging as a prominent paradigm for robots to acquire skills from expert demonstrations. Offline imitation learning (OIL) further refines this by learning from pre-recorded datasets, enhancing data efficiency and safety. Within OIL, behavior cloning (BC) serves as a foundational approach, but traditional BC policies, which predict immediate actions, are susceptible to compounding errors. Small deviations can accumulate, driving the robot into states not encountered during training (out-of-distribution, OOD).

Existing research approaches in visuomotor policy learning generally fall into two categories:
- **Continuous Action Prediction:** Methods in this category, such as Diffusion Policy (DP) and ACT, predict short-horizon continuous action sequences. While they improve short-term consistency, they still face challenges with compounding errors over extended durations and struggle to handle multi-modal action distributions, where a single observation might correspond to several valid actions. Averaging across such modes can lead to ambiguous or unsafe behaviors. Temporal ensembling has been applied to smooth motions, but its effectiveness can be limited in dynamic or multi-modal scenarios.
- **Keypose-Based Prediction:** These methods, including PerAct and RVT, focus on predicting discrete, salient points (keyposes) in a trajectory. This approach mitigates compounding errors by directing the robot toward specific critical states. However, they typically rely on external path planners to connect these keyposes, leading to an open-loop control design. This design can limit adaptability to dynamic environments and restrict real-time applicability due to the latency introduced by external planning modules.

Some hybrid approaches have attempted to combine continuous action prediction with keypose guidance. However, these often involve multi-stage architectures, which can increase inference latency and propagate errors between modules. They also frequently struggle with the multi-modal nature of robotic actions, leading to mode switching and reduced responsiveness.

SegDiff is situated within this landscape by seeking to integrate the strengths of both continuous and keypose-based prediction paradigms. It aims to develop a closed-loop visuomotor policy that addresses the compounding errors of continuous methods and the real-time adaptability limitations of keypose-based methods, particularly in dynamic and multi-modal environments. The work attempts to enhance action consistency and responsiveness by explicitly modeling trajectories between keyposes and introducing a mechanism for dynamic temporal refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and adaptive closed-loop visuomotor policy for robot manipulation that overcomes the limitations of current imitation learning approaches. Specifically, SegDiff aims to:

1. **Mitigate Compounding Errors for Long-Horizon Manipulation:** Existing continuous action prediction methods typically operate on a sliding-window paradigm, predicting short sequences. While this provides some consistency, minor errors at each step can quickly accumulate over a long task horizon, leading to significant deviations from the expert trajectory and potential task failure. The motivation is to enable long-horizon prediction while maintaining control stability and accuracy.

2. **Enhance Robustness to Multi-Modal Action Distributions:** Robotic manipulation tasks often present multi-modal action distributions, where multiple distinct sequences of actions could achieve the same goal (e.g., grasping an object from different sides). Current continuous prediction methods, especially those based on averaging or standard temporal ensembling, can produce an average action that is not representative of any single valid mode, resulting in ambiguous or unsafe robot behaviors. A key objective is to handle these multi-modalities gracefully, ensuring action consistency and preventing undesirable mode switches.

3. **Improve Real-Time Adaptability in Dynamic Environments:** Keypose-based methods, while effective at addressing compounding errors by focusing on critical states, typically rely on open-loop external planners. This design restricts their ability to adapt to unexpected changes in the environment or perturbations during execution, limiting their real-time applicability. The motivation is to develop a policy that can continuously refine its plan and respond to dynamic changes without incurring high latency from multi-stage inference or external planning.

4. **Integrate Advantages of Both Paradigms:** The research is motivated by the observation that both continuous prediction (for smoothness and immediate action) and keypose prediction (for structural guidance and long-term planning) offer distinct advantages. The objective is to combine these benefits in a seamless, end-to-end architecture, avoiding the complexity and error propagation issues of previous multi-stage hybrid approaches.

5. **Develop a Coherent Temporal Ensembling Mechanism:** While temporal ensembling is used in some continuous prediction methods to smooth actions, it can struggle when predictions switch between inconsistent modes or when the buffered actions become outdated due to environmental changes. The goal is to develop a more sophisticated temporal ensembling mechanism that can actively validate and refine the action buffer, ensuring both consistency and responsiveness.

By addressing these challenges, SegDiff aims to provide a more reliable, consistent, and adaptive policy for robotic manipulation in complex and unpredictable real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SegDiff introduces a closed-loop visuomotor policy that integrates elements of continuous action prediction and keypose-based control. The methodology is structured around two main components: Segmented Trajectory Modeling (STM) and Dynamic Temporal Ensembling (DTE).

#### 3.1. Segmented Trajectory Modeling (STM)

The core idea of STM is to reframe the prediction target. Instead of predicting short, fixed-horizon action sequences (continuous prediction) or discrete keyposes (keypose prediction), SegDiff learns to predict the continuous trajectory from the robot's current state to the *next* keypose. This approach anchors the prediction to task-critical states, aiming to mitigate compounding errors and provide clear directional guidance.

1. **Keypose Extraction**: Keyposes are extracted from expert demonstrations. These are defined as points where the gripper state changes (opens or closes) or the end-effector velocity approaches zero, following the method used in PerAct. The full demonstration trajectory is then segmented into sub-trajectories based on these identified keyposes. Each segment begins at an arbitrary timestep `t` and ends at the subsequent keypose `a_k_m`. The last action `a_T` is always included as a keypose.

2. **Training Data Formulation**: Each training sample consists of a historical observation window `o_{t-h:t}`, the current robot state `s_t`, and the corresponding sub-trajectory `a_{t:k_m}` (from current state `t` to the next keypose `k_m`).

3. **Trajectory Interpolation**: Since the length of these sub-segments (`k_m - t`) is variable, all trajectories are interpolated to a fixed length `L`. Cubic spline interpolation is used for translation, and spherical linear interpolation is used for rotation, ensuring smooth and consistent trajectory representations.

4. **Diffusion Model Training**: A diffusion model is employed to learn the conditional distribution of these fixed-length sub-segments. The training objective (`L_SegDiff`) minimizes the difference between the noise predicted by the model and the actual noise added to the interpolated ground-truth sub-segment, conditioned on observations and robot state. This process allows the model to generate diverse, continuous trajectories towards specific keyposes.

The theoretical analysis (provided in the supplementary material) suggests that STM mitigates compounding errors because error propagation within each segment is bounded by the open-loop dynamics constant, which is smaller than the closed-loop constant governing sliding-window methods. This effectively transforms a long-term compounding error problem into a series of bounded, short-horizon regulation problems.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Temporal Ensembling (DTE) for Inference

During inference, SegDiff operates using a Receding Horizon Control (RHC) scheme. At each step `t`, it predicts a trajectory `τ_t`, executes an initial portion (`n_exec` actions), and stores the remainder in an action buffer `b_t` for future blending. DTE is designed to address two challenges with naive buffer reuse: inconsistencies due to multi-modal action distributions and invalidation of the buffer by dynamic environmental changes.

1. **DDIM Inversion for Prior-Informed Sampling**: SegDiff leverages DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) inversion. Given an existing action buffer `b_t` (a trajectory), DDIM inversion can reverse the diffusion process to find the initial noise vector (`ϵ_prior`) that would generate `b_t`. This `ϵ_prior` can then be used to re-denoise a new trajectory (`τ_prior`) conditioned on the *latest* observation `o_{t+1}`, effectively regenerating a trajectory in the same action mode as `b_t` but updated for the current context.

2. **Two Prediction Candidates**: At each inference step, two trajectories are generated:
 - `τ_rand`: A standard prediction sampled from random Gaussian noise, representing the policy's most responsive estimate based solely on the current observation.
 - `τ_prior`: A prior-informed prediction obtained by performing DDIM inversion on the current action buffer `b_t` to get `ϵ_prior`, and then denoising from `ϵ_prior` conditioned on the latest observation. This aims to maintain consistency with the buffer's action mode.

3. **Mode-Level Consistency and Buffer Validity Metrics**: To decide how to blend these candidates with the action buffer, two RMSE-based metrics are computed:
 - `d_valid` = RMSE(`b_t`, `τ_prior`): Quantifies buffer validity. A large value indicates `b_t` is no longer feasible under the current observation (e.g., due to environmental changes).
 - `d_mode` = RMSE(`τ_rand`, `τ_prior`): Quantifies mode discrepancy. A large value suggests `τ_rand` and `τ_prior` (and thus `b_t`) belong to different action modes.
 A threshold `δ` (determined from the maximum reconstruction error on the training set) is used to evaluate the significance of these deviations.

4. **Dynamic Temporal Ensembling Logic**: The final action sequence `τ_DTE` is computed based on the following rules:
 - If `b_t` is valid (`d_valid ≤ δ`) and `τ_rand` is mode-consistent (`d_mode ≤ δ`): `τ_DTE` is a blend of `τ_rand` and `b_t`, prioritizing `τ_rand` for responsiveness.
 - If `b_t` is valid (`d_valid ≤ δ`) but a mode switch is detected (`d_mode > δ`): `τ_DTE` is a blend of `τ_prior` and `b_t`, suppressing the mode switch to maintain consistency.
 - If `b_t` is invalid (`d_valid > δ`): `τ_DTE` is solely `τ_rand`, discarding the outdated buffer for rapid adaptation.
 An update factor `η ∈` controls the blending ratio.

5. **Execution and Buffer Update**: The first `n_exec` actions of `τ_DTE` are executed, and the remainder is interpolated to length `L` to form the new buffer `b_{t+1}`. This process continues until the target keypose is reached. The DDIM inversion steps are decoupled from sampling steps, allowing for a balance between efficiency and precision, and `τ_rand` and `τ_prior` can be generated in parallel to minimize latency.

#### 3.3. Model Architecture

SegDiff utilizes a ResNet18 encoder (without pre-training) for each camera view to process historical visual observations. BatchNorm layers are replaced by GroupNorm, and global average pooling is replaced with spatial softmax pooling to better capture spatial information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SegDiff was evaluated across both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating performance gains over existing approaches.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

**Benchmarks and Baselines:**
- **RLBench:** Evaluated on 10 representative tasks and the full RLBench-60 benchmark, which uses scripted demonstrations. Baselines included ACT, Diffusion Policy (DP), Chain-of-Action (CoA), and a fine-tuned Octo policy.
- **RoboMimic:** Evaluated on three tasks from the PH dataset, which contains human expert demonstrations via teleoperation. Baselines included DP and AWE (which emphasizes critical waypoints).

**Quantitative Results:**
- **RLBench (10 Tasks):** SegDiff achieved an average success rate of 86.7%, outperforming all baselines by at least 11.1%. It demonstrated the best performance on 9 out of 10 tasks, with notable improvements in precision-sensitive tasks (e.g., over 30% increase for "Turn Tap").
- **RLBench (Full-60 Benchmark):** SegDiff showed improved robustness and generalization, outperforming DP by 34% and CoA by 9% on average success rate.
- **RoboMimic:** SegDiff exhibited better data efficiency in low-demonstration regimes compared to DP and AWE, while maintaining competitive performance as the number of demonstrations increased. This suggests its keypose-anchored trajectory modeling effectively mitigates compounding errors with less data.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

**Tasks and Baselines:**
- Five real-world manipulation tasks were designed to test specific capabilities:
 - **Cube:** Position generalization.
 - **Flip Cup:** Complex 6-DoF manipulation.
 - **Package:** Multi-modal action distributions.
 - **Banana:** Real-time adaptation to dynamic environments.
 - **Screw Cap:** High-precision operations.
- Baselines included DP (with and without temporal ensembling) and ACT.

**Quantitative Results and Analysis:**
- SegDiff consistently achieved superior or comparable success rates across all real-world tasks.
- **Flip Cup:** SegDiff achieved a higher success rate by accurately planning motion to a precise grasp pose.
- **Package (Multi-modality):** SegDiff achieved 100% success, attributed to its Dynamic Temporal Ensembling mechanism preventing modality switching. In contrast, DP (with Temporal Ensembling) and ACT frequently failed due to oscillations between different action modalities.
- **Banana (Dynamic Adaptation):** SegDiff demonstrated faster recovery and correct actions when the object was perturbed, due to its long-horizon planning and ability to promptly discard an unusable action buffer. DP (with Temporal Ensembling) and ACT were negatively influenced by outdated buffers, and DP (without Temporal Ensembling) struggled with its short prediction horizon.
- **Screw Cap (Precise Manipulation):** SegDiff was the only method that achieved partial success, indicating an advantage in high-precision operations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Segmented Trajectory Modeling (STM):** Comparison with a Diffusion Policy baseline (without STM and DTE) indicated that STM accelerated convergence and led to higher performance with fewer training steps on RLBench tasks.
- **Dynamic Temporal Ensembling (DTE):**
 - **Simulation:** DTE showed no significant performance improvement in the simulation environment, which typically features perturbation-free, monomodal trajectories.
 - **Real-World:** DTE demonstrated substantial advantages. It boosted success rates in the dynamically perturbed "Banana" task and enabled superior performance in the multi-modal "Package" task compared to purely random sampling (RAND). The "INV" mode (inverted noise sampling) performed better than RAND in multi-modal tasks but negatively impacted simpler tasks, validating DTE's conditional logic.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**
 - **Interpolation Length (L):** Peak success rate on RLBench was observed at L=25, with SegDiff performing robustly across various L values.
 - **Update Factor (η):** Performance degraded when η=0 (no receding horizon update) or η=1 (discarding the action buffer entirely), supporting the necessity and efficacy of DTE's blending approach.
 - **Threshold (δ) for DTE:** DTE exhibited relative robustness to the selection of the threshold δ (based on maximum, 99th, or 95th percentile of reconstruction RMSE on the training set).

#### 4.4. Efficiency Analysis

- The number of DDIM inversion steps in DTE is decoupled from sampling steps, allowing for flexible configurations that balance efficiency and precision.
- On a real robot system, SegDiff introduced approximately 33.7 ms of additional latency per control cycle (a ~5.4% increase over standard Diffusion Policy), mainly due to trajectory interpolation and DDIM inversion. This overhead is considered negligible for high-frequency robot deployment while providing improved robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SegDiff offers several significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation and visuomotor policy learning:

1. **Effective Integration of Paradigms:** SegDiff successfully bridges the gap between continuous action prediction and keypose-based control. By explicitly modeling continuous trajectories anchored to keyposes (Segmented Trajectory Modeling), it combines the smooth motion generation capabilities of continuous methods with the structured task guidance of keypose-based approaches. This integration addresses a long-standing challenge in imitation learning.

2. **Robustness to Compounding Errors:** The segmented trajectory modeling approach fundamentally alters how errors propagate. By consistently re-anchoring predictions to the next keypose, SegDiff effectively transforms a long-term compounding error problem into a sequence of locally bounded, short-horizon regulation problems. This theoretical advantage is borne out in improved performance on long-horizon and high-precision tasks.

3. **Enhanced Adaptability and Consistency in Dynamic and Multi-Modal Environments:** The Dynamic Temporal Ensembling (DTE) mechanism, leveraging DDIM inversion, is a notable advancement. It enables the robot to adapt to dynamic environmental changes by intelligently discarding outdated action buffers while simultaneously preventing inconsistent mode switches in multi-modal scenarios. This capability is critical for deploying robots in complex and unpredictable real-world settings where both environmental perturbations and diverse task execution strategies are common.

4. **Improved Generalization and Data Efficiency:** Demonstrated superior performance across a diverse range of simulated and real-world tasks, including those requiring high precision, 6-DoF manipulation, and dynamic adaptation. Its performance in low-data regimes on RoboMimic suggests potential for better data efficiency by focusing learning on task-critical states.

5. **Practical Applicability:** The design of SegDiff maintains real-time responsiveness, with negligible additional computational overhead on a physical robot system. This is crucial for practical robot deployment where control loops operate at high frequencies.

The potential impact of SegDiff lies in its contribution to the development of more reliable, consistent, and adaptive visuomotor policies for complex robotic manipulation. By addressing core limitations of existing paradigms, it could facilitate the broader adoption of imitation learning for industrial automation, domestic robotics, and other applications requiring dexterous and robust robot control. Future research avenues identified, such as automated keypose discovery, indicate ongoing efforts to reduce manual annotation and further enhance autonomy.
