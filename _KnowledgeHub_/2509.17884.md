# The Surprising Effectiveness of Linear Models for Whole-Body Model-Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University and the Florida Institute for Human and Machine Cognition demonstrate that a single time-invariant linear dynamics model within a convex Model Predictive Control framework can effectively achieve dynamic locomotion, disturbance rejection, and navigation on both quadrupedal and hydraulic humanoid robots, enabling high control rates and robust performance. This approach bypasses the computational demands of full nonlinear model predictive control, showing its capability for fundamental legged locomotion tasks.

## Problem

- Legged robots exhibit underactuated, non-smooth, and highly nonlinear dynamics, posing challenges for robust control.
- Traditional Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) is computationally expensive, requiring the online solution of large, non-convex optimal control problems.
- NMPC approaches often lack convergence guarantees and are difficult to tune and formally analyze due to their complexity.

## Method

- A ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework leveraging a single time-invariant linear approximation of the robot's whole-body dynamics is proposed.
- Robot dynamics are linearized about a fixed standing pose and discretized, resulting in an affine linear state-space model.
- Contact conditions are handled by pre-specifying contact modes, transforming non-convex constraints into convex equality or inequality constraints.
- The resulting convex Quadratic Program (QP) allows for offline pre-factorization of its Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) system, reducing online computation to matrix-vector products and back-substitutions.

## Results

- The Unitree Go2 quadruped successfully demonstrated disturbance rejection from pushes, navigated to goal positions from substantial initial errors (up to 90-degree yaw), and stepped onto a 24 cm high box, handling significant joint deviations.
- The IHMC Nadia hydraulic humanoid performed stable walking in-place for over a minute and robust forward walking with stride lengths up to 30 cm across multiple trials, indicating dynamic motion capabilities.
- Online QP solutions using OSQP required only **0.10** 	 **0.02** ms for the Nadia humanoid's KKT system after offline factorization, facilitating closed-loop control rates of 500 Hz for the quadruped and 333 Hz for the humanoid.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single time-invariant linear dynamics model can be sufficiently effective for many fundamental legged locomotion tasks, obviating the need for complex online nonlinear dynamics reasoning.
> - The convexity of the MPC formulation, combined with offline pre-factorization of the KKT system, enables real-time control rates of hundreds of Hertz on complex robots.
> - Adopting a linear, convex MPC framework enhances solution reliability, predictability, and analytical tractability compared to non-convex NMPC.
