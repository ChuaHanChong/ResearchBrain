# History-Guided Iterative Visual Reasoning with Self-Correction

> [!summary] Summary
> The H-GIVR framework enhances multimodal large language models by integrating history-guided iterative visual reasoning and self-correction during inference. This method enables dynamic error correction and repeated visual observation, consistently improving reasoning accuracy across five VQA benchmarks, with accuracy gains exceeding 50% on multiple-choice datasets and requiring an average of 2.57 to 4.04 model calls per question.

## Problem

- Existing self-consistency methods for MLLMs treat multiple reasoning attempts as independent, failing to leverage historical outputs for dynamic error correction or iterative refinement.
- MLLMs generally lack human-like iterative verification and dynamic adjustment of reasoning based on prior iterations and re-examination of visual cues during inference.
- Improving MLLM performance often relies on increasing model size, leading to exponentially growing and unsustainable training costs, necessitating efficient inference-time enhancement strategies.

## Method

- The H-GIVR framework introduces a =="Visual Description" component==, guiding the MLLM to first generate a detailed image description, serving as explicit visual evidence for subsequent answer generation.
- A =="Consistency-Iterative Reasoning" component== dynamically updates an "Answer List" with past responses, feeding it back into the model's prompt as historical context to encourage reflection and verification.
- An =="Image Re-observation Mechanism"== periodically prompts the model to reprocess the original image and generate an updated visual description, mitigating memory loss and insufficient initial visual understanding.

## Results

- H-GIVR achieved consistent performance improvements across five VQA benchmarks and three MLLMs, with accuracy gains exceeding **50%** on multiple-choice datasets and ranging from **2.8%** to **69.2%** on open-ended tasks.
- The framework demonstrated computational efficiency, requiring an average of **2.57** to **4.04** model calls per question, making it more efficient than some baselines while maintaining competitive or superior accuracy.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Visual Description and Consistency-Iterative Reasoning modules individually contributed to accuracy improvements, with their combination in H-GIVR yielding the highest overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating historical reasoning through an iterative feedback loop allows MLLMs to actively correct visual understanding errors and adapt their reasoning dynamically during inference.
> - Periodically re-observing the visual input during iterative reasoning significantly improves the model's robustness and accuracy by reinforcing attention to key image content and mitigating memory decay.
> - Achieving internal consistency among iteratively generated answers serves as an effective and computationally efficient stopping criterion, indicating convergence towards a reliable solution without complex training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated considerable advancements in vision-language reasoning, including multi-step reasoning and cross-modal alignment. Historically, improving model performance often involved increasing model size, but this approach incurs exponentially growing training costs, suggesting it is not a sustainable long-term strategy. Consequently, prompt engineering has emerged as a significant area for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models without solely relying on model scale.

Existing research in prompt engineering has shown that explicit prompts can guide models to generate reasoning steps, and self-consistency methods, which involve repeated sampling combined with majority voting, can improve the stability and reliability of model outputs. However, a common limitation of many current self-consistency methods is their reliance on a fixed "repeated sampling and voting" paradigm. These methods typically treat multiple generations as independent repetitions and do not explicitly reuse historical outputs or dynamically adjust reasoning based on prior iterations. This makes it challenging for models to actively correct visual understanding errors and to adapt their reasoning processes dynamically, thus limiting further performance improvements on complex tasks.

The broader landscape of self-correction in large language models (LLMs) includes two main categories: parameter adjustment during training (e.g., reinforcement learning approaches) and interactive guidance during inference. While parameter adjustment methods can effectively address issues like distribution shift, they often involve complex processes and high computational costs. Inference-time guidance, which leverages prompt design to stimulate the models' inherent reflective potential, offers a more lightweight alternative. This paper operates within the inference-time guidance paradigm.

Furthermore, existing iterative self-improvement frameworks, such as RISE and Exploratory Iteration, have focused on recursive introspection and maintaining divergent thinking, emphasizing the importance of self-consistency and cognitive behaviors like verification and feedback. However, these methods primarily target unimodal contexts or do not fully extend dynamic self-correction mechanisms to multimodal scenarios during inference. In multimodal research, efforts to achieve accurate cross-modal alignment largely concentrate on modal fusion and representation alignment during the training phase. While some works have explored self-consistency in visual localization through fine-tuning, frameworks enabling dynamic, inference-time self-correction for cross-modal understanding, without complex training, remain less explored.

This work addresses the identified gaps by proposing a novel inference-time framework that aims to provide MLLMs with a human-like ability for iterative verification and dynamic error correction, specifically in multimodal contexts. It acknowledges the inherent cognitive limitations and alignment errors of existing MLLMs and seeks to compensate for these flaws through a lightweight, interactive reflection-correction mechanism during inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning reliability and accuracy of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in vision-language tasks by enabling them to actively correct visual understanding errors and dynamically adjust their reasoning during an iterative process.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and limitations within current MLLM methodologies:

1. **Limitations of Traditional Self-Consistency:** Existing self-consistency methods typically employ a "repeated sampling and voting" paradigm where multiple reasoning attempts are treated as independent. This approach fails to leverage historical reasoning information, preventing models from learning from past outputs or dynamically correcting errors. The motivation is to move beyond this fixed paradigm to one that incorporates iterative refinement.
2. **Absence of Human-like Verification in MLLMs:** Human reasoning often involves iterative verification and dynamic error correction. For instance, when solving problems, individuals repeatedly review their answers, incorporating previous responses and re-examining visual cues. Current MLLMs, despite being designed to mimic human conversational logic, largely lack this iterative verification capability, especially in integrating visual and textual information dynamically.
3. **Challenges in Dynamic Error Correction:** MLLMs frequently struggle to correct errors in their visual understanding or reasoning process once an initial incorrect interpretation has been made. The static nature of most inference procedures limits the model's ability to adjust its internal state or re-evaluate visual evidence based on intermediate reasoning steps.
4. **Computational Cost of Model Scaling:** While increasing model size can improve performance, it leads to significant increases in training costs, making it an unsustainable strategy for continuous improvement. This motivates the exploration of prompt engineering and inference-time strategies that can enhance performance without requiring extensive model retraining or scaling.
5. **Need for Robust Cross-Modal Alignment:** Achieving accurate cross-modal alignment is a fundamental challenge in vision-language tasks. Existing approaches primarily focus on training-time modal fusion and representation alignment. There is a need for inference-time mechanisms that allow models to dynamically evaluate and refine their cross-modal understanding without complex, resource-intensive training.

To address these motivations, the proposed H-GIVR framework aims to:
- Equip MLLMs with a mechanism to observe images multiple times throughout the reasoning process.
- Enable MLLMs to use previously generated answers as references, facilitating dynamic error correction in subsequent steps.
- Improve answer accuracy through iterative consistency, mirroring human problem-solving behavior.
- Achieve these improvements while maintaining a low computational cost, making advanced cross-modal reasoning more accessible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The History-Guided Iterative Visual Reasoning with Self-Correction (H-GIVR) framework is designed to integrate historical reasoning and repeated visual observation into the iterative process of multimodal large language models. The framework consists of two core components and two key judgment mechanisms, working together to simulate human-like verification and error correction.

**Problem Definition:**
For a given visual question answering (VQA) task with question $x_i$ and associated image $v_i$, the model generates an image description $f_k(v_i)$ and an answer $p_k(x_i, v_i)$ at each iteration $k$. The framework aims to collect a set of image descriptions $F(v_i)$ and answers $P(x_i, v_i)$. The process terminates when two identical answers $p_\alpha(x_i, v_i)$ and $p_\beta(x_i, v_i)$ are found within the answer set, and that consistent answer is output as the final result.

**Core Components:**

1. **Visual Description:**
 - **Functionality:** This component guides the MLLM to first focus on the input image ($v_i$) and generate a detailed visual description ($f(v_i)$). This description is then appended to the question text and serves as explicit visual evidence during the subsequent answer generation stage.
 - **Purpose:** By separating visual understanding from immediate answer generation, this mechanism aims to strengthen the model's perception and utilization of visual information. It seeks to ensure a deeper understanding of image content, thereby preventing reasoning processes from drifting due to inadequate initial visual comprehension.

2. **Consistency-Iterative Reasoning:**
 - **Functionality:** Unlike traditional self-consistency methods that treat multiple generations independently, H-GIVR maintains a dynamically updated "Answer List" containing all previously generated answers. In each subsequent round of reasoning, this Answer List is fed back into the model's prompt as historical context alongside the original question and visual information. The model is explicitly instructed to consider these historical answers carefully.
 - **Purpose:** This component simulates the human behavior of reviewing and refining conclusions based on prior attempts. By incorporating past answers as references, the model is equipped with the ability to reflect on and verify its own outputs, guiding it towards a more consistent and potentially correct final answer. The paper also notes an empirical observation that including incorrect historical answers can still encourage convergence toward the correct answer.

**Key Judgment Mechanisms:**

1. **Image Re-observation Mechanism:**
 - **Functionality:** This mechanism is activated periodically. Specifically, when the iteration count reaches an even number, the model is prompted to independently reprocess the original image ($v_i$) and generate an *updated* visual description. This new description is added to the `FeatureSet`.
 - **Purpose:** This simulates the human habit of periodically re-examining an image to capture missed details or correct misunderstandings. It is designed to mitigate issues like memory loss or insufficient initial understanding of visual information, reinforcing the model's awareness of key image content across multiple reasoning steps, thereby improving reasoning robustness and accuracy.

2. **Answer Confirmation Mechanism:**
 - **Functionality:** The iterative reasoning process continues until a stable state is reached. This is defined as when the model generates an answer that is identical to an answer already present in the `AnswerList`. Once this condition is met, the framework stops iterating and outputs this consistent answer as the final result. To manage computational efficiency and simulate time constraints, a maximum of 10 iterations is set. If the maximum iterations are reached without convergence, the latest generated answer is returned. A specific rule addresses "None" outputs: if several consecutive "None"s are followed by a valid answer, that first valid answer is accepted.
 - **Purpose:** This mechanism serves as an efficient stopping criterion, signaling that the model has reached a point of internal consistency. It prevents unnecessary computations while ensuring that the final output is a result of iterative refinement and self-validation.

**Overall Workflow (Algorithm 1 Summary):**
1. Initialize an empty `AnswerList` and `FeatureSet`, set iteration count to 0.
2. Generate an initial visual description (`feature`) from the image ($v_i$) and add it to `FeatureSet`.
3. Enter a loop for up to `max_iterations`:
 - Increment iteration count.
 - Construct a `prompt` using the question ($x_i$) and current `FeatureSet`.
 - If `AnswerList` is not empty, append it to the `prompt` as historical context.
 - Generate a `current_answer` using the MLLM with the constructed `prompt`.
 - Check for convergence: If `current_answer` is already in `AnswerList`, set it as `A_final` and break the loop.
 - Add `current_answer` to `AnswerList`.
 - If the iteration count is even, trigger the `Image Re-observation Mechanism`: generate a `new_feature` from $v_i$ and add it to `FeatureSet`.
4. If the loop finishes due to `max_iterations`, `A_final` is the latest answer.
5. Return `A_final`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the H-GIVR framework's effectiveness and computational efficiency across five VQA benchmarks (ScienceQA, A-OKVQA, OK-VQA, VQAv2, TextVQA) using three MLLMs (Llama3.2-vision:11b, Qwen2.5vl:7b, Gemma3:12b) and three application settings (Standard, Simple CoT, Complex CoT).

**Q1: Performance Across Multiple Scenarios**
H-GIVR consistently demonstrated performance improvements across all tested datasets, models, and application settings.
- **Significant Gains:** Particularly strong gains were observed on multiple-choice datasets. For Llama3.2-vision, Qwen2.5vl, and Gemma3 models, accuracy improvements exceeded 50% in both the Standard and Simple CoT settings, with minimum improvements of 19% even in the Complex CoT setting.
- **Open-ended Tasks:** On more challenging open-ended datasets (OK-VQA, VQAv2, TextVQA), H-GIVR also yielded benefits, with improvements ranging from 2.8% to 69.2%.
- **Prompt Robustness:** When additional prompt information (Simple CoT or Complex CoT) was provided, H-GIVR remained effective, although its accuracy was sometimes lower than when used without explicit prompts (Standard setting). This indicates that H-GIVR can be applied directly without requiring extensive prompt engineering, reducing usage barriers.

**Q2: Computational Resource Requirements**
The framework exhibited computational efficiency.
- **Model Calls:** H-GIVR required an average of 2.57 to 4.04 model calls per question across all experiments. This suggests controlled computational cost.
- **Task Type Impact:** Open-ended questions generally required more model calls than multiple-choice questions, indicating higher decision uncertainty and a need for more reasoning steps to converge.
- **Prompting Impact on Calls:** Interestingly, while Simple CoT and Complex CoT settings often required fewer calls than the Standard setting, their accuracy was frequently lower. This suggests that explicit prompts might, in some cases, cause the model to fixate on earlier answers rather than engaging in deeper iterative correction.

**Q3: Comparison with Existing Baseline Methods**
H-GIVR was compared against classic prompting methods for unimodal LLMs, including Few-Shot Chain-of-Thought (FS-CoT), Auto-CoT, Active Prompt, and Self-Consistency.
- **Superior Performance:** H-GIVR generally achieved better performance across the five datasets for both Llama3.2-vision and Qwen2.5vl models compared to other baselines. Performance gains ranged from 0.49% to 16.60%.
- **Efficiency Advantage:** While Self-Consistency occasionally showed slightly higher accuracy in specific cases (e.g., Llama3.2-vision on VQAv2), its model calls were fixed at 5, whereas H-GIVR's average was 3.58. This indicates a competitive performance with a clear advantage in computational efficiency for H-GIVR.

**Q4: Effectiveness of Core Components (Ablation Study)**
An ablation study on the Qwen2.5vl 7B model in the Standard setting was conducted to evaluate the contributions of the Visual Description and Consistency-Iterative Reasoning modules.
- **Visual Description (VD) Impact:** Introducing only the Visual Description module resulted in a substantial average accuracy increase from 40.05% (baseline) to 70.93%.
- **Consistency-Iterative Reasoning (CIR) Impact:** Using only the Consistency-Iterative Reasoning module further improved average accuracy to 72.11% from the baseline.
- **Combined Effect:** The full H-GIVR framework, incorporating both modules, achieved the highest average accuracy of 72.76%. These results confirm the individual effectiveness of both modules and demonstrate their complementary nature in jointly enhancing reasoning ability and answer accuracy.

**Q5: Effectiveness in Specific Domains**
A fine-grained analysis on the ScienceQA dataset (categorized by subject: Language Science, Social Science, Natural Science, and difficulty levels: G1-6, G7-12) assessed domain adaptability.
- **Domain Adaptability:** H-GIVR performed well across most sub-domains. It showed strong subject adaptability, particularly in Social Science, where it significantly improved answer accuracy.
- **Exceptions:** In a few instances within the Language Science category, H-GIVR slightly underperformed the Self-Consistency method.
- **Difficulty Levels:** The framework maintained stable performance advantages across different question difficulty levels (G1-6 and G7-12), indicating good generalization ability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The H-GIVR framework represents an advancement beyond traditional self-consistency methods for multimodal large language models (MLLMs). By moving away from a fixed "repeated sampling and voting" paradigm, it introduces a more dynamic and reflective approach to visual reasoning.

**Key Contributions and Advancements:**
- **Dynamic Error Correction:** H-GIVR enables MLLMs to actively leverage historical reasoning information as a key reference for subsequent decisions. This allows for dynamic adjustment of reasoning and correction of visual understanding errors during an iterative process, mimicking human verification behaviors.
- **Repeated Visual Observation:** The framework guides models to repeatedly observe and re-evaluate visual content throughout the reasoning process, which helps to deepen understanding, reduce the impact of memory decay, and refine visual interpretations.
- **Improved Accuracy with Efficiency:** The experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in reasoning accuracy across various VQA benchmarks, models, and application settings. These gains are achieved while maintaining a controllable and relatively low computational cost, with an average of 2.57 to 4.04 model calls per question.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Reduced Deployment Barriers:** H-GIVR's design reduces the reliance on elaborate prompt engineering and extensive model scaling to achieve high performance. This makes advanced cross-modal reasoning more accessible to a broader range of users, including those without specialized expertise in MLLM prompt optimization.
- **Computational Efficiency and Sustainability:** The lightweight and inference-time nature of H-GIVR minimizes the computational resources required compared to methods involving model retraining or significantly increased model sizes. This efficiency can contribute to a reduction in the carbon footprint associated with running large-scale multimodal models, aligning with efforts toward more sustainable AI development.
- **Enhanced Reliability in Applications:** By improving the accuracy and robustness of visual question answering (VQA) systems, H-GIVR has the potential to enhance the reliability of MLLM-powered applications in real-world scenarios. Examples include educational tools (e.g., answering questions about diagrams), accessibility aids (e.g., describing images for visually impaired users), and various forms of content analysis where accurate visual-textual understanding is critical.
- **Foundational Research:** The framework's simulation of human-like iterative verification and error correction behavior contributes to the understanding of how to imbue AI systems with more sophisticated cognitive processes, paving the way for further research into reflective and self-improving agents.

The authors foresee no immediate negative ethical consequences associated with this work, as its focus is on developing more efficient, accurate, and user-friendly AI technologies.
