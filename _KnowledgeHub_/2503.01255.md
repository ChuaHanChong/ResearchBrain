# Impact of Static Friction on Sim2Real in Robotic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Lenovo Research Shanghai identified static friction as a critical, overlooked contributor to the Sim2Real gap in robotic reinforcement learning, particularly for their Saturn Lite hexapod robot. They developed a static friction-aware domain randomization strategy, enabling the robot to perform stable locomotion and stair climbing in real-world scenarios, a task it previously failed.

## Problem

- Conventional Sim2Real methods, including standard domain randomization, failed to transfer policies effectively from simulation to the physical Saturn Lite hexapod robot, leading to poor real-world performance on tasks like stair climbing.
- The precise physical discrepancies contributing to the Sim2Real gap for their specific hexapod robot were not well understood, with static friction often overlooked in existing domain randomization schemes.
- Training directly in the real world is impractical due to high costs, time consumption, and potential damage to robotic hardware.

## Method

- A control-theoretic joint model was developed to analyze joint dynamics, followed by systematic parameter identification via a least squares method to measure specific physical properties, including static friction, for the ==Saturn Lite== robot.
- The authors proposed a static friction-aware domain randomization strategy that significantly expanded the randomization range for static friction (using a "deception method") to accommodate its high values and nonlinearity, integrating it alongside other standard randomized parameters.
- Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), an Actor-Critic RL framework with a teacher-student architecture, was employed for policy training, guided by a novel reward function designed to promote stable tripod gait in the hexapod.

## Results

- Parameter identification showed Saturn Lite's shank motors had a mean static friction of **0.442** N·m, significantly higher than Go1's **0.0481** N·m, with its static friction-to-maximum-torque ratio at **0.98%**, versus Go1's **0.13%**.
- The proposed static friction-aware domain randomization enabled the Saturn Lite robot to achieve stable walking on flat ground and successfully navigate stairs in the real world, a task previously impossible with conventional methods.
- In contrast, conventional domain randomization only allowed the robot to walk backward on flat ground and fail on stairs, while the Actuator Net method resulted in unsteady forward walking on flat ground and system failure in Sim2Sim, demonstrating its limited generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Static friction can constitute a substantial portion of joint torque, especially in robots with specific design features like waterproofing and heavier links, as demonstrated by Saturn Lite's shank motors having a static friction-to-maximum-torque ratio almost an order of magnitude higher than a comparable robot.
> - Directly incorporating high static friction into simulation or fine-tuning with it can lead to training instability; a wide randomization range (the "deception method") is effective for training robust policies despite this nonlinearity.
> - Accurate physical parameter identification, beyond generic assumptions, is crucial for bridging the Sim2Real gap, as an overlooked parameter like static friction can be a primary cause of real-world performance failures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the persistent and critical **Sim2Real gap** in robotic reinforcement learning (RL). This gap refers to the common challenge where policies trained successfully in simulated environments often perform poorly when transferred to physical robots. Bridging this gap is fundamental to the widespread adoption of RL in real-world robotics, as direct real-world training is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and potentially damaging to hardware.

The broader research landscape for Sim2Real solutions includes several prominent approaches:
- **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC):** These methods are mentioned but are often hampered by the need for highly accurate environmental models and struggle with unpredictable external disturbances.
- **Model-Free Reinforcement Learning (MFRL):** While data-inefficient, MFRL is the predominant method, relying heavily on high-fidelity simulations for data generation, which in turn exacerbates the Sim2Real problem if simulation accuracy is lacking.
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** This is a widely adopted and effective technique that involves randomizing various physical properties (e.g., robot mass, friction, sensor noise, actuator dynamics) in simulation to train a more robust policy that generalizes to real-world variations. The paper explicitly states that conventional DR, while common, was insufficient for their robot.
- **Teacher-Student Learning with Privileged Observations:** Algorithms like Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), which this paper uses, enhance learning by allowing a "teacher" policy access to real-world inaccessible information during training, with a "student" policy learning to mimic the teacher using only accessible observations.
- **Actuator Modeling (e.g., Actuator Net):** This approach directly models real-world actuator dynamics by collecting data from physical robots and integrating these learned models into the simulation. The paper evaluates Actuator Net as a comparative method.
- **Physics Engine Fidelity:** The paper also touches upon the trade-offs between simulation speed (e.g., Isaac Gym/Sim) and physical accuracy (e.g., MuJoCo), highlighting that discrepancies in physics engines themselves can contribute to the Sim2Real gap.

This work specifically identifies **Static friction** as an underexplored but critical parameter contributing to the Sim2Real gap. While previous DR efforts have randomized parameters like viscous friction, rotor inertia, and PD gains, Static friction has often been overlooked or assumed to be negligible. The authors' systematic analysis, driven by the practical failure of conventional methods on their robot, reveals that Static friction can constitute a significant portion of joint torque, especially in specific robot designs (e.g., waterproof joints). By proposing and validating a "Static friction-aware domain randomization" strategy, the paper fills a notable gap in the existing Sim2Real literature. It demonstrates that a deeper, robot-specific understanding of physical parameters is crucial, going beyond generic randomization schemes. This work thus enhances the robustness of DR methods and contributes to the broader goal of making RL-trained policies more reliable for real-world robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stemmed from a practical challenge: the **failure of conventional Sim2Real transfer methods for the Saturn Lite hexapod robot, particularly on complex terrains like stairs.** Despite using established domain randomization techniques, the robot trained in simulation performed "significantly underperforming" in real-world scenarios. This indicated a fundamental mismatch between the simulated and real environments that existing approaches could not resolve. The inherent drawbacks of real-world training—including potential damage to the robot, high costs, and significant time investment—further underscored the necessity of robust Sim2Real solutions.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the paper were:

1. **Systematic Identification and Demonstration of Static Friction's Impact:** The central objective was to formally identify and quantify the influence of Static friction on the Sim2Real gap in robotic reinforcement learning. This involved hypothesis formulation, parameter identification through empirical measurements, and comparative experiments.
2. **Development of a Control-Theoretic Joint Model:** To gain a deeper understanding of the joint dynamics and pinpoint critical parameters, the authors aimed to develop a second-order control model for the robot's motors. This model would serve as a basis for analyzing which physical parameters are most impactful and therefore require randomization.
3. **Proposal of a Static Friction-Aware Domain Randomization Solution:** Based on their analysis, the paper aimed to integrate Static friction into the domain randomization process to mitigate its adverse effects on Sim2Real transfer. This included devising a strategy to overcome the increased training difficulty introduced by the nonlinearity of friction modeling.
4. **Comprehensive Validation:** A crucial objective was to conduct rigorous Sim2Sim and Sim2Real experiments. These experiments would compare the proposed Static friction-aware domain randomization with conventional domain randomization (without Static friction) and the Actuator Net method, thereby empirically validating the effectiveness and superior performance of their approach.
5. **Enabling Robust Locomotion and Terrain Adaptation:** Ultimately, the research aimed to enable the Saturn Lite robot to autonomously and stably adapt to various terrains (specifically flat ground and stairs) in the real world, relying solely on proprioceptive sensors, using the developed Sim2Real methodology.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach, combining a novel robotic platform, a cutting-edge reinforcement learning algorithm, detailed control-theoretic analysis, and comprehensive experimental validation.

**A. Robotic Platform:**
The experiments were conducted on the **Saturn Lite**, a hexapod robot developed by Lenovo's Shanghai Research Institute. This 32 kg robot features 18 degrees of freedom (DoF), with each leg having three DoFs. A notable design characteristic is its emphasis on endurance, achieving 2 hours of operation compared to 0.33 hours for a Unitree Go1 quadruped, albeit with a significantly higher body mass and similar joint torque capabilities. The robot's configuration uses a tripod gait, requiring careful control to achieve stable locomotion on diverse terrains without exteroceptive sensors.

**B. Reinforcement Learning Training Methodology:**
The core RL algorithm utilized was **Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA)**, an Actor-Critic framework known for its effectiveness in legged robot locomotion. RMA employs a two-phase training process:
- **Teacher Phase:** A teacher policy is trained with access to "privileged observations" (e.g., terrain elevation maps, robot mass, joint parameters like inertia, damping, and Static friction, ground friction coefficient) and "explicit observations" (linear base velocity, foot contact states). These observations are unavailable in reality but aid learning.
- **Student Phase:** A student policy learns to mimic the teacher's actions using only proprioceptive observations (angular velocity, roll/pitch angles, joint angles/velocities, previous action, command input) and historical proprioceptive data. The student estimates explicit observations and terrain features from this history, bypassing the need for privileged information during real-world deployment.

**C. Reward Function Design:**
To specifically guide the hexapod robot towards a desired "trot gait" (alternating contact groups of three legs) rather than a pronk gait (all legs simultaneously) or an unstable three-legged hop, the authors developed a novel reward function. This function introduced `r_trot` to ensure only one contact group is active at a time and `r_unsync` to balance the usage of the two contact groups over time.

**D. Sim2Real Implementation Methods:**
The paper meticulously compared three different Sim2Real approaches:

1. **Conventional Domain Randomization (without Static Friction):** This baseline method randomized typical parameters such as joint armature, viscous damping, Kp, motor strength, ground friction, payload, center of mass (CoM), and external push velocity. Static friction was notably *excluded* from this randomization.

2. **Actuator Net:** As an alternative, Actuator Net aimed to bridge the gap by modeling real-world actuator dynamics. This involved:
 - Initially deploying a robot trained with conventional DR (which could only walk backward on flat ground) to collect real-world joint position and velocity history data.
 - Training an Actuator Net (`f_motor`) to predict joint torques from this data, effectively creating a learned actuator model.
 - Replacing the conventional PD controller in simulation with this trained Actuator Net and retraining the teacher-student policy.

3. **Proposed Static Friction-Aware Domain Randomization:** This was the central contribution, built upon detailed analysis:
 - **Control-Theoretic Joint Model:** A second-order dynamic model was formulated for a single robot joint (Equation 12), incorporating joint inertia ($I_j$), viscous friction ($B_j$), and Static friction ($b_{c_j}$), along with PD control parameters ($k_p, k_d$).
 - **Systematic Parameter Identification:** Using a least squares method (Equation 13) with sinusoidal excitation, the physical parameters ($I_j, B_j, b_{c_j}$) for the shank motors of both Go1 and Saturn Lite robots were identified under no-load conditions.
 - **Addressing Training Difficulty:** Directly incorporating the identified high Static friction or attempting fine-tuning with it proved problematic, leading to training failures or jittering. The authors proposed a **"deception method"** which involved significantly *expanding the randomization range of Static friction* (a multiplier of [0.0, 1.2]). This wide range allowed the policy to experience scenarios with negligible friction, facilitating learning, while also exposing it to high friction states, enhancing robustness to real-world variations and wear.
 - **Domain Randomization Parameters:** The full set of randomized parameters for this method included joint armature, joint damping, **joint Static friction**, Kp, motor strength, ground friction, payload, CoM, push interval, and push velocity (Table II).

**E. Experimental Validation:**
The three methods were rigorously compared through both **Sim2Sim** experiments (using Webots as the simulation platform) and **Sim2Real** experiments on the physical Saturn Lite robot. Performance was evaluated based on stable flat-ground walking and the ability to navigate stairs, using only proprioceptive sensing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, providing strong evidence for the critical impact of Static friction on Sim2Real performance and the effectiveness of the proposed solution.

**A. Measurement and Evaluation of Joint Parameters:**
- **High Static Friction in Saturn Lite:** Parameter identification revealed a stark difference in Static friction between the Saturn Lite and the Go1 robot. The Saturn Lite's shank motors exhibited a mean Static friction of **0.442 N·m**, significantly higher than the Go1's **0.0481 N·m**.
- **High Friction-to-Torque Ratio:** Crucially, the **Static friction-to-maximum-torque ratio ($f/\tau_{max}$) for Saturn Lite was 0.98%**, nearly an order of magnitude higher than Go1's 0.13%. This indicates that Static friction accounts for a disproportionately large fraction of the available joint torque in Saturn Lite.
- **Root Cause Identification:** This elevated Static friction was attributed to the robot's design choices, specifically **waterproofing treatment and heavier links**. Experimental removal of sealing rings from the motors demonstrated a 70% reduction in Static friction, confirming the waterproofing as a major contributing factor.

**B. Comparative Sim2Sim and Sim2Real Experiments:**

1. **Domain Randomization Without Static Friction:**
 - **Sim2Sim:** Achieved the *best performance* in Webots, indicating that the core RL algorithm (RMA) and overall training setup were sound without this specific physical discrepancy.
 - **Sim2Real:** Performed poorly. The robot could only walk backward on flat ground and *fell when attempting forward motion*. It was entirely unable to climb stairs. This stark contrast between simulation and reality highlighted a significant Sim2Real gap despite conventional randomization.

2. **Actuator Net:**
 - **Sim2Sim:** Exhibited "significant abnormal behavior" and eventually led to *system failure*. This suggests that the actuator model learned from limited real-world data (backward walking) did not generalize well to the varied dynamics encountered in a comprehensive simulation.
 - **Sim2Real:** Enabled *unsteady forward walking on flat ground* but *failed to climb stairs*. This indicated a partial improvement over conventional DR for flat terrain but confirmed limitations in generalization to more complex tasks and potentially to other robots (as the model was specific to the data collected from one robot).

3. **Proposed Static Friction-Aware Domain Randomization:**
 - **Sim2Sim:** The robot displayed "irregular jittering and jumping" but was still *capable of ascending and descending stairs*. This demonstrated the RMA algorithm's adaptability even with the added complexity of friction, albeit with a slight degradation in simulation-only performance compared to the frictionless DR.
 - **Sim2Real:** Achieved the *most robust performance*. The robot demonstrated *stable walking on flat ground and successful stair navigation*. This was a significant improvement over both other methods, proving the efficacy of including Static friction in the randomization process.

**C. Implications of Findings:**
The results strongly validate the hypothesis that Static friction is a critical, often overlooked parameter in Sim2Real, especially for robots with high friction-to-torque ratios. The success of Static friction-aware DR in Sim2Real, despite a slight sacrifice in Sim2Sim optimality, underscored the importance of accurate physical modeling and randomization for real-world deployment. The "deception method" (wide randomization range for Static friction) proved effective in managing the training complexity introduced by friction's nonlinearity, allowing the policy to learn robust behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a significant contribution to the field of robotic reinforcement learning by addressing a critical, yet previously underexplored, aspect of the Sim2Real gap.

**A. Novel Contribution and Paradigm Shift:**
The paper is, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the first to systematically identify and demonstrate the profound impact of Static friction on Sim2Real transferability in robotic RL. This moves beyond generic assumptions about minor physical discrepancies and highlights a specific, quantifiable parameter that can severely impede real-world performance. It suggests a paradigm shift where researchers should consider conducting detailed parameter identification for their specific robotic platforms to uncover such hidden influencers, rather than relying solely on generic domain randomization strategies.

**B. Practical Implications for Sim2Real and Robot Design:**
- **Enhanced Domain Randomization:** The proposed Static friction-aware domain randomization, especially with the "deception method" (wide randomization range), provides a validated and effective strategy for bridging the Sim2Real gap for robots where Static friction is significant. This is particularly relevant for robots with specialized designs, such as those requiring waterproofing (which often increases seal friction) or robust, heavy-duty joints.
- **Guidance for Hardware Design:** The findings offer crucial insights for robotic hardware designers. It suggests that the Static friction-to-torque ratio of robot joints should be a key consideration during the design phase, particularly for platforms intended for RL deployment. Engineers might need to balance design requirements (e.g., waterproofing, payload capacity) with their impact on friction and its subsequent challenges for control and learning.
- **Robustness to Mechanical Degradation:** The authors postulate that models trained with randomized Static friction inherently gain robustness against mechanical wear and fatigue over the robot's operational lifespan, as wear can alter friction characteristics. While direct validation of this hypothesis was outside the scope of the current experiments, it presents a compelling area for future research and suggests a built-in advantage for longevity and consistent performance.

**C. Broader Applicability:**
While validated on a hexapod robot, the underlying principle of scrutinizing specific physical parameters and adapting domain randomization accordingly is highly generalizable. Other types of robots (quadrupeds, humanoids, manipulators) and different physical characteristics might similarly suffer from overlooked discrepancies, making this methodological approach broadly applicable for improving Sim2Real transfer across diverse robotic systems.

In conclusion, this paper not only provides a concrete solution to a practical Sim2Real problem encountered with a specific hexapod robot but also uncovers a fundamental physical parameter often overlooked in robotic RL. By demonstrating the critical role of Static friction and offering a robust randomization strategy, the research advances the state of the art in Sim2Real and provides valuable guidance for both algorithmic development and robotic hardware design, ultimately moving closer to deployable and reliable real-world robotic systems.
