# Impact of Static Friction on Sim2Real in Robotic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Lenovo Research Shanghai identified static friction as a critical, overlooked contributor to the Sim2Real gap in robotic reinforcement learning, particularly for their Saturn Lite hexapod robot. They developed a static friction-aware domain randomization strategy, enabling the robot to perform stable locomotion and stair climbing in real-world scenarios, a task it previously failed.

## Problem

- Conventional Sim2Real methods, including standard domain randomization, failed to transfer policies effectively from simulation to the physical Saturn Lite hexapod robot, leading to poor real-world performance on tasks like stair climbing.
- The precise physical discrepancies contributing to the Sim2Real gap for their specific hexapod robot were not well understood, with static friction often overlooked in existing domain randomization schemes.
- Training directly in the real world is impractical due to high costs, time consumption, and potential damage to robotic hardware.

## Method

- A control-theoretic joint model was developed to analyze joint dynamics, followed by systematic parameter identification via a least squares method to measure specific physical properties, including static friction, for the ==Saturn Lite== robot.
- The authors proposed a static friction-aware domain randomization strategy that significantly expanded the randomization range for static friction (using a "deception method") to accommodate its high values and nonlinearity, integrating it alongside other standard randomized parameters.
- Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), an Actor-Critic RL framework with a teacher-student architecture, was employed for policy training, guided by a novel reward function designed to promote stable tripod gait in the hexapod.

## Results

- Parameter identification showed Saturn Lite's shank motors had a mean static friction of **0.442** N·m, significantly higher than Go1's **0.0481** N·m, with its static friction-to-maximum-torque ratio at **0.98%**, versus Go1's **0.13%**.
- The proposed static friction-aware domain randomization enabled the Saturn Lite robot to achieve stable walking on flat ground and successfully navigate stairs in the real world, a task previously impossible with conventional methods.
- In contrast, conventional domain randomization only allowed the robot to walk backward on flat ground and fail on stairs, while the Actuator Net method resulted in unsteady forward walking on flat ground and system failure in Sim2Sim, demonstrating its limited generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Static friction can constitute a substantial portion of joint torque, especially in robots with specific design features like waterproofing and heavier links, as demonstrated by Saturn Lite's shank motors having a static friction-to-maximum-torque ratio almost an order of magnitude higher than a comparable robot.
> - Directly incorporating high static friction into simulation or fine-tuning with it can lead to training instability; a wide randomization range (the "deception method") is effective for training robust policies despite this nonlinearity.
> - Accurate physical parameter identification, beyond generic assumptions, is crucial for bridging the Sim2Real gap, as an overlooked parameter like static friction can be a primary cause of real-world performance failures.
