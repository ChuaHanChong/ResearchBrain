# Continual Reinforcement Learning by Planning with Online World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> An Online Agent (OA) framework for Continual Reinforcement Learning mitigates catastrophic forgetting by integrating sparse online world model learning with model predictive control planning. The approach maintains high performance on all learned tasks, achieves the lowest regret on the Continual Bench environment, and includes theoretical no-regret guarantees.

## Problem

- Catastrophic forgetting prevents reinforcement learning agents from continually acquiring new skills without losing proficiency in previous ones.
- Current Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) methods often rely on task-specific components or large replay buffers, hindering the development of truly online, memory-efficient agents.
- Existing CRL benchmarks suffer from issues like high computational demands, inconsistent state spaces, or insufficient task overlap for evaluating knowledge transfer.

## Method

- A Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) framework, the **Online Agent (OA)**, is proposed, which combines online world dynamics learning with ==model predictive control (MPC)== planning.
- The world model utilizes ==Follow-The-Leader (FTL)== shallow models with ==sparse feature encoding== and analytical incremental updates, ensuring efficient, non-forgetting learning of environmental dynamics.
- Policy generation for specific tasks is achieved via MPC, using the learned world model for simulation and the ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)== for optimizing action sequences.
- A new benchmark, "Continual Bench," is introduced, featuring a unified world dynamics and consistent state space across spatially arranged tasks to facilitate robust CRL evaluation.

## Results

- The Online Agent (OA) consistently maintains high success rates on all previously learned tasks, demonstrating robust resistance to catastrophic forgetting comparable to a "Perfect Memory" baseline.
- On the Continual Bench environment, OA achieved the highest average performance (**72.93%**) and the lowest regret (**27.62%**) among all tested model-based and model-free methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that OA's sparse world model efficiently scales its capacity, and the agent consistently delivered superior average performance across various buffer budget constraints, highlighting its memory efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified world dynamics model can serve as a shared, stable component across diverse tasks, enabling continuous learning and adaptation in reinforcement learning agents through planning.
> - Follow-The-Leader (FTL) shallow models, combined with sparse feature encoding and analytical incremental updates, provide an efficient and theoretically no-regret method for online world model learning.
> - Integrating an online-learned world model with Model Predictive Control (MPC) allows for flexible, task-specific policy generation, circumventing the need to continually re-learn value or policy networks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of Continual Learning (CL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically addressing the critical problem of catastrophic forgetting in Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL). CRL aims to develop agents that can continually learn and adapt to an endless sequence of tasks without forgetting previously acquired skills.

Traditionally, CRL research often falls into a "multitask" paradigm, where agents might use task-specific components (e.g., separate neural network heads, task IDs) or replay buffers to mitigate forgetting. The authors distinguish their approach by defining a more stringent "Online Agent (OA)" scenario. An OA is expected to learn seamlessly in an environment without explicit task boundaries, relying on shared learning components that update incrementally throughout its lifetime. This perspective pushes the boundaries beyond merely managing a sequence of distinct tasks to enabling truly lifelong learning.

The paper highlights two major challenges preventing the development of such OAs:
1. **Lack of online alternatives for all RL components:** Task-dependent elements like value functions and policies are difficult to share and update continually.
2. **Catastrophic forgetting under severe data distributional shift:** Shared components must remain stable while adapting to new tasks.

To address these, the authors propose a Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) framework. MBRL is particularly suitable for CRL because a learned world dynamics model can, in principle, be shared across tasks, as it describes the environment itself, independent of specific reward functions. This allows for the flexible generation of task-specific policies through planning, circumventing the need to continually re-learn task-specific value or policy networks.

The paper also critically evaluates the shortcomings of existing CRL benchmarks, such as Atari games, CORA, and Continual-World. These benchmarks are often criticized for computational demands, lack of meaningful knowledge transfer between tasks, or issues with consistent state-space definitions that hinder the study of unified world dynamics. The introduction of "Continual Bench" aims to provide a more suitable and practical environment for evaluating CRL agents, specifically designed to test both forgetting and transfer in a consistent, lightweight setting.

By building upon their prior work on FTL shallow models for online world modeling, this research contributes to the growing body of literature on efficient and theoretically grounded online learning algorithms. It contrasts with deep learning-based CL methods that often rely on large memory buffers, regularization techniques, or architectural modifications, by demonstrating that a shallow, analytically updated model can achieve superior continual learning performance in an online setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a truly "Online Agent (OA)" for Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) that can endlessly evolve and solve multiple tasks presented sequentially, primarily by addressing the formidable challenge of catastrophic forgetting.

The key motivations driving this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting:** This is the most significant obstacle in CRL. When an agent learns a new task, it often forgets how to perform previous tasks. The paper aims to propose a mechanism that makes the agent "immune to forgetting" by design.
- **Developing Naturalistic Online Agents:** Current CRL approaches often adopt a "multitask" view, relying on task-specific components or explicit task IDs. The authors argue for a more naturalistic scenario where an agent learns incrementally without clear task boundaries, requiring learning components that are shared and continually updated.
- **Identifying a Shared, Learnable Component:** The paper identifies the world dynamics model as the core component that can be shared across diverse tasks. Unlike task-dependent value functions or policies, a unified world model can describe the environment's physics and transitions regardless of the specific goal.
- **Leveraging Efficient Online World Modeling:** Motivated by the success of Follow-The-Leader (FTL) shallow models in online learning, the authors seek to apply and extend these to model complex world dynamics in RL. This involves adapting these models for incremental updates and providing theoretical guarantees for their performance.
- **Bridging Theory and Practice for Online Models:** While FTL shallow models have shown empirical promise, a critical motivation for this paper is to provide a theoretical foundation. The authors aim to theoretically demonstrate that their sparse online world model is "no-regret" under mild assumptions, solidifying its suitability for continual learning.
- **Addressing Limitations in CRL Benchmarking:** Existing CRL benchmarks suffer from issues like high computational demands, lack of meaningful task overlap for transfer, or inconsistent state representations that make it difficult to evaluate truly continual learning. This motivated the development of "Continual Bench" to provide a lightweight, consistent, and realistic environment for robust evaluation.

In essence, the authors seek to build a continually learning agent that is efficient, theoretically sound, and capable of operating in a truly online fashion, free from the limitations of explicit task information or excessive memory requirements, thus moving closer to the vision of general-purpose autonomous agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology revolves around an **Online Agent (OA)** that tackles Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) by coupling **online world model learning** with **model predictive control (MPC) planning**.

#### 3.1. Online World Model Learning

The core of OA is its ability to learn a unified world dynamics model incrementally without forgetting. This is achieved using **Follow-The-Leader (FTL) shallow models**, specifically building on the work of Liu et al. (2024).

- **Model Architecture:** The world model takes an input `x_t = [s_t, a_t]` (current state and action) and predicts the state difference `y_t = s_{t+1} - s_t`. It's structured as `y = W * sigma(P * x)`, where `P` is a fixed random projection matrix, `sigma` is an element-wise activation function (sigmoid), and `W` are the learnable weights. This "shallow but wide" architecture is known for universal approximation capacity.
- **Sparse Feature Encoding:** A high-dimensional, sparse feature encoder `phi(x_t)` (called Losse features in Liu et al., 2024) is used to project `x_t` into a sparse representation. This sparsity is crucial for computational efficiency.
- **FTL Learning with Regularized Least Squares:** The learnable weights `W` are updated at each time step by solving a regularized least squares problem (ridge regression) using all accumulated data `Phi_{t-1}` and `Y_{t-1}`. The solution is analytical: `W^(t) = (Phi_{t-1}^T Phi_{t-1} + (1/lambda)I)^-1 Phi_{t-1}^T Y_{t-1}`.
- **Incremental Sparse Update:** To avoid recomputing the inverse for the entire `D`-dimensional `W` (where `D` is large), the authors employ an efficient incremental update rule (Eq. 4). This rule leverages the sparsity of `phi(x_t)` – only `K` (much smaller than `D`) non-zero nodes are activated per input, allowing for local updates on a sub-matrix of `A^(t-1)` and `B^(t-1)`.
- **Importance of Regularization:** The paper highlights that the `(1/lambda)I` regularization term is critical. It ensures that the local update has a unique minimizer and guarantees convergence, especially during early learning when data is sparse and `A^(t-1)_ss` might be rank-deficient.

#### 3.2. Regret Analysis

A significant theoretical contribution of the paper is proving that the sparse online model learning (Eq. 4) is **no-regret**.

- **Theorem 1:** Under mild assumptions (Assumptions 1-3 regarding feature stability, bounded inputs/outputs, and sparsity parameter `K`), the proposed sparse online model achieves a regret bound of `O(sqrt(K^2 D log(T)))`. This means the accumulated sub-optimality compared to an optimal offline learner grows sub-linearly with the number of time steps `T`, guaranteeing continual learning without unbounded performance degradation.
- **Proof Sketch:** The proof decomposes the total regret into two terms: one reflecting the gap between the sparse online solution and the full least squares solution at each step, and another for the regret of the full online least squares solution. Both terms are then bounded using matrix perturbation analysis, properties of Schur complements, and the chosen hyperparameters.

#### 3.3. Planning with Online World Models

With the learned world model, OA derives its policy through **planning** using **Model Predictive Control (MPC)**.

- **Cross-Entropy Method (CEM):** CEM, a stochastic derivative-free optimization technique, is used to solve the finite-horizon planning problem (Eq. 2). CEM iteratively samples action sequences from a Gaussian distribution, evaluates them by simulating in the learned world model, selects "elite" candidates, and updates the Gaussian parameters (mean `mu`, standard deviation `sigma`) to better fit the elite set.
- **MPC Strategy:** MPC takes the first action from the optimized sequence, executes it in the real environment, observes the new state, and then re-plans from scratch. This closed-loop approach allows for continuous adaptation.
- **CEM Enhancements:** The authors incorporate several improvements for efficiency:
 - **Shift-initialization:** Reuses the previously planned action sequence by shifting it one step forward to initialize the next planning iteration.
 - **Colored noise:** Introduces temporal correlation in action sequence sampling for deeper exploration.
 - **Memory:** Retains a fraction of high-quality elite candidates from previous CEM iterations to accelerate convergence.

#### 3.4. Continual Bench: A Dedicated Environment for CRL Evaluation

To provide a robust evaluation platform, the authors developed **Continual Bench**.

- **Motivation:** Addresses the limitations of existing benchmarks, such as inconsistent state spaces (e.g., in Continual-World where different tasks share observation elements but imply conflicting physics), high computational cost (image-based environments), or lack of meaningful task overlap for transfer.
- **Design Principles:**
 - **Unified World Dynamics:** All tasks share a consistent state and action space, ensuring a single, underlying physics model can describe the entire environment. This directly supports the OA's assumption of a unified world model.
 - **Spatial Arrangement of Tasks:** Six diverse tasks (e.g., pick-place, door-open, button-press) from Meta-World primitives are spatially arranged in a circle. This design maximizes spatial separation for adjacent tasks, increasing non-stationarity and challenging forgetting, while still allowing for potential knowledge transfer.
 - **Lightweight and Realistic:** Built on the Mujoco physics engine, it offers a realistic continuous control environment that is computationally less demanding than image-based alternatives.
 - **Focus on Forgetting and Transfer:** The task arrangement and consistent state space explicitly facilitate the study of both forgetting (by switching tasks) and transfer (due to shared robotic arm and object interactions).
- **State and Action Space:** A 26-dimensional state vector (including hand, gripper, and various object positions/angles) and a 4-dimensional action space (end-effector position change, gripper torque).

This comprehensive methodology, combining theoretically-backed online model learning with efficient planning and a tailored benchmark, forms the core of the paper's contribution to CRL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of OA on the newly introduced Continual Bench demonstrates its superiority over several strong continual learning baselines within a model-based planning framework.

#### 4.1. Learning Curves of Model-Based Agents (Figure 5)

- **Plasticity:** All agents, including OA and baselines, show the ability to learn new tasks effectively during their respective learning periods, highlighting the general plasticity of model-based approaches that reuse world dynamics for planning.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting in Baselines:** The "Fine-tuning" baseline immediately forgets previous tasks upon switching to a new one, showing a drastic drop in success rate for old tasks. Regularization-based (SI) and replay-based (Coreset) methods alleviate forgetting to some extent, but their performance on older tasks still diminishes over time, especially as more tasks are introduced.
- **OA's Non-Forgetting Capability:** OA consistently maintains high performance across all previously seen tasks throughout the entire learning process. It effectively matches the performance of the "Perfect Memory" baseline, which has access to all past data, but without its computational overhead. This is a crucial validation of OA's ability to resist catastrophic forgetting.

#### 4.2. Average Performance and Regret (Figure 6a, Table 1)

- **Continual Improvement:** OA and Perfect Memory are the only agents that demonstrate continuous improvement in average performance (AP) across all seen tasks. Other baselines struggle to maintain or improve AP as more tasks are encountered, due to forgetting.
- **Quantitative Superiority:** Table 1 provides aggregated quantitative results:
 - **Model-based vs. Model-free:** Model-based agents generally exhibit lower regret than model-free (SAC-based) agents, underscoring the benefit of a shared world model for generalization across tasks.
 - **OA's Leading Performance:** OA achieves the highest Average Performance (72.93%) and the lowest Regret (27.62%) among all tested methods. Remarkably, OA even outperforms the "Perfect Memory" baseline in terms of regret (27.62% vs. 30.95%). This suggests that OA's incremental, analytical updates are not only efficient but also more effective in finding optimal solutions than SGD-based updates on a growing dataset, which can be less efficient and less stable.
- **World Model Accuracy (Figure 8 in Appendix C):** A direct examination of world model errors (MSE) further corroborates these findings. While baseline deep models (except Perfect Memory) show increasing error on older task data, OA's sparse world model maintains consistently low error across all tasks, directly demonstrating its resilience to catastrophic forgetting at the model level.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis (Figure 6 b, c, d)

- **Sparse Model Utilization (Figure 6b):** The ratio of activated weights in OA's sparse world model gradually increases as more tasks are learned. Even when approaching full utilization, OA continues to learn effectively. This contrasts with some iterative CL methods that might exhaust their solution space.
- **Buffer Budget Comparison (Figure 6c):** Under various buffer size constraints, OA consistently achieves the best final average performance. This implicitly highlights its memory efficiency, as it does not rely on explicit replay buffers like Coreset.
- **Effect of Sparsity (Figure 6d):** Varying the `Lambda` parameter (number of bins for Losse features, controlling sparsity) shows that sparser models (larger `Lambda`) can offer greater capacity and achieve higher performance. The key advantage is that the computational cost for updates and inference remains nearly constant despite increased `D` (wider network), due to the fixed number of activated nodes `K`. This demonstrates flexibility in scaling the model for different environmental complexities.

In summary, the empirical results robustly confirm that the proposed Online Agent, leveraging sparse online world models and MPC planning, effectively addresses catastrophic forgetting in CRL. It achieves state-of-the-art performance with significant efficiency advantages, validated on a purpose-built benchmark environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Continual Reinforcement Learning by Planning with Online World Models" offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of reinforcement learning.

- **Effective Solution to Catastrophic Forgetting in CRL:** The most direct and impactful contribution is providing an effective method to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in continual reinforcement learning. By demonstrating that an Online Agent (OA) can continually learn and perform well on a sequence of tasks without degrading performance on older ones, the paper addresses a fundamental challenge that has hindered the development of truly lifelong learning agents. This moves beyond merely alleviating forgetting to achieving a "non-forgetting capability."

- **Strong Theoretical Foundation:** The theoretical guarantee of a no-regret bound for the sparse online world model is a crucial advancement. This provides strong mathematical backing for the proposed approach, reassuring its long-term stability and performance in continual learning settings. Such theoretical guarantees are often lacking in deep continual learning methods, which rely heavily on empirical observation.

- **Efficiency and Scalability of Online Learning:** The use of Follow-The-Leader (FTL) shallow models with sparse feature encoding and analytical incremental updates provides a highly efficient learning mechanism. Unlike many deep learning methods that require large replay buffers or computationally expensive retraining on growing datasets (like "Perfect Memory"), OA maintains a constant computational overhead per update. This efficiency is paramount for agents operating in real-world, lifelong learning scenarios where computational resources and memory might be limited.

- **Enabling Truly Online Agents:** By separating the world dynamics learning (shared across tasks) from the task-specific policy generation (via planning with reward functions), the OA framework allows for seamless adaptation to new tasks without explicit task IDs. This moves CRL closer to a "naturalistic" setting where agents continuously learn and adapt in dynamic environments, rather than merely switching between predefined tasks. The ability to generalize to new tasks zero-shot once a reward function is given further highlights this advantage.

- **Introduction of Continual Bench:** The creation of Continual Bench addresses a critical need for a standardized, lightweight, and well-designed benchmark for CRL. By focusing on a unified world dynamics, a consistent state space, and a spatial arrangement of diverse tasks, it overcomes limitations of previous benchmarks and facilitates clearer evaluation of both forgetting and transfer. This contribution will likely accelerate research and comparison of new CRL algorithms.

- **Path Towards Autonomous Artificial Agents:** The paper's findings offer a promising blueprint for developing future autonomous agents capable of continuous, lifelong learning. Such agents would be able to operate robustly in complex, ever-changing environments, constantly updating their understanding of the world and adapting their behaviors to new challenges and goals without needing to be reset or extensively re-trained.

- **Broader Implications for Model-Based RL:** The success of this model-based approach in CRL highlights the enduring power and flexibility of learning world models. It reinforces the idea that understanding the environment's dynamics can be a more robust and transferable form of knowledge than task-specific policies or value functions, even in the face of significant non-stationarity.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as dealing with higher-dimensional, pixel-based observations or explicit exploration, its fundamental contributions in combating catastrophic forgetting with an efficient, theoretically grounded online model-planning framework mark a significant step forward in the quest for truly intelligent and adaptive AI systems.
