# Group Critical-token Policy Optimization for Autoregressive Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Group Critical-token Policy Optimization (GCPO) to enhance autoregressive image generation by applying Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) only to identified critical image tokens. This method achieves improved compositional generation and image quality with enhanced training efficiency, optimizing just 30% of tokens while outperforming uniform optimization.

## Problem

- Existing RLVR-based visual generation methods apply uniform policy optimization across all image tokens, neglecting their varying importance.
- This uniform optimization leads to inefficient training and potentially suboptimal performance in autoregressive image generation.
- The varying importance of tokens for RLVR's training remains largely unexplored in visual generation, unlike in LLM research.

## Method

- **GCPO** identifies ==critical image tokens== from three perspectives: causal dependency (initial tokens), entropy-induced spatial structure (high entropy gradient tokens), and RLVR-focused token diversity (low similarity tokens).
- A dynamic token-level advantage weight (DAW) is introduced for critical tokens to control exploration and stabilize the generative process based on confidence divergence.
- The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective function is modified to apply policy gradients exclusively to the identified critical tokens, weighted by DAW.

## Results

- GCPO consistently achieved higher performance on compositional image generation benchmarks like GenEval and T2I-CompBench, with Janus-Pro-**7B**+GCPO scoring **0.90** on GenEval.
- The framework outperformed uniform GRPO across metrics for image quality and human preference alignment (DEQA, HPSv2, ImageReward, PickScore).
- GCPO demonstrated superior computational efficiency by achieving better results than GRPO while optimizing only **30%** of the image tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Only a subset of 'critical' image tokens disproportionately influences the overall image structure, content, and alignment, making targeted optimization highly effective.
> - Critical tokens can be systematically identified by leveraging autoregressive causal dependencies, spatial structural information (entropy gradients), and token diversity within a generated group.
> - Focusing RLVR on these critical tokens, alongside dynamic weighting for exploration, leads to significantly more efficient and stable training for autoregressive image generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of two rapidly advancing fields: autoregressive (AR) visual generation and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

Autoregressive models (e.g., DALL-E, LlamaGen, Lumina-mGPT) have made significant strides in high-quality image synthesis by sequentially predicting image tokens, akin to how Large Language Models (LLMs) generate text. Concurrently, RLVR, initially popularized by models like OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek R1 for enhancing reasoning and preference alignment in LLMs, is now being extended to visual generation. Recent efforts (e.g., SimpleAR, T2i-R1, Focus-Diff, ReasonGen-R1) have applied RLVR, particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to improve aspects like aesthetic quality, prompt alignment, and task controllability in text-to-image models.

However, a critical limitation in existing RLVR-based visual generation methods is their tendency to apply uniform policy optimization across all image tokens. This approach overlooks the heterogeneous contributions of different tokens; some tokens might define global image structure, while others pertain to background details or local textures. This paper identifies this "uniform optimization" as a key obstacle, arguing that the varying importance of tokens for RLVR's training remains largely unexplored in visual generation.

Inspired by concurrent LLM research that distinguishes between "critical" (high entropy, reasoning-related) and "knowledge-related" tokens for more effective RL, this work aims to adapt this concept to the more complex domain of visual generation. The authors highlight that visual generation presents unique challenges due to the causal nature of AR modeling (early tokens constrain later ones) and the inherent spatial structure of images. By proposing a token-wise optimization strategy, "Group Critical-token Policy Optimization (GCPO)," this paper carves out a novel niche, aiming to significantly enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of RLVR for AR image generation by focusing computational resources on the most impactful tokens. It moves beyond merely applying existing RLVR frameworks to visual tasks and instead innovates on the underlying optimization strategy for the visual modality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind GCPO stems from the observation that not all image tokens contribute equally to the training objective of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in autoregressive (AR) image generation. Existing methods typically treat all tokens uniformly, leading to inefficient optimization and potentially suboptimal performance. The authors argue that, similar to how critical tokens influence reasoning in LLMs, certain visual tokens are more crucial for shaping the overall image structure, content, and alignment with human preferences or specific task requirements.

The key objectives of this research are:
1. **Identify Critical Image Tokens:** To systematically determine which tokens are most "critical" in RLVR-based AR generation. This involves defining criticality from multiple, visually relevant perspectives, moving beyond simple entropy measurements that might be sufficient for textual tokens.
2. **Develop an Effective Token-wise Optimization Strategy:** To propose a novel RLVR framework, Group Critical-token Policy Optimization (GCPO), that facilitates targeted and efficient policy optimization primarily on these identified critical tokens.
3. **Enhance Exploration and Training Stability:** To introduce a dynamic token-level advantage weight for critical tokens, based on confidence divergence between the policy and reference models, thereby enabling more reasonable exploration and stabilizing the generative process.
4. **Improve Performance and Efficiency:** To demonstrate that by focusing optimization on a smaller subset of critical tokens (e.g., 30%), GCPO can achieve superior performance compared to traditional GRPO which optimizes all tokens, across various text-to-image benchmarks and model architectures.

Ultimately, the goal is to overcome the limitations of uniform token optimization, leading to more performant, efficient, and controllable autoregressive visual generative models under the RLVR paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Group Critical-token Policy Optimization (GCPO) framework builds upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) but introduces two fundamental modifications: a sophisticated critical token selection strategy and a dynamic token-level advantage weight.

#### 3.1. Critical Token Identification

GCPO identifies critical tokens from three distinct perspectives, leveraging both the autoregressive nature of the generation process and the spatial characteristics of images:

1. **Causal Dependency of AR (Initial Tokens):** Due to the unidirectional causal attention mechanism of AR models, early generated tokens (e.g., `z_1` to `z_K_init`) fundamentally influence all subsequent tokens and the global structure of the image. Perturbation experiments confirm that changes to early tokens significantly impact the overall image, while later tokens affect only local details. Therefore, the initial `K_init` tokens are designated as critical.
2. **Entropy-Induced Spatial Structure (High Entropy Gradient Tokens):** Inspired by LLM work, the authors initially explored raw token entropy but found its distribution inconsistent with image structure across different prompts. Instead, they observed that tokens with high *entropy gradients* consistently correspond to image structures and regions bridging distinct visual areas, becoming more pronounced with RL training. To calculate this, the raw entropy sequence is reshaped into a 2D map, a local averaging operation (`H_t` = mean of `H_t` and its upper-left, upper, upper-right, and left neighbors) is applied to mitigate noise and account for local spatial and causal dependencies, and then central difference is used to compute the gradient. `K_struct` tokens with the largest gradients are selected.
3. **RLVR-Focused Token Diversity (Low Similarity Tokens):** In the GRPO setting, samples within a group guide policy optimization. Tokens exhibiting low visual similarity across a group of generated images (at the same sequence position) indicate greater diversity and provide richer information for GRPO, particularly in complex regions. Conversely, highly similar tokens (e.g., in background regions) offer limited learning signals. For each token position `t`, pairwise cosine similarity between token embeddings (`e_t,j`, `e_t,k`) across `G` images is calculated, and the average similarity `S_t` is derived. `K_similarity` tokens with the lowest average `S_t` are selected.

The final set of critical tokens, `Z_select`, is the union of these three subsets (`Z_init ∪ Z_struct ∪ Z_sim`). By default, each subset contributes 10% of the total token sequence length, meaning GCPO typically operates on 30% of the image tokens.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Advantage Weight (DAW)

To provide fine-grained control over exploration and stabilization, GCPO introduces a dynamic token-level advantage weight, `w_t`. This weight is motivated by the observation that different critical tokens require distinct exploration constraints (e.g., initial tokens needing moderate exploration, while structure-related tokens needing stronger exploration). `w_t` is calculated as the cumulative average of the confidence divergence (log probability difference) between the updating policy model (`C_policy_j`) and the reference model (`C_ref_j`) for preceding tokens `j` up to `t`, with clipping (`ϵ_w`) to prevent instability:
`w_t = (1/t) * Sum_{j=1 to t} clip(C_policy_j - C_ref_j, -ϵ_w, ϵ_w)`
This allows the model to adaptively adjust the exploration magnitude based on how much the policy has diverged from the reference at a given token position.

#### 3.3. GCPO Objective Function

The core GRPO objective function is modified to incorporate these strategies. The GCPO objective (`J_GRPO(θ)`) includes two main changes compared to standard GRPO:
1. **Token Filtering:** An indicator function `I[z_t ∈ Z_select]` is applied, ensuring that policy gradients are calculated and applied *only* for the tokens identified as critical.
2. **Weighted Advantage:** The advantage term for each critical token (`A_i`) is multiplied by its corresponding dynamic advantage weight (`w_i_t`), allowing for differentiated exploration during optimization.

This targeted approach allows GCPO to focus computational effort on the most influential parts of the image generation process, leading to more efficient and effective learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on multiple text-to-image benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of GCPO across various autoregressive models and unified multimodal models.

#### 4.1. Performance on Compositional Image Generation (GenEval & T2I-CompBench)

- **GenEval:** GCPO consistently achieved significant improvements over GRPO across all three base models tested (LlamaGen, Janus-Pro 1B, and Janus-Pro 7B). Notably, Janus-Pro-7B+GCPO attained the highest overall score of 0.90, representing a substantial gain, particularly in the challenging "Counting" task (+0.19 over GRPO baseline).
- **T2I-CompBench:** GCPO outperformed GRPO in the majority of tasks, showing strong generalization capabilities even on data substantially different from the Geneval-style training set. It achieved impressive performance gains of up to 20% on "Shape" (+0.1174), "Texture" (+0.1181), and "Spatial" (+0.1343) tasks, highlighting its ability to improve the compositional understanding and generation accuracy of models. Qualitative results (Fig. 6) visually confirm GCPO's superiority in accurately rendering object shapes and spatial relationships compared to GRPO.

#### 4.2. Image Quality and Human Preference Alignment (DEQA, HPSv2, ImageReward, PickScore)

- Under the HPS-based reward setting, GCPO consistently outperformed GRPO across all three models. Janus-Pro-7B+GCPO achieved the best scores across all metrics (DEQA, HPSv2, ImageReward, PickScore), indicating superior image quality and alignment with human preferences.
- Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 6, Fig. 9-12 in Appendix D) further illustrate that images generated by GCPO exhibit more natural and vivid details, higher aesthetic quality, and better adherence to complex prompts.

#### 4.3. Efficiency and Ablation Studies

- **Token Efficiency:** A crucial finding is that GCPO achieves better performance than GRPO while leveraging *only 30%* of the image tokens for optimization. This demonstrates significant computational efficiency without compromising (and in fact, improving) results.
- **Critical Token Strategies:** Ablation studies confirmed that all three proposed critical token identification strategies (initial tokens, high entropy gradient tokens, low similarity tokens) individually contribute to performance gains, outperforming random selection. Combining all three strategies yielded the best results within the 30% token budget, validating their collective importance and the correctness of the identification approach.
- **Dynamic Advantage Weight (DAW):** The introduction of the dynamic advantage weight further improved all evaluation metrics, highlighting its effectiveness in balancing exploration and stabilizing the training process for critical tokens.
- **Importance of Critical Tokens:** A direct comparison showed that optimizing only the 30% critical tokens led to more performance improvement than optimizing the remaining 70% of "other" tokens, underscoring the disproportionate importance of the identified critical tokens for effective model training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Group Critical-token Policy Optimization (GCPO) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of autoregressive visual generation, particularly in its integration with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). Its contributions hold substantial implications for both research and practical applications:

1. **Enhanced Efficiency in RLVR for Vision:** GCPO's ability to achieve superior performance by optimizing only a small subset (30%) of image tokens marks a major leap in training efficiency for AR visual models. This efficiency gain is crucial for scaling RLVR to even larger generative models and datasets, making advanced training techniques more accessible and cost-effective.
2. **Improved Generative Quality and Controllability:** By focusing optimization on truly critical tokens that define global structure, distinct visual regions, and diversity, GCPO leads to demonstrably better compositional image generation and stronger alignment with human preferences. This translates to higher-quality, more semantically accurate, and controllable images, which is vital for applications requiring precise content generation.
3. **Deeper Understanding of Visual Token Roles:** This research offers a systematic method for identifying and understanding the varying importance of tokens in AR visual generation. The three-pronged approach (causal dependency, entropy gradients, token diversity) provides novel insights into how visual information is processed and how different parts of an image contribute to its overall quality and adherence to prompts. This understanding can inform future model architectures and training paradigms.
4. **Bridging LLM and Vision RL:** Successfully adapting the concept of "critical tokens" from LLM reasoning to visual generation highlights shared underlying principles across modalities. This cross-modal synergy could inspire further research into universal token-level optimization strategies applicable to broader multimodal AI tasks.
5. **Broad Applicability:** The demonstrated effectiveness of GCPO across both standard AR models (LlamaGen) and unified multimodal models (Janus-Pro) suggests its broad applicability. This versatility means that various existing and future generative architectures can benefit from this fine-grained optimization strategy.
6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** GCPO opens new avenues for research into more sophisticated token selection mechanisms, dynamic weighting schemes, and the application of token-level RL to other generative tasks like video generation or 3D content creation. It encourages a shift from uniform optimization to more intelligent, resource-aware learning strategies.

In summary, GCPO not only pushes the state-of-the-art in autoregressive image generation but also provides a more principled and efficient approach to applying reinforcement learning, making high-quality, controllable visual content generation more feasible and effective.
