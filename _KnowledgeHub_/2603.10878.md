# RL-Augmented MPC for Non-Gaited Legged and Hybrid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical control framework integrates a high-level Reinforcement Learning agent with a low-level Model Predictive Control system to enable adaptive, non-gaited locomotion in legged and hybrid robots. This architecture achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on diverse platforms and learned energy-efficient, acyclic contact patterns for complex terrains, improving sample efficiency compared to end-to-end RL methods.

## Problem

- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) for legged locomotion faces significant computational complexity due to the discrete nature of contact events, leading to Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming problems that hinder real-time application.
- Model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods for locomotion are typically sample-inefficient, often require extensive domain randomization and meticulous reward shaping for sim-to-real transfer, and can result in less interpretable policies.
- Existing hybrid approaches often rely on predefined periodic gaits or simplified dynamic models, limiting the emergence of truly adaptive, non-periodic locomotion behaviors that mimic biological systems.

## Method

- A hierarchical control architecture was developed, combining a high-level ==Reinforcement Learning== (RL) policy for contact scheduling and navigation commands with a low-level Model Predictive Control (MPC) system for motion execution.
- The RL agent learns acyclic contact patterns by influencing the MPC's phase scheduling through 'injection actions' at a slower frequency, offloading the combinatorial complexity to a low-level MPC that solves a parametric Nonlinear Program using an ILQR-based method, explicitly enforcing full rigid-body dynamics and handling various constraints (e.g., friction cone, joint limits).
- A custom software framework was designed to support massively parallel execution of MPC instances on CPUs, enabling efficient training of the RL policies by accelerating experience collection.

## Results

- Policies were obtained within 4–10 million environment steps, demonstrating an order of magnitude higher sample efficiency compared to typical blind end-to-end RL approaches.
- Achieved robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on the real Centauro robot for both legged and hybrid locomotion tasks without fine-tuning, exhibiting stable, adaptive, acyclic contact sequences that dynamically adjust gait frequency and transition between single and double flight phases when tracking time-varying cyclic waypoints and navigating non-flat terrains.
- Hybrid locomotion policies for Centauro achieved substantially greater energy efficiency (average CoT of ~**0.12**) compared to legged locomotion (average CoT of ~**0.35**) under identical task conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Delegating the combinatorial problem of contact timing to a high-level RL agent significantly improves the tractability of MPC for dynamic legged locomotion, allowing the MPC to focus on robust full rigid-body motion execution.
> - The combination of a learned high-level policy with a robust model-based controller leads to improved sample efficiency in RL training and enables successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without extensive domain randomization.
> - The architecture naturally facilitates the emergence of adaptive, acyclic, and energy-efficient gaits in both legged and hybrid robots, allowing dynamic adjustments to contact sequences based on task demands and environmental conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robot locomotion draws inspiration from biological systems, which exhibit agility and robustness through adaptive gait selection. Research in this area traditionally bifurcates into model-based and model-free approaches.

Model-based techniques, particularly Model Predictive Control (MPC), leverage a system's dynamic model to formulate optimal control problems. MPC has demonstrated effectiveness in generating dynamic locomotion by enabling online re-planning, explicit constraint handling, and interpretability. However, the direct optimization of contact forces and timings in MPC leads to a Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming problem, which is computationally intensive for real-time applications. Consequently, simplified dynamics models or heuristics are often employed to manage this complexity, often limiting the expressiveness of the motion or requiring predefined contact schedules.

In contrast, model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches learn policies directly through interaction with the environment, mapping observations to actions without explicit dynamic modeling. Many state-of-the-art RL methods for locomotion utilize contact-implicit policies, thus avoiding the combinatorial nature of contact scheduling. However, these methods typically require extensive domain randomization and meticulous reward shaping to achieve robust sim-to-real transfer and are generally sample inefficient, necessitating massive parallel simulation for training. Model-based RL attempts to mitigate sample inefficiency but often results in less interpretable policies and limited transferability.

To leverage the strengths of both paradigms, hybrid architectures combining learning and optimal control have emerged. Prior hybrid methods include using simplified pendulum models for reference generation with predefined contact schedules, learning foothold locations with MPC executing motion, or employing planners with tracking policies. Some approaches also address contact scheduling directly, such as using Monte-Carlo Tree Search or evolutionary strategies to parameterize contact phases for simplified MPCs.

This work contributes to the hybrid architecture landscape by proposing a contact-explicit hierarchical system where a high-level RL agent directly learns acyclic contact patterns and navigation commands, while a low-level MPC handles the full rigid-body motion execution. This approach differentiates itself by:
- **Learning acyclic gaits directly through trial and error:** Unlike methods relying on predefined gaits or demonstrations, this architecture discovers adaptive contact patterns.
- **Offloading combinatorial complexity:** It shifts the burden of contact timing from the MPC to the RL agent, making the MPC tractable.
- **Enhancing robustness and transferability:** By using the same MPC during training and deployment, the method aims for robustness, sample efficiency, and zero-shot sim-to-sim and sim-to-real transfer without extensive domain randomization.
- **Scalable training:** The development of a custom software framework for parallel MPC execution facilitates efficient training.

Therefore, this research aims to bridge the gap between the computational tractability of model-based methods and the adaptability of model-free learning, specifically for generating dynamic and non-periodic locomotion behaviors in complex robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a hierarchical control architecture that integrates Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Model Predictive Control (MPC) for robust and adaptive non-gaited legged and hybrid locomotion. This architecture is designed to enable robots to dynamically choose and adapt contact patterns in response to task demands and environmental conditions.

The motivations underpinning this objective are multifaceted:

- **Addressing the Combinatorial Complexity of Contact Scheduling:** Traditional MPC for legged locomotion often faces a computational bottleneck due to the discrete nature of contact events, leading to Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming problems that are difficult to solve in real-time. The research aims to alleviate this burden by delegating the complex task of contact timing and sequencing to a high-level RL agent, allowing the low-level MPC to operate with a predefined (but adaptable) contact schedule over its horizon.
- **Enabling the Emergence of Acyclic and Adaptive Gaits:** Biological systems exhibit diverse, non-periodic gait patterns that adapt dynamically. Existing robotic locomotion approaches often rely on predefined periodic gaits or are learned through demonstrations, which can limit adaptability. A key motivation is to enable the RL agent to discover and generate acyclic contact patterns through trial and error, fostering more natural and task-adaptive behaviors without requiring explicit gait definitions.
- **Improving Sample Efficiency and Generalization in RL:** Pure model-free RL methods for locomotion are typically sample-inefficient and heavily depend on domain randomization and reward shaping for sim-to-real transfer. By coupling RL with a robust model-based controller (MPC), the goal is to improve sample efficiency, simplify reward design, and enhance generalization capabilities, facilitating zero-shot transfer from simulation to diverse robotic platforms and real-world hardware.
- **Enhancing Robustness through Control Division:** The hierarchical structure is motivated by the desire to leverage the strengths of both control paradigms: the MPC provides explicit constraint handling, interpretability, and robust motion execution, while the RL agent offers adaptability and the ability to learn complex decision-making. This division of roles is intended to yield a system that is both robust to disturbances and adaptable to varied tasks.
- **Developing a Scalable Training Framework:** The computational demands of training RL policies coupled with MPCs are significant. A practical motivation is to develop a modular and scalable software framework capable of running numerous MPC instances in parallel on CPUs, while interfacing efficiently with GPU-accelerated simulation environments. This framework is essential for achieving practical training times for such complex architectures.
- **Demonstrating Versatility Across Platforms and Locomotion Modes:** The research seeks to validate the architecture's versatility by applying it to various robotic platforms (ranging from 50 kg to 120 kg, with different morphologies) and locomotion tasks, including both standard legged and hybrid wheeled-legged locomotion, on flat and non-flat terrains. This demonstrates the broad applicability of the proposed method.

In summary, the core motivation is to create an intelligent and robust locomotion system that overcomes the limitations of purely model-based or model-free approaches by combining them synergistically, leading to adaptive, non-periodic gaits and reliable real-world performance without extensive manual tuning or domain randomization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is centered on a hierarchical architecture that couples a high-level Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy with a low-level Model Predictive Control (MPC) system.

#### 3.1. Low-Level Model Predictive Control (MPC)

The low-level MPC is responsible for motion execution and adheres to a predefined contact schedule over its optimization horizon.
- **Formulation:** The MPC solves a parametric Nonlinear Program (NLP) formulated as an inverse-dynamics problem. This formulation explicitly enforces full rigid-body dynamics via a floating-base constraint, offering numerical advantages. The decision variables include generalized configurations, velocities, and accelerations, as well as foot contact forces. The floating base orientation is handled using a unit quaternion, and twists/accelerations are expressed in a world-aligned local body frame for computational efficiency.
- **Solution Approach:** An equality-constrained multiple-shooting variant of the Iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (ILQR) is employed, with inequality constraints approximated via quadratic barriers. The solver operates in a real-time iteration scheme, using the shifted previous solution as an initial guess. To compensate for computation delay, control inputs for the underlying joint impedance controller are taken from the second node of the previous solution.
- **Cost and Constraint Formulation:** The MPC employs a consistent set of costs and constraints across platforms, with minor adjustments for hybrid locomotion. Key elements include:
 - **Equality Constraints:** Integrator dynamics and inverse dynamics.
 - **Contact Constraints:** Rolling contact (for hybrid platforms) or no-slip point contact constraints for active contacts, force vanishing for flight phases.
 - **Inequality Constraints:** Friction cone, contact force unilaterality, and joint velocity limits.
 - **Costs:** Regularization on states and inputs (velocities, accelerations, contact forces), terminal costs for desirable end-of-horizon configurations (posture, capture point), and base twist tracking. Vertical foot tracking costs guide foot trajectories during flight.
- **Horizon Control and Acyclic Contact Scheduling:** The MPC defines distinct "flight" and "contact" phases for each foot. A scalar "injection action" (`χ_MPC`) per foot dictates whether a new flight phase of a specified length (`T_fl`) should be injected at a fixed "injection node" (`i*`) within the horizon. Injections are only permitted if they do not overlap existing flight phases for the same foot. When no injections occur, unit-length contact phases are appended to maintain a full horizon. Foot trajectories during flight are parametrized by a cubic polynomial based on desired clearance (`H_c`) and landing height difference (`H_l`). This design allows the RL policy to generate fully acyclic gaits by influencing the MPC's phase scheduling.
- **Closing the State Loop:** The MPC's initial state constraint can be configured for open-loop (using previous solution), full closed-loop (using fully observed state), or partial closed-loop (constraining only measured components, typically IMU and joint encoders).

#### 3.2. High-Level Control: Navigation and Contact Scheduling (RL Agent)

A high-level RL policy, trained in simulation, generates the contact schedules and base twist commands for the MPC.
- **Task Formulation:** The primary task is an infinite-horizon base twist tracking task. Instead of directly tracking a twist, the agent is given a target planar position (`p_cmd`), from which a desired twist (`ξ_cmd`) is computed via a linear ramp. This indirect approach provides a more informative and realistic training experience.
- **Markov Decision Process (MDP) Formulation:**
 - **Actions (`a`):** The policy directly controls the MPC base twist reference (`ξ_MPC`) and the foot injection actions (`χ_MPC`). The policy runs at a slower frequency (`f_π`) than the MPC (`f_MPC`), with `n_rep` MPC steps per policy action.
 - **Observations (`o`):** The observation space integrates data from various sources:
 - **Task (`O_task`):** Current desired twist (`ξ_cmd`) in a base-local frame.
 - **Robot (`O_robot`):** Proprioceptive data including normalized gravity vector, angular velocity, joint positions, and velocities. Linear velocity and contact forces are estimated from the last MPC solution.
 - **MPC (`O_MPC`):** A minimal subset of MPC information deemed sufficient: estimates of linear velocity and contact forces, MPC "health" index (`δ_MPC`) reflecting convergence/feasibility, and the duration and position of the earliest flight phase for each contact.
 - **Impedance Controller (`O_IMP`):** Reference joint positions, velocities, and feedforward torques.
 - **Action History (`O_act`):** A history of past actions.
 Notably, no clock-based information is explicitly provided, as the action parameterization implicitly handles timing.
 - **Rewards:** The reward function is a weighted sum of three components:
 - **Tracking Reward (`r_ξ`):** Encourages the robot to match the commanded base twist, emphasizing tracking along specific axes.
 - **Action Regularization (`r_a`):** Penalizes the rate of change of the agent's actions (both twist and contact flags) to promote smoother behaviors.
 - **Cost of Transport (CoT) Reward (`r_CoT`):** Promotes energy-efficient locomotion by penalizing average mechanical power consumption.
 - **Episodes Definition:** Training episodes are truncated at a maximum duration to promote experience diversity. The target planar position is resampled upon truncation. Early termination occurs if the robot capsizes or the MPC health index exceeds a threshold, indicating divergence. Resets fully reinitialize the robot and MPC state.
- **Training Algorithm:** Given the computational expense of transition sampling from the underlying MDP (due to MPC computation), an off-policy RL approach, Soft Actor Critic (SAC), is employed in combination with parallel experience collection.

#### 3.3. Software Architecture

A modular and scalable software framework was developed to support massively parallel experience collection.
- **Components:** It comprises a world simulation (IsaacSim for training, MuJoCo for evaluation), an MPC cluster (thousands of CPU-based instances running in parallel via a custom library built on Horizon framework), and an agent training module (managing the SAC agent).
- **Parallelism:** MPC solutions are computed in parallel with physics integration, allowing for the modeling of real-world action delays. Shared memory is used for coordination between MPC subprocesses.
- **Real-world Deployment:** The framework supports real-world deployment via the XBot2 middleware.

This comprehensive approach combines the precision and constraint handling of MPC with the adaptive learning capabilities of RL, facilitated by a custom software infrastructure designed for efficiency and scalability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several findings demonstrating the effectiveness and characteristics of the proposed RL-Augmented MPC architecture across various robotic platforms and locomotion tasks.

- **Training Performance and Efficiency:**
 - Policies were obtained within 4–10 million environment steps, which corresponds to approximately 9–29 simulated days of experience. This is reported to be significantly more sample efficient (an order of magnitude fewer samples) compared to typical blind end-to-end RL policies, although wall-time efficiency does not match that of purely end-to-end methods.
 - During training, the framework achieved real-time factors up to 50, even on complex robots like Centauro (43 degrees of freedom).
 - Policy inference incurred negligible overhead, with evaluation on Centauro requiring only 0.334 ms on an onboard CPU, allowing real-time operation of the MPC.

- **Emergence of Acyclic Gaits and Adaptive Locomotion:**
 - For legged locomotion (e.g., Centauro), the trained policies exhibited distinctly acyclic contact sequences and timing adaptations. When tracking time-varying cyclic waypoints, the robot adapted its gait, transitioning between single and double flight phases. While trotting patterns emerged for steady forward motion, contact durations adapted smoothly with changes in commanded twist, leading to reduced gait frequency as the robot approached target positions. Asymmetric trotting patterns were also observed.
 - For hybrid wheeled-legged locomotion, policies primarily utilized wheels for velocity tracking. Stepping behaviors emerged specifically for reorienting the robot before resuming wheeled motion. These hybrid policies achieved smoother tracking, faster convergence, and lower variance compared to legged policies, even with higher maximum task velocities.

- **Influence of Policy Frequency (`n_rep`):**
 - An ablation study showed that for hybrid locomotion, the `n_rep` parameter (number of MPC steps per policy action) had limited influence on convergence.
 - In contrast, for legged locomotion, `n_rep` was critical. Inappropriate values often led to convergence to poor local minima where the robot remained stationary and failed to develop stepping behaviors.

- **Energy Efficiency:**
 - A comparative analysis on Centauro revealed that hybrid locomotion was substantially more energy-efficient than legged locomotion under identical task conditions. The hybrid policy achieved an average Cost of Transport (CoT) of approximately 0.12 at convergence, compared to 0.35 for legged locomotion.

- **Zero-Shot Domain Transfer (Sim-to-Sim and Sim-to-Real):**
 - The architecture demonstrated zero-shot cross-simulator generalization, with policies successfully transferring from IsaacSim (training environment) to MuJoCo for all tested platforms.
 - Crucially, zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was achieved on the real Centauro robot for both legged and hybrid locomotion tasks, without domain randomization or fine-tuning. This was performed with the MPC running in open-loop mode, relying on MPC state estimates for odometry. All computations were executed onboard and in real-time. The transferred policies maintained non-periodic gait patterns, adaptive behaviors, and robust sustained operation on the physical hardware.

- **Extension to Non-Flat Terrains:**
 - The architecture was extended to handle unstructured environments. For Centauro navigating a terrain of stepped pyramids (where step heights exceeded wheel radius), the policy, augmented with heightmap observations and control over flight phase parameters (clearance, landing height, duration), successfully ascended and descended. Large steps were primarily used for ascent, while smaller steps were occasionally employed on flat sections to overcome non-holonomic constraints.

These results collectively indicate that the proposed RL-Augmented MPC architecture is capable of learning complex, adaptive locomotion behaviors, is robust to domain changes, and can operate effectively on real-world robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of legged and hybrid robot locomotion by introducing a hierarchical control architecture that effectively merges the strengths of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC). Its significance and potential impact can be understood in several key areas:

- **Addressing the Contact Scheduling Problem:** The core contribution lies in offloading the combinatorial burden of contact timing from the computationally intensive MPC to a high-level RL agent. This allows the low-level MPC to utilize full rigid-body dynamics for robust motion execution, while the RL agent learns complex, non-periodic contact sequences. This approach enhances the tractability of online optimal control for highly dynamic legged behaviors, moving beyond predefined or simplified gait patterns.

- **Enabling Adaptive and Acyclic Gaits:** The ability of the RL agent to learn acyclic contact schedules through trial and error, without relying on predefined gaits or demonstrations, represents a step toward more biologically plausible and adaptable robot locomotion. This allows robots to dynamically adjust their foot placements and timings in response to varying task demands and environmental conditions, leading to more agile and versatile movements than those achievable with fixed-gait controllers.

- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Real-World Applicability of RL:** By coupling RL with a robust model-based controller, the architecture demonstrates improved sample efficiency compared to end-to-end RL methods. This is crucial for reducing the vast amount of simulated experience typically required for training, making RL more practical for complex robotic tasks. Furthermore, the demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-sim and, critically, zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without domain randomization, highlights a major advancement in the deployability of learned policies on physical hardware. This significantly lowers the barriers to real-world deployment for complex locomotion controllers.

- **Robustness and Generalization:** The use of the same MPC during both training and deployment contributes to the system's inherent robustness and generalization capabilities. This partial decoupling of the learned policy from low-level dynamics allows the RL agent to focus on higher-level decision-making (gait and navigation), while the MPC handles variations in robot dynamics and external disturbances, as evidenced by successful transfers across different simulators and to real hardware.

- **Scalable Software Infrastructure:** The development of a modular and scalable software framework capable of running thousands of MPC instances in parallel facilitates efficient training, which is essential for developing complex hierarchical controllers. This infrastructure itself can serve as a valuable tool for future research in hybrid control and robot learning.

- **Versatility Across Platforms and Terrains:** The validation on diverse robotic platforms (quadrupeds, wheeled-legged humanoids like Centauro) and across various locomotion modes (legged, hybrid wheeled-legged, flat and non-flat terrains) demonstrates the architecture's broad applicability. This suggests that the approach could be adapted to a wide range of existing and future robotic systems, potentially expanding the capabilities of robots operating in complex, unstructured environments.

**Potential impacts include:**
- **Advancement in Autonomous Robotics:** Enabling robots to navigate and interact with complex environments more effectively and efficiently.
- **Enhanced Robot Capabilities:** Paving the way for robots that can perform dynamic tasks requiring agile and adaptive locomotion, such as search and rescue, logistics, or exploration in challenging terrains.
- **Reduced Development Cycles:** By minimizing the need for extensive domain randomization and real-world fine-tuning, the approach could accelerate the development and deployment of advanced robot controllers.

Future work, such as learning more general policies that transfer across different controllers and platforms, explicit control over contact landing locations, and systematic evaluation of robustness to external disturbances, indicates clear paths for extending this promising framework to even broader applications and capabilities.
