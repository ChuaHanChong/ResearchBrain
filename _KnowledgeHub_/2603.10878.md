# RL-Augmented MPC for Non-Gaited Legged and Hybrid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical control framework integrates a high-level Reinforcement Learning agent with a low-level Model Predictive Control system to enable adaptive, non-gaited locomotion in legged and hybrid robots. This architecture achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on diverse platforms and learned energy-efficient, acyclic contact patterns for complex terrains, improving sample efficiency compared to end-to-end RL methods.

## Problem

- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) for legged locomotion faces significant computational complexity due to the discrete nature of contact events, leading to Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming problems that hinder real-time application.
- Model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods for locomotion are typically sample-inefficient, often require extensive domain randomization and meticulous reward shaping for sim-to-real transfer, and can result in less interpretable policies.
- Existing hybrid approaches often rely on predefined periodic gaits or simplified dynamic models, limiting the emergence of truly adaptive, non-periodic locomotion behaviors that mimic biological systems.

## Method

- A hierarchical control architecture was developed, combining a high-level ==Reinforcement Learning== (RL) policy for contact scheduling and navigation commands with a low-level Model Predictive Control (MPC) system for motion execution.
- The RL agent learns acyclic contact patterns by influencing the MPC's phase scheduling through 'injection actions' at a slower frequency, offloading the combinatorial complexity from the MPC.
- The low-level MPC solves a parametric Nonlinear Program using an ILQR-based method, explicitly enforcing full rigid-body dynamics and handling various constraints (e.g., friction cone, joint limits) based on the contact schedule provided by the RL agent.
- A custom software framework was designed to support massively parallel execution of MPC instances on CPUs, enabling efficient training of the RL policies by accelerating experience collection.

## Results

- Policies were obtained within 4–10 million environment steps, demonstrating an order of magnitude higher sample efficiency compared to typical blind end-to-end RL approaches.
- Achieved robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on the real Centauro robot for both legged and hybrid locomotion tasks, demonstrating stable, non-periodic gait patterns and adaptive behaviors on physical hardware without fine-tuning.
- Hybrid locomotion policies for Centauro achieved substantially greater energy efficiency (average CoT of ~**0.12**) compared to legged locomotion (average CoT of ~**0.35**) under identical task conditions.
- Trained policies exhibited adaptive, acyclic contact sequences, dynamically adjusting gait frequency and transitioning between single and double flight phases when tracking time-varying cyclic waypoints and navigating non-flat terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Delegating the combinatorial problem of contact timing to a high-level RL agent significantly improves the tractability of MPC for dynamic legged locomotion, allowing the MPC to focus on robust full rigid-body motion execution.
> - The combination of a learned high-level policy with a robust model-based controller leads to improved sample efficiency in RL training and enables successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without extensive domain randomization.
> - The architecture naturally facilitates the emergence of adaptive, acyclic, and energy-efficient gaits in both legged and hybrid robots, allowing dynamic adjustments to contact sequences based on task demands and environmental conditions.
