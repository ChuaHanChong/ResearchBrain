# BEHAVIOR Robot Suite: Streamlining Real-World Whole-Body Manipulation for Everyday Household Activities

> [!summary] Summary
> The BEHAVIOR ROBOT SUITE (BRS) is an integrated framework enabling real-world whole-body manipulation for everyday household activities using the Galaxea R1 robot. It combines a low-cost, intuitive teleoperation interface with a novel learning algorithm to achieve robust and safe performance on diverse tasks, often surpassing human teleoperation in contact-rich scenarios.

## Problem

- Household robotics lacked comprehensive systems for complex, real-world tasks requiring whole-body mobile manipulation, such as bimanual coordination and extensive reach.
- Collecting scalable, high-quality demonstration data for high-DoF mobile manipulators was challenging due to limitations of existing teleoperation interfaces, often leading to embodiment mismatch or poor user experience.
- Learning algorithms struggled to model coordinated whole-body actions for high-dimensional robots, resulting in amplified end-effector drift, out-of-distribution states, and safety violations.

## Method

![[2503.05652-method.png]]
*WB-VIMA architecture: egocentric colored point cloud and proprioception fuse via multi-modal visuomotor attention, then an autoregressive whole-body action decoder denoises base, torso, and arm actions hierarchically within the embodiment space.*

- Developed the **BEHAVIOR ROBOT SUITE (BRS)**, an integrated system built around the ==Galaxea R1== robot, a wheeled dual-arm manipulator with a ==4-DoF torso==, designed for extensive reach and bimanual capabilities.
- Introduced **JoyLo**, a cost-effective, ==puppeteering-based teleoperation== interface utilizing Nintendo Joy-Cons, offering intuitive, simultaneous whole-body control and haptic feedback for high-quality data collection.
- Proposed **WB-VIMA**, a transformer-based visuomotor policy that employs ==autoregressive whole-body action decoding== and ==multi-modal observation attention== to learn coordinated and safe actions from collected data.

## Results

- WB-VIMA achieved an average sub-task success rate of **88%** and an average entire-task success rate of **58%** across **five** challenging real-world household tasks, with **13×** and **21×** greater performance than DP3 and RGB-DP respectively and a near-zero safety violation rate.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both autoregressive whole-body action decoding and multi-modal observation attention are critical for WB-VIMA's performance, with their removal leading to significant degradation.
- JoyLo delivered superior data collection performance, achieving a **5×** higher task success rate and **23%** shorter median completion time in user studies vs. VR controllers, while also providing higher data quality (lowest singularity ratio).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autoregressive decoding of whole-body actions, by predicting mobile base, torso, and arm movements hierarchically, is essential for mitigating error propagation and achieving coordinated movements in high-DoF mobile manipulators.
> - Dynamic fusion of multi-modal observations (colored point clouds and proprioception) via attention mechanisms allows visuomotor policies to extract salient features and maintain robust perception, preventing overfitting to single sensory inputs.
> - A direct-mapping, kinematic-twin teleoperation interface with haptic feedback (JoyLo) significantly enhances data quality and collection efficiency for complex mobile manipulation tasks, outperforming IK-based and VR methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of versatile and capable robots for everyday household tasks represents a significant, long-standing challenge in human-centered robotics. Historically, robotics research in this domain has tackled problems by either focusing on defining benchmarks for everyday activities or by building robotic systems, often leveraging learning-based methods, to automate specific tasks. However, a major gap exists in comprehensively addressing the complexity of real-world household tasks that demand **whole-body mobile manipulation**.

Current limitations in the broader research landscape include:
- **Insufficient Whole-Body Capabilities:** Many existing robotic systems for household tasks either employ stationary arms or rely on mobile bases with single arms and limited torso articulation, restricting their ability to perform tasks requiring extensive reach, bimanual coordination, or complex navigation. As highlighted in the paper's analysis of the BEHAVIOR-1K benchmark, successful execution of diverse household tasks critically hinges on three core capabilities: bimanual coordination, stable and precise navigation, and extensive end-effector reachability.
- **Hardware-Software Disconnect:** While advanced robotic hardware with high degrees of freedom (DoFs) is essential for whole-body manipulation, its complexity often complicates visuomotor policy learning. Existing frameworks frequently struggle to integrate hardware design, data collection, and learning algorithms synergistically.
- **Data Collection Challenges:** Collecting high-quality, scalable demonstration data for complex, high-DoF mobile manipulators in unstructured, real-world environments is a major bottleneck. Teleoperation interfaces often suffer from issues such as high cost, embodiment mismatch (e.g., motion retargeting from human to robot), limited applicability across diverse robot platforms, or poor user experience, particularly for mobile and bimanual tasks. The paper compares BRS against other notable frameworks like Mobile ALOHA, TidyBot++, ACE, and BiDex, showcasing how they often lack one or more of the comprehensive features BRS offers, such as simultaneous control of arms, torso, and mobile base, haptic feedback, or a novel learning algorithm tailored for whole-body actions.
- **Policy Learning Limitations:** Many learning-based methods, especially behavior cloning, struggle to accurately model coordinated whole-body actions. They often flatten the action space, ignoring the inherent hierarchical dependencies and error propagation within a high-DoF robot body. This leads to issues like amplified end-effector drift, out-of-distribution states, and increased safety violations. Prior works often neglect dynamic fusion of multi-modal observations, over-relying on single sensory inputs.

BRS carves out a unique position by presenting a **holistic framework** that directly addresses these limitations. It integrates a purpose-built robot embodiment, a novel, cost-effective teleoperation interface (JoyLo) designed specifically for whole-body mobile manipulation, and a sophisticated learning algorithm (WB-VIMA) that explicitly models the complexities of whole-body control and multi-modal observations. This integrated approach marks a significant advancement toward enabling generalist robots that can reliably perform everyday activities in unmodified human environments, pushing the boundaries beyond simpler stationary or single-arm manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **streamline real-world whole-body manipulation for everyday household activities**, ultimately moving closer to enabling robots to assist humans effectively in their homes.

The key motivations driving this work stem from a detailed analysis of the challenges posed by real-world household tasks:
- **The Demands of Household Activities:** Everyday tasks, as exemplified by the BEHAVIOR-1K benchmark, are inherently complex and varied. They necessitate three fundamental whole-body control capabilities:
 - **Bimanual coordination:** Essential for tasks like lifting heavy objects or opening doors while carrying items.
 - **Stable and precise navigation:** Required for traversing a house and reaching specific locations.
 - **Extensive end-effector reachability:** Critical for interacting with objects distributed across a wide range of heights and locations, from ground level to high shelves.
- **The Hardware-Software Dilemma:** While advanced robotic hardware (e.g., dual arms, mobile base, flexible torso) is necessary to achieve these capabilities, its high dimensionality and complexity pose significant hurdles for visuomotor policy learning. This includes the challenge of scaling data collection and accurately modeling the intricate coordination required for whole-body actions.
- **Limitations of Existing Solutions:** Current robotic systems and learning algorithms often fail to provide a comprehensive solution that simultaneously addresses hardware embodiment, efficient data collection, and effective policy learning for high-DoF mobile manipulators. Many approaches are fragmented, leading to suboptimal performance, poor generalization, and safety concerns in real-world settings.

Based on these motivations, BRS sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
1. **Develop a comprehensive framework:** Introduce an integrated system (BRS) capable of tackling diverse real-world household tasks by unifying robotic embodiment, data collection, and learning.
2. **Create an efficient and cost-effective teleoperation interface (JoyLo):** Design a low-cost, user-friendly tool for whole-body teleoperation to facilitate scalable and high-quality data collection for complex mobile manipulators.
3. **Propose a novel learning algorithm (WB-VIMA):** Develop a visuomotor policy learning algorithm that can effectively model and generate coordinated whole-body actions by leveraging embodiment hierarchy and fusing multi-modal observations.
4. **Demonstrate capabilities in challenging real-world tasks:** Evaluate the integrated BRS system on a suite of diverse and complex household tasks in unmodified human living environments, showcasing its practical applicability and robust performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The BEHAVIOR ROBOT SUITE (BRS) is presented as a comprehensive framework, integrating three main components: a carefully chosen robotic embodiment, a novel teleoperation interface for data collection, and a custom-designed learning algorithm.

#### 3.1. Robotic Embodiment: The Galaxea R1 Robot

The core hardware platform is the **Galaxea R1 robot**, a wheeled dual-arm manipulator with a 4-DoF torso, selected specifically to enable the three critical capabilities identified for household tasks.
- **Physical Configuration:** It features two 6-DoF arms, each with a parallel jaw gripper (5 kg payload), allowing bimanual manipulation. The 4-DoF torso (two for waist/hip, two for knee-like motions) enables transitions between standing/squatting, expanding reach from ground level to 2 meters vertically and 2.06 meters horizontally.
- **Mobility:** An omnidirectional mobile base provides stable and precise navigation across ground planes, with speeds up to 1.5 m/s and yaw rotation up to 3 rad/s.
- **Sensing:** Equipped with a ZED 2 RGB-D camera (head), two ZED-Mini RGB-D cameras (wrist), and a RealSense T265 tracking camera for visual odometry. These provide high-fidelity global and egocentric 3D perception via fused colored point clouds (60 Hz) and real-time velocity/acceleration estimates (200 Hz).
- **Control:** Arms and torso are controlled via joint impedance controllers, while the mobile base is controlled via velocity commands. An NVIDIA Jetson Orin handles onboard computation.

#### 3.2. Data Collection Interface: JoyLo

To address the challenges of data collection for such a high-DoF robot, the authors introduce **JoyLo**, a cost-effective, whole-body teleoperation interface.
- **Design Philosophy:** JoyLo adopts a puppeteering-based approach using kinematic-twin arms, making it intuitive and precise for manipulation tasks. It leverages off-the-shelf Nintendo Joy-Con controllers for their compact size, integrated thumbsticks, and customizable buttons.
- **Control Mapping:** The left thumbstick controls mobile base velocity, the right controls waist and hips, arrow keys adjust torso height, and triggers operate grippers. This allows simultaneous and efficient control of all whole-body movements.
- **Haptic Feedback:** The kinematic-twin JoyLo arms are coupled with the robot arms, providing haptic feedback via a joint impedance controller. This discourages abrupt user motions and provides proportional resistance during robot contact, enhancing realism and safety without requiring additional force sensors.
- **Cost and Accessibility:** Constructed from 3D-printed links, low-cost Dynamixel motors, and Joy-Cons, the total cost is under $500, making it highly accessible.
- **Data Acquisition:** JoyLo collects comprehensive multimodal data (RGB images, depth images, point clouds, joint states, odometry, action commands) at 10 Hz, with the robot controller running at 100 Hz.

#### 3.3. Learning Algorithm: Whole-Body Visuomotor Attention (WB-VIMA) Policy

WB-VIMA is a novel transformer-based model designed to learn coordinated whole-body actions from the data collected via JoyLo.
- **Autoregressive Whole-Body Action Decoding:** This is a key innovation to mitigate error amplification in high-DoF mobile manipulators. WB-VIMA explicitly models the inherent hierarchy in the robot's embodiment by autoregressively predicting actions:
 1. **Mobile Base Action (a_base):** Predicted first.
 2. **Torso Action (a_torso):** Predicted conditioned on a_base.
 3. **Arms and Grippers Action (a_arms):** Predicted conditioned on both a_base and a_torso.
 This ensures that downstream joints account for upstream motion, reducing error propagation. The policy jointly learns three independent denoising diffusion networks (ϵ_base, ϵ_torso, ϵ_arms) for these predictions. For efficient inference, only action readout tokens are diffused.
- **Multi-Modal Observation Attention:** To fuse complex sensory inputs, WB-VIMA integrates egocentric colored point clouds (processed by a PointNet encoder) and robot proprioception (processed by an MLP). These are transformed into tokens and fed into a transformer decoder, which applies causal self-attention over a visuomotor sequence (current and past observations, along with action readout tokens). This dynamic aggregation of multi-modal data prevents overfitting to a single modality and extracts salient, task-relevant features.
- **Training and Deployment:** WB-VIMA is trained using the AdamW optimizer with a DDPM noise scheduler for denoising. During deployment, it uses an asynchronous inference scheme on NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs, achieving low latency by continuously running inference in the background and discarding initial actions to compensate for processing delays.

#### 3.4. Experimental Design

The methodology includes rigorous evaluation on five challenging real-world household tasks inspired by BEHAVIOR-1K: "Clean House After a Wild Party," "Clean the Toilet," "Take Trash Outside," "Put Items onto Shelves," and "Lay Clothes Out."
- **Data Collection:** Between 98 and 138 real-world trajectories were collected for each long-horizon task using JoyLo.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Each policy was evaluated 15 times with randomized robot starting positions, target object placements, object instances, and distractors. Metrics included sub-task and entire-task success rates, as well as safety violations (collisions, motor power losses).
- **Baselines:** WB-VIMA was compared against DP3, RGB-DP, and ACT, as well as human teleoperation performance.
- **Ablation Studies:** Variants of WB-VIMA (without autoregressive decoding, without multi-modal attention) were tested to quantify the contribution of each novel component. A simulated table-wiping task further supported these ablations.
- **User Study:** A user study with 10 participants compared JoyLo against VR controllers and Apple Vision Pro in the OmniGibson simulator to assess efficiency, user-friendliness, and data quality (replay success rate, singularity ratio) during data collection for a complex task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted provide strong evidence for the effectiveness and significance of the BRS framework, particularly its JoyLo interface and WB-VIMA policy.

#### 4.1. BRS Enables Diverse Household Activities, WB-VIMA Outperforms Baselines (Q1)

- **High Success Rates:** WB-VIMA achieved an impressive average sub-task success rate of 88% and an average entire-task success rate of 58%, with a peak of 93% on certain tasks ("Put Items onto Shelves").
- **Superiority over Baselines:** For end-to-end task success, WB-VIMA demonstrated substantially higher performance, achieving 13× and 21× greater success rates than DP3 and RGB-DP, respectively. ACT failed to complete any full tasks and rarely succeeded in sub-tasks.
- **Addressing Whole-Body Challenges:** The baselines struggled due to their inability to model hierarchical dependencies in the 21-DoF action space, leading to amplified end-effector drift and out-of-distribution states.
- **Safety and Perception:** WB-VIMA maintained a near-zero safety violation rate, attributed to its use of colored point-cloud observations that provide explicit 3D perception and semantic understanding, enabling coordinated and safe actions. Baselines exhibited more safety violations (e.g., DP3 with 13 violations in "Clean House After a Wild Party").
- **Emergent Capabilities:** The policies showed emergent capabilities for long-horizon, multi-stage tasks, a result of the synergy between multi-modal observation attention (extracting salient features) and autoregressive whole-body action decoding (generating coherent actions).
- **Outperforming Human Teleoperation:** On contact-rich sub-tasks like opening a toilet cover or a wardrobe, WB-VIMA policies even surpassed human teleoperation success rates, suggesting that learning from successful demonstrations can lead to more precise and coordinated maneuvers than human operators might achieve in real-time under certain conditions.

#### 4.2. Synergistic Components are Key to WB-VIMA's Performance (Q2)

- **Ablation Study Confirmation:** The ablation studies clearly demonstrated that both autoregressive whole-body action decoding and multi-modal observation attention are critical for WB-VIMA's strong performance.
- **Impact of Autoregressive Decoding:** Removing autoregressive action decoding led to significant performance degradation, with up to a 53% drop in success rate for tasks critically dependent on coordinated whole-body actions (e.g., "Put Items onto Shelves"). This highlights the importance of modeling hierarchical interdependencies.
- **Importance of Multi-Modal Attention:** Disabling multi-modal observation attention reduced performance across all tasks and resulted in four collisions, indicating that the model ignored visual inputs and overfit to proprioception, leading to poor visual awareness.
- **Progressive Improvement:** A simulation roadmap showed that adding multi-modal observation attention improved a vanilla diffusion policy by 27%, and subsequently adding autoregressive whole-body action decoding further boosted success by 45%, underscoring the compounding benefits of these innovations.

#### 4.3. JoyLo is an Efficient, User-Friendly Interface for High-Quality Data (Q3)

- **Superior User Experience:** In a user study, JoyLo achieved the highest success rate and fastest completion time compared to VR controllers and Apple Vision Pro. It delivered a 5× higher task success rate and 23% shorter median completion time than VR controllers, with no participants completing the entire task using Apple Vision Pro.
- **Precision and Coordination:** JoyLo particularly excelled at articulated object manipulation (e.g., 67% higher success in "open dishwasher" than VR controllers) due to its direct joint mapping and kinematic-twin arm constraints, which enable users to generate smooth and accurate actions.
- **High Data Quality:** JoyLo provided the highest data quality, evidenced by the lowest singularity ratio (78% and 85% lower than VR controllers and Apple Vision Pro, respectively) and consistent replay success of collected trajectories. This contrasts with IK-based methods, which often produce jerky motions and suboptimal solutions.
- **User Preference Shift:** While 70% of participants initially expected IK-based interfaces to be more intuitive, they unanimously preferred JoyLo after the study, highlighting its practical advantages for mobile whole-body manipulation over static tabletop setups.

#### 4.4. Coordinated Torso and Mobile Base Movements Enhance Maneuverability (Q4)

- **Critical for Complex Interactions:** The research demonstrated that coordinated whole-body movements, involving the torso and mobile base, are crucial for interacting with heavy articulated objects such as opening doors or dishwashers.
- **Efficiency and Safety:** WB-VIMA policies utilize the robot's hip and mobile base to generate necessary inertia for these tasks. Without such coordination, the robot fails, and arm joint effort surges, risking hardware damage. This underscores the necessity of a truly whole-body approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The BEHAVIOR ROBOT SUITE (BRS) represents a significant step forward in the quest to enable robots to reliably perform complex everyday household activities. Its contributions extend across hardware, data collection, and policy learning, offering a holistic framework with substantial implications:

- **Integrated, Holistic Solution for Mobile Manipulation:** Unlike fragmented approaches that focus solely on hardware, data, or learning, BRS offers a comprehensive, synergistic framework. This integration of a capable robot embodiment, an intuitive data collection interface, and a robust learning algorithm is crucial for tackling the inherent complexity of real-world whole-body mobile manipulation tasks. This integrated design paradigm can serve as a blueprint for future developments in generalist robotics.

- **Accessibility and Scalability of Data Collection:** The introduction of JoyLo is a major advancement in addressing the critical bottleneck of data collection for high-DoF mobile manipulators. Its low cost (under $500), ease of use, and ability to provide high-quality, coordinated whole-body demonstrations will significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners. By enabling efficient and scalable data collection, JoyLo can accelerate progress in imitation learning for complex tasks, making such research more accessible to a wider community. The haptic feedback mechanism further enhances the quality and safety of collected data.

- **Robust and Safe Whole-Body Policy Learning:** WB-VIMA's novel learning algorithm directly tackles the challenges of modeling high-dimensional, interdependent actions and fusing multi-modal sensory data. The autoregressive whole-body action decoding mechanism is particularly impactful, as it inherently mitigates error propagation and ensures coordinated movements, leading to superior task success rates and significantly reduced safety violations. The multi-modal observation attention allows for robust perception in varied and unstructured environments. These algorithmic innovations push the state-of-the-art in visuomotor policy learning for complex robot platforms.

- **Real-World Applicability and Generalization:** The rigorous evaluation of BRS on five diverse and challenging real-world household tasks, conducted in unmodified human living environments, underscores its practical relevance. The ability to handle long-range navigation, bimanual coordination, interaction with articulated and deformable objects, and manipulation in confined spaces demonstrates a strong foundation for developing truly capable household robots that can generalize across varied scenarios. This moves beyond controlled lab settings and simulated environments, bridging the gap to practical deployment.

- **Advancing Human-Centered AI and Embodied AI:** BRS directly contributes to the vision of human-centered AI by developing robots that can seamlessly integrate into human environments and assist with daily tasks. By open-sourcing the entire framework (hardware design, teleoperation code, algorithm code, and documentation), the authors empower the broader research community to build upon this work, fostering collaborative innovation in embodied AI and accelerating the development of truly helpful robots.

The paper also constructively outlines several limitations and future work directions, such as incorporating active perception, mitigating compounding errors in long-horizon tasks, improving point cloud quality, and leveraging multi-embodiment or synthetic data. These identified areas highlight a clear path for continued research, demonstrating the foundational nature of BRS and its potential to inspire further breakthroughs in making versatile, autonomous robots a reality for everyday life.
