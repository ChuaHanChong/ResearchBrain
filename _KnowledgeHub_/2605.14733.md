# Video-Zero: Self-Evolution Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Video-Zero, an annotation-free self-evolution framework developed by Tsinghua University and collaborators, enhances multimodal large language models for video understanding by explicitly grounding generated supervision in temporal evidence. This approach consistently improved performance across temporal grounding, long-video understanding, and video reasoning benchmarks, achieving up to +12.89 mIoU on ANet-RTL for Qwen3-VL-4B.

## Problem

- High cost and scarcity of human-annotated video data impede the development of robust video understanding models.
- Existing self-evolution frameworks for video often lack explicit temporal grounding, leading to supervision signals weakly related to dynamic video content and reliance on non-temporal shortcuts.
- Prior self-evolution loops primarily focused on question difficulty, which is insufficient for video understanding tasks requiring genuine temporal reasoning over events.

## Method

- An iterative, evidence-centered ==Questioner–Solver co-evolution== framework, **Video-Zero**, trains video understanding models using unlabeled video data.
- The ==Questioner== generates supervision by first identifying informative temporal evidence spans in a video and then crafting question-answer pairs conditioned on this localized evidence, optimized by rewards for ==learnability==, ==video dependency==, and ==evidence quality==.
- The ==Solver== learns to answer questions and localize supporting temporal evidence from this generated supervision, utilizing ==pseudo-labels derived from rollout consistency== and a reward function that combines answer correctness with temporal alignment.

## Results

- Demonstrated consistent performance improvements across **13 benchmarks** for multiple MLLM backbones (Qwen3-VL, MiMo-SFT, InternVL3.5), with up to **+12.89 mIoU** on ANet-RTL and **+9.73 mIoU** on ActivityNet.
- Enhanced capabilities in temporal grounding, long-video understanding, and video reasoning tasks, indicating improved identification and aggregation of temporally distributed video information.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of the evidence-centric Questioner, its multi-dimensional reward function (learnability, video dependency, evidence quality), and the Solver's temporal alignment reward in driving performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective temporal grounding of supervision in specific video segments, rather than just question difficulty, is crucial for successful self-evolution in video understanding.
> - An evidence-centric self-evolution paradigm allows models to genuinely identify, aggregate, and reason over distributed temporal information within video sequences.
> - Iterative co-evolution between a Questioner generating evidence-grounded supervision and a Solver learning from it drives continuous performance gains in video understanding capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on "Video-Zero" is situated within the evolving field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and self-improvement paradigms, specifically self-evolution. Recent advancements in VLMs have demonstrated the emergence of reasoning capabilities not only from extensive human-annotated data but also from iterative self-improvement processes. In these self-evolution paradigms, models generate and learn from their own supervision signals within a closed-loop system, typically involving a Questioner that creates supervision from unlabeled data and a Solver that learns from these generated questions.

While this self-evolution approach has shown promise in text and image domains, its application to video understanding remains less explored and presents distinct challenges. Video data is characterized by its length, dynamism, and redundancy, with relevant information often being sparse and localized temporarily. Prior attempts to adapt self-evolution to videos often faced two key limitations:
1. **Lack of Temporal Grounding:** Generating questions from entire videos without specific temporal focus can lead to supervision signals that are weakly related to the actual video content. Models trained with such signals may rely on language priors or static visual cues rather than engaging in genuine temporal reasoning.
2. **Insufficient Focus on Grounding:** Existing self-evolution frameworks tend to organize their loops primarily around question difficulty. However, in the video domain, a difficult question might still be answerable using shortcuts like language biases or static scene knowledge, without requiring true temporal understanding. The relevance of the question to the video's dynamic content, specifically to localized temporal evidence, is a critical factor often overlooked.

"Video-Zero" addresses these limitations by arguing that the core bottleneck in video self-evolution is not merely difficulty, but effective grounding of supervision to temporal evidence. The paper aims to fill the gap in effective annotation-free self-evolution for video understanding by proposing a framework that explicitly centers the self-evolution process on temporally localized evidence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an annotation-free self-evolution framework, named Video-Zero, that can enhance the video understanding capabilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) directly from unlabeled video data. This objective is driven by several motivations stemming from the challenges of video understanding and the limitations of existing self-evolution paradigms:

1. **Mitigating High Annotation Costs:** High-quality video supervision data is recognized as both costly and scarce. Traditional methods for improving video understanding models often necessitate extensive human annotation for tasks like video question-answering, temporal grounding, or event localization. The project aims to reduce this reliance on expensive manual labeling by enabling models to generate their own effective supervision.

2. **Addressing Weak Grounding in Naive Video Self-Evolution:** The authors identify that simply extending existing self-evolution approaches from text or image domains to video is insufficient. When questions are generated from an entire video without a local focus, they frequently result in queries that are superficially challenging but are weakly connected to the actual video's temporal dynamics. This can lead to models learning to rely on language priors, common sense, or static visual cues, rather than developing robust temporal reasoning skills. The motivation is to shift from full-video generation to a more fine-grained, temporally localized mechanism.

3. **Beyond Difficulty-Driven Optimization:** Previous self-evolution loops have largely optimized the Questioner based on the difficulty of generated samples. The authors contend that for video understanding, this criterion alone is inadequate. A question might be difficult but still solvable without needing to comprehend specific temporal events or changes within the video. Therefore, a key motivation is to introduce a more comprehensive utility function for the Questioner that considers factors beyond difficulty, specifically focusing on the relevance between the generated question and the underlying video content, and the quality of the supporting temporal evidence.

4. **Enhancing Temporal Reasoning:** The core motivation is to foster models that genuinely reason over temporal dynamics. By designing a system where supervision is explicitly linked to temporal evidence, the objective is to guide MLLMs to identify, integrate, and interpret task-relevant information that is often sparse and localized within video sequences.

In summary, the key objective is to establish an "evidence-centric" self-evolution paradigm that ensures generated supervision is not only challenging and learnable but also intrinsically grounded in and dependent on specific temporal events or segments within the video.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Video-Zero proposes an evidence-centered Questioner–Solver co-evolution framework designed to improve video understanding models using unlabeled video data. The methodology revolves around an iterative closed loop comprising evidence discovery, grounded supervision generation, and evidence-aligned learning.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Iterative Training Pipeline

The framework initiates with a pretrained backbone model that serves as the foundation for both the Questioner policy (πQ) and the Solver policy (πS). For each iteration (t), the current Solver (St) provides feedback to optimize the next Questioner (Qt+1). This optimized Questioner then generates an evidence-grounded training dataset (Dt+1) from unlabeled videos. Subsequently, the Solver is trained on Dt+1 with evidence-aligned supervision to produce the next Solver (St+1). This iterative process aims to create a curriculum where the Questioner provides progressively better supervision, and the Solver becomes more adept at learning from it and providing more reliable feedback.

#### 3.2. Questioner: Evidence-Grounded Supervision Generation

The Questioner is responsible for discovering informative temporal evidence and constructing question-answer pairs conditioned on this evidence.

- **Evidence-Centric Generation Policy:** The generation process is factorized into two steps:
 1. The Questioner first identifies one or more informative temporal evidence spans (e) within an input video (v).
 2. It then constructs an evidence-grounded question-answer pair (q, aQ) that is conditioned on both the video and the identified evidence spans (πQ(e, q, aQ | v) = πQ(e | v)πQ(q, aQ | v, e)). This approach ensures that question generation is guided by localized temporal content. The Questioner outputs structured samples including temporal spans, evidence descriptions, question text, and answer candidates.

- **Question Utility and Reward Design (rQ):** To guide the Questioner's optimization, a comprehensive utility criterion is defined, extending beyond mere question difficulty. This utility formulation includes three dimensions:
 1. **Learnability (r_learn):** This component assesses whether a generated question is within an appropriate difficulty range for the current Solver. It is estimated by the Solver's rollout consistency. The Solver is queried multiple times with the video, and the majority-vote answer (ˆa) is identified. A consistency score (c) is calculated as the fraction of responses matching ˆa. The learnability reward (r_learn = min(c, 1-c)) favors questions with intermediate consistency, indicating they are challenging but still learnable. Overly easy questions receive a small penalty.
 2. **Video Dependency (r_dep):** This reward measures the extent to which solving a question genuinely requires video content, as opposed to reliance on non-video shortcuts (e.g., language priors). It compares the Solver's agreement with the Questioner-provided answer (aQ) under two conditions: with video access and without video access. The video dependency score (∆_video) is the difference in agreement ratios between these two conditions (r_dep = max(∆_video, 0)). A higher positive ∆_video indicates stronger video dependence.
 3. **Evidence Quality (r_evid):** This component ensures that the question is grounded in high-quality temporal evidence. It is computed from the structured Questioner output itself, without external semantic labels. This reward combines factors such as span validity and localization (s_span), preference for salient events or interactions (s_event), and the presence of temporal cues in the evidence description (s_temp). It penalizes excessively broad or degenerate evidence spans.
 The final Questioner reward (rQ) is a combination of a format term (r_format), ensuring valid output structure, and the utility term (UQ) aggregating normalized r_learn, r_dep, and r_evid.

#### 3.3. Solver Learning with Evidence-Aligned Supervision

The Solver is trained to not only answer questions correctly but also to localize the supporting temporal evidence.

- **Pseudo Supervision from Rollout Consistency:** For each Questioner-generated question (q), pseudo answer labels and temporal spans are derived from the current Solver’s rollout distribution conditioned on the video (v) and question.
 1. The Solver samples multiple responses, each containing an answer and a predicted temporal span.
 2. A pseudo answer label (ˆy) is obtained via majority voting over these sampled answers.
 3. A confidence score (s) is calculated as the support ratio for ˆy. Samples are retained for training only if their confidence falls within an intermediate range (0.3 ≤ s ≤ 0.8), filtering out trivial or excessively noisy samples.
 4. A consensus temporal target (ˆτ) is formed by taking the median start and end times from the predicted spans of rollouts that agree with the pseudo label ˆy. If this consensus span is invalid, the Questioner-provided evidence span is used as a fallback.

- **Temporal Alignment Reward (rS):** The Solver is trained using a reward that combines pseudo answer correctness with temporal alignment to the consensus target (ˆτ).
 - rS = 1[ˆa_p = ˆy] (1 + αr_align), where ˆa_p is the Solver's predicted answer, and α=0.5 controls the alignment contribution.
 - The alignment reward (r_align) is calculated using the Intersection-over-Union (IoU) between the predicted span (τ_p) and the consensus target (ˆτ), multiplied by a boundary term that encourages start and end-time consistency. This multiplicative form ensures that temporal alignment is rewarded only if the answer is correct, promoting joint learning of factual correctness and temporal grounding.

#### 3.4. Iterative Co-Evolution and Optimization

Both the Questioner and Solver are optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This involves sampling a group of outputs for each prompt, computing scalar rewards (r_Q for Questioner, r_S for Solver), and then calculating group-normalized advantages for policy updates with a clipped GRPO objective and KL regularization. The shared GRPO procedure with respective rewards facilitates the alternating optimization process.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup Details

- **Backbones:** Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct were primarily used, with cross-architecture tests on MiMo-SFT-7B and InternVL3.5-4B.
- **Training Data:** 600 randomly sampled raw videos from Open-o3-Video, with all original annotations discarded. Videos were sampled at 2 FPS with a maximum of 32 frames (main experiments).
- **Iterations:** The main experiments involved five self-evolution iterations.
- **Evaluation:** 13 benchmarks spanning temporal grounding, long-video understanding, and video reasoning, using standard metrics (e.g., mIoU, Recall, Accuracy).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Video-Zero consistently improves the performance of multiple video VLM backbones across various video understanding tasks without requiring human annotation.

#### 4.1. Temporal Grounding Performance

- Video-Zero consistently enhanced both Qwen3-VL-4B and Qwen3-VL-8B models across temporal localization benchmarks (ANet-RTL, ActivityNet, Charades) and grounded video QA (NExT-GQA). For instance, the Qwen3-VL-4B model showed peak improvements of +12.89 mIoU on ANet-RTL and +9.73 mIoU on ActivityNet.
- In contrast, prior self-evolution baselines (VisPlay, V-Zero) exhibited smaller or less consistent gains, suggesting that explicitly organizing supervision around temporal evidence is crucial for these tasks.
- The improvements on NExT-GQA indicated that the models not only predicted better timestamps but also learned to connect answers with their supporting temporal evidence.

#### 4.2. Long-Video Understanding and Video Reasoning Performance

- Video-Zero also yielded broad improvements across long-video understanding (LongVideoBench, Video-MME-Long, LSDBench, VideoNIAH) and video reasoning benchmarks (LongVideoReason, MMVU, VideoMathQA, VideoMMMU) for both backbone sizes.
- Since these tasks do not directly reward timestamps, these gains imply that evidence-centric self-evolution improves the models' ability to identify, aggregate, and reason over temporally distributed video information more reliably. For example, Qwen3-VL-4B achieved a peak improvement of +3.56% on LongVideoReason and +4.00% on VideoMMMU.

#### 4.3. Cross-Architecture Transfer

- The framework successfully generalized to MiMo-SFT-7B and InternVL3.5-4B models. Both backbones demonstrated improvements on most benchmarks, particularly on reasoning-intensive tasks (e.g., InternVL3.5-4B showed an +11.19% improvement on VideoMathQA). This indicates that the benefits of evidence-centered self-evolution are not specific to a particular model family but represent a more general training mechanism.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on Qwen3-VL-4B (at Iteration 4) provided insights into the contribution of each component:
- **Full Model Performance:** The complete Video-Zero framework achieved the best average performance across grounding, video QA, and reasoning.
- **Questioner Design:**
 - Replacing the evidence-centric Questioner with a "Description-only Questioner" (lacking explicit evidence focus) resulted in a substantial performance drop, especially for temporal grounding. This highlighted the necessity of the Questioner identifying answer-supporting evidence.
 - Removing "Video Dependency," "Evidence Quality," or "Learnability" from the Questioner's reward design consistently degraded performance, confirming the importance of these factors for generating useful, video-dependent, and learnable supervision.
- **Solver Design:**
 - Removing the "Temporal Reward" from the Solver's training led to the largest overall performance degradation, emphasizing the critical role of aligning predictions with supporting temporal evidence.
- **Co-evolution Mechanism:**
 - A "Frozen Questioner" (i.e., using a fixed set of self-generated data without iterative refinement) underperformed the full co-evolutionary model, confirming that iterative Questioner–Solver interaction drives the gains.

#### 4.5. Iterative Co-evolution Dynamics

- Performance on temporal grounding benchmarks improved beyond the first iteration, indicating that Video-Zero is an iterative process, not a one-shot data generation method.
- Analysis of the Questioner's evolution showed that later iterations generated supervision that was increasingly video-dependent (larger "video dependency gap") and more challenging (lower base-Solver accuracy). This continuous evolution enabled the generation of stronger supervision.
- However, this also introduced a trade-off: as questions became more complex and video-dependent, the reliability of pseudo-labels (accuracy against an external Oracle) decreased in later iterations, suggesting a potential limit to the gains.

#### 4.6. Robustness and Scaling

- **Frame-Budget Robustness:** Video-Zero consistently improved temporal grounding under various maximum frame budgets (32f, 64f, 128f), indicating its robustness to different visual context lengths.
- **Training Data Scaling:** Increasing the unlabeled video pool from 600 to 2000 videos led to further improvements, particularly in temporal grounding, suggesting that a larger dataset enhances the Questioner's ability to discover diverse temporal evidence.
- **Random Seed Robustness:** The framework demonstrated stable performance across different random seeds, with consistent peak iteration-level averages for both temporal grounding and video QA, indicating reliability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Video-Zero" research presents a significant contribution to the field of video understanding, particularly concerning the development of multimodal large language models.

1. **Reduced Reliance on Human Annotation:** A primary impact is the demonstration of an effective annotation-free self-evolution framework. This addresses the challenge of costly and scarce high-quality human-annotated video data, potentially democratizing access to advanced video model development by reducing a major resource barrier.

2. **Addressing a Fundamental Bottleneck:** The work identifies and effectively addresses a core limitation in applying self-evolution to video data: the need for strong temporal grounding. By centering the self-evolution process on temporally localized evidence rather than merely question difficulty, Video-Zero provides a more principled approach to generating useful supervision for video understanding.

3. **Enhanced Temporal Reasoning Capabilities:** The consistent improvements across temporal grounding, long-video understanding, and video reasoning benchmarks indicate that Video-Zero enables models to better identify, integrate, and reason over critical temporal information within videos. This fosters the development of MLLMs with more robust and genuine temporal understanding, moving beyond reliance on superficial cues or language priors.

4. **Generalizability and Transferability:** The framework's effectiveness across multiple model architectures (Qwen3-VL, MiMo-SFT, InternVL3.5) and diverse tasks suggests its broad applicability. This indicates that the evidence-centric paradigm provides a model-agnostic signal for evidence identification and utilization over time, making it a potentially foundational approach for future video VLM training.

5. **Iterative Self-Improvement:** The analysis of iterative co-evolution dynamics highlights that the gains accumulate over time through the alternating Questioner–Solver loop. This demonstrates a robust mechanism for continuous model improvement, where the evolving capabilities of the Solver enable the Questioner to generate progressively more informative and challenging supervision.

**Potential Limitations and Future Directions (as discussed in Appendix):**
While impactful, the work acknowledges certain limitations:
- **Scalability to Larger Models:** Due to computational constraints, the framework was not validated on very large video VLMs (e.g., 32B, 72B+ parameters). Future work will explore its full scaling behavior.
- **Inherited Biases and Errors:** The reliance on model-generated questions, answers, and temporal evidence means that any biases or errors present in the underlying video VLMs could be propagated. For sensitive applications, further caution and domain-specific validation would be necessary.

Future research aims to explore these limitations by investigating larger backbones, developing stronger filtering and verification mechanisms for generated supervision, and ensuring responsible deployment with clear documentation and guidelines for intended use. Overall, Video-Zero offers a promising direction for creating more autonomous and capable video understanding systems.
