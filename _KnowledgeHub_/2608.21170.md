# Is Visual Prompting All You Need? Studying VLM Spatial Reasoning under Progressive Visual Scaffolds

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper studies how visual presentation shapes VLM spatial planning by adding lightweight input-side scaffolds to SPaRC grid puzzles: start/exit markers, axis labels, in-cell coordinates, and text-rendered rule symbols, while board layout, rules, and target solution stay fixed. Across seven open-weight VLMs the scaffolds lift task accuracy by up to 34.0 percentage points and make GRPO post-training effective where naive images yield near-zero gains.

## Problem

- Prior work reads VLM planning failures as reasoning limits, yet end-to-end accuracy conflates perceiving the visual input with reasoning over what was perceived.
- Existing mitigations, model fine-tuning, merging, inference-time crop or refocus tools, and image overlays, never isolate which dimensions of visual representation actually induce planning difficulty.
- Diagnostic studies replace images with text or probe isolated subtasks, sidestepping the visual interface and obscuring how presentation propagates into downstream planning failures.

## Method

- A cumulative ==scaffold hierarchy== over SPaRC boards: **S/E Markers**, **Axis Labels**, **Axis Labels + S/E**, **Cell Coordinates**, **Text Symbols**, each making endpoint identity, spatial indexing, or symbol semantics explicit.
- Dual evaluation: end-to-end solving scored by ==Final Task Accuracy== plus four ==path-level validity conditions== (endpoint correctness, connectivity, non-intersection, rule-cell avoidance), and a separate board-element ==object-detection probe== with confusion matrices.
- A ==degradation-and-recovery stress test== (Low Contrast, Low Resolution, Rotated, each overlaid with **Cell Coordinates**) plus ==GRPO== post-training of Qwen 3 VL 4B and 8B Thinking across three input conditions.

## Results

- Qwen 3.5 397B rises from **14.4%** to **48.4%** under Text Symbols, exceeding the **38.0%** text-formulation baseline; Qwen 3.5 27B moves from **8.2%** to **40.0%**.
- Rule detection improves (Gemma 4 31B **85.4%** to **97.0%**) and correlates with task accuracy at **r=0.96**; endpoint correctness jumps from **11.8%** to about **90%**, while rule violations stay flat.
- GRPO adds **+4.6** points on scaffolded input for the 8B model versus near-zero on original images; Cell Coordinates absorb contrast and resolution damage but lose **9.0** points under rotation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Benchmark scores measure the perceptual interface as much as reasoning; hold the task fixed, intervene on the input, and observe where the errors move.
> - Perceptual support pays off only for models with spare planning capacity; the two weakest models stayed below 1% accuracy under every scaffold.
> - Grounding errors yield to presentation while rule-satisfaction errors persist, so constraint reasoning needs planning-aware training or search, not better input rendering.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated rapid advancements in integrating visual perception with language-based reasoning, enabling their application across various multimodal tasks, including embodied artificial intelligence and world models. These models typically augment large language models (LLMs) with pre-trained visual encoders, such as CLIP-ViT. Despite this progress, the specific mechanisms underlying VLMs' multimodal understanding abilities remain areas of active investigation.

Recent research has indicated that observed failures in multimodal reasoning by VLMs are often attributable to limitations in visual information grounding, verbalization, or bypassing, rather than solely to downstream reasoning capacities. Existing approaches to address such failures include model-side adaptations, inference-time visual tools, or input-side image scaffolding. However, these prior works have not consistently isolated how the visual representation itself influences task difficulty, particularly for planning problems. Diagnostic studies frequently substitute images with textual descriptions or focus on isolated subtasks, which may obscure the impact of the visual interface on subsequent planning performance.

This study positions itself within this landscape by directly examining the relationship between visual presentation and VLM performance. It specifically investigates whether VLM failures in spatial planning tasks stem from an inability to "see" (perceptual grounding) or an inability to "plan" (downstream reasoning). By systematically manipulating the visual input while keeping the underlying planning problem constant, the authors aim to disentangle these intertwined failure modes. The work draws parallels with findings from fields such as convolutional networks, where the explicit inclusion of coordinate information (e.g., CoordConv) significantly improved performance on spatial tasks, suggesting that fundamental aspects of visual representation can be critical. This paper seeks to provide diagnostic insights into how variations in visual representation affect VLM behavior in a controlled spatial reasoning environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate how the visual presentation of a task influences the performance and failure characteristics of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly when the core reasoning problem remains unchanged. This overarching goal is motivated by several key considerations:

1. **Disentangling Perception and Reasoning Failures:** Current VLM benchmarks often conflate issues arising from visual perception and those originating from downstream reasoning. For tasks requiring visual spatial planning, an incorrect output could equally reflect a failure to correctly perceive elements in the visual input (visual grounding) or an inability to apply logical rules to the perceived information. The study aims to clarify whether VLMs "fail to plan, or fail to see."
2. **Understanding the Impact of Input Representation:** While existing work explores various prompting strategies and model architectures, less attention has been paid to the intrinsic properties of the visual input representation itself and how subtle changes in its accessibility affect VLM performance. The authors hypothesize that lightweight modifications to the visual input, designed to make spatial structure more explicit, could significantly alter VLM behavior.
3. **Utilizing a Controllable Testbed:** The SPaRC (Spatial Pathfinding Reasoning Challenge) benchmark is chosen as a testbed due to its grid-based structure, which offers a controllable environment for manipulating visual presentation. SPaRC puzzles require both accurate perception of grid elements (start/end points, rule symbols) and logical reasoning to construct a valid path, making it suitable for diagnosing the interplay between these two capabilities.
4. **Developing Diagnostic Interventions:** The study proposes and implements a hierarchy of "visual scaffolds." These scaffolds are lightweight, input-side modifications that progressively enhance the perceptual clarity of spatial information within the visual modality. They are intended as diagnostic tools to probe VLM behavior, rather than general-purpose prompting strategies. By observing how model performance and error profiles shift with increasing perceptual support, the researchers aim to pinpoint the sources of difficulty.
5. **Informing VLM Development and Benchmarking:** By revealing how visual presentation shapes VLM capabilities, the research seeks to provide insights that can guide the development of more robust VLMs and the design of more informative benchmarks. Understanding which aspects of visual input are challenging for current models can inform the creation of better visual encoders or training methodologies, while clarifying what a benchmark *actually* measures can lead to more targeted evaluations.
6. **Investigating Interactions with Post-Training:** The study also aims to explore how improved perceptual access through scaffolding might interact with post-training methods, specifically reinforcement learning (RL) via GRPO. This investigates whether a clearer visual input can enhance a model's ability to learn from reward signals, thereby improving its reasoning policy.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond aggregate performance metrics and delve into the underlying causes of VLM successes and failures in spatial reasoning by systematically manipulating the visual information available to the models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic approach to investigate the impact of visual presentation on VLM spatial reasoning, primarily using the SPaRC benchmark.

#### 3.1. SPaRC as a Testbed

The study utilizes SPaRC (Spatial Pathfinding Reasoning Challenge) as its experimental platform. SPaRC consists of 1,000 2D grid-based pathfinding puzzles (500 for training, 500 for testing) inspired by "The Witness" game. Each puzzle presents an `m × n` grid containing rule cells (R), a designated start node (S), and an end node (E). The objective is to generate a sequence of coordinates representing a valid continuous path from S to E that satisfies various explicit rule constraints (e.g., item collection, path breaks, color separation, pairing, edge count, shape fitting, shape subtraction). Puzzles are categorized into five difficulty levels.

#### 3.2. Input-Side Visual Scaffolding Hierarchy

To systematically vary perceptual accessibility, the authors developed a hierarchy of six visual representations, progressively adding explicit spatial structure while maintaining the underlying planning task:
- **Original:** The baseline visual puzzle image without any modifications.
- **S/E Markers (S/E):** Explicitly marks the start (S) and end (E) cells on the board. This is designed to assist with endpoint localization.
- **Axis Labels (Axis):** Adds row and column indices along the board borders, similar to spreadsheet coordinates. Expected to improve global spatial referencing.
- **Axis Labels + S/E:** Combines both Axis Labels and S/E Markers.
- **Cell Coordinates (Cell Coord.):** Prints the (x,y) coordinates directly inside each grid cell, aiming to reduce ambiguity in local cell localization and binding.
- **Text Symbols (Text Sym.):** Builds upon Cell Coordinates by replacing graphical rule symbols with explicit text labels (e.g., a star icon becomes the word "star"). This intervention explicitly clarifies symbol semantics.
This hierarchy is cumulative, meaning later variants incorporate features of earlier ones. The scaffolds are explicitly designed as task-specific diagnostic interventions, not general-purpose prompting strategies, leveraging SPaRC's known board structure.

#### 3.3. Visual Degradation and Recovery

To further test the grounding hypothesis, three forms of visual degradation were introduced to the original board images:
- **Low Contrast:** Desaturates board colors to make symbols less visually distinct.
- **Low Resolution:** Downsamples the image, reducing fine-grained pixel detail.
- **Rotated:** Rotates the entire board, disrupting its canonical orientation.
For each degraded variant, a corresponding "recovery" variant was created by overlaying the "Cell Coordinates" representation onto the degraded image. This allows assessing whether degraded performance can be mitigated by restoring access to explicit spatial structure without altering symbol identities to text.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Tasks and Metrics

Model behavior was assessed using two complementary evaluation tasks:
- **End-to-End Task Solving:**
 - **Primary Metric:** **Final Task Accuracy**, defined as the percentage of puzzles where the generated path is completely correct.
 - **Path-Level Validity Conditions:** To analyze failure modes, paths were evaluated against four conditions:
 - **Correct Start/End:** Path connects the designated start and exit cells.
 - **Connected Path:** Consecutive path coordinates are Manhattan-adjacent.
 - **Non-Intersecting:** Path does not revisit any coordinate.
 - **No Rule Violation:** Path does not enter any rule cell.
 - **Fully Valid:** The percentage of paths satisfying all four validity conditions.
- **Object Detection:** Models were prompted to identify rule symbols on the board.
 - **Metrics:**
 - **Exact-Match Board Accuracy:** Percentage of boards where all rule symbols were identified correctly.
 - **Average Rule Detection Accuracy:** Per-rule accuracy averaged across boards.
 - **Per-Type Detection Accuracy:** Accuracy for each specific symbol type.
This task was performed independently of path planning, using a separate prompt, allowing for direct assessment of visual grounding accuracy.

#### 3.5. GRPO Post-Training Experiments

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning technique, was applied to two Qwen 3 VL models (4B Thinking and 8B Thinking). Training was conducted under three input conditions: the SPaRC text baseline, the Original visual board, and the fully scaffolded Text Symbols variant. The reward signal was based on whether the predicted path satisfied all validity conditions. This aimed to determine how input format influences the effectiveness of RL post-training.

#### 3.6. Evaluated Models and Hardware

Seven open-weight VLMs were evaluated, including Mistral Small 3.2, Gemma 3 27B, Llama 4 Scout, GLM 4.6V, Gemma 4 31B, Qwen 3.5 27B, and Qwen 3.5 397B. These models span dense and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, with parameter counts ranging from 24B to 397B. All inferences were performed on 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs (80GB VRAM), with 4-bit weight quantization applied to the largest models (Qwen 3.5 397B and GLM 4.6V) to fit VRAM constraints. GRPO training utilized up to four nodes, each with four A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings concerning the influence of visual scaffolding on VLM performance in spatial reasoning tasks.

#### 4.1. Effect of Visual Scaffolding on Task Solving

- **Significant Accuracy Improvements:** Input-side visual scaffolding consistently and substantially improved task-solving accuracy across most evaluated VLMs. For instance, the Qwen 3.5 397B model's accuracy increased by 34.0 percentage points from 14.4% on the Original board to 48.4% with Text Symbols. Similarly, Qwen 3.5 27B improved from 8.2% to 40.0% with Cell Coordinates.
- **Model-Dependent Gains:** Stronger frontier-scale models benefited most from perceptual support, suggesting that a certain level of inherent reasoning capacity is required to leverage the improved visual input. Weaker models (e.g., Gemma 3 27B, Mistral Small 3.2) showed negligible gains, remaining below 1% accuracy.
- **Outperformance of Text Baseline:** The best scaffolded visual inputs for stronger models (e.g., Qwen 3.5 397B achieving 48.4% with Text Symbols) exceeded the performance on the SPaRC text-formulation baseline (38.0% for the same model), indicating that text is not necessarily an upper bound for reasoning ability on this task when visual presentation is optimized.
- **Difficulty Scaling:** Even with scaffolding, accuracy decreased monotonically as puzzle difficulty increased for all models, highlighting that perceptual support aids but does not eliminate the need for complex planning, particularly on harder instances.

#### 4.2. Effect of Visual Scaffolding on Perceptual Recognition

- **Improved Rule Detection:** Scaffolded inputs generally enhanced rule symbol recognition. For example, Gemma 4 31B's average rule accuracy increased from 85.4% on the Original board to 97.0% with Text Symbols, and Qwen 3.5 397B improved from 87.3% to 98.1% with Cell Coordinates.
- **Reduced Confusion:** Analysis of per-type confusion patterns (e.g., for Qwen 3.5 397B) revealed that on the Original board, structural elements like Start, End, and Gap cells were frequently misclassified as "Path." Text Symbols significantly reduced these confusions, leading to near-perfect detection for many symbol types, demonstrating that explicit rendering clarifies ambiguous visual input.
- **Strong Correlation with Task Accuracy:** A strong positive correlation (r = 0.96, R² = 0.92, p < 0.001) was observed between exact-match board detection accuracy and end-to-end task-solving accuracy across models and board representations. This suggests that improved perceptual grounding is a significant mediator of the observed gains in planning accuracy.

#### 4.3. Path-Level Error Analysis

- **Grounding-Related Error Reduction:** The most significant improvements in path-level validity were seen in "Correct Start/End" and "Fully Valid" metrics. On the Original board, only 11.8% of paths connected the correct endpoints, and 8.9% were fully valid. With Cell Coordinates and Text Symbols, "Correct Start/End" reached approximately 90%, and "Fully Valid" rose to about 48%.
- **Persistent Reasoning Challenges:** Metrics such as "Non-Intersecting" remained high and largely unchanged across conditions (84.0–88.1%). Crucially, "No Rule Violation" (checking paths avoiding rule cells) showed no clear trend and remained a challenge, fluctuating between 60.0% and 68.1%. This indicates that while scaffolding effectively addresses endpoint and path connectivity issues (grounding), rule satisfaction (downstream reasoning) remains comparatively difficult.

#### 4.4. Recovery from Visual Degradation

- **Resilience to Basic Degradations:** Overlaying Cell Coordinates on boards with Low Contrast or Low Resolution largely recovered task-solving performance, with only marginal accuracy drops compared to clean Cell Coordinates inputs. This supports the hypothesis that explicit spatial scaffolding can restore impaired visual grounding.
- **Vulnerability to Rotation:** Rotation proved more disruptive. Even with Cell Coordinates overlaid, performance dropped more significantly, suggesting that orientation changes introduce spatial alignment challenges that the current scaffolding cannot fully mitigate. Object detection results confirmed this, with rule detection for Cell Coordinates degrading sharply under rotation.

#### 4.5. Effect on GRPO Post-Training

- **Enhanced Trainability with Scaffolding:** GRPO post-training showed near-zero accuracy gains on the Original visual input. In contrast, GRPO yielded the largest absolute accuracy improvements on the Text Symbols scaffolded input (e.g., +3.8 percentage points for Qwen 3 VL 4B Thinking), surpassing gains observed on the text-only baseline. This indicates that visual scaffolding makes task-relevant structure accessible, allowing reinforcement learning to exploit it effectively, thereby improving the reasoning policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this research offer significant implications for the understanding, development, and evaluation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

**Redefining VLM Failure Modes:** The study empirically demonstrates that visual presentation fundamentally changes both VLM task accuracy and the distribution of their failure modes, even when the underlying reasoning problem is held constant. This clarifies that apparent multimodal reasoning failures can often be attributed to limitations in visual grounding—how visual information is perceived and interpreted—rather than solely to deficits in downstream logical reasoning. This challenges the common assumption that poor performance on visual tasks primarily reflects a lack of planning ability.

**Separating Perception from Reasoning:** By progressively enhancing perceptual support through visual scaffolds, the research provides a methodology for diagnostically separating grounding-sensitive errors from residual reasoning challenges. The improvements in endpoint localization and path connectivity under scaffolding, contrasted with the persistent difficulty in rule satisfaction, highlight distinct areas of VLM capability and weakness. This distinction is crucial for targeted improvements.

**Implications for VLM Benchmarking:** The findings suggest that future benchmarks for multimodal planning and reasoning should not only control the inherent complexity of the reasoning problem but also carefully consider and control the perceptual interface through which that problem is presented. Different visual representations can lead to different measurements of "ability," making it difficult to compare models or assess true reasoning capacity if visual grounding is a confounding factor. Benchmarks could benefit from including scaffolded variants to offer a clearer view of a model's pure reasoning skills.

#### 5.1. Guiding VLM Development

- **Visual Encoders:** The large gains from scaffolding point to a need for more robust, coordinate- or layout-aware visual encoders that can explicitly capture and represent spatial structure, even under visual degradation. This could involve incorporating inductive biases that make spatial indexing more explicit within the visual processing pipeline.
- **Training Methodologies:** The increased effectiveness of GRPO post-training on scaffolded inputs suggests that optimizing visual input can unlock the potential of reinforcement learning or other fine-tuning techniques for improving VLM reasoning policies. This implies that model training should not solely focus on complex reasoning algorithms but also on making the visual input as interpretable as possible for the model.
- **Search-Based Decoding:** The persistence of rule-satisfaction errors, even with explicit visual cues, indicates that there remain challenges in long-horizon planning and logical constraint satisfaction. This suggests that future research could focus on planning-aware post-training or integrating search-based decoding mechanisms to improve adherence to complex rules.

**Generalizability of Experimental Logic:** While the specific empirical claims are restricted to the SPaRC benchmark and its grid-based regularity, the underlying experimental logic—systematically intervening on the input, holding the task fixed, and observing shifts in error profiles—is portable. This diagnostic approach can be applied to other multimodal tasks and domains (e.g., natural image understanding, robotics) to uncover specific perceptual bottlenecks or reasoning limitations.

In summary, this work underscores that the visual interface is not merely a passive conduit for information but an active determinant of VLM performance and the interpretation of their capabilities. Optimizing visual presentation can significantly enhance VLM accuracy, clarify the sources of errors, and thereby inform more effective development and evaluation strategies for multimodal AI.
