# Pic2Word: Mapping Pictures to Words for Zero-shot Composed Image Retrieval

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Pic2Word, a framework that enables Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval (ZS-CIR) by mapping visual embeddings from a reference image into pseudo-language tokens compatible with a pre-trained language model. This approach facilitates multimodal query composition without triplet-labeled data, achieving competitive performance against supervised methods across diverse CIR tasks such as domain conversion and object composition.

## Problem

- High cost and effort required to collect triplet-labeled datasets (query image, text modification, target image) for supervised Composed Image Retrieval (CIR).
- Limited generalization capabilities of existing supervised CIR models, which perform poorly on tasks different from their training data.
- Inability to effectively leverage readily available large-scale, weakly labeled image-caption pairs and unlabeled image datasets for CIR.

## Method

- Developed **Pic2Word**, a two-stage framework that adapts a frozen, pre-trained ==Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)== model for zero-shot CIR.
- Introduced a lightweight ==mapping network==, fMf_MfM, trained unsupervisedly on unlabeled images to convert CLIP visual embeddings into ==pseudo-language token== embeddings.
- Composed query by inserting the pseudo-language token alongside a user's text description into a prompt, which is then processed by CLIP's language encoder for retrieval.

## Results

- Pic2Word demonstrated competitive performance against and often surpassed zero-shot baselines and several supervised methods on diverse CIR tasks, achieving **R@10 of 10.1** for domain conversion and **R@1 of 11.5** for object composition.
- The learned pseudo-language token effectively captures image information, achieving **99.8% Recall@1** for reconstructing the input image on the CC3M validation set.
- The method significantly outperforms supervised baselines when fewer than approximately **1,000** triplet-labeled training samples are available, highlighting its advantage in data-scarce scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing images as language-interpretable pseudo tokens allows pre-trained language models to effectively perform multimodal composition for image retrieval.
> - Zero-shot CIR can achieve performance comparable to or exceeding several supervised methods, demonstrating viability without expensive triplet-labeled data.
> - Adapting powerful vision-language foundation models like CLIP for new tasks, by leveraging their inherent compositional abilities, offers a path to greater generalization and data efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) is an emerging field focused on retrieving images using a multimodal query comprising both an image and a text description. This approach offers advantages over purely image-based retrieval by allowing users to specify nuanced modifications or compositional elements, leading to higher precision in retrieval. Recent advancements in large-scale image-text models have increased attention on CIR for various real-world applications, such as e-commerce product search and content creation.

Existing CIR methodologies predominantly rely on supervised learning, where models are trained using triplet-labeled datasets. These datasets consist of a query image, a text modification, and a corresponding target image. Approaches in this domain often involve "late-fusion" techniques, where visual and linguistic features are extracted independently by different encoders and then combined. While some state-of-the-art models leverage pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP as backbones, they still require a subsequent supervised fine-tuning stage on task-specific CIR datasets.

This paper positions itself by identifying two primary limitations of the current supervised CIR paradigm: the high cost of collecting triplet-labeled training data and the limited generalization capabilities of models trained on such data to diverse or unseen CIR tasks.

Within the broader landscape of vision-language foundation models (e.g., CLIP, ALIGN), which are trained on vast datasets of weakly labeled image-caption pairs, there has been a growing interest in adapting these models to new tasks with minimal or no additional annotation. These foundation models have demonstrated capabilities in zero-shot classification, few-shot learning, image captioning, and Visual Question Answering (VQA). This work extends this trend by proposing a zero-shot approach to CIR, aiming to leverage the inherent compositional abilities of pre-trained language models.

The concept of representing image regions or entire images as "word tokens" in vision-language models has been explored in prior work. However, these methods typically involve detecting objects or require fine-tuning word tokens with class-wise annotations or specific groups of images. This work differentiates itself by proposing an approach that adapts pre-trained contrastive vision-language models for zero-shot CIR without requiring object detection, class annotations, or fine-tuning of word tokens at inference time. It specifically introduces Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval (ZS-CIR) as a novel task to address the data scarcity and generalization issues inherent in conventional supervised CIR.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and develop a method for Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval (ZS-CIR). This task aims to build a CIR model that can perform diverse retrieval tasks without requiring expensive triplet-labeled training datasets.

The motivation behind pursuing ZS-CIR stems from several challenges associated with current supervised CIR methods:

- **High Cost of Data Labeling:** Existing CIR models critically depend on large volumes of triplet-labeled data, which includes a reference image, a text description, and a target image. The collection of such datasets involves a two-stage process: first, identifying pairs of related reference and target images, and then soliciting human descriptions that articulate the modification needed to transform the reference into the target. Both steps are resource-intensive and incur significant labeling costs, thereby limiting the scalability and broad applicability of CIR systems.
- **Limited Model Generalization:** Models trained on specific supervised CIR datasets tend to be specialized for the particular use cases they were trained on (e.g., fashion attribute manipulation). Consequently, these models often exhibit poor generalization performance when applied to different CIR tasks, such as object composition or domain conversion. This lack of generalization necessitates training a new model for each distinct CIR task, which is impractical for real-world deployment across varied applications.
- **Leveraging Weakly Labeled and Unlabeled Data:** To circumvent the need for expensive triplet annotations, the research is motivated by the potential to utilize more abundant and cheaper data sources. These include large-scale image-caption pairs and unlabeled image datasets, which are increasingly available. By developing a method that can learn from such weakly supervised or unsupervised data, the overhead of data collection can be significantly reduced.
- **Harnessing Pre-trained Vision-Language Model Capabilities:** Pre-trained vision-language models, such as CLIP, have demonstrated a strong ability to align image and text embeddings and possess inherent linguistic compositional capabilities. The motivation is to leverage these capabilities, particularly the language encoder's proficiency in composing diverse concepts and attributes, to perform CIR. The core idea is to seamlessly integrate image information into the language space, allowing the language encoder to process combined image-text queries effectively.

By addressing these motivations, the project seeks to enable a single, generalized CIR model that is robust across various tasks and economically feasible to train, moving beyond the limitations of task-specific supervised models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named Pic2Word, introduces a two-stage framework designed to enable Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval (ZS-CIR) without requiring triplet-labeled training data. It primarily leverages a pre-trained Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) model.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)

The method begins by utilizing a frozen, pre-trained CLIP model. CLIP consists of a vision encoder ($f_\theta$) and a language encoder ($f_\phi$), along with a word embedding layer ($E_w$). These components are jointly trained on vast image-caption datasets to align visual representations ($\tilde{v}$) and linguistic representations ($\tilde{u}$) in a shared embedding space. The training objective is a symmetric multi-class N-pair contrastive loss, which maximizes the similarity between an image and its paired caption while minimizing similarity with unpaired samples. The output embeddings are normalized to unit L2-norm for similarity comparison.

#### 3.2. Learning the Pic2Word Mapping Network

The core of Pic2Word involves training a lightweight mapping network, $f_M$, which converts an input visual embedding from the frozen CLIP vision encoder into a pseudo-language token embedding. This process is designed such that the pseudo token is compatible with and interpretable by the frozen CLIP language encoder.

- **Mapping Network Architecture:** $f_M$ is implemented as a three-layered fully-connected network with ReLU activations, except for the output layer, containing approximately 0.8 million parameters.
- **Training Objective:** Given an unnormalized visual embedding $\tilde{v}$ from the frozen vision encoder, $f_M$ generates a pseudo-language token embedding $s = f_M(\tilde{v})$. This token $s$ is then appended to a generic textual prompt, such as "a photo of," to form a composite token embedding $\hat{s}$. This $\hat{s}$ is fed into the frozen CLIP language encoder $f_\phi$ to produce a language embedding $\tilde{p}$.
- **Loss Function:** The mapping network $f_M$ is trained to minimize a contrastive loss between the normalized image embedding $v$ (from the original image) and the normalized language embedding $p$ (derived from the pseudo token). This loss function is structurally similar to CLIP's contrastive loss but is optimized solely for the parameters of $f_M$, with all other CLIP components (vision encoder, language encoder, word embeddings) kept frozen. This process enables $f_M$ to learn to reconstruct the visual representation in the language embedding space.
- **Training Data:** Crucially, this training stage for the mapping network is unsupervised, requiring only unlabeled images (specifically, images from the Conceptual Caption dataset are used), without the need for image-text pairs or any labeled image data beyond what was used for initial CLIP pre-training.

This approach facilitates "early-fusion" in the language token space, allowing the pre-trained language model to leverage its compositional capacity effectively, unlike traditional "late-fusion" methods that combine features after independent extraction. The method represents each image as a single word token, although the authors note potential for extension to multiple tokens.

#### 3.3. Inference

During inference, the goal is to compose an image and a text query to retrieve relevant candidate images.

- **Pseudo Token Generation:** For a given query image, its visual embedding is passed through the trained mapping network $f_M$ to generate the pseudo-language token embedding.
- **Prompt Composition:** This pseudo token is then inserted into a pre-defined prompt template alongside the user-provided text description. The choice of prompt template depends on the specific CIR task:
 - **Domain Conversion:** E.g., "[domain] of [*]", where `[*]` represents the pseudo token and `[domain]` is the target domain (e.g., "origami").
 - **Object/Scene Composition:** E.g., "a photo of [*], [obj1] and [obj2]", where `[*]` is the pseudo token and `[obj_i]` are textual descriptions of other objects or scenes.
 - **Sentence Specification:** E.g., "a photo of [*], [text]", where `[text]` is a modifying sentence.
- **Query Feature Generation:** The complete composite prompt (containing the pseudo token) is fed into the frozen CLIP text encoder to produce the final query feature.
- **Retrieval:** This query feature is then compared (using cosine similarity) against the visual features of a database of candidate images (extracted by the frozen CLIP vision encoder) to retrieve the most similar images. The prompt design is noted as influential on performance, with simple prompts used without further tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations of Pic2Word across various Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval (ZS-CIR) tasks, comparing it against zero-shot baselines and several supervised CIR methods.

#### 4.1. General Performance Across Diverse CIR Tasks

Pic2Word demonstrates generalization capabilities across different CIR tasks, including domain conversion, object composition, scene manipulation, and fashion attribute manipulation. The method generally achieves performance comparable to or exceeding several supervised CIR methods, despite being trained without triplet-labeled data.

#### 4.2. Domain Conversion (ImageNet)

In the domain conversion task using ImageNet and ImageNet-R datasets (evaluating conversion to 'cartoon', 'origami', 'toy', and 'sculpture' domains), Pic2Word significantly outperforms all zero-shot baselines and the supervised Combiner models trained on other CIR datasets (CIRR and Fashion-IQ). For example, Pic2Word achieved an average R@10 of 10.1 and R@50 of 23.2, whereas the best supervised baseline (CIRR Combiner) achieved 8.0 and 18.5, respectively. This indicates the effectiveness of Pic2Word in combining domain-specific text with image features.

#### 4.3. Object Composition (COCO)

For object composition on the COCO dataset, Pic2Word surpasses the zero-shot baselines, achieving R@1 of 11.5, R@5 of 24.8, and R@10 of 33.4. It performed better than a supervised Combiner model trained on CIRR (9.9 R@1) but was outperformed by a Combiner model trained on Fashion-IQ (13.2 R@1). This suggests that supervised models can learn dataset-specific biases, which may not transfer well across different CIR tasks.

#### 4.4. Scene Manipulation (CIRR) and Fashion Attribute Manipulation (Fashion-IQ)

On the CIRR and Fashion-IQ benchmarks, Pic2Word outperforms all zero-shot baselines and some supervised approaches.
- **CIRR (Scene Manipulation):** Pic2Word achieved R@1 of 23.9, R@5 of 51.7, and R@10 of 65.3, outperforming the zero-shot baselines and several supervised methods like CIRR TIRG (14.6 R@1) and CIRR ARTEMIS (17.0 R@1). However, it was slightly outperformed by the optimally tuned CIRR Combiner (30.3 R@1).
- **Fashion-IQ (Fashion Attribute Manipulation):** Pic2Word scored R@10 of 24.7 and R@50 of 43.7 on average across clothing categories. While outperforming most zero-shot baselines and some supervised methods like CIRPLANT (18.9 R@10), it was surpassed by the Fashion-IQ Combiner (35.6 R@10) and ALTEMIS (26.1 R@10), both of which are supervised methods specifically trained on Fashion-IQ.
The results on CIRR and Fashion-IQ indicate that captions alone can be informative, and some supervised models learn to prioritize text or disregard irrelevant reference images, highlighting dataset-specific biases that zero-shot methods do not explicitly learn.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Pseudo-Language Token Information Content

An evaluation on the CC3M validation set demonstrated that the language embedding generated by the pseudo token effectively captures image information. The pseudo token's ability to reconstruct the input image achieved a Recall@1 of 99.8% and Recall@5 of 100%, indicating that it represents unique image features with high fidelity.

#### 4.6. Insights into Existing Composed Retrieval Datasets

The study found that the optimal interpolation weight for combining text and image features (as used in some baselines) is highly dependent on the dataset. This suggests that supervised models can learn these dataset-specific biases in feature weighting, which is a challenge for zero-shot methods.

#### 4.7. Comparison with Supervised Baselines Under Data Scarcity

When comparing Pic2Word to the supervised Combiner baseline with varying amounts of training data, Pic2Word generally outperforms the supervised baseline when fewer than approximately 1,000 triplet-labeled samples are available for the supervised model. This highlights the advantage of Pic2Word in low-data regimes.

#### 4.8. Impact of Mapping Network Training Data

Training the mapping network with 50% of the Conceptual Caption 3 Million (CC3M) dataset yielded performance comparable to using the full CC3M dataset, while using only 10% led to performance degradation. Using a larger dataset, CC12M, did not provide a clear advantage over CC3M, suggesting CC3M is sufficient for training the mapping network.

#### 4.9. Mapping Network Architecture Design

The analysis of the mapping network architecture indicated that a certain level of expressiveness, including non-linear activation functions, is necessary. A purely linear mapping network exhibited significantly reduced performance, underscoring the need for sufficient complexity to faithfully predict pseudo-language tokens.

#### 4.10. Failure Cases

The method experienced difficulties with sketch-to-natural image retrieval tasks. While successful in converting non-natural domains (e.g., real image to sculpture), retrieving natural images from sketch inputs was challenging. This is attributed to natural images not typically being described with explicit domain words in pre-training datasets, unlike domains such as "art" or "sketch."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Pic2Word and Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval (ZS-CIR) presents several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity in CIR:** The most immediate impact is on overcoming the significant hurdle of data acquisition for Composed Image Retrieval. By demonstrating that effective CIR models can be trained using only readily available image-caption pairs and unlabeled images, Pic2Word bypasses the expensive and labor-intensive process of generating triplet-labeled datasets. This makes advanced image retrieval techniques more accessible and broadly applicable, particularly for domains where annotated data is scarce or costly to obtain.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The development of a single model capable of performing diverse CIR tasks (e.g., domain conversion, object composition, attribute editing) without task-specific fine-tuning marks a step towards more generalized and flexible image retrieval systems. This reduces the need for developing and maintaining multiple specialized models, streamlining development and deployment processes.
- **Maximizing Utility of Vision-Language Foundation Models:** This work illustrates an effective strategy for adapting powerful pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP to complex downstream tasks in a zero-shot manner. By treating images as language tokens, Pic2Word leverages the inherent compositional strengths of these foundation models, extending their utility beyond their original pre-training objectives without extensive additional supervision.
- **Introduction of a New Research Paradigm:** The formal introduction and empirical validation of Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval as a task opens a new direction for research in multimodal retrieval. It encourages the development of novel methodologies that prioritize learning from weak supervision or unsupervised data, shifting focus away from reliance on expensive, domain-specific labeled datasets.
- **Practical Applications Across Industries:** The improved accessibility and generalization of CIR can have practical implications in various sectors:
 - **E-commerce:** Users could more precisely articulate fashion modifications ("longer sleeves and black") or object compositions ("a mouse, laptop, remote, bed") to find desired products, enhancing search relevance.
 - **Content Creation and Editing:** Artists and designers could use image-text queries to explore stylistic variations or scene manipulations, such as "an origami of [query image]" or "a photo of [query image] in the pool," facilitating creative workflows.
 - **Internet Search:** Enhanced search capabilities could allow users to refine image searches with highly specific visual and textual criteria, leading to more accurate results.
- **Methodological Innovation:** The "Pic2Word" mechanism itself, which involves learning a mapping network to convert visual embeddings into pseudo-language tokens for early-fusion within the language encoder, represents a novel approach to multimodal composition. This technique could inspire similar strategies for integrating diverse modalities into powerful pre-trained language models for other complex tasks.
