# ACE-Ego-0: Unifying Egocentric Human and Robotic Data for VLA Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> The ACE-Ego-0 framework unifies diverse egocentric human videos and robot demonstrations for Vision-Language-Action pretraining, employing a reliability-aware approach to effectively leverage noisy human pseudo-actions. This method demonstrated improved performance on both simulation benchmarks and real-world bimanual robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- The acquisition of large-scale and diverse robot demonstration data is costly and labor-intensive, limiting the scalability and generalizability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Integrating heterogeneous data sources like egocentric human videos and robot demonstrations is challenging due to discrepancies in spatial representations, embodiment structures, and temporal dynamics.
- Actions inferred from human videos ('pseudo-actions') are inherently noisy compared to sensor-logged robot data, potentially degrading policy learning if treated equivalently.

## Method

- A ==Unified Action Representation== projects both robot and human actions into a canonical head-camera coordinate frame, using morphology conditioning and time-aligned action chunking to normalize various data conventions.
- A Reliability-Aware Training Objective employs spatiotemporal weighting for action dimensions and separate primary/auxiliary losses to robustly integrate high-fidelity robot data with noisy human pseudo-actions.
- A multi-stage egocentric video-to-action conversion pipeline processes human videos to generate pseudo-action labels, including 3D hand reconstruction, action parameterization, and extensive quality control filters.

## Results

- ACE-Ego-0 achieved **72.8%** average success rate on the RoboCasa GR1 TableTop benchmark and **91.12%** on RoboTwin **2.0** Easy/Clean setting, outperforming comparative baselines.
- On a real-world ARX bimanual platform, the framework attained an average success rate of **78.3%**, demonstrating a **6.6%** improvement over fine-tuned π0.5 and strong transfer capabilities.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the reliability-aware human auxiliary loss was critical, and human data augmentation in data-scarce scenarios yielded a **4x** improvement in success rate for specific tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effectively leveraging large-scale egocentric human videos, which are cheaper and more diverse, can overcome the robot data bottleneck and enhance VLA model scalability.
> - Explicitly modeling and accounting for data heterogeneity (spatial, structural, temporal) and supervision quality (reliable robot vs. noisy human) is crucial for successful joint training.
> - The reliability-aware training approach allows noisy human data to provide beneficial auxiliary supervision without corrupting the learning of robust control policies from high-fidelity robot data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robotic systems capable of operating across diverse real-world environments is a central objective in embodied AI. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a promising approach to this goal by integrating perception, language, and action into a unified framework. A key challenge in scaling VLA models is the acquisition of large-scale and diverse embodied data. While prior efforts, such as RT-1, RT-2, and the Open X-Embodiment initiative, have demonstrated the benefits of large robot datasets, collecting high-fidelity robot demonstrations remains costly and labor-intensive, thereby limiting both dataset size and the diversity of observed behaviors.

Consequently, researchers have explored leveraging large-scale egocentric human videos as a complementary data source due to their lower collection cost and broader coverage of real-world interactions. However, integrating these heterogeneous data sources poses several challenges. Existing cross-embodiment VLA methods, which primarily rely on teleoperated robot demonstrations, address some aspects of representation heterogeneity through shared action spaces, embodiment-specific tokenizers, or latent action representations. However, they often fall short in comprehensively addressing discrepancies across spatial representations, embodiment structures, and temporal dynamics. Furthermore, when human videos are used, action labels are typically extracted via vision-based pipelines, resulting in "pseudo-actions" that are inherently noisy compared to sensor-logged robot data. Previous methods often treat these pseudo-actions equivalently to robot data, potentially introducing noise and degrading policy learning.

ACE-Ego-0 addresses these dual challenges of comprehensive representation heterogeneity and supervision quality mismatch, aiming to unify diverse robot and human egocentric data within a single pretraining framework. It positions itself as an advancement in VLA pretraining by systematically aligning heterogeneous data and explicitly accounting for the varying fidelity of supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified VLA pretraining framework that can effectively leverage both large-scale robot demonstrations and egocentric human videos. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming the Robot Data Bottleneck:** The scalability and diversity of robot data collection are fundamental limitations for developing general-purpose VLA models. Collecting demonstrations is expensive and time-consuming, hindering the creation of datasets large enough to achieve broad generalization comparable to foundation models in other domains.
2. **Harnessing Egocentric Human Video:** Egocentric human videos offer a cost-effective and abundant source of diverse real-world manipulation experience. These videos capture a wider range of skills and environments than typically found in robot datasets. The motivation is to unlock this vast resource for VLA model pretraining.
3. **Addressing Data Heterogeneity:** Jointly training on human and robot data is challenging due to inherent discrepancies across data sources. These include:
 - **Spatial Differences:** Robot end-effector poses are recorded in various robot-centric or world-centric coordinate frames, while human hand poses may be in local hand space or subject to different reconstruction conventions.
 - **Structural Differences:** Robots have distinct kinematic chains, joint limits, and physical dimensions (embodiment structures), which differ significantly from human morphology.
 - **Temporal Differences:** Data can be collected at varying control frequencies, meaning a fixed number of future steps may correspond to different physical durations across datasets.
 - **Supervision Quality Differences:** Sensor-logged robot actions provide high-fidelity supervision, whereas actions inferred from human videos (pseudo-actions) are prone to tracking errors, occlusions, and estimation biases, resulting in inherently noisy labels.
4. **Developing a Robust Pretraining Framework:** The goal is to create a framework that can not only align heterogeneous data representations but also robustly integrate noisy human pseudo-action supervision without degrading the learning of robust control policies from high-fidelity robot data.

In summary, the core motivation is to achieve more generalizable VLA policies by enabling effective, scalable pretraining across highly diverse and heterogeneous embodied data sources, with a specific focus on mitigating the challenges posed by representation misalignment and varying supervision fidelity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ACE-Ego-0 addresses the challenges of representation heterogeneity and supervision-quality mismatch through a two-fold framework: a Unified Action Representation and a Reliability-Aware Training Objective, complemented by a scalable egocentric video-to-action conversion pipeline.

#### 3.1. Unified Action Representation

To enable joint training on diverse robot trajectories and human videos, ACE-Ego-0 constructs a shared action interface that normalizes spatial, structural, and temporal conventions across datasets.

- **Canonical Action Space Construction:** Actions from both robot and human data are projected into the head-camera coordinate frame. This approach aligns actions and observations in a unified coordinate system, simplifying policy learning and enabling transfer to new embodiments by only updating a camera extrinsic.
 - **Robot Action Convention:** For robots, bimanual end-effector poses (position and 6D continuous orientation) are transformed from their source frame into the head-camera frame. This is combined with gripper commands and arm activity flags to form a unified action vector.
 - **Human End-Effector Equivalents:** For human hands, the wrist joint is designated as the end-effector origin. A stable hand-centric orientation frame is constructed using the palm plane and wrist-to-finger vectors, then converted to the 6D representation. Gripper openness is approximated by the normalized thumb-to-palm distance. This standardization allows human motions to be represented in a robot-compatible, 22-dimensional bimanual action vector.
- **Cross-Embodiment Morphology Conditioning:** To account for varying kinematic structures, the action expert is conditioned on a morphology token. For robots, this token is dynamically computed from its URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) graph via a joint-centric graph neural network encoder. For human videos, a learned surrogate embedding is used, capturing visual and dataset-specific priors. These morphology tokens are injected only during action decoding, keeping the VLM backbone embodiment-agnostic.
- **Time-Aligned Action Chunking:** Datasets with different control frequencies are unified by defining action chunks based on a target physical duration (T*) rather than a fixed number of frames. The step horizon H_d for each dataset d is calculated as `round(f_d * T*)`. This ensures temporal consistency. A structured batch sampling strategy groups mini-batches by task cluster, normalized episode phase, and horizon bucket to minimize padding overhead and stabilize training.

#### 3.2. Reliability-Aware Training Objective

To prevent noisy human pseudo-actions from degrading the policy, a reliability-aware training objective is introduced.

- **Spatiotemporal Reliability Weighting:** A weight `W_{t,j}` is defined for each action dimension `j` at step `t`, factoring into a static channel-level prior `ρ_j` (reflecting intrinsic tracking stability, e.g., position channels get higher weight) and a dynamic step-level weight `w_{t,j}`. The `w_{t,j}` further decomposes into a dataset-level prior `w_data` and a local step-level smoothness factor `w_step` (attenuating implausible kinematic jumps).
- **Robot Primary Loss:** High-fidelity robot data supervise the primary action expert using a standard conditional flow-matching formulation. This objective focuses on predicting the velocity field from a noisy action interpolant to a clean action target.
- **Human Auxiliary Loss:** Noisy human pseudo-actions contribute through a robust auxiliary loss based on Huber regression. This loss is scaled by `W_{t,j}`, concentrating supervision on reliable position channels and safely discounting noisy rotation and gripper signals. Before loss calculation, human action targets are smoothed to suppress high-frequency pose jitter.
- **Joint Training Objective:** The overall loss is a weighted combination of the robot primary loss and the human auxiliary loss, balancing their contributions.

#### 3.3. Heterogeneous Pretraining Data and Egocentric Video-to-Action Conversion

ACE-Ego-0's pretraining pool comprises over 6.0K hours of data, including sensor-logged multi-embodiment robot demonstrations, simulation rollouts, and 1.48K hours of pseudo-action-labeled egocentric human videos.

- **Robot and Simulation Data:** Includes AgiBot, Galaxea, RoboCasa, and self-collected Galbot data, covering humanoid, single-arm, and mobile bimanual embodiments with varying control frequencies and reference frames. These provide high-fidelity supervision.
- **Human Egocentric Videos:** Sourced from Ego4D, EgoExo4D, EPIC-KITCHENS-100, HOI4D, EgoDex, and Xperience-10M. These are processed through a five-stage pipeline to generate pseudo-action labels:
 1. **Dataset Curation:** Selection of egocentric video datasets with diverse real-world interaction scenes and action-centric captions, followed by standardization and clip splitting (4-30s duration).
 2. **Video Selection:** An ego-interaction filter removes clips with limited human-object interaction (e.g., strong face detections indicating non-egocentric views) and an image captioning-based filter retains clips with manipulation verbs and manipulable object nouns.
 3. **3D Hand Reconstruction:** Involves 2D tracking (SAM3-based) for consistent hand bounding boxes, per-frame local MANO pose estimation (HaMeR), and a two-stage global trajectory optimization to mitigate depth ambiguity, occlusions, and temporal jitter, resulting in temporally coherent 3D hand trajectories.
 4. **Action Parameterization:** Reconstructed human hand motions are converted into the unified 22-dimensional bimanual action vector, using wrist as origin, palm-plane orientation, and normalized thumb-to-palm distance as a gripper proxy.
 5. **Quality Control:** Post-processing filters remove corrupted or implausible episodes, including:
 - **Completeness Filter:** Ensures no NaN/Inf values, contiguous frames, and valid quaternion normalization.
 - **Static Filter:** Discards episodes with insufficient hand motion.
 - **Spike Filter:** Rejects trajectories with anomalous inter-frame positional changes, indicative of tracking failures.
 - **Bimanual Filter:** Removes episodes with implausible dual-arm behaviors based on inter-hand distance or correlation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ACE-Ego-0 was evaluated on two simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa GR1 TableTop and RoboTwin 2.0) and a real-world bimanual robotic platform (ARX), demonstrating consistent performance improvements.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **RoboCasa GR1 TableTop:** On this 24-task humanoid tabletop benchmark, ACE-Ego-0 achieved an average success rate of 72.8%, surpassing all compared baselines including DIAL (70.2%), JoyAI-RA (63.2%), ABot-M0 (58.3%), and FLARE (55.0%). The improvements were observed across both articulated-object interaction and pick-and-place tasks.
- **RoboTwin 2.0:** On this bimanual tabletop manipulation benchmark with 50 tasks and strong domain randomization, ACE-Ego-0 attained average success rates of 91.12% on the Easy/Clean setting and 90.62% on the Hard/Randomized setting. This performance exceeded JoyAI-RA (90.48% Easy, 89.28% Hard) and other methods like Hy-VLA, ABot-M0, LingBot-VLA, Motus, and π
 0.5
 .

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Evaluation

On an ARX bimanual platform performing six manipulation tasks, ACE-Ego-0 demonstrated strong transfer capabilities:

- **Overall Performance:** ACE-Ego-0 achieved an average success rate of 78.3%, outperforming fine-tuned π
 0.5
 (71.7%) by 6.6% and GR00T-N1.7 (35.6%) by a substantial margin.
- **Task-Specific Performance:** It showed particular strength in tasks requiring tight spatiotemporal coordination, such as "Scoop Coffee" (86.7% success vs. 70.0% for π
 0.5
 and 36.7% for GR00T-N1.7), and consistent performance in multi-class object placement like "Category Sorting" (90.0% vs. 80.0% for π
 0.5
 and 83.3% for GR00T-N1.7). Performance on long-horizon tasks like "Pack Shoes" showed a general degradation across all models, indicating persistent challenges in managing compounding trajectory drift.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Component Ablation (on RoboCasa GR1 TableTop):**
 - Removing **morphology tokens** decreased success rate from 72.8% to 70.9% (−1.9%), indicating their role in providing kinematic information across different robot platforms.
 - Removing **time-aligned action chunking** reduced success rate to 71.7% (−1.1%), highlighting the importance of temporal consistency.
 - Removing the **reliability-aware human auxiliary loss** resulted in the largest drop to 69.2% (−3.6%), confirming that explicitly weighting noisy pseudo-actions is crucial for effective joint training.
- **Data Source Ablation (on RoboCasa GR1 TableTop):**
 - A Qwen-initialized model without embodied pretraining achieved 65.4% success.
 - Adding **robot data only** (Robot Only) improved performance to 68.3% (+2.9%).
 - Adding **human videos** on top of robot data (Robot + Human, full ACE-Ego-0) further increased success to 72.8% (+4.5%), demonstrating the complementary behavioral coverage provided by human data.

#### 4.4. Human Data for Augmented Fine-Tuning

In a data-scarce scenario for the "Sweep Cubes" task, fine-tuning with only 34 robot demonstrations (0.062 m² end-effector workspace coverage) yielded a 10% success rate. Augmenting the fine-tuning mixture with 419 episodes of human video (4.8x broader workspace coverage at 0.296 m²) increased the success rate to 40%, demonstrating a 4x improvement and validating the value of human data in improving task-specific adaptation under limited robot data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ACE-Ego-0 represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied AI, particularly in the domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key advancements:

1. **Enabling Scalable VLA Pretraining:** The framework provides a method to move beyond the limitations of costly and labor-intensive robot data collection by effectively integrating large-scale egocentric human videos. This ability to leverage a vast, cheaper, and more diverse data source is critical for scaling VLA models to achieve broader generalizability, akin to the scaling trends observed in large language and vision models.
2. **Comprehensive Heterogeneity Resolution:** ACE-Ego-0 systematically addresses multiple dimensions of data heterogeneity—spatial, structural, and temporal—through its unified action representation. By converting all actions to a camera-space coordinate frame, incorporating morphology conditioning for both robots and humans, and employing time-aligned action chunking, the framework establishes a robust common ground for diverse embodied experiences. This holistic approach facilitates more effective joint training across vastly different platforms.
3. **Robust Learning from Noisy Data:** The introduction of a reliability-aware training objective is a significant advancement for integrating human pseudo-action labels. By explicitly accounting for the varying fidelity of supervision, ACE-Ego-0 allows noisy human data to provide beneficial auxiliary supervision without corrupting the high-fidelity control policies learned from sensor-logged robot data. This mechanism enhances the robustness and practical utility of learning from less-than-perfect real-world human demonstrations.
4. **Improved Performance and Transferability:** The consistent state-of-the-art performance demonstrated on challenging simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa, RoboTwin 2.0) and strong transfer capabilities to real-world bimanual manipulation tasks on an ARX platform underscore the effectiveness of the proposed framework. The improvements in tasks requiring complex bimanual coordination, long-horizon planning, and object interaction suggest that the learned policies are more robust and capable.
5. **Accelerating Robot Skill Acquisition:** The finding that human-augmented fine-tuning can substantially improve performance in data-scarce regimes highlights a direct pathway to accelerating the acquisition of new robot skills. This reduces the dependency on extensive robot-specific data collection for every new task, potentially democratizing access to advanced robotic capabilities.

The work's impact could be seen in accelerating the development of truly general-purpose robot policies that are more adaptable to new environments and tasks, by enabling them to learn from the rich and varied experiences captured in human videos. Future work extending to mobile manipulation, whole-body humanoid control, or incorporating richer sensing modalities (e.g., dexterous hands, force/torque) could further broaden its applicability.
