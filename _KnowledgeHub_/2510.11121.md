# Refining Hybrid Genetic Search for CVRP via Reinforcement Learning-Finetuned LLM

> [!summary] Summary
> A reinforcement learning framework, RFTHGS, was developed to fine-tune a 14B-parameter LLM, enabling it to autonomously generate high-performance crossover operators for the Hybrid Genetic Search (HGS) algorithm, leading to an improvement in Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP) solutions that surpasses both expert-designed operators and larger commercial LLMs.

## Problem

- Combinatorial Optimization Problems (COPs) like Vehicle Routing Problems (VRPs) are NP-hard and traditionally require extensive domain expertise and manual effort for heuristic design.
- Existing LLM-based VRP solvers often generate suboptimal or infeasible solutions and depend on large, computationally expensive general-purpose LLMs, which struggle to match the performance of specialized solvers.
- A persistent performance gap exists between LLM-driven approaches and advanced conventional or deep learning-based solvers for large-scale combinatorial optimization problems.

## Method

- **RFTHGS**, an iterative reinforcement learning framework, was introduced to fine-tune a smaller, open-source LLM (Qwen-14B) specifically for generating effective crossover operators within the ==Hybrid Genetic Search== (HGS) algorithm for CVRP.
- A multi-tiered, curriculum-based reward function was designed, progressively guiding the LLM to first generate compilable and executable code, then to optimize for superior solution quality compared to expert-designed operators.
- An anti-plagiarism cache utilizing Abstract Syntax Trees (ASTs) and incremental compilation were implemented to promote operator diversity and accelerate the evaluation process during training.

## Results

- The RFTHGS-fine-tuned LLM consistently generated crossover operators that achieved superior performance on CVRPLIB benchmarks (up to 1000 nodes) compared to expert-designed operators and a diverse set of state-of-the-art baselines, including GPT-4o and advanced conventional solvers.
- RFTHGS achieved a **100%** successful compilation rate for generated operators, a notable improvement over commercial LLMs like GPT-4o, which had a **19%** compilation rate, indicating higher reliability in code generation.
- Training dynamics showed stable convergence, with generated operator performance surpassing expert-designed baselines around step 200 and further improving, validating the effectiveness of the multi-tiered curriculum learning approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Smaller, specialized LLMs, when fine-tuned via reinforcement learning with a focused objective, can generate algorithmic components that outperform those created by human experts for complex optimization tasks.
> - A progressive, multi-faceted reward system effectively trains LLMs to produce functional and high-quality code, leading to significant enhancements in solver performance.
> - The fine-tuned LLM demonstrates robust generalization capabilities, maintaining high performance on significantly larger problem instances (up to 1000 nodes) than those seen during training (n<400), and across different execution budgets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Combinatorial Optimization Problems (COPs), prevalent in logistics, scheduling, and supply chain management, are often NP-hard, making exact solutions computationally intractable for large instances. Historically, these problems have been addressed through specialized algorithms, heuristics, and metaheuristics, which typically demand substantial domain expertise and manual design efforts. The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has introduced a new paradigm, offering automated approaches to reduce reliance on manual algorithm design. This work specifically investigates the application of LLMs to Vehicle Routing Problems (VRPs), a significant subclass of COPs.

Initial explorations into using LLMs as end-to-end VRP solvers demonstrated limitations, including the generation of infeasible or suboptimal solutions, often attributed to LLM hallucinations. Consequently, a shift occurred towards integrating LLMs as intelligent operators within established optimization frameworks, such as evolutionary algorithms. In this hybrid approach, LLMs function as strategic generators or refiners within an iterative process. Prior research has explored LLMs guiding evolutionary processes (e.g., using in-context prompting for crossover and mutation), or evolutionary computation guiding LLM-generated heuristic refinement (e.g., EoH, ReEvo). Other methods, such as ARS and DRoC, prioritize general-purpose frameworks for diverse VRP variants, aiming for broader generalization by leveraging predefined structures or retrieval mechanisms to invoke external solvers.

Despite these advancements, a performance gap persists between LLM-based approaches and conventional or deep learning-based solvers, particularly for large-scale problems. This paper addresses a specific challenge within this landscape: whether smaller, specialized LLMs, when finely tuned, can optimize key components within advanced, expert-engineered solvers to achieve performance beyond what human experts have designed. This represents a frontier in neuro-symbolic optimization, moving beyond general-purpose LLM prompting towards targeted, reinforcement learning-driven fine-tuning of compact models for specific algorithmic enhancements. The work differentiates itself by focusing on a fine-tuning approach rather than relying on massive, proprietary LLMs for prompt-based heuristic generation, a common strategy in much of the existing literature.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from several limitations observed in current LLM applications for combinatorial optimization:

1. **Dependence on Large, General-Purpose Models:** State-of-the-art approaches often rely on massive, general-purpose LLMs like GPT-4, which can be computationally expensive, API-dependent, and prone to generating syntactically invalid or non-executable code. Their broad capabilities do not always translate to optimal performance in specialized algorithmic design tasks.
2. **Performance Gap with Advanced Solvers:** Despite advancements, LLM-generated solutions for VRPs frequently lag behind those produced by highly optimized conventional and deep learning-based solvers. Bridging this gap is crucial for the practical applicability of LLMs in real-world scenarios that depend on high-quality solutions.
3. **Manual Heuristic Design Challenges:** Traditional development of effective heuristics and metaheuristics for COPs requires significant domain expertise and substantial manual effort, a process that LLMs could potentially automate or enhance.
4. **Lack of Specialized Fine-tuning for Solver Components:** While prompt-based methods and co-evolutionary approaches exist, there has been limited exploration into fine-tuning smaller, open-source LLMs specifically to generate high-performance *components* within established, state-of-the-art solvers.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Demonstrate Superiority of Specialized LLMs:** To empirically show that a smaller, specialized LLM, when fine-tuned using reinforcement learning, can generate algorithmic components (specifically, crossover operators for the Hybrid Genetic Search (HGS) solver) that outperform those designed by human experts. This challenges the paradigm that only massive, general-purpose LLMs are effective for such tasks.
2. **Develop a Novel RL-based Fine-Tuning Framework (RFTHGS):** To introduce a comprehensive reinforcement learning framework for fine-tuning an LLM to autonomously generate effective crossover operators for the HGS algorithm, thereby enhancing its performance on large-scale Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problems (CVRP).
3. **Design a Multi-Tiered, Curriculum-Based Reward Function:** To create a structured reward scheme that progressively guides the LLM's learning process. This involves initially rewarding the generation of compilable code, then executable operators, and finally, components that exceed the performance of expert-designed ones based on solution quality.
4. **Implement Mechanisms for Robust Training:** To incorporate an operator caching mechanism that leverages Abstract Syntax Trees (ASTs) to deter plagiarism and promote diversity among generated operators, preventing reward hacking and encouraging exploration. Additionally, to employ incremental compilation to accelerate the evaluation process during training.
5. **Achieve High Performance and Generalization:** To evaluate the fine-tuned LLM's generated operators on standard CVRPLIB benchmarks, demonstrating substantial improvements over expert-designed operators within HGS, and exhibiting strong generalization capabilities to larger problem instances not seen during training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces RFTHGS (Reinforcement learning framework for Fine-Tuning a small LLM for Hybrid Genetic Search), an iterative, closed-loop reinforcement learning framework. This framework fine-tunes a large language model to generate crossover operators for the Hybrid Genetic Search (HGS) algorithm, aiming to improve its performance on the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP).

**Overall Workflow:**
Each iteration of RFTHGS follows a sequence:
1. **Prompt Generation:** The LLM receives a few-shot Chain of Thought (CoT) context, which includes instructions on desired operator properties (e.g., diversity, quality) and examples of existing operators, illustrating the required structure and syntax. The LLM does not access the full HGS library or specific CVRP instances during generation.
2. **Operator Generation:** Based on the prompt, the LLM generates new crossover operators. This action comprises a reasoning segment and the optimized code.
3. **Operator Evaluation:** The generated operator is integrated into the HGS library. An incremental compilation technique is used to recompile only the modified code and its dependencies, significantly reducing the computational overhead compared to full recompilation. The modified HGS solver is then tested on a fixed benchmark set of CVRP instances.
4. **Reward Calculation:** Performance metrics (compilability, executability, and solution quality improvement) are combined into a multi-faceted reward signal.
5. **LLM Refinement:** The calculated reward is used to update the LLM's policy via a reinforcement learning algorithm.

**One-Step Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) Formulation:**
The operator optimization task is formalized as a one-step POMDP:
- **State (X):** A tokenized prompt given to the LLM, comprising task instructions and examples of the target operator. The environment is partially observable as the LLM only perceives a subset (the target operator) of the full solver repository.
- **Action (Y):** A sequence of tokens generated by the LLM, consisting of a reasoning segment (between `<think>` and `</think>` tokens) and the optimized C++ code for the operator.
- **State Transition:** The process is one-step, meaning the state terminates after an action is generated, without further transitions.
- **Reward (r):** A scalar value evaluating the quality of the LLM-generated operator, detailed below.
- **Policy Network:** A large language model (specifically, Qwen-14B), parameterized by $\theta$, which defines the conditional probability distribution $p(\cdot|X)$ from which actions are sampled.

**Multi-Faceted Reward Design with Anti-Plagiarism Cache:**
A multi-tiered, curriculum-based reward function guides the LLM through progressive learning stages:
1. **Compilability Reward:** A reward of -0.8 is assigned for syntactically correct and compilable code. A penalty of -1 is given for invalid output. This stage aims to rapidly transition the LLM from generating uncompilable code.
2. **Executability Reward:** If the operator compiles, an additional reward of -0.7 is given for successful execution without runtime errors or timeouts.
3. **Performance Improvement Reward:** For compilable and executable operators, a linear reward is assigned based on their relative improvement in solution quality over a baseline expert-designed operator. This is measured on a predefined set of CVRP instances (30 instances from CVRPLIB X with at most 400 nodes, not exposed to the model during generation). The reward is calculated as $\max(-0.7, [\phi_{J}^{\text{HGS}}(o_{\text{expert}}) - \phi_{J}^{\text{HGS}}(o)] / \phi_{J}^{\text{HGS}}(o_{\text{expert}}))$, where $o$ is the generated operator, $o_{\text{expert}}$ is the baseline, and $\phi_{J}^{\text{HGS}}(\cdot)$ is the average result on $J$ random CVRP instances.

**Anti-Plagiarism Cache with Abstract Syntax Tree (AST):**
To mitigate reward hacking and encourage diversity, an operator buffer mechanism is employed. AST representations of all few-shot examples in the prompt and previously generated operators are cached. If a newly generated operator's AST substantially matches an existing one in the cache, a penalty is invoked by the reward function, discouraging direct copying and promoting exploration.

**HGS with Incremental Compilation as the Evaluator:**
To efficiently evaluate generated operators, each one is integrated into the HGS library. Incremental compilation is utilized, selectively recompiling only the modified code and its dependencies. This method reduces recompilation time to approximately 25% of compiling the entire library, significantly accelerating the training process.

**Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:**
DAPO (Dynamic Advantage Policy Optimization), an improved variant of GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), is used for training the LLM. Key adjustments of DAPO leveraged include:
- **Clip-Higher Mechanism:** Decouples the clipping range for positive and negative responses, allowing the policy to aggressively increase probabilities for promising tokens, thus promoting greater exploration.
- **Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss:** Calculates and aggregates the loss over all tokens in a batch before averaging, providing more precise updates for long reasoning chains.
- The Dynamic Sampling feature of DAPO was deprecated in this work because the continuous nature of the reward signal ensures that most sampled responses provide meaningful learning signals, unlike discrete reward settings where many samples might yield zero advantage.

**Experiment Settings:**
- The policy is initialized with the Qwen-14B reasoning LLM.
- DAPO parameters: $\epsilon_{\text{high}} = 0.28$, $\epsilon_{\text{low}} = 0.2$.
- Batch size: 16; Rollout group size: 16 (resulting in 256 operators per step).
- Training instances: A fixed set of 30 CVRP instances from CVRPLIB X with at most 400 nodes.
- Testing: 16 operators are sampled, and the best-performing one is reported. Evaluation is performed on CVRPLIB X benchmarks, including instances up to 1000 nodes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of RFTHGS in generating high-performance crossover operators for the Hybrid Genetic Search (HGS) solver.

**Performance on CVRPLIB:**
- **Superior Performance:** RFTHGS consistently outperforms a diverse set of baselines on CVRPLIB instances across various problem sizes (up to 1000 nodes). These baselines include state-of-the-art conventional solvers (HGS-PyVRP, OR-Tools, LKH), neuro-combinatorial optimization techniques (POMO, MTPOMO, MVMoE, RF-POMO, RF-MoE-L, AM, DeepACO, NeuroLKH, NeuOpt), and prompt-based methods using commercial LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-o3, GPT-o4-mini).
- **Outperforming Large Proprietary LLMs:** A significant finding is that the 14B-parameter LLM, fine-tuned by RFTHGS, surpasses trillion-parameter commercial LLMs like GPT-4o, GPT-o3, and GPT-o4-mini. While GPT models often introduce numerous modifications, these changes frequently fail to improve performance, resulting in solution costs identical to the original, unmodified operator. In contrast, RFTHGS produces targeted, effective modifications leading to consistent performance gains.
- **Compilation Rate:** RFTHGS achieves a 100% successful compilation rate (16/16 sampled operators), which significantly exceeds the rates of GPT models (e.g., GPT-4o achieved 3/16, GPT-o3 9/16, GPT-o4-mini 3/16). This indicates RFTHGS's ability to reliably generate syntactically correct and functional code.

**Generalization Performance:**
- **Problem Size Generalization:** Despite being trained exclusively on CVRP instances with fewer than 400 nodes, RFTHGS demonstrates robust generalization to significantly larger, unseen problem instances, up to 1000 nodes. This suggests the learned crossover operators are effective across different scales.
- **Iteration Generalization:** Models trained with an 800-iteration budget maintain strong performance when evaluated at higher budgets (1000 iterations), indicating that the optimized operators retain their efficacy beyond their specific training configuration. This combined generalization underscores the robustness and out-of-distribution scalability of the method.

**Learning Pattern Analysis:**
- **Stable Convergence:** The training dynamics show a stable and monotonic increase in average reward, with smooth convergence. A critical inflection point occurs around step 200, where the performance of the generated operator begins to surpass the expert-designed baseline, continuing to improve thereafter.
- **Curriculum Learning Validation:** Analysis of the reward distribution heatmap reveals a clear curriculum learning pattern. Initially, reward density is concentrated at lower values as the model learns to generate syntactically correct and compilable code. Subsequently, the distribution shifts towards intermediate rewards as operators become executable. Finally, the density migrates to the highest reward region, signifying the refinement towards operators that consistently outperform human-designed counterparts. This progression validates the effectiveness of the multi-tiered reward structure.

**Ablation Study on Reward Design:**
- An ablation study comparing the continuous reward design (Equation 3) with a discrete reward variant (Equation 4) demonstrated the clear advantage of the continuous approach. RFTHGS with continuous rewards consistently outperformed the discrete variant, especially on larger problem sizes. This outcome indicates that providing proportional feedback for performance gains is crucial for sustained refinement of the generated operators, as opposed to a binary reward where the learning signal diminishes once the baseline is surpassed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of combinatorial optimization and the application of large language models for algorithmic design.

- **Challenging the LLM Paradigm:** The work provides empirical evidence that smaller, specialized LLMs, when appropriately fine-tuned via reinforcement learning, can generate critical algorithmic components that exceed the performance of expert-engineered components within state-of-the-art solvers. This challenges the prevalent notion that only massive, general-purpose LLMs are capable of advanced reasoning and code generation for such complex tasks, suggesting a more efficient and accessible pathway for LLM integration into optimization.
- **Advancing Automated Heuristic Design:** RFTHGS offers a novel framework for automating and enhancing the design of optimization operators. By leveraging solution quality as the primary feedback signal and employing a multi-tiered reward structure, the method reduces the reliance on manual domain expertise and iterative human refinement, potentially accelerating the development of new and improved heuristics.
- **Bridging the Performance Gap:** The demonstrated ability to consistently outperform leading neuro-combinatorial baselines, prompt-based LLM methods, and even expert-designed operators in HGS, indicates a tangible step towards bridging the performance gap between LLM-generated solutions and the best conventional solvers for practical, large-scale problems.
- **Robust Generalization:** The strong generalization capabilities across problem scales (from small to 1000-node instances, despite training on instances with n < 400) suggest the practical applicability of this approach to real-world, industry-level vehicle routing problems. This out-of-distribution performance is crucial for solver development.
- **Efficient and Reliable Code Generation:** The high successful compilation rate achieved by RFTHGS, in contrast to the lower rates of general-purpose LLMs, highlights the importance of domain-specific fine-tuning for generating reliable and functional code snippets for solver integration. The use of incremental compilation further makes this process computationally feasible.
- **Future Directions:** The success with crossover operators within HGS opens avenues for exploring the fine-tuning of LLMs to evolve other operators or components within HGS, or to tackle a broader range of VRP variants and other combinatorial optimization problems. This research paves the way for a new class of LLM-enhanced optimization algorithms.
