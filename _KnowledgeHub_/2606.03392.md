# OpenEAI-Platform: An Open-source Embodied Artificial Intelligence Hardware-Software Unified Platform

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenEAI-Platform introduces an open-source hardware-software unified platform for embodied AI, featuring a low-cost 6+1 DoF robotic arm and a reproducible Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. The platform aims to enhance research accessibility and reproducibility by providing transparent designs and achieves competitive performance on real-world manipulation tasks using only open-source data.

## Problem

- High cost and limited transparency of commercial robotic arms impede widespread research and detailed hardware-software interaction studies in embodied AI.
- State-of-the-art VLA models suffer from reproducibility issues due to reliance on extensive proprietary datasets and non-public training infrastructure.
- Existing open-source robot datasets are fragmented with inconsistent action/state definitions and formats, hindering unified training efforts for generalist VLA models.

## Method

- Developed OpenEAI-Arm, a low-cost (approx. 790 USD) 6+1 DoF open-source robotic arm, optimized for manipulation operability and endurance efficiency via a multi-objective genetic algorithm.
- Introduced OpenEAI-VLA, an open-source Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model utilizing a Qwen3-VL-Instruct 4B backbone and a generative action head, trained through a two-stage recipe on open-source datasets with multimodal co-training.
- Implemented a robust control stack for OpenEAI-Arm, combining Dynamics-Compensated PID with three-point rolling Bézier action chunking, to ensure smooth and high-frequency execution of discrete VLA outputs.

## Results

- OpenEAI-Arm was built for approximately 790 USD, providing manipulation operability comparable to commercial arms costing over 8k USD, and achieving a 0.75 average task success rate when deploying a π₀ policy.
- OpenEAI-VLA, trained exclusively on open-source datasets, achieved competitive success rates across four real-world tasks (e.g., 0.94 on Clean Table, 0.83 on Fold T-shirt), matching or moderately exceeding some state-of-the-art VLA models.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the control stack's necessity, with removing dynamics compensation increasing acceleration errors by approximately 70 times, and OpenEAI-VLA's dataset adapters proved indispensable (0.0 success rate without them).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-objective optimization, balancing manipulation operability and endurance efficiency, is effective for designing low-cost, high-performance robotic arms.
> - A robust control stack, including dynamics compensation and smooth action chunking, is crucial for stable and precise execution of VLA policies on cost-effective hardware.
> - Competitive VLA policy performance can be achieved solely using open-source datasets, with dataset adapters and multimodal co-training being critical for handling data heterogeneity and preserving spatial grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) investigates the emergence of intelligent behavior from the interaction between an agent's physical body, its environment, and its control algorithms. Within this domain, hardware attributes such as sensors, actuators, and mechanical structures are considered as crucial as the learned policies that govern behavior. Recent advancements in embodied AI have been largely propelled by policy learning, with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models gaining prominence. Noteworthy examples include the Physical Intelligence series, such as π₀ and π₀.₅, which have demonstrated advanced performance.

Despite these developments, significant challenges persist in the broader research landscape. A primary barrier to reproducible research and scalable data collection in VLA systems is the reliance of state-of-the-art models, like π₀ and π₀.₅, on extensive proprietary datasets. On the hardware side, many real-world VLA deployments utilize commercial robotic arms, often with a 6 joints + 1 gripper degree-of-freedom (DoF) configuration akin to Stanford's Mobile ALOHA platform. However, the performance of these commercial arms can be limited by specific servo types or structural designs, affecting workspace and payload capacities. Furthermore, the substantial acquisition cost of commercial robotic arms restricts their large-scale deployment for data collection and hinders detailed investigation into the interplay between VLA algorithms and low-level control mechanisms. The proprietary, "black-box" nature of these arms further complicates the study of complex coupling effects. Additionally, existing open-source robot datasets often form "data islands" due to inconsistent action and state definitions and formats, impeding unified training efforts.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing OpenEAI-Platform, an integrated, fully open-source hardware-software platform. It aims to bridge the gap between advanced VLA algorithms and accessible, reproducible robotic hardware, thereby mitigating the bottlenecks associated with cost, proprietary data, and hardware-software integration that currently impede scalable and reproducible progress in real-world embodied manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of OpenEAI-Platform is to address the bottlenecks in reproducibility and data scalability within real-world embodied manipulation research. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges identified in the current state of the field:

1. **High Cost and Limited Accessibility of Robotic Hardware:** Commercial robotic arms, while offering robust performance, are often prohibitively expensive. This high cost restricts their availability for widespread academic research and limits the feasibility of deploying multiple arms for large-scale data collection. The motivation here is to develop a low-cost, yet capable, robotic manipulator that can democratize access to real-world embodied AI experimentation.
2. **Lack of Transparency in Commercial Robotic Systems:** Commercial arms are typically "black-box" platforms, exposing only high-level interfaces. This lack of access to low-level control parameters, dynamic models, and actuation stacks (e.g., torque control and feedback) makes it challenging for researchers to investigate the intricate coupling between VLA algorithms and the underlying hardware. A key motivation is to provide fully open-source hardware designs and control software to enable in-depth customization and study of these interactions.
3. **Reproducibility Crisis in VLA Model Development:** Many state-of-the-art VLA models, despite impressive performance, are difficult to reproduce. This is often due to the reliance on large-scale proprietary datasets and non-public training infrastructure or recipes. The project is motivated to create a VLA model and a complete training pipeline that is entirely based on open-source data and publicly available code, ensuring full reproducibility.
4. **Fragmentation of Open-Source Robot Datasets:** Existing open-source robot datasets often present inconsistent action and state definitions, data formats, and sensor conventions. This heterogeneity creates "data islands" that complicate efforts to leverage diverse datasets for broad pretraining of VLA models. A significant objective is to develop a unified interface and conversion pipeline for integrating these disparate datasets into a coherent format.
5. **Need for an Integrated, End-to-End Platform:** The development of embodied AI benefits from a holistic approach where hardware, low-level control, data collection, and VLA policies are considered as a unified system. The motivation is to integrate these components into a single, cohesive, open-source platform to facilitate seamless research and development from hardware design to policy deployment.

By addressing these motivations, OpenEAI-Platform aims to foster a more accessible, transparent, and reproducible research environment, thereby accelerating progress in real-world embodied manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OpenEAI-Platform integrates a low-cost 6+1 DoF robotic arm, OpenEAI-Arm, with a reproducible VLA model, OpenEAI-VLA, within a unified hardware-software framework. The methodology encompasses detailed design and control of the robotic arm, as well as a structured training approach for the VLA policy.

**OpenEAI-Arm Design:**
The robotic arm adopts a 6-DoF serial configuration, analogous to a human arm, with two DoFs at the shoulder and three at the wrist to enhance dexterity for desktop manipulation. The design process involved a multi-objective optimization problem to determine the arm's geometry, specifically its Modified Denavit-Hartenberg (MDH) parameters. This optimization utilized the NSGA-III algorithm, targeting two primary metrics:
- **Manipulation Operability (F1):** This metric quantifies the richness of the kinematic solution space in task-relevant regions. It is computed by uniformly sampling joint configurations within joint limits, mapping them to end-effector positions, and counting the number of feasible configurations falling into discretized voxels of the workspace. This "operability density" is then combined with a "task density" (derived from teleoperation trajectories, representing frequently visited regions) using a normalized, geometric-mean overlap. A higher F1 indicates more redundant solutions in high-frequency task regions, which is beneficial for complex manipulation.
- **Endurance Efficiency (F2):** This metric characterizes the long-term operational costs, such as mechanical wear and energy consumption. It models the "extent of effort" required for the arm to maintain its motion state by computing the normed joint torques for static equilibrium at various configurations. These efforts are averaged per voxel to create an endurance cost field, which is then normalized and converted into an efficiency metric. This efficiency field is combined with the task density. Higher F2 indicates lower energy cost in task-dense regions.
The optimization process was guided by standard adult anthropometric data, with decision variables including specific link lengths and gripper length. The resulting design achieved a material cost of approximately 790 USD. A two-finger parallel gripper design was also developed, featuring sharpened tips for soft objects, an S-curve profile for cylindrical objects, and a modular interface for easy substitution.

**OpenEAI-Arm Control:**
The low-level control system aims to execute the VLA model's discrete target pose sequences as smooth, continuous, and trackable joint commands.
- **Dynamics-Compensated PID (FF-PID):** To achieve reliable tracking under low-cost manufacturing conditions, a robust FF-PID strategy is employed. The feedforward torque (τ_ff) is calculated based on the Euler-Lagrange dynamic model of the arm, including inertia (M(q)¨q), Coriolis/centrifugal (C(q,˙q)˙q), and gravity (G(q)) terms. This compensates for known dynamic characteristics. The feedback torque (τ_pid) is a standard PID controller that mitigates residual tracking errors. A friction compensation term is also included to neutralize inherent friction nonlinearities.
- **Three-Point Rolling Bézier Action Chunking:** VLA models output discrete key points, which can cause discontinuities in joint velocity and acceleration. To ensure motion smoothness and satisfy actuation limits, a three-point rolling Bézier action chunking interpolation scheme is used. It interpolates between adjacent keypoints using quintic polynomials. Endpoint derivatives (velocity and acceleration) required for interpolation are estimated online using a rolling three-point finite-difference window (current, target, and subsequent target joint positions). The resulting trajectories are time-parameterized using a seven-phase S-curve profile, which enforces bounds on velocity, acceleration, and jerk to maintain smooth segment transitions.

**OpenEAI-VLA Design and Training:**
OpenEAI-VLA is an open-source VLA system designed to overcome dataset heterogeneity and reproducibility issues. It follows a two-stage training recipe:
- **Model Architecture:**
 - **Backbone VLM:** Qwen3-VL-Instruct 4B serves as the vision-language backbone.
 - **Learnable Feature Query:** Instead of feeding all VLM hidden states to the action head, a fixed-length learnable token sequence (q) is appended to the VLM input. The final-layer hidden states corresponding to this query (h_q) are extracted as compact conditioning embeddings. This mechanism creates a fixed-bandwidth bottleneck, stabilizing the VLM-to-action interface and making the action head's computation independent of input token counts.
 - **Action Head:** A generative action head predicts continuous action chunks. It is implemented as an 18-layer Diffusion-Transformer (DiT)-style decoder with 32 attention heads per layer. At each denoising step, it takes a noisy action chunk, current robot state, and the VLM-derived conditioning embeddings (h_q) to output a velocity field for conditional flow matching.
 - **Dataset Adapters:** Dataset-specific projectors align heterogeneous state and action conventions from different datasets into a unified shared space.
- **Two-Stage Training Recipe:**
 - **Pretraining Stage:** The model is pretrained on a weighted mixture of large open-source Open X-Embodiment subsets. These datasets are first converted into a unified format using a standardized preprocessing and filtering pipeline. The action expert is optimized using a π₀-style conditional flow matching objective (L_EM). Critically, the full VLM backbone is frozen during this stage, and only the learnable feature query, dataset adapters, and the DiT-based action head are trained to preserve pretrained multimodal knowledge.
 - **Task-Specific Fine-tuning Stage:** OpenEAI-VLA is adapted to new tasks on OpenEAI-Arm using a small number of human demonstrations. To prevent degradation of multimodal understanding, fine-tuning is augmented by co-training with multimodal datasets such as COCO, VQA-v2, and PixMo-Points. The training objective jointly optimizes the flow-matching control loss (L_EM) for robot data and the VLM next-token prediction loss (L_NTP) for multimodal data.

**Data Collection Pipeline (Appendix A.2):**
The platform supports multiple data collection methods, with all outputs aligned into a unified data format. These include:
- **Drag Collection Mode:** Direct manual manipulation of the arm.
- **Multi-Arm Teleoperation Mode:** Master-slave setup using another OpenEAI-Arm or a 3D-printed model, integrating the GELLO system.
- **VR Teleoperation Mode:** Using VR glasses and controllers to generate joint-space commands via inverse kinematics.
- **UMI Integration:** Incorporating visual SLAM-based tracking for pose estimation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of OpenEAI-Platform involved comprehensive comparisons of both the hardware (OpenEAI-Arm) and the VLA policy (OpenEAI-VLA) across four real-world manipulation tasks: Clean Table, Make Tea, Fold Towel, and Fold T-shirt.

**Hardware Comparison with Commercial Robotic Arms:**
OpenEAI-Arm was characterized and compared against two commercial 6-DoF arms, ARX R5 and AgileX Piper, focusing on cost-effectiveness and performance.
- **Specifications:** OpenEAI-Arm demonstrated a workspace volume and torque margin comparable to commercial arms. It achieved this with a lower weight (3.3 kg vs. 3.9 kg for ARX R5 and 4.2 kg for Piper) and a significantly reduced material cost (approximately 0.79 kUSD, compared to 8.60 kUSD for ARX R5 and 2.16 kUSD for Piper). In terms of derived metrics, OpenEAI-Arm exhibited manipulation operability (0.547) comparable to ARX R5 (0.546) and substantially higher than Piper (0.179). Its endurance efficiency was 0.567.
- **Policy Deployment:** To assess its usability, the same π₀ policy was deployed on all three arms under identical settings. OpenEAI-Arm achieved the highest average task success rate of 0.75, slightly surpassing ARX R5 (0.71). Piper recorded a lower average success rate of 0.64, with observed failures in grasp initiation and precise placement attributed to its distinct kinematic and actuation design. These results indicated that OpenEAI-Arm provides a stable and competitive platform for deploying modern VLA policies.

**Model Comparison with Other VLA Models:**
OpenEAI-VLA was evaluated against several representative VLA models (ACT, Octo, OpenVLA-oft, π₀, π₀.₅) on OpenEAI-Arm across the four tasks, using the same in-domain dataset for fine-tuning and a unified evaluation protocol.
- **Performance Trends:** Earlier generalist policies (Octo, OpenVLA-oft) showed limited effectiveness, often failing at grasp initiation or contact transitions. ACT performed moderately on rigid-object tasks (Clean Table average 0.72) but struggled with long-horizon deformable tasks (e.g., Fold T-shirt success rate of 0.0), indicating compounding errors. In contrast, large-scale flow-based VLAs (π₀, π₀.₅) achieved strong and stable performance.
- **OpenEAI-VLA Performance:** OpenEAI-VLA achieved competitive results. It scored 0.94 on Clean Table, 0.70 on Make Tea, 0.80 on Fold Towel, and 0.83 on Fold T-shirt. Its average performance moderately exceeded π₀ on the first three tasks and matched the best performance achieved by π₀.₅ on Fold T-shirt (0.83). This performance was achieved despite OpenEAI-VLA being pretrained exclusively on open-source datasets, suggesting effective leveraging of its VLM backbone for spatial grounding and action generation. The observed performance gap to π₀.₅ was attributed to the latter's substantially larger and higher-quality proprietary pretraining corpus.

**Ablation Studies:**
Two sets of ablation studies were conducted to quantify the contributions of the control stack and key design choices within OpenEAI-VLA.
- **Control Stack Ablation:** The platform's control stack, including FF-PID and three-point rolling Bézier action chunking, demonstrated the best overall performance in terms of average position, velocity, acceleration errors, and torque amplitude. Removing chunk-level smoothing led to a 6.1% increase in position error and degraded other metrics. Removing dynamics feedforward compensation resulted in a significant increase in acceleration errors (approximately 70 times) and torque errors (approximately 3.4 times), underscoring the necessity of both modules for stable, high-frequency execution.
- **OpenEAI-VLA Policy Ablation:** OpenEAI-VLA achieved the best average success rate of 0.82. The study revealed that removing dataset adapters completely collapsed performance (0.0 success rate), indicating their critical role in handling heterogeneous action/state conventions. Using an older VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) reduced the average score to 0.51, highlighting the importance of a strong, up-to-date VLM. Removing multimodal co-training during fine-tuning sharply decreased the success rate to 0.30, confirming its contribution to preserving spatial grounding and localization. Regarding VLM adaptation, early unfreezing of the VLM backbone negatively impacted performance (0.52 average success), while delayed unfreezing recovered much of the performance (0.72), suggesting that maintaining fixed pretrained VLM priors early in training enhances stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OpenEAI-Platform represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence, addressing several critical limitations that currently hinder research and development. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Reproducibility in Embodied AI Research:** By making the complete hardware designs (mechanical designs and Bill of Materials), low-level control codes, dataset conversion tools, and training/inference scripts fully open-source, OpenEAI-Platform directly tackles the reproducibility crisis in embodied AI. Researchers can now replicate, extend, and adapt the entire system, fostering a more transparent and verifiable research environment. This contrasts with previous state-of-the-art systems that often rely on proprietary datasets and non-public training infrastructures.

2. **Increased Accessibility and Reduced Cost:** The OpenEAI-Arm’s low material cost (approximately 790 USD) significantly lowers the barrier to entry for academic institutions, small research groups, and individual researchers. This increased accessibility can democratize participation in embodied AI research, allowing for wider deployment of robotic systems and facilitating large-scale data collection efforts that were previously economically prohibitive.

3. **Standardized Platform for Benchmarking and Comparison:** The provision of a unified, open-source platform helps to overcome challenges related to cross-embodiment generalization and inconsistent data formats. This standardization allows for more direct and fair comparisons of different VLA algorithms and hardware designs, leading to more robust and comparable research findings across the community.

4. **Advancement of VLA Model Capabilities with Open Data:** OpenEAI-VLA's competitive performance, despite being trained exclusively on open-source datasets, demonstrates that high-quality VLA policies can be developed without reliance on proprietary, large-scale data. This finding can stimulate further innovation in leveraging public datasets and developing efficient training strategies, reducing dependency on closed ecosystems.

5. **Facilitating Research into Hardware-Software Co-design:** The open and transparent nature of OpenEAI-Arm, including its low-level control, enables researchers to investigate the complex interplay between VLA algorithms and underlying hardware properties. This capability is crucial for understanding how hardware characteristics influence policy learning and for developing more robust and adaptable robotic systems.

6. **Promotion of Collaborative and Cumulative Progress:** By offering an end-to-end open platform, the work encourages a collaborative approach to scientific discovery. The community can collectively build upon the released components, contributing to the iterative improvement of both hardware and learning algorithms, thereby accelerating overall progress in embodied manipulation with minimal duplicated effort.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as integrating torque/impedance control, improved sensing, and force-tactile grippers for OpenEAI-Arm, and strengthening OpenEAI-VLA through larger open-source pretraining mixtures and objectives for out-of-distribution robustness, further illustrates the platform's potential as a foundational tool for advanced research directions in embodied AI.
