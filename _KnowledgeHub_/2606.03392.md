# OpenEAI-Platform: An Open-source Embodied Artificial Intelligence Hardware-Software Unified Platform

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenEAI-Platform introduces an open-source hardware-software unified platform for embodied AI, featuring a low-cost 6+1 DoF robotic arm and a reproducible Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. The platform aims to enhance research accessibility and reproducibility by providing transparent designs and achieves competitive performance on real-world manipulation tasks using only open-source data.

## Problem

- High cost and limited transparency of commercial robotic arms impede widespread research and detailed hardware-software interaction studies in embodied AI.
- State-of-the-art VLA models suffer from reproducibility issues due to reliance on extensive proprietary datasets and non-public training infrastructure.
- Existing open-source robot datasets are fragmented with inconsistent action/state definitions and formats, hindering unified training efforts for generalist VLA models.

## Method

- Developed OpenEAI-Arm, a low-cost (approx. 790 USD) 6+1 DoF open-source robotic arm, optimized for manipulation operability and endurance efficiency via a multi-objective genetic algorithm.
- Introduced OpenEAI-VLA, an open-source Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model utilizing a Qwen3-VL-Instruct 4B backbone and a generative action head, trained through a two-stage recipe on open-source datasets with multimodal co-training.
- Implemented a robust control stack for OpenEAI-Arm, combining Dynamics-Compensated PID with three-point rolling Bézier action chunking, to ensure smooth and high-frequency execution of discrete VLA outputs.

## Results

- OpenEAI-Arm was built for approximately 790 USD, providing manipulation operability comparable to commercial arms costing over 8k USD, and achieving a 0.75 average task success rate when deploying a π₀ policy.
- OpenEAI-VLA, trained exclusively on open-source datasets, achieved competitive success rates across four real-world tasks (e.g., 0.94 on Clean Table, 0.83 on Fold T-shirt), matching or moderately exceeding some state-of-the-art VLA models.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the control stack's necessity, with removing dynamics compensation increasing acceleration errors by approximately 70 times, and OpenEAI-VLA's dataset adapters proved indispensable (0.0 success rate without them).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-objective optimization, balancing manipulation operability and endurance efficiency, is effective for designing low-cost, high-performance robotic arms.
> - A robust control stack, including dynamics compensation and smooth action chunking, is crucial for stable and precise execution of VLA policies on cost-effective hardware.
> - Competitive VLA policy performance can be achieved solely using open-source datasets, with dataset adapters and multimodal co-training being critical for handling data heterogeneity and preserving spatial grounding.
