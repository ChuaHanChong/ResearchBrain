# RELIC: Interactive Video World Model with Long-Horizon Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> RELIC, an interactive video world model from researchers including those at Adobe Research, enables real-time, memory-aware exploration of diverse scenes for extended durations (up to 20 seconds) from a single image and text prompt. It demonstrates superior visual quality and precise action control compared to state-of-the-art baselines, generalizing across various artistic styles.

## Problem

- Existing world models struggle to simultaneously achieve real-time, long-horizon video generation with consistent spatial memory and precise user control.
- Most approaches lack efficient memory mechanisms that avoid high computational overhead, hindering real-time performance and long-range consistency.
- Current models often fail to generalize effectively to arbitrary scenes or diverse artistic styles, and struggle with maintaining consistent scenes during drastic viewpoint changes or revisits.

## Method

- A two-stage distillation pipeline converts a fine-tuned 20-second bidirectional video diffusion teacher into a real-time causal autoregressive student model.
- A novel memory compression mechanism in the student model utilizes a rolling-window cache and spatially downsampled KV tokens for older latents, enabling efficient long-horizon spatial memory.
- Training involves a custom-curated Unreal Engine dataset with balanced action distributions and a "replayed back-propagation" technique for memory-efficient distillation of long video sequences.

## Results

- Achieved superior visual quality across VBench metrics (e.g., Subject Consistency, Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality) and exhibited the lowest Relative Pose Error (RPE) for action accuracy compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Demonstrated robust long-horizon spatial memory, accurately recovering previously generated content without explicit 3D representations, and generalized to diverse artistic styles from a single initial frame.
- Enabled real-time video generation at 16 FPS at 480x832 resolution on 4 H100 GPUs, supporting continuous exploration for up to 20 seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time long-horizon streaming, consistent spatial memory, and precise user control can be unified within a single framework, challenging the traditional trade-offs.
> - Implicit 3D-consistent content retrieval is achievable without explicit 3D representations, through the use of compressed historical latent tokens and absolute camera poses.
> - Memory-efficient distillation of complex, long-horizon autoregressive models is possible through the proposed "replayed back-propagation" technique, making large-scale training tractable.
