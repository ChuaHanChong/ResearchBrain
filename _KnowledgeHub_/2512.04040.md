# RELIC: Interactive Video World Model with Long-Horizon Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> RELIC, an interactive video world model from researchers including those at Adobe Research, enables real-time, memory-aware exploration of diverse scenes for extended durations (up to 20 seconds) from a single image and text prompt. It demonstrates superior visual quality and precise action control compared to state-of-the-art baselines, generalizing across various artistic styles.

## Problem

- Existing world models struggle to simultaneously achieve real-time, long-horizon video generation with consistent spatial memory and precise user control.
- Most approaches lack efficient memory mechanisms that avoid high computational overhead, hindering real-time performance and long-range consistency.
- Current models often fail to generalize effectively to arbitrary scenes or diverse artistic styles, and struggle with maintaining consistent scenes during drastic viewpoint changes or revisits.

## Method

- A two-stage distillation pipeline converts a fine-tuned 20-second bidirectional video diffusion teacher into a real-time causal autoregressive student model.
- A novel memory compression mechanism in the student model utilizes a rolling-window cache and spatially downsampled KV tokens for older latents, enabling efficient long-horizon spatial memory.
- Training involves a custom-curated Unreal Engine dataset with balanced action distributions and a "replayed back-propagation" technique for memory-efficient distillation of long video sequences.

## Results

- Achieved superior visual quality across VBench metrics (e.g., Subject Consistency, Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality) and exhibited the lowest Relative Pose Error (RPE) for action accuracy compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Demonstrated robust long-horizon spatial memory, accurately recovering previously generated content without explicit 3D representations, and generalized to diverse artistic styles from a single initial frame.
- Enabled real-time video generation at 16 FPS at 480x832 resolution on 4 H100 GPUs, supporting continuous exploration for up to 20 seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time long-horizon streaming, consistent spatial memory, and precise user control can be unified within a single framework, challenging the traditional trade-offs.
> - Implicit 3D-consistent content retrieval is achievable without explicit 3D representations, through the use of compressed historical latent tokens and absolute camera poses.
> - Memory-efficient distillation of complex, long-horizon autoregressive models is possible through the proposed "replayed back-propagation" technique, making large-scale training tractable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The concept of "world models," first popularized by Ha and Schmidhuber (2018), aims to enable AI agents to understand and simulate our three-dimensional physical world. This overarching vision is fundamental for advancing various downstream applications such as autonomous driving, spatial intelligence, and robotics. Recent breakthroughs in video generation, particularly those employing diffusion models, have propelled the field closer to realizing immersive and interactive experiences by learning controllable autoregressive (AR) video models from large-scale datasets.

However, the development of practical video world models faces three significant, interconnected challenges:
1. **Real-time Long-Horizon Streaming:** The ability to generate continuous video at low latency in response to user inputs over extended durations.
2. **Consistent Spatial Memory:** Ensuring the model "remembers" previously explored or seen content, allowing for consistent scene reconstruction upon revisitation.
3. **Precise User Control:** Enabling accurate and intuitive interaction with the generated world through user actions like camera movements.

Most existing approaches tend to address these aspects in isolation. For instance, many video generation models focus on high visual quality for short clips, or stable long-horizon generation in narrow domains, but struggle with interactive control, real-time performance, or robust memory. Approaches that incorporate long-term memory mechanisms often incur substantial computational overhead, conflicting with real-time requirements. Models leveraging explicit 3D scene representations can be accurate but are typically bottlenecked by reconstruction accuracy and runtime costs. Similarly, techniques for real-time streaming often rely on short context windows, inherently limiting long-range consistency.

RELIC positions itself as a "unified framework" that simultaneously tackles these three challenges. It builds upon autoregressive video-diffusion distillation techniques, specifically the Self-Forcing paradigm and Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), which have shown promise in reducing exposure bias and enabling efficient, few-step video generation. By addressing the critical trade-offs between real-time performance, long-term memory, and generalization, RELIC aims to establish a strong foundation for the next generation of interactive world modeling, moving beyond specialized solutions to a more general-purpose, scalable, and adaptable framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RELIC is to create a truly interactive video world model capable of memory-aware, long-duration exploration of arbitrary scenes in real-time, given only a single initial image and a text description. The authors are motivated by the fundamental challenges that hinder existing world models from achieving this holistic capability:

1. **Overcoming the Real-time vs. Memory Trade-off:** Long-term spatial consistency typically demands significant computational complexity and memory bandwidth, which directly conflicts with the need for real-time responsiveness. RELIC aims to break this trade-off by developing an efficient memory mechanism.
2. **Enabling Long-Horizon Consistency:** Prior work often relies on short-context training windows (e.g., 5-second videos), which limits the model's ability to maintain consistency over longer trajectories or handle drastic viewpoint changes necessary for true world exploration. The motivation here is to enable supervision that enforces spatial and temporal consistency over significantly longer durations (up to 20 seconds).
3. **Achieving Precise and Flexible User Control:** Many datasets for world exploration have imbalanced or tightly coupled action distributions, making it difficult for models to learn disentangled, precise control over camera movements. RELIC seeks to provide robust action-following capabilities through carefully curated data and architectural design.
4. **Generalization to Diverse Scenes:** While some models perform well in training-aligned domains (e.g., specific games), they often fail to generalize to arbitrary scenes or diverse artistic styles. RELIC aims for broad generalization beyond realistic environments.

In essence, RELIC's core motivation is to synthesize a functional, user-controllable virtual world from minimal input, delivering a fluid, consistent, and explorative experience previously constrained by technical limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RELIC adopts a multi-faceted approach, building on a two-stage distillation pipeline from a bidirectional video diffusion teacher to a causal autoregressive student model.

#### 3.1. Data Curation for Interactive Video World Model

A critical component of RELIC is its custom-curated synthetic video dataset rendered in Unreal Engine (UE). This dataset, comprising over 1400 human-controlled camera trajectories from 350 diverse 3D scenes (indoor and outdoor), addresses limitations of real-world datasets:
- **Balanced Action Distributions:** Trajectories include single and mixed actions, as well as revisitations, to facilitate learning precise decoupled controls and long-range memory.
- **Physically Plausible Motion:** Trajectories are collision-constrained and filtered for unnatural camera motions, instability, and rendering artifacts.
- **Precise Annotations:** Frame-aligned 6-DoF camera poses (absolute positions and orientations) and 13-DoF per-frame action labels (translational and rotational velocities) are derived. Actions are normalized by clip-average displacement magnitude for consistent behavior across scenes.
- **Segmented Captions:** Long videos are split into 5-second segments with GPT-5 generated captions, constrained to describe only static, scene-level attributes to avoid conflict with dynamic user actions.
- **Time-Reverse Augmentation:** A "palindrome-style" augmentation is introduced during training to explicitly encourage the model to learn spatial long-context memory retrieval by revisiting past viewpoints.

#### 3.2. Action-Conditioned Teacher for Long Video Generation

The framework starts with a Wan-2.1 (Wan et al., 2025) 14B-parameter bidirectional video diffusion model, pretrained for 5-second video generation.
- **Base Architecture:** Utilizes a spatio-temporal VAE (8x spatial, 4x temporal compression) and Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). It takes an input image and text description.
- **Action Control:** Integrates a 13-DoF action space (6 translational, 6 rotational, 1 static action), represented as continuous relative velocities. Both relative actions and derived 6-DoF absolute camera poses are embedded and injected into every transformer block. Relative actions guide frame-to-frame transitions, while absolute poses proxy for spatial content retrieval.
- **Long-Horizon Training:** The teacher model is fine-tuned on the UE dataset to extend its generation duration from 5 seconds to 20 seconds (317 frames) using a curriculum learning strategy (5s -> 10s -> 20s). YaRN technique (Peng et al., 2023) is applied to extend Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) for query and key tokens, aiding in longer sequence adaptation.

#### 3.3. Autoregressive Student for Real-Time Streaming (with Memory)

The 20-second bidirectional teacher is distilled into a few-step, memory-aware causal student AR video diffusion model, also based on Wan2.1-14B.
- **Causal Attention:** Replaces bidirectional attention with block-wise causal attention, generating latent frames in a block-causal manner for streaming.
- **Memory Mechanism:** To overcome the KV-cache memory bottleneck of full-history storage, RELIC employs a novel memory-compression mechanism:
 - **Rolling-Window Cache:** Stores uncompressed KV tokens for recent latents (e.g., `i-w` to `i`).
 - **Compressed Long-Horizon Spatial Memory Cache:** Stores spatially downsampled KV tokens (1x, 2x, 4x interleaving compression) for older latents (up to `i-w`). This reduces token count by ~4x, significantly lowering memory and attention FLOPs while retaining reconstructable information. This design enables *implicit* 3D-consistent content retrieval without explicit 3D representations.

#### 3.4. Distillation Framework

RELIC employs a variant of the Self-Forcing paradigm (Huang et al., 2025d) with Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) for training the AR student.
- **ODE Initialization with Hybrid Forcing:** The student is initialized with teacher weights and trained to regress precomputed ODE trajectories. A hybrid forcing strategy (combining teacher forcing for clean compressed latents and diffusion forcing for noisy uncompressed latents) warms up long-horizon memory retrieval.
- **Long-Video Distillation with Replayed Back-propagation:** To make distillation of 20-second videos computationally tractable (avoiding memory-intensive full-sequence autograd), a "replayed back-propagation" technique is introduced:
 1. The student first rolls out the full sequence with autograd disabled.
 2. DMD score-difference maps are computed and cached for the entire sequence.
 3. The AR rollout is replayed block-by-block with autograd enabled, back-propagating the *cached* score differences to update gradients for that block. The computation graph for each block is freed immediately. This reduces peak GPU memory to that of a single block.

#### 3.5. Runtime Efficiency Optimization

For real-time inference (16 FPS):
- `torch.compile` is used to reduce kernel launch overhead and memory costs.
- KV cache stores in FP8 E4M3 format for reduced memory usage.
- FlashAttention v3 with FP8 kernels is used on NVIDIA Hopper GPUs.
- Manual fusion and refactoring of PyTorch operations based on profiling.
- Parallelization strategy: FSDP, sequence parallelism for linear layers and cross-attention, and tensor parallelism for self-attention (sharding KV cache across devices).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RELIC demonstrates significant advancements in interactive video world modeling, achieving compelling quantitative and qualitative results:

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison

Evaluated on a benchmark of 220 Adobe Stock images (realistic and non-realistic styles) with 20-second action scripts against state-of-the-art baselines like Matrix-Game-2.0 and Hunyuan-GameCraft.
- **Visual Quality:** RELIC achieves the strongest overall performance across VBench metrics (Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Motion Smoothness, Dynamic Degree, Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality). Despite being trained at 480p, it performs comparably to (or better than) 720p models like Hunyuan-GameCraft in Image Quality and significantly outperforms others in Aesthetics.
- **Action Accuracy:** Using reconstructed camera trajectories (via ViPE) and Sim(3) Umeyama alignment, RELIC shows the lowest Relative Pose Error (RPE) for both translation and rotation, indicating the most faithful adherence to intended camera movements.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Capabilities Showcase

- **Diversity and Generalization:** RELIC successfully generalizes to a wide range of artistic styles (oil paintings, comics, vector art, low-poly renders), generating worlds from a single initial frame. It naturally exhibits correct distance awareness and strong 3D shape understanding without explicit 3D modeling.
- **Long-Horizon Memory:** The model demonstrates robust spatial memory retrieval. It can accurately recover previously generated scene content with minimal detail loss, even after the content has been out of view for extended periods. This is achieved implicitly through compressed historical video tokens and absolute camera poses, without explicit 3D representations or handcrafted memory heuristics.
- **Adjustable Velocity:** Users can control exploration speed by adjusting a displacement coefficient, allowing for a wide spectrum of translational and rotational velocities while maintaining high quality and temporal stability.
- **Multi-Key Control:** RELIC reliably responds to complex multi-key inputs combining translation and rotation, enabling rich and intuitive real-time interaction and exploration.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Action Control:** Qualitatively, RELIC consistently follows commanded actions, unlike Matrix-Game-2.0 (which can introduce artifacts or drift) and Hunyuan-GameCraft (which sometimes misinterprets actions, e.g., panning instead of tilting).
 - **Memory:** Baselines like Hunyuan-GameCraft and Matrix-Game-2.0 often "forget" objects or scene context upon returning to a previously visited viewpoint, whereas RELIC maintains persistent object presence.
 - **Comparison with Marble:** Compared to commercial systems like Marble (based on Gaussian Splatting), RELIC avoids reconstruction artifacts such as "Gaussian floaters," delivering cleaner and more stable outputs.

#### 4.3. System Performance

- Implemented as a 14B-parameter model.
- Achieves real-time generation at 16 FPS at 480x832 resolution on 4 H100 GPUs.
- Supports generation for up to 20 seconds.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RELIC represents a significant step forward in the field of interactive world modeling by effectively unifying three crucial ingredients: real-time long-horizon streaming, consistent spatial memory, and precise user control. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

1. **Unified Framework:** By tackling all three challenges simultaneously, RELIC moves beyond fragmented solutions, offering a more complete and practical world model. This unified approach demonstrates that it is possible to achieve high performance in all these areas, a challenging feat often thought to involve inherent trade-offs.
2. **Efficient Memory Mechanism:** The novel compressed historical latent tokens within the KV cache, combined with relative actions and absolute camera poses, provides an elegant and computationally efficient solution for long-horizon spatial memory. This "implicit 3D-consistent content retrieval" without explicit 3D representations is a notable architectural innovation that can inspire future memory designs in generative models.
3. **Scalable Distillation for Long Videos:** The "replayed back-propagation" technique introduced for memory-efficient distillation of long-horizon student models from long-context teachers is a crucial methodological advancement. It addresses a fundamental computational bottleneck in training complex autoregressive models for extended sequences, making such large-scale distillation more tractable.
4. **High-Quality, Controllable Generation:** The combination of a meticulously curated Unreal Engine dataset, a robust action-conditioned teacher, and optimized distillation processes results in high-quality video generation with superior action-following accuracy and spatial consistency compared to prior work. The ability to generalize to diverse art styles further broadens its applicability.
5. **Foundation for Embodied AI and Content Creation:** RELIC lays a scalable and adaptable foundation for general-purpose world simulators. This has profound implications for:
 - **Embodied AI:** Creating realistic and interactive training environments for agents to learn navigation, manipulation, and interaction within simulated worlds.
 - **Immersive Virtual Content Creation:** Empowering artists and designers to rapidly generate and explore virtual environments from simple inputs, accelerating workflows in gaming, virtual reality, and film production.
 - **Scientific Simulation:** Offering a tool for simulating complex scenarios that require dynamic, controllable 3D environments.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge several limitations. The generated videos still exhibit limited diversity and restricted scene dynamics due to training predominantly on static Unreal Engine scenes. Generating "extremely long videos" (on the scale of minutes) remains a challenge. Furthermore, the combination of a large model size, KV cache requirements, and iterative denoising steps still imposes significant inference latency under resource-constrained settings, despite the optimizations.

**Conclusion:** Despite these limitations, RELIC successfully demonstrates that integrating compressed historical latents with full-horizon supervision from a long-context teacher can effectively mitigate issues like drifting and memory forgetting in video generation. The architectural and methodological innovations in RELIC establish it as a strong contender and a flexible foundation for advancing interactive world modeling, paving the way for future capabilities across numerous domains in generative AI.
