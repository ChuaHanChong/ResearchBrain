# Octax: Accelerated CHIP-8 Arcade Environments for Reinforcement Learning in JAX

> [!summary] Summary
> OCTAX introduces a JAX-native, GPU-accelerated suite of classic CHIP-8 arcade game environments for Reinforcement Learning research. The platform achieves up to 350,000 environment steps per second and integrates a pipeline for automated game generation using Large Language Models.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning research is hampered by computational bottlenecks, as most classic arcade game environments are CPU-bound, limiting scalability and statistical validity.
- The JAX ecosystem lacked end-to-end GPU-accelerated environments for classic image-based arcade games, leading to costly CPU-GPU data transfers during training.
- Prohibitive computational costs for large-scale RL experiments often force researchers to use limited random seeds, undermining statistical reliability and reproducibility.

## Method

- Developed a ==JAX-native== CHIP-8 emulator designed for full GPU acceleration, leveraging the virtual machine's simple instruction set and small memory footprint for efficient vectorized execution.
- Integrated the emulator with an `OctaxEnv` wrapper, providing a standard RL interface compatible with Gymnasium and Gymnax APIs, including game-specific reward and termination logic.
- Implemented a novel LLM-assisted pipeline that generates complete CHIP-8 games from high-level descriptions, including automated creation of game assembly code and Python wrapper functions for reward/termination.

## Results

- Achieved up to 350,000 environment steps per second (1.4 million frames/second) with 8,192 parallel environments on consumer-grade hardware, demonstrating near-linear scaling.
- Showed a **14x** improvement in computational efficiency compared to highly optimized CPU-based solutions (e.g., EnvPool for ALE Pong) at high parallelization levels.
- Successfully generated a new CHIP-8 game ('Target Shooter') across three progressive difficulty levels using the LLM-assisted pipeline, with PPO agents exhibiting clear performance stratification corresponding to difficulty.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherently constrained design of the CHIP-8 virtual machine enables highly efficient, vectorized GPU emulation, allowing thousands of simultaneous game instances.
> - An end-to-end JAX implementation can effectively eliminate the CPU-GPU data transfer bottleneck, providing significant orders-of-magnitude speedups for classic arcade environments.
> - Large Language Models can serve as powerful tools for automating the creation and modification of RL environments, enabling dynamic curriculum generation and custom task design.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Reinforcement Learning (RL) has seen tremendous advancements, but its progress is increasingly hampered by computational constraints. Modern RL algorithms, particularly deep RL, demand extensive experimentation to ensure statistical validity, often requiring thousands of parallel environment interactions and extensive hyperparameter sweeps. However, as highlighted by the authors, most existing RL environments, especially classic arcade game benchmarks like the Atari Learning Environment (ALE), remain predominantly CPU-bound. This creates a severe bottleneck, where the environment simulation becomes the limiting factor, making large-scale experimentation prohibitively expensive in terms of time and computational resources. The paper cites examples like the Rainbow paper, which required 34,200 GPU hours, an unattainable cost for many research groups.

OCTAX directly addresses this critical scalability challenge. It positions itself within the evolving landscape of high-performance RL environments, which has seen several approaches:

- **Traditional Game-based RL Environments:** Benchmarks like ALE (Atari 2600 games) and newer platforms like NetHack Learning Environment (for long-term planning) and Crafter (for resource management) provide rich, human-relevant challenges. While valuable, their CPU-based implementations exacerbate the scalability problem.
- **CPU High-Performance Solutions:** Projects like EnvPool and PufferLib optimize CPU-based environment execution using highly optimized C++ or C implementations. While achieving impressive throughput (e.g., 1 million Atari frames per second), they still suffer from costly CPU-GPU data transfers during training and require C/C++ expertise in a predominantly Python-driven RL community.
- **GPU-Accelerated RL Environments (Non-JAX):** Solutions like CUDA Learning Environment (CuLE) and Isaac Gym (for robotics) move environment execution to GPUs, achieving orders-of-magnitude speedups. However, they often introduce NVIDIA hardware dependence and significant per-environment engineering costs, limiting their general applicability and portability.
- **JAX-based Environments:** The advent of JAX, with its composable function transformations (`jit`, `vmap`, `grad`) and native acceleration on GPUs/TPUs, has enabled a new class of natively accelerated RL environments. Prominent examples include Brax (physics simulation), Gymnax (classic control and simplified environments), Jumanji (diverse problems), Pgx (board games), and specialized gridworld environments like XLand-MiniGrid and Navix. PuzzleJAX also offers dynamically compilable puzzle games.

Despite the breadth of JAX-based environments, a "critical gap remains: classic arcade games." While MinAtar offers simplified Atari versions, the *full visual complexity and authentic game mechanics* of classic arcade games have been largely absent from the JAX ecosystem. OCTAX precisely fills this void by providing the first end-to-end JAX implementation of such environments through CHIP-8 emulation. By leveraging CHIP-8's inherent simplicity and constraint-driven design within the JAX framework, OCTAX offers the computational benefits of GPU acceleration without sacrificing the engaging and cognitively diverse gameplay that makes classic arcade games valuable benchmarks for algorithmic development. This positions OCTAX as a crucial tool for the JAX-RL community, offering an end-to-end GPU alternative to the CPU-bound Atari benchmark.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of OCTAX is to **accelerate Reinforcement Learning (RL) research by providing a high-performance, scalable, and GPU-accelerated suite of classic arcade game environments implemented in JAX.**

The core motivations driving this work are rooted in several significant challenges facing contemporary RL research:

- **Computational Bottleneck in RL Experimentation:** Modern RL research demands extensive experimentation, often involving thousands of parallel environments and large-scale hyperparameter sweeps, to achieve statistically robust results and validate algorithmic advances. However, environment execution, particularly for complex game simulations, remains a primary computational bottleneck due as existing environments are largely CPU-bound. This mismatch between algorithmic demand and environmental compute capacity severely limits the scale and depth of experiments.
- **Undermining Statistical Validity:** The prohibitive computational costs often force researchers to report results with a limited number of random seeds (e.g., fewer than five), which undermines statistical reliability and can lead to misleading conclusions about algorithmic performance. OCTAX aims to make larger-scale, more statistically sound experiments computationally feasible.
- **Lack of End-to-End GPU Acceleration for Classic Arcade Games in JAX:** While JAX has emerged as a powerful framework for accelerated numerical computation and differentiable programming, enabling GPU acceleration for agent training, it has lacked native, image-based classic arcade environments that can run entirely on GPUs. Existing benchmarks like ALE are CPU-based, necessitating costly CPU-GPU data transfers that negate the benefits of GPU-accelerated training.
- **Enhancing Reproducibility and Accessibility:** By reducing the computational resources required for large-scale experiments (e.g., enabling experiments that once needed top-tier clusters to run efficiently on a single GPU), OCTAX aims to improve reproducibility and make advanced RL research more accessible to smaller laboratories and institutions with limited computational budgets, thus reducing energy consumption.
- **Facilitating Environment Design and Generation:** Beyond simply providing fixed environments, the authors aim to leverage the simplicity of CHIP-8 and the power of Large Language Models (LLMs) to create a modular and extensible platform. This includes enabling automated environment generation and modification, opening new avenues for research in curriculum learning, open-ended learning, and continually evolving tasks.

In essence, OCTAX seeks to bridge the gap between the computational demands of cutting-edge RL algorithms and the limitations of existing environment simulation platforms, specifically by bringing the rich, diverse challenges of classic arcade games into the high-performance, JAX-native, GPU-accelerated ecosystem.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OCTAX's methodology revolves around leveraging the inherent simplicity and constraints of the CHIP-8 virtual machine specification to create highly performant, JAX-native, and GPU-accelerated RL environments. The approach can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Choice of CHIP-8 Emulation

The authors strategically chose CHIP-8, a 1970s virtual machine, for several reasons:
- **Atari-like Complexity with Computational Advantages:** CHIP-8 games offer image-based environments (64x32 monochrome display) with similar cognitive demands to Atari games (puzzle, action, strategy) but with significantly lower computational overhead due to its constrained design (16 registers, 4KB memory, 35-instruction set).
- **Efficient Vectorized Emulation:** The simple instruction set and small memory footprint enable thousands of simultaneous game instances without memory constraints and facilitate efficient vectorized emulation on modern GPUs.
- **Deterministic Execution:** Ensures experimental reproducibility.
- **Modifiability and Analyzability:** The simple assembly code of CHIP-8 games can be easily generated, modified, and analyzed, supporting advanced research in environment design and curriculum learning.

#### 3.2. JAX-based Implementation of CHIP-8

The core of OCTAX is an end-to-end JAX implementation of the CHIP-8 emulator, designed for full GPU acceleration:
- **ROM Loading and Initialization:** Game data (`.ch8` files) is loaded into the emulator's 4KB memory. The system initializes with font data, registers (V0-VF, I, PC), and the 64x32 display buffer, following standard CHIP-8 specifications.
- **Fetch-Decode-Execute Cycle:** The classic processor cycle is implemented using JAX primitives.
 - `fetch`: Retrieves 16-bit instructions and advances the program counter.
 - `decode`: Extracts instruction components (opcodes, registers, immediate values) via bitwise operations.
 - `execute`: Dispatches instructions to specialized handlers using JAX's `lax.switch` for GPU-compatible execution.
- **Vectorized Instruction Execution:** All instruction handlers adhere to JAX's functional programming model, treating state immutably and returning updated copies. ALU operations, control flow (jumps, calls, conditionals using `lax.cond`), and crucially, the display system, all leverage vectorized operations. This allows the display to render sprites across the entire framebuffer simultaneously across thousands of parallel environments.

#### 3.3. Environment Integration via `OctaxEnv` Wrapper

The `OctaxEnv` wrapper transforms the CHIP-8 emulator into a standard RL interface, compatible with both Gymnasium and Gymnax APIs:
- **Game Timing:** Each RL step executes multiple CHIP-8 instructions (default frame skip) to maintain authentic game timing relative to the original 700Hz instruction frequency.
- **Observations:** Consist of the 64x32 monochrome display, typically stacked over 4 frames to produce (4, 64, 32) boolean arrays, similar to Atari observations.
- **Action Space Optimization:** Maps discrete RL outputs to game-specific key subsets (e.g., only two keys for Pong) plus a no-op option, reducing action space size and aiding learning.
- **Reward and Termination Logic:** Addresses CHIP-8's non-standardized scoring and termination by providing game-specific `score_fn` and `terminated_fn` functions. These functions analyze ROM structure and dynamically monitor memory/register changes to extract score (e.g., from `V5` in Brix, `V14` in Pong) and termination conditions (e.g., `V14 == 0` for lives in Brix, `V1 == 2` for game over in Tetris).
- **Initialization Handling:** Supports `startup_instructions` to automatically bypass menu screens and begin gameplay immediately upon environment reset.

#### 3.4. LLM-Assisted Environment Generation

A novel aspect of the methodology is the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) for automated environment creation:
- **Problem:** Manual extraction of reward/termination logic can be challenging for some games.
- **LLM Role:** Initially, LLMs were used to analyze decompiled CHIP-8 assembly code to generate `score_fn` and `terminated_fn` functions.
- **Automated Pipeline:** This led to a seven-step pipeline for generating *entire* CHIP-8 games from high-level descriptions:
 1. Construct a corpus of CHIP-8 tutorials, documentation, and programming examples.
 2. Embed this corpus into a prompt to guide the LLM.
 3. Provide a high-level game description (mechanics, objectives).
 4. LLM generates initial CHIP-8 code.
 5. Automated feedback loop between LLM and CHIP-8 compiler iteratively refines code until successful compilation.
 6. Python wrapper functions (`score_fn`, `terminated_fn`) are automatically generated.
 7. Game description is augmented for difficulty, and the process repeats for curriculum generation.
- **Proof-of-Concept:** Demonstrated with a "Target Shooter" game across three difficulty levels, showcasing LLMs' ability to create meaningful difficulty gradients and maintain consistent register mappings for RL compatibility.

This comprehensive methodology allows OCTAX to provide not just a high-performance emulation platform but also a flexible and extensible framework for RL research and environment development.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of OCTAX yielded several significant findings, demonstrating its capabilities in both facilitating RL agent training and achieving superior computational performance.

#### 4.1. RL Agent Learning Dynamics

- **Diverse Learning Profiles:** PPO agents were trained across 16 diverse CHIP-8 games. The learning curves (Interquartile Mean returns over 5M timesteps, averaged across 12 random seeds) revealed distinct learning profiles:
 - **Rapid Plateau Games (e.g., Airplane, Brix, Deep, Filter, Blinky):** Showed quick initial learning followed by stable, high performance, indicating clear reward signals and tractable challenges.
 - **Gradual Improvement Games (e.g., Submarine, Tank, UFO):** Exhibited sustained learning throughout training, suggesting sparser reward structures or more complex strategic requirements that demand longer training horizons.
 - **Limited Performance Games (e.g., Tetris, Worm):** Demonstrated significant variance with little absolute progress, highlighting challenges for standard policy gradient methods in environments requiring long-term planning or suffering from extremely sparse rewards. For instance, in Worm (a Snake clone), agents often managed to eat only a single apple before dying.
- **Cognitive Diversity:** These results confirm that OCTAX's CHIP-8 environments offer a wide range of cognitive demands, making them suitable for studying various aspects of learning and control within a unified technical framework.
- **Efficient Training:** Individual training runs averaged 65 minutes each, with 24 concurrent experiments running on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU. This achieved an average throughput of approximately 30,800 environment steps per second across all parallel sessions.

#### 4.2. Performance and Scalability

- **Orders-of-Magnitude Speedup:** OCTAX demonstrated near-linear scaling in environment throughput, reaching up to **350,000 steps per second (equivalent to 1.4 million frames per second)** with 8,192 parallel environments on consumer-grade hardware (NVIDIA RTX 3090). This performance was achieved before hitting VRAM limitations.
- **Superior to CPU-based Solutions:** When benchmarked against EnvPool (a highly optimized CPU-based solution for ALE Pong), OCTAX showed a **14x improvement in computational efficiency** at high parallelization levels. EnvPool plateaued around 25,000 steps per second due to CPU saturation, whereas OCTAX continued to scale significantly higher.
- **Reduced Computational Cost:** This massive speedup translates directly into a dramatic reduction in experimental time, making comprehensive hyperparameter sweeps and extensive ablation studies feasible in hours rather than days or weeks.
- **Linear Memory Scaling:** GPU memory usage scaled linearly with the number of parallel environments, consuming approximately 2 MB of GPU memory per environment, demonstrating efficient resource management.

#### 4.3. LLM-Assisted Environment Generation

- **Successful Automated Creation:** The LLM-assisted pipeline successfully generated a new CHIP-8 game, "Target Shooter," across three progressive difficulty levels: static targets, time-limited targets, and moving targets with time constraints.
- **Meaningful Difficulty Gradient:** PPO agents trained on these LLM-generated environments exhibited clear performance stratification:
 - **Level 1 (Static Targets):** Agents achieved optimal returns of 10.0 with rapid convergence (by 1M timesteps).
 - **Level 2 (Time-Limited Targets):** Agents plateaued at 9.0 returns with moderate learning speed.
 - **Level 3 (Moving, Time-Limited Targets):** Agents reached 8.0 returns with the slowest progression.
- **Proof-of-Concept for Novel Research:** The inverse relationship between difficulty level and both final performance and sample efficiency validates the feasibility of automated environment generation for RL research. This opens promising avenues for curriculum learning, open-endedness, and continual learning scenarios.

In summary, OCTAX successfully demonstrated that GPU-accelerated CHIP-8 emulation provides a robust, high-performance, and cognitively diverse platform for RL research, outperforming existing CPU-bound alternatives and introducing novel capabilities for environment generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OCTAX represents a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning research, offering a powerful new tool that addresses several long-standing challenges and opens new avenues for exploration.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Rigor and Reproducibility in RL Research

- **Addressing the Statistical Validity Crisis:** The most immediate impact of OCTAX is its ability to enable larger-scale experimentation. By achieving orders-of-magnitude speedups, researchers can now conduct studies with many more random seeds and extensive hyperparameter sweeps. This directly combats the issue of undersampling in RL experiments, leading to more statistically valid and reliable results, thereby improving the overall rigor and reproducibility of RL research.
- **Democratization of RL Research:** The reduction in computational cost—enabling experiments that once required high-end clusters to run efficiently on a single GPU—democratizes access to cutting-edge RL research. This significantly benefits smaller research laboratories and institutions with limited computational resources, fostering broader participation and innovation in the field.

#### 5.2. Environmental and Economic Benefits

- **Reduced Energy Consumption:** By allowing complex, large-scale experiments to run efficiently on less hardware, OCTAX contributes to a substantial reduction in energy consumption associated with RL training. This aligns with a growing movement towards more "Greener Deep Reinforcement Learning," as explicitly noted by the authors. This not only has environmental benefits but also reduces the economic cost of research.

#### 5.3. Novel Research Directions

- **LLM-Assisted Environment Generation:** The successful demonstration of LLM-assisted CHIP-8 game generation is a pioneering achievement. This modular and extensible design opens up unprecedented possibilities for:
 - **Automated Curriculum Learning:** Generating a sequence of tasks with increasing difficulty for agents to master.
 - **Open-Ended Learning:** Creating an endless stream of novel environments and challenges, pushing agents towards more generalized intelligence.
 - **Continual Learning:** Providing diverse tasks for agents to continually adapt and learn without forgetting previous knowledge.
 - **Custom Task Design:** Researchers can quickly prototype and generate custom environments tailored to specific research questions without deep game development expertise.
- **JAX Ecosystem Integration:** OCTAX fills a critical gap in the JAX ecosystem by providing end-to-end GPU-accelerated classic arcade game environments. This allows RL researchers to leverage the full power of JAX's composable function transformations for both agent and environment, streamlining the entire research pipeline.

#### 5.4. Future Expansion and Potential

The platform's modular design and the existence of hundreds of compatible CHIP-8 ROMs online suggest significant potential for community contributions and further expansion. Future work includes extending support to Super-CHIP8 and XO-CHIP variants, which offer higher resolution, color graphics, and expanded instruction sets, allowing for more sophisticated games while retaining computational efficiency. The planned support for multi-agent/multi-player mechanics and its high-throughput capabilities for offline RL research further broaden its potential impact, enabling the creation of large-scale datasets and robust evaluation of offline algorithms.

#### 5.5. Acknowledged Limitations

While highly impactful, the authors are transparent about limitations. The GPU-based architecture, despite its speed, faces performance constraints due to CHIP-8’s variable instruction execution complexity. JAX synchronization across parallel environments means each step's execution time depends on the slowest instruction, which can lead to minor bottlenecks. Additionally, the absence of established maximum scores for many CHIP-8 games limits the ability to assess whether RL agents are approaching theoretical performance limits, making it challenging to evaluate algorithmic performance ceilings definitively.

In conclusion, OCTAX is more than just a new set of environments; it is a foundational platform that addresses critical computational and methodological limitations in RL. By making large-scale, statistically robust, and reproducible experimentation more accessible and by pioneering LLM-assisted environment generation, OCTAX promises to significantly accelerate progress in the development of advanced reinforcement learning algorithms.
