# Temporally Centered SIGReg Improves Multi-Task LeWorldModel Learning: From Analysis to Method

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Tokyo developed Temporally Centered LeWorldModel (TC-LeWM) to address the performance degradation of LeWorldModel (LeWM) in multi-task learning, which occurred due to its marginal Gaussian prior. TC-LeWM improved average task success rates on the LIBERO benchmark from 53.2% to 73.6% for 10-task training and from 44.4% to 73.5% for 40-task training, while learning more organized and robust latent representations.

## Problem

- LeWorldModel (LeWM) experienced performance degradation when scaled from single-task to multi-task learning, despite maintaining global representation non-collapse.
- The marginal Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg) in LeWM was found to compress distinct task-dependent latent clusters, leading to representation aliasing.
- This structural incompatibility resulted in substantially lower downstream behavior-cloning performance in multi-task control settings.

## Method

- Introduce Temporally Centered LeWorldModel (**TC-LeWM**), which applies the ==Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg)== to ==temporally centered latent residuals==.
- This approach removes the direct regularization pressure on the separation between task-dependent latent cluster centers.
- It allows the full latent space to develop multi-modal structures while maintaining the anti-collapse property of SIGReg.

## Results

- TC-LeWM increased average task success rates on the LIBERO benchmark from **53.2%** to **73.6%** for 10-task training and from **44.4%** to **73.5%** for 40-task unified training.
- The learned latent representations from TC-LeWM were more organized, exhibiting clearer task separation, coherent progress-aligned trajectories, and significantly reduced short-timescale variation (**2%** residual-energy fraction).
- TC-LeWM demonstrated enhanced robustness to **eight** state-preserving visual perturbations, showing smaller relative latent displacement than Raw LeWM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Marginal Gaussianization, as enforced by SIGReg, systematically penalizes separated multi-component latent structures, causing contractive pressure on their global spread.
> - This pressure leads to representation aliasing across different tasks when the task-dependent latent clusters are compressed too close together.
> - Applying SIGReg to temporally centered latent residuals removes this undesirable pressure on low-frequency, task-dependent structure while preserving the essential anti-collapse mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models are a prominent area of research in reinforcement learning and artificial intelligence, focusing on learning predictive latent representations of environment dynamics. These models enable agents to anticipate action consequences, facilitating planning and policy learning. Early world model approaches, such as those by Ha & Schmidhuber (2018) and Hafner et al. (2019, 2020), often rely on objectives like observation reconstruction or reward prediction. While effective, these methods can introduce extraneous visual details or necessitate task-specific supervision, which may be unavailable in large reward-free multi-task datasets.

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) have emerged as an alternative paradigm. JEPAs learn representations by predicting target embeddings directly in latent space, bypassing raw observation reconstruction. Examples include I-JEPA (Assran et al., 2023) for images and V-JEPA (Assran et al., 2025) for video. While offering benefits like reconstruction-free learning, non-contrastive joint-embedding objectives are susceptible to representation collapse, necessitating mechanisms such as momentum target encoders, stop-gradient asymmetry, or explicit variance regularization (Grill et al., 2020; Chen & He, 2021; Bardes et al., 2022; Zbontar et al., 2021).

LeWorldModel (LeWM) (Maes et al., 2026) is a recent instantiation of JEPA tailored for action-conditioned world modeling. It enables end-to-end, reconstruction-free world-model learning directly from pixels. A key component of LeWM is the Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg) (Balestriero & LeCun, 2025), which applies an Epps–Pulley (EP) normality criterion to random latent projections to encourage the latent marginal distribution toward an isotropic Gaussian. This mechanism aims to prevent representation collapse without requiring pretrained encoders, momentum targets, or reward supervision.

The current work identifies a limitation of marginal SIGReg within LeWM: its performance degrades significantly in multi-task training scenarios, despite maintaining global non-collapse. This degradation indicates a structural incompatibility between the marginal Gaussian prior enforced by SIGReg and the complex, task-dependent latent structures required for effective multi-task control. The paper positions itself as an analysis of this specific multi-task failure mode and proposes a methodological refinement to address it, thereby advancing the stability and scalability of end-to-end multi-task world-model learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed degradation of LeWorldModel (LeWM) performance when scaling from single-task to multi-task learning settings. While LeWM, utilizing the Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg), demonstrates stable end-to-end world-model learning from pixels in single-task contexts by preventing representation collapse, this efficacy does not reliably extend to multi-task training. Specifically, multi-task training with the standard (marginal) SIGReg leads to substantially inferior downstream behavior-cloning performance.

The paper identifies the following core issues and objectives:

1. **Document and Quantify Multi-Task Degradation:** A key objective was to empirically demonstrate and quantify the decline in performance of Raw LeWM when transitioning from independent single-task training to joint multi-task training on benchmarks like LIBERO-Long. This involved showing that, while representations might remain statistically non-collapsed, their geometric properties become misaligned with the distinctions necessary for multi-task control.

2. **Analyze the Causal Mechanism of Degradation:** The central motivation was to trace the root cause of this performance drop. The authors hypothesized a conflict between the marginal Gaussianization enforced by SIGReg and the inherent multi-task latent structure. They aimed to show that marginal Gaussianization compresses the separation between task-dependent latent clusters relative to their within-cluster variation. This compression was theorized to introduce representation aliasing across different tasks and states, making the learned representations sensitive to minor visual perturbations and hindering a downstream policy's ability to discriminate between task-relevant states.

3. **Propose and Validate a Solution to Mitigate Degradation:** Based on the identified failure mode, the objective was to develop a modified regularization strategy that addresses the structural incompatibility. The proposed solution, Temporally Centered LeWorldModel (TC-LeWM), involves applying SIGReg to *temporally centered residuals* rather than the full latent marginal distribution. This approach aims to:
 - Remove the direct regularization pressure on the separation among cluster centers.
 - Eliminate the requirement that the full latent space adhere to a single isotropic Gaussian distribution.
 - Retain the anti-collapse effect of SIGReg, which is crucial for stable self-supervised learning.

4. **Demonstrate Improved Performance, Latent Structure, and Robustness:** A final objective was to empirically evaluate TC-LeWM against Raw LeWM across various multi-task scenarios (suite-wise 10-task and unified 40-task training) on the LIBERO benchmark. This evaluation included not only downstream control performance (task success rates) but also an in-depth analysis of the learned latent representations' geometry, temporal stability, organization, and robustness to visual perturbations. The goal was to show that TC-LeWM yields higher success rates, learns more organized and meaningful latent structures (e.g., clear task separation and progress-dependent trajectories), and exhibits greater resilience to visual noise.

In summary, the motivation was to address a fundamental limitation in current reconstruction-free world models—specifically, LeWM's marginal SIGReg—when applied to complex multi-task learning, thereby paving the way for more stable and scalable end-to-end multi-task robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach involving empirical evaluation, theoretical analysis through controlled Monte Carlo experiments, and the development and validation of a new regularization method.

#### 3.1. Problem Identification and Analysis

1. **Empirical Degradation Documentation:** The initial step involved demonstrating the performance degradation of Raw LeWM. This was achieved by comparing its closed-loop success rates on the LIBERO-Long benchmark under two conditions: independent single-task training and joint 10-task training. The observed decline in average success rate for joint training across multiple tasks provided the empirical basis for the subsequent investigation.

2. **Latent Decomposition:** To analyze the influence of marginal SIGReg, the authors decomposed the latent representation ($z_t$) into a local temporal mean ($\bar{z}_t$) and a complementary short-timescale residual ($r_t = z_t - \bar{z}_t$). This decomposition allowed for the separate examination of low-frequency, task-dependent structure (captured by $\bar{z}_t$) and short-timescale variation (captured by $r_t$). This was extended to one-dimensional projections (x = w^T z_t = w^T \bar{z}_t + w^T r_t), where w^T \bar{z}_t is denoted 'c' and w^T r_t is 'ϵ'.

3. **Controlled Monte Carlo Analysis:** The core of the mechanistic analysis involved a Monte Carlo study of the Epps–Pulley (EP) objective, which is at the heart of SIGReg.
 - **Mixture Model:** The projected low-frequency component 'c' was modeled as a balanced K-component Gaussian mixture, and the projected residual 'ϵ' as independent Gaussian noise (N(0, σ^2_r)). This resulted in a projected latent marginal as a K-component Gaussian mixture.
 - **Randomized Center Construction:** For various numbers of components (K ∈ {2, 5, 10, 20}), M=500 randomized component-center configurations were generated. The normalized global spread (ρ = std_k[μ_k]/σ) of these centers was systematically varied to isolate its effect on the EP objective.
 - **EP Diagnostic:** For each configuration and ρ value, the shape-only EP diagnostic was computed, which standardizes each batch to control for global location and scale.
 - **Metrics:** The analysis quantified:
 - **Excess EP loss:** The increase in EP loss relative to ρ=0, indicating penalization of separated multi-component structure.
 - **Radial pressure:** The component of the gradient that reduces ρ, showing contractive pressure on the global center spread.
 - **Contraction probability:** The fraction of configurations where this contractive pressure was detectable.
 - **Comparison:** The measured ρ values from Raw LeWM's learned representations on LIBERO-Long were compared against the trends observed in the Monte Carlo analysis.

#### 3.2. Proposed Method: Temporally Centered LeWorldModel (TC-LeWM)

1. **Modification to SIGReg Target:** TC-LeWM maintains the same LeWM encoder and action-conditioned latent predictor. The fundamental change is in the target of the SIGReg loss. Instead of applying SIGReg to the full latent marginal distribution ($Z$), it is applied to the temporally centered residuals ($R$). The residual for each camera view and temporal position is computed as $r_t^{(k)} = z_t^{(k)} - \bar{z}_t^{(k)}$, where $\bar{z}_t^{(k)}$ is the local temporal mean of $z_t^{(k)}$ within a window $W_t$. The training objective becomes $L_{TC} = L_{pred} + \lambda L_{SIGReg}(R)$.
2. **Rationale for Effectiveness:** The paper argues that this modification works because:
 - **No Direct Pressure on Low-Frequency Separation:** Under the multi-component mixture model, the residual EP objective becomes invariant to the component-center spread ρ. This removes the direct contractive pressure on task-dependent low-frequency structure.
 - **Retained Anti-Collapse Property:** Regularizing the residual still prevents representation collapse. If latents become constant within a temporal window, residuals become zero, which is penalized by EP as a degenerate distribution. The combination of prediction and residual regularization encourages non-degenerate, predictable temporal variation.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

1. **Benchmark:** The LIBERO benchmark, comprising four suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), each with ten manipulation tasks, was used. Observations consist of two RGB camera views.
2. **Architecture:** A shared ViT-S/16 encoder processes views independently, extracting CLS tokens and patch tokens. The latent predictor is a block-causal Transformer.
3. **World-Model Training:** The encoder and predictor were trained using the LeWM objective. SIGReg was applied only to the CLS-token representations from both camera views. Checkpoints were selected using the inverse-dynamics-model (iDM) metric.
4. **Downstream Policy Learning:** After world-model training, the encoder was frozen. A task-conditioned flow-matching behavior-cloning policy was trained on the frozen visual representations (CLS and patch tokens). The policy generated 8-step action chunks using a DiT architecture with AdaLN-Zero conditioning and rectified flow matching. Actions were executed open-loop.
5. **Task Aggregation Settings:**
 - **Single-task:** Independent world model and policy for each task.
 - **10-task (suite-wise):** All ten tasks within a suite shared one pipeline.
 - **40-task (unified):** All forty tasks from all four suites shared a single pipeline.
6. **Comparison:** Raw LeWM (SIGReg on marginal CLS tokens) was compared with TC-LeWM (SIGReg on temporally centered CLS token residuals). The temporal window size for TC-LeWM was set to W=8 frames (1.4 s), with an ablation study on window size conducted.
7. **Evaluation Metrics:** Closed-loop task success rates were reported as mean±std over three training seeds, each with three evaluation seeds and 50 rollouts per task. Published results from Diffusion Policy, Octo, and OpenVLA were included as contextual references.
8. **Latent Structure Analysis:**
 - **Temporal Stability:** Distributions of frame-level and window-mean latent norms, and residual-energy fraction were computed.
 - **Organization:** Global and per-task Principal Component Analysis (PCA) projections of concatenated CLS features were visualized, colored by task identity or normalized task progress.
9. **Perturbation Robustness:** The relative latent displacement induced by eight state-preserving visual perturbations (translation, pixel noise, brightness, contrast, rotation, zoom, Gaussian blur, partial occlusion) was measured. Displacement was normalized by the median distance between distinct clean observations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the degradation of marginal SIGReg in multi-task settings and the benefits of the proposed Temporally Centered LeWorldModel (TC-LeWM).

#### 4.1. Degradation of Raw LeWM under Multi-Task Training

Empirical evaluation confirmed that Raw LeWM experiences negative transfer during multi-task learning. On the LIBERO-Long suite, Raw LeWM's average success rate decreased from 40.0% under independent single-task training to 29.9% under 10-task joint training, representing a 10.1 percentage point reduction. Eight of the ten tasks showed deterioration. This indicates that global non-collapse, while maintained, does not guarantee a suitable representation for shared multi-task control.

#### 4.2. Mechanism of Marginal SIGReg Degradation

The Monte Carlo analysis provided a mechanistic explanation for this degradation:
- **Excess EP Loss:** The excess EP loss, relative to a zero spread, increased with the normalized component-center spread (ρ) of randomized multi-component mixtures. This indicates that the EP objective penalizes well-separated multi-component structures.
- **Contractive Radial Pressure:** The corresponding radial gradient signal was consistently positive for ρ values greater than a small-ρ weak-gradient region. A positive signal implies a gradient-descent update component that reduces ρ, causing contraction of the global center spread.
- **Systematic Contraction:** The fraction of randomized configurations exhibiting detectable contractive pressure increased with ρ, suggesting this tendency is systematic across different center geometries.
- **Latent Aliasing:** This contractive pressure can lead to representation aliasing when the global center spread becomes comparable to the within-component standard deviation (ρ ≲ 1). Raw LeWM's learned representations on LIBERO-Long had a ρ of 0.74, placing them in this low-separation region, consistent with the observed aliasing.

#### 4.3. Performance of TC-LeWM on LIBERO Benchmark

TC-LeWM consistently improved downstream control performance across various task aggregation settings:
- **Suite-wise 10-task Training:** TC-LeWM significantly improved success rates over Raw LeWM across all four LIBERO suites, raising the average from 53.2% to 73.6% (a 20.4 percentage point increase). Notable gains were observed on LIBERO-Object (+41.4 points) and LIBERO-Long (+21.9 points). Without external pretraining, TC-LeWM slightly outperformed the published from-scratch Diffusion Policy (73.6% vs. 72.4%) and approached the performance of fine-tuned Octo and OpenVLA (75.1% and 76.5%, respectively).
- **From Single-Task to 10-Task Joint Training:** Unlike Raw LeWM's degradation, TC-LeWM improved from 49.6% (single-task) to 51.8% (10-task joint training) on LIBERO-Long, demonstrating robustness to multi-task interference (+2.2 percentage points change vs. -10.1 for Raw LeWM). TC-LeWM also outperformed Raw LeWM in single-task training (49.6% vs. 40.0%), suggesting benefits beyond just mitigating multi-task interference.
- **Unified 40-Task Training:** Under unified training on all forty LIBERO tasks, TC-LeWM maintained its performance (73.5% average success rate), showing only a minor change (-0.1 points) compared to suite-wise 10-task training. In contrast, Raw LeWM further degraded from 53.2% to 44.4% (-8.8 points). The performance gap between TC-LeWM and Raw LeWM widened to 29.1 percentage points in this setting, indicating TC-LeWM's scalability to larger and more heterogeneous multi-task environments.

#### 4.4. Latent Structure and Perturbation Robustness

Analysis of the learned latent representations revealed distinct differences:
- **Temporal Stability and Latent Organization:** Raw LeWM concentrated latent norms on a thin shell with high short-timescale variation (24% residual-energy fraction) and exhibited extensive task overlap in global PCA projections. Conversely, TC-LeWM showed a broader, multimodal distribution of latent norms, substantially less short-timescale variation (2% residual-energy fraction), and organized representations into contiguous task- and scene-related structures along a low-dimensional manifold in global PCA. Its episode-level normalized cluster spread (ρ) was 2.54, significantly higher than Raw LeWM's 0.74.
- **Intra-Task Temporal Structure:** Raw LeWM displayed fragmented trajectories with substantial overlap between different task progress stages in per-task PCA projections. TC-LeWM, however, exhibited more continuous and progress-aligned organization, where nearby representations consistently corresponded to similar stages of task execution.
- **Robustness to Visual Perturbations:** TC-LeWM demonstrated smaller relative latent displacement across all eight tested state-preserving visual perturbations (e.g., translation, rotation, blur, occlusion) compared to Raw LeWM. This indicates improved robustness to visual changes that preserve the underlying semantic state, suggesting that Raw LeWM's compressed latent geometry makes it more sensitive to out-of-distribution conditioning.

#### 4.5. Ablation on Temporal Window Size

An ablation study on the temporal window size (W) for computing residuals showed that TC-LeWM's effectiveness is not highly sensitive to the precise choice of W (e.g., W=4, 8, 32, or full episode length) as long as it is greater than 1 (W=1 leads to collapse). All tested non-degenerate window sizes substantially outperformed Raw LeWM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides contributions to the field of self-supervised learning for world models, particularly in multi-task scenarios, with several potential impacts:

1. **Identification of a Critical Failure Mode:** The paper rigorously identifies and mechanistically explains a structural incompatibility between marginal Gaussian priors, as enforced by SIGReg in LeWM, and the complex, multi-modal latent structures inherent in multi-task learning. This finding highlights that global non-collapse is insufficient for effective multi-task control if the regularization strategy inadvertently compresses task-relevant distinctions. This understanding is significant for guiding the design of future self-supervised objectives in diverse domains.

2. **Development of a Robust Regularization Method:** The introduction of Temporally Centered SIGReg (TC-SIGReg) offers a simple yet effective modification that addresses the identified failure mode. By applying regularization to temporally centered residuals, the method removes direct pressure on task-dependent low-frequency latent structure while retaining the essential anti-collapse property. This provides a practical and principled route toward more stable and scalable end-to-end multi-task world-model learning.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Scalability:** TC-LeWM achieves substantial improvements in downstream behavior-cloning success rates on the challenging LIBERO benchmark. Its performance under unified 40-task training demonstrates greater scalability and robustness to increased task diversity compared to Raw LeWM. Without external pretraining, TC-LeWM rivals or exceeds the performance of some established baselines, including those requiring pre-trained encoders or fine-tuning large-scale models, suggesting its efficiency and efficacy.

4. **Improved Latent Representations:** The analysis of latent space organization demonstrates that TC-LeWM learns more structured, temporally stable, and perturbation-robust representations. These representations exhibit clearer task separation, more coherent progress-aligned trajectories, and reduced sensitivity to visual noise. Such high-quality latent spaces are crucial for reliable policy learning and generalization in complex environments.

5. **Implications for Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability to learn robust, multi-task world models directly from pixels, without extensive supervision or complex heuristics, is particularly impactful for embodied AI and robotics. It facilitates learning from large, reward-free demonstration datasets, which are becoming increasingly prevalent. The work lays groundwork for developing generalist robot policies that can operate across a wide range of tasks and visual conditions.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper suggests future work on scaling TC-LeWM to even larger reward-free datasets and integrating it with advanced models like Vision-Language Action Models or World-Action-Models for open-world manipulation. These directions indicate the potential for TC-LeWM to contribute to the development of more capable and autonomous intelligent agents.

In summary, this research not only solves a specific problem within LeWM but also offers broader insights into the design of self-supervised learning objectives for multi-task scenarios, with significant potential to advance the field of model-based reinforcement learning and its applications in robotics.
