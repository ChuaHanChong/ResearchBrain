# Long-Horizon Q-Learning: Accurate Value Learning via n-Step Inequalities

> [!summary] Summary
> Long-horizon Q-learning (LQL) introduces a method to improve off-policy value learning by augmenting standard TD error with hinge penalties derived from n-step optimality inequalities. This approach leads to more accurate and stable Q-function estimates, particularly for long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks, with minimal computational overhead.

## Problem

- Deep value-based reinforcement learning suffers from instability due to compounding estimation errors, especially when bootstrapping with off-policy data and function approximation.
- Traditional multi-step backups (e.g., n-step returns) introduce off-policy bias, as they incorporate rewards from potentially suboptimal logged actions, leading to inaccurate value updates.
- Prior methods for long-horizon consistency (like optimality tightening) often require additional Q-evaluations or auxiliary networks, leading to computational inefficiency.

## Method

- **LQL** augments the standard temporal-difference (TD) error with two-sided hinge penalty terms derived from optimality inequalities across trajectories.
- It uses Lower-Bound penalties to prevent Q-values from being too low and Upper-Bound penalties to prevent them from being too high relative to optimal values.
- The method reuses existing network computations for the penalties, incurring a minimal per-update slowdown without needing auxiliary networks or additional forward passes.

## Results

- LQL achieved higher average success rates across various robot locomotion and manipulation tasks, outperforming 1-step TD and traditional n-step TD (e.g., **71.2%** vs. **48.6%** on OGBench for Best-of-N policies).
- It demonstrated a substantial advantage on extremely long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks like `humanoidmaze-giant`, achieving **75.7%** success where 1-step TD failed entirely (**0%**).
- The algorithm introduced only a small average per-update slowdown of **4.7%** and maintained Q-value estimates within analytically valid ranges, preventing runaway overestimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimality inequalities over trajectories provide a principled and computationally efficient way to impose long-horizon consistency on learned Q-values.
> - Asymmetric hinge penalties effectively correct value function inconsistencies without relying on high-variance importance sampling or additional complex architectures.
> - Trajectory length can serve as a robust scaling axis for value-based reinforcement learning when combined with these principled long-horizon constraints.
