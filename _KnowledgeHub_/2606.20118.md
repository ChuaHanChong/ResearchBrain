# Pose6DAug: Physically Plausible Multi-view Object Swapping for Robot Data Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> Pose6DAug is a failure-driven, 3D-aware data augmentation framework for VLA manipulation policies. It reuses a policy's own successful episodes, keeps their action trajectory, and swaps only the manipulated object: a SAM3D-reconstructed target mesh is rendered along a shared world-frame 6D pose trajectory into inpainted backgrounds across all calibrated cameras. Fine-tuning GR00T-1.5 on this data raises success on novel objects by 16.5% relative to the best baseline.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action policies fail on novel object instances whose appearance or geometry deviates from training, and collecting multi-view teleoperation data per failure case is prohibitively costly.
- Simulation-based trajectory adaptation needs manual subtask segmentation and successful rollout execution, so hard object geometries yield no valid demonstrations and coverage collapses.
- Video-generative editing works per view without knowledge of the 3D trajectory, breaking multi-view consistency, object identity, and contact geometry under gripper occlusion and egocentric views.

## Method

- ==Target mesh reconstruction== plus ==6D pose extraction==: an image-to-3D model builds the target mesh in a canonical frame shared with the source, so the source pose trajectory transfers directly and grasp contact is preserved.
- ==Grounded geometric perturbations==, rotation/flip, translation along the gripper approach axis, and vertical rescaling to a graspable height, expand diversity while keeping every configuration physically graspable.
- ==3D mesh-pose guided video composition==: masks erase the source object, a ==video inpainting model== completes background, then depth-ordered layering composites the rendered mesh under restored robot and gripper pixels.

## Results

- On failure episodes of RoboCasa Counter-to-Cabinet, average success reaches **22.8%** versus **16.4%** for the 2D video-editing baseline and **15.8%** for the simulation-based one, with **24.5%** turnover ratio.
- Training on augmented episodes alone over the eight hardest out-of-distribution meshes gives **21.2%** success (**34/160**) versus **15.0%** and **5.7%**, recovering **7 of 8** objects instead of **5** and **2**.
- Across all **200** rollouts, overall success rises to **49.5%** and OOD from **36.9%** to **42.9%**, while the simulation baseline drops below the **47.2%** base policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enforcing multi-view consistency by construction, one mesh rendered at one world-frame pose per camera, beats post-hoc regularization of independently edited views for policy training data.
> - Per-view generative edits do not merely add noise: identity and scale drift across cameras can supply conflicting supervision that degrades in-distribution competence below the base policy.
> - Reusing verified successful trajectories decouples physical validity from rollout search, so augmentation coverage never collapses on hard geometries, but grasp compatibility bounds how different a swap can be.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies represent a significant advancement in general-purpose robotic manipulation, leveraging large-scale visual and linguistic data to enable robots to perform complex tasks. A recurring challenge in the deployment of these policies is their performance degradation when encountering novel objects—those whose appearance or geometry deviates significantly from the training distribution. This issue necessitates fine-tuning, which traditionally involves collecting new, high-quality, multi-view teleoperation data for each specific failure case. This data collection process is costly, time-consuming, and presents a substantial bottleneck to scaling robot learning.

Prior research has explored various strategies to mitigate the need for extensive real-world data collection:
- **Simulation-based trajectory adaptation (e.g., MimicGen):** These methods adapt existing end-effector trajectories in a simulator to generate new demonstrations for novel object poses. While capable of producing physically plausible motions, they often require exhaustive search or rejection sampling to find valid trajectories for complex tasks. Furthermore, they necessitate manual division of trajectories into subtasks and rely on successful rollout execution in simulation. This can limit their coverage and scalability, as valid rollouts may not be achievable for challenging object geometries within a feasible number of attempts.
- **Image-based data augmentation using diffusion models (e.g., VACE):** Advances in video generative models have enabled the synthesis of visual counterfactuals by inpainting objects into recorded video frames. However, these 2D approaches typically operate without explicit knowledge of the underlying 3D scene or object trajectory. Edits are often applied independently per frame and per view, making them prone to generating physically implausible augmentations and failing to maintain consistent object identity, scale, and geometry across multiple camera viewpoints over time. This lack of multi-view consistency is particularly problematic for VLA models, which learn from diverse egocentric and exocentric observations.

Pose6DAug positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing methods. It aims to bridge the gap between kinematic validity and visual photorealism by performing object swapping directly in 3D space. Unlike 2D generative models, it ensures multi-view consistency and physical plausibility, and unlike simulation-based methods, it avoids the reliance on successful simulation rollouts for complex, real-world tasks. The framework leverages existing successful episodes to generate targeted augmentations for specific failure modes, thereby reducing the need for new data collection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, particularly when confronted with novel, out-of-distribution (OOD) objects. The motivation for this objective stems from several critical challenges in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Performance Gap on Novel Objects:** Despite significant advancements, VLA policies frequently exhibit a performance degradation when deployed on object instances that differ in appearance or geometry from their training data. This limits their applicability in dynamic, open-world environments where an infinite variety of objects may be encountered.
2. **Prohibitive Cost of Data Collection:** The standard remedy for improving performance on novel objects—fine-tuning with new demonstrations—is highly resource-intensive. Collecting high-quality, multi-view robotic demonstration data, especially through human teleoperation, requires substantial time, labor, and specialized equipment. This cost prevents the scalable deployment of VLA policies across a wide range of objects and tasks.
3. **Limitations of Existing Augmentation Strategies:**
 - **Simulation-based methods**, while generating physically valid trajectories, are often constrained by the feasibility of simulating complex interactions and the need for exhaustive search or manual intervention to produce successful rollouts. They may fail to generate useful data for particularly challenging object geometries or task configurations, leading to limited coverage.
 - **2D generative models** offer flexibility in synthesizing visual changes but lack inherent 3D awareness. This deficiency results in augmentations that can be physically implausible (e.g., incorrect grasp points) or inconsistent across multiple camera views (e.g., object scale, position, or identity varying between cameras). For VLA models that rely on coherent multi-view observations, such inconsistencies introduce noisy and unreliable training signals.

To address these motivations, Pose6DAug sets forth the following specific objectives:

- **Synthesize Targeted Counterfactual Demonstrations:** Develop a method to transform existing successful manipulation episodes into new demonstrations that specifically target the failure modes of a policy on novel objects, thereby providing relevant training data without new human teleoperation.
- **Ensure Physical Plausibility:** The synthesized object substitutions must maintain kinematic and physical validity. This means the augmented object's movement must align with the robot's kinematics, and the interaction, such as grasping, must appear physically admissible.
- **Guarantee Multi-View Consistency:** The augmented object must appear consistent in terms of its 3D geometry, pose, and identity across all simultaneous camera views (both egocentric and exocentric) and throughout the temporal sequence of the manipulation trajectory. This is critical for providing coherent visual supervision to VLA models.
- **Operate Without New Data Collection:** Leverage the "physical validity" inherent in successful robot trajectories from existing datasets, thus avoiding the resource-intensive process of acquiring new teleoperated demonstrations.
- **Enhance Data Diversity with Grounded Perturbations:** Systematically introduce physically valid geometric variations (e.g., rotations, translations, scaling) to the swapped objects. These perturbations remain grounded in 3D space, ensuring that the augmented object remains in a graspable and plausible configuration, thereby improving policy robustness to minor object variations.

By achieving these objectives, Pose6DAug aims to provide a practical and scalable path for improving the generalization capabilities of VLA policies to a broader spectrum of novel objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Pose6DAug is a 3D-aware data augmentation framework designed to synthesize targeted counterfactual demonstrations from existing successful episodes. Its core principle is to replace a manipulated "source object" with a "target object" in a successful manipulation episode, while rigorously preserving the physical plausibility of the original action trajectory and ensuring multi-view consistency. This is achieved by operating directly in 3D space rather than relying on 2D video editing techniques.

The method targets three core constraints for physically consistent augmentation across multiple camera views:
1. The replacement object must follow a geometrically accurate 3D trajectory reflecting the original manipulation.
2. The replacement must be conditioned on the observed grasp to maintain physical plausibility.
3. The edited object must appear consistently across all camera views and timesteps.

The input to the pipeline includes RGB videos from N calibrated cameras (with known intrinsics and extrinsics), per-timestep object poses, gripper state over time, per-frame object masks, a source object mesh (M_s), and a target object mesh (M_t) reconstructed from an observation. The output consists of augmented multi-view videos where the source object is replaced by the target, preserving robot motion, grasp contact, and cross-view geometric consistency.

The pipeline is decomposed into three main stages:

#### 3.1. Target Mesh Reconstruction and 6D Pose Extraction

- **Target Mesh Reconstruction:** The 3D mesh (M_t) of the target object is reconstructed using an off-the-shelf image-to-3D model, such as SAM3D. The process simulates a realistic scenario where only an observation of the target object is available, rather than using ground-truth simulator assets. Both the source mesh (M_s) and the reconstructed target mesh (M_t) are assumed to reside in a shared, aligned canonical coordinate frame. This alignment allows the direct transfer of the source object's pose trajectory to the target object, which inherently preserves the gripper-object contact configuration of the original grasp (up to subsequent scaling).
- **6D Pose Extraction:** The motion of the manipulated object is represented as a continuous sequence of 6D poses (T_w_o(t) ∈ SE(3)) in a shared world coordinate frame. In simulator-based environments like RoboCasa, ground-truth poses are directly available. For real-world applications, these poses can be obtained from external 6D pose estimators. Multi-view consistency of the object's spatial position and orientation is maintained by deriving each camera's object pose (T_c_i_o(t)) from the world-frame pose and known camera extrinsics (T_c_i_w).

#### 3.2. Object Augmentation

To increase the diversity of the training data and enhance generalization, three 3D-grounded augmentation strategies are applied to the object meshes:

- **Rotation:** The object mesh is randomly rotated or flipped to expose the policy to various plausible orientations. This fosters robustness to different object configurations during grasping.
- **Translation Editing:** The object mesh is translated along the gripper's approach axis, adjusting the relative distance and offset between the gripper and the object. This broadens the range of grasp approaches the model learns from.
- **Scaling:** To account for variations in object dimensions within a category (e.g., different bottle heights), the object mesh is scaled by a factor `s` (e.g., along the vertical axis). This ensures that the target object's size remains within a graspable range relative to the gripper, preserving physically plausible contact after substitution. The resulting scaled mesh is denoted `s · M_t`.

All these perturbations are applied in 3D, ensuring that the augmented object remains in a physically plausible and graspable configuration.

#### 3.3. 3D Mesh-Pose Guided Video Composition

Instead of per-frame generative editing, which can lead to inconsistencies, the augmented video is composed through geometry-guided rendering. This approach inherently maintains multi-view and temporal consistency because all cameras render the same 3D mesh at the same world-frame pose.

- **Background Completion:** For each frame and camera view, the source object is first removed using its provided segmentation mask. The masked region is then filled using a video inpainting model to generate a clean background plate. Generative methods are explicitly limited to this background completion.
- **Object Rendering and Compositing:** At each timestep `t` and for each camera `C_i`, the scaled target mesh (`s · M_t`) is rendered using its specific camera-frame pose (T_c_i_o(t)) and the camera intrinsics (K_i). The rendered object is then composited onto the inpainted background using a strict depth-ordered layering: the clean background plate is placed first, the target object rendering is overlaid, and finally, the robot and gripper regions from the original frame are restored on top using their segmentation masks. This back-to-front compositing accurately preserves occlusion relationships, such as gripper fingers appearing in front of a grasped object.
- **Multi-View Consistency:** By virtue of all camera views rendering the *same* 3D mesh at a *shared world-frame pose*, the appearance, geometry, and contact points of the target object remain geometrically consistent across all egocentric and exocentric views by construction. This eliminates the need for complex cross-view or cross-frame regularization, which is often required in 2D editing approaches.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology was evaluated on the RoboCasa365 benchmark, specifically focusing on the "Counter-to-Cabinet" pick-and-place task. GR00T-1.5 served as the base robot foundation model. Augmentation targets were identified by performing rollouts with the base policy and selecting objects where success rates were ≤30%. For each hard target, 16 augmented episodes were generated, incorporating random rotations, translations, and conditional scaling. The model was fine-tuned on combined datasets or exclusively on augmented hard samples.

**Baselines:**
- **VACE:** A 2D video editing framework applied independently to each camera view, representing approaches without explicit 3D awareness.
- **MimicGen:** A simulation-based trajectory adaptation system, requiring successful rollouts in a simulator to generate valid data. This baseline assesses methods that prioritize physical plausibility via simulation but may suffer from coverage limitations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Pose6DAug focused on its ability to improve task success rates and generalize to novel object instances, measured using Success Rate, Turnover Ratio, and Class Recovery metrics. Experiments were conducted on the RoboCasa365 benchmark using the Counter-to-Cabinet task with GR00T-1.5 as the base policy.

**Q1: Does augmentation recover failures when combined with the original data?**
This question was addressed by evaluating performance exclusively on episodes where the base policy initially failed (Table 1).
- **Success Rate:** Pose6DAug achieved the highest average success rate of 22.8% on failure episodes, outperforming VACE (16.4%) and MimicGen (15.8%). This indicates that Pose6DAug's augmented data is more effective at converting previously failed attempts into successes.
- **Turnover Ratio:** Pose6DAug also demonstrated the highest turnover ratio at 24.5%. This means it enabled the policy to achieve at least one success on a greater fraction of previously intractable object instances, compared to VACE (18.2%) and MimicGen (17.2%). This metric highlights Pose6DAug's capacity to extend the policy's capabilities to new, difficult cases.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** VACE, which applies 2D edits independently per view, showed limited gains and sometimes degraded performance, attributed to inconsistencies in its generated videos. MimicGen, while simulation-based, produced fewer valid augmented episodes due to its reliance on successful simulation rollouts, which were not always achievable for challenging objects.

**Q2: Can augmented data alone teach manipulation of hard objects?**
To isolate the quality of the augmentation, this experiment involved fine-tuning the base policy exclusively on augmented episodes targeting 8 challenging, out-of-distribution object instances (Table 2).
- **Success Rate:** Pose6DAug achieved a 21.2% success rate, significantly outperforming VACE (15.0%) and MimicGen (5.7%). This result demonstrates Pose6DAug's ability to provide high-quality, effective training signals even when learning solely from synthesized demonstrations for difficult objects.
- **Class Recovery:** Pose6DAug successfully recovered 7 out of the 8 hard object instances, meaning it enabled the policy to achieve at least one successful rollout for these previously failing objects. In contrast, VACE recovered 5 instances, and MimicGen recovered only 2.

**Overall Performance on All Episodes (Appendix Table 3):**
An evaluation on the full set of episodes, including both successful and failed instances, showed that Pose6DAug achieved the highest overall success rate (49.5%). It improved out-of-distribution (OOD) performance from 36.9% (base policy) to 42.9%, without compromising in-distribution performance. Conversely, MimicGen's overall performance (46.0%) was slightly below the base policy (47.2%), suggesting that its augmented data could degrade the model's existing capabilities. VACE maintained near-baseline performance with modest OOD gains.

**Qualitative Analysis (Figure 3):**
Qualitative comparisons revealed critical differences:
- **VACE:** Demonstrated identity drift (object appearance changing across frames), multi-view inconsistencies (object shape/color varying between cameras), and failed edits where the original object remained. These issues generate unreliable training signals.
- **MimicGen:** While operating in simulation, it produced physically implausible action trajectories for difficult geometries, resulting in unsuccessful grasps and a lack of usable augmented data for these instances.
- **Pose6DAug:** Maintained consistent identity, geometry, and contact across egocentric and exocentric viewpoints. The replacement object followed a physically valid motion trajectory, resulting in visually coherent augmented episodes suitable for reliable policy learning.

In summary, the findings indicate that Pose6DAug's 3D-grounded augmentation strategy is effective. It not only improves aggregate performance but also specifically extends the policy's capability to previously failed and hard-to-manipulate object instances, achieving a 16.5% relative improvement in success rate over the state-of-the-art baseline on novel objects, while preserving in-distribution performance. The method's ability to generate high-quality, physically plausible, and multi-view consistent data is a key factor in its superior performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Pose6DAug holds several significant implications for the field of robot learning and the deployment of VLA policies:

- **Addressing the Data Bottleneck for VLA Generalization:** The primary significance of Pose6DAug lies in its ability to generate high-quality, targeted data augmentation without requiring new, costly multi-view teleoperation demonstrations. This directly addresses a fundamental bottleneck in scaling VLA policies to generalize to an endless variety of novel objects in real-world environments. By leveraging existing successful episodes, it offers a more scalable and cost-effective approach to improve policy robustness.
- **Ensuring Physical Plausibility and Multi-view Consistency:** Pose6DAug's core contribution is its 3D-aware approach to object swapping. This ensures that synthesized demonstrations maintain both physical admissibility (kinematically consistent object motion, preserved grasp contact) and multi-view consistency (geometric and temporal coherence across all camera views). These properties are critical for VLA policies, which rely on coherent multi-modal inputs, and distinguish Pose6DAug from 2D generative methods that often produce physically implausible or view-inconsistent augmentations.
- **Improved Performance on Novel Objects:** The experimental results demonstrate a notable improvement in success rates (16.5% relative improvement over state-of-the-art baselines on novel objects) and turnover ratios for challenging and out-of-distribution object instances. This empirical validation underscores the practical value of Pose6DAug in enabling robots to robustly manipulate objects they have not encountered during initial training, without degrading in-distribution performance.
- **Bridging Simulation and Real-world Gaps:** By operating on the principles of kinematic validity (similar to simulation-based methods) and incorporating visual photorealism (similar to generative models through background inpainting), Pose6DAug effectively bridges capabilities. It allows for scalable data generation that is grounded in physical laws, a feature often lacking in purely 2D approaches, while avoiding the limitations of simulation rollouts for complex scenarios.
- **Enabling Failure-Driven Learning:** The framework's "failure-driven" nature allows for targeted augmentation. Instead of generating data indiscriminately, it focuses on object instances where the base policy fails, providing precise supervision to overcome specific weaknesses. This targeted approach can lead to more efficient policy improvement.
- **Foundational for Future Robot Learning:** The research highlights that grounding visual augmentation in 3D geometry is a promising direction for scalable and robust robot learning. This principle could inspire future work in synthesizing more complex interactions, environments, and even entire trajectories, thereby broadening the capabilities of generalist robots.

**Potential Impact and Limitations:**
The method could impact various robotic applications requiring robust object manipulation, such as warehouse automation, assistive robotics, and domestic tasks. However, its reliance on the quality of external vision models (e.g., 3D mesh extractors, video inpainting networks) means it inherits their potential failure modes. Discrepancies in object scale or geometry between source and target objects can still lead to physically implausible grasps, suggesting a need for automated affordance matching. Furthermore, accurately rendering complex lighting interactions (shadows, reflections) remains a challenge, pointing to future work in neural relighting techniques.

From a societal impact perspective, while Pose6DAug is beneficial, it introduces augmented data that could potentially lead to unexpected behaviors in robot policies. For safety-critical applications, such as assistive robots for vulnerable populations, these unexpected behaviors must be carefully managed. The authors recommend thorough investigations and robust mitigation strategies to ensure the reliability and safety of systems employing this method.
