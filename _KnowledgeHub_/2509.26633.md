# OmniRetarget: Interaction-Preserving Data Generation for Humanoid Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation and Scene Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> Amazon FAR researchers developed OmniRetarget, a framework that generates high-fidelity, physically plausible, and interaction-preserving kinematic trajectories from human demonstrations for humanoid robots. This approach enables the training of complex loco-manipulation and scene interaction skills with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and a minimal reinforcement learning formulation.

## Problem

- The scarcity of high-quality, physically plausible training data severely bottlenecks deep reinforcement learning for complex humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation.
- Current motion retargeting techniques struggle with the embodiment gap, producing physically implausible artifacts and often failing to preserve critical human-object/environment interactions.
- Poor reference data forces extensive and fragile reward engineering in downstream reinforcement learning, complicating policy training and reducing robustness.

## Method

- Introduces **OmniRetarget**, an interaction-preserving data generation engine leveraging an interaction mesh and a customized Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) solver.
- Employs Laplacian deformation energy minimization alongside hard kinematic constraints, including collision avoidance, joint limits, and foot contact stability, to ensure physically plausible and interaction-preserving retargeted motions.
- Incorporates a systematic data augmentation pipeline to efficiently generate diverse training scenarios for varying object configurations and terrain from single human demonstrations.

## Results

- Enabled learning of complex, long-horizon whole-body loco-manipulation and scene interaction skills on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid, including dynamic platform climbing and wall-flips, all demonstrating zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- Achieved superior kinematic quality compared to baselines, exhibiting near-zero penetration (0.00-0.01 duration) and completely eliminating foot skating (0 duration, 0 max velocity) across various tasks.
- Resulted in significantly higher success rates (**82.20%**-**94.73%**) and lower variance for downstream reinforcement learning policies compared to those trained on data from prior retargeting methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating high-fidelity, artifact-free reference motions for humanoid robots necessitates explicitly modeling and preserving robot-object-terrain interactions through an interaction mesh and hard kinematic constraints.
> - Providing inherently high-quality, physically plausible reference data allows for a dramatically simplified reinforcement learning formulation, reducing the need for extensive reward engineering.
> - Systematic data augmentation, which re-solves the constrained retargeting optimization for varied scene elements, is effective for generating diverse training examples from limited human demonstrations and improving policy generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "OmniRetarget" addresses a fundamental challenge in enabling humanoid robots to perform complex whole-body locomotion and manipulation tasks: the acquisition of high-quality, physically plausible training data. A prevailing approach in robotics for teaching complex skills to humanoids involves retargeting human motions to serve as kinematic references for reinforcement learning (RL) policies. However, this paradigm encounters significant limitations.

Existing motion retargeting pipelines often struggle with the "embodiment gap" – the substantial morphological differences between humans and robots. This gap frequently leads to the generation of physically implausible artifacts in the retargeted motions, such as foot-skating and penetrations into objects or the environment. Furthermore, many conventional retargeting methods predominantly focus on kinematic matching and typically neglect the intricate human-object and human-environment interactions that are critical for expressive and functional locomotion and loco-manipulation.

Researchers have explored two main strategies to overcome these challenges. The first is teleoperation, where a human operator's motions are retargeted in real-time to control the robot. While this approach benefits from immediate human adaptation, it is labor-intensive and does not scale efficiently for generating large datasets. The second strategy, which "OmniRetarget" focuses on, is offline interaction retargeting. This aims to holistically adapt both the human's motion and their scene interactions to a robot's specific embodiment.

The paper identifies that most existing offline retargeting methods, such as PHC, GMR, and VideoMimic, rely on unconstrained or softly-penalized optimization schemes. This often results in suboptimal motions that contain artifacts and lack explicit consideration for interaction preservation, instead using simpler keypoint matching. The resulting lower-quality reference motions, in turn, complicate the training of downstream RL policies, often necessitating extensive reward engineering or manual data cleaning. While some methods, like IMMA, have utilized interaction meshes, they have not been open-sourced and typically overlook crucial aspects such as kinematic limits and interactions with the environment or manipulated objects.

In contrast to these prior approaches, "OmniRetarget" positions itself as a solution to this data bottleneck by introducing a framework that explicitly models and preserves the crucial spatial and contact relationships through an "interaction mesh" within a constrained optimization formulation. This approach aims to generate kinematically feasible, artifact-free trajectories that intrinsically capture task-relevant interactions. Furthermore, the work extends beyond basic retargeting by developing a systematic data augmentation pipeline, which is an area less explored for whole-body loco-manipulation compared to robotic manipulation. By providing high-quality, interaction-preserving data, "OmniRetarget" seeks to simplify and improve the efficiency of RL policy training, aligning with recent findings that high-fidelity references significantly reduce the need for complex reward engineering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent data bottleneck that impedes the development of humanoid robots capable of complex whole-body scene and object interaction tasks. While deep reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated success in robot control, applying it to high-dimensional humanoid systems is challenging due to the difficulty of efficient exploration and the sensitivity of learning to reward engineering.

The overarching goal is to enable humanoid robots to perform complex, long-horizon, and agile loco-manipulation skills in diverse environments. To achieve this, the authors identify several key objectives:

1. **Address the Embodiment Gap and Data Quality Issues:** A central objective is to overcome the limitations of current motion retargeting pipelines, which produce physically implausible motions (e.g., foot-skating, penetration) due to the significant morphological differences between humans and robots. These artifacts render existing references less effective for RL training, often necessitating compensatory reward engineering. The framework aims to generate kinematically feasible and physically plausible trajectories.
2. **Preserve Essential Human-Object and Human-Environment Interactions:** Existing methods frequently neglect the critical spatial and contact relationships between the human, objects, and the environment. A key objective is to explicitly model and preserve these "interactions" in the retargeted robot motions, which are essential for expressive locomotion and complex loco-manipulation tasks.
3. **Develop an Interaction-Preserving Retargeting Framework with Hard Constraints:** The research aims to introduce a novel framework, OMNIRETARGET, that utilizes an "interaction mesh" to model these relationships. Crucially, this framework must incorporate hard physical constraints, such as collision avoidance, joint limits, velocity limits, and foot contact stability, to ensure the physical feasibility of the generated motions.
4. **Enable Systematic Data Augmentation from Single Demonstrations:** Data collection for complex robot tasks is costly and time-consuming. A significant objective is to develop a pipeline that can automatically augment a single human demonstration into a diverse, large-scale dataset. This augmentation should span various robot embodiments, object configurations, and terrain features, thereby efficiently expanding the coverage of training scenarios.
5. **Facilitate Minimal-Reward RL Training:** With high-quality, artifact-free reference motions, the motivation is to simplify downstream RL policy training. The objective is to demonstrate that policies can be successfully trained using a minimal reward formulation and simple domain randomization, thereby reducing the burden of extensive reward engineering and hyperparameter tuning often required when dealing with noisy references.
6. **Achieve Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate that RL policies trained on the high-quality, augmented data generated by OMNIRETARGET can be successfully transferred to a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1) in a zero-shot manner, enabling the execution of complex, dynamic, and long-horizon tasks in the real world.
7. **Contribute to Open Science:** The authors intend to publicly release all code, retargeted datasets, and trained policies to foster accelerated progress in humanoid robotics research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OMNIRETARGET is presented as an interaction-preserving data generation engine designed to transform human demonstrations into high-quality kinematic references for humanoid whole-body control. The methodology encompasses three main components: interaction-preserving motion retargeting, systematic data augmentation, and a minimal formulation for downstream reinforcement learning (RL) training.

#### 3.1. Interaction-Preserving Motion Retargeting

The core of OMNIRETARGET's retargeting process is based on an "interaction mesh" and a constrained optimization formulation.

- **Interaction Mesh Construction:** The interaction mesh is a volumetric structure whose vertices consist of user-defined key robot or human joints, along with points sampled from manipulated objects and the environment. Delaunay tetrahedralization is employed to create this mesh. To accurately preserve contact relationships, object and environment surfaces are sampled more densely than body joints. The method is robust to precise keypoint placement, requiring only semantically consistent correspondences between human and robot (e.g., hand to hand).
- **Optimization Objectives:**
 - The primary objective is to minimize the Laplacian deformation energy ($E_L$) between the source (human) and target (robot) interaction meshes. The Laplacian coordinate for a keypoint is defined as the difference between the point and the weighted average of its neighbors. The deformation energy quantifies the change in these Laplacian coordinates between the human and robot meshes.
 - An additional cost term `||q_t - q_{t-1}||_Q^2` is included to encourage temporal smoothness in the robot's configuration.
- **Hard Kinematic Constraints:** The optimization seeks the robot configuration `q_t` (floating base pose and joint angles) that minimizes the objective while satisfying several hard physical constraints:
 - **Collision Avoidance:** `φ_j(q_t) ≥ 0`, where `φ_j` is the signed distance function for each potential collision pair.
 - **Joint Limits:** `q_min ≤ q_t ≤ q_max` to ensure joint angles remain within the robot's physical capabilities.
 - **Velocity Limits:** `v_min ⋅ dt ≤ q_t - q_{t-1} ≤ v_max ⋅ dt` to constrain joint velocities.
 - **Foot Skating Prevention:** `p_F_t = p_F_{t-1}` for any stance foot (defined as a foot with horizontal velocity below 1 cm/s in the source motion).
- **Solver:** The problem is a constrained, non-convex program solved sequentially for each timestep using a customized Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP)-style solver. In each iteration, the objective is quadratically approximated, and the hard constraints are linearized around the current solution. The optimization for frame `t` is warm-started with the optimal solution from the previous frame `q*_{t-1}` to ensure temporal consistency and accelerate convergence. The implementation utilizes the automatic differentiation framework in Drake to handle derivatives involving quaternion-based floating base orientations.
- **Generalizability:** The pipeline is designed to be highly general, adapting to different robot embodiments (e.g., Unitree G1, H1, Booster T1) by modifying keypoint correspondences and the robot's collision model. It supports various interaction types, including robot-object interactions (from OMOMO), robot-terrain interactions (from in-house MoCap), and robot-only motions (from LAFAN1).

#### 3.2. Terrain, Object Shape, and Spatial Augmentation

A key aspect of OMNIRETARGET is its capability for systematic data augmentation, generating diverse training examples from a single human demonstration. For each augmented scenario, the optimization problem is re-solved with fixed source keypoints and augmented target scene components.

- **Robot-Object Augmentation:**
 - **Spatial Configuration:** Object initial poses are modified by applying translations and rotations. These offsets are blended with the original object motion using an exponential decay schedule to create perturbed object trajectories.
 - **Shape Augmentation:** The three dimensions of the object can be scaled.
 - **Interaction Mesh in Object Frame:** For robot-object interactions, the interaction mesh is constructed in the object's local coordinate frame. This ensures that Laplacian coordinates, which encode relative spatial relationships, remain invariant to the object's global rotation and translation, preserving the intended interaction geometry.
 - **Anchoring Constraints:** To prevent trivial rigid transformations of the robot along with the object, additional cost terms and constraints (`||q_t - q*_{t_bar}||_W`) are introduced. These terms penalize deviations of specific robot parts (e.g., lower body) from the nominal trajectory, encouraging the robot to discover new coordination patterns.
- **Robot-Terrain Augmentation:** Environmental features such as platform height and depth can be scaled. When terrain is elevated, a grid of points on the ground surface is uniformly sampled into the interaction mesh to encourage stable ground contact.

#### 3.3. RL Training with Minimal Formulation

OMNIRETARGET's generation of high-quality, artifact-free references allows for a simplified RL training setup.

- **Observations:** The observation space is purely proprioceptive, meaning the agent receives no explicit scene or object information. It includes reference joint position/velocity, reference pelvis position/orientation error, robot's pelvis linear/angular velocity, joint position/velocity, and the policy's action from the previous timestep. For agile motions, pelvis linear position error and velocity are masked out due to potential state estimation unreliability.
- **Rewards:** Only five reward terms are used, directly adopting weights and hyperparameters from BeyondMimic without tuning:
 - Body Tracking (DeepMimic-style, for position, orientation, linear, and angular velocity).
 - Object Tracking (DeepMimic-style, for object position and orientation, where applicable).
 - Action Rate penalty.
 - Soft Joint Limit penalty.
 - Self-Collision binary penalty.
- **Termination:** Training episodes terminate if body tracking deviations exceed a threshold or, for object loco-manipulation, if the object deviates significantly (e.g., >1.0m or >45°) from its reference trajectory.
- **Domain Randomization:** A minimal set of four domain randomization terms are applied: torso Center of Mass (CoM) position, joint default position, random pushes, and observation noise. Object physical parameters (mass, CoM, inertia, shape) are also randomized. This is a significantly reduced set compared to many prior works.
- **Policy Training:** Similar motions are grouped to train multi-task policies (e.g., all box-moving motions share a single policy), while others use one policy per reference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of OMNIRETARGET is presented through two main avenues: demonstrating complex whole-body scene-object interaction capabilities and a quantitative benchmark against prior retargeting pipelines.

#### 4.1. Whole-Body Scene-Object-Interaction

- **Agile Loco-Manipulation:** OMNIRETARGET successfully enabled RL policies to learn agile, whole-body motions for complex scene interactions and loco-manipulation tasks in simulation, with subsequent zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Unitree G1 humanoid.
 - The robot demonstrated diverse behaviors, including natural box-carrying (retargeted from OMOMO), dynamically climbing a 0.9m-high platform (70% of its height), and crawling over slopes, all exhibiting accurate contact sequences.
 - A **long-horizon (30-second), multi-stage parkour course** was achieved, involving carrying a 4.6 kg chair, using it as a stepstone to climb a platform, leaping off, and performing a roll to absorb landing impact. This showcased the framework's ability to produce precise and versatile reference motions for complex sequences.
 - A **high-dynamic wall-flip motion** was demonstrated on hardware, with the robot completing the flip in approximately 0.5 seconds, reaching a peak angular velocity of 15 rad/s and a linear velocity of 3.5 m/s. This was achieved by relaxing specific termination and reward conditions for end-effector position and foot orientation to accommodate the robot's rigid foot mechanics, resulting in a 5/5 success rate in real-world experiments.
- **Sim-to-Real with Augmented Data:** The augmented motions generated by OMNIRETARGET proved effective for training and deployment.
 - A single nominal motion could be generalized into tasks like box-picking with varied shapes and positions, and platform climbing at different heights. These augmented motions transferred to hardware successfully without reward tuning.
 - In contrast, policies trained solely with domain randomization (perturbing object shapes/poses without kinematic augmentation) performed poorly due to the policies struggling to explore sufficiently far from the nominal reference.
 - Quantitatively, training and evaluating on the full augmented dataset yielded a 79.1% success rate, comparable to 82.2% when evaluating only on nominal motions, indicating that kinematic augmentation significantly expands task coverage without notable performance degradation.

#### 4.2. Benchmark Against Prior Retargeting Pipelines

OMNIRETARGET was compared against widely-used open-source baselines: PHC, GMR, and VideoMimic. The evaluation utilized a dataset comprising 2.78 hours of box-carrying motions (OMOMO), 1 hour of in-house MoCap data, and 4.6 hours of LAFAN1 motions.

- **Kinematics Quality (Table II):** OMNIRETARGET consistently outperformed baselines across most kinematic metrics.
 - **Penetration:** OMNIRETARGET exhibited minimal penetration (0.00-0.01 normalized duration, 1.34-1.37 cm maximum depth). Baselines showed substantially higher penetration (e.g., GMR with 0.83 duration and 8.50 cm max depth for robot-object tasks). The minor penetrations in OMNIRETARGET were attributed to constraint linearization and could be managed by RL.
 - **Foot Skating:** OMNIRETARGET achieved zero foot skating. Baselines, in contrast, consistently showed foot skating (e.g., VideoMimic with 0.12 duration and 1.50 cm/s max velocity for robot-object).
 - **Contact Preservation:** OMNIRETARGET demonstrated high contact preservation (0.96 for robot-object, 0.72 for robot-terrain). While GMR achieved the highest contact preservation for robot-object (0.99), this often resulted in high penetration, as the robot's hands were driven inside the object due to keypoint matching.
 - **Locomotion (LAFAN1):** For pure locomotion, OMNIRETARGET's motions exhibited fewer physical artifacts compared to the publicly available Unitree LAFAN1 dataset, achieving 100% success rate with better hard constraint satisfaction (0 penetration/skating duration vs. 0.09/0.06 for Unitree).
 - **Artifacts (Fig. 7):** Visual comparisons showed baselines consistently generating significant artifacts such as penetration, lack of contact, and motions that were not interaction-preserving.
- **Downstream RL Performance (Table II):** The quality of retargeted motions directly correlated with downstream RL success rates.
 - OMNIRETARGET achieved the highest success rates across tasks, exceeding baselines by over 10% and exhibiting lower variance, indicating more stable learning.
 - For robot-object interaction, OMNIRETARGET achieved 82.20% success, while baselines ranged from 3.85% (VideoMimic) to 71.28% (PHC).
 - For robot-terrain interaction, OMNIRETARGET achieved 94.73% success, compared to baselines ranging from 51.75% (VideoMimic) to 78.94% (GMR).
 - VideoMimic showed the weakest interaction preservation and lowest RL success, attributed to its soft collision penalties conflicting with keypoint matching and a coarse collision model. The results underscore that both contact preservation and penetration reduction are critical for generalizing RL policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of OMNIRETARGET signifies a notable contribution to the field of humanoid robotics, with several potential impacts:

- **Addressing the Data Bottleneck:** The primary significance of this work lies in its direct approach to solving the critical data bottleneck in humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation. By generating high-quality, interaction-preserving, and physically plausible kinematic trajectories, OMNIRETARGET provides a robust foundation for training complex robot skills.
- **Enhancing RL Efficiency and Robustness:** The study demonstrates that superior reference motion quality fundamentally simplifies downstream reinforcement learning. By producing artifact-free data, OMNIRETARGET enables RL policies to be trained with a minimal reward formulation and reduced domain randomization, eliminating the need for extensive reward engineering and manual data cleaning often associated with lower-quality references. This can lead to more efficient and stable learning processes.
- **Enabling Complex Humanoid Skills:** The ability to generate accurate kinematic references for intricate human-object and human-environment interactions allows humanoid robots to learn and perform complex, long-horizon, and dynamic skills, such as parkour-style maneuvers, precise object manipulation, and agile terrain traversal, which were previously difficult to achieve. The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of these skills on a physical robot highlights the practical applicability of the framework.
- **Scalable Data Generation:** The systematic data augmentation pipeline is a key innovation, allowing a single human demonstration to be expanded into a diverse, large-scale dataset across various robot embodiments, object configurations, and environmental features. This significantly reduces the time and cost associated with data collection, making it more feasible to generate data for a broader range of real-world scenarios.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The emphasis on hard kinematic constraints and interaction preservation in the retargeted motions contributes to bridging the simulation-to-reality gap. The generated data is inherently more consistent with real-world physics, facilitating robust zero-shot transfer of policies from simulation to physical hardware.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to publicly releasing the code, retargeted datasets, and trained policies will provide valuable resources for the wider robotics community. This open-source approach has the potential to accelerate research and development in humanoid control, benchmarking, and the exploration of new capabilities for agile and versatile robots.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work lays groundwork for future exploration into handling noisier motion sources, such as video data, and developing more autonomous visuomotor policies. It also provides a robust baseline for evaluating more advanced RL formulations.

Ultimately, OMNIRETARGET represents a paradigm shift toward principled data generation in robotics, moving away from post-hoc compensation for data deficiencies. This approach is poised to contribute to the development of more capable and adaptable humanoid robots that can seamlessly interact with complex human-centric environments.
