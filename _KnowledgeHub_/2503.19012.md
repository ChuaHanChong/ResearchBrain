# DiffV2IR: Visible-to-Infrared Diffusion Model via Vision-Language Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northwestern Polytechnical University developed DiffV2IR, a visible-to-infrared diffusion model that generates high-quality, semantically-aware infrared images by integrating progressive learning and vision-language understanding. The framework achieved state-of-the-art performance, with an FID of 70.29 on M3FD and 39.99 on FLIR-aligned datasets, while also introducing the large-scale IR-500K dataset to address data scarcity.

## Problem

- Existing visible-to-infrared (V2IR) translation methods struggled to generate semantically accurate infrared images, often overlooking the thermal properties tied to scene semantics.
- Models faced difficulty generalizing across diverse infrared imagery due to variations in wavelength spectrums, camera sensors, and environmental conditions.
- A critical lack of large-scale, diverse, and publicly available infrared datasets impeded the development of robust, data-hungry V2IR models.

## Method

- Introduced **DiffV2IR**, a ==latent diffusion model== framework incorporating a ==Progressive Learning Module== and a ==Vision-Language Understanding Module==.
- Compiled the extensive IR-500K dataset, featuring 500,000 infrared images and 70,000 visible-infrared pairs, to provide sufficient training data.
- Employed a three-phase ==Progressive Learning Module== to adapt a pre-trained diffusion model to infrared characteristics, cross-modal translation, and specific style refinement.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art quantitative performance, obtaining an FID of **70.29** on the M3FD dataset and **39.99** on the FLIR-aligned dataset, outperforming fifteen other methods.
- Demonstrated superior qualitative results, effectively correcting for halo effects around light sources, preserving fine details and structure in low-light visible conditions, and ensuring stronger semantic consistency in generated infrared images.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Progressive Learning Module and Vision-Language Understanding Module components contribute positively to improved V2IR translation quality and accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating vision-language embeddings and segmentation maps into diffusion models allows for generating physically plausible and semantically consistent infrared images, moving beyond pixel-level transformations.
> - A progressive learning strategy, from general infrared representation to cross-modal mapping and stylization, effectively adapts pre-trained diffusion models to specialized, data-scarce cross-spectral tasks.
> - Addressing data scarcity through a comprehensive, curated dataset (IR-500K) is fundamental for advancing robust and generalizable models in niche domains like visible-to-infrared translation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image-to-image translation has seen rapid advancements, driven largely by generative adversarial networks (GANs) and more recently by diffusion models. Visible-to-infrared (V2IR) image translation, however, presents unique challenges that differentiate it from generic image translation tasks (e.g., style transfer, image editing). This paper positions itself as a significant contribution to overcoming these specific hurdles, thereby pushing the boundaries of cross-modal image synthesis.

Historically, V2IR has been approached as a standard image-to-image synthesis problem using methods like VAEs and GANs. Early attempts focused on capturing low-level semantics such as edges, structural similarity, or geometry information to guide the translation process. While these methods offered initial improvements, they often fell short in fully exploiting crucial semantic information and struggled with the high diversity and variability of infrared imagery, which is influenced by numerous factors like wavelength spectrum, camera sensors, and environmental conditions (e.g., temperature, shadows).

The advent of diffusion models, such as Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) and Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs), has revolutionized image generation due to their superior sample quality and stable training processes. This work capitalizes on this advancement by building upon pre-trained latent diffusion models (specifically Stable Diffusion). However, it acknowledges that general diffusion models lack inherent understanding of the infrared modality, making direct application suboptimal. DiffV2IR addresses this gap by specifically adapting diffusion models for V2IR, introducing novel modules for progressive learning and vision-language understanding.

Furthermore, the paper tackles the critical issue of data scarcity in the infrared domain. Unlike visible images, large, diverse, and publicly available infrared datasets are rare, hindering the development of robust, generalizable models. By collecting and curating the extensive IR-500K dataset, the authors contribute a vital resource that can catalyze future research in this specialized area. This dataset contribution is particularly noteworthy in the current era of "foundation models" which thrive on vast amounts of diverse data.

Finally, the integration of vision-language understanding (VLUM) aligns with the broader trend in AI research towards multi-modal models that combine visual and textual information. This approach allows the model to leverage rich semantic context (e.g., object types, environmental conditions) that is crucial for generating physically plausible and semantically consistent infrared images, moving beyond mere pixel-level transformations. In essence, DiffV2IR moves V2IR translation from a purely image-pixel manipulation task to a more intelligent, context-aware synthesis problem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an advanced framework for visible-to-infrared (V2IR) image translation that can generate high-quality, semantically-aware infrared images from visible inputs. This overarching goal is driven by the inherent challenges of V2IR, which the authors meticulously identify and aim to overcome:

1. **Semantic-aware Translation (Challenge 1):** Infrared imaging visualizes emitted infrared radiation, which is fundamentally tied to object temperatures and radiant existence. Consequently, V2IR translation is highly dependent on a deep understanding of scene semantics, including object categories, materials, and contextual factors like solar radiation, lighting, and shadows, all of which influence thermal signatures. Existing methods often treat V2IR as a general image translation problem, neglecting this crucial semantic dependency. The motivation here is to imbue the translation model with "scene understanding" to produce physically consistent infrared outputs.

2. **Managing Diverse Wavelength Spectrums and Camera Variations (Challenge 2):** Infrared imagery can vary significantly due to different wavelength ranges (e.g., NIR, SWIR, MWIR, LWIR) and differences in camera sensors. This diversity leads to wide variations in pixel values for visually similar scenes, making it difficult for models to generalize and produce stable, high-quality translations across different infrared styles. The motivation is to develop a robust model capable of handling this inherent variability and adapting to specific infrared imaging styles.

3. **Addressing Data Scarcity (Challenge 3):** Unlike the abundance of visible image datasets, publicly available infrared datasets are limited in scale and diversity due to the specialized nature and cost of infrared cameras. This scarcity severely hampers the training of large, data-hungry models (like diffusion models) for V2IR. The motivation is to compile a comprehensive infrared dataset to enable the development of more capable and generalizable V2IR models.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a novel framework, DiffV2IR, designed to explicitly tackle each challenge, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in V2IR translation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DiffV2IR framework is built upon a novel integration of a progressive learning strategy, vision-language understanding, and a newly compiled large-scale dataset, all leveraging the power of latent diffusion models.

#### 3.1. Foundation: Latent Diffusion Model and IR-500K Dataset

- **Preliminary:** The framework is based on Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs), specifically fine-tuning a pre-trained Stable Diffusion (SD) model. LDMs operate in a compressed latent space, making them computationally efficient for high-resolution image generation. They predict noise added to a latent representation of an image, which is then iteratively removed to generate a clear image, conditioned on an input `c`.
- **IR-500K Dataset:** To combat data scarcity, the authors assembled IR-500K, an extensive dataset comprising 500,000 infrared images, collected by integrating almost all substantial publicly accessible infrared datasets and supplementary online data. This dataset offers broad diversity in scene types, object categories, and camera perspectives (e.g., natural landscapes, cityscapes, aerial views, surveillance). Additionally, 70,000 visible-infrared image pairs were meticulously selected and processed for precise cross-modal training.

#### 3.2. Progressive Learning Module (PLM)

PLM is a multi-stage learning strategy designed to gradually instill infrared knowledge and V2IR translation capabilities into the diffusion model, adapting it from a visible-image-centric foundation to a specialized V2IR translator.

- **Phase #1: Infrared Representation Internalization:** This initial phase establishes foundational knowledge of infrared imaging properties. A Stable Diffusion model is fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on the entire IR-500K dataset, prompted with the phrase "an infrared image." This process teaches the model to associate infrared characteristics with the term "infrared" without significantly altering the pre-trained model's extensive visible image generation capabilities.
- **Phase #2: Cross-Modal Transformation Learning:** In this phase, the model learns the direct mapping between visible and infrared modalities. The 70,000 visible-infrared image pairs are used to train the diffusion model, enabling it to convert visible images into infrared ones while preserving structural integrity. This phase aims to build a generalized V2IR translation capability, strengthening the model's ability to handle diverse inputs.
- **Phase #3: Stylization Refinement:** To address the challenge of diverse infrared styles (due to varying wavelengths and camera sensors), this final phase refines the model's output to adapt to specific spatio-temporal and environmental variations. A smaller dataset of visible-infrared image pairs in a desired style is used to fine-tune the model, allowing for style-controllable V2IR translation.

#### 3.3. Vision-Language Understanding Module (VLUM)

VLUM is integrated into the diffusion process to achieve semantic-aware and structure-preserving V2IR translation, crucial for producing physically accurate infrared images.

- **Vision-Language Embeddings:** Blip is used to generate detailed vision-language embeddings from visible images. These embeddings capture not only key object semantics but also critical contextual information (e.g., weather, lighting, shadows) that influences thermal radiation. A CLIP-based text encoder processes this descriptive text, and a cross-attention mechanism incorporates these encoded tokens into the denoising U-Net, guiding the generation process semantically.
- **Segmentation Map Embeddings:** To ensure structural integrity, embeddings from segmentation maps generated by SAM (Segment Anything Model) are incorporated. These embeddings provide rich layout and structural knowledge. The segmentation map and the conditional visible image are concatenated with the noise map after latent encoding, and extra input channels are added to the first convolutional layer of the denoising U-Net.
- **Classifier-free Guidance:** The framework employs classifier-free guidance to enhance control over the generated images. It combines conditional and unconditional outputs, with specific guidance scales (`sV` for visible image, `sS` for segmentation map, `sT` for vision-language) that are optimized to balance the intensity of each conditioning, allowing for fine-grained control over the translation process. During training, conditionings are randomly dropped to facilitate unconditional learning.

The DiffV2IR pipeline orchestrates these components: PLM progressively trains the diffusion model, and VLUM provides rich multi-modal conditioning (visible image, semantic descriptions, structural maps) to guide the V2IR translation toward high-quality, semantically accurate, and structurally consistent infrared images.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling quantitative and qualitative results that demonstrate the superior performance of DiffV2IR over existing state-of-the-art methods in visible-to-infrared (V2IR) image translation.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons

DiffV2IR was rigorously evaluated against fifteen contemporary methods, including GAN-based approaches (e.g., Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, DR-AVIT) and diffusion-based models (e.g., InstructPix2Pix, ControlNet, PID, Pix2PixTurbo), on two challenging datasets: M3FD and FLIR-aligned. The standard metrics used were Fréchet Inception Distance (FID↓), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR↑), and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM↑).

- **M3FD Dataset:** DiffV2IR achieved the best performance across all three metrics: an FID of 70.29, PSNR of 19.30, and SSIM of 0.6620. This significantly surpasses the second-best results, such as InstructPix2Pix (FID 81.64, PSNR 17.92, SSIM 0.6328) and Pix2PixTurbo (FID 98.12, PSNR 16.80, SSIM 0.5964). GAN-based methods generally showed lower performance, and even other diffusion models like ControlNet and T2I-Adapter, despite their advancements, did not match DiffV2IR's capability, often struggling with either fidelity (FID) or structural preservation (SSIM).
- **FLIR-aligned Dataset:** Similarly, on the FLIR-aligned dataset, DiffV2IR maintained its leading position with an FID of 39.99, PSNR of 18.63, and SSIM of 0.4658. While Pix2Pix achieved the highest PSNR in this dataset, its FID was significantly higher (98.81), indicating lower perceptual quality. PID and InstructPix2Pix showed reasonable performance but were still outperformed by DiffV2IR. The results consistently demonstrated DiffV2IR's ability to produce more realistic and structurally accurate infrared images, even in a less complex scenario where GANs typically perform better.

The authors attribute the superior performance of DiffV2IR, InstructPix2Pix, and Pix2PixTurbo to their approach of directly working with visible images as conditions, rather than converting them into noise or feature maps, which often leads to better preservation of original image information.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Comparisons

Qualitative results (Figure 3 and Figure II/III in supplementary) visually confirmed DiffV2IR's excellence, especially in addressing common pitfalls of other methods:

- **Halo Effect Correction:** DiffV2IR accurately handles halos around light sources. While visible images show high brightness around light sources, infrared images should only highlight objects emitting heat. Many SOTA methods incorrectly translate bright visible halos into infrared halos, whereas DiffV2IR produces physically credible infrared images by understanding thermal radiation principles.
- **Structure and Detail Preservation in Low-Light:** In challenging low-light visible conditions, several competing methods fail to maintain image structure and details in the translated infrared output. DiffV2IR, leveraging its enhanced infrared knowledge and vision-language understanding, successfully preserves these crucial elements, generating detailed and coherent infrared images.
- **Semantic Consistency:** The generated images by DiffV2IR are shown to be more semantically consistent with the visible input and align better with physical principles of infrared imaging compared to other methods that often produce ambiguous or degraded outputs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of both the Progressive Learning Module (PLM) and Vision-Language Understanding Module (VLUM):

- **Progressive Learning Module (PLM):** Experiments revealed that each of the three phases of PLM (infrared representation internalization, cross-modal transformation learning, and stylization refinement) positively contributes to the overall V2IR translation performance. The full three-phase PLM (✓ ✓ ✓) achieved the best results among configurations without VLUM, underscoring the benefits of gradual knowledge acquisition.
- **Vision-Language Understanding Module (VLUM):** Integrating VLUM components (segmentation maps and vision-language embeddings) further enhanced performance. Both individual embeddings improved quality, but their combined use in the unified DiffV2IR framework yielded the highest performance across all metrics, validating the importance of multi-modal semantic and structural guidance.
- **Hyper-parameters:** Tuning denoising steps and classifier-free guidance scales (sT, sV, sS) demonstrated that specific settings (e.g., 100 denoising steps, sT=7.5, sV=1.5, sS=1.5) lead to an optimal balance between inference consumption and translation quality.

These findings collectively establish DiffV2IR as a highly effective and robust framework for V2IR translation, validated by both quantitative superiority and qualitative realism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DiffV2IR framework represents a significant advancement in the challenging domain of visible-to-infrared (V2IR) image translation, offering substantial implications across various scientific and applied fields.

- **Breakthrough in Cross-Modal Image Generation:** By explicitly addressing the unique challenges of V2IR – semantic dependence, spectral diversity, and data scarcity – DiffV2IR pushes the boundaries of cross-modal image generation. Its ability to produce high-quality, semantically consistent, and physically plausible infrared images from visible inputs marks a crucial step beyond generic image translation tasks.
- **Enhanced Semantic Understanding in Image Synthesis:** The Vision-Language Understanding Module (VLUM) highlights the critical role of multi-modal semantics (both textual descriptions and structural segmentation maps) in achieving realistic cross-spectral translation. This approach provides a blueprint for integrating rich contextual understanding into other complex image synthesis problems, moving generative models towards more intelligent, context-aware content creation.
- **Robustness and Generalization through Progressive Learning:** The Progressive Learning Module (PLM) demonstrates an effective strategy for adapting powerful pre-trained foundation models (like Stable Diffusion) to specialized, data-scarce domains. This multi-stage knowledge learning, transitioning from broad infrared representation to specific stylization, enhances the model's ability to generalize across diverse infrared appearances and environmental conditions, leading to more stable and adaptable solutions.
- **Pivotal Dataset Contribution:** The introduction of the IR-500K dataset is a significant contribution to the research community. By compiling and curating such a large and diverse collection of infrared images, the authors provide an invaluable resource that can accelerate future research and development in infrared image processing, generation, object detection, and other related computer vision tasks. This addresses a critical bottleneck in the field and facilitates the training of more robust and generalizable models.
- **Broadening Real-world Applications:** The improved fidelity and semantic accuracy of V2IR translation have wide-ranging practical implications:
 - **Autonomous Driving and Robotics:** Generating realistic infrared data can augment training datasets for perception systems, enhancing night vision and adverse weather capabilities for self-driving cars and robots.
 - **Security and Surveillance:** High-quality infrared synthesis can improve night vision monitoring, threat detection, and anonymization techniques.
 - **Environmental Monitoring:** Better V2IR translation can aid in analyzing thermal patterns in natural landscapes, contributing to climate studies, wildlife tracking, and agricultural monitoring.
 - **Defense and Reconnaissance:** Creating synthetic infrared imagery for training and simulation purposes can be vital for military and intelligence applications.
 - **Medical Imaging:** While the paper notes a limitation in face translation, the underlying principles could potentially be adapted for certain medical imaging applications where thermal patterns are relevant.
- **Methodological Innovation for Diffusion Models:** This work showcases a powerful methodology for extending the capabilities of large-scale diffusion models beyond their initial training domains. The combination of LoRA-based fine-tuning, multi-stage learning, and diverse multi-modal conditioning provides a compelling example for tackling other challenging cross-domain or cross-modal generation tasks.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as specialized focus on broad scenes over specific applications like face translation, and potential negative impacts like the misuse for creating misleading content, the overall contribution of DiffV2IR is profoundly positive. It not only significantly advances the state-of-the-art in V2IR translation but also offers innovative methodological insights and a crucial dataset that will catalyze future research in multi-modal generative AI and its real-world applications.
