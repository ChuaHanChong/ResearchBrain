# RoboStream: Weaving Spatio-Temporal Reasoning with Memory in Vision-Language Models for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and YXGN Robotics introduce RoboStream, a training-free framework that integrates spatio-temporal reasoning and causal memory into Vision-Language Models for robotic manipulation. This system leverages Spatio-Temporal Fusion Tokens and a Causal Spatio-Temporal Graph to track objects and action effects, achieving up to 88.9% success in real-world occlusion tasks and 90.5% average success on complex long-horizon tasks in simulation, significantly outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based robotic planners struggle with long-horizon manipulation due to ungrounded spatial perception, leading to accumulated errors and spatial hallucinations.
- Current methods lack untracked causal history, meaning they do not maintain a structured record of action-triggered state changes, causing object loss under occlusion or failed precondition checks.
- Traditional VLM planners often treat each action step as an independent observation-to-action mapping, failing to build a continuous understanding of the evolving environment.

## Method

- RoboStream constructs Spatio-Temporal Fusion Tokens (STF-Tokens) by binding visual evidence with metric 3D geometric attributes for each task-relevant object, creating unified, object-centric representations.
- A 4D Causal Spatio-Temporal Graph (CSTG) is built to track inter-object spatial relations and action causality over time, maintaining persistent object identities and logging action-triggered state transitions within a sliding-window memory.
- The VLM performs chain-of-thought spatial verification using context-augmented prompts that include the CSTG's current object states, spatial relations, and causal history, generating semantic action directives that are then instantiated into 6-DoF poses.

## Results

- RoboStream-235B achieved 44.4% and 66.7% success rates on hard-level real-world block building and disassembly, and 88.9% in object hiding and restoration tasks requiring tracking under total occlusion, significantly surpassing baselines (<11.1% for SoFar/VoxPoser).
- In simulation (RLBench), RoboStream-235B achieved a 90.5% average success rate on complex long-horizon tasks, demonstrating that spatio-temporal reasoning and structured memory are more critical than merely increasing VLM parameter scale.
- Ablation studies showed that removing the CSTG caused an average success rate collapse from 90.5% to 14.5%, and removing STF-Tokens led to an 11.0% absolute drop, confirming the essential roles of both components for long-horizon task performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit 3D geometry into object tokens provides deterministic spatial references and significantly enhances precise 6-DoF spatial reasoning, moving beyond pixel-level to object-level inference.
> - Persistent state tracking via a structured causal memory (CSTG) is fundamental for long-horizon tasks, enabling precondition verification, re-planning upon detected failures, and preserving object permanence under occlusion.
> - The training-free nature of RoboStream allows it to scale with VLM backbone capacity and generalize across different robotic embodiments and viewpoints without requiring environment-specific adaptation or fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which leverage extensive semantic knowledge and visual perception from internet-scale data. These VLM-based planners have demonstrated capabilities in short-horizon tasks, such as precise grasping, pose-aware placement, and decomposing language instructions into subgoals.

However, a recognized limitation in the broader research landscape is the challenge of extending these successes to long-horizon manipulation tasks. Current VLM-based planners often treat each action step as an independent observation-to-action mapping. This approach necessitates re-inferring scene geometry from raw pixels at every decision point and lacks awareness of how previous actions have altered the environment. Consequently, perceptual errors can accumulate over time, temporarily occluded objects may be forgotten, and compounding failures can lead to precondition violations, ultimately resulting in task failure in multi-step scenarios.

Existing research has explored various strategies to enhance VLM capabilities for robotics. These include:
- **Spatial Understanding with VLMs**: Approaches utilize tool augmentation, where VLMs orchestrate external visual foundation models or geometric engines, or 3D-data-driven fine-tuning to improve spatial representations. While these methods enhance spatial awareness, they often introduce computational overhead, require explicit module coupling, or incur significant data and training costs.
- **VLMs for Robotic Manipulation**: Research focuses on logic-grounded task planning, geometry-aware constraint generation, and closed-loop reasoning. While these contribute to structured action sequences, precise spatial grounding, and adaptability, they frequently reconstruct geometric constraints at each step without persistent tracking or primarily react to failures without reasoning about action-induced state changes or preconditions.
- **Long-Horizon Task Planning**: Strategies involve hierarchical decomposition and chain-of-thought methods. These organize multi-step execution or strengthen planning through explicit future-state reasoning. However, they generally lack persistent memory mechanisms to track the causal consequences of actions and how the environment state evolves over extended periods, particularly under partial observability.

RoboStream addresses these gaps by proposing a training-free framework that integrates persistent spatio-temporal reasoning and causal memory directly into the VLM planning loop. It aims to overcome the limitations of isolated step-wise planning by establishing persistent object grounding and tracking action-triggered state transitions, drawing inspiration from human cognitive abilities to maintain a continuous mental model of the world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable reliable long-horizon robotic manipulation, identified as a crucial step towards achieving open-world embodied intelligence. The motivation stems from observed limitations in current VLM-based robotic planners, which hinder their performance in complex, multi-step tasks that involve dynamic environments and object interactions.

The paper identifies two primary representation deficits in existing VLM-based planners that contribute to cascading failures in long-horizon scenarios:

1. **Ungrounded Spatial Perception**:
 - **Problem**: Current systems infer spatial relations implicitly from raw image pixels without explicitly anchoring them to 3D geometry or binding object identities across consecutive steps. This means that at each decision step, the system effectively reconstructs the scene geometry anew.
 - **Consequences**: Small inaccuracies in pixel-level inference accumulate over time, leading to spatial hallucinations where objects are misplaced or misidentified, and precondition violations, as the robot operates based on an inconsistent understanding of the environment's geometric state.
 - **Motivation for addressing**: Robotic manipulation intrinsically involves physical contact and motion, which continually alter object positions and spatial relationships. A robust planner requires a persistent, geometrically anchored understanding of these evolving states to perform reliably over extended sequences.

2. **Untracked Causal History**:
 - **Problem**: Existing methods typically do not maintain a structured record linking executed actions to the resulting state transitions in the environment. This means that once an action is performed, its effects on the world state, particularly for objects that move out of sight, are not explicitly tracked.
 - **Consequences**: Planners remain unaware of objects that have been displaced or entirely lose track of objects that become occluded. This leads to failures when subsequent actions depend on the altered states of these untracked objects, resulting in precondition violations and an inability to recover from unexpected changes or restore previous configurations.
 - **Motivation for addressing**: Human intelligence maintains a coherent mental model that continuously integrates spatial relations and causal consequences of actions. To replicate this, a robotic system needs to track not only the current geometric state but also the causal chain of events that led to it, allowing for inference about hidden objects and anticipated future states.

To address these deficits, the authors propose RoboStream, a framework designed to imbue VLMs with the capacity for spatio-temporal reasoning and persistent memory. The core motivation is to move VLM-based robot planning beyond reactive, instantaneous observation-to-action mapping towards a more proactive and context-aware approach that can maintain a consistent world model over long horizons, even under partial observability. This is achieved without additional training or fine-tuning, aiming for broader applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboStream is presented as a training-free framework that integrates spatio-temporal reasoning and persistent memory into the VLM planning loop. It operates through three sequential stages: Object-Centric Perception and Spatio-Temporal Token Fusion, 4D Causal Spatio-Temporal Graph Construction, and VLM-based Reasoning and Planning.

#### 3.1. Overview

The framework takes RGB-D observations, a goal specification (image or natural language), and prior memory as input. It first constructs Spatio-Temporal Fusion Tokens (STF-Tokens) by binding visual evidence to 3D geometric attributes. These tokens are then organized into a Causal Spatio-Temporal Graph (CSTG), which maintains persistent object identities and logs action-triggered state transitions within a sliding-window memory. This structured memory, along with current visual inputs, is fed into a VLM for chain-of-thought verification to generate semantic action directives, which are subsequently instantiated into 6-DoF poses for robot control.

#### 3.2. Object-Centric Perception

This stage aims to create scene representations focused on task-relevant entities. Given an RGB-D observation (image and depth map), goal specification, and accumulated memory, a VLM is prompted to identify and output open-vocabulary descriptors for task-relevant objects. Conditioning on prior memory (M<t) ensures consistent object identities across steps and filters out irrelevant entities. An open-vocabulary segmentation model (SAM3) uses these descriptors to generate pixel-accurate masks for each object. Combined with the depth map, each mask defines a 3D point cloud for that object, serving as input for the next stage.

#### 3.3. Spatio-Temporal Token Fusion

For each identified object, a Spatio-Temporal Fusion Token (STF-Token, τ_t_i) is constructed. This token integrates visual appearance with metric 3D geometry into a unified, object-centric representation.
- **Visual Evidence**: The object's masked RGB image region is processed by a VLM visual encoder to produce patch tokens. Patches with high Intersection over Union (IoU) with the object mask are selected, projected into the language space, and aggregated to form the object's visual evidence (v_t_i). This process compresses visual information and avoids redundant background processing.
- **3D Geometry**: A shape representation vector (s_t_i) is defined using Gaussian distribution parameters (mean and standard deviation) and geometric extrema along x, y, z axes. This captures geometric extent robustly, reducing outlier noise. The object's 3D centroid (c_t_i) is extracted as the median of its point cloud.
- **Token Construction**: Both the centroid (c_t_i) and shape vector (s_t_i) are serialized using a text tokenizer into the same language embedding space as the visual evidence (v_t_i). This allows the VLM to jointly process heterogeneous visual and spatial signals. The complete per-object state in an STF-Token includes visual evidence, 3D centroid, Gaussian shape parameters, and current timestamp (τ_t_i = {v_t_i, c_t_i, s_t_i, t}). This grounding in explicit 3D geometry and temporal context is designed to enable deterministic spatial queries and mitigate cascading errors.

#### 3.4. 4D Causal Spatio-Temporal Graph

The Causal Spatio-Temporal Graph (CSTG, G_CST_t) is constructed to track inter-object spatial relations and action causality over time. It recursively integrates new STF-Tokens with the historical graph state.
- **Spatial Scene Graph**: At each timestamp (t), a spatial scene graph (G_t = (V_t, E_t)) is formed. Nodes (v_i ∈ V_t) correspond to objects, each maintaining a sliding-window buffer of its STF-Tokens (e.g., for the last K steps). Edges (e_ij ∈ E_t) encode pairwise spatial relations, such as Euclidean distance and directional offset between object centroids.
- **Causal Memory Log**: Semantic events are detected by comparing STF-Tokens across the sliding window. These events can be object-level (e.g., planned displacement, collision, occlusion) or task-level (e.g., action execution, subtask completion). Each event is recorded in a memory log (H_K_t) with its timestamp, location, and causal source.
- **CSTG Formation**: The CSTG (G_CST_t) is the combination of the spatial scene graph and the causal memory log. This structured representation allows for deterministic verification of action preconditions at each planning step and facilitates re-planning upon detected failures, preventing error accumulation. It also preserves object permanence under occlusion by recording last-known states.

#### 3.5. VLM-based Causal Reasoning and Planning

In the final stage, RoboStream prepares a context-augmented prompt (P_t) for the VLM. This prompt includes information from the CSTG (current object states, spatial relations, causal history), an annotated observation (image with object identity labels), and the goal specification.
The VLM then performs a chain-of-thought spatial verification process. It reviews historical states from the CSTG, checks action preconditions against explicit object geometry, and resolves causal conflicts arising from occlusion or dependencies. Based on this reasoning, the VLM generates a semantic action directive (ˆa_t). Finally, this semantic directive is instantiated into a precise 6-DoF action (a_t) by resolving object identities against the explicit 3D geometry embedded within the STF-Tokens stored in the CSTG. This decoupled design allows the VLM to focus on high-level semantic reasoning while offloading precise coordinate extraction to a deterministic geometric resolution module.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboStream was evaluated across five benchmarks, encompassing simulated and real-world environments. The evaluations tested the framework's capabilities in long-horizon manipulation, zero-shot generalization, and 6-DoF spatial reasoning. Three VLM backbones (Qwen3-VL-8B, -32B, -235B) were used without environment-specific adaptation or fine-tuning.

#### 4.1. Real-World Robotic Manipulation

- **Setup**: 21 real-world tasks on a Franka Research 3 arm, using an Intel RealSense D435i RGB-D camera and 17 colored objects. Tasks included Block Building (Bottom-to-Top, 3 difficulty levels), Block Disassembly (Top-to-Bottom), and Block Hide and Restore (under full occlusion).
- **Results**: RoboStream variants consistently outperformed baselines (SoFar, VoxPoser).
 - On Hard-level block building and disassembly, RoboStream-235B achieved 44.4% and 66.7% success rates, respectively, compared to <11.1% for SoFar and VoxPoser.
 - In Task C (Block Hide and Restore), which requires tracking under total occlusion, RoboStream-235B achieved 88.9% success, and even RoboStream-8B achieved 33.3%, while SoFar and VoxPoser scored 0%.
 - Performance scaled with backbone capacity, indicating that STF-Tokens and CSTG are complementary and enhance with VLM capacity.

#### 4.2. Simulation Object Manipulation Evaluation on SIMPLER

- **Setup**: Zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization on Google Robot and WidowX + Bridge configurations, testing short-horizon tasks.
- **Results**:
 - **Google Robot**: RoboStream-8B achieved higher success rates than SoFar on "Pick Coke Can" and "Move Near" tasks under VM evaluation and competitive performance under VA evaluation, outperforming even OXE-finetuned RT-2-X zero-shot.
 - **WidowX + Bridge**: RoboStream-8B achieved a higher average success rate than zero-shot SoFar and Bridge-finetuned SpatialVLA, without requiring any fine-tuning or domain adaptation.
- **Conclusion**: These results indicate robust spatial grounding and transferability of the geometry-grounded representation across different embodiments and viewpoints.

#### 4.3. Simulation Long-Horizon Task Evaluation on RLBench

- **Setup**: 8 complex long-horizon tasks, including goal-image-guided and text-guided tasks, evaluated over 25 episodes per task.
- **Results**: RoboStream consistently outperformed SoFar and VoxPoser.
 - RoboStream-235B achieved a 90.5% average success rate.
 - A strong scaling effect was observed, with performance increasing with model capacity.
 - Even RoboStream-8B substantially exceeded baselines, suggesting that spatio-temporal reasoning and structured memory are more critical for physical consistency in long-horizon tasks than simply increasing VLM parameter scale.

#### 4.4. 6-DoF Spatial Reasoning Evaluation

- **Setup**: Evaluated on Open6DOR V2 and 6-DoF SpatialBench, using PointSO for 6-DoF prediction, comparing against SoFar (including 8B and 32B variants).
- **Results**:
 - **6-DoF SpatialBench**: RoboStream-32B achieved superior overall performance compared to SoFar and GPT-4o, with the most significant gains on absolute and relative position tasks.
 - **Open6DOR V2**: RoboStream-32B consistently outperformed SoFar across position, rotation, and integrated 6-DoF metrics.
- **Conclusion**: STF-Tokens, by distilling visual evidence into structured instance representations (centroid, Gaussian shape, orientation), provide deterministic spatial references that enhance precise 6-DoF spatial reasoning, moving beyond pixel-level to object-level inference.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Setup**: Conducted on RoboStream-235B across 8 long-horizon RLBench tasks to isolate the contributions of CSTG and STF-Tokens.
- **Results**:
 - **Removing CSTG**: Average success rate collapsed from 90.5% to 14.5%, indicating that persistent state tracking is fundamental for long-horizon tasks.
 - **Removing STF-Tokens**: Average success rate dropped from 90.5% to 79.5% (an 11.0% absolute drop), demonstrating that explicit 3D geometric grounding is necessary for physical precision and to prevent compounding spatial inaccuracies.
 - **Removing both**: Yielded only a 12.0% success rate, confirming the mutual dependence of both components. STF-Tokens provided a marginal improvement even without CSTG in short-horizon accuracy, but their full potential was realized when coupled with CSTG for correct action sequencing and precise execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboStream addresses fundamental representation limitations in VLM-based planners for robotics, specifically **Ungrounded Spatial Perception** and **Untracked Causal History**. By introducing **Spatio-Temporal Fusion Tokens (STF-Tokens)**, it provides persistent object grounding by binding visual appearance with 3D geometric attributes. The **Causal Spatio-Temporal Graph (CSTG)**, in turn, resolves the issue of untracked causal history by maintaining records of action-triggered state transitions, which enables inference of hidden object states and object permanence under occlusion.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Reliability for Long-Horizon Tasks**: RoboStream's ability to maintain a persistent world model and track causal state transitions significantly improves performance on complex, multi-step robotic manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world environments. This moves VLM-based robotics closer to robustly handling real-world scenarios that demand sustained spatial and causal awareness.
- **Training-Free Framework**: The framework's design, which does not require additional training or fine-tuning, implies greater generality and easier deployment across various robotic platforms and tasks. This reduces the substantial data and computational costs often associated with fine-tuning large models for specific robotic applications.
- **Validation of Core Components**: The consistent outperformance of RoboStream against existing state-of-the-art baselines across diverse benchmarks, coupled with a detailed ablation study, provides empirical evidence that explicit spatio-temporal reasoning and persistent memory are critical for long-horizon stability. This suggests these architectural components are more decisive than simply increasing the scale of VLM parameters in isolation.
- **Bridging Perception and Reasoning**: The integration of explicit 3D geometry into object tokens and the structuring of causal memory allows VLMs to perform more grounded and consistent reasoning, mitigating issues like spatial hallucinations and perceptual amnesia.

The potential impact of RoboStream is towards enabling more capable and autonomous robots for open-world embodied intelligence. By providing a mechanism for robots to maintain a coherent understanding of their environment's state and history, it can facilitate advancements in areas such as intelligent assistance, complex assembly, and adaptive interaction with dynamic environments.

However, the paper also acknowledges limitations inherent to decoupled planning-execution frameworks. Imperfections in perception (e.g., object parsing errors, unstable grasping) or low-level control can still propagate into execution failures, even when high-level planning is correct. This highlights a tension between the semantic generalization of VLMs and the precision required for physical robot control. Future work is suggested to explore hybrid approaches, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models as downstream controllers, or fully end-to-end VLAs that internalize spatio-temporal reasoning and causal memory within the action generation loop, to bridge this gap. Extension to contact-rich, dexterous, and more open-world manipulation scenarios also constitutes a promising direction.
