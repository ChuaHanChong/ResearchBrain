# GROW$^2$: Grounding Which and Where for Robot Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> GROW² is a framework that enables robots to select suitable tools from open-category objects in cluttered scenes and precisely localize their 3D interaction regions. It achieves zero-shot generalization by decomposing the problem into semantic reasoning via Vision-Language Models and geometric grounding using vision foundation models, resulting in an 86.7% average success rate in simulation and 78.0% in real-world robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robot tool use often assumes pre-specified tools, limiting generalization to novel objects or task scenarios.
- Existing affordance prediction methods struggle with precise 3D localization from single-view observations and generalizing to open-category objects.
- Robots lack the ability to semantically understand and geometrically ground the functional properties (affordances) of everyday objects for improvised tool use.

## Method

- **GROW²** decomposes the ==open-world affordance grounding== problem hierarchically into ==semantic tool/part selection== and ==geometric 3D affordance localization==.
- It integrates ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== for semantic reasoning in tool and part identification with ==vision foundation models== (==SAM3==, ==SAM3D==) for precise 3D geometric grounding, using ==in-context learning== with ==chain-of-thought== prompting.
- The framework reconstructs complete 3D affordance regions from single RGB-D observations by combining ==3D reconstruction== with ==multi-view 2D segmentation fusion==, aligning meshes via ==ICP registration== and clustering back-projected points with ==DBSCAN== to mitigate occlusion issues.

## Results

- GROW² achieved top performance on affordance prediction benchmarks, including the highest Similarity Metric (**0.418**) on AGD20K and highest average Intersection over Union (**9.0**) on PIAD.
- In simulated robot tool use, the framework demonstrated an average success rate of **86.7%** across five diverse task types, outperforming baselines like MOKA (**33.0%**) and DINOv3 (**21.0%**).
- Real-world experiments on a Franka robot yielded a **78.0%** average success rate, significantly surpassing the best baseline, MOKA (**30.0%**), confirming its robustness in practical settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging object parts as an intermediate abstraction bridges semantic understanding from VLMs with geometric grounding from vision foundation models, enabling zero-shot generalization.
> - A multi-view 2D segmentation and fusion approach, applied to a 3D reconstructed mesh, effectively generates complete and accurate 3D affordance regions from a single-view observation, overcoming occlusions.
> - In-context learning with chain-of-thought prompting significantly enhances the VLM's robustness in selecting appropriate tools and their interaction parts based on geometric, semantic, and physical cues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses the problem of robot tool use, specifically focusing on the challenge of "open-world affordance grounding." Historically, robot tool use research has often assumed that the tool object is either pre-specified or selected from a fixed, predefined set with known tool-function mappings. For example, some prior approaches focus on generating actions under constraints given a known tool, while others attempt tool selection based on geometric information or data-driven methods that rely on heavy supervision or in-domain data. These methods generally exhibit limitations in generalizing to novel tools, unseen task-object pairs, or unstructured environments where creative improvisation is required.

Furthermore, existing methods for affordance prediction, which aim to identify potential interaction regions, typically fall into a few categories: keypoint-based methods, 2D affordance region methods, and 3D affordance prediction methods. Keypoint-based approaches are efficient but can lack expressiveness, while 2D methods integrate well with modern vision models but miss crucial 3D geometric information for manipulation. End-to-end 3D affordance prediction often requires extensive 3D training data, leading to poor generalization to open-category objects and novel functions. Many 3D methods also assume complete 3D observations, which is impractical, or rely on costly and slow multi-view camera setups to mitigate occlusion.

GROW² differentiates itself by tackling the more complex scenario where the robot must *both* select an appropriate tool from open-category objects in a cluttered scene *and* precisely localize its 3D affordance regions. The core innovation lies in its hierarchical decomposition of this problem. Instead of relying on data-heavy end-to-end training or requiring pre-specified tools, GROW² leverages object parts as an intermediate abstraction. This approach allows it to integrate the semantic reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with the geometric grounding strengths of vision foundation models, thereby enabling zero-shot generalization to a wide array of objects and tasks without extensive task-specific training data. It also addresses the practical limitation of single-view observations by reconstructing complete 3D affordance regions, avoiding the need for multi-view sensing setups commonly used to overcome occlusions. This situates GROW² as an advancement toward more adaptable and generalized robot manipulation in unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to utilize tools in improvised ways, extending their manipulation capabilities beyond their intended functions, akin to human ingenuity. This capability is critical for robots operating in unstructured environments such as homes or warehouses, where pre-programmed solutions or a fixed set of tools may be insufficient.

The central problem addressed is "open-world affordance grounding." This encompasses two specific sub-objectives:
1. **Tool Selection:** Identifying which object in a cluttered scene can serve as a suitable tool for a given task, even if its conventional purpose differs from the required function.
2. **Affordance Localization:** Precisely determining the relevant interaction regions (affordance regions) on both the selected tool and the target object, in 3D, to enable effective manipulation.

The motivation for this research stems from two primary challenges inherent in open-world affordance grounding:
- **Semantic Understanding of Open-Category Objects:** Robots need to semantically interpret the functional properties (affordances) of objects that may belong to open categories. For example, to "cut a cake," a robot should identify an object with a sharp edge, discerning a plate from a dustpan based on its functional suitability, despite potential geometric similarities. Traditional approaches often lack this higher-level semantic reasoning.
- **Precise Geometric Grounding and Generalization:** The identified affordances must be mapped to precise 3D geometric regions for downstream robotic manipulation. This grounding must be adaptable to the specific task and environment and, crucially, generalize to objects not encountered during training. Existing end-to-end neural network approaches, which map tasks to affordance regions directly, are typically data-intensive and struggle with such generalization.

The authors propose that object parts (e.g., a blade, a handle, a rim) offer a natural abstraction that bridges both semantic understanding and geometric structure. This insight motivates the hierarchical decomposition of the problem. By separating tool and part selection (semantic) from 3D affordance grounding (geometric), GROW² aims to leverage the strengths of existing foundation models—VLMs for commonsense reasoning and vision foundation models for visual grounding—without requiring large, annotated affordance datasets. This approach is intended to overcome the limitations of prior work concerning generalization, data requirements, and handling single-view observations, ultimately facilitating more robust and creative robot tool use.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GROW² (GROunding Which and Where) addresses open-world affordance grounding by decomposing the problem into two hierarchical levels based on object parts. The input consists of a single-view RGB-D observation (I, D) of a scene and a natural language instruction *l* specifying a task and a target object *oB*. The output comprises the selected tool object *oA* and the corresponding 3D interaction regions.

#### 3.1. Tool and Part Selection (Semantic Level)

This stage leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform semantic reasoning for tool and part identification.
- **Object and Part Extraction:**
 1. A VLM first analyzes the RGB image *I* to enumerate all present objects, generating a list of object names {*o_i*}.
 2. For each identified object name, SAM3 segments the object in the image, producing an image crop (*I_i*).
 3. The VLM is then queried for each object-image crop pair (*o_i*, *I_i*) to generate a set of part candidates *P_i* = {*p_i,1*..., *p_i,j*} for that object, utilizing the VLM's knowledge of object structures and affordances.
- **Tool and Part Selection:**
 1. Given the RGB image *I*, the task instruction *l*, and the extracted object-part candidates {(o_i, P_i)}, the VLM is prompted to select specific entities.
 2. The VLM identifies: the tool object *oA*, the target object *oB*, a part *pG* on *oA* for the robot to grasp, a part *pA* on *oA* for performing the tool function, and a part *pB* on *oB* for interaction with the tool.
 3. To enhance robustness, in-context learning is employed, providing the VLM with a small set of tool and part selection examples and chain-of-thought guidance within the prompt. This guides the VLM in step-by-step reasoning based on geometry, semantics, and physics, to produce the output: {[oA, (pG, pA)], [oB, pB]}.

#### 3.2. 3D Affordance Grounding (Geometric Level)

Once the tool, target, and task-relevant parts (*pG*, *pA*, *pB*) are semantically selected, this stage grounds them into precise 3D affordance regions. This process is designed to overcome occlusions inherent in single-view observations without requiring multi-camera setups.
- **3D Reconstruction and Registration:**
 1. For each selected object (tool *oA* and target *oB*), SAM3 segments the object from the single-view RGB-D observation (*I*, *D*) to obtain a masked RGB-D crop (*I_i*, *D_i*).
 2. The RGB-D crop is fed into SAM3D, a 3D reconstruction model, to generate a 3D mesh *M*.
 3. To ensure the reconstructed mesh retains the object's original pose and scale relative to the camera, the depth crop *D_i* is converted into a point map *X_i* and used as a geometric condition during reconstruction.
 4. For further geometric accuracy, the reconstructed mesh *M* is aligned to the partial observed point map *X_i*. This is achieved by rendering *M* from the original camera view to produce a partial point map *X_M* and then performing ICP-based (Iterative Closest Point) rigid registration to find a transformation *T* from *X_M* to *X_i*. Applying *T* to *M* yields the registered object mesh *cM*.
- **Multi-view 2D Segmentation and Fusion:**
 1. For each task-relevant part *p* ∈ {*pG*, *pA*, *pB*}, the registered mesh *cM* is rendered from multiple (K=8) viewpoints to generate a set of 2D images {*bI_k*}. This multi-view rendering helps ensure that the target part is visible and not self-occluded.
 2. For each rendered image *bI_k*, SAM3 is used to segment the part described by *p*, producing a 2D segmentation mask *s_k*. If SAM3 struggles with complex text queries, a VLM (Qwen3-vl) is used as a fallback to translate the description into bounding boxes, which then condition SAM3 for fine-grained segmentation.
 3. The multiple 2D segmentation masks {*s_k*} are then aggregated into a single 3D affordance region. Each mask *s_k* is back-projected onto the reconstructed mesh *cM* using its corresponding camera parameters, generating a point set on the object surface.
 4. These point sets from all views are merged by taking their union. DBSCAN is then applied to cluster these points, remove outliers, and retain only the largest cluster as the final, predicted 3D affordance region.

This methodology combines symbolic VLM reasoning with precise geometric grounding using vision foundation models, allowing GROW² to generalize effectively without extensive end-to-end training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of GROW² was evaluated across three experimental settings: affordance prediction benchmarks, simulated robot tool use, and real-world robot tool use.

#### 4.1. Affordance Prediction Experiments

GROW² was tested on established 2D and 3D affordance prediction benchmarks, as well as a newly introduced multi-object benchmark.
- **AGD20K (2D Affordance Grounding):** GROW² demonstrated competitive performance, achieving the highest Similarity Metric (SIM = 0.418) and Normalized Scanpath Saliency (NSS = 1.334) compared to state-of-the-art baselines (Cross-View-AG, LOCATE, 3DOI, AffordanceLLM, UAD). Its Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD) was slightly higher (1.506), which the authors suggest might be due to its fine-grained predictions potentially diverging from the more diffuse ground-truth distributions in AGD20K.
- **PIAD (3D Affordance Grounding):** GROW² outperformed baselines (PFusion, XMF, IAGNet, LASO, GEAL) by achieving the highest average Intersection over Union (aIoU = 9.0) and Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC = 74.2), along with the lowest Mean Absolute Error (MAE = 0.102). This indicates its capability in localizing 3D affordance regions accurately.
- **GROW²Bench (Multi-Object Affordance Prediction with Tool Selection):** This new benchmark requires both tool identification and affordance prediction in cluttered scenes. GROW² achieved the highest IoU across all ten task types (e.g., average IoU significantly higher than baselines like MOKA, SD-DINO, and DINOv3). Baselines like DINOv3 and SD-DINO, relying on image feature matching for tool selection, often failed due to a lack of global semantic understanding. MOKA, while using a VLM for tool selection, struggled with precise spatial reasoning for affordance prediction through visual prompting, leading to hallucinations. GROW²'s hierarchical approach and semantic reasoning via VLMs, followed by geometric grounding, allowed it to perform better in tool selection and part localization.

#### 4.2. Simulation Experiments (Robot Tool Use)

To assess the practical utility of predicted affordances, GROW² was evaluated in a SAPIEN 3 simulation environment using a Franka Emika Panda arm.
- **Setup:** Five task types (Pound, Cut, Lift, Sweep, Pour) were tested across 100 diverse scenes featuring 85 distinct object assets. Low-level skills were implemented using Contact-GraspNet for grasp pose sampling and RRT-Connect for motion planning, conditioned on the predicted affordance regions.
- **Results:** GROW² achieved the highest success rates across all five task types, with an average success rate of 86.7%. This significantly surpassed baselines, including ICP (31.0%), DINOv3 (21.0%), SD-DINO (36.0%), MOKA (33.0%), and even UAD (47.0%) and GEAL (42.0%) which were provided with the ground-truth tool. The "Lift" task, challenging for baselines due to its requirement for precise matching of tool and target affordances, was handled effectively by GROW².
- **Ablation Study (GROW² w/o multi-view):** An ablation experiment showed that removing the 3D reconstruction and multi-view grounding stage led to a considerable drop in average success rate (from 86.7% to 62.7%). This highlights the importance of these geometric stages in resolving occlusions and improving affordance localization for successful manipulation.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

GROW² was deployed on a real Franka Research 3 robot arm with an Intel RealSense L515 camera for RGB-D observations.
- **Setup:** Five manipulation tasks were tested across 10 scenes per task, each containing a target object and at least five candidate tools. GROW² was compared against MOKA, identified as the strongest baseline from previous experiments.
- **Results:** GROW² achieved a substantially higher average success rate (78.0%) compared to MOKA (30.0%). The system demonstrated reliability in real-world settings without requiring extra in-domain training data. The robustness of GROW² to noisy real-world depth observations was attributed to its computation of affordance regions from reconstructed meshes via accurate multi-view rendering, in contrast to baselines negatively affected by depth inaccuracies.

In summary, the findings consistently demonstrate that GROW²'s hierarchical approach to grounding (Which and Where) enables superior performance in open-world affordance prediction and robust robot tool use across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios, outperforming existing methods by effectively leveraging foundation models for both semantic understanding and precise 3D geometric grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GROW² framework represents a notable advancement in the field of robot manipulation, particularly in the domain of tool use and affordance grounding. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Creativity:** GROW² equips robots with the ability to identify and utilize everyday objects as tools in improvised ways, moving beyond pre-programmed or explicitly trained functions. This capacity for "creative tool use" significantly expands the feasible manipulation strategies available to robots, making them more adaptable and autonomous in unstructured, dynamic environments like homes, hospitals, or warehouses.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** A key contribution is the method's zero-shot generalization capability to open-category objects and novel tasks. By decomposing affordance grounding into semantic and geometric levels and leveraging the commonsense reasoning of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the visual grounding of vision foundation models, GROW² bypasses the need for large, task-specific, and labor-intensive datasets. This greatly reduces the cost and effort associated with deploying robots in new scenarios.
- **Novel Single-View 3D Affordance Grounding:** The approach to reconstruct complete and function-faithful 3D affordance regions from a single RGB-D observation, via 3D reconstruction and multi-view 2D segmentation fusion, is a significant technical innovation. It effectively mitigates the common challenge of occlusions without requiring costly and complex multi-camera setups, making real-world deployment more practical and efficient.
- **Improved Robustness in Unstructured Environments:** By grounding affordances precisely and robustly, GROW² enables higher success rates in complex manipulation tasks, as demonstrated in both simulation and real-world experiments. This robustness is crucial for reliable robot operation outside of controlled laboratory settings.
- **Framework for Integrating Foundation Models:** The hierarchical structure of GROW² provides an interpretable and effective way to combine the strengths of different foundation models. VLMs excel at semantic reasoning and understanding natural language instructions, while vision foundation models are proficient in visual grounding. This synergistic integration represents a promising direction for developing more capable robotic systems.
- **Benchmark Contribution:** The introduction of GROW²Bench, a benchmark for evaluating tool selection in multi-object settings, addresses a gap in existing datasets and provides a valuable resource for future research in this area.

**Potential Impact:**
The immediate impact of GROW² lies in making robots more versatile and less dependent on meticulous environmental engineering or extensive task-specific training. This can accelerate the deployment of robots in service industries, logistics, and assistive roles where diverse, unpredictable object interactions are common.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges certain limitations:
- **Failure Modes:** The primary source of failure currently lies in the VLM's tool and part selection, which can sometimes be inappropriate or prone to hallucinations. Future work could explore agentic frameworks with iterative scene inspection to improve VLM robustness.
- **Efficiency:** The current latency of approximately 16.6 seconds per scene, primarily due to sequential queries to VLMs and vision foundation models, limits its application in dynamic manipulation scenarios. Future research could focus on distilling GROW² into more lightweight, efficient models for real-time performance.

Overall, GROW² represents a substantial step towards more intelligent and adaptable robots capable of understanding and interacting with their environment in human-like, resourceful ways, paving the way for broader robot integration into complex real-world settings.
