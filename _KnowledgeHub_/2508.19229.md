# StepWiser: Stepwise Generative Judges for Wiser Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> STEPWISER introduces a method for training generative judges that meta-reason about an LLM's intermediate reasoning steps, significantly improving judgment accuracy and enabling more capable policy models for multi-step tasks. It achieves this by combining online reinforcement learning with dense, relative reward signals for individual reasoning chunks.

## Problem

- Current Process Reward Models (PRMs) function as "black-box" classifiers, lacking transparency and interpretability in their step-by-step evaluations.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for PRMs relies on expensive, static human annotations, which limits scalability and generalization.
- Ensuring the logical validity of intermediate reasoning steps in LLM Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is critical, but traditional step segmentation often leads to incoherent units.

## Method

- Develops a self-segmentation method to create ==Chunks-of-Thought==, training the policy LLM to generate reasoning in logically coherent units.
- Automates stepwise data annotation using ==Monte Carlo Q-value estimation==, which are then converted into binary labels with novel "relative reward" thresholding strategies (Rel-Effective, Rel-Ratio).
- Trains a generative judge model via online Reinforcement Learning (==GRPO==), which first generates its own Chain-of-Thought (meta-reasoning) to justify its verdict before providing a binary judgment.
- Employs prompt dataset balancing and stability enhancements during RL training to address class imbalance and prevent model collapse.

## Results

- STEPWISER judges achieved substantially higher accuracy on ProcessBench (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-chunk with Rel-Effective scored **61.9**), outperforming all SFT-trained and sparse RL-trained baselines.
- Using STEPWISER for inference-time "Chunk-Reset Reasoning" improved policy model accuracy (e.g., 7B model from **57.4%** to **63.3%**), demonstrating its utility in real-time self-correction.
- Policy models fine-tuned on data selected by STEPWISER judges achieved superior performance (**63.0%** average accuracy for 7B model), showcasing its effectiveness in data curation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of online RL, generative meta-reasoning by the judge, and prompt dataset balancing for the framework's superior performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative judges that explicitly meta-reason about intermediate steps (generating their own CoT for evaluation) are significantly more accurate and transparent than discriminative black-box judges.
> - Dense, step-level supervision derived from Monte Carlo rollouts, especially using relative reward signals, provides a superior learning signal compared to sparse, outcome-only rewards.
> - Online Reinforcement Learning is crucial for training robust stepwise judges, overcoming the generalization limitations of SFT on static datasets.
> - Self-segmentation into "chunks-of-thought" creates more coherent and evaluable reasoning units, improving annotation efficiency and judge context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to their application in increasingly complex problem-solving tasks, particularly those requiring multi-step reasoning, such as mathematical problem-solving or code generation. Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and ReAct (Yao et al., 2022) have enabled LLMs to decompose problems and articulate their reasoning processes. However, the logical validity and correctness of these intermediate reasoning steps remain a critical challenge. Ensuring the reliability of these steps is essential for building trustworthy and effective AI agents.

This paper positions itself directly within the evolving landscape of **process supervision** and **reward modeling** for LLMs. Traditionally, LLM evaluation and training, especially through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), has relied on **outcome reward models (ORMs)**, which provide a single, often sparse, reward based on the final answer or overall trajectory success. While effective, ORMs struggle with long-horizon reasoning tasks where mistakes in intermediate steps can be hard to pinpoint.

**Process Reward Models (PRMs)** emerged to address this by offering step-by-step feedback. Early work by Lightman et al. (2023) demonstrated the promise of PRMs, but their reliance on extensive human annotation for each reasoning step was a significant bottleneck. Subsequent research has focused on automating this annotation, notably using Monte Carlo (MC) rollouts to estimate Q-values for intermediate steps (Wang et al., 2023).

STEPWISER builds upon this lineage but introduces a crucial paradigm shift: reframing **stepwise reward modeling from a classification task to a reasoning task itself**. Previous PRMs often functioned as "black-box" classifiers, outputting a score or label without explanation. The current work aligns with a growing trend towards **generative judges**, where the model generates an explicit Chain-of-Thought to justify its evaluation before delivering a verdict (e.g., Zhang et al., 2024b; Chen et al., 2025). This move enhances transparency and potentially accuracy.

Furthermore, while some existing methods leverage RL for reward modeling, they often rely on sparse, trajectory-level supervision (e.g., DPO-like objectives or methods like Zha et al., 2025). STEPWISER argues for and demonstrates the efficacy of **dense, stepwise supervision via rollouts** combined with **online reinforcement learning** for training its generative judge. This distinction is vital, as it offers a richer learning signal. The paper also tackles the practical challenge of defining what constitutes a "step" in a CoT, proposing a novel self-segmentation technique to create "chunks-of-thought," an area where concurrent work also exists (He et al., 2025).

In essence, STEPWISER contributes to making LLM reasoning more robust and verifiable by:
1. **Enhancing granularity**: Moving from coarse final outcome rewards to finer-grained process rewards.
2. **Boosting transparency**: Shifting from discriminative, black-box judges to generative, meta-reasoning judges that explain their verdicts.
3. **Improving learning efficiency**: Leveraging dense, online RL signals over sparse, offline or trajectory-level supervision.
4. **Optimizing process definition**: Introducing self-segmentation for more coherent reasoning units.

This work is critical for the development of more advanced, autonomous AI agents capable of complex problem-solving, where not just the answer but the logical steps leading to it must be sound.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the STEPWISER research are driven by the limitations of current approaches to supervising multi-step reasoning in LLMs:

1. **Address the "Black-Box" Nature of Existing Process Reward Models (PRMs):** Current PRMs primarily function as classifiers, providing a binary label (correct/incorrect) or a score for each reasoning step without explaining *why* a step is valid or flawed. This lack of interpretability hinders understanding, debugging, and ultimately, trust in the model's evaluations. The authors aim to develop a **generative judge** that can articulate its rationale (i.e., meta-reason) for its judgments, making the evaluation process transparent.

2. **Overcome Generalization Issues of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Static Datasets:** Many PRMs rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with static datasets of human-annotated reasoning steps. Such datasets are expensive to create, limited in scope, and may not generalize well to novel reasoning patterns or out-of-distribution problems. The authors seek a training methodology that is more scalable and adaptive, specifically through **reinforcement learning (RL)**, which allows for online learning and continuous improvement without constant human re-annotation.

3. **Improve the Logical Validity of Intermediate Reasoning Steps:** As LLMs increasingly employ multi-step reasoning strategies (like CoT) for complex tasks, ensuring the logical soundness of each intermediate step is paramount. The primary goal is to build a superior stepwise judge that can accurately assess the quality of these steps, which can then be used to guide and enhance the reasoning capabilities of the policy LLM itself.

4. **Develop a More Effective and Efficient Stepwise Supervision Mechanism:** The paper aims to provide a comprehensive "recipe" for building such a judge that is both accurate and practical. This involves addressing critical sub-problems such as:
 - **Defining "steps" clearly:** Current heuristic-based segmentation often leads to incoherent or non-informative steps, making effective evaluation difficult. The objective is to create **logically coherent and self-contained reasoning units** through an automated self-segmentation mechanism.
 - **Deriving rich and effective learning signals:** Moving beyond simple absolute Q-values to consider how steps contribute to progress, using "relative outcomes of rollouts" for more nuanced reward assignments.
 - **Leveraging LLM's intrinsic reasoning for judgment:** Instead of merely classifying, training the judge to perform its own CoT-style analysis to reach a verdict, thereby utilizing the model's inherent reasoning abilities for the evaluation task itself.

In summary, the motivation is to advance the state-of-the-art in process supervision by developing a transparent, robust, and scalable generative stepwise judge trained via reinforcement learning, thereby enabling more reliable and interpretable multi-step reasoning in LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The STEPWISER method is a three-component pipeline designed to create a stepwise generative judge trained by reinforcement learning (RL) to meta-reason about an LLM's reasoning steps.

### 4. CoT Generation with Self-Segmentation (Chunks-of-Thought)

The first challenge addressed is the ambiguous definition of a "step" in an LLM's Chain-of-Thought (CoT). Traditional methods using delimiters like "Step 1" or double line breaks often result in fragmented or logically incomplete segments.
- **Problem:** Standard segmentation yields too many, often non-coherent, chunks that separate explanations from mathematical expressions.
- **Solution:** The authors propose "Chunks-of-Thought" via self-segmentation.
 - **Principles:** They define clear criteria for high-quality reasoning chunks: unified purpose (single objective), logical cohesion (continuous flow), and clear transitions (new phase in problem-solving).
 - **Data Generation:** An LLM (Llama-3.1-70B-it) is prompted with these rules to automatically segment initial reasoning trajectories generated by the base policy model.
 - **Fine-tuning:** The base policy model is then fine-tuned on this segmented data, learning to generate its own reasoning while simultaneously self-segmenting it into coherent chunks. This reduces the number of steps per trajectory, making subsequent annotation computationally less expensive and providing better context for the judge.

### 5. Stepwise Data Annotation (Target Rewards for Chunks)

Once the policy model generates "chunks-of-thought," these chunks need quality labels for training the judge. Instead of human labeling, an automated approach based on Monte Carlo (MC) Q-value estimation is used.
- **Q-value Estimation:** For each reasoning chunk $a_i$ within a trajectory, its quality is estimated by generating $M=16$ full completions (rollouts) *starting from that chunk* and calculating the average final reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect answer). This approximates $Q_\pi(s_{i-1}, a_i)$, the expected final reward from state $s_{i-1}$ after taking action $a_i$.
- **Labeling Approaches (Binary Target Labels):** The Q-value estimates are converted into binary labels (+/-) using three strategies:
 - **Absolute Q-value Thresholding (Abs-Q):** A chunk is labeled '+' if its Q-value estimate is greater than 0, and '-' if it's 0.
 - **Relative Effective Reward Thresholding (Rel-Effective):** Inspired by value change, this method labels chunks based on $Q_\pi(s_{i-1}, a_i) + \alpha \cdot A_\mu(s_{i-1}, a_i)$, where $A_\mu$ is an estimated advantage function reflecting the improvement in Q-value.
 - **Relative Ratio Thresholding (Rel-Ratio):** Labels are assigned based on the ratio of Q-values before and after the chunk: $Q_\pi(s_{i-1}, a_i) / Q_\pi(s_{i-2}, a_{i-1}) > \gamma$. This directly rewards progress.
- **Rationale:** These labels, derived from unbiased Q-value estimates, provide a dense and reliable signal for each step, capturing whether a chunk leads to a successful outcome or an error.

### 6. Training the Judge via Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The final component involves training the judge model using the labeled chunks and RL.
- **Generative Judge Formulation:** Instead of a simple classification head, the judge is designed to be generative. It receives the problem, reasoning history, and the current chunk to evaluate. It then first generates its own **Chain-of-Thought analysis** (meta-reasoning) to justify its verdict, followed by a final binary judgment (e.g., "\boxed{Positive}" or "\boxed{Negative}"). This forces the judge to "show its work."
- **Prompt Dataset Balancing:** A critical, often overlooked, step is addressing class imbalance in the generated stepwise labels (e.g., 70% positive samples for Abs-Q). The authors down-sample the majority class to create a balanced training set, which is found to be essential for stable RL training and preventing the model from developing a bias towards the majority class.
- **Reward and RL Training:**
 - **Reward:** The judge receives a reward of 1 if its final judgment matches the target label ($y_i$) derived from the Monte Carlo estimations, and 0 otherwise.
 - **Algorithm:** **GRPO (Generalized Proximal Policy Optimization)** is used as the optimization algorithm, known for its effectiveness in RL for LLMs.
 - **Stability Enhancements:** Techniques like "clip higher" (Yu et al., 2025) are adopted to mitigate rapid entropy decrease and prevent the model from getting stuck in local optima, particularly for binary classification tasks.

The trained STEPWISER judge can then be deployed either during policy model training (for data selection) or during inference (for guiding search and self-correction), leveraging its ability to transparently evaluate and meta-reason about intermediate steps.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the superior performance and utility of the STEPWISER framework across various evaluation metrics and application scenarios.

#### 7.1. Evaluation on ProcessBench (Judge Accuracy)

- **Superior Judgment Accuracy:** STEPWISER significantly outperforms all variants of SFT-trained discriminative judges and existing RL-trained outcome-based judges on ProcessBench, a benchmark for identifying the first incorrect step in reasoning trajectories. For instance, the Qwen2.5-7B-chunk STEPWISER with Rel-Effective signal achieved an average score of 61.9, substantially higher than the discriminative baseline's 39.7 and other community-trained PRMs (e.g., Math-Shepherd-PRM-7B at 31.5).
- **Benefit of Explicit Reasoning:** The results confirm that combining explicit reasoning generation (CoT by the judge) with online RL training is a more effective strategy for stepwise judgment.
- **Dense vs. Sparse Supervision:** STEPWISER, trained with dense, step-level signals from Monte-Carlo estimation, vastly outperforms models trained with sparse, trajectory-level (outcome-only) rewards (e.g., RL-TANGO-Qwen2.5-7B-it, 43.9 vs. STEPWISER's 61.9). This highlights the critical role of rich, step-level supervision.
- **Modest Gains from Majority Voting:** While majority voting (generating multiple judgments and picking the most frequent) showed consistent, albeit modest, improvements (+1.3 to +2.2 points on ProcessBench average), the gains were not as dramatic as typically seen in math reasoning tasks. This is attributed to the binary nature of the judgments (correct/incorrect), which limits the output space.

#### 7.2. Analysis of the Performance Gap (Ablation Studies)

Ablation studies were performed to isolate the contributions of key STEPWISER components:
- **Importance of Online RL:** A STEPWISER judge trained with offline rejection sampling fine-tuning (RS-FT) performed worse than the full RL-trained model (23.1 vs. 36.2 for 1.5B model with Rel-Ratio). This indicates that online RL is crucial for capturing the complexity of CoT reasoning and preventing performance stagnation seen with static datasets.
- **Importance of Generative CoT Reasoning:** A discriminative judge (no CoT by the judge) trained with RL still underperformed the full generative STEPWISER judge (34.3 vs. 36.2 for 1.5B model). The gap was even larger for the 7B model (47.9 vs. 60.5). This suggests that generating explicit rationales ("meta-reasoning") provides a more expressive and accurate evaluation process, especially with stronger base models.
- **Crucial Role of Prompt Dataset Balancing:** Removing the prompt dataset balancing step led to a substantial performance drop (60.5 to 47.9 for 7B model with Rel-Ratio). Without balancing, the model develops an overly optimistic bias, frequently predicting steps as "correct" due to the skewed distribution of positive samples, leading to training instability and model collapse.

#### 7.3. Utility in Downstream Applications

STEPWISER was evaluated in two practical applications:
- **Inference-Time Search via Chunk-Reset Reasoning:** The judge was used to evaluate chunks during generation. If a chunk was rejected, the model re-sampled from that point.
 - **Improved Solution Quality:** This strategy led to significant improvements in final answer accuracy. For the 1.5B model, accuracy increased from 31.2% (base model) to 36.9% (STEPWISER with Rel-Ratio), and for the 7B model, from 57.4% to 63.3%.
 - **Superior Error Detection:** The "Rejected Length" metric showed that STEPWISER-guided generation discarded more flawed chunks, indicating its superior ability to identify incorrect steps and guide the model towards better reasoning paths.
- **Training Data Selection via Stepwise Rejection Sampling Fine-tuning:** STEPWISER was used to select high-quality reasoning trajectories for fine-tuning a policy model.
 - **Better Fine-tuned Models:** Models fine-tuned on data selected by the STEPWISER judge achieved the highest performance (63.0% average accuracy for 7B model with Rel-Effective), outperforming data selected by a discriminative judge (61.9%) or outcome-based selection (60.9%).
- **Effectiveness of Relative Reward Signals:** Across all experiments (judge accuracy, inference-time search, data selection), reward signals that capture *relative progress* (Rel-Effective, Rel-Ratio) consistently yielded better judges and downstream performance compared to the Absolute Q-value (Abs-Q) signal.

#### 7.4. Self-Segmentation Impact

Self-segmentation fine-tuning allowed the base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B-chunk) to generate reasoning in a more organized manner, significantly reducing the number of chunks (e.g., from 9.6 to 6.0 for 1.5B model). This improvement in chunk coherence provided better context for the judge model and reduced computational costs for step-wise annotation, while maintaining or slightly improving the base model's overall performance.

### 8. Significance and Potential Impact

The STEPWISER research represents a significant advancement in the pursuit of more reliable, transparent, and capable Large Language Models, particularly in multi-step reasoning tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced LLM Reliability and Trustworthiness:** By enabling LLMs to meta-reason about their own intermediate steps and articulate their judgments, STEPWISER contributes directly to building more trustworthy AI systems. Users can gain insights into *why* a particular step is considered correct or incorrect, moving beyond opaque "black-box" outputs. This transparency is crucial for high-stakes applications like scientific research, legal analysis, or complex engineering.

2. **Improved LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** The ability of STEPWISER to accurately identify and provide feedback on individual reasoning chunks can be directly used to improve the LLM's core reasoning abilities.
 - **During Training:** It allows for the selection of higher-quality reasoning trajectories for fine-tuning, leading to more robust and accurate policy models. This can accelerate the development of specialized reasoning agents.
 - **During Inference:** Its use in "Chunk-Reset Reasoning" allows LLMs to self-correct in real-time, discarding flawed reasoning paths and exploring better alternatives. This is a powerful mechanism for scaling sequential computation and making LLMs more robust to early mistakes, leading to higher-quality final solutions without necessarily increasing the total accepted token length.

3. **Scalable and Automated Process Supervision:** STEPWISER addresses the scalability bottleneck of human-annotated process supervision by proposing an automated pipeline for generating dense, step-level rewards. This makes process supervision feasible for larger, more diverse datasets and for online, continuous learning. The self-segmentation technique further optimizes this process by creating more meaningful and fewer chunks, reducing computational overhead for annotation.

4. **Novel Paradigm for Reward Modeling:** The shift from discriminative classification to generative meta-reasoning for judges, combined with online RL and relative reward signals, sets a new standard for process reward modeling. It demonstrates that leveraging an LLM's inherent generative and reasoning capabilities to *evaluate* reasoning is a highly effective strategy. The consistent superiority of relative reward signals (Rel-Effective, Rel-Ratio) also highlights the importance of rewarding progress and change in value, rather than just absolute correctness.

5. **Foundation for Advanced AI Agents:** The ability to accurately and transparently evaluate intermediate reasoning steps is a cornerstone for developing more sophisticated and autonomous AI agents. Such agents could engage in more complex planning, scientific discovery, or even collaborative problem-solving, where continuous self-assessment and error correction are paramount.

6. **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated in mathematical reasoning, the principles of STEPWISER—self-segmentation, dense relative reward signals, and generative RL-trained judges—are likely transferable to other domains requiring multi-step reasoning, such as code generation, scientific hypothesis testing, or complex decision-making processes.

In conclusion, STEPWISER provides a robust and innovative framework for supervising and enhancing LLM reasoning. By making reward models "think" about the reasoning process, it paves the way for LLMs that are not only more capable but also more transparent, reliable, and amenable to continuous improvement, marking a significant step towards more intelligent and trustworthy AI.
