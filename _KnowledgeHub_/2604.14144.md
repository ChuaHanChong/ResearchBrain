# SpatialEvo: Self-Evolving Spatial Intelligence via Deterministic Geometric Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and StepFun developed SpatialEvo, a self-evolving framework for 3D spatial reasoning that leverages deterministic geometric environments to provide objective, noise-free ground truth. This approach enables visual-language models to continuously improve their spatial understanding without relying on human annotations or biased pseudo-labels, achieving superior performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks while retaining general visual capabilities.

## Problem

- Developing large-scale spatial reasoning capabilities for visual-language models (VLMs) is hindered by the high cost and static nature of geometric annotations.
- Existing self-evolving methods for reasoning tasks typically construct biased pseudo-labels from model predictions, which can reinforce errors rather than correct them.
- There is a need for a scalable paradigm that can continuously adapt to a model's evolving weaknesses and generate more challenging examples without proportional increases in human effort.

## Method

- Introduces **SpatialEvo**, a self-evolving framework that uses a ==Deterministic Geometric Environment (DGE)== to programmatically compute exact, noise-free ground truth for **16** categories of spatial reasoning tasks directly from unannotated 3D scene assets.
- Implements ==Spatially-Grounded Policy Co-Evolution==, where a single VLM acts as both a ==Questioner== (generating physically valid spatial questions) and a ==Solver== (deriving precise answers against DGE-verified ground truth).
- Incorporates an ==adaptive curriculum learning scheduler== that dynamically adjusts the training distribution to concentrate resources on the model's weakest task categories, fostering an endogenous curriculum.

## Results

- **SpatialEvo** achieved the highest average scores on spatial reasoning benchmarks, scoring **51.1** (3B) and **54.7** (7B), outperforming baseline methods that use static data tuning or self-supervised RL.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the Deterministic Geometric Environment (DGE) and its physical grounding, with replacing DGE ground truth with majority-voting pseudo-labels causing the largest degradation (**5.1-point** average drop).
- The framework maintained competitive performance on general visual understanding benchmarks (e.g., **MMStar**, **RealWorldQA**), demonstrating that specialized spatial training does not degrade broader cognitive capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ground truth for 3D spatial reasoning is a deterministic consequence of underlying geometry, allowing objective, noise-free supervision directly from the environment, unlike many other reasoning domains.
> - A shared-parameter VLM can effectively co-evolve by taking on dual roles of Questioner and Solver, with the DGE providing objective physical feedback to correct hallucinations and probe cognitive boundaries.
> - Dynamically adjusting training focus to areas of model weakness through an adaptive scheduler leads to more efficient and targeted learning, enabling an emergent curriculum without manual design.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spatial reasoning in three-dimensional environments is a foundational capability for embodied intelligence, robot navigation, and scene question answering. Developing models that can continuously improve their spatial understanding at scale has been hindered by the high cost of acquiring geometric annotations for training data. Existing research has largely pursued two main avenues:

1. **Large-scale annotated datasets**: Approaches such as SpatialVLM, SpatialBot, and SpatialLadder have made progress by fine-tuning models on extensive datasets containing multi-view images paired with geometric question-answer data. However, these datasets are static, meaning their training distribution is fixed at creation time. This inherent limitation prevents them from adapting to the model's evolving weaknesses, generating more challenging examples as the model improves, or scaling without a proportional increase in human annotation effort.

2. **Self-evolving paradigms**: This approach aims for continuous model improvement by distilling training signals from raw inputs through iterative self-play. While promising for tasks like natural language and general vision, existing self-evolving methods face a critical challenge: the ground truth cannot be directly observed from the environment. Consequently, these methods typically construct pseudo-labels by aggregating the model's own predictions, often through majority voting or self-consistency mechanisms. This process introduces systematic bias, as pseudo-labels inherit the model's existing errors, potentially reinforcing rather than correcting them during training.

SpatialEvo addresses these limitations by leveraging a property unique to 3D spatial reasoning: the ground truth for a spatial question is a deterministic consequence of the underlying geometry. Unlike many other domains, correct answers to spatial questions can be computed exactly and programmatically from fundamental scene assets like dense point clouds and calibrated camera poses, without requiring any model involvement or human annotation. This work differentiates itself by replacing the unreliable model consensus used in prior self-evolving frameworks with objective, physical feedback derived directly from the geometric environment, thereby providing noise-free supervision. It represents a shift towards enabling models to continuously improve their spatial reasoning by interacting with a verifiably consistent physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SpatialEvo stems from the aforementioned limitations in scaling spatial reasoning capabilities for visual-language models (VLMs). The researchers identify the continuous bottleneck of geometric annotation costs and the inherent weaknesses of current training paradigms:

- **Static data limitations**: Existing large-scale spatial datasets, while valuable, offer a fixed training distribution. This prevents dynamic adaptation to a model's current cognitive state, hindering the generation of increasingly challenging examples and requiring continuous, expensive human annotation for scaling.
- **Bias in self-evolving methods**: Current self-evolving VLMs, when applied to reasoning tasks, construct ground truth from model predictions. This "model consensus" approach risks propagating and reinforcing the model's own geometric errors due to systematic biases in pseudo-label generation.

Building on the insight that 3D spatial reasoning ground truth is deterministically computable from underlying geometry, the research sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Self-Evolving Framework**: To introduce a novel self-evolving paradigm specifically for 3D spatial reasoning that can continuously enhance a model's capabilities without relying on manual annotation or biased pseudo-labels.
2. **Establish a Deterministic Geometric Environment (DGE)**: To design and implement an environment that formalizes 16 categories of spatial reasoning tasks, defining explicit geometric validation rules. This DGE would programmatically compute exact, noise-free ground truth directly from unannotated 3D scene assets (point clouds and camera poses), acting as an objective physical feedback mechanism.
3. **Implement Spatially-Grounded Policy Co-Evolution**: To enable a single, shared-parameter VLM to co-evolve by performing dual roles:
 - **Questioner**: Generating physically valid spatial questions grounded in observed scene geometry.
 - **Solver**: Deriving precise answers to these questions by referencing DGE-verified ground truth, thereby internalizing spatial reasoning principles.
4. **Incorporate Adaptive Curriculum Learning**: To develop a task-adaptive scheduler that dynamically adjusts the training distribution. This scheduler would concentrate training resources on the model's weakest task categories, fostering the endogenous emergence of a curriculum without manual design or explicit difficulty sequencing.
5. **Validate Performance and Generalization**: To demonstrate that the SpatialEvo framework achieves superior performance on a diverse set of spatial reasoning benchmarks while maintaining or improving general visual understanding, without compromising broader cognitive capabilities.

These objectives collectively aim to establish a more scalable, robust, and efficient training paradigm for spatial intelligence in VLMs, grounded in the objective consistency of the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpatialEvo operates as a self-evolving framework for 3D spatial reasoning, fundamentally composed of two interacting components: the Deterministic Geometric Environment (DGE) and Spatial-Grounded Policy Co-Evolution.

**Deterministic Geometric Environment (DGE)**

The DGE is the core component providing objective, noise-free supervision. It functions as a "Geometric Oracle" that computes exact ground truth directly from 3D scene assets without any model intervention.

- **Task Space Formulation**: The DGE formalizes 16 distinct spatial reasoning tasks, categorized by observational granularity:
 - **Multi-image scene-level tasks (6)**: Object counting, object size, absolute distance, relative distance, relative direction, and room size estimation, requiring integration of global 3D layout across multiple frames.
 - **Single-image tasks (3)**: Single-view relative direction, camera-to-object distance, and inter-object depth ordering, assessing understanding of single-frame perspective geometry.
 - **Dual-image tasks (7)**: Inter-camera relative position, camera-to-object position, camera-to-region position, camera motion estimation, visibility comparison, inter-camera elevation, and attribute measurement, focusing on geometrically consistent inference across viewpoints.
 These tasks span metric measurement, topological relations, and camera pose reasoning, all programmatically solvable from scene geometry.
- **Task-Specific Geometric Validation Rule Sets**: For each of the 16 tasks, the DGE employs pre-defined rule sets that decompose complex spatial reasoning into executable atomic criteria. These rules ensure question validity along three dimensions:
 - **Premise Consistency**: All referenced scene entities (e.g., frame indices, object categories, spatial regions) must exist and be uniquely localizable in the underlying assets.
 - **Inferential Solvability**: Geometric premises must be unambiguously computable (e.g., minimum point cloud density for metric tasks, sufficient viewpoint disparity for orientation).
 - **Geometric Degeneracy Filtering**: Physically unstable, highly ambiguous, or low-training-value edge cases are identified and discarded. Numerical tolerances for continuous tasks are defined at the reward stage, not the validity stage.
- **Automated Verification Pipeline**: This pipeline converts 3D scene datasets into interactive ground-truth judging engines:
 - **Stage 1: Entity Parsing**: A lightweight Large Language Model (LLM) extracts structured semantic elements (e.g., frame indices, object categories) from the Questioner's free-form natural language questions, normalizing them for geometric computation.
 - **Stage 2: Legality Verification**: Extracted entities are validated against the task-specific rule sets. If a question violates physical constraints or references non-existent entities, the DGE returns a negative reward to the Questioner, preventing propagation of invalid questions.
 - **Stage 3: Ground-Truth Synthesis**: For valid questions, the DGE uses a geometric toolkit to perform precise numerical computations (e.g., coordinate transformations, bounding box fitting, depth-map projection) in the global coordinate frame, generating exact ground-truth answers and recording intermediate geometric states.

**Spatial-Grounded Policy Co-Evolution**

This mechanism drives the advancement of spatial intelligence within a single Vision-Language Model ($\pi_\theta$) using the GRPO algorithm.

- **Spatial Self-Play Mechanism**: The VLM alternates between Questioner and Solver roles via role-conditioned prompting. Parameter sharing enables knowledge transfer between roles.
 - **Questioner**: Observes the global 3D scene layout from multi-view RGB images and generates physically valid, reasoning-worthy spatial questions for a given task type.
 - **Solver**: Independently derives precise answers to these questions, grounded by the deterministic ground truth from the DGE. This encourages the model to probe its cognitive boundaries as a Questioner and correct hallucinations against objective reality as a Solver.
- **Task-Adaptive Scheduling**: A lightweight scheduler dynamically adjusts the task sampling distribution to achieve curriculum learning. It infers feasible tasks for the current scene, maintains cumulative scores and sample counts for each task, and estimates historical effective accuracy (smoothed with a pseudo-count prior for stability). Sampling weights are negatively correlated with accuracy, with a minimum exploration weight, ensuring training focuses on the model's weakest areas.
- **Questioner Reward Design ($r_Q$)**: $r_Q = \alpha f_{fmt} + (1-\alpha) f_{valid} \cdot f_{obs}$, with $\alpha=0.1$.
 - $f_{fmt}$: Binary score for adherence to predefined output format.
 - $f_{valid}$: Geometric validity score from DGE, which is 0 for invalid questions and can be adjusted downwards for formally valid but low-value questions (e.g., object count of exactly one).
 - $f_{obs}$: Visual observation quality score, assessed by a lightweight LLM judge, evaluating whether the Questioner's observation description leverages global spatial information and provides sufficient visual grounding for the question. The multiplicative term $f_{valid} \cdot f_{obs}$ ensures questions are both geometrically sound and visually grounded.
- **Solver Reward Design ($r_A$)**: Differentiated based on question validity.
 - If question is valid: $r_A = \alpha f_{fmt} + (1-\alpha) f_{acc}$, where $f_{acc}$ is anchored to DGE-computed ground truth. For continuous values, a relative-error threshold grid is used; for discrete values, exact match or partial credit applies.
 - If question is invalid: $r_A = \alpha f_{fmt} + (1-\alpha) f_{explain}$, where $f_{explain}$ is the quality of the Solver's explanation for the DGE's invalidation reason, scored by a lightweight LLM judge. This ensures that even invalid questions provide valuable learning signals about task rules and geometric constraints.
- **GRPO Training Procedure**: For each training scene, the scheduler samples a task. The Questioner generates multiple candidate questions. The DGE verifies them, assigns Questioner rewards, and provides ground truth or invalidity reasons. Unique questions are passed to the Solver, which generates multiple candidate answers. Solver rewards are assigned against DGE ground truth or explanation quality. Advantage values are normalized within groups. Gradients from both Questioner and Solver are applied jointly to the single, shared VLM.

**Experimental Setup**
The DGE was constructed using training splits from ScanNet, ScanNet++, and ARKitScenes. Experiments were conducted using Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as backbone models, trained entirely via online reinforcement learning using GRPO. Baselines included methods employing static data tuning or RL with curated annotations, and self-supervised RL. Evaluation encompassed nine benchmarks, covering diverse spatial reasoning aspects and general visual understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on SpatialEvo yielded several findings regarding its performance, architectural contributions, and learning dynamics.

**Overall Performance**
SpatialEvo demonstrated consistent performance gains across different model scales. It achieved the highest average scores at both 3B (51.1) and 7B (54.7) scales when compared to baseline methods, indicating a general improvement in spatial reasoning capabilities.

**Core Spatial Reasoning Benchmarks**
The framework showed substantial improvements on benchmarks directly related to the multi-view scene-level and perspective-dependent task categories integrated into the DGE. For instance, on VSI-Bench, SpatialEvo scored 39.2 (3B) and 46.1 (7B), outperforming baselines such as SpatialLadder (45.4 for 7B) and SpaceR (36.8 for 7B). Similar gains were observed on EmbSpatial and ViewSpatial, confirming the effectiveness of DGE-driven self-evolution in internalizing embodied and perspective-dependent spatial reasoning, which transferred to novel benchmarks.

**General Capability Retention**
SpatialEvo maintained competitive performance on general visual understanding benchmarks, specifically MMStar and RealWorldQA. On MMStar, scores of 55.2 (3B) and 62.5 (7B) were achieved, remaining close to the untuned baseline and surpassing annotation-dependent methods. This suggests that the specialized spatial training does not lead to degradation in broader visual understanding.

**Baseline Degradation Analysis**
Analysis of baseline methods revealed performance degradation in certain areas. SpatialLadder and ViLaSR exhibited significant drops on V-STAR (spatio-temporal reasoning), indicating that their training pipelines might introduce distributional shifts. SpaceR showed a decline on CoreCognition, suggesting that fixed reward functions tailored to specific spatial tasks can limit broader cognitive abilities. SpatialEvo avoided these issues, demonstrating a balance between spatial specialization and general capability preservation.

**Ablation Studies (Qwen2.5-VL-7B)**
A systematic ablation study confirmed the contribution of SpatialEvo's core components:
- **Architectural Design**:
 - Removing the **Questioner** resulted in a 1.6-point average performance drop, particularly affecting VSI-Bench and ViewSpatial.
 - Removing the **Solver** led to a larger 3.2-point drop, with VSI-Bench showing a sharp decline, emphasizing the importance of online geometric derivation.
 - Replacing DGE ground truth with **majority-voting pseudo-labels** (dubbed "w/o Physical Grounding") caused the largest degradation, a 5.1-point average drop, with VSI-Bench plummeting to 18.8. This directly validated the critical role of deterministic physical feedback in preventing the consolidation of systematic prediction biases.
 - Removing the **adaptive task scheduler** resulted in a marginal 0.3-point drop, indicating its contribution to dynamic resource allocation.
- **Questioner Reward**:
 - Removing the **geometric validity reward** led to a 0.8-point drop, increasing the generation of physically invalid questions.
 - Removing the **visual observation reward** caused a smaller but consistent 0.2-point drop, suggesting the Questioner generated questions with insufficient visual grounding.
- **Solver Reward**:
 - Removing the **explanation reward for invalid questions** resulted in a 0.4-point drop, particularly on complex spatial benchmarks like VSI-Bench and ViewSpatial. This validated that learning from invalid questions and their geometric constraints is an effective training signal.

**Online Evolution vs. Static Learning (VSI-Bench, Qwen2.5-VL-3B)**
A controlled comparison showed that SpatialEvo's online self-evolution achieved an average score of 46.3, surpassing static reinforcement learning (SpatialLadder RL: 40.1) and supervised fine-tuning (SFT on SpatialLadder Data: 43.7, SFT on SpatialEvo Offline Data: 43.9) baselines, even with a narrower task scope. This demonstrated the advantage of continuous alignment of training samples to the solver’s cognitive frontier through real-time interaction.

**Curriculum Emergence Analysis**
The adaptive scheduler's effect was evident across self-evolution iterations. The full SpatialEvo with the scheduler showed monotonically increasing average performance on VSI-Bench over four iterations, while the version without the scheduler stagnated or declined. Reward trajectories confirmed that Questioner validity rewards stabilized, and Solver accuracy increased while invalid question ratio decreased. The "Scene-Task Curriculum Dynamics" plot revealed that the scheduler dynamically adjusted sampling rates, up-weighting harder categories (e.g., Relative Direction, Relative Distance) and down-weighting easier ones (e.g., Room Area, Object Size), demonstrating an endogenously emergent curriculum.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SpatialEvo represents a significant development in the field of 3D spatial reasoning for vision-language models, primarily through its novel approach to self-evolution.

**Paradigm Shift in Self-Evolution**: The most notable contribution is the introduction of a self-evolving paradigm for 3D spatial reasoning that bypasses the limitations of previous methods reliant on model consensus for pseudo-label generation. By exploiting the deterministic nature of geometric ground truth in 3D environments, SpatialEvo establishes a framework where objective, noise-free physical feedback directly supervises model learning. This shift from "model-as-judge" to "environment-as-judge" provides a more reliable and robust training signal, preventing the reinforcement of model biases and errors.

**Scalability and Efficiency in Data Acquisition**: The framework addresses the critical bottleneck of costly geometric annotation. By converting unannotated 3D scene assets into inexhaustible sources of noise-free supervision through the Deterministic Geometric Environment (DGE), SpatialEvo offers a scalable pathway for continuous model improvement. This reduces reliance on manually labeled datasets, which are expensive to create, static in nature, and cannot adapt to a model's evolving capabilities.

**Robustness and Precision**: Anchoring training signals to objective physical laws, rather than subjective or statistical model agreements, ensures that every gradient update is grounded in verifiable geometric reality. This is expected to lead to models with a more precise and physically consistent understanding of 3D spaces. The ability of the Solver to learn from invalid questions, by explaining the DGE's rejection reasons, further deepens the model's understanding of task rules and geometric constraints.

**Adaptive and Endogenous Learning**: The task-adaptive scheduler enables an emergent curriculum, where training automatically concentrates on the model's weakest categories. This dynamic allocation of learning resources, driven endogenously by model performance, offers a more efficient and targeted training process compared to static curricula or uniform sampling.

**Broader Impact on Embodied Intelligence**: The physically grounded self-evolution paradigm demonstrated by SpatialEvo has implications beyond spatial reasoning. It could serve as a foundational reference for broader embodied intelligence research, particularly in scenarios where models need to learn and adapt by interacting with verifiable physical environments. This approach aligns with the goal of creating intelligent agents that can continuously acquire capabilities through self-exploration rooted in objective world feedback.

**Potential Future Directions**: While SpatialEvo demonstrates significant advancements, the paper acknowledges certain limitations. Its core reliance on high-fidelity 3D assets (e.g., dense point clouds, calibrated camera poses) currently restricts its applicability mainly to static indoor environments. Future work could explore reducing this dependency by leveraging alternative or implicit spatial representations, or by developing techniques for on-demand geometry construction, to enhance scalability and generalization to more diverse and dynamic environments. Improving the robustness of LLM-based entity parsing in the DGE pipeline and mitigating sensitivity to point cloud quality are also avenues for further research.
