# REFLECT: Summarizing Robot Experiences for Failure Explanation and Correction

> [!summary] Summary
> The REFLECT framework, developed by researchers at Columbia University, enables robots to explain and correct their own failures by transforming multimodal robot experiences into a hierarchical, task-informed textual summary for Large Language Models. This approach achieved high success rates in failure explanation and correction planning on the new RoboFail dataset, demonstrating the critical role of multisensory data and structured prompting for effective robot autonomy.

## Problem

- Robots operating in complex, real-world environments inevitably encounter failures, requiring tedious, manual human intervention for diagnosis and debugging.
- Traditional robot failure explanation methods are often specific to pre-defined scenarios or limited tasks, lacking generalizability across diverse failure types.
- Bridging the gap between continuous, high-dimensional, multimodal robot sensor data and the text-based input format of Large Language Models (LLMs) is a major challenge for applying LLMs to embodied AI.

## Method

- A ==Hierarchical Robot Summary== module converts raw multimodal sensory data (RGB-D, audio) into a structured, multi-level textual format (==scene graphs==, event-based captions, subgoal summaries) suitable for LLM processing.
- A ==Progressive Failure Explanation== module uses an LLM (GPT-4) to detect failures at the subgoal level, then iteratively drills down to event-based summaries for detailed 'what happened' and 'what was wrong' explanations.
- A ==Failure Correction Planner== leverages the LLM-generated failure explanation to formulate high-level correction plans, which are then mapped to low-level ==robot action primitives==.

## Results

- **REFLECT** achieved superior performance in failure explanation, localization, and correction planning, with ~**88.4%** explanation success for execution failures in simulation and ~**68.8%** in real-world settings.
- Ablation studies showed that incorporating audio information was critical, improving explanation and localization by approximately **20%** for execution failures in real-world scenarios.
- The structured, task-relevant hierarchical summary significantly outperformed generic captioning or direct LLM summarization of raw sensor data, while progressive explanation improved LLM reasoning accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating multisensory data, especially audio, is crucial for detecting and explaining a wider range of robot execution failures that visual information alone might miss.
> - A hierarchical, task-informed textual summary of robot experiences effectively bridges the gap between raw multimodal sensor data and the linguistic input required by LLMs for robust reasoning.
> - A progressive explanation approach, moving from high-level subgoal verification to detailed event-based analysis, significantly improves the accuracy of failure localization and explanation for LLMs.
