# REFLECT: Summarizing Robot Experiences for Failure Explanation and Correction

> [!summary] Summary
> The REFLECT framework, developed by researchers at Columbia University, enables robots to explain and correct their own failures by transforming multimodal robot experiences into a hierarchical, task-informed textual summary for Large Language Models. This approach achieved high success rates in failure explanation and correction planning on the new RoboFail dataset, demonstrating the critical role of multisensory data and structured prompting for effective robot autonomy.

## Problem

- Robots operating in complex, real-world environments inevitably encounter failures, requiring tedious, manual human intervention for diagnosis and debugging.
- Traditional robot failure explanation methods are often specific to pre-defined scenarios or limited tasks, lacking generalizability across diverse failure types.
- Bridging the gap between continuous, high-dimensional, multimodal robot sensor data and the text-based input format of Large Language Models (LLMs) is a major challenge for applying LLMs to embodied AI.

## Method

- A ==Hierarchical Robot Summary== module converts raw multimodal sensory data (RGB-D, audio) into a structured, multi-level textual format (==scene graphs==, event-based captions, subgoal summaries) suitable for LLM processing.
- A ==Progressive Failure Explanation== module uses an LLM (GPT-4) to detect failures at the subgoal level, then iteratively drills down to event-based summaries for detailed 'what happened' and 'what was wrong' explanations.
- A ==Failure Correction Planner== leverages the LLM-generated failure explanation to formulate high-level correction plans, which are then mapped to low-level ==robot action primitives==.

## Results

- **REFLECT** achieved superior performance in failure explanation, localization, and correction planning, with ~**88.4%** explanation success for execution failures in simulation and ~**68.8%** in real-world settings.
- Ablation studies showed that incorporating audio information was critical, improving explanation and localization by approximately **20%** for execution failures in real-world scenarios.
- The structured, task-relevant hierarchical summary significantly outperformed generic captioning or direct LLM summarization of raw sensor data, while progressive explanation improved LLM reasoning accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating multisensory data, especially audio, is crucial for detecting and explaining a wider range of robot execution failures that visual information alone might miss.
> - A hierarchical, task-informed textual summary of robot experiences effectively bridges the gap between raw multimodal sensor data and the linguistic input required by LLMs for robust reasoning.
> - A progressive explanation approach, moving from high-level subgoal verification to detailed event-based analysis, significantly improves the accuracy of failure localization and explanation for LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the nexus of several critical and rapidly evolving fields: **Explainable AI (XAI) in Robotics, Large Language Models (LLMs) for Embodied AI, Multimodal Perception, and Robot Failure Diagnosis and Recovery.**

Traditionally, robot failure analysis has often been a tedious, manual process, requiring human experts to sift through logs and debug complex code. Early attempts at **robot failure explanation** in the Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) community (e.g., Das and Chernova, Diehl and Ramirez-Amaro, Song et al., Inceoglu et al.) typically addressed specific, pre-defined failure scenarios or focused on limited tasks. These methods often relied on hand-crafted rules or narrow-scope models. REFLECT distinguishes itself by aiming for a more generalizable approach that can detect and explain a wider range of failures without strong prior assumptions about task configuration or failure types.

The emergence of **Large Language Models (LLMs)** has revolutionized AI, demonstrating remarkable reasoning, planning, and language generation capabilities on textual data. This has naturally led to significant interest in integrating LLMs into **embodied AI** systems to enhance robot intelligence (e.g., Ahn et al., Huang et al., Singh et al.). However, a major challenge in this integration is bridging the gap between the continuous, high-dimensional, and multimodal sensory data from a robot and the textual input format expected by LLMs. This paper directly addresses this challenge by proposing a structured way to "transform the robot failure reasoning task into a language reasoning task," thereby effectively grounding LLms in robot experiences. It extends prior work on self-reflective and self-corrective LLMs by operating directly on raw multisensory robot observations and performing temporal reasoning over long-horizon tasks, rather than relying on assumed ground truth feedback associated with isolated actions.

The emphasis on **multisensory data** (RGB-D, audio, proprioception) and their structured integration into a "task-informed scene graph" and "audio summary" is crucial. This aligns with a broader trend in embodied AI to leverage rich, diverse sensory inputs for a more comprehensive understanding of the environment and robot-environment interactions. The paper's contribution to zero-shot, task-centric, and action-centric captioning of robot sensory data directly addresses a gap identified in **dense video captioning** for robot tasks, which traditionally suffered from a lack of large-scale annotated datasets.

Furthermore, the creation of the **RoboFail dataset** (including both simulation and real-world failure demonstrations) is a significant contribution to the community. Benchmarks are vital for systematic evaluation and advancement in any research field, and RoboFail directly addresses the scarcity of publicly available datasets for robot failure analysis, explanation, and recovery. This dataset fosters reproducible research and encourages the development of more robust and explainable embodied AI systems.

In summary, REFLECT pushes the boundaries of explainable robotics by harnessing the power of LLMs, developing novel methods for multimodal robot experience summarization, and providing a much-needed benchmark dataset, thereby facilitating the development of more autonomous, robust, and transparent robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind REFLECT stems from the inherent challenge of deploying robotic systems in complex, real-world environments for long-horizon tasks: **failures are inevitable.** When a robot fails, it is critical for the system to understand *why* the failure occurred and ideally, *how* to recover. Without this capability, debugging becomes a tedious manual process for human operators, and the robot's autonomy is severely limited.

The authors hypothesize that an effective robot failure reasoning framework must address three key components:
1. **Summarize "what happened":** Convert unstructured, multimodal robot sensory data (e.g., visual, audio, proprioceptive) into a structured, unified, and intelligible format. This involves abstracting raw sensor streams into meaningful events and states.
2. **Reason "what was wrong":** Infer from the summarized experience whether the expected outcomes of the robot's high-level plan were achieved, and if not, pinpoint the misalignment or root cause of the failure. This requires a strong logical reasoning capability.
3. **Plan "what to do":** Based on the failure reasoning, generate an executable plan to correct the identified failure and allow the robot to complete the original task. This emphasizes the closed-loop nature of robust autonomous systems.

Given the recent advancements of Large Language Models (LLMs) in reasoning and planning, the core objective of REFLECT is to **leverage LLMs for robot failure explanation and correction.** The key challenge, then, becomes *how to effectively transform the complex, multimodal robot failure reasoning task into a language reasoning task* that LLMs can process. This involves developing a strategy for generating a textual summary of robot sensory data that possesses two crucial attributes:
- **Multisensory:** The summary must integrate information from all available sensory modalities (visual, audio, contact) because different failure types might be more evident through one modality than another (e.g., dropping an object visible via audio, or object state changes only evident visually).
- **Hierarchical:** The summary needs to operate at multiple levels of abstraction. A high-level summary can quickly localize a failure by comparing plan to outcome, while lower-level summaries provide sufficient environmental context for LLMs to generate detailed, informative explanations useful for planning corrections.

In essence, REFLECT aims to make robotic systems more **explainable** (by providing natural language failure descriptions) and more **robust/autonomous** (by enabling self-correction), thereby reducing human intervention and increasing the feasibility of deploying robots in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The REFLECT framework is composed of three main modules: a **Hierarchical Robot Summary** module, a **Progressive Failure Explanation** module, and a **Failure Correction Planner**.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Robot Summary

This module is responsible for converting raw, continuous robot observations into a structured, multi-level textual summary suitable for LLM processing. It consists of three levels:

- **L0: Sensory-Input Summary:** This lowest level processes raw multimodal data into a unified structure.
 - **Visual Summary:** RGB-D observations are used to generate *task-informed scene graphs*. This involves object detection, projection to 3D point clouds, and computing inter-object and robot-object spatial relations (e.g., `inside`, `on top of`, `near`). CLIP embeddings are used for detecting object states (e.g., `microwave turned on/off`). Crucially, point clouds are aggregated over time, and scene graphs focus on task-relevant objects to ensure succinctness and relevance.
 - **Audio Summary:** Audio streams are segmented, and pre-trained audio-language models (e.g., AudioCLIP) are used to detect audio events (e.g., `something drops`, `water runs`) by computing similarity with candidate event labels.
- **L1: Event-Based Summary:** This level selects "key frames" from the L0 data, which represent significant changes or events, and converts their corresponding scene graphs and audio summaries into natural language captions. Key frames are identified when the scene graph changes, an audio event starts/ends, or a subgoal execution ends. The captions provide detailed, temporal descriptions (e.g., `[timestep] Action: [robot action]. Visual observation: ... Auditory observation: ...`).
- **L2: Subgoal-Based Summary:** This highest level summarizes observations at the end of each high-level subgoal (e.g., "put pot in sink"). It is designed for quick identification of misalignments between the robot's high-level plan and execution outcomes, ignoring low-level action details unless a failure is detected.

#### 3.2. Progressive Failure Explanation

This module leverages the hierarchical summary to detect, localize, and explain failures using an LLM (specifically, GPT-4). The process is progressive:
1. **Success Verification:** The algorithm first iterates through each subgoal (using the L2 summary) and prompts the LLM to verify if the subgoal was satisfied ("Yes" or "No").
2. **Execution Analysis (if subgoal fails):** If an LLM indicates a subgoal was not satisfied, the system shifts to an "execution failure." It retrieves relevant historical observations from the L1 (event-based) summary leading up to the failure point. The LLM is then prompted to explain *what happened* at that time and *what caused the failure*, providing a detailed, action-level explanation.
3. **Plan Analysis (if all subgoals succeed but task fails):** If all subgoals are achieved but the overall task still fails, it implies a "planning failure." In this case, the LLM is given the original robot plan, the final state of the environment (from L0), and the overall task goal. It is then prompted to identify *what was wrong with the robot plan* that led to task failure.

#### 3.3. Failure Correction Planner

Once a failure explanation is generated by the LLM, this module uses it to guide the LLM in generating a high-level correction plan. The prompt for the LLM includes the task name, original plan, the detailed failure explanation, the current (failed) final state, and the desired goal state. The LLM outputs a sequence of high-level actions (e.g., `toggle off (stoveburner-2)`). To ensure executability, these LLM-generated actions are then mapped to low-level action primitives available in the environment using a pre-trained sentence embedding model to find the closest match.

#### 3.4. RoboFail Dataset

To systematically evaluate REFLECT, the authors created the RoboFail dataset.
- **Simulation Data:** 100 failure demonstrations across 10 common household tasks in the AI2THOR simulation environment. This includes RGB-D, audio, robot state data, and ground-truth metadata.
- **Real-world Data:** 30 failure demonstrations across 11 tasks collected using a UR5e robot arm in a toy kitchen environment. This includes RGB-D from an Intel RealSense D415, recorded sound from a RØDE VideoMic Pro+, and robot proprioception. For real-world data, off-the-shelf models (MDETR for object detection, CLIP for object states, AudioCLIP for sound) are used for perception.
The dataset categorizes failures into "Planning Failures" and "Execution Failures," each with sub-types, providing a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating different aspects of failure analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of REFLECT on the RoboFail dataset, compared against several baselines and ablations, yielded compelling results, affirming the design choices of the framework:

1. **REFLECT's Overall Superiority:** REFLECT consistently achieved the highest performance across all metrics—Explanation (Exp), Localization (Loc), and Correction Planning Success Rate (Co-plan)—in both simulation (AI2THOR) and real-world environments.
 - In **simulation**, REFLECT achieved high success rates: Exp ~88.4% for execution failures and 84.2% for planning failures; Loc ~96.0% and 80.7% respectively; Co-plan ~79.1% and 80.7%.
 - In **real-world** settings, performance slightly decreased due to inherent perception errors, but REFLECT still maintained a clear lead: Exp ~68.8% and 78.6%; Loc ~93.8% and 78.6%.
 - The slightly lower localization accuracy for planning failures is attributed to their less discrete, single-timestep nature.

2. **Criticality of Multisensory Data (Audio):** The "w/o sound" ablation showed a significant drop (around 20% in Exp and Loc for execution failures, real-world). This highlights the crucial role of audio information in detecting subtle but important failure events like objects dropping or state changes (e.g., stove burner turning on when visually occluded) that are difficult or impossible to infer from visual cues alone.

3. **Importance of Task-Relevant and Structured Summaries:**
 - The "BLIP2 caption" baseline, which uses a general-purpose image captioning model, performed poorly across all metrics (e.g., ~6.25% Exp in real-world execution failures). This demonstrates that generic captions lack the specific task-relevant details (object states, spatial relations) necessary for effective robot failure reasoning, emphasizing the strength of REFLECT's zero-shot, task-informed scene graph-based captioning.
 - The "LLM summary" ablation, where LLM attempts to summarize raw sensory input text, also performed significantly worse. This indicated that direct LLM summarization often loses crucial information necessary for precise failure explanation, validating the need for a carefully designed hierarchical summary structure.

4. **Benefits of Progressive Failure Explanation:** The "w/o progressive" ablation resulted in lower explanation and localization accuracy. This confirms that REFLECT's progressive algorithm, which first uses the high-level subgoal summary for quick localization and then drills down to event-based details, is more effective. This structured prompting helps the LLM focus its reasoning and avoid getting sidetracked by irrelevant information, leading to more accurate failure causes. For instance, REFLECT could infer a fridge was closed when picking an egg, while "w/o progressive" only noted an empty gripper.

5. **Hierarchical Structure's Necessity:** The "Subgoal only" ablation performed worse, particularly in explanation. While subgoal-level summaries are good for initial checks, the event-based summary (L1) is vital for providing the intermediate environment observations and detailed context needed for truly informative failure explanations (e.g., identifying *when* and *why* an object was dropped, not just that it wasn't in the gripper at the end of a subgoal).

6. **Failure Explanation Aids Correction Planning:** The "w/o explanation" baseline showed a substantial decrease in correction planning success rate. This finding underscores one of REFLECT's core hypotheses: a precise failure explanation acts as a critical guiding signal for the LLM-based planner, enabling it to generate context-aware and effective recovery plans rather than merely repeating the original, flawed sequence of actions.

7. **LLM Capability Matters (GPT-4 vs. GPT-3.5):** A comparison revealed that GPT-4 significantly outperformed GPT-3.5 in both localization and correction planning. GPT-4's superior reasoning abilities allowed it to more accurately pinpoint failures and formulate more effective corrective plans, highlighting the importance of using more advanced foundation models for complex reasoning tasks in robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The REFLECT framework represents a significant step forward in developing more autonomous, robust, and transparent robotic systems, with broad implications for both research and practical applications.

1. **Advancing Explainable Robotics (XAI):** REFLECT provides a robust and generalizable method for robots to explain their failures in natural language. This capability is crucial for building trust between humans and robots, particularly as robots are deployed in more complex and safety-critical environments. By offering clear, concise explanations of "why" a task failed, REFLECT enables non-expert users to understand robot behavior, facilitate debugging without deep technical knowledge, and potentially assist robots more effectively in recovery, moving beyond the limitations of prior task-specific XAI systems.

2. **Enhancing Robot Autonomy and Robustness:** The ability for a robot to automatically diagnose its own failures and generate corrective plans is a cornerstone of true autonomy. REFLECT's end-to-end framework for failure detection, explanation, and self-correction drastically reduces the need for human intervention, thereby increasing the operational uptime and reliability of robotic systems in dynamic, unstructured settings. This is a critical step towards realizing robots that can operate reliably in complex, real-world scenarios.

3. **Bridging Large Language Models (LLMs) and Embodied AI:** One of the most significant impacts of this work is its effective strategy for grounding powerful, text-based LLMs in the rich, continuous, and multimodal sensory experiences of a robot. By systematically summarizing visual, auditory, and proprioceptive data into a hierarchical textual format, REFLECT demonstrates a viable pathway for LLMs to meaningfully reason about the physical world and complex robot-environment interactions. This paves the way for future research into more sophisticated language-driven robot intelligence, including nuanced understanding of affordances, causality, and long-term consequences of actions.

4. **Enabling Future Research through Benchmarking:** The creation of the **RoboFail dataset** (comprising both simulated and real-world failure scenarios) is a valuable contribution to the research community. It addresses a critical gap in available benchmarks for robot failure analysis, explanation, and recovery. This dataset will serve as a vital resource for other researchers to develop, evaluate, and compare novel algorithms, accelerating progress in the field of robust and explainable embodied AI.

5. **Practical Implications for Robot Deployment:** In industries such as logistics, manufacturing, and service robotics, failures can lead to significant downtime and cost. REFLECT's ability to automate aspects of failure diagnosis and recovery could lead to more efficient and resilient robotic operations. For developers and engineers, the natural language explanations can significantly streamline debugging processes, reducing development cycles and maintenance overhead.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the reliance on heuristics for scene graph generation and challenges with low-level control failures, these also highlight promising avenues for future work. The framework lays a strong foundation for integrating advanced perception models and further refining the LLM's understanding of intricate robot states and dynamics, ultimately contributing to the realization of highly intelligent and trustworthy robotic assistants.
