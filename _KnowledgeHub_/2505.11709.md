# EgoDex: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Large-Scale Egocentric Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Apple introduce EgoDex, a dataset featuring 829 hours of egocentric human video annotated with 3D hand and upper-body pose information across 194 diverse tabletop manipulation tasks. This resource provides a large-scale foundation for imitation learning and the development of dexterous robot policies, demonstrating that model performance improves with increased data volume.

## Problem

- The robotics field lacks large-scale, richly annotated datasets for dexterous manipulation, hindering the application of data-driven learning paradigms.
- Existing data collection methods like teleoperation are bottlenecked by physical constraints and scalability, while in-the-wild internet videos lack precise 3D annotations and motor action labels.
- Many robot manipulation datasets primarily focus on simple tasks (e.g., pick-and-place), lacking diversity in complex, long-horizon dexterous behaviors.

## Method

- Developed **EgoDex**, a dataset of 829 hours of ==egocentric video== collected using Apple Vision Pro, featuring 338,000 demonstrations of 194 distinct tabletop manipulation tasks.
- Annotated the video data with high-fidelity ==3D position and orientation (SE(3) poses)== for the head, shoulders, arms, and 25 joints in each hand, captured at 30 Hz using ==ARKit==.
- Introduced systematic benchmarks for ==dexterous trajectory prediction== and ==visually goal-conditioned inverse dynamics==, leveraging multimodal inputs including egocentric video, skeletal pose, and natural-language descriptions.

## Results

- Encoder-decoder Transformer architectures consistently showed a slight performance advantage over decoder-only models, with flow matching and denoising diffusion policies outperforming behavior cloning when sampling multiple trajectories (K>1).
- Performance degraded with longer prediction horizons (e.g., a **31%** improvement in average distance when reducing horizon from 2s to 1s) but significantly improved with visual goal-conditioning (e.g., **22%** reduction in average distance).
- Model performance (average and final distance) consistently improved as the training dataset size increased, while a larger model capacity (**500M** vs **200M** parameters) did not yield further gains on the current dataset scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric human video, when augmented with precise 3D hand pose annotations, offers a passively scalable and diverse data source for learning dexterous manipulation, analogous to internet-scale datasets for NLP and 2D vision.
> - The collection of diverse everyday manipulation behaviors, beyond basic actions, is crucial for advancing robot dexterity and generalizability.
> - Model performance in dexterous manipulation tasks consistently scales with dataset size, empirically supporting the value of large-scale data for foundation models in robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in robot imitation learning: the scarcity of large-scale, richly annotated data for dexterous manipulation. While fields like natural language processing and 2D computer vision have benefited from Internet-scale datasets, a comparable corpus for robot manipulation has been absent. This data scarcity has historically hindered the application of "the bitter lesson" – the observation that simple supervised learning with vast amounts of data is often more effective than complex architectural designs – to robotics.

Existing approaches to data collection for robot imitation learning face distinct limitations. Teleoperation, used in datasets such as RoboTurk, BridgeData, RT-X, and DROID, provides high-fidelity robot control data but is bottlenecked by the physical operation of robots, making scaling beyond hundreds of hours challenging and resource-intensive. Furthermore, teleoperated data often lacks generalizability across diverse robot hardware embodiments and camera viewpoints.

Another approach involves leveraging in-the-wild Internet videos and images for visual representation learning, as seen in works by Radosavovic et al. and Ma et al. While these sources offer vast quantities of data, they typically lack the precise 3D annotations and motor action labels necessary for learning dexterous manipulation.

This research proposes an alternative path: collecting large-scale egocentric human video augmented with paired 3D hand pose annotations. This approach is identified as passively scalable, analogous to the growth of text and image data on the Internet. Prior works, such as Ego4D and EPIC-KITCHENS, have explored large-scale egocentric video but generally lack native hand pose annotations and do not specifically focus on diverse object manipulation. Similarly, specialized hand-object interaction datasets like HOI4D provide 3D hand pose but are orders of magnitude smaller due to reliance on manual annotation and often prioritize grasping over complex, long-horizon manipulation.

The EgoDex dataset differentiates itself from a closely related work, EgoMimic (Kareer et al., 2025), primarily through its scale and annotation detail. While EgoMimic explored egocentric video with 3D hand tracking, it encompassed approximately 4 hours of data and recorded only wrist positions. EgoDex significantly expands upon this by providing 829 hours of data with a broader task distribution and more comprehensive dexterous annotations, including 3D positions and orientations for the head, shoulders, arms, and 25 joints in each hand. The paper positions EgoDex as a foundational dataset, aiming to serve a role similar to ImageNet for computer vision, in driving progress in dexterous manipulation and related fields.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the data scarcity problem in imitation learning for dexterous manipulation by creating and releasing EgoDex, a large-scale, diverse, and richly annotated dataset of human dexterous manipulation from egocentric video.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key observations and challenges:

1. **"The Bitter Lesson" in Robotics:** The success of large language models and large vision models has demonstrated the effectiveness of simple supervised learning when applied to vast amounts of data. The authors aim to apply this principle to robot manipulation, which has historically lacked such data resources.
2. **Limitations of Existing Data Collection Paradigms:**
 - **Teleoperation:** While providing high-quality robot control data, teleoperation is resource-intensive and physically constrained, limiting its scalability.
 - **In-the-Wild Internet Video:** While abundant, this unstructured video data lacks the precise 3D annotations (e.g., hand pose, joint positions) and explicit motor actions required for learning dexterous manipulation.
3. **Potential of Egocentric Human Video:** Egocentric human video is identified as a passively scalable data source. The widespread adoption of wearable headsets and smart glasses suggests that this data type could become increasingly available, making learning from such data a critical area of research for future autonomous agents.
4. **Need for Rich and Precise Annotations:** Existing large-scale egocentric datasets (e.g., Ego4D) do not typically include native hand pose annotations or focus specifically on manipulation. To enable effective imitation learning for dexterous tasks, detailed and accurate 3D pose information for hands and fingers is crucial.
5. **Diversity in Manipulation Behaviors:** Many existing robot manipulation datasets primarily focus on simpler tasks like pick-and-place. This work aims to capture a wide range of complex, everyday dexterous manipulation behaviors that go beyond basic actions, such as tying shoelaces, folding laundry, unscrewing bottle caps, and dealing cards.
6. **Establishing Benchmarks:** Beyond data collection, the paper is motivated to introduce systematic benchmarks and metrics for evaluating imitation learning policies on dexterous human manipulation data. This provides a standardized framework for measuring progress and identifying areas for future research.

Ultimately, by releasing EgoDex, the authors aim to accelerate progress in robotics, computer vision, and the development of foundation models by providing a previously unavailable scale and quality of data for learning dexterous interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the systematic collection, annotation, and benchmarking of a large-scale egocentric video dataset focused on dexterous human manipulation.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

All data for EgoDex was collected using Apple Vision Pro devices running visionOS 2. This hardware was selected for its high-resolution and high-frequency passthrough capabilities, wide field of view, and the ability to record precisely what the collector sees without pose offsets. Data was collected with bare hands, eliminating the need for additional hardware apparatus. The ARKit production-grade pose tracking software integrated into visionOS was utilized for real-time tracking of human poses.

Data was recorded in approximately 10-15 minute "sessions," which contained multiple individual "episodes." Episode boundaries were delineated by pause and resume actions within the data collection application. Raw video data, which would have occupied over 500 TB, was compressed to 2.0 TB to facilitate storage, transfer, and efficient loading during training using PyTorch torchcodec, which decodes only necessary frames.

#### 3.2. Dataset Scale and Modalities

The EgoDex dataset comprises 829 hours of egocentric video at 30 frames per second (FPS) and 1080p resolution. This amounts to 338,000 task demonstrations across 194 distinct tabletop manipulation tasks, totaling 90 million frames.

The data includes comprehensive annotations across several modalities:
- **Egocentric RGB Video:** 1920 × 1080 resolution at 30 Hz.
- **Camera Intrinsics and Extrinsics:** Recorded at 30 Hz.
- **3D Pose Information:** 3D position and orientation (SE(3) poses, represented as 4×4 homogeneous transformation matrices) for all upper body joints, including the head, shoulders, arms, and 25 joints for each hand (a total of 72 joints for the upper body and two hands combined). This data is captured at 30 Hz.
- **Pose Prediction Confidence Values:** Scalars between 0 and 1, indicating ARKit's prediction confidence for each skeletal joint. A confidence of zero indicates full occlusion. Wrist confidence values denote overall hand detection, while finger joint confidences are relative to the wrist.
- **Natural Language Annotations:** Data collectors provided task names, brief descriptions, environment details, and object details. These raw metadata fields were then processed by GPT-4 to generate detailed natural language descriptions for each manipulation episode.

#### 3.3. Task Types and Diversity

The dataset categorizes tasks into three types to optimize data collection efficiency:
- **Reversible Tasks:** Pairs of inverse tasks where the final state distribution of one matches the initial state distribution of its inverse (e.g., connecting and removing a charger).
- **Reset-Free Tasks:** Tasks where the final state naturally falls within its own initial state distribution, negating the need for manual resets (e.g., throwing and catching a ball).
- **Reset Tasks:** Standard tasks requiring a manual reset of the environment after each demonstration.

EgoDex prioritizes diversity in dexterous manipulation behaviors and objects rather than scene diversity (which is limited to tabletop environments). Tasks range from simple pick-and-place to complex actions like tightening screws, tying shoelaces, dealing cards, and slotting batteries. The dataset also includes benchmark tasks from FurnitureBench assembly. An analysis of verb distribution indicates a broader range of manipulation actions compared to prior robot teleoperation datasets, with most EgoDex verbs having over 1,000 demonstrations.

#### 3.4. EgoDex Benchmarks and Evaluation

**Action Representation:** For policy training, actions (`a_t`) are represented as the 3D position of each wrist, the 6D orientation of each wrist, and the 3D position of each fingertip. This results in a 48-dimensional action space per timestep. Poses are expressed in the current camera frame, and actions are predicted as relative trajectories over a fixed time horizon (`H`).

**Benchmark Tasks:** Two main benchmarks are proposed:
1. **Dexterous Trajectory Prediction:** The policy `f_θ(o_{0..t}, s_{0..t}, l) = â_{t:t+H}` predicts future hand trajectories based on egocentric image observations (`o`), skeletal pose observations (`s`), and a natural language description (`l`).
2. **Inverse Dynamics (Visually Goal-Conditioned Policy):** The policy `f_θ(o_{0..t}, s_{0..t}, o_{t+H}, l) = â_{t:t+H}` predicts trajectories given current observations, language, and a goal image observation (`o_{t+H}`) at the end of the prediction horizon.

These benchmarks are parameterized by prediction horizon `H` (e.g., 1, 2, or 3 seconds). A fixed 1% subset of the dataset is reserved as a held-out test set for reproducible evaluations.

**Evaluation Metrics:** To account for the multimodal nature of human motion, a "best of K" metric is used. For each test sample, the trained model generates `K` candidate trajectories. The "distance" is then calculated as the Euclidean distance between the ground truth trajectory and the closest predicted trajectory among the `K` samples. This distance is averaged over each timestep and 12 keypoints (wrists and fingertips) within the predicted chunk, providing an average positional error in 3D space (meters). The final value is averaged across the entire test set.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

Experiments utilized state-of-the-art imitation learning policies from the X-IL framework. Models included Transformer architectures (encoder-decoder and decoder-only) and policy representations (behavior cloning, denoising diffusion, and flow matching). Additional experiments investigated the impact of prediction horizon, visual goal-conditioning, dataset size, and model size. All models were trained for 50,000 gradient steps with a batch size of 2048, distributed across 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Image observations were resized to 224x224 and processed by a pre-trained ResNet, while language annotations used a frozen CLIP encoder.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation provides insights into the performance of various imitation learning policies on the EgoDex dataset and the factors influencing trajectory prediction accuracy.

1. **Model Architecture Performance:**
 - Encoder-decoder Transformer architectures consistently demonstrated a slight performance advantage over decoder-only counterparts across all policy representations (Behavior Cloning, Denoising Diffusion, Flow Matching). For example, the encoder-decoder models generally achieved lower average and final distance metrics compared to their decoder-only equivalents (Table 2).

2. **Policy Representation Effectiveness:**
 - For `K > 1` (sampling multiple trajectories to find the best match), flow matching (FM) and denoising diffusion (DDPM) policies generally outperformed behavior cloning (BC). For instance, the encoder-decoder FM model achieved the lowest average distance (0.038m) and final distance (0.041m) for `K=10`. This suggests that these generative models are more effective at capturing the inherent multimodality of human motion.
 - Conversely, for `K=1` (deterministic prediction), behavior cloning models yielded lower average and final distances compared to DDPM and FM. This indicates that while DDPM and FM can sample closer trajectories in a multimodal distribution, the average deterministic prediction of BC may be more accurate.

3. **Impact of Prediction Horizon:**
 - As anticipated, increasing the prediction horizon (`H`) resulted in a degradation of performance. For a decoder-only BC model, reducing the horizon from 2 seconds to 1 second improved average and final distance metrics by 31% and 21%, respectively. Conversely, increasing the horizon from 2 seconds to 3 seconds worsened these metrics by 18% and 11% (Table 3). Similar trends were observed with the encoder-decoder FM model (Table 6).

4. **Effect of Visual Goal-Conditioning:**
 - The inclusion of a visual goal image as input significantly improved prediction accuracy. For a decoder-only BC model, visual goal-conditioning reduced the average distance by 22% and the final distance by 53% (Table 4). This suggests that providing an explicit visual target helps ground the predicted trajectory and mitigates the challenges posed by motion multimodality.

5. **Data Scaling:**
 - Performance metrics (average and final distance) consistently improved as the training dataset size increased (Figure 5). This finding provides empirical support for the collection of large-scale datasets like EgoDex, indicating that the models benefit from more data.

6. **Model Capacity:**
 - A larger decoder-only BC model with 500 million parameters did not show improved performance compared to the default 200 million parameter model on the EgoDex dataset. Both models achieved identical average and final distance metrics (0.045m and 0.062m, respectively). This may suggest that for the current dataset scale, a medium-sized model capacity is sufficient, or that further performance gains might require even larger datasets or different model architectures.

7. **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:**
 - Evaluation on a separate set of six out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks revealed varying generalization capabilities (Table 5). Some OOD tasks (e.g., Jigsaw Puzzle, Tetra Board) showed performance comparable to in-distribution tasks, while others (e.g., Blowdry Hair, Stamp Paper) resulted in worse performance. This suggests that models trained on EgoDex can generalize to OOD tasks that share some similarity with the training distribution, but performance degrades for tasks that are more distinct.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of EgoDex carries several implications for the advancement of research in robotics, computer vision, and the development of foundation models.

**Advancement in Robotics:**
- **Addressing the Embodiment Gap:** EgoDex provides a large-scale corpus of human dexterous manipulation, which is critical for bridging the gap between human capabilities and current robot performance. This data can facilitate the training of robot policies through various paradigms, including co-training with smaller robot datasets, pre-training on human data followed by fine-tuning with robot data (analogous to LLM training), learning robust visual encoders for data-efficient robot imitation learning, or extracting manipulation priors for reinforcement learning.
- **Enabling Complex Manipulation:** By offering diverse manipulation behaviors beyond simple pick-and-place, EgoDex can enable robots to learn and execute more intricate and long-horizon tasks, moving towards human-like dexterity.

**Impact on Computer Vision:**
- **Enhanced Perception Tasks:** The dataset's egocentric video combined with precise 3D hand and upper-body pose annotations makes it a valuable resource for research in action recognition, human-object interaction detection, and understanding object affordances. Researchers can study contact points, grasps, and trajectories when using tools, leading to more sophisticated human activity understanding.
- **Egocentric Vision Research:** EgoDex expands the scope of egocentric vision research by providing structured data for detailed analysis of agent-centric interactions, which differs significantly from traditional third-person or general video datasets.

**Development of Video Generation and World Models:**
- **Egocentric World Models:** Despite advancements in language-conditioned video generation and world models, there is a research gap in egocentric perspectives. EgoDex's combination of large-scale video, 3D pose, and language annotations enables the training of egocentric world models. These models can tackle unique challenges such as significant viewpoint variability, maintaining temporal and spatial consistency amid camera movements, and accurately reflecting agent-centric intentions and interactions.
- **Decision-Making for RL Agents:** Egocentric world models trained on EgoDex could serve as powerful simulators for reinforcement learning agents, allowing them to predict future visual dynamics and outcomes based on actions, thereby enhancing decision-making capabilities.

**Broader Accessibility and Acceleration of Research:**
- **Public Availability:** The public release of EgoDex democratizes access to a previously unavailable scale and quality of dexterous manipulation data. This fosters community-wide collaboration and accelerates research across various domains by providing a common benchmark and training resource.
- **Empirical Validation of Data Scaling:** The experimental results demonstrate that model performance scales with dataset size, providing empirical evidence that motivates continued large-scale data collection efforts in robotics.

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the dataset's focus on tabletop environments and potential imperfections in model-predicted dexterous annotations during occlusion or high-speed motions. Future work is suggested to include procedural background randomization and data collection in more diverse environments to enhance generalizability.
