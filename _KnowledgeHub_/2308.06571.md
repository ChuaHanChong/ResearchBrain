# ModelScope Text-to-Video Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> ModelScopeT2V, an open-source diffusion-based model from Alibaba Group, is introduced for generating videos from text prompts, achieving competitive visual fidelity, motion continuity, and semantic alignment. It demonstrates strong quantitative performance against existing methods while providing a publicly available baseline for text-to-video research.

## Problem

- Generating high-quality videos from text prompts often resulted in suboptimal fidelity and a lack of smooth motion continuity due to inherent model training difficulties.
- The field of text-to-video synthesis lacked robust, publicly accessible open-source models and codebases, which hindered further research and development.
- Existing methods struggled to effectively extend the successful principles of text-to-image diffusion models to the more complex temporal domain of video synthesis.

## Method

- Introduced **ModelScopeT2V**, a simple and trainable ==latent video diffusion model== that evolves from the ==Stable Diffusion== architecture.
- Integrated ==spatio-temporal blocks== into the denoising ==UNet== to explicitly model complex spatial and temporal dependencies, enhancing frame consistency and smooth movement.
- Implemented a ==multi-frame training== strategy that combines large-scale image-text (LAION-5B) and video-text (WebVid) paired datasets to enhance semantic understanding and temporal dynamics.

## Results

- Achieved competitive or superior quantitative performance on the MSR-VTT dataset in a zero-shot setting, recording the lowest **FID-vid (11.09)** and **FVD (550)** scores among compared state-of-the-art models.
- Produced qualitatively realistic and semantically consistent videos with a wider range of motion, balancing visual realism and strong text-to-video alignment.
- Released as the first open-source diffusion-based text-to-video generation model, fostering community engagement and serving as a robust baseline for future advancements and practical applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent Diffusion Models can be effectively extended for video generation by incorporating specialized spatio-temporal blocks that factorize convolution and attention over space and time.
> - Explicitly modeling temporal dependencies within the UNet architecture is crucial for generating smooth motion continuity and consistent visual features across video frames.
> - Leveraging a multi-frame training approach with both image and video datasets is an efficient strategy to overcome the scarcity of large-scale video data while enhancing the model's semantic diversity and temporal learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Artificial intelligence has advanced content generation across various modalities, including text, image, and audio, based on intuitive instructions. Within visual content generation, significant progress has been observed in text-to-image synthesis, particularly with the advent of diffusion models like Stable Diffusion. These models have enabled the generation and editing of static images with increasing fidelity and diversity.

However, the field of video generation has presented distinct challenges. Key hurdles include the inherent difficulty of training models that produce high-quality videos, often resulting in outputs with suboptimal fidelity and a lack of motion continuity. This complexity has limited the widespread availability of robust and publicly accessible video generation models, in contrast to the well-established open-source frameworks for image generation. The absence of a publicly available codebase for text-to-video synthesis has been identified as a factor potentially impeding further research and development in this area.

ModelScopeT2V positions itself within this landscape by addressing the challenges in video generation and aiming to provide an accessible and trainable baseline. It leverages the architectural foundation of existing text-to-image diffusion models, specifically Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) like Stable Diffusion, and extends them to the temporal domain. By introducing an open-source model, ModelScopeT2V seeks to parallel the catalyzing effect that open-source image generation models have had on their respective field, thereby fostering innovation and accelerating progress in text-to-video synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce ModelScope Text-to-Video (ModelScopeT2V), a simple and trainable baseline model for text-to-video generation. This model is designed to produce videos that adhere to textual prompts, exhibiting both visual fidelity and motion continuity.

The motivation for developing ModelScopeT2V stems from several considerations:

- **Addressing Challenges in Video Generation:** Current video generation methods frequently encounter difficulties in achieving high fidelity and smooth motion continuity, issues that ModelScopeT2V aims to mitigate through its design.
- **Leveraging Advancements in Text-to-Image Synthesis:** The success of text-to-image diffusion models, particularly Stable Diffusion, provided a foundation for adapting similar principles to video generation. The authors aim to explore how Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) can be effectively extended for temporal tasks.
- **Catalyzing Open-Source Research:** Recognizing the impact of open-source tools on research progress, especially in text-to-image synthesis, a key motivation was to provide a publicly available codebase for text-to-video generation. This is intended to serve as a baseline to encourage further research, development, and exploration within the community.

To achieve these objectives, the paper highlights two specific technical contributions:

1. **Architectural Adaptation:** The integration of spatio-temporal blocks into the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) architecture. These blocks are designed to explicitly model temporal dependencies, thereby enabling the generation of consistent frames and smooth movement transitions essential for video synthesis.
2. **Enhanced Pre-training Strategy:** The proposal of a multi-frame training mechanism that utilizes both image-text paired datasets and video-text paired datasets. This strategy aims to enhance the model's semantic understanding and diversity by leveraging the extensive semantic knowledge available in image datasets, while simultaneously learning temporal dynamics from video datasets.

Through these contributions, ModelScopeT2V aims to provide a robust and accessible framework for advancing text-to-video synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ModelScopeT2V is a text-to-video synthesis model that builds upon the architecture of text-to-image diffusion models, specifically evolving from Stable Diffusion. Its methodology involves three primary components: a VQGAN, a text encoder, and a denoising UNet, integrated with a multi-frame training strategy.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture of ModelScopeT2V

The model operates as a latent video diffusion model.
- **VQGAN (Vector Quantized Generative Adversarial Network):** This component is responsible for converting data between the high-dimensional visual space (RGB videos) and a lower-dimensional latent space. During training, a ground-truth video $v_{gt}$ (composed of $F$ frames) is encoded into latent variables $Z_{gt}^0$. For inference, the generated latent variables $Z_{pr}^0$ are decoded back into a generated video $v_{pr}$. The VQGAN parameters are initialized from Stable Diffusion and remain frozen during training.
- **Text Encoder:** A pre-trained CLIP ViT-H/14 is utilized to convert a given text prompt $p$ into a text embedding $c$. This embedding serves as conditional information to guide the video generation process, ensuring semantic alignment between the input text and the output video.
- **Denoising UNet ($\epsilon_\theta$):** This is the core of the latent video diffusion model, responsible for the iterative denoising process in the latent space.
 - **Diffusion Process (Training):** During training, Gaussian noise is progressively added to the ground-truth latent variable $Z_{gt}^0$ over $T$ steps, generating a sequence of noisy latent variables $Z_{gt}^t$.
 - **Denoising Process (Inference):** During inference, the UNet predicts the noise at each step, starting from random noise $Z_{pr}^T$, to gradually reconstruct a clear latent video representation $Z_{pr}^0$.
 - **Text Conditioning:** The UNet is augmented with a cross-attention mechanism, similar to Stable Diffusion, where the text embedding $c$ acts as the key and value in multi-head attention layers within the spatial attention blocks. This mechanism facilitates the integration of textual information into the visual features, promoting alignment between language and vision.
 - **Loss Function:** The model's objective is to minimize the discrepancy between the predicted noise $\epsilon_{pr}^{\hat{t}}$ and the ground-truth noise $\epsilon_{gt}^{\hat{t}}$ using an L2 norm.

#### 3.2. Spatio-temporal Block

A key architectural innovation in ModelScopeT2V is the spatio-temporal block, designed to capture complex spatial and temporal dependencies within the latent space. This block enhances video synthesis quality by factorizing convolution and attention mechanisms over space and time. Each spatio-temporal block comprises four sub-components:
- **Spatial Convolution:** Employs 2D CNNs with a 3x3 kernel size to extract features within individual frames (spatial dimensions $H/8 \times W/8$).
- **Temporal Convolution:** Utilizes 1D CNNs with a kernel size of 3 to extract features across the $F$ frames (temporal dimension).
- **Spatial Attention:** Operates on the spatial dimension ($HW/64$) of latent features using a Transformer-based architecture. This includes both a cross-attention module (conditioning visual features on textual features) and a self-attention module (modeling spatial relationships within visual features).
- **Temporal Attention:** Operates on the temporal dimension ($F$) using a Transformer-based self-attention module, capturing correlations across different time steps.

The model allocates a significant portion of its parameters (0.5 billion out of 1.7 billion total) to these temporal capabilities (temporal convolutions and temporal attentions), accounting for approximately 39% of the UNet's parameters.

#### 3.3. Multi-frame Training

To address the limited availability of large-scale video-text paired datasets compared to image-text datasets, ModelScopeT2V incorporates a multi-frame training strategy.
- **Dataset Utilization:** The model is trained on a combination of LAION-5B (image-text pairs) and WebVid (video-text pairs).
- **Dynamic Frame Length:** The model architecture is designed to adapt to varying frame numbers, allowing images to be treated as videos with a frame length of 1.
- **Resource Allocation:** A portion of the GPUs (one eighth) is dedicated to training on image-text datasets, while the remaining GPUs handle video-text datasets.
- **Benefits:** This approach aims to enhance semantic diversity, prevent catastrophic forgetting of image-domain knowledge acquired from pre-trained Stable Diffusion weights, and leverage the strengths of both data types for more robust video generation. Temporal convolution and attention mechanisms remain active during multi-frame training, supporting flexible frame settings.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Diffusion Process:** Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) with $T=1,000$ steps for training.
- **Inference:** DDIM sampler with classifier-free guidance, performed in 50 steps.
- **Initialization:** VQGAN and Denoising UNet are initialized from Stable Diffusion 2.1. Temporal convolution and attention outputs are initialized as zeros to allow gradual learning of temporal correspondence.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with a learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$.
- **Training Resources:** Trained on NVIDIA A100 GPUs for 267 thousand iterations, using batch sizes of 1,400 for images and 3,200 for videos.
- **Data Processing:** Videos from WebVid are sampled at 3 frames per second, selecting 16 frames from the middle square portion. VQGAN's compression factor is 8, converting 256x256 RGB images to 32x32 latent representations. The text encoder uses a maximum text length of 77 tokens with an embedding dimension of 768.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ModelScopeT2V was conducted on the MSR-VTT dataset under a zero-shot setting, meaning the model was not trained on this dataset. The generated videos had a resolution of 256x256.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

The model's performance was quantitatively assessed using three metrics: FID-vid (Fréchet Inception Distance for video), FVD (Fréchet Video Distance), and CLIPSIM (CLIP Similarity). For FID-vid and FVD, 2,048 randomly selected video clips from the MSR-VTT test set were used, generating 16 frames at 3 FPS. CLIPSIM was evaluated using approximately 60,000 sentences from the entire test split. Lower FID-vid and FVD scores indicate higher visual similarity to ground truth, while higher CLIPSIM scores indicate better semantic alignment with text prompts.

ModelScopeT2V demonstrated competitive or superior performance compared to several state-of-the-art models:
- **FID-vid:** ModelScopeT2V achieved a score of 11.09, which was the lowest (best) among the compared models, including NÜWA (47.68), CogVideo (English) (23.59), and Make-A-Video (13.17).
- **FVD:** ModelScopeT2V obtained a score of 550, which was the lowest (best) among the compared models for which data was available, including CogVideo (English) (1294) and MagicVideo (1290).
- **CLIPSIM:** ModelScopeT2V achieved a score of 0.2930. This score was competitive, being higher than NÜWA (0.2439), CogVideo (English) (0.2631), and Video LDM (0.2929). It was marginally lower than Make-A-Video (0.3049), which reportedly utilized additional training data from HD-VILA-100M.

These quantitative results indicate that videos generated by ModelScopeT2V exhibit high visual fidelity and motion quality, as well as strong semantic alignment with the input text prompts.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons were made between ModelScopeT2V and Make-A-Video, as well as Imagen Video. All compared methods generated 16-frame videos for consistent duration, with ModelScopeT2V and Make-A-Video producing 1:1 aspect ratio videos, while Imagen Video produced 2:5.

- **Comparison with Make-A-Video:**
 - ModelScopeT2V generated videos with a degree of realism for subjects such as a "robot" and a "dog" that was superior to Make-A-Video in specific examples. This was attributed to ModelScopeT2V's joint training on image-text pairs, which enhances its comprehension of text-visual correspondence.
 - ModelScopeT2V demonstrated a wider range of motion in its generated videos.
 - While Make-A-Video sometimes showed closer alignment to the overall scene prompt (e.g., "industrial site" depicting a storm), ModelScopeT2V offered distinct interpretations, such as two "abandoned" factories from various camera angles.
- **Comparison with Imagen Video:**
 - Imagen Video generally produced more vivid and contextually relevant video content. However, specific instances showed limitations, such as a distorted dog's tongue.
 - ModelScopeT2V effectively depicted the content of prompts, although with some roughness in details.
 - The comparatively higher performance of Imagen Video was attributed to its use of a larger T5 text encoder and a larger-scale training dataset, which are not utilized in ModelScopeT2V.

Overall, the qualitative results suggest that ModelScopeT2V is capable of generating semantically consistent and visually plausible videos, offering a balance of realism and motion, and demonstrating a strong foundation for future improvements.

#### 4.3. Model Parameters

The ModelScopeT2V model has a total of approximately 1.7 billion parameters. Within the denoising UNet, which contains 1.345 billion parameters, 552 million parameters (approximately 39%) are dedicated to temporal capabilities, specifically temporal convolution and temporal attention blocks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ModelScopeT2V represents a notable contribution to the field of generative AI, particularly in text-to-video synthesis, due to several factors:

- **Open-Source Availability:** It is presented as the first open-source diffusion-based text-to-video generation model. This public release is significant as it provides a readily accessible framework for researchers and developers, potentially reducing barriers to entry and accelerating innovation in a field that has historically lacked such foundational open-source tools.
- **Baseline for Future Research:** The model is anticipated to serve as a powerful and effective baseline. Its open-source nature can foster a collaborative research environment, allowing others to build upon its architecture and training strategies, test new hypotheses, and develop advanced text-to-video generation techniques.
- **Architectural Advancement:** The integration of spatio-temporal blocks into a Latent Diffusion Model architecture specifically addresses the challenge of modeling temporal dynamics in video generation. This design allows for the generation of frames with spatial consistency and smooth movement transitions, crucial for realistic video output.
- **Effective Training Strategy:** The multi-frame training approach, which leverages both image-text and video-text paired datasets, demonstrates an effective method for overcoming the scarcity of large-scale video data. By combining the semantic richness of image datasets with the temporal information from video datasets, the model enhances its overall comprehension and generation capabilities.
- **Competitive Performance:** The model achieves competitive or superior quantitative and qualitative performance against existing state-of-the-art methods across established metrics. This demonstrates the efficacy of its design choices and training methodology.
- **Community Engagement and Practical Applications:** The public availability of code and online demos has already led to community engagement, with projects extending its usage (e.g., sd-webui-text2video, Text-To-Video-Finetuning) and successful utilization in real-world applications, such as the creation of short videos. This indicates the model's practical utility and potential for broader adoption.
- **Foundation for Expansion:** The research outlines future directions, including the adoption of additional conditions (e.g., multi-condition approaches, LoRA techniques) and the generation of longer videos. This indicates ModelScopeT2V is positioned as a foundation for continuous development and exploration in complex video generation tasks.
