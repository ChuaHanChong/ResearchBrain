# RoboOcc: Enhancing the Geometric and Semantic Scene Understanding for Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboOcc introduces a 3D occupancy prediction method for robots that enhances geometric and semantic scene understanding by leveraging explicit opacity and geometry properties of Gaussian-based representations. The framework achieved an IoU of 56.48 and an mIoU of 47.67 on the Occ-ScanNet dataset, improving upon prior state-of-the-art by 2.53 IoU and 2.19 mIoU, while also demonstrating greater robustness to Gaussian overlaps.

## Problem

- Existing Gaussian-based 3D occupancy prediction methods do not sufficiently incorporate the geometric properties of 3D Gaussians, leading to coarse-grained geometric modeling and invalid occupancy representations.
- Current approaches often neglect the opacity properties of 3D Gaussians, contributing to semantic ambiguity, especially when Gaussians overlap in dense indoor scenes, which degrades prediction accuracy.
- These limitations hinder the generation of precise geometric and semantically consistent scene understanding from monocular RGB inputs, crucial for robust robotic perception.

## Method

- Developed **RoboOcc**, a framework that generates ==3D semantic Gaussians== from monocular RGB inputs and converts them into dense ==3D occupancy predictions== through a multi-stage architecture.
- Introduced an ==Opacity-guided Self-Encoder (OSE)== that uses an ==opacity-guided gated sparse convolution== to reduce semantic ambiguity by focusing on non-empty Gaussians, particularly in overlapping regions.
- Designed a ==Geometry-aware Cross-Encoder (GCE)== which generates ==ellipsoidal reference points== and employs ==dynamic semantic and geometric weighting== to achieve fine-grained geometric modeling by enhancing awareness of Gaussian geometry.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance for local 3D occupancy prediction on Occ-ScanNet, with **56.48 IoU** and **47.67 mIoU**, marking an improvement of **2.53 IoU** and **2.19 mIoU** over previous methods.
- Demonstrated superior performance for global 3D occupancy prediction on EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet, attaining **53.30 IoU** and **44.05 mIoU**, an increase of **1.78 IoU** and **1.52 mIoU**.
- Exhibited enhanced robustness to increased Gaussian overlap, experiencing only a **1.81% IoU drop** under challenging conditions, significantly less than the **9.49% drop** observed in previous state-of-the-art approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding sparse convolutions with Gaussian opacity information effectively mitigates semantic ambiguity, improving voxel semantic assignment in cluttered indoor scenes.
> - Integrating Gaussian geometry properties into the cross-interaction process between Gaussians and image features enables more precise and fine-grained geometric modeling of the environment.
> - The combination of opacity-guided self-interaction and geometry-aware cross-interaction provides complementary benefits, leading to improved overall geometric fidelity and semantic consistency in 3D occupancy prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges within the field of 3D scene understanding, a fundamental area for advancements in embodied intelligence and computer vision, particularly for robotic applications. 3D scene understanding enables robots to perceive their environment, make informed decisions, and execute various tasks such as navigation, manipulation, and interaction.

Traditionally, 3D occupancy prediction methods capture scene geometry by predicting the occupancy state of voxels in 3D space. Initial approaches often relied on dense voxel representations, which provided robustness and scalability but introduced computational redundancy due to the inherent sparsity of real-world environments. This led to the development of object-centric scene representations, which describe regions of interest more flexibly than fixed grids or sparse voxels, aiming to be more efficient.

Recent advancements have explored the use of 3D Gaussians as a flexible, object-centric scene representation. Methods such as GaussianFormer and EmbodiedOcc have demonstrated the utility of 3D Gaussians for 3D semantic occupancy prediction, particularly in indoor environments. These methods represent scenes as collections of 3D Gaussians, each characterizing a spatial region with properties like geometry, opacity, and semantic category. This approach aims to balance the sparsity of occupancy space with the diversity of object scales.

Despite the improvements offered by Gaussian-based representations, existing methods have been identified as not fully leveraging the geometric and opacity properties of these Gaussians. This limitation has been observed to hinder fine-grained modeling of complex indoor scenes and contribute to semantic ambiguities, particularly when Gaussians overlap. The RoboOcc research aims to address these specific shortcomings, thereby advancing the capabilities of 3D occupancy prediction for robots using monocular RGB input. By enhancing the utilization of Gaussian properties, the work seeks to improve both the geometric fidelity and semantic consistency of scene understanding, which is critical for robots operating in detailed indoor environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the RoboOcc research is to enhance the geometric and semantic scene understanding capabilities of robots through an improved 3D occupancy prediction method. This enhancement specifically targets the limitations identified in current Gaussian-based scene representations.

The motivation for this work stems from two main observations regarding existing Gaussian-based 3D occupancy prediction methods:

1. **Ineffective Exploitation of Geometry Properties:** Current methods do not sufficiently incorporate the geometry properties of 3D Gaussians into the network's processing. While sampling points might be generated based on Gaussian ellipsoids, the interaction process often fails to truly perceive and refine the Gaussian shapes. This leads to several issues:
 - **Coarse-grained Geometric Modeling:** Without effective refinement of their elliptical geometries, 3D Gaussians may produce inaccurate occupancy predictions and fail to capture the fine-grained details necessary for indoor scenes. Indoor environments typically require more precise object classification and shape descriptions compared to sparse outdoor scenes.
 - **Invalid Occupancy:** Suboptimally refined Gaussian geometries can result in inaccurate representations of occupied space.

2. **Neglect of Opacity Properties:** The opacity properties of 3D Gaussians, which indicate the density or "solidity" of a Gaussian, are often not effectively integrated into the Gaussian query update process. This neglect has notable consequences:
 - **Semantic Ambiguity in Overlapping Gaussians:** When multiple Gaussians overlap in space, especially in dense indoor scenes, a lack of opacity guidance can lead to confusion in assigning semantic categories to voxels during the Gaussian-to-Voxel splatting process. This ambiguity degrades the accuracy of semantic predictions.
 - **Performance Degradation with Increased Overlap:** Ablation studies on Gaussian parameters in previous works revealed that increasing Gaussian overlap (e.g., by reducing Gaussian count or increasing maximum scale) severely exacerbates semantic ambiguity and leads to performance degradation.

To address these motivations, the RoboOcc project sets out to develop specific architectural components:
- An **Opacity-guided Self-Encoder (OSE)** designed to alleviate semantic ambiguity, particularly in regions where Gaussians overlap.
- A **Geometry-aware Cross-Encoder (GCE)** aimed at achieving fine-grained geometric modeling of the surrounding scene by better incorporating Gaussian geometry properties.

Ultimately, the goal is to enable robots to obtain more spatially precise geometry and less semantically ambiguous predictions of their surrounding scenes from monocular RGB inputs, both for local instantaneous observations and global long-term scene understanding through exploration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboOcc framework is designed to generate 3D semantic Gaussians from monocular image inputs and subsequently convert them into dense 3D occupancy predictions. The overall architecture comprises three main stages: Image Encoder, Gaussian Encoder, and Gaussian-to-Voxel Splatting.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The method addresses two distinct prediction tasks:
1. **Local 3D Occupancy Prediction:** Given a monocular RGB image (I), extrinsic matrix (E), and intrinsic matrix (K), the model predicts the local 3D occupancy ($O_{local}$) within the camera frustum.
2. **Global 3D Occupancy Prediction:** For continuous perception, the model updates the global 3D occupancy ($O_t$) at timestamp `t` by incorporating the current image ($I_t$, $E_t$, $K_t$) and the historical global occupancy ($O_{t-1}$).

#### 3.2. Overall Architecture

- **Image Encoder:** An EfficientNet-B7 is employed to extract multi-scale semantic features from the input monocular RGB image.
- **Gaussian Initialization:** A set of 3D Gaussian anchors ($A$) and 3D Gaussian queries ($Q$) are randomly initialized. Each Gaussian anchor is defined by its mean ($m$), scale ($s$), rotation ($r$), opacity ($o$), and semantic category ($c$). Gaussian queries are projected into a depth map generated by a frozen DepthAnything model to obtain pixel-aligned depth features.
- **Gaussian Encoder:** This is the core component responsible for updating the Gaussian-based representation. It facilitates Gaussian self-interaction (using the Opacity-guided Self-Encoder), Gaussian-to-image cross-interaction (using the Geometry-aware Cross-Encoder), and updates between Gaussians. This process is iterative, with Gaussians being updated and optimized through repeated application of the self-encoder, cross-encoder, and decoder.
- **Gaussian-to-Voxel Splatting:** After the Gaussians are updated, this module aggregates neighboring Gaussians to generate dense semantic occupancy predictions for 3D voxel positions. For each voxel, it identifies overlapping Gaussians and weights their semantic properties, with closer and higher-opacity Gaussians contributing more to the final semantic category of the voxel.

#### 3.3. Opacity-guided Self-Encoder (OSE)

The OSE aims to alleviate semantic ambiguity, particularly concerning overlapping Gaussians, by making the network more attentive to non-empty Gaussians.
- **Mechanism:** It utilizes an opacity-guided gated sparse convolution (OGSP Conv). Instead of standard sparse convolution which can be constrained in receptive field and may not differentiate foreground from background Gaussians effectively, OSE incorporates opacity information.
- **Function:** Given Gaussian queries ($Q$) and opacity properties ($o$), the OGSP Conv computes a weighted foreground-background score using the sigmoid function on both the opacity and a 1x1 sparse convolution of the queries. This score then modulates the output of a 3x3 sparse convolution on the Gaussian queries, effectively guiding the network to focus on relevant Gaussians. Multi-scale modules are also used to expand the spatial receptive field.

#### 3.4. Geometry-aware Cross-Encoder (GCE)

The GCE is designed to extract fine-grained semantic and structural features from the image by enhancing the network's awareness of Gaussian geometry.
- **Reference Point Generation:** Initially, a series of offsets ($\Delta m_0$) are generated. These offsets are then transformed into Gaussian geometric offsets ($\Delta m$) by multiplying them with the Gaussian's scale ($s$) and rotation ($r$) properties. These geometric offsets are added to the Gaussian means ($m$) to create ellipsoidal reference points ($P$) for each Gaussian, representing its geometric extent.
- **Image Feature Sampling:** These 3D reference points ($P$) are projected onto multi-scale image feature maps ($F$) using the camera's extrinsic ($E$) and intrinsic ($K$) matrices. A deformable attention function (Sampling) is used to extract sampled queries ($Q_p$) corresponding to these projected points.
- **Mixing Mechanism:** A mixing mechanism is applied to aggregate semantic and geometric features from the sampled queries ($Q_p$):
 - **Semantics-aware Gaussian Queries ($Q_s$):** Dynamic semantic weights ($W_s$) are predicted from the Gaussian queries ($Q$) using a linear layer. These weights are then used to strengthen key semantic features in the sampled queries ($Q_p$), producing $Q_s$.
 - **Geometry-aware Gaussian Queries ($Q_g$):** Dynamic geometric weights ($W_g$) are predicted from concatenated Gaussian scale ($s$) and rotation ($r$) properties (forming geometric vectors $G$). These weights are then applied to the semantics-aware queries ($Q_s$) to generate the final geometry-aware Gaussian queries ($Q_g$), which represent a fine-grained geometric distribution.

#### 3.5. Refinement and Splatting

Gaussian anchors are refined by using the updated Gaussian queries to predict new property vectors via Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). This iterative refinement process updates the geometry, opacity, and semantic categories of the Gaussians. Finally, the Gaussian-to-Voxel splatting process converts the refined sparse Gaussians into dense voxel occupancy predictions.

#### 3.6. Loss Functions

The local occupancy prediction module is trained using a combination of loss functions: focal loss ($L_{focal}$), Lovasz-softmax loss ($L_{lov}$), and scene-class affinity losses ($L_{geo}$ and $L_{sem}$), following previous work like EmbodiedOcc.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RoboOcc method was evaluated through extensive experiments on the Occ-ScanNet and EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet datasets, assessing its performance in both local and global 3D occupancy prediction tasks.

#### 4.1. Local Occupancy Prediction

- **Quantitative Results:** On the Occ-ScanNet validation set, RoboOcc demonstrated improved performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. It achieved an IoU of 56.48 and an mIoU of 47.67. This represents an improvement of 2.53 in IoU and 2.19 in mIoU over EmbodiedOcc, the previous state-of-the-art method. The results were consistently higher across various semantic categories, including ceiling, floor, wall, chair, bed, sofa, table, tvs, furniture, and objects.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Qualitative visualizations on the Occ-ScanNet-mini dataset indicated that RoboOcc could model scenes with greater fidelity. It demonstrated an enhanced ability to capture scene layout and classify diverse semantic instances more accurately than compared methods.

#### 4.2. Global Occupancy Prediction

- **Quantitative Results:** For global 3D occupancy prediction on the EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet validation set, RoboOcc also outperformed the state-of-the-art. It achieved an IoU of 53.30 and an mIoU of 44.05. This marks an improvement of 1.78 in IoU and 1.52 in mIoU over EmbodiedOcc. The results suggest that RoboOcc exhibits generality and extensibility, particularly in integrating scene context information over time.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted on the Occ-ScanNet-mini dataset to evaluate the contributions of the proposed components and the sensitivity to Gaussian parameters.

- **Analysis of Components:**
 - **Geometry-aware Cross-Encoder (GCE):** The inclusion of GCE independently resulted in a slight increase in IoU and mIoU (from 53.93 IoU and 46.20 mIoU without either component to 54.15 IoU and 46.48 mIoU with GCE). This indicates that GCE contributes to fine-grained scene modeling through spatial geometry awareness.
 - **Opacity-guided Self-Encoder (OSE):** The OSE showed a more substantial impact on performance. Its isolated inclusion led to a notable increase (from 53.93 IoU and 46.20 mIoU to 56.55 IoU and 47.61 mIoU). This suggests that mitigating semantic ambiguity is a critical factor in indoor occupancy prediction.
 - **Combined GCE and OSE:** When both GCE and OSE were incorporated, the model achieved the highest performance, with an IoU of 57.25 and an mIoU of 47.71. This indicates that the components complement each other in enhancing scene understanding.

- **Analysis of Gaussian Parameters:** Experiments varied the number and maximum scale of Gaussians to simulate increased overlap.
 - **EmbodiedOcc Performance Degradation:** When the number of Gaussians was reduced (from 16200 to 8100) and the max scale increased (from 0.08m to 0.20m), EmbodiedOcc experienced a significant performance drop, decreasing IoU by 9.49% and mIoU by 11.39%. This confirms its sensitivity to Gaussian overlaps and the resulting semantic ambiguity.
 - **RoboOcc Robustness:** In contrast, RoboOcc demonstrated greater robustness. Under the same maximum parameter variation (8100 Gaussians, 0.20m max scale), its IoU decreased by only 1.81%, and its mIoU by 3.56%.
 - **Comparative Advantage:** At a medium parameter variation setting (12150 Gaussians, 0.16m max scale), RoboOcc outperformed EmbodiedOcc by a margin of 8.47 in IoU and 6.27 in mIoU. This substantiates the effectiveness of RoboOcc's modules in mitigating semantic ambiguity and achieving fine-grained modeling even under conditions of increased Gaussian overlap.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboOcc research contributes to the field of 3D scene understanding by addressing specific limitations in Gaussian-based representations for robotic perception. The proposed method demonstrates an ability to improve both geometric fidelity and semantic consistency in 3D occupancy prediction, which has several implications.

The enhanced geometric and semantic understanding provided by RoboOcc is relevant for a range of downstream robotic tasks. More precise geometric modeling can enable robots to navigate complex indoor environments with fewer collisions, perform more accurate object manipulation, and engage in more informed decision-making. The reduction of semantic ambiguity can lead to better identification and classification of objects, which is crucial for human-robot interaction and tasks requiring fine-grained object understanding.

The robustness of RoboOcc to variations in Gaussian parameters, particularly in scenarios with increased Gaussian overlap, suggests its potential for more stable performance in diverse and dense indoor scenes. This could lead to more reliable real-world deployments of embodied AI systems. The demonstration of strong performance in both local and global occupancy prediction further indicates its applicability for both instantaneous environment awareness and long-term, explorative scene mapping by robots.

While the work shows advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, including challenges related to category imbalance in semantic learning (e.g., rare classes like windows and tvs) and the current scope being limited to 11 common semantic categories. These limitations suggest avenues for future research, such as developing more robust semantic learning techniques for imbalanced datasets and expanding the model's capacity to handle a wider diversity of real-world object categories. Overall, the work provides a foundation for developing more effective scene understanding methods in indoor occupancy prediction, potentially influencing the design of perception systems for future robotic platforms.
