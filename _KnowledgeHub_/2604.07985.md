# Predicting the Benefit of Retrieval Augmentation in Open-Domain Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper studies predicting, per question, whether retrieval augmentation will improve answer quality over closed-book generation. It benchmarks pre-retrieval, post-retrieval, and post-generation gain predictors, introduces Bert-Gen supervised cross-encoders and Internal Prober, a dual-LoRA probe of frozen-LLM hidden states needing no decoding. Predicted gain drives selective RAG that beats always-retrieving across three QA datasets, two LLMs, two retrievers.

## Problem

- Retrieval augmentation is not universally beneficial: distracting or irrelevant passages can mislead the LLM, yet systems still apply retrieval uniformly to every question.
- Prior adaptive-RAG work learns retrieval policies from pre-retrieval question complexity, so it ignores actual retrieval quality and the model's own parametric knowledge.
- Existing gain predictors are either weak score-distribution heuristics or post-generation methods that require decoding full answers, which is too costly for latency-sensitive pipelines.

## Method

- Formalizes RAG gain as a log-ratio of shallow semantic answer-quality metrics (E5 cosine, cross-encoder, NLI entailment) with and without retrieved context, predicted without reference answers.
- Bert-Ret and Bert-Gen supervised regressors fine-tune ModernBERT-large end-to-end with MSE over question, top-5 passages, and one or both generated answers.
- Internal Prober attaches two LoRA adapters to one frozen LLM, probing final hidden states of question-only and question-plus-passages streams, with regression heads jointly predicting three gains.

## Results

- Pre-retrieval predictors correlate near zero (0-0.1) with true gain; unsupervised post-retrieval ones reach only 0.1-0.2, while supervised Bert-Ret reaches 0.45-0.49 on NQ-Open.
- Bert-Gen-2A is best overall, peaking at Pearson 0.87-0.89 on TriviaQA; Internal Prober trails it but beats Bert-Ret and uncertainty, cutting generated-token compute 82-95%.
- Selective RAG beats always-RAG: Bert-Gen-2A recovers up to 84% of the always-RAG-to-oracle gap, and Internal Prober wins under an unseen GPT-4o judge metric.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Classical query-performance-prediction signals transfer poorly to generation: retrieval-list score statistics say little about whether context actually helps the model answer.
> - A model's pre-generation hidden states already encode whether external knowledge is needed, so probing can substitute for most of the cost of speculative decoding.
> - Training a predictor jointly on several quality metrics improves robustness under evaluation metrics it never saw, though the probe needs white-box model access.
