# FlexiTac: A Low-Cost, Open-Source, Scalable Tactile Sensing Solution for Robotic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> FlexiTac introduces a low-cost, open-source, and scalable piezoresistive tactile sensing solution for robotic end-effectors, integrating flexible sensor pads with compact readout electronics for real-time, synchronized data. This system costs approximately $30 per unit and simplifies manufacturing, enabling improved repeatability and integration into various robot learning pipelines, including robust real-to-sim-to-real training.

## Problem

- Robotic end-effectors exhibit limited dexterity compared to human capabilities, largely due to a lack of dense tactile feedback.
- Existing high-resolution tactile sensors are often expensive, hindering widespread adoption and scalability across diverse robotic platforms.
- Complex manufacturing processes and integration challenges limit the ease of deployment and robustness of current tactile sensing solutions.

## Method

- **FlexiTac** is an open-source, modular ==plug-in tactile sensing platform== comprising thin flexible ==piezoresistive sensor pads== and a compact multi-channel readout board.
- Utilizes a ==Flexible Printed Circuit (FPC)-based design==, directly integrating electrodes to improve manufacturing throughput, repeatability, and customization.
- Employs a compact readout board with ==Arduino Nano== and multiplexers, streaming synchronized tactile measurements at **100 Hz**.

## Results

- Complete **FlexiTac** unit costs approximately **$30** per unit, with further reductions at higher production volumes.
- Integrates onto diverse end-effectors (rigid and soft grippers); supports configurations including **8x16, 16x16, 32x12, 32x32**.
- Supports visuo-tactile fusion, cross-embodiment skill transfer, and improved real-to-sim-to-real for tasks like nut-and-bolt assembly.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The FPC-based sensor design dramatically enhances manufacturing efficiency and repeatability, reducing fabrication time per pad to approximately five minutes.
> - Incorporating narrow slots between FPC electrodes improves mechanical compliance and localizes deformation, thereby increasing sensor sensitivity.
> - Consistent calibration and normalization between real and simulated tactile signals, using a Kelvin-Voigt model, enable stable reinforcement learning fine-tuning and effective sim-to-real transfer.
