# Reduced-Order Model Guided Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control for Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Caltech developed a hierarchical control framework for humanoid locomotion, combining a Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulum (HLIP) model with Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC). This approach generated natural gaits, emergent arm swinging, and achieved superior velocity tracking, disturbance rejection (62% success rate vs. 30% for HLIP-only), and robustness over rough terrain in simulations on a 24-DoF humanoid.

## Problem

- Humanoid locomotion control is challenged by high-dimensional, nonlinear, and hybrid dynamics, making robust and expressive whole-body control difficult.
- Reduced-order models offer computational efficiency and stability for basic gaits but lack whole-body expressiveness and capabilities for complex, contact-rich interactions.
- Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC) directly applied to bipedal locomotion often struggles with local minima, requires extensive parameter tuning, and can produce unnatural gaits like foot dragging.

## Method

- A hierarchical framework integrates a Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulum (HLIP) model to generate nominal, stable walking gaits and foot placement strategies.
- The HLIP-generated reference is blended with a stable standing reference and then fed into a Contact-Implicit ==Model Predictive Control== (CI-MPC) component, which performs full-body optimization.
- CI-MPC, using an Inverse Dynamics Trajectory Optimization (IDTO) approach, implicitly solves for optimal contact forces, schedules, and whole-body motions over a receding horizon for a 24-DoF humanoid model.

## Results

- The framework produced natural heel-to-toe and toe-to-heel walking gaits, along with emergent arm swinging, which were not reliably achieved by CI-MPC alone.
- It demonstrated improved velocity tracking with reduced oscillation and smooth, complete stops, outperforming independent HLIP or CI-MPC approaches in simulations.
- Enhanced robustness was observed against external disturbances (**62%** success rate vs. **30%** for HLIP-only) and rough terrain with state/model uncertainty (e.g., **70%** success rate under specific noise vs. **0%** for HLIP-only), while operating in real-time at 50 Hz.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Guiding a complex, non-convex whole-body optimizer (CI-MPC) with a simpler, dynamically stable reduced-order model (HLIP) significantly improves the robustness and naturalness of generated gaits.
> - The hierarchical integration enables the emergence of un-programmed, expressive whole-body behaviors like natural arm swinging and adaptive contact modulation, enhancing overall stability and versatility.
> - Blending locomotion references with a standing posture reference ensures smooth transitions between standing and walking, allowing for robust starting and stopping behaviors in humanoids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robot locomotion control presents challenges due to the high-dimensional, nonlinear, and hybrid dynamics inherent in these systems. Current approaches can be broadly categorized into two main streams: reduced-order models and whole-body control methods.

Reduced-order models, such as the Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP), Spring-Loaded Inverted Pendulum (SLIP), and their hybrid variants like the Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulum (HLIP), simplify the robot's dynamics to achieve computational efficiency and robust gait generation. These models are effective for basic locomotion and fast real-time control, often providing stable foot placement strategies. However, their abstraction of the full dynamics results in a loss of whole-body expressiveness, limiting complex behaviors involving arm movements or nuanced contact interactions.

In contrast, whole-body control methods aim to manage the full dynamics of the robot. Direct trajectory optimization for multi-domain, multi-contact scenarios is computationally intensive, often requiring offline computation or simplifying assumptions. Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers a solution by optimizing over a receding horizon, but conventional MPC often requires a pre-determined contact sequence. Recent advancements in Contact-Implicit MPC (CI-MPC) have addressed this by optimizing over both the contact sequence and whole-body dynamics simultaneously. CI-MPC enables robots to plan through multiple hybrid contact modes and interact dynamically with the environment. Despite these capabilities, CI-MPC methods are susceptible to local minima in their non-convex optimization problems and typically necessitate extensive parameter tuning, frequently leading to less natural gaits or issues like foot dragging in bipedal locomotion.

This research aims to integrate the strengths of both paradigms. It proposes a framework that leverages the stability and computational efficiency of a reduced-order model (HLIP) to guide the expressive, contact-aware capabilities of a whole-body controller (CI-MPC). By combining HLIP's ability to generate nominal gaits and robust foot placement with CI-MPC's capacity for full-body coordination and adaptive contact sequencing, the work seeks to develop a more robust, versatile, and computationally feasible control strategy for humanoids operating in complex, real-world environments. This approach addresses the limitations of each method when applied in isolation, attempting to achieve dynamic stability, expressive motion, and real-time performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a humanoid locomotion control framework that combines the computational efficiency and stability of reduced-order models with the whole-body expressiveness and contact-rich interaction capabilities of advanced model predictive control. Specifically, the authors aim to address the following challenges:

- **Overcoming limitations of reduced-order models:** While models like HLIP are effective for generating stable bipedal gaits, they abstract away the robot's full kinematics and dynamics. This simplification results in a loss of whole-body coordination, preventing the robot from naturally incorporating arm movements for balance, bracing against environmental objects, or executing complex maneuvers beyond basic walking. The objective is to reintroduce this whole-body expressiveness.
- **Mitigating difficulties in Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC):** CI-MPC has shown promise for generating complex, contact-rich motions. However, its direct application to bipedal locomotion often suffers from practical issues. The underlying non-convex optimization problem is prone to local minima, leading to sub-optimal or unnatural gaits (e.g., foot dragging) without extensive and often challenging parameter tuning. The motivation is to provide CI-MPC with a robust and dynamically feasible initial guess or reference trajectory to guide its optimization, thereby improving its reliability and the quality of generated motions.
- **Enabling robust and versatile locomotion in unstructured environments:** Humanoid robots need to operate in environments built for humans, which often involve rough terrain, obstacles, and unexpected disturbances. Existing controllers frequently struggle with these conditions. The objective is to create a control framework capable of achieving robust walking over unmodeled terrain, recovering from external disturbances, and actively interacting with the environment (e.g., bracing with arms) while maintaining real-time performance.
- **Achieving real-time, online replanning:** For practical deployment, humanoid controllers must be able to replan trajectories online at a sufficiently high frequency. Direct solutions to whole-body optimal control problems are often too slow for real-time applications, especially when considering multiple contact modes. The motivation is to achieve real-time (e.g., 50 Hz) control, allowing for continuous adaptation to changing environmental conditions and robot states.

By integrating HLIP for nominal gait generation and CI-MPC for whole-body dynamic optimization, the authors hypothesize that they can leverage the strengths of both methodologies. HLIP provides a stable and computationally cheap template for bipedal motion and a reasonable contact sequence, which can guide CI-MPC away from local minima and reduce tuning effort. CI-MPC then refines this guidance by incorporating full-body dynamics, optimizing contact forces, and adapting the contact schedule to achieve nuanced, expressive, and robust behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed control framework employs a hierarchical architecture that integrates a Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulum (HLIP) model with a Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC) approach. The overall architecture is depicted in Figure 2 of the paper.

**A. System Dynamics**
The robot's full 3D dynamics are modeled using Euler-Lagrange equations, representing a system with $n$ generalized coordinates $q$ and velocities $v$. The state is $x = [q^\top, v^\top]^\top$. Contact forces, $\lambda(q)$, are modeled as a differentiable function of the robot's state, enabling implicit handling of contact events within the optimization framework. The dynamics equation is:
$D(q) \dot{v} + H(q, v) = Bu + J_c(q)^\top \lambda(q)$, where $D(q)$ is the mass-inertia matrix, $H(q, v)$ contains Coriolis, centrifugal, and gravitational terms, $B$ is the input selection matrix for joint torques $u$, and $J_c(q)$ is the contact Jacobian mapping contact forces $\lambda$ to the whole-body dynamics.

**B. Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulum (HLIP) Model**
The HLIP model provides a reduced-order representation for generating nominal walking gaits. It considers a planar linear inverted pendulum with a fixed center of mass height. The model tracks the horizontal position and velocity of a point mass relative to the stance foot. It treats double support phases as instantaneous and stabilizes the step-to-step dynamics using a foot placement controller. For 3D locomotion, two orthogonal planar HLIPs are composed. The HLIP generates a hybrid solution $\chi(x_{H_0}) = (\Lambda, I, C)$, which comprises an indexing set for single support phases, time intervals, and continuous solutions.

**C. Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CI-MPC)**
The CI-MPC component is responsible for full-body coordination and contact management. It solves a trajectory optimization problem in a receding horizon fashion to minimize a cost function involving state tracking and control effort. The objective is to find optimal control inputs $u(t)$ over a horizon $T$:
$\min_{u(t)} \int_0^T l(x(t), u(t), t)dt + l_f(x(T))$
subject to the full-body dynamics (1) and initial state constraints. The running cost $l$ and terminal cost $l_f$ are quadratic, penalizing deviations from a reference trajectory and control effort.

The authors adopt the Inverse Dynamics Trajectory Optimization (IDTO) approach for CI-MPC. This method discretizes the trajectory into $N$ steps, using generalized positions $q$ as the sole decision variables. Velocities and accelerations are computed via finite differences. Contact forces $\lambda$ are modeled as a smooth, compliant function of $q$. This formulation allows the inverse dynamics to be written as a function of $q$, resulting in a compact nonlinear least squares problem. Critically, contact schedules and reaction forces are solved implicitly, enabling automatic consideration of various contact modes. The CI-MPC implementation utilizes a simplified differentiable contact model for optimization, while the simulation environment uses a physics-based rigid contact model.

**D. Control Approach**
The proposed control approach is hierarchical:
1. **User Commands:** High-level commands are provided, including desired Cartesian velocities (forward $v_{x,d}$, lateral $v_{y,d}$) in the robot's body frame, desired angular velocity around the z-axis ($\omega_{z,d}$), and desired center of mass height ($z_{0,d}$) in the world frame. These are combined into a command vector $c_d \in \mathbb{R}^4$.
2. **Reference Trajectory Synthesis (HLIP-based):** The HLIP model generates reference trajectories. Given the robot's current center of mass (COM) state, the HLIP is flowed forward to produce a stable COM trajectory. This trajectory is then embedded onto the full 3D robot kinematics. An error tracking problem is formulated for outputs like COM height, torso orientation, and swing foot position/orientation relative to the stance foot. These desired outputs are used in an inverse kinematics (IK) problem, solved analytically for efficiency, to obtain reference joint positions for the legs ($q^\ell_k$). Leg velocities are derived via finite differences. The torso maintains an upright configuration tracking user commands, and arm references are fixed. This produces an HLIP-based full-system state reference $\{x_H\}_k$.
3. **Trajectory Blending:** To ensure smooth transitions between standing and walking, a blending mechanism is introduced. The final reference trajectory for CI-MPC, $\{x_{ref}\}_k$, is a weighted combination of the HLIP-based reference $\{x_H\}_k$ and a stable standing reference $\{x_S\}_k$:
 $\{x_{ref}\}_k = \alpha\{x_H\}_k + (1 - \alpha)\{x_S\}_k$.
 The blending scalar $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ is a hyperbolic tangent function of a weighted norm $\phi$, which depends on the commanded velocities and the current base velocity. This ensures that the standing reference dominates at low velocities, allowing for smooth stops, while the HLIP reference takes over as velocities increase.
4. **Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control:** The blended reference $\{x_{ref}\}_k$, which primarily captures leg motion, is then fed into the CI-MPC solver. CI-MPC tracks this reference while simultaneously generating optimal arm motions and adjusting the contact schedule as needed, ensuring dynamic feasibility and full-body coordination. The optimization considers 24 degrees of freedom for the Achilles humanoid, including heel, toe, and hand contact points, resulting in 64 possible hybrid domains.
5. **Low-level Control:** The CI-MPC outputs an optimal sequence of configurations $\{q^*\}$. Optimal velocities $\{v^*\}$ are derived via backward differences, and optimal torques $\{u^*\}$ via inverse dynamics. These are then interpolated at a higher frequency. The total low-level control input sent to the robot is $u_{tot} = u^* + K_p(q_j - q^*_j) + K_d(v_j - v^*_j)$, which includes a feed-forward torque term and PD gains for tracking the interpolated joint positions and velocities.

The framework operates online at 50 Hz, with the CI-MPC solver typically requiring 3 iterations per step.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed HLIP-guided CI-MPC framework was validated through extensive simulation experiments on a novel 24-degree-of-freedom (DoF) humanoid robot named Achilles, which features underactuated roll actuation at the feet. The simulations were conducted in a Drake environment, contrasting CI-MPC's simplified compliant contact model with Drake's rigid contact model.

**A. Walking Characteristics and Velocity Tracking:**
- **Gait Generation:** With HLIP guidance, the robot autonomously developed natural walking gaits. During forward walking, a heel-to-toe contact sequence emerged, while backward walking produced a toe-to-heel sequence. In contrast, CI-MPC alone often resulted in irregular gaits with foot dragging.
- **Arm Swinging:** At higher velocities and lower walking heights, the robot exhibited emergent arm swinging behavior, demonstrating whole-body coordination without explicit arm trajectory planning.
- **Velocity Tracking Performance:** Comparative analysis (Figure 5) showed that the combined HLIP-guided CI-MPC approach provided tighter velocity tracking with reduced oscillation compared to using HLIP or CI-MPC independently. The reference blending mechanism enabled the robot to achieve smooth and complete stops, unlike HLIP-only control which often continued to move slightly.

**B. Disturbance Rejection:**
- The framework's robustness to external disturbances was evaluated by applying randomly generated forces to the robot's base. The proposed approach achieved a 62% success rate in remaining standing for 4 seconds after a disturbance, significantly outperforming HLIP-only control, which had a 30% success rate.
- The system demonstrated the ability to modulate its nominal contact sequence in response to disturbances (Figure 7), allowing the robot to deviate from its planned path to recover stability and then return to the nominal orbit.

**C. Robustness under Model and State Uncertainty on Rough Terrain:**
- Preliminary tests explored the framework's robustness under various forms of uncertainty (Table IV), including Gaussian noise in state estimates ($\sigma_x$), random alterations to link masses ($\sigma_m$), and unmodeled rough terrain (randomly generated spheres).
- Under no uncertainty (ideal conditions), HLIP-only succeeded in 1 out of 10 trials on rough terrain, while the proposed method succeeded in 7 out of 10.
- When faced with state estimation noise (e.g., $\sigma_x = 0.05$), HLIP-only failed in all 10 trials, whereas the proposed method achieved an 80% success rate without mass error or rough terrain.
- Combining state estimation noise ($\sigma_x = 0.05$), link mass error ($\sigma_m = 0.2$), and rough terrain, the proposed method still achieved a 10% success rate, while HLIP-only failed in all trials.
- Under specific conditions ($\sigma_x = 0.01, \sigma_m = 0.1$, rough terrain), the proposed method achieved a 70% success rate, compared to 0% for HLIP-only.
These results suggest a measurable level of robustness to common sources of real-world inaccuracies, distinguishing it from the HLIP-only controller.

**D. Real-time Performance:**
- The framework operates online in real-time at 50 Hz, which is crucial for dynamic and interactive humanoid control. The CI-MPC solver typically completed within 3 iterations per control step.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed HLIP-guided CI-MPC framework represents a contribution to the field of humanoid locomotion control by addressing the inherent trade-offs between computational efficiency, dynamic stability, and whole-body expressiveness.

**A. Addressing Key Challenges:**
The research effectively mitigates several challenges associated with high-dimensional, nonlinear, and hybrid humanoid dynamics.
- By using HLIP to generate nominal gaits, the framework provides CI-MPC with a dynamically stable and computationally efficient reference, thereby guiding the complex optimization process away from local minima and reducing the need for extensive parameter tuning that often plagues CI-MPC implementations. This is a significant step towards making CI-MPC more tractable for bipedal locomotion.
- The integration allows for the emergence of complex, whole-body behaviors, such as natural arm swinging during walking and bracing against environmental objects, which are not explicitly programmed but arise from CI-MPC's full-body optimization. This addresses the limitation of reduced-order models that sacrifice whole-body expressiveness for simplicity.
- The demonstrated robustness to external disturbances, model uncertainty, and state estimation noise in simulation suggests improved reliability in more realistic and unstructured environments. This is critical for transitioning humanoids from controlled laboratory settings to real-world applications.

**B. Enabling Versatile and Dynamic Interaction:**
The framework's ability to automatically discover heel-to-toe and toe-to-heel gaits, coupled with its capacity to adjust contact schedules on the fly, allows the robot to adapt to varying conditions. The successful demonstration of walking over rough terrain, bracing against walls, and recovering from pushes highlights its potential for versatile multi-contact stabilization and interaction with unmodeled environmental features. This enhances the robot's autonomy and ability to leverage its environment for stability.

**C. Real-time Applicability:**
Operating online at 50 Hz, the control framework meets the computational demands for real-time applications. This speed is crucial for reactive behaviors and continuous adaptation, paving the way for more dynamic and responsive humanoid robots.

**D. Future Impact:**
The research establishes a foundation for future work, particularly hardware validation, which is identified as a direct next step. Successfully translating these simulation results to physical robots could significantly advance the capabilities of humanoids in fields requiring navigation in human-centric spaces, such as disaster response, logistics, and assistance. The insights gained regarding parameter tuning trade-offs and the limitations related to simplified collision geometries and self-collisions also provide clear directions for further methodological refinements, such as incorporating richer reduced-order models or advanced collision avoidance techniques. The work suggests a viable path towards developing humanoids that are not only robust and stable but also capable of complex, expressive, and adaptable behaviors in unpredictable environments.
