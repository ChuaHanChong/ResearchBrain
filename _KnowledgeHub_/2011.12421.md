# Bi-directional Domain Adaptation for Sim2Real Transfer of Embodied Navigation Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Bi-directional Domain Adaptation (BDA) spends scarce real data on adapting the simulator rather than the policy: a CycleGAN real2sim observation adaptor maps deployment images back to the simulation distribution, and a sim2real MLP dynamics adaptor predicts residual state-transition error to reset the simulator toward real outcomes. On PointGoal Navigation, BDA with 5k real transitions matches a policy fine-tuned on 585k, a 117x data reduction.

## Problem

- Domain randomization needs expert-chosen parameter distributions: too wide makes learning hard, too narrow leaves the reality gap open and policies fail to generalize.
- Prior adaptation work closes either the visual gap or the dynamics gap alone, so residual noise in the untreated channel still collapses transfer performance.
- The status quo spends scarce real-world samples fine-tuning the policy directly, which is sample-inefficient because real rollouts are slow, costly, and unresettable.

## Method

- Observation adaptation: an unpaired ==CycleGAN== ==real2sim observation adaptor== maps real RGB-D images to simulation appearance at test time, decoupling sensing from acting so sensor changes need no policy retraining.
- Dynamics adaptation: a 3-layer ==sim2real MLP dynamics adaptor== regresses the residual position and orientation change between sim and real transitions under a weighted MSE that doubles position weight over heading.
- Pipeline: one behavior policy collects paired sim/real rollouts; the simulator is augmented by resetting states to dynamics-adaptor predictions, then the policy is fine-tuned inside that augmented simulator via **Bi-directional Domain Adaptation (BDA)**.

## Results

- Noise collapses zero-shot transfer: a noise-free-trained policy drops from **0.84** SPL to **0.04** SPL under sensor noise and **0.56** SPL under dynamics noise in the target lab environment.
- Full BDA with **5k** target samples reaches within **5%** SPL of the 1M-step direct fine-tuning oracle, while single-direction ablations lag it by **25%** (observation-only) and **62%** (dynamics-only).
- Sample efficiency across three noise settings gives **36x**, **117x**, and **32x** data reduction (**61x** average); the observation adaptor alone raises SPL by **65%** and cuts real-to-sim FID from **100.74** to **83.05**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scarce high-fidelity data buys more when spent correcting the cheap data generator than when spent fine-tuning the policy directly on it.
> - Adaptation direction should follow domain asymmetry: adapt observations toward simulation to keep the policy frozen, adapt dynamics toward reality because reality cannot be reset.
> - Navigation policies are far more brittle to minor sensor noise than to actuation noise, and mild actuation noise can even help agents escape obstacle deadlocks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) models have demonstrated capabilities in various high-dimensional tasks, including navigation, manipulation, and locomotion. However, these models typically require extensive amounts of training data. Obtaining this data in real-world environments is often resource-intensive, time-consuming, and difficult to reproduce. Consequently, a common practice involves training RL agents in simulation environments, where data collection is scalable, safe, and cost-effective.

This approach introduces the "sim-vs-real gap," a challenge where policies trained solely in simulation often fail to generalize effectively when deployed in real-world settings. This gap arises because simulators, despite their advancements, do not perfectly replicate the complexities of reality, including noisy dynamics, sensor inaccuracies, and environmental variations. Bridging this gap is a central problem in robotics and reinforcement learning, often referred to as 'sim2real transfer.'

Existing approaches to sim2real transfer include:
- **Domain Randomization:** Techniques that train robust policies by exposing agents to a diverse range of simulation environments with randomized visual or physical properties. While effective, determining the optimal distribution of randomization parameters can be challenging.
- **Domain Adaptation:** Methods that adapt data from a source domain (simulation) to resemble a target domain (reality), or vice versa. This can involve visual adaptation for sensor data or policy adaptation for dynamic changes.
- **Residual Policy Learning:** Approaches where a pre-trained sub-optimal policy is augmented with a learned residual policy to correct for environmental changes or improve performance, thereby speeding up adaptation.

This work contributes to the domain adaptation literature by proposing a novel bi-directional approach that concurrently addresses both visual and dynamics discrepancies between simulation and reality. Unlike methods that focus solely on one aspect (e.g., visual transfer or dynamics transfer) or rely on direct policy fine-tuning, this paper posits that adapting the simulator itself using limited real-world data is a more sample-efficient strategy for maximizing agent performance in reality. The authors reframe the sim2real problem as optimally leveraging abundant low-fidelity simulation data and scarce high-fidelity real-world data.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a sample-efficient method for transferring deep reinforcement learning policies from simulation to reality for embodied navigation agents, specifically addressing the "sim-vs-real gap."

The motivation stems from several factors:
- **Data Scarcity in Reality:** Training deep RL models requires vast amounts of data. While simulators offer an abundance of data, real-world data collection is expensive, slow, and prone to reproducibility issues. This creates a fundamental trade-off between data availability and fidelity.
- **Generalization Failure:** Policies trained exclusively in simulation often exhibit poor generalization to novel real-world environments due to discrepancies in visual observations (e.g., lighting, textures, sensor noise) and physical dynamics (e.g., actuation noise, friction, environmental interactions).
- **Inefficiency of Direct Fine-tuning:** The status quo in machine learning often involves pre-training a policy in simulation and then fine-tuning it directly on the real robot using limited real-world data. The authors contend that this approach may not be the most effective use of valuable real-world data, as adapting the policy directly can still be sample-inefficient, especially when the initial sim-vs-real gap is substantial.
- **Need for Robustness:** Robots deployed in dynamic real-world environments require policies that can quickly adapt to minor changes or noise without extensive retraining. Current RL algorithms are susceptible to overfitting, learning behaviors specific to their training environments.

To address these motivations, the authors propose Bi-directional Domain Adaptation (BDA), based on the hypothesis that real-world data is better utilized to adapt the simulator (reducing the sim-vs-real gap) rather than directly adapting the policy. BDA aims to bridge the gap in two distinct directions:
1. **Real2Sim Observation Adaptation (OA):** To bridge the visual domain gap. This approach involves training a module to make real-world observations "look" like simulated ones. The motivation for real2sim (rather than sim2real) is to decouple sensing and acting. If sensor characteristics change in reality, only the observation adaptor needs retraining, while the policy can remain fixed, thus avoiding expensive re-training of the policy.
2. **Sim2Real Dynamics Adaptation (DA):** To bridge the dynamics domain gap. This involves training a module to predict residual errors between state transitions in simulation and reality. The motivation for sim2real dynamics adaptation is to leverage the fundamental asymmetry between domains: simulators can be reset to arbitrary states, allowing for the augmentation of simulated trajectories to reflect real-world dynamics.

By addressing both visual and dynamics discrepancies in a targeted manner, BDA aims to create a more realistic simulated environment for policy training, leading to more robust and sample-efficient transfer to the real world.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed method, Bi-directional Domain Adaptation (BDA), integrates two distinct adaptation modules to address the visual and dynamics discrepancies between simulation and reality. The overall process is summarized by Algorithm 1.

**A. System Architecture (Algorithm 1 Overview)**
1. **Initial Policy Training:** A behavior policy, πsim, is first trained in a standard simulation environment.
2. **Data Collection:** The pre-trained πsim is used to collect rollouts in both the source simulation and the target (real) environment. This yields unpaired RGB-D images (Isim, Ireal) for observation adaptation and state-action pairs (sreal, areal) for dynamics adaptation.
3. **Observation Adaptation (OA) Training:** The collected unpaired images are used to train the real2sim observation adaptation module.
4. **Dynamics Adaptation (DA) Training:** The collected real-world state-action pairs are used to train the sim2real dynamics adaptation module.
5. **Augmented Simulator Finetuning:** The source simulator is augmented with the trained DA module (SimDA). The initial policy πsim is then fine-tuned within this augmented simulator.
6. **Deployment:** The fine-tuned policy is deployed to the real world, augmented with the OA module applied to its sensory inputs (πSim OA+DA).

**B. Observation Adaptation (OA)**
- **Purpose:** To close the visual domain gap by translating real-world observations to resemble simulated observations.
- **Approach:** Leverages CycleGAN, a pixel-level image-to-image translation technique. CycleGAN does not require paired images, which simplifies data collection between simulation and reality.
- **Mechanism:** OA learns two mappings: Gsim: Ireal → Isim (real-to-sim) and Greal: Isim → Ireal (sim-to-real), along with adversarial discriminators Dreal and Dsim. The cycle-consistency loss ensures that Greal (Gsim (Ireal)) ≈ Ireal and Gsim (Greal (Isim)) ≈ Isim.
- **Rationale for Real2Sim:** Decouples sensor adaptation from policy training. If real-world sensor noise changes, only OA needs retraining, not the entire policy. This allows parallel training of OA and the RL policy, reducing overall training time.

**C. Dynamics Adaptation (DA)**
- **Purpose:** To close the dynamics domain gap by learning the discrepancies in state transitions between simulation and reality.
- **Approach:** A 3-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) regression network is trained to estimate the residual error between simulated and real-world state transitions (Tsim → Treal).
- **Mechanism:** DA learns to predict the change in position and orientation (∆sreal) given a real-world state (srealt) and action (arealt). It uses a weighted Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function, placing higher weight on position prediction. The state is represented by (x, y, θ).
- **Simulator Augmentation:** Once trained, DA is used to augment the source simulator (SimDA). During policy training in SimDA, after the simulator predicts ssim t+1, DA predicts the real-world state ˆsreal t+1, and the simulator's state is reset to ˆsreal t+1. This exposes the policy to trajectories closer to reality.
- **Rationale for Sim2Real:** Exploits the ability of simulators to be reset to arbitrary states, which is not feasible in reality.

**D. Experimental Setup: Sim2Sim Transfer for PointGoal Navigation**
- **Task:** PointGoal Navigation (PointNav), where an agent navigates to a specified relative goal from egocentric RGB-D observations in an unseen environment within a time limit. Success is defined as issuing a STOP command within 0.2m of the goal.
- **Sim2Sim as Stand-in:** Due to practical constraints, Sim2Sim transfer is used as a stand-in for Sim2Real. Policies are trained in a "source" simulator and transferred to a "target" simulator that has added observation and dynamics noise to mimic reality. This allows for controlled experimentation across different noise models.
- **Simulation Environment:** Habitat-Sim, which has demonstrated high sim2real predictivity.
- **Robot Model:** Circular base (0.175m radius, 0.61m height), matching LoCoBot dimensions.
- **Sensors:** Egocentric RGB and Depth sensors (Intel D435 camera specs, 70-degree FOV, 10m depth clipping), GPS+Compass (providing accurate localization and heading).
- **Sensor Noise:** Gaussian, Speckle, and Poisson noise for RGB; Redwood noise for Depth. Various multipliers are used to create different noise levels.
- **Actions:** Discrete action space: turn-left 30°, turn-right 30°, forward 0.25m, and STOP.
- **Actuation Noise:** To simulate real-world non-deterministic actions, translational and rotational actuation noise models (characterized by PyRobot from real LoCoBot data) are applied to the target simulator. These include Proportional, Dynamic Window Approach, and Linear Quadratic Regulator controllers.
- **Testing Environment:** A virtualized 6.5m x 10m real lab environment (LAB), modeled using Matterport Pro2 3D camera scans, with varying obstacle configurations. Models were trained on the Gibson dataset (72 high-quality indoor environments) and never exposed to LAB during training.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SUCC) and Success weighted by (normalized inverse) Path Length (SPL).
- **Experimental Protocol:**
 - **Source Environment:** Gibson dataset without any sensor or actuation noise (Gibson no noise).
 - **Target Environments:** 10 configurations of LAB with varying combinations of RGBD observation noise (O), dynamics noise (D), or both (O+D) (e.g., LABO, LABD, LAB O+D).
 - **RL Models:** DD-PPO algorithm with ResNet50 visual encoder and a 2-layer LSTM, trained for 100 million steps.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the sim2real gap, the effectiveness of OA and DA individually, and the sample efficiency of the full BDA pipeline compared to direct fine-tuning.

**A. Sim2Real Gap Magnitude**
- A policy trained without noise (π100MGibson no noise) showed good transfer to a noise-free LAB environment (0.84 SPL).
- However, when dynamics noise was introduced (LABD), SPL dropped to 0.56 (a 28% relative drop).
- Sensor noise had a more significant impact: in LABO, SPL dropped to 0.04 (an 81% relative drop). When both sensor and dynamics noise were present (LAB O+D), SPL was 0.06 (a 78% relative drop).
- Conversely, policies trained with noise (π100MGibson O+D) performed poorly in noise-free environments.
- **Finding:** RL agents exhibit high sensitivity to perceptual and dynamic noise, indicating a substantial sim2real gap that primarily manifests through sensor noise.

**B. Observation Adaptation (OA) Performance**
- OA, trained for 200 epochs using 5,000 unpaired images, effectively translated noisy RGB-D images to resemble noise-free simulated images, demonstrating its ability to remove Gaussian noise and smooth textures.
- Equipping the base policy (π100MGibson no noise) with OA (π BDA−5kGibsonOA) drastically improved SPL in environments with observation noise (LABO), leading to an average 65% increase in SPL compared to the unadapted policy.
- **Quantitative Metrics (FID):** FID scores between real and simulated images were reduced from 100.74 to 83.05 after OA, and from 98.73 (Gaussian noise) to 88.44. This indicates that adapted images perceptually align more closely with target simulation images.
- **Finding:** The real2sim OA module significantly bridges the visual domain gap, enabling policies to generalize much better to environments with sensor noise.

**C. Dynamics Adaptation (DA) Performance**
- DA, trained with 5,000 real-world state-action samples, augmented the source simulator. Fine-tuning the base policy with DA (π BDA−5kGibsonDA) resulted in an average 15% improvement in success and 11% improvement in SPL in environments with dynamics noise (LABD) compared to the unadapted policy.
- Comparison with real-world actuation noise models (PyRobot benchmarks) showed that the DA-learned noise model from LAB was of similar magnitude, suggesting it provided a reasonable approximation of real-world dynamics.
- **Finding:** The sim2real DA module effectively captures and mitigates the effects of dynamics noise, leading to improved policy performance in environments with actuation variability.

**D. BDA vs. Fine-tuning and Sample Efficiency**
- **Individual Module Limitations:** While OA and DA improved performance individually, they failed to generalize robustly when both types of noise were present or when the other type of noise was introduced. For example, π BDA−5kGibsonOA struggled in LABD and LAB O+D, and π BDA−5kGibsonDA struggled in LABO and LAB O+D. This highlights the necessity of addressing both gaps.
- **Full BDA Performance:** The full BDA pipeline (π BDA−5kGibson OA+DA), utilizing both OA and DA and trained with 5,000 target environment samples, demonstrated robustness across all combinations of sensor and actuation noise. It nearly matched the performance of an oracle baseline (π1MGibson O+D), which was directly fine-tuned in the target environment for 1,000,000 steps (a scenario not feasible in reality). On average, there was only a 5% difference in performance between π BDA−5kGibson OA+DA and π1MGibson O+D in environments with combined noise.
- **Sample Efficiency:** This is a key finding. BDA required only 5,000 samples from the target environment (estimated 7 hours of data collection). In contrast, the oracle baseline required approximately 585,000 samples (estimated 836 hours or 3.5 months of data collection) to achieve comparable SPL.
- **Speed-up:** BDA reduced the required target environment data by 36x, 117x, and 32x in different noise settings, achieving an average speed-up of 61x.
- **Observation on "Sliding":** The authors noted that in environments without dynamics noise and with "sliding" (a physics simulator behavior) disabled, agents tended to get stuck. The slight actuation noise introduced by DA allowed agents to free themselves, resembling real-world interactions. This suggests that dynamics noise can sometimes inadvertently aid navigation in certain simulated conditions by preventing agents from being perfectly stuck.
- **Finding:** BDA effectively bridges both visual and dynamics gaps, achieving robust performance comparable to extensively fine-tuned policies with significantly fewer real-world samples, demonstrating substantial sample efficiency.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research introduces Bi-directional Domain Adaptation (BDA) as a method for robust and sample-efficient sim2real transfer for embodied navigation agents. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Addressing the Sim2Real Gap with High Sample Efficiency:** The most prominent contribution is the demonstration that BDA can achieve performance comparable to extensive direct fine-tuning using significantly less real-world data (a 61x average speed-up, and up to 117x in specific cases). This is critical for practical robotics applications, where real-world data collection is a major bottleneck.
- **Holistic Domain Adaptation:** By simultaneously addressing both visual and dynamics discrepancies, BDA provides a more comprehensive solution to the sim2real problem than methods that focus solely on one aspect. The findings indicate that both observation and dynamics adaptation are necessary for robust generalization across varied noisy environments.
- **Decoupled Adaptation Modules:** The design choice of real2sim observation adaptation allows for the decoupling of sensing and acting. This modularity means that changes in sensor characteristics in the real world require retraining only the observation adaptor, leaving the policy intact, which can save substantial computational resources and time.
- **Leveraging Simulator Asymmetry:** The sim2real dynamics adaptation capitalizes on the ability of simulators to be reset to arbitrary states. By dynamically modifying simulated trajectories to reflect real-world dynamics, the policy is exposed to more realistic scenarios during training, fostering greater robustness.
- **Reduced Reliance on Paired Data:** The use of CycleGAN for observation adaptation removes the need for accurately paired images between simulation and reality, simplifying data collection processes which are often difficult and costly.
- **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated on the PointGoal Navigation task and using sim2sim transfer as a proxy, the core principles of BDA—addressing visual and dynamics gaps separately and leveraging residual error learning for dynamics—are hypothesized to be broadly applicable to a range of other robotics and reinforcement learning problems involving sim2real transfer. This could extend to manipulation, locomotion, and other complex autonomous tasks.
- **Encouraging Future Research:** The paper highlights the significant sensitivity of RL agents to seemingly minor perceptual changes (noise) and the necessity of considering this in future research. The methodology provides a framework for others to build upon, particularly in exploring different observation and dynamics adaptation techniques within a bi-directional framework.

In conclusion, BDA offers a practical and powerful framework for bridging the sim2real gap, making reinforcement learning more viable for real-world robotic deployment by substantially reducing the need for expensive real-world training data.
