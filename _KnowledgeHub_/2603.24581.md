# Latent-WAM: Latent World Action Modeling for End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent-WAM, developed by researchers including those from the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, presents an end-to-end autonomous driving framework that generates spatially-aware and dynamics-informed latent world representations. This approach achieved an EPDMS of 89.3 on NAVSIM v2, outperforming prior perception-free methods by 3.2 EPDMS, and demonstrated strong generalization on HUGSIM with a 45.9 Road Completion score, all while using a compact 104M parameter model.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end world-model-based autonomous driving systems often suffer from inadequate representation compression, leading to computational inefficiencies.
- Many latent world models lack intrinsic 3D spatial understanding or rely on external depth estimation models during inference, introducing latency and complexity.
- Prior approaches frequently underutilize historical and dynamic information, often only predicting the immediate next latent state, limiting comprehensive temporal reasoning.

## Method

- A ==Spatial-Aware Compressive World Encoder (SCWE)== distills geometric knowledge from a foundation model into a vision backbone and compresses multi-view images into compact, spatially-aware scene tokens using learnable queries.
- A ==Dynamic Latent World Model (DLWM)== employs a causal Transformer architecture with ==3D-RoPE== to autoregressively predict future world status representations, conditioned on historical visual and ego motion information.
- A Trajectory Decoder generates multiple candidate planning trajectories based on the learned world status representations, with the entire system jointly optimized end-to-end for planning, world modeling, and geometric alignment.

## Results

- Achieved an EPDMS of **89.3** on the NAVSIM v2 benchmark, surpassing prior state-of-the-art perception-free methods by **3.2** EPDMS and exhibiting strong rule compliance metrics.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization on the HUGSIM benchmark, achieving a Road Completion (RC) score of **45.9** and matching the highest HUGSIM Driving Score (HD-Score) of **28.9** among baselines.
- Qualitative analysis revealed that models trained with geometric distillation produced more focused attention maps on task-relevant regions (e.g., lane markings) and generated trajectories that aligned more closely with human driving behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling geometric knowledge from a pre-trained foundation model into the vision backbone is more effective for imbuing spatial understanding than directly concatenating frozen geometric features.
> - Highly compressed scene representations (e.g., 16 learnable scene queries) can effectively retain planning-relevant information without significant performance degradation, improving computational efficiency.
> - Modeling temporal dynamics through autoregressive multi-step future world status prediction, complemented by ego status supervision, significantly enhances planning performance by leveraging richer historical context.
