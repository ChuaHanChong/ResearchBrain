# Latent-WAM: Latent World Action Modeling for End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent-WAM, developed by researchers including those from the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, presents an end-to-end autonomous driving framework that generates spatially-aware and dynamics-informed latent world representations. This approach achieved an EPDMS of 89.3 on NAVSIM v2, outperforming prior perception-free methods by 3.2 EPDMS, and demonstrated strong generalization on HUGSIM with a 45.9 Road Completion score, all while using a compact 104M parameter model.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end world-model-based autonomous driving systems often suffer from inadequate representation compression, leading to computational inefficiencies.
- Many latent world models lack intrinsic 3D spatial understanding or rely on external depth estimation models during inference, introducing latency and complexity.
- Prior approaches frequently underutilize historical and dynamic information, often only predicting the immediate next latent state, limiting comprehensive temporal reasoning.

## Method

- A ==Spatial-Aware Compressive World Encoder (SCWE)== distills geometric knowledge from a foundation model into a vision backbone and compresses multi-view images into compact, spatially-aware scene tokens using learnable queries.
- A ==Dynamic Latent World Model (DLWM)== employs a causal Transformer architecture with ==3D-RoPE== to autoregressively predict future world status representations, conditioned on historical visual and ego motion information.
- A Trajectory Decoder generates multiple candidate planning trajectories based on the learned world status representations, with the entire system jointly optimized end-to-end for planning, world modeling, and geometric alignment.

## Results

- Achieved an EPDMS of **89.3** on the NAVSIM v2 benchmark, surpassing prior state-of-the-art perception-free methods by **3.2** EPDMS and exhibiting strong rule compliance metrics.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization on the HUGSIM benchmark, achieving a Road Completion (RC) score of **45.9** and matching the highest HUGSIM Driving Score (HD-Score) of **28.9** among baselines.
- Qualitative analysis revealed that models trained with geometric distillation produced more focused attention maps on task-relevant regions (e.g., lane markings) and generated trajectories that aligned more closely with human driving behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling geometric knowledge from a pre-trained foundation model into the vision backbone is more effective for imbuing spatial understanding than directly concatenating frozen geometric features.
> - Highly compressed scene representations (e.g., 16 learnable scene queries) can effectively retain planning-relevant information without significant performance degradation, improving computational efficiency.
> - Modeling temporal dynamics through autoregressive multi-step future world status prediction, complemented by ego status supervision, significantly enhances planning performance by leveraging richer historical context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

End-to-end autonomous driving systems aim to directly map sensor inputs to driving actions or trajectories, often through a single differentiable network. This approach is characterized by its data-driven nature and scalability potential. Early methods in this domain typically relied on perception and prediction modules that often required extensive auxiliary task designs and perception annotations, which can limit their scalability and data efficiency.

Recently, world-model-based approaches have gained traction. These methods learn dense scene representations through temporal self-supervised learning, thereby reducing reliance on explicit perception labels. These world models can generally be categorized into two groups:
1. **Explicit Video Generation Models:** These approaches (e.g.,) learn driving planning by generating future video frames. While they provide dense supervision, they often incur substantial computational overhead, and the learned intermediate representations may focus on visual details not directly relevant to planning.
2. **Implicit Future Latent Prediction Models:** This group (e.g.,) learns planning-relevant representations by predicting future latent states. These latent representations are generally more lightweight and capture dynamic information via temporal self-supervision. However, existing latent world models have been noted to suffer from several limitations: inadequately compressed representations, insufficient spatial understanding (sometimes requiring external depth estimation models during inference), and underutilization of historical and dynamic information, often merely predicting the next frame's representation from the current one. These limitations have been observed to contribute to sub-optimal planning performance, particularly under constrained data and computational budgets.

Latent-WAM is positioned within the second category, specifically addressing the identified deficiencies of implicit future latent prediction models. It aims to improve representation quality by enhancing compression and spatial awareness, while simultaneously improving the utilization of temporal dynamics, all within an efficient, end-to-end framework that reduces dependence on external modules and extensive labeled data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient end-to-end autonomous driving framework, Latent-WAM, that facilitates strong trajectory planning by leveraging spatially-aware and dynamics-informed latent world representations. The motivations stem from observed limitations in existing world-model-based planners, particularly those relying on implicit future latent prediction.

Specifically, the authors identify three key areas for improvement in current world-model-based planners:
1. **Inadequate Representation Compression:** Existing methods often process or retain more visual information than necessary for planning, leading to computational inefficiencies and potentially distracting from planning-critical features. The objective is to achieve highly compressed scene representations that retain planning-relevant information.
2. **Limited Spatial Understanding:** Many latent world models either lack an intrinsic understanding of 3D spatial geometry or depend on external, perception-based models for depth estimation during inference, which can introduce latency and complexity. A core motivation is to instill robust spatial understanding directly into the learned representations without relying on auxiliary inference-time modules.
3. **Underutilized Temporal Dynamics:** Prior approaches frequently model temporal dynamics by predicting only the immediate next latent state, potentially overlooking richer historical context and longer-term dynamic relationships. The motivation is to develop a mechanism that effectively captures and utilizes historical visual and motion representations to predict future world states in a more comprehensive, causal manner.

To address these motivations, Latent-WAM is designed with two core modules:
- **Spatial-Aware Compressive World Encoder (SCWE):** This module is motivated by the need for compressed, spatially-aware representations. Its objective is to distill geometric knowledge from a pre-trained geometric foundation model into a vision backbone and then compress multi-view image features into a compact set of scene tokens using learnable queries. This aims to reduce the computational load for subsequent world modeling while embedding crucial spatial information.
- **Dynamic Latent World Model (DLWM):** This module is motivated by the need to better utilize temporal dynamics for future prediction. Its objective is to employ a causal Transformer architecture to autoregressively predict future world status. This prediction is conditioned on both historical compressed visual representations and ego vehicle motion information (ego status), thereby enabling the model to learn dynamic understanding through self-supervised visual prediction and supervised motion prediction.

Overall, the motivation is to develop an autonomous driving system that achieves high planning performance (trajectory accuracy, safety, and rule compliance) with enhanced data efficiency and a smaller model footprint, making it more practical for real-world deployment under constrained data and compute budgets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Latent-WAM employs a three-module architecture: a Spatial-Aware Compressive World Encoder (SCWE), a Dynamic Latent World Model (DLWM), and a Trajectory Decoder. These modules are jointly optimized to learn scene representations with enhanced spatial and dynamic understanding capabilities.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The framework operates by first processing multi-view images through the SCWE to generate compact, spatially-aware scene tokens. These tokens, combined with ego status information, form a unified world status representation. The DLWM then processes a sequence of these world status representations to predict future world states autoregressively. Finally, a lightweight Trajectory Decoder utilizes the learned world status representations to generate a planned trajectory.

#### 3.2. Spatial-Aware Compressive World Encoder (SCWE)

The SCWE is designed to distill geometric knowledge into the vision backbone and compress multi-view image information into a compact set of tokens.

- **Scene Compression:** Multi-view images are initially embedded into image patch tokens using a DINO encoder. A set of learnable scene queries are introduced and concatenated with these image patch tokens. This combined input is fed into the DINO encoder, which projects the features into a lower-dimensional latent space, yielding frame-wise and view-specific compressed scene representations (scene tokens) alongside refined image tokens. This process aims to reduce the dimensionality of visual information while preserving planning-relevant cues.
- **Geometric Alignment:** To imbue the DINO encoder with spatial understanding, a distillation mechanism is employed. A frozen geometric foundation model (WorldMirror) is used to extract patch-level geometric features from the multi-view images. The image patch tokens output by the DINO encoder are projected into the same dimension as these geometric features. A cosine similarity loss is then applied between the projected encoder outputs and the pre-computed geometric features. This loss encourages the vision backbone to learn representations aligned with the geometric properties of the scene. The geometric features are pre-computed and cached offline to avoid repeated inference costs during training.

#### 3.3. Dynamic Latent World Model (DLWM)

The DLWM is responsible for modeling world state transition dynamics through autoregressive prediction in the latent space.

- **World Latent Status Aggregation:** Scene tokens from different camera views, produced by the SCWE, are aggregated to form a holistic perception representation. This aggregated representation is then concatenated with ego status embeddings (derived from driving commands, velocity, and acceleration via a single-layer MLP) to construct a unified frame-wise Scene-Ego world status representation. This aggregated representation serves as the input for dynamic world modeling.
- **Causal World Model Prediction:** The DLWM formulates world state transition as an autoregressive prediction problem. It employs a causal Transformer decoder to predict future world status representations. A set of learnable future world status queries are processed, conditioned on historical world status representations. During training, a target encoder, an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) copy of the SCWE, generates ground truth future world status representations for self-supervised learning.
- **Teacher Forcing Attention Mask:** To enable efficient training while maintaining causal consistency, a frame-wise attention mask is utilized. Within each frame block, tokens can attend bidirectionally. However, across frames, tokens can only attend to those appearing earlier in the sequence, enforcing temporal causality. This allows for parallel prediction of future world states while preventing error accumulation.
- **3D-RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding):** To explicitly encode spatio-temporal relationships into the query and key-value vectors of the Transformer, 3D-RoPE is applied. The head dimension of the attention mechanism is split into three parts to encode temporal coordinates, camera indices, and token indices, allowing the model to differentiate positions along these dimensions.
- **Ego Status Supervision:** To guide the world model in learning accurate dynamics, ego status prediction is supervised. Ego status embeddings are extracted from the predicted future world status. These embeddings are then fed into separate MLPs to predict future driving commands, velocity, and acceleration, which are supervised against ground truth values.

#### 3.4. Trajectory Planning

A Trajectory Decoder, structured as a standard Transformer, is responsible for generating planning trajectories. It takes the current world status representation and a set of learnable trajectory queries as input. Conditioned on the driving command, the decoder generates multiple candidate trajectories. A lightweight MLP then decodes these trajectory tokens into K candidate trajectories, each comprising a sequence of (x, y, theta) poses over a defined prediction horizon. The trajectory corresponding to the current driving command is selected as the final plan.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference

- **Training Objective:** The model is trained end-to-end using a combined loss function. This includes an L1 loss for trajectory imitation ($L_{traj}$), a cosine similarity loss for geometric alignment ($L_{align}$), an MSE loss for future world state prediction ($L_{wm}$), and a composite ego status loss ($L_{ego}$). $L_{ego}$ comprises a Cross-Entropy loss for driving commands ($L_{cmd}$) and MSE losses for velocity ($L_{v}$) and acceleration ($L_{a}$).
- **Inference:** During inference, only the online SCWE and the Trajectory Decoder are utilized. The geometric foundation model and the EMA target encoder are not required, which contributes to lower inference latency.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The vision encoder utilizes DINOv2-Base (86.6M parameters). N=16 learnable scene queries are used, projecting to a latent dimension of 256. The temporal horizon is 4 frames, with 3 camera views (left, front, right) at 224x448 resolution. WorldMirror serves as the frozen geometric foundation model. The DLWM and trajectory decoder each consist of 4 Transformer layers with 8 attention heads. The inference model comprises 104M parameters. Training occurs on 32 A100 GPUs for 100 epochs, using AdamW optimizer and BF16 mixed-precision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Latent-WAM was evaluated on two benchmarks, NAVSIM v2 and HUGSIM, demonstrating performance improvements compared to existing methods.

#### 4.1. Performance on NAVSIM v2

On the NAVSIM v2 benchmark, Latent-WAM achieved an EPDMS of 89.3. This result surpassed prior state-of-the-art perception-free methods by 3.2 EPDMS. It also exhibited strong rule compliance metrics, ranking among the top for Driving Direction Compliance (DDC), Traffic Light Compliance (TLC), and Lane Keeping (LK). While slightly behind perception-based methods in some safety metrics, Latent-WAM demonstrated competitive performance with a significantly smaller model (104M parameters at inference) and reduced training data requirements. The Ego Progress (EP) score of 87.7 was slightly lower than some competitors, which the authors suggest might be due to a planning strategy that prioritizes safety over aggressive progress.

#### 4.2. Performance on HUGSIM

For closed-loop evaluation, Latent-WAM was tested on HUGSIM in a zero-shot generalization setting (trained on NAVSIM only, without fine-tuning). It achieved a Road Completion (RC) score of 45.9 and an HUGSIM Driving Score (HD-Score) of 28.9. These scores ranked first in RC and matched the highest HD-Score among the evaluated baselines, indicating its ability to generalize to novel photorealistic environments.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Each Component:**
 - A baseline using direct image patch tokens and a trajectory decoder achieved 87.9 EPDMS.
 - Compressing scene tokens resulted in a negligible decrease (87.7 EPDMS), indicating effective compression.
 - Adding geometric information significantly improved performance to 88.6 EPDMS, highlighting its importance.
 - Incorporating the DLWM (capturing future dynamics) further increased performance to 88.0 EPDMS (from 87.7).
 - Adding ego status supervision improved the score to 88.3 EPDMS, suggesting the benefit of self-state awareness.
 - The full model, combining all components, achieved the peak performance of 89.3 EPDMS, indicating synergistic contributions.

- **Impact of Geometric Information:**
 - Without geometric information, the model achieved 88.3 EPDMS.
 - Directly concatenating frozen geometric features as key-value inputs paradoxically degraded performance to 88.0 EPDMS, attributed to potential misalignment with the planning objective.
 - Distilling geometric knowledge into the vision backbone (as implemented in SCWE) yielded the highest performance of 89.3 EPDMS, suggesting that integrating spatial awareness into the learned representations is more effective than simple concatenation.

- **Vision Backbone for Geometric Distillation:**
 - Using DINO-Small achieved 86.3 EPDMS, indicating capacity limitations.
 - DINO-Base with full fine-tuning achieved 89.3 EPDMS, suggesting sufficient capacity is crucial.
 - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA resulted in substantial performance degradation for both DINO-Small-LoRA (84.7 EPDMS) and DINO-Base-LoRA (68.5 EPDMS). This suggests that LoRA's low-rank constraints were insufficient for effective distillation of high-dimensional geometric features.

- **World Model Prediction Temporal Stride:**
 - Predicting only the final frame (0 → 8) yielded 88.4 EPDMS.
 - Incorporating historical frames and intermediate future predictions with a stride of 4 (−3 → 0 → 4 → 8) improved performance to 89.3 EPDMS, indicating the benefit of multi-step supervision.
 - A denser stride of 2 (−3 → −2 → · · · → 8) resulted in 89.1 EPDMS, showing no further improvement and potentially incurring higher computational overhead due to increased target density without extending the temporal reasoning range.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

- **Trajectory Comparison:** Visualizations on NAVSIM demonstrated that Latent-WAM's predicted trajectories aligned more closely with human trajectories and maintained safer distances from other vehicles compared to Epona and World4Drive.
- **Spatial Understanding:** Cross-attention maps between scene tokens and image patches were visualized. Models trained with geometric distillation exhibited more focused attention on task-relevant regions such as lane markings, scene structures, and drivable areas. In contrast, models without distillation showed scattered attention, allocating weight to irrelevant background elements. The attention patterns also showed correlation with driving intent (e.g., focusing on the path for a left turn), indicating that the World Encoder learns to selectively attend to spatial information relevant to the driving context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Latent-WAM introduces a framework for end-to-end autonomous driving that addresses several limitations of existing world-model-based approaches. By integrating spatial-aware compression and dynamic latent modeling, the research demonstrates a method for building compact, planning-relevant world representations.

The significance of this work lies in its ability to achieve competitive, and in some benchmarks, state-of-the-art performance (89.3 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2, 45.9 RC and 28.9 HD-Score on HUGSIM) while operating with significantly reduced training data requirements and a compact model size (104M parameters at inference). This demonstrates a potential pathway toward more data-efficient and computationally lightweight autonomous driving systems.

The core contributions – the Spatial-Aware Compressive World Encoder and the Dynamic Latent World Model – offer specific advancements:
- The SCWE's distillation of geometric knowledge into the vision backbone, combined with scene compression via learnable queries, suggests a method for generating semantically rich yet compact scene representations without requiring additional inference-time modules for spatial understanding. This could lead to more robust and less latency-prone perception for planning.
- The DLWM's use of a causal Transformer with 3D-RoPE for autoregressive future world status prediction contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of temporal dynamics, allowing for better anticipatory planning.

The qualitative analyses, particularly the visualization of attention maps, suggest improved interpretability of the model's decision-making process, as it focuses on specific geometric structures and driving intentions.

The potential impact of Latent-WAM extends to:
- **Increased Accessibility:** The reduced data and computational requirements could lower the barriers to entry for developing and deploying autonomous driving systems, particularly for entities with limited resources.
- **Improved Scalability and Robustness:** By minimizing reliance on extensive perception annotations and auxiliary tasks, the framework could lead to more scalable and robust end-to-end driving algorithms that are less brittle to variations in sensor input or environmental conditions.
- **Advancements in World Models:** The methodologies for efficient scene compression, geometric distillation, and comprehensive dynamic latent prediction contribute to the broader development of world models in robotics and embodied AI, potentially inspiring new approaches for learning robust and efficient environment representations.

Overall, Latent-WAM represents a step towards more efficient, capable, and practical end-to-end autonomous driving systems, particularly by addressing the representation quality and temporal dynamics modeling in latent world models.
