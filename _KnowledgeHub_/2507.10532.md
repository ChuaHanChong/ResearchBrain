# Reasoning or Memorization? Unreliable Results of Reinforcement Learning Due to Data Contamination

> [!summary] Summary
> The research investigates why Qwen2.5 LLMs show performance gains from random rewards on math benchmarks, concluding that these gains stem primarily from test data contamination and memorization. Only accurate reward signals led to genuine improvements in mathematical reasoning on a novel, leakage-free benchmark called RandomCalculation.

## Problem

- Unreliable results were observed when applying reinforcement learning with spurious (random or incorrect) rewards to the Qwen2.5 series of large language models, particularly in mathematical reasoning tasks.
- A debate existed on whether reported performance gains with random rewards indicated genuine reasoning enhancements or were artifacts of data contamination.
- There was a lack of truly contamination-free benchmarks to reliably evaluate the impact of reinforcement learning on LLM reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- The study systematically audited existing mathematical benchmarks (e.g., MATH-500, AMC) for ==data contamination== using novel ==partial-prompt completion and answer accuracy metrics==.
- A novel, automatically generated, ==leakage-free dataset named *RandomCalculation*== was introduced to provide an unbiased evaluation of arithmetic reasoning.
- ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== was empirically evaluated with correct, random, inverted, and majority-voted incorrect reward signals on both contaminated and the clean *RandomCalculation* benchmarks.

## Results

- Qwen2.5-Math-7B exhibited partial-prompt completion rates exceeding **50%** and answer accuracy over **50%** on MATH-500 when prompted with only **60%** of a problem, demonstrating high memorization.
- On the contamination-free *RandomCalculation* dataset, only correct reward signals improved Qwen2.5-Math-7B's performance, while random or incorrect rewards resulted in unstable training and no consistent improvements.
- Analysis revealed significantly higher lexical overlap (ROUGE-L) and lower KL divergence between model outputs before and after RL training on contaminated benchmarks, indicating memory retrieval rather than genuine policy shifts due to spurious rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Widely used mathematical benchmarks, such as MATH-500, are significantly contaminated, causing models like Qwen2.5 to exhibit high memorization of problems and solutions from their pre-training data.
> - The apparent effectiveness of spurious rewards on contaminated benchmarks is largely due to an 'exploitation bias' in the GRPO algorithm, which reinforces the retrieval of existing memorized content rather than fostering novel reasoning.
> - On clean, leakage-free data, only genuinely correct and continuous reward signals lead to stable and consistent improvements in mathematical reasoning, while random or incorrect rewards provide no reliable gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and increasingly scrutinized issue in the field of large language models: the reliability and trustworthiness of benchmark evaluations. In particular, it delves into the "reasoning abilities" of LLMs, especially in mathematical domains, and the role of reinforcement learning (RL) in enhancing these capabilities.

The broader research landscape for LLMs has seen rapid advancements, with models like OpenAI's GPT series, DeepSeek-R1, and the Qwen family achieving state-of-the-art results across various tasks, including complex mathematical reasoning. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a powerful technique to fine-tune LLMs, particularly in domains where correct answers are unambiguous, allowing for precise reward signals.

However, a surprising observation in recent literature (e.g., Zuo et al. 2025; Shao et al. 2025) has been that certain LLMs, specifically the Qwen2.5 series, appeared to show performance gains on mathematical benchmarks even when trained with *random or incorrect reward signals*. This counter-intuitive finding was less pronounced or absent in other model families like Llama, prompting a debate about the underlying mechanisms. Two main hypotheses arose: either Qwen possessed exceptionally strong inherent mathematical capacity that could benefit from even noisy signals, or its pre-training on massive web-scale corpora made it susceptible to data contamination, leading to memorization rather than genuine reasoning.

This paper directly confronts this debate, challenging the optimistic interpretations of spurious reward effectiveness. It builds upon a growing body of work that highlights the potential for benchmark leakage (e.g., Xu et al. 2024) and the need for rigorous, contamination-free evaluation in LLM research. By systematically investigating data contamination and proposing a novel clean benchmark, this work aims to provide a more reliable foundation for understanding LLM capabilities and the true impact of RL-based fine-tuning. It thus contributes to the crucial discourse on scientific rigor and reproducibility in a rapidly evolving AI landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate the "unreliable results" observed when applying reinforcement learning with spurious rewards to the Qwen2.5 series of large language models, particularly in mathematical reasoning tasks. The authors aim to discern whether reported performance gains with random or incorrect reward signals are indicative of genuine reasoning enhancements or are merely artifacts of data contamination.

Specifically, the key objectives are:

- **Systematically audit existing mathematical benchmarks:** The authors seek to quantify the extent of potential data contamination in widely used benchmarks such as MATH-500, AMC, and AIME for the Qwen2.5 models.
- **Develop a truly clean evaluation benchmark:** To overcome the limitations of potentially contaminated datasets, the paper aims to introduce a novel, automatically generated dataset, *RandomCalculation*, designed to be entirely leakage-free and allow for trustworthy evaluation.
- **Empirically evaluate RLVR with diverse reward signals on clean data:** The core objective is to conduct controlled experiments using correct, random, and inverted reward signals on the *RandomCalculation* dataset to isolate the true impact of reinforcement learning on mathematical reasoning in the absence of contamination.
- **Uncover the underlying mechanism:** The research endeavors to explain why spurious rewards appeared effective on contaminated benchmarks for Qwen but not on clean data or for other model families like Llama.
- **Provide recommendations for future research:** Based on their findings, the authors intend to offer guidelines for more reliable evaluation practices in the field of LLM reasoning.

The motivation behind these objectives is profound. If LLM performance gains, especially from advanced techniques like RL, are largely attributable to memorization of training data rather than true generalization or acquisition of new reasoning skills, then the progress of the field is being misjudged. This could lead to misallocated research efforts, overestimation of model capabilities, and a lack of understanding of fundamental learning mechanisms. By scrutinizing these "surprising" results, the authors aim to re-establish a foundation of trustworthy evaluation, ensuring that future advancements in LLM reasoning are built on genuine capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a multi-faceted empirical approach to address their objectives, combining benchmark auditing, novel dataset creation, controlled reinforcement learning experiments, and fine-grained analytical comparisons.

#### 3.1. Model Selection

The study primarily focuses on the **Qwen2.5 series** (Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, and Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct) due to its reported exceptional performance in mathematical benchmarks and its susceptibility to spurious rewards in prior work. For a controlled comparison, **Llama3.1-8B** and **Llama3.1-8B-Instruct** are also evaluated, as they have comparable parameter counts but exhibit different behaviors under RL.

#### 3.2. Evaluation of Memorization Capability (Data Contamination Audit)

To assess data contamination in widely used benchmarks (MATH-500, AMC, AIME2024, AIME2025, MinervaMath, LiveMathBench), the authors introduce two novel metrics:
- **Partial-Prompt Completion Rate:** Models are prompted with truncated versions (40%, 60%, or 80% prefixes) of original problems. The completion quality is measured by ROUGE-L and Exact Match (EM) scores between the generated continuation and the ground truth.
- **Partial-Prompt Answer Accuracy:** For the same partial prompts, the model's unconstrained continuation is checked for the presence of the correct final answer. High accuracy here indicates the model can "recover" the answer from an incomplete problem, strongly suggesting memorization.
Experiments are conducted under various decoding configurations (Greedy and Avg@16, with and without official chat templates) to provide a comprehensive view of baseline performance and template-related biases.

#### 3.3. Dataset Construction: RandomCalculation

Recognizing the limitations of potentially contaminated existing benchmarks, the authors propose **RandomCalculation**, a novel, automatically generated dataset of arithmetic expressions.
- **Generation Algorithm:** Algorithm 1 (in Appendix) constructs expressions of arbitrary length (1 to 20 computation steps) using random operands (integers 0-100, fractions, squares, cubes) and basic arithmetic operators. This programmatic generation guarantees that every instance is unique and post-dates the pre-training corpora of models like Qwen, thereby ensuring it is leakage-free.
- **Dataset Structure:** The dataset comprises 20 sub-datasets, each with 1,000 unique problems corresponding to different calculation steps.

#### 3.4. RLVR-Based Evaluation

The study uses **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** (Shao et al. 2024) as the RL algorithm. Key aspects of the RL setup include:
- **Reward Signals:**
 - **Correct:** Standard verifiable reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) for existing benchmarks.
 - **Random:** Assigns 1 with a probability γ (0.5 in experiments) and 0 otherwise.
 - **Inverted:** Flips the correct signal (0 for correct, 1 for incorrect).
 - **Mv-incorrect:** Rewards 1 if the model output matches a majority-voted incorrect label from the model itself.
- **Continuous Reward for RandomCalculation:** For the new dataset, a specialized continuous reward function (ranging from 0 to 1) is designed. It penalizes both absolute and relative errors to provide richer feedback and stabilize training, addressing the challenge of sparse binary rewards for high-precision decimal answers.
- **Training Protocol:** Experiments on RandomCalculation involved two sub-datasets (5-step and 10-step problems), each with 700 training problems and 300 for validation.

#### 3.5. Fine-grained Analysis

To further understand the observed phenomena, the authors perform:
- **Decoding Configuration Analysis:** Investigate how different decoding settings (with/without chat templates, greedy/sampling) affect initial model performance on MATH-500, revealing potential biases.
- **Response Similarity:** Measure ROUGE-L and KL divergence between model outputs *before* and *after* RL training. A higher similarity for contaminated benchmarks would indicate memorization or retrieval, while lower similarity for clean benchmarks would suggest genuine policy shifts.
- **Token-level Probability Analysis:** Compare the probabilities assigned to answer-relevant numeric tokens by models before and after RL, particularly under random reward settings, to understand the token-level dynamics of policy updates.

This comprehensive methodology allows the authors to dissect the complex interplay between model architecture, pre-training data, reinforcement learning signals, and evaluation benchmarks, ultimately shedding light on the true nature of LLM reasoning improvements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several critical findings that collectively point to data contamination as the primary driver behind the "unreliable results" of reinforcement learning with spurious rewards on the Qwen2.5 series.

#### 4.1. Evidence of Data Contamination in Public Benchmarks

- **High Memorization in Qwen2.5:** Qwen2.5 models (especially Qwen2.5-Math-7B) demonstrate remarkably high partial-prompt completion rates and answer accuracy on MATH-500, AMC, and AIME2024. For instance, Qwen2.5-Math-7B can reconstruct over 50% of the remaining problem and achieve over 50% answer accuracy on MATH-500 even when prompted with only the first 60% of a problem. Even with only 40% of the problem, it still recovers nearly 40% of problems and answers 41.2% correctly.
- **Contrast with Llama3.1 and Clean Benchmarks:** In stark contrast, Llama3.1-8B shows minimal memorization (e.g., 3.8% completion rate and 2.4% answer accuracy on MATH-500 with 60% prompt). Crucially, on newly released and presumed clean benchmarks like LiveMathBench, Qwen's completion rate drops to 0.0% and its partial-prompt answer accuracy falls to around 2.0%, similar to Llama's performance. These results strongly confirm that Qwen's pre-training on massive web-scale corpora has inadvertently incorporated standard math benchmark problems.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Model outputs often include coherent reasoning chains and even syntactically valid Python code (which is not executed), suggesting direct memorization of problems and solutions from publicly available resources.

#### 4.2. Impact of Spurious Rewards on Contaminated vs. Clean Benchmarks

- **Spurious Gains on MATH-500 are Misleading:** On MATH-500, random rewards and majority-voted incorrect rewards appear to boost Qwen2.5-Math-7B's accuracy. However, this "gain" is largely an artifact of the base model adapting to the instruction-tuning template format and converging to its best initial performance (achieved without a template), indicating memory recall rather than genuine reasoning. Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, lacking such memorization, shows little to no benefit or even degradation.
- **Only Correct Rewards are Effective on RandomCalculation:** On the newly introduced, leakage-free RandomCalculation dataset, the picture changes dramatically. Only accurate, continuous reward signals yield consistent and stable performance improvements for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, allowing it to surpass its initial performance ceiling. Conversely, random or incorrect rewards lead to highly unstable training with no reliable improvement; inverted rewards rapidly degrade performance.
- **Qwen's Inherent Capacity:** While Qwen2.5 demonstrates stronger initial mathematical capabilities than Llama3.1 on RandomCalculation, these inherent strengths are not the cause for the spurious reward benefits on contaminated datasets. Llama3.1-8B-Instruct fails to surpass its initial Max@16 even with correct rewards on RandomCalculation, further highlighting Qwen's stronger base.

#### 4.3. Mechanism of Spurious Reward Effectiveness: Memory Retrieval

- **Exploitation Bias of GRPO:** The authors attribute the apparent effectiveness of spurious rewards on contaminated data to an "exploitation bias" in the GRPO algorithm's clipped objective. For high-probability tokens (which include memorized correct answers from contaminated data), the gradient tends to be non-negative, effectively up-weighting these tokens without significant penalty. This allows the model to retrieve memorized answers. For low-probability or less certain tokens (as in clean, challenging problems), the gradient is close to zero, leading to no meaningful optimization.
- **Higher Response Similarity:** A comparison of model outputs (ROUGE-L and KL distance) before and after RL training reveals significantly higher lexical overlap and lower KL divergence for MATH-500 compared to RandomCalculation. This indicates that on contaminated benchmarks, the RL process primarily reinforces the retrieval of existing memorized content rather than fostering novel reasoning paths. Spurious rewards even achieve *higher* ROUGE-L than correct rewards, suggesting they specifically encourage retrieval of similar outputs, whereas correct rewards might guide exploration towards genuinely new solutions.

In summary, the paper convincingly demonstrates that the previously observed "magical" success of spurious rewards on Qwen models in mathematical reasoning benchmarks is an illusion caused by test data contamination, leading to memorization rather than true reasoning acquisition.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the evaluation, development, and interpretation of large language models, particularly concerning their reasoning capabilities and the role of reinforcement learning.

**Re-evaluating Trustworthiness of LLM Benchmarks:** The most immediate impact is a strong caution against blindly trusting performance metrics on widely used benchmarks, especially for models pre-trained on vast web-scale datasets. The paper provides compelling evidence that benchmarks like MATH-500, AMC, and AIME can be significantly contaminated, leading to inflated performance figures that reflect memorization rather than genuine reasoning. This calls for a critical re-evaluation of past and ongoing research that relies on these benchmarks.

**Reframing the Role of Reinforcement Learning in LLMs:** The findings challenge previous optimistic interpretations regarding the effectiveness of even noisy or random rewards in enhancing LLM reasoning. By demonstrating that such "gains" vanish on clean datasets, the paper clarifies that proper, well-aligned reward signals are crucial for genuine reasoning improvements. This re-establishes the fundamental principle of RL, where an accurate reward signal is essential for effective learning, and corrects a potential misconception that might have misdirected research efforts.

**Necessity for Clean and Robust Evaluation Protocols:** The introduction of *RandomCalculation* as a leakage-free benchmark underscores the urgent need for more robust and contamination-resistant evaluation methodologies. This work will likely catalyze the development of more dynamic, automatically generated, or continuously updated benchmarks across various reasoning domains to ensure that reported advancements reflect true capabilities. Researchers and benchmark developers are encouraged to follow suit and audit their datasets rigorously.

#### 5.1. Guiding Future LLM Research and Development

- **Benchmark Selection:** Future studies aiming to evaluate reasoning abilities, especially with RL-based fine-tuning, should prioritize uncontaminated benchmarks or explicitly verify data leakage.
- **Model Diversity:** Evaluating methods across diverse model families (beyond just Qwen or Llama) is crucial to ensure conclusions are generalizable and not model-specific artifacts.
- **Understanding Mechanisms:** The detailed analysis of exploitation bias in RL algorithms and the distinction between memorization and reasoning provides deeper insights into how LLMs learn and how their learning processes can be inadvertently biased by data characteristics.

**Advancing the Science of AI Evaluation:** Beyond mathematical reasoning, the principles and methodologies presented in this paper—particularly the partial-prompt completion/accuracy metrics—can be adapted to audit other reasoning domains (e.g., code generation, as shown in the appendix) and benchmark types for contamination. This contributes to a more scientific and rigorous approach to evaluating AI systems, fostering transparent and reproducible research.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as restricting experiments to a subset of Qwen and Llama models and not exhaustively evaluating all RL algorithms, its core message is robust. This work is a timely and important contribution that will undoubtedly shape how the AI community approaches LLM evaluation and the interpretation of performance gains, pushing for greater scientific rigor in an era of rapid progress.
