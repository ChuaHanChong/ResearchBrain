# NS-VLA: Towards Neuro-Symbolic Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> NS-VLA introduces a neuro-symbolic framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, integrating structured reasoning with online reinforcement learning to improve robotic manipulation. The framework achieves enhanced generalization, data efficiency, and exploration capabilities across various benchmarks like LIBERO and CALVIN.

## Problem

- Current end-to-end VLA models lack structural awareness, treating action generation as a direct mapping without explicitly recognizing reusable primitive actions, leading to limited generalization.
- Achieving high performance in VLA often necessitates extensive datasets of demonstrations and computationally intensive models, which are resource-intensive and impractical for new tasks.
- Methods primarily based on supervised fine-tuning are confined to imitating expert trajectories, restricting the agent's capacity for novel environmental exploration beyond demonstrated actions.

## Method

- A neuro-symbolic encoding module grounds natural language instructions and visual observations into a sequence of structured primitives (neuro-symbols) and compact state representations, with a plan-constrained symbolic classifier.
- A symbolic solver translates inferred neuro-symbols into continuous robot actions using query-driven visual token sparsification and a causal Transformer for chunked action generation.
- An online ==reinforcement learning== optimization strategy refines trainable modules through environment interaction, utilizing a primitive-segmented reward and a Group-Relative Optimization Strategy (GRPO) with KL regularization for stable learning.

## Results

- NS-VLA achieved an average success rate of **69.1%** on the LIBERO benchmark with 1-shot training and **79.4%** on LIBERO-Plus, outperforming VLA-Adapter (**65.3%**) and OpenVLA-OFT (**69.6%**) respectively.
- The framework demonstrated high data efficiency, showing minimal performance degradation when moving from full demonstration to one-shot training, and maintained high success rates across different VLM backbone sizes.
- On the CALVIN ABC→D split, NS-VLA achieved a **91.2%** 5-task success rate, surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods and enabling significantly broader action coverage and environmental exploration compared to imitation-only approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating neuro-symbolic reasoning to extract and orchestrate structured primitives enhances understanding of task compositionality, leading to improved generalization and data efficiency.
> - Combining a sparse, task-relevant visual representation with a symbolic action generator provides robustness against visual and spatial perturbations, allowing for more stable continuous control.
> - An online reinforcement learning objective with a structured reward system enables active exploration beyond expert demonstrations, facilitating the discovery of optimal or novel behaviors and improving adaptability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to enable embodied agents, particularly robots, to understand natural language instructions, interpret visual observations, and execute corresponding action sequences for manipulation tasks.

Historically, VLA models have evolved from connecting pre-trained vision-language backbones to continuous control mechanisms, often employing techniques like denoising diffusion or flow matching. More recently, the emergence of multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to approaches that fine-tune these backbones to directly predict action sequences. Concurrently, advancements in applying reinforcement learning (RL) to LLMs have enabled VLA models to move beyond static offline datasets, facilitating active exploration within environments.

Despite these developments, the paper identifies three primary challenges inherited by current VLA methods from multimodal LLMs:
1. **Lack of structural awareness:** End-to-end VLA models often treat action generation as a direct mapping without explicitly recognizing reusable primitive actions or their underlying structural relationships, leading to limited generalization. Robotic manipulation tasks are typically decomposable into such primitives.
2. **Heavy reliance on large-scale data and complex architectures:** Achieving high performance often necessitates extensive datasets of demonstrations and computationally intensive models, which can be impractical to acquire and deploy for every task.
3. **Limited interaction with explorability:** Methods primarily based on supervised fine-tuning are constrained to imitating expert trajectories, restricting the agent's capacity for novel environmental exploration beyond demonstrated actions.

The proposed Neuro-Symbolic Vision-Language-Action (NS-VLA) framework addresses these limitations by integrating symbolic reasoning with neural pattern recognition. This approach positions NS-VLA within the neuro-symbolic AI paradigm, which aims to combine the strengths of both neural networks (for perception and continuous control) and symbolic systems (for structured reasoning, interpretability, and data efficiency). By doing so, NS-VLA seeks to develop VLA models that are more structurally aware, data-efficient, robust to perturbations, and capable of active environmental exploration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel VLA framework, NS-VLA, that addresses the identified challenges in current VLA models for robotic manipulation tasks. The motivation stems from the observed shortcomings of existing end-to-end VLA methods, specifically:

- **To instill structural awareness and improve generalization:** Current end-to-end VLA models often struggle to capture the inherent compositional structure of robotic manipulation tasks, where complex behaviors are composed of reusable atomic primitives. This lack of structural understanding limits their ability to generalize to novel tasks or variations. NS-VLA aims to explicitly extract and orchestrate these structured primitives, thereby enabling the model to learn and reuse common behavioral patterns across different tasks. This is motivated by the hypothesis that explicit symbolic representation of primitives will lead to improved generalization capabilities compared to purely neural approaches.

- **To reduce reliance on large-scale data and complex architectures:** Generating massive, high-quality robotic demonstrations for every conceivable task is resource-intensive and often impractical. Existing VLA models, particularly those leveraging large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), typically require substantial data and complex models to achieve proficiency. NS-VLA is motivated by the need for a more data-efficient approach, proposing to achieve this by using a lightweight architecture that generates actions through logical reasoning on symbolic primitives rather than direct regression from raw inputs. This aims to lessen the computational burden and the dependency on extensive datasets.

- **To enable expanded exploration beyond demonstrations:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) based VLA methods, while achieving high trajectory precision, are confined to the expert trajectories seen during training. This imitation-only paradigm restricts the agent's ability to discover optimal or novel strategies by interacting with the environment. NS-VLA is motivated to overcome this limitation by incorporating an online reinforcement learning objective. This allows the agent to actively explore the environment, learn from environmental feedback, and refine its policy beyond the boundaries of initial demonstrations, potentially leading to more robust and adaptable behaviors.

In summary, the key objectives are to design a VLA framework that is structure-aware, data-efficient, and capable of active, expansive exploration, moving beyond the current limitations of end-to-end imitation learning in robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NS-VLA framework integrates neuro-symbolic principles with online reinforcement learning, structured into three main components: a neuro-symbolic encoding module, a symbolic solver, and an online RL optimization strategy.

#### 3.1. Neuro-Symbolic Encoding and Embedding

This module is responsible for grounding natural language instructions and visual observations into discrete primitives and compact state representations.

- **Neuro-Symbolic Encoder:** A pre-trained VLM encoder processes the current observation ($o_t$) and instruction ($x$) to extract token-level features ($\psi_t$). At the beginning of each episode, this VLM generates a sequence of structured primitives, denoted as a plan $p = (u^{(1)}, \ldots, u^{(M)})$, where each $u^{(m)}$ is a "neuro-symbol" specifying "what to manipulate and how." This plan remains fixed throughout the episode to ensure temporal consistency. Execution progress is tracked by a plan pointer $m_t$, which is constrained to advance monotonically, either staying at the current primitive ($m_{t-1}$) or moving to the next one ($\min(m_{t-1} + 1, M)$).

- **Symbolic Classifier:** This component predicts which primitive from the generated plan is currently being executed. It consists of an MLP ($g_{\phi}$) that maps pooled VLM features ($\bar{\psi}_t$) to a probability distribution over the primitive set $U$. To ensure coherence with the overall plan, the inference is plan-constrained: the decision is restricted to the admissible indices $K(m_{t-1}) = \{m_{t-1}, \min(m_{t-1} + 1, M)\}$. The next plan pointer $\hat{m}_t$ is selected by maximizing this masked distribution, and the executed primitive $\hat{u}_t$ is set accordingly. The classifier is optimized using a segment-end window supervision loss, focusing on frames near primitive transition boundaries in expert demonstrations to sharpen the distinction between primitives.

#### 3.2. Symbolic Solver

The symbolic solver translates the inferred neuro-symbol $\hat{u}_t$ into continuous robot actions, designed for real-time performance.

- **Visual Token Sparsification:** To address computational redundancy from dense VLM token sequences, a query-driven attention layer is introduced. The predicted primitive $\hat{u}_t$ is embedded into a query vector ($q_t$). Relevance scores ($\alpha_{t,i}$) are computed between this query and each visual token from the VLM encoder. During training, a differentiable soft Top-K gating mechanism is used to select the K most relevant tokens. During inference, a hard Top-K selection identifies these tokens. The selected tokens are then aggregated into a compact context vector ($c_t$), effectively reducing the visual representation from N tokens to a single vector, thus focusing on task-relevant visual information.

- **Action Generator:** A causal Transformer serves as the action generator. At each step, it concatenates the sparse visual summary ($c_t$), the primitive embedding (Embed($\hat{u}_t$)), and the proprioceptive state ($S_t$) into an input token ($e_t$). The Transformer then outputs an open-loop action chunk $A_t = (a_t, \ldots, a_{t+H-1})$ of length H. This chunked action generation is intended to improve online efficiency and control stability.

#### 3.3. Online Reinforcement Learning Optimization

The online phase is formulated as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), where trainable modules are refined through environment interaction. The pre-trained VLM encoder and plan generator remain frozen to prevent representation drift.

- **RL Formulation:** The hierarchical policy $\pi_{\Theta}$ (where $\Theta = \{\phi, \theta\}$ are the learnable parameters for the classifier and action generator) determines both the primitive $u_t$ and the action chunk $A_t$ conditioned on the history $H_t$.

- **Primitive-Segmented Reward:** A structured reward system is designed to guide learning:
 - **Segmentation:** The segment index $\sigma_t = \hat{m}_t$ is tracked, and a boundary ($b_t=1$) is detected when the index changes.
 - **Segment milestone reward:** A reward ($r_{seg_t} = b_t$) is given upon successful primitive transition.
 - **Within-segment progress shaping:** For dense guidance, a potential-based reward shaping term ($r_{prog_t} = \gamma\Phi_{t+1} - \Phi_t$) is employed. This potential $\Phi_t$ measures the proximity of the current latent visual state ($\ell_t$) to a set of reference prototypes ($\mu_{\sigma_t,c}$) associated with the active segment $\sigma_t$. These prototypes are constructed from successful segments in a buffer. The total reward $r_t$ combines the sparse task signal ($r_{task_t}$), segment milestone reward, and progress shaping.

- **Group-Relative Optimization Strategy (GRPO):** Parameters are optimized using GRPO, which computes group-normalized advantages ($A_i$) from a group of G rollouts for each instruction. To prevent policy collapse and stabilize learning under sparse rewards, a KL regularization term is added to penalize deviation from a behavior-cloning reference policy ($\pi_{BC}$). This ensures policy updates remain close to a stable behavioral prior while leveraging online exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and CALVIN robotic manipulation benchmarks, addressing specific research questions (RQs).

**RQ1: Does NS-VLA outperform other VLA methods?**
- **Overall Performance:** NS-VLA demonstrated higher success rates compared to previous methods across various challenging settings.
 - On the LIBERO benchmark under a 1-shot training regime (one demonstration per task), NS-VLA achieved an average success rate (SR) of 69.1%, outperforming the next best baseline, VLA-Adapter (65.3%).
 - When trained on the full LIBERO dataset and tested on the more challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark, NS-VLA achieved an average SR of 79.4%, surpassing OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%) and RIPT-VLA (68.4%).
- **Data Utilization and Generalization:** NS-VLA exhibited minimal performance degradation when transitioning from full demonstration training to one-shot training, and maintained high success rates under environmental perturbations in LIBERO-Plus. This indicates its capability to capture task correlations from limited data and its robustness to visual and spatial disturbances.

**RQ2: Does the main component of NS-VLA work, and how is its comparative analysis?**
- **Ablation Study:** Removing any of NS-VLA's key components (Primitive Classifier, Vision Extractor, Action Generator, or Reinforcement Learning) led to a degradation in performance (Figure 5a), indicating the individual contributions of each module.
- **Structural Robustness:** Ablating the Primitive Classifier or the Action Generator resulted in performance decline under LIBERO-Plus perturbations (Figure 5b). This suggests the Primitive Classifier is important for grounding continuous control by identifying active operations, and the Action Generator contributes to robustness against distribution shifts compared to unstructured end-to-end decoding.
- **Efficacy of Task-Relevant Visual Grounding:** The Vision Extractor, responsible for dynamically attending to relevant visual regions, was shown to effectively filter background distractions and enhance resilience to visual perturbations (Figures 5a and 5c).
- **Optimization Stability:** The proposed soft-constrained GRPO objective ($J_{GRPO}$) achieved higher success rates with fewer training iterations compared to a standard GRPO baseline (Figure 5d), confirming its role in stable convergence and improved action generation.

**RQ3: How are data efficiency of NS-VLA?**
- **Data Efficiency:** NS-VLA maintained high success rates across different VLM backbone sizes (2B, 4B, 8B, with 98.6%, 98.8%, 98.9% SR respectively on LIBERO, Figure 6a). This suggests effective neuro-symbolic disentanglement is not overly dependent on model scale.
- **Accelerated Convergence:** Comparing one-shot against full-demonstration training, NS-VLA exhibited accelerated convergence with minimal performance degradation (Figure 6b). This finding supports the notion that the symbolic plan prior acts as a strong inductive bias, reducing data requirements and annotation costs.

**RQ4: How are generalizability of NS-VLA?**
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** On the CALVIN ABC→D split, NS-VLA achieved a 5-task success rate of 91.2% and an average sequence length of 4.72, surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods (e.g., VLA-Adapter with 80.0% SR and 4.50 avg. length, Figure 7a).
- **Robustness to Perturbations:** Under the 7-dimensional perturbation suite of LIBERO-Plus, NS-VLA consistently sustained a higher average success rate compared to baselines across all metrics (Figure 7b), confirming its ability to internalize intrinsic task associations and map this structural knowledge to zero-shot scenarios.

**RQ5: How are exploration space of NS-VLA?**
- **Expanded Exploration:** Compared to Diffusion, Flow Matching, and End-to-End VLA methods, NS-VLA's online RL component enabled significantly broader action coverage and active environmental exploration (Figure 8b).
- **Trajectory Generation:** A case study illustrated that NS-VLA explores a distribution of potential candidate trajectories to identify and execute optimal paths, as opposed to end-to-end methods that infer a single deterministic trajectory (Figure 8a). This mechanism allows for superior exploration quality and robust generalization in data-scarce regimes by filtering suboptimal behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NS-VLA framework represents a significant step towards developing more robust, efficient, and generalizable embodied agents for robotic manipulation. Its key contributions and potential impacts include:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Data Efficiency:** By explicitly incorporating neuro-symbolic reasoning to extract and orchestrate structured primitives, NS-VLA enables better understanding of task compositionality. This leads to superior zero-shot generalizability and high data efficiency, particularly evident in one-shot learning scenarios and under environmental perturbations. This can reduce the intensive data collection typically required for training VLA models, making robotic learning more accessible and scalable for new tasks.
- **Improved Robustness and Exploration:** The framework's design, including a robust symbolic solver and online reinforcement learning, allows agents to adapt to dynamic and uncertain environments. The ability for active exploration beyond expert demonstrations facilitates the discovery of optimal or novel behaviors, leading to more resilient and adaptable robotic policies that are less prone to performance degradation in varied conditions.
- **Foundation for Neuro-Symbolic Embodied AI:** This work lays a foundation for future neuro-symbolic embodied agents by demonstrating the benefits of combining neural pattern recognition with symbolic reasoning. The explicit primitive decomposition and plan-constrained execution provide a structured approach to complex tasks, potentially offering greater interpretability and verifiability compared to purely end-to-end neural systems.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations and outlines future directions:
- **Refining Primitive Granularity and Automatic Discovery:** The current reliance on manually defined action primitives could be overcome by developing methods for automatic primitive discovery, enabling dynamic adjustment of granularity from low-level motions to high-level semantic skills.
- **Architectural Evolution for Tighter Neuro-Symbolic Integration:** Future work could focus on bi-directional feedback between continuous control policies and symbolic planners, and integrating Graph Neural Networks to model dynamic object relationships, enhancing reasoning in complex environments.
- **Scaling to Diverse Real-World Environments:** Validating NS-VLA on physical robot platforms and larger, multi-robot datasets is crucial to bridge the sim-to-real gap and ensure robustness to real-world noise and dynamics.
- **Lifelong Learning and Human-Interactive Correction:** Integrating human-in-the-loop mechanisms for real-time corrections and leveraging these as reinforcement learning signals could enable agents to continuously acquire new skills and adapt to user preferences without catastrophic forgetting.

The work currently relies on standard simulation benchmarks and poses no immediate ethical concerns. However, the authors highlight the importance of establishing strict safety protocols for physical deployment to ensure safe human-robot interaction, aiming to advance general-purpose robotic technology with positive societal impacts.
