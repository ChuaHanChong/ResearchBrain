# NS-VLA: Towards Neuro-Symbolic Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> NS-VLA introduces a neuro-symbolic framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, integrating structured reasoning with online reinforcement learning to improve robotic manipulation. The framework achieves enhanced generalization, data efficiency, and exploration capabilities across various benchmarks like LIBERO and CALVIN.

## Problem

- Current end-to-end VLA models lack structural awareness, treating action generation as a direct mapping without explicitly recognizing reusable primitive actions, leading to limited generalization.
- Achieving high performance in VLA often necessitates extensive datasets of demonstrations and computationally intensive models, which are resource-intensive and impractical for new tasks.
- Methods primarily based on supervised fine-tuning are confined to imitating expert trajectories, restricting the agent's capacity for novel environmental exploration beyond demonstrated actions.

## Method

- A neuro-symbolic encoding module grounds natural language instructions and visual observations into a sequence of structured primitives (neuro-symbols) and compact state representations, with a plan-constrained symbolic classifier.
- A symbolic solver translates inferred neuro-symbols into continuous robot actions using query-driven visual token sparsification and a causal Transformer for chunked action generation.
- An online ==reinforcement learning== optimization strategy refines trainable modules through environment interaction, utilizing a primitive-segmented reward and a Group-Relative Optimization Strategy (GRPO) with KL regularization for stable learning.

## Results

- NS-VLA achieved an average success rate of **69.1%** on the LIBERO benchmark with 1-shot training and **79.4%** on LIBERO-Plus, outperforming VLA-Adapter (**65.3%**) and OpenVLA-OFT (**69.6%**) respectively.
- The framework demonstrated high data efficiency, showing minimal performance degradation when moving from full demonstration to one-shot training, and maintained high success rates across different VLM backbone sizes.
- On the CALVIN ABC→D split, NS-VLA achieved a **91.2%** 5-task success rate, surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods and enabling significantly broader action coverage and environmental exploration compared to imitation-only approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating neuro-symbolic reasoning to extract and orchestrate structured primitives enhances understanding of task compositionality, leading to improved generalization and data efficiency.
> - Combining a sparse, task-relevant visual representation with a symbolic action generator provides robustness against visual and spatial perturbations, allowing for more stable continuous control.
> - An online reinforcement learning objective with a structured reward system enables active exploration beyond expert demonstrations, facilitating the discovery of optimal or novel behaviors and improving adaptability.
