# VideoREPA: Learning Physics for Video Generation through Relational Alignment with Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoREPA introduces a representation alignment method to inject physics understanding from video foundation models into pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models. This approach, which does not require external physics simulators or specialized datasets, improved the Physical Commonsense (PC) score of a CogVideoX-5B baseline by 24.1% on the VideoPhy benchmark, reaching 40.1.

## Problem

- Text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models often generate videos lacking physical plausibility, exhibiting inconsistencies in object behavior and motion dynamics.
- Current T2V models possess significantly weaker implicit physics understanding compared to video foundation models (VFMs) like VideoMAEv2.
- This gap results in issues such as unrealistic object deformation, irrational movements, and implausible interactions.

## Method

- Proposes **VideoREPA**, a representation alignment method for injecting physics understanding from video foundation models (VFMs) into pre-trained text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models.
- Employs a Token Relation Distillation (TRD) loss that aligns relational patterns (spatial and temporal) between tokens from VFMs and T2V models, providing soft guidance.
- The method finetunes pre-trained ==latent diffusion== models by combining the standard diffusion loss with the TRD loss, without requiring external physics simulators or specialized datasets.

## Results

- VideoREPA-5B achieved a Physical Commonsense (PC) score of 40.1 on the VideoPhy benchmark, marking a 24.1% improvement over the CogVideoX-5B baseline (32.3).
- Showed improved physical consistency across interaction types, including 42.9% for Solid-Solid, 16.7% for Solid-Fluid, and 20.6% for Fluid-Fluid interactions.
- Surpassed other physics-aware methods on open-domain datasets and improved the PC score by 4.57 points on the VideoPhy2 benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enhanced physics understanding directly improves the generation quality and physical consistency of videos produced by T2V models.
> - Aligning relational structures between tokens through "soft" guidance (Token Relation Distillation) is effective for knowledge transfer to pre-trained diffusion models without degrading their existing capabilities.
> - Video foundation models possess implicit physics understanding that can be distilled into generative models, bridging the gap between generation and comprehension.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Text-to-video (T2V) generation has achieved remarkable progress with diffusion models producing high-fidelity videos from textual descriptions. However, these models struggle with a fundamental limitation: generating physically plausible content that adheres to real-world physics laws. Current T2V models like CogVideoX and HunyuanVideo can create visually impressive videos but often exhibit inconsistencies in object behavior, motion dynamics, and physical interactions.

*The VideoREPA framework transfers physics understanding from video foundation models to text-to-video diffusion models through spatio-temporal relational alignment.*

VideoREPA addresses this challenge by proposing the first representation alignment method specifically designed for injecting physics knowledge into pre-trained video diffusion models. Rather than requiring external physics simulators or specialized datasets, VideoREPA leverages the implicit physics understanding captured by video foundation models (VFMs) and transfers this knowledge to generative models through a sophisticated alignment process.

### 2. The Physics Understanding Gap

The authors identified a critical disparity between generative capabilities and physics understanding in current T2V models. While models like CogVideoX excel at producing realistic-looking videos, they possess significantly weaker physics comprehension compared to much smaller video self-supervised learning models like VideoMAEv2.

This gap manifests in several ways:
- **Object Shape Irregularity**: Generated objects may deform unrealistically
- **Motion Irrationality**: Movements that violate basic physics principles
- **Implausible Interactions**: Objects behaving inconsistently during contact or collision

The research builds on the principle that enhanced understanding leads to improved generation quality. By bridging this physics understanding gap, VideoREPA aims to enable T2V models to generate videos that are both visually appealing and physically consistent.

### 3. VideoREPA Methodology

VideoREPA operates by finetuning pre-trained latent diffusion models through a specialized alignment process. The method addresses key limitations of existing representation alignment (REPA) techniques when applied to video generation:

**Challenges with Standard REPA:**
1. **Spatial vs. Spatio-Temporal Focus**: Traditional REPA aligns spatial features in static images, insufficient for capturing temporal dynamics crucial for physics
2. **Application Context Mismatch**: REPA was designed for accelerating from-scratch training, not knowledge transfer in finetuning
3. **Hard Alignment Issues**: Direct feature similarity losses can destabilize well-established representations in pre-trained models
4. **Temporal Compression Complexity**: Video diffusion models use significant temporal compression, complicating alignment

**VideoREPA's Solution:**
Instead of direct feature alignment, VideoREPA aligns the relational structure between tokens, providing "soft" guidance more suitable for finetuning established models. This approach captures both within-frame coherence and cross-frame dynamics essential for physics understanding.

### 4. Token Relation Distillation Loss

The core innovation of VideoREPA lies in its Token Relation Distillation (TRD) loss, which aligns relational patterns rather than raw features between the video foundation model and the video diffusion model.

**TRD Loss Components:**

1. **Spatial Relations**: For each frame, cosine similarities are computed between all pairs of spatial tokens within that frame for both VFM features and VDM features. This captures within-frame object coherence and shape regularity.

2. **Temporal Relations**: Cross-frame cosine similarities are computed between tokens in one frame and tokens in other frames, capturing inter-frame dynamics and motion rationality.

The TRD loss is formulated as:

```
L_TRD = L1_distance(S_spatial_VFM, S_spatial_VDM) + L1_distance(S_temporal_VFM, S_temporal_VDM)
```

where S represents the similarity matrices derived from VFM and VDM features.

The final training objective combines standard diffusion loss with TRD:

```
L_total = L_diff + λ * L_TRD
```

**Implementation Details:**
- VFM features and VDM latents often have different dimensions due to varying compression ratios
- The method interpolates VDM latent dimensions to match VFM feature dimensions
- Video frames are processed at uniformly lower resolution for VFM feature extraction to manage computational load
- The first encoded frame is excluded from alignment to prioritize dynamic content

### 5. Experimental Results

VideoREPA demonstrates substantial improvements in physical plausibility across multiple benchmarks:

**VideoPhy Benchmark Results:**
- VideoREPA-5B achieved a Physical Commonsense (PC) score of **40.1**, representing a **24.1% improvement** over the CogVideoX-5B baseline (32.3)
- Improvements across interaction types: 42.9% for Solid-Solid, 16.7% for Solid-Fluid, and 20.6% for Fluid-Fluid interactions
- Outperformed WISA, a specialized physics-aware method, on open-domain datasets

**VideoPhy2 Benchmark Results:**
- VideoREPA-2B showed a significant improvement of **4.57 points** in PC score over its baseline

*Qualitative comparisons showing VideoREPA generating more physically consistent videos compared to baseline methods across various scenarios.*

### 6. Key Findings and Ablations

The research includes comprehensive ablation studies that provide crucial insights:

**TRD Loss Components:**
Both spatial and temporal components of the TRD loss proved essential. Removing either component led to degraded performance, confirming their importance for capturing both within-frame coherence and inter-frame dynamics.

*Performance varies with alignment depth, with optimal results at 18 layers.*

**Standard REPA vs. TRD:**
Direct application of standard REPA loss resulted in significant degradation of video quality, strongly supporting the need for TRD's "soft" relational guidance approach.

*The TRD loss weight (λ) of 0.5 provides optimal balance between physics understanding and generation quality.*

**Optimal Configuration:**
- Alignment depth: 18 layers
- TRD loss weight (λ): 0.5
- VFM choice: VideoMAEv2 performed best as alignment target
- Dimension strategy: Interpolating VDM dimensions to match VFM features

### 7. Implications and Future Work

VideoREPA represents a significant advancement in physics-aware video generation with several important implications:

**Scientific Contributions:**
- First REPA method designed for finetuning video diffusion models to inject specific physics knowledge
- Demonstrates the principle that enhanced understanding directly improves generation quality
- Provides a framework for knowledge transfer between foundation models without requiring specialized datasets

**Practical Impact:**
- Enhanced realism makes T2V models more suitable for applications in film, animation, and virtual environments
- Reduced reliance on physics simulators or specialized datasets broadens applicability
- Improved physical consistency is crucial for training data generation in robotics and autonomous systems

**Future Directions:**
The success of VideoREPA in finetuning suggests exciting possibilities for integrating physics-knowledge distillation during pre-training of next-generation video foundation models. While computationally challenging currently, this approach could lead to inherently more physics-aware video generation models.

The method also opens avenues for extending relational alignment to other forms of knowledge transfer, such as enhancing understanding of social dynamics, temporal causality, or domain-specific expertise in generative models.

VideoREPA establishes a new paradigm for improving generative models by leveraging the complementary strengths of understanding and generation capabilities across different foundation models, paving the way for more sophisticated and reliable AI-generated content.
