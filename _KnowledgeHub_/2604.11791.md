# A Mechanistic Analysis of Looped Reasoning Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This mechanistic analysis investigates how looped reasoning language models organize computation, finding that individual layers converge to distinct latent fixed points and attention patterns stabilize. The study demonstrates that these models spontaneously organize into "stages of inference" that mirror those of feedforward Transformers, even when trained from scratch, offering insights into the underlying computational dynamics.

## Problem

- Despite empirical success, the internal mechanisms distinguishing looped reasoning language models from standard feedforward architectures remain largely unexplored.
- A lack of detailed analysis exists on how looped models organize computation across (effective) depth, particularly concerning the observed "stages of inference."
- The influence of architectural choices (e.g., recurrent block size, input injection, normalization) on the dynamics and stability of looped models is not well understood.

## Method

- Conducted a ==mechanistic analysis== of pretrained (**Ouro**, **Huginn**, **Retrofitted Llama**) and custom-trained **looped Transformer** models, focusing on ==cyclic recurrence== and ==input injection==.
- Proposed and empirically validated theoretical propositions concerning ==cyclic fixed points== in latent states and the ==stabilization of attention patterns== within recurrent blocks.
- Analyzed the emergence and stability of ==stages of inference== using metrics like ==ColSum Concentration==, comparing them against feedforward models and across different architectural configurations.

## Results

- Huginn-0125 and retrofitted Llama rapidly converged to distinct cyclic fixed points in their residual streams, which led to consistent attention patterns across recurrences.
- Many looped models, including those trained from scratch, spontaneously organized into distinct, repeating stages of inference mirroring feedforward computations.
- Models exhibiting stable fixed-point convergence maintained consistent inference stages when evaluated with significantly higher, unseen numbers of test-time recurrences, while non-converging models showed unstable behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Looped models often exhibit cyclic fixed-point behavior where each layer within a recurrent block converges to a distinct latent fixed point, leading to stable and repeating attention patterns.
> - Recurrent blocks learn and repeat distinct "stages of inference" that closely resemble those found in feedforward Transformers, suggesting these stages are fundamental for effective language modeling.
> - Architectural factors like input injection robustly encourage stable fixed-point convergence, while specific normalization strategies can either hinder (Ouro) or prevent (Huginn) the formation of certain inference stages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) predominantly rely on the Transformer architecture, which processes information sequentially in a feedforward manner. As LLM capabilities have advanced, considerable effort has been directed towards enhancing their reasoning abilities. Initial approaches included "chain-of-thought" (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning, exemplified by the DeepSeek-R1 architecture (Guo et al., 2025).

More recently, architectural innovations have sought to integrate reasoning capabilities directly into the model structure, notably through "recurrent looping" of LLM layers in the latent dimension. This approach, which re-applies layers to repeatedly act on latent states, echoes earlier designs such as Adaptive Computation Time (Graves, 2016) and has been explored in models like Huginn-0125 (Geiping et al., 2025), Ouro (Zhu et al., 2025), and TRM (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025). Such "looped reasoning language models" have demonstrated empirical success, but the underlying mechanisms distinguishing their internal dynamics from standard feedforward models remain largely unexplored.

This paper contributes to the mechanistic interpretability of LLMs by investigating these looped architectures. It builds upon previous observations that weight-tied Transformer models can converge to fixed points or steady states with repeated iterations (Bai et al., 2019; Anil et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023). It also connects to the concept of "stages of inference" (Lad et al., 2024; Queipo-de Llano et al., 2025), which describes distinct computational phases emerging at different depths in feedforward LLMs, characterized by attention head behavior and information mixing. While some prior work has analyzed latent dynamics in recurrent Transformers (Pappone et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2025), this paper offers a detailed internal analysis of single, deeper cyclic blocks, examining how individual layers within these loops behave. By focusing on the convergence of latent states and attention patterns, this research aims to bridge the gap between empirical performance and a fundamental understanding of how looped architectures process information. The work also situates itself within the broader context of adaptive test-time computation, where models adjust their computational effort based on input complexity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a mechanistic analysis of the latent states within looped language models. Specifically, the study aims to investigate how the internal dynamics of these models differ from or mirror those of standard feedforward architectures, with a particular focus on the "stages of inference" observed in both model types.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that despite the promising empirical performance of looped reasoning language models, the mechanisms underpinning their operation are poorly understood. There is a lack of detailed analysis concerning how these models organize computation across (effective) depth, particularly in comparison to feedforward models.

The detailed objectives include:

- **Analyzing Cyclic Recurrence:** To investigate the behavior of repeatedly applied recurrent blocks and demonstrate whether each layer within a cycle converges to a distinct fixed point, leading to a consistent cyclic trajectory in the latent space.
- **Stabilization of Attention Patterns:** To provide empirical evidence that as these fixed points are reached, the behavior of attention heads stabilizes, resulting in consistent attention patterns across recurrences.
- **Comparison of Inference Stages:** To determine if recurrent blocks learn and repeat stages of inference that closely mirror those observed in feedforward models, but organized cyclically in depth with each iteration.
- **Investigating Architectural Influences:** To study how specific architectural choices, such as recurrent block size, the use of input injection, and normalization strategies, influence the emergence and stability of the observed cyclic fixed points and stages of inference.
- **Translating Insights to Design:** Ultimately, the work aims to generate mechanistic insights that can inform and guide the practical design of more efficient and effective looped language model architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted analytical approach, combining theoretical propositions with extensive empirical validation across various pretrained and custom-trained looped Transformer models.

#### 3.1. Model Definition and Nomenclature

The study focuses on "cyclic recurrence," where a fixed sequence of Transformer blocks (a "k-stacked block" S_k) is reapplied multiple times. This forms a "(k ⊗ l)-Recurrent block" R_l,k, signifying l repetitions of a k-stacked block. The concept of "input injection" is introduced, where an additional input Z (typically initialized from a Normal distribution) is projected and combined with the original input X at the start of each stack. The overall architecture is described by a tuple (p, k, c), representing prelude layers (p), recurrent layers (k), and coda layers (c), with an optional subscript `I` for input injection, and `l` for the number of recurrences.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Framework for Fixed Points

The paper establishes two theoretical propositions:
- **Proposition 4.1 (Cyclic recurrent blocks reach cyclic fixed points):** If a k-recurrent block reaches a fixed point for its entire output, then any cyclic permutation of the blocks within it will also have reached a fixed point. This implies a consistent cyclic trajectory rather than convergence to a single state for all layers.
- **Proposition 4.2 (Recurrent attention patterns change slowly under state convergence):** If the residual stream between successive recurrences of a layer is bounded and converging, then the attention patterns of that layer will also converge, given tied weights and bounded inputs. This links latent state convergence to stable attention dynamics.

#### 3.3. Empirical Validation of Fixed-Point Behavior

- **Pretrained Models:** The analysis is conducted on three pretrained looped LLMs: Ouro 1.4B (Zhu et al., 2025), Huginn-0125 (Geiping et al., 2025), and Retrofitted Llama (McLeish et al., 2025). These models exhibit different architectural designs and training strategies (Table 1, Table 2 in appendix).
- **Data:** Experiments are primarily conducted on a random subset of 256 examples from the GSM8k test set (Cobbe et al., 2021), a reasoning dataset. Additional validation on the non-reasoning HellaSwag dataset (Zellers et al., 2019) is performed in the appendix.
- **Metrics for Convergence:**
 - **Residual Stream Differences:** The Frobenius norm of the difference between the residual stream after successive recurrences of the same layer is measured to assess convergence.
 - **Fixed Point Analysis:** An "approximate fixed point" for each layer is determined by running models for 128 recurrences. The norm of the difference and cosine similarity between the current layer's output and its corresponding approximate fixed point are calculated.
 - **Attention Pattern Similarity:** The Frobenius norm between attention matrices at different depths (including across recurrences) is computed and visualized to show cyclic similarity. Cosine similarity of residual streams is also used.
 - **Latent Space Trajectories:** Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to hidden states of the final sequence position across recurrences to visualize trajectories in latent space (e.g., Fig. 1, 5).

#### 3.4. Impact of Architectural Choices

To isolate the influence of architectural elements on fixed-point emergence and stability, experiments are conducted on randomly initialized, small-scale models.
- **Variables:** The study investigates the impact of:
 - **Recurrent Block Size:** Models with 4, 12, and 16 layers.
 - **Input Injection:** Presence or absence of input injection.
 - **Normalization Structure:** Different normalization schemes, including pre-norm (used in retrofitted models), and those analogous to Huginn-0125 and Ouro models.
- **Metrics:** Cosine similarity of residual streams to the approximate fixed point (both the same layer's fixed point and the fixed point with the lowest similarity to the first layer's).

#### 3.5. Stages of Inference Analysis

- **Primary Metric:** **ColSum Concentration (C):** A metric (Queipo-de Llano et al., 2025) derived from the normalized entropy of the column sums of the attention matrix. High C indicates high concentration of attention mass.
- **Additional Metrics (Appendix):**
 - **Sink Rate:** Fraction of attention heads focusing heavily on a "sink" token (e.g., BOS token).
 - **Mixing Score:** Average row entropy of attention matrices, indicating how widely information is mixed.
 - **Residual Entropy:** Matrix-based entropy of the residual stream, reflecting information compression.
 - **Prediction and Suppression Neurons:** Fraction of universal neurons (Gurnee et al., 2024) that predict or suppress values.
- **Visualization:** Metrics are plotted against "recurrent position" (total effective depth) and "% Block Depth" (relative depth within a single recurrent block), allowing comparison across recurrences and with feedforward baselines.
- **Self-Organization Study:** To assess if stages of inference are emergent or training-induced, small-scale Looped Transformers are pre-trained from scratch with minimal biases (constant recurrence schedule of 4, standard cross-entropy loss only on the final latent state, pre-norm).
- **Stability to Unseen Recurrences:** Models are evaluated with a significantly higher number of recurrences (128) than their training regime to observe the stability of stages of inference in an extrapolation setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The mechanistic analysis revealed several insights into the internal dynamics of looped reasoning language models, particularly concerning their convergence behavior and the organization of inference stages.

#### 4.1. Cyclic Fixed-Point Behavior

- **Convergence to Distinct Fixed Points:** Many looped language models, including Huginn-0125 and retrofitted Llama, demonstrated a tendency towards cyclic fixed-point behavior. Each layer within the recurrent block was observed to converge to a distinct fixed point in the latent space. Consequently, the recurrent block as a whole traced out a consistent cyclic trajectory rather than collapsing to a single, identical state across all layers (Fig. 1, 5).
- **Stabilization of Attention Patterns:** This convergence of latent states was found to imply that attention-head behavior also stabilized. Empirical results showed that the Frobenius norm of attention matrices between successive recurrences, particularly for the same layer, decreased rapidly (Fig. 2, 21), indicating that attention patterns became consistent across iterations.
- **Differential Convergence:** While models like Huginn-0125 and retrofitted Llama rapidly converged to fixed points in their residual streams (Fig. 4, 22), Ouro 1.4B did not exhibit strict fixed-point convergence, even though the differences between successive residual stream states became small (Fig. 3, 24). This suggested distinct limiting behaviors depending on architectural choices.
- **Rarity of Non-Fixed-Point Behavior:** Although phenomena like "orbits" and "sliders" (non-fixed-point limiting behaviors) have been noted in prior work, this study found them to be rare in practice for most tokens and prompts (Table 3, 4 in appendix C). Even when such behaviors were present, they did not significantly disrupt the observed stages of inference (Fig. 16, 17, 18 in appendix C).

#### 4.2. Impact of Architecture Choices

- **Role of Input Injection:** Input injection was empirically confirmed to encourage stable fixed-point convergence across various normalization types (Fig. 6, 29, 30, 31). Models utilizing input injection (e.g., Huginn-0125, retrofitted models) showed faster and more robust convergence to fixed points.
- **Normalization Influence:** The type of normalization significantly influenced fixed-point behavior. Pre-normalization, without input injection, could lead to a "degenerate" fixed point where all layers converged to the same state. The specific normalization structure of Ouro (normalizing attention/MLP outputs but only residual stream at the end of the block) appeared to hinder strict fixed-point convergence, even with input injection. Conversely, Huginn-0125's normalization, which repeatedly normalizes the residual stream, prevented the magnitude growth necessary for certain inference stages.

#### 4.3. Stages of Inference in Looped Models Mirror Feedforward Computation

- **Cyclic Repetition of Inference Stages:** The study demonstrated that recurrent blocks in many looped models learn and repeat distinct "stages of inference" that closely resemble those found in feedforward Transformers. Within each loop, metrics like ColSum concentration followed a characteristic pattern, which was then replicated in subsequent loops (Fig. 8, 34, 37, 38, 39). Individual layers, after an initial transient phase, quickly converged to constant behavior, and their cyclic application created these repeating inference stages (Fig. 7).
- **Emergence Across Training Regimes:** This mirroring of feedforward stages was observed even in models trained from scratch with recurrence (e.g., Ouro 1.4B adopting Llama-like stages), and retrofitted models faithfully reproduced the stages of their base architectures.
- **Normalization and Stage Formation:** Huginn-0125 was an exception, not demonstrating clear stages of inference (Fig. 36). This was attributed to its normalization strategy preventing the growth in residual stream magnitude, which is associated with "compression behavior" and the formation of attention sinks and specific inference stages in feedforward models (Fig. 9, 41).
- **Self-Organization:** Small-scale Looped Transformers, pre-trained from scratch with minimal biases towards feedforward stages (e.g., constant recurrence, loss only on the final latent state), also spontaneously organized into multiple distinct mixing stages in recurrent depth (Fig. 10, 54, 55, 56). This suggested that these inference stages are beneficial for language modeling and naturally emerge during training.

#### 4.4. Stability to Unseen Numbers of Recurrences

- **Fixed-Point Stability:** Models that achieved stable fixed-point convergence (e.g., retrofitted Llama, Huginn-0125) exhibited consistent stages of inference for an arbitrary, unseen number of test-time recurrences (Fig. 11, 12, 48, 49, 51, 52, 53). Individual layer behavior quickly stabilized and remained constant.
- **Unstable Stages in Non-Fixed-Point Models:** Conversely, models that did not converge to a strict fixed point (e.g., Ouro 1.4B) demonstrated unstable stages of inference when evaluated beyond their training recurrence depth. Individual layers in these models continued to change throughout later recurrences (Fig. 11, 12, 47, 50).
- **Correlation with Generalization:** Existing research suggests that this stability correlates with out-of-domain performance, where fixed-point converging models tend to avoid performance degradation when extrapolating to higher recurrence depths.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a mechanistic analysis of looped reasoning language models, offering insights into their internal computational organization and contributing to the understanding of how these architectures process information.

**Bridging Mechanistic Insights and Architectural Design:** The findings establish that recurrent blocks in many looped models tend to mirror the stages of inference observed in feedforward Transformers. This connection means that insights gained from the well-studied feedforward setting can potentially be translated to looped models. This could offer actionable pathways for architectural design, such as:
- **Efficient Attention Sparsification:** Tailoring attention sparsification strategies based on the predictable stages of inference within each recurrent block.
- **Leaner Parameterization:** Optimizing the parameterization of middle-stage MLPs, particularly in stages where representations are consistently compressed and low-rank, potentially reducing model size without significant performance loss.

**Novel Perspective on Stages of Inference:** Looped architectures decouple functional depth from the number of unique parameters. This provides a novel lens for investigating why stages of inference form in LLMs. The study challenges previous suggestions that these stages primarily exist to mitigate the "harms" of Transformer depth. Instead, it shows that looped models develop these same beneficial stages while simultaneously improving performance with greater recurrent depth, implying these stages are fundamental for effective language modeling.

**Emergent Behavior in Training:** The discovery that stages of inference can self-organize during training, even without explicit biases towards feedforward patterns, suggests that these computational patterns are beneficial and naturally emerge from the optimization process for language modeling tasks.

**Guiding Stability in Adaptive Computation:** The identification of architectural factors (like input injection and specific normalization structures) that promote stable fixed-point convergence provides practical guidance for designing robust looped models that can generalize effectively to unseen test-time recurrences without performance degradation. This is crucial for models that aim to adaptively scale computation based on task difficulty.

**Limitations:** The study's focus is exclusively on cyclic recurrence, meaning its analysis does not extend to alternative recurrent architectures with multiple separate recurrent blocks. While empirical evidence for architectural influences on stability is provided, a deeper analytical explanation for *why* these factors lead to stable limiting behavior is not fully established. Additionally, the research does not definitively determine whether stable limiting behavior is universally desirable or might, in some contexts, be restrictive for complex reasoning tasks.
