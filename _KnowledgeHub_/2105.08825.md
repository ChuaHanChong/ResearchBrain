# Multi-Person Extreme Motion Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Collaborative motion prediction forecasts the future 3D poses of two closely interacting people. The method runs two person-specific predictors built on temporal attention plus a graph convolutional decoder, linked by a Cross-Interaction Attention module that refines each person's attention keys and values with the partner's motion. Trained on the new ExPI Lindy Hop dataset, it beats single-person predictors by 10-40% short term.

## Problem

- Existing 3D motion predictors treat every pose sequence as isolated, forecasting one person's future solely from their own past and ignoring correlated partner motion.
- Public multi-person pose datasets contain only weak interactions such as talking, shaking hands or walking together, or pseudo 3D annotations, so strong coupling stays untrained.
- Context-aware forecasting work models human-object or social scene cues in 2D or weak-interaction settings, leaving highly synchronized whole-skeleton coupling between two partners unexplored.

## Method

- **Cross-Interaction Attention (XIA)**: a multi-head ==attention== block queried by the partner's vector, taking the person's own vector as key and value, with skip connection and fully connected layers.
- Two parallel person-specific pipelines using MLP temporal attention over past sub-sequences with ==DCT==-encoded values feeding a ==graph convolutional== predictor; XIA refines keys and values of both branches.
- **ExPI** dataset and protocol: 115 Lindy Hop sequences, 30K frames, 3D poses and textured meshes, leader-centred pose normalization, three data splits, and the JME and AME metrics.

## Results

- On the common-action split, average JME reaches **55**/**112**/**162**/**238** mm at 0.2/0.4/0.6/1.0 seconds, versus **62**/**126**/**177**/**251** mm for the strongest single-person baseline.
- Gains over four state-of-the-art single-person predictors reach **10-40%** for horizons up to 500 ms and **5-30%** for 500-1000 ms, concentrated on limb joints.
- Ablation: refining keys and values yields **238 mm** at one second against **249 mm** without XIA; unseen-action split average JME of **121 mm** at 0.4 seconds beats all baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Partner motion is a strong predictive signal in tightly coupled activity, and conditioning attention keys and values on it beats naively mixing or concatenating two skeletons.
> - Improvements concentrate in limbs and airborne feet where interaction physically happens, so whole-body average metrics can hide where a coupling mechanism actually helps.
> - Training one model across diverse actions regularizes better than action-specific models, while lab-captured extreme-interaction data stays scarce and horizons beyond two seconds remain open.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion prediction generally focuses on forecasting future 3D poses given a sequence of past 3D skeletons. Prior research in this area has primarily concentrated on single individuals, treating each person's motion as an isolated entity. These methods often employ recurrent neural networks (RNNs) such as LSTMs and their variants, sometimes incorporating residual connections to model velocities. More recent advancements have utilized graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to capture spatial connectivity among human joints, often combined with attention mechanisms to weigh historical information. Transformer-based architectures and generative models (e.g., VAEs, GANs) have also been explored to address the sequential and stochastic nature of human motion, aiming for diverse and plausible future predictions.

However, a notable gap exists in the study of motion prediction for multiple interacting individuals, particularly in scenarios involving strong, correlated movements. While some works have explored contextual information in human interaction for tasks like 3D human pose estimation or human trajectory prediction (e.g., using social pooling or graph attention networks for pedestrian forecasting), these often deal with weaker interactions or focus on a single center point rather than the dynamics of the full human skeleton in 3D space. Existing multi-person 3D datasets, such as 3DPW or CMU-Mocap, typically feature weak human interactions like walking together or shaking hands, which do not fully represent highly collaborative or physically intertwined actions. Furthermore, datasets suitable for studying extreme poses in an interactive context have been largely unavailable or not publicly released.

This research addresses this specific void by shifting the focus from isolated single-person motion to collaborative multi-person motion, explicitly accounting for strong human-to-human interactions. It introduces a novel dataset designed for such scenarios and proposes a methodology to exploit inter-person dependencies, thus expanding the scope of human motion prediction to more complex and realistic interactive settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of 3D human motion prediction by addressing the limitations of existing methods, which predominantly focus on single individuals in isolation. The authors aim to introduce and provide solutions for the novel task of **collaborative motion prediction**, specifically for two persons engaged in highly interactive tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:

- **Inherent Interaction in Real-World Scenarios:** In many real-world situations, humans do not act in isolation. Their movements are frequently dependent on or correlated with the movements of others. Current motion prediction models, by treating each person independently, overlook this crucial interaction information, potentially limiting their predictive accuracy in collaborative contexts.
- **Potential for Improved Accuracy through Interaction:** The hypothesis is that by explicitly modeling the mutual influence and cross-dependencies between interacting individuals' pose sequences, the accuracy of future motion predictions can be significantly improved. This is particularly relevant in activities requiring high levels of synchronization and cooperation, such as team sports or collaborative assembly tasks.
- **Lack of Suitable Datasets:** A significant obstacle to researching multi-person interactive motion prediction has been the absence of appropriate datasets. Existing datasets either focus on single persons, or if they include multiple persons, the interactions are typically weak or mild, not representative of highly correlated and physically demanding actions. Furthermore, there was a recognized need for datasets featuring "extreme poses," which are challenging for current models and often arise in dynamic interactive scenarios.
- **Establishing a Baseline for a New Task:** By introducing this novel problem formulation, the authors also aim to provide a foundational benchmark (including a dataset, evaluation metrics, and a baseline method) to stimulate further research and development in collaborative motion prediction.

To achieve these objectives, the research outlines several key contributions:

- **Defining Collaborative Motion Prediction:** Formalizing the task of jointly predicting the future motion of two strongly interacted persons.
- **Developing the ExPI Dataset:** Creating and publicly releasing "Extreme Pose Interaction (ExPI) Dataset," a new lab-based dataset of professional dancers performing highly interactive Lindy Hop aerial steps, annotated with 3D body poses and shapes. This dataset is designed to capture extreme poses and close cooperation.
- **Proposing the Cross-Interaction Attention (XIA) Module:** Introducing a novel neural network module that leverages historical motion information from both individuals to learn and predict cross-dependencies between their pose sequences.
- **Establishing Evaluation Protocols:** Defining standardized train/test splits and two new evaluation metrics (Joint Mean Error and Aligned Mean Error) specifically tailored for the collaborative motion prediction task.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between existing single-person motion prediction and the more complex reality of human interaction, driven by the belief that inter-person dynamics provide valuable predictive signals, and enabled by the creation of a specialized dataset.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a framework for collaborative motion prediction, which involves jointly forecasting the future 3D poses of two interacting individuals.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The standard single-person motion prediction task is defined as learning a mapping $M : P_{t_I:t-1} \rightarrow P_{t:t_E}$, where $P_{t_I:t-1}$ represents past 3D poses and $P_{t:t_E}$ are the future poses. This work extends this to collaborative motion prediction for two persons, denoted as `l` (leader) and `f` (follower). The task becomes learning a mapping $M_C : P^l_{t_I:t-1}, P^f_{t_I:t-1} \rightarrow P^l_{t:t_E}, P^f_{t:t_E}$, aiming to predict future motions for both individuals by exploiting their interaction. A joint pose $P^c_t = [P^l_t, P^f_t]$ is used to represent the couple's pose at time $t$.

#### 3.2. The Extreme Pose Interaction (ExPI) Dataset

To facilitate research on collaborative motion prediction, the authors collected and released the ExPI dataset, specifically tailored for highly interactive and extreme motions.

- **Content:** ExPI contains Lindy Hop dancing actions performed by two professional couples. It features 16 distinct actions, 7 of which are common to both couples and 9 are couple-specific. The dataset comprises 115 sequences with 30,000 visual frames and 60,000 3D instances.
- **Data Collection:** Data was captured in a multi-camera motion capture studio equipped with 68 synchronized color cameras and 20 Mocap cameras.
- **Annotation:** For each frame, 3D position of 18 joints per person (total 36 keypoints for the couple) and 3D shapes as textured meshes are provided.
- **Post-processing:** A critical step involved manual labeling of missing Mocap points due to occlusions, which is a common issue in interactive, lab-based Mocap datasets involving extreme poses. A 3D hand-labeling toolbox was developed to project 2D labels from orthogonal views back into 3D space, approximating the missing joint positions.
- **Data Analysis:**
 - **Diversity:** ExPI exhibits higher pose diversity compared to H3.6M. At a tolerance of 50mm, ExPI has 52% distinct poses, while H3.6M has 24%.
 - **Extremeness:** The dataset features significantly more "extreme poses" than H3.6M, as measured by the standard deviation of joint coordinates over time. This indicates a higher range of motion and dynamic movements.

#### 3.3. Data Splits

Three distinct data splits were defined to evaluate different aspects of model generalization:

- **Common Action Split:** Trains on common actions from one couple and tests on the same actions performed by a different couple.
- **Single Action Split:** Trains and tests action-specific models (e.g., trains on a specific action from Couple 2, tests on the same action from Couple 1).
- **Unseen Action Split:** Trains on all common actions from both couples and tests on the couple-specific actions (unseen during training).

#### 3.4. Proposed Method: Cross-Interaction Attention (XIA) Network

The proposed method is built upon a baseline architecture inspired by existing single-person motion prediction models (specifically, Hisrep), but augmented with a novel **Cross-Interaction Attention (XIA) module** to integrate inter-person information.

- **Pipeline Overview:** Two parallel motion prediction pipelines are maintained, one for the leader and one for the follower. Each pipeline uses an attention model for temporal attention (identifying relevant past sub-sequences) and a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) for spatial attention (modeling joint dependencies).
- **Temporal Attention Component:** For each person, a query `Q` is learned from the most recent observation. Keys `K_i` are learned from starting chunks of past sub-sequences, and values `V_i` are derived from Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) representations of longer past sub-sequences.
- **Cross-Interaction Attention (XIA) Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To enable information sharing between the leader's and follower's prediction processes.
 - **Mechanism:** XIA is based on a standard multi-head attention mechanism. For a given person's key or value (`w`), the XIA module takes `w` and the corresponding key/value from the interacted person (`w_int`) as inputs. It uses `w_int` as the query and `w` as both the key and value for the multi-head attention. This process generates a refined vector `~w = FC(MHA(w_int, w, w) + w)`, effectively allowing one person's motion information to contextualize and refine the other's.
 - **Integration:** XIA modules are strategically placed to refine the keys and values (`~K^l_i = XIA(K^f_i, K^l_i)` and `~V^l_i = XIA(V^f_i, V^l_i)`, and vice-versa for the follower) before they are used in the temporal attention mechanism.
- **Pose Normalization for Prediction:** Raw poses in world coordinates are normalized to a common coordinate system based on the leader's root joint (mid-hips). The leader's root defines the origin, the root-left hip vector defines the x-axis, and the leader's neck determines the XOZ plane. This normalization preserves the relative positions and orientations of the two persons, allowing for direct computation of pose errors in this consistent frame.
- **Training:** The model is trained for 25 epochs. The loss function is a weighted sum of the follower's and leader's MPJPE loss over 10 predicted frames: $L = L_f + 10^{-e} L_l$, where `e` is the epoch number. This exponential down-weighting of the leader's loss compensates for its faster convergence due to being the reference for normalization. Predicted motions are recursively fed back as input for longer prediction horizons.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics

Two metrics are proposed, both based on the Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE), to evaluate multi-person motion prediction:

- **Joint Mean Error (JME):** $JME(P, G) = MPJPE(P, G)$. This metric computes the MPJPE between the normalized predicted poses `P` and ground truth `G` of both persons. JME provides an overall error measure, considering both the accuracy of individual poses and their relative spatial relationship.
- **Aligned Mean Error (AME):** $AME(P, G) = MPJPE(T_A(\hat{P}, \hat{G}), \hat{G})$. Here, `P` and `G` are first independently normalized for each person (`^P`, `^G`) to remove individual positional bias. Then, a Procrustes analysis is performed to find the best rigid alignment $T_A$ between the combined estimated pose and ground truth. AME measures the pure pose error after minimizing the alignment error, providing insight into the shape accuracy irrespective of rigid body transformations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the proposed Cross-Interaction Attention (XIA) network was conducted on the ExPI dataset, comparing its performance against four state-of-the-art single-person motion prediction methods (Res-RNN, LTD, Hisrep, MSR) adapted for multi-person scenarios by training separate models for each individual.

#### 4.1. Performance on Common Action Split

- **Superior Accuracy:** The XIA method consistently outperformed all compared single-person baseline methods across virtually all actions, prediction horizons (short-term $\le 500$ ms, long-term $500$ ms to $1000$ ms), and both evaluation metrics (JME and AME).
- **Significant Improvement:** For short-term predictions, XIA achieved accuracy improvements of approximately 10-40% compared to baselines (measured by average JME error). For long-term predictions, the improvement ranged from 5-30%.
- **Joint-wise Analysis:** An analysis of per-joint improvements revealed that XIA yielded substantial reductions in error for limb keypoints (arms and legs) and the follower's feet. This suggests that the cross-interaction attention mechanism is particularly effective at capturing and leveraging the dependencies inherent in movements where physical contact or close coordination of limbs is prevalent, and for highly dynamic joints like the feet during aerial maneuvers.

#### 4.2. Performance on Single Action Split and Unseen Action Split

- **Action-Specific Performance:** When evaluated on the single action split, where models are trained specifically for each action, XIA continued to demonstrate superior performance for the majority of actions, and comparable performance for the remaining ones.
- **Generalization to Unseen Actions:** On the unseen action split, XIA maintained its lead over the baseline methods for most actions. This indicates the robustness and generalization capabilities of the proposed method to actions not encountered during training, suggesting that the learned interaction patterns are transferable to new collaborative movements.
- **Impact of Data Diversity:** The authors observed that performance on the single action split was generally lower than on the common action split. This finding implies that training on a broader range of actions, even if performed by different individuals, contributes to better regularization and improved performance for challenging collaborative extreme motion prediction tasks.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Visual comparisons (e.g., Figure 1 in the paper) illustrate that the poses predicted by the XIA method are perceptibly closer to the ground truth than those predicted by the baseline methods. Notably, XIA demonstrated successful prediction even for highly extreme actions where other methods exhibited significant deviations or outright failures.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Multi-person Baseline Strategy:** The ablation study for adapting single-person models to multi-person scenarios showed that training separate, person-specific models for the leader and follower (`sep`) yielded the best performance compared to mixing their data (`mix`) or concatenating their poses into a single input vector (`cat`). This supports the authors' approach of using two parallel pipelines.
- **Efficacy of XIA Module:** The results clearly demonstrated that integrating the XIA module significantly improved performance over a baseline model trained with parallel person-specific models but without any explicit cross-interaction (`w.o. XIA`). This validates the core hypothesis that exploiting interaction information enhances prediction.
- **XIA Component Contribution:** Further investigation into different configurations of the XIA module revealed that updating both the keys and values of the temporal attention mechanism using XIA (`XIA kv`) produced the most favorable results, suggesting that refining both aspects of historical motion information from the interacting partner is beneficial.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of human motion prediction by addressing a previously under-explored and challenging area: multi-person extreme motion prediction in highly interactive contexts. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Establishment of a Novel Research Task:** By formally introducing and defining "collaborative motion prediction," the paper opens a new avenue for research in understanding and forecasting human movement. This task moves beyond the isolated individual paradigm, bringing motion prediction closer to real-world social and collaborative scenarios.
- **Provision of a Unique Dataset (ExPI):** The creation and public release of the ExPI dataset is a substantial contribution. It addresses a critical data scarcity for studying highly interactive, extreme 3D human poses. Unlike existing datasets with weak interactions, ExPI's focus on professional dancers performing complex Lindy Hop aerials provides rich, dynamic data suitable not only for motion prediction but also for related tasks such as single-frame pose estimation in challenging conditions and multi-view 3D reconstruction of interacting individuals.
- **Methodological Advancement with Cross-Interaction Attention (XIA):** The proposed XIA module offers a concrete mechanism to model cross-dependencies between interacting persons. Its ability to consistently outperform state-of-the-art single-person methods, particularly in highly dynamic limb movements, demonstrates the value of incorporating contextual interaction information. This module can serve as a foundational component for future models aiming to capture complex social dynamics.
- **Establishment of a Performance Baseline:** The work provides a strong initial baseline for the collaborative motion prediction task using the ExPI dataset and defined evaluation metrics. This facilitates future comparative studies and encourages further development of more sophisticated models.
- **Broadening Applications of Motion Prediction:** The insights and methodologies developed in this paper have potential implications for several application domains:
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** Improved prediction of human partner movements could lead to more fluid and safer collaborative robotics.
 - **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Generating more realistic and responsive avatar movements in interactive virtual environments.
 - **Sports Science and Coaching:** Analyzing and predicting complex team movements and partner dynamics in sports like dance or team sports.
 - **Assistive Technologies:** Developing predictive systems that anticipate movements of individuals in shared physical spaces, potentially enhancing safety or assistance.
 - **Computer Animation:** Creating more natural and believable animations for multiple interacting characters.

While the acknowledged limitations concerning the difficulty of collecting large-scale, high-quality extreme pose interaction data for deep learning architectures persist, this research lays essential groundwork by providing both a challenging dataset and an effective baseline method. It highlights the importance of interaction modeling in understanding and predicting human motion in complex, real-world settings.
