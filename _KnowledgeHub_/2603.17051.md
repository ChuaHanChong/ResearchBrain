# Astrolabe: Steering Forward-Process Reinforcement Learning for Distilled Autoregressive Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKUST, JD Explore Academy, and HKU developed Astrolabe, an online reinforcement learning framework for distilled autoregressive video models, enhancing human preference alignment without compromising inference speed. It demonstrated quantitative improvements in visual aesthetics (e.g., Self-Forcing's HPSv3 improved by +1.36) and motion quality across various short and long video generation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Distilled autoregressive video models, while efficient, often generate videos with visual artifacts or unnatural motion due to their distillation objective focusing on matching a teacher model's distribution rather than human preferences.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for generative models are computationally expensive and memory-intensive, particularly those relying on reverse-process optimization and full trajectory storage, making them unsuitable for efficient streaming video models.
- Challenges in applying RL to long video generation include the difficulty of temporal credit assignment and the susceptibility to reward hacking, where models exploit reward functions narrowly.

## Method

- An efficient ==forward-process RL formulation== is introduced in **Astrolabe**, enabling ==trajectory-free== online optimization by defining ==implicit policies==, thus avoiding complex likelihood estimations and the substantial memory overhead of reverse-process RL.
- A ==memory-efficient streaming rollout== strategy uses a ==rolling KV cache== with ==frame sinks== and ==group-wise clip-level sampling== to bound memory usage and enable scalable, long video generation through parallel processing of local chunks.
- A ==multi-reward system== (visual quality, motion quality, text-video alignment) combined with an ==uncertainty-aware selective KL penalty== is implemented to stabilize training, prevent reward hacking, and ensure balanced, high-quality generations.

## Results

- **Astrolabe** consistently improved visual aesthetics (e.g., HPSv3 increased by up to **+1.65** for LongLive) and motion quality (e.g., Motion Quality increased by up to **+0.13** for LongLive) across Self-Forcing, LongLive, and Causal Forcing models, without affecting inference speed.
- The framework effectively extended alignment to long videos (**30–60 seconds**) under single and multi-prompt conditions, with Causal Forcing's HPSv3 increasing from **9.28** to **10.52** for 30-second videos.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the multi-reward formulation to prevent reward hacking, and the effectiveness of the streaming training scheme which reduced memory consumption by approximately **2×** while boosting performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Forward-process RL can effectively align distilled autoregressive video models with human preferences while preserving their inherent efficiency, bypassing the limitations of traditional reverse-process RL methods.
> - A streaming training scheme with a detached historical KV cache enables scalable and memory-efficient reinforcement learning for long video generation, mitigating the 'train-short/test-long' mismatch and temporal credit assignment problems.
> - Robust reward design, including multi-faceted rewards and an adaptive uncertainty-aware regularization strategy, is crucial for preventing reward hacking and achieving stable, balanced improvements in complex generative tasks like video synthesis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has witnessed advancements, particularly with diffusion models, which can synthesize high-quality videos. However, these conventional diffusion models typically suffer from high latency due to multi-step denoising processes and their reliance on bidirectional attention mechanisms that process all frames jointly, precluding real-time streaming generation.

To address these limitations, a paradigm shift has led to the development of distilled autoregressive (AR) video models. These models, exemplified by architectures such as Self-Forcing, LongLive, and Causal Forcing, distill knowledge from pre-trained bidirectional video diffusion models using techniques like distribution matching distillation (DMD). This distillation process enables efficient, streaming inference through KV-caching, supporting the generation of longer videos. While these distilled models achieve efficiency, the distillation objective primarily focuses on mimicking the teacher's output distribution rather than aligning with nuanced human visual preferences. Consequently, their generated outputs frequently exhibit visual artifacts or unnatural motion dynamics.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated efficacy in aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences. This success has motivated its application to generative models, including diffusion models, with approaches like DiffusionDPO, DanceGRPO, and Flow-GRPO. However, these existing RL methods for diffusion models, especially those relying on reverse-process optimization, often require estimating log-probabilities along sampling trajectories. This couples the optimization process to specific solvers and necessitates storing intermediate trajectory states, introducing substantial memory and computational overhead. Such overhead negates the efficiency benefits that distilled streaming video models are designed to provide. Some forward-process RL methods, like DiffusionNFT and WorldCompass, exist for diffusion and AR world models, respectively, but extending them effectively to highly efficient *distilled AR video models* remains an open challenge. Prior reward-guided distillation methods have also been explored but are limited by their passive approach to biasing supervised distillation loss, lacking active exploration or penalties for suboptimal samples.

Astrolabe addresses this specific gap by proposing an RL framework tailored for distilled AR video models, aiming to enhance human preference alignment without incurring the prohibitive computational and memory costs associated with traditional RL applications in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Astrolabe stems from two interconnected challenges in modern video generation:

1. **Misalignment of Distilled Autoregressive Models with Human Preferences**: Distilled autoregressive (AR) video models are designed for efficient, real-time streaming video generation. They achieve this by converting computationally intensive bidirectional diffusion processes into more lightweight autoregressive forms. However, the distillation process itself is optimized to match the distribution of a teacher model, not to directly incorporate human aesthetic or perceptual preferences. As a result, videos generated by these models often contain artifacts, exhibit unnatural motion, or lack overall aesthetic quality from a human perspective. Improving the perceptual quality and temporal consistency of these efficient models is crucial for their practical deployment.

2. **Inefficiency and Incompatibility of Existing RL Frameworks for Distilled AR Video Models**: While reinforcement learning (RL) has proven effective in aligning other generative models (like LLMs) with human preferences, applying existing RL methodologies to distilled AR video models presents significant technical hurdles:
 - **Costly Re-distillation**: One approach to incorporate human feedback might involve re-distilling the model with a reward-biased objective. This is computationally expensive, negating the efficiency gains sought by using distilled models in the first place.
 - **Memory and Computational Overhead of Reverse-Process RL**: Most online RL frameworks for generative models, especially those based on reverse-process optimization, require calculating log-probabilities across entire sampling trajectories. This necessitates storing extensive intermediate states and couples the learning process to specific solvers. For long video generation, where sequences can span many frames, this leads to prohibitive memory consumption and computational overhead, eroding the core advantage of streaming architectures.
 - **Credit Assignment Problem in Long Videos**: Standard sequence-level RL rollouts struggle with temporal credit assignment, where a global reward for a long video makes it difficult to pinpoint specific localized degradations or improvements.
 - **Reward Hacking**: Generative models trained with RL can sometimes learn to exploit narrow aspects of a reward function, leading to outputs that score high numerically but are aesthetically or semantically poor in broader contexts (reward hacking).

Given these challenges, the key objectives of the Astrolabe project were to:
- Develop an **efficient online RL framework** specifically designed for *distilled autoregressive video models*.
- Introduce a **forward-process RL formulation** that avoids the need for expensive reverse-process unrolling or full trajectory storage, thereby preserving the inherent efficiency of streaming architectures.
- Propose a **streaming training scheme** to enable scalable alignment of long videos by performing RL updates on local clip windows while maintaining long-range coherence through conditioning on prior context.
- Integrate mechanisms to **mitigate reward hacking**, ensuring that preference alignment leads to genuinely improved and aesthetically balanced generations rather than narrow optimization.

By achieving these objectives, Astrolabe aims to provide a robust and scalable solution for enhancing the perceptual quality and temporal consistency of efficient video generation models, making them more aligned with human expectations for real-time interactive applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Astrolabe presents an online reinforcement learning framework designed to align distilled autoregressive video models with human preferences. The methodology is structured around three core technical contributions: an efficient forward-process RL formulation, a streaming training scheme for scalability, and a multi-reward system with regularization to prevent reward hacking.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The framework builds upon existing concepts:
- **Autoregressive Video Diffusion Models**: These models factorize the joint video distribution and predict a velocity field conditioned on text and the Key-Value (KV) cache of preceding frames. They mitigate "exposure bias" (mismatch between training and inference contexts) through methods like Self-Forcing.
- **Forward-Process Reinforcement Learning**: Inspired by DiffusionNFT, Astrolabe adapts the principle of applying rewards directly to the forward sampling process. This involves defining implicit positive and negative policies using an interpolation between the current and an old velocity predictor (Equation 1) and optimizing a policy loss that contrasts these against a target forward velocity (Equation 2). This formulation is "trajectory-free," meaning it only requires clean generated samples and avoids the complex likelihood estimations and trajectory storage of reverse-process RL. Astrolabe notably discards the adaptive loss weighting from DiffusionNFT, finding it causes gradient explosion in distilled AR settings with large discretization gaps.

#### 3.2. Memory-Efficient Streaming Rollout

To overcome the memory and temporal credit assignment bottlenecks of standard RL for long videos, Astrolabe introduces a group-wise streaming rollout strategy:
- **Rolling KV Cache with Frame Sinks**: Memory usage is bounded by maintaining a fixed-size visual context window (C_n). This window comprises two components:
 - A **frame sink (S frames)**: A set of permanently retained frames that anchors global semantic context, preventing long-range visual drift.
 - A **rolling window (L frames)**: The most recent frames providing fine-grained local conditioning.
 This design ensures that the KV memory footprint remains constant, irrespective of the total video length, which is crucial for real-time streaming.
- **Clip-level Group-wise Sampling**: Instead of generating multiple independent long trajectories, the visual history is sampled once, and its KV cache is frozen as a shared prefix. At each step, using the memory-efficient KV states of C_n, the model decodes G independent candidate clips in parallel. This approach limits generation overhead to local chunks and shares the frozen context prefix, significantly reducing rollout time and memory requirements during RL.

#### 3.3. Online RL Optimization

The framework's optimization strategy involves:
- **Clip-level Forward-Process RL**: For each generated candidate clip, a composite reward R(x_n^(i), c) is evaluated. An advantage A^(i) is then computed by group-wise mean-centering and normalized to ˜r_i ∈. The model is optimized using the implicit policy loss (Equation 2), where the target forward velocity is derived from a noised version of the candidate clip. This optimization is conditioned on the text prompt and the shared KV cache.
- **Streaming Long Tuning**: To mitigate the "train-short/test-long" mismatch common in AR models, the training explicitly simulates long-sequence inference dynamics. A full forward pass accumulates the KV cache up to the active training window. Critically, the KV cache of all preceding frames is *detached* from the computation graph. This detached cache provides historical context, mimicking the noisy conditions of autoregressive generation, while gradients are only backpropagated through the active window. This method inherently bounds training memory, avoiding backpropagation through extended trajectories.

#### 3.4. Reward Design and Regularization

To prevent reward hacking and stabilize training, Astrolabe employs a structured reward system and regularization:
- **Multi-reward Formulation**: A composite reward is formulated by integrating three distinct quality dimensions:
 - **Visual Quality (VQ)**: Measured by the mean HPSv3 score over the top 30% of frames to mitigate penalties from transient motion blur.
 - **Motion Quality (MQ)**: Evaluated using a pre-trained VideoAlign model on grayscale inputs to focus specifically on temporal consistency rather than texture or color.
 - **Text-Video Alignment (TA)**: Measured by standard RGB VideoAlign to assess the semantic correspondence between the text prompt and the video.
- **Uncertainty-Aware Penalty**: To prevent uniform KL regularization from suppressing high-quality generations, a selective KL penalty is introduced. This penalty targets potential reward hacking by quantifying sample uncertainty based on rank discrepancy between a primary reward model and several auxiliary models (∆^(i)_rank). Samples exhibiting high positive rank discrepancies (indicating likely reward hacking) are masked (M^(i)). The KL penalty (L_KL) is then applied *only* to these masked, high-uncertainty samples. This preserves optimization flexibility for confidently high-quality data.
- **Stabilization**: The `θ_old` policy is updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA). Furthermore, the reference policy `θ_ref` is conditionally reset to the current `θ` if the policy deviation surpasses a threshold (`τ_KL`) or after a certain number of epochs (`K_max`), adapting to distributional shifts during online learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of Astrolabe was validated through extensive experiments across various settings and base models, comparing both quantitative metrics and qualitative generation examples.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

Astrolabe was integrated with distilled autoregressive models trained via Self-Forcing, Causal-Forcing, and LongLive. Training utilized prompts from the VidProM dataset. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning was performed using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). To optimize memory, the current and old policies shared a single frozen base model, with switching between their lightweight LoRA weights. Training was distributed across 48 NVIDIA H200 GPUs. Rewards were computed using VideoAlign and HPSv3.

#### 4.2. Short-Video Single-Prompt Generation

- **Quantitative Results (Table 1)**: Evaluations based on VBench protocols demonstrated that Astrolabe consistently improved performance across all Self-Forcing variants, LongLive, and Causal Forcing models. Notable gains were observed in HPSv3 (visual aesthetics) and Motion Quality (temporal consistency). For instance, Self-Forcing's HPSv3 improved from 9.36 to 10.72 (+1.36) and MQ from 1.65 to 1.71 (+0.06). LongLive showed similar improvements in HPSv3 (+1.65) and MQ (+0.13). These enhancements were achieved while maintaining the exact inference speed of the original checkpoints.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 3)**: Visual comparisons showed that Astrolabe-enhanced models generated videos with sharper textures and superior motion coherence, aligning more closely with human preferences.

#### 4.3. Long-Video Single-Prompt Generation

- **Quantitative Results (Table 2)**: Using VBench-Long protocols for 30-second videos, Astrolabe demonstrated performance improvements across long-video benchmarks. This indicates that the alignment optimizations learned on short videos effectively extrapolated to extended temporal horizons. For example, Causal Forcing's HPSv3 increased from 9.28 to 10.52 (+1.24) and MQ from 1.65 to 1.74 (+0.09).
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 4)**: The generated long videos exhibited enhanced visual details and more stable temporal coherence throughout the sequences compared to the baselines.

#### 4.4. Long-Video Multi-Prompt Generation

- **Quantitative Results (Table 3)**: Astrolabe was evaluated on interactive multi-prompt generation using narrative scripts (6x10-second prompts for 60-second videos). The framework improved overall generation quality, with gains in visual aesthetics and long-range motion consistency, as measured by CLIP Scores computed at 10-second intervals. LongLive's Aesthetic Score improved from 59.89 to 60.75, and its CLIP scores generally maintained or slightly improved across intervals.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 6)**: Visual examples illustrated noticeable improvements in visual aesthetics and fine details during complex narrative transitions in extended video generation.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted on Causal-Forcing to validate individual components:
- **Streaming Training Scheme (Table 4a)**:
 - Sequence-level rollout with full backpropagation led to Out-Of-Memory (OOM) errors.
 - Clip-level group-wise sampling with detached context (Astrolabe's approach) achieved the best trade-off, reducing memory consumption by approximately 2x compared to clip-level full backpropagation, while improving both HPSv3 and MQ scores.
- **Reward Design (Table 4c, Figure 7a)**:
 - Optimizing for a single reward (e.g., VQ only) induced reward hacking, causing the model to collapse into static frames (high HPSv3, low MQ).
 - The multi-reward formulation (VQ+MQ+TA) prevented this overfitting, yielding balanced improvements across metrics.
- **Regularization (Table 4b, Figure 8a)**:
 - Omitting KL regularization led to instability and early plateaus in MQ.
 - Uniform KL regularization over-constrained learning.
 - The uncertainty-aware selective KL penalty with EMA reference updates adaptively penalized only high-uncertainty predictions, balancing exploration with stable convergence across all metrics.
- **Removing Adaptive Weighting (Figure 8b)**: The adaptive loss weighting from DiffusionNFT, which uses a self-normalized x_0 denominator, was found to destabilize training in distilled AR settings due to volatile x_0 norms causing gradient explosions. Removing this scaling factor ensured steady, monotonic reward improvements.
- **Impact of β (Figure 7b)**: The interpolation strength parameter β, which controls the implicit guidance direction, was found to directly influence temporal dynamics. A value of β=1 yielded higher overall visual and motion quality compared to β=0.1.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Astrolabe represents a notable advancement in the alignment of efficient video generation models with human preferences, addressing a critical bottleneck in the deployment of real-time interactive video synthesis systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Efficient Human Preference Alignment**: The core contribution of Astrolabe is its ability to integrate reinforcement learning effectively into distilled autoregressive video models without compromising their inherent efficiency. By formulating a forward-process RL approach, it circumvents the substantial memory and computational overhead associated with traditional reverse-process RL methods, which are ill-suited for streaming architectures. This makes human preference alignment a viable post-training step for these models.

2. **Scalability to Long Video Generation**: A significant challenge in video synthesis is generating long, temporally coherent sequences without prohibitive resource demands. Astrolabe's streaming training scheme, employing a rolling KV-cache and segment-wise optimization with detached historical gradients, provides a scalable solution. This allows for RL updates on local video clips while maintaining long-range consistency, enabling the generation of extended videos (e.g., 30-60 seconds) that are perceptually superior to previous methods.

3. **Robustness Against Reward Hacking**: The framework's multi-reward objective, which incorporates visual quality, motion quality, and text alignment, coupled with an uncertainty-aware selective KL penalty, enhances training stability and mitigates the problem of reward hacking. This ensures that the model optimizes for a more balanced and aesthetically pleasing output rather than exploiting narrow aspects of a single reward function. This contributes to more generalizable and higher-quality generations.

4. **Generalizability Across Base Architectures**: The demonstrated effectiveness of Astrolabe across multiple distilled AR video models (Self-Forcing, LongLive, Causal Forcing) indicates its general applicability. This suggests that the framework can serve as a robust alignment solution for a range of existing and future efficient video generation models, enhancing their practical utility.

5. **Impact on Interactive Content Creation**: By facilitating the creation of high-quality, temporally consistent, and aesthetically pleasing videos in real-time, Astrolabe has the potential to impact fields such as interactive media, content creation, virtual reality, and gaming. It could enable more responsive and preference-aligned video generation tools for artists, developers, and general users.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as reliance on the capabilities of reward models and inherent bottlenecks of base architectures, Astrolabe provides a foundation for future research in developing more robust reward models and exploring how RL can push the boundaries of model capabilities within existing architectural constraints.
