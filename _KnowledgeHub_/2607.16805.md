# Scene-SAM3D: Multi-View Scene Asset Generation Without Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Scene-SAM3D is a training-free pipeline that extends the frozen single-image SAM3D generator to calibrated multi-view scene asset generation. It picks an anchor plus complementary helper views by world-space occupancy coverage, fuses helper velocities only in early sampling steps under anchor-guided sign consensus, then refines object placement by rigid Gaussian rendering, cutting scene-level Chamfer distance 43.8% on Replica and roughly 20% of flow-model compute.

## Problem

- Single-image 3D generators such as SAM3D carry strong object priors but break on real scenes with severe occlusion, truncation, and no single best interpreting view.
- Stochastic view conditioning is cheap yet never reconciles cross-view evidence, so the generation trajectory drifts between incompatible single-view interpretations across sampling steps.
- MultiDiffusion velocity averaging assumes canonical-space compatibility; conflicting views yield duplicated layered geometry, and cost scales with objects times views times all sampling steps.

## Method

- ==Occupancy-Coverage View Selection== voxelizes masked depth backprojections in the world frame, taking the anchor with largest occupied volume and greedily adding helpers that maximize newly covered occupancy.
- ==Step-Efficient Conflict-Aware Fusion== early-stops helper views at a midpoint step, and applies ==sign consensus== that averages only velocity cells agreeing with the anchor, or with the majority sign where the anchor is blind.
- ==Rigid-Object Gaussian Layout Refinement== freezes asset geometry and optimizes per-instance similarity transforms via ==differentiable rasterization== losses on mask, foreground RGB, depth, silhouette boundary, scene composition, and inter-object penetration, completing the training-free **Scene-SAM3D** pipeline.

## Results

- Coverage view selection alone reduces SAM3D instance Chamfer distance **32.9%** on Replica and **27.2%** on ScanNet++; the full method beats MultiDiffusion fusion by **3.9%**/**2.0%** CD at four views.
- Gaussian layout refinement replacing ICP lowers Replica scene CD from **8.64** to **4.86** (**43.8%**), raises IoU **40.8%**, lowers LPIPS **24.4%**, with a **30.9%** CD gain on ScanNet++.
- Ablations favor anchor privilege only inside observed voxels, CD **39.37** versus **41.37** all-vote and **40.38** anchor-everywhere; ten-view cost drops from **3,120** TFLOPs and **43.18s** to **2,520** and **34.83s**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Which view you condition on matters more than how many: 3D occupancy coverage beats larger 2D masks and random selection across every generator tested.
> - Multi-view evidence pays off mainly during early structure formation; late-trajectory fusion amplifies canonical conflicts, so early stopping buys accuracy and compute at once.
> - Decouple shape from placement: sign-level agreement filters conflicting generative modes, while rendering-based pose optimization fixes layout without editing frozen generated geometry.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of high-quality 3D scene assets is a critical area of research with significant implications for embodied applications such as robotic manipulation, navigation, and simulation, as well as immersive augmented and virtual reality (AR/VR) environments. Traditionally, obtaining such assets has involved reconstructing 3D meshes from 2D visual observations.

Recent advancements in foundation models, such as SAM3D, Trellis, and Amodal3R, have facilitated the generation of structured, object-centric 3D shapes, appearances, and camera-relative poses from a single 2D image. These single-view models, while powerful for individual objects, encounter limitations when applied to real-world scenes. Such scenes typically involve pervasive occlusions, redundant observations from multiple viewpoints, and inherent cross-view inconsistencies, making reliable scene generation challenging.

To address the limitations of single-view approaches, several methods have attempted to leverage multiple observations. Two common strategies include stochastic view conditioning, which samples different views during generation but does not explicitly reconcile them, and multi-view velocity fusion (e.g., MultiDiffusion), which averages velocity fields from multiple views to improve reconstruction quality. However, existing multi-view fusion methods present their own set of challenges:
1. **Incompleteness and Hallucinations**: Even with multiple views, objects may not be fully covered, leading to missing geometry or hallucinated structures in unobserved regions.
2. **Canonical Space Disagreement**: Multi-view fusion implicitly assumes compatibility of per-view updates in a shared canonical space. When this assumption is violated, direct averaging can result in duplicated or layered structures.
3. **Pose Conflicts**: Different views may yield inconsistent predictions for an object's rotation, scale, and translation (RST), leading to incompatible object placements and orientations in a shared world frame.
4. **Computational Cost**: Multi-view velocity fusion can be computationally expensive, as inference cost scales with both the number of objects and the number of selected views.

This research paper positions itself within this landscape by identifying these limitations and proposing a structured approach to multi-view scene asset generation. It extends a prominent single-view object generation model (SAM3D) to a multi-view scene context without requiring fine-tuning of the base model. The work addresses the problems of redundant observations, cross-view inconsistencies, and incoherent scene layouts by formulating the task as a selection, fusion, and alignment problem, thereby attempting to bridge the gap between powerful single-object generators and the demands of complex multi-object scene reconstruction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a training-free framework, named Scene-SAM3D, that extends existing single-view 3D object generation models, specifically SAM3D, to enable calibrated multi-view generation of high-quality 3D scene assets. The framework aims to overcome the inherent challenges encountered when generating complete and spatially coherent 3D scenes from multiple visual observations.

The motivations for pursuing this objective are rooted in several identified limitations and challenges:

1. **Demand for Faithful Scene Assets**: High-quality and spatially coherent 3D scene assets are essential for numerous applications, including realistic digital environments for AR/VR, and robust embodied simulations for robotics. Existing single-image 3D generation models, despite their strong object priors, are inherently limited in generating assets for complex real-world scenes characterized by occlusions and varied viewpoints.
2. **Inefficiency of Redundant Multi-View Observations**: Real-world calibrated captures often produce long multi-view sequences with substantial redundancy. Simply using all available views for generation is computationally expensive and does not necessarily lead to improved results, as many views may offer little new information.
3. **Cross-View Inconsistencies and Conflicts**: A major challenge in multi-view generation is reconciling potentially conflicting information across different views. Individual views can provide different predictions for an object's rotation, scale, and translation (RST). When aggregated without careful consideration, these discrepancies can lead to geometric inconsistencies, such as duplicated structures, misaligned parts, or incompatible canonical object states. Direct averaging of velocity fields, as in MultiDiffusion, can exacerbate these conflicts.
4. **Decoupling Object Reconstruction from Scene Layout**: The view most favorable for reconstructing the fine-grained geometry of a specific object instance may not be the most reliable for accurately estimating its placement, orientation, and scale within the broader scene context. This suggests a need to separate object-level shape reconstruction from scene-level layout refinement, allowing each to be informed by optimal evidence.
5. **Lack of Coherent Scene-Level Placement**: After individual objects are generated, their placement into a shared world frame often suffers from inaccuracies due to noise in masks, depth estimates, or initial pose predictions. Basic methods like direct backprojection or ICP (Iterative Closest Point) refinement may be insufficient, leading to floating objects, collisions, or incorrect spatial relationships. A robust mechanism for scene-level spatial coherence is required.

In response to these motivations, the authors propose a pipeline that addresses multi-view scene generation as a structured selection–fusion–alignment problem, focusing on selecting complementary views, fusing them with conflict awareness, and refining object layouts through comprehensive multi-view feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Scene-SAM3D is presented as a training-free framework that extends the SAM3D model from single-view object generation to calibrated multi-view scene asset generation. The methodology is structured into three main stages: Occupancy-Coverage View Selection, Step-Efficient Conflict-Aware Multiview Fusion, and Rigid-Object Gaussian Layout Refinement.

**A. Occupancy-Coverage View Selection**
This initial stage aims to select a compact set of complementary views, consisting of a single anchor view and multiple helper views, to reduce observation redundancy and provide comprehensive 3D evidence.
1. **Anchor View Selection**: The method measures view coverage in 3D rather than 2D image plane. For each object and view, the instance mask and an estimated depth map are used to project masked observations into a world-space point set. These point sets are then voxelized to obtain a visible occupancy map (`Ω_vis_i`). The per-view 3D coverage score (`c_i`) is defined as the volume of `Ω_vis_i` relative to the total merged occupancy of all views (`Ω_all`). The view with the highest `c_i` (largest world-space occupied volume) is selected as the anchor view, prioritizing views that capture a broader 3D extent of the object.
2. **Helper View Selection**: After the anchor is chosen, helper views are greedily added. Starting with the anchor's occupancy, each subsequent helper view is selected to maximize its *complementary* occupancy – the new 3D evidence it provides beyond what is already covered by previously selected views. This process ensures that helper views genuinely contribute missing information.

**B. Step-Efficient Conflict-Aware Multiview Fusion**
This stage integrates the selected multi-view evidence efficiently while mitigating inconsistencies that arise from direct multi-view averaging.
1. **Early-stop Helper Fusion**: The observation that multi-view cues are most beneficial during the early stages of generation (when coarse object structure and missing geometry are determined) is leveraged. Multi-view fusion (involving both anchor and helper views) is applied only before a specified midpoint (`T_mid`) in the sampling trajectory. After `T_mid`, the generation process continues using only the anchor view's conditions. This strategy reduces computational cost (from `O((M+1)T)` to `O(T + M * T_mid)` conditional evaluations per object) while preserving the impact of helper-view information during the critical structure-forming phase.
2. **Sign-Consensus Latent Fusion**: During the early-stop fusion phase, a sign-consensus operator is applied to the structured velocity latent.
 - **Anchor-observed region**: For regions directly observed by the anchor, the anchor is treated as the primary reference. Only velocity proposals from other views whose signs agree with the anchor's velocity sign in that region are averaged, thereby protecting reliable local evidence.
 - **Anchor-unobserved region**: For regions not observed by the anchor, all selected views contribute votes for the dominant sign. Only proposals that align with this majority sign are averaged. This mechanism completes unobserved geometry through multi-view consensus while suppressing conflicting updates.

**C. Rigid-Object Gaussian Layout Refinement**
The final stage focuses on improving the scene-level spatial coherence by refining the object-level similarity transforms (rotation, scale, translation) of generated assets without modifying their inherent geometry or appearance.
1. **Gaussian Proxy Creation**: For each generated object asset, a downsampled, object-centric Gaussian proxy is constructed. The Gaussian parameters (geometry, opacity, appearance) of these proxies remain fixed.
2. **Joint Optimization of Transforms**: The method jointly optimizes the object-level similarity transforms (`T_w←o_k`) for all eligible instances in the scene. This transform is uniformly applied to all Gaussian primitives within an instance.
3. **Multi-View Rendering Feedback**: A differentiable Gaussian rasterizer is used to render the transformed proxies from various calibrated multi-view perspectives. This generates opacity maps, which, along with scene-level color and depth, provide multi-view constraints.
4. **Layout Loss Function**: The object transforms are optimized using a composite loss function (`L_layout`) that includes terms for supervising instance masks (`L_mask`), foreground appearance (`L_fg`), depth (`L_depth`), silhouette boundaries (`L_bnd`), scene-level rendering consistency (`L_comp`), inter-object penetration (`L_pen`), and regularization towards the initial layout (`L_reg`).
This optimization process is lightweight and typically converges within a few hundred iterations, resulting in a refined scene asset with improved spatial coherence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Scene-SAM3D was conducted on the synthetic Replica dataset and the real-world ScanNet++ dataset, comparing against various single-view and multi-view 3D generation baselines.

**Instance-Level Performance (Table 1, Figure 4):**
- **Impact of View Selection**: Occupancy-Coverage View Selection consistently improved reconstruction quality across both single-view and multi-view settings. For instance, replacing random views with coverage-selected views for SAM3D reduced Chamfer Distance (CD) by 32.9% on Replica and 27.2% on ScanNet++.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: Scene-SAM3D, utilizing its proposed fusion strategy, demonstrated superior performance in exploiting complementary views. It achieved the best CD, Completeness (Comp), and F@0.05 metrics on both datasets.
 - Compared to MD+SAM3D (using 4-view coverage), Scene-SAM3D reduced CD by 3.9% and Completeness error by 4.4% on Replica, and reduced CD by 2.0% while improving F@0.05 by 1.7% on ScanNet++.
- **Qualitative Observations**: Scene-SAM3D produced more complete object geometries and effectively suppressed duplicated, misaligned, or hallucinated structures often observed in other multi-view fusion methods (e.g., duplicated table legs in MultiDiffusion). It also demonstrated the ability to recover parts that were missing or fragmented in the ground-truth meshes in some cases. Single-view methods frequently produced incomplete structures due to occlusion and truncation.

**Scene-Level Performance (Table 2, Table 6, Figure 5, Figure 8, Figure 9):**
- **Layout Refinement Effectiveness**: The Rigid-Object Gaussian Layout Refinement (GS) significantly improved scene-level geometry and reconstruction quality.
 - Compared to ICP refinement (using the same 4-view coverage and fusion strategy), GS reduced scene-level CD by 43.8% on Replica and 30.9% on ScanNet++.
 - It also improved IoU by 40.8% on Replica and 42.5% on ScanNet++, and lowered LPIPS by 24.4% on Replica and 15.9% on ScanNet++.
- **Qualitative Observations**: Scene-level results indicated that direct backprojection of SAM3D-predicted RST parameters, even with ICP refinement, was insufficient for accurate indoor scene layout. Baselines often showed undersized objects, flipped orientations, or incorrect inter-object relationships (e.g., floating objects or collisions). Scene-SAM3D with GS refinement produced more plausible RST parameters and spatial relationships, aligning more accurately with ground truth and showing reduced surface and alignment errors in visualized error maps.

**Efficiency (Table 4):**
- The Step-Efficient Conflict-Aware Multiview Fusion strategy yielded notable efficiency gains. Under ten-view inference, the method reduced both flow-model sampling FLOPs and wall-time latency by approximately 20% compared to the MultiDiffusion fusion baseline.

**Ablation Studies (Table 3, Figure 6, Table 5):**
- **Anchor Guidance**: Using the anchor view as a reference only within its directly observed regions (Ω_obs_a) during velocity fusion provided the best performance, effectively leveraging reliable local evidence while preventing the propagation of potentially hallucinated anchor predictions into unobserved areas.
- **Fusion Steps**: Multi-view fusion was most beneficial during the early stages of the generation process (coarse structure formation). Continuing fusion from middle to end stages offered only marginal gains and could introduce canonical conflicts, supporting the design of early-stop fusion.
- **View Selection and Fusion Strategies**: The proposed occupancy-coverage view selection and conflict-aware fusion strategy consistently outperformed alternative strategies, including random selection, k-means++ feature-diversity selection, stochastic conditioning, and MultiDiffusion fusion, across most geometry-related metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Scene-SAM3D framework represents a structured and efficient approach to address several critical challenges in the field of 3D scene asset generation from multi-view inputs. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Realism and Coherence for 3D Scenes**: By effectively integrating multi-view evidence and mitigating inconsistencies, the method produces more complete and geometrically accurate object instances. Furthermore, the rigid-object Gaussian layout refinement significantly improves the spatial coherence of entire scenes, leading to more realistic and plausible arrangements of objects in a shared world frame. This is crucial for creating convincing digital environments for AR/VR, robotics, and other simulation-based applications.
2. **Leveraging Existing Foundation Models without Fine-tuning**: A key contribution is the development of a training-free pipeline that extends powerful pre-trained single-view 3D foundation models, such as SAM3D, to multi-view scene generation. This approach avoids the high computational cost and data requirements associated with fine-tuning large models for new tasks, making advanced 3D generation capabilities more accessible and adaptable to real-world multi-view datasets.
3. **Addressing Core Multi-View Challenges**: The method directly tackles fundamental problems in multi-view 3D reconstruction and generation:
 - **Redundancy**: The occupancy-coverage view selection strategy efficiently identifies and utilizes only the most informative and complementary views, reducing computational overhead.
 - **Inconsistency**: The step-efficient conflict-aware multi-view fusion, particularly with its sign-consensus mechanism, effectively reconciles conflicting geometric signals across views, preventing common artifacts like duplicated or fragmented structures.
 - **Layout Accuracy**: The rigid-object Gaussian layout refinement provides a robust solution for accurate object placement, scale, and orientation within a scene, moving beyond the limitations of single-view pose estimation and simpler alignment methods.
4. **Improved Efficiency**: The introduction of step-efficient fusion, which intelligently deploys helper views only during critical early-stage structure formation, reduces the computational burden (FLOPs and wall-time latency) of multi-view inference. This makes the generation process more practical for deployment in scenarios with computational constraints.
5. **Foundation for Future Research**: The paper's structured decomposition of multi-view scene generation into selection, fusion, and alignment stages provides a clear conceptual framework. The identified challenges and proposed solutions pave the way for future research directions, such as exploring joint multi-instance generation for better scene-level scalability and developing stage-aware view strategies that differentiate between geometry and texture generation needs. This work contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in creating rich, dynamic 3D digital content from diverse visual inputs.
