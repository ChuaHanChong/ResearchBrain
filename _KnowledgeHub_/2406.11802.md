# PhyBench: A Physical Commonsense Benchmark for Evaluating Text-to-Image Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyBench introduces the first comprehensive benchmark to evaluate Text-to-Image models' understanding of physical commonsense, revealing that state-of-the-art models generally struggle to infer physical laws from implicit prompts, particularly in mechanics, thermodynamics, and material properties. The study demonstrates that while models can generate images based on explicit instructions, they lack profound intrinsic reasoning about the physical world.

## Problem

- Text-to-Image (T2I) models frequently generate images that violate fundamental physical laws, such as incorrect gravity or material interactions.
- Existing T2I evaluation benchmarks primarily focus on explicit instructions for content, style, or compositional accuracy, overlooking models' implicit understanding of physical commonsense.
- There was no comprehensive, quantitative benchmark specifically designed to assess T2I models' ability to independently discern and apply physical laws from implicit textual prompts.

## Method

- The authors developed **PhyBench**, a dataset of 700 implicit prompts covering 31 physical scenarios across Mechanics, Optics, Thermodynamics, and Material Properties, designed to test implicit physical reasoning.
- They implemented **PhyEvaler**, an automated evaluation framework leveraging GPT-4o, augmented with bounding box information for improved spatial reasoning, to score generated images against human-aligned criteria.
- The study generated and evaluated images from six prominent T2I models (**DALL-E** 3, Midjourney, Gemini, Stable Diffusion series) on PhyBench, and conducted an ablation study using explicit prompts to diagnose model limitations.

## Results

- T2I models generally scored poorly in physical commonsense, particularly in thermodynamics (highest 1.31/3) and material properties (highest 1.28/3 for physical, 0.84/3 for chemical), while showing relatively better performance in optics.
- Proprietary models like DALL-E 3 typically outperformed open-source alternatives, though all models struggled significantly with implicitly inferred physical laws.
- Rewriting implicit prompts to explicitly state physical outcomes drastically improved model performance across all categories (e.g., DALL-E 3's thermodynamics score jumped from 1.09 to 2.46), confirming a lack of intrinsic reasoning rather than generation capability issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current T2I models exhibit a significant deficiency in understanding and applying physical commonsense from implicit prompts, with the notable exception of optical phenomena.
> - The performance gap between implicit and explicit prompts is substantial, indicating that models act more as "text-to-image translation tools" than systems with deep physical understanding.
> - GPT-4o, when augmented with bounding box information for spatial grounding, provides a robust and human-aligned automated scoring mechanism for physical commonsense evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Text-to-Image (T2I) generation has witnessed remarkable advancements, with models like DALL-E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, and Gemini capable of generating visually stunning and diverse images from textual prompts. These models have democratized creative expression and opened doors for applications in design, entertainment, and virtual world creation.

However, as T2I models become more sophisticated, the need for robust and comprehensive evaluation benchmarks becomes paramount. Historically, T2I evaluation has focused on metrics such as image quality, text-image alignment, compositional accuracy, safety, and bias. Benchmarks like DrawBench, HE-T2I, and T2I-CompBench evaluate models' abilities to generate specific objects, styles, and arrangements based on explicit instructions. Similarly, efforts like Safe Diffusion and Stable Bias address the ethical implications of T2I generation.

PhyBench critically identifies a significant gap in this landscape: the evaluation of **physical commonsense**. While models can generate intricate scenes, they frequently fail to adhere to fundamental physical laws, leading to illogical or impossible depictions (e.g., an elephant and a mouse balancing a seesaw). This limitation is a substantial hurdle for applications requiring realistic world simulation, robotics, and accurate depiction of real-world interactions. The paper argues that current benchmarks, primarily using *explicit prompts*, assess basic text-to-image translation but do not delve into the models' *internal knowledge* or *reasoning* capabilities concerning physics.

This work positions itself as the *first comprehensive and quantitative benchmark* specifically designed to assess T2I models' understanding of physical commonsense using *implicit prompts*. This approach is crucial because it tests whether models can *independently discern* and apply physical laws, rather than just following explicit instructions. By highlighting the lack of physical reasoning, PhyBench pushes the research agenda beyond mere image fidelity to a deeper understanding of artificial intelligence, aligning with a broader trend in AI research that seeks to develop models with genuine cognitive abilities rather than just pattern recognition. It serves as a diagnostic tool, identifying a critical area where even state-of-the-art T2I models fall short.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PhyBench stems from the observation that despite substantial progress in image generation quality, current Text-to-Image (T2I) models frequently produce images that defy fundamental physical laws. This deficiency is not merely an aesthetic concern but a critical limitation for applications requiring a realistic understanding of the world, such as virtual world simulation, robotics, and intelligent agents interacting with physical environments. The authors note that existing evaluation benchmarks for T2I models have largely overlooked this aspect, focusing instead on accuracy, bias, and safety, thereby neglecting the assessment of models' internal knowledge, particularly physical commonsense.

Given this gap, the key objectives of the paper are multifaceted:

- **To Introduce PhyBench:** Develop and present a novel, comprehensive T2I evaluation dataset specifically tailored to assess physical commonsense. This benchmark aims to encompass a broad range of physical scenarios and principles.
- **To Quantitatively Evaluate Leading T2I Models:** Use PhyBench to rigorously assess the performance of prominent proprietary and open-source T2I models (e.g., DALL-E 3, Gemini, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion series) in generating physically accurate images from implicit textual prompts.
- **To Develop a Robust Evaluation Methodology:** Create an effective and human-aligned automated evaluation framework, PhyEvaler, leveraging advanced large language models like GPT-4o, to score the generated images accurately and efficiently.
- **To Diagnose Model Strengths and Weaknesses:** Identify specific areas where T2I models excel or struggle regarding physical commonsense, thereby providing insights into their underlying knowledge representation and reasoning capabilities.
- **To Advocate for Deeper AI Understanding:** Promote a shift in T2I research focus from merely generating images based on textual translation to endowing models with a profound internal understanding and reasoning about physical laws, positioning them as potential "world simulators" rather than just image generation tools.
- **To Demonstrate the Impact of Prompt Engineering:** Investigate how explicitly incorporating physical principles into prompts affects model performance, shedding light on whether failures stem from a lack of knowledge or an inability to infer information from implicit cues.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors' methodology centers on the meticulous construction of PhyBench, its application to diverse T2I models, and a robust evaluation framework.

#### 3.1. PhyBench Construction

PhyBench is a comprehensive dataset designed to evaluate physical commonsense across four main categories: Mechanics, Optics, Thermodynamics, and Material Properties. It comprises 700 prompts covering 31 distinct physical scenarios.

- **Categorization:**
 - **Mechanics (24% of prompts):** Focuses on forces like gravity, buoyancy, pressure, elasticity, normal force, and surface tension. Prompts like "an empty plastic bottle at the bottom of the sea" assess understanding of pressure. (170 prompts)
 - **Optics (24%):** Covers light phenomena such as reflection, refraction, scattering, dispersion, absorption, and linear propagation. An example prompt is "a plastic straw partially immersed in a clear glass filled with water" to evaluate refraction. (170 prompts)
 - **Thermodynamics (17%):** Explores phase changes, given the limitations of T2I models to depict complex thermodynamic laws. Prompts like "The state change of dry ice at room temperature" assess sublimation. (120 prompts)
 - **Material Properties (35%):** Divided into physical properties (color, hardness, viscosity, solubility, conductivity, flame reaction) and chemical properties (combustibility, redox potential, acidity, dehydration, molecular structure, stability). Examples include "an egg and a hard rock collide" for hardness and "The scene after an iron rod has been exposed to oxygen for decades" for redox. (240 prompts)

- **Prompt Design Philosophy:**
 - **Implicit Prompts:** A core principle is to use prompts that do *not* explicitly state the physical outcome. This assesses whether T2I models can *independently discern* and apply physical commonsense (e.g., "an elephant and a mouse standing on either side of a seesaw" rather than specifying which side is down).
 - **Simplicity:** Scenarios are kept simple, often involving few objects, to isolate the evaluation on physical knowledge rather than complex scene generation or prompt-image alignment.
 - **Comprehensiveness:** The dataset aims to cover a wide range of common physical phenomena.

- **Data Collection Pipeline (Fig. 4):**
 1. **Task Proposal:** Define the four main physical knowledge types.
 2. **Detailed Physical Laws:** Identify specific physical principles within each category using textbooks, the internet, and GPT-4.
 3. **Scenario Construction:** Manually design appropriate and simple scenarios for each principle, including an implicit prompt and an explicit description of the correct physical outcome.
 4. **Prompt Augmentation:** Use GPT-4 to expand the prompt set by substituting objects and environments (e.g., replacing "plastic bottle" or "bottom of the sea").
 5. **Quality Checks:** Rigorous manual inspection and filtering ensure prompt simplicity, implicitness, and the correctness/completeness of the expected physical phenomena.

#### 3.2. Image Generation on PhyBench

The study evaluated six prominent T2I models: DALL-E 2, DALL-E 3, Midjourney, Gemini, Stable Diffusion XL (SD XL), and Stable Diffusion 3 (SD 3). For each implicit prompt, 4 images were generated per model to account for randomness and ensure thorough evaluation. Special prefixes were used for DALL-E 3 and Gemini (e.g., "I NEED to test... DO NOT add any detail, just use it AS-IS:") to prevent them from internally rewriting the prompts. For Stable Diffusion models, GPT-4 was used to reformat prompts into suitable short phrases.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Method (PhyEvaler)

The authors developed PhyEvaler, an automated evaluation framework leveraging GPT-4o, designed to align closely with human judgment.

- **Scoring Criteria:** Each generated image was scored on two aspects:
 - **Scene Score (0-2 points):** Assesses if the image accurately depicts the objects and scene described in the prompt.
 - **Physical Correctness Score (0-3 points):** Measures the accuracy of the physical principles represented in the image.
- **GPT-4o as Human-Aligned Scorer:**
 - GPT-4o was instructed to generate *item-specific scoring criteria* for each prompt, including a description of the scene, relevant physical laws, and detailed grading instructions.
 - Using these criteria, GPT-4o then scored the generated images and provided justifications. This method, shown to be effective in prior work, was validated to align well with human ratings.
- **Improving Spatial Relationship Judgment:** To mitigate GPT-4o's occasional hallucinations in spatial reasoning (e.g., gravity, buoyancy), the bounding box information of main subjects, generated by GroundingDino, was incorporated into the scoring prompt. This simple yet effective approach significantly improved the accuracy of machine scoring for such scenarios.
- **Human Evaluation:** All paper authors contributed to human evaluation, with each image scored by 10 different human evaluators according to the same criteria. The average human scores were used as the ground truth to assess the correlation and alignment of PhyEvaler.

#### 3.4. Robustness Analysis (Ablation Study)

- **Prompt Rewriting (PhyRewriter):** To investigate if model failures were due to a lack of intrinsic knowledge or an inability to infer from implicit prompts, GPT-4 was used to rewrite implicit prompts into *explicit* ones, detailing the physical outcomes. This was tested on SD XL and DALL-E 3.
- **Comparison with Reasoning-Capable T2I Models:** A qualitative analysis was conducted with RPG (a T2I agent framework with LLM reasoning capabilities). However, its low success rate in image generation precluded quantitative comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of six prominent T2I models on PhyBench revealed several critical insights into their understanding of physical commonsense:

- **Overall Poor Performance, Except for Optics:** Current T2I models generally struggle significantly to generate images that adhere to physical commonsense. The primary exception is optical scenarios, where models achieve relatively high scores. This suggests that optical phenomena (like straight-line light propagation or scattering) are likely well-represented in their pre-training data, leading to better understanding.
- **Mechanics: Significant Challenges:**
 - Even the best-performing model, DALL-E 3, achieved a physics score of only 1.71 out of 3 in mechanics. Other models scored below 1.5. This indicates a profound difficulty in understanding fundamental concepts like gravity, buoyancy, and pressure (Table 3).
 - The incorporation of **bounding box information** from GroundingDino significantly improved GPT-4o's scoring accuracy and its correlation with human evaluations for spatial reasoning tasks in mechanics, demonstrating the value of this augmentation (Table 4).
- **Thermodynamics & Material Properties: Most Challenging:**
 - Models performed particularly poorly in thermodynamics (highest score: Gemini, 1.31/3) and material properties (Physical: DALL-E 3, 1.28/3; Chemical: DALL-E 3, 0.84/3) (Tables 6, 7, 8).
 - This poor performance is attributed to models failing to understand implicit prompts related to phase changes or the intrinsic physical/chemical attributes of materials, often depicting incorrect states or properties. The authors hypothesize that specific scenarios related to these categories might be underrepresented in training data.
- **Proprietary vs. Open-Source Models:** There is a noticeable performance gap, with advanced closed-source models like DALL-E 3 generally outperforming open-source models (e.g., Stable Diffusion 3, Stable Diffusion XL) in understanding physical commonsense (Fig. 6).
- **Effectiveness of PhyEvaler:** The machine scoring results from GPT-4o (PhyEvaler) showed a high correlation with human manual evaluation outcomes. This validates GPT-4o as an effective and human-aligned scorer for evaluating physical commonsense in T2I models.
- **Prompt Rewriting Significantly Improves Performance:** The ablation study, where implicit prompts were rewritten to explicitly state the physical outcomes, led to dramatic improvements across all models and categories, especially in thermodynamics and material properties.
 - For instance, DALL-E 3's thermodynamics score jumped from 1.09 to 2.46 with rewritten prompts, and SDXL's improved from 0.59 to 2.08 (Table 6).
 - This crucial finding suggests that current T2I models can follow explicit instructions well but *lack the intrinsic reasoning capability* to infer physical laws from implicit descriptions. They function primarily as "text-to-image translation tools" rather than models possessing deep physical understanding.
- **Limitations of Reasoning-Capable T2I Agents:** Qualitative analysis of RPG, a T2I agent framework, revealed that it also struggles to accurately model physical scenarios, even with explicit prompts. This indicates that current "reasoning" capabilities in T2I models often focus on layout and object details, not a deeper understanding of physical laws, and are likely limited by a lack of appropriate training data.

In summary, while T2I models can render scenes, their ability to incorporate physical commonsense from implicit prompts is generally weak, with optics being an outlier. The study powerfully demonstrates that these models excel at pattern matching and translation but lack profound reasoning about the physical world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of PhyBench marks a significant step forward in the evaluation of Text-to-Image (T2I) models, offering a novel and critical lens through which to assess their capabilities beyond aesthetic quality and basic content generation. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Pioneering Benchmark for Physical Commonsense:** PhyBench is the first comprehensive and quantitative benchmark explicitly designed to evaluate T2I models' understanding of physical commonsense. This fills a crucial gap in the existing evaluation landscape, which previously focused on metrics like accuracy, bias, and safety. By introducing a new dimension of evaluation, PhyBench pushes the boundaries of what it means for a generative AI model to be "intelligent."

- **Diagnostic Tool for Model Development:** The benchmark serves as a powerful diagnostic tool, clearly exposing the fundamental limitations of current state-of-the-art T2I models in physical reasoning. The finding that models often fail in mechanics, thermodynamics, and material properties—despite excelling in optics—provides concrete targets for future research and development. It challenges researchers to move beyond merely improving image fidelity and focus on endowing models with a deeper, internal understanding of the physical world.

- **Driving Research Towards Deeper Reasoning:** The stark contrast in performance between implicit and explicit prompts is a key takeaway. It powerfully demonstrates that while T2I models are adept at "text-to-image translation," they largely lack "profound reasoning regarding physical commonsense." This insight is pivotal, urging the AI community to invest in developing models that can infer and apply physical laws autonomously, rather than requiring explicit instructions. This could lead to new architectural designs, training methodologies, and data augmentation strategies specifically aimed at instilling physical intelligence.

- **Advancing World Simulation and Embodied AI:** An accurate understanding of physical commonsense is indispensable for building realistic world simulators, creating effective embodied AI agents (e.g., for robotics), and developing virtual environments where objects interact logically. By identifying the current shortcomings, PhyBench paves the way for T2I models to contribute more meaningfully to these high-impact applications, ultimately bringing AI closer to a genuine understanding of our physical reality.

- **Validation of LLM-based Evaluation:** The successful deployment of PhyEvaler, using GPT-4o as a human-aligned scorer, demonstrates the growing utility of large language models in complex evaluation tasks. This methodology, especially with the incorporation of spatial grounding techniques (GroundingDino), offers an efficient and scalable alternative to extensive human annotation, which can be costly and time-consuming for nuanced evaluations. This could accelerate the development of new benchmarks across various domains.

- **Implications for Training Data:** The observed weaknesses in certain physical domains (e.g., thermodynamics, material properties) suggest a potential lack of relevant or diverse training data that implicitly or explicitly encodes these physical laws. This points to the need for more carefully curated datasets that emphasize physical consistency and diverse real-world scenarios, going beyond purely aesthetic considerations.

While the authors acknowledge the limitation that current strategies like prompt rewriting don't address the core issue of autonomous understanding, PhyBench provides the essential foundation for defining this challenge and measuring progress towards T2I models that genuinely understand and apply physical commonsense. It is a crucial contribution that will undoubtedly inspire a new wave of research focused on building more robust and intelligent generative AI systems.
