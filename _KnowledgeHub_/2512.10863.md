# MMSI-Video-Bench: A Holistic Benchmark for Video-Based Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and several universities developed MMSI-Video-Bench, a holistic and human-annotated benchmark for video-based spatial intelligence. The benchmark evaluates multimodal large language models (MLLMs) across perception, planning, prediction, and cross-video reasoning tasks, revealing that current MLLMs achieve only 38.0% accuracy compared to 96.4% for human performance.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) lack comprehensive evaluation of spatial intelligence from continuous video inputs, often focusing on discrete images or limited aspects of spatial understanding.
- Many current benchmarks rely on automatic question generation, which can lead to limited diversity, biases, and susceptibility to template overfitting, thereby hindering the assessment of true reasoning capabilities.
- There is a need for a rigorous, holistic benchmark that can assess a full spectrum of spatial cognitive abilities, including perception, planning, prediction, and complex cross-video reasoning, crucial for developing embodied AI assistants.

## Method

- Developed MMSI-Video-Bench, a ==multi-level benchmark== featuring 1,106 fully human-annotated multiple-choice questions designed by 3DV experts, covering ==five main categories of spatial intelligence==.
- Curated a diverse dataset of approximately 20,000 video clips from 25 public sources and newly recorded in-house videos, ensuring broad coverage of real-world scenarios (indoor, outdoor, robotics).
- Established a ==multi-stage cross-evaluation protocol== with 100% approval from reviewing researchers to ensure high-quality, unambiguous, and challenging questions requiring non-trivial reasoning.

## Results

- The best-performing MLLM, Google’s Gemini 3 Pro, achieved only **38.0%** accuracy on MMSI-Video-Bench, while human performance averaged **96.4%**.
- A detailed error analysis showed that Geometric Reasoning Errors were the most prevalent, particularly in Spatial Construction tasks, highlighting persistent struggles with inferring basic spatial relations.
- Uniform frame sampling significantly outperformed consecutive sampling or adaptive keyframe sampling, demonstrating that broad temporal coverage is crucial for complex spatial and temporal reasoning in video-based tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a substantial performance gap of nearly 60% compared to human ability on video-based spatial intelligence tasks, indicating significant room for improvement.
> - MLLMs struggle across all categories of spatial intelligence, including Spatial Construction, Motion Understanding, Planning, Prediction, and Cross-Video Reasoning, with Prediction tasks being the most challenging.
> - Neither providing more visual information (Sufficient-Coverage) nor using Chain-of-Thought prompting consistently improved MLLM performance, and attempts to integrate 3D spatial cues or use spatially fine-tuned models did not generalize effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has been one of the most significant advancements in AI in recent years, demonstrating impressive capabilities in understanding and generating both language and images. The ultimate aspiration for these models, as articulated in the paper, is to evolve into embodied general-purpose AI assistants, akin to the fictional JARVIS. A critical step towards this vision is endowing MLLMs with robust "spatial intelligence"—the ability to perceive, reason about, and interact with physical space based on continuous visual inputs.

The paper identifies a crucial gap in the current research landscape: the lack of a comprehensive benchmark that holistically assesses progress toward video-based spatial intelligence. Existing benchmarks, while valuable, suffer from several limitations:
- **Modality limitations:** Many focus on discrete single images or multiple static images, failing to capture the dynamic and continuous nature of real-world interactions in videos.
- **Lack of holism:** Question types often cover limited aspects of spatial understanding, not a full spectrum of perception, planning, prediction, and complex reasoning.
- **Reliance on automatic generation:** Benchmarks relying on templated or automatic question generation risk limiting question diversity, introducing biases, or becoming susceptible to template overfitting, thereby hindering the evaluation of true reasoning capabilities.
- **Insufficient data diversity:** Data sources and scene types in existing benchmarks may not be comprehensive enough to reflect the complexities of diverse real-world environments.

MMSI-Video-Bench directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel benchmark designed to be holistic, fully human-annotated, diverse, and inherently video-based. It distinguishes itself from prior video-based benchmarks (e.g., VSI-Bench, OST-Bench, SPAR-Bench, STI-Bench, EgoExoBench) by offering a more comprehensive evaluation that explicitly integrates complex reasoning about spatial layouts, motion understanding, decision-making, and critically, *cross-video reasoning*. This latter aspect, involving memory updates and multi-view integration across discontinuous observations, is particularly challenging and under-explored in existing evaluations. The paper's claim of yielding the largest human–AI performance gap among existing video-based spatial benchmarks further underscores its rigor and the significant challenge it poses to current state-of-the-art models, thereby setting a new standard for evaluating spatial intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MMSI-Video-Bench is to provide a rigorous and comprehensive tool to measure the progress of MLLMs in acquiring human-like spatial intelligence from continuous visual inputs. This capability is deemed crucial for the development of embodied general-purpose AI assistants that can effectively operate in physical environments.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Introduce a Holistic and Challenging Benchmark:** To establish a new benchmark, MMSI-Video-Bench, that holistically evaluates video-based spatial intelligence, covering perception, understanding, reasoning, and decision-making over spatio-temporal information in videos.
2. **Operationalize a Multi-Level Spatial Intelligence Framework:** To define and instantiate a four-level framework comprising Perception, Planning, Prediction, and Cross-Video Reasoning, ensuring a comprehensive assessment of various spatial cognitive abilities.
3. **Ensure High-Quality, Unambiguous Annotation:** To create a fully human-annotated benchmark, meticulously designed and reviewed by 3DV experts with explanatory rationales, thereby guaranteeing precision, uniqueness of answers, and the requirement for non-trivial reasoning for each question. This addresses the issues of bias and limited diversity associated with automated question generation.
4. **Leverage Diverse Data Sources:** To curate a broad spectrum of real-world scenarios by integrating 25 publicly available datasets and newly recorded in-house videos, ensuring the benchmark's applicability across various environments (indoor, outdoor, robotics, etc.).
5. **Conduct Comprehensive Model Evaluation:** To evaluate a wide range of state-of-the-art open-source and proprietary MLLMs, thereby identifying their current capabilities and limitations in video-based spatial intelligence.
6. **Provide Diagnostic Insights:** To perform fine-grained error analysis to expose systematic failures in specific types of spatial reasoning (e.g., geometric reasoning, motion grounding, prompt alignment), offering actionable insights for future model development.
7. **Explore Improvement Strategies:** To conduct preliminary explorations into potential model improvements, such as the integration of 3D spatial cues and chain-of-thought prompting, and analyze their effectiveness.
8. **Enable Targeted Capability Assessment:** To derive domain-oriented sub-benchmarks (Indoor Scene Perception Bench, Robot Bench, and Grounding Bench) to allow for more focused evaluation of models tailored for specific applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The construction of MMSI-Video-Bench is based on a structured, multi-level framework for video-based spatial intelligence and a rigorous human-annotation protocol.

#### 3.1. Multi-Level Spatial Intelligence Framework

The benchmark operationalizes spatial intelligence across five main categories, further divided into 13 subtypes:
- **Spatial Construction:** Assesses understanding of static spatial states and relations (position, shape, front-back, near-far) from partial and sequential video observations. Subtypes include Instance/Scene Spatial Attribute, Instance-Instance Spatial Relation, Instance-Scene Spatial Relation, Scene-Scene Spatial Relation, Camera-Instance Spatial Relation, and Camera-Scene Spatial Relation.
- **Motion Understanding:** Evaluates reasoning about long-term motion dynamics, including camera motion, individual instance motion, and interactive motion between instances.
- **Planning:** Requires models to devise actions towards a specific goal based on visual cues from videos.
- **Prediction:** Tests the ability to infer or imagine future spatial states under hypothetical conditions.
- **Cross-Video Reasoning:** Extends evaluation beyond single videos to tasks requiring integration of information across multiple, often temporally or spatially discontinuous, video segments. This includes Memory Update (retaining and updating context across time) and Multi-View Integration (combining observations from different viewpoints).

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction

- **Data Collection & Preprocessing:** A diverse pool of approximately 20,000 video clips was curated from 25 public datasets (e.g., ScanNet, RealEstate10k, Ego4D, Waymo) and augmented with 140 newly recorded, anonymized in-house videos. This ensures coverage of tabletop, indoor, outdoor, robotics, and sports scenarios. Videos were downsampled to appropriate frame rates and timestamped for precise temporal referencing.
- **Human Annotation:** A team of eleven trained 3DV researchers manually designed each of the 1,106 multiple-choice questions. For each sample, they selected a video clip, formulated a novel question, wrote a unique correct answer, and crafted plausible distractors, along with concise rationales to aid verification.
- **Data Quality Control:** A strict multi-stage cross-evaluation protocol was implemented, requiring 100% approval from reviewing researchers based on three criteria: clarity (unambiguous question), correctness (unique and factual answer), and challenge (requires non-trivial reasoning).

#### 3.3. Evaluation Settings and Metrics

To evaluate 25 state-of-the-art MLLMs (both open-source and proprietary), two primary settings were established:
- **Uniform-50:** Models receive 50 uniformly sampled frames from the video. This accommodates input limitations of many models.
- **Sufficient-Coverage:** Models receive all frames used during annotation, ensuring no visual information is omitted.
- **Baselines:** Random guessing and human performance (96.4% average accuracy) served as reference points.
- **Metric:** Exact-match accuracy for multiple-choice questions.

#### 3.4. Error Analysis

To diagnose model failures, a non-overlapping error categorization scheme was developed:
- **Detailed Grounding Error:** Failures in fine-grained perception (missing objects, subtle changes, misidentifying events).
- **ID Mapping Error:** Failures in consistent identity tracking across frames (occlusion, rapid motion, visual similarity).
- **Geometric Reasoning Error:** Mistakes in inferring spatial relations (positions, distances).
- **Prompt Alignment Error:** Misunderstandings in interpreting prompts or integrating them with visual evidence.
- **Latent Logical Inference Error:** Failures requiring integration of implicit cues or commonsense knowledge (e.g., estimating speed, correlating information across viewpoints).
This analysis was applied to 520 incorrectly answered cases from four representative models.

#### 3.5. Preliminary Improvement Explorations

- **3D Spatial Cues:** Models were augmented with multi-view observations rendered from 3D reconstructions (using VGGT) of the scene, fed alongside original video frames.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Models were guided with explicit step-by-step prompts (Understand/Analyze, Locate/Gather Evidence, Reason/Solve).

#### 3.6. Frame Sampling Study

The impact of frame count (1, 10, 50) and sampling strategies (consecutive vs. uniform) was investigated. Adaptive Keyframe Sampling (AKS), a semantic-similarity-based approach, was also tested against uniform sampling.

#### 3.7. Domain-Oriented Sub-benchmarks

Three sub-benchmarks were derived for targeted evaluation:
- **Indoor Scene Perception Bench:** Focuses on indoor environment understanding (Static-Scene Instance-Centric, Static-Scene Camera-Centric, Dynamic-Scene).
- **Robot Bench:** Assesses capabilities for embodied scenarios (Manipulation, Navigation).
- **Grounding Bench:** Requires localizing target objects or specific time points based on spatial reasoning (Target Grounding, Time Localization).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 25 MLLMs on MMSI-Video-Bench yielded several critical findings:

1. **Substantial Human–AI Performance Gap:** The most striking result is the enormous gap between MLLM performance and human ability. The best-performing model, Google’s Gemini 3 Pro, achieved only 38.0% accuracy, lagging humans (96.4%) by nearly 60%. Many models performed close to random guessing (24.1%), highlighting the extreme challenge posed by the benchmark. This gap is reported as the largest among existing video-based spatial benchmarks.

2. **MLLMs Struggle Across All Categories:** Contrary to expectations or prior benchmarks focusing primarily on static spatial perception, current MLLMs demonstrated significant weaknesses across all five main categories: Spatial Construction, Motion Understanding, Planning, Prediction, and Cross-Video Reasoning. This indicates that their limitations are not confined to a single aspect of spatial intelligence.

3. **Sufficient-Coverage Does Not Guarantee Better Performance:** Surprisingly, providing models with the complete set of frames (Sufficient-Coverage) did not consistently outperform uniform sampling of 50 frames (Uniform-50). In many cases, performance remained flat or even dropped, suggesting that additional, redundant visual information can sometimes hinder reasoning, rather than help.

4. **Proprietary Models Outperform Open-Source Models:** Proprietary models consistently achieved higher scores than open-source alternatives. Within open-source models, performance generally correlated with larger parameter scales. "Thinking mode" (akin to Chain-of-Thought prompting) offered only marginal improvements.

5. **Most Challenging Tasks:**
 - **Main Category:** Prediction tasks were found to be the most challenging, as they require models to infer future outcomes based on physical priors and hypothetical conditions, going beyond direct perception.
 - **Subtype:** Within Spatial Construction, Camera–Instance Spatial Relation was the most difficult, likely due to its requirement for combining egocentric-to-scene spatial reasoning with fine-grained instance grounding.

6. **Failure of Spatially Fine-tuned Models to Generalize:** Models explicitly fine-tuned on spatial reasoning data or equipped with latent spatial representations (e.g., Spatial-MLLM, VLM3R) showed no improvement or even degradation on MMSI-Video-Bench compared to their base models. This suggests that current methods for enhancing spatial capabilities are often dataset-specific and fail to generalize to the comprehensive and diverse challenges of MMSI-Video-Bench.

7. **Insights from Frame Sampling Study:**
 - Performance improves with increasing frame counts (1, 10, 50), confirming the necessity of sufficient visual information.
 - Uniform sampling significantly outperforms consecutive sampling, demonstrating that broad temporal coverage across the video is crucial for reasoning-intensive tasks.
 - Adaptive Keyframe Sampling (AKS), which selects frames based on semantic similarity, failed to improve performance and sometimes caused degradation. This implies that semantic cues alone are insufficient for identifying "key" frames needed for complex spatial and temporal reasoning in this benchmark.

8. **Detailed Error Analysis:**
 - **Geometric Reasoning Error** was the most prevalent overall, particularly in Spatial Construction tasks, indicating persistent struggles with inferring even basic spatial relations.
 - **Detailed Grounding Error** was a major limitation in Motion Understanding tasks, where models struggled with fast, subtle, or long-duration movements.
 - **Prompt Alignment Error** significantly impacted Planning and Prediction, as models failed to correctly integrate high-level goals or conditions with video evidence.
 - **Latent Logical Inference Error** was most prominent in Cross-Video Reasoning, indicating difficulty in identifying and utilizing correspondences across multiple videos or leveraging implicit cues.

9. **Preliminary Improvement Attempts Ineffective:**
 - **3D Spatial Cues:** Equipping models with 3D spatial cues generated by VGGT yielded no significant improvement. This was attributed to limitations of current 3D reconstruction tools in complex scenes (introducing noise) and models' inability to effectively leverage or interpret the provided 3D cues.
 - **Chain-of-Thought Prompting:** Simply prompting models to "think step by step" did not consistently improve performance, suggesting that the underlying limitation lies in the models' intrinsic reasoning abilities rather than just their prompt-following capacity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MMSI-Video-Bench represents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal AI, with several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Establishes a New Standard for Spatial Intelligence Evaluation:** By comprehensively covering perception, planning, prediction, and cross-video reasoning across diverse real-world video data, MMSI-Video-Bench sets a new, higher standard for evaluating MLLMs' spatial intelligence. It moves beyond simpler single-image or limited video understanding tasks, pushing the boundaries of what is expected from intelligent agents.

2. **Reveals Critical Limitations of Current MLLMs:** The discovery of a substantial human–AI performance gap (nearly 60%) and the widespread failure of even state-of-the-art models across all task categories clearly indicates that current MLLMs are still far from achieving human-level spatial intelligence in dynamic, video-based environments. This finding provides a stark reality check and highlights the vast room for improvement.

3. **Provides Actionable Diagnostic Insights:** The fine-grained error analysis is particularly impactful. By categorizing and quantifying specific failure modes (geometric reasoning, detailed grounding, prompt alignment, latent logical inference), the benchmark offers concrete diagnostic signals. Researchers can now identify *where* and *why* models fail, directing future development efforts towards addressing these precise weaknesses rather than broadly attempting to improve "spatial reasoning."

4. **Drives Innovation in Embodied AI and AGI:** Robust spatial intelligence from continuous visual input is fundamental for embodied AI agents, robotics, and the broader goal of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). By exposing current models' deficiencies, MMSI-Video-Bench will spur the development of new architectures, training methodologies, and representation learning techniques specifically designed to enhance spatial reasoning in dynamic environments.

5. **Informs Data Collection and Model Design:** The findings regarding frame sampling strategies (uniform over consecutive, and failure of AKS) provide crucial insights for future data preprocessing and model input design. They suggest a need for more sophisticated, reasoning-aware keyframe selection methods and possibly end-to-end video processing architectures that can effectively integrate information across long temporal spans without being overwhelmed by redundancy.

6. **Promotes Interdisciplinary Research:** The challenges identified (e.g., in 3D reconstruction tools and integrating 3D cues) highlight the need for interdisciplinary efforts, combining advancements in 3D vision, computer graphics, and multimodal learning to create robust spatial representations that MLLMs can effectively utilize.

7. **Facilitates Targeted Development with Sub-benchmarks:** The provision of domain-oriented sub-benchmarks allows researchers to evaluate models for specific applications (e.g., indoor robotics, navigation), fostering specialized development and providing clearer metrics for progress in niche areas.

In conclusion, MMSI-Video-Bench is not merely another dataset; it is a meticulously constructed, challenging, and insightful diagnostic tool that rigorously tests the frontiers of video-based spatial intelligence in MLLMs. Its findings serve as a critical roadmap, guiding the scientific community toward the foundational breakthroughs necessary to realize truly intelligent and embodied AI systems.
