# NebulaVLA: A Dual-Frequency Vision-Language-Action Model With Guide Action for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> NebulaVLA decouples VLA control into an asynchronous dual-frequency system: a 10Hz Qwen3-VL System 2 for semantic planning and a 20Hz DiT System 1 for reactive control, unified via GESTURE-7 language-grounded action encoding and a Guide Action mechanism enforcing chunk-boundary continuity. Achieves 85.5% success on LIBERO-Plus and 2.7x faster action generation than synchronous baselines.

## Problem

- Monolithic synchronous VLA architectures force uniform temporal resolution, preventing concurrent support of low-frequency semantic grounding and high-frequency motor control.
- Discrete action tokenization maps continuous kinematics to categorical tokens, demanding huge vocabularies and demonstration data, hindering unified semantics across heterogeneous robot embodiments.
- Temporal mismatch between asynchronous real-time inference and chunked action generation induces jitter, with prior fixes relying on post-hoc smoothing outside the generative process.

## Method

- **NebulaVLA**'s heterogeneous ==dual-frequency architecture==: 10Hz Qwen3-VL System 2 for deliberative reasoning decoupled from a 20Hz Q-Former plus DiT System 1 for reactive control, enabling distributed deployment.
- **GESTURE-7** representation: a 7D end-effector vector with position, orientation, and a natural-language gesture keyword shared across embodiments, trained via three-stage SFT then GRPO-based RL then flow-matching distillation.
- **Guide Action** mechanism: conditions next action-chunk denoising on unexecuted prior trajectory anchors via binary mask constraints, reinstating known actions after each diffusion step for seamless boundary stitching.

## Results

- NebulaVLA reaches **85.5%** overall success rate on LIBERO-Plus, beating InternVLA-M1 (81.3%), OpenVLA-OFT+ (79.6%), GR00T N1.5 (59.0%), and pi0.5 (58.0%).
- On AgiBot A2 real-world tasks, NebulaVLA-Heterogeneous hits **92.08%** and **92.5%** success on two tasks while cutting inference latency to **42ms** versus 81ms for InternVLA-M1.
- Ablations show three-stage training adds **1.7-2.5%** success rate, and Guide Action cuts mean joint jerk by **25.6%** average, up to **45.1%** on individual joints, versus naive async.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting semantic reasoning and motor control into different frequencies resolves an efficiency-performance trade-off that monolithic architectures cannot escape, not just an engineering shortcut.
> - Grounding continuous action representations in shared natural-language keywords, rather than discrete tokenization, offers a simpler path to cross-embodiment generalization.
> - Embedding smoothness constraints directly into the generative denoising process, inspired by inpainting, outperforms post-hoc trajectory smoothing for eliminating chunk-boundary artifacts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is undergoing a transition from traditional modular pipelines to end-to-end learning systems, with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models at the forefront. VLA models aim to integrate perception, language understanding, and motor control within a single architecture, enabling robots to follow natural language instructions and execute complex tasks with improved generalization capabilities. Early foundational work, such as RT-1, established Transformer-based policies, while subsequent developments like OpenVLA focused on scalable open-source frameworks, and the InternVLA series introduced precise spatial grounding and unified understanding-generation-action paradigms.

Despite these advancements, contemporary VLA models encounter several challenges that limit their real-world deployment. First, monolithic synchronous architectures often present a rigid trade-off between efficiency and performance, as they struggle to accommodate both low-frequency semantic reasoning and high-frequency motor control within a uniform temporal resolution. Second, inadequate embodiment representation hinders generalization across different robot morphologies, requiring extensive data and large vocabularies to map continuous kinematics to discrete action tokens. Third, maintaining action smoothness and continuity during execution remains difficult, with temporal mismatches between asynchronous real-time inference and action chunk generation frequently leading to jitter and discontinuities.

NebulaVLA is positioned within this landscape as a system designed to address these identified bottlenecks. It aligns with emerging research that explores fast-slow system decoupling, as exemplified by academic efforts such as HiRT and industrial applications like Figure Helix. By proposing a dual-frequency architecture, a unified action representation, and an intrinsic mechanism for trajectory smoothing, NebulaVLA aims to enhance the efficiency, generalizability, and execution quality of VLA models for practical robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of addressing three core limitations in robotic manipulation: the efficiency-performance trade-off, challenges in cross-embodiment generalization, and issues with execution smoothness. The motivations for tackling these specific problems are detailed below:

- **Addressing the Efficiency-Performance Trade-off in VLA Models:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing synchronous VLA architectures often process all information (from high-level semantic understanding to low-level motor commands) at a uniform temporal resolution. This design choice forces a compromise: either the system operates slowly to allow for complex semantic reasoning, or it sacrifices the depth of reasoning to achieve fast, reactive control. This bottleneck restricts computational efficiency and response times, making real-time deployment in dynamic environments challenging.
 - **Objective:** To optimize computational resource utilization and modularity by decoupling high-level semantic reasoning from low-level action control. This involves designing an asynchronous dual-frequency architecture where different components operate at frequencies appropriate to their computational demands and control requirements.

- **Enhancing Cross-Embodiment Generalization:**
 - **Motivation:** Robots vary significantly in their physical morphology (e.g., parallel grippers versus dexterous hands). Current methods for action representation, particularly discrete tokenization, often require vast amounts of demonstration data to span the action space for each specific robot type. This approach limits the ability of VLA models to transfer learned skills across heterogeneous platforms, as a unified semantic understanding of actions across different embodiments is lacking.
 - **Objective:** To bridge semantic gaps across heterogeneous robots by introducing a unified, language-grounded action representation. This representation aims to enable different robot embodiments to interpret and execute semantically similar actions, thereby improving generalization and reducing the data dependency for new robot morphologies.

- **Ensuring Action Smoothness and Continuity During Execution:**
 - **Motivation:** In real-world robotic deployment, the asynchronous nature of policy inference and action execution often leads to temporal misalignments. When actions are generated in chunks, the transition between these chunks can be abrupt, resulting in jerky movements, kinematic discontinuities, and reduced operational reliability. Existing solutions often rely on post-hoc smoothing, which can introduce latency or compromise semantic consistency.
 - **Objective:** To enforce kinematic continuity and enhance execution smoothness by integrating constraints directly into the action generation process. This aims to prevent jitter and discontinuities at action chunk boundaries without requiring external post-processing methods.

By addressing these three objectives, the authors aim to develop a VLA model that is more efficient, versatile across different robot types, and performs smoother, more reliable physical manipulations in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NebulaVLA employs a hierarchical fast-slow architecture, a unified action representation, and a mechanism for ensuring trajectory smoothness, integrated through a multi-stage training process.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

NebulaVLA adopts an asynchronous dual-frequency architecture that decouples high-level semantic planning from low-level reactive control.
- **System 2 (S2):** Operates at a lower frequency (approximately 10 Hz) and is responsible for high-level semantic reasoning. It uses Qwen3-VL as its vision-language backbone to process natural language instructions and sparse visual inputs, outputting abstract action guidance.
- **System 1 (S1):** Operates at a higher frequency (approximately 20 Hz) as a low-level controller. It conditions on the guidance from S2, high-frequency visual inputs, and action history. S1 utilizes a Q-Former to compress inputs and generates execution actions through a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). DINOv2 is contrastively fine-tuned with visual inputs and end-effector states to improve visual-proprioceptive alignment.
This decoupling is designed to optimize compute-latency and enable distributed deployment, balancing computational efficiency with control responsiveness.

#### 3.2. Unified Representation: GESTURE-7

GESTURE-7 (Gesture Enumerated State Token for Unified Robot End-effector-7D) is a natural language-based representation for end-effector states. Each state is encoded as a 7-dimensional vector `[x, y, z, r, p, w, gesture]`.
- `(x, y, z)`: Cartesian position of the end-effector in centimeter units.
- `(r, p, w)`: Orientation (roll, pitch, yaw) in integer degrees.
- `gesture`: A natural language keyword (e.g., "grasp", "push") indicating the target hand posture.
This representation unifies action descriptions across heterogeneous robots (e.g., dexterous hands, parallel grippers) by mapping synonymous actions to shared keywords. Position predictions are quantized to integer centimeters and orientation angles to the nearest degree to reduce the learning burden and mitigate prediction hallucination.

#### 3.3. Guide Action Mechanism

The Guide Action mechanism is introduced to ensure trajectory continuity and smoothness during asynchronous action chunk generation. Inspired by image outpainting techniques, it embeds chunk concatenation directly into the generative pipeline.
- When generating a new action chunk, the mechanism conditions its generation on a set of prior unexecuted actions from the preceding chunk (e.g., actions `a_4` to `a_7` from the previous chunk are used to guide the generation of the new chunk's initial actions `a'_4` to `a'_7`).
- During the diffusion denoising process, a binary mask `M` is applied. For the "anchor region" (`M[1..k]=1`), the known actions from the previous chunk are strictly preserved. For the "generation region" (`M[k+1..T]=0`), the model predicts new actions.
- After each denoising step, the known actions in the anchor region are forcibly reinstated using the mask, ensuring that the newly generated chunk aligns seamlessly with the previously executed trajectory. This approach eliminates the need for post-hoc smoothing methods.

#### 3.4. Training Process

NebulaVLA's training pipeline consists of three sequential stages:
- **Stage 1: System 2 Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - Initializes S2 (Qwen3-VL) with original weights.
 - Focuses on establishing visual-grounded proprioceptive understanding and trajectory prediction.
 - Tasks include:
 1. Pose Regression: Predicting current end-effector state in GESTURE-7 format.
 2. Keypoint Regression: Regressing 2D hand keypoint coordinates from visual observations.
 3. 2D Waypoint Prediction: Predicting future trajectory waypoints in the image plane at 2Hz.
 4. 3D Trajectory Prediction: Predicting the robot's end-effector GESTURE-7 trajectory at 2Hz over a future horizon.
 5. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation: Generating subtask reasoning, natural language subtask descriptions, and future GESTURE-7 trajectories conditioned on visual inputs, instructions, and current states.
- **Stage 2: System 2 Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - Further enhances S2's trajectory planning using a GRPO-based reinforcement learning approach.
 - The same task suite as Stage 1 is used, but with specialized reward functions defined for GESTURE-7 distance, keypoint localization accuracy, waypoints reward (combining Euclidean and Dynamic Time Warping distances), 3D trajectory reward (point-wise GESTURE-7 distance), and a format reward for structural consistency.
- **Stage 3: Supervised Fine-Tuning with Continuous Action:**
 - Jointly optimizes the weights of both S2 and S1, with their respective visual encoders frozen.
 - An L2 loss is used to supervise continuous action outputs, specifically restricted to the newly generated action region (`M=0`) guided by Guide Action. This stage distills the priors learned by S2 into S1 and ensures frequency alignment between training and inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of NebulaVLA included both simulation experiments on LIBERO-Plus and real-world experiments on an AgiBot A2 robot platform, complemented by ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results on LIBERO-Plus

NebulaVLA demonstrated improved robustness and overall performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- **Overall Success Rate:** NebulaVLA achieved an 85.5% average success rate, surpassing InternVLA-M1 (81.3%), OpenVLA-OFT+ (79.6%), GR00T N1.5 (59.0%), and π0.5 (58.0%).
- **Perturbation Dimensions:**
 - Near-optimal performance was observed in Language scores.
 - Marked improvements over InternVLA-M1 and OpenVLA-OFT+ were shown in Robot Initial State and Layout (approximately 8% gains).
 - Comparable or superior results were achieved in Light, Background, and Noise dimensions.
 - NebulaVLA also led in Spatial, Object, and Goal dimensions (6% and 10% over InternVLA-M1 and OpenVLA-OFT+, respectively), matched InternVLA-M1 in Long tasks, and maintained 2–7% margins in Spatial and Object.
- **Camera Performance:** NebulaVLA exhibited marginally lower performance in the Camera perturbation dimension compared to InternVLA-M1. The authors attributed this to GESTURE-7’s training hypothesis, which implicitly established fixed visual-spatial mappings, leading to degradation when camera pose perturbations disrupted this correspondence.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

Experiments on an AgiBot A2 robot platform evaluated the effectiveness of the heterogeneous-frequency architecture and Guide Action mechanism for tasks like Pick-and-Place and Packaging Line Material Feeding.
- **Success Rates and Latency:** NebulaVLA-Heterogeneous achieved the highest success rates (92.08% for Pick-and-Place, 92.5% for Feeding) and the lowest average inference latency (42 ms). This contrasted with InternVLA-M1 (77.91%/72.5% success, 81 ms latency) and a homogeneous-frequency variant of NebulaVLA (NebulaVLA-Homo, 87.14%/90% success, 115 ms latency).
- **Heterogeneous Architecture Benefits:** The heterogeneous design reduced average step latency from 115 ms to 42 ms and improved success rates, demonstrating the advantage of decoupling System 1 (fast action response) and System 2 (deep semantic reasoning). This design also supports distributed deployment on separate computational devices, offering engineering benefits for edge computing scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Training Strategy Ablation:**
 - Compared different stages of the training pipeline on LIBERO-Plus. NebulaVLA(ALL) (85.5%) showed improvements over NebulaVLA(w/o SFT, w/o RL) (83.8%) and NebulaVLA(w/o RL) (83.0%), confirming the effectiveness of the progressive, stage-wise optimization.
 - S2 SFT improved performance in the Layout dimension (+6.2%), establishing spatial priors. S2 RL further enhanced performance in Language and Layout.
 - Performance degradation in the Camera perturbation dimension for S2 SFT and RL was observed, supporting the hypothesis that GESTURE-7's implicit fixed visual-spatial mappings are sensitive to camera pose variations.
- **Guide Action Ablation:**
 - **Qualitative Visualization:** Trajectory visualizations indicated that the native asynchronous algorithm produced pronounced jumps at action chunk boundaries. In contrast, Guide Action eliminated these discontinuities by using preceding guide points to align each new chunk’s starting state with the previous trajectory.
 - **Quantitative Smoothness:** Measured by mean absolute jerk (x10^-3) across joints on real-world trials. Guide Action reduced jerk in all evaluated joints (7-13). The most substantial reductions were observed in joints 12 (41.2% reduction, from 4.95 to 2.91) and 13 (45.1% reduction, from 3.37 to 1.85). An average reduction of 25.6% across all joints validated that Guide Action effectively suppressed boundary discontinuities and maintained kinematic continuity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of NebulaVLA presents advancements for the practical deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotics. The research systematically addresses three challenges: computational efficiency, cross-embodiment generalization, and execution smoothness.

The introduction of a heterogeneous dual-frequency architecture is significant for optimizing computational resources and reducing inference latency. By decoupling high-level semantic reasoning (System 2 at approximately 10 Hz) from reactive motor control (System 1 at approximately 20 Hz), NebulaVLA enables faster action generation, demonstrated by a roughly 2.7x acceleration and reduced real-world latency. This design allows for more responsive control and supports modular, distributed deployment, which can be beneficial for edge computing scenarios and industrial applications where computational resources may be constrained or distributed across different hardware.

The proposed GESTURE-7 action representation offers a mechanism for enhancing cross-embodiment generalization. By unifying action semantics across diverse robot platforms through natural language keywords and a 7D continuous end-effector vector, it has the potential to simplify the transfer of learned policies between robots with different morphologies. This could reduce the extensive data requirements typically associated with adapting VLA models to new robotic systems.

Furthermore, the Guide Action mechanism contributes to more reliable and physically smoother robot execution. By intrinsically enforcing kinematic continuity during the diffusion denoising process, it mitigates boundary jitter between action chunks without relying on post-processing. This integration of smoothness constraints directly into the generative pipeline can lead to more predictable and robust real-world manipulation.

While the current frequency allocation is empirically determined and static, and minor response latency can arise from fixed guide anchor points, NebulaVLA provides a structured and efficient framework. The findings suggest that this approach can advance the state-of-the-art in VLA models, making them more adaptable and effective for complex robotic manipulation tasks in practical settings. Continued research could explore dynamic frequency scheduling and further refine the guide action mechanism to address current limitations.
