# CLIP-RT: Learning Language-Conditioned Robotic Policies from Natural Language Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Seoul National University and Tommoro Robotics developed CLIP-RT, a system that enables non-experts to train robots for manipulation tasks using natural language for both data collection and policy training. The approach leverages CLIP for language-conditioned visuomotor policies and includes a novel data augmentation technique, leading to improved generalization on novel tasks and efficient real-world performance compared to larger existing models.

## Problem

- Collecting high-quality, diverse robot demonstration data for training visuomotor policies is labor-intensive and requires specialized expertise or expensive hardware, limiting accessibility and scalability.
- Bridging the abstraction gap between natural language instructions and low-level robot actions is challenging, as current VLA models often rely on abstract action tokens or continuous low-level commands that don't fully leverage rich semantic understanding.
- Current robotic systems struggle to quickly acquire diverse manipulation skills from non-expert users and generalize effectively to novel real-world tasks not covered by vast pretraining datasets.

## Method

- A language-based teleoperation framework allows non-experts to collect robot demonstrations by guiding the robot with natural language supervision, which an LLM translates into low-level actions.
- ==Stochastic Trajectory Augmentation (STA)== expands the collected demonstrations by generating diverse alternative trajectories to waypoints and automatically labeling recovery actions for robustness.
- **CLIP-RT**, a vision-language-action model, uses pre-trained CLIP encoders and a contrastive imitation learning objective to map image observations and instructions to discrete natural language motion primitives.

## Results

- CLIP-RT achieved an average success rate of **53%** on 9 novel real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming OpenVLA (7B parameters) which dropped from **51%** to **29%** on the same tasks, demonstrating superior generalization.
- In simulated environments, CLIP-RT+ (1.3B parameters) attained a **93.1%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, comparable to the state-of-the-art OpenVLA-OFT (7.7B parameters), while exhibiting significantly higher inference efficiency (**163.8** Hz throughput).
- Human intervention via natural language dramatically improved success rates on challenging tasks (e.g., from 20-**30%** to 70-**100%** with 2-4 corrections), and integration with GPT-4o enabled CLIP-RT to handle out-of-distribution instructions requiring commonsense knowledge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing robotic actions as natural language motion primitives effectively leverages pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP for visuomotor control, improving generalization.
> - Stochastic Trajectory Augmentation (STA), a novel data augmentation method, significantly enhances policy robustness and generalization by diversifying limited human demonstrations.
> - A contrastive imitation learning objective enables CLIP-RT to learn robust vision-language-action alignments directly from natural language supervision, making policies adaptable to linguistic variations and new tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing towards intelligent systems capable of understanding and executing complex tasks in unstructured environments. A significant challenge lies in **grounding natural language instructions to visuomotor control**, enabling robots to follow human commands intuitively. Current state-of-the-art approaches primarily involve **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** that train robotic policies using large-scale datasets, such as the Open X-Embodiment dataset. Models like RT-1, RT-2, and OpenVLA have demonstrated remarkable success by fine-tuning large vision-language models (VLMs) or large language models (LLMs) to predict low-level robotic actions.

However, a major bottleneck in this landscape is the **data collection process for robotic manipulation**. Acquiring real-world robot demonstration data often necessitates specialized expertise in robot control or expensive hardware (e.g., teleoperation systems, VR setups). This severely limits the accessibility, scalability, and diversity of collected datasets. Consequently, even advanced VLA models struggle to easily expand their skill sets to a wide array of novel real-world tasks.

CLIP-RT directly addresses this critical bottleneck by proposing a novel paradigm for robot learning: **natural language as the primary interface for both data collection and policy training.** Unlike existing VLA models that discretize continuous actions into arbitrary tokens or rely on generated sequences of low-level commands, CLIP-RT leverages natural language not just for instructions but also as the *representation of motion primitives*. This distinction allows it to directly adapt powerful pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP, which are inherently designed to align visual and linguistic concepts, for robotic control.

Furthermore, CLIP-RT distinguishes itself from other works that use natural language for data collection (e.g., RT-H, OLAF) by focusing on **teaching *new* skills through language-based teleoperation** for complete demonstration trajectories, rather than merely refining existing learned policies. Its contrastive imitation learning approach, inspired by CLIP's success in representation learning, offers a unique way to train visuomotor policies by explicitly measuring the similarity between contextual information (image and instruction) and language-based motion supervision, fostering more discriminative and generalizable representations of robotic behaviors. By enabling non-experts to train robots via natural language, CLIP-RT seeks to democratize robot learning, shifting the paradigm from specialized expert data collection to intuitive human-robot collaboration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **make robot learning more intuitive, accessible, and scalable for non-experts**, ultimately enabling robots to acquire diverse manipulation skills directly from natural language supervision.

The motivation stems from two primary challenges in current robot learning paradigms:
1. **High Barrier to Data Collection:** Collecting high-quality, diverse robot demonstration data in real-world environments is typically a labor-intensive process requiring significant expertise in robot control or access to specialized teleoperation hardware. This limits the participation of non-experts and hinders the scalability and diversity of available datasets, preventing robots from learning a wide range of real-world tasks.
2. **Bridging the Abstraction Gap:** There's a fundamental challenge in effectively grounding natural language instructions to low-level visuomotor control. While many VLA models exist, they often rely on mapping language to abstract action tokens or continuous low-level commands, which may not fully leverage the rich semantic understanding embedded in large language and vision models.

To address these, the paper sets out to achieve two specific goals:
1. **Develop a data collection framework that enables non-experts to gather robotic demonstrations solely through natural language supervision.** This aims to democratize the data collection process, making it as simple as telling the robot what to do.
2. **Propose a novel vision-language-action (VLA) model, CLIP-RT, that learns robust and generalizable language-conditioned visuomotor policies directly from this natural language supervision.** This involves leveraging the strengths of pre-trained VLMs and a training paradigm optimized for language-based action representations.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to facilitate a future where individuals without specialized robotics knowledge can intuitively teach robots new skills, drastically expanding the applicability and utility of robotic systems in everyday environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CLIP-RT framework comprises two main components: a novel **data collection framework** designed for non-experts and a **vision-language-action (VLA) model** that learns from this language-based supervision.

#### 3.1. Data Collection Framework

The framework aims to collect in-domain robot data purely through natural language, and it consists of two steps:
1. **Language-Based Teleoperation:**
 - Humans provide natural language instructions for a desired skill (e.g., "pour the dog food into the bowl").
 - At each state, the human provides natural language *supervision* (e.g., "move left a lot") to guide the robot.
 - A Large Language Model (LLM) (specifically, GPT-4 is mentioned in Appendix C) translates this natural language supervision into low-level robotic end-effector actions (a 7-dimensional vector for delta positions/orientations and gripper state). This translation uses a detailed text prompt provided to the LLM, defining the robot's environment, coordinate system, and rules for mapping language to actions.
 - The robot executes the translated action, and the current image observation is recorded. This process yields a sequence of tuples `(visual_observation, instruction, natural_language_supervision, low_level_action)`.
 - This method allows non-experts to intuitively guide the robot, generating initial demonstration trajectories.

2. **Stochastic Trajectory Augmentation (STA):**
 - This phase augments the human-collected demonstrations to enhance robustness and generalization.
 - **Waypoints:** Key states in demonstrations (e.g., gripper state changes, reversal of cumulative delta position) are identified.
 - **Diversification Phase:** Alternative trajectories are built towards each waypoint. The algorithm computes cumulative actions between waypoints and iteratively samples feasible action increments (e.g., from {0.01, 0.05, 0.1} meters for position changes) that sum up to the original cumulative action. This generates varied paths to the same goal.
 - **Recovery Phase:** When the robot approaches a waypoint, deliberate deviations are introduced (e.g., random shifts in X/Y, positive Z for safety). A "recovery action" (the negation of the deviation) is automatically labeled and recorded, training the robot to recover from off-nominal states. The deviation action itself is *not* used for training, only the recovery.
 - STA expands dataset diversity beyond simple human demonstrations, enabling policies to adapt to novel situations and recover from disturbances.

#### 3.2. CLIP-RT Model Architecture and Training

CLIP-RT (CLIP-based Robotics Transformer) is a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that learns language-conditioned visuomotor policies:
1. **Natural Language Supervision:** Unlike existing VLA models, CLIP-RT's actions are explicitly represented by a predefined list of 58 natural language motion primitives (e.g., "move arm back by 10cm", "close the gripper", "rotate gripper 90 degrees clockwise). These are mapped to specific low-level end-effector actions via a lookup table (Appendix B).
2. **Contrastive Imitation Learning (CIL):**
 - Inspired by CLIP, CLIP-RT uses a contrastive objective. It takes triplets `(image_observation, instruction, language_supervision)`.
 - It employs pre-trained CLIP's image encoder `f(·)` and text encoder `g(·)`.
 - The model learns to maximize the similarity between the "context" (combined image and instruction embeddings: `f(v_i) + g(ℓ_i)`) and the "language supervision" embedding (`g(u_j)`) for correct pairs, while minimizing it for incorrect pairs.
 - A key detail is that `y_ij` (label for pairwise similarity) is 1 if `i=j` (positive pair) *or* if the low-level actions `a_i` and `a_j` associated with `u_i` and `u_j` are the same, even if the language supervision `u_i` and `u_j` are semantically interchangeable (e.g., "move upwards" and "raise the arm"). This makes the training robust to linguistic variations.
3. **Training Process:**
 - **Pretraining:** CLIP-RT is initially trained on the massive Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset (2.4M trajectories, 18.1M transitions). Since OXE lacks natural language supervision, the paper converts existing low-level robotic actions into one of 899 templated natural language supervisions (Appendix A) by identifying the dominant axis and value of each action. This allows CLIP-RT to leverage large-scale robot data while staying within its language-based action representation.
 - **In-Domain Fine-tuning:** After pretraining, CLIP-RT is fine-tuned on the smaller, in-domain dataset collected by the proposed language-based teleoperation and STA framework (21K transitions across 18 tasks).
4. **Closed-Loop Robot Control:**
 - At inference, CLIP-RT computes pairwise similarities between the current context (image + instruction) and the embeddings of all 58 predefined language-based motion primitives.
 - The motion primitive with the highest similarity score is selected.
 - This selected language motion is translated into a low-level end-effector action using a predefined lookup table.
 - Inverse Kinematics (IK) is used to execute the action.
 - Crucially, CLIP-RT is a *discriminative* model, predicting actions in a single forward pass, making it efficient (16Hz on H100 GPU).
5. **VLM Backbone:** The backbone is ViT-H-14-378-quickgelu, an open-source CLIP model with approximately 1 billion parameters. It consists of a Transformer-based image encoder and text encoder, leveraging the OpenCLIP codebase.

#### 3.3. Adaptation for Simulated Environments (CLIP-RT+)

To evaluate CLIP-RT's generality on datasets with continuous actions, like LIBERO, a variant called **CLIP-RT+** is introduced.
- Instead of predicting discrete language motions, a 0.3B-parameter action decoder is added to the original CLIP-RT.
- This decoder takes the image and instruction embeddings from CLIP-RT and learns to predict continuous 7D (or 8D as defined in Appendix B) end-effector actions using an L1 regression objective.
- Action chunking and parallel decoding are employed for efficiency, resulting in a 1.3B-parameter model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both real-world and simulated environments demonstrate the strong capabilities and generalization potential of CLIP-RT.

#### 4.1. Real-World Robotic Manipulation Performance

- **Common Tasks:** CLIP-RT achieved an average success rate of 54% across 9 tasks aligned with Open X-Embodiment, outperforming all baselines. While OpenVLA (7B parameters) showed competitive or slightly better performance on simpler tasks (Point, Pull, Push, Move), CLIP-RT significantly surpassed it on more complex tasks requiring higher-level reasoning (Flip, Knock Over, Slide).
- **Novel Tasks:** On 9 novel manipulation tasks (barely observed during pretraining), CLIP-RT maintained a strong average success rate of 53%. In stark contrast, OpenVLA experienced a significant performance drop from 51% to 29%, highlighting CLIP-RT's superior generalization to unseen tasks. A t-test confirmed the statistical significance of this difference (p = 1.74×10⁻⁹).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **CLIP-RT-Action:** A variant representing actions as learned text tokens instead of natural language. CLIP-RT (using natural language supervision) consistently outperformed CLIP-RT-Action (43% -> 54% on Common tasks, similar on Novel tasks), suggesting that natural language supervision leverages the rich vision-language representations of the pre-trained CLIP model more effectively.
 - **CLIP-RT-Passive:** Excluded data from Stochastic Trajectory Augmentation (STA). Its poor performance indicated the critical role of STA in enhancing robustness and generalization by expanding data diversity.
 - **CLIP-RT-Zero:** Trained only on OXE without in-domain fine-tuning. It performed poorly (8% average success), underscoring the necessity of in-domain data.

#### 4.2. In-Depth Analysis of Generalization

- **Multi-Task vs. Single-Task Policies:** Both CLIP-RT and OpenVLA performed better with multi-task policies than single-task policies. However, CLIP-RT showed a larger performance gap between its multi-task and single-task variants (11.1% drop for CLIP-RT vs. 3.3% for OpenVLA). This suggests that CLIP-RT, by using language-based motion primitives as building blocks, more effectively learns and leverages shared structures across diverse robotic tasks, leading to more generalizable and transferable policies.
- **Few-Shot Generalization:** CLIP-RT demonstrated improved performance in few-shot policy learning, especially in the single-demonstration setting, which is crucial for rapid skill acquisition in robotics where comprehensive pretraining data may not cover all real-world tasks.

#### 4.3. Collaborative Capabilities

- **Collaboration with Humans:** CLIP-RT's language-based motion prediction enabled effective human intervention. On challenging tasks ("Play with the Car", "Hide the Pooh") where CLIP-RT initially had low success rates (20-30%), even a few human corrections (2-4 interventions per episode) dramatically improved success rates to 70-100%. This highlights the intuitiveness of language as an intervention interface.
- **Collaboration with Large Pretrained Models (GPT-4o):** CLIP-RT can integrate feedback from LLMs like GPT-4o for action refinement. GPT-4o, given an image and instruction, proposed "appropriate" or "inappropriate" actions, which were used to boost or penalize CLIP-RT's action scores. This "GPT-guided" approach allowed CLIP-RT to handle instructions requiring commonsense knowledge or high-level reasoning (e.g., "Stamp below an American corporation founded in July 2003, headquartered in Austin, Texas"), demonstrating enhanced generalization to out-of-distribution instructions.

#### 4.4. Performance in Simulated Environments

- **CLIP-RT+ (LIBERO Benchmark):** When adapted to the LIBERO simulation benchmark with a continuous action decoder, CLIP-RT+ (1.3B parameters) achieved an average success rate of 93.1%, comparable to the state-of-the-art OpenVLA-OFT (7.7B parameters, 95.3%).
- **Efficiency:** CLIP-RT+ demonstrated significant inference efficiency improvements, achieving a throughput of 163.8 Hz and latency of 0.049 seconds on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT by approximately 49% in both metrics and OpenVLA (vanilla) by 39x. This highlights its lightweight design and efficient discriminative prediction.
- **Generalization to Novel Objects:** CLIP-RT+ achieved a near-perfect 99.2% success rate on LIBERO-Object, suggesting that CLIP's generalization capabilities to novel visual categories successfully transfer to simulation.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Failure Cases

The paper identifies several failure modes:
- **Inaccurate Visual Grounding:** Difficulty in comprehending object attributes (e.g., pointing to the wrong colored dice) or precise spatial relationships between objects.
- **Fine-Grained Control:** Challenges with tasks requiring high precision (e.g., "Stamp on <obj>") due to reliance on 2D image inputs for 3D spatial inference.
- **Occlusions:** Single-viewpoint images leading to the robot arm obstructing the object of interest.
- **STA Heuristics Limitations:** STA's reliance on heuristics may not capture the full diversity of recovery scenarios (e.g., handling object slips), limiting generalization to unforeseen circumstances.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CLIP-RT represents a significant step towards democratizing robot learning and broadening the accessibility of robotic systems. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

1. **Enabling Non-Experts to Teach Robots:** By providing an intuitive natural language interface for both data collection (language-based teleoperation) and policy training, CLIP-RT removes a major barrier to robot adoption and skill acquisition. Non-experts can now directly teach robots desired skills in real-world environments, fostering wider applications beyond industrial settings.
2. **Highly Generalizable and Transferable Policies:** The use of pre-trained CLIP models, coupled with contrastive imitation learning on language-based motion primitives, allows CLIP-RT to learn robust vision-language representations. This results in policies that generalize effectively to novel tasks and objects and learn shared structures across diverse manipulation skills, as demonstrated by its superior performance on novel tasks and few-shot learning capabilities compared to larger models.
3. **Efficient and Lightweight Models:** CLIP-RT's 1B parameter count is significantly smaller (7x fewer parameters) than competitive VLA models like OpenVLA (7B), yet it achieves superior performance in many real-world tasks and comparable performance in simulation with significantly higher inference throughput. This efficiency makes it more practical for real-world deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
4. **Enhanced Human-Robot and AI-AI Collaboration:** The language-based action representation inherently facilitates collaboration. Humans can easily understand and correct robot behaviors via natural language, leading to rapid skill refinement. Furthermore, CLIP-RT's ability to integrate with large language models like GPT-4o for high-level reasoning and action refinement opens new avenues for tackling complex, knowledge-intensive tasks that might be outside the robot's direct training distribution.
5. **New Paradigm for Robot Learning Datasets:** The data collection framework, especially Stochastic Trajectory Augmentation (STA), offers a scalable way to diversify limited human demonstrations, improving policy robustness without extensive manual effort. This could lead to richer, more varied datasets for robot learning.

In essence, CLIP-RT pushes the boundaries of intuitive human-robot interaction and skill learning, paving the way for more adaptable, general-purpose robots that can be easily taught and deployed by a wider user base.

### 6. Limitations and Future Work

The paper acknowledges several limitations and outlines future research directions:

1. **Inherent Limitations in Human Language Supervision:** The current approach assumes that users can provide supervision at an appropriate "intermediate" level of abstraction (e.g., "move arm to the right"). Non-experts might struggle with this calibration. Future work could explore adaptive models that interpret instructions across varying abstraction levels or a two-stage pipeline for translating high-level commands into intermediate ones.
2. **Lack of Temporal Context:** CLIP-RT, like many current VLA models, predicts single actions at a time without considering action history or predicting sequences. This limits its ability to perform tasks requiring an understanding of past actions or states (e.g., "Shake the water bottle"). Future research could incorporate mechanisms for maintaining a memory of prior actions and states, such as hierarchical history encoding.
3. **Handling Complex Tasks and Long-Term Planning:** The tasks addressed in the paper are relatively short-horizon. Extending capabilities to complex, long-horizon tasks (e.g., "folding laundry") requires novel approaches. A promising strategy involves integrating CLIP-RT's manipulation skills with high-level task planners that decompose complex goals into sequences of primitive skills (e.g., "set the dinner table" into "retrieve plates," "place utensils").
4. **Reliance on Single-Viewpoint Images and 2D Input:** The current system uses 2D RGB images from a single camera, which can lead to occlusions (robot arm blocking the object) and difficulty inferring precise 3D spatial information needed for fine-grained control (e.g., stamping). Utilizing RGB-D images, point clouds, or multiple camera angles could alleviate these issues.
5. **Heuristic-Based Stochastic Trajectory Augmentation (STA):** STA relies on heuristics for generating diverse trajectories and recovery actions, which might not capture the full spectrum of possible scenarios, particularly complex failure recovery. More sophisticated, data-driven methods for augmentation could be explored.
6. **Information Loss from Continuous to Discrete Actions:** While CLIP-RT performs well with language-based discrete actions, its core classification-based approach can suffer information loss when directly applied to continuous action trajectories, as observed in some LIBERO experiments. Adapting it for continuous data, as with CLIP-RT+, by adding a regression head, is a promising direction, suggesting a hybrid approach for different data types.

Overall, while CLIP-RT makes significant strides in language-conditioned robot learning, future work will need to address its limitations in handling temporal dynamics, complex multi-step tasks, and richer sensory inputs to achieve truly general-purpose robotic intelligence.
