# CLIP-RT: Learning Language-Conditioned Robotic Policies from Natural Language Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Seoul National University and Tommoro Robotics developed CLIP-RT, a system that enables non-experts to train robots for manipulation tasks using natural language for both data collection and policy training. The approach leverages CLIP for language-conditioned visuomotor policies and includes a novel data augmentation technique, leading to improved generalization on novel tasks and efficient real-world performance compared to larger existing models.

## Problem

- Collecting high-quality, diverse robot demonstration data for training visuomotor policies is labor-intensive and requires specialized expertise or expensive hardware, limiting accessibility and scalability.
- Bridging the abstraction gap between natural language instructions and low-level robot actions is challenging, as current VLA models often rely on abstract action tokens or continuous low-level commands that don't fully leverage rich semantic understanding.
- Current robotic systems struggle to quickly acquire diverse manipulation skills from non-expert users and generalize effectively to novel real-world tasks not covered by vast pretraining datasets.

## Method

- A language-based teleoperation framework allows non-experts to collect robot demonstrations by guiding the robot with natural language supervision, which an LLM translates into low-level actions.
- ==Stochastic Trajectory Augmentation (STA)== expands the collected demonstrations by generating diverse alternative trajectories to waypoints and automatically labeling recovery actions for robustness.
- **CLIP-RT**, a vision-language-action model, uses pre-trained CLIP encoders and a contrastive imitation learning objective to map image observations and instructions to discrete natural language motion primitives.

## Results

- CLIP-RT achieved an average success rate of **53%** on 9 novel real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming OpenVLA (7B parameters) which dropped from **51%** to **29%** on the same tasks, demonstrating superior generalization.
- In simulated environments, CLIP-RT+ (1.3B parameters) attained a **93.1%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, comparable to the state-of-the-art OpenVLA-OFT (7.7B parameters), while exhibiting significantly higher inference efficiency (**163.8** Hz throughput).
- Human intervention via natural language dramatically improved success rates on challenging tasks (e.g., from 20-**30%** to 70-**100%** with 2-4 corrections), and integration with GPT-4o enabled CLIP-RT to handle out-of-distribution instructions requiring commonsense knowledge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing robotic actions as natural language motion primitives effectively leverages pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP for visuomotor control, improving generalization.
> - Stochastic Trajectory Augmentation (STA), a novel data augmentation method, significantly enhances policy robustness and generalization by diversifying limited human demonstrations.
> - A contrastive imitation learning objective enables CLIP-RT to learn robust vision-language-action alignments directly from natural language supervision, making policies adaptable to linguistic variations and new tasks.
