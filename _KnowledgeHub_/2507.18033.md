# OpenNav: Open-World Navigation with Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Toronto present OpenNav, a framework that enables robots to perform open-world navigation by interpreting complex, free-form language instructions and synthesizing dense trajectory points. It achieves zero-shot open-world navigation with improved success rates and lower navigation errors compared to text-only approaches, while maintaining geometry-compliant and collision-free trajectories.

## Problem

- Existing robot navigation methods are often confined to structured or "closed-set" scenarios, struggling with generalization in complex, unstructured open-world environments characterized by open-set objects and inherent uncertainty.
- Many approaches decouple perception (e.g., object detection) from LLM-based reasoning, losing crucial spatial and visual information, which limits adaptability to rich real-world scenes.
- Translating an MLLM's abstract reasoning into concrete, continuous, and geometry-compliant physical robot actions remains challenging, often relying on limited, predefined motion primitives rather than direct trajectory synthesis.

## Method

- Introduces **OpenNav**, a zero-shot ==Vision-Language Navigation (VLN)== framework that leverages Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), an open-vocabulary perception system (OVPS), and classical motion planning.
- Employs a single, task-agnostic prompt for the MLLM (ChatGPT-4o) that guides its reasoning and code generation for dense trajectories, without requiring pre-trained skills or in-context examples.
- Integrates the MLLM with an OVPS that provides both textual descriptions and annotated RGB images for robust object understanding, and uses classical A* planning with dynamic Bird-Eye-View (BEV) value maps for geometry-compliant, collision-free trajectory refinement.

## Results

- OpenNav achieved an **84%** success rate in ambiguous object navigation tasks, substantially outperforming text-only LLM-TG which had a **26%** success rate.
- The framework demonstrated a lower average navigation error of **1.68** in ambiguous object navigation compared to LLM-TG's **4.91**.
- OpenNav produced trajectories with the best language alignment (Fréchet Distance: **12.60**, NDTW: **0.38**) while maintaining a low collision rate (2/30), effectively balancing intent with safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing multimodal input (annotated images and text) to MLLMs significantly enhances their ability to resolve ambiguities and robustly interpret instructions in open-world scenarios, outperforming text-only approaches.
> - MLLMs can effectively synthesize executable Python code for generating dense, task-aligned trajectories, bridging the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level robot control.
> - A hybrid approach combining MLLM-based semantic reasoning for coarse trajectory generation with classical geometry-compliant path planning is crucial for achieving both language alignment and collision-free, safe navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the emergence and proliferation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs). These models exhibit powerful capabilities in aligning natural language with visual information and performing complex semantic reasoning. However, as the authors highlight, most of these advancements have been confined to structured or "closed-set" scenarios, such as indoor tabletop manipulation or object goal navigation with a limited, predefined set of objects and instructions. The "open-world" presents a significantly greater challenge, characterized by open-set objects, unstructured layouts, and inherent environmental uncertainty.

**Current Limitations and Gaps Addressed by OpenNav:**

- **Closed-Set vs. Open-World:** Existing methods often struggle to generalize beyond their training data, failing when confronted with novel objects or unconstrained environments. OpenNav directly tackles outdoor navigation, a quintessential open-world problem.
- **Decoupled Perception and Planning:** Many approaches separate object detection (often relying on closed-set detectors) from LLM-based reasoning, leading to a loss of crucial spatial and subtle visual information. This decoupling limits adaptability to the richness and variability of real-world applications. OpenNav proposes a multi-expert system that integrates an Open-Vocabulary Perception System (OVPS) directly with the MLLM, providing a more holistic scene understanding.
- **High-Level Planning vs. Low-Level Control:** A fundamental challenge is translating an MLLM’s abstract reasoning into concrete physical actions. LLMs are often used as high-level planners, generating sequences of predefined motion primitives (e.g., "go forward," "turn left"), rather than directly synthesizing continuous, geometry-compliant trajectories. This restricts the flexibility of language instructions in controlling nuanced robot motion. OpenNav distinguishes itself by directly generating dense sequences of trajectory points, unifying high-level decision-making with low-level motion planning.
- **Reliance on In-Context Examples:** Many "zero-shot" frameworks, while impressive, still rely on providing numerous in-context examples to the LLM during inference to guide its behavior. OpenNav explicitly aims for a true zero-shot capability, requiring no such examples or pre-trained skills.
- **Vision-Language Navigation (VLN):** While VLN has progressed from discrete, teleportation-based navigation in simulators to continuous environments, many solutions still use mid-level actions or waypoint models that lack robust motion planning or obstacle avoidance. OpenNav pushes the boundary by integrating obstacle avoidance and geometry-compliant trajectory refinement directly into the MLLM-guided planning process.
- **Code as Policy:** The concept of using LLMs to generate executable code for robot control has gained traction (e.g., SayCan, VoxPoser). OpenNav builds on this but extends it to open-world navigation, demonstrating the MLLM's ability to not only generate code but also interact with perception systems, debug, and re-execute, all without task-specific prompt engineering or in-context examples.

By addressing these limitations, OpenNav positions itself at the forefront of robust, generalizable embodied intelligence, aiming to bridge the critical gap between powerful language models and their real-world deployment in complex, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable robots to perform **open-world navigation** by interpreting and decomposing complex, free-form language instructions and then synthesizing a dense sequence of trajectory points to achieve diverse navigation tasks, even when encountering **open-set objects** and **open-set instructions**.

The key motivations driving this work stem from observed limitations in current approaches:

- **Bridging the Language-Action Gap:** A primary motivation is to overcome the challenge of translating sophisticated language descriptions and common-sense reasoning from MLLMs into actual, physical robot actions beyond mere invocation of limited, predefined motion primitives.
- **Exploiting MLLM Capabilities:** The authors hypothesize that MLLMs, with their strong cross-modal understanding, robust scene comprehension, and crucially, their code-generation capabilities, can effectively integrate semantic knowledge with spatial information. This integration is believed to reinforce a robot’s spatial understanding, which is often lacking in language-only models.
- **Achieving Zero-Shot Generalization:** The goal is to develop a framework that can operate effectively in new environments and with novel instructions and objects without requiring extensive re-training, fine-tuning, or providing in-context examples. This is critical for practical, scalable robot deployment.
- **Enhancing Robustness and Flexibility:** Current systems often struggle with object detection errors, linguistic ambiguities, and the sheer variability of open-world scenarios. The authors aim to create a system that is robust against these uncertainties, capable of flexible and hierarchical environmental understanding.
- **Integrating High-Level Reasoning with Low-Level Control:** A key motivation is to unify high-level decision-making (from MLLMs) with geometry-compliant, low-level motion control (from classical planners). This hybrid approach seeks to combine human-like reasoning with the precision and safety of traditional robotics.
- **Leveraging Real-World Data and Hardware:** The motivation extends to validating the proposed framework not just in simulations but on large-scale autonomous vehicle datasets (AVDs) and real ground robots, demonstrating its applicability and robustness in practical outdoor navigation tasks.

In essence, the authors are motivated to push the boundaries of embodied AI by developing a truly generalizable, interpretable, and robust navigation system that can seamlessly bridge the gap between human language and robotic execution in the unpredictable real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OpenNav proposes a zero-shot Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) framework that leverages the strengths of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), open-vocabulary perception, and classical motion planning. The methodology is structured around a multi-expert system designed to interpret complex, free-form language instructions and generate dense, geometry-compliant trajectories.

The core components of OpenNav are:

#### 3.1. A. Open-Set Zero-Shot Vision-Language Navigation Pipeline

1. **Task-Agnostic Prompt Design for Open-Set Instructions:**
 - To achieve zero-shot generalization across diverse outdoor navigation tasks, OpenNav uses a single, task-agnostic prompt for the MLLM (specifically ChatGPT-4o). This prompt is designed to guide the MLLM's reasoning and code generation without relying on predefined skills or in-context examples.
 - The prompt comprises five key components:
 - **Available Functions:** Lists callable APIs, such as `det_object` for open-set perception and `A_star_plan(start, end)` for trajectory planning, and `visual_3D(traj)` for visualization.
 - **Environment Description:** Provides details about the coordinate system, robot pose, and sensor configurations.
 - **Collision Avoidance Guidance:** Explicitly instructs the MLLM to consider obstacles and traversable areas.
 - **Initial Planning:** Prompts the MLLM to perform step-by-step reasoning and cross-modal understanding of sensory observations, including task comprehension and decomposition into subtasks (e.g., object detection, trajectory shape planning).
 - **Code Generation:** Directs the MLLM to either call existing APIs or generate Python code to produce trajectory segments, which are then concatenated. This component also includes mechanisms for detecting and debugging execution errors automatically.

2. **Handling Open-Set Objects:**
 - OpenNav employs a **multi-expert object perception system** to manage the variability of objects and their descriptions (category, feature-based, functional).
 - The first expert is the **Open Vocabulary Perception System (OVPS)**, a set of open-source vision-language models responsible for object detection, segmentation, caption generation, and extracting attributes like position, size, and nearest reachable points (NRPs) on drivable regions.
 - The second expert is the **MLLM itself**, which leverages its multimodal understanding and reasoning capabilities to interpret the OVPS outputs, resolve ambiguities, and make informed decisions.
 - OVPS output is provided to the MLLM as an annotated RGB image (with object contours and numerical identifiers) alongside textual descriptions, enabling richer environmental perception than text alone.

3. **Instruction Grounding and Zero-Shot Trajectory Synthesis via MLLM:**
 - The MLLM processes the environmental perception (annotated images, text descriptions) and the task instruction. It performs semantic understanding and task planning, identifying targets, relevant objects, and spatial relationships (e.g., "on your right").
 - The MLLM then infers the desired *trajectory shape* (e.g., via intermediate waypoints) and synthesizes a Python script to generate the trajectory (specifying start, intermediate, and end points).
 - This script is executed in a Python interpreter. Crucially, the MLLM automatically detects and debugs errors, re-executing the script until a valid dense 3D trajectory sequence is produced. This "code as policy" approach allows for flexible, task-aligned trajectory generation.

4. **Geometry-Compliant Trajectory Refinement:**
 - Recognizing that MLLMs may struggle with precise, smooth, and collision-free trajectory generation, OpenNav integrates classical path planning.
 - The MLLM-generated trajectory (a "coarse trajectory") is projected onto a 2D Bird-Eye-View (BEV) value map.
 - This **BEV value map** is dynamically updated: regions along the MLLM's coarse trajectory are assigned a lower cost, encouraging the planner to follow this path. Occupancy information (from the occupancy map) assigns high costs to obstacles, ensuring collision avoidance. Semantic information can also influence costs (e.g., higher cost for sidewalks vs. paved roads).
 - An **A* algorithm** then uses this value map as its objective function to generate a refined, collision-free trajectory that is both geometry-compliant and aligned with the linguistic instruction.
 - Finally, B-spline smoothing is applied to the refined trajectory for smoothness.

#### 3.2. B. Open Vocabulary Perception System (OVPS)

The OVPS is crucial for providing rich, multimodal environmental information to the MLLM:

1. **Open Vocabulary Object Perception Module:**
 - Takes an RGB image `I(t)` as input.
 - Uses **Recognize Anything Model (RAM)** to identify open-set object categories.
 - Passes recognized categories and the image to **Grounding DINO** for open-set object detection and bounding box generation.
 - Uses **TAP (Tokenize Anything via Prompting)**, guided by Grounding DINO's bounding boxes, to perform segmentation and captioning of primary objects, yielding masks and textual descriptions.
 - Crucially, it generates *both* text-based prompts (captions, global position, sizes, NRPs) *and* annotated RGB images (object contours with numerical identifiers) for the MLLM. This multimodal input preserves visual details and aids MLLMs in differentiating detected objects.

2. **Map Reconstruction Module:**
 - Built upon the OpenGraph framework.
 - Utilizes multisensor calibration and fusion to project 3D LiDAR point clouds onto a 2D image plane, extracting object-specific 3D point clouds aligned with their masks.
 - Generates a 2D **occupancy map** (for collision avoidance) and a 2D **semantic map** (for class information), which together form the initial BEV value map for trajectory planning and 3D visualization.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Primarily SemanticKITTI, a large-scale outdoor Autonomous Vehicle Dataset (AVD), for controlled experiments. Real-world validation performed on a Husky robot (both indoor and outdoor scenes).
- **Task Types:** "Move-to-object" tasks (specific, ambiguous) and "High-level language instruction" tasks (requiring inference and decomposition).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success Rate (SR) and Navigation Error (NE) for navigation tasks. Fréchet Distance and Normalized Dynamic Time Warping (NDTW) for trajectory shape similarity. Collision rate for safety.
- **Baselines:** Compared against LLM-TG (a text-only MLLM planning framework) and ablation studies with A* (geometry-only) and VLT-Code (MLLM-only direct trajectory generation).

This comprehensive methodology allows OpenNav to effectively address the challenges of open-world navigation by synergistically combining the semantic reasoning and generative capabilities of MLLMs with the precision and safety guarantees of classical robotic perception and planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate OpenNav's effectiveness in open-world navigation, particularly its robustness to open-set instructions and objects, and its ability to generate language-aligned, geometry-compliant trajectories.

#### 4.1. A. Navigation under Open-Set Instruction and Objects

The comparison with LLM-TG, a text-only MLLM planning approach, highlights the critical advantage of OpenNav's multimodal input and integrated perception-planning framework:

- **Specific Object Navigation:** Both OpenNav and LLM-TG perform similarly, achieving high success rates (100%) when instructions are unambiguous and the target object is clearly defined and uniquely present.
- **Ambiguous Object Navigation:** This is where OpenNav demonstrates a significant superiority.
 - OpenNav achieves a **success rate of 84%** (42/50 tasks), while LLM-TG significantly degrades to **13/50 tasks (26% success rate)**.
 - **Navigation Error (NE):** OpenNav shows a much lower average navigation error (1.68) compared to LLM-TG (4.91).
 - **Reasons for LLM-TG Failures:**
 1. **Dependence on Object Detector Accuracy:** LLM-TG fails when the underlying object detector makes errors (e.g., misclassifying a "cylindrical building" as a "trash bin"). Relying solely on the textual caption means the MLLM cannot correct for these perception errors.
 2. **Limited Granularity of Captioned Information:** When multiple similar objects exist or subtle spatial cues are involved (e.g., "house on your right"), text-only descriptions often lack the necessary detail for disambiguation.
 - **OpenNav's Advantage:** By providing *annotated images* alongside text, OpenNav allows the MLLM to leverage its multimodal understanding. It can "see" the object's visual characteristics and numerical label, even if the initial textual caption from the OVPS is flawed, enabling it to verify or correct the target identification. It successfully uses spatial relations from the image to disambiguate targets.
- **High-Level Language Instruction Tasks:** OpenNav also shows a substantial advantage in these complex scenarios (40/50 successes vs. 23/50 for LLM-TG). This reinforces the hypothesis that a unified perception-and-planning framework is crucial, as robots need to "plan based on what they see" rather than being limited by abstracted textual descriptions.

#### 4.2. B. Language Guided Zero-Shot Trajectory Synthesis (Ablation Study)

This experiment evaluates how well the generated trajectories align with human intent and environmental constraints:

- **A* Algorithm (Geometry-only):**
 - **Collision Rate:** 0/30 collisions (excellent in collision avoidance).
 - **Trajectory Similarity:** Poor (Fréchet Distance: 24.20, NDTW: 0.08). This is expected, as A* prioritizes the shortest path between start and goal, lacking any semantic understanding or ability to shape trajectories based on human-like intent.
- **VLT-Code (MLLM-only, direct trajectory generation):**
 - **Collision Rate:** 16/30 collisions (very high). This demonstrates that while MLLMs can infer task intent, they lack the intrinsic depth and geometry information needed for collision-free path generation.
 - **Trajectory Similarity:** Improved over A* (Fréchet Distance: 17.53, NDTW: 0.16). This indicates that the MLLM can generate trajectories that align more closely with linguistic instructions than a purely geometric planner.
- **OpenNav (Full Framework):**
 - **Collision Rate:** 2/30 collisions (significantly reduced, comparable to A*).
 - **Trajectory Similarity:** Best performance (Fréchet Distance: 12.60, NDTW: 0.38). OpenNav demonstrates a clear and substantial improvement in both metrics.
 - **Conclusion:** OpenNav successfully integrates the MLLM's reasoning and code-generation capabilities (for intent-aligned coarse trajectories) with the geometry-based precision of classical planners (via dynamic BEV value maps for refinement). This hybrid approach allows the robot to balance adhering to linguistic instructions with ensuring safety and collision avoidance. Examples show OpenNav effectively incorporating intermediate waypoints and avoiding obstacles based on semantic understanding guided by the MLLM.

In summary, the results strongly support OpenNav's design choices, proving that multimodal inputs and a tightly integrated planning-perception loop are essential for robust open-world navigation with large language models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OpenNav represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied intelligence and robotic navigation, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Pioneering Zero-Shot Open-World Navigation:** To the best of the authors' knowledge, OpenNav is the first framework to directly generate dense navigation trajectories using an MLLM for outdoor environments, capable of handling open-set instructions and open-set objects in a true zero-shot manner. This means it requires no pre-trained skills, motion primitives, or in-context examples, drastically increasing its deployability and versatility.
- **Bridging High-Level Language and Low-Level Control:** A critical challenge in robotics is translating abstract language commands into concrete robot actions. OpenNav successfully bridges this gap by enabling MLLMs to synthesize executable trajectory code, moving beyond the traditional role of LLMs as mere high-level planners or action sequence generators. This allows for far more nuanced and flexible control over robot motion.
- **Robustness through Multimodal Integration:** The proposed multi-expert system, which seamlessly integrates state-of-the-art MLLMs with an open-vocabulary perception system, significantly enhances the robot's scene comprehension. The use of a multimodal interface (providing both textual and visual annotated information to the MLLM) proves crucial in improving robustness against object detection errors and linguistic ambiguities, which are common in real-world, unstructured environments.
- **Synergy of AI and Classical Robotics:** OpenNav effectively combines the strengths of advanced MLLMs (reasoning, code generation, function-calling) with classical planning techniques (like A* and occupancy maps). This hybrid approach ensures that while the robot understands human-like instructions and environmental semantics, its movements are also geometry-compliant, smooth, and collision-free. This demonstrates a promising paradigm for future robotic systems.
- **Practical Validation:** The validation of OpenNav on large-scale autonomous vehicle datasets (SemanticKITTI) and, crucially, on a real-world Husky ground robot in both indoor and outdoor scenes, underscores its practical applicability and robustness. This moves the research beyond theoretical claims or simulator-only experiments, showcasing its potential for real-world deployment.
- **Advancement in Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling robots to understand and execute complex, free-form natural language instructions, OpenNav significantly improves the naturalness and intuitiveness of human-robot interaction. This fosters a more accessible and adaptable relationship between humans and autonomous systems.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens up new avenues for research, particularly in extending this method to even more complex and dynamic environments, potentially incorporating real-time learning from interaction and even more sophisticated common-sense reasoning for truly adaptive navigation.

In conclusion, OpenNav represents a substantial contribution to embodied AI, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in language-guided robot navigation. Its ability to operate in open-world settings without prior examples marks a significant step towards developing more intelligent, autonomous, and versatile robotic systems capable of performing diverse tasks in unpredictable real-world scenarios.
