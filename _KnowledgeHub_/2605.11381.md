# Kairos: A Scalable Serving System for Physical AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Kairos is a scalable serving system explicitly designed for Physical AI workloads, uniquely addressing the generate-execute loop's characteristics by dynamically adapting action generation through diffusion confidence. The system also employs an execution-aware scheduler, achieving up to 2.67 times longer average execution horizons at comparable accuracy and reducing end-to-end latency by 31.8–66.5% compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Existing AI serving systems, primarily designed for digital AI, are ill-suited for the unique generate-execute loop, action chunking, and dynamic world interaction inherent in Physical AI tasks.
- The use of static, conservative execution horizons in Physical AI leads to inefficient resource utilization, with potential for executing 62-87% more actions without accuracy degradation.
- Current inference schedulers are 'execution-unaware,' ignoring real-world robot execution times, which results in suboptimal task prioritization and inflated end-to-end latency across robot fleets.

## Method

- **Kairos** introduces a ==dynamic execution horizon policy== that leverages inherent ==diffusion confidence signals== from physical AI models to adapt the number of actions executed per chunk without additional inference cost.
- It implements an ==execution-aware scheduler== that prioritizes tasks by unifying inference and real-world execution durations into a ==wait ratio== metric to minimize overall robot idle time.
- The system supports ==hybrid edge-cloud deployments== through ==intelligent offloading==, where requests are dynamically dispatched to the optimal compute tier based on estimated latencies.

## Results

- The diffusion confidence-based dynamic execution horizon consistently Pareto-dominated static horizons, achieving up to **2.67 times longer** average execution at comparable accuracy, or improving task accuracy by up to **30%**.
- Kairos reduced average end-to-end latency by **31.8–66.5%** and P95 latency by **22.2–52.0%** under peak load compared to FIFO and Autellix baselines.
- In hybrid edge-cloud deployments, Kairos lowered average latency by **36.9–47.7%** compared to edge-only and **51.9–67.9%** compared to cloud-only serving at high arrival rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The iterative diffusion process used by physical AI models inherently provides a per-action confidence signal that can be exploited to dynamically adjust execution horizons on a per-round basis.
> - True task urgency in Physical AI is determined by when the next action chunk is critically needed by the robot, necessitating a scheduler that accounts for both inference and real-world execution durations.
> - A synergistic approach combining dynamic action chunking with an execution-aware scheduler yields greater efficiency and lower end-to-end latency than either mechanism alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Physical AI, encompassing intelligent systems that interact with the real world, is experiencing substantial growth. This expansion is driven by the emergence of frontier foundation models, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA), Video Action Models (VAM), and World Action Models (WAM), which enable robotic platforms to map sensory observations to motor commands with increased generality. As these physical AI models grow in size and the deployment of robot fleets scales to hundreds or thousands, offloading inference to shared serving infrastructure becomes a critical requirement for cost efficiency.

However, the paper identifies a significant gap: existing AI serving systems, primarily designed for digital AI tasks like large language models (LLMs) for text generation, are fundamentally unsuited for the unique characteristics of Physical AI. Digital AI inference is typically decoupled from the real world, producing tokens that remain valid regardless of consumption timing. In contrast, Physical AI tasks involve a continuous "generate-execute" loop, characterized by:
1. **Action Chunking**: Models generate actions in discrete chunks, which robots execute sequentially over time.
2. **Asynchronous Interleaving**: Inference for the next action chunk often runs in parallel with the execution of the current chunk to minimize robot idling.
3. **Dynamic World Interaction**: The physical world is dynamic, causing sensory observations and the actions derived from them to grow stale as time progresses and actions are executed. This necessitates frequent re-planning with fresh observations.

These characteristics introduce two critical challenges that current digital AI serving systems do not address:
- **Accuracy-Efficiency Tradeoff**: The "execution horizon" (the number of actions executed from a chunk before new inference) directly impacts this tradeoff. A shorter horizon means more frequent, accurate re-planning but higher inference load. A longer horizon reduces inference cost but risks executing stale, inaccurate actions. Current practice sets this horizon statically and conservatively, leading to inefficient resource utilization. The paper indicates that 62-87% of rounds could execute up to 2.8 times more actions without accuracy loss, presenting a significant missed efficiency opportunity.
- **Execution-Aware Scheduling**: Physical AI task latency is not solely determined by inference time but heavily influenced by real-world execution times, which can be significant and variable. Existing schedulers prioritize tasks based on inference time or queuing delays, oblivious to the robot's ongoing execution state. This can lead to suboptimal prioritization, with 50-93% of tasks being misidentified in terms of their true urgency, resulting in inflated end-to-end latency across robot fleets.

Kairos positions itself as the first serving system explicitly designed to overcome these challenges. It addresses the fundamental differences between digital and physical AI serving by deeply integrating the generate-execute loop into its core design, thereby bridging a critical gap in infrastructure support for scalable Physical AI deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to design and evaluate a scalable serving system, Kairos, that specifically addresses the limitations of existing digital AI serving infrastructure when applied to Physical AI workloads. The motivation stems from the rapid growth of Physical AI and the increasing scale of both foundation models and robot fleets, which necessitate efficient and performant serving solutions.

The key objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Dynamically Manage the Accuracy-Efficiency Tradeoff in Action Generation**:
 - **Motivation**: In Physical AI, models generate "chunks" of actions. The number of actions from this chunk that a robot executes before requesting a new inference (the "execution horizon") is crucial. A shorter horizon leads to more frequent re-planning with fresher observations, improving accuracy, but increases inference load and cost. A longer horizon reduces inference costs but risks executing stale actions, degrading accuracy, especially in complex or dynamic environments. Current industry practice involves setting a static, conservative execution horizon for a task based on its most complex phase, leading to excessive inference and inefficient resource utilization during simpler phases. Empirical data showed that 62-87% of task rounds could safely execute significantly more actions (up to 2.8x) without accuracy degradation.
 - **Objective**: To develop a mechanism within the serving system that can dynamically adapt the execution horizon on a per-round basis, balancing accuracy requirements with inference efficiency. This adaptation must occur without incurring additional inference costs.

2. **Develop an Execution-Aware Scheduling Mechanism for Multi-Robot Fleets**:
 - **Motivation**: Physical AI tasks involve interleaved phases of action generation (inference) and real-world action execution by the robot. The execution phase can be substantial, often dominating the end-to-end task latency. Furthermore, execution times vary widely across tasks, robots, and even different rounds within the same task. Existing serving schedulers, designed for digital AI (e.g., LLMs), are "execution-unaware"; they only consider inference queuing delays or generation times. This obliviousness leads to suboptimal prioritization, as tasks with long execution phases but short inference phases might be incorrectly deprioritized, increasing overall end-to-end task latency for the entire robot fleet. For example, the paper found that existing schedulers misidentify the urgency of 50-93% of tasks, leading to inflated latencies.
 - **Objective**: To design a scheduler that integrates information about both generation (inference) and execution phases into its prioritization logic. The goal is to minimize the true "wait time" of a robot, defined by when its next action chunk is actually needed, thereby reducing average end-to-end task latency across concurrent robot requests.

3. **Implement and Evaluate a Comprehensive Serving System for Physical AI**:
 - **Motivation**: The theoretical insights regarding dynamic horizons and execution-aware scheduling need to be translated into a practical, implementable system. Furthermore, the effectiveness and scalability of such a system must be rigorously validated across a diverse range of physical AI models, tasks, robot platforms (both simulated and real), and deployment scenarios (e.g., online serving with varying loads, offline serving with varying fleet sizes, and hybrid edge-cloud configurations).
 - **Objective**: To build a functional prototype of Kairos and conduct extensive experimental evaluations to demonstrate its feasibility, performance benefits (e.g., latency reduction), and generalizability compared to state-of-the-art digital AI serving practices.

In essence, Kairos aims to create a serving system that moves beyond passively providing inference, becoming an active participant in the robot's control loop to optimize both individual task performance and fleet-wide resource utilization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Kairos's design is predicated on the principle of "execution-awareness," meaning it actively involves the serving system in the physical AI generate-execute loop. This is achieved through two primary mechanisms: a dynamic execution horizon policy guided by inference confidence and an execution-aware scheduler.

#### 3.1. Dynamic Execution Horizon with Inference Confidence

- **Problem Addressed**: The inefficiency of static execution horizons and the difficulty of dynamically adapting them without expensive reference trajectories or additional inference.
- **Core Insight**: The iterative diffusion process used by physical AI models to generate action chunks inherently provides a per-action confidence signal without additional computational cost. Actions that are still undergoing significant revisions at the final diffusion steps are less certain, while those with stable, diminishing updates are more confident.
- **Mechanism**:
 1. **Diffusion Process Exploitation**: Physical AI models generate action chunks through a fixed number of iterative refinement steps (K steps). At each step, an update is produced for every action in the chunk.
 2. **Confidence Indicator**: Kairos uses the magnitude of the final diffusion step update for each action as a confidence indicator. A larger final update suggests the model is still uncertain about that particular action.
 3. **Execution Horizon Policy Module**: After each generation round, Kairos exposes these per-action, per-step diffusion updates (U) to a configurable policy module.
 4. **Default Threshold-Based Policy**: Kairos provides a default policy that, for each action in the generated chunk, compares the magnitude of its final diffusion step update against a threshold (1+t) times the mean of its earlier updates.
 5. **Horizon Determination**: The policy sequentially scans the actions from A1 onwards. The execution horizon (H) for the current round is set to (i-1), where Ai is the first action whose final update magnitude exceeds the defined threshold. Actions from Ai onwards are discarded. This ensures that only confidently generated actions are executed.
 6. **Minimum Horizon**: Kairos ensures a minimum number of actions per round by setting H = max(H_thresh, H_min), where H_min is the smallest static horizon evaluated.

#### 3.2. Execution-Aware Scheduler

- **Problem Addressed**: Suboptimal end-to-end latency caused by existing schedulers being unaware of robot execution phases and the asynchronous nature of Physical AI tasks.
- **Core Insight**: Task urgency in Physical AI is determined by when the *next action chunk is truly needed*, considering both inference and execution durations. Kairos unifies these delays into a "wait ratio" priority signal.
- **Wait Time Formalization**:
 1. **Optimal Progress**: Kairos quantifies wait time by comparing a task's actual progress against its optimal progress, which involves maximally overlapping generation and execution.
 2. **Phase Dominance**: The measurement of wait time depends on whether a round is "generation-dominated" or "execution-dominated."
 - If execution dominates (robot is still busy), wait time is measured as the idle gap between the current execution ending and the *next* execution starting.
 - If generation dominates (inference is the bottleneck), wait time is measured as the gap between the current generation ending and the *next* generation starting.
 3. **State Management**: Kairos maintains a per-task state record of generation and execution intervals. The client continuously sends `Last Exec Info` (previous execution start/end timestamps and remaining actions) to the server, allowing Kairos to incrementally build a complete history and compute wait times.
- **Scheduling Policy (Algorithm 1)**:
 1. **Wait Ratio Calculation**: For each pending request, a `wait ratio` (`w_r`) is computed as the sum of accumulated wait times (`W_j`) divided by the task's elapsed lifetime (`t_now - t_r_start`). A higher wait ratio indicates disproportionate delay.
 2. **Hierarchical Priority Bucketing**: To mitigate issues with continuous wait ratios (e.g., round-robin behavior), Kairos discretizes `w_r` into `B` equal-width buckets (default 10). Requests are binned, and buckets are processed from highest to lowest priority.
 3. **Aging Mechanism**: To prevent starvation, tasks that have been skipped for `A` consecutive rounds are promoted to a higher priority bucket.
 4. **Within-Bucket Ordering**: Within each bucket, ties are broken by prioritizing tasks with the *longest estimated execution latency (descending order)*. This strategy aims to reduce tail latency by ensuring long-running execution phases do not starve other tasks. The current round's execution latency is estimated based on the `last observed execution duration`, leveraging the empirical observation that execution phases are often continuous (52.3-85.4% of consecutive rounds differ by <10%). This estimate is also scaled by an aging factor `(1 + skipped)` to prevent generation-dominant tasks from being perpetually deprioritized.
 5. **Observation Re-fetching**: If an observation becomes stale during queuing, the scheduler re-fetches it from the client to ensure the most up-to-date input for inference.
 6. **Dynamic Batching**: Dispatched requests are batched up to an engine's maximum batch size, determined by offline-profiled latency-vs-batch-size curves (`Engine Profiles`).
- **Hybrid Edge-Cloud Support**:
 1. **Tiered Profiles**: `Engine Profiles` include compute and network latencies for both edge and cloud serving tiers.
 2. **Intelligent Offloading**: The scheduler prioritizes dispatching to the edge server but dynamically offloads requests to the cloud if the estimated cloud latency (including WAN round-trip time) is lower than the expected queuing delay at the edge. For models with state, historical data transfer costs are also factored into the estimation.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- Kairos is implemented in approximately 3.7K lines of Python, built atop LeRobot, an open-source framework for physical AI.
- The client-side integrates with LeRobot's robot runtime via a lightweight shim, communicating with the server via gRPC. It sends `Task ID`, `Round ID`, `observation`, and `Last Exec Info`.
- The server is an asynchronous event loop. Models are compiled with `torch.compile` for optimization. The `Action Manager` on the return path applies the dynamic horizon policy before returning actions to the client.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Kairos encompassed a range of physical AI models, simulation benchmarks, and real-robot experiments, comparing its performance against FIFO and Autellix (a multi-round-aware scheduler) baselines.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of Diffusion Confidence for Dynamic Horizon

- **Pareto-Dominance**: Across six diverse physical AI workloads, the diffusion confidence-based dynamic execution horizon (Kairos's approach) consistently Pareto-dominated the static execution horizon baseline.
- **Efficiency Gains**: At comparable task accuracy, the dynamic horizon achieved up to 2.67 times longer average execution horizons, indicating significantly reduced inference load.
- **Accuracy Improvements**: When maintaining comparable average execution horizons, Kairos improved task accuracy by up to 30%.
- **Conclusion**: This validates that adapting the execution horizon dynamically, on a per-round basis, offers superior accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs compared to static, task-level settings.

#### 4.2. End-to-End System Performance

- **Online Serving with Varying Request Rates (Single Edge Server)**:
 - Kairos consistently achieved lower end-to-end latency, with performance gains increasing under higher task arrival rates.
 - At peak load, Kairos reduced **average latency by 31.8–66.5%**, **P25 latency by 39.5–88.4%**, and **P95 latency by 22.2–52.0%** compared to both FIFO and Autellix.
 - Autellix, a multi-round scheduler, often performed worse than FIFO, which was attributed to its sole reliance on total generation time for prioritization, leading to starvation of shorter tasks.
 - **Ablation Studies (Impact of components)**:
 - Kairos with only dynamic execution horizon (using baseline schedulers) reduced latency, confirming load reduction from fewer inference requests. However, a significant latency gap (up to 43.9% P25) remained compared to full Kairos, indicating the scheduler's importance.
 - Kairos with only the execution-aware scheduler (using static horizons) still yielded substantial latency reductions (P25 21.1–63.5%), validating the scheduler's effectiveness in prioritizing requests based on both phases.
 - The full Kairos system, combining both dynamic horizon and execution-aware scheduling, delivered the largest latency reductions, demonstrating synergistic benefits.

- **Online Serving Across Hybrid Edge-Cloud Deployments**:
 - In a hybrid setup (edge A6000 + cloud A100 over a 100ms WAN link), Kairos (hybrid) reduced average latency by **36.9–47.7%** over Kairos (edge-only) and **51.9–67.9%** over Kairos (cloud-only) at the highest arrival rates.
 - Cloud-only serving generally performed the worst due to WAN latency overhead per request, except for one workload (`mimic-video`) where the cloud GPU's faster compute outweighed the WAN cost.
 - This demonstrates Kairos's ability to generalize to heterogeneous environments, leveraging cloud resources to absorb load and reduce latency.

- **Offline Serving with Varying Fleet Sizes (Dedicated Robots)**:
 - When serving dedicated robot fleets (10 to 100 concurrent robots), Kairos consistently outperformed baselines, with gains scaling with fleet size.
 - At 10 robots, Kairos showed **20.4–21.4% average latency reduction**.
 - At 50 robots, this increased to **33.9–37.8% reduction**.
 - At 100 robots, the reduction reached **41.0–42.8%**.
 - This indicates effective scalability, as the dynamic horizon reduces total inference requests, and the execution-aware scheduler efficiently utilizes serving capacity under increasing contention.

#### 4.3. Trace Fidelity Evaluation with Real Robots

- Experiments on a bimanual SO-101 robot showed that contention-induced delays (simulated by replaying traces) did not impact task accuracy, maintaining consistency with contention-free runs. This confirmed the validity of the trace-driven evaluation methodology for scalability studies.

#### 4.4. Sensitivity and Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Bucket Size**: An optimal range of 5–10 buckets for wait ratio discretization balanced priority granularity and scheduling stability, preventing performance degradation from too few (up to 33.9% worse latency) or too many buckets.
- **Impact of Intra-Bucket Policy**: Sorting within buckets by estimated execution latency (descending) reduced tail latency (P95 by up to 17.9%) and improved average latency compared to a FIFO policy within buckets.
- **Impact of Network Latency in Hybrid Setup**: Hybrid serving performance was more sensitive to base network latency than bandwidth, particularly for workloads with small observation payloads, as base latency primarily governs offloading opportunities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Kairos represents a significant contribution to the field of Physical AI serving systems, addressing a critical and previously underexplored area. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering a New Category of Serving Systems**: Kairos is presented as the first serving system specifically designed for the unique inference and execution characteristics of Physical AI. By making the "generate-execute" loop a first-class citizen, it fundamentally re-evaluates and optimizes how AI models interact with real-world robotic systems. This establishes a new architectural paradigm distinct from traditional digital AI serving.

2. **Enabling Scalable and Efficient Physical AI Deployments**: The core mechanisms of Kairos directly address the challenges of efficiency and performance at scale.
 - **Dynamic Resource Optimization**: The diffusion confidence-based dynamic execution horizon allows for intelligent, per-round adaptation of inference frequency. This translates to substantial reductions in inference load and computational costs (up to 2.67x longer execution horizons at matched accuracy) for large robot fleets, without compromising task accuracy.
 - **Minimized End-to-End Latency**: The execution-aware scheduler, by integrating real-world execution durations into its prioritization logic, significantly reduces end-to-end task latency (31.8–66.5% reduction over baselines). This is crucial for responsive and robust operation of robots in dynamic, time-sensitive environments, such as warehouses or healthcare settings.

3. **Accelerating Adoption of Advanced Robotics**: By making the serving of large Physical AI foundation models more efficient, performant, and scalable, Kairos removes a significant bottleneck to their widespread adoption. This could accelerate the deployment of advanced robotic platforms, including dexterous manipulators and humanoids, across various sectors like logistics, manufacturing, and domestic applications.

4. **Architectural Flexibility with Hybrid Edge-Cloud Support**: Kairos's intelligent offloading capabilities for hybrid edge-cloud deployments offer practical and flexible architectural solutions. This allows organizations to leverage both low-latency edge computing and the scalable capacity of cloud GPUs, optimizing for specific operational constraints like latency, cost, and bursty workloads.

5. **Foundational for Future Research and Development**: The work highlights the necessity of deeply integrating physical world characteristics into AI infrastructure design. This can inspire further research in several areas:
 - **Beyond Diffusion Confidence**: Exploring other real-time indicators for dynamic control and task adaptation.
 - **Advanced Scheduling Algorithms**: Developing more sophisticated execution-aware scheduling policies that account for complex task interdependencies or varying robot capabilities.
 - **Unified AI/Robot Stack**: Driving towards more tightly integrated software and hardware stacks that seamlessly manage the interplay between AI inference and physical execution.

6. **Community Contribution**: The planned open-sourcing of Kairos is a valuable contribution, providing a concrete system and baseline for the Physical AI research and development community to build upon and further advance the field.

In summary, Kairos provides a robust and empirically validated solution to a critical challenge in Physical AI, paving the way for more efficient, performant, and scalable deployments of intelligent robotic systems in the real world.
