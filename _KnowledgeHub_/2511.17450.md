# Planning with Sketch-Guided Verification for Physics-Aware Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> SketchVerify introduces a training-free framework for physics-aware video generation, iteratively refining motion plans using lightweight video sketches and a multimodal verifier. This approach achieves substantially more physically plausible and temporally consistent video outputs while being significantly more efficient than prior iterative refinement methods.

## Problem

- Traditional image-to-video (I2V) models often struggle to produce videos with physically realistic and temporally consistent object motion, leading to unnatural artifacts.
- Single-shot planning methods for motion guidance are prone to error propagation, while iterative refinement methods that generate full videos are computationally expensive and slow, hindering their practical application.
- Ensuring adherence to fundamental physical laws (e.g., non-penetration, gravity, stable deformation) in generated videos is a critical challenge for real-world applications.

## Method

- The **SketchVerify** framework iteratively refines motion plans at test time by generating multiple candidate object trajectories from a high-level **MLLM** planner.
- Instead of full video generation, lightweight video sketches are rendered from these trajectories by compositing object masks onto a static background, significantly reducing computational cost.
- A multimodal vision-language verifier (Gemini 2.5-Flash) evaluates these sketches for semantic alignment with instructions and adherence to key physical laws, scoring and selecting the optimal plan before final video synthesis.

## Results

- SketchVerify achieves the highest instruction following (2.08) and physical law adherence (e.g., 1.00 for Newton/Gravity/Fluid) on WorldModelBench, with an overall "Sum" score of 8.71.
- It demonstrates a planning time of 4.71 minutes, an approximately 93% speed-up compared to iterative refinement methods that rely on full video generation (e.g., PhyT2V at 61.86 minutes).
- On the PhyWorldBench, SketchVerify attains the highest overall score (19.84), boosting object-event accuracy by 22% and physical accuracy by 18% relative to baseline I2V models like Wan-2.1.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling motion plan verification from full video synthesis via lightweight video sketches allows for efficient test-time iterative refinement, providing a nearly 93% speed-up over full video-based iterative methods.
> - Multimodal verification (visual input + language) is crucial for accurately assessing both semantic instruction following and fine-grained physical plausibility, outperforming language-only verifiers.
> - Iterative sampling and scoring of candidate trajectories by MLLM planners and verifiers enables the selection of dynamically coherent and physically consistent motion plans prior to expensive pixel-level diffusion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of **Image-to-Video (I2V) generation**: producing videos that are not only visually high-quality and semantically aligned with a given prompt but also exhibit **physically realistic and temporally consistent motion**.

Traditional video generative models, while impressive in visual fidelity, often struggle with interpreting fine-grained motion instructions and adhering to plausible physical dynamics. Objects might float unnaturally, penetrate surfaces, or accelerate/decelerate in physically impossible ways. This limitation hinders their application in domains like robotic manipulation, autonomous driving, and game content creation, where accurate physical interactions are paramount.

Existing approaches to improve motion fidelity broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Single-shot planning**: These methods generate a single control sequence (ee.g., object trajectories) using large language models (LLMs) or multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) to guide video generation. While straightforward, they are vulnerable to inaccuracies in the initial plan, which can propagate and lead to physically implausible or inconsistent motions without any self-correction mechanism. Examples include methods based on bounding-box trajectories or scene-level keyframes.
2. **Iterative refinement**: This approach attempts to mitigate instability by progressively updating prompts or control signals over multiple steps. Models like RPG, PhyT2V, and VideoRepair iteratively evaluate and refine fully generated videos. While this can enhance visual realism, it incurs substantial computational overhead due to repeated calls to computationally intensive diffusion-based video generators. This makes test-time optimization prohibitively expensive and slow, often requiring tens of minutes for a single generation cycle.

"Planning with Sketch-Guided Verification for Physics-Aware Video Generation" (SketchVerify) positions itself as a novel solution that effectively bridges the gap between these two paradigms. It aims to achieve the benefits of iterative refinement—improved motion fidelity and physical plausibility—without incurring the exorbitant computational cost of repeatedly synthesizing full videos. By introducing a **training-free, sketch-verification-based planning framework**, SketchVerify enables efficient test-time search and refinement of motion plans, fundamentally altering how dynamic coherence is achieved in video generation. It tackles the core problem of ensuring *physically plausible object dynamics* within generated videos by shifting the verification process to a lightweight, pre-synthesis stage, thereby improving the reliability and applicability of generative video models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind SketchVerify are clearly articulated by the authors:

1. **Improve Motion Quality and Physical Plausibility**: The central goal is to generate videos with "dynamically coherent trajectories"—meaning motions that are both physically plausible (adhering to Newtonian physics, non-penetration, gravity, and shape stability) and semantically consistent with the user's instructions. Existing models frequently fail to meet these criteria, producing unrealistic movements.
2. **Enhance Temporal Consistency**: Beyond individual physical laws, the method aims for long-term consistency in object movements and interactions across video frames, mitigating artifacts like objects stretching unnaturally or appearing/disappearing abruptly.
3. **Overcome Limitations of Single-Shot Planning**: The authors motivate their work by highlighting the "vulnerability to inaccuracies or noise" in single-shot planning approaches. These methods lack a feedback loop, leading to error propagation and implausible motions. SketchVerify seeks to introduce a robust correction mechanism.
4. **Address Computational Inefficiency of Iterative Refinement**: While iterative refinement methods can improve video quality, their reliance on "multiple calls to the video generator" makes them computationally expensive and slow. A key motivation for SketchVerify is to achieve comparable or superior quality while being "substantially more efficient" by decoupling refinement from full video synthesis.
5. **Develop a Training-Free Solution**: The framework is designed to operate "training-free" at test time. This means it does not require additional large-scale datasets or extensive fine-tuning for specific physical laws or motion patterns, promoting zero-shot generalization across diverse scenarios.
6. **Decouple Motion Planning from Video Synthesis**: By refining motion plans at a "sketch or layout level" before full diffusion-based video generation, the method aims to separate the complex task of reasoning about object dynamics from the computationally intensive process of generating visual details. This modularity is crucial for efficiency and quality control.

In essence, SketchVerify seeks to deliver highly realistic and controllable video generation by intelligently planning and verifying motion before committing to expensive pixel-level synthesis, offering a pragmatic and effective solution to a long-standing challenge in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SketchVerify proposes a novel, training-free, and test-time planning framework that iteratively refines motion plans using lightweight video sketches before full video generation. The approach is structured into three main modules: High-Level Planning and Object Parsing, Test-Time Planning (the core innovation), and Trajectory-Conditioned Video Generation.

**1. High-Level Planning and Object Parsing:**
- **High-Level Planning:** Given a natural language prompt, an MLLM (GPT-4.1) decomposes the overall instruction into a sequence of actionable sub-instructions (e.g., "approach the ball," "pick it up"). This mitigates the complexity of long-horizon planning.
- **Object and Background Extraction:** The MLLM also identifies movable objects mentioned in the prompt. A detector-segmenter pair (GroundedSAM) then precisely extracts masks for these objects from the initial image. To create a clean canvas for sketches, the masked object regions are removed from the initial image and inpainted using Omnieraser, resulting in a static background.

**2. Test-Time Planning (SketchVerify Core):**
This module is the heart of SketchVerify, designed to efficiently search for optimal motion trajectories without generating full videos.
- **Trajectory Sampling:** For each sub-instruction, the MLLM Planner (GPT-4.1) generates *K* multiple candidate trajectories for the movable object(s). Each trajectory is represented as a sequence of bounding boxes over a specific number of frames, conditioned on the current visual context (the initial image or the last frame of the previously verified sketch).
- **Video Sketch Rendering:** Instead of generating computationally expensive full videos, each candidate trajectory is converted into a "lightweight video sketch." This involves cropping the segmented object from the initial frame and compositing it frame by frame onto the static background according to the bounding box coordinates in the trajectory. These sketches efficiently visualize the planned motion, preserving essential spatial and temporal structure for verification.
- **Verifier-Guided Scoring:** A multimodal vision-language verifier (Gemini 2.5-Flash) evaluates each video sketch using a two-stage strategy:
 - **Semantic Alignment:** The verifier assesses how well the depicted motion in the sketch aligns with the sub-instruction's intended behavior (e.g., "does the object indeed move towards the basket?"). This yields a semantic score.
 - **Physical Plausibility:** Simultaneously, the verifier evaluates the sketch's adherence to four key physical laws using structured prompts and few-shot in-context learning:
 - **Newtonian Consistency**: Plausible acceleration/deceleration.
 - **Penetration Violation**: Objects should not pass through static scene elements.
 - **Gravitational Coherence**: Vertical motion should be realistic and gravity-consistent.
 - **Deformation Consistency**: Object size and shape should remain stable.
 Each law yields a scalar score, which is then aggregated with the semantic score using weighted combinations.
- **Iterative Trajectory Selection:** The trajectory with the highest aggregated verification score is selected as the optimal motion plan for that sub-instruction. If all candidate scores fall below a quality threshold, the planner is re-prompted with feedback to generate a new batch of trajectories. This iterative loop continues until a satisfactory plan is identified or a retry limit is reached. The last frame of the selected sketch becomes the context for the next sub-instruction, ensuring temporal continuity.

**3. Trajectory-Conditioned Video Generation:**
- **Unified Motion Plan:** Once all sub-instructions have verified trajectories, they are merged into a unified, dense trajectory plan (sequence of object positions over all frames).
- **Final Video Synthesis:** This verified motion plan is then passed to a pre-trained trajectory-conditioned image-to-video diffusion model (ATI-14B). The diffusion model uses this high-quality plan to guide its denoising process, focusing on generating visual fidelity while ensuring the final video adheres to the pre-verified semantically coherent and physically consistent motions.

This modular design ensures that the computationally intensive task of pixel-level video generation only occurs once, after a high-quality, physically plausible motion plan has been established efficiently at test time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SketchVerify demonstrates significant improvements across multiple dimensions of video generation quality and efficiency.

**Quantitative Results:**

- **WorldModelBench Performance:**
 - SketchVerify consistently outperforms state-of-the-art open-source I2V models (e.g., CogVideoX, Cosmos, Wan-2.1) and planning-based baselines (VideoMSG, PhyT2V) across instruction following, physical law coherence, and commonsense consistency.
 - It achieves the highest instruction following score (2.08) and superior physical law adherence (e.g., 1.00 for Newton, Gravity, Fluid; 0.92 for Penetration; 0.89 for Deform).
 - The overall "Sum" score, aggregating all metrics, is highest for SketchVerify (8.71), indicating superior performance across the board.
 - Crucially, SketchVerify achieves this while being **substantially more efficient** in planning time. It completes planning in 4.71 minutes, a nearly 93% speed-up compared to iterative refinement methods like PhyT2V (61.86 minutes) that rely on full video generation.
- **PhyWorldBench Performance:**
 - On this benchmark, specifically designed to test fine-grained physical realism, SketchVerify achieves the highest overall score (19.84) and the strongest performance in the "Physical Standard" category (23.52).
 - It boosts object-event accuracy by 22% and physical accuracy by 18% relative to a strong baseline like Wan-2.1, highlighting its ability to maintain physical consistency and object stability.

**Qualitative Results:**
Visual comparisons (Fig. 4, 7, 8) illustrate that SketchVerify generates videos with:
- **More Realistic Motion:** E.g., smooth forward jumps with realistic limb coordination in human actions, continuous downhill flow for snow, accurate object contact in video games, and stable grasping in robotics.
- **Fewer Artifacts:** Baselines often exhibit unnatural stretching, floating objects, penetration errors, or misaligned collisions, which are significantly reduced or eliminated by SketchVerify.
- **Improved Instruction Following:** Videos more accurately depict the intended actions (e.g., snow melting and sliding down the mountain) compared to baselines where objects remain static or motions are incorrect.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Verifier Modality:** Multimodal verification (visual input + language) significantly improves instruction following and physical plausibility compared to language-only verifiers. Visual context is crucial for capturing fine-grained motion cues and identifying physical violations.
- **Verifier Scale:** Using stronger and larger MLLM verifiers (e.g., Gemini 2.5 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-3B) leads to better semantic and physical reasoning, resulting in higher quality motion plan selection.
- **Planner Choices:** Similarly, using stronger MLLM planners (e.g., GPT-4.1 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-3B) improves instruction following and physical coherence, highlighting the importance of the planner's reasoning capacity.
- **Sampling Budget (K):** Increasing the number of sampled candidate trajectories (K) consistently enhances final motion quality. Even a small budget (K=1) shows gains over no verification (K=0), and K=5 achieves the strongest results, indicating that moderate test-time sampling is effective.
- **Verification Strategy:** Sketch-based verification matches or surpasses the quality of full video-based verification while offering a nearly 10x speedup in planning time. Sketches isolate motion from appearance, enabling cleaner and more reliable spatial-temporal evaluation without diffusion artifacts that might mislead a verifier.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SketchVerify represents a significant advancement in the field of generative video models, offering a practical and effective solution to long-standing challenges in producing physically plausible and temporally coherent videos. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enhanced Physical Realism and Consistency**: By introducing a rigorous verification step that evaluates candidate motion plans against fundamental physical laws, SketchVerify drastically improves the realism and consistency of generated videos. This is crucial for real-world applications where physically impossible actions (e.g., objects floating, passing through each other) undermine credibility and utility.
2. **Unprecedented Efficiency in Quality Control**: The core innovation of sketch-guided verification allows for rapid and efficient evaluation of motion plans without the computational burden of synthesizing full videos. This "order of magnitude" speed-up compared to previous iterative refinement methods makes high-quality video generation more feasible for researchers and practitioners, accelerating development and deployment cycles.
3. **Training-Free Generalization**: The framework's training-free nature at test time is a major advantage. It avoids the need for extensive, specialized datasets or costly fine-tuning for different physical scenarios, enabling broader zero-shot generalization across diverse environments and motion types. This democratizes access to advanced video generation capabilities.
4. **Improved Controllability and Instruction Following**: By embedding a detailed planning and verification loop, SketchVerify ensures that generated videos adhere more faithfully to complex natural language instructions. This level of fine-grained control over object trajectories and interactions is vital for applications requiring precise outcomes.
5. **Broader Applicability across Domains**: The ability to generate videos with reliable physics and consistent motion opens doors for more robust integration into various high-impact domains:
 - **Robotics**: Simulating physically accurate robot movements and interactions with environments for training, testing, and planning.
 - **Autonomous Driving**: Generating realistic traffic scenarios and object behaviors for perception and planning system development.
 - **Gaming and Content Creation**: Creating dynamic, believable character animations and environmental interactions for immersive experiences.
 - **Scientific Simulation**: Visualizing complex physical phenomena with greater accuracy.
6. **Modular and Extensible Design**: By decoupling motion planning and verification from the final video synthesis, SketchVerify provides a modular framework. This allows for independent advancements in planners, verifiers, and video generators, potentially leading to a virtuous cycle of improvements. As MLLMs become more powerful in reasoning and perception, the performance of SketchVerify is likely to further improve without fundamental architectural changes.

While limitations such as fine-grained 3D physics modeling and potential MLLM judgment errors exist, SketchVerify offers a compelling paradigm shift towards more intelligent, efficient, and physically grounded video generation, paving the way for more reliable and controllable AI systems.
