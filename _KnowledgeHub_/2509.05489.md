# Self-Aligned Reward: Towards Effective and Efficient Reasoners

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed the Self-Aligned Reward (SAR), an intrinsic signal, to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of large language models in reasoning tasks. This novel approach combines verifiable rewards with a self-guided perplexity-based metric, yielding models that achieve higher correctness while generating 30% shorter, more concise responses.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning for LLMs relies on coarse, binary verifiable rewards, which fail to provide fine-grained feedback on reasoning quality and efficiency.
- This coarse feedback leads to LLMs generating verbose, redundant, or "overthinking" responses, increasing computational costs and inference latency.
- Current methods to reduce verbosity (e.g., heuristic length penalties) often compromise reasoning accuracy by inadvertently penalizing essential intermediate steps.

## Method

- Introduces the **Self-Aligned Reward** (SAR), an unsupervised ==intrinsic signal== derived from the model's policy, defined by the relative perplexity difference between an answer conditioned on the query and the standalone answer.
- SAR quantifies how much an answer's likelihood is reduced without the query context, thereby rewarding conciseness and query-specificity by favoring answers highly dependent on the query.
- Integrates SAR with traditional binary verifiable rewards using a weighting factor within Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithms.

## Results

- SA-GRPO consistently achieved an average accuracy improvement of at least **4%** and reduced answer length by **30%** across four LLMs and seven benchmarks compared to vanilla GRPO.
- The method established a Pareto-optimal frontier in the accuracy-efficiency trade-off, outperforming length-based baselines by improving both metrics simultaneously.
- SAR-enhanced models demonstrated strong generalization to out-of-domain logical reasoning tasks and preserved the frequency of advanced reasoning behaviors (e.g., backtracking, verification) despite generating fewer tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SAR provides a fine-grained, nuanced reward signal that discriminates between concise and redundant correct answers, and offers partial credit for partially correct reasoning paths.
> - The mechanism of SAR promotes efficient exploitation of query information by assigning high scores to tokens that introduce new query-dependent information and low scores to repetitive content.
> - SAR enables simultaneous improvements in both reasoning accuracy and generation efficiency, demonstrating a content-aware approach that prunes verbosity without sacrificing critical reasoning steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) capable of complex reasoning, particularly in domains such as mathematics and logic, has seen significant advancements through reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. A common approach involves using verifiable rewards, where an LLM's generated solution is checked against a ground truth or a set of rules, resulting in a binary (correct/incorrect) or coarse-grained signal. This paradigm has been effective in enhancing raw reasoning capabilities.

However, the existing research landscape identifies a limitation with these coarse verifiable rewards: they fail to provide fine-grained feedback on the quality and efficiency of the reasoning process itself. This often leads to LLMs generating verbose, redundant, or "overthinking" responses, which can increase computational costs, inference latency, and user frustration. Efforts to mitigate this "overthinking" have included heuristic approaches such as length penalties or brevity-oriented objectives. While these methods can reduce output verbosity, they frequently introduce a trade-off, potentially penalizing essential intermediate reasoning steps and thus compromising accuracy. The use of external reward models is another strategy, but these are susceptible to "reward hacking" and introduce additional training complexity.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing an internally grounded, fine-grained reward mechanism. It positions itself within the area of self-judgment capabilities of LLMs, which has been explored for tasks like hallucination detection, internal state probing, and confidence estimation using entropy-based metrics. Unlike prior work that often sacrifices accuracy for efficiency or relies on external signals, this research introduces a novel intrinsic reward that aims to improve both aspects simultaneously, providing a more balanced and effective approach to training reasoning LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance both the reasoning accuracy and the generation efficiency of Large Language Models (LLMs) when trained with Reinforcement Learning (RL). This dual objective aims to produce LLMs that not only provide correct answers but also do so concisely, reducing unnecessary computational overhead and response length.

The motivation behind this work stems from several limitations observed in current RL-based LLM training for reasoning tasks:

- **Coarse-grained Feedback from Verifiable Rewards:** Current RL methods predominantly rely on verifiable rewards, which are typically binary (correct/incorrect) or offer limited granularity. While effective for initial accuracy gains, this coarse feedback fails to differentiate between an overly verbose but correct solution and a concise, correct one. It also treats partially correct responses the same as entirely wrong ones if the final answer is incorrect. This lack of fine-grained signal can lead LLMs to generate extensive, repetitive, or "overthinking" reasoning paths.
- **Inefficiency and Cost Implications:** The "overthinking" behavior induced by coarse rewards results in unnecessarily long responses. This verbosity translates directly to increased computational costs (more tokens to process) and higher latency during inference, making the LLMs less practical for real-world applications where speed and resource efficiency are important.
- **Accuracy Compromise with Existing Efficiency Solutions:** To counter verbosity, some existing methods introduce explicit length penalties or brevity objectives. However, these often operate in a blunt manner, penalizing any additional tokens regardless of their contribution to the reasoning process. This can inadvertently suppress crucial intermediate steps, leading to a decline in reasoning accuracy. Consequently, these approaches struggle to achieve a satisfactory balance between efficiency and correctness.
- **Undesirability of External Reward Models:** Another alternative involves training external reward models to provide more nuanced feedback. However, these models can be complex to develop, prone to "reward hacking" (where the LLM learns to exploit the reward model rather than truly improve), and introduce additional dependencies in the training pipeline.
- **Need for Internally Grounded, Fine-grained Guidance:** The identified limitations highlight a need for an intrinsic, self-guided signal that can complement verifiable rewards. Such a signal should be capable of discerning between necessary and redundant reasoning, promoting concise and query-specific responses without compromising the accuracy of complex reasoning. This research seeks to fill this gap by developing such an internal mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is the introduction of the Self-Aligned Reward (SAR), a self-guided signal designed to complement traditional verifiable rewards in reinforcement learning (RL) for LLMs.

**Reinforcement Learning Formulation:**
The work utilizes two prevalent RL algorithms: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (specifically, the unbiased Dr. GRPO variant). These algorithms optimize a policy $\pi_{\theta}$ to maximize an expected reward. The standard verifiable reward ($R_{VR}$) in these contexts is binary: 1 if the ground truth is found in the answer, 0 otherwise (Equation 5). This binary reward forms the basis for comparison with SAR-enhanced methods.

**Self-Aligned Reward (SAR) Definition:**
SAR, denoted as $R_{SA}$, is an unsupervised signal derived entirely from the model's policy without external feedback. It is defined based on the relative perplexity difference between an answer conditioned on the query and the standalone answer:
$R_{SA} = \text{clip}\left( \frac{\text{ppl}(a_i) - \text{ppl}(a_i|q)}{\text{ppl}(a_i)}, -1, 1 \right)$ (Equation 6)
where:
- $\text{ppl}(a)$ is the perplexity of the answer $a$ in isolation (without the query context).
- $\text{ppl}(a|q)$ is the perplexity of the answer $a$ conditioned on the query $q$.

Perplexity, defined as $e^{-\frac{1}{|a|}\sum_{j=1}^{|a|} \log P(a_j|a_{1...j-1})}$ or $e^{-\frac{1}{|a|}\sum_{j=1}^{|a|} \log P(a_j|q,a_{1...j-1})}$, quantifies the uncertainty of the model in generating a sequence of tokens. A lower perplexity indicates higher confidence. The SAR formulation essentially measures "how much less likely the answer becomes if the query is not present." A higher $R_{SA}$ value is achieved when the conditioned perplexity $\text{ppl}(a|q)$ is significantly lower than the standalone perplexity $\text{ppl}(a)$, indicating that the answer is highly dependent on and aligned with the query, implying conciseness and query-specificity. Conversely, irrelevant or verbose text would result in similar perplexities, leading to a smaller $R_{SA}$.

**Integration with RL Algorithms:**
SAR is combined with the binary verifiable reward ($R_{VR}$) using a weighting factor $\alpha$:
$R_{\text{SA-PPO/GRPO}}(q, a_i, \text{gt}) = R_{VR} + \alpha R_{SA}$ (Equation 6)
The resulting algorithms are termed SA-PPO and SA-GRPO.

**Analysis and Evaluation Methods:**
1. **Quantitative Case Analysis:** The paper evaluates SAR's ability to provide fine-grained signals by calculating advantage values for six different types of answers (correct and concise, correct but redundant, partly correct, completely wrong, correct but no thought, incorrect and no thought) sampled from math benchmarks. This is compared against standard GRPO and length-based rewards (GRPO-O1, GRPO-ER).
2. **Token-level Importance Analysis:** To understand how SAR promotes efficient exploitation of query information, the self-aligned reward is decomposed into token-level contributions $v(a_j)$. This metric (Equation 8) measures how much the log-probability of a token changes when conditioned on the query, indicating its informational value relative to the query.
3. **Experimental Settings:**
 - **Datasets:** Five math reasoning benchmarks (GSM8k, MATH, NuminaMath 1.5 for training; GSM-symbolic, AIME for out-of-domain evaluation) and two logical reasoning benchmarks (LogicBench, ProntoQA) for further out-of-domain generalization.
 - **Models:** Four base LLMs were used: Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, Phi-3.5-mini, and Gemma3-1B.
 - **Baselines:** PPO, GRPO (verifiable reward only), GRPO-O1 (GRPO with O1-pruner length penalty), and GRPO-ER (GRPO with Efficient Reasoner length penalty).
 - **Metrics:** Accuracy, average answer length (efficiency), and an Accuracy-Efficiency trade-off Score (AES = $\Delta$len + $\gamma\Delta$acc, with $\gamma=5$).
4. **Ablation Study:** The criticality of the verifiable signal and the conditioned perplexity drop in SAR is investigated by ablating these components, comparing SA-GRPO with models using only SAR, only entropy minimization ($-\log \text{ppl}(a|q)$), or a combination of verifiable reward and entropy minimization.
5. **Reasoning Behavior Analysis:** The impact of SAR on advanced reasoning behaviors (backtracking, verification, subgoal setting, enumeration) is assessed by using GPT-4o to annotate the frequency of these behaviors in model outputs, comparing SA-GRPO with GRPO and length-based methods.
6. **Training Cost Analysis:** The computational overhead of SAR during training is quantified by comparing GPU hours for SA-GRPO against GRPO and other baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness and efficiency of Self-Aligned Reward (SAR) in training large language models for reasoning tasks.

- **SAR Provides Accurate and Fine-Grained Signals (Table 2):**
 - Quantitative analysis across different answer types demonstrated that SAR provides a nuanced reward landscape. It assigned the highest advantage values to correct and concise answers.
 - Correct but redundant answers received a lower reward than concise correct ones, promoting efficiency.
 - Partially correct answers with a wrong final result were assigned a higher reward than completely irrelevant or wrong answers, indicating SAR's ability to provide partial credit.
 - Notably, SAR penalized "no thought" answers (e.g., memorized correct answers) even if they were short and correct, suggesting it encourages a meaningful reasoning process.
 - In contrast, verifiable rewards (GRPO) failed to differentiate among correct answers or between partially correct and completely wrong ones. Length-based methods (O1, ER) focused primarily on shortness, sometimes favoring irrelevant but short answers over longer, partially correct ones.

- **SAR Promotes Efficient Exploitation of Query Information (Figure 2):**
 - Token-level analysis of SAR's contribution ($v(a_j)$) revealed that tokens that draw new information from the query (e.g., specific numbers or names mentioned for the first time) received high scores. This indicates that SAR encourages the model to actively integrate query context.
 - Repeating information already generated resulted in low scores. This mechanism explains why SAR favors concise answers and discourages redundancy, as later tokens struggle to introduce novel, query-dependent information once much of the query has been incorporated.

- **Significant Gains in Accuracy and Efficiency (Table 3, Figure 1):**
 - Across four different LLMs (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, Phi-3.5-mini, Gemma3-1B) and seven benchmarks, integrating SAR with GRPO (SA-GRPO) consistently outperformed baseline GRPO.
 - SA-GRPO achieved an average accuracy improvement of at least 4% while simultaneously reducing answer length by 30% compared to vanilla GRPO.
 - SA-GRPO produced answers of comparable or shorter length than length-based methods (GRPO-O1, GRPO-ER) but with significantly higher accuracy. Similar trends were observed for SA-PPO.
 - Traditional RL (PPO, GRPO) improved accuracy but increased answer length. Length-based methods (GRPO-O1, GRPO-ER) reduced length but often compromised accuracy. SAR emerged as the only method that consistently improved both.

- **Pareto-Optimal Frontier in Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off (Figure 3):**
 - SA-GRPO consistently achieved a Pareto-optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency. Its curves in the performance gain vs. length reduction plots were positioned superiorly compared to GRPO-O1 and GRPO-ER.
 - Unlike length-based methods, which frequently showed a reduction in accuracy for efficiency gains, SA-GRPO demonstrated improvements on both axes, positioning its results above the x-axis (positive performance gain) while also achieving significant length reduction.

- **Generalization to Out-of-Domain Logical Reasoning (Table 4):**
 - SAR-enhanced methods (SA-PPO, SA-GRPO) maintained robustness and efficiency on out-of-domain logical reasoning benchmarks (LogicBench, ProntoQA).
 - SA-GRPO improved accuracy compared to GRPO in 5 out of 8 cases and outperformed length-based methods in all cases on these benchmarks, while also reducing answer length.

- **Ablation Study: Verifiable Reward and Conditioned Perplexity Drop are Critical (Table 5):**
 - Removing the verifiable reward (using only $R_{SA}$) led to significantly poor accuracy, indicating the continued necessity of ground-truth signals for developing reasoning skills.
 - Using only entropy minimization ($R_{EM}$) or combining it with $R_{VR}$ resulted in inferior performance compared to SA-GRPO in both accuracy and efficiency. This suggests that maximizing the *conditioned perplexity drop* (as in SAR) is more effective than simple entropy minimization, which can suffer from issues like overconfidence or entropy collapse.

- **Preservation of Reasoning Behaviors (Table 6):**
 - SA-GRPO maintained similar frequencies of advanced reasoning behaviors (backtracking, verification, subgoal setting, enumeration) as vanilla GRPO, despite generating 30% fewer tokens.
 - In contrast, length-based methods (GRPO-O1/ER) showed fewer reasoning behaviors, as these often require additional tokens that are penalized by simple length-based reward functions. This highlights SAR's ability to distinguish useful reasoning from unnecessary verbosity.

- **Efficient Training Cost (Table 7):**
 - SAR introduced negligible computational overhead during the "Update" phase of RL training. The calculation of $\text{ppl}(a)$ (the only additional computation beyond what GRPO already performs) requires only a single forward pass.
 - SA-GRPO even reduced the "Rollout" phase time due to shorter answer lengths, indicating that enhancing reasoning efficiency can also accelerate RL training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Self-Aligned Reward (SAR) as a novel, internally grounded, and fine-grained signal that addresses a critical limitation in the application of reinforcement learning (RL) to large language models (LLMs) for reasoning tasks. The findings suggest several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Advancement in RL for LLM Training:** SAR provides a method to overcome the inherent limitations of coarse-grained or binary verifiable rewards, which have historically led to inefficient and verbose LLM outputs. By offering a more nuanced supervisory signal, this work represents an important step in making RL-based LLM training more effective.
- **Simultaneous Improvement of Accuracy and Efficiency:** Traditionally, there has been a trade-off between reasoning accuracy and generation efficiency in LLMs. Approaches designed to improve one often compromise the other. SAR demonstrates that it is possible to achieve simultaneous improvements in both, with reported gains of up to 4% in accuracy and 30% reduction in response length. This dual benefit is particularly valuable for deploying LLMs in real-world applications.
- **Enhanced Practicality and Cost-Effectiveness:** The reduction in answer length directly translates to lower computational costs, faster inference times, and reduced latency. This makes LLMs trained with SAR more practical and cost-effective for deployment in environments where computational resources are a concern or where rapid response times are critical.
- **Content-Aware Efficiency:** Unlike heuristic length penalties that might indiscriminately remove important reasoning steps, SAR's mechanism is content-aware. It preserves essential reasoning behaviors (such as backtracking, verification, and subgoal setting) while pruning unnecessary verbosity. This ensures that efficiency gains do not come at the expense of the depth or quality of reasoning.
- **Broad Applicability and Generalizability:** The robustness of SAR demonstrated across various LLM architectures (different sizes and families), diverse mathematical reasoning benchmarks, and even out-of-domain logical reasoning tasks indicates its broad applicability. This suggests that SAR is a generic mechanism that can be integrated into various RL frameworks to improve a wide range of reasoning-oriented LLMs.
- **Foundation for Future Research in Intrinsic Rewards:** This work underscores the potential of incorporating intrinsic model self-assessments into the RL framework. It paves the way for further research into developing sophisticated internal reward signals that can guide LLMs toward more human-like, efficient, and reliable reasoning. Future directions might include exploring hybrid reward functions that integrate multimodal signals or more complex internal state representations to extend SAR's benefits to vision-language models or other complex multimodal tasks.
- **Acceleration of RL Training:** The finding that SAR can reduce the "Rollout" phase time during training suggests that improving inference efficiency can feedback positively into the training loop, potentially accelerating the overall RL training process. This offers an additional layer of efficiency beyond just inference.

In summary, SAR represents a meaningful contribution to the field of LLM training, offering a principled method to achieve more effective and efficient reasoners, thereby enhancing their utility in complex problem-solving domains.
