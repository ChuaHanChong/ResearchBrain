# Generating Robot Constitutions & Benchmarks for Semantic Safety

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind researchers introduce ASIMOVBenchmark, a multimodal dataset for evaluating semantic safety in robotics, along with a framework for automatically generating and refining robot constitutions from real-world data, demonstrating improved alignment with human preferences and increased resilience to adversarial attacks when using constitutional AI mechanisms.

## Problem

- Current robotics safety research focuses mainly on immediate physical hazards while neglecting higher-level semantic safety concerns
- Lack of comprehensive multimodal datasets and benchmarks for evaluating foundation model safety in robotics
- Manual creation of robot safety rules is limited in scope and adaptability

## Method

- Development of **ASIMOVBenchmark** using text/image generation and real injury data
- Automated framework for generating ==robot constitutions== from real-world scenarios
- ==Auto-amending== process to refine constitutions and improve alignment

## Results

- Foundation models show strong base capabilities but significant room for improvement in alignment
- Auto-generated constitutions improve model alignment with human preferences, with the auto-amending process further increasing constitution alignment rates
- Constitutional approach demonstrates enhanced resistance to adversarial attacks

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Longer constitutions tend to achieve better alignment with human preferences
> - Auto-amendment process makes constitutions more general and robust
> - Separate "safety brain" architecture improves resilience to adversarial attacks

## Detailed Report

### 1. Research Landscape

- **Context:** This work addresses the emerging area of semantic safety in robotics, which is becoming increasingly important as robots powered by large language models (LLMs) and vision language models (VLMs) are deployed in human-centric environments.
- **Positioning:**
 - **Builds on Constitutional AI:** The paper extends the Constitutional AI approach, which aims to align AI systems with human values by using a set of principles (a constitution) to guide the AI's behavior.
 - **Addresses Limitations of RLHF:** Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) is currently a major approach to aligning language models with human values, but it has challenges around scalability and interpretability. The authors are building on the Constitutional AI approach to address these issues.
 - **Data-Driven Constitution Generation:** Contrasts with manually crafted rules by proposing a data-driven approach to automatically generate robot constitutions from real-world data.
 - **Multimodal Focus:** Unlike previous work on Constitutional AI that primarily focuses on text, this paper incorporates multimodal inputs (vision and language) to improve the safety and alignment of embodied agents.
 - **Red-Teaming and Safety Benchmarks:** Leverages image generation techniques to synthesize potentially unsafe scenarios, addressing the difficulty and risks associated with collecting real-world safety data.
 - **Semantic Safety in Robotics:** This research is part of a broader effort to incorporate semantic notions of safety into robotics and integrate LLMs/VLMs with low-level safety-critical controllers.
- **Related Work:** The paper extensively references related work in the following areas:
 - Alignment from human feedback (Amodei et al., 2016; Christian, 2021; Ji et al., 2023; Russell, 2016)
 - Preference learning approaches (Bai et al., 2022a; Christiano et al., 2017; Ouyang et al., 2022)
 - Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) (Biyik and Sadigh, 2018; Casper et al., 2023; Hejna III and Sadigh, 2023; Sadigh et al., 2017)
 - Constitutional AI (Ahn et al., 2024b; Bai et al., 2022b)
 - Constitutional Embodied AI (Vanhoucke, 2025; Kundu et al., 2023a)
 - Automated constitution discovery (Chen et al., 2024; Petridis et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2024)
 - Semantic safety in robotics (Bajcsy and Fisac, 2024; Varley et al., 2024; Elhafsi et al., 2023; Santos et al., 2024; Sinha et al., 2024; Lidard et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2023; Ahn et al., 2024a)

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objectives:**
 - Develop a large-scale, multimodal benchmark (ASIMOVBenchmark) for evaluating and improving the semantic safety of foundation models in robotics.
 - Create a framework for automatically generating robot constitutions from real-world data to govern robot behavior using Constitutional AI mechanisms.
 - Develop an auto-amending process to refine robot constitutions and improve their alignment with human preferences.
- **Motivation:**
 - The increasing use of LLMs and VLMs in robotics creates new safety risks due to the potential for hallucinations, jailbreaking, and a lack of understanding of physical safety in grounded scenarios.
 - Existing robotics safety research primarily focuses on collision avoidance and hazard reduction in the immediate vicinity of a robot, neglecting higher-level semantic safety concerns.
 - There is a lack of multimodal datasets for training and evaluating the safety of foundation models in embodied AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **ASIMOVBenchmark Dataset Generation:**
 - Leverages text and image generation techniques to create undesirable situations from real-world visual scenes and human injury reports from hospitals.
 - Combines data from RoboVQA (Sermanet et al., 2024), NEISS (National Electronic Injury Surveillance System), and LLM-generated content.
 - The dataset comprises three types of safety-related questions: Multimodal, Injury, and Dilemmas.
- **Robot Constitution Generation:**
 - Employs a bottom-up approach to automatically generate constitutions from data by synthesizing and summarizing rules to counter undesirable situations.
 - Uses multimodal generative models to create constitutions tailored to specific environments.
- **Auto-Amending Process:**
 - Proposes automated amendment strategies to increase the alignment rate of both generated and human-written constitutions.
 - Uses counterfactual reasoning to identify corner cases and refine the rules of behavior.
- **Evaluation:**
 - Evaluates the performance of foundation models (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4 Turbo) on the ASIMOVBenchmark.
 - Measures the alignment rate between model assessments of semantic safety and human preferences.
 - Compares the performance of models with and without robot constitutions.
 - Assesses the impact of auto-amendment steps on alignment metrics.
 - Experiments with chain-of-thought prompting and anti-jailbreaking prompting to improve safety reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **ASIMOVBenchmark:**
 - The ASIMOVBenchmark provides a comprehensive and challenging resource for evaluating semantic safety in robotics.
 - Current foundation models exhibit strong base capabilities in evaluating semantic safety but still have a significant gap in alignment with human preferences.
- **Robot Constitutions:**
 - Automatically generated robot constitutions improve the alignment of foundation models with human preferences.
 - Longer constitutions tend to be more aligned than shorter ones.
 - The auto-amending and auto-merging processes can boost alignment.
- **Auto-Amending:**
 - The auto-amendment process significantly improves the alignment rate of robot constitutions.
 - Auto-amending tends to make constitutions more general.
- **Adversarial Robustness:** Constitutions significantly improve resilience to adversarial attacks, especially when using a separate, secured "safety brain."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Robot Safety:** This research contributes to the development of safer and more reliable robots that can operate in human-centric environments.
- **Improved AI Alignment:** The data-driven approach to robot constitution generation can improve the alignment of AI systems with human values and preferences.
- **Scalable Safety Benchmarks:** The ASIMOVBenchmark provides a valuable resource for the research community to evaluate and compare different approaches to semantic safety in robotics.
- **Ethical Considerations:** The paper addresses the ethical implications of deploying AI-controlled robots and promotes the development of responsible AI practices.
- **Future Directions:** This work paves the way for future research in the following areas:
 - Developing more sophisticated methods for generating and refining robot constitutions.
 - Exploring the use of formal methods to verify the safety and reliability of robot constitutions.
 - Integrating robot constitutions with low-level robot control systems.
 - Developing secure and robust mechanisms for deploying robot constitutions in real-world environments.
- **Impact** The work suggests that human interpretability and modifiability of constitutions inferred from data makes them an ideal medium for behavior governance of AI-controlled robots.
- The authors make it clear that the constitutions generated should be for research purposes only, and not intended for deployments requiring safety guarantees.
