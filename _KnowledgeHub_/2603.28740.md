# FocusVLA: Focused Visual Utilization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FocusVLA, a new paradigm for auto-regressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enhances visual information utilization for robotic manipulation through Modality Cascaded Attention and a dual-level Focus Attention mechanism. This approach achieved an average 98.7% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and 58% on RoboTwin easy tasks, while also demonstrating a 1.5x to 5x speedup in training convergence.

## Problem

- Existing auto-regressive VLA models suffer from architectural biases, such as those in mixed attention mechanisms, causing them to overlook fine-grained visual details critical for precise manipulation.
- Information overload from an excessive quantity of visual tokens dilutes the model's attention, hindering its ability to concentrate on task-relevant regions.
- Task-irrelevant background noise in visual inputs leads to a low signal-to-noise ratio, negatively impacting the accuracy and precision of action generation.

## Method

- **FocusVLA** implements a ==Modality Cascaded Attention== mechanism that replaces mixed attention with a sequential integration of action latents, action queries, and visual features, eliminating structural biases.
- A dual-level ==Focus Attention== mechanism addresses information overload by pruning redundant visual patches (patch-level focus) based on their relevance to actions, preserving top-K informative tokens.
- Focus Attention also incorporates a ==channel-level focus== using an element-wise gate to suppress task-irrelevant background noise and enhance the quality of task-relevant visual signals.

## Results

- FocusVLA achieved a **98.7%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark (multi-weight setting) and **97.0%** (single-weight setting), outperforming larger 7B-scale models and other 0.5B models.
- On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, FocusVLA significantly outperformed baselines, achieving a **58%** success rate in easy tasks and **15%** in hard tasks, demonstrating improved precision in fine-grained manipulation.
- The model showed enhanced training efficiency, achieving an average **1.5x** speedup in convergence across all LIBERO task suites and up to a **5x** speedup on the LIBERO-Spatial suite compared to VLA-Adapter.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary limitation of existing auto-regressive VLA policies stems from the inefficient utilization of visual information rather than the intrinsic quality or representation capacity of the underlying visual-language models.
> - Architectural biases, information overload from too many visual tokens, and a low signal-to-noise ratio in visual inputs are the main bottlenecks preventing VLA models from achieving fine-grained perception and robust manipulation.
> - Explicitly directing the model's attention to task-relevant visual details through structured and focused attention mechanisms compels reliance on precise visual information, thereby improving manipulation accuracy, robustness, and training efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, a dominant paradigm in robotic manipulation. VLA models aim to enable robotic policies to generate control signals by conditioning on visual observations and language instructions. The broader VLA landscape is generally categorized into two main policy types: diffusion/flow-matching policies and auto-regressive policies.

Diffusion and flow-matching policies, exemplified by works such as pi0 and EVO-1, are known for producing smooth actions and often leverage large-scale pretraining. However, they typically incur higher inference costs and slower real-time responsiveness. In contrast, auto-regressive policies, including models like RT-1, OpenVLA-OFT, and VLA-Adapter, are recognized for their in-domain fitting capability. This paper specifically focuses on advancing the performance of auto-regressive VLA policies.

The authors identify a limitation within current auto-regressive policies: their struggle with tasks requiring fine-grained perception and robust manipulation. They contend that this limitation stems not from the quality or representation capacity of the visual-language models (VLMs) used as backbones, but rather from inefficiencies in *how* the extracted visual-language information is utilized by the action policy. Existing auto-regressive models, such as OpenVLA-OFT and VLA-Adapter, are cited for either omitting direct visual feature utilization during action generation or introducing architectural biases that cause models to overlook fine-grained visual details.

This work positions itself to bridge the gap between vision and action by proposing mechanisms for more effective visual information utilization within auto-regressive VLA frameworks. It complements other research directions in VLA, such as enhancing visual representations (e.g., using depth or 3D features) or improving inference efficiency through token pruning. The authors distinguish their work by systematically investigating and addressing fundamental bottlenecks in visual utilization, rather than solely focusing on representation quality or computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of auto-regressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by improving the effective utilization of visual information for action generation. The authors propose that the current limitations of these models, particularly in tasks demanding fine-grained perception and robust manipulation, are not primarily due to the inherent quality of visual representations but rather the inefficiency with which visual information is processed and leveraged.

The motivation for this work is rooted in a detailed diagnostic analysis that identifies three specific bottlenecks constraining the performance of existing auto-regressive VLA policies:

1. **Architectural Bias**: Current VLA architectures, specifically those employing mixed attention mechanisms (e.g., VLA-Adapter), can create "structural shortcuts." These shortcuts allow the model to bypass concrete visual details by favoring easier pathways, such as relying predominantly on learnable action queries. This architectural predisposition causes the model to overlook fine-grained visual information, leading to less precise actions, especially in tasks requiring sensitive manipulation. The authors illustrate this with examples where existing models exhibit scattered and unfocused attention patterns (Fig. 1 and Fig. 2).

2. **Information Overload**: The input to VLA models often includes an excessive quantity of visual tokens. This abundance of visual data can dilute the model's attention, making it challenging for the policy to concentrate on the critical regions relevant to the manipulation task. The presence of numerous visual tokens can overwhelm the attention mechanism, hindering its ability to identify and focus on key task-relevant areas.

3. **Task-Irrelevant Noise**: A significant amount of background and irrelevant visual information often accompanies task-relevant signals. This leads to a low signal-to-noise ratio during cross-modal interactions between vision and action. Meaningful task-relevant signals can be obscured or buried within environmental noise, which negatively impacts the accuracy and precision of action generation.

Motivated by these identified issues, the authors aim to develop a novel VLA paradigm, FocusVLA, that explicitly directs the model's attention to task-relevant visual details. This is intended to compel the model to rely on precise visual information for action generation, thereby improving manipulation accuracy, robustness, and training efficiency across various tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involved a preliminary diagnostic study to understand the limitations of existing VLA models, followed by the development of FocusVLA, a novel architecture designed to address the identified issues.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: VLA-Adapter Architecture

The work builds upon the VLA-Adapter architecture, which integrates vision features ($C_V_t$) and action queries ($C_{AQ}_t$) from a Vision-Language Model (VLM) into an action latent ($A_t$) at each policy layer $t$. VLA-Adapter uses a mixed attention mechanism where attention weights ($W_t$) are calculated by concatenating attention scores from vision, action queries, and action latents, then applying a softmax and a single-parameter gate ($g$). The aggregated action latent ($\hat{A}_t$) is computed based on these weights and passed through a residual Feed-Forward Network (FFN) to update $A_{t+1}$. This mixed attention design, particularly the single-parameter gate and parallel processing of modalities, was a point of investigation for its potential architectural biases.

#### 3.2. Diagnostic Experiments on Visual Utilization

To validate the identified bottlenecks, the authors conducted a series of controlled experiments using four policy variants and three distinct visual representations on the LIBERO-Long benchmark.

- **Policy Variants (Fig. 3):**
 - **(a) Vanilla:** A baseline model utilizing visual tokens without explicit constraints, reflecting a naive integration of visual features.
 - **(b) Pooling:** Addresses information overload by reducing the number of visual tokens through a 2x2 pooling operation (patch-level optimization).
 - **(c) 1-param gate:** Addresses low signal-to-noise ratio by attenuating visual signal intensity using a single-parameter gate, similar to VLA-Adapter (channel-level optimization).
 - **(d) Cascaded attention:** Addresses structural bias by altering feature interaction patterns through a cascaded multi-head attention mechanism (structure-level optimization).

- **Visual Representations:**
 - **DINOv2+SigLIP (DS):** 2D features without explicit task-relevant information.
 - **VLM:** Output visual features from PrismaticVLM (Qwen2.5-0.5B), providing 2D features with task-relevant semantic information.
 - **VGGT:** Features providing implicit 3D information without explicit task-relevant semantic information.

The diagnostic experiments aimed to answer two questions: (1) Do raw visual representations contribute positively to action generation, and how can this potential be realized? (2) Is the mechanism for visual utilization more critical than the quality of representations? The results of these experiments, summarized in Section 3.2, indicated that existing policies suffer from token quantity imbalance, low signal-to-noise ratio, and structural bias, and that performance is primarily limited by visual utilization efficiency rather than representation quality.

#### 3.3. FocusVLA Methodology

Based on the insights from the diagnostic study, FocusVLA was developed with two core components:

#### 3.3.1. Modality Cascaded Attention

To eliminate structural bias and shortcut pathways, FocusVLA replaces the mixed attention of VLA-Adapter with a cascaded design (Fig. 5). Instead of combining all modalities in a single attention distribution, the action latent ($A_t$) sequentially integrates information from different modalities at each layer:
1. **Self-Attention:** $H_A = \text{Attn}(A_t, A_t)$ (action latent attending to itself).
2. **Action Query Attention:** $H_{AQ} = \text{Attn}(A_t, C_{AQ}_t)$ (action latent attending to action queries).
3. **Visual Attention:** $H_V = \text{Attn}(A_t, C_V_t)$ (action latent attending to visual features).

The outputs ($H_A, H_{AQ}, H_V$) are then concatenated and processed by a fusion MLP ($\sigma_{\text{fusion}}$) to produce $\hat{A}_t$, which is finally passed through a residual FFN to yield $A_{t+1}$. This sequential integration aims to force the model to explicitly extract and utilize task-critical details from visual features before generating actions.

#### 3.3.2. Focus Attention

To address the quantity (information overload) and quality (task-irrelevant noise) limitations within the visual attention component ($H_V$), FocusVLA introduces a dual-level Focus Attention mechanism:

- **Patch-level Focus:** This mechanism prunes redundant and task-irrelevant visual patches. It operates by selecting a subset of visual tokens based on their relevance to the action. Specifically, cross-attention scores between action queries ($A_t$) and vision keys ($C_V_t$) are computed. Only the top-K tokens with the highest scores are retained, while others are masked out (Equation 9). For the values in this attention, the raw features ($C_V_0$) from the visual backbone are used instead of deeper VLM features. This design ensures that fine-grained spatial details from task-relevant regions are preserved, while irrelevant background patches are discarded. The default setting for visual tokens in Focus-Attn is 256, down from 512 raw visual tokens (Table 3).

- **Channel-level Focus:** To suppress task-irrelevant background noise and enhance signal quality, Focus Attention incorporates an element-wise gate, inspired by Gated Attention (Equation 11). This adaptive gate operates on the output of the vision-to-action attention ($H_V$), modulating its channels by an MLP-derived gate ($\sigma_g(A_t)$). This fine-grained control allows the model to selectively suppress noisy channels while preserving task-relevant visual signals, thereby strengthening the model's instruction-following capability.

In summary, FocusVLA integrates these components to transform dispersed visual utilization into a focused, task-aligned interaction, aiming for more precise and robust action generation. The implementation details include using Qwen2.5-0.5B as the VLM backbone, DINOv2+SigLIP for additional visual features, and training with AdamW optimizer on NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated FocusVLA across simulated and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks, with comprehensive ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments: LIBERO Benchmark

FocusVLA was evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark, comprising Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long task suites.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** FocusVLA achieved a 98.7% average success rate under the multi-weight setting (one policy per task), surpassing larger 7B-scale models (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT at 97.1%, Spatial Forcing at 98.5%) despite having only 0.5B parameters. In the single-weight setting (one shared policy for four tasks), FocusVLA achieved a 97.0% average success rate, outperforming other 0.5B models like EVO-1 (94.8%) and VLA-Adapter-Pro (95.6%). These results suggest that efficient visual utilization contributes to high action precision and behavioral fitting.
- **Task-Level Performance:** FocusVLA achieved near-perfect performance on Spatial (99.6%) and Object (100.0%) suites, and strong performance on Goal (98.8%) and Long (96.2%) tasks (Table 4).

#### 4.1.2. RoboTwin Benchmark

FocusVLA was tested on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, which features fine-grained and bimanual manipulation tasks under easy and hard settings.
- **Performance Advantage:** FocusVLA significantly outperformed baseline models (DP, pi0, VLA-Adapter) across various tasks. On average, it achieved 58% success rate in easy tasks and 15% in hard tasks, compared to VLA-Adapter's 32% and 8% respectively (Fig. 6, Table 5). This advantage was particularly notable in tasks requiring precise manipulation, such as "Hugging Mug" and "Stack Blocks Two," where FocusVLA's ability to leverage visual details led to improved precision.

#### 4.1.3. Ablation Study (LIBERO multi-weights setting)

- **Attention Pattern:** Replacing mixed attention with Modality Cascaded Attention improved the average success rate from 93.6% to 97.0%. This supports the hypothesis that eliminating structural bias and fostering focused attention improves visual utilization.
- **Patch-level Focus:** The number of visual tokens influenced performance. Using 512 tokens (no pruning) yielded lower performance (97.0%), while reducing to 128 tokens also degraded performance (96.3%). The optimal performance was observed with 256 visual tokens (98.0%), suggesting a balance between reducing redundancy and retaining sufficient informative cues (Table 2, Fig. 8a).
- **Channel-level Focus:** An element-wise gate performed best (98.2% average SR), outperforming no gate (98.0%) and a single-parameter gate (97.3%). This indicates the effectiveness of fine-grained modulation in suppressing task-irrelevant noise and enhancing instruction following (Table 2, Fig. 8b).
- **Visual Representation:** Combining VLM features with DINOv2+SigLIP (DS) features resulted in the highest average success rate of 98.7%. This suggests a synergistic benefit from leveraging VLM's semantic awareness and DS's spatial fidelity, overcoming limitations of using either in isolation. VGGT, despite its 3D modeling ability, yielded lower performance (96.8%) due to weaker and less stable gradients when injected at the policy stage (Table 2).

#### 4.1.4. Training Efficiency Analysis

FocusVLA demonstrated superior training efficiency compared to VLA-Adapter. It achieved an average 1.5x speedup in convergence across all LIBERO task suites. Specifically, on the LIBERO-Spatial suite, FocusVLA required 5k steps, achieving a 5x speedup over VLA-Adapter's 25k steps (Fig. 7). This is attributed to FocusVLA's ability to extract task-relevant cues directly from visual representations, distributing the informational burden and facilitating faster convergence.

#### 4.2. Real-world Experiments

Real-robot experiments were conducted on a Realman platform with bimanual manipulators across three tasks with varying backgrounds, spatial arrangements, and target objects.
- **Performance:** FocusVLA consistently achieved higher success rates across all real-world tasks compared to baselines (Fig. 9). For example, it showed robustness in "grasping fruit" under background variation and precision in "stacking cups" under spatial variation.
- **Precision and Robustness:** FocusVLA demonstrated more accurate target localization and precise object interaction, even under real-world visual disturbances, which was a notable advantage over VLA-Adapter (Fig. 17).
- **Failure Cases:** While successful, the study also noted limitations in generalization, such as failures due to non-standard initial robot arm states or simultaneous changes in both background color and texture, indicating that robustness to initialization shifts and complex environmental variations remains a challenge (Fig. 18).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents FocusVLA, a paradigm designed to improve the visual utilization capabilities of auto-regressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The work is significant for several reasons:

Firstly, it empirically identifies and validates three fundamental bottlenecks—architectural bias, information overload, and task-irrelevant noise—that limit the performance of auto-regressive VLA policies. A key contribution is the finding that performance is primarily constrained by *how* visual information is utilized, rather than solely by the intrinsic quality of the visual representations. This insight shifts the focus of research from merely improving visual encoders to developing more effective mechanisms for integrating visual data into action policies.

Secondly, FocusVLA introduces two architectural innovations: Modality Cascaded Attention and Focus Attention. Modality Cascaded Attention addresses architectural bias by enforcing sequential, decoupled modality interactions, compelling the model to engage with task-relevant visual details. Focus Attention, with its patch-level and channel-level mechanisms, directly tackles information overload and task-irrelevant noise by pruning redundant visual tokens and adaptively suppressing noisy feature channels. These methods lead to a more targeted and precise utilization of visual information.

Thirdly, the empirical results demonstrate that FocusVLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks, including LIBERO and RoboTwin, in both simulated and real-world environments. The model shows consistent improvements in success rates across various tasks, particularly those requiring fine-grained perception and dexterous manipulation. This indicates that improved visual utilization translates into more robust and accurate robotic control.

Fourthly, FocusVLA also demonstrates increased training efficiency, achieving faster convergence rates compared to previous methods. This reduction in training time, exemplified by a 1.5x overall speedup on LIBERO and up to a 5x speedup on specific task suites, suggests a more efficient learning process. This enhanced efficiency has practical implications for developing and deploying VLA models, potentially reducing computational resources and development cycles.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for the field of robotic manipulation. By enabling VLA models to leverage visual details more effectively, FocusVLA contributes to the development of more capable and generalizable robots that can perform complex tasks with higher precision and robustness. This advancement can facilitate broader adoption of VLA systems in various applications, from industrial automation to service robotics, where fine-grained control and adaptability to diverse visual environments are critical.

While FocusVLA shows progress, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the need to explore visual utilization within the VLM itself, scale up model size and training data for broader generalization, and improve robustness to real-world data limitations. These identified areas represent avenues for future research that could further enhance the capabilities of VLA systems.
