# FocusVLA: Focused Visual Utilization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FocusVLA, a new paradigm for auto-regressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enhances visual information utilization for robotic manipulation through Modality Cascaded Attention and a dual-level Focus Attention mechanism. This approach achieved an average 98.7% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and 58% on RoboTwin easy tasks, while also demonstrating a 1.5x to 5x speedup in training convergence.

## Problem

- Existing auto-regressive VLA models suffer from architectural biases, such as those in mixed attention mechanisms, causing them to overlook fine-grained visual details critical for precise manipulation.
- Information overload from an excessive quantity of visual tokens dilutes the model's attention, hindering its ability to concentrate on task-relevant regions.
- Task-irrelevant background noise in visual inputs leads to a low signal-to-noise ratio, negatively impacting the accuracy and precision of action generation.

## Method

- **FocusVLA** implements a ==Modality Cascaded Attention== mechanism that replaces mixed attention with a sequential integration of ==action latents==, ==action queries==, and visual features, eliminating structural biases.
- A dual-level ==Focus Attention== mechanism addresses information overload by pruning redundant visual patches (==patch-level focus==) based on their relevance to actions, preserving top-K informative tokens.
- Focus Attention also incorporates a ==channel-level focus== using an ==element-wise gate== to suppress task-irrelevant background noise and enhance the quality of task-relevant visual signals.

## Results

- **FocusVLA** achieved a **98.7%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark (multi-weight setting) and **97.0%** (single-weight setting), outperforming larger 7B-scale models and other 0.5B models.
- On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, **FocusVLA** significantly outperformed baselines, achieving a **58%** success rate in easy tasks and **15%** in hard tasks, demonstrating improved precision in fine-grained manipulation.
- The model showed enhanced training efficiency, achieving an average **1.5x** speedup in convergence across all LIBERO task suites and up to a **5x** speedup on the LIBERO-Spatial suite compared to VLA-Adapter.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary limitation of existing auto-regressive VLA policies stems from the inefficient utilization of visual information rather than the intrinsic quality or representation capacity of the underlying visual-language models.
> - Architectural biases, information overload from too many visual tokens, and a low signal-to-noise ratio in visual inputs are the main bottlenecks preventing VLA models from achieving fine-grained perception and robust manipulation.
> - Explicitly directing the model's attention to task-relevant visual details through structured and focused attention mechanisms compels reliance on precise visual information, thereby improving manipulation accuracy, robustness, and training efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. LIBERO Benchmark

FocusVLA was evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark, comprising Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long task suites.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** FocusVLA achieved a 98.7% average success rate under the multi-weight setting (one policy per task), surpassing larger 7B-scale models (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT at 97.1%, Spatial Forcing at 98.5%) despite having only 0.5B parameters. In the single-weight setting (one shared policy for four tasks), FocusVLA achieved a 97.0% average success rate, outperforming other 0.5B models like EVO-1 (94.8%) and VLA-Adapter-Pro (95.6%). These results suggest that efficient visual utilization contributes to high action precision and behavioral fitting.
- **Task-Level Performance:** FocusVLA achieved near-perfect performance on Spatial (99.6%) and Object (100.0%) suites, and strong performance on Goal (98.8%) and Long (96.2%) tasks (Table 4).

### 2. RoboTwin Benchmark

FocusVLA was tested on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, which features fine-grained and bimanual manipulation tasks under easy and hard settings.
- **Performance Advantage:** FocusVLA significantly outperformed baseline models (DP, pi0, VLA-Adapter) across various tasks. On average, it achieved 58% success rate in easy tasks and 15% in hard tasks, compared to VLA-Adapter's 32% and 8% respectively (Fig. 6, Table 5). This advantage was particularly notable in tasks requiring precise manipulation, such as "Hugging Mug" and "Stack Blocks Two," where FocusVLA's ability to leverage visual details led to improved precision.

### 3. Ablation Study (LIBERO multi-weights setting)

- **Attention Pattern:** Replacing mixed attention with Modality Cascaded Attention improved the average success rate from 93.6% to 97.0%. This supports the hypothesis that eliminating structural bias and fostering focused attention improves visual utilization.
- **Patch-level Focus:** The number of visual tokens influenced performance. Using 512 tokens (no pruning) yielded lower performance (97.0%), while reducing to 128 tokens also degraded performance (96.3%). The optimal performance was observed with 256 visual tokens (98.0%), suggesting a balance between reducing redundancy and retaining sufficient informative cues (Table 2, Fig. 8a).
- **Channel-level Focus:** An element-wise gate performed best (98.2% average SR), outperforming no gate (98.0%) and a single-parameter gate (97.3%). This indicates the effectiveness of fine-grained modulation in suppressing task-irrelevant noise and enhancing instruction following (Table 2, Fig. 8b).
- **Visual Representation:** Combining VLM features with DINOv2+SigLIP (DS) features resulted in the highest average success rate of 98.7%. This suggests a synergistic benefit from leveraging VLM's semantic awareness and DS's spatial fidelity, overcoming limitations of using either in isolation. VGGT, despite its 3D modeling ability, yielded lower performance (96.8%) due to weaker and less stable gradients when injected at the policy stage (Table 2).

### 4. Training Efficiency Analysis

FocusVLA demonstrated superior training efficiency compared to VLA-Adapter. It achieved an average 1.5x speedup in convergence across all LIBERO task suites. Specifically, on the LIBERO-Spatial suite, FocusVLA required 5k steps, achieving a 5x speedup over VLA-Adapter's 25k steps (Fig. 7). This is attributed to FocusVLA's ability to extract task-relevant cues directly from visual representations, distributing the informational burden and facilitating faster convergence.

#### 4.1. Real-world Experiments

Real-robot experiments were conducted on a Realman platform with bimanual manipulators across three tasks with varying backgrounds, spatial arrangements, and target objects.
- **Performance:** FocusVLA consistently achieved higher success rates across all real-world tasks compared to baselines (Fig. 9). For example, it showed robustness in "grasping fruit" under background variation and precision in "stacking cups" under spatial variation.
- **Precision and Robustness:** FocusVLA demonstrated more accurate target localization and precise object interaction, even under real-world visual disturbances, which was a notable advantage over VLA-Adapter (Fig. 17).
- **Failure Cases:** While successful, the study also noted limitations in generalization, such as failures due to non-standard initial robot arm states or simultaneous changes in both background color and texture, indicating that robustness to initialization shifts and complex environmental variations remains a challenge (Fig. 18).

#### 4.2. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents FocusVLA, a paradigm designed to improve the visual utilization capabilities of auto-regressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The work is significant for several reasons:

Firstly, it empirically identifies and validates three fundamental bottlenecks—architectural bias, information overload, and task-irrelevant noise—that limit the performance of auto-regressive VLA policies. A key contribution is the finding that performance is primarily constrained by *how* visual information is utilized, rather than solely by the intrinsic quality of the visual representations. This insight shifts the focus of research from merely improving visual encoders to developing more effective mechanisms for integrating visual data into action policies.

Secondly, FocusVLA introduces two architectural innovations: Modality Cascaded Attention and Focus Attention. Modality Cascaded Attention addresses architectural bias by enforcing sequential, decoupled modality interactions, compelling the model to engage with task-relevant visual details. Focus Attention, with its patch-level and channel-level mechanisms, directly tackles information overload and task-irrelevant noise by pruning redundant visual tokens and adaptively suppressing noisy feature channels. These methods lead to a more targeted and precise utilization of visual information.

Thirdly, the empirical results demonstrate that FocusVLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks, including LIBERO and RoboTwin, in both simulated and real-world environments. The model shows consistent improvements in success rates across various tasks, particularly those requiring fine-grained perception and dexterous manipulation. This indicates that improved visual utilization translates into more robust and accurate robotic control.

Fourthly, FocusVLA also demonstrates increased training efficiency, achieving faster convergence rates compared to previous methods. This reduction in training time, exemplified by a 1.5x overall speedup on LIBERO and up to a 5x speedup on specific task suites, suggests a more efficient learning process. This enhanced efficiency has practical implications for developing and deploying VLA models, potentially reducing computational resources and development cycles.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for the field of robotic manipulation. By enabling VLA models to leverage visual details more effectively, FocusVLA contributes to the development of more capable and generalizable robots that can perform complex tasks with higher precision and robustness. This advancement can facilitate broader adoption of VLA systems in various applications, from industrial automation to service robotics, where fine-grained control and adaptability to diverse visual environments are critical.

While FocusVLA shows progress, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the need to explore visual utilization within the VLM itself, scale up model size and training data for broader generalization, and improve robustness to real-world data limitations. These identified areas represent avenues for future research that could further enhance the capabilities of VLA systems.
