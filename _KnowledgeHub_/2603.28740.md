# FocusVLA: Focused Visual Utilization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FocusVLA, a new paradigm for auto-regressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enhances visual information utilization for robotic manipulation through Modality Cascaded Attention and a dual-level Focus Attention mechanism. This approach achieved an average 98.7% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and 58% on RoboTwin easy tasks, while also demonstrating a 1.5x to 5x speedup in training convergence.

## Problem

- Existing auto-regressive VLA models suffer from architectural biases, such as those in mixed attention mechanisms, causing them to overlook fine-grained visual details critical for precise manipulation.
- Information overload from an excessive quantity of visual tokens dilutes the model's attention, hindering its ability to concentrate on task-relevant regions.
- Task-irrelevant background noise in visual inputs leads to a low signal-to-noise ratio, negatively impacting the accuracy and precision of action generation.

## Method

- **FocusVLA** implements a ==Modality Cascaded Attention== mechanism that replaces mixed attention with a sequential integration of ==action latents==, ==action queries==, and visual features, eliminating structural biases.
- A dual-level ==Focus Attention== mechanism addresses information overload by pruning redundant visual patches (==patch-level focus==) based on their relevance to actions, preserving top-K informative tokens.
- Focus Attention also incorporates a ==channel-level focus== using an ==element-wise gate== to suppress task-irrelevant background noise and enhance the quality of task-relevant visual signals.

## Results

- **FocusVLA** achieved a **98.7%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark (multi-weight setting) and **97.0%** (single-weight setting), outperforming larger 7B-scale models and other 0.5B models.
- On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, **FocusVLA** significantly outperformed baselines, achieving a **58%** success rate in easy tasks and **15%** in hard tasks, demonstrating improved precision in fine-grained manipulation.
- The model showed enhanced training efficiency, achieving an average **1.5x** speedup in convergence across all LIBERO task suites and up to a **5x** speedup on the LIBERO-Spatial suite compared to VLA-Adapter.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary limitation of existing auto-regressive VLA policies stems from the inefficient utilization of visual information rather than the intrinsic quality or representation capacity of the underlying visual-language models.
> - Architectural biases, information overload from too many visual tokens, and a low signal-to-noise ratio in visual inputs are the main bottlenecks preventing VLA models from achieving fine-grained perception and robust manipulation.
> - Explicitly directing the model's attention to task-relevant visual details through structured and focused attention mechanisms compels reliance on precise visual information, thereby improving manipulation accuracy, robustness, and training efficiency.
