# Unified 4D World Action Modeling from Video Priors with Asynchronous Denoising

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Xiaomi Robotics developed X-WAM, a Unified 4D World Action Model, integrating real-time robotic action execution with high-fidelity 4D world synthesis (video and 3D reconstruction). The model achieved an average success rate of 79.2% on 24 RoboCasa manipulation tasks, outperforming prior VLA-based methods by 12.1 percentage points, and demonstrated efficient real-time operation on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Existing unified world action models operate mainly in 2D pixel-space, lacking explicit 3D spatial awareness, which leads to physically implausible predictions and hinders geometrically faithful reconstruction.
- Efficiently integrating 3D perception into pretrained video foundation models without significantly degrading existing knowledge or incurring prohibitive computational overhead.
- Balancing the conflicting requirements of high-fidelity, high-dimensional video generation (requiring many denoising steps) and rapid, low-dimensional robotic action decoding (requiring few steps) within a single diffusion model framework.

## Method

- **X-WAM** introduces a lightweight ==depth adaptation module== as an interleaved branch within a pretrained ==Diffusion Transformer==, using ==unilateral attention== to inject 3D spatial awareness.
- Proposes ==Asynchronous Noise Sampling (ANS)==, coupling noise levels during training to align with asynchronous inference schedules for joint video/action optimization.
- Employs a ==unified denoising sequence== for multi-modal prediction (RGB, depth, proprioceptive states, actions); derives camera poses from end-effector poses for consistent 3D reconstruction.

## Results

- **X-WAM** achieved **79.2% average success** across 24 RoboCasa tasks, surpassing Cosmos Policy by **12.1 pp**.
- Superior 4D reconstruction: **+2.34 dB** PSNR; **Chamfer Distance 0.0049 vs. 0.0680** for two-stage approaches.
- ANS enabled a **4.5x speedup** in action latency (4665ms → 1033ms) with only **5 denoising steps**, supporting real-time deployment at **15 Hz**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit 3D spatial awareness through a lightweight, interleaved depth branch significantly enhances policy performance and 4D reconstruction quality in unified models without substantial computational overhead or disrupting pretrained visual priors.
> - Asynchronous Noise Sampling effectively resolves the modality mismatch problem in joint video-action generation, enabling faster action decoding for real-time robotic control while maintaining high-fidelity video generation.
> - Unified 4D world action models can concurrently achieve superior policy success rates, high-fidelity 4D world generation, and efficient real-time execution by carefully designing architectural adaptations and noise scheduling strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI has seen accelerated development, largely driven by the emergence of robotic foundation models. Current approaches generally fall into two distinct paradigms: policy models and world models.

Policy models, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAMs), primarily focus on predicting executable actions for robot control. VLA models leverage pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate motor commands, demonstrating proficiency in instruction following and semantic reasoning. However, they are often noted for their limitations in geometric intuition and understanding of physical dynamics. WAMs extend this by integrating video generation models to jointly predict future observations and actions, aiming to enhance physical understanding and generalization through visual priors.

In contrast, world models concentrate on simulating future observations, excelling at generating realistic visual predictions of physical dynamics, often conditioned on text or actions. These models, however, do not directly produce executable actions for robotic control. The segregation of these paradigms has historically limited the synergistic benefits and representational efficiencies that could arise from a combined approach.

Recent research has begun to bridge this gap by developing unified world action models. These frameworks attempt to jointly model video generation and action prediction, sharing representations across modalities to improve both future prediction quality and policy execution. Despite progress, a significant limitation of these unified models is their confinement to 2D pixel-space observations, which inherently lacks explicit spatial awareness and 3D geometric grounding. The physical world is three-dimensional, and ignoring this dimension can lead to predictions that are physically implausible and prevent accurate 3D reconstruction. Furthermore, existing unified models often struggle to balance the computational demands of high-fidelity video generation (requiring many denoising steps) with the need for efficient, real-time action decoding (requiring fewer steps).

This work positions itself as a development within the unified world action modeling paradigm by addressing these limitations. It seeks to incorporate explicit 3D spatial information, thereby elevating unified models from 2D pixel predictors to spatially aware 4D dynamics simulators capable of joint generation, reconstruction, and policy execution, while also optimizing for real-time action efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop X-WAM, a Unified 4D World Action Model that integrates real-time robotic action execution with high-fidelity 4D world synthesis (combining video and 3D reconstruction) within a single architectural framework. This objective is motivated by several critical limitations identified in prior unified world models.

Firstly, previous unified models, such as UWM, predominantly operate within a 2D pixel space. This lack of explicit spatial awareness and 3D geometric grounding leads to several issues:
- **Physical Implausibility:** Models may hallucinate future states that are physically impossible due to a lack of understanding of 3D geometry.
- **Limited Reconstruction Fidelity:** Without explicit 3D modeling, geometrically faithful 3D reconstruction of the environment is not possible, hindering applications requiring precise spatial understanding.
- **Bottleneck in Manipulation Tasks:** For robotic manipulation, explicit spatial comprehension is crucial for tasks demanding precise interaction with objects and environments. Operating solely in 2D space creates a bottleneck for generalization and robust performance in real-world, 3D settings.

The motivation is to overcome this 2D limitation by incorporating explicit 3D perception directly into the unified framework. This involves enabling the model to not only generate future video frames but also to reconstruct the 3D spatial structure of the environment, thereby providing a more comprehensive "4D" understanding (3D space + time).

Secondly, a significant challenge in unified world action models arises from the inherent modality mismatch between generating high-dimensional video trajectories and predicting low-dimensional robotic actions.
- **Divergent Computational Needs:** High-fidelity video synthesis typically necessitates a substantial number of denoising steps in diffusion models to achieve visual quality. In contrast, low-dimensional robotic actions can often be accurately decoded with significantly fewer denoising steps, even from noisy latent representations.
- **Trade-off between Quality and Efficiency:** Prior methods have struggled to balance these divergent requirements. A unified model that uses many steps for both modalities would be too slow for real-time action execution, while one using few steps for both would compromise video generation quality.

The motivation behind addressing this is to jointly optimize for both high-quality world generation (including 4D reconstruction) and efficient, real-time robotic action execution. This necessitates a mechanism that allows for rapid action decoding without sacrificing the fidelity of future visual predictions, ultimately enabling practical deployment on physical robots with acceptable latency.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Incorporate explicit 3D spatial awareness into a unified video-action modeling paradigm to enable 4D world synthesis.
2. Develop a lightweight and efficient method for integrating 3D perception that does not disrupt pretrained visual knowledge or introduce excessive computational overhead.
3. Design a noise sampling and denoising strategy that accommodates the distinct computational requirements of video generation and action prediction, thereby balancing generation quality and action decoding efficiency.
4. Demonstrate that a single unified framework can concurrently achieve high policy success rates, high-fidelity visual generation, and accurate spatial reconstruction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

X-WAM is designed as a unified framework to address video generation, 3D spatial reconstruction, policy success rate, and efficient action execution. It is built upon two core technical designs: a lightweight depth adaptation module and Asynchronous Noise Sampling (ANS).

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

X-WAM is fine-tuned from a pretrained video generation Diffusion Transformer (DiT), specifically Wan2.2-TI2V-5B.
- **Inputs:** The model takes a language instruction `c`, the initial proprioceptive state `s_0`, and multi-view initial RGB observations `O_0`.
- **Outputs:** It jointly predicts future RGB videos `O_{1:H}`, depth videos `D_{1:H}`, proprioceptive states `s_{1:H}`, and actions `a_{1:K}`. Here, `H` and `K` denote the prediction horizons for video/state and actions, respectively.
- **Encoding:** RGB videos are encoded into latent representations `z_O` using the original causal VAE encoder `E`. Proprioceptive states `s` and robot actions `a` are projected into the latent space via learnable MLPs: `z_s = MLP_s(s)` and `z_a = MLP_a(a)`.
- **Unified Denoising Sequence:** The latent representations of the three modalities are concatenated into a single denoising sequence `Z = [z_{O_0}, z_{O_{1:H}}, z_{s_0}, z_{s_{1:H}}, z_{a_{1:K}}]`. This sequence is processed by bidirectional full attention within the Diffusion Transformer. The initial observation `z_{O_0}` and state `z_{s_0}` are treated as clean samples (t=0) and remain fixed during denoising.
- **Asymmetric Prediction Horizons:** X-WAM predicts H=8 future RGB frames and states, and K=32 future actions. This asymmetry accounts for the higher control frequency required for robot execution (K/H = 4x video frame rate).
- **Multi-View Compatibility:** Learnable view embeddings are added to the tokens of each viewpoint to indicate the view index, enabling multi-view compatibility without altering pretrained positional encodings.
- **Camera Pose Derivation:** Instead of predicting explicit camera extrinsics, X-WAM predicts the end-effector pose `T_ee` and derives the wrist camera pose using a fixed hand-to-eye calibration matrix `T_h2e`: `T_wrist = T_ee ⋅ T_h2e`. This allows for multi-view RGB-D fusion into a unified 3D representation.

#### 3.2. Lightweight Depth Adaptation

To incorporate 3D spatial awareness efficiently and effectively, X-WAM employs a lightweight depth adaptation module:
- **Depth Representation:** Depth maps are predicted within the same VAE latent space as RGB frames. Each single-channel depth map is replicated three times to form a pseudo-RGB image, encoded by the same causal VAE, and decoded symmetrically.
- **Structural Adaptation:** Instead of expanding the sequence length (which would incur quadratic attention cost) or modifying channel dimensions (which shifts input distribution), the approach replicates the final `M` blocks of the pretrained DiT (where `M < N` total blocks) to form an auxiliary depth prediction branch.
- **Unilateral Attention:** After the initial `N-M` shared blocks, the main (`Z_m`) and depth (`Z_D`) branches are initialized. In the subsequent `M` layers, `DepthBlock_j` processes `Z_D` while attending to the main branch's input `Z_m` via cross-attention. Crucially, the main branch remains unaffected by depth tokens, preserving the integrity of the pretrained weights.
- **Supervision:** The depth branch is trained to regress the inverse depth of the current video frame using Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss.
- **Inference Flexibility:** This auxiliary depth branch can be optionally toggled on or off during inference, allowing for reduced overhead when only actions are needed.

#### 3.3. Asynchronous Noise Sampling (ANS)

ANS is proposed to manage the distinct denoising requirements for high-fidelity video and efficient action decoding.
- **Asynchronous Inference:** During inference, ANS utilizes a specialized schedule. Actions and proprioceptive states are denoised for `T_a` steps, then immediately dispatched. Video denoising continues for a longer duration, `T_O` steps (`T_a < T_O`). During the remaining `T_O - T_a` steps, the already-denoised actions serve as a clean, fixed condition for the video-only denoising.
- **Coupled Noise Sampling During Training:** To align the training and inference noise distributions, ANS samples the joint noise levels (`t_O`, `t_a`) from a coupled distribution. This distribution has two modes:
 1. Action-conditioned video generation: `t_a = 0` (noise-free actions), `t_O` sampled from `U(0,1)` with probability `p`.
 2. Asynchronous joint generation: `t_a` sampled from `U(0,1)`, and `t_O` sampled from `Beta(1.5, 1)` rescaled to `[t_a, 1]` with probability `1-p`. This ensures `t_O >= t_a`, reflecting the inference scenario where video typically requires more denoising than actions. This coupled sampling prevents training configurations that are inconsistent with asynchronous inference.

#### 3.4. Training Details

- **Loss Function:** X-WAM is fine-tuned using the flow matching framework. The model `f_θ` predicts the velocity field `v = ε - z_0`. The velocity prediction loss `L_m` is applied to each modality (video `O`, state `s`, action `a`). The depth branch is supervised with `L_depth = ||D̂ - D*||^2` (MSE on inverse depth). The total objective is `L_total = L_O + λ_sL_s + λ_aL_a + λ_DL_depth`.
- **Data:** The model is trained on over 5,800 hours of diverse robotic data, encompassing both real-robot and simulated datasets. Data undergoes preprocessing, filtering (removing base locomotion, dexterous manipulation, failed executions), and unification into a consistent coordinate system and representation. Depth maps for datasets lacking them are extracted using Video Depth Anything.
- **State and Action Representation:** A universal interface defines states as 16-dimensional absolute vectors (position, quaternion, gripper for two arms) and actions as 14-dimensional relative vectors (Δposition, Δaxis-angle, gripper action for two arms).
- **Inference:** UniPC multistep scheduler is used, with separate instances for video and state/action modalities following the asynchronous procedure. Classifier-free guidance scale is set to 1.0.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of X-WAM focused on three dimensions: policy execution, 4D reconstruction and generation, and ablation studies, complemented by real-world robotic experiments.

#### 4.1. Policy Evaluation

X-WAM's policy execution capability was assessed on two robotic manipulation benchmarks: RoboCasa and RoboTwin 2.0.
- **RoboCasa:** X-WAM achieved an average success rate (SR) of 79.2% across 24 manipulation tasks. This performance surpassed existing baselines, including VLA-based methods like π_0 (62.5%) and GR00T-N1.5 (64.1%), and WAM-based methods such as UWM (60.8%), DreamZero (62.4%), and Cosmos Policy (67.1%). X-WAM demonstrated a 12.1 percentage point improvement over the strongest baseline, Cosmos Policy.
- **RoboTwin 2.0:** On this dual-arm manipulation benchmark, X-WAM achieved 89.8% SR under the Clean setting and 90.7% SR under the Randomized setting. These results exceeded prior methods like Motus (88.7% Clean, 87.0% Randomized) and other VLA and WAM baselines.
The consistent outperformance on both benchmarks suggests that the integration of explicit 3D spatial awareness and large-scale pretraining significantly contributes to policy robustness and generalization.

#### 4.2. 4D Reconstruction and Generation

The 4D reconstruction and generation quality were evaluated on the RoboCasa environment using a suite of metrics:
- **Visual Fidelity (RGB):** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS.
- **Depth Quality (Single-View):** Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel), δ1 accuracy.
- **Geometric Quality (Multi-View):** Chamfer Distance (CD) for reconstructed point clouds.
X-WAM demonstrated superior performance across all metrics compared to baselines. For instance, against a two-stage pipeline combining DreamZero for RGB video generation with Depth Anything 3 (DA3) for post-hoc depth estimation, X-WAM improved PSNR by 2.34 dB and reduced Chamfer Distance from 0.0680 to 0.0049. This indicates that end-to-end joint modeling produces more accurate spatial reconstructions than decoupled approaches.
Compared to Robot4DGen, a geometry-aware 4D video generation method, X-WAM achieved better visual fidelity (LPIPS 0.0513 vs. 0.1026). An ablative variant, "X-WAM w/o depth + DA3" (which used DA3 for depth instead of the integrated depth branch), showed degraded depth accuracy (AbsRel 0.1045 vs. 0.0349) and point cloud quality (CD 0.0401 vs. 0.0049), validating the effectiveness of X-WAM's integrated depth branch in producing geometrically consistent predictions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on RoboCasa investigated the impact of X-WAM's core design choices.
- **Depth Architecture Design:**
 - "No depth" variant yielded an SR of 63.0% and 1033 ms latency.
 - "Sequence concatenation" (explicit depth tokens) achieved the best quality metrics but incurred a nearly doubled action latency of 1888 ms.
 - "Channel concatenation" also introduced overhead (1266 ms) and performed worse in SR (64.2%).
 - X-WAM's "Interleaved branch" achieved 67.8% SR and matched the "no depth" latency of 1033 ms, while delivering superior quality over both "no-depth" and "channel-concatenation" variants. This confirmed that explicit spatial modeling improves policy performance, and the interleaved branch is an efficient integration strategy.
- **Noise Scheduling Strategy (ANS):**
 - Synchronous denoising variants, while yielding strong RGB metrics, resulted in high action latency (4665 ms).
 - Asynchronous inference reduced latency to 1033 ms (a 4.5x speedup).
 - "Decoupled train + Async infer" achieved a competitive SR (67.2%) but degraded reconstruction quality (e.g., PSNR 22.60, AbsRel 0.0430).
 - X-WAM's ANS (coupled training + asynchronous inference) achieved the highest SR (67.8%) and the best depth metrics at the 1033 ms latency, while maintaining RGB quality competitive with synchronous baselines. This validated that coupled noise sampling is effective in aligning training and inference distributions for optimal asynchronous denoising.

#### 4.4. Real Robot Experiments (Earphone Packing)

X-WAM was deployed on a real dual-arm robotic platform for an earphone packing task.
- **Scalability and Generalization:** X-WAM consistently outperformed Xiaomi-Robotics-0 (XR-0), a VLA model baseline. For scalability, X-WAM showed superior long-horizon reliability when packing 2 or 3 earphones (e.g., 93.8% progress vs. 79.1% for 2 earphones). For generalization, X-WAM exhibited stronger robustness under novel object placements (70.8% vs. 58.3%) and unseen distractors (75.0% vs. 66.7%).
- **Efficiency:** X-WAM consistently achieved lower completion times across all settings, attributed to asynchronous denoising with real-time chunking enabling faster inference and more efficient trajectories. Another baseline, π_0.5, failed to complete the task.
These real-world results indicate that X-WAM's explicit 3D spatial awareness and efficient asynchronous execution translate into tangible benefits for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks requiring precise geometric reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces X-WAM, a Unified 4D World Action Model that integrates robotic action execution with high-fidelity 4D world synthesis. This contribution addresses notable limitations in prior unified world models, primarily their confinement to 2D pixel space and the trade-off between visual generation quality and action decoding efficiency.

The work's significance lies in several key areas:
- **Advancement of Unified World Action Models:** By incorporating explicit 3D spatial awareness, X-WAM elevates unified models beyond 2D pixel prediction to a more physically grounded 4D dynamics simulation. This enables a more comprehensive understanding and representation of the physical world, which is crucial for embodied AI.
- **Improved Robotic Policy Performance:** The integration of 3D information, facilitated by the lightweight depth adaptation module, demonstrably enhances policy success rates on complex manipulation benchmarks (RoboCasa and RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world tasks (earphone packing). This suggests that a deeper understanding of the environment's geometry contributes directly to more robust and generalized robotic control.
- **High-Fidelity 4D Reconstruction:** X-WAM's ability to generate high-quality multi-view RGB-D videos and reconstruct accurate 3D point clouds from these predictions provides a rich, dynamic spatial representation of the future world. This is valuable for tasks requiring detailed environmental understanding, planning, and simulation.
- **Efficient Real-time Execution:** The introduction of Asynchronous Noise Sampling (ANS) is a practical contribution that resolves the long-standing challenge of balancing video generation quality with action decoding speed. By decoupling denoising schedules and aligning training with asynchronous inference, X-WAM enables rapid action dispatch for real-time robotic control while maintaining high-fidelity visual outputs, making it suitable for deployment on physical robots.
- **Cross-Task Synergy:** The unified framework demonstrates that a single model can simultaneously optimize for diverse objectives—policy execution, visual generation, and spatial reconstruction—leading to greater representational efficiency and potential for cross-task synergy in embodied AI systems.

The potential impact of X-WAM is on fostering the development of more capable, robust, and general-purpose robotic systems. By providing a unified foundation that deeply understands and simulates the 4D world, this work could enable robots to perform more complex, long-horizon tasks, generalize to novel environments and objects, and exhibit more intelligent and adaptive behaviors in real-world scenarios. This moves the field closer to truly autonomous and versatile embodied intelligence.
