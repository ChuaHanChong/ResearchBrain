# Lightning OPD: Efficient Post-Training for Large Reasoning Models with Offline On-Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers introduce Lightning OPD, an offline on-policy distillation framework for large language models that removes the need for a live teacher server during training. This approach achieves comparable or better performance than standard online OPD with up to a 4.0x speedup and enables scaling to Mixture-of-Experts architectures, while also highlighting the importance of "teacher consistency."

## Problem

- Standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD) requires a live teacher server to continuously score student rollouts, creating a persistent infrastructure bottleneck.
- The continuous teacher server requirement leads to substantial computational overhead, making large-scale OPD experiments costly, complex, and difficult to reproduce.
- High resource demands limit the accessibility of effective on-policy distillation for researchers and institutions with limited access to extensive serving infrastructure.

## Method

- **Lightning OPD** develops a ==two-stage offline on-policy distillation== framework that inherently enforces ==teacher consistency==.
- The SFT training data and the OPD stage both use responses generated by the ==same teacher model== to avoid gradient bias.
- ==Teacher log-probabilities== are precomputed once for reference policy rollouts, eliminating the need for a live teacher server during the main OPD training phase.

## Results

- **Lightning OPD** achieves performance comparable to or exceeding standard OPD across math reasoning (e.g., AIME 2024: **68.1%** for 4B model versus **65.4%** for standard OPD) and code generation benchmarks.
- The framework provides up to a **4.0x** training speedup for an 8B model, reducing total GPU hours from **120 to 30** by eliminating the live teacher server.
- Lightning OPD successfully scales to **30B-parameter** Mixture-of-Experts models, where standard OPD was deemed infeasible due to memory constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Teacher consistency" – using the same teacher model for SFT data generation and the OPD stage – is a critical design principle for effective OPD, even in the online setting.
> - Offline OPD can achieve comparable performance to its online counterpart by ensuring teacher consistency and precomputing teacher signals, decoupling teacher and student during training.
> - The offline gradient naturally incorporates an implicit regularization effect that prevents excessive policy drift from the reference policy without explicit KL penalties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has seen substantial advancements in capabilities such as mathematical reasoning, code generation, and multi-step agent planning. These capabilities are often developed through a multi-stage post-training pipeline. This pipeline typically begins with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on high-quality datasets, followed by a reinforcement learning (RL) stage to further refine the model's reasoning abilities.

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) has emerged as an alternative to the traditional RL stage for post-training LLMs. OPD involves training a student model to emulate the token-level output distribution of a stronger teacher model, utilizing dense per-token advantage signals derived from student-generated rollouts. Compared to Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), OPD is characterized by providing richer supervision, offering increased training stability, and generally incurring lower training costs while achieving comparable or superior performance across various tasks.

Despite its advantages, standard OPD presents a significant practical challenge: it requires a live teacher server to score every student rollout throughout the training process. This requirement introduces a persistent infrastructure bottleneck, necessitating a dedicated multi-GPU teacher server to run in parallel with the student training job. This setup results in substantial computational overhead, rendering large-scale OPD experiments costly and complex to execute and reproduce, particularly for academic researchers or institutions with limited access to extensive serving infrastructure.

This research addresses this specific limitation by investigating whether the benefits of on-policy supervision can be retained while eliminating the need for a continuously running teacher server. The work positions itself as a development within LLM post-training that seeks to enhance the efficiency and accessibility of an established, effective technique (OPD) without compromising its performance. It also introduces a foundational design principle, "teacher consistency," which is argued to be critical for the effective implementation of OPD, whether online or offline, thereby refining the understanding of best practices in distillation pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the significant infrastructure and computational overhead associated with standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD) for large language models. Standard OPD requires a live teacher server to actively generate log-probabilities for every student-generated rollout throughout the entire training duration. This continuous interaction creates an infrastructure bottleneck, consuming considerable GPU resources by co-hosting both student and teacher models. Such a setup renders large-scale OPD experiments resource-intensive, expensive, and difficult to reproduce, posing a barrier to widespread adoption and academic research.

The central objective was to determine if OPD could be performed in an offline manner, thereby eliminating the need for a live teacher server during training. This offline approach would involve precomputing the teacher's log-probabilities once over a set of rollouts and then reusing these values throughout the subsequent training process. This would free up GPU resources and reduce the operational complexity.

Initially, a naive application of this offline precomputation strategy failed to reliably match the performance of standard OPD. This led to a deeper investigation into the underlying causes, revealing a previously overlooked condition termed "teacher consistency." The researchers identified that effective OPD—both offline and online—requires that the same teacher model be used both for generating the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) training trajectories and for providing the reference distribution during the OPD stage. Prior practices, often influenced by conventions from RL from verifiable rewards (RLVR), frequently violated this condition by curating SFT datasets using one teacher (e.g., a higher-performing model) and then using a different teacher for the OPD stage.

The detection of teacher inconsistency as a critical factor became a key motivation. The research hypothesized that violating this condition introduces a gradient bias that degrades model performance, a bias that is particularly pronounced in the offline setting.

Based on this insight, the key objectives for proposing Lightning OPD were:
1. **Develop an Offline OPD Framework**: To design an offline on-policy distillation framework that inherently enforces teacher consistency.
2. **Eliminate Live Teacher Server**: To entirely remove the requirement for a live teacher server during the OPD training phase.
3. **Achieve Comparable Performance**: To demonstrate that this offline approach can achieve performance comparable to or exceeding standard OPD on various reasoning tasks.
4. **Improve Training Efficiency**: To significantly reduce the overall training cost and GPU hours required for OPD.
5. **Provide Theoretical Guarantees**: To formally analyze the proposed method, proving that under teacher consistency, Lightning OPD shares the same optimal solution as standard OPD, exhibits bounded gradient discrepancy, and naturally incorporates an implicit regularization effect that prevents policy drift.
6. **Scale to Larger Models**: To show the applicability and benefits of Lightning OPD for larger and more complex architectures, such as Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, where standard OPD faces feasibility challenges.

In summary, the motivation was to make on-policy distillation a more practical and accessible post-training paradigm by addressing its infrastructure demands, and the core objective was achieved by identifying and incorporating the principle of teacher consistency into an efficient offline framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on Lightning OPD, a two-stage offline on-policy distillation framework designed to enhance efficiency by eliminating the need for a live teacher server during training, while maintaining performance by enforcing a novel principle called "teacher consistency."

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The core of OPD is to optimize a student policy $\pi_\theta$ using an advantage function $A_t(\theta) = \log \pi_T(a_t | s_t) - \log \pi_\theta(a_t | s_t)$, where $\pi_T$ is the teacher model. Standard OPD optimizes an objective $J_{on}(\theta)$ over rollouts sampled from the *current* student policy $\pi_\theta$. Lightning OPD, in contrast, optimizes an objective $J_{off}(\theta)$ over rollouts sampled from a *fixed* reference policy $\pi_{ref}$. The advantage term $A_t(\theta)$ is treated as a fixed scalar (stop-gradient) during parameter updates for both methods.

#### 3.2. Lightning On-Policy Distillation Framework

Lightning OPD comprises two distinct stages:

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
1. A base model $\pi_{base}$ is fine-tuned on a supervised dataset $D_{SFT}$.
2. Crucially, $D_{SFT}$ is constructed from trajectories $(q_i, x_i)$ where responses $x_i$ are sampled from a chosen teacher model $\pi_T$ for prompts $q_i \in \mathcal{Q}_{SFT}$.
3. This fine-tuning process yields the reference policy $\pi_{ref}$.
4. **Teacher Consistency**: A key departure from previous practices is the requirement that the *same teacher model $\pi_T$* used to generate $D_{SFT}$ must also be the teacher used in the subsequent OPD stage. This condition, termed "teacher consistency," is presented as a prerequisite for the offline approximation to be effective.

**Stage 2: Offline On-Policy Distillation (OPD)**
This stage is divided into two phases:

1. **Phase 1: Preprocessing (Offline Data Collection)**:
 - For each prompt $q_j$ in a task-specific prompt dataset $\mathcal{Q}_{OPD}$, a single rollout $x_j$ is sampled from the *fixed reference policy $\pi_{ref}$* (obtained from Stage 1).
 - The teacher model $\pi_T$ (the *same* one used for SFT) is queried *once* to compute and store the per-token log-probabilities $\log \pi_T(a_j_t | s_j_t)$ for all tokens $t$ in each sampled rollout $x_j$.
 - This collection of $(q_j, x_j, \{\log \pi_T(a_j_t | s_j_t)\})$ forms the offline dataset $D_{OPD}$.
 - This phase is a one-time operation, after which the teacher server is no longer needed.

2. **Phase 2: Training (Student Policy Optimization)**:
 - The student model $\pi_\theta$ is initialized from the reference policy $\pi_{ref}$.
 - Training proceeds on mini-batches sampled from the precomputed $D_{OPD}$.
 - At each training step, the advantage $A_t(\theta) = \log \pi_T(a_t | s_t) - \log \pi_\theta(a_t | s_t)$ is computed. The $\log \pi_T$ term is read directly from $D_{OPD}$, while the $\log \pi_\theta$ term is computed online from the current student model.
 - The student parameters $\theta$ are updated using this advantage-weighted policy gradient.
 - Crucially, *no live teacher server is required* during this training phase, as all necessary teacher information is already stored in $D_{OPD}$.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Analysis

The theoretical analysis supports the framework under several standard assumptions: bounded absolute advantage, support coverage (student policy's support is within the reference policy's support), and bounded score function. An additional assumption of bounded teacher mismatch is used for analyzing teacher inconsistency.

- **Theorem 3.5 (Gradient Discrepancy Bound)**: Shows that the $L_2$ norm of the difference between the online gradient $\nabla J_{on}(\theta)$ and the offline gradient $\nabla J_{off}(\theta)$ is bounded by $G \cdot \sigma_A \cdot \sqrt{\chi^2(\pi_\theta \parallel \pi_{ref})}$. This implies that the gradients are identical at initialization ($\pi_\theta = \pi_{ref}$) and remain close as long as the policy drift (quantified by $\chi^2$ divergence) from $\pi_{ref}$ is small.
- **Theorem 3.6 (Shared Fixed Point)**: Demonstrates that the online objective $J_{on}(\theta)$ is equivalent to $-\text{KL}(\pi_\theta \parallel \pi_T)$. If the teacher policy $\pi_T$ is perfectly representable by the student model $\pi_\theta$, then both online and offline OPD objectives share the same global maximum (where $\pi_\theta = \pi_T$) and thus the same zero-gradient fixed point.
- **Theorem 3.7 (Gradient Decomposition)**: Expresses the offline gradient as $\nabla J_{off}(\theta) = \nabla J_{on}(\theta) - \text{Cov}_{\pi_{ref}}[w(x;\theta), f(x;\theta)]$. The covariance term vanishes at initialization and grows with policy drift, acting as an implicit regularization that naturally prevents excessive deviation from $\pi_{ref}$ without requiring explicit KL penalties.
- **Theorem 3.8 & 3.9 (Teacher Consistency Analysis)**: These theorems quantify the impact of violating teacher consistency ($\pi_{SFT_T} \neq \pi_{OPD_T}$). Theorem 3.8 shows that such a mismatch increases the gradient discrepancy bound and, more significantly, introduces a persistent, irreducible additive bias in the offline gradient, $\Vert \nabla J_{off}(\theta) - \nabla J^{\delta=0}_{off}(\theta) \Vert_2 \leq G \sigma_{\Delta}$, where $\sigma_{\Delta}$ measures the teacher mismatch. Theorem 3.9 extends this by demonstrating that even standard (online) OPD suffers from a similar gradient bias when teacher consistency is violated, particularly at initialization. This establishes teacher consistency as a general design principle for effective OPD, not just for the offline variant.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The framework was evaluated on math reasoning (DAPO-Math-17k) and code generation (EpiCoder-func-380k) tasks using Qwen3 model families:
- Student: Qwen3-4B-Base, Teacher: Qwen3-8B
- Student: Qwen3-8B-Base, Teacher: Qwen3-32B
- Student: Qwen3-30B-A3B (MoE), Teacher: Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507

Benchmarks included AIME 2024/2025, HMMT 2025 for math, and LiveCodeBench v5/v6 for code, with pass@1 reported. Training settings for SFT and OPD stages were carefully aligned between Lightning OPD and standard OPD to ensure a fair comparison. The OPD stage was trained for 150 steps, observed to be sufficient for convergence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Lightning OPD achieves performance comparable to standard OPD while providing significant training efficiency improvements, underpinned by the principle of teacher consistency.

#### 4.1. Performance on Reasoning Benchmarks

- **Comparable Accuracy**: Lightning OPD consistently achieves performance on par with standard OPD across all evaluated math reasoning (AIME 2024, AIME 2025, HMMT 2025) and code generation (LiveCodeBench v5, v6) benchmarks. In several instances, Lightning OPD marginally exceeds standard OPD.
 - For the Qwen3-4B-Base student (teacher: Qwen3-8B), Lightning OPD scored 68.1% on AIME 2024, compared to 65.4% for standard OPD and 56.7% for the SFT baseline. On LiveCodeBench v6, it achieved 40.3% compared to standard OPD's 39.3% and SFT's 31.5%.
 - Lightning OPD notably surpassed a recent OPD baseline, ExOPD, achieving 68.1% versus 61.0% on AIME 2024, and 40.3% versus 29.0% on LiveCodeBench v6 for the 4B model.
 - For the Qwen3-8B-Base student (teacher: Qwen3-32B), Lightning OPD achieved 69.9% on AIME 2024 (standard OPD: 68.5%, SFT: 63.7%) and 49.5% on LiveCodeBench v5 (standard OPD: 47.3%, SFT: 44.7%).
- **Consistent Gains**: The improvements from the OPD stage (both standard and Lightning) over the SFT baseline are substantial and consistent across different model scales and task domains, indicating the effectiveness of on-policy distillation.

#### 4.2. Training Cost and Efficiency

- **Significant Speedup**: Lightning OPD demonstrated considerable training speedup by eliminating the live teacher server.
 - For the Qwen3-4B-Base model, a 3.6× speedup was observed, reducing total GPU hours from 72 to 20.
 - For the Qwen3-8B-Base model, a 4.0× speedup was achieved, bringing the total pipeline cost down from 120 to 30 GPU hours.
- **Cost Breakdown**: The actual OPD training stage in Lightning OPD consumes only a moderate fraction of the total budget (8 GPU hours for 4B, 16 for 8B). The remaining cost is attributed to one-time offline operations: rollout collection (10 GPU hours for both) and teacher log-probability precomputation (2 GPU hours for 4B, 4 for 8B). This contrasts with standard OPD's continuous, parallel resource demands.

#### 4.3. Scaling to Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Architectures

- **Feasibility with MoE**: Lightning OPD successfully scaled to a Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base MoE model (with 3B active parameters), using Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 as the teacher.
- **Overcoming Memory Limitations**: Standard OPD was infeasible at this scale on a single 8×H100 node due to memory constraints from co-hosting a 30B student and 30B teacher. Lightning OPD, by precomputing teacher log-probabilities, bypassed this bottleneck.
- **Performance on MoE**: The MoE model achieved 71.0% on AIME 2024 and 60.8% on LiveCodeBench v5, representing performance improvements over the SFT baseline (66.8% and 39.4% respectively).

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: Teacher Consistency

- **Empirical Validation**: The ablation study rigorously confirmed the "teacher consistency" principle. When the SFT-stage teacher and OPD-stage teacher were the same model (the "consistent setting"), it consistently yielded the best performance for both standard OPD and Lightning OPD across all tested student-teacher combinations.
- **Performance Degradation**: Mismatched teachers consistently degraded accuracy.
- **Greater Sensitivity for Lightning OPD**: Lightning OPD showed greater sensitivity to teacher inconsistency compared to standard OPD. For instance, at the 8B scale, a mismatch from Qwen3-32B SFT to QwQ-32B OPD resulted in a 6.8-point drop for Lightning OPD on AIME 2024, whereas standard OPD dropped by 3.7 points. This is attributed to the fixed nature of `π_ref` rollouts in Lightning OPD, which compounds the gradient bias from a mismatched SFT teacher.

#### 4.5. Training Dynamics

- **Implicit Regularization**: Analysis of training dynamics showed that the mean per-token importance weight ($\pi_\theta / \pi_{ref}$) dropped to approximately 0.94 within the initial 20 steps and then plateaued, with its standard deviation stabilizing below 0.1. This behavior supports the theoretical claim of an implicit regularization effect that prevents excessive policy drift, without requiring explicit KL penalties.
- **Rapid Convergence**: Performance metrics, such as AIME 2024 score, converged rapidly, with most of the gains achieved within the first 50 OPD steps, justifying the chosen training budget of 150 steps.
- **Robustness to SFT Quality**: Lightning OPD's performance gains over the SFT baseline were consistent and stable across different SFT checkpoint qualities, indicating its robustness to the specific SFT training length.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Lightning OPD as an approach to on-policy distillation that offers both theoretical grounding and practical benefits for the post-training of large language models.

**Enhanced Efficiency and Accessibility**: The primary significance lies in the substantial improvement in training efficiency, demonstrated by up to a 4.0× reduction in GPU hours for an 8B model. By completely eliminating the requirement for a live teacher server during the main training phase, Lightning OPD addresses a critical infrastructure bottleneck inherent in standard OPD. This significantly lowers the computational and operational barrier for performing advanced LLM post-training, making high-quality on-policy distillation more accessible to a broader range of researchers and institutions, including those in academia or with limited access to large-scale serving infrastructure.

**Enabling Scalability to MoE Models**: The successful application of Lightning OPD to a 30B-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model, where standard OPD was deemed infeasible due to memory constraints, highlights its utility for pushing the boundaries of LLM research. This capability allows for the effective post-training of increasingly large and complex models, unlocking new avenues for developing more capable reasoning systems.

**Introduction of Teacher Consistency as a Design Principle**: The identification and formalization of "teacher consistency" is a notable contribution. This principle, which requires the SFT-stage teacher and the OPD-stage teacher to be the same model, is demonstrated to be crucial for effective OPD, even in the online setting. This insight refines existing understanding of OPD pipelines and provides a clear, actionable design guideline for future research and implementation. The theoretical analysis supporting this principle, including the demonstration of gradient bias when it is violated, adds a foundational understanding to the field.

**Theoretical Guarantees for Offline Distillation**: The theoretical analysis provides robust guarantees for Lightning OPD. Proofs demonstrating a shared optimum with standard OPD under teacher consistency, bounded gradient discrepancy, and an implicit regularization effect contribute to the reliability and trustworthiness of the offline approach. These guarantees suggest that the practical benefits of on-policy distillation can be largely retained without the conventional deployment burden.

**Broader Adoption of On-Policy Distillation**: By decoupling the student training from the continuous presence of a teacher server, Lightning OPD encourages broader adoption of on-policy distillation in scenarios where a persistent teacher server is impractical or too costly. This can accelerate progress in various domains requiring sophisticated reasoning, such as mathematics and code generation, by making an effective post-training technique more widely applicable.

In summary, Lightning OPD offers a practical and theoretically supported solution to a significant challenge in LLM post-training, effectively balancing the benefits of on-policy learning with the demands of computational efficiency and scalability, while also contributing a fundamental design principle to the field of knowledge distillation.
