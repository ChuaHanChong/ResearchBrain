# World4RL: Diffusion World Models for Policy Refinement with Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Tsinghua University developed World4RL, a framework that leverages a high-fidelity diffusion world model to refine robotic manipulation policies within imagined environments. The approach achieved an average success rate of 67.5% across Meta-World tasks in simulation and 93.3% on real-world Franka Emika Panda robot tasks, consistently outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Initial robotic manipulation policies trained via imitation learning are often limited by scarce and undiverse expert data, leading to narrow skill coverage and inconsistencies.
- Training reinforcement learning policies directly on physical robots is costly, time-consuming, and risky, while traditional simulators introduce a performance-degrading "sim-to-real gap."
- Existing learned world models frequently suffer from blurry generations, compounding errors over long rollouts, or are not optimized for end-to-end policy learning, hindering effective policy refinement.

## Method

- World4RL proposes a two-stage framework where an initial policy, a reward classifier, and a diffusion transition world model are first pre-trained on a mix of expert, policy rollout, and random rollout data.
- The diffusion transition model, utilizing a novel two-hot action encoding, predicts future observations with high fidelity given current observations and actions, acting as the dynamic simulator.
- In the policy optimization stage, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) refines the pre-trained policy by interacting with the frozen diffusion world model in imagined rollouts, guided by sparse rewards from the classifier and controlled exploration.

## Results

- World4RL achieved superior world model fidelity, with lowest FVD (326.5), FID (17.1), and LPIPS (0.0192) scores compared to NWM, iVideoGPT, and DiWA across Meta-World benchmarks.
- The framework attained an average success rate of 67.5% on Meta-World tasks, outperforming imitation learning (BC 51.5%), offline RL (TD3+BC 57.7%), and other world-model-based methods (DiWA 59.8%).
- On real-world Franka Emika Panda robot tasks, World4RL demonstrated strong transferability, achieving a 93.3% average success rate, an absolute gain of 25% over the initial BC policy (68.3%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models can effectively serve as high-fidelity learned world models for robotic manipulation, generating sharper and more physically consistent imagined rollouts than prior recurrent state-space models.
> - The two-hot action encoding scheme significantly improves the world model's ability to handle continuous action inputs, maintaining continuity while providing a lightweight discrete structure for accurate dynamics modeling.
> - Controlled policy exploration (clipping action standard deviations) and diversified training data (including random rollouts) are crucial for stabilizing reinforcement learning and preventing out-of-distribution actions within the learned imagined environment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation policies are frequently initialized using imitation learning, which relies on expert demonstrations. However, the performance of such policies is constrained by the limited quantity and diversity of available expert data, leading to issues like inconsistency and narrow coverage. Offline reinforcement learning (RL) attempts to extract more effective policies from existing data but can suffer from overestimation biases, particularly with limited datasets.

Online RL offers a method for refining pre-trained policies through active interaction. Nevertheless, training policies directly on physical robots is costly, time-consuming, and carries significant safety risks, impeding large-scale deployment. Alternatively, training in simulated environments is safer and more efficient but introduces the "sim-to-real gap," where discrepancies between simulation physics and real-world dynamics lead to policies that do not transfer effectively to physical robots.

Recent advancements in generative models, particularly diffusion models, have demonstrated substantial capabilities in generating high-fidelity images and videos. This progress presents an opportunity to develop learned world models that can serve as realistic and flexible simulators, potentially mitigating the sim-to-real gap and enabling efficient policy refinement without extensive real-world interaction.

Previous works have explored world models for planning (e.g., IRASim, NWM) or for policy learning using recurrent state-space models (e.g., DiWA based on RSSM). These approaches, however, often face limitations such as blurry generations, compounding rollout errors, or reliance on computationally intensive test-time planning. This research positions itself by investigating how diffusion model-based world models, known for their generation fidelity, can be integrated to directly enhance pre-trained robotic manipulation policies, aiming to overcome the limitations of existing methods and enable end-to-end policy optimization in imagined environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named World4RL, that leverages diffusion-based world models as high-fidelity simulators to refine pre-trained robotic manipulation policies entirely within imagined environments. This approach aims to address several limitations in current robotic learning paradigms:

- **Overcoming limitations of imitation learning and offline RL**: Pre-trained policies from imitation learning are restricted by data scarcity and narrow coverage. While offline RL can extract better policies, it struggles with overestimation in limited datasets. World4RL seeks to provide a mechanism for continuous policy improvement beyond these initial constraints.
- **Mitigating costs and risks of real-robot training**: Direct online reinforcement learning on physical robots is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe. The framework aims to eliminate the need for online real-world interactions during policy refinement by conducting all optimization within a learned world model.
- **Bridging the sim-to-real gap without traditional simulators**: Training in traditional simulators introduces a sim-to-real gap, where policies trained in a simulated environment perform poorly when transferred to a real robot. By learning a world model directly from real-world data (or a mix of real and imagined data), World4RL intends to create a simulator that more accurately reflects real-world dynamics, thereby facilitating better transfer.
- **Enabling direct end-to-end policy optimization**: Unlike prior world model applications primarily focused on planning, World4RL is motivated to enable direct, end-to-end policy optimization using reinforcement learning algorithms within the imagined environment. This shift aims to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of policy learning.
- **Improving fidelity and consistency of imagined rollouts**: Existing world models, especially those based on recurrent state-space models, can suffer from blurry generations and compounding errors over long imagined trajectories. A key motivation is to leverage the generative capabilities of diffusion models to produce sharper, more temporally coherent, and physically consistent imagined rollouts, which are crucial for stable RL.
- **Tailoring world models for robotic manipulation**: Robotic manipulation tasks involve high-dimensional action spaces and complex interactions. The research is motivated to design specific components, such as an efficient action encoding scheme and appropriate diffusion backbone architectures, to adapt world models effectively to the nuances of these tasks.

In summary, the motivation is to create a robust, sample-efficient, and safe framework for robotic policy refinement by harnessing the advanced generative capabilities of diffusion models to construct a high-fidelity, learned world simulator.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

World4RL is a two-stage framework for robotic manipulation policy refinement, building upon an initial policy trained via imitation learning. The core idea is to employ a high-fidelity diffusion world model as an imagined environment for subsequent policy optimization.

**A. Overall Framework Structure**

World4RL comprises three main components:
1. **Diffusion Transition Model**: A diffusion model that predicts future observations given current observations and actions. This model acts as the dynamic simulator.
2. **Reward Classifier**: A binary classifier that evaluates imagined next observations to provide a sparse success signal, crucial for reinforcement learning in tasks with sparse rewards.
3. **RL-refined Policy**: An agent policy, initialized through imitation learning, that is subsequently refined using reinforcement learning within the imagined environment provided by the world model.

The training pipeline consists of two stages: Pre-training and Policy Optimization.

**B. Pre-training Stage**

This stage focuses on establishing the foundational components before reinforcement learning begins.

1. **Policy Pre-training**:
 - An initial policy ($\pi_\xi$) is trained using imitation learning (Behavior Cloning, BC) on a dataset of expert demonstrations ($D_{exp}$).
 - The policy is parameterized as a stochastic Gaussian policy, outputting the mean and standard deviation of an action distribution conditioned on observation.
 - The training objective is to maximize the log-likelihood of expert actions. This step ensures a stable starting point for the policy, enabling it to perform reasonable actions.

2. **Reward Classifier Pre-training**:
 - A binary reward classifier ($C_\psi$) is introduced to determine if an observed state ($x_{t+1}$) is a success, outputting a probability $r \in$.
 - The classifier is trained on both expert demonstrations ($D_{exp}$, annotated with success labels) and rollout data collected by the pre-trained BC policy ($D_{rollout}$). This diversified training data aims to make the classifier robust to states encountered by the learned policy.
 - A pre-trained ResNet18 is used as the visual backbone, and its parameters are optimized using binary cross-entropy loss.

3. **Diffusion Transition Model Pre-training**:
 - The world dynamics are modeled by a diffusion transition model ($D_\theta$), which predicts the next observation ($x^0_{t+1}$) given a history of observations ($x^0_{t-T:t}$) and actions ($a_{t-T:t}$).
 - It adopts the pre-conditioned denoising formulation of EDM, where a learnable network $F_\theta$ reverses a gradual noising process.
 - **Two-hot Action Encoding**: To handle continuous action inputs effectively within the diffusion model, a two-hot encoding scheme is employed. This method maps each continuous action dimension to its two nearest discrete bins, creating a lossless, differentiable, and interpolation-based representation. This preserves continuity while providing a lightweight discrete structure, avoiding reconstruction errors associated with other encoding methods. The encoded action representation ($z$) replaces raw actions as conditional input to the diffusion model.
 - **Training Data Composition**: The diffusion model is trained on a mixture of datasets to ensure robust and generalizable dynamics modeling:
 - Expert demonstrations ($D_{exp}$) provide high-quality, successful trajectories.
 - Policy rollouts ($D_{rollout}$) generated by the pre-trained BC policy introduce states relevant to the agent's current skill level.
 - Random rollouts ($D_{rand}$) generated by uniformly sampling actions expose the model to a broader range of state-action transitions, preventing overfitting and improving generalization during imagined rollouts.
 - The training objective involves minimizing the difference between the denoised prediction and the true next observation. U-Net 2D serves as the architecture for $F_\theta$.

**C. Policy Optimization Stage**

In this stage, the pre-trained world model is frozen and utilized as a simulator for reinforcement learning.

- **RL Algorithm**: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to optimize the policy ($\pi_\xi$) and a value model ($V_\phi$). PPO aims to maximize cumulative rewards while maintaining stability through a clipped objective function.
- **Imagined Rollouts**: The policy interacts with the frozen diffusion world model. Given a current observation, the policy samples an action, which is then two-hot encoded. The diffusion transition model then predicts the next observation. This process generates synthetic trajectories without real-world interaction.
- **Sparse Reward Signal**: The reward classifier ($C_\psi$) evaluates each imagined next state, providing a sparse binary reward (success/failure) for policy updates.
- **Controlled Policy Exploration**: To mitigate instability caused by out-of-distribution (OOD) actions within the learned world model, a controlled exploration mechanism is implemented. The standard deviation ($\sigma$) of the Gaussian policy's action distribution is clipped to a conservative upper bound (e.g., $e^0$ instead of $e^2$). This constraint keeps sampled actions closer to the distribution covered during world model training, enhancing the reliability of imagined transitions.
- The policy and value models are updated iteratively using the generated imagined rollouts and the sparse rewards from the classifier.

This two-stage approach leverages the high-fidelity of diffusion models to create a reliable imagined environment, enabling sample-efficient and safe policy refinement without requiring further real-world interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to assess World4RL's capabilities in modeling robotic dynamics, enhancing policy learning, and transferring to real-world robots.

**A. World Model Fidelity**

- **Quantitative Metrics**: World4RL was evaluated against NWM, iVideoGPT, and DiWA (including a single-task variant) on Meta-World benchmark environments. World4RL consistently achieved the lowest scores across key video prediction metrics:
 - **FVD (Fréchet Video Distance)**: 326.5 (policy rollouts) and 400.1 (random rollouts), significantly lower than NWM (547.4, 851.9), iVideoGPT (450.3, 531.3), and DiWA (803.6, 1231.0). Lower FVD indicates higher video-level consistency and fidelity.
 - **FID (Fréchet Inception Distance)**: 17.1 (policy) and 23.4 (random), outperforming NWM (30.5, 34.9), iVideoGPT (18.7, 20.7), and DiWA (62.9, 96.5). Lower FID indicates higher distributional quality of generated images.
 - **LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity)**: 0.0192 (policy) and 0.0246 (random), lower than NWM (0.0268, 0.0259), iVideoGPT (0.0256, 0.0283), and DiWA (0.0804, 0.1364). Lower LPIPS indicates higher perceptual similarity between predictions and ground truth.
- **Qualitative Observations**: Visualizations demonstrated that World4RL produces more coherent and physically consistent rollouts that closely match ground-truth trajectories, even for failed executions. In contrast, baseline models often generated blurrier predictions, inconsistent dynamics, or in some cases, scenes from entirely different tasks, particularly when faced with multi-task datasets.
- **Model Scale**: World4RL (330M parameters) is comparable to NWM (320M) and smaller than iVideoGPT (430M), suggesting that its superior performance is attributable to architectural design and temporal consistency rather than simply model size.

**B. Policy Learning Enhancement**

- **Success Rate on Meta-World**: World4RL achieved an average success rate of 67.5% across six Meta-World tasks, outperforming all other baselines:
 - **Imitation Learning**: BC (51.5%) and Diffusion Policy (45.0%). World4RL showed absolute gains of up to 21% (coffee-pull-v2, lever-pull-v2) over BC.
 - **Offline RL**: TD3+BC (57.7%) and IQL (42.0%).
 - **World-Model-Based Methods**: IRASim (57.0%), DiWA (59.8%), and TD-MPC2 (60.0%). World4RL surpassed these by a margin, indicating the benefit of high-fidelity diffusion-based dynamics for stable policy optimization.
- **Sample Efficiency**: Compared to hybrid offline-to-online approaches like Uni-O4 and RLPD, World4RL achieved comparable or superior performance using only fixed expert and policy rollout data (total 10.0k steps), without any additional online interaction. Uni-O4 and RLPD required 470k and 346k online steps, respectively, to reach a similar performance level. This demonstrates World4RL's strong sample efficiency.

**C. Real-World Transfer**

- World4RL was evaluated on six real-world manipulation tasks using a Franka Emika Panda robot.
- It achieved an average success rate of 93.3% over 20 trials per task, significantly outperforming the initial BC policy (68.3%) and Diffusion Policy (88.3%).
- Qualitative observations suggested that policies refined with World4RL executed tasks more decisively and accurately, exhibiting less hesitation compared to BC or Diffusion Policy.

**D. Ablation Studies**

- **Action Encoding**: Comparisons of different action encoding strategies for the world model showed that two-hot encoding consistently yielded the best performance in terms of FVD, FID, and LPIPS compared to one-hot, linear projection, FAST tokenization, and VQ-VAE. This indicates that preserving action continuity while introducing a discrete structure is beneficial for accurate dynamics modeling.
- **Policy Optimization Performance**:
 - Removing **action standard deviation clipping** during PPO training led to clear performance drops and significantly larger variance, highlighting the importance of controlled policy exploration for optimization stability and final performance.
 - Excluding **random rollouts** from world model training also resulted in performance degradation, demonstrating that broader action-space coverage during training is crucial for reliable imagined rollouts.

In summary, World4RL demonstrated superior performance in simulating robotic manipulation environments, enabled more effective and sample-efficient policy refinement compared to various baselines, and showed strong transfer capabilities to real-world robot platforms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The World4RL framework presents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation by integrating high-fidelity diffusion world models with reinforcement learning for policy refinement. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable pathway to overcome several long-standing challenges in robotic learning:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Sim-to-Real Gap**: By enabling policy refinement entirely within an imagined environment learned from limited offline data, World4RL effectively bypasses the high costs and safety risks associated with real-robot online training. The high fidelity of the diffusion world model also mitigates the sim-to-real gap, a critical barrier for transferring policies from traditional simulators to physical robots.
- **Enhanced Policy Performance and Robustness**: The framework consistently yields substantial improvements in success rates compared to imitation learning, offline RL, and other world-model-based methods, both in simulation and real-world settings. This indicates that diffusion models can serve as robust and reliable simulators for effective end-to-end policy optimization.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency**: World4RL achieves comparable or superior performance to hybrid offline-to-online approaches without requiring any additional online samples, relying solely on fixed datasets. This makes it particularly suitable for real-world deployments where collecting interaction data is expensive and time-consuming.
- **Foundation for Future Robot Learning Systems**: The approach establishes diffusion models as a "unifying bridge" between visual prediction and reinforcement learning. This could pave the way for more generalist robot learning systems that can autonomously improve their skills without constant human intervention or costly data collection.
- **Key Design Insights**: The research validates the importance of specific design choices, such as the two-hot action encoding scheme for handling continuous action spaces in diffusion models and the role of controlled exploration and diverse training data for stable policy optimization in imagined environments. These insights can inform the development of future world model-based reinforcement learning systems.

**Potential Impact**:
The successful deployment of World4RL on real-world robots, achieving a 25% absolute gain in success rates, suggests a practical pathway for industrial and service robotics. It could accelerate the development and deployment of complex manipulation skills, making robots more adaptable and capable in various unstructured environments. By reducing the need for extensive real-world trials, it could also lower the barriers to entry for researchers and developers in robotics.

**Future Work**:
The authors acknowledge that the current implementation uses moderate visual resolution and model capacity due to computational constraints. Future research aims to:
- Enhance the fidelity of world models through richer visual representations and increased generative expressiveness to further improve imagined rollout quality.
- Develop more robust reinforcement learning algorithms that can effectively learn under the inherent imperfections of learned dynamics, pushing the boundaries of what can be achieved with imagined environments.
