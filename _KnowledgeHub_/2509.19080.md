# World4RL: Diffusion World Models for Policy Refinement with Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Tsinghua University developed World4RL, a framework that leverages a high-fidelity diffusion world model to refine robotic manipulation policies within imagined environments. The approach achieved an average success rate of 67.5% across Meta-World tasks in simulation and 93.3% on real-world Franka Emika Panda robot tasks, consistently outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Initial robotic manipulation policies trained via imitation learning are often limited by scarce and undiverse expert data, leading to narrow skill coverage and inconsistencies.
- Training reinforcement learning policies directly on physical robots is costly, time-consuming, and risky, while traditional simulators introduce a performance-degrading "sim-to-real gap."
- Existing learned world models frequently suffer from blurry generations, compounding errors over long rollouts, or are not optimized for end-to-end policy learning, hindering effective policy refinement.

## Method

- World4RL proposes a two-stage framework where an initial policy, a reward classifier, and a diffusion transition world model are first pre-trained on a mix of expert, policy rollout, and random rollout data.
- The diffusion transition model, utilizing a novel two-hot action encoding, predicts future observations with high fidelity given current observations and actions, acting as the dynamic simulator.
- In the policy optimization stage, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) refines the pre-trained policy by interacting with the frozen diffusion world model in imagined rollouts, guided by sparse rewards from the classifier and controlled exploration.

## Results

- World4RL achieved superior world model fidelity, with lowest FVD (326.5), FID (17.1), and LPIPS (0.0192) scores compared to NWM, iVideoGPT, and DiWA across Meta-World benchmarks.
- The framework attained an average success rate of 67.5% on Meta-World tasks, outperforming imitation learning (BC 51.5%), offline RL (TD3+BC 57.7%), and other world-model-based methods (DiWA 59.8%).
- On real-world Franka Emika Panda robot tasks, World4RL demonstrated strong transferability, achieving a 93.3% average success rate, an absolute gain of 25% over the initial BC policy (68.3%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models can effectively serve as high-fidelity learned world models for robotic manipulation, generating sharper and more physically consistent imagined rollouts than prior recurrent state-space models.
> - The two-hot action encoding scheme significantly improves the world model's ability to handle continuous action inputs, maintaining continuity while providing a lightweight discrete structure for accurate dynamics modeling.
> - Controlled policy exploration (clipping action standard deviations) and diversified training data (including random rollouts) are crucial for stabilizing reinforcement learning and preventing out-of-distribution actions within the learned imagined environment.
