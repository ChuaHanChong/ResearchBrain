# Chain-of-Frames: Advancing Video Understanding in Multimodal LLMs via Frame-Aware Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Chain-of-Frames (CoF) is a method that trains video Large Language Models to produce reasoning traces explicitly referencing specific video frames. This approach enhances interpretability and achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple video understanding benchmarks, including VSI-BENCH, VIDEO-MME, and MVBENCH.

## Problem

- Current video Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods lack explicit temporal grounding, making reasoning steps difficult to verify.
- Generating high-quality video reasoning datasets is costly and time-consuming due to complex multi-stage annotation processes.
- Many advanced video reasoning techniques necessitate auxiliary networks or complex inference pipelines, increasing computational overhead.

## Method

- Develops ==Chain-of-Frames (CoF)==, a method where video LLMs explicitly reference specific video frames within their reasoning traces.
- Leverages the natural ==video encoding format== of modern multimodal LLMs, such as InternVL, which interleave frames with textual identifiers.
- Fine-tunes these models using a newly constructed dataset, ==COF-DATA==, which integrates both real-world video data and programmatically generated synthetic video data.

## Results

- CoF-enhanced models consistently improved performance by **3.4%** to **16.0%** across five video understanding benchmarks compared to their base models.
- CoF-InternVL3-8B achieved state-of-the-art results on VSI-BENCH, VIDEO-MME, and MVBENCH, outperforming larger open-source and proprietary models like GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro on certain tasks.
- Models trained with CoF demonstrated improved accuracy in quantitative reasoning and reduced hallucination, with the 8B model using frame references in **76.9%** of its responses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly grounding reasoning steps to specific video frames significantly enhances the interpretability and verifiability of a model's decision-making process.
> - A combined approach using both real-world and synthetically generated data is effective for creating large-scale, diverse, and high-quality training datasets for video reasoning.
> - Modern video LLMs' inherent video encoding formats (interleaving frames with textual identifiers) provide a natural foundation for integrating frame-aware reasoning without requiring additional architectural complexity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a specific challenge within the rapidly evolving field of multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly their application to video understanding. Traditional LLMs have demonstrated advanced step-by-step reasoning capabilities, often referred to as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. This paradigm has been extended to multimodal LLMs, enabling them to process and reason about information from both visual and textual inputs.

For video understanding, however, applying CoT presents additional complexities. Models must not only interpret the semantics of text prompts but also comprehend temporal and causal relationships across a sequence of video frames. Existing approaches to integrate CoT into video LLMs generally fall into two categories:
1. **Multi-stage methods**: These approaches employ complex inference frameworks, often involving auxiliary networks or architectural modifications, to extract and integrate relevant key frames from videos into the reasoning trace. Examples include VideoEspresso and Video-of-Thought. While these can improve video reasoning, they are often computationally expensive, less generalizable across tasks, may lose the full temporal context by selecting only a subset of frames, and require complex, costly procedures for collecting training data (e.g., iterative human/LLM annotations).
2. **Single-stage methods**: These methods aim to generate textual reasoning explanations directly, followed by an answer, in a single step (e.g., VideoCoT). A notable limitation of these approaches is a frequent lack of explicit temporal grounding, leading to potential inconsistencies in reasoning related to specific moments in the video. Moreover, the data generation for such methods can still be expensive and limited in scale.

This paper positions itself by proposing a novel single-stage reasoning approach that explicitly integrates temporal information at the frame level, thereby aiming to overcome the computational overhead and loss of context associated with multi-stage methods, while addressing the temporal inconsistencies of prior single-stage approaches. The authors seek to establish a simpler, unified, and self-contained solution for video LLMs that offers explicit temporal grounding without relying on auxiliary modules or complex data generation pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning capabilities of multimodal LLMs for video understanding by introducing "Chain-of-Frames" (CoF) reasoning. CoF aims to provide temporally grounded, step-by-step reasoning in a single-stage inference process.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are:

1. **Address Limitations in Video Reasoning:** Current video LLMs struggle with accurately grounding their reasoning in the temporal dimension of a video. Existing CoT methods either lack explicit temporal alignment with specific frames or rely on computationally intensive multi-stage pipelines that select and process only a subset of frames, potentially losing broader temporal context. The authors aim to bridge this gap by directly referencing specific frames within the natural language reasoning trace.
2. **Simplify Inference Architectures:** The complex, multi-stage inference frameworks prevalent in current video CoT methods (which often employ auxiliary networks for tasks like key frame selection or caption generation) increase computational cost and reduce generality. A key objective is to propose a unified, single-stage inference approach that aligns more closely with the natural CoT prompting successfully applied in language-only LLMs, thereby reducing latency and operational complexity.
3. **Develop Efficient and Scalable Data Generation:** Collecting high-quality, video-grounded reasoning traces for training is a significant bottleneck. Existing methods often depend on expensive human or iterative LLM annotations, limiting dataset scale and potentially introducing inaccuracies. This work aims to create an efficient data generation pipeline capable of producing a large, diverse dataset (COF-DATA) of frame-grounded reasoning traces from both real and synthetic videos at a lower cost, particularly leveraging the precise annotations available in synthetic environments.
4. **Improve Performance Across Diverse Video Understanding Tasks:** The overarching goal is to demonstrate that incorporating frame-aware reasoning through CoF leads to consistent and substantial improvements in the accuracy of video LLMs across a wide array of challenging video understanding benchmarks, including those requiring quantitative reasoning, temporal dynamics analysis, and hallucination detection.
5. **Enhance Model Interpretability:** By explicitly referencing specific frames within the generated reasoning traces, the CoF approach is intended to provide clearer insights into the LLM's decision-making process, thereby improving the interpretability of its internal functioning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on "Chain-of-Frames" (CoF), a strategy that integrates explicit temporal grounding into the reasoning traces generated by video LLMs. This is achieved through a single-stage inference process, differentiating it from multi-stage approaches.

**Chain-of-Frames (CoF) Concept:**
CoF involves generating reasoning traces in natural language that explicitly refer to relevant frames in a video, using identifiers such as "Frame 1", "Frame 2", etc. This frame position-based representation is designed to be agnostic to video duration and sampling frequency, promoting consistency across varied video data. The approach aims to be simple, unified, and self-contained, not requiring auxiliary modules for frame selection or caption generation.

**COF-DATA Training Data Collection Pipeline:**
A two-step pipeline was developed to construct COF-DATA, a large dataset of question-answer pairs with corresponding frame-grounded reasoning traces.

1. **Frame ID Alignment:**
 - **Downsampling:** Videos are downsampled (e.g., to 1 frame per second) to manage context length limitations inherent in video LLMs.
 - **Clipping and Recalibration:** Each frame is mapped to its timestamp, and videos are clipped to a maximum duration (e.g., 30 seconds) to ensure the segment includes all frames with available captions. Frame IDs are then re-calibrated to reflect their new positions within the clipped video, maintaining alignment with existing annotations.

2. **Generation of Question, CoF Trace, and Answer:**
 - **COF-DATA_real (from real videos):**
 - **Source:** Training split of the VIDEOESPRESSO dataset, which provides captions for key frames of real-world videos.
 - **Method:** A Llama3.1-8B-Instruct LLM is prompted using these temporally aligned frame-level annotations to generate triplets of questions, answers, and reasoning traces that include explicit frame references. This process does not involve processing the raw video content during data generation. This yields semantic questions covering diverse aspects of a video.
 - **COF-DATA_synth (from synthetic videos):**
 - **Source:** Training split of the CLEVRER dataset, comprising synthetic videos of 3D objects with rich, frame-level annotations (e.g., shape, material, color, velocity, location, visibility).
 - **Method:** Manual templates are employed to directly generate quantitative questions (e.g., object count, appearance order, relative distance), answers, and chain-of-frames reasoning traces from these highly precise object-specific annotations. This approach significantly reduces data generation cost and enables large-scale, accurate data creation without requiring an LLM in the loop.
 - **Final COF-DATA:** The combined dataset totals 164,186 samples. Samples where frames are referenced within the question (as this is atypical at test time) are filtered out. A controlled fraction of samples without explicit frame references in the reasoning trace is retained to avoid forcing CoF-like reasoning when unnecessary.

**Model Fine-tuning:**
- **Baseline Models:** The CoF approach was applied to three recent open-source video LLMs: InternVL2.5-4B, InternVL3-8B, and Phi-3.5-Vision-4B. InternVL models are particularly suited due to their native interleaved frame-text format (e.g., "Frame-1, Frame-2"), which reinforces temporal structure.
- **Configuration:**
 - InternVL2.5-4B and Phi-3.5-Vision-4B: Full fine-tuning of both the LLM and projection modules, with the vision encoder kept frozen.
 - InternVL3-8B: LoRA-based fine-tuning was used to optimize memory usage.
- **Training Parameters:** Training was conducted on a single H100 node with 4 GPUs, using a learning rate of 2 × 10⁻⁶, a batch size of 2, and a single epoch.
- **Inference:** For evaluation, 30 frames were uniformly sampled from each input video to ensure consistent temporal coverage, irrespective of video length.

**Evaluation Benchmarks:**
The models were evaluated on five established benchmarks, chosen for their diversity in tasks, video types, and durations:
1. **VSI-BENCH:** Focuses on quantitative reasoning tasks (e.g., object counting, appearance order).
2. **VIDEO-MME:** A comprehensive benchmark covering six visual domains and varying video lengths (2 to 60 minutes).
3. **MVBENCH:** Includes 20 tasks requiring multi-frame analysis and understanding of temporal dynamics.
4. **VIDHAL:** Benchmarks temporal hallucinations in video LLMs.
5. **EVENTHALLUSION:** Assesses event-related hallucinations and susceptibility to language priors/vision-language correlation biases.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the efficacy of the Chain-of-Frames (CoF) approach:

1. **Performance Improvement Over Base Models:**
 - CoF-enhanced models consistently outperformed their corresponding base models across nearly all evaluated benchmarks.
 - CoF-InternVL3-8B achieved an average performance increase of 5.1% over the original InternVL3-8B.
 - CoF-InternVL2.5-4B demonstrated a 3.8% improvement over InternVL2.5-4B.
 - The observed performance gains were more pronounced for the larger InternVL3-8B model, suggesting that more capable LLMs may derive greater benefits from CoF-based fine-tuning.

2. **Competitiveness with State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Video LLMs:**
 - Despite having significantly fewer parameters than many leading models, CoF-InternVL3-8B achieved the best results on VSI-BENCH and MVBENCH, surpassing even larger closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro) and open-source models (e.g., Qwen2-VL-72B, LLaVA-OneVision-72B).
 - It also attained the second-best accuracy on VIDEO-MME and VIDHAL, and was the top-performing open-source LLM on EVENTHALLUSION.
 - CoF-InternVL2.5-4B also demonstrated competitive performance, outperforming several larger open-source models and, in some instances, closed-source ones. These results indicate the effectiveness of CoF reasoning in improving video understanding across diverse and challenging benchmarks.

3. **Superiority Over Existing Video CoT Methods:**
 - Comparative analysis against non-publicly available prior video CoT models (e.g., M-LLM, Video-of-Thought) on shared benchmarks (VIDEO-MME and NEXTQA) showed that CoF-based models outperformed these baselines.
 - For example, CoF-InternVL3-8B achieved a 4.9% greater improvement on NEXTQA compared to its baseline, while M-LLM achieved 0.8% over its own baseline. This was achieved while employing a simpler, single-stage inference approach, contrasting with the multi-stage, key-frame retrieval methods of the compared models.

4. **Influence of Synthetic Training Data:**
 - Models trained exclusively on COF-DATA_synth (synthetic video data) consistently outperformed those trained solely on COF-DATA_real (real video data) across most benchmarks. This outcome is significant because synthetic data typically has a substantial distributional gap from real-world benchmarks, yet the model effectively learned the underlying tasks and transferred this knowledge.
 - The combined COF-DATA (real and synthetic) generally yielded the best performance, indicating the value of diverse reasoning traces in training. The low cost and scalability of synthetic data generation were highlighted as a promising strategy.

5. **Effectiveness of Chain-of-Frames vs. Other Reasoning Variants:**
 - Ablation studies comparing CoF against other reasoning strategies (CoT prompting, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with QA only, SFT with CoT without frame references) demonstrated the specific benefits of explicit temporal grounding.
 - CoT prompting alone provided some improvements over standard prompting.
 - SFT with QA-only showed mixed results, sometimes degrading performance.
 - SFT with CoT (without frame references) improved results across most benchmarks but had limitations in specific tasks (e.g., EVENTHALLUSION).
 - The proposed SFT with CoF consistently achieved the highest accuracy across all benchmarks, with improvements ranging from 4.8% to 8.7% over the original InternVL2.5-4B, underscoring the consistent benefits of explicit temporal grounding.

6. **Adaptive CoF Reasoning at Inference Time:**
 - Analysis of generated reasoning traces showed that CoF models learned to actively utilize frame references. Approximately 76.9% of answers generated by CoF-InternVL3-8B during inference included explicit frame references.
 - The number of referenced frames varied adaptively depending on the benchmark and task, indicating that the model selectively employs frame-aware reasoning where most relevant, rather than uniformly. For instance, hallucination benchmarks (VIDHAL, EVENTHALLUSION) showed a higher propensity for frame references.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Chain-of-Frames" (CoF) approach represents a notable advancement in the field of video understanding by multimodal Large Language Models, with several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** CoF introduces a method for temporally grounded reasoning in video LLMs that is demonstrated to be effective. By explicitly linking reasoning steps to specific video frames, it allows models to establish a stronger connection between visual content and textual explanation, leading to more accurate and coherent understanding of dynamic events.

2. **Simplified and Efficient Inference:** The single-stage nature of CoF reasoning eliminates the need for complex, multi-stage pipelines and auxiliary modules (e.g., for key frame extraction) prevalent in previous works. This simplification can lead to reduced computational overhead, lower latency during inference, and a more streamlined architectural design for video LLMs, making them more practical for real-world applications.

3. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Data Generation:** The proposed data generation pipeline, particularly its effective leveraging of synthetic video data (CLEVRER), offers a scalable and significantly less costly alternative to manual or iterative LLM-based annotation processes. The finding that synthetic data, despite its distribution gap, can effectively train models for real-world tasks highlights a promising direction for addressing the data scarcity challenge in multimodal LLM development.

4. **Improved Performance and Competitiveness:** The consistent and substantial performance gains observed across diverse benchmarks, often surpassing larger and more complex state-of-the-art models, position CoF as a competitive method for video understanding. This suggests that the quality of reasoning, as facilitated by CoF, can be a more critical factor than simply model size or architectural complexity.

5. **Increased Interpretability and Trust:** The explicit inclusion of frame references in the reasoning traces provides a direct window into how the LLM arrives at its conclusions. This enhanced interpretability is crucial for understanding model behavior, identifying potential biases, and building trust in AI systems, especially in sensitive applications.

6. **Generalizability Across Architectures:** The successful application of CoF to multiple distinct LLM architectures (InternVL and Phi-3.5-Vision) indicates its generalizability and potential as a foundational technique that can be integrated into various existing and future video LLMs.

Looking forward, the research opens avenues for further exploration, such as scaling up the size and diversity of CoF training data and experimenting with even larger model scales. It also raises questions regarding the adaptation of CoF to video LLMs that employ dynamic frame rates, rather than fixed FPS, which could further broaden its applicability. Overall, Chain-of-Frames offers a practical and effective strategy for advancing the temporal reasoning capabilities and overall performance of multimodal LLMs in video understanding.
