# Towards Intelligible Human-Robot Interaction: An Active Inference Approach to Occluded Pedestrian Scenarios

> [!summary] Summary
> Autonomous vehicles from Tongji University demonstrate enhanced safety and explainability in occluded pedestrian scenarios by employing a novel active inference framework. This framework, incorporating cognitively-inspired mechanisms like Conditional Reset and Hypothesis Injection, proactively manages uncertainty to achieve significantly reduced collision rates compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Autonomous driving systems face critical safety challenges from sudden appearances of occluded pedestrians, characterized by high uncertainty.
- Traditional rule-based or purely data-driven methods struggle with these safety-critical 'long-tail' corner cases due to data scarcity and lack interpretability.
- Existing models often exhibit reactive or data-dependent behaviors, failing to proactively reason about unobserved hazards in a human-like manner.

## Method

- Developed an active inference framework, formalized as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), to enable human-like, belief-driven, proactive decision-making.
- Introduced a Conditional Reset mechanism for belief updating to ensure persistent vigilance for unobserved pedestrians under uncertainty.
- Integrated a Hypothesis Injection technique into the planning process to facilitate proactive reasoning by considering counterfactual 'what-if' scenarios.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an overall collision rate of **5.3%**, significantly outperforming Rule-based (**41.3%**), PPO-LSTM (**27.5%**), and Reactive (**82.2%**) baselines.
- Ablation studies showed Conditional Reset prevented belief decay, leading to successful collision avoidance, while Hypothesis Injection reduced collision rates (e.g., from **9.5%** to **2.3%** in dynamic scenarios) by promoting proactive caution.
- The framework demonstrated real-time computational feasibility, with an average planning step time of **17.69** ms on a GPU, meeting the requirements for 10 Hz real-time control loops.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining a persistent belief in potential, unobserved hazards (via Conditional Reset) is crucial for autonomous agents to exhibit cautious and safe behavior.
> - Proactive planning that considers diverse, counterfactual 'what-if' scenarios (via Hypothesis Injection) significantly improves safety in high-uncertainty situations.
> - Active inference provides an interpretable, mechanism-inspired framework for human-like decision-making that effectively balances pragmatic goals with information-seeking in safety-critical human-robot interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of safe and reliable autonomous driving systems necessitates effective human-robot interaction, particularly in complex and uncertain scenarios such as pedestrian occlusions. Traditional approaches to autonomous driving, including rule-based systems and purely data-driven methods like reinforcement learning (RL), imitation learning, and large language models (LLMs), face limitations. These limitations include the scarcity of data for safety-critical "long-tail" corner cases and a general lack of interpretability in their decision-making processes.

In response to these challenges, mechanism-inspired approaches are gaining attention. Among these, the free energy principle and its process theory, active inference (AI), offer a biologically inspired and theoretically rigorous framework. Active inference has found applications in various domains, including robotics, autonomous driving, and human-computer interaction (HCI), often characterized as a paradigm for human-like decision-making. Existing research has explored active inference for adaptive driving and traffic occlusion scenarios, positioning it as a method for mechanism-inspired, human-like driving and a suitable framework for modeling the human-computer interaction loop.

This work builds upon these foundations by specializing an active inference framework for the specific challenge of occluded pedestrians. It extends prior work by developing a richer generative model for pedestrians and introducing novel mechanisms designed to emulate human-like belief maintenance and proactive reasoning. Unlike game-theoretic approaches that often rely on set-based reachability analysis for worst-case safety guarantees, or purely descriptive cognitive models that aim to reproduce existing human behavior, this research focuses on engineering a prescriptive, safe, and interpretable autonomous agent. Furthermore, it differentiates itself from data-driven methods that learn policies or model components directly from large datasets, instead emphasizing a mechanism-based approach for robustness in safety-critical scenarios where data may be scarce.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the critical safety challenge posed by the sudden appearance of occluded pedestrians in autonomous driving scenarios. This problem is characterized by high inherent uncertainty, which conventional rule-based or purely data-driven methods struggle to manage effectively.

The specific objectives of the proposed framework are:
- To develop a novel active inference framework that enables an autonomous agent to perform human-like, belief-driven, proactive decision-making in occluded pedestrian scenarios.
- To formalize the interaction as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) with a comprehensive set of latent pedestrian states.
- To introduce and integrate two cognitively-inspired mechanisms into the active inference loop: a Conditional Reset mechanism for belief updating to ensure belief persistence under uncertainty, and a Hypothesis Injection technique for planning to enable proactive reasoning through counterfactual "what-if" scenario consideration.
- To demonstrate that the proposed approach can significantly reduce collision rates compared to existing baseline methods (reactive, rule-based, and reinforcement learning approaches).
- To validate that the framework generates explainable and human-like driving behavior that directly reflects the agent's internal belief state regarding latent risks.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted. The authors highlight that while data-driven methods have achieved considerable success, they often fall short in safety-critical corner cases due to data scarcity and their "black-box" nature, which hinders interpretability. Active inference, as a first-principles framework from computational neuroscience, offers a promising alternative due to its biological inspiration and inherent interpretability. By endowing the agent with a belief-driven mechanism, the researchers aim to move beyond simple reactive or data-dependent behaviors towards a proactive and adaptive system that can reason about unobserved hazards, mirroring human cognitive processes under uncertainty. This is presented as a "learning-free, mechanism-inspired method" designed to facilitate trustworthy human-robot interaction by providing legible and context-aware behavior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework operationalizes Active Inference through a continuous perception-action loop, where the ego-vehicle constantly updates its internal belief based on new observations and uses this belief to select actions that minimize expected free energy. The entire interaction is formalized as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).

**System State, Action, and Observation:**
- **System State:** The environment state includes the ego-vehicle's kinematic state (position, velocity, acceleration), a static occluding object, and a hidden pedestrian state. The hidden pedestrian state encompasses an existential state (presence or absence), a kinematic state (position, velocity, acceleration), and latent behavioral parameters (e.g., aggressiveness, motion activation distance).
- **Action:** The ego-vehicle's actions comprise longitudinal and lateral accelerations.
- **Observation:** Observations include a binary pedestrian visibility status, the measured pedestrian position (if visible), and a collision status.

**Belief Representation:**
The agent's belief over hidden states is approximated by a set of `N` weighted particles, forming a Rao-Blackwellized Particle Filter (RBPF). Each particle's state vector includes the ego-vehicle state, the pedestrian's current kinematic state, a binary variable for pedestrian existence, and the particle's initial kinematic state hypothesis (serving as a stable anchor for belief maintenance). Latent behavioral parameters are implicitly captured through a stochastic transition model. The continuous kinematic state of each particle is managed by an embedded Kalman Filter.

**Belief Updating with Conditional Reset:**
This process, driven by the minimization of Variational Free Energy (VFE), updates the agent's belief based on new observations. It involves a prediction step and a correction step.
1. **Prediction Step:** Particles are propagated through an internal world model (comprising a transition model for state evolution and an observation model for sensory outcomes).
2. **Correction Step:**
 - **Conditional Reset:** A key mechanism introduced here. If the pedestrian is currently unobserved and the particle's hypothesized occlusion zone remains unresolved, the particle's pedestrian kinematic state is reset to its initial hypothesis. This prevents the belief in a potential but unobserved pedestrian from prematurely decaying due to lack of direct observation, mirroring human vigilance.
 - Posterior particle weights are calculated based on the likelihood of the observation given the modified prior state. This likelihood function considers the consistency between the particle's pedestrian existence belief and the observation (conditioned on occlusion resolution), as well as measurement likelihoods (kinematic and collision status) when the pedestrian is observed.
 - If the pedestrian is observed, the Kalman filter corrects the kinematic state using the measurement; otherwise, the state carries over from the conditional reset.
3. **Resampling:** Systematic resampling is applied to mitigate particle degeneracy, ensuring computational effort is focused on the most plausible state hypotheses.

**Planning with Hypothesis Injection:**
Planning is framed as an inference problem to find an optimal action sequence that minimizes Expected Free Energy (EFE) over a future horizon `T`. EFE naturally balances pragmatic value (goal-seeking, collision avoidance) and epistemic value (information-seeking).
1. **Hypothesis Injection for Counterfactual Reasoning:** To emulate human-like "what-if" reasoning, the planner uses a temporary, augmented belief for its rollouts. A small fraction ($\rho_H$) of particles are assigned counterfactual pedestrian behaviors (e.g., "Surge," "Reverse," "Freeze"), forcing the planner to consider worst-case scenarios. This augmentation is confined to the planner and does not affect the agent's persistent factual belief.
2. **MPPI-CEM Planner:** A hybrid Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) and Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) approach is employed.
 - A set of candidate policies (action sequences) is sampled.
 - Each policy's total EFE is calculated by summing single-step EFEs. The single-step EFE is approximated via particle rollouts and comprises:
 - *Pragmatic Value:* Encodes preference for desired driving behavior and collision avoidance.
 - *Epistemic Value:* Encourages information gathering to resolve uncertainty about pedestrian visibility.
 - CEM iterations efficiently refine the policy sampling distribution towards low-cost regions.
 - MPPI computes the optimal policy as a weighted average, where weights are proportional to the exponentiated negative cost. The first action of this optimal policy is executed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive simulation experiments to evaluate the proposed framework, focusing on ablation studies of its key mechanisms, computational efficiency, and comparative analysis against baseline methods.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Conditional Reset:** Experiments demonstrated that without the Conditional Reset mechanism, the agent's belief in an unobserved pedestrian decayed to zero, leading to aggressive acceleration into occluded areas and subsequent collisions. Conversely, with Conditional Reset, the agent maintained a persistent belief in potential hazards, leading to cautious deceleration and successful collision avoidance. This highlights the mechanism's role in preserving vigilance under uncertainty.
- **Hypothesis Injection:** Varying the Hypothesis Injection Ratio ($\rho_H$) revealed its direct influence on cautiousness. A low $\rho_H$ resulted in insufficient deceleration and collisions, as the agent planned only for the most likely outcomes. Increasing $\rho_H$ compelled the planner to account for diverse, worst-case pedestrian movements, leading to more cautious behavior, wider safety margins, and aggressive deceleration. Quantitatively, increasing $\rho_H$ from 0.0 to 0.8 reduced the overall collision rate from 9.5% to 2.3%, with significant improvements in high-dynamic uncertainty scenarios (e.g., Deceptive Accelerating Pedestrian collision rate reduced from 19.2% to 1.7%).
- **Initial Presence Belief ($B_0(z_p)$):** The agent's prior belief about pedestrian existence directly modulated its baseline caution. A zero initial belief led to overconfidence and collisions. Increasing $B_0(z_p)$ resulted in progressively more cautious behavior, characterized by earlier and more significant deceleration, and safer trajectories. The overall collision rate decreased from 75.0% (at $B_0(z_p) = 0.0$) to 5.3% (at $B_0(z_p) = 0.8$). This mechanism proved critical in scenarios with high suddenness, such as Sudden Appearance Pedestrian, where absence of initial belief led to catastrophic failure.

**Computational Efficiency:**
The framework was implemented using JAX on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. For the configuration used in the main experiments (100 particles, 100 candidate policies), the average planning step computation time was 17.69 ms. Even under demanding conditions (500 particles, 400 candidate policies), the latency increased to 88.95 ms. These times are reported to be well within the 100 ms threshold required for 10 Hz real-time control loops, indicating computational efficiency and scalability.

**Comparison with Baseline Methods:**
The proposed method was compared against three baselines: Reactive (reacts only to visible threats), Rule-based (fixed deceleration rule near occlusion), and PPO-LSTM (model-free reinforcement learning).
- **Safety Performance:** The proposed method demonstrated superior safety, achieving an overall Collision Rate (CR) of 5.3%. This was significantly lower than the Rule-based method (41.3%), PPO-LSTM (27.5%), and the Reactive baseline (82.2%).
- **Behavioral Adaptability:** Qualitative analysis revealed that baselines exhibited rigid, non-adaptive behaviors, often executing similar trajectories regardless of the evolving risk. For example, the PPO-LSTM agent converged to a generic policy that was brittle in high-risk encounters. In contrast, the proposed method showed proactive and adaptive behavior, dynamically adjusting its maneuver and defensive posture (e.g., swerving or decelerating) based on perceived risk, leading to safer and more efficient interactions.
- **Scenario Robustness:** While baselines sometimes performed adequately in specific scenarios (e.g., Rule-based in Sudden Stop Pedestrian, PPO-LSTM in Sudden Appearance Pedestrian), they often failed dramatically in others. The proposed method demonstrated consistent robustness across all five challenging pedestrian behavioral modes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of human-robot interaction by introducing a framework for autonomous driving that aims to be both safe and interpretable, particularly in challenging occluded pedestrian scenarios. The proposed active inference approach offers a departure from purely data-driven "black-box" models, moving towards systems that can reason about unobserved hazards and justify their actions through their internal belief states.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Enhanced Safety and Interpretability:** By grounding decision-making in active inference and incorporating cognitively-inspired mechanisms, the framework generates driving behaviors that are demonstrably safer and more explainable. This addresses a critical need in autonomous systems for trustworthy operation in complex human environments, where understanding the agent's rationale can be as important as its performance.
- **Human-like Proactive Reasoning:** The introduction of Conditional Reset and Hypothesis Injection mechanisms allows the autonomous agent to emulate human cognitive traits such as persistent vigilance and counterfactual "what-if" reasoning. These mechanisms enable the agent to act proactively and defensively, preparing for worst-case scenarios even when direct sensory evidence is lacking.
- **Foundation for Adaptive and Configurable Risk Sensitivity:** The framework provides intuitive parameters—the Initial Presence Belief and Hypothesis Injection Ratio—that can be used to calibrate the agent's risk sensitivity. This offers a pathway for system designers and manufacturers to define distinct driving profiles, allowing vehicles to adapt their interactive behavior to specific safety requirements, prevailing environmental contexts (e.g., through integration with Vision-Language Models for contextual risk assessment), and potentially user preferences.
- **Computational Feasibility:** The demonstrated real-time computational efficiency suggests the framework's practical applicability for deployment in autonomous driving systems.

While the current evaluation is based on idealized simulations and limited baselines, the findings indicate a significant step towards autonomous systems capable of navigating complex human environments robustly and intelligibly. The work sets the stage for future research into broader benchmarking, integration of game-theoretic concepts for richer interaction modeling, user studies to assess social acceptability and comfort, and ultimately, real-world deployment to validate performance in actual traffic conditions.
