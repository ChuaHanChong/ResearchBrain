# Robust Model-Based Reinforcement Learning with an Adversarial Auxiliary Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from IDLab, University of Antwerp - imec developed Robust MBPO (RMBPO), an algorithm that enhances policy robustness in model-based reinforcement learning by integrating an adversarial auxiliary model. RMBPO improves performance over baseline MBPO in MuJoCo control tasks under distorted physical parameters and varying action noise, without requiring special simulator access during training.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning policies often lack robustness and exhibit significant performance drops when deployed in environments with slight variations or a sim-to-real gap.
- Existing Robust Markov Decision Process (RMDP) methods often require additional conditions like re-sampling transitions or access to a parametric simulator during training, limiting their applicability.
- There is a need for robust RL methods that can generalize and perform reliably despite environmental alterations, especially in online, high-dimensional settings.

## Method

- A novel algorithm, Robust Model-Based Policy Optimization (RMBPO), integrates an auxiliary adversarial model into the standard MBPO framework to approximate pessimistic state transitions.
- The auxiliary model is trained to remain within a Kullback-Leibler (KL) uncertainty set around the nominal model while simultaneously minimizing the expected return under the current policy, thereby learning pessimistic dynamics.
- Policy optimization uses simulated experiences generated by this pessimistic auxiliary model, effectively solving a two-player zero-sum Markov game to maximize worst-case performance.

## Results

- RMBPO demonstrated superior performance over MBPO in a majority of MuJoCo continuous control tasks (InvertedPendulum-v2, Hopper-v3, Walker2d-v3) when evaluated in environments with distorted physical parameters (e.g., mass, friction).
- The algorithm consistently exhibited greater robustness to varying action noise scales during evaluation in Hopper-v3 and Walker2d-v3 environments compared to MBPO.
- Analysis showed that the auxiliary model altered predictions for state variables like angular velocity and x-velocity to represent more challenging control scenarios, supporting the hypothesis of learning pessimistic transitions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The auxiliary model learns to make state transitions pessimistic by predicting outcomes that are less favorable for the agent's performance, such as increased angular velocity of the torso and decreased x-velocity for the Hopper-v3.
> - The hyperparameter "eta" effectively controls the degree of pessimism and the size of the effective KL uncertainty set, allowing for tunable robustness.
> - Training an adversarial auxiliary model that modifies next-state distributions based on the nominal model's output enables robust policy learning without requiring direct environment distortion or repeated transition re-sampling.
