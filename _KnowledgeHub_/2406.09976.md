# Robust Model-Based Reinforcement Learning with an Adversarial Auxiliary Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from IDLab, University of Antwerp - imec developed Robust MBPO (RMBPO), an algorithm that enhances policy robustness in model-based reinforcement learning by integrating an adversarial auxiliary model. RMBPO improves performance over baseline MBPO in MuJoCo control tasks under distorted physical parameters and varying action noise, without requiring special simulator access during training.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning policies often lack robustness and exhibit significant performance drops when deployed in environments with slight variations or a sim-to-real gap.
- Existing Robust Markov Decision Process (RMDP) methods often require additional conditions like re-sampling transitions or access to a parametric simulator during training, limiting their applicability.
- There is a need for robust RL methods that can generalize and perform reliably despite environmental alterations, especially in online, high-dimensional settings.

## Method

- A novel algorithm, Robust Model-Based Policy Optimization (RMBPO), integrates an auxiliary adversarial model into the standard MBPO framework to approximate pessimistic state transitions.
- The auxiliary model is trained to remain within a Kullback-Leibler (KL) uncertainty set around the nominal model while simultaneously minimizing the expected return under the current policy, thereby learning pessimistic dynamics.
- Policy optimization uses simulated experiences generated by this pessimistic auxiliary model, effectively solving a two-player zero-sum Markov game to maximize worst-case performance.

## Results

- RMBPO demonstrated superior performance over MBPO in a majority of MuJoCo continuous control tasks (InvertedPendulum-v2, Hopper-v3, Walker2d-v3) when evaluated in environments with distorted physical parameters (e.g., mass, friction).
- The algorithm consistently exhibited greater robustness to varying action noise scales during evaluation in Hopper-v3 and Walker2d-v3 environments compared to MBPO.
- Analysis showed that the auxiliary model altered predictions for state variables like angular velocity and x-velocity to represent more challenging control scenarios, supporting the hypothesis of learning pessimistic transitions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The auxiliary model learns to make state transitions pessimistic by predicting outcomes that are less favorable for the agent's performance, such as increased angular velocity of the torso and decreased x-velocity for the Hopper-v3.
> - The hyperparameter "eta" effectively controls the degree of pessimism and the size of the effective KL uncertainty set, allowing for tunable robustness.
> - Training an adversarial auxiliary model that modifies next-state distributions based on the nominal model's output enables robust policy learning without requiring direct environment distortion or repeated transition re-sampling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes across various domains, including robotics, board games, and arcade games. However, a significant challenge limiting its widespread real-world application is the lack of robustness and safety in learned policies. Specifically, policies trained in a particular Markov Decision Process (MDP) often exhibit a considerable performance drop when evaluated in environments that are only slightly altered, a phenomenon frequently referred to as the "simulation-to-reality" (sim2real) gap or related to inherent variability in real control systems.

Addressing this issue, a range of approaches has emerged, including domain randomization and transfer learning. This paper situates its work within the framework of Robust Markov Decision Processes (RMDPs), which aims to maximize the worst-case performance of an RL agent over an uncertainty set of possible MDPs. Prior research utilizing the RMDP formalism has demonstrated enhanced robustness against environmental perturbations. However, many existing RMDP methods frequently necessitate additional conditions on the training environment, such as the ability to re-sample transitions multiple times or access to a parametric simulator during training.

This work contributes to the field of robust model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) by proposing an algorithm that operates within the RMDP framework without imposing these additional environmental requirements. It builds upon ideas from prior work in adversarial RL and robust offline RL, such as Rigter et al. (2022) and Pinto et al. (2017), which leverage adversarial components. While Pinto et al. (2017) also utilize an adversarial approach for robustness, their methodology requires simulator access during training, a condition this paper seeks to avoid. Similarly, Wang et al. (2024) explore KL uncertainty sets but require a simulator capable of arbitrary next-state sampling. This paper's method can be seen as a model-based counterpart or complement to recent model-free robust RL approaches (Zhou et al., 2024; Queeney & Benosman, 2024) by specifically focusing on learning a pessimistic world model within the MBRL paradigm. By developing a robust MBRL algorithm that does not rely on specific simulator features or extensive environmental interaction beyond typical MBRL needs, this research aims to make robust RL more generally applicable in settings where environmental access is limited or costly.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the robustness of learned policies in Reinforcement Learning, particularly within a model-based setting. The motivation for this objective stems from critical limitations observed in current RL applications. While RL agents demonstrate impressive performance in controlled environments, their efficacy often degrades significantly when faced with even minor discrepancies between the training and deployment environments. This lack of robustness impedes the adoption of RL in real-world scenarios, where factors such as the "simulation-to-reality" gap and inherent variability in physical systems are commonplace. The paper highlights the necessity for policies that can generalize and perform reliably despite these environmental alterations.

To formally address this problem, the authors adopt the framework of Robust Markov Decision Processes (RMDPs). In an RMDP, an agent operates in an environment where the true MDP is unknown but belongs to a predefined uncertainty set. The objective shifts from maximizing expected return in a single MDP to maximizing the expected return in the *worst-case* MDP within that uncertainty set. This robust objective is formulated as a two-player zero-sum Markov game, where an agent's policy aims to maximize cumulative reward, while an adversary seeks to find an MDP within the uncertainty set that minimizes the policy's return.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are:

1. **Develop a novel robust model-based RL algorithm:** The primary goal is to introduce an algorithm that can improve policy robustness in an online setting. This is achieved by incorporating an auxiliary pessimistic world model into a standard MBRL framework (specifically, MBPO). This auxiliary model is designed to be updated adversarially.
2. **Overcome limitations of existing robust RL methods:** A key motivation is to provide a robust RL solution that does not impose additional requirements on the training environment. Many existing robust RL techniques require access to a parametric simulator for environment distortion or the ability to re-sample transitions multiple times. This work explicitly aims to avoid such dependencies.
3. **Empirical evaluation on high-dimensional benchmarks:** The authors intend to assess the practical effectiveness of their proposed algorithm by evaluating its empirical performance on high-dimensional continuous control tasks, specifically using Gym MuJoCo benchmarks.
4. **Analyze the learned pessimistic model:** A further objective is to investigate how the predictions of the learned robust auxiliary model differ from those of the nominal model. This analysis aims to provide insight into how pessimism is achieved and how the auxiliary model modifies state transitions to represent a worst-case scenario.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to contribute towards making model-based RL agents more robust and thus more suitable for real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology focuses on enhancing policy robustness within a model-based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) framework by introducing an adversarial auxiliary model. The approach leverages the Robust Markov Decision Process (RMDP) formalism, wherein the objective is to maximize performance in the worst-case MDP within a defined uncertainty set.

#### 3.1. Model-Based Reinforcement Learning Context

The work operates within the standard Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, where an agent learns an optimal policy to maximize discounted cumulative rewards. In MBRL, the agent learns a parameterized model of the environment's dynamics, $p_{\theta}(s'|s, a)$, from collected interaction data. This learned model is then used to generate simulated experiences, which augment real experiences for policy updates. The paper integrates its novel component into the Model-Based Policy Optimization (MBPO) algorithm, which uses a neural network to predict the mean and covariance of next states and rewards, and then employs Soft-Actor Critic (SAC) for policy updates using both real and simulated data.

#### 3.2. Robust Markov Decision Processes and KL Uncertainty Set

The robust objective, $J_{P,\pi} = \max_{\pi \in \Pi} \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} E_{P,\pi}[\sum \gamma^t r(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})]$, necessitates solving an inner-loop problem of finding the worst-case MDP, $P^* \in \mathcal{P}$, for a given policy. The paper selects the Kullback-Leibler (KL) uncertainty set, $P_{KL} = \{ P \in P_{feasible} | D_{KL}(P || \bar{P}) \le \epsilon \}$, where $\bar{P}$ is the nominal transition kernel and $\epsilon$ is a threshold. This set includes all MDPs whose transition dynamics are within a specified KL divergence from the nominal model. The combined optimization problem is conceptualized as a two-player zero-sum Markov game, where the policy optimizes to maximize return, and an adversary seeks to minimize it by selecting the worst-case MDP.

#### 3.3. Auxiliary Adversarial Model

The core innovation is the introduction of an auxiliary parameterized model, $g_{\psi}$, designed to approximate the pessimistic transition model ($P^*$). Unlike direct approximation of $P^*$, this auxiliary model takes as input the outputs (mean $\mu_{\theta}$ and covariance $\Sigma_{\theta}$) of the nominal learned transition model $p_{\theta}(s', r|s, a)$, in addition to the current state and action. Both $p_{\theta}$ and $g_{\psi}$ are assumed to define diagonal multivariate Gaussian distributions, enabling closed-form computation of the KL divergence $D_{KL}(g_{\psi} || p_{\theta})$. This $D_{KL}(g_{\psi} || p_{\theta})$ is used as an approximation for $D_{KL}(g_{\psi} || \bar{P})$, allowing $g_{\psi}$ to represent a model within the KL uncertainty set centered around the *learned* nominal dynamics.

#### 3.4. Training the Auxiliary Model

The auxiliary model $g_{\psi}$ is trained to achieve two objectives:
1. **Maintain proximity to the nominal model:** Ensure that $g_{\psi}$ remains within the defined KL uncertainty set around $p_{\theta}$.
2. **Be pessimistic:** Minimize the expected return under the current policy by identifying low-reward transitions.

These objectives are balanced by minimizing a composite loss function, $J_g(\psi)$:
$J_g(\psi) = E_{(s',r)\sim g_{\psi}, s \sim d^{\psi,\theta}_{\pi}, a \sim \pi} [ D_{KL}(g_{\psi} || p_{\theta}) + \eta \cdot \ln(g_{\psi}(s', r|s, a, p_{\theta}(\cdot|\cdot)) \cdot sg(r + \gamma V^{\theta,\phi}_{\psi}(s')) ]$

- The first term, $D_{KL}(g_{\psi} || p_{\theta})$, penalizes deviations from the nominal model, enforcing the uncertainty set constraint.
- The second term incentivizes $g_{\psi}$ to assign higher likelihood to transitions with low returns (calculated using the approximate value function $V(.)$ with a `stop_grad` operator) and lower likelihood to high-return transitions. This mechanism makes $g_{\psi}$ a pessimistic approximation.
- The hyperparameter $\eta$ controls the influence of the value function term, determining the degree of pessimism and the size of the effective KL uncertainty set.

#### 3.5. Proposed Algorithm: Robust MBPO (RMBPO)

The RMBPO algorithm integrates the auxiliary model into MBPO. The training process involves alternating updates:
1. **Nominal Model Update:** The nominal model $p_{\theta}$ is updated by maximizing the likelihood of real environmental experiences.
2. **Auxiliary Model Update:** The auxiliary model $g_{\psi}$ is updated based on the loss function $J_g(\psi)$, using real environment data and the outputs of $p_{\theta}$.
3. **Policy Optimization:** After model updates, on-policy rollouts are performed. Critically, these rollouts use the pessimistic transitions predicted by the auxiliary model $g_{\psi}$ (which takes inputs from $p_{\theta}$). The unrolled data is then used to update the policy $\pi_{\phi}$ via SAC.
This alternating optimization effectively sets up the two-player Markov game, where $g_{\psi}$ acts as the inner-loop adversary and SAC optimizes the outer-loop policy. Additionally, a small amount of Gaussian noise is added to actions in the environment to ensure a well-defined uncertainty set.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research addressed two primary questions: whether the auxiliary model learns pessimistic state transitions and if it enhances policy robustness.

#### 4.1. Learning of Pessimistic State Transitions

To investigate whether the auxiliary model $g_{\psi}$ learns pessimistic state transitions, a case study was conducted on the MuJoCo Hopper-v3 environment. This involved examining how $g_{\psi}$ modifies state transitions compared to the nominal model $p_{\theta}$ and the influence of the hyperparameter $\eta$ on these modifications.

- **Influence of $\eta$**: The results indicate that increasing the value of $\eta$ consistently leads to larger deviations of the robust predictions (from $g_{\psi}$) from the nominal model's predictions ($p_{\theta}$). This suggests that $\eta$ effectively controls the degree of pessimism.
- **Specific State Variable Modifications**:
 - The three most influenced state variables were the angular velocity of the torso, the thigh hinge, and the leg hinge.
 - The robust model showed an increase in the angular velocity of the torso. The authors interpret this as making the Hopper more difficult to control, thereby increasing its propensity to fall.
 - Conversely, the angular velocities of the actuated parts, such as the leg and thigh hinges, were observed to decrease. This modification is also interpreted as making the system harder to control from the agent's perspective.
 - Additionally, the adversarial model was found to lower the x-velocity of the Hopper-v3, which correlates with a reduced reward as the agent is typically rewarded for rapid forward movement.
- **Conclusion on Pessimism**: These observations are presented as support for the hypothesis that the auxiliary model learns to make state transitions more pessimistic by predicting outcomes that are less favorable for the agent's performance. The authors note that further examination would be beneficial for definitive confirmation.

#### 4.2. Policy Robustness Enhancement

The main evaluation focused on comparing the robustness of RMBPO against MBPO in various MuJoCo control tasks under distorted evaluation environments. Agents were trained in the nominal environment, and their performance was then assessed across a range of modified simulation parameters. Results were averaged over three seeds, with bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals reported.

- **Distorted Simulation Parameters**:
 - In InvertedPendulum-v2, the pendulum mass was distorted.
 - In Hopper-v3, the torso mass and friction coefficient were distorted.
 - In Walker2d-v3, the torso mass and friction coefficient were distorted.
- **RMBPO Performance**: In the majority of these experimental setups, RMBPO demonstrated superior performance compared to MBPO. This improvement was observed in both the mean return and the lower limit of the confidence interval across different distortion levels.
- **Comparison with Existing Works**: The observed performance of RMBPO is noted to fall between that of non-robust RL algorithms and RARL (Pinto et al., 2017). This is an expected outcome, as RARL can directly distort the simulator during training, a capability RMBPO does not require.
- **Robustness to Action Noise**: A separate set of experiments examined robustness to modifications in the standard deviation of action noise during evaluation. A noise scale of 1 represented the nominal training environment. RMBPO consistently exhibited greater robustness to varying action noise scales in both Hopper-v3 and Walker2d-v3 environments compared to MBPO.

These findings collectively support the claim that the proposed auxiliary adversarial model contributes to making the learned policy more robust against environmental perturbations and variations in action noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a novel methodology for developing robust policies within the framework of model-based reinforcement learning, particularly for high-dimensional and online settings. By introducing an auxiliary adversarial model, the work offers a method to address the critical issue of RL policy fragility in environments that deviate from training conditions, without imposing stringent requirements on the training environment itself, such as the need for a parametric simulator or repeated transition re-sampling.

The demonstrated improvements in policy robustness across various MuJoCo control tasks are significant. The ability of RMBPO to maintain higher performance than MBPO in environments with distorted physical parameters (like mass and friction) and varying action noise scales suggests a practical step towards bridging the "simulation-to-reality" gap. Enhanced robustness is a crucial factor for the wider adoption of RL in real-world applications, especially in safety-critical domains like robotics and autonomous systems, where even minor discrepancies between simulation and reality can lead to performance degradation or unsafe behaviors. By learning a pessimistic world model, the agent can potentially generalize better to unforeseen but plausible environmental variations.

The analytical investigation into how the auxiliary model modifies state transitions provides insights into the mechanism of pessimism. Understanding that the model tends to predict outcomes that make control harder or reduce rewards contributes to the interpretability of robust RL methods.

Potential impacts include:
- **Increased Reliability of RL Systems:** The method can contribute to more reliable RL agents capable of operating effectively in uncertain or variable real-world environments.
- **Reduced Sim2Real Gap:** By training policies that are inherently more robust to environmental variations, the technique could alleviate some challenges associated with transferring policies from simulation to physical hardware.
- **Broader Applicability:** Since the method avoids specific simulator requirements, it could be more broadly applicable in scenarios where detailed environmental control or simulation capabilities are limited.

The authors also outline future work, including extending the methodology to a wider range of algorithms and environments, incorporating a secondary non-robust policy to maintain exploration capabilities, and integrating the approach with latent space models like PlaNet and DreamerV3. These directions suggest a pathway for further strengthening the approach and extending its reach within the broader field of robust and model-based RL.
