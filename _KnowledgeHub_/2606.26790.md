# OPID: On-Policy Skill Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> OPID (On-Policy Skill Distillation) introduces a framework that enhances large language model (LLM) agents by extracting hierarchical, natural-language skills directly from their completed on-policy trajectories. This method consistently improves agent performance across embodied reasoning, web navigation, and search-based QA tasks, demonstrating up to a 26.5 point increase in success rate on WebShop for smaller LLMs and better sample efficiency compared to outcome-only reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Traditional outcome-based Reinforcement Learning for LLM agents provides sparse and delayed rewards, making fine-grained credit assignment difficult in long-horizon tasks.
- Existing skill-conditioned self-distillation methods often rely on external skill libraries or privileged contexts, leading to high maintenance costs and potential context mismatches with the agent's current policy.
- The lack of fine-grained, state-conditioned feedback hinders policy refinement at critical decision points in multi-turn interactions.

## Method

- An LLM-based analyzer extracts hierarchical skills (episode-level for global workflows, step-level for critical local decisions) directly from the agent's own successful and failed on-policy trajectories.
- A critical-first routing mechanism selectively applies the most appropriate skill (step-level at critical timesteps, episode-level otherwise) and injects it into the interaction history to calculate token-level skill advantages via self-distillation.
- These token-level skill advantages are combined with episode-level outcome rewards to form a comprehensive **OPID** advantage, which is then used to optimize the policy, ensuring skills are internalized for inference without external dependencies.

## Results

- OPID consistently improved agent success rates by **7.1** to **26.5** points across ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-based QA tasks, significantly outperforming outcome-only GRPO baselines.
- The framework matched or surpassed strong hybrid and self-distillation baselines, achieving the best average performance on ALFWorld (+**1.7** to +**5.0** points) and Search-based QA (+**0.2** to +**0.4** points) for various LLM backbones.
- OPID demonstrated enhanced training efficiency by reducing average episode lengths and achieving superior performance with less training data, and showed better cross-domain generalization, improving success rates by +**7.7** points on unseen ALFWorld tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extracting skills directly from on-policy trajectories ensures that the distilled supervision is inherently distribution-matched to the agent's current behavior, avoiding issues of external skill reliance and context mismatch.
> - A hierarchical approach to skills (combining broad episode-level and precise step-level guidance), coupled with critical-first routing, provides comprehensive and targeted supervision for both global task flow and local decision-making.
> - Internalizing hindsight skills into the policy parameters during training allows agents to leverage this knowledge effectively at inference time without requiring external prompts or computational overhead, leading to more robust and generalizable behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the rapidly developing field of training Large Language Models (LLMs) as interactive agents capable of performing complex, long-horizon tasks. These agentic LLMs are increasingly deployed in various environments, including embodied household settings (e.g., ALFWorld), web navigation (e.g., WebShop), and search-augmented reasoning (e.g., Search-based QA). Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a primary paradigm for post-training such agents, allowing direct optimization of policies based on environmental feedback. Outcome-based RL methods, like GRPO, provide a stable optimization backbone for on-policy rollouts.

However, a significant challenge in agentic RL is the sparsity and delay of environmental rewards. Terminal rewards indicate overall success or failure but provide limited information for intermediate decision-making, especially in long-horizon tasks where early mistakes can cascade. This leads to a credit-assignment problem, making it difficult for agents to learn fine-grained decision-level adjustments.

To address this, on-policy distillation and self-distillation techniques have been explored to provide dense, token-level supervision. These methods train models on their own sampled outputs, often using auxiliary teacher signals or by comparing the policy under different contexts. A promising direction within this area is skill-conditioned self-distillation, where procedural knowledge (e.g., subgoals, rules) augments a "teacher" branch, and its preferences are distilled into the student policy.

Existing skill-based methods, however, frequently rely on external skill libraries, retrieved skill files, or maintained skill memories. This approach introduces challenges such as the cost and complexity of maintaining these external resources and the potential for a mismatch between retrieved skills and the actual state distribution induced by the current policy. Such mismatches can be particularly detrimental in multi-turn interactions, where minor deviations can lead to significant state drift, rendering externally provided skills less effective.

OPID addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that extracts skill supervision directly from the agent's *own completed on-policy trajectories*. This design choice aims to provide dense, distribution-matched hindsight supervision without the need for external skill repositories or privileged contexts during inference, thereby positioning itself as an advancement in skill-distillation for agentic RL that prioritizes on-policy, internalized learning.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for OPID stems from the limitations observed in current Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigms for training Large Language Model (LLM) agents, particularly in long-horizon tasks.

**Primary Motivation: Addressing Sparse and Delayed Rewards in Outcome-Based RL**
Traditional outcome-based RL methods, while effective for task-level optimization, provide only coarse supervision. Rewards in agentic environments are typically sparse, delayed, and high-variance, often only indicating a terminal success or failure. This means an agent receives feedback on whether an entire trajectory achieved its goal, but not which specific intermediate decisions or actions contributed positively or negatively to that outcome. This "missing credit-assignment signal" is particularly problematic in long-horizon tasks, where:
- An early error can derail an entire episode, but its consequence might only be observed much later.
- Repeated invalid actions can accumulate without immediate negative feedback.
- The impact of a local decision may only become apparent several turns after it is made.
This lack of fine-grained feedback hinders the policy's ability to refine its decision-making at critical junctures.

**Secondary Motivation: Overcoming Challenges of Existing Skill-Conditioned Self-Distillation**
While on-policy self-distillation and skill-conditioned methods offer a potential solution by providing dense, token-level guidance, current approaches present their own set of challenges:
- **Reliance on External Knowledge**: Many existing skill-based methods depend on external skill libraries, manually curated action templates, or privileged context retrieved from external sources. This necessitates non-trivial maintenance, including insertion, refinement, deletion, and retrieval mechanisms for these skills.
- **Context Mismatch**: Skills retrieved from external memories or pre-defined libraries may not perfectly align with the state distribution induced by the agent's *current* policy. In dynamic, multi-turn interactions, even small deviations from an assumed trajectory can lead to state drift, making externally provided skills unreliable or potentially harmful.

**Key Objectives of OPID:**
Based on these motivations, OPID aims to achieve the following objectives:
1. **On-Policy Hindsight Skill Extraction**: Develop a mechanism to directly extract skill supervision from the agent's *own completed on-policy trajectories*. This ensures that the derived skills are inherently distribution-matched to the agent's current behavior, avoiding the issues of external skill maintenance and context mismatch.
2. **Hierarchical Skill Representation**: Create a hierarchical representation of hindsight knowledge, distinguishing between:
 - **Episode-level skills**: To capture global workflows, overarching strategies, or general failure-avoidance rules for an entire trajectory.
 - **Step-level skills**: To provide precise, state-conditioned guidance for specific, critical timesteps where pivotal decisions are made.
3. **Critical-First Skill Routing**: Implement a routing mechanism that selectively applies the most appropriate skill granularity at each decision step, prioritizing precise step-level skills at critical junesteps and defaulting to broader episode-level guidance otherwise.
4. **Integration with RL via Self-Distillation**: Convert these routed hindsight skills into dense, token-level shaping signals, which are then combined with the primary outcome-based RL objective for policy optimization. This aims to provide fine-grained credit assignment without diminishing the overall task-level objective.
5. **Inference-Time Independence**: Ensure that the learned policy does not require any analyzer, external skill retrieval, or privileged context during inference, meaning skills are fully internalized into the model's parameters during training.

### 3. Methodology and approach

OPID operates in three main stages: hierarchical skill extraction from on-policy trajectories, critical-first skill routing and self-distillation, and policy optimization combining skill and outcome advantages.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The agentic task is modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). At each timestep $t$, an agent observes $o_t$ and generates a textual response or action $y_t$ according to its policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot | h_t)$, where $h_t$ is the interaction history. A completed trajectory $\tau = \{(o_t, y_t, r_t)\}_{t=0}^{T-1}$ receives an outcome reward $R(\tau)$, typically sparse and terminal. The objective is to maximize $J(\pi_\theta) = E_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta}[R(\tau)]$. Following the GRPO-style training, a group of $N$ trajectories $G_q = \{\tau^{(1)}, \dots, \tau^{(N)}\}$ is sampled for each task prompt $q$.

#### 3.2. On-Policy Skill Extraction

To provide fine-grained supervision beyond sparse terminal rewards, OPID extracts natural-language skills from completed on-policy trajectories. These skills are hierarchical:
- **Episode-level skills ($s^{\text{ep}}_\tau$)**: These summarize the global behavioral pattern of an entire trajectory. For successful trajectories, they capture reusable workflows. For failed trajectories, they describe failure-avoidance rules. These skills are broad and provide stable default guidance.
- **Step-level skills ($s^{\text{step}}_{\tau,t}$)**: These capture local decision knowledge at specific "critical" timesteps $t$. Critical steps are those where the final outcome strongly depends on a particular choice (e.g., avoiding invalid actions, correcting subgoals). These skills are precise but sparse and state-dependent.

An LLM-based analyzer, denoted $A(\tau)$, processes an ordered trajectory record (task prompt, observations, responses, feedback, outcome) to extract these skills: $A(\tau) = [s^{\text{ep}}_\tau, \{s^{\text{step}}_{\tau,t}\}_{t \in C_\tau}]$, where $C_\tau$ is the set of identified critical timesteps.

#### 3.3. Critical-First Skill-Conditioned Self-Distillation

This stage involves routing the appropriate skill and converting its effect into token-level self-distillation signals.
- **Critical-First Skill Routing**: Instead of applying all skills or a single skill type uniformly, OPID uses a critical-first routing mechanism. For a given timestep $t$ in trajectory $\tau$, the routed skill $s_{\tau,t}$ is:
 - $s^{\text{step}}_{\tau,t}$ if $t \in C_\tau$ (a critical timestep).
 - $s^{\text{ep}}_\tau$ otherwise (a normal timestep).
 This prioritizes the more specific step-level skill when available, falling back to the broader episode-level skill as a default.

- **Skill Injection**: The selected skill $s_{\tau,t}$ is injected into the agent's interaction history $h_{\tau,t}$ using a deterministic skill-injection function $H(\cdot, \cdot)$, creating an augmented history $\tilde{h}_{\tau,t} = H(h_{\tau,t}, s_{\tau,t})$.

- **Paired Scoring and Skill Advantage Calculation**: The policy $\pi_{\theta^{\text{old}}}$ (the policy before the current update) re-scores the *same sampled response* $y_{\tau,t}$ under two contexts:
 1. The original context: $\ell^{\text{old}}_{\tau,t,\ell} = \log \pi_{\theta^{\text{old}}}(y_{\tau,t,\ell} | h_{\tau,t}, y_{\tau,t,<\ell})$
 2. The skill-augmented context: $\ell^{\text{skill}}_{\tau,t,\ell} = \log \pi_{\theta^{\text{old}}}(y_{\tau,t,\ell} | \tilde{h}_{\tau,t}, y_{\tau,t,<\ell})$
 The token-level skill-based self-distillation advantage is then defined as the log-probability shift:
 $A^{\text{skill}}_{\tau,t,\ell} = (\ell^{\text{skill}}_{\tau,t,\ell} - \ell^{\text{old}}_{\tau,t,\ell})m_{\tau,t,\ell}$, where $m_{\tau,t,\ell}$ is a valid response-token mask. A positive $A^{\text{skill}}$ indicates the skill made the token more likely, suggesting consistency; a negative value indicates less alignment. This provides dense, token-level guidance.

#### 3.4. Policy Optimization with Skill Advantage

The final advantage for policy optimization combines the episode-level outcome feedback with the token-level skill supervision.
- **Episode Advantage**: For each trajectory $\tau$ in a group $G_q$, a GRPO-style episode-relative advantage $A^{\text{ep}}_\tau$ is computed by normalizing its outcome reward $R(\tau)$ within its prompt group: $A^{\text{ep}}_\tau = (R(\tau) - \mu_q) / \sigma_q$, where $\mu_q$ and $\sigma_q$ are the group mean and standard deviation of rewards. This scalar advantage is broadcast to all valid response tokens: $A^{\text{ep}}_{\tau,t,\ell} = A^{\text{ep}}_\tau m_{\tau,t,\ell}$.

- **Combined OPID Advantage**: The final advantage for a token $\ell$ at timestep $t$ in trajectory $\tau$ is:
 $A^{\text{OPID}}_{\tau,t,\ell} = A^{\text{ep}}_{\tau,t,\ell} + \lambda_{\text{skill}} A^{\text{skill}}_{\tau,t,\ell}$, where $\lambda_{\text{skill}}$ is a coefficient weighting the skill advantage. This formulation ensures that outcome rewards remain the primary RL signal, augmented by fine-grained skill supervision.

- **Policy Optimization**: The policy $\pi_\theta$ is optimized using a standard clipped policy objective (similar to PPO), incorporating the combined $A^{\text{OPID}}$ advantage:
 $L_{\text{policy}}(\theta) = -E_{\tau,t,\ell} [\min(\rho_{\tau,t,\ell}(\theta)A^{\text{OPID}}_{\tau,t,\ell}, \text{clip}(\rho_{\tau,t,\ell}(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)A^{\text{OPID}}_{\tau,t,\ell})] + \beta L_{\text{KL}}(\theta)$,
 where $\rho_{\tau,t,\ell}(\theta)$ is the token-level importance ratio, $\epsilon$ is the clipping hyperparameter, and $\beta L_{\text{KL}}(\theta)$ is a KL regularization term.

Crucially, the analyzer, routed skills, and skill-conditioned scoring are *only used during training*. At inference time, the learned policy operates solely based on the ordinary interaction history $h_t$, without any additional computational overhead or external dependencies.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experimental evaluation of OPID was conducted on three representative long-horizon agentic benchmarks: ALFWorld (embodied reasoning), WebShop (web navigation), and Search-based QA (search-augmented reasoning), using various LLM backbones (Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct and Qwen3-1.7B-Instruct). The main findings are detailed as follows:

#### 4.1. Consistent Performance Improvement Over Outcome-Only RL

OPID consistently improved agent performance compared to GRPO, an outcome-only on-policy RL baseline.
- **Qwen2.5-3B**: Gains of +9.3 points on ALFWorld (84.3 vs. 75.0), +8.6 points on Search-based QA (45.0 vs. 36.4), and +10.9 points on WebShop (74.2 vs. 63.3).
- **Qwen2.5-7B**: Improvements of +8.8 points, +7.2 points, and +7.1 points on ALFWorld, Search-based QA, and WebShop, respectively.
- **Qwen3-1.7B (smaller backbone)**: Particularly pronounced gains of +12.8 points on ALFWorld and +26.5 points on WebShop, indicating a significant benefit for smaller models.
These results demonstrate that dense, distribution-matched hindsight supervision from OPID consistently strengthens agent learning beyond what sparse outcome rewards alone can achieve, especially for long-horizon embodied and web-shopping tasks.

#### 4.2. Competitiveness with Strong Hybrid and Self-Distillation Methods

Beyond improving over outcome-only RL, OPID matched or surpassed strong hybrid and self-distillation baselines.
- **ALFWorld**: OPID achieved the best average performance on Qwen2.5-7B (+1.7 points over the strongest baseline) and Qwen3-1.7B (+5.0 points over the strongest baseline).
- **Search-based QA**: OPID attained the best average on both Qwen2.5 backbones, outperforming the strongest baseline by +0.4 points (Qwen2.5-3B) and +0.2 points (Qwen2.5-7B).
- **WebShop**: OPID achieved the best success rate on Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen3-1.7B, exceeding the strongest competing method by +6.2 points on Qwen3-1.7B, while remaining competitive on Qwen2.5-7B.
This indicates that trajectory-derived, distribution-matched skills can effectively complement outcome supervision and compete with methods relying on external skill contexts or complex hybrid training signals.

#### 4.3. Internalization of Skills vs. Inference-Time Dependence

The results suggest that OPID's benefits stem from internalizing hindsight skills into the policy parameters, rather than merely relying on skill prompts during inference.
- **Skill-GRPO vs. GRPO**: Skill-GRPO, trained with skills but evaluated without them, underperformed ordinary GRPO on ALFWorld at all model scales (e.g., -14.8 points on Qwen2.5-3B), indicating a train-test context mismatch.
- **OPID vs. Skill-GRPO**: OPID, also evaluated without skill input, significantly outperformed Skill-GRPO (e.g., +24.1 points on Qwen2.5-3B for ALFWorld).
- **OPID vs. Skill-GRPO***: OPID also outperformed Skill-GRPO* (which *retains* privileged skill context during validation) on ALFWorld and Search-based QA for both Qwen2.5 backbones.
These comparisons indicate that OPID effectively transfers trajectory-derived knowledge into the model parameters, enabling the policy to leverage skills without requiring external prompts during execution.

#### 4.4. Improved Training Dynamics and Efficiency

- **Training Progression (ALFWorld)**: OPID demonstrated faster policy refinement and maintained superior performance compared to GRPO, indicating that hindsight skill supervision accelerates learning beyond outcome rewards.
- **Episode Length**: OPID reduced the average episode length (15-16 steps) compared to GRPO (17-18 steps), suggesting that agents learned more direct and efficient action sequences. This is attributed to episode-level skills guiding coherent workflows and step-level skills preventing local errors.
- **Sample Efficiency**: Under reduced training data, OPID consistently outperformed GRPO across all data scales. OPID trained with 60% of the data achieved performance close to GRPO trained with the full dataset (71.9% vs. 75.0% on ALFWorld). With 80% of the data, OPID surpassed full-data GRPO (78.9% vs. 75.0%). This highlights OPID's ability to extract more supervision from each interaction, reducing the dependency on large numbers of rollouts.

#### 4.5. Enhanced Cross-Domain Generalization

On the ALFWorld unseen split, OPID achieved an average success rate of 78.6%, outperforming GRPO by +7.7 points. The gains were notable on tasks like "Look" (+26.7 points) and "Heat" (+18.5 points). This suggests that the extracted and internalized skills represent reusable behavioral structures and decision rules that generalize to unseen environment configurations, rather than merely memorizing training trajectories.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Hierarchical Skills**: Removing either episode-level or step-level skills individually led to a performance decrease on both ALFWorld and WebShop, confirming the complementary contributions of both skill granularities. Episode-level skills provide crucial default guidance, while step-level skills are vital for critical local decisions.
- **Impact of Critical-First Routing**: A variant that combined episode-level and step-level skills without the critical-first routing strategy underperformed the full OPID, indicating that selectively routing the most appropriate skill granularity is more effective than direct superposition.

#### 4.7. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative examples, such as an ALFWorld "clean and place" task, illustrated that GRPO-trained agents exhibited "hallucinated target" errors and inefficient behaviors, failing to complete tasks within limits. In contrast, OPID-trained agents followed coherent "locate-clean-place" workflows, grounded actions in observations, and completed tasks efficiently, suggesting improved local object-grounding and episode-level task flow learning.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The OPID framework presents several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the development of intelligent agents based on Large Language Models.

**Contribution to Agentic Reinforcement Learning:**
- **Addresses Sparse Reward Problem**: OPID provides a direct and effective mechanism to alleviate the sparse and delayed reward problem inherent in many long-horizon agentic tasks. By extracting dense, token-level supervision from on-policy trajectories, it offers a fine-grained credit assignment signal that complements coarse terminal rewards.
- **On-Policy, Distribution-Matched Supervision**: A key innovation is deriving skills directly from the agent's own interaction history. This "on-policy hindsight skill extraction" ensures that the distilled knowledge is inherently matched to the agent's current state distribution, mitigating the context mismatch issues faced by methods relying on static, external skill libraries or off-policy retrieval.
- **Inference-Time Efficiency**: The design ensures that skills are internalized into the policy during training and are not required at inference time. This means OPID-trained agents do not incur additional computational overhead (e.g., analyzer calls, skill retrieval latency) during deployment, making them more practical for real-world applications.

**Impact on LLM Agent Capabilities:**
- **Improved Performance and Sample Efficiency**: The empirical results demonstrate consistent improvements in overall task performance, sample efficiency, and robustness across diverse agentic benchmarks (embodied, web, search-based). This indicates that agents can learn more effectively and achieve higher success rates with fewer interactions.
- **Enhanced Behavioral Robustness**: By internalizing hierarchical skills, agents can develop more coherent workflows, reduce repetitive or invalid actions, and make more grounded decisions, as evidenced by the qualitative analysis and reduced episode lengths. This translates to more reliable and efficient agent behavior.
- **Better Generalization**: The improved performance on unseen tasks in ALFWorld suggests that the distilled skills capture transferable behavioral structures and decision rules, rather than merely overfitting to training environments. This points to agents that are more capable of adapting to novel situations.

**Broader Research Implications:**
- **Paradigm Shift in Trajectory Utilization**: OPID proposes that agent trajectories are not merely samples for reward optimization but also rich, reusable records of decision knowledge that can be distilled back into the policy. This perspective could inspire further research into alternative ways of leveraging interaction data beyond traditional RL objectives.
- **Foundational for Future Agent Architectures**: The concept of internalizing skills rather than perpetually querying external knowledge aligns with building more autonomous and self-sufficient agents. This could form a foundational component for more complex hierarchical or meta-learning agent architectures.

**Potential Future Directions (as outlined by the authors):**
- **Broader Interactive Environments**: Evaluating OPID in more complex environments like OdysseyArena, AgentBench, or VisualWebArena to test its utility in longer horizons, richer interfaces, and more open-ended exploration.
- **Enriching Skill Structure**: Combining on-policy skill extraction with higher-level reasoning abstractions (e.g., search-discovered patterns, MCTS-derived thought structures) to enable agents to aggregate skills across trajectories, identify recurring failure modes, and form compositional behavioral rules.
- **Deployment-Oriented Optimizations**: Investigating more efficient inference and scoring mechanisms (e.g., speculative decoding, retrieval-parallel decoding) to further reduce the training cost associated with skill-conditioned distillation.
- **Perceptual and Embodied Settings**: Extending OPID to physically embodied AI benchmarks (e.g., RobotEQ) to explore if hindsight skill supervision can help agents acquire socially and spatially grounded decision rules in real-world or simulated physical environments.

Overall, OPID represents a step towards developing more capable, efficient, and robust LLM agents by addressing a fundamental challenge in agentic RL through a novel, on-policy skill distillation approach.
