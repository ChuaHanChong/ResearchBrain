# TacEvo: Self-Evolving Architecture Discovery for Robotic Tactile Perception via LLM-Driven Quality-Diversity Search

> [!summary] Summary
> TacEvo is a self-evolving architecture discovery framework that leverages Large Language Models for neural network code generation and Quality-Diversity optimization to design specialized architectures for robotic tactile perception. The system autonomously identifies diverse architectures that achieve performance competitive with or superior to expert-designed baselines in 3-axis force prediction and 7-class grating classification tasks, maintaining high code generation reliability.

## Problem

- Designing high-performance neural architectures for vision-based tactile sensing (VBTS) requires extensive manual effort, expert intuition, and iterative tuning due to unique image characteristics.
- Traditional Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods incur high computational costs and often operate within predefined, limited search spaces, restricting the discovery of novel or specialized architectures.
- Open-ended Large Language Model (LLM) code generation for architectures faces challenges such as search collapse into narrow families, production of untrainable code, and inefficient exploration without explicit diversity mechanisms.

## Method

- **TacEvo** integrates a Large Language Model (LLM) as a ==stochastic evolutionary operator== capable of performing code-level mutations and crossovers on neural network architectures for tactile perception.
- Employs ==Centroidal Voronoi Tessellation MAP-Elites (CVT MAP-Elites)== as a ==Quality-Diversity (QD)== optimization mechanism to maintain a diverse repertoire of high-performing architectures.
- Utilizes custom behavioral descriptors, ==Architectural Diversity== and ==Efficiency Ratio==, to guide the QD search, along with a ==dual-archive system== that tracks both elite architectures and successful LLM prompting strategies.

## Results

- Demonstrated consistent improvement in architectural quality during the search phase, with the best validation fitness improving by **56.1%** for force prediction and **96.1%** for grating classification.
- Discovered architectures for 3-axis force prediction that were statistically competitive with an expert-designed baseline, with two TacEvo variants showing no significant difference (**p_adj = 0.25 and 0.40**).
- Generated architectures for 7-class grating classification that statistically outperformed the expert baseline, with all four top TacEvo variants showing significant improvement (all **p_adj < 0.05**) and two achieving a perfect median accuracy of **100.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can serve as reliable, open-ended evolutionary operators for neural architecture generation, achieving high rates of valid and trainable code (94-96%) when integrated into a closed-loop system.
> - Combining LLM-driven code generation with Quality-Diversity optimization effectively prevents search collapse and fosters the discovery and preservation of structurally diverse, high-quality architectures tailored for specialized tasks.
> - A dual-archive system allows for the co-evolution of neural network architectures and the LLM prompting strategies, enabling the framework to continually learn and refine both the designs themselves and the methods for generating them.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic systems designed for physical interaction with their environment often require perception capabilities beyond those provided by conventional vision sensors. Vision-based tactile sensors (VBTSs) address this limitation by converting contact-induced surface deformations into images, enabling robots to infer properties such as contact forces, incipient slip, and fine surface textures. However, the unique characteristics of tactile images—where informative cues derive from subtle, geometry-driven deformation patterns—mean that effective neural architectures for processing these images typically require task- and sensor-specific designs. This design process has traditionally relied on expert intuition, iterative tuning, and significant manual engineering effort.

Neural Architecture Search (NAS) emerged as an approach to automate architecture design, aiming to identify optimal network configurations for specific tasks while considering computational constraints. Traditional NAS methods, often based on reinforcement learning or evolutionary algorithms, have achieved performance on mainstream vision benchmarks. However, these methods commonly incur high computational costs due to the need for extensive candidate model training and evaluation. Furthermore, many existing NAS techniques are confined to predefined, hand-specified search spaces, which can limit the exploration of novel architectural motifs and potentially bias discoveries towards conventional designs. This limitation is particularly pertinent in specialized domains like tactile sensing, where unique physical interactions may necessitate architectures with distinct inductive biases.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have introduced an alternative paradigm for neural architecture design. LLMs possess capabilities for generating code, which can be leveraged to produce architectural variations at the code level rather than selecting from a fixed set of operations. This approach substantially broadens the potential search space and allows for the implicit utilization of architectural knowledge embedded within large code and text corpora. However, open-ended LLM generation presents its own set of challenges, including the risk of the search collapsing into narrow families of architectures without explicit diversity pressure, the generation of syntactically correct but untrainable or incompatible code, and inefficient exploration due to repeated proposals of similar candidates. For practical application in robotics, LLM-driven architecture discovery must integrate downstream task feedback, demonstrate reliability in code generation, and actively maintain a diverse set of viable solutions.

This work, TacEvo, is positioned at the intersection of these fields. It aims to develop a framework that integrates LLM-driven code generation with quality-diversity (QD) optimization, specifically MAP-Elites, to address the limitations of conventional NAS and the challenges of unconstrained LLM generation within the context of vision-based tactile perception. By doing so, TacEvo seeks to establish a closed-loop, self-evolving discovery process that can autonomously propose, test, and refine neural architectures for specialized robotic sensing applications. This approach differentiates itself from broader coding agents by focusing on tactile-specific architecture discovery under fixed sensor I/O constraints and utilizing QD descriptors tailored to structural and efficiency trade-offs in tactile perception models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a self-evolving architecture discovery framework, named TacEvo, that autonomously improves neural network designs for robotic tactile perception. This framework is intended to operate as a closed loop, where architectural hypotheses are repeatedly proposed, tested against downstream task performance, and used to update a memory of effective designs and generation strategies.

The motivation for TacEvo stems from several challenges inherent in the design of neural architectures for vision-based tactile sensing (VBTS) and the limitations of existing automated design methodologies:

- **Reliance on Expert Intuition for Tactile Architectures:** VBTS images exhibit unique statistics due to their origin from subtle, geometry-driven deformation patterns. Consequently, achieving high performance typically necessitates neural architectures specifically tailored to the task and sensor. Designing these specialized architectures is a complex process, heavily dependent on human expert intuition, extensive manual iteration, and significant engineering effort. This dependence creates a bottleneck in the development and deployment of tactile perception systems.
- **Computational Cost and Limited Novelty in Traditional NAS:** While Neural Architecture Search (NAS) aims to automate architecture design, many classical approaches are computationally expensive, requiring the training and evaluation of a large number of candidate models. This cost limits their practicality in resource-constrained robotics settings. Furthermore, methods that improve efficiency, such as weight-sharing, often operate within fixed, hand-specified search spaces. This restriction can bias the search towards conventional architectural motifs and limit the discovery of genuinely novel designs that might be better suited to the unique physics of tactile contact and deformation.
- **Challenges of Open-Ended LLM-Driven Generation:** Large Language Models (LLMs) offer the potential for open-ended architecture exploration by generating code-level variations. However, this flexibility introduces practical difficulties. Without explicit diversity pressures, the search can converge prematurely to narrow families of architectures. Moreover, LLM-generated code, while syntactically correct, may be untrainable or incompatible with task-specific input/output constraints. Naive exploration can also lead to inefficient use of evaluation resources by repeatedly proposing similar candidates. To be practical for robotics, an LLM-driven system must be grounded in downstream task feedback, reliably produce executable and trainable code, and actively maintain a broad spectrum of viable solutions.
- **Need for Continuous Learning and Adaptation:** Robotic systems frequently operate in dynamic environments with varying deployment constraints and sensing conditions. A system that can continually improve and adapt its perceptual models by maintaining a repertoire of solutions, rather than optimizing for a single design, would offer greater robustness and flexibility.

In response to these motivations, TacEvo's objectives include:
- Leveraging LLMs to perform code-level mutations and crossovers, enabling more flexible and open-ended exploration of the architecture design space.
- Integrating a Quality-Diversity (QD) optimization approach (specifically CVT MAP-Elites) to prevent search collapse, preserve diverse high-quality architectures, and explore trade-offs between different architectural characteristics.
- Developing and utilizing specific behavioral descriptors—Architectural Diversity and Efficiency Ratio—to guide the QD search towards structurally heterogeneous and computationally diverse solutions relevant to tactile perception.
- Establishing a dual-archive system that not only stores elite architectures but also tracks and ranks the prompting strategies used to generate them, thereby allowing for the co-evolution of architectures and the LLM's generation policy.
- Ensuring the reliability of LLM-generated code through systematic validation and integrating low-fidelity training to provide rapid feedback during the search phase.

By addressing these objectives, TacEvo aims to provide a practical, autonomous framework for AI-assisted scientific discovery in specialized robotic sensing, reducing manual effort and potentially leading to more effective and diverse tactile perception models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TacEvo implements a self-evolving architecture discovery framework that integrates an LLM-based program generator with Quality-Diversity (QD) optimization. The system operates as a closed loop that iteratively refines neural architectures for vision-based tactile sensing.

**A. System Overview**
The core process in TacEvo involves repeated cycles of proposing architectural hypotheses, testing them through downstream task training, and updating an explicit memory of effective solutions. This memory comprises a repertoire of diverse, high-performing architectures and records of successful prompting configurations.

**B. Search Space and Baseline Constraints**
TacEvo conducts its search at the level of executable network code. The process is initialized with a manually designed baseline Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), which defines the initial genetic foundation. All candidate architectures generated by the system must adhere to strict task compatibility constraints, specifically preserving the input/output (I/O) contract of the downstream task (e.g., fixed-dimensional regression output or a set number of class logits). Generated architectures must also implement a valid forward pass with consistent tensor shapes and remain trainable using standard gradient-based optimization. Within these boundaries, the system is permitted to modify network depth, width, connectivity, pooling strategies, normalization choices, activation functions, and incorporate higher-level motifs like residual or skip connections, or attention blocks.

**C. LLM as an Evolutionary Operator**
The Large Language Model (LLM) functions as a stochastic evolutionary operator responsible for editing code. Its generation process is guided by a task-specific system prompt that enforces formatting and I/O requirements, and an instruction prompt that specifies the desired architectural transformation.
- **Operator Selection**: At the beginning of each generation, a single evolutionary operator (mutation or crossover) is sampled with a predefined probability (e.g., 0.85 for mutation, 0.15 for crossover). This chosen operator is then applied to all candidate proposals within that generation.
- **Mutation**: For mutation generations, an elite parent architecture is randomly selected from the network archive. An instruction prompt is drawn from a dynamically sampled pool, and the LLM generates a mutated child architecture based on these inputs.
- **Crossover**: For crossover generations, two elite parent architectures are selected from the network archive. A fixed crossover instruction is provided to the LLM, which then produces a hybrid child architecture.
- **Validation Gate**: All LLM-generated outputs undergo a rigorous validation process. Candidates are only considered valid if they are syntactically correct, instantiable (can be created as an object), and trainable (support forward and backward computation under the task loss). Invalid candidates are discarded immediately, preventing resource expenditure on non-functional designs.

**D. Quality-Diversity Optimization**
TacEvo employs Centroidal Voronoi Tessellation MAP-Elites (CVT MAP-Elites) to structure the feedback-driven search and maintain a diverse repertoire of solutions.
- **Network Archive (A_N)**: This archive stores elite architectures. Each evaluated architecture is assigned a scalar fitness value (its final validation loss from low-fidelity training) and a behavioral descriptor vector.
- **Niche-based Selection**: CVT MAP-Elites partitions the behavioral descriptor space into distinct niches using a fixed set of centroids. The network archive stores at most one elite per niche. A new candidate architecture is inserted into its corresponding niche if that niche is empty or if the candidate's fitness is superior to that of the current occupant. This mechanism promotes diversity by ensuring that different architectural motifs, as captured by their descriptors, are preserved, rather than allowing the search to converge on a single optimal design.

**E. Network Archive Descriptors**
Two main behavioral descriptors are defined and min-max normalized to to guide the QD search:
1. **Architectural Diversity**: This descriptor quantifies the structural heterogeneity of an architecture, combining three metrics:
 - **Normalized Operation Entropy (H_norm)**: Measures the diversity of operation types present in the network, normalized by the total number of operation types.
 - **Connection Density (Density)**: Represents how richly layers are interconnected, including skip connections, as a ratio of actual to possible connections.
 - **Layer Heterogeneity (CV)**: The mean coefficient of variation across various layer properties, such as channel counts, kernel sizes, or hidden widths, reflecting the variance in structural parameters.
 - The Architectural Diversity score is the average of these three normalized components.
2. **Efficiency Ratio**: This descriptor captures a compute-structure trade-off, defined as the ratio of Floating Point Operations (FLOPs) to the product of parameter count and width-to-depth ratio (FLOPs / (Params × (W/D))). A higher Efficiency Ratio indicates architectures that are more computationally dense relative to their structural complexity and shape. This descriptor encourages the exploration of architectures across different efficiency regimes.

**F. Prompt Tracking, Exploration Control, and Fast Feedback**
- **Prompt Archive (A_P) and Curiosity Signals**: TacEvo maintains a parallel CVT MAP-Elites archive, A_P, for prompt configurations `s = (i, T)` (prompt index `i` and generation temperature `T`). Prompts used for mutation are sampled from a per-generation random pool. When a mutation-generated candidate improves the network archive, its prompt configuration `s` receives a binary success signal `r(s) = 1`, which drives niche competition in the prompt archive. An auxiliary `curiosity score c(s)` provides a more graded measure of prompt usefulness, rewarding successful prompts proportionally to the achieved loss and penalizing unproductive ones.
- **Exploration Control via Temperature Scheduling**: A global temperature `T_g` is maintained and updated each generation based on the progress of the network archive's best fitness. If the best fitness improves, the temperature is reduced (promoting exploitation); if it stagnates, the temperature is increased (promoting exploration). A small intra-generation perturbation `ϵ` is added to `T_g` to further diversify proposals.
- **Low-Fidelity Evaluation for Fast Feedback**: To ensure scalability, candidate architectures are evaluated using a low-fidelity training budget. Each valid candidate is trained for a short duration (e.g., 10 epochs), and its final validation loss serves as its fitness. This rapid feedback mechanism is crucial for efficiently guiding the evolutionary search.

**G. Experimental Setting**
- **Sensor and Tasks**: The ViTacTip vision-based tactile sensor is used. Tasks include 3-axis force prediction (regression) and 7-class grating classification (fine-grained classification).
- **Datasets**: Force prediction uses 3,000 labeled images; grating classification uses 3,507 images. Images are 256x256 and split 80%/10%/10% for train/validation/test.
- **Baseline**: A CNN with three convolutional blocks and a fully connected head.
- **Search Configuration**: 20 generations, 50 LLM candidates per generation (using Claude 3 Haiku). Mutation probability is 0.85, crossover 0.15.
- **Evaluation Protocol**: Search-phase dynamics are tracked using low-fidelity training results. Post-search, the top discovered models undergo high-fidelity evaluation (full training) across 20 random seeds for comparison against the expert baseline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TacEvo encompassed both search-phase dynamics under low-fidelity training and post-search high-fidelity evaluation of discovered architectures against an expert baseline.

**A. Search-phase Dynamics: Evidence of Self-evolving Discovery**
1. **Quality Improvement**: TacEvo demonstrated a consistent improvement in architectural quality over the 20 generations of the search. The best validation fitness (lowest loss) showed a clear downward trend for both tasks. Specifically, the best fitness improved by 56.1% for Force Prediction (measured in MSE) and 96.1% for Grating Classification (measured in cross-entropy). The most substantial improvements typically occurred within the first 7-10 generations, after which the search began to stabilize, suggesting efficient navigation of the architecture space.
2. **Generation Reliability**: The LLM-driven code generation process exhibited high reliability. A high proportion of generated candidates were consistently trainable across generations, with average success rates of 96.0% for Force Prediction and 94.5% for Grating Classification. This indicates that the closed-loop system can operate autonomously with minimal intervention for code failures.
3. **Diversity and Exploration**: TacEvo maintained continuous exploration of the architectural design space without converging to a single motif. The final CVT archive heatmaps visually confirmed a broad distribution of elite architectures across the defined descriptor space (Architectural Diversity and Efficiency Ratio). Quantitatively, the Network Archive coverage reached 88.0% for Force Prediction and 92.0% for Grating Classification by the end of the search, indicating that the Quality-Diversity mechanism successfully illuminated and preserved diverse architectural niches. The prompt archive also showed diverse entries, reflecting a range of useful generation strategies.

**B. Post-search High-fidelity Evaluation: Force Prediction**
- A high-fidelity evaluation was conducted on 20 independent training seeds for the expert baseline and four top TacEvo-discovered variants.
- The baseline achieved a median Mean Squared Error (MSE) of 0.006, which was the lowest median observed.
- Statistical analysis using Mann-Whitney U tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction revealed that two TacEvo variants (Variant 3 and Variant 4) were statistically indistinguishable from the expert baseline (p_adj = 0.25 and 0.40, respectively).
- Two other variants (Variant 1 and Variant 2) showed statistically significant differences from the baseline (p_adj < 0.001).
- These results indicate that TacEvo successfully discovered multiple architecturally distinct solutions that were competitive with the manually designed expert model for 3-axis force prediction.

**C. Post-search High-fidelity Evaluation: Grating Classification**
- Similar high-fidelity evaluation was performed for grating classification, measuring test-set accuracy over 20 seeds.
- All four TacEvo variants achieved higher median accuracy compared to the expert baseline. The baseline's median accuracy was 99.7%, while TacEvo Variants 1 and 3 achieved a perfect median accuracy of 100.0%.
- Statistical testing confirmed that the improvements of all four TacEvo variants over the baseline were statistically significant (all p_adj < 0.05).
- Confusion matrices for a representative seed showed that while all models achieved strong diagonal performance, some TacEvo variants (e.g., Variants 3 and 4) achieved perfect classification without misclassifications, surpassing the baseline and other variants which exhibited a single misclassification.
- These findings demonstrate that TacEvo can autonomously discover architectures that outperform a human-designed baseline on a fine-grained tactile discrimination task.

**D. Qualitative Summary of Discovered Architectural Motifs**
Analysis of the top-performing TacEvo architectures revealed recurrent motif-level changes:
- **Force Regression**: Competitive variants often incorporated lightweight spatial attention mechanisms. A common modification was replacing max pooling layers with strided convolutions. Modest adjustments in convolutional depth and shape, along with occasional 1x1 convolution refinements, were also observed.
- **Grating Classification**: The most successful architectures for this task frequently featured wider convolutional layers and deeper fully connected classifier heads. A notable change was the replacement of the ReLU activation function with GELU. The introduction of residual blocks and the removal of dropout layers, coupled with the addition of Batch Normalization or Layer Normalization in the head, suggested that fine-grained texture discrimination benefited from higher-capacity and better-conditioned feature processing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of TacEvo carries several significant implications and potential impacts for robotic tactile perception, neural architecture search, and AI-assisted scientific discovery:

1. **Automation of Specialized AI Model Design**: TacEvo provides a functional framework for the autonomous discovery of neural architectures tailored for specialized robotic sensing. This capability reduces the heavy reliance on expert intuition, manual iteration, and extensive engineering effort traditionally required for designing high-performance models in domains like vision-based tactile perception. This automation can accelerate the development cycle of robotic perception systems.

2. **Enhanced Architectural Novelty and Diversity**: By employing an LLM as a code-level evolutionary operator, TacEvo moves beyond the confines of predefined, hand-designed search spaces characteristic of many traditional Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods. This open-ended generation, combined with Quality-Diversity (QD) optimization, enables the discovery of novel architectural motifs and a diverse repertoire of high-quality solutions. These diverse solutions may possess inductive biases that are more intrinsically aligned with the underlying physics of contact and deformation, potentially leading to performance improvements that might be overlooked by human designers or constrained search algorithms.

3. **Reliable LLM-driven Code Generation**: A key challenge in leveraging LLMs for code generation is the potential for producing invalid or untrainable programs. TacEvo addresses this through a robust validation gate, achieving high generation reliability (94-96% trainable candidates). This reliability is crucial for enabling autonomous, large-scale exploration without frequent manual intervention, making LLMs more practical as operators in discovery loops.

4. **Co-evolution of Architectures and Generation Strategies**: The dual-archive formulation, which maintains both a network archive of elite architectures and a prompt archive tracking prompting strategies, allows for the co-evolution of architectural designs and the policies that generate them. This feedback loop enables the system to learn not only what constitutes an effective architecture but also how to effectively prompt the LLM to generate such architectures, contributing to a more efficient and intelligent search process over time.

5. **Improved Performance in Robotic Tactile Tasks**: The empirical results demonstrate that TacEvo-discovered architectures are competitive with an expert-designed baseline on 3-axis force prediction and, importantly, statistically outperform the baseline on a fine-grained grating classification task. This practical superiority in challenging tactile discrimination tasks highlights the framework's ability to yield perceptually more capable models for robots interacting with the physical world.

6. **Adaptability and Robustness for Robotics**: By preserving a diverse repertoire of high-quality solutions, the QD approach inherent in TacEvo can offer significant benefits for robotic applications. Robots often face varying deployment constraints, environmental conditions, and task requirements. A diverse set of architectures allows for the selection and refinement of models that best suit these varying circumstances, contributing to more adaptive and robust robotic systems without restarting the optimization process from scratch.

7. **Broader Implications for AI-assisted Scientific Discovery**: The methodology presented in TacEvo—combining LLMs for open-ended hypothesis generation with feedback-driven QD search and domain-specific behavioral descriptors—can serve as a template for AI-assisted scientific discovery in other specialized engineering and scientific domains beyond tactile sensing. It demonstrates a practical mechanism for automating the hypothesis–evaluation–memory loop, which is fundamental to scientific progress.
