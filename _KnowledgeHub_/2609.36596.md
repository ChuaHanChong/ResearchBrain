# HACo: Learning Haptic Active Compliance for Force-Aware Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HACo is a haptic active-compliance policy for bimanual dexterous manipulation. It learns controller-executable compliant actions recorded during compliance-regulated teleoperation and adds auxiliary supervision from the gap between those commands and the observed hand state. A GR00T-based flow-matching action expert reads kinematics-aligned fingertip tactile and joint-torque features through gated haptic cross-attention. On five real force-sensitive tasks it averages 83% success, versus 35% for the best baseline.

## Problem

- Tactile-augmented policies rarely combine fingertip touch with joint torque, missing that touch resolves local shear and slip while torque captures loads transmitted through the hand beyond tactile coverage.
- Common imitation targets are flawed: observed configurations omit motion blocked by contact, while nominal teleoperation commands encode excessive loads because the operator cannot feel the robot's contact forces.
- Classical impedance, admittance and hybrid position-force control rely on contact assumptions, hand-set gains and analytic force-motion mappings that scale poorly to high-DoF hands with changing multi-point contacts.

## Method

- **Compliance-regulated teleoperation**: Cartesian arm ==admittance== driven by excess wrench, plus a ==regularized least-squares== fingertip-unloading solve through contact Jacobians, turns nominal retargeted commands into recorded controller-executable compliant action targets.
- **Compliant-Intent Supervision**: a GR00T N1.7-initialized ==flow-matching== action expert jointly predicts compliant actions and the auxiliary discrepancy between compliant hand command and observed hand state, weighted 0.5 and never executed.
- A **kinematics-aligned haptic encoder** fuses each fingertip's wrench history and deformation map with its own finger-chain joint torques before cross-digit ==Transformer== fusion, and a zero-initialized gated **Compliance Grounding Module** ==cross-attends== action features to these tokens.

## Results

- Over 20 real rollouts per task on five bimanual force-sensitive tasks, HACo averages **83%** success versus T-Rex **35%**, GR00T plus tactile **22%**, GR00T **15%** and ViTacFormer **4%**.
- Removing all haptics drops success to **27%**; tactile-only reaches **68%** and torque-only **45%**. Adding torque lifts cap unscrewing from **70%** to **95%**, and coupled encoding adds **13 points**.
- Without compliant-intent supervision success falls to **73%**, and to **59%** with nominal targets; HACo cuts mean fingertip force by **19%** versus that variant. Visuo-haptic fusion scores **41%**, action-suffix fusion **47%**, ungated CGM **66%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The choice of action target matters as much as the observations: imitating force-regulated commands, rather than raw teleop commands or observed states, encodes compliance without explicit force prediction or tracking.
> - Distributed load sensing complements fingertip touch: joint torque reveals contacts beyond tactile coverage, and binding each fingertip to its own kinematic chain before cross-finger fusion improves coordination.
> - Add a new sensing modality to a pretrained VLA through zero-initialized gated cross-attention from the action stream; naive token concatenation disrupts pretrained representations and performs much worse.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Imagine a robot hand that already grips one playing card and must slide a second card into that same grip. It has to align and slide the card without bending or dropping either one. Two obvious sources of training data both fall short. A command based only on the operator's intended movement may press too hard. A record of the hand's observed pose omits the movement that contact prevented.

HACo adjusts the command using contact loads before execution, and a policy then learns that adjusted motion. Across five real-world bimanual tasks, HACo's mean success rate is 83%, versus 35% for the strongest evaluated baseline, T-Rex, a tactile-reactive policy. Each task is scored by completed physical rollouts out of 20, and the mean is the macro-average of the five task success rates. It is neither a force score nor a measure of speed.

Author demo of the “Insert poker · multi-contact friction” task. The right hand transfers one card to the left hand. It then picks up, aligns, and slides the second card into the same grip. The intended finish is both cards held in the left hand, without bending or dropping either.

### 2. Regulating the demonstration, then learning it

Here, *compliance* means adjusting motion references in response to interaction loads. During data collection, the robot's low-level position/impedance controllers remain in place. An outer regulator modifies the operator's retargeted arm and hand references:

- **Arm:** excessive estimated wrench drives an admittance response.
- **Hand:** fingertip forces are limited, and a smooth joint adjustment is computed.

The resulting references are recorded as controller-executable compliant actions.

The choice of action target matters. Observed hand configurations capture realized motion, but they miss motion blocked by sustained contact. Nominal commands retain intent, but they may induce excessive load. HACo therefore predicts the regulated reference.

HACo also trains on an auxiliary target called **compliant intent**. This is the time-aligned difference between the compliant hand command and the observed hand state:

$$\Delta q^{\text{ci}}_{t} = q^{\text{cmp}}_{t} - q^{\text{obs}}_{t}$$

Compliant intent is supervised during training, but it is not added to the action sent to the controllers. HACo also does not issue force targets.

HACo uses two kinds of physical feedback:

- **Fingertip tactile sensing** captures local contact response.
- **Joint torque** captures loads transmitted through the articulated hand, though this signal is spatially ambiguous.

A Compliance Grounding Module uses gated haptic cross-attention so action features can query this haptic representation.

Tactile inputs and joint-torque history are encoded as haptic features, alongside image, language, and state streams. The haptic expert aligns tactile and torque features from the same finger before cross-finger fusion. Action features then query this representation through gated compliance cross-attention. The outputs are compliant action and compliant intent.

### 3. What the comparisons test

In the aggregate comparison, HACo's 83% mean success exceeds T-Rex's 35%, and the advantage holds on all five tasks. This establishes an advantage in the authors' reported comparison. It does not isolate one architectural feature as the cause of the full gap.

The mechanism test is a cumulative ablation that removes compliance-learning components in sequence:

1. **Full HACo** (compliant action with compliant-intent supervision): 83%.
1. **Compliant action, supervision removed:** 73%.
1. **Nominal teleoperator actions replace the compliant target, with no supervision:** 59%.

The drop from step 2 to step 3 supports the compliant target as the larger contribution in this comparison. The drop from step 1 to step 2 shows that the auxiliary supervision adds a further benefit. The authors also report that mean fingertip force over contact-active samples is 19% lower for HACo than for the nominal-action variant, which they describe as consistent with more compliant interaction.

Table II. Entries are successful rollouts out of 20, and parenthesized numbers give the mean decrease from full HACo. The “Compliance learning (cumulative removal)” rows show 83% for full HACo, 73% without compliant-intent supervision, and 59% for nominal action without supervision.

### 4. Contribution and scope

In the card task, neither intended motion nor observed pose alone is a sufficient action target. The regulated reference retains task motion while incorporating unloading. The ablation supports that target as more important than the auxiliary discrepancy, and it still shows a benefit from the auxiliary discrepancy.

The evaluation covers five real-world bimanual tasks, with 20 rollouts per task-method pair. The hardware's fingertip tactile coverage leaves palm and phalange contacts indirectly inferred through torque.
