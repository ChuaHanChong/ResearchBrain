# EvoRL: A GPU-accelerated Framework for Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoRL introduces a JAX-based, GPU-accelerated framework for Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning, achieving up to 60x speed-ups for Evolutionary Algorithms and 30-40x for Population-Based AutoRL over existing solutions on robotic locomotion tasks. The framework enables more scalable and efficient research by executing the entire training pipeline, including environments, on GPUs.

## Problem

- Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning (EvoRL) methods are computationally intensive due to their population-based nature, limiting scalability and algorithmic exploration.
- Existing EvoRL implementations suffer from CPU-GPU communication bottlenecks, unscalable CPU-based environments, and inefficient sequential processing of Evolutionary Computation (EC) operations.
- These computational inefficiencies hinder comprehensive algorithmic design choices, restrict population sizes, and increase training times in large-scale settings.

## Method

- **EvoRL** is an end-to-end GPU-accelerated framework built with Python and ==JAX==, designed to run the entire EvoRL training pipeline on accelerators, including environments and EC processes.
- It employs ==hierarchical parallelism== through `jax.vmap()` for parallel environments, agents, and training steps, along with ==Just-In-Time (JIT) compilation== to maximize GPU utilization.
- The framework provides a modular architecture and a unified platform supporting a diverse suite of RL, EC, and hybrid EvoRL algorithms (e.g., **A2C**, **PPO**, **CMA-ES**, **OpenES**, **ERL**, **CEM-RL**, **PBT**).

## Results

- EvoRL achieved speed-ups exceeding **60x** for Evolutionary Algorithms (OpenES) and **30-40x** for Population-Based AutoRL (PBT) compared to existing CPU-based or hybrid frameworks (e.g., RLlib, Ray).
- The framework demonstrated robust scalability across increasing population sizes, network depths, and fitness evaluation episodes, effectively managing configurations where competing implementations faced out-of-memory issues.
- Empirical evaluations on robotic locomotion tasks showed that CEM-RL consistently achieved faster convergence and higher performance than ERL and standalone TD3, while a PBT-CSO variant outperformed standard PBT and fixed-hyperparameter baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Executing the complete EvoRL training pipeline, including environment simulations and EC operations, entirely on GPUs can drastically reduce computational bottlenecks and accelerate research.
> - Hierarchical vectorization combined with JIT compilation is highly effective for achieving significant speed-ups and robust scalability for population-based algorithms on modern accelerators.
> - A modular, JAX-based framework facilitates the integration and fair benchmarking of diverse RL and EC components, promoting the development of new EvoRL paradigms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes across various domains, including board games, video games, and robotic control, primarily driven by advances in gradient-based optimization within deep learning. However, RL methods face challenges such as training instability due to non-stationary data sources, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and the need to balance exploration and exploitation.

Evolutionary Computation (EC), particularly Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs), presents an alternative approach for policy search. EAs are population-based methods that use episodic returns as feedback signals, providing diverse exploration in the parameter space and a built-in mechanism for exploitation-exploration balance. They are gradient-free and can handle delayed or sparse rewards without relying on the Markov Decision Process (MDP) assumption or discount factors, offering flexibility. Nevertheless, EAs typically exhibit high sample complexity and can have slower convergence rates compared to gradient-based RL, as they rely on heuristic-driven optimization.

Recent research has focused on integrating EC and RL, a paradigm referred to as Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning (EvoRL), to leverage their complementary strengths while mitigating individual weaknesses. Key hybrid approaches include Evolution-guided Reinforcement Learning (ERL), where EC and RL components mutually assist each other (e.g., ERL, CEM-RL), and Population-Based AutoRL, where EAs act as meta-algorithms to optimize RL components, such as hyperparameter tuning (e.g., Population-Based Training, PBT). These hybrid frameworks aim to combine the diversity of population-based search with the efficiency of gradient-based optimization.

Despite the potential of EvoRL, its development is constrained by significant computational costs. Population-based approaches inherently increase sample complexity and optimization overhead. Existing EvoRL studies, particularly within the ERL paradigm, often focus on sample-efficient algorithms, which can lead to limited population sizes or increased training time due to additional mechanisms. This limits the exploration of algorithmic design choices and scalability in large-scale settings. The computational bottlenecks stem from several factors:
1. **Heterogeneous Device Communication:** Environments are often CPU-based, while agent decision-making and learning occur on GPUs, leading to frequent and dense data transfers.
2. **CPU-bound Environments:** Traditional CPU-based simulators lack the scalability to support large populations efficiently.
3. **Inefficient EC Operations:** Many EC operators are executed on CPUs or implemented inefficiently, contributing to computational bottlenecks.
4. **Sequential Processing:** Components in the training pipeline are frequently executed sequentially (e.g., Python for-loops), underutilizing GPU parallelism.

In parallel with EvoRL's emergence, the field of high-performance computing has seen the rise of GPU-accelerated environments and frameworks. Simulators like Isaac Gym and JAX-based environments (e.g., Brax, Jax2D) enable entire simulation pipelines to run on GPUs, eliminating CPU-GPU bottlenecks and offering significant speed-ups. Concurrently, JAX-based libraries (e.g., EvoJax, evosax, EvoX) have enabled GPU acceleration for EAs, demonstrating efficiency gains for large-scale optimization.

This paper positions EvoRL as a framework designed to bridge the gap between the algorithmic promise of EvoRL and the computational efficiency offered by modern GPU-accelerated environments and JAX-based computation models. It aims to provide an end-to-end, GPU-accelerated platform that integrates all components of EvoRL training, thereby addressing the computational limitations that hinder the field's advancement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing EvoRL stems from the computational challenges inherent in current Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning (EvoRL) methodologies. While EvoRL, which integrates Evolutionary Computation (EC) with Reinforcement Learning (RL), offers advantages such as population-based exploration and gradient-free optimization, its population-based nature significantly increases computational costs. These costs restrict researchers from thoroughly exploring algorithmic design choices, limit the scalability of algorithms to large-scale settings (e.g., large population sizes), and hinder their application to complex problems.

The specific computational inefficiencies identified in existing EvoRL implementations include:
- **CPU-GPU Bottlenecks:** The common practice of running environment simulations on CPUs while agent decision-making and learning occur on GPUs necessitates frequent data transfer, consuming a substantial portion of training time.
- **Lack of Environment Scalability:** CPU-based environments are insufficient for efficiently supporting EvoRL algorithms that require large population sizes.
- **Inefficient EC Operations:** Operations within EC components are often implemented inefficiently on CPUs or fail to leverage parallel computing capabilities effectively.
- **Sequential Pipeline Execution:** Many training pipeline components are executed sequentially (e.g., Python `for` loops), leading to underutilization of modern GPU parallel computing architectures.

To address these limitations and facilitate future research in EvoRL, the authors established the following key objectives for the EvoRL framework:

1. **End-to-End GPU Acceleration:** To eliminate communication overhead and maximize computational efficiency, the framework aims to execute the entire EvoRL training pipeline on accelerators (GPUs), including environment simulations, EC processes, and RL learning processes.

2. **Superior Speed and Scalability:** The framework is designed to achieve significant speed-ups and scalability. This is accomplished by employing hierarchical parallelism through vectorization techniques and seamlessly integrating JIT compilation across the entire training pipeline. This approach is intended to enable the efficient training of large populations on a single machine.

3. **Comprehensive Algorithmic Platform:** EvoRL aims to provide a unified platform encompassing a diverse suite of algorithms. This includes robust implementations of traditional RL algorithms (e.g., A2C, PPO, DDPG, TD3, SAC), Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) for policy search (e.g., CMA-ES, OpenES, ARS), and hybrid EvoRL paradigms such as Evolutionary-guided RL (ERL, CEM-RL) and Population-Based AutoRL (PBT).

4. **Modular and User-Friendly Design:** The framework is intended to feature a modular architecture and a user-friendly interface. This design objective aims to allow researchers to easily integrate new components, customize existing algorithms, and conduct fair benchmarking and ablation studies with minimal effort, thereby lowering the barrier to developing efficient EvoRL algorithms.

By achieving these objectives, EvoRL seeks to provide a foundational and efficient infrastructure that supports advanced research and development in the field of Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning, enabling the exploration of more complex algorithmic designs and larger-scale applications that were previously computationally prohibitive.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvoRL is designed as an end-to-end GPU-accelerated framework for Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning, built on Python with JAX. Its architecture emphasizes modularity, extensibility, and high performance to address the computational challenges in EvoRL research.

#### 3.1. Programming Model

The framework adopts an object-oriented functional programming model, where classes define execution logic, and states are maintained externally. This approach aims to enhance modularity and composability:
- **`Env` Class:** Provides a unified interface for interacting with various JAX-based environments, abstracting away implementation specifics and supporting general-purpose wrappers like `VmapAutoReset` for parallel environment execution and automatic resets.
- **`Agent` Class:** Encapsulates the learning agent, defining its behavior for training and evaluation. It manages policy and optional value networks, as well as loss functions for gradient-based updates.
- **`SampleBatch` Class:** A flexible data structure for storing and managing transitions, accommodating continuous trajectories or shuffled batches.
- **`Workflow` Class:** Defines the overarching training logic for algorithms. Each algorithm derives from this class, with the `step` method encapsulating a single training iteration. This method is designed to leverage JAX's Just-In-Time (JIT) compilation and vectorization. The `learn` method orchestrates the entire training loop, managing tasks like termination checks, evaluation, logging, and checkpointing.
- **`EC` Module:** Offers a suite of components for EC, including various EAs (e.g., CMA-ES, OpenES, ARS) and related operators (mutation, selection). These can function as policy optimizers or be integrated into RL workflows.
- **`Utilities`:** Includes a GPU VRAM-based replay buffer to minimize CPU-GPU communication, neural network toolkits, versatile logging tools, and an `Evaluator` module for assessing agent performance, including population-level trajectory collection.

#### 3.2. Computation Model

EvoRL's computational architecture addresses the underutilization of modern accelerators by traditional RL training pipelines, particularly for tasks with relatively small neural networks. It leverages JAX's capabilities for high-performance computing:
- **Vectorization (`jax.vmap`):** This technique is applied for automatic batching of transformations within complex functions, exploiting GPU features such as high-bandwidth memory, parallel processing architectures (SIMD, SIMT), and specialized matrix computation units (e.g., Tensor Cores).
- **Hierarchical Parallelism:** EvoRL implements a three-level vectorization architecture to support diverse algorithmic requirements:
 1. **Parallel Environments:** Vectorizes environments to enable batched observations and rewards during agent-environment interactions (rollouts).
 2. **Parallel Agents:** Vectorizes the rollout process across multiple independent agents, allowing simultaneous interaction with environments, crucial for EAs and ERL algorithms.
 3. **Parallel Training:** Extends vectorization to parallelize the training process across multiple agents in ERL, and for Population-based AutoRL algorithms, vectorizes the entire `Workflow.step` logic for parallel execution.
- **JIT Compilation:** Applied throughout the entire training logic within the `Workflow.step` method. This optimizes redundant or small operations in the computation graph, further enhancing performance.

#### 3.3. Platform Components

The framework supports a range of algorithms across different paradigms:
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Includes implementations of on-policy methods (e.g., A2C, PPO) and off-policy algorithms (e.g., DQN, IMPALA, DDPG, TD3, SAC), covering both discrete and continuous action spaces.
- **Evolutionary Computation (EC):** Provides robust implementations of widely used EAs like CMA-ES, OpenES, VanillaES, and ARS, along with an adapter for EvoX algorithms.
- **Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning (EvoRL):** Supports two primary paradigms:
 - **Evolutionary-guided RL (ERL):** Implementations of ERL and CEM-RL, along with their variants.
 - **Population-Based AutoRL:** Implementations of PBT and its extensions.
The modular design of EvoRL facilitates the integration of new components into existing pipelines, enabling customized algorithms and fair benchmarking studies. For instance, alternative EAs can replace the default Cross-Entropy Method in CEM-RL without significant code modifications. This aims to standardize the development and evaluation of EvoRL algorithms, which has been inconsistent in prior research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated EvoRL's performance, scalability, and efficiency using continuous robotic locomotion tasks in Brax, a GPU-accelerated physics engine. Comparisons were made against existing implementations like RLlib and fastpbrl, which often rely on CPU-based or hybrid CPU-GPU architectures.

#### 4.1. Acceleration and Scalability Performance

- **EAs for Policy Search (OpenES):**
 - EvoRL's end-to-end GPU-accelerated pipeline for OpenES achieved a maximum speed-up exceeding 60x compared to RLlib, which utilizes a distributed CPU-based framework.
 - On-the-fly noise generation in EvoRL demonstrated negligible performance overhead compared to pre-built noise tables, indicating efficient GPU-based computation.
 - EvoRL demonstrated robust scalability with increasing population sizes, hidden layer depth, and number of episodes for fitness evaluation.

- **ERL Algorithms (CEM-RL):**
 - EvoRL's fully GPU-accelerated pipeline for CEM-RL achieved a 5-9x speed-up over the original Python-based implementation.
 - It demonstrated a 1.45x speed-up over the fastpbrl implementation (which uses JAX for RL updates but CPU-based environments and multiprocessing for rollouts) before fastpbrl encountered out-of-memory issues at larger population sizes.
 - EvoRL exhibited robust scalability with increased population sizes, handling configurations where fastpbrl faced memory limitations.

- **Population-based AutoRL Algorithms (PBT):**
 - EvoRL's PBT implementation (tuning PPO and SAC hyperparameters) achieved a maximum speed-up of 30-40x compared to Ray's PBT implementation (hybrid CPU-GPU) and fastpbrl's PBT implementation (hybrid CPU-GPU with CPU-based replay buffer).
 - EvoRL avoided memory constraints observed in competing frameworks, enabling support for larger populations and more computationally intensive configurations.

These results indicate that EvoRL's hierarchical vectorization on fully GPU-accelerated pipelines provides significant speed-ups and scalability across various EvoRL algorithms.

#### 4.2. Use Cases

- **EAs for Policy Search:**
 - Benchmarking of CMA-ES, OpenES, VanillaES, and ARS on robotic locomotion tasks revealed insights into network size and fitness evaluation noise.
 - Larger networks (e.g., hidden layers) sometimes led to faster convergence but not always to superior final performance, occasionally showing degradation compared to smaller networks (e.g., hidden layers).
 - Increasing the number of episodes for fitness evaluation (e.g., 16 vs. 1) generally improved the accuracy of fitness estimates in noisy environments but could reduce exploration from fitness noise, potentially leading to suboptimal solutions.
 - ARS and OpenES generally achieved better performance compared to VanillaES and CMA-ES in these tasks. CMA-ES had high memory requirements, limiting its applicability with larger networks.

- **ERL Algorithms (ERL and CEM-RL):**
 - CEM-RL consistently demonstrated faster convergence and higher performance compared to the original ERL algorithm across all tested environments.
 - A modification to use a fixed number of RL updates per iteration (instead of dynamically scaling with cumulative timesteps) generally reduced the total computational cost while achieving comparable or superior performance. This suggests that consistent early-stage updates can be beneficial for learning foundational skills.
 - When compared to a standalone TD3 baseline, CEM-RL exhibited greater training stability and achieved higher episodic returns. Evaluation based on actual training time further supported CEM-RL's superior computational efficiency over TD3.

- **Population-based AutoRL (PBT):**
 - An extended PBT variant, PBT-CSO, incorporating Competitive Swarm Optimization (CSO) operators for hyperparameter updates, was introduced and evaluated.
 - PBT-CSO consistently outperformed the original PBT in specific environments, suggesting that more refined hyperparameter update strategies can enhance training performance.
 - Both PBT and PBT-CSO significantly outperformed a baseline of 128 parallel PPO agents with fixed, commonly adopted hyperparameters, highlighting the effectiveness of population-based hyperparameter tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EvoRL framework addresses critical computational limitations currently faced by the Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning (EvoRL) field. By providing the first end-to-end GPU-accelerated framework, it tackles the inefficiencies associated with heterogeneous device communication, CPU-bound environments, and sequential processing prevalent in prior EvoRL implementations.

The framework's primary significance lies in its ability to enable more efficient and scalable EvoRL research through several key contributions:
- **Reduced Computational Barriers:** The comprehensive GPU acceleration, hierarchical vectorization, and JIT compilation techniques eliminate significant computational bottlenecks. This allows researchers to conduct experiments with larger population sizes, more complex models, and extended training durations that were previously computationally prohibitive on a single machine.
- **Unified and Standardized Platform:** EvoRL provides a cohesive environment for integrating diverse RL, EC, and hybrid EvoRL algorithms. This standardization facilitates fair benchmarking and ablation studies, which have been challenging due to inconsistent implementations and experimental setups in prior research.
- **Enhanced Algorithmic Exploration:** The modular and extensible architecture empowers researchers to rapidly design, customize, and evaluate new EvoRL paradigms. This includes exploring novel combinations of EC and RL components, experimenting with different evolutionary operators, and investigating the impact of various integration mechanisms.
- **Accessibility and User-Friendliness:** By abstracting away much of the complexity associated with managing heterogeneous computing resources and parallel processing, EvoRL lowers the technical barrier for researchers to engage with and contribute to the EvoRL field.
- **Timely Development:** The framework is developed in alignment with the growing adoption of GPU-accelerated environments (e.g., Brax) and JAX-based scientific computing ecosystems, positioning it to leverage ongoing advancements in hardware and software.

The potential impact of EvoRL includes accelerating the discovery and development of novel EvoRL algorithms. By providing a robust and performant infrastructure, it can foster innovation in areas such as automated reinforcement learning (AutoRL), exploration strategies, and policy optimization for complex, real-world tasks. As the capabilities of GPU-accelerated environments continue to expand, EvoRL is designed to be a foundational tool that drives the field forward, enabling the application of sophisticated EvoRL methods to increasingly demanding problems.
