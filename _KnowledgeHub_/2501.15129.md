# EvoRL: A GPU-accelerated Framework for Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoRL introduces a JAX-based, GPU-accelerated framework for Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning, achieving up to 60x speed-ups for Evolutionary Algorithms and 30-40x for Population-Based AutoRL over existing solutions on robotic locomotion tasks. The framework enables more scalable and efficient research by executing the entire training pipeline, including environments, on GPUs.

## Problem

- Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning (EvoRL) methods are computationally intensive due to their population-based nature, limiting scalability and algorithmic exploration.
- Existing EvoRL implementations suffer from CPU-GPU communication bottlenecks, unscalable CPU-based environments, and inefficient sequential processing of Evolutionary Computation (EC) operations.
- These computational inefficiencies hinder comprehensive algorithmic design choices, restrict population sizes, and increase training times in large-scale settings.

## Method

- **EvoRL** is an end-to-end GPU-accelerated framework built with Python and ==JAX==, designed to run the entire EvoRL training pipeline on accelerators, including environments and EC processes.
- It employs ==hierarchical parallelism== through `jax.vmap()` for parallel environments, agents, and training steps, along with ==Just-In-Time (JIT) compilation== to maximize GPU utilization.
- The framework provides a modular architecture and a unified platform supporting a diverse suite of RL, EC, and hybrid EvoRL algorithms (e.g., **A2C**, **PPO**, **CMA-ES**, **OpenES**, **ERL**, **CEM-RL**, **PBT**).

## Results

- EvoRL achieved speed-ups exceeding **60x** for Evolutionary Algorithms (OpenES) and **30-40x** for Population-Based AutoRL (PBT) compared to existing CPU-based or hybrid frameworks (e.g., RLlib, Ray).
- The framework demonstrated robust scalability across increasing population sizes, network depths, and fitness evaluation episodes, effectively managing configurations where competing implementations faced out-of-memory issues.
- Empirical evaluations on robotic locomotion tasks showed that CEM-RL consistently achieved faster convergence and higher performance than ERL and standalone TD3, while a PBT-CSO variant outperformed standard PBT and fixed-hyperparameter baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Executing the complete EvoRL training pipeline, including environment simulations and EC operations, entirely on GPUs can drastically reduce computational bottlenecks and accelerate research.
> - Hierarchical vectorization combined with JIT compilation is highly effective for achieving significant speed-ups and robust scalability for population-based algorithms on modern accelerators.
> - A modular, JAX-based framework facilitates the integration and fair benchmarking of diverse RL and EC components, promoting the development of new EvoRL paradigms.
