# HOI-M3:Capture Multiple Humans and Objects Interaction within Contextual Environment

> [!summary] Summary
> HOI-M3 is a large-scale dataset capturing multiple humans interacting with multiple movable objects in daily rooms, tracked in 3D from 42 synchronized 4K cameras plus object-mounted IMUs through a mask, collision and smoothness constrained joint optimization. It spans 199 sequences, 181M frames and 90 scanned objects, and ships two baselines: one-stage monocular multi-HOI capture and a conditional diffusion generator.

## Problem

- Existing human-object interaction MoCap datasets record one person with a single object, so learned capture methods cannot model social scenes containing several people and objects.
- Human-scene datasets freeze the environment as a static CAD model or RGB-D scan, ignoring dynamic objects that people pick up, carry and pass around.
- Severe mutual occlusion in multi-person multi-object scenes defeats sparse-camera pipelines, and prior annotation needs tedious staged optimization or manual correction, blocking scale.

## Method

- Dense hybrid capture rig: 42 synchronized 4K cinema cameras plus IMUs embedded in 90 pre-scanned object meshes, with jump-based RGB-IMU synchronization and triangulated rigid-offset calibration.
- ==Inertial-aided multi-object tracking== optimizes translation and a rotation offset over IMU rotations under ==differentiable-rendering== mask, offscreen, collision and smoothness energies; SAM masks plus EasyMoCap SMPL fitting handle humans.
- Two baselines: a one-stage monocular network regressing a center heatmap, human mesh map and object mesh map with FoV-normalized depth, and a Pointnet-conditioned ==DDPM== generating 500-dimensional multi-HOI motion.

## Results

- One-stage capture reaches **68.5** PCK_rel and **235.0** chamfer distance, versus **43.9** and **1454.3** for PHOSA, **10.4** and **465.8** for CHORE, neither giving absolute depth.
- Diffusion generation scores FID **16.502** for people and **10.609** for objects separately but **36.906** jointly, with penetration rising from **1.452%** to **9.265%**.
- Six monocular pose-and-shape estimators degrade sharply here, best MPJPE **309.81** and PA-MPJPE **127.74**, confirming multi-person multi-object occlusion is far from solved.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cheap object-mounted IMUs plus dense-view segmentation masks substitute for missing object keypoint detectors, a practical recipe for annotating rigid 6D object pose at scale.
> - Modeling entities jointly is far harder than modeling them separately: joint FID more than doubles and interpenetration jumps roughly sixfold over per-entity generation.
> - Fixed indoor rooms with constant illumination cap generalization; sheer frame count does not substitute for scene, lighting and outdoor diversity in a benchmark.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a recognized gap in the field of human-object interaction (HOI) modeling within computer vision. Current advancements in understanding and generating human behaviors, particularly human-object interactions, have largely focused on scenarios involving a single human interacting with an individual object or a static scene. This limitation is primarily attributed to a scarcity of suitable large-scale, high-quality datasets that capture the complexities of real-world social environments.

Existing motion capture (MoCap) and motion generation (MoGen) datasets have significantly progressed in modeling isolated human motions or single human-object interactions. For instance, datasets like GRAB, BEHAVE, InterCap, NeuralDome, CHAIRS, and OMOMO provide data for single human-object interactions, often with parametric body models and object poses. Similarly, works focusing on Human-Scene Interaction (HSI), such as PROX, SAMP, and HUMANISE, consider human activities within static environments, overlooking dynamic object interactions. While some datasets utilize dense camera setups or IMUs, they typically do not scale to multiple interacting agents.

The core challenge in extending HOI modeling to multiple humans and multiple objects lies in accurately capturing and tracking motions in densely occluded, contextual environments. This demands sophisticated hardware setups, including dense multi-view cameras and object-mounted Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs), coupled with robust and time-consuming data processing pipelines involving segmentation, sensor alignment, and complex joint optimization. The absence of a comprehensive dataset featuring accurate 3D tracking of multiple humans and objects in rich, interactive settings has constrained the development of data-driven methods for perception and generation in such complex social scenarios.

This research contributes to the landscape by introducing HOI-M$^3$, a novel dataset designed to overcome this data scarcity. By providing accurate 3D tracking for multiple humans and multiple objects in diverse contextual environments, HOI-M$^3$ aims to serve as a foundational resource. It seeks to enable a shift in HOI research from isolated interactions to more ecologically valid scenarios involving rich social dynamics, thereby facilitating advancements in areas like embodied AI, robotics, gaming, and virtual/augmented reality.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and release HOI-M$^3$ (Multiple huMans and Multiple objects), a novel, large-scale, and high-quality dataset specifically designed for modeling the interactions of multiple humans and multiple objects within contextual environments. This objective is driven by a series of motivations stemming from the limitations of current human-object interaction (HOI) research.

A central motivation is the observation that human interactions in daily life are inherently social and multifaceted, often involving multiple individuals engaging with numerous objects simultaneously. Despite this reality, existing HOI datasets predominantly focus on isolated interactions between a single human and a single object. This fundamental data scarcity has restricted the scope of data-driven HOI modeling and subsequently limited its applicability in domains requiring a comprehensive understanding of complex social behaviors, such as embodied AI, robotics, gaming, and virtual/augmented reality.

The authors identify several technical challenges that have hindered the creation of such a dataset:
1. **Severe Occlusion:** In real-world multi-human, multi-object scenarios, occlusions are frequent and complex, making accurate 3D motion capture difficult.
2. **Hardware Requirements:** Capturing sufficient motion observations necessitates dome-like dense camera setups and multi-modal inputs, including object-mounted Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs).
3. **Robust Capture Methods:** Even with dense, multi-modal input, achieving accurate 3D tracking requires a labor-intensive and computationally demanding pipeline, involving pre-processing steps like human-object segmentation and sensor alignment, followed by robust joint optimization processes, and sometimes manual correction for extreme occlusions.

To address these challenges, HOI-M$^3$ aims to provide:
- Accurate 3D tracking labels for both humans and objects, derived from dense RGB and IMU inputs.
- A comprehensive collection of diverse human subjects and objects participating in rich activities across various daily scenarios.
- High-quality data, captured with synchronized multi-view cameras and IMUs.
- A large-scale dataset in terms of recording time and number of frames, with rich modalities including segmentation annotations and pre-scanned object geometries.

Beyond the dataset itself, the research has a secondary objective: to introduce and provide strong baseline methods for two novel data-driven tasks that are enabled by HOI-M$^3$. These tasks are:
1. **Monocular capture of multiple HOI:** Developing a method to estimate 3D poses of multiple humans and objects from a single RGB image.
2. **Unstructured generation of multiple HOI:** Creating a model capable of synthesizing intricate social interactions involving multiple humans and objects.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors intend for HOI-M$^3$ to serve as a foundational data resource and a reliable benchmark, stimulating further research into the complex dynamics of multiple human-object interactions and behavior analysis.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The methodology centers on the meticulous creation of the HOI-M$^3$ dataset and the development of baseline methods for two novel downstream tasks.

#### 3.1. HOI-M$^3$ Dataset Collection and Processing

The HOI-M$^3$ dataset comprises 199 sequences, totaling 181 million video frames, recorded over 20 hours. It features interactions involving 31 human subjects (20 males, 11 females) and 90 diverse 3D objects across five distinct daily scenarios: Bedroom, Dining Room, Living Room, Fitness Room, and Office. Each sequence includes a minimum of 2 persons and 5 objects.

- **Data Capture System:**
 - A custom room-like dome with a square-shaped multi-layer framework was employed, featuring 42 Z CAM cinema cameras for dense-view coverage. The system is 2.9 meters high with a 7.8-meter octagonal cross-section. White backdrops were used to simulate daily scenarios.
 - Cameras capture at 4K resolution and 60 frames per second (Fps).
 - Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) were strategically embedded into each of the 90 pre-scanned objects to enhance tracking precision.
 - Publicly available tools were utilized for intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameter estimation.

- **Data Annotation and Preprocessing:**
 - **Object Geometry:** 90 common everyday objects were pre-scanned using Polycam to obtain their 3D geometries.
 - **Segmentation:** The Segment Anything Model (SAM) was used to generate initial human and object masks, which were then refined by professional human annotators for the first frame of each camera view. XMem was subsequently used for video object tracking (VOS) across the remaining frames.
 - **Synchronization and Calibration:** Temporal synchronization between RGB frames and IMU signals was achieved by having subjects perform a controlled jump, with peaks manually identified. Rigid offset calibration between IMU and RGB systems involved embedding an IMU within a pre-scanned object, marking three points in each camera view for triangulation, and then extracting per-frame rotations from IMU signals.

- **Human Motion Capture:**
 - **2D Pose Detection:** The off-the-shelf 2D pose detection model ViTPose was applied to multi-view videos to identify 2D human keypoints.
 - **Cross-view Matching:** A cross-view affinity matrix and an established algorithm were used to establish correspondences for humans across different camera views.
 - **3D Keypoint Reconstruction:** 3D keypoint trajectories were reconstructed through triangulation.
 - **SMPL Fitting:** The Easymocap toolbox was used to fit the SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) body model (6090 vertices, 24 joints) to the reconstructed 3D keypoints, providing parametric body shape and pose.

- **Inertial-aided Multi-object Tracking:**
 - A robust optimization scheme was proposed to accurately estimate 3D translation (T) and rotation offset (R_off) for each object, leveraging plausible rotations (R_IMU) from IMUs.
 - The optimization minimizes a weighted sum of four energy terms:
 - `E_mask`: Penalizes discrepancies between the rendered projection of the object mesh and the ground truth masks (from SAM) across all 42 views. Differentiable rendering is used.
 - `E_offscreen`: A regularization term preventing objects from moving out of the camera's field of view.
 - `E_collision`: Imposes a penalty for interpenetration between humans and/or objects, promoting physically plausible interactions.
 - `E_smt`: A smooth constraint that encourages the estimated object motion to align with the smoothness of the original IMU signal, reducing per-frame fitting artifacts.

#### 3.2. Downstream Tasks and Baselines

Two novel data-driven tasks with accompanying baseline methods were developed using the HOI-M$^3$ dataset.

- **1. Monocular Multiple HOI Capture:**
 - **Objective:** To estimate 3D poses of multiple humans and objects from a single RGB image.
 - **Approach:** A one-stage, learning-based method that represents each person or object instance as a singular point in image coordinates. The pipeline predicts multiple feature maps:
 - **Human-object center heatmap:** Predicts the 2D probability of human root positions and object center positions.
 - **Human mesh map:** Regresses SMPL parameters (pose, shape) and root depth for each detected human. Root depth is normalized by the field of view.
 - **Object mesh map:** Regresses 6D pose parameters (rotation, translation) and center depth for each detected object. A 6D rotation representation is used for stability.
 - **Network Architecture:** ResNet-34 backbone, pre-trained on ImageNet, with prediction heads consisting of convolutional layers.
 - **Loss Functions:** A weighted sum of L1 norms for SMPL parameters (`L_theta`, `L_beta`), object pose (`L_object`), 3D keypoints (`L_3D`), 2D keypoints (`L_2D`), mean squared error for the center heatmap (`L_hm`), and L1 for depth (`L_depth`).

- **2. Multiple Interaction Generation:**
 - **Objective:** To generate unstructured motions involving multiple people and objects.
 - **Approach:** A tailored diffusion model, adapted from Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) and Ego-Ego.
 - **Representation:** Human and object parameters (pose, shape, global translation/orientation for humans; translation/pose for objects) are concatenated into a high-dimensional vector. The model supports up to 5 people and 10 objects, resulting in a 500-dimensional representation.
 - **Conditional Inputs:** The model conditions generation on object geometry (extracted by Pointnet), the number of people, and the number of objects, which are fed as embeddings to the network.
 - **Network Architecture:** The denoising network is a transformer model comprising four self-attention blocks.
 - **Training:** The model learns a reverse diffusion process to gradually denoise sampled data to generate realistic interaction sequences, supervised by an L1 reconstruction loss of the original data.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents detailed findings regarding the HOI-M$^3$ dataset and the performance of the proposed baseline methods for the introduced downstream tasks.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics and Comparisons

- HOI-M$^3$ is established as a dataset featuring 199 sequences, 181 million frames, and over 20 hours of interactions involving 31 human subjects and 90 diverse objects. This scale significantly surpasses existing publicly available HOI/HSI datasets in terms of frames and recording time.
- The dataset uniquely provides accurate 3D tracking for interactions involving multiple humans and multiple dynamic objects in contextual environments, addressing a critical gap where prior datasets focused primarily on single human-object interactions or human-static scene interactions.
- Statistical analysis indicates diversity in human body shapes (height, weight, gender) and object scales, contributing to the dataset's comprehensiveness.
- The high-quality data is captured using a dense array of 42 synchronized 4K RGB cameras at 60 FPS and object-mounted IMUs, ensuring precise tracking labels.

#### 4.2. Monocular Multiple HOI Capturing Benchmark

- The proposed monocular multiple HOI capturing method, trained on HOI-M$^3$, was benchmarked against two state-of-the-art single HOI capturing methods (PHOSA and CHORE) on the HOI-M$^3$ dataset. For a fair comparison, the single HOI methods were evaluated on human-object pairs with the highest Intersection over Union (IOU).
- **Human Pose Estimation:** The HOI-M$^3$ method demonstrated superior performance. It achieved a PCK$_\text{rel}$ (Percentage of Correct 3D Keypoints, relative to root) of 68.5% compared to 43.9% for PHOSA and 10.4% for CHORE. In terms of absolute camera-centric pose (PCK$_\text{abs}$), the HOI-M$^3$ method achieved 5.9%, while PHOSA and CHORE could not provide valid PCK$_\text{abs}$ due to limitations of their camera models.
- **Object Pose Estimation:** The HOI-M$^3$ method also outperformed baseline methods in object tracking accuracy. It yielded a Chamfer distance of 235.0 and a mean vertex-to-vertex (v2v) distance of 297.8, significantly lower (better) than PHOSA (Chamfer: 1454.3, v2v: 691.4) and CHORE (Chamfer: 465.8, v2v: 340.2).
- **Qualitative Observations:** Despite quantitative improvements, qualitative results indicated that accurately capturing "vivid motions" of multiple human-object interactions from monocular input remains a challenging direction, suggesting ample room for future research.

#### 4.3. Multiple HOI Generation Benchmark

- The diffusion model developed for multiple HOI generation was evaluated using Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) for assessing generated motion quality and Penetration (Pene) for evaluating physical plausibility (interpenetration).
- **FID Scores:** For 20 samplings, the model achieved FID scores of 16.502 (people), 10.609 (objects), and 36.906 (joint motion). These results are presented with 95% confidence intervals.
- **Penetration Scores:** The penetration rates were 1.452% (people), 3.887% (objects), and 9.265% (joint).
- **Qualitative Observations:** The model demonstrated the ability to synthesize semantically corresponding motions given specific object geometries and a predefined number of people and objects, highlighting the utility of HOI-M$^3$ for generating diverse social interactions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Inertial-aided Multi-object Tracking

- A qualitative ablation study on the object tracking pipeline demonstrated the importance of each constraint.
- The offscreen loss (`E_offscreen`) was critical in preventing degenerate tracking solutions where objects moved outside the camera view.
- IMU initialization was found to be crucial for robustly recovering object rotation from human-object masks.
- The collision constraint (`E_collision`) was essential for ensuring physically realistic interactions between humans and objects, preventing interpenetration.

#### 4.5. Monocular 3D Human Pose and Shape Estimation Benchmark (Appendix)

- Additional benchmarks on HOI-M$^3$ for established monocular 3D human pose and shape estimation methods (e.g., HMR, SPIN, HybrIK, PARE, CLIFF) indicated that scenarios with multiple persons and significant object occlusions pose a considerable challenge, resulting in lower performance metrics (e.g., higher MPJPE, lower 3DPCK) compared to results obtained on simpler datasets.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The introduction of HOI-M$^3$ carries significant implications for the broader research landscape in computer vision and related fields, addressing a long-standing challenge of data scarcity for complex social interactions.

**Addressing Data Scarcity and Enabling New Research:**
The most direct significance is HOI-M$^3$'s role as the first large-scale, high-quality 3D dataset specifically designed for interactions involving multiple humans and multiple dynamic objects within contextual environments. This unprecedented resource directly fills a critical gap that has previously limited the advancement of data-driven methods for perception and generation of such complex scenarios. By providing accurate 3D tracking labels derived from dense multi-view RGB and object-mounted IMU inputs, the dataset opens up new research avenues that were previously difficult to explore due to insufficient data.

**Advancing HOI Perception and Generation:**
- **Enhanced Perception:** The dataset facilitates the development of more robust and generalizable methods for multi-person pose and shape estimation, particularly in challenging daily situations with severe occlusions by objects. The provided high-precision ground truth enables strong supervision for both monocular and multi-view multiple HOI capture tasks. The benchmark results for monocular multi-HOI capture demonstrate the dataset's value in pushing the state-of-the-art beyond single-person, single-object limitations.
- **Progress in Generation:** HOI-M$^3$'s extensive collection of diverse, synchronized interaction sequences is crucial for advancing generative models. It enables the creation of models capable of synthesizing intricate, physically plausible interactions involving multiple humans and objects, moving beyond existing methods that primarily generate single-person or simple two-human motions. The proposed diffusion model baseline showcases this potential.

**Establishing a Reliable Benchmark:**
The dataset serves as a comprehensive and reliable benchmark for evaluating future research in multiple HOI. The provided baseline methods for monocular capture and unstructured generation, along with their preliminary results, clearly indicate that these tasks remain challenging. This highlights HOI-M$^3$'s utility in setting new performance targets and fostering innovation in these complex domains.

**Broadening Applicability and Impact:**
The ability to accurately perceive and generate complex human-object interactions has transformative potential across various applications:
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Enables more intelligent and socially aware robots that can navigate and interact naturally with multiple humans and objects in shared environments.
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Facilitates the creation of more immersive and realistic virtual experiences by accurately modeling and synthesizing multi-agent interactions.
- **Gaming:** Enhances character animation and interaction realism in video games, leading to more dynamic and believable virtual worlds.
- **Behavior Analysis:** Provides a rich foundation for studying human social behaviors, group dynamics, and interaction patterns with the environment.

**Community Contribution:**
The commitment to release the HOI-M$^3$ dataset, corresponding code, and pre-trained models to the community underscores its potential to stimulate widespread research and collaboration, accelerating progress in the field of human-object interactions within complex, contextual environments.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as the dataset being currently restricted to indoor settings, fixed illumination conditions, and a finite number of scenes due to hardware and resource constraints, these do not diminish the fundamental contribution of establishing the first benchmark of its kind. HOI-M$^3$ represents a significant step towards a more holistic understanding and modeling of human behavior in its natural, multi-agent context.
