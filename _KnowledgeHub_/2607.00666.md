# Domain Arithmetic: One-Shot VLA Adaptation under Environmental Shifts

> [!summary] Summary
> DART introduces an architecture-agnostic method for one-shot VLA adaptation, extracting a domain-specific vector from a single demonstration via weight arithmetic to modify a pre-trained policy. This approach achieved up to a 38.4 percentage point gain over zero-shot baselines in real-world robot tasks and demonstrated robust performance across various simulated environmental and embodiment shifts.

## Problem

- VLA models experience significant performance degradation when deployed in novel environments with "environmental shifts" like camera pose changes or robot embodiment variations.
- Adapting VLA models to new environments traditionally requires extensive and costly expert demonstrations for each task, creating a deployment bottleneck.
- Current limited-data fine-tuning approaches often fail to generalize effectively to tasks not directly included in the adaptation data within the new domain.

## Method

- **DART** extracts a ==domain-specific vector== from a single target-domain demonstration by subtracting a ==task-specific update vector== (derived from a source-domain demonstration of the same task).
- This extracted ==domain vector== is then added to the ==base VLA policy weights== to adapt its multi-task capabilities to the new environment.
- The method incorporates ==subspace filtering== and ==scaling== techniques to refine the domain vector, mitigating noise and enhancing adaptation robustness.

## Results

- DART achieved a **24.6** percentage point gain over zero-shot baselines on average in simulated LIBERO viewpoint shift tasks, with an average success rate of **79.1%**.
- In real-world UR10e robot experiments, the method yielded an **81.7%** average success rate, outperforming zero-shot by **38.4** percentage points and one-shot fine-tuning by **26.7** percentage points.
- The approach demonstrated robustness and architectural generality, adapting successfully across various environmental shifts, different VLA architectures, and cross-embodiment transfers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - One-shot fine-tuning leads to update vectors that are additively decomposable into dominant task-specific and subtle domain-specific components.
> - By leveraging this additive composition, domain-specific knowledge can be isolated through arithmetic operations on VLA model weight update vectors.
> - Environmental shifts create structured changes in VLA model weights, which can be extracted as a reusable "domain vector" for efficient adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated capabilities in various multi-task scenarios within their trained environments, primarily due to their integration of pre-trained vision-language backbones and large-scale robotic datasets. However, a significant challenge arises when these models are deployed in novel environments that exhibit "environmental shifts." These shifts can encompass changes in camera pose, variations in sensor calibration, or modifications to the robot's embodiment (e.g., transitioning from a Panda to a UR5e robot arm). Such environmental discrepancies commonly lead to a substantial degradation in the VLA model's performance on learned tasks.

Existing approaches to adapt VLA models to new environments often involve collecting extensive expert demonstrations for each task in the target domain. This process is resource-intensive and creates a significant bottleneck for practical deployment. While methods exist to improve generalization through data augmentation, leverage semantic visual features, or introduce architectural modifications, these often carry limitations regarding data collection costs, generalizability across different model architectures, or applicability to restricted types of shifts. Furthermore, fine-tuning on limited data frequently fails to generalize to tasks not directly included in the adaptation data, indicating a task-specific rather than domain-general adaptation.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing Domain ARiThmetic (DART), an architecture-agnostic VLA adaptation method. It is positioned as a solution for scenarios demanding extreme data efficiency, specifically one-shot adaptation, where a model adapts using only a single demonstration of a single task in the target environment. By drawing inspiration from weight arithmetic used in vision and language models, DART aims to selectively transfer domain-specific knowledge to the base VLA model while preserving its multi-task capabilities, thereby offering an efficient alternative to data-intensive fine-tuning or architectural modifications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient method for adapting Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to new environments under environmental shifts, specifically requiring only a single demonstration for adaptation. This goal is motivated by several challenges inherent in deploying VLA models in real-world scenarios:

1. **Performance Degradation under Environmental Shifts:** VLA models, despite their multi-task capabilities in source environments, consistently experience significant performance decline when faced with environmental changes. These shifts can include variations in camera viewpoints, different sensor calibrations, or changes in robot embodiment. Such degradation necessitates post-hoc adaptation to ensure reliable task execution in the target domain.
2. **Prohibitive Data Collection Costs:** Traditional VLA adaptation methods typically rely on extensive expert demonstrations for every policy task within the new environment. Collecting such large datasets is costly and time-consuming, creating a severe bottleneck for practical, widespread deployment, particularly in dynamic or resource-constrained settings like household environments.
3. **Limitations of Existing Adaptation Approaches:** Current methods, such as full-data fine-tuning or data augmentation strategies, incur high data collection costs. Other approaches, like those utilizing semantic-rich features or architectural modifications, may limit generalizability across different VLA backbones or deployment configurations. Additionally, fine-tuning on limited data often results in policies that perform well only on the specific adapted tasks but fail to generalize to other unseen tasks within the same target domain.

To overcome these limitations, the authors aim for **one-shot VLA adaptation**. The core motivation behind DART stems from the following insights and hypotheses:

- **Transferable Domain Knowledge from Limited Data:** A single demonstration, while insufficient for comprehensive task learning, may contain transferable domain knowledge that can enable a source-trained VLA model to adapt its existing multi-task capabilities to the new environment without learning tasks from scratch.
- **Additive Composability of Weight Update Directions:** Through subspace alignment analysis, the researchers observed that parameter changes resulting from one-shot fine-tuning (update-vectors) are predominantly task-specific but also contain identifiable, albeit smaller, domain-specific directions. Building on prior work in vision and language models, they hypothesize that these task- and domain-specific directions are additively composable in the weight space. This implies that domain-specific knowledge can be isolated by removing task-specific components through arithmetic operations.
- **Structured Nature of Domain-Specific Updates:** Further analysis suggests that domain-specific updates learned from fine-tuning are not arbitrary but structured. Similar environmental changes tend to produce similar update directions, implying that the model organizes domain knowledge compositionally in its weight space.

Based on these motivations, the objective is to leverage this additive property to extract a reusable "domain vector" from a single target-domain demonstration, which can then be added to the base VLA model to adapt its multi-task capabilities to the novel environment efficiently and without retraining for every task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DART operates on the principle of weight arithmetic, drawing an analogy to vector operations in semantic spaces. The method aims to extract a domain-specific vector by performing subtraction between update-vectors, then adding this isolated domain vector to a pre-trained base VLA policy.

#### 3.1. VLA Fine-tuning and Update-Vector Definition

The approach assumes access to a base VLA policy, $\theta_0$, trained on a suite of tasks in a source environment ($E_{src}$). For adaptation to a target environment ($E_{tgt}$), only a single demonstration, $D_{m,tgt}$, for one specific task, $T_m$, is provided. The objective is to produce adapted parameters, $\theta^*$, that perform well across all tasks in $E_{tgt}$. Adaptation is performed through behavior cloning (BC) fine-tuning.

An "update-vector" for a given layer $l$, $\Delta^{(l)}_{m,tgt}$, is defined as the difference between the fine-tuned parameters $\theta^{(l)}_{m,tgt}$ (after one-shot fine-tuning on $D_{m,tgt}$) and the base policy parameters $\theta^{(l)}_0$: $\Delta^{(l)}_{m,tgt} = \theta^{(l)}_{m,tgt} - \theta^{(l)}_0$.

#### 3.2. Analysis of One-shot Fine-tuning Properties

Prior to proposing DART, the authors conducted analyses to understand why one-shot fine-tuning alone fails to generalize.
- **Subspace Alignment Between Update-Vectors:** Using a subspace alignment score, $\gamma(\Delta_i, \Delta_j)$, which quantifies the fractional overlap between the singular subspaces of two update-vectors, they found that update-vectors from the same adaptation task exhibit strong alignment across different domains. This suggests that one-shot updates are dominated by task-specific directions. However, a subtle but consistent domain-shared component was also observed through slightly higher overlap among vectors targeting the same domain across different tasks.
- **Additive Composition of One-shot Update-Vector:** To test if task and domain directions are additively composable, prototypes were created: task prototype ($\bar{\Delta}_m$, averaged across domains for task $T_m$), domain prototype ($\bar{\Delta}_{tgt}$, averaged across tasks for domain $E_{tgt}$), and global prototype ($\bar{\Delta}$, averaged across all tasks and domains). A composed estimate $\hat{\Delta}_{m,tgt} = \bar{\Delta}_m + \bar{\Delta}_{tgt} - \bar{\Delta}$ showed the highest alignment with individual $\Delta_{m,tgt}$ vectors. This supports the hypothesis that task- and domain-specific adaptations map to linearly combinable directions in weight space.

#### 3.3. Domain Vector Extraction

Building on the additive composition property, DART extracts the domain-specific direction ($\delta^{(l)}_{tgt}$) by subtracting task-specific directions from the target update-vector $\Delta^{(l)}_{m,tgt}$. To estimate these task directions without requiring multiple target-domain demonstrations, a source-domain demonstration $D_{m,src}$ for the *same task* $T_m$ is utilized. A source update-vector, $\Delta^{(l)}_{m,src} = \theta^{(l)}_{m,src} - \theta^{(l)}_0$, is computed. The domain vector is then formulated as:
$\delta^{(l)}_{tgt} = \Delta^{(l)}_{m,tgt} - \Delta^{(l)}_{m,src}$.
This operation is designed to neutralize common task-specific components present in both update-vectors, leaving primarily domain-specific information that encodes the environmental shift.

#### 3.4. Subspace Alignment for Enhanced Domain Vector

Direct subtraction can introduce noise or retain source-domain artifacts. To mitigate this, DART incorporates two enhancements:

- **Subspace Filtering:** The goal is to prevent source-specific noise from corrupting the domain vector by filtering out basis vectors of $\Delta^{(l)}_{m,src}$ that weakly align with $\Delta^{(l)}_{m,tgt}$.
 1. Both $\Delta^{(l)}_{m,tgt}$ and $\Delta^{(l)}_{m,src}$ are decomposed using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to obtain their left singular vectors ($U^{(l)}_{tgt}$ and $U^{(l)}_{src}$).
 2. An interaction matrix $C^{(l)} = (U^{(l)}_{tgt})^\top U^{(l)}_{src}$ is formed to measure the alignment between the basis vectors.
 3. The overlap energy $e^{(l)}_j = ||C^{(l)}_{:,j}||^2_2$ quantifies how much of the $j$-th source basis vector lies within the target subspace.
 4. A dynamic cutoff, based on the subspace alignment score $\gamma^{(l)}(\Delta_{m,src}, \Delta_{m,tgt})$, is used to select source basis vectors that are sufficiently aligned. The filtered source update-vector $\tilde{\Delta}^{(l)}_{m,src}$ is then constructed using these aligned bases.
- **Subspace Scaling:** After filtering, the refined domain vector $\tilde{\delta}^{(l)}_{tgt}$ is obtained by scaling the difference $(\Delta^{(l)}_{m,tgt} - \tilde{\Delta}^{(l)}_{m,src})$ by the subspace alignment score $\gamma^{(l)}$. This down-weights contributions from potentially noise-dominant or irrelevant domain vectors that result from fundamentally misaligned updates.

Finally, the adapted policy parameters $\theta^*$ are obtained by adding the scaled and filtered domain vector to the base policy: $\theta^* = \theta_0 + \alpha \cdot \tilde{\delta}_{tgt}$, where $\alpha$ is a scalar coefficient controlling the adaptation strength.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated DART across simulated and real-world environments using two VLA models: $\pi_{0.5}$ (flow-matching-based) and $\pi_0$-FAST (autoregressive). Baselines included Zero-shot (no adaptation), One-shot Fine-tuning (FT), FLA (vision encoder adaptation), and RETAIN (model merging).

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Novel Visual Domains (LIBERO Benchmark, $\pi_{0.5}$):** DART demonstrated consistent improvements over all baselines. Across Small, Medium, and Large viewpoint shifts, DART achieved an average success rate of 79.1%, a 24.6 percentage point (pp) gain over Zero-shot and a 4.8 pp gain over FLA. This indicates that the extracted domain vectors effectively transfer domain-specific knowledge while maintaining the base model's multi-task capabilities.
- **Combined Visual Perturbations (LIBERO Benchmark, $\pi_{0.5}$):** Under scenarios combining viewpoint shifts with camera noise and illumination changes (View+Noise, View+Noise+Light), DART maintained its performance advantage, achieving an average success rate of 75.0%. This suggests robustness to more complex environmental shifts.
- **Applicability to Alternative VLA Architecture (LIBERO Benchmark, $\pi_0$-FAST):** DART was also evaluated with $\pi_0$-FAST, an autoregressive VLA model. It consistently outperformed baselines with an average success rate of 79.4% across viewpoint shifts, demonstrating architectural generality.
- **Cross-embodiment Transfer (MimicGen Benchmark, $\pi_{0.5}$):** DART was applied to transfer policies from a Panda robot to a UR5e robot. For tasks like Stack and Stack Three, DART yielded average progress rates of 84.3% and success rates of 69.4%, showing its effectiveness in bridging physical domain gaps without algorithmic modifications.

#### 4.2. Real-world Results

- **UR10e Robot with Viewpoint Shifts ($\pi_{0.5}$):** In a real-world setting involving five tasks (three pick-and-place, two fine-grained manipulation) and a third-person camera viewpoint shift, DART achieved an average success rate of 81.7%. This was a substantial improvement of 38.4 pp over Zero-shot and 26.7 pp over One-shot FT, with adaptation based on a single Stack Cube demonstration. This highlights DART's effectiveness under real-world variability and data scarcity.

#### 4.3. Detailed Analysis

- **Ablation Study:** Removing subspace filtering and scaling from DART still showed a substantial improvement over One-shot FT, validating the core analogy-based weight arithmetic. Incorporating subspace filtering further improved performance by suppressing noisy source-domain artifacts. Subspace scaling yielded additional gains, suggesting modulation of misaligned or low-quality domain vectors is beneficial.
- **Merging Multiple Domain Vectors:** Domain vectors for different target domains (e.g., Small, Medium, Large viewpoint shifts in LIBERO) were successfully merged into a single transferable vector using existing model-merging methods. This merged vector successfully adapted the base model to all three domains, supporting the composable nature of domain vectors and suggesting reduced memory overhead in deployment.
- **Scaling Coefficient ($\alpha$) and Fine-tuning Steps:** DART showed stable performance across a wide range of the scaling coefficient $\alpha$, with subspace alignment components contributing to this stability. It also maintained strong performance even with small numbers of fine-tuning steps, enabling time-efficient adaptation, unlike One-shot FT which degraded over time due to catastrophic forgetting.
- **Source-Domain Forgetting (Supplementary):** DART preserved the base policy's performance in the source domain after adaptation, indicating that the adapted model can be used across both source and target environments without forgetting.
- **Layer Choice (Supplementary):** Adapting all layers (vision encoder, language model, action expert) yielded the best performance, with vision encoder and language model adaptation contributing most significantly. This aligns with observations that language model layers showed lower subspace alignment, suggesting they capture substantial domain-specific knowledge.
- **Impact of Source Adaptation Task (Supplementary):** While optimal performance was achieved when source and target adaptation tasks perfectly matched, selecting a source task via feature cosine similarity still outperformed random selection, suggesting robustness to imperfect task matching in larger datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Domain ARiThmetic (DART) represents a contribution to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model adaptation, particularly for real-world robotic deployment. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** DART's primary impact lies in enabling one-shot VLA adaptation. By requiring only a single demonstration for a single task in a new environment, it significantly reduces the data collection and annotation burden, which is a major bottleneck in deploying VLA models in diverse, real-world scenarios. This efficiency lowers the cost and time associated with adapting robots to new operational environments.
- **Broad Generalizability:** The method's effectiveness across various environmental shifts—including diverse visual changes (camera viewpoint, noise, illumination) and cross-embodiment transfers (e.g., Panda to UR5e)—demonstrates its versatility. Furthermore, its successful application to different VLA architectures (flow-matching-based $\pi_{0.5}$ and autoregressive $\pi_0$-FAST) indicates its architecture-agnostic nature, making it broadly applicable to current and future VLA models.
- **Practical Deployment Facilitation:** The robustness of DART to the scaling coefficient $\alpha$ and its consistent performance even with a limited number of fine-tuning steps make it a practical tool for rapid adaptation in real-world settings. The ability to merge multiple domain vectors into a single consolidated vector further reduces memory overhead, simplifying the management of adapted policies across numerous target domains.
- **Advancement in Understanding VLA Models:** The empirical evidence supporting the additive decomposition of task- and domain-specific directions within VLA model weights offers insights into how these complex models encode and process different types of knowledge. This understanding can inform the design of future VLA architectures and more principled adaptation strategies.
- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** The demonstrated ability of DART to adapt to a new domain while largely preserving performance in the original source domain addresses the common challenge of catastrophic forgetting in continual learning. This allows for adapted policies to be utilized across multiple environments, enhancing their utility.

While DART shows degradation under severe shifts and requires a small hyperparameter search for $\alpha$, its strengths in data efficiency, generalizability, and interpretability suggest a trajectory towards more accessible and scalable VLA deployments. Future work exploring more robust domain vector extraction under extreme shifts or hyperparameter-free scaling could further amplify its utility.
