# A Survey on Vision-Language-Action Models for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> This comprehensive survey provides the first structured overview of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for Autonomous Driving (VLA4AD), consolidating fragmented research by categorizing over 20 models and their evolutionary stages. It identifies key architectural components, relevant datasets, and pressing challenges, aiming to guide future development of interpretable and robust autonomous vehicles.

## Problem

- The rapidly expanding and fragmented research literature on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving (AD) lacked a comprehensive and structured overview.
- Prior AD paradigms (modular, end-to-end vision-to-action, and perception-centric vision-language models) faced limitations like opaque reasoning, brittle semantics, an 'action gap' between understanding and control, or restricted human interaction.
- There was a need to formalize architectural components, trace evolutionary progress, and consolidate evaluation benchmarks specific to VLA4AD systems to enable systematic comparison and accelerate research.

## Method

- The authors conducted a systematic literature review, creating a taxonomy of multimodal interfaces, core modules (==Vision Encoders==, Language Processors, Action Decoders), and driving outputs for VLA4AD systems.
- They traced the evolutionary progress of VLA4AD through four distinct stages: from language as explainer, to modular, unified end-to-end, and finally reasoning-augmented models.
- The survey consolidated over twenty representative VLA4AD models, along with relevant datasets and evaluation benchmarks, detailing their methodologies, inputs, language integration, and action outputs.

## Results

- A formalized framework for VLA4AD architectures was established, categorizing inputs, processing modules, and outputs, providing clarity for understanding diverse models.
- A comprehensive comparison of over 20 VLA4AD models (2023-2025) was provided, along with a consolidation of key datasets and evaluation benchmarks for the field.
- Six major open challenges were identified (e.g., robustness, real-time performance, data bottlenecks, multimodal alignment), alongside promising future research directions for advancing transparent and instruction-following autonomous vehicles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA4AD represents a crucial evolution in autonomous driving, addressing limitations of prior paradigms by integrating visual perception, language understanding, and vehicle control into unified policies.
> - The progression of VLA4AD shows an increasing integration of language from passive explanation to active, long-horizon reasoning within the control loop, often leveraging Chain-of-Thought approaches.
> - Effective evaluation of VLA4AD systems requires joint assessment of closed-loop driving performance, open-loop prediction accuracy, language competence (e.g., command following, consistency), and robustness to various perturbations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving (AD) has undergone significant transformations, evolving through several paradigms:

- **Classical Modular Pipelines:** These systems, characterized by distinct perception, prediction, planning, and control modules, have achieved reliability under common conditions. However, their hand-engineered nature leads to brittleness at module boundaries and difficulty in handling "long-tail" corner cases requiring high-level reasoning or nuanced human interaction. Errors can also propagate without correction.
- **End-to-End Autonomous Driving (E2E AD):** Emerging as an alternative, E2E AD systems aim to map raw sensor inputs directly to control commands. While simplifying the pipeline, these Vision-to-Action (VA) systems often suffer from brittle semantics, opaque reasoning (limiting safety auditing), and restricted human-vehicle interaction due to their lack of inherent language proficiency. They also struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.
- **Vision-Language Models for Autonomous Driving (VLM4AD):** The advent of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) introduced Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to AD. These models, by aligning pixels with text, enable semantic scene understanding, offer explanations, answer questions, and improve generalization to rare objects. However, VLM4AD approaches are predominantly perception-centric; their language outputs are often loosely coupled to low-level control, creating an "action gap" where reasoning about the scene does not directly translate into precise driving decisions. They also face challenges with spatial awareness, ambiguous numerical outputs, planning latency, and the risk of hallucination.

This survey paper positions **Vision-Language-Action Models for Autonomous Driving (VLA4AD)** as the next frontier, addressing the limitations of prior paradigms by fully integrating visual perception, natural language understanding, and vehicle control within a single, unified policy. VLA4AD systems are designed to bridge the action gap inherent in VLM4AD, enabling autonomous vehicles to interpret high-level instructions, reason about complex traffic scenarios, generate human-readable rationales, and make informed driving decisions directly.

The authors highlight the timeliness of this survey due to several converging trends: the availability of petabyte-scale multi-sensor logs, efficient adaptation techniques for foundation LLMs, and the rise of synthetic and interactive datasets for stress-testing language-conditioned behaviors. Despite the surge in VLA architectures, the literature remains fragmented, and no comprehensive survey has yet consolidated this rapidly emerging paradigm. This paper fills that critical gap, providing a structured overview, clarifying terminology, and charting a path for future research, thus acting as a foundational reference for the VLA4AD community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the rapid progress of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and their pivotal role in paving the way for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) paradigms in autonomous driving. While VLA models promise autonomous vehicles capable of interpreting high-level instructions, reasoning about complex traffic scenes, and making informed decisions, the literature in this nascent field is fragmented and rapidly expanding. This makes it challenging for researchers to grasp the current state-of-the-art, identify common trends, and pinpoint critical open problems.

To address this challenge, the authors set forth several key objectives for their survey:

1. **Formalize Architectural Building Blocks:** To establish a common language and understanding, the survey aims to formalize the shared architectural components found across diverse VLA4AD models, providing a clear taxonomy of multimodal interfaces, core modules, and driving outputs.
2. **Trace Evolutionary Progress:** The authors seek to systematically trace the development of VLA4AD, from early models where language served a purely explanatory role to modern, reasoning-centric VLA agents that tightly couple language with action. This historical perspective illuminates the progression and highlights key conceptual shifts.
3. **Compare Representative Models:** The survey intends to offer a detailed comparison of over twenty representative VLA4AD models, analyzing their input modalities, how language is incorporated, the form of action output, and their core contributions to the field.
4. **Consolidate Datasets and Benchmarks:** A crucial objective is to collect and review existing datasets and evaluation benchmarks specifically relevant to VLA4AD. This includes highlighting protocols that can jointly measure driving safety, accuracy, and the quality of explanations, moving beyond traditional perception or control metrics.
5. **Detail Open Challenges:** The paper aims to meticulously identify and elaborate on the substantial barriers that VLA4AD systems currently face before real-world deployment. These challenges include robustness, real-time efficiency, and formal verification.
6. **Outline Future Directions:** By synthesizing current achievements and identifying existing gaps, the survey seeks to chart promising future research directions, guiding the community toward advancing more interpretable, socially aligned, and reliable autonomous vehicles.

In essence, the overarching goal is to provide a concise yet complete reference that consolidates the rapidly expanding body of VLA4AD research, fosters reproducibility by highlighting standardized benchmarks, and accelerates cross-model comparisons, ultimately shaping the next generation of transparent, instruction-following, and socially compliant autonomous vehicles.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology employed by the authors is primarily one of systematic literature review, synthesis, and categorization. Their approach is structured to provide a comprehensive and coherent understanding of the VLA4AD landscape:

1. **Contextualization of Autonomous Driving Paradigms:** The authors begin by providing a historical and conceptual context, tracing the evolution of autonomous driving from classical modular pipelines to end-to-end learning, then to VLM-augmented systems, and finally introducing VLA4AD as the latest wave. This sets the stage for understanding why VLA is necessary and how it addresses the shortcomings of previous approaches.

2. **Architectural Formalization (Section 3):**
 - **Multimodal Inputs:** They dissect the various forms of input data, including diverse visual sensor data (monocular, stereo, multi-camera, surround-view, BEV maps), other sensor data (LiDAR, RADAR, IMUs, GPS, proprioceptive data), and different types of natural language inputs (direct commands, environmental queries, task-level specifications, multi-turn dialogues, Chain-of-Thought prompting, spoken language).
 - **Core Architectural Modules:** The paper defines the fundamental components of a VLA4AD system: Vision Encoders (e.g., DINOv2, ConvNeXt-V2, CLIP, 3D priors like PointVLA), Language Processors (e.g., LLaMA2, GPT-style transformers, instruction-tuned variants, retrieval-augmented prompting, LoRA), and Action Decoders (e.g., autoregressive tokenizers, diffusion heads, flow-matching/policy gradient experts, hierarchical controllers).
 - **Driving Outputs:** They categorize the output modalities, ranging from low-level control signals (steering, throttle, braking) to higher-level trajectory or waypoint planning, and even language-grounded explanations.

3. **Progressive Evolution Analysis (Section 4):** The authors categorize VLA4AD models into four distinct stages of development, illustrating the conceptual shift and increasing integration of language with action:
 - **Pre-VLA: Language Model as Explainer:** Language used passively for description, separate from control.
 - **Modular VLA Models for AD:** Language as an active planning component within a multi-stage architecture.
 - **Unified End-to-End VLA Models for AD:** Direct mapping from sensor inputs and language commands to actions in a single forward pass.
 - **Reasoning-Augmented VLA Models for AD:** Incorporating long-horizon reasoning, memory, and interactivity (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, tool use) directly into the control loop.
 - This section includes **Table 1**, which provides a comparative summary of over 20 representative VLA4AD models from 2023-2025, detailing their inputs, language integration, outputs, and core contributions.

4. **Dataset and Benchmark Consolidation (Section 5):** The survey systematically reviews key datasets and evaluation suites (e.g., BDD 100K/BDD-X, nuScenes, Bench2Drive, Reason2Drive, DriveLM-Data, Impromptu VLA, NuInteract, DriveAction). **Table 2** summarizes these, outlining their domain, scale, modalities, and tasks, emphasizing those that enable joint assessment of control performance and language fidelity.

5. **Training and Evaluation Strategies (Section 6):**
 - **Training Paradigms:** The paper discusses common training approaches, including Supervised Imitation Learning (IL), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and multi-stage training curricula (pre-training, modality alignment, targeted augmentation, model compression). It also addresses the challenge of balancing language and control in joint losses and strategies for scalability and efficiency (e.g., LoRA, Mixture-of-Experts, token reduction).
 - **Evaluation Protocols:** Four complementary metric pillars are identified for evaluating VLA4AD agents: closed-loop driving performance, open-loop prediction accuracy, language competence (command following, BLEU/CIDEr, consistency, human ratings), and robustness under stress (sensor perturbations, adversarial prompts, OOD events).

6. **Identification of Open Challenges and Future Directions (Sections 7 & 8):** Based on their extensive review, the authors synthesize the most pressing research fronts (e.g., robustness, real-time performance, data bottlenecks, multimodal alignment, multi-agent complexity, domain adaptation) and outline promising future research threads (e.g., foundation-scale driving models, neuro-symbolic safety kernels, fleet-scale continual learning, standardized traffic language, cross-modal social intelligence).

Through this structured and analytical approach, the authors provide a comprehensive overview that not only summarizes existing work but also critically assesses its strengths, weaknesses, and potential future trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

This survey meticulously consolidates the nascent field of VLA4AD, yielding several key findings and a structured understanding of its current state:

1. **Formalized VLA4AD Architecture:** The survey successfully formalizes the core architectural components of VLA4AD systems. It identifies multimodal inputs (visual, other sensors like LiDAR/RADAR, and natural language commands/queries), three core processing modules (Vision Encoder, Language Processor, Action Decoder), and diverse driving outputs (low-level actions, trajectory planning, and language-grounded explanations). This standardization provides a clear framework for understanding and comparing different VLA models.

2. **Four Waves of VLA4AD Evolution:** The paper effectively traces the historical progression of VLA4AD, categorizing it into four distinct and successive waves:
 - **Pre-VLA (Language Model as Explainer):** Early efforts where language models provided passive descriptions or rationales, but control remained separate (e.g., DriveGPT-4). This stage highlighted the potential for interpretability but also the "semantic gap" to action.
 - **Modular VLA Models for AD:** Language became an active component in planning, often generating intermediate, human-readable representations which were then converted into actions (e.g., OpenDriveVLA, CoVLA-Agent, RAG-Driver, SafeAuto). This reduced the semantic gap but retained multi-stage pipelines with associated latencies and cascading failure risks.
 - **Unified End-to-End VLA Models for AD:** Enabled by large multimodal foundation models, these systems directly map sensor inputs and language commands to trajectories or control signals in a single forward pass, leading to highly reactive systems (e.g., EMMA, SimLingo, DiffVLA). However, they sometimes struggled with long-horizon reasoning.
 - **Reasoning-Augmented VLA Models for AD:** The latest wave, placing VLMs/LLMs at the core of the control loop for long-horizon reasoning, memory, and interactivity, often using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or tool-augmented approaches (e.g., ORION, Impromptu VLA, AutoVLA). These models not only act but also explain and anticipate.

3. **Comprehensive Model Comparison:** The survey provides a detailed comparative table (Table 1) summarizing over 20 representative VLA4AD models from 2023-2025. This table highlights their sensor inputs, specific VLM/LLM components, how language is incorporated, the form of their action outputs, and their unique contributions. This is an invaluable resource for researchers seeking to understand the landscape of existing solutions.

4. **Consolidated Datasets and Benchmarks:** The paper systematically reviews and consolidates key datasets (e.g., BDD-X, nuScenes, Impromptu VLA, NuInteract) and benchmarks (e.g., Bench2Drive, Reason2Drive, DriveLM-Data, DriveAction). Table 2 provides a clear overview of these resources, their modalities, scale, and the tasks they support. A critical finding is the emphasis on datasets that enable *joint measurement* of driving safety, accuracy, and explanation quality, reflecting the multimodal and interpretive nature of VLA4AD.

5. **Elaborated Training and Evaluation Strategies:** The survey details the dominant training paradigms (Imitation Learning, Reinforcement Learning, and multi-stage curricula combining pre-training, modality alignment, augmentation, and compression). It also outlines a comprehensive set of evaluation protocols, emphasizing the dual objective of VLA4AD: reliable control and faithful communication. These include closed-loop driving metrics, open-loop prediction metrics, language competence assessments (e.g., command following, BLEU scores, reasoning consistency), and robustness evaluations against various perturbations.

6. **Identified Open Challenges:** Despite rapid advancements, the survey highlights six major open challenges that impede large-scale real-world deployment:
 - **Robustness & Reliability:** Mitigating hallucination, mis-parsing, and ensuring stability under sensor/linguistic noise.
 - **Real-time Performance:** Achieving high inference speeds (≥30 Hz) on automotive hardware with large VLM/LLM components.
 - **Data & Annotation Bottlenecks:** The scarcity and cost of tri-modal supervision (image + control + language).
 - **Multimodal Alignment:** Principled and temporally consistent fusion of heterogeneous sensor modalities (camera, LiDAR, radar, HD-maps).
 - **Multi-agent Social Complexity:** Scaling coordination, establishing trust, and ensuring security in V2V communication.
 - **Domain Adaptation & Evaluation:** Addressing sim-to-real transfer, cross-region generalization, continual learning, and the need for standardized "AI driver's licence" tests.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant importance for the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language-Action Models for Autonomous Driving (VLA4AD), and its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Consolidating a Fragmented Field:** As the first comprehensive survey specifically dedicated to VLA4AD, this work addresses a critical need in a research area characterized by rapid expansion and fragmentation. It unifies diverse methodologies, terminology, and architectural patterns under a coherent taxonomy, providing much-needed clarity for both newcomers and seasoned researchers. This consolidation will undoubtedly accelerate research by offering a structured foundation for understanding the current landscape.

2. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** By meticulously detailing open challenges (e.g., robustness, real-time performance, data bottlenecks, multimodal alignment, multi-agent complexity, domain adaptation), the survey effectively highlights critical areas requiring concentrated research effort. Furthermore, its articulation of "Future Directions" (e.g., foundation-scale driving models, neuro-symbolic safety kernels, fleet-scale continual learning, standardized traffic language, cross-modal social intelligence) serves as a roadmap, inspiring and orienting future investigations toward impactful and practical solutions.

3. **Fostering Reproducibility and Comparison:** The comprehensive review of existing datasets, benchmarks, and evaluation protocols is crucial for promoting reproducibility and enabling meaningful cross-model comparisons. By consolidating these resources and advocating for standardized "AI driver's licence" tests that jointly assess control reliability and language fidelity, the paper encourages a more rigorous and comparable scientific discourse. The accompanying GitHub repository ("JohnsonJiang/Awesome-VLA4AD") further amplifies this impact by providing an active, open-source resource for the community.

4. **Advancing Interpretable and Socially Aligned Autonomous Vehicles:** A core promise of VLA4AD is the development of autonomous vehicles that are not only safe and capable but also interpretable, instruction-following, and socially compliant. This survey underscores the progress made toward vehicles that can justify their decisions verbally, understand nuanced commands, and interact more naturally with human road users. By highlighting advancements in Chain-of-Thought reasoning, explainable outputs, and the integration of symbolic safety checks, the paper contributes directly to the realization of transparent and trustworthy autonomous systems.

5. **Bridging Academia and Industry:** The diverse authorship, including researchers from top universities and Xiaomi Corporation, reflects the interdisciplinary nature of VLA4AD and its strong industrial relevance. The insights gleaned from this survey are directly applicable to industrial R&D efforts, helping to bridge the gap between academic prototypes and deployable production systems. Addressing challenges like real-time efficiency and scalability is paramount for industry, and this survey explicitly calls out such needs.

In conclusion, this survey is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a foundational document that structures, evaluates, and propels the VLA4AD field forward. Its comprehensive nature, critical analysis, and forward-looking perspective are instrumental in shaping the development of a new generation of autonomous vehicles that are intelligent, communicative, and seamlessly integrated into our society.
