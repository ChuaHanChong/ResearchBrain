# VISReg: Variance-Invariance-Sketching Regularization for JEPA training

> [!summary] Summary
> VISReg, a heuristic-free self-supervised learning method from Altos Labs and Brown University, effectively prevents embedding collapse in JEPA training by decoupling regularization into scale, shape, and centering components, using Sliced Wasserstein Distance for precise distributional control. The approach achieves superior out-of-distribution generalization, robustness to low-quality datasets, and scalable performance compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Preventing "embedding collapse" in self-supervised learning, particularly in Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), without relying on ad-hoc heuristics.
- Existing explicit regularization methods (e.g., VICReg) only control second-order statistics, failing to enforce full embedding distributional shape for stable, information-rich representations.
- Sketching-based methods (e.g., SIGReg) suffer from diminishing or vanishing gradients when embedding collapse occurs, making them ineffective at critical training junctures.

## Method

- **VISReg** decomposes ==regularization== into independent ==scale, shape, and centering objectives==, combined with a standard ==invariance loss== for ==JEPA== training.
- ==Scale regularization== pushes each embedding dimension's standard deviation towards 1, providing a robust gradient signal against ==embedding collapse==.
- ==Shape regularization== enforces the full distributional form by aligning the ==Sliced Wasserstein Distance== of 1D random projections of normalized embeddings to an ==isotropic Gaussian prior==, rigorously controlling the embedding space's geometry.

## Results

- VISReg consistently outperforms all baseline methods, including those relying on heuristics, in average accuracy across **6** diverse out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets, learning highly generalizable features.
- The method demonstrates superior robustness on low-quality datasets like **ImageNet-LT** (long-tailed) and **Galaxy10** (low-rank), outperforming **DINO** which failed to learn meaningful embeddings on ImageNet-LT.
- Achieves a computational complexity of **O(N DK)**, linear in batch size (N), embedding dimension (D), and number of slices (K), showing improved efficiency and scalability for large-scale pre-training compared to VICReg's **O(N D^2)** and other sketching methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling scale and shape regularization allows for precise control over embedding magnitude and geometric structure independently, enhancing interpretability and flexibility.
> - Utilizing Sliced Wasserstein Distance for shape regularization provides a strictly more expressive and robust approach to distributional alignment than covariance regularization, addressing limitations of prior methods.
> - VISReg maintains strong and consistent gradient signals even during embedding collapse, a critical improvement over methods like SIGReg which experience vanishing gradients in such scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of self-supervised learning (SSL) has advanced significantly, moving from contrastive learning paradigms (e.g., SimCLR, MoCo, SwAV) towards Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) (e.g., DINO, I-JEPA, LeJEPA). JEPAs aim to learn meaningful representations by predicting latent embeddings of different views of an input, typically without relying on negative samples. A core challenge in SSL, particularly in JEPA training, is preventing "embedding collapse," where the model learns trivial, constant representations that carry no useful information.

Existing approaches to prevent collapse broadly fall into two categories:
- **Heuristic-based methods:** These introduce architectural asymmetries or complex training strategies, such as momentum encoders (BYOL, DINO, MoCo), stop-gradients (SimSiam, BYOL), centering, and sharpening (DINO). While effective, these heuristics can make the training dynamics theoretically opaque and increase the engineering burden.
- **Explicit regularization methods:** These aim to directly constrain the statistical properties of the embedding space. Examples include Barlow Twins, which minimizes redundancy in cross-correlation matrices, and VICReg, which decomposes regularization into variance, invariance, and covariance terms. More recently, sketching-based methods like SIGReg (proposed within the LeJEPA framework) have emerged, seeking to align the embedding distribution to an isotropic Gaussian using statistical tests.

This paper positions VISReg as a refinement and integration of these explicit regularization strategies. It addresses specific limitations identified in prominent methods:
- **VICReg:** Its covariance regularization only captures second-order statistics, which is insufficient to enforce the full distributional shape required for stable, information-rich embeddings. A distribution can match in mean and covariance but still be far from a target isotropic Gaussian.
- **SIGReg:** While it directly addresses distributional shape via sketching, it suffers from diminishing or vanishing gradients when the embedding space collapses, precisely when a strong corrective signal is most needed. Furthermore, it does not explicitly decouple scale from shape regularization, which can limit flexibility across different training regimes.

VISReg aims to bridge these gaps by developing a principled, heuristic-free regularization method that offers robust collapse prevention, strong distributional control, flexible objective decomposition, and efficient scalability. It seeks to combine the interpretability and flexibility of VICReg's decomposed objectives with the rigorous distributional control of sketching-based methods, while overcoming their respective shortcomings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop a novel regularization method for JEPA training that prevents embedding collapse without relying on ad-hoc heuristics.** This addresses the complexity and theoretical opacity often associated with architectural tricks or implicit regularization in many SSL methods.
2. **Enforce the full distributional shape of the embedding space.** The paper argues that regulating only second-order statistics (like covariance in VICReg) is insufficient for stable and informative self-supervised learning. The goal is to align the embedding distribution more precisely with a desired prior, specifically an isotropic Gaussian.
3. **Ensure robust gradient signals even under embedding collapse.** A critical limitation of some existing sketching-based methods (like SIGReg) is that their corrective gradients diminish as collapse occurs, making them ineffective when training becomes unstable. VISReg aims to provide a consistent and strong gradient signal to counteract collapse.
4. **Decouple scale and shape regularization.** By separating the control of the embedding's magnitude (scale) from its geometric structure (shape), VISReg aims to enhance interpretability and provide greater flexibility to tune the regularization components based on data characteristics or training scenarios.
5. **Achieve favorable scaling behavior.** The method should be computationally efficient, scaling linearly with key parameters like batch size, embedding dimension, and number of random projections, making it practical for large-scale foundation model pre-training.
6. **Improve generalization capabilities, particularly for out-of-distribution (OOD) data and low-quality datasets.** Learning generalizable features is crucial for foundation models. VISReg seeks to demonstrate superior performance in OOD scenarios and robustness when trained on datasets with challenging characteristics (e.g., long-tailed distributions, low-rank features).

The motivation stems from the perceived shortcomings of existing SSL regularization techniques. While heuristic-based methods are effective, their black-box nature hinders understanding and adaptation. Explicit regularization methods like VICReg offer interpretability but lack comprehensive distributional control. Sketching methods like SIGReg provide stronger distributional control but fail when collapse is imminent due to vanishing gradients and lack flexibility. VISReg is motivated by the need for a principled, robust, flexible, and scalable regularization approach that can yield high-quality, generalizable visual representations for diverse downstream tasks and data qualities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VISReg (Variance-Invariance-Sketching Regularization) is designed as a heuristic-free self-supervised learning method. Its core approach involves decomposing the regularization objective into distinct scale, shape, and center components, combined with a standard invariance loss.

#### 3.1. Regularization Loss ($L_{Reg}$)

The regularization loss is decoupled into three independent objectives:

1. **Scale Regularization ($L_{scale}$):** This component, inspired by VICReg, controls the magnitude of the embedding space. Given a centered embedding $\hat{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$ (where $N$ is batch size, $D$ is embedding dimension), $L_{scale}$ is defined as:
 $L_{scale} = \frac{1}{D} \sum_{j=1}^{D} (1 - \sigma_j(\hat{Z}))^2$
 where $\sigma_j(\cdot)$ is the standard deviation of the $j$-th dimension. This term ensures that the standard deviation of each embedding dimension is pushed towards 1, providing a robust, constant gradient signal during collapse.

2. **Shape Regularization ($L_{shape}$):** This is the key distinguishing feature of VISReg, replacing VICReg's covariance term. It aims to enforce the full distributional shape of the embedding space by aligning it with an isotropic Gaussian prior.
 - **Normalization and Decoupling:** To isolate geometric structure from magnitude, the centered embeddings $\hat{Z}$ are first normalized: $\tilde{Z} = \frac{\hat{Z}}{\text{sg}(\sigma) + \epsilon}$, where $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ is a stop-gradient operator. This ensures that gradients from the shape loss do not interfere with the variance regulation, providing a principled decomposition of objectives.
 - **Sliced Wasserstein Distance (SWD):** The method leverages the Cramér-Wold theorem, which states that two high-dimensional probability distributions are identical if and only if all their one-dimensional random projections are identical. VISReg uses the Sliced Wasserstein Distance (SWD) to align the distribution of these 1D projections.
 - **1D Wasserstein Calculation:** For each of $K$ random 1D projections ($P_k = \tilde{Z}w_k$, where $w_k \in \mathbb{R}^D$ are random unit vectors), the 2-Wasserstein distance ($W_2$) is computed between the empirical distribution of $P_k$ and the quantiles of a standard Gaussian distribution ($q_N$). For 1D distributions, $W_2$ has a closed-form solution: the L2 distance between their sorted samples (quantiles).
 $L_{shape} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \|\text{sort}(\tilde{Z}w_k) - q_N\|_2^2$
 This process is strictly more expressive than covariance regularization as it aligns full marginal distributions along projected directions, not just second-order statistics.

3. **Centering Loss ($L_{center}$):** An empirical observation is incorporated by including a term that regularizes the batch mean towards zero:
 $L_{center} = \|\mu\|_2^2$
 where $\mu$ is the batch mean. This helps increase training robustness.

The overall regularization loss is a weighted sum of these components:
$L_{Reg} = \lambda_{scale} L_{scale} + \lambda_{shape} L_{shape} + \lambda_{center} L_{center}$
The default weights $\lambda^*=1$ are used for high-quality datasets, but $\lambda_{shape}$ can be increased for low-quality data.

#### 3.2. Invariance Objective ($L_{pred}$)

Following the LeJEPA framework, VISReg uses an invariance objective that minimizes the L2 distance between the embedding of each augmented view ($z_i$) and the mean embedding of the global views ($\mu_g$):
$L_{pred} = \frac{1}{V} \sum_{i=1}^{V} \|\mu_g - z_i\|_2^2$
where $V$ is the total number of views.

#### 3.3. Full VISReg Objective

The complete VISReg objective combines the invariance and regularization losses:
$L_{VISReg} = (1 - \lambda) L_{pred} + \lambda L_{Reg}$

#### 3.4. Architecture and Training Setup

- **Backbones:** Vision Transformers (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) are used as backbones.
- **Projector:** A 3-layer MLP (2048 → 2048 → $d_p$) with batch normalization and GELU activations projects the concatenated CLS tokens from the last two backbone layers into the regularization dimension $d_p$.
- **Data Augmentation:** DINO-style multi-crop augmentation is applied, typically with 4 global views and 6 local views.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer, cosine annealing learning rate schedule, and bfloat16 mixed precision.
- **Pre-training Datasets:** ImageNet-1K and ImageNet-22K.

#### 3.5. Scaling Analysis

The method analyzes its computational complexity. The regularization loss is dominated by projection and sorting operations: $O(N DK + K N \log N)$. Since $\log N \ll D$ at scale, the effective complexity is $O(N DK)$, which is linear in batch size ($N$), embedding dimension ($D$), and number of slices ($K$).
The paper further demonstrates that by distributing the generation of random slices across multiple GPUs, $K$ can effectively remain constant at scale, maintaining linear complexity.

#### 3.6. Evaluation

VISReg's performance is evaluated across a range of tasks:
- **Linear Probe Classification:** On ImageNet-1K and various in-domain and out-of-distribution (OOD) downstream datasets.
- **Transfer Learning:** Fine-tuning on several datasets.
- **Dense Prediction:** Linear segmentation on ADE20K.
- **Image Generation Guidance:** Using VISReg features to guide generative models.
- **Robustness to Low-Quality Data:** Tested on ImageNet-LT (long-tailed) and Galaxy10 (low-rank).
- **Hyperparameter Ablations:** Comprehensive study of $\lambda$, learning rate, batch size, and projection dimension.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

1. **Robust Gradient Signal:** VISReg demonstrates strong and consistent gradient magnitudes even when embeddings approach collapse, unlike SIGReg, which experiences diminishing gradients in such scenarios (Figure 2). This contributes to enhanced training stability.
2. **Scalability:**
 - VISReg exhibits a computational complexity of $O(N DK)$, linear in batch size ($N$), projection dimension ($D$), and number of slices ($K$), which is more efficient than VICReg's $O(N D^2)$.
 - Empirical simulations show that SWD-based methods, including VISReg, are more efficient in both speed and memory consumption compared to SIGReg and other regularization techniques (Figure 3).
 - The paper establishes that the number of slices ($K$) can be distributed across multiple GPUs, allowing $K$ to be treated as a constant when scaling, thus preserving the $O(N DK)$ complexity (Figure 6).
3. **Robustness to Low-Quality Datasets:**
 - On ImageNet-LT (long-tailed), VISReg significantly outperforms all baselines (SWD, SIGReg, VICReg, DINO) across overall, many-shot, medium-shot, and few-shot categories, especially when the shape loss weight is adjusted (Table 1). DINO failed to learn meaningful embeddings on this dataset.
 - On Galaxy10 (low-rank), VISReg achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy than other regularization methods, while DINO again struggles (Table 2). This demonstrates the method's resilience to challenging data characteristics.
4. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Ablation studies provide guidance on optimal settings for the total regularization weight ($\lambda$), learning rate, batch size, and projection dimension (Table 3).
5. **Effect of Projection Dimension:** The optimal projection dimension is task-dependent. Larger dimensions generally improve in-domain linear probe accuracy, while smaller dimensions can lead to better out-of-distribution performance and dense prediction (Table 4). A dimension of 256 was chosen as a balanced setting.
6. **In-Domain Performance:** VISReg achieves competitive linear probe accuracy on ImageNet-1K and an average across various in-domain downstream datasets compared to heuristic-based methods. It shows stronger in-domain performance than other heuristic-free methods like MAE and LeJEPA (Table 5).
7. **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** This is a major finding. VISReg consistently outperforms all other methods, including those relying on heuristics, in average accuracy across a diverse set of 6 OOD datasets (Table 6). Notably, VISReg pre-trained on ImageNet-22K with ViT-L/14 matches the OOD performance of DINOv2, despite DINOv2 being trained on a 10x larger dataset (LVD-142M). This indicates that VISReg learns highly generalizable features.
8. **Transfer Learning Capability:** VISReg demonstrates strong transfer learning capabilities, consistently yielding higher fine-tuning accuracies on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, Flowers, ImageNet1K, and Galaxy10 compared to DINO and supervised learning baselines, even when its linear probe accuracy on in-domain data might be slightly lower than DINO (Table 7).
9. **Dense Instance Prediction:** VISReg achieves good performance on linear segmentation for ADE20K, performing on par with DINO, though a notable gap remains to top models like MoCoV3 (Table 8).
10. **Image Generation Guidance:** When used to guide generative models (e.g., SiT-B/2), VISReg features lead to improved image generation quality metrics (IS, gFID, Precision, Recall) compared to DINO guidance (Table 9).
11. **Loss-Accuracy Correlation:** The training loss curve shows a strong negative Pearson correlation (-0.996) with the online linear probe accuracy curve, suggesting that the loss serves as a reliable indicator of learning progress (Figure 7).
12. **Qualitative Feature Visualization:** PCA visualizations of patch-level features indicate that VISReg learns more granular details and concepts than DINOv1 (Figures 8 and 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of VISReg carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of self-supervised learning and its applications:

1. **Advancement in Principled SSL:** VISReg provides a theoretically grounded, heuristic-free approach to self-supervised learning that effectively addresses embedding collapse. By integrating the interpretability and flexibility of decomposed regularization (like VICReg's variance term) with the distributional rigor of optimal transport-based sketching, it represents a step towards more robust and understandable SSL methods. This can reduce the reliance on complex, often empirical, architectural modifications and training tricks.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstrated superior performance on out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets and robustness to low-quality data (long-tailed, low-rank) is a critical advantage. This indicates that VISReg learns more general and transferable features. For foundation models, OOD generalization is paramount for broad applicability across diverse, unforeseen scenarios and domains. This makes VISReg-trained models potentially more valuable in real-world applications where data quality can be inconsistent or target domains differ significantly from pre-training data.

3. **Improved Scalability:** The linear complexity $O(N DK)$ and the practical demonstration of constant $K$ at scale (by distributing slices across GPUs) signify that VISReg is well-suited for training very large foundation models on massive datasets. This efficiency can accelerate research and development in large-scale SSL.

4. **Broader Practical Utility for Foundation Models:**
 - **AI for Science/Healthcare:** Its robustness to low-quality datasets and strong OOD performance are particularly relevant for scientific domains (e.g., medical imaging, astronomy) where data is often sparse, noisy, or difficult to acquire, and models need to generalize to novel conditions.
 - **Transfer Learning:** The strong transfer learning capabilities indicate that models pre-trained with VISReg can be fine-tuned effectively for various downstream tasks, reducing the need for extensive labeled data in target applications.
 - **Generative Models:** The ability of VISReg embeddings to provide effective guidance for generative models suggests a synergistic potential for developing more powerful and controllable generative AI systems.

5. **Diagnostic Tool for Training:** The high correlation between the training loss and online linear probe accuracy offers a valuable diagnostic tool, allowing researchers to monitor the learning progress more directly and efficiently, potentially reducing the need for frequent, computationally expensive downstream evaluations during hyperparameter tuning or large-scale pre-training.

In summary, VISReg offers a technically sound, robust, and scalable solution for self-supervised learning, yielding representations with superior generalization capabilities. Its contributions could enhance the practical utility and reliability of foundation models across a wide spectrum of real-world applications.
