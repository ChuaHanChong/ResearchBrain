# ManiSkill: Generalizable Manipulation Skill Benchmark with Large-Scale Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> ManiSkill introduces a robotic manipulation benchmark that leverages a full-physics simulator and 162 diverse articulated objects from PartNet-Mobility, providing ego-centric 3D visual inputs and 36,000 successful demonstration trajectories. This framework enables research into generalizable manipulation skills across multiple learning paradigms, revealing that object-level generalization remains a significant challenge with baseline success rates around 8-11%.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation benchmarks lacked sufficient intra-class object diversity, limiting the development of truly generalizable skills for novel objects.
- Many prior benchmarks relied on fixed 2D image inputs or abstract action spaces, not fully utilizing modern 3D sensing capabilities or capturing complex low-level physical interactions.
- There was a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality demonstration datasets, which hampered research in learning-from-demonstrations (LfD) and offline reinforcement learning (RL) for robotics.

## Method

- Developed ManiSkill, a benchmark built on the SAPIEN full-physics simulator, integrating 162 manually curated and verified articulated objects from PartNet-Mobility with high topological and geometric variations.
- Designed four diverse manipulation tasks, providing ego-centric point cloud or RGB-D observations from a robot-mounted panoramic camera, emphasizing 3D visual perception.
- Generated a large dataset of approximately 36,000 successful demonstration trajectories (1.5 million frames) using an RL-based divide-and-conquer approach to support LfD and offline RL research.

## Results

- Baseline agents demonstrated low overall success rates for object-level generalization on test environments, ranging from 0.08 for `MoveBucket` to 0.11 for `OpenCabinetDoor` using the best performing BC with PointNet + Transformer agent.
- Performance on single, fixed object instances was substantially higher than performance across diverse objects within the same category, underscoring the difficulty of generalization.
- The `PointNet + Transformer` architecture generally showed improved sample efficiency and performance compared to a single `PointNet` across tasks, indicating the benefits of advanced 3D feature extraction and relational modeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving object-level generalization in physical manipulation from 3D visual inputs remains a significant and challenging open problem, with all baseline agents demonstrating low success rates on test environments.
> - More advanced 3D deep learning architectures, such as PointNet + Transformer, consistently yield better performance than simpler PointNet models, highlighting the need for sophisticated perception models.
> - Simple Behavior Cloning (BC) often performs comparably or slightly better than more complex offline RL algorithms (BCQ, TD3+BC) when trained on exclusively successful demonstrations, especially given the high dimensionality and task specifics.
