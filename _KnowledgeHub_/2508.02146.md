# ScrewSplat: An End-to-End Method for Articulated Object Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> ScrewSplat offers an end-to-end method for articulated object recognition, combining screw theory with 3D Gaussian Splatting to infer 3D geometry and kinematic structures from multi-view RGB images. The framework achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in identifying joint axes and part geometry, enabling zero-shot, text-guided manipulation of objects by robots in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to robustly recognize articulated objects (e.g., doors, drawers) in complex environments due to the need to understand both 3D geometry and kinematic structure.
- Existing supervised learning approaches for articulated object recognition do not generalize well to novel or unseen object categories.
- Current unsupervised methods often require strong assumptions (e.g., known joint counts), auxiliary inputs like depth data (which can be unreliable), or complex multi-stage processing pipelines.

## Method

- **ScrewSplat** integrates ==screw theory== with ==3D Gaussian Splatting== in a unified, differentiable, end-to-end framework to jointly optimize geometry and kinematic parameters.
- The method uses ==part-aware Gaussian primitives==, ==screw primitives== with confidence scores, and joint angle vectors, all optimized concurrently from multi-view RGB images.
- A ==parsimony loss== term regularizes the selection of screw primitives, implicitly determining the number and reliability of active joints without requiring prior knowledge.

## Results

- ScrewSplat achieved state-of-the-art accuracy in articulated object recognition, showing lower Chamfer distances for geometry and superior angular/position errors for joint axis estimation compared to baselines.
- The framework successfully reconstructs 3D geometry and accurately identifies kinematic structures for both single and multi-joint objects using only multi-view RGB images.
- The recovered kinematic models facilitated zero-shot, text-guided robotic manipulation, allowing a robot to adjust object joint angles based on natural language prompts in real-world experiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tightly integrating screw theory, a continuous parameterization of joint axes, into a differentiable neural rendering framework allows for robust kinematic learning.
> - Combining part-aware Gaussian primitives with screw primitives, incorporating confidence scores and part probabilities, effectively addresses the hybrid continuous-discrete nature of articulated object recognition.
> - An end-to-end, RGB-only approach, without relying on auxiliary depth data or strong prior assumptions, significantly improves the practicality and generalizability of articulated object recognition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "ScrewSplat" addresses the critical problem of **articulated object recognition**, a fundamental capability for robots operating in human environments. Articulated objects—items with movable parts like doors, laptops, and drawers—are ubiquitous, and successful manipulation requires robots to understand not only their 3D geometry but also their underlying kinematic structure (joint types, axes, and limits).

The broader research landscape for articulated object recognition can be broadly categorized into:
1. **Supervised Learning Approaches:** These methods rely on large-scale datasets with annotated joint axes and part information to train deep neural networks. While achieving strong performance on known categories, they inherently struggle with generalization to unseen or novel object types due to the limitations of supervised learning (Appendix A.1).
2. **Per-Object Optimization Methods (Unsupervised/Category-Agnostic):** These approaches aim to infer kinematic structures by directly fitting 3D representations to observations for each object individually, without category-specific pre-training. ScrewSplat falls squarely into this category.

Within the per-object optimization landscape, ScrewSplat is positioned as a significant advancement over existing methods. Prior works, such as PARIS, DTA, ArtGS, and ArticulatedGS, have made strides in category-agnostic recognition, often leveraging modern neural rendering techniques like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) or Gaussian Splatting for 3D reconstruction. However, these methods typically suffer from critical limitations that "ScrewSplat" seeks to overcome:
- **Strong Assumptions:** Many require prior knowledge of the number of articulated components, predefined joint types, or are restricted to single-joint objects.
- **Auxiliary Inputs:** Some rely on depth images (e.g., DTA, PARIS*), which can be noisy, especially for transparent or reflective objects, limiting robustness in real-world scenarios.
- **Complex Multi-Stage Pipelines:** Several involve intricate intermediate steps, such as point correspondence matching or part clustering, which increase complexity and introduce potential points of failure, hindering end-to-end learning.

ScrewSplat enters this landscape by proposing a **simple, end-to-end method that operates solely on RGB observations**, without any of the aforementioned strong assumptions or intermediate procedures. By integrating screw theory with Gaussian splatting in a unified, differentiable framework, it directly tackles the hybrid, combinatorial nature of articulated object recognition (continuous geometry/angles vs. discrete part labels/joint types/counts). This approach pushes the boundaries of unsupervised articulated object recognition, making it more practical and robust for real-world robotic applications. The paper also acknowledges alternative "active interaction" strategies (Appendix A.3), suggesting a potential future integration point for its observation-based method.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a robust and practical method for **articulated object recognition** that can enable robots to interact intelligently with everyday objects. This encompasses identifying both the 3D geometry and the underlying kinematic structure of objects with movable parts.

The specific key objectives and motivations driving the ScrewSplat research are:
- **Address Practicality Gaps of Existing Methods:** The primary motivation stems from the limitations of current articulated object recognition approaches. Supervised methods lack generalization to novel categories, while unsupervised (per-object) methods often rely on restrictive assumptions (known joint counts/types), demand auxiliary depth inputs (which can be unreliable), or involve complex, multi-stage pipelines prone to error propagation. ScrewSplat aims to overcome these by creating a solution that is simple, robust, and free from such constraints.
- **Infer Kinematic Structure from RGB Only:** A core objective is to infer the complex kinematic structure (joint axes, types, and angles) directly from multi-view RGB images alone. This eliminates the need for potentially noisy depth sensors or pre-existing 3D models, making the system more adaptable to diverse real-world lighting and material conditions.
- **Develop an End-to-End, Unified Optimization Framework:** The paper explicitly seeks an end-to-end method that avoids intermediate steps like point correspondence or explicit part clustering. The motivation is to formulate the problem as a joint optimization of geometry, joint axes, joint types, and joint angles, allowing for smooth, differentiable learning across these coupled continuous and discrete variables.
- **No Prior Knowledge Requirement:** A key goal is to develop a category-agnostic method that does not require prior knowledge of the number of articulated parts or their specific joint types (e.g., revolute or prismatic). This significantly enhances the method's generalizability to unknown objects.
- **Enable Zero-Shot, Text-Guided Manipulation:** Beyond just recognition, the research aims to demonstrate the practical utility of the recovered kinematic model by enabling intuitive robot manipulation. Specifically, the objective is to allow robots to physically adjust joint angles based on high-level, natural language commands (text prompts) in a zero-shot manner.

In essence, ScrewSplat is motivated by the need for a more generalizable, practical, and robust articulated object recognition system that can empower robots to seamlessly interact with the complex, movable objects found in human environments, moving beyond constrained laboratory settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ScrewSplat's methodology integrates fundamental robotics principles (screw theory) with advanced neural rendering techniques (3D Gaussian Splatting) into a unified, end-to-end, and differentiable optimization framework. The approach circumvents the limitations of prior methods by avoiding strong assumptions, auxiliary data, and complex intermediate steps.

#### 3.1. Core Components

The method introduces three key components that are jointly optimized:
- **Part-aware Gaussian Primitives ($H_i$):** Each Gaussian is augmented beyond standard Gaussian Splatting parameters (pose $T_i$, scale $s_i$, opacity $\sigma_i$, color $c_i$) with a probability simplex $m_i \in \Delta^{n_s}$. This $m_i$ distribution assigns probabilities to the Gaussian belonging to the static base part ($m_{i0}$) or one of $n_s$ movable parts associated with screw primitives ($m_{ij}$ for $j \ge 1$).
- **Screw Primitives ($A_j$):** Each of the $n_s$ screw primitives is defined by a screw axis $S_j \in \mathbb{R}^6$ (representing either a revolute or prismatic joint motion, as per screw theory) and a confidence score $\gamma_j \in$. This confidence score is crucial for dynamically determining the number and reliability of active joints.
- **Joint Angle Vectors ($\theta_k$):** For each of the $n_a$ observed object configurations, a vector $\theta_k = (\theta_{k1}, \dots, \theta_{kn_s}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n_s}$ captures the joint angles corresponding to each screw primitive.

#### 3.2. RGB Rendering Procedure with ScrewSplat

The novel rendering procedure enables the joint optimization. For each part-aware Gaussian primitive $H_i$, it replicates $(n_s+1)$ Gaussians ($G_{ij}$).
- The Gaussian $G_{i0}$ represents the static base part, retaining $H_i$'s original pose $T_i$ and scaling opacity by $m_{i0}$.
- For movable parts, $G_{ij}$ (where $j \ge 1$) is transformed using the matrix exponential of the corresponding screw axis $S_j$ and joint angle $\theta_{kj}$ ($e^{[S_j]\theta_{kj}} T_i$). Its opacity is scaled by both $m_{ij}$ and the screw confidence $\gamma_j$.
All replicated Gaussians are then rendered into an RGB image using a standard $\alpha$-blending approach, conditioned on the current object configuration $k$ and camera pose.

#### 3.3. Loss Function

The optimization minimizes a composite loss function $L$:
$L = L_{\text{render}} + \beta \sum_j \sqrt{\gamma_j}$
- $L_{\text{render}}$: This is the photo-consistency loss, a combination of L1 loss and D-SSIM loss between the rendered RGB image and the ground-truth RGB image. It drives the geometric and appearance reconstruction.
- Parsimony Loss ($\beta \sum_j \sqrt{\gamma_j}$): This regularization term encourages the selection of a minimal set of highly confident screw primitives. It penalizes having many low-confidence screws, thus implicitly determining the number of active joints and promoting the identification of the most reliable ones.

#### 3.4. Optimization Strategy

The optimization process is iterative and built upon the principles of Gaussian Splatting:
- **Initialization:** Part-aware Gaussians are initialized similarly to standard Gaussian Splatting (with uniform part probabilities). Screw axes are randomly initialized, and screw confidences are set high. Joint angles are initialized to zero.
- **Periodic Re-initialization:** Crucially, screw confidences $\gamma_j$ and part probabilities $m_i$ are periodically reset (e.g., to 0.9 and uniform distributions, respectively). This mechanism helps ScrewSplat escape local minima and effectively discover new, meaningful screw primitives. Simultaneously, joint angles and Gaussian poses are adjusted to move the object to a canonical pose, aiding stability.
- **Meaningful Screw Selection:** After initial convergence, screw primitives with low confidence ($\gamma_j < 0.1$) or negligible motion (small joint angle range) are pruned. Gaussian primitives associated with pruned movable parts are re-assigned to the static base.
- **Learning Rates & Efficiency:** Specific learning rates are applied to all trainable parameters (Gaussian components, screw axes, confidences, part probabilities, joint angles). Rendering and backpropagation are optimized by only considering screw primitives above a confidence threshold.

#### 3.5. Text-Guided Articulated Object Manipulation

The optimized ScrewSplat model acts as a differentiable RGB renderer $\pi(\theta)$, mapping joint angles to visual appearance. This enables:
- **Current State Estimation:** The current joint angle can be estimated by minimizing the rendering loss between an observed image and the rendered image from $\pi(\theta)$.
- **Text-Guided Control:** Given a current object state description ($t_c$) and a target prompt ($t_p$), the model infers target joint angles by optimizing a **directional CLIP loss**. This loss minimizes the angular difference between the directional shifts in the CLIP latent space for images and text, respectively ($ \Delta I(\theta) $ vs. $ \Delta T $).
- **Bayesian Optimization:** Due to the potentially complex loss landscape, Bayesian optimization is employed to search for optimal joint angles.
- **Robot Trajectory Planning:** Once target joint angles are determined, a simple robot trajectory is planned, identifying an affordance point on the object and generating a path along the screw axis. Inverse kinematics then translate this into robot movements.

This comprehensive approach allows ScrewSplat to robustly recognize articulated objects from raw RGB input and translate that understanding into actionable robot manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented in the paper convincingly demonstrate ScrewSplat's superior performance in articulated object recognition and its utility in text-guided manipulation.

#### 4.1. Articulated Object Recognition Performance

- **State-of-the-Art Accuracy:** ScrewSplat consistently achieves state-of-the-art recognition accuracy across a diverse set of articulated objects, outperforming existing baselines (PARIS, PARIS*, DTA, and an ablation model ScrewSpawn) across geometry, motion, and appearance metrics.
 - **Geometry Metrics:** Measured by Chamfer-l2 distance (CD-s for static, CD-m for movable, CD-w for whole object). ScrewSplat generally yields lower (better) Chamfer distances, indicating more accurate 3D geometry reconstruction, especially for movable parts.
 - **Motion Metrics:** Measured by angular error (Ang.) and axis position error (Pos.) for screw axes. ScrewSplat significantly outperforms baselines, demonstrating highly accurate estimation of joint axes.
 - **Appearance Metrics:** Measured by PSNR and SSIM on rendered images under unseen joint angles. ScrewSplat achieves substantially higher scores, indicating superior rendering fidelity and ability to generalize appearance to novel configurations.

- **Single-Joint Objects:**
 - ScrewSplat shows consistent best performance across all metrics for single-joint objects (e.g., FoldChair, Fridge, Laptop, Oven, Scissor, Stapler, USB, Washer, Knife, Storage box).
 - Baselines like PARIS and PARIS* struggle, often failing to recognize objects beyond simple cases. DTA performs better than PARIS but still shows limitations and failure cases (e.g., stapler).
 - The ablation model ScrewSpawn (single screw) generally fails to accurately recover geometry and kinematics, highlighting the necessity and effectiveness of ScrewSplat's multi-screw and confidence-based formulation.

- **Multi-Joint Objects:**
 - ScrewSplat demonstrates superior performance in recovering kinematic structures for multi-joint objects. For instance, in a 3-joint object, DTA fails to correctly identify more than one axis, whereas ScrewSplat successfully recognizes all screw axes.
 - Quantitatively, for multi-joint objects, ScrewSplat achieves substantially higher accuracy in reconstructing movable part geometry (CD-m) and estimating joint axes (Ang., Pos.), even if DTA might have slightly better static part geometry in some specific cases.

- **Robustness to Input Data (Ablation Study):** An ablation study comparing ScrewSplat performance with observations from two versus five object configurations shows that while more configurations generally lead to better and more robust results, ScrewSplat can still perform reasonably well even with limited (two) configurations, albeit with some sensitivity to hyperparameters.

#### 4.2. Articulated Object Manipulation Results

- **Simulated Environment:** ScrewSplat successfully enables text-guided manipulation in simulation. By providing current state descriptions and target text prompts (e.g., "open laptop"), the optimized model infers the correct joint angles to achieve the desired configuration. This validates the model's ability to interpret high-level semantic goals.
- **Real-World Robotic Manipulation:** The paper demonstrates a full pipeline for real-world text-guided robotic manipulation:
 1. **Recognition:** ScrewSplat accurately reconstructs geometry and kinematics of real-world objects from multi-view RGB observations (including challenging translucent objects like a drawer).
 2. **Current State Estimation:** The model precisely estimates the object's current joint angle.
 3. **Text-Guided Manipulation:** A robot manipulator successfully adjusts the object's joint angles according to text prompts (e.g., "open the drawer").
- **Effectiveness of Directional CLIP Loss and Bayesian Optimization:** An additional analysis reveals that using directional CLIP loss is more effective than simple cosine similarity loss for text-guided manipulation, as it better captures relative semantic changes. Furthermore, Bayesian optimization outperforms gradient-based methods, suggesting a complex loss landscape where a broader search strategy is beneficial for finding optimal joint angles.

In summary, ScrewSplat not only pushes the state-of-the-art in robust, end-to-end articulated object recognition from RGB input but also translates this understanding into practical and intuitive robot manipulation capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "ScrewSplat" paper introduces a highly significant advancement in articulated object recognition and its application to robotics, with several potential impacts:

- **Breakthrough in Robust and Generalizable Recognition:** By overcoming the critical limitations of prior work—namely, reliance on strong assumptions, auxiliary depth data, and complex multi-stage pipelines—ScrewSplat offers a more robust and truly generalizable solution. Its end-to-end nature and exclusive use of RGB observations make it highly practical for deployment in diverse, unstructured real-world environments where depth sensors might be unreliable (e.g., for transparent or reflective objects) or unavailable. This is a crucial step towards creating truly autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.

- **Novel Methodological Contribution:** The core methodology of tightly integrating screw theory with Gaussian Splatting, coupled with confidence scores for screw axes and a part probability simplex for Gaussians, is a significant technical contribution. This differentiable, relaxed formulation effectively addresses the challenging hybrid (continuous-discrete) optimization problem of articulated object recognition, enabling joint learning of geometric and kinematic parameters in a unified framework. The periodic re-initialization strategy is also a clever technique to navigate complex loss landscapes.

- **Enabling Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** The demonstration of zero-shot, text-guided robotic manipulation is a major step towards more intuitive and accessible human-robot interfaces. Instead of programming precise joint angles, users can instruct robots using natural language (e.g., "open the door," "close the laptop"), making robotic systems easier to operate for non-experts. This capability significantly enhances the practical utility of articulated object recognition, bridging the gap between perception and action.

- **Foundation for Advanced Robotic Manipulation:** The accurate recovery of an object's kinematic model is foundational for many complex manipulation tasks, including planning precise grasps, executing dynamic movements, and performing assembly operations. ScrewSplat provides this foundation robustly, paving the way for robots to perform more intricate interactions with a wider variety of objects.

- **Catalyst for Future Research Directions:** The paper not only presents a strong solution but also thoughtfully outlines its limitations and promising future directions. These include improving optimization stability for high-DoF objects, addressing shadow effects for more realistic rendering and accurate part segmentation, and extending the framework to recognize spatial kinematic chains (like robot manipulators) and deformable objects. These proposed extensions highlight the versatility of the ScrewSplat framework and its potential to inspire further research in geometric learning, robot perception, and manipulation.

In conclusion, ScrewSplat represents a substantial leap forward in the field of articulated object recognition. Its elegant integration of established robotics theory with state-of-the-art neural rendering, combined with its practical benefits of being end-to-end and RGB-only, positions it as a highly impactful work that will likely influence future developments in robotics and AI for real-world applications.
