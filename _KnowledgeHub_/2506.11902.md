# TreeRL: LLM Reinforcement Learning with On-Policy Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> TreeRL, developed by researchers from Tsinghua University and Caltech, introduces an on-policy reinforcement learning framework that integrates tree search to enhance large language model reasoning. It achieves superior performance over traditional ChainRL on challenging math and code benchmarks by leveraging an efficient entropy-guided tree search (EPTree) and deriving fine-grained process supervision from the generated reasoning trees.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning for LLMs (ChainRL) relies on sparse, delayed rewards from final outcomes, making it difficult to learn from intermediate reasoning steps.
- Existing tree search applications for LLMs are often used for inference-time enhancement or offline data generation, leading to potential distribution mismatch and reward hacking during online training.
- Inefficient exploration of the vast reasoning space by independent chain sampling and the computational inefficiency of classical MCTS for modern LLM inference engines.

## Method

- Introduces ==EPTree (Entropy-Guided Tree Search)==, an efficient and parallelizable tree search algorithm that iteratively expands branches from high-uncertainty (high-entropy) tokens, optimizing for diversity and PassRate under a given inference budget.
- Develops an ==on-policy process supervision== mechanism that derives fine-grained reward signals for each reasoning step by calculating global and local advantages based on the success rate of descendant nodes in the EPTree.
- Integrates EPTree and its derived process rewards directly into the LLM's on-policy reinforcement learning loop, updating the policy using a policy gradient method while ==re-weighting rewards for non-leaf steps==.

## Results

- EPTree consistently achieved a **3%** higher PassRate compared to independent multi-chain sampling under the same inference cost, demonstrating superior efficiency and diversity in generation.
- TreeRL outperformed traditional ChainRL across all evaluated math and code reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH500, AIME2024, LiveCodeBench), showing consistent performance gains after approximately **100 training steps**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of entropy-guided forking and the combined global and local advantage signals for process supervision, both of which were crucial for optimal RL training performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Entropy-guided forking (EPTree) efficiently generates more diverse and high-quality responses than traditional sampling methods, leading to improved search PassRate.
> - Deriving on-policy process supervision signals, using a combination of global and local advantages re-weighted by subtree size, provides effective and stable feedback for LLM reinforcement learning.
> - Directly integrating tree search into the on-policy RL loop mitigates issues of distribution mismatch and reward hacking often seen with offline reward models or static data generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large language models (LLMs) have recently demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in complex reasoning tasks across various domains, including mathematics, programming, and autonomous agents. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a particularly effective paradigm for further enhancing these reasoning abilities by optimizing LLM policies through direct feedback signals.

The current landscape of RL for LLMs predominantly relies on methods that independently sample multiple trajectories (often referred to as "chains") and assign rewards based on the final outcome (outcome supervision). While effective, this "ChainRL" approach has limitations: it may not efficiently explore the complex reasoning space, and the reward signal, being sparse and tied only to the final result, might not provide sufficient guidance for intermediate steps.

In parallel, tree search algorithms, such as Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), have achieved remarkable success in domains like game playing (e.g., AlphaZero) by enabling deeper exploration of action spaces and providing dense feedback. However, their application to LLM reinforcement learning for reasoning tasks has remained largely underdeveloped. Existing efforts to integrate tree search with LLMs typically fall into two categories:
1. **Inference-time enhancement:** Using tree search to improve the quality of generated responses during the inference phase, often guided by an external reward model.
2. **Offline data generation:** Employing tree search to produce high-quality data (e.g., step-by-step reasoning chains or preference pairs) for *offline* training methods like finetuning or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

A significant challenge with these existing approaches, especially those relying on offline data or separate reward models, is the potential for **distribution mismatch** and **reward hacking** as the RL training progresses. Furthermore, classical MCTS, designed for step-by-step generation, can be computationally inefficient for modern LLM inference engines, which are optimized for parallel generation. Efforts to derive process reward models (PRMs) from tree search for offline training have also shown limited performance improvements in RL training.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the largely unexplored potential of *on-policy RL training directly incorporating tree search* to enhance LLM reasoning. Unlike previous methods, TreeRL aims to leverage the benefits of tree search – better exploration and fine-grained process supervision – in an *on-policy* setting, thereby mitigating issues associated with static, offline reward models and the inefficiency of traditional tree search adaptations for LLMs. This positions TreeRL as a novel contribution that bridges the gap between the power of tree search and the specific requirements of LLM reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind TreeRL stems from the limitations observed in current reinforcement learning approaches for enhancing LLM reasoning, coupled with the under-utilization of powerful tree search techniques in an on-policy RL context for LLMs.

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

1. **Address Sparse and Delayed Rewards in ChainRL:** Traditional ChainRL methods rely on independent sampling of trajectories and often use sparse, final-outcome-based rewards. This makes it difficult for LLMs to learn from intermediate reasoning steps, especially in complex, multi-step problems where correct final answers are rare. TreeRL aims to provide denser, more granular feedback throughout the reasoning process.
2. **Mitigate Distribution Mismatch and Reward Hacking:** Existing methods that employ tree search for *offline* training (e.g., generating data for DPO or finetuning) or rely on a separate, pre-trained reward model often suffer from distribution mismatch (where the policy diverges from the data distribution the reward model was trained on) and reward hacking (where the policy learns to exploit flaws in the reward model rather than truly solving the task). TreeRL seeks to eliminate the need for a separate reward model by directly deriving on-policy process rewards from the tree search, making it inherently more resistant to these issues.
3. **Improve Exploration Efficiency in the Reasoning Space:** Independent chain sampling can be inefficient in exploring the vast reasoning space of LLMs. Tree search, by its nature, allows for more systematic and directed exploration. TreeRL aims to harness this exploratory power to find more diverse and correct reasoning paths.
4. **Develop an LLM-Friendly Tree Search Algorithm:** Classical Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) is often inefficient for LLM inference due to its iterative, step-by-step nature, which is not optimized for modern LLM inference engines. A key objective for TreeRL is to introduce an efficient and parallelizable tree search strategy specifically tailored for LLMs that can generate more diverse and effective responses under a given inference budget.
5. **Directly Integrate Tree Search into On-Policy RL Training:** The overarching goal is to go beyond using tree search solely for inference or offline data generation. TreeRL aims to seamlessly incorporate tree search directly into the on-policy RL training loop, allowing the LLM policy to learn and adapt from the fine-grained process signals generated by the search itself.

In essence, TreeRL is motivated by the vision of enabling LLMs to learn complex reasoning more effectively by combining the power of reinforcement learning with the structured exploration and fine-grained feedback capabilities of on-policy tree search, all while addressing practical computational challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TreeRL proposes a novel reinforcement learning framework that integrates on-policy tree search to enhance LLM reasoning capabilities. The core of its methodology revolves around two main components: an efficient entropy-guided tree search strategy called EPTree, and a mechanism for deriving on-policy process supervision signals from the generated tree structures.

#### 3.1. Entropy-Guided Tree Search (EPTree)

EPTree is designed to be an efficient and effective tree search algorithm specifically optimized for LLM RL training. Its primary goal is to generate diverse and correct answers (maximizing PassRate) under a given inference budget, while being highly parallelizable and efficient, unlike traditional MCTS which struggles with LLM inference engines.

The EPTree algorithm operates in iterative steps:

1. **Initialization:** The process begins by generating `M` independent responses (chains) from the current policy model for a given prompt. These `M` responses serve as the initial set of trees for further expansion.
2. **Forking Token Selection:** Instead of random branching or exhaustive exploration, EPTree strategically expands the search by identifying "forking points." For each existing tree (or chain), the algorithm selects the `Top-N` tokens that exhibit the highest **uncertainty**, as measured by their cross-entropy with respect to the policy model's predictions. These high-entropy tokens signify points where the model is most "unsure" and where alternative continuations might yield significantly different outcomes. To encourage exploration of new reasoning paths, tokens near the end of sequences are masked.
3. **Expansion:** From each selected forking point (prefix), the policy model generates `T` new candidate responses. These new responses extend the branch from the uncertain token until completion, forming new branches in the tree structure.
4. **Iteration:** The forking and expansion process is repeated for `L` iterations. This iterative expansion, guided by uncertainty, allows EPTree to efficiently explore a diverse set of reasoning paths. The search configuration is denoted by `(M, N, L, T)`, representing the number of parallel trees, forking points per iteration, iterations, and branching factor, respectively.

**Key advantages of EPTree:**
- **Efficiency:** EPTree typically requires only a few iterations (e.g., around 2) to build diverse and informative trees, making it much faster than step-by-step MCTS.
- **Diversity:** By branching from high-uncertainty points, EPTree effectively generates a larger number of diverse responses compared to independent multi-chain sampling under the same inference cost, providing richer data for RL training.
- **Targeted Exploration:** Entropy-guided forking directs the search to promising areas of the reasoning space where the model is most uncertain, leading to potentially better solutions.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning with EPTree (Process Supervision)

The hierarchical tree structure generated by EPTree provides a rich source of fine-grained, on-policy process supervision signals, which TreeRL leverages for RL training.

1. **Value Estimation:** For any node (step) `s_n` in the tree, its "value," `V(s_n)`, is computed as the proportion of correct leaf nodes among all its descendant leaves. This value reflects the potential of that specific step to lead to a correct final answer.
2. **Process Reward Definition (Advantages):** The paper defines the process supervision signal for each step using two types of "advantages," reflecting how much a step improves the likelihood of a correct solution compared to other steps:
 - **Global Advantage (G_A(s_n)):** `V(s_n) - V(root)`. This measures the potential of step `s_n` relative to the average correctness ratio of *all* generated responses (the "root" value of the entire tree). A positive global advantage means `s_n` is on a path more likely to lead to a correct answer than an average path.
 - **Local Advantage (L_A(s_n)):** `V(s_n) - V(p(s_n))`. This quantifies the improvement provided by step `s_n` compared to its immediate parent step `p(s_n)`. A positive local advantage indicates that `s_n` is a "good" step that improves upon the parent's potential.
 - **Combined Reward:** The final process reward `R(s_n)` for a step `s_n` is defined as the sum of its global and local advantages: `R(s_n) = G_A(s_n) + L_A(s_n)`. This formulation can be seen as a special case of Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).
3. **Training with Process Supervision:**
 - In each RL iteration, EPTree generates `M` trees for a given prompt.
 - For every step (token generation) within these trees, the process supervision signal `R(s_n)` is calculated.
 - **Reward Re-weighting:** To prevent overfitting, especially for non-leaf steps that appear in multiple sequences, their assigned reward `R(s_n)` is downweighted by dividing it by the square root of the number of leaf nodes in its subtree (`sqrt(|L(s_n)|)`). This ensures that steps contributing to many branches don't disproportionately influence the policy update.
 - Finally, the LLM policy model is updated using a policy gradient method (e.g., Proximal Policy Optimization-like objective) to maximize these calculated process rewards.

**Overall Approach:** By coupling EPTree with this on-policy process supervision mechanism, TreeRL avoids the pitfalls of offline reward models and distribution mismatch. The advantage signals are derived directly from the active search process, making them inherently aligned with the current policy and resistant to reward hacking, thereby fostering a more effective and stable RL training environment for complex LLM reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments on challenging college-level and competition-level math and code reasoning benchmarks to evaluate TreeRL against traditional ChainRL and other baselines. The experiments were performed using various SFT models as initialization, including Qwen-2.5-14B, GLM4-9B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distilled-Qwen-2.5-7B. Key metrics included PassRate (for evaluating search algorithms) and Accuracy (for evaluating RL-trained policies). Crucially, comparisons were made under similar inference token budgets to ensure fairness.

#### 4.1. EPTree Search Performance

- **Superior PassRate:** EPTree consistently demonstrated a significant advantage in PassRate over both the baseline independent multi-chain sampling method and traditional Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) under the same inference cost. Specifically, EPTree typically outperformed multi-chain sampling by around 3% in PassRate.
- **Efficiency over MCTS:** While MCTS performs poorly under limited inference budgets due to its iterative nature, EPTree's advantage over MCTS widened as the inference cost increased, highlighting its greater efficiency for LLM generation.
- **Generation Diversity:** EPTree was shown to produce approximately twice the number of diverse responses compared to i.i.d. multi-chain sampling, given the same generation token budget. This increased diversity provides richer training signals for the RL phase.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning Training Results

- **TreeRL Outperforms ChainRL:** TreeRL, powered by EPTree sampling and its derived process supervision, achieved superior performance compared to traditional ChainRL (using independent multi-chain sampling) across all evaluated math and code reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, Omni-MATH-500, AIME2024, AMC, OlympiadBench, and LiveCodeBench).
- **Consistent Improvement During Training:** While both TreeRL and ChainRL showed similar performance in the early stages of training, TreeRL began to exhibit a clear advantage after approximately 100 training steps and continued to improve consistently. This indicates that TreeRL achieves better "prompt efficiency," delivering enhanced performance with the same number of training prompts.
- **General Task Performance:** An ablation study on general tasks (MMLU-Pro, Arena-Hard, IFEval) showed that TreeRL performs comparably to ChainRL, indicating that its specific advantages are most pronounced in complex reasoning tasks, but it does not degrade performance on general tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Entropy-Guided Forking:**
 - Comparing EPTree (entropy-based forking) with random forking revealed that entropy-guided branching yields a better PassRate with fewer generation tokens. This validates the design choice of using uncertainty to guide exploration.
 - Both entropy-guided and random forking tree sampling strategies consistently outperformed multi-chain sampling, underscoring the general benefit of tree search.
- **Analysis of Forking Tokens:** A case study identified that frequently selected forking tokens included mathematical operators (`\(`), logical conjunctions, and transitional terms (`Since`, `But`). Notably, the token `wait` also appeared frequently, often used by "self-reflection" models, suggesting that EPTree effectively identifies critical decision points in the reasoning process.
- **Effectiveness of Process Supervision Signals:**
 - The combination of reweighted global and local advantages for process supervision (`(G_A + L_A) / sqrt(n)`) yielded the best RL training performance.
 - Removing either the global or local advantage component led to a decline in performance, confirming their complementary roles.
 - Using a smaller subset of sampled responses resulted in less improvement compared to utilizing the entire set of traces provided by EPTree. This suggests that the increased number of informative training traces (even within the same inference cost budget) generated by tree search also contributes significantly to RL performance.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that TreeRL effectively combines an efficient tree search strategy (EPTree) with a robust on-policy process supervision mechanism. This synergy enables LLMs to learn more effectively from complex reasoning tasks, leading to superior performance compared to conventional RL methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TreeRL represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model training, particularly for complex reasoning tasks. Its introduction of an on-policy reinforcement learning framework that directly incorporates tree search has several far-reaching implications:

1. **Pioneering On-Policy Tree Search for LLM RL:** TreeRL is a groundbreaking step in directly integrating tree search into the *on-policy* RL training loop for LLMs. This moves beyond the prior limitations of using tree search solely for inference-time improvements or for generating offline training data, which are prone to distribution mismatch and reward hacking. By providing an online, adaptive mechanism for feedback, TreeRL sets a new precedent for how LLMs can learn from structured exploration.

2. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** The demonstrated superior performance on challenging math and code reasoning benchmarks highlights TreeRL's potential to significantly advance LLM capabilities in domains requiring multi-step, logical deduction. This is crucial for developing more robust and reliable AI systems capable of tackling real-world problems.

3. **Efficient Exploration and Reward Signal:** The EPTree algorithm's ability to generate more diverse and effective responses under the same inference budget, guided by token-level uncertainty, is a notable contribution. This efficient exploration, coupled with the dense, fine-grained process supervision signals derived directly from the tree structure (global and local advantages), provides a richer and more effective learning signal than traditional sparse outcome-based rewards. This directly addresses the challenge of credit assignment in long reasoning chains.

4. **Mitigation of Reward Model Issues:** By calculating advantage signals directly from the on-policy generated trees, TreeRL eliminates the need for a separate, pre-trained reward model. This inherently makes the training process more resistant to issues like distribution shift and reward hacking, leading to more stable and trustworthy policy optimization.

5. **Improved Prompt Efficiency:** The finding that TreeRL achieves better "prompt efficiency" (enhanced performance with the same number of training prompts) signifies a more effective utilization of training data. This is particularly valuable in resource-intensive LLM training scenarios.

6. **Open-Source Contribution:** The open-sourcing of TreeRL (`THUDM/TreeRL`) is a vital contribution to the research community. It lowers the barrier for other researchers and practitioners to experiment with and build upon this novel approach, accelerating progress in LLM reasoning and RL.

**Potential Future Impact:**

- **Foundation for Advanced Reasoning Systems:** TreeRL's methodology could serve as a foundational component for building next-generation AI systems that require deep, verifiable reasoning, such as automated theorem provers, complex code generators, or scientific discovery agents.
- **Inspiration for New RL Algorithms:** The success of on-policy tree search for LLMs may inspire new RL algorithms that more tightly integrate search and learning, potentially drawing more parallels to the success seen in domains like AlphaZero.
- **Demands for Optimized LLM Inference Engines:** The acknowledged limitation regarding current LLM inference engine optimizations for tree search highlights an important area for future development. The growing interest in tree search methods will likely drive the creation of more specialized and efficient inference architectures.
- **Richer Process Supervision Research:** The work opens avenues for further research into more sophisticated definitions of process signals, optimal weighting schemes for different steps, and advanced step-level reward normalization techniques, pushing the boundaries of fine-grained supervision in LLMs.

In conclusion, TreeRL not only offers a powerful new method for enhancing LLM reasoning but also provides a conceptual leap in how tree search and reinforcement learning can be synergistically applied in the context of large language models, promising more intelligent and reliable AI systems.
