# Scaling Manual-Grounded Appliance Manipulation with Data Synthesis and Unified Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> MAGE synthesizes manual-grounded appliance manipulation data via a Hierarchical Appliance Graph spanning document/page/part/state layers, producing UseAppliance (22 categories, 89K+ part boxes, 53K+ plans, 33K+ recovery steps); trains AppliancePlan, a 7B end-to-end model beating GPT-5 over 10x on open-loop planning and transferring to real robots at 40.00% success.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs and embodied planning models lack large-scale, high-quality training data tailored to manual-grounded, state-dependent appliance manipulation.
- Prior appliance-interaction methods handle only short-horizon single-device actions like pressing one button, without manual-grounded long-horizon operation.
- Manual-based planners like CheckManual and ApBot use multi-stage zero-shot pipelines with cascaded external modules, causing latency and compounding errors and limited action parameterization.

## Method

- **Hierarchical Appliance Graph (HAG)**: four-layer graph representation (document, page, part, state) encoding containment, reference, and state-transition relations for combinatorial data generation.
- **MAGE** pipeline: VLM-powered manual collection agent plus human-verified part/state annotation, then ==graph-traversal task-plan synthesis== and generative-world-model observation synthesis with injected interruptions.
- **AppliancePlan**: Qwen2.5-VL-7B trained with three main losses (grounding, open-loop planning, closed-loop adjustment) plus three auxiliary losses (manual-part alignment, key-step action prediction, part state judgment).

## Results

- AppliancePlan attains **47.86%/31.36%** open-loop task completion/success versus best baseline 4.36%/2.68%, over **10x** higher, ranking first on every appliance category.
- On grounding, AppliancePlan reaches **22.96%** IoU / **22.24%** mAP@0.5, beating strongest baseline ApBot by **12.36** IoU and **10.14** mAP@0.5 points.
- Real-robot tests on six appliances show **49.87%/40.00%** completion/success versus 19.35%/3.33% for GPT-5 and 9.12%/3.33% for ApBot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - General-purpose MLLM language and vision capability does not transfer to structured multi-step planning with precise numeric parameters like rotation angles.
> - Auxiliary objectives targeting specific failure modes (alignment for grounding, key-step prediction for parameters, state judgment for recovery) each independently drive their targeted capability.
> - A graph-structured manual representation enables systematic state-space coverage in synthetic data, avoiding the bias toward common configurations seen in human demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics aims to enable autonomous agents to perform complex tasks in unstructured environments, such as households. A significant challenge in this domain is equipping robots with the ability to operate diverse household appliances. This task is considerably more complex than generic object manipulation (e.g., pick-and-place) due to several factors: appliances exhibit wide variations in their physical interfaces, operational logic, and state transitions; successful operation often requires long-horizon planning; and execution must be robust to disturbances and state-dependent.

Prior research in appliance manipulation has explored two main directions. One focuses on direct interaction with household devices, typically addressing short-horizon behaviors like opening a microwave door, but often without explicitly incorporating manual-grounded operation. The second direction integrates appliance manuals for action planning. Approaches such as CheckManual and ApBot have demonstrated the utility of manual information. However, these methods typically rely on multi-stage zero-shot pipelines built upon external modules, leading to high inference latency and compounding errors. Furthermore, their action spaces may be limited, precluding the prediction of continuous parameters like rotation angles in a single step.

The broader landscape of embodied planning has seen advancements with multimodal foundation models (MLLMs) and vision-language-action (VLA) models, such as SayCan, PaLM-E, RT-2, and OpenVLA. These models have improved long-context understanding and multimodal reasoning, enhancing capabilities in generic object manipulation and long-horizon plan generation. However, their performance on manual-grounded appliance operation has remained limited. A core limitation identified across these existing methods, particularly for the intricate and diverse nature of appliance manipulation, is the absence of large-scale, high-quality training data specifically tailored to manual-grounded, long-horizon, and state-dependent tasks. This work directly addresses this data bottleneck and proposes a unified planning approach to overcome the aforementioned limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to enable robots to reliably operate household appliances, a capability identified as crucial for practical home autonomy. The authors identify a fundamental bottleneck preventing existing large models (MLLMs and embodied planning models) from performing effectively in this domain: the lack of a sufficiently diverse, task-oriented dataset to support the necessary long-horizon, state-dependent, and robust planning.

To address this, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a scalable data synthesis pipeline:** The first objective is to create a method for automatically generating large-scale, high-quality training data for manual-grounded appliance manipulation. This pipeline should be capable of producing diverse data types, including part grounding annotations, long-horizon manipulation plans, and closed-loop recovery data, directly from appliance manuals. This is intended to bridge the gap between human understanding of manuals and robot operational requirements.
2. **Construct a comprehensive dataset:** A subsequent objective is to build the first large-scale dataset specifically designed for manual-grounded appliance manipulation planning. This dataset should feature high quality through human verification, rich diversity across appliance categories and operational procedures, multi-task coverage for grounding, open-loop planning, and closed-loop adjustment, and extensibility to new appliances.
3. **Propose an end-to-end planning model:** The research aims to develop a unified, end-to-end model capable of performing manual-grounded appliance manipulation planning. This model should integrate various capabilities—appliance part grounding, open-loop manipulation planning, and closed-loop planning adjustment—within a single architecture, thereby avoiding the compounding errors and high latency often associated with multi-stage zero-shot pipelines.
4. **Demonstrate effective sim-to-real transfer:** A practical objective is to validate the developed model and data synthesis approach through real-robot experiments. This is crucial for confirming that performance gains observed in simulation benchmarks translate to physical execution in real-world household environments, thus representing a step toward general-purpose household robotics.

In essence, the research is motivated by the recognized complexity of appliance operation for robots and the identified data scarcity, aiming to provide both a scalable data generation solution and an integrated planning model to advance robotic capabilities in household environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This work introduces a multi-faceted approach involving a data synthesis pipeline, a resulting dataset, and a unified planning model.

#### 3.1. MAGE: Data Synthesis Pipeline

MAGE (Manual-grounded Appliance data GEneration pipeline) is a scalable pipeline built upon a Hierarchical Appliance Graph (HAG). It automates HAG construction and data generation, incorporating human verification at strategic stages.

##### 3.1.1. Hierarchical Appliance Graph (HAG) Formulation

The HAG formalizes appliance manual knowledge into a four-level hierarchy:
- **Document layer:** Represents the entire manual document.
- **Page layer:** Contains typed page nodes (e.g., product-overview, operating-instructions) linked to the document root.
- **Part layer:** Includes part nodes linked to their describing pages, each with a visual bounding box in corresponding appliance views.
- **State layer:** Represents discrete or continuous part configurations (e.g., knob angles, button states). Directed action edges connect state nodes, encoding atomic actions that transition parts between states.
This HAG formulation aims to provide combinatorial task coverage, locally verifiable annotations, and domain-agnostic extensibility.

##### 3.1.2. Appliance Manual Collection

This initial stage builds the document and page layers of the HAG. A Vision-Language Model (VLM)-powered agent autonomously searches manufacturer websites and databases, downloads candidate manuals, and filters low-quality ones. The agent then classifies each page into one of nine semantic types (e.g., product overview, operating instructions) to populate the page layer.

##### 3.1.3. Appliance Part and State Annotation

This stage completes the part and state layers of the HAG:
- **Part Grounding Annotation:** For manuals with clear cover photos, an open-vocabulary object detector identifies the appliance region. Canonical views (front, oblique front-side, oblique top-down) are synthesized. Human annotators, guided by product-overview pages, label visible parts with bounding boxes on these views, with spatial augmentation.
- **State & Transition Annotation:** For each part node, a state-type label is assigned, and a directed state-transition graph is constructed. A VLM proposes candidate states and inter-state transitions from relevant manual pages, which are then human-verified. Parts are unified into discrete state graphs (e.g., binary parts, discrete mode selectors, discretized continuous parts like knobs), with action edges (e.g., button-press, rotation) reflecting manual-specified granularity.

##### 3.1.4. Manipulation Task and Planning Creation

This stage synthesizes open-loop task-plan pairs by treating task generation as structured graph traversal.
- **Task and Plan Templates:** Manual-designed task and plan templates are created for each appliance based on its manual and HAG. Task templates include placeholders for target states, durations, and interacting objects. Plan templates are ordered sequences of atomic actions from the RealAppliance-Bench vocabulary, specifying target parts, operation parameters, objects, locations, and durations.
- **Template-based Generation:** Concrete task instances are generated by sampling initial-goal state pairs from the HAG and drawing task attributes (objects, durations). These fill the task templates. Plan templates are then instantiated by filling operation placeholders from the state difference and sampled task attributes. An LLM rewrites the filled task templates into diverse natural-language instructions. Uniform sampling of state pairs ensures systematic state-space coverage.

##### 3.1.5. Observation Generation and Interruption Injection

This stage generates planning-aligned observation images and closed-loop recovery data.
- **Visual State Prediction:** Seedream 4.0 is used as a generative world model to iteratively generate observation images. Given an appliance photo and task description, an initial observation is generated. For subsequent steps, each action is converted into a textual prompt describing the expected visual change, and the model generates the next observation. Cross-step consistency checking and human verification are applied.
- **Closed-loop Interruption Injection:** Hypothetical disturbances are injected into intermediate steps of the observation sequences to create closed-loop adjustment supervision. Three categories of interruptions are defined: binary-state errors, button mis-touches, and rotation deviations. For each interruption, a post-disturbance observation image is generated, and the corrective action is annotated, conditioned on the task context and disturbed observation. This yields training triples for recovery action prediction.

#### 3.2. UseAppliance Dataset

The MAGE pipeline is applied to 22 appliance categories to build UseAppliance, the first large-scale dataset for manual-grounded appliance manipulation planning. It features 89K+ part bounding boxes, 53K+ manipulation tasks, and 33K+ closed-loop adjustment steps, covering a diverse range of control interfaces and operation procedures.

#### 3.3. AppliancePlan Model

##### 3.3.1. Model Architecture

AppliancePlan is an end-to-end model built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. It takes visual inputs (manual pages, observation images) and textual task instructions. A vision encoder and vision-language merger project these into the LLM token space, which then predicts the output autoregressively.

##### 3.3.2. Model Training

AppliancePlan is trained with a combination of three main objectives and three auxiliary objectives, all formulated as next-token prediction and optimized with cross-entropy loss.

**Main Planning Objectives:**
1. **Appliance Part Grounding:** Bridges the domain gap between symbolic part descriptions in manuals and pixel-level appearance. Given manual pages, an observation image, and a part name, the model predicts the bounding box coordinates.
2. **Open-loop Manipulation Planning:** Trains the model to reason about action sequences. Given manual pages, an initial observation, a task instruction, and an atomic action set, the model generates the full parameterized open-loop plan as an autoregressive sequence.
3. **Closed-loop Planning Adjustment:** Trains the model to predict corrective actions. Given the current observation, task instruction, reference plan, and executed action history, the model predicts the next corrective action.

**Auxiliary Training Objectives:**
1. **Manual-Appliance Part Alignment:** Strengthens bidirectional part-manual correspondence. This includes manual-side part identification (predicting part name from bounding box), observation-side part identification (predicting part name from center-point coordinate), and part localization (predicting center-point coordinate from part name).
2. **Key-Step Action Prediction:** Focuses supervision on steps requiring precise action parameter prediction (e.g., rotation angles, button-press counts). It involves next-step prediction, remaining-step prediction, and parameter VQA for key actions.
3. **Part State Judgment:** Provides explicit state-recognition supervision through VQA. The model receives manual pages, an observation image, and a question about a part's state, predicting the ground-truth state label.

The overall training objective combines these main and auxiliary losses. Training is performed for 96 GPU-hours on NVIDIA H200, using DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 with bfloat16 mixed precision. Inference uses a temperature of 0.1 for plan generation stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates AppliancePlan on the RealAppliance-Bench simulation environment and through real-robot experiments. All evaluations are out-of-distribution, as the training data (UseAppliance) has no overlap with RealAppliance-Bench.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on RealAppliance-Bench

RealAppliance-Bench features 100 high-fidelity digital appliance assets across 14 categories. Four evaluation tracks are used:

- **Appliance Part Grounding:** AppliancePlan achieved an average Intersection-over-Union (IoU) of 22.96% and a mean Average Precision at IoU 0.5 (mAP@0.5) of 22.24%. This performance surpassed the strongest embodied baseline, ApBot (10.60% IoU, 12.10% mAP@0.5), by 12.36 IoU points and 10.14 mAP@0.5 points, indicating the effectiveness of explicit manual-conditioned training.
- **Open-loop Manipulation Planning:** AppliancePlan achieved a 47.86% task completion rate and a 31.36% task success rate. This represents a significant improvement, over 10 times the performance of the best baseline (Qwen3-VL 235B Thinking, with 4.36% completion and 1.73% success; or GLM-4.5V with 3.73% completion and 2.68% success). The low baseline performance underscores that general language and vision models struggle with structured multi-step planning requiring precise parameters, a gap addressed by AppliancePlan's training on parameterized atomic actions. AppliancePlan also ranked first across all appliance categories in this track.
- **Closed-loop Planning Adjustment:** AppliancePlan demonstrated a 37.12% step-wise success rate for closed-loop adjustment. This performance exceeded the best open-source baseline, RoboBrain 2.0-7B (31.77%), by 5.35 points, and the best proprietary MLLM, Gemini 2.5 Pro (31.73%), by 5.39 points. This indicates the model's robustness in detecting and correcting execution deviations.
- **Sequential Planning and Adjustment:** This track evaluates end-to-end performance, where the model generates an initial plan and then performs online corrections. AppliancePlan achieved a 44.59% completion rate and a 28.07% success rate. This significantly outperformed the best baseline (Qwen3-VL 235B Instruct, with 5.84% completion and 4.08% success), confirming that the gains in planning and correction capabilities translate to long-horizon execution, including recovery from self-generated errors.

#### 4.2. Ablation of Auxiliary Training Objectives

An ablation study examined the contribution of each auxiliary objective:
- **Removing Manual-Appliance Part Alignment** resulted in the largest degradation in grounding performance, confirming its role in accurate spatial localization through bidirectional part-manual correspondence.
- **Removing Key-Step Action Prediction** led to the most substantial decline in long-horizon metrics, particularly in the sequential planning setting. This indicates that focused supervision on parameter-critical steps is crucial for precise action prediction.
- **Removing Part State Judgment** primarily impacted closed-loop recovery performance, validating its function in enabling state-aware plan revision.
The results suggest that each auxiliary objective contributes to its targeted capability, and their combined use yields the most balanced performance across grounding, planning, and closed-loop correction.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Evaluation

AppliancePlan was deployed on a Franka Emika Panda robot with an eye-in-hand RealSense D415 camera and a third-view D415 camera. Tests were conducted on six common household appliances (microwave, air fryer, toaster, rice cooker, coffee machine, and blender), with 10 manipulation tasks per appliance.
AppliancePlan achieved an average of 49.87% task completion and 40.00% task success across the six appliances. This significantly outperformed GPT-5 (19.35% completion, 3.33% success) and ApBot (9.12% completion, 3.33% success). The larger margin in task success, compared to completion rates, indicates that general-purpose MLLMs and pipeline methods may perform early steps correctly but often fail on state-dependent decisions required for full task completion, while AppliancePlan demonstrates better overall task completion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of embodied robotics, particularly for household automation. The introduction of MAGE, a scalable data synthesis pipeline based on the Hierarchical Appliance Graph (HAG), addresses a critical data bottleneck that has hindered the progress of large models in manual-grounded appliance manipulation. The ability to automatically generate diverse and high-quality data—including part grounding, long-horizon planning, and closed-loop recovery information—directly from appliance manuals represents a fundamental shift in how training data can be acquired for complex robotic tasks.

The creation of UseAppliance, the first large-scale dataset tailored for manual-grounded appliance manipulation planning, is a significant contribution. Its characteristics of high quality, rich diversity, multi-task coverage, and extensibility provide a robust foundation for training more capable robotic systems. By encompassing 22 appliance categories and a wide array of control interfaces and operation procedures, the dataset offers a comprehensive learning resource that can generalize across different household devices.

The development of AppliancePlan, an end-to-end model for manual-grounded appliance manipulation planning, demonstrates how to effectively leverage this synthesized data. The model's architecture, combined with its training objectives (both main and auxiliary), allows it to surpass existing proprietary MLLMs and embodied planning baselines by a substantial margin on all evaluated tracks of RealAppliance-Bench, including over a 10-fold improvement in open-loop planning task success. Its robust performance in the sequential planning and adjustment setting, which integrates planning and online correction, indicates a move towards more adaptive and fault-tolerant robotic systems.

Furthermore, the successful sim-to-real transfer demonstrated through real-robot experiments on six household appliances confirms the practical viability of this approach. The significant performance gains on physical robots, compared to baselines, indicate that the methods are not confined to simulation but can operate effectively in tangible environments.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Advancement in Household Robotics:** It represents a tangible step towards general-purpose household robots that can independently operate a wide array of appliances, thereby enhancing home autonomy and potentially assisting in daily tasks for various user groups.
- **New Data Generation Paradigm:** The MAGE pipeline offers a generalizable methodology for creating structured, task-oriented data from documentation, which could be extended to other domains requiring instruction-following and state-dependent actions.
- **Improved Embodied AI:** The insights from the model's design and the ablation studies highlight the importance of specific training objectives, such as manual-appliance part alignment and key-step action prediction, for achieving precision and robustness in complex manipulation tasks. This could inform the development of future multimodal foundation models for embodied AI.
- **Benchmarking and Research Direction:** The dataset and model establish new benchmarks and provide a solid foundation for future research in manual-grounded manipulation, encouraging further exploration into scalability, robustness, and generalization across diverse robotic platforms and tasks.

In summary, this research addresses a critical gap in data and methodology for appliance manipulation, presenting a comprehensive solution that pushes the boundaries of current robotic capabilities and lays groundwork for more intelligent and adaptable autonomous agents in household settings.
