# EgoHTR: Egocentric 4D Demonstrations of Human Terrain Traversal

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich and collaborating institutions introduce EgoHTR, a dataset and reconstruction pipeline offering centimeter-precise 4D human motion sequences grounded in high-resolution 3D scene meshes. This resource provides multi-modal data of human-terrain interactions in diverse unstructured environments, facilitating the training of robust humanoid robot locomotion policies and benchmarking human pose and shape estimation.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with robust locomotion in unstructured and complex terrain, primarily due to the inherent complexity of real-world interactions and the vast exploration space for high-degree-of-freedom systems.
- Existing human motion datasets lack dense scene information, are often restricted to controlled environments, or focus on flat terrains, which limits their utility for context-aware robot control and high-fidelity 4D human-scene reconstruction.
- Achieving a balance between data scalability and reconstruction accuracy in 'in-the-wild' scenarios is challenging, with current vision-based human-scene reconstruction methods often exhibiting perceptual failures and accuracy limitations.

## Method

- Developed a multi-modal sensor suite combining egocentric Project ==Aria glasses==, an IMU-based ==Rokoko Pro II MoCap Suit==, and a ==Leica BLK2GO== 3D Scene Scanner for robust data acquisition in unstructured environments.
- Implemented a three-stage human-scene reconstruction pipeline for precise 4D human motion: body model parameterization (==SMPL-X== with IK refinement), temporal alignment using synchronization events, and spatial alignment with 3D scanned scenes.
- Compiled the **EgoHTR** dataset, comprising 55 continuous human-terrain interaction sequences (1.37 hours) featuring 8 subjects navigating 7 diverse indoor and outdoor scenes with varied agile motions.

## Results

- EgoHTR achieves a mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) of **73.2 mm** and Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (PA-MPJPE) of **54.3 mm**, showing a **6.2%** and **2.0%** improvement respectively over a strong baseline (SLOPER4D) under more demanding conditions.
- The dataset provides the first global Human Pose and Shape (HPS) estimates, reporting a world MPJPE of **151.3 mm**, world-aligned MPJPE of **66.7 mm**, and a root translation error (RTE) of **0.09%**.
- Perceptive locomotion policies trained using EgoHTR's terrain-aligned data were successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for tasks like beam balancing and box-climbing, demonstrating the dataset's utility in enabling context-aware robot control where lower precision data would fail.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anchoring the human body model to egocentric SLAM from Aria glasses and dense scene scans allows for robust, temporally stable, and centimeter-precise global human-scene alignment, even in challenging conditions.
> - A global error threshold of approximately 0.1 meters in human-scene reconstruction is critical; exceeding this value prevents successful policy training for foothold-critical tasks in humanoid robot locomotion.
> - Existing state-of-the-art exocentric, egocentric, and IMU-based human mesh recovery methods demonstrate significant limitations, such as high failure rates from occlusions, poor generalization to dynamic motions, or temporal drift, when applied to complex, unstructured terrain scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of humanoid robots in unstructured and complex terrain environments remains a challenge in robotics. Current approaches based on classic reinforcement learning (RL) often encounter difficulties due to the inherent complexity of real-world interactions and the vast exploration space associated with high-degree-of-freedom robotic systems. While methods that leverage human priors have demonstrated promise in providing kinematic constraints for efficient learning, their application to context-aware robot control is limited by the scarcity of suitable training data.

Existing human motion datasets exhibit several limitations that hinder progress in this area. Many datasets either lack dense scene information, are restricted to spatially constrained laboratory or indoor environments, or focus exclusively on flat and structured terrains. Recent efforts to address this data gap through vision-based human-scene reconstruction often suffer from perceptual failures and accuracy limitations, primarily due to their reliance on exocentric sensing modalities and training data that lacks sufficient contextual diversity. This scenario highlights a need for an open-source data capture pipeline that can balance the competing demands of data scalability and reconstruction accuracy, particularly for in-the-wild scenarios.

This work introduces the Egocentric Human-Terrain Reconstruction (EgoHTR) dataset and an associated reconstruction pipeline to bridge this identified gap between humanoid learning and high-fidelity scene reconstruction. EgoHTR positions itself as a resource for generating multi-modal, scene-aligned 4D human motion sequences in diverse and challenging environments. This is intended to facilitate the development of perceptive locomotion policies for humanoid robots and to establish a robust benchmark for 4D human-scene reconstruction. The dataset's focus on unstructured, rough terrain, combined with its multi-modal and egocentric data capture, distinguishes it from prior datasets which often specialize in specific modalities, environments, or motion types (e.g., marker-based systems for kinematics without scene context, exocentric vision systems for indoor 4D human-scene analysis, or wearable sensor systems lacking environmental grounding).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the deployment of humanoid robots in unstructured terrain by providing high-fidelity, context-aware human demonstration data. The motivation stems from several identified limitations in the current state of robotics and human motion data collection:

1. **Addressing the Gap in Humanoid Robot Locomotion:** Current reinforcement learning policies, particularly those based on human demonstrations, struggle to achieve robust performance in unstructured environments. This is attributed to the "sheer complexity of real-world interactions" and the "restricted motion synthesis" that results from existing datasets lacking sufficient contextual awareness. The project aims to enable more effective training of perceptive locomotion policies for humanoid robots by providing data that grounds human motion within detailed 3D scene geometry.

2. **Overcoming Data Scarcity and Limitations:** A significant motivation is the "scarcity of current training datasets" that offer multi-modal recordings of diverse human motions in rough terrain. Existing datasets are noted for their deficiencies:
 - Absence of dense scene information.
 - Spatial confinement to controlled setups.
 - Exclusive focus on indoor or flat/structured environments.
 - Perceptual failures and accuracy limitations in recent vision-based human-scene reconstruction methods, often due to reliance on exocentric modalities and context-limited training.

3. **Balancing Scalability and Reconstruction Accuracy:** The authors identify an "inherent difficulty of in-the-wild collection," which typically necessitates a compromise between the ability to scale data collection and the accuracy of the resulting reconstruction. A key objective is to develop an open-source capture pipeline that achieves an optimal trade-off, enabling "centimeter-precision 4D reconstruction of dynamic trajectories" in truly unstructured settings.

4. **Enabling Context-Aware Robot Control:** For humanoid robots to perform "foothold-critical tasks" in complex topography, the training data must specify "both where the foot lands and when it bears load" with high precision. Current methods, which often supervise general joint tracking rather than explicit end-effector alignment, fail to provide this necessary contact precision. A central objective of EgoHTR is to provide this precise human-terrain coupling through scene-aligned motions.

To achieve these objectives, the work makes three specific contributions:
- **Reconstruction Pipeline:** Development and release of a scalable 4D human-scene reconstruction pipeline to facilitate community-driven dataset extensions.
- **Multi-Modal Dataset:** Introduction of the EgoHTR dataset, featuring multi-modal human motion sequences precisely grounded within high-resolution rough 3D scene meshes.
- **Applications and Benchmarking:** Establishment of a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating human pose and shape (HPS) estimation and 4D human-scene reconstruction on rough terrain, and provision of data for training and deploying context-aware locomotion policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a multi-modal sensor suite and a three-stage human-scene reconstruction pipeline designed to capture 4D human motion in diverse, unstructured environments with centimeter-level precision.

#### 3.1. Capture System

The core of the data acquisition relies on a portable multi-sensor setup, aiming for a balance between deployment usability and reconstruction accuracy:

- **Project Aria Glasses Gen. 1:** A head-mounted multi-sensor device worn by the subject. This provides egocentric RGB frames (1408x1408 at 30 fps), a closed-loop SLAM trajectory of the camera poses (1000 fps), a processed semi-dense point cloud, hand tracking data, and an audio stream. An optional second pair can be worn by an observer for additional perspectives.
- **IMU-based Rokoko Pro II MoCap Suit:** An egocentric motion capture suit that outputs relative joint angles and offsets in BVH format (30 fps) and raw IMU measurements (100 fps). This suit provides kinematic data for the human subject.
- **Leica BLK2GO 3D Scene Scanner:** A handheld imaging laser scanner used to capture high-resolution, dense 3D point clouds and continuous high-resolution imagery of the environment. The raw SLAM output is processed into a high-fidelity, colorized 3D mesh. The scanner's initial camera pose and image provide the geometric anchor for global alignment and define the unified world frame (W).
- **Fixed Exocentric Camera (Optional):** A GoPro Hero 10 camera can be used to record high-resolution, static third-person video footage of the human-scene interaction.

This integrated sensor suite enables the collection of rich sensory streams necessary for multi-modal human-scene sequence reconstruction in unconstrained real-world environments.

#### 3.2. Human-Scene Reconstruction

The acquired raw data undergoes a three-stage reconstruction process:

1. **Body Model Parameterization (Stage I):**
 - **SMPL-X Model:** The human subject's pose and shape are parameterized using the SMPL-X model, which represents body, face, and hands.
 - **Kinematics Retargeting:** The MoCap suit provides kinematics for 22 primary body joints. Hand and facial parameters are fixed to an identity pose for modularity. Body shapes ($\beta$) and facial expressions ($\psi$) are assumed constant within a sequence.
 - **Rotational Retargeting (Ia):** Global quaternions from the Rokoko suit's source skeleton are converted into relative rotation matrices required by SMPL-X. A per-joint offset matrix is applied to compensate for skeletal axis misalignment and structural differences, following established methods.
 - **Inverse Kinematics (IK) Refinement (Ib):** To mitigate joint position errors from structural differences between skeletons, an optimization-based IK step refines the pose. This minimizes the 3D positional discrepancy between selected limb joints of the SMPL-X model and the corresponding MoCap joint positions.

2. **Temporal Alignment (Stage II):**
 - **Synchronization Event:** Data streams from the MoCap suit and Aria glasses are synchronized using a distinct hand clap at the beginning of each sequence. This produces near-simultaneous, high-amplitude peaks in both the raw hand joint IMU signals (100 Hz) and the Aria glasses' audio stream (48 kHz).
 - **Precision and Drift:** This method trades sub-millisecond lab precision for in-the-wild mobility, achieving a cross-sensor alignment error of less than 60 ms. Sequences are limited to a maximum length of 5 minutes to keep hardware clock drift negligible, removing the need for continuous time synchronization.

3. **Spatial Alignment (Stage III):**
 - **Body-to-Aria Alignment:** The body model kinematics are anchored to the closed-loop trajectory provided by the Aria glasses' SLAM system. This is achieved by defining a static translation offset between the Aria camera and the MoCap suit's head joint, assuming the glasses are fixed relative to the head. This approach leverages Aria SLAM's accuracy to eliminate global IMU drift.
 - **Aria-to-Scene Alignment:** The reconstructed human sequence is aligned with the 3D scanned scene. A coarse mapping between the Aria world frame ($W_A$) and the scanner's world frame ($W=W_S$) is initialized using the Visual Grounded Geometry Transformer (VGGT) to estimate the relative transform between the initial scanner image and Aria glasses images. This coarse alignment is then refined using Iterative Closest Point (ICP), registering the semi-dense Aria point cloud against the dense environmental scene scan.

#### 3.3. Motion Capture Ground Truth

For rigorous validation, a dedicated marker-based optical motion capture system (Qualisys) is used to acquire ground truth data in lab conditions. Retroreflective markers track 3D trajectories with millimeter accuracy, which are then processed by a skeleton solver and retargeted to the SMPL-X model with IK refinement. Temporal synchronization for ground truth data also uses hand claps, verified by visual and acoustic cues. Spatial alignment involves tracking a dedicated marker on the Aria glasses, registering its trajectory to the Aria SLAM trajectory, and refining with ICP, applying a constant offset for the body model's root.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents the EgoHTR dataset and validates its quality through quantitative and qualitative evaluations, demonstrating its utility in robotic locomotion and human mesh recovery applications.

#### 4.1. EgoHTR Dataset Overview

- **Scale and Diversity:** EgoHTR comprises 55 continuous human-terrain interaction sequences, totaling 1.37 hours of parameterized motion data (approximately 150,000 synchronized frames at 30 fps), with an average sequence length of 90 seconds. It features 8 subjects (4 male, 4 female) navigating 7 diverse indoor and outdoor scenes ranging from 25 m² to 1000 m².
- **Motion Variety:** The dataset captures a range of agile motions, including parkour, flips, crawling, jumping, bar balancing, and narrow path crossing.
- **Environmental Complexity:** Environments vary from controlled laboratory settings (e.g., parkour boxes, stepping stones) to unstructured, in-the-wild scenes such as office rooms, cluttered gym halls, and outdoor debris fields with collapsed buildings and subterranean pipes.
- **Multi-Modality:** 36 sequences (0.88 hours) include synchronized data from a secondary observer-worn Aria glasses. Most sequences also feature an external fixed-camera image stream. A dedicated marker-based motion capture ground truth test set, totaling 0.7 hours, is provided for lab conditions.

#### 4.2. Dataset Evaluation

The EgoHTR dataset was evaluated against motion capture ground truth and compared to existing state-of-the-art baselines.

- **Quantitative Accuracy:**
 - **Local Pose Estimation:** EgoHTR achieves a mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) of 73.2 mm and a Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (PA-MPJPE) of 54.3 mm. Compared to SLOPER4D, a strong baseline, EgoHTR shows a 6.2% lower MPJPE and a 2.0% lower PA-MPJPE. This improvement is observed under substantially more challenging conditions, encompassing highly dynamic motions and severe rough terrain, in contrast to SLOPER4D's focus on basic walking in structured urban environments.
 - **Robustness:** The egocentric sensing approach of EgoHTR mitigates high failure rates observed in exocentric methods (e.g., PROX, RICH, SLOPER4D) when encountering occlusions or dynamic motions, and addresses the scalability limitations of HPS to rough terrain.
 - **Global HPS Estimation:** EgoHTR is reported as the first human motion dataset to provide global HPS estimates, with a world MPJPE (W-MPJPE) of 151.3 mm, a world-aligned MPJPE (WA-MPJPE) of 66.7 mm, and a root translation error (RTE) of 0.09%.
- **Qualitative Evaluation:** EgoHTR enables accurate and physically plausible human motion reconstruction in demanding environments. It limits common reconstruction artifacts such as foot skating, ground penetration, and temporally inconsistent body motion. The integration of Aria glasses ensures accurate localization of body poses within the global scene, leading to temporally stable human-scene alignment over extended sequences. The system demonstrates robustness in challenging conditions, including rough terrain, severe self-occlusions, rapid motion, and unusual body configurations, where purely vision-based approaches often destabilize or fail. No meaningful qualitative degradation was observed between controlled indoor motion capture recordings and in-the-wild outdoor sequences, indicating strong robustness.
- **Ground Truth Verification:** Comparisons of two state-of-the-art baselines (EgoAllo and JOSH) evaluated on EgoHTR versus the dedicated MoCap ground truth showed marginal differences in local pose metrics (MPJPE, PA-MPJPE) and small differences in global trajectory tracking (RTE). This consistency suggests that EgoHTR provides a robust approximation of ground truth, supporting its use as a reliable evaluation baseline.

#### 4.3. Applications

The dataset's utility is demonstrated through two primary application areas:

- **Perceptive Locomotion from Demonstrations:**
 - **Robot Training:** EgoHTR provides terrain-aligned reference motions with per-frame foot-contact annotations, crucial for training Unitree G1 humanoid robots in simulation using PPO.
 - **Foot-Contact Reward:** The introduction of a temporal foot-contact reward accelerates policy convergence and increases success rates, particularly on sparse terrain such as stepping stones, where distance-only tracking might lead to suboptimal solutions.
 - **Precision Requirement:** An ablation study injecting Gaussian noise into reference root positions revealed that training performance tolerated noise up to approximately 0.05 m but collapsed above 0.1 m. This finding indicates that global drift in existing monocular human-scene reconstruction methods (typically exceeding 0.1 m) is too high for reliable policy training. EgoHTR's precision, achieved by anchoring the body model to Aria SLAM and dense scene scans, meets this centimeter-precise threshold, enabling policy training where alternatives fail.
 - **Hardware Deployment:** Trained motion policies were successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 robot for beam balancing and box-climbing motions.
- **Human Mesh Recovery:**
 - **Benchmarking:** The multi-modality of EgoHTR allows for comprehensive benchmarking of 4D human-scene reconstruction methods across exocentric, egocentric, and inertial paradigms.
 - **Limitations of SOTA:** Exocentric monocular approaches exhibit low success rates on EgoHTR due to vulnerability to occlusions and motion blur. State-of-the-art egocentric models, often trained on flat ground motions, demonstrate high local joint errors when attempting dynamic motions in complex environments. IMU-based posers, while immune to visual occlusions, suffer from temporal drift leading to physically impossible scene interactions.
 - **Future Potential:** The dataset can serve as a resource to fine-tune existing end-to-end models that currently lack a deep understanding of human-scene interactions, potentially bypassing the need for extensive post-hoc optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EgoHTR dataset and its accompanying pipeline represent a contribution to the fields of robotics, computer vision, and human motion analysis. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Addressing a Critical Data Gap:** EgoHTR is noted as the first dataset to provide 4D human-scene motion on rough terrain under in-the-wild conditions. This directly addresses a long-standing data scarcity for grounded, versatile human demonstrations in unconstrained environmental contexts, which is crucial for advancing humanoid robot locomotion.
- **Enabling Context-Aware Robotic Locomotion:** By offering centimeter-precise, scene-aligned human motion data, EgoHTR provides a foundational resource for training context-aware locomotion policies. The experimental findings rigorously demonstrate that high precision (below 0.1m global error) is strictly necessary for successful policy training in foothold-critical tasks, validating the necessity of such a high-fidelity dataset. This can lead to the development of more robust and adaptable humanoid robots capable of navigating complex, real-world terrains beyond controlled laboratory settings.
- **Establishing a Challenging Benchmark:** The dataset establishes a comprehensive and challenging benchmark for evaluating state-of-the-art human pose and shape (HPS) estimation and 4D human-scene reconstruction methods. The inclusion of diverse, challenging scenarios (rough terrain, occlusions, rapid motion, confined spaces) allows for a more rigorous assessment of method robustness and accuracy, highlighting current limitations in existing approaches.
- **Facilitating Community-Driven Research:** The open-sourcing of the capture and reconstruction pipeline is intended to foster community-driven dataset extensions. This design allows for continuous integration of new sequences, multi-person capture, and text annotations, potentially leading to a larger, more diverse dataset capable of training foundation models for robot learning.
- **Advancing Human Mesh Recovery and Motion Synthesis:** EgoHTR's multi-modal, high-resolution data is valuable for improving data-driven human mesh recovery, particularly in complex human-scene interactions. Furthermore, it provides the local geometric precision needed to advance human motion synthesis models, enabling them to generate more physically plausible and contextually aware motions in unstructured environments.

In conclusion, EgoHTR provides a foundation for the development of more intelligent, perceptive, and context-aware embodied agents and marks a step towards bridging the gap between human demonstrations and robust robot learning in challenging real-world environments.
