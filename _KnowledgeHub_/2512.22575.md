# ParaMaP: Parallel Mapping and Collision-free Motion Planning for Reactive Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ParaMaP is an integrated framework enabling real-time, collision-free robot manipulation in dynamic environments through parallel GPU-accelerated mapping and sampling-based model predictive control. The system leverages a novel 'gather-then-transform' strategy for efficient Euclidean Distance Field construction and a Lie-algebra-based pose error metric, achieving planning update rates exceeding 150 Hz and significantly lower final pose errors (2.778 mm position, 0.049 rad orientation) compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Constructing dense distance fields (e.g., ESDF) for environment representation is computationally expensive, creating a bottleneck for real-time robot manipulation.
- Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) often struggles in unknown and dynamically changing environments without efficient online mapping, leading to limited real-time applicability.
- Depth sensors frequently perceive parts of the robot's own body, leading to false self-collision detections in environment maps and erroneous collision avoidance maneuvers.

## Method

- Developed a parallelized mapping module using a ==voxel-centric occupancy update== scheme and a ==gather-then-transform strategy== for efficient Euclidean Distance Field (EDF) construction, alongside a ==robot-masked update mechanism==.
- Formulated a Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) planner that integrates ==Lie-algebra-based pose error== on SE(3) and distance-field-based collision costs for robust, reactive motion planning.
- Implemented the entire mapping and planning pipeline on the GPU using CUDA for full parallelization, enabling high-frequency replanning required for dynamic environments.

## Results

- Achieved substantially lower total mapping times compared to a GIE-Mapping baseline on public datasets, with the Euclidean Distance Transform (EDT) showing the most significant speedup due to the new strategy.
- Demonstrated superior motion planning performance in simulations, with an average planning time of **5.322ms**, shorter motion times (**12.412s**), and smaller final pose errors (**2.778mm** position, **0.049rad** orientation) compared to STORM and RRTConnect.
- The integrated ParaMaP pipeline operated at update rates exceeding **150Hz**, enabling real-time, reactive collision avoidance with both static and dynamic obstacles in cluttered simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'gather-then-transform' strategy for EDT construction on the GPU significantly reduces global memory traffic and latency, allowing for high-resolution distance fields at high update rates.
> - A robot-masked update mechanism effectively prevents false self-collision detections by excluding the robot's own geometry from the environment map, enhancing collision avoidance reliability.
> - Incorporating a Lie-algebra-based pose error metric on SE(3) provides a geometrically consistent and accurate objective for the SMPC planner, leading to faster convergence and more precise goal reaching.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is increasingly utilized in both industrial and service sectors, often operating in structured environments with predefined tasks. However, the expansion of robotics into dynamic and unstructured environments, requiring interactive manipulation and human-robot collaboration, necessitates advancements in real-time perception, environment representation, and motion planning.

Traditional environment representation methods, such as occupancy grids, provide basic collision checking but lack continuous distance information vital for optimization-based planning. More sophisticated methods like Truncated Signed Distance Functions (TSDF) and Euclidean Signed Distance Functions (ESDF) offer dense, continuous distance fields. However, their construction and maintenance are computationally intensive, presenting a bottleneck for real-time applications, particularly on resource-limited platforms. Efforts to accelerate these methods using GPU-based parallel computing exist, but optimizing their efficiency remains an ongoing area of research.

In motion planning, sampling-based algorithms, exemplified by the Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL), can find feasible paths but may not generate smooth, dynamically executable trajectories. Gradient-based optimization methods address smoothness and dynamic feasibility by formulating planning as a continuous optimization problem. Nevertheless, these methods can encounter difficulties with objective functions containing discontinuities, such as collision costs. Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) approaches leverage stochastic trajectory sampling and parallel computation to achieve robust real-time performance without relying on explicit gradients. However, many existing SMPC applications have been validated primarily in structured environments with known or pre-computed obstacle geometries, limiting their direct applicability in unknown and dynamically changing settings. Furthermore, integrating the robot's own body into the perception stream can lead to false self-collision detections, a challenge that requires specific mitigation strategies.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing an integrated framework that combines an efficient, parallelized mapping system with a sampling-based model predictive control planner. It aims to bridge the gap between real-time, high-resolution environment representation and reactive, collision-free motion planning in unknown and dynamic environments, leveraging GPU acceleration for the entire pipeline. The paper specifically contributes to improving the computational efficiency of distance field construction and enhancing the robustness and accuracy of sampling-based motion planning in scenarios requiring continuous perception updates and online replanning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the ongoing challenge of achieving real-time, collision-free motion planning for robotic manipulation in environments that are initially unknown or dynamically changing. Such scenarios require continuous perception updates and frequent online replanning, which often strain computational resources and introduce latency. The paper identifies several specific challenges:

1. **Computational Bottleneck of Environment Representation:** Constructing dense distance fields like ESDF, crucial for optimization-based planning, is computationally expensive. Traditional ray-casting methods for occupancy grid updates are difficult to parallelize efficiently due to potential I/O conflicts. Existing GPU-accelerated methods, while improving, still face overheads from memory management (e.g., tensor transpositions).
2. **Limitations of Sampling-based MPC in Unknown Environments:** While SMPC offers robust real-time performance without explicit gradients, its application has predominantly been in structured environments where obstacle information is known or pre-computed. Adapting SMPC for environments with continuous perception updates and dynamic obstacles requires tight integration with an efficient online mapping system.
3. **False Self-Collision Detections:** Depth sensors inevitably perceive parts of the robot's own body. If this geometry is fused into the environment map, it can be mistakenly treated as an external obstacle, leading to erroneous collision avoidance maneuvers. Existing remedies like geometry-level subtraction can introduce regions of missing depth, posing further challenges for reliable environment representation.
4. **Geometrically Inconsistent Pose Tracking:** In SMPC frameworks, the formulation of pose error can impact convergence speed and accuracy. A geometrically consistent metric for both translational and rotational deviations on the Lie group SE(3) is crucial for accurate goal reaching.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Develop a Novel Sampling-based Motion Planning Formulation:** Create a formulation for robotic manipulation that effectively integrates distance-field-based collision costs with a Lie-algebra-based pose tracking objective on SE(3) within a stochastic MPC framework. This aims to enable safe and reactive motion generation in environments with unknown obstacles.
2. **Incorporate a Geometrically Consistent Pose Error Metric:** Introduce a Lie-algebra-based pose error metric defined on SE(3) into the SMPC formulation. The objective is to provide a unified and geometrically consistent treatment of rotational and translational deviations, thereby enabling fast and accurate convergence to target poses under practical execution constraints.
3. **Design an Efficient Parallel Mapping and Euclidean Distance Field Construction Method:** Develop a method based on a "gather-then-transform" strategy and robot-masked updates. This aims to support low-latency distance queries and reliable collision avoidance during online replanning by avoiding expensive tensor permutations and preventing false self-collision detections.
4. **Achieve Real-time and High-frequency Replanning:** Implement the entire mapping and planning pipeline on the GPU using a unified CUDA-based architecture. The goal is to exploit GPU parallelism to support real-time, high-frequency replanning necessary for reactive robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named ParaMaP, integrates parallel mapping and motion planning, implementing the entire pipeline on a GPU using CUDA to achieve high-frequency replanning.

#### 3.1. Parallel Occupancy Grid Map and Euclidean Distance Field Construction

The mapping module focuses on constructing a dense distance-field-based environment representation with low latency.

##### 3.1.1. Occupancy Grid Map Construction

The method adopts a **voxel-centric occupancy update scheme**, a departure from traditional ray-casting approaches. Instead of projecting rays from the sensor, each 3D voxel's volume is projected onto the sensor measurement domain (e.g., a 2D image plane for cameras or spherical coordinates for LiDAR). This voxel-centric formulation enables independent occupancy evaluation for each voxel, preventing I/O conflicts common in parallel ray-casting. Voxels are classified as occupied, free, or unknown based on how their projected depth compares to the measured depth.
A crucial aspect for robotic manipulation is the **robot-masked update mechanism**. During sensor fusion, any depth returns or voxels that fall inside the robot's simplified sphere model are explicitly discarded or reset to a non-occupied state. This prevents the robot's own geometry from being integrated into the occupancy map, thereby avoiding false self-collision detections during planning.

##### 3.1.2. Euclidean Distance Field Construction

Following the occupancy grid map construction, a continuous Euclidean Distance Field (EDF) is generated. This is achieved using the classical **three-pass separable 3D EDT** (Euclidean Distance Transform), applying the 1D Felzenszwalb–Huttenlocher (FH) squared distance transform sequentially along the y-, x-, and z-axes.
The primary innovation in this stage is the **gather-then-transform strategy**. Unlike some prior work that uses explicit tensor permutations to ensure data contiguity for FH transforms, ParaMaP executes FH transforms directly on the native XYZ memory layout. For the x-pass (and similarly for the z-pass), each CUDA thread gathers its 1D line of data (which is stride-separated in global memory) into a compact, per-thread shared-memory buffer. The FH transform is then performed locally within this contiguous buffer. This strategy eliminates significant global-memory traffic associated with full-grid permutations, structuring memory accesses for coalesced global-memory reads and minimizing shared-memory usage. The distance field is also recomputed only within a local volume, further reducing computational overhead.

#### 3.2. Motion Planning with Sampling-Based Model Predictive Control (SMPC)

The motion planning problem is formulated as a time-discretized stochastic optimization problem, aiming to compute a feasible trajectory from an initial joint configuration to a desired end-effector pose while respecting kinematic limits, avoiding collisions, and enabling real-time replanning.

##### 3.2.1. Problem Formulation

The objective is to find an optimal control sequence $U = \{u_0..., u_{H-1}\}$ that minimizes an expected finite-horizon cost $J(U)$. This cost comprises a terminal cost $ℓ_f$ that penalizes deviation from the goal end-effector pose at the horizon $H$, and a running cost $ℓ$ accumulated over intermediate steps. The running cost accounts for collision avoidance, smoothness, dynamic feasibility, and other objectives. The optimization is subject to robot dynamics, forward kinematics, joint limits, and collision-free constraints with both the environment ($Q_{free}^{env}$) and itself ($Q_{free}^{self}$). The process noise $\epsilon_k$ in the robot dynamics makes the problem stochastic.

##### 3.2.2. Cost Function Construction

The overall objective function integrates various components:

- **Pose Tracking Cost:** A **Lie-algebra-based error metric defined directly on the Lie group SE(3)** is employed. The relative transformation from the goal frame to the current end-effector frame is transformed into a 6-D pose error vector $\xi(k)$ using the matrix logarithm. This provides a geometrically consistent measure of pose discrepancy, integrating both rotational and translational errors with a positive definite weighting matrix $Q_k$.
- **Collision Avoidance Cost:** The robot's geometry is approximated by a set of spheres. Distances to environment obstacles are obtained from the ESDF (ϕ) (as $d_{env_{i,k}} = ϕ(p_{i,k}) - r_i$), and self-collision distances are calculated between sphere pairs. A smooth penalty is activated when distances fall below a predefined activation distance $d_{act}$. Since the mapping module uses a robot-masked distance field, the SMPC framework only requires point-wise distance queries from the cleaned EDF, aligning with the gradient-free nature of SMPC.
- **Joint Limit Cost:** Penalties are applied if joint positions, velocities, or accelerations exceed slightly tightened admissible ranges, ensuring smooth transitions near boundaries.
- **Smoothness Cost:** Penalizes excessive joint accelerations to promote smooth joint-space motion.
- **Null-Space Regularization Cost:** For redundant manipulators (e.g., 7-DoF), this term biases the robot toward a preferred reference configuration, helping to avoid kinematic singularities and reduce self-collision risks without affecting the end-effector pose.
- **Terminal Cost:** Similar to the pose tracking cost, this is applied at the end of the prediction horizon ($k=H$) using the Lie-algebraic pose error $\xi(H)$ and a terminal weighting matrix $Q_H$ to ensure accurate convergence.

##### 3.2.3. Trajectory Optimization Process

The constrained optimal control problem is transformed into an unconstrained optimization problem by incorporating hard constraints as penalty terms in the cost function. This is solved using an SMPC framework:

1. **Sampling:** At each time step, $M$ candidate control sequences $U^{(m)}$ are sampled by perturbing a nominal control sequence $\bar{U}$ with zero-mean noise $\epsilon^{(m)}_k$.
2. **Rollout:** For each sampled sequence, the robot dynamics are simulated over the prediction horizon to generate a candidate state trajectory. All running costs and the terminal cost are evaluated, yielding a total trajectory cost $S^{(m)}$.
3. **Weighting:** A soft weighting scheme is applied. Each trajectory is assigned an importance weight $w^{(m)}$ based on its cost $S^{(m)}$, normalized by the minimum cost $S_{min}$ in the batch and a temperature parameter $\lambda$.
4. **Update:** The optimal control sequence $U^{\star}$ is refined as a weighted average of the sampled disturbances added to the nominal sequence.
5. **Receding Horizon:** Following the MPC principle, only the first control input $u^{\star}_0$ of the optimized sequence is executed. The process then repeats at the next time step with updated state feedback.

The sampling and rollout steps are mutually independent and designed for full parallelization on the GPU, contributing to the framework's efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of ParaMaP was validated through extensive simulations and real-world experiments on a 7-DoF robotic manipulator. All experiments were conducted on a system equipped with an Intel i9-10900K CPU and an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Comparisons

##### 4.1.1. Mapping Evaluation

The proposed mapping algorithm was benchmarked against GIE-Mapping on two public datasets: the Flat Dataset (depth images) and the Dynablox Dataset (LiDAR point clouds).

- **Flat Dataset:** Across voxel sizes of 0.05 m, 0.10 m, and 0.20 m, ParaMaP consistently achieved lower total mapping times (OGM + EDT) compared to GIE-Mapping. This improvement was primarily attributed to a substantially faster EDT update, with a minor reduction in OGM computation time.
- **Dynablox Dataset:** Similar trends were observed with LiDAR data, using a larger EDT volume (20 m × 20 m × 6 m). ParaMaP showed lower total mapping times for voxel sizes of 0.10 m, 0.20 m, and 0.40 m. The performance gain resulted from reduced computation in both OGM and EDT, with the EDT module contributing the majority of the improvement. The proposed EDT update remained efficient even at fine voxel resolutions.

These results indicate that ParaMaP’s gather-then-transform strategy for EDT construction effectively reduces global-memory movement and improves computational efficiency, making it suitable for high-resolution maps and fast update rates required in dynamic scenes.

##### 4.1.2. Motion Planning Evaluation

The proposed SMPC planner was compared against STORM and RRTConnect (from OMPL via MoveIt) in a Gazebo simulation with known obstacles. Both ParaMaP and STORM used 512 samples per replanning iteration. Metrics included planning time, motion time, path length, and final pose error (position and orientation).

- **Planning Time:** ParaMaP demonstrated the shortest average planning time at 5.322 ms per iteration, significantly outperforming STORM (18.875 ms) and RRTConnect (61.202 ms). This was attributed to its fully parallelized, GPU-centric implementation of sampling, rollout, and cost evaluation.
- **Motion Metrics:** ParaMaP produced shorter joint-space path lengths (12.341 rad) and shorter motion times (12.412 s) compared to STORM (15.316 rad, 16.580 s) and RRTConnect (13.153 rad, 13.720 s).
- **Pose Error:** ParaMaP achieved the smallest final pose errors at convergence: 2.778 mm for position and 0.049 rad for orientation. This was superior to STORM (9.299 mm, 0.327 rad) and RRTConnect (3.674 mm, 0.104 rad).

These findings suggest that the Lie-algebra-based pose error formulation employed in ParaMaP provides a geometrically consistent error metric on SE(3), leading to faster convergence and more accurate goal reaching within the SMPC framework.

##### 4.1.3. Integrated System Evaluation (Simulation)

An integrated evaluation of the complete ParaMaP pipeline (online mapping + motion planning) was performed in a cluttered Gazebo environment with static and dynamic obstacles. The robot navigated a multi-stage task involving movement between two goal poses and reacting to a manually controlled dynamic obstacle.

- **Reliable Operation:** The robot successfully planned and executed collision-free motions, responding to initially unknown static obstacles and dynamically avoiding the moving obstacle. The robot-masked distance field and reactive SMPC planner enabled reliable task execution.
- **System-level Runtime:** The SMPC planner averaged 5.653 ms per replanning iteration, while the mapping update averaged 0.892 ms per iteration. This resulted in a combined mapping and planning pipeline operating at update rates exceeding 150 Hz.

This demonstrated the framework's capability for prompt and safe responses to dynamic obstacles.

#### 4.2. Real-world Experiments

Real-world experiments were conducted on a 7-DoF Flexiv Rizon 4 robotic manipulator equipped with an Intel RealSense D435i depth camera.

- **Static Obstacle Avoidance:** In scenarios with previously unknown static obstacles, the robot successfully reached target end-effector poses based on online perception and mapping.
- **Dynamic Obstacle Reaction:** When a human arm was introduced as a dynamic obstacle, the robot reactively updated its map and replanned its motion online to avoid collisions. It subsequently resumed motion toward the target pose after the human arm was removed.

These real-world results confirm the practical utility and robustness of ParaMaP for integrated online perception, mapping, and motion planning in environments with both static and dynamic elements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ParaMaP framework offers advancements in the field of reactive robot manipulation by addressing critical challenges in real-time performance, collision avoidance, and accurate goal reaching in unknown and dynamic environments. The significance of this work can be understood through its technical contributions and broader implications:

**Technical Contributions:**

- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** The "gather-then-transform" strategy for Euclidean Distance Field construction, integrated with a voxel-centric occupancy update, significantly reduces the latency of environment mapping. By avoiding explicit tensor transpositions and exploiting GPU parallelism, the system achieves update rates that enable high-frequency replanning, overcoming a common bottleneck in real-time systems.
- **Improved Safety and Robustness:** The robot-masked distance field update mechanism is a practical solution to prevent false self-collision detections arising from self-observation by depth sensors. This enhances the reliability of collision avoidance, allowing the robot to operate more safely in close proximity to itself and the environment.
- **Accurate and Consistent Pose Tracking:** The incorporation of a Lie-algebra-based pose error formulation on SE(3) provides a geometrically consistent objective for the SMPC planner. This leads to improved convergence speed and accuracy in reaching desired end-effector poses, ensuring precise manipulation capabilities.
- **Integrated Real-time Operation:** The unified CUDA-based architecture for the entire mapping and planning pipeline demonstrates the feasibility of combining computationally intensive perception and control tasks into a single, high-performance system capable of operating at over 150 Hz.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Expansion of Robotic Applications:** The ability of robots to operate safely and reactively in unstructured, dynamic, and initially unknown environments opens new avenues for deployment in diverse fields. This includes more flexible manufacturing and assembly (e.g., 3C assembly), dynamic logistics and material handling (e.g., POU material handling), and advanced service robotics (e.g., household assistance).
- **Facilitation of Human-Robot Collaboration:** By enabling robots to perceive and react to human presence and motion in real-time, the framework can enhance the safety and fluency of human-robot collaboration scenarios, moving beyond caged or strictly partitioned workspaces.
- **Enabling Adaptive Autonomy:** The framework provides a robust foundation for robots to autonomously adapt their behavior in response to unforeseen environmental changes, reducing the need for extensive pre-programming or manual intervention. This is crucial for developing more intelligent and versatile robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The methods developed, particularly in parallel mapping and geometrically consistent SMPC, offer a basis for further research into areas such as uncertainty-aware planning, long-horizon prediction, and multi-robot coordination in dynamic settings.

In conclusion, ParaMaP contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in reactive robot manipulation by delivering a computationally efficient and robust framework for real-time online perception, environment representation, and motion planning.
