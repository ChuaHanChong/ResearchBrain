# ParaMaP: Parallel Mapping and Collision-free Motion Planning for Reactive Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ParaMaP is an integrated framework enabling real-time, collision-free robot manipulation in dynamic environments through parallel GPU-accelerated mapping and sampling-based model predictive control. The system leverages a novel 'gather-then-transform' strategy for efficient Euclidean Distance Field construction and a Lie-algebra-based pose error metric, achieving planning update rates exceeding 150 Hz and significantly lower final pose errors (2.778 mm position, 0.049 rad orientation) compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Constructing dense distance fields (e.g., ESDF) for environment representation is computationally expensive, creating a bottleneck for real-time robot manipulation.
- Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) often struggles in unknown and dynamically changing environments without efficient online mapping, leading to limited real-time applicability.
- Depth sensors frequently perceive parts of the robot's own body, leading to false self-collision detections in environment maps and erroneous collision avoidance maneuvers.

## Method

- Developed a parallelized mapping module using a voxel-centric occupancy update scheme and a 'gather-then-transform' strategy for efficient Euclidean Distance Field (EDF) construction, alongside a robot-masked update mechanism.
- Formulated a Sampling-based ==Model Predictive Control== (SMPC) planner that integrates Lie-algebra-based pose error on SE(3) and distance-field-based collision costs for robust, reactive motion planning.
- Implemented the entire mapping and planning pipeline on the GPU using CUDA for full parallelization, enabling high-frequency replanning required for dynamic environments.

## Results

- Achieved substantially lower total mapping times compared to a GIE-Mapping baseline on public datasets, with the Euclidean Distance Transform (EDT) showing the most significant speedup due to the new strategy.
- Demonstrated superior motion planning performance in simulations, with an average planning time of **5.322** ms, shorter motion times (**12.412** s), and smaller final pose errors (**2.778** mm position, **0.049** rad orientation) compared to STORM and RRTConnect.
- The integrated ParaMaP pipeline operated at update rates exceeding 150 Hz, enabling real-time, reactive collision avoidance with both static and dynamic obstacles in cluttered simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'gather-then-transform' strategy for EDT construction on the GPU significantly reduces global memory traffic and latency, allowing for high-resolution distance fields at high update rates.
> - A robot-masked update mechanism effectively prevents false self-collision detections by excluding the robot's own geometry from the environment map, enhancing collision avoidance reliability.
> - Incorporating a Lie-algebra-based pose error metric on SE(3) provides a geometrically consistent and accurate objective for the SMPC planner, leading to faster convergence and more precise goal reaching.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Technical Contributions

- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** The "gather-then-transform" strategy for Euclidean Distance Field construction, integrated with a voxel-centric occupancy update, significantly reduces the latency of environment mapping. By avoiding explicit tensor transpositions and exploiting GPU parallelism, the system achieves update rates that enable high-frequency replanning, overcoming a common bottleneck in real-time systems.
- **Improved Safety and Robustness:** The robot-masked distance field update mechanism is a practical solution to prevent false self-collision detections arising from self-observation by depth sensors. This enhances the reliability of collision avoidance, allowing the robot to operate more safely in close proximity to itself and the environment.
- **Accurate and Consistent Pose Tracking:** The incorporation of a Lie-algebra-based pose error formulation on SE(3) provides a geometrically consistent objective for the SMPC planner. This leads to improved convergence speed and accuracy in reaching desired end-effector poses, ensuring precise manipulation capabilities.
- **Integrated Real-time Operation:** The unified CUDA-based architecture for the entire mapping and planning pipeline demonstrates the feasibility of combining computationally intensive perception and control tasks into a single, high-performance system capable of operating at over 150 Hz.

### 2. Potential Impact

- **Expansion of Robotic Applications:** The ability of robots to operate safely and reactively in unstructured, dynamic, and initially unknown environments opens new avenues for deployment in diverse fields. This includes more flexible manufacturing and assembly (e.g., 3C assembly), dynamic logistics and material handling (e.g., POU material handling), and advanced service robotics (e.g., household assistance).
- **Facilitation of Human-Robot Collaboration:** By enabling robots to perceive and react to human presence and motion in real-time, the framework can enhance the safety and fluency of human-robot collaboration scenarios, moving beyond caged or strictly partitioned workspaces.
- **Enabling Adaptive Autonomy:** The framework provides a robust foundation for robots to autonomously adapt their behavior in response to unforeseen environmental changes, reducing the need for extensive pre-programming or manual intervention. This is crucial for developing more intelligent and versatile robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The methods developed, particularly in parallel mapping and geometrically consistent SMPC, offer a basis for further research into areas such as uncertainty-aware planning, long-horizon prediction, and multi-robot coordination in dynamic settings.

In conclusion, ParaMaP contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in reactive robot manipulation by delivering a computationally efficient and robust framework for real-time online perception, environment representation, and motion planning.
