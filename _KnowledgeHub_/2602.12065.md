# Scene2Demo: Self-Evolving Embodied Data Generation via Object-Action Graph

> [!summary] Summary
> SCENE2DEMO turns a single real-world RGB image plus a user query into executable robot demonstration data. A five-module workflow grounded in an object-action graph produces scene-grounded task descriptions, symbolic subtasks, BDDL configs, object-size fixes, and primitive action flows; two VLM feedback agents then inspect multi-view rollouts and revise failed flows. It reaches 71.6% execution success over 102 generated scene-task pairs and supports downstream behavior cloning.

## Problem

- Generative video models produce physically inconsistent rollouts and fail to generalize physical laws, so they are unreliable substrates for action-conditioned robot interaction data.
- Prior automated generators like RoboGen and GenSim2 rely on unconstrained LLM decomposition, yielding monolithic, inspectable-only-as-a-whole pipelines with unstable, varying subtask structures across repeated runs.
- Existing pipelines plan long-horizon tasks open-loop, ignoring accumulated navigation and placement errors, and lack a mechanism to diagnose and repair failed or incomplete executions.

## Method

- ==Object-action graph== formalism: nodes are object-action-time configurations, edges are intra-subtask primitive transitions or inter-subtask transfer links, giving a compositional-reachability condition for long-horizon plans.
- Five role-specialized modules: task understanding, symbolically constrained task decomposition, ==BDDL==-style init/goal config generation, gripper-driven object-size adjustment, and action-flow plus action-transfer planning over a primitive action library.
- Self-evolution loop: a **Robotic Safety Inspector** emits step-wise critiques from multi-view frames, and a **Robotic Task Supervisor** aggregates them with revision history to apply sequence modification or parameter adjustment.

## Results

- Across 102 automatically generated scene-task pairs from 34 real scenes, execution success reaches **71.6%** overall: 66.7% on articulated open/close and 74.2% on rigid pick/place.
- Self-evolution lifts long-horizon complete-task success from **0/0/40/0% to 40/30/70/70%** on four tasks, beating GenSim2 (0/5/55/0%) and RoboGen (all 0%).
- Behavior cloning on 100 generated demonstrations reaches **96.0%** (24/25) on Open Refrigerator and **92.0%** on Pick Up Glass; multi-view feedback beats single-view, and look-back window 1 beats 2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining LLM decomposition with symbolic task definitions buys reproducibility: identical subtask sequences across repeated runs, where unconstrained planners emit varying subtask counts.
> - Closed-loop visual critique pays off mostly in later subtasks, where navigation and placement errors accumulate; early opening and pickup steps usually succeed on the first plan.
> - More context is not free: widening the look-back window degraded complete-task success while inflating VLM input length and latency, an argument for minimal temporal context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in SCENE2DEMO addresses the significant challenge of acquiring large quantities of executable, physically valid, and structured interaction data for embodied robot learning. The manual collection of such data is often prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, thereby limiting progress in areas like imitation learning, reinforcement learning from demonstrations, and world model training.

The paper situates its contributions within several existing research areas:

- **Generative Video Models and Physics Simulators:** While generative video models have advanced in visual prediction and interactive scene synthesis, they often struggle with physical consistency, making them less reliable for robot interaction data. Physics simulators like iGibson, ThreeDWorld, OmniGibson, and IsaacGym, which provide explicit states, collision handling, and action-conditioned transitions, are identified as a more direct and suitable substrate for generating such data. SCENE2DEMO operates within this simulation-based paradigm.
- **Automated Scene, Task, and Demonstration Generation:** Previous efforts have explored using foundation models to automate the generation of robot scenes, tasks, and data. Systems like RoboGen and GenSim2 have made strides in automated robot data generation, often leveraging multimodal and reasoning Large Language Models (LLMs). SCENE2DEMO builds upon these efforts but distinguishes itself by separating the generation process into inspectable modules and by imposing constraints on task decomposition using symbolic definitions, rather than solely relying on unconstrained LLM outputs. Furthermore, it leverages existing interactive scene reconstruction pipelines, such as Digital Cousins, as its simulation substrate.
- **LLM/VLM Agents and Workflow-Based Reasoning:** The framework draws inspiration from agentic workflows that combine reasoning, acting, feedback, and memory, exemplified by works like ReAct, Reflexion, and AutoGen. In robotics, approaches like SayCan, Code-as-Policies, and various LLM/VLM agent frameworks for manipulation have shown the utility of language-guided planning. SCENE2DEMO adopts this modular, workflow-based perspective, grounding it specifically in offline embodied data generation, where a shared primitive action library serves as the executable interface.
- **Self-Evolution, Failure Correction, and Policy Learning:** The concept of self-evolution in SCENE2DEMO aligns with feedback-driven robot execution and VLM-guided failure correction research, including Inner Monologue and VLM-based systems for detecting, explaining, or correcting manipulation failures. The system’s ultimate goal of generating successful demonstrations connects it to existing demonstration-generation systems like MimicGen and MoMaGen, as well as behavior cloning and visuomotor policy learning methods such as RT-1 and Diffusion Policy.

In summary, SCENE2DEMO differentiates itself by integrating a self-evolving framework, a structured object-action graph for robust task generation, and a visual-feedback-driven self-evolution mechanism within an offline embodied data generation pipeline. This approach aims to provide stable, executable, and high-quality data for downstream robot learning, addressing limitations of prior unconstrained or less robust generation methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an automated and self-evolving system capable of generating executable robot task data from a single real-world RGB image and a user query. This system, named SCENE2DEMO, aims to overcome the significant bottlenecks associated with manual data collection for embodied robot learning, which often suffers from high cost, lack of scalability, and difficulty in ensuring physical validity and structure.

The core motivation stems from the recognition that embodied robot learning, encompassing areas like imitation learning, reinforcement learning from demonstrations, and world model training, critically depends on abundant, high-quality interaction data. Without an efficient means to generate such data, the development and deployment of robust robot policies are severely hindered.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Automated Data Production:** To build an automated engine that can take a real-world scene image and a user task query (e.g., "pick up the glass") and automatically produce a comprehensive set of outputs. These outputs include:
 - An interactive simulated scene, reconstructed to reflect the real-world observation.
 - Executable task configurations, defining the initial and goal states for the robot.
 - Primitive action flows, outlining the sequence of low-level robot actions required to complete a task.
 - Multi-view execution videos, providing visual records of the robot's performance.
 - Offline robot-learning datasets, structured observation-action pairs suitable for training.
- **Ensuring Executability and Physical Validity:** Unlike general generative video models that may produce physically inconsistent results, the system prioritizes generating data that is both executable by a robot in a simulated environment and physically plausible. This is achieved by operating within a physics-based simulation substrate.
- **Improving Task Planning Stability and Executability:** A significant motivation is to enhance the reliability of long-horizon task planning. Traditional approaches that rely solely on unconstrained LLMs for task decomposition can lead to inconsistent or non-executable plans. SCENE2DEMO aims to mitigate this by formulating task generation through an "object-action graph," which introduces symbolic task definitions and constraints on subtask decomposition and inter-task transfers. This structured approach is intended to produce stable, parseable, and consistent task units essential for robotic execution.
- **Self-Correction of Execution Failures:** Recognizing that initial plans may fail due to various imperfections (e.g., scene reconstruction errors, object-size mismatches, navigation offsets, or accumulated errors in long-horizon tasks), a crucial objective is to incorporate a feedback-driven "self-evolution" mechanism. This mechanism allows the system to inspect visual rollouts, diagnose failures, and revise action flows autonomously, thereby improving the overall success rate of generated demonstrations.
- **Supporting Downstream Policy Learning:** Ultimately, the generated data must be suitable for training robot policies. A key objective is to validate that the autonomously generated and refined demonstrations can effectively support standard downstream policy learning methods, such as behavior cloning, demonstrating the practical value of the framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SCENE2DEMO is structured as a self-evolving framework that converts a real-world RGB image and a user query into executable embodied task data through a multi-module workflow. The methodology encompasses three main stages: scene-conditioned simulation substrate generation, graph-based task generation, and self-evolution via agentic feedback.

#### 3.1. Scene-Conditioned Simulation Substrate

The initial step involves reconstructing an interactive simulated scene from a single real-world RGB observation. SCENE2DEMO leverages an existing ACDC-based reconstruction pipeline (following Digital Cousins). This pipeline processes the input image ($X_0$) to generate a simulated initial state ($S_0$) that includes object labels, masks, point clouds, and depth estimations. It then matches these real-world elements to virtual assets from a dataset like BEHAVIOR-1K using models such as CLIP and DINOv2, ensuring the simulated environment is interactive and physically grounded within a platform like OmniGibson. This substrate provides collision handling, explicit states, and action-conditioned transitions.

#### 3.2. Graph-Based Task Generation

This stage translates the user query into a structured, executable task sequence using a workflow of five specialized modules, underpinned by an object-action graph formalism.

- **Object-Action Graph and Symbolic Task Interface:** The core abstraction is an object-action graph where nodes represent object-action-time configurations and edges denote either primitive action transitions within a subtask (intra-task) or transfer links between consecutive subtasks (inter-task).
 - **Object Roles:** For each "simple task," objects are assigned roles: a target object ($o_t$), a first support object ($o_{s1}$) for preconditions, and a second support object ($o_{s2}$) for desired post-execution relations.
 - **Simple Task Definition:** A simple task ($T$) is defined by its target object, support objects, and symbolic initial ($s_{init}$) and goal ($s_{goal}$) states (e.g., `ontop(glass 0, table 0)`).
 - **Complex Long-Horizon Task Definition:** A complex task is an ordered sequence of simple tasks ($T = \{T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_K\}$). This formalization constrains the decomposition process, ensuring stability and parseability.

- **Workflow Modules:**
 1. **Module 1: Task Understanding:** An LLM/VLM expands the user query into a scene-grounded task description, replacing generic terms with specific scene object IDs (e.g., "cup" becomes "glass_0").
 2. **Module 2: Task Decomposition:** The LLM decomposes the expanded task into a sequence of simple subtasks, adhering to the symbolic definitions of simple and complex tasks. This module also assigns object roles for each subtask.
 3. **Module 3: BDDL Configuration Generation:** For each simple subtask, the LLM generates BDDL-style predicates that define its precise initial ($s_{init}$) and goal ($s_{goal}$) conditions for simulation.
 4. **Module 4: Object Size Adjustment:** To ensure physical feasibility with the robot's gripper, the system dynamically adjusts the scale of manipulable objects if their reconstructed size is incompatible. Fixed fixtures are not scaled, while manipulable items are scaled to fit a target gripper width.
 5. **Module 5: Action Flow and Action Transfer Planning:** This module generates executable primitive-action programs.
 - **Intra-task Action Flow:** For each simple task ($T_k$), an action flow ($\pi(T_k) = \{a_1(\theta_1|T_k), \ldots, a_n(\theta_n|T_k)\}$) is planned from a library of 21 atomic primitives (e.g., NAVIGATE, GRASP, ARTICULATE OPEN). These primitives are parameterized by task-conditioned values.
 - **Inter-task Action Transfer:** For complex tasks, this component ensures logical connectivity between consecutive subtasks ($T_k$ and $T_{k+1}$). An "action transfer" ($e_k$) is defined to check if the terminal state of $T_k$ can serve as a valid initialization for $T_{k+1}$, modeled as a probabilistic compatibility check.
 - **Compositional Reachability:** The methodology includes a theoretical proposition (Proposition 1) that outlines sufficient conditions for a long-horizon task to be compositionally reachable within the scene-specific object-action graph, given valid task decomposition and compatible boundary conditions between subtasks.

#### 3.3. Self-Evolution for Task Progression

This stage provides a closed-loop feedback mechanism to refine failed or incomplete subtasks, as initial plans may fail due to inherent uncertainties.

- **Robotic Safety Inspector (Module 6):**
 - During execution of an action flow ($\hat{\pi}_{\tau}(T_k)$) in simulation, multi-view video frames (Global, Head, Wrist cameras) are collected, with a look-back window (e.g., one previous action step).
 - A VLM analyzes these visual rollouts locally, performing motion verification, state contrast, and anomaly detection to generate step-wise critiques ($m_{\tau,j}$).
- **Robotic Task Supervisor (Module 7):**
 - This agent aggregates the current action flow, the sequence of step-wise critiques, the complete visual rollout, the task context, and a history of previous evolution attempts.
 - Using this information, it performs root cause analysis and revises the action flow ($\hat{\pi}_{\tau+1}(T_k)$). Revisions can involve sequence modification (e.g., adding/removing primitives), parameter adjustment (e.g., modifying distances, angles), or navigation waypoint changes.
 - The supervisor ensures that revisions adhere to the primitive action library. The simulator is reset to the subtask's initial state before each retry to ensure consistent evaluation. This iterative process continues until the subtask goal is met or a maximum iteration limit is reached.
 - The history helps avoid repeatedly trying previously failed configurations.

#### 3.4. Expert Demonstrations Generation

Upon successful execution, SCENE2DEMO stores the generated data, including multi-view execution videos and offline robot-learning datasets (e.g., HDF5 files containing point clouds and robot actions), which can then be used for downstream policy learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SCENE2DEMO focused on its capabilities as an automated embodied data generation engine, specifically assessing the executability of generated tasks, the impact of self-evolution, comparative performance against baselines, and the utility of generated data for downstream policy learning.

- **Executability of Primitive Scene-Task Pairs:**
 - SCENE2DEMO was evaluated on 102 automatically generated primitive scene-task pairs, reconstructed from 34 diverse real-world scenes.
 - It achieved an overall execution success rate (SR) of 71.6%.
 - For articulated-object operations (e.g., open/close tasks, T5), the success rate was 66.7% across 36 pairs.
 - For rigid-object manipulation (e.g., pick/place tasks, T6), the success rate was 74.2% across 66 pairs.
 - These results demonstrate that the generated task configurations and initial primitive action flows are executable across a variety of reconstructed environments.

- **Self-Evolution Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks:**
 - On four representative long-horizon tasks (T1-T4), self-evolution consistently improved execution performance compared to primitive-only planning.
 - For example, on "Put the glass into the refrigerator" (T1), the success rate improved from 0% (primitive-only) to 40% (with self-evolution, using Qwen3-VL as VLM). For "Put the apple into the bowl" (T3), the SR increased from 40% to 70%.
 - Detailed analysis indicated that self-evolution was particularly effective for later subtasks, where accumulated navigation and placement errors often require multiple correction iterations, while initial subtasks were more frequently solved by the initial planning.

- **Comparison with Existing Automated Data Generation Pipelines:**
 - Direct comparisons with RoboGen and GenSim2, under automated data generation settings, showed that SCENE2DEMO achieved stronger complete-task success on the four representative long-horizon tasks.
 - SCENE2DEMO also exhibited competitive subtask-level execution quality.
 - For instance, on T1, SCENE2DEMO (Prim+Evo) achieved 40% SR, while GenSim2 (Primitive) and RoboGen (Primitive) both achieved 0% SR. On T3, SCENE2DEMO (Prim+Evo) reached 70% SR, outperforming GenSim2 (Primitive) at 55% SR and RoboGen (Primitive) at 0% SR.
 - For simple task categories (T5, T6), SCENE2DEMO was competitive, achieving 66.7% on T5 and 74.2% on T6. GenSim2 showed higher success on T5 (100%) but lower on T6 (38.8%).

- **Repeated Decomposition and Ablation Studies:**
 - A study on repeated decomposition demonstrated that SCENE2DEMO, by using symbolic task definitions, consistently decomposed the same instruction into the same executable subtasks. In contrast, unconstrained baselines often produced varying subtask counts for identical queries, supporting the claim of improved decomposition stability.
 - Wall-clock time analysis showed that a simple task without evolution took about 2 minutes. Long-horizon tasks, including self-evolution, typically took 15-30 minutes per complete demonstration. This is deemed acceptable for an offline data generation engine.
 - Ablation studies on visual feedback (camera views) and temporal context (look-back window for critiques) showed that a combination of Global and Head camera views provided the best performance, with the Wrist view offering negligible additional benefit. A look-back window of one previous action step (p1=1) was found to be optimal, as increasing it did not improve performance and increased latency.

- **Downstream Policy Learning:**
 - Behavior cloning policies trained on 100 autonomously generated expert demonstrations from SCENE2DEMO (with domain randomization) achieved robust performance.
 - Specifically, policies reached 96.0% success (24/25 trials) on "Open Refrigerator" and 92.0% success (23/25 trials) on "Pick Up Glass".
 - These results validate that the observation-action datasets generated by SCENE2DEMO are directly usable and effective for training downstream imitation learning policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SCENE2DEMO framework offers several contributions to embodied AI research, with notable significance and potential impact:

- **Scalable and Reproducible Data Generation:** The primary impact is the provision of a self-evolving, automated framework for generating large volumes of executable robot interaction data in simulation. This addresses a critical bottleneck in embodied AI, significantly reducing the manual effort, cost, and time typically required for data collection. By producing structured task configurations, action traces, execution videos, and offline learning datasets, SCENE2DEMO enhances the scalability and reproducibility of robot learning experiments.
- **Enhanced Task Planning Stability:** The formulation of task generation through an object-action graph, coupled with symbolic task definitions, improves the stability and executability of long-horizon task decomposition. This structured approach helps overcome inconsistencies often seen with unconstrained LLM planning, leading to more reliable robot task execution.
- **Robustness Through Self-Correction:** The visual-feedback-driven self-evolution mechanism empowers the system to autonomously identify and rectify failures in action flows. This iterative refinement process, guided by step-wise visual inspection and task-level supervision, increases the success rate of generated demonstrations, particularly for complex tasks where errors can accumulate. This capability contributes to the generation of higher-quality and more robust training data.
- **Direct Utility for Downstream Learning:** The demonstration that behavior cloning policies trained on SCENE2DEMO's autonomously generated data achieve high success rates (e.g., 96% and 92% on representative tasks) validates the practical value of the framework. This indicates that the generated datasets are directly usable for various robot learning paradigms, including imitation learning, reinforcement learning from demonstrations, and world model training, thereby accelerating research and development in these areas.
- **Pre-deployment Safety and Inspection:** As all data generation and refinement occur in simulation, SCENE2DEMO provides a safe environment to identify and correct potentially unsafe or failed behaviors before any real-world deployment. The modular design, with inspectable components such as VLM critiques and supervisor explanations, also facilitates better understanding and debugging of robotic policies.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to directions for future research:

- **Limited Primitive Action Library and Controller Capability:** The current framework is constrained by its primitive action library and IK-based controller, primarily supporting basic actions like open/close, pick/place, and navigation. It does not yet accommodate fine-grained dexterous manipulation. Future work could integrate more advanced RL-based agents or learned low-level controllers to expand the action space.
- **Reliance on Scene Reconstruction Quality:** SCENE2DEMO's performance is dependent on the quality of the underlying scene reconstruction pipeline (Digital Cousins). Imperfections in scene reconstruction or missing interactive assets can lead to failures. Enhancements in scene reconstruction and asset-grounding methods could improve robustness.
- **Dependency on VLM Feedback Quality:** The self-evolution mechanism heavily relies on the visual reasoning capabilities of the underlying Vision-Language Model (VLM). Improving the VLM's diagnostic and repair abilities is crucial. A promising avenue involves building a "skill documentation library" from successful tasks to store and retrieve reusable correction strategies and action patterns, thereby strengthening the feedback agents' intelligence.

In conclusion, SCENE2DEMO represents a step toward automating the tedious process of embodied data generation, offering a systematic and robust approach that promises to accelerate the development of capable robotic agents.
