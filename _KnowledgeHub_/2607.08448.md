# Harness VLA: Steering Frozen VLAs into Reliable Manipulation Primitives via Memory-Guided Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Harness VLA presents a memory-augmented agentic framework that orchestrates a frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model as a retryable contact-rich primitive alongside analytic primitives, enabling reliable execution of natural-language instructions in diverse and perturbed environments. The system achieved an 82.4% aggregate success rate on LIBERO-Pro tasks with perturbations, considerably outperforming prior baselines.

## Problem

- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are brittle and often fail when deployed in scenarios that deviate from their training distributions, such as with semantic retargeting or spatial layout shifts.
- Large Language Model (LLM) coding agents excel at high-level reasoning but rely on analytic primitives that are not well-suited for delicate, irregular, or contact-rich physical interactions.
- Developing robotic manipulation systems capable of reliably executing free-form natural-language instructions across diverse objects, layouts, and robot embodiments, especially under deployment perturbations, remains a challenge.

## Method

- **Harness VLA** introduces an ==asymmetric hierarchical framework== where an LLM-driven planner orchestrates a fixed, small primitive library.
- A frozen VLA model is integrated as a specialized, retryable primitive for contact-rich interactions, while analytic primitives handle non-contact execution, staging, and navigation.
- The system employs a memory-augmented agentic design with **Task Specific Memory** (parameterized JSONL traces) and **Global Memory** (generalized heuristics) to learn from autonomous exploration and generalize across task instances.

## Results

- Achieved **82.4%** aggregate success on LIBERO-Pro with instruction-redirection and position-swap perturbations (Harness VLA CC), marking a substantial gain over the best reported baseline (RATS **43.8%**).
- Demonstrated improved performance on RoboCasa365, reaching **55.4%** task-weighted success, and strong zero-shot clean-to-randomized transfer on RoboTwin C2R, achieving up to **58.4%** success.
- Maintained competitive performance on standard LIBERO tasks (**96.0%** aggregate success) while successfully generalizing to perturbed environments without requiring VLA fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Planner-level semantic re-grounding enables the system to reliably restore task-conditioned behavior and re-bind targets under instruction redirection and layout shifts, extending the operating range of frozen VLAs.
> - Planner-staged and retryable invocations of the VLA primitive significantly improve the reliability of brittle contact-rich skills by allowing dynamic re-staging and error recovery.
> - Analytic primitives effectively isolate non-contact execution, such as free-space transport and pre-contact staging, allowing the VLA to specialize in learned contact-rich phases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of language-conditioned robotic manipulation is broadly approached by two primary paradigms: end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and Large Language Model (LLM) coding agents. End-to-end VLA models, exemplified by systems like RT-2, OpenVLA, $\pi^0$, and NORA, are designed to learn visuomotor control directly from robot trajectories. They demonstrate proficiency in local, image-conditioned contact tasks, such as grasping irregular objects or actuating fixtures with tight tolerances. However, these models often exhibit diminished performance when deployed in scenarios that deviate from their training distributions, such as tasks involving semantic retargeting, goal re-binding, spatial layout shifts, or the composition of short skills into longer routines, leading to failures even when only minor perturbations are introduced.

Conversely, LLM coding agents, including systems like Code as Policies and ProgPrompt, leverage the semantic and compositional reasoning capabilities of large language models to synthesize executable programs over curated perception and control Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). While effective for high-level planning and reasoning, these agents often rely on analytic primitives (e.g., deterministic kinematic controllers) that are not well-suited for physically delicate or irregular contact-rich interactions. A common approach to extend the capabilities of coding agents is to expand their primitive or skill libraries, which introduces complexities related to skill validation, reusability, and safety under varying scene conditions.

Harness VLA positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms by proposing an asymmetric hierarchical framework. Instead of aiming for stronger end-to-end VLAs or continuously expanding skill libraries, this work focuses on orchestrating a *fixed* and *small* primitive library, which includes a frozen VLA as a specialized contact-rich primitive. This approach seeks to exploit the strengths of both VLA models (local contact skills) and LLM agents (semantic reasoning and long-horizon composition) while mitigating their respective weaknesses, particularly regarding robustness to deployment perturbations and reliable execution of physically complex interactions. The work aims to extend the effective operating range of pretrained VLAs without requiring fine-tuning, by offloading semantic re-grounding, non-contact execution, and VLA re-staging to a higher-level planner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic manipulation system capable of reliably executing free-form natural-language instructions across diverse objects, layouts, and robot embodiments, particularly in the presence of deployment perturbations. The motivation for this objective stems from the observed limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Addressing VLA Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** End-to-end VLA models, while capable of strong local visuomotor skills, are often brittle outside their training data distribution. They struggle when task requirements change (e.g., grasping a different object or placing it in a new location), or when the environment layout is altered. This leads to common failures such as repeating outdated behaviors or derailing entire rollouts due to a single unstable contact. The motivation is to enable pretrained VLAs to operate reliably in perturbed environments without the need for computationally expensive and data-intensive fine-tuning for every new task variant.

2. **Enhancing LLM Agent's Physical Interaction Capabilities:** LLM coding agents excel at high-level semantic reasoning and composing logical steps. However, their reliance on analytic, model-based primitives makes them less effective for complex physical interactions like grasping irregularly shaped objects, placing objects with tight tolerances, or manipulating articulated fixtures. These tasks often require learned visuomotor policies that traditional analytic controllers cannot easily emulate. The motivation is to integrate the strength of VLAs for these "physically delicate interactions" without forcing the LLM to manage low-level, continuous action generation or continually expand its primitive library.

3. **Optimizing Skill Orchestration with a Fixed Primitive Library:** Current LLM agents often scale by expanding their primitive or skill libraries, which can lead to issues with skill validation, reusability, and maintaining safety guarantees. The authors are motivated to explore whether a *fixed* and *small* set of primitives, judiciously orchestrated by an intelligent agent, can achieve broad generalization. This involves learning *when* and *how* to use existing primitives effectively, rather than inventing new ones.

Based on these motivations, the core contributions and objectives of Harness VLA are:

- **To propose a memory-augmented agentic framework:** This framework uses a frozen VLA as a single, retryable contact-rich primitive, composing it with a small set of fixed analytic primitives. The goal is to extend a pretrained VLA from local contact control to long-horizon, perturbed manipulation tasks without requiring VLA fine-tuning or deployment-time primitive expansion.
- **To empirically demonstrate the sufficiency of a small fixed primitive library:** The research aims to show that a planner, when trained to learn the operating range of each fixed primitive, can effectively orchestrate them. This includes using repeated, planner-staged invocations to reframe brittle VLA attempts and employing analytic primitives to handle the non-contact structural aspects of tasks.
- **To achieve strong benchmark results:** The framework aims to demonstrate superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art baselines across various manipulation benchmarks, including standard and perturbed tabletop tasks (LIBERO and LIBERO-Pro), household kitchen manipulation (RoboCasa365), and clean-to-randomized bimanual manipulation (RoboTwin C2R).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Harness VLA introduces a memory-augmented agentic framework for language-conditioned robotic manipulation. The system architecture, as illustrated in Figure 1, comprises an agentic planner, a fixed primitive library, and two memory modules: Task Specific Memory and Global Memory.

**Problem Formulation and Agentic Execution Loop:**
Tasks are defined by natural-language descriptions ($\ell$) and a binary completion predicate (G) that provides a sparse success signal. The environment ($E$) provides multimodal observations ($o_t = (I^{rgb}_t, I^d_t, q_t)$), including RGB images, depth maps, and robot proprioceptive state. The system operates in an autoregressive, turn-based interaction loop. The agentic planner (Π) receives the current observation, task description, and context from memory, then emits a structured JSON invocation for a selected primitive ($c_t \in P$). The physics engine executes the primitive until its post-condition is met, then returns the next observation. This loop continues until task completion or budget exhaustion.

**The Harness VLA Architecture:**
The core of the system is a Read-Eval-Print Loop (REPL)-style harness, which allows the planner to focus on compositional reasoning by delegating fine-grained execution to the primitive library. Multimodal observations (RGB for qualitative reasoning, depth and proprioception for metric data) are crucial inputs. The agent's lifecycle involves two phases:

1. **Exploratory Bootstrapping Phase:**
 - The agent autonomously explores a single "reference instantiation" (seed 0) of a task to find a working solution. During this phase, the planner has access to a `RESET` primitive and a generous time budget.
 - Exploration focuses on orchestrating the fixed primitives: trying different staging orders, pre-contact poses, VLA invocation timings, and early-return thresholds. The planner observes physical effects and corrects course upon failure.
 - Upon successful task completion, the experience is abstracted into two memory modules:
 - **Task Specific Memory:** Stores a parameterized JSONL trace of successful primitive invocations. Concrete spatial coordinates are replaced with symbolic perception queries to enable reusability across different spatial layouts.
 - **Global Memory:** Stores generalized heuristics, including success rules (e.g., optimal prompting strategies) and failure models (e.g., empty-grasp executions, false success detections), aggregated from exploration across tasks to prevent repeating past errors.

2. **Deployment Evaluation Phase:**
 - During formal evaluation on unseen environment variations (e.g., position swaps, instruction redirections, multiple initial state seeds), the `RESET` primitive is disabled, and the step budget is shortened.
 - The planner retrieves the pre-computed JSONL trace from Task Specific Memory, dynamically grounds it using live RGB-D observations, and references success rules and failure models from Global Memory to execute the trajectory deterministically. Performance in this phase constitutes the reported benchmark results.

**Unified Primitive Interface (P):**
The primitive library `P` is the exclusive action interface for the planner, with each primitive invoked via a single JSON object. The planner binds arguments from language, RGB-D, proprioception, and memory. The library is fixed and small, comprising two families:

1. **Analytic Primitives:** Deterministic, model-based controllers based on robot kinematics, requiring no training data.
 - **Composite:** `MOVE TO` (end-effector Cartesian target), `MOVE POSE` (position with orientation covariation), `NAVIGATE TO` (mobile base to planar goal, for RoboCasa365).
 - **Atomic:** `ROTATE WRIST`, `ROTATE PITCH` (wrist orientation set-points), `SET GRIPPER` (gripper open/close), `RELEASE` (open gripper with release post-condition), `MOVE BASE` (local base-velocity set-point, for RoboCasa365).

2. **VLA Primitive:** `VLA ACT` is the single learned primitive. It encapsulates a frozen pretrained visuomotor policy for contact-rich interactions (grasping, constrained placement, fixture actuation, etc.). The planner provides a task-conditioned prompt and an early-return predicate ($\tau$), and the VLA executes action chunks until $\tau$ is met or a chunk budget is exhausted. This allows the VLA to act as a local contact specialist, while the planner retains control over semantic grounding, spatial re-binding, navigation, and re-staging.

**Experimental Setup:**
Harness VLA is evaluated on four benchmark families: LIBERO, LIBERO-Pro, RoboCasa365, and RoboTwin C2R. Different benchmark-specific frozen VLAs are used for the `VLA ACT` primitive: RLinf's $\pi^{0.5}$-SFT for LIBERO/LIBERO-Pro, RLDX-1 for RoboCasa365, and a post-trained LingBot-VLA for RoboTwin C2R. The planner backbones used are Codex and Claude Code (CC). Evaluation covers both few-shot (with Task Specific Memory) and zero-shot (without target-setting Task Specific Memory) regimes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Harness VLA across various benchmarks demonstrates its effectiveness, particularly in scenarios involving deployment perturbations and complex interactions.

**Overall Benchmark Performance (Few-shot evaluation with Task Specific Memory):**

- **Standard LIBERO:** Harness VLA (CC) achieved an aggregate success rate of 96.0% (384/400 trials), including 100.0% on OBJECT tasks and 93.0% on LIBERO-10. This performance is competitive with, and slightly higher than, the direct frozen RLinf baseline (95.3%) used within its `VLA ACT` primitive, indicating that the framework preserves in-distribution performance.
- **LIBERO-Pro:** On tasks with instruction-redirection (T) and position-swap (S) perturbations, Harness VLA showed substantial improvements. Harness VLA (CC) achieved 82.4% aggregate success, and Harness VLA (Codex) achieved 72.1%. These figures represent a significant gain over the strongest reported baseline, RATS (43.8%), and the direct RLinf baseline (50.0%). The results suggest that the primitive composition mechanism enables the planner to re-bind targets, re-stage the scene, and route execution through analytic control, effectively extending the frozen VLA's operating range.
- **RoboCasa365:** For household kitchen tasks involving mobile staging and articulated fixtures, Harness VLA (Codex) reached 55.4% and Harness VLA (CC) reached 48.6% task-weighted success rates. This is an improvement over the RLDX-1 baseline (30.0%), demonstrating the framework's ability to handle longer composite routines and mobile manipulation through planner-driven navigation, staging, and re-staging.

**Overall Benchmark Performance (Zero-shot evaluation without target-setting Task Specific Memory):**

- **LIBERO-Pro Goal (Zero-shot):** When evaluated in a strict zero-shot setting (without retrieving task-specific JSONL trace for the target setting), Harness VLA (CC) outperformed Cap-X across perturbation regimes. It achieved 31.0% on position-swap (Pos-S) and 79.0% on instruction-redirection (Task-T), compared to Cap-X's 25.6% and 16.8%, respectively. This indicates that the planner's reasoning and the fixed primitive interface can synthesize effective compositions even without prior task-specific memory retrieval.
- **RoboTwin C2R (Clean-to-Randomized):** For bimanual manipulation, evaluating zero-shot clean-to-randomized transfer, Harness VLA (CC) achieved 58.4% and Harness VLA (Codex) 58.0%, using LingBot-VLA as the frozen backend. This is an improvement over the direct LingBot-VLA baseline (50.4%), demonstrating effective transfer from a clean-setting trace to randomized task instances without additional bootstrapping or VLA fine-tuning.

**Experiment Analysis (Mechanism Analyses):**

1. **Key Finding 1: Planner-level semantic re-grounding restores task-conditioned behavior.** The framework's architecture allows the planner to handle semantic grounding explicitly. Unlike monolithic VLAs (e.g., RLinf), which may repeat training-time behaviors under task or scene perturbations, Harness VLA's planner parses task descriptions, resolves current contact targets from live RGB-D observations, and uses analytic primitives for staging. This allows the frozen VLA to focus solely on executing the contact-rich operation under the planner-provided binding, making the overall system more robust to instruction redirection and layout shifts (Figure 3).

2. **Key Finding 2: Planner-staged VLA invocation improves frozen-policy reliability.** The planner treats `VLA ACT` as a local, retryable contact-rich primitive. It can stage the robot into a favorable pre-contact configuration using analytic primitives, invoke the VLA, observe the outcome, and re-stage (by changing approach pose, viewpoint, or local staging) if the contact post-condition is not met. This adaptive invocation mechanism significantly enhances reliability, particularly under deployment perturbations or for physically brittle short-horizon contacts, preventing a single failure from derailing the entire rollout (Figures 4 and 5). The ability to perform multiple VLA invocations per episode rapidly increases success rates up to a saturation point.

3. **Key Finding 3: Analytic primitives isolate non-contact execution from contact-rich control.** Analytic primitives manage the non-contact structural aspects of tasks, such as free-space transport, pre-contact staging, wrist/base reorientation, and post-contact repositioning. This allows `VLA ACT` to be reserved for learned contact-rich phases like grasping, constrained placement, and fixture actuation. This division of labor prevents the VLA from being responsible for long-horizon composition or every intermediate motion. Statistical analysis of primitive usage (Tables 18 and 19) shows that analytic primitives constitute the majority of calls in LIBERO (84.2%) and RoboCasa365 (64.7%), and a substantial portion in RoboTwin C2R (52.6%), while VLA ACT handles the critical contact phases (Figure 6 and 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Harness VLA framework presents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation by demonstrating a method to enhance the reliability and generalization of vision-language-action (VLA) models.

**Significance:**

- **Extending VLA Capabilities Without Fine-tuning:** A core significance is the ability to extend the effective operating range of *frozen*, pre-trained VLA models to tasks outside their original training distribution and under various perturbations, without requiring additional fine-tuning. This reduces the substantial computational and data burden associated with adapting VLAs to new scenarios.
- **Asymmetric Hierarchical Control:** The research validates an asymmetric hierarchical control paradigm where a high-level LLM-driven planner handles semantic reasoning, spatial grounding, long-horizon composition, and non-contact execution, while a low-level frozen VLA is delegated as a specialized, retryable primitive for contact-rich interactions. This division of labor leverages the strengths of both symbolic reasoning and learned visuomotor control.
- **Robustness to Perturbations:** By explicitly addressing semantic re-grounding, goal re-binding, and spatial layout shifts at the planner level, Harness VLA achieves substantially improved robustness compared to end-to-end VLAs, which often fail catastrophically under such deployment perturbations.
- **Memory-Guided Generalization:** The introduction of Task Specific Memory (for task-specific procedural traces) and Global Memory (for general success rules and failure models) provides a novel mechanism for the agent to learn from experience and generalize solutions across different task instances, enabling few-shot adaptation and improved recovery from failures.
- **Fixed and Small Primitive Library:** The work demonstrates that it is not always necessary to continually expand the primitive library for an LLM agent. Instead, learning the operating range and orchestration of a small, fixed set of primitives can be highly effective.

**Potential Impact:**

- **More Deployable Robotic Systems:** The improved robustness and generalization capabilities of Harness VLA could lead to more reliable and deployable robotic systems in unstructured environments, such as homes, kitchens, or warehouses, where variations in objects, layouts, and task instructions are common.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By reducing the need for extensive VLA fine-tuning for every new task variant or perturbation, the framework could lower the development costs and time associated with deploying robots for a wider range of manipulation tasks.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability to execute free-form natural language instructions reliably makes robots more accessible and intuitive for human users, potentially broadening the applications of robotics in service and assistance roles.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a strong foundation for future research in several areas, including exploring more sophisticated feedback loops between the planner and VLA, integrating reinforcement learning with human preferences for joint fine-tuning, developing finer-grained image captioning for complex scenes, and combining fixed-vocabulary composition with automatic skill discovery systems.
- **Interpretable Robot Behavior:** The structured, primitive-based approach and the explicit memory modules could contribute to more interpretable and auditable robot behaviors, which is crucial for debugging and ensuring safety in real-world applications.

**Limitations (as stated in the paper):**
The current framework has an open feedback loop between the high-level planner and low-level VLA. It lacks joint fine-tuning via environmental rewards and human preferences, suggesting future integration with sample-efficient reinforcement learning. Additionally, the absence of fine-grained image captioning constrains structural reasoning in highly cluttered, long-horizon tasks. Future work could also explore combining this fixed-vocabulary composition strategy with automatic skill-discovery systems to abstract missing patterns.
