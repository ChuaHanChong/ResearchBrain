# GEVRM: Goal-Expressive Video Generation Model For Robust Visual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The GEVRM framework presents a closed-loop Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for robot visual manipulation, incorporating the Internal Model Control (IMC) principle to enhance robustness against external perturbations. It demonstrates improved generalization and stability in action execution and visual goal generation across simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot manipulation exhibit a lack of robustness to external perturbations (e.g., lighting changes, sensor noise), leading to inaccurate actions and poor generalization in real-world deployments.
- Current methods like image augmentation only offer limited generalization, and prior generative models for visual planning produce insufficiently expressive goal states, neglecting the robot's ability to respond to external interference.
- There was an unexplored opportunity to integrate classical control theory principles, such as Internal Model Control (IMC), into modern deep learning VLA frameworks to systematically address robustness challenges.

## Method

- A text-guided video diffusion transformer model is developed as a ==Robot Behavior Planner==, generating highly expressive and temporally consistent future visual planning goals using multi-stage VAE compression and a random mask mechanism.
- The Internal Model Control (IMC) principle is instantiated through a state alignment mechanism, which uses prototypical contrastive learning in a latent space to implicitly infer and distinguish external perturbations.
- A goal-guided diffusion policy, jointly optimized with the state encoding via a behavior cloning objective, decodes robust robot actions based on the perturbation-aware internal embeddings.

## Results

- GEVRM significantly outperformed baselines (e.g., AVDC, SuSIE) in goal generation quality on BridgeData (FID **35.70**, FVD **4.16**, SSIM **0.89**) and CALVIN datasets, producing fewer hallucinations and maintaining 3D consistency under perturbations.
- Achieved a **0.26** success rate for 5-chained instructions in an unseen CALVIN environment (D) and demonstrated superior robustness, with an average achieved task length of **1.62** under various perturbations, outperforming SuSIE (**1.11**) and GR-1 (**1.44**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of VAE fine-tuning and the state alignment component for overall performance, while T-SNE visualizations showed that state alignment enhances cluster centers and improves intra-/inter-category separation of latent representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating the classical Internal Model Control (IMC) principle into VLA models provides a principled and effective method for enhancing robustness to external perturbations, moving beyond simple data augmentation.
> - Generating highly expressive and temporally consistent future visual planning goals is crucial for robust decision-making, enabling robots to handle complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
> - Prototypical contrastive learning effectively aligns current state representations with goal state representations, allowing the model to implicitly account for and distinguish environmental disturbances, thereby improving decision policy stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of **embodied artificial intelligence**, specifically focusing on **vision-language-action (VLA) models** for robot decision-making and manipulation. Recent advancements in large-scale robot data collection, universal state representation learning, and expressive policy learning have propelled VLA models to the forefront, enabling robots to perform complex tasks in various environments.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: the **lack of robustness of existing VLA models to external perturbations** encountered during real-world deployment. Current VLA models, often trained in carefully controlled environments or simulators, struggle when faced with unforeseen state information caused by fluctuating lighting conditions, sensor noise, or unexpected environmental changes. This fragility leads to inaccurate actions and a significant decline in generalization performance, hindering their practical applicability.

The paper contrasts its approach with existing techniques like image augmentation, commonly used in computer vision and reinforcement learning to enhance model robustness. While image augmentation helps alleviate overfitting and resist minor input perturbations, the authors argue that it only allows generalization within a "narrow task distribution" and lacks "strong resilience to environmental perturbations" for complex visual manipulation tasks. Similarly, prior work leveraging generative models for visual planning (e.g., predicting future videos or target images) is criticized for producing goal states that are "not expressive enough" and for focusing primarily on controlled environments, neglecting the robot's responses to external interference.

GEVRM draws inspiration from the **classical Internal Model Control (IMC) principle** from automation systems. IMC, a well-established control strategy, enhances system robustness by embedding an internal model within the controller that simulates external perturbations and reference inputs, allowing for effective disturbance rejection and accurate reference tracking. The authors highlight that this principle is also believed to be fundamental to how intelligent mammals generate actions and has shown promise in robot motion control, though its application has been limited to specific scenarios.

The novelty of GEVRM lies in its **instantiation and integration of the IMC principle into the VLA framework** for general visual-language manipulation tasks. This is a significant conceptual leap, moving beyond simple data augmentation or basic generative planning to a more principled, control-theoretic approach to robustness in embodied AI. By doing so, GEVRM aims to provide a VLA model that can implicitly infer and distinguish perturbations, generate highly expressive future visual planning goals, and produce robust actions even in challenging, non-ideal environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the robustness and generalization performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot visual manipulation in the face of inevitable external perturbations.**

The motivation for this objective stems from several critical observations:
- **Fragility of Current VLA Models:** Despite significant advancements, existing VLA models are highly susceptible to external perturbations (e.g., lighting changes, sensor noise) during deployment. These perturbations introduce unpredictable state information, leading to inaccurate actions and poor generalization.
- **Limitation of Existing Robustness Techniques:** Traditional methods like image augmentation and current generative planning models fall short. Image augmentation offers only limited generalization, and existing visual planners often generate future goal states that lack sufficient expressiveness or struggle with temporal/spatial inconsistencies.
- **Inspiration from Internal Model Control (IMC):** The classical IMC principle, which demonstrates how an internal model can effectively offset disturbances and track reference inputs in closed-loop systems, offers a powerful theoretical framework for addressing robustness. The authors are motivated by the biological and engineering evidence supporting the efficacy of internal models in generating robust actions.
- **Unexplored Integration of IMC with VLA:** The specific challenge is how to effectively "instantiate the internal model in the VLA framework to improve the robustness of decision actions," which had not been thoroughly explored for complex, general visual-language manipulation tasks.

To achieve its primary objective, GEVRM aims to:
1. **Generate Highly Expressive Future Visual Planning Goals:** Develop an advanced text-guided video generation model that can synthesize detailed and consistent future visual states as reference inputs for the robot, overcoming the limitations of less expressive goal images from prior work.
2. **Implicitly Infer and Distinguish Perturbations:** Implement a mechanism, inspired by IMC, to evaluate external perturbations by simulating robot responses through state alignment. This allows the model to implicitly understand and account for environmental disturbances.
3. **Induce Robust Decision Actions:** Design a goal-guided policy that, conditioned on the expressive future goals and the perturbation-aware internal embeddings, can generate actions that are consistently effective and stable even in perturbed environments.

In essence, the research seeks to bridge the gap between theoretical control principles (IMC) and practical challenges in deep learning-based robot manipulation, leading to more reliable and adaptable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GEVRM proposes a novel closed-loop VLA method that integrates the Internal Model Control (IMC) principle to enhance the robustness of robot visual manipulation. The approach decouples the problem into two hierarchical levels: Robot Behavior Planning (goal generation) and Robot Action Prediction (state alignment and goal-guided policy).

#### 3.1. Robot Behavior Planner (Goal Generation)

The first component is a **text-guided video diffusion transformer model** (`P_ϕ`) that acts as a visual planner, generating future goal image frames (`τ_t:T`) based on historical video observations (`τ_0:t`) and abstract textual task descriptions (`g`). To achieve "highly expressive future goal states," three core aspects are considered:

1. **Video Spatio-temporal Compression:** To mitigate the massive computational resources required by Diffusion Transformers (DiT) operating on high-resolution pixel data, a multi-stage compression strategy is employed:
 - **2D VAE:** Initially, a 2D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) (Rombach et al., 2022) reduces the spatial dimension of original pixel space by a factor of 8x8.
 - **3D VAE:** Subsequently, a 3D VAE (Yu et al., 2023) further condenses the temporal dimension by a factor of 4x. This 3D VAE utilizes Causal 3D convolutional layers, ensuring that each frame's output depends only on its predecessors, which is crucial for temporal consistency in video generation. The decoding process reverses this, restoring temporal then spatial dimensions.
2. **Random Mask Mechanism:** An efficient random mask mechanism (Tay et al., 2022) is implemented during training. This involves randomly unmasking portions of video frames (e.g., initial, final, or arbitrary frames) to teach the planner to anticipate subsequent frames from various temporal snapshots. During testing, only historical frames are available, so unmasking the first 'h' frames is weighted most heavily (75%) during training, with other strategies as supplementary. This mechanism enhances the model's understanding of object dynamics and temporal correlations, contributing to 3D consistency.
3. **Model Backbone and Training Paradigm:**
 - The DiT module is derived from a **pre-trained text-guided video generation model** (Zheng et al., 2024), leveraging its advanced generative capabilities.
 - A **frozen T5 encoder** (Raffel et al., 2020) processes language instructions.
 - The planner is fine-tuned using **Rectified Flow** (Liu et al., 2022), an efficient training paradigm for generative models that learns a mapping from noise to real image distribution by solving an ordinary differential equation. This approach reduces video sampling steps, speeding up training and inference.

#### 3.2. Robot Action Prediction

The expressive goal states generated by the planner guide the prediction of robot actions, comprising two parts:

1. **State Alignment to Simulate Responses:** This is the core implementation of the IMC principle in a learning-based framework.
 - **Visual Encoders:** Residual Networks (ResNet 34) (He et al., 2016) serve as visual encoders (`f_ψ` and `f_ψ'`) for both the current state (`x`) and the goal state (`x_goal`), converting raw pixel data into enriched visual representations.
 - **Internal Embeddings and Prototypical Contrastive Learning:** The current state representation (`f_ψ(x)`) is optimized to simulate the robotic response, which is inherently encoded within the visual goal state (`f_ψ'(x_goal)`). This is achieved in a latent space (`z`, `bz`) using **prototypical contrastive learning** (Caron et al., 2020; Yarats et al., 2021; Deng et al., 2022).
 - Positive pairs (current and goal states from the same trajectory) and negative pairs are used.
 - Latent features are L2-normalized onto a unit sphere.
 - Cluster assignment probabilities (`p_source`, `p_target`) are predicted by comparing latent features to trainable, normalized prototypes (`E`).
 - The Sinkhorn-Knoppal algorithm (Cuturi, 2013) is applied to obtain target probabilities (`q_source`, `q_target`) and avoid trivial solutions.
 - The state alignment objective (`J_ψ`) maximizes prediction accuracy between these probabilities, effectively aligning the current state representation with the future goal state representation, enabling the model to implicitly infer and distinguish perturbations.
2. **Goal-guided Action Prediction:**
 - **Diffusion Policy:** A goal-guided **diffusion policy** (`π_φ`) is employed to decode action outputs from the aligned internal embeddings (current state `z` and goal state `bz`). This policy models the multi-modal task distribution of robot manipulation, using a latent variable model with Markov noise and a de-noising process.
 - **Action Space:** The policy predicts a 7-DoF robot action, including end-effector position and gripper state, using only third-view RGB images.
 - **Behavior Cloning Objective:** The policy is trained using a behavior cloning objective (`J_φ`) that minimizes the difference between predicted noise and actual noise in the reverse diffusion process, conditioned on the internal embeddings.
 - **Joint Optimization:** The state encoding and goal-guided diffusion policy are jointly optimized through a combined objective `J = J_φ + λJ_ψ`, where `λ` is a temperature parameter balancing expert imitation and state alignment. This joint optimization ensures the policy can track expressive goals robustly even in the presence of perturbations.

#### 3.3. Test-time Execution Pipeline of GEVRM

During deployment, GEVRM iteratively solves new manipulation tasks:
1. Given a new initial state and language command, the **robot behavior planner** samples a set of future goal states.
2. For a fixed interval (`L_test`) of time steps, the current state and a sampled goal state are passed through their respective state encoders to obtain **internal embeddings**.
3. The **goal-guided policy** then samples an action based on these internal embeddings.
4. The action is executed, the environment updates, and the process repeats until `L_test` steps are completed.
5. After `L_test` steps, new goal states are refreshed by the behavior planner, and the cycle continues. This iterative process ensures adaptive planning and execution in dynamic environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GEVRM's effectiveness is evaluated through extensive experiments on simulated (CALVIN) and realistic (BridgeData) robot manipulation benchmarks, focusing on goal generation quality, action execution success, and robustness to perturbations.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Goal Generation

- **Generalization on Unseen Environments:** GEVRM significantly outperforms baselines (AVDC, SuSIE, GR-1) on both BridgeData and CALVIN datasets across all metrics (FID, FVD, LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR). For instance, on BridgeData, GEVRM achieved FID of 35.70, FVD of 4.16, and SSIM of 0.89, notably better than AVDC (FID 246.45, FVD 22.89, SSIM 0.73) and SuSIE (FID 114.79, FVD -, SSIM 0.71). Similar improvements were observed on CALVIN. This indicates GEVRM's enhanced expressive capabilities in modeling intricate textures and temporal coherence in robotic image sequences.
- **Robustness to Perturbations:** When tested under perturbed environments (Image Shift, Image Occlusions, Color Jitter), baselines generated severe hallucinations and distorted objects. In contrast, GEVRM produced fewer hallucinations and generated expressive goal states faithfully following language instructions. This confirms GEVRM's ability to better understand physical world laws and maintain 3D consistency of objects even under external disturbances.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Action Execution

- **Generalization on Unseen Environments (CALVIN ABC → D):** GEVRM demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in completing chained language instructions in an unseen environment (D, after training on A, B, C). For 5-chained instructions, GEVRM achieved a success rate of 0.26, matching SuSIE and outperforming UniPi (0.04), HiP (0.00), and GR-1 (0.10). For shorter chains (e.g., 1-chained), GEVRM achieved 0.92 success, surpassing all baselines. This highlights the method's superior goal generation ability and its capacity to induce more generalized decision actions in novel settings.
- **Robustness to External Perturbations (CALVIN ABC → Perturbed D):** GEVRM shows significant improvements in action execution under various external perturbations (Image Shift, Rotation, Color Jitter, Occlusions, Noise Interference). Averaged across five perturbed tasks, GEVRM achieved an average achieved task length of 1.62, outperforming SuSIE (1.11), RoboFlamingo (1.31), and GR-1 (1.44). For instance, under "Image Occlusions," GEVRM's 5-chained success rate was 0.20, higher than SuSIE's 0.24 and GR-1's 0.23, demonstrating robust decision-making in challenging scenarios.
- **Real-world Deployment:** GEVRM was successfully deployed on a UR5 robotic arm for pick-and-place tasks (cup, bowl, tiger plush toy). The visual results in the appendix show the policy executing effectively in real-world scenarios. Success rates varied (e.g., ~0.8 for regular shapes, ~0.6 for irregular plush toys), demonstrating practical applicability and identifying areas for further improvement.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Importance of VAE Fine-tuning:** Omitting VAE fine-tuning significantly degraded model performance. This confirms that pre-training on diverse video data enhances spatio-temporal consistency, and subsequent fine-tuning on robot data is crucial for generalizing decision-making.
- **Importance of State Alignment (SA):** Removing the state alignment component also led to a significant performance drop. This validates that state alignment is vital for bolstering the policy's visual state representation, allowing it to better account for perturbations and generalize tasks.
- **Hyperparameter λ:** The hyperparameter `λ`, which balances expert imitation and state alignment, showed minimal variation in performance across tested values, indicating robustness to its adjustment, with `λ=1` identified as optimal.
- **State Representation Visualization (T-SNE):** T-SNE visualizations of latent space representations (Figure 6, Appendix Figure 8) clearly showed that state alignment enhances cluster centers, improves intra-category cohesion, and increases inter-category separation, making representations more distinguishable and temporally consistent. This provides a clear explanation for *why* state alignment improves robustness.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The study confirmed that Rectified Flow (for behavior planner) and increased open-loop control steps (for goal-guided policy) significantly reduce inference time without a substantial loss in task success rate, making GEVRM computationally practical for real-time robot control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GEVRM framework represents a substantial step forward in developing robust and generalizable robotic systems, particularly for visual manipulation tasks in unpredictable real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Integration of IMC with VLA:** The most significant contribution is the successful integration of the classical Internal Model Control (IMC) principle, traditionally found in control theory, into a modern deep learning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework. This bridges a critical gap between control theory and embodied AI, offering a principled approach to tackle the pervasive problem of robustness to external perturbations. This conceptual innovation could inspire future research to explore other classical control principles within advanced AI models.
- **Enhanced Robustness to External Perturbations:** GEVRM directly addresses the fragility of existing VLA models, which often fail in non-ideal deployment scenarios. By implicitly inferring and distinguishing perturbations through state alignment and prototypical contrastive learning, GEVRM enables robots to maintain performance integrity even under fluctuating lighting, sensor noise, or environmental changes. This is crucial for expanding the reliability and applicability of robots beyond carefully curated lab settings.
- **Highly Expressive Visual Goal Generation:** The use of an advanced text-guided video diffusion generation model provides robots with "highly expressive future visual planning goals." This capability allows for more detailed and temporally consistent sub-goal planning, which is vital for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks and overcoming limitations of simpler target image generation methods.
- **Improved Generalization Performance:** The empirical results on unseen and perturbed environments (e.g., CALVIN benchmark) clearly demonstrate GEVRM's superior generalization ability compared to state-of-the-art baselines. This improved generalization means that robots trained with GEVRM are more likely to succeed in novel tasks and environments without extensive re-training.
- **Practical Applicability for Real-World Robotics:** The successful deployment of GEVRM on a real robotic arm (UR5) for pick-and-place tasks validates its practical potential. The architecture's efficiency, demonstrated through ablation studies on inference time, suggests it can operate at frequencies suitable for real-time robot control, moving closer to deployable intelligent robots.
- **Advancement Towards Embodied General Intelligence:** By making robotic systems more robust and adaptable to unforeseen circumstances, GEVRM contributes directly to the long-term goal of embodied general intelligence. Robots that can reliably operate in dynamic and uncertain environments are a cornerstone for more autonomous and versatile AI agents.

**Potential Limitations and Future Work:**
While GEVRM makes significant strides, the authors acknowledge that future work could focus on "incorporating more general high-quality video generation models into the VLA framework to cope with complex and diverse manipulation tasks of real-world robots." This suggests that even more sophisticated generative capabilities might further enhance performance, particularly in scenarios with extreme environmental variability or highly complex object interactions. The real-world experiments also point to challenges with irregularly shaped or soft objects, indicating room for improvement in perception and grasping accuracy.

In conclusion, GEVRM offers a robust and theoretically grounded framework for VLA models, addressing a critical bottleneck in real-world robot deployment. Its innovative integration of IMC principles with modern generative AI techniques positions it as an important contribution towards more reliable, adaptable, and ultimately, more intelligent robotic systems.
