# FocalPolicy: Frequency-Optimized Chunking and Locally Anchored Flow Matching for Coherent Visuomotor Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> FocalPolicy, developed by researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the University of Trento, introduces a visuomotor policy that addresses inter-chunk discontinuities by integrating frequency-optimized chunking and locally anchored flow matching. This approach achieved an average success rate of 83.6% across 53 simulation tasks and demonstrated superior performance in real-world robotic manipulation by generating smoother, more coherent action trajectories.

## Problem

- Existing action chunking methods for visuomotor policies often neglect inter-chunk coherence, leading to discontinuities and compounding errors in long-horizon trajectories.
- Current policies struggle to balance fine-grained precision for immediate actions with maintaining structural coherence for subsequent, distal actions.
- Standard consistency flow matching (CFM) methods for action generation can suffer from inefficient target-signal propagation, particularly when modeling complex multi-chunk action distributions.

## Method

- A Foresight Composite Objective (FCO) is introduced, which uses a hybrid loss to supervise time-domain alignment for proximal actions and frequency-domain regularization (via Discrete Cosine Transform) for multi-chunk distal trajectories, ensuring both precision and coherence.
- Locally Anchored Sampling (LAS) is proposed as an optimized sampling strategy for consistency ==flow matching== training, biasing anchor points towards the terminal state to enhance target-signal propagation efficiency.
- The policy models a 'macro-trajectory' by concatenating multiple consecutive action chunks, providing a broader context for learning long-horizon coherence using a 1D CNN-based U-Net architecture.

## Results

- FocalPolicy achieved an average success rate of **83.6%** across 53 Adroit and MetaWorld simulation tasks, outperforming leading baselines such as DP3 (**79.9%**) and FlowPolicy (**79.4%**).
- Qualitative and quantitative metrics (e.g., Action Total Variation, Trajectory Smoothness) confirmed that FocalPolicy consistently generated smoother, more coherent action sequences with reduced compounding errors compared to baselines.
- The policy demonstrated superior and robust performance on six complex real-world, multi-stage manipulation tasks, maintaining coherence and achieving higher success rates, especially in the final stages of operations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Differentiated supervision, applying fine-grained control to immediate actions and coarse structural regularization to future actions, is effective for bridging inter-chunk discontinuities.
> - Frequency-domain representations (DCT) can effectively capture global motion trends and provide non-conflicting gradient signals with time-domain losses, enhancing long-horizon trajectory coherence.
> - Biasing target distribution supervision in consistency flow matching training towards the terminal state significantly improves learning efficiency and stability for complex, multi-chunk action distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper operates within the field of visuomotor policies for robotic manipulation, specifically leveraging imitation learning from expert demonstrations. A significant challenge in this area involves generating smooth and coherent action trajectories over long horizons. Robotic policies often suffer from compounding errors, where small inaccuracies accumulate over time, leading to trajectory deviations and task failures.

To mitigate these compounding errors, action chunking has been widely adopted. This approach allows policies to predict multi-step action sequences (chunks) in a single inference step, rather than individual actions. Recent advancements have focused on accelerating the generation of these action chunks and improving their accuracy, often through methods like self-consistency constraints or consistency flow matching (CFM) for one-step trajectory generation. Some research has also explored enhancing distributional fidelity or incorporating frequency-domain supervision within these frameworks.

However, existing chunk-based methods predominantly concentrate on optimizing the *intra-chunk* action distributions, meaning they ensure the smoothness and accuracy within a single predicted action segment. A persistent limitation is their tendency to neglect the *inter-chunk* coherence, leading to discontinuities at the boundaries between consecutively generated action chunks. These discontinuities can impede the learning of robust, long-horizon actions and hinder the reliable deployment of visuomotor policies in real-world scenarios. Increasing the inference horizon to address this directly can lead to policy degradation due to the increased complexity of the action distribution.

FocalPolicy addresses this specific gap by proposing a "foresight-aware" policy design. Unlike prior approaches that focus on inference-time modifications to mitigate discontinuities, FocalPolicy aims to inherently learn action coherence at the fundamental policy training level. It attempts to balance fine-grained precision for immediate actions with structural coherence for longer, subsequent trajectories, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and deployable visuomotor policies for complex manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a visuomotor policy capable of generating smooth and coherent action trajectories for complex manipulation tasks, explicitly addressing the limitations of existing methods regarding inter-chunk discontinuities. The motivation stems from several key observations and challenges:

1. **Challenge of Inter-chunk Discontinuities**: Current action chunking-based visuomotor policies primarily focus on optimizing the action distribution *within* each predicted chunk (intra-chunk fidelity). However, they often overlook the temporal coherence *between* successive chunks (inter-chunk coherence). This oversight results in discontinuities at chunk boundaries, which accumulate into significant errors over long-horizon tasks, hindering the learning of fluid and continuous manipulation sequences. These discontinuities are identified as a major bottleneck for coherent long-horizon action generation.

2. **Balancing Proximal Precision and Distal Foresight**: Generating effective manipulation trajectories requires a policy to simultaneously achieve high precision for immediate, proximal actions and maintain a coherent, longer-term plan for distal, future actions. Existing methods struggle to balance this requirement, often compromising one for the other. A policy that can plan fine-grained immediate actions while also accounting for coarse subsequent trajectories is necessary.

3. **Limitations of Current Solutions**:
 - Directly increasing the inference horizon to capture longer-term coherence leads to more complex action distributions, which can degrade policy learning performance.
 - Inference-time algorithms designed to enforce coherence after a policy is trained do not fundamentally address the lack of coherence learned during the training phase.
 - Consistency flow matching (CFM) offers efficient one-step generation but, in its standard form, relies solely on self-consistency between adjacent time steps *within* an action chunk, lacking explicit guidance for long-horizon motion trends and multi-chunk distributions. This limits its ability to model complex, multi-chunk action trajectories efficiently.

To address these motivations, FocalPolicy sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Develop a Foresight-Aware Visuomotor Policy**: Design a policy that intrinsically learns to synergize proximal precision with distal coherence, thereby bridging inter-chunk discontinuities.
- **Introduce a Foresight Composite Objective (FCO)**: Formulate a novel training objective that supervises time-domain alignment for proximal actions (ensuring fine-grained accuracy) while simultaneously regularizing the frequency-domain structure over multiple future action chunks (improving cross-chunk coherence). This objective aims to explicitly guide the policy towards learning coherent long-horizon actions.
- **Design Locally Anchored Sampling (LAS)**: Propose an efficient sampling strategy for consistency flow matching training. This strategy aims to enhance target signal propagation efficiency, particularly when modeling the complex action distributions inherent in multi-chunk trajectories, by reinforcing direct supervision of the target distribution.

These objectives are motivated by the goal of enabling visuomotor policies to generate smooth, continuous, and robust long-horizon manipulation trajectories, improving their reliability and deployability in real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FocalPolicy is designed as a foresight-aware visuomotor policy that integrates Frequency-Optimized Chunking with Locally Anchored flow matching. The overall approach aims to achieve a balanced focus on proximal action precision and distal trajectory coherence.

**Core Framework**:
FocalPolicy builds upon the Consistency Flow Matching (CFM) framework, specifically adapting the FlowPolicy approach. In this framework, at each environment timestep, the policy predicts an action chunk `A_t` comprising `H` future actions. FocalPolicy extends this by modeling `N` such consecutive action chunks, forming a "macro-trajectory" of total length `L = N * H`. The policy's network architecture is a 1D CNN-based U-Net, commonly used in visuomotor policies for 3D point-cloud observations.

The methodology introduces two main components:

#### 3.1. Foresight Composite Objective (FCO)

The FCO is designed to address the problem of inter-chunk discontinuities by differentiating the learning focus: ensuring fine-grained fidelity for immediate (proximal) actions and maintaining coarse structural coherence for future (distal) trajectories.

1. **Macro-Trajectory Definition**: Instead of focusing on single chunks, FCO explicitly concatenates `N` consecutive expert action chunks into a single macro-trajectory `M_t` of horizon length `L = N * H`. This provides a broader context for learning coherence.

2. **Frequency-Domain Representation**: To capture global motion trends and fine-grained details within these macro-trajectories, the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) is applied. The DCT coefficients `C_d[u]` for each action dimension aggregate information across all timesteps in the macro-trajectory, providing a global structural view. Low-frequency components are understood to encode global motion trends, while high-frequency components represent local details, aligning with the "differentiated focus" requirement.

3. **Hybrid Loss Formulation**: The FCO loss, `L_FCO`, is a weighted combination of two components:
 - **Proximal Time-Domain Consistency Loss (`L_time`)**: This component is derived from the CFM objective but is applied exclusively to the *first H timesteps* (the proximal single chunk) of the predicted macro-trajectory. It enforces consistency constraints between interpolated states at student time `τ` and anchored time `r` for these immediate actions, ensuring fine-grained precision.
 - **Multi-Chunk Frequency-Domain Loss (`L_freq`)**: This component is defined as the squared L2 distance between the DCT coefficients of the *entire predicted macro-trajectory* and the *entire expert macro-trajectory*. Its role is to regularize the frequency-domain structure over multiple future action chunks, promoting cross-chunk coherence by capturing and aligning global motion patterns.
 - The total objective is `L_FCO = L_time + λ * L_freq`, where `λ` is a balancing coefficient (set to 10⁻⁴).

4. **Gradient Compatibility**: Theoretical analysis in the paper's appendix suggests that `L_freq` provides non-conflicting gradient signals with `L_time`. Specifically, it indicates that the frequency-domain loss, when applied to the full macro-trajectory while the time-domain loss is applied to only the prefix, offers additional supervisory signals. Furthermore, the DCT representation can concentrate macroscopic drift errors into low-frequency coefficients, enhancing their numerical detectability during optimization ("sensitivity gain").

#### 3.2. Locally Anchored Sampling (LAS)

LAS is designed to improve the training efficiency of consistency flow matching, particularly when dealing with the increased distributional complexity introduced by macro-trajectories.

1. **Challenge in CFM**: Standard CFM enforces consistency between predictions at randomly sampled adjacent time points (`τ` and `τ + Δτ`). However, this can lead to inefficient target-signal propagation, especially as `τ` moves away from the terminal state, where the model's intermediate predictions are less accurate. This is formalized as "Target-signal Propagation Efficiency" `E(τ)`.

2. **Anchoring Strategy**: Instead of sampling the anchor time `r` uniformly, LAS samples `r` from a logit-normal distribution that is heavily concentrated near the terminal time (`τ=1`).
 - `r = sigmoid(μ_r + σ_r * ϵ)`, where `ϵ` is a standard normal variable.
 - By biasing `r` towards `τ=1` (e.g., `μ_r = 4.0, σ_r = 1.6` for a median `r ≈ 0.982`), the consistency constraint primarily pulls the model towards the highly accurate target distribution at the terminal state.
 - The stochasticity in `r` (not fixing `r = 1`) is maintained to stabilize training and preserve temporal continuity along the flow.

3. **Benefits**: This strategy reinforces direct supervision from the target distribution, leading to enhanced target-signal propagation efficiency. Theoretical analysis (Appendix B) supports that this biased sampling, under an assumption of monotonically decreasing teacher error towards the terminal state, yields strictly higher propagation efficiency compared to standard uniform sampling. This helps stabilize training and allows for more effective learning of complex action distributions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FocalPolicy was evaluated extensively in both simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating its effectiveness in generating coherent visuomotor policies.

1. **Superior Simulation Performance**:
 - Across 53 tasks from the Adroit and MetaWorld benchmarks, FocalPolicy achieved an average success rate of 83.6%, which surpassed leading baselines such as DP3 (79.9%), FlowPolicy (79.4%), and FreqPolicy (75.1%).
 - On a subset of 7 representative tasks, FocalPolicy again showed the highest average success rate (69.7%).

2. **Module Generalizability**:
 - The individual modules, FCO and LAS, demonstrated generalizability when integrated into existing baselines. DP3 combined with FCO showed an average success rate of 60.6% (a 2.8% improvement over standalone DP3). FlowPolicy integrated with LAS achieved 63.3% (a 7.9% improvement over standalone FlowPolicy). This indicates that the benefits of FCO and LAS are not exclusive to the FocalPolicy architecture.

3. **Mitigation of Compounding Errors and Discontinuities**:
 - **Qualitative Visualization**: In open-loop rollouts, FocalPolicy's predicted end-effector trajectories tracked expert demonstrations more closely than FlowPolicy, exhibiting visually lower compounding errors.
 - **Quantitative Metrics**: FocalPolicy consistently achieved lower Action Total Variation (ATV) scores (Table 3) and lower Trajectory Smoothness (TS) scores (Table 10) compared to FlowPolicy across various tasks. Lower ATV and TS scores indicate higher temporal coherence and smoother action sequences, confirming that FCO effectively mitigates inter-chunk discontinuities.

4. **Enhanced Learning Efficiency**:
 - Learning curves (Figure 4) showed that FocalPolicy converged faster to higher success rates than FlowPolicy. This improvement in learning efficiency is attributed to the Locally Anchored Sampling (LAS) strategy, which enhances target-signal propagation during consistency flow training.

5. **Robust Real-World Performance**:
 - FocalPolicy was evaluated on six complex, long-horizon, multi-stage real-world manipulation tasks. It consistently achieved superior average success rates compared to DP3, FreqPolicy, and FlowPolicy (Figure 6, Table 11). The performance advantage was particularly pronounced in the final stages of multi-stage tasks, suggesting its ability to maintain coherence over extended operations.

6. **Ablation Study Findings**:
 - **Component Effectiveness**: Removing either LAS or FCO (or both) led to significant performance degradation, confirming their individual and complementary contributions.
 - **Loss Formulation Nuances**: Variants where FCO's time-domain loss was applied to the full macro-trajectory or its frequency-domain loss was restricted to the proximal chunk performed worse, validating the proposed "differentiated focus" strategy. Specifically, `L_time-Only FCO` showed the most significant drop (34.3% on Adroit), highlighting the importance of frequency-domain supervision for global trends.
 - **Stochasticity in LAS**: A deterministic `Fixed-r LAS` (r=1) was inferior to the stochastic version, indicating that some randomness in anchoring, while biased towards the terminal, is beneficial for training stability.
 - **Horizon Hyperparameters**: Optimal performance was observed with an action chunk size H=4 and N=3 aggregated chunks (total macro-trajectory length L=12). This balance allows for rapid replanning with short chunks while providing sufficient foresight through multi-chunk frequency regularization.
 - **Lambda Sensitivity**: An optimal balancing coefficient of `λ = 10⁻⁴` was identified for the FCO, effectively balancing proximal precision and distal coherence.
 - **Spectral Components**: Utilizing both low-frequency (LF) and high-frequency (HF) components of the DCT was more effective than using either component in isolation, confirming that the full spectrum contributes to capturing both global trends and local details.

7. **Generalization to Diverse Benchmarks**:
 - FocalPolicy demonstrated strong generalization by outperforming baselines on the RoboMimic (human-demonstrated data) and LIBERO (complex task suite) benchmarks, while maintaining a low inference latency of 5.40 ms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on FocalPolicy contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by directly addressing a core challenge in visuomotor policy learning: the generation of coherent and smooth action trajectories over long horizons. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Trajectory Coherence**: FocalPolicy introduces a novel approach that explicitly mitigates inter-chunk discontinuities, which have been a persistent issue in chunk-based visuomotor policies. By balancing proximal precision with distal foresight through the Foresight Composite Objective (FCO), the policy learns to produce smoother, more continuous, and less error-prone trajectories. This improvement directly translates to more reliable and robust robot operation in sequential and multi-stage tasks.

2. **Improved Learning Efficiency and Stability**: The Locally Anchored Sampling (LAS) strategy enhances the efficiency of target signal propagation during consistency flow matching training. This leads to faster convergence and more stable learning of complex action distributions, which is particularly beneficial for multi-chunk trajectories. This efficiency can reduce the computational resources and time required for training robust policies.

3. **Modular and Generalizable Components**: Both FCO and LAS are designed as modular components, demonstrated to be integrable into existing action chunking and consistency flow-based policies (e.g., DP3, FlowPolicy). This generalizability suggests that the proposed techniques can benefit a broader range of current and future visuomotor architectures, extending their impact beyond the specific FocalPolicy implementation.

4. **Advancement in Real-World Robotic Manipulation**: The superior performance of FocalPolicy on challenging real-world, multi-stage manipulation tasks highlights its practical relevance. The ability to maintain coherence over longer task durations makes it more suitable for deploying robots in complex, unstructured environments where long-horizon planning and execution are critical. This could enable more autonomous and capable robotic systems in manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics.

5. **A Differentiated Approach to Planning**: The concept of "differentiated focus learning" – applying fine-grained supervision to proximal actions and coarse structural regularization to distal actions – offers a valuable paradigm for designing future robot learning algorithms. This approach acknowledges the inherent differences in requirements for immediate control versus long-term strategic planning.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as not addressing high-dimensional decision-making, suboptimal demonstrations, or out-of-distribution generalization, the foundational improvements in trajectory coherence, learning efficiency, and real-world performance represent a notable step forward. FocalPolicy's systematic approach to integrating time and frequency-domain supervision with optimized sampling contributes to the ongoing development of more capable and reliable imitation learning-based robotic systems.
