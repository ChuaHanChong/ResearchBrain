# Where Success Breaks: Failure-Boundary Learning for Robust Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DLS reframes post-SFT adaptation of flow-based vision-language-action policies as failure-boundary learning. A sim-real co-trained prior rolls out on-policy in a ManiSkill digital twin; semantic progress localization converts privileged simulator predicates into phase-indexed progress rewards, and directional boundary shaping applies push-pull ranking updates on the denoising velocity field without likelihoods or critics. It reaches 84.4% in-distribution and 72.2% unseen real-robot success on pi-0.5.

## Problem

- Supervised fine-tuning on expert demonstrations only pulls policies toward successful actions, giving no signal about where closed-loop behavior stops being reliable.
- Likelihood-recovery RL discretizes the denoising SDE at high memory and solver-coupling cost, while critic-driven shaping suffers critic overfitting and reward hacking.
- Preference ranking stays driven by binary terminal outcomes, and stage-aware reward designs lock dense supervision to value-based PPO optimization with learned value networks.

## Method

- **DLS** Stage 1 builds a real-grounded behavioral prior by ==sim-real co-training== with ratio alpha on 50 real demonstrations plus 1,000 ==MimicGen==-synthesized digital-twin trajectories from five seed rollouts.
- ==Semantic progress localization== maps trajectories onto ordered task phases with ==gated cumulative weights==, giving stage-indexed credit that separates clean success, inefficient success, and the failure locus.
- ==Directional boundary shaping== builds ==mirrored velocity branches== around the stored rollout velocity and ranks their ==Mahalanobis errors== via a ==signed-label Softplus loss==, anchored by ==real-data SFT regularization==.

## Results

- DLS reaches **84.4%**/**72.2%** (in-distribution/unseen) on pi-0.5 and **78.9%**/**71.1%** on pi-0, with the margin over PPO widening from **+3.3%** to **+4.4%** and **+1.1%** to **+5.5%**.
- With 50 real demonstrations it beats real-only SFT trained on 100 by **+17.8%** absolute under unseen conditions (**72.2%** vs **54.4%**), and lifts Plugin **+36.7%** over its prior.
- Ablations: semantic progress localization beats step-count penalty and distance potential by over **6.7%** success; removing the SFT anchor causes collapse, with peak performance at regularization weight 0.5-1.0.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Where a rollout fails carries more learning signal than whether it failed; phase-gated credit concentrates gradients at the active competence boundary.
> - Simulation coverage and real-world grounding are orthogonal prerequisites: sim-only priors misalign failure loci, real-only priors confine exploration, neither substitutes for the other.
> - Contrastive ranking on paired velocity-field branches enables negative-example learning for flow policies without action likelihoods, critics, or intra-group rollout comparison.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as a foundational technology for robot manipulation tasks. A common approach for adapting these models to downstream tasks involves supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using expert demonstrations. However, a known limitation of imitation learning, including SFT, is its inherent structural asymmetry: policies are trained to replicate successful behaviors but receive minimal or no explicit signal regarding actions that lead to task failure or how to recover from deviations. This means that when a robot's closed-loop execution deviates from the expert demonstration distribution, the SFT-trained policy may lack guidance, leading to breakdowns or an inability to recover. This limitation manifests as a lack of robustness, particularly in novel or out-of-distribution conditions.

The existing research landscape for post-SFT optimization of flow-based VLAs, which generate actions via multi-step denoising, generally addresses this through several families of methods:
1. **Likelihood-Recovery Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Approaches like PPO or GRPO attempt to apply standard RL objectives by discretizing the denoising SDE, which can introduce solver coupling and memory overhead.
2. **Critic-Driven Shaping**: These methods learn value or reward critics to provide denser supervisory signals, bypassing the need for action likelihoods. However, they can suffer from issues such as critic overfitting and reward hacking.
3. **Preference-Based Ranking**: This strategy involves contrastive comparisons of trajectories or partial trajectories, avoiding likelihoods and critics, but often relies on coarse binary terminal outcomes which limit the granularity of the learning signal.
4. **Stage-Aware Reward Design**: This involves decomposing manipulation tasks into sequential sub-stages and assigning rewards based on progress through these stages, typically used in conjunction with PPO and value networks.

Furthermore, the paper situates itself within the context of Sim-to-Real transfer for manipulation policies. Existing approaches in this domain include:
- **Distribution-Matching Transfer**: Techniques like domain randomization or high-fidelity digital twins aim to reduce the visual and dynamics gap between simulation and the real world, allowing policies trained solely in simulation to generalize.
- **Supervised Co-Training**: Simulation is used as a scalable source of demonstrations, with policies jointly trained on both real and simulated data.
- **Online Sim-Real Co-Training**: This more nascent area incorporates RL into the co-training loop, using joint SFT for initialization and then running online RL in simulation while regularizing against a real-world anchor.

This work addresses the gaps in these areas by proposing a method that explicitly learns "failure boundaries" rather than solely focusing on successful behaviors. It aims to provide progress-localized and generation-aware signals for flow-based VLAs without relying on action likelihoods or learned critics, and it strategically exploits privileged simulator states, which are often underexploited in existing sim-to-real paradigms, to generate structured training signals. By focusing on where policies break, the authors aim to improve robustness under randomized initial states and unseen visual conditions, moving beyond the limitations of demonstration-fitting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models beyond the capabilities provided by supervised fine-tuning (SFT) over expert demonstrations. The core motivation stems from a recognized "structural asymmetry" in SFT: while SFT effectively teaches policies where successful behavior resides, it inherently lacks any explicit signal about the boundaries where such behavior ceases to be reliable or transitions into failure. This absence of a "failure boundary" signal leads to several challenges during closed-loop execution, where small errors can cause the robot to deviate from the expert demonstration distribution, resulting in task failures with limited guidance for recovery.

The authors propose that robust VLA adaptation should therefore be conceptualized as **Failure-Boundary Learning**. This involves three specific operations:
1. **Discovering** self-generated failures under closed-loop execution.
2. **Localizing** precisely where a rollout breaks down in terms of task progress.
3. **Shaping** the policy to move away from failure-producing directions.

To achieve this, the training signal for robust adaptation needs to fulfill three critical requirements:
- **On-policy and Scalable**: Manually curated failure data is insufficient because it cannot encompass the diverse and specific errors a current policy might generate. Discovering these failure boundaries necessitates extensive trials, which are prohibitively costly and slow through physical robot interaction. Simulation offers the necessary scale.
- **Progress-Localized**: A simple binary "success" or "failure" label for an entire episode is insufficient. Failures occurring at different stages of a task (e.g., a grasping failure versus a placement misalignment) imply distinct corrective signals. The signal should identify not just *whether* an episode failed, but *where* it failed and how much progress was made prior to breakdown, allowing for the preservation of partially successful behaviors.
- **Generation-Aware**: Flow-based VLAs generate actions through a multi-step denoising process. Unlike traditional RL, exact action likelihoods are not directly available for standard policy gradient updates, complicating the application of conventional RL techniques that rely on such probabilities or value functions.

The motivation for the proposed Discover–Localize–Shape (DLS) pipeline is to address these requirements by leveraging a real-grounded behavioral prior, scaling failure discovery through digital twin rollouts, precisely localizing failure points using privileged simulator states, and implementing a novel directional shaping mechanism that avoids the need for action likelihoods or learned critics. The ultimate goal is to improve policy robustness under challenging conditions, such as randomized initial states and unseen visual environments, which frequently lead to failures in SFT-only models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Discover–Localize–Shape (DLS)** pipeline frames VLA adaptation as Failure-Boundary Learning and operates in two distinct stages.

**Stage 1: Real-Grounded Behavioral Prior**
This initial stage aims to establish a foundational policy that exhibits nominal behavior.
- **Problem Formulation**: The manipulation task is modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). Flow-based VLAs learn a time-dependent velocity field, $v_{\theta}(a_t, t, o, \ell)$, which iteratively denoises a noisy action ($a_t$) towards a target action ($a_0$) over continuous time $t \in$. The policy is trained by minimizing a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loss, $L_{SFT}$, which measures the squared difference between the predicted velocity field and the expert action-generating velocity.
- **Simulated Data Generation**: To scale up expert demonstrations beyond limited real-world data, a digital twin of the real robot setup is created in ManiSkill, with camera configurations calibrated to match real-world hand-eye matrices. MimicGen is then used to synthesize a large dataset of simulated demonstrations (1,000 trajectories from 5 real-world seeds) by decomposing real seeds into task-relevant segments and applying geometric transformations and randomized initial object positions.
- **Co-training Objective**: The policy is anchored to the deployment domain while enhancing behavioral diversity using a Sim-Real mixture objective, $L_{Sim-Real}(\theta) = \alpha L_{SFT}(\theta; D_{Sim}) + (1-\alpha) L_{SFT}(\theta; D_{Real})$. Here, $\alpha \in$ controls the balance between simulated ($D_{Sim}$) and real ($D_{Real}$) demonstration data, allowing for richer exploration in Stage 2.

**Stage 2: Failure-Boundary Learning via Online Exploration**
Building upon the real-grounded prior, this stage actively discovers and learns from failures.
- **On-policy Boundary Discovery**: The prior policy, $\pi_{old}^{\theta}$, is deployed in a large number (N=128) of parallel simulation environments (digital twins) for online exploration. A Flow-SDE sampler is used to inject stochasticity into the action generation process, encouraging the policy to explore and generate its own failure modes. Each denoising step results in a transition tuple, including the initial noisy action, the subsequent action, the old policy's velocity, time, state, observation, language instruction, and a phase-mapped reward ($r_i$) (assigned by SPL), which are stored in an exploration buffer $B$.
- **Semantic Progress Localization (SPL)**:
 - This component addresses the limitation of coarse binary rewards by providing progress-aware signals. It models manipulation as a hybrid state transition system, mapping each trajectory state $s_i$ to an ordered sequence of $Z$ task phases, $\psi(s_i) \in \{1, \dots, Z\}$.
 - Phase transitions are determined by privileged simulator predicates (e.g., end-effector pose, object poses, gripper state, goal alignment), which are directly accessible in the digital twin.
 - Within each phase $z$, a continuous intra-phase metric, $\phi_z(s_i) \in$, quantifies progress towards the next phase boundary.
 - A state potential, $\Phi(s_i) = W_{\psi(s_i)-1} + w_{\psi(s_i)} \cdot \phi_{\psi(s_i)}(s_i)$, is defined, where $w_z$ are credit weights for each phase and $W_{z-1}$ is the cumulative baseline.
 - The trajectory's semantic credit, $c_{SPL} = \max_i \Phi(s_i)$, and the deepest phase reached, $\hat{z}_{max} = \max_i \psi(s_i)$, are localized. The cumulative baselines $W_{z-1}$ enforce sequential task progression, preventing shortcut behaviors.
 - SPL generates a stage-indexed reward map $\{r_z\}_{z=1}^Z$:
 - Successful trajectories (where $c_{SPL}=1.0$) receive a reward that includes an efficiency penalty based on episode length.
 - Completed stages of partial failures (where $c_{SPL} < 1.0$ and $z < \hat{z}_{max}$) receive credit equal to $c_{SPL}$.
 - The exact failure locus (where $c_{SPL} < 1.0$ and $z = \hat{z}_{max}$) receives zero credit, concentrating the corrective signal.
 - This progress-aware reward is converted into a signed label $y = 2r_{\psi(s_i)} - 1 \in [-1, 1]$, providing a per-tuple training signal without requiring learned components or human annotation.
- **Directional Boundary Shaping (DBS)**:
 - Instead of using action likelihoods or critic networks, DBS directly shapes the flow velocity field.
 - For each tuple from the exploration buffer, the current policy's stochastic prediction $v_{\theta,t}$ and the old rollout policy's velocity $v_{old,t}$ are used.
 - Two mirrored velocity branches, $v^{\pm}_{\theta,t} = v_{old,t} \pm \beta \Delta v_t$, are constructed, where $\Delta v_t = v_{\theta,t} - v_{old,t}$ and $\beta$ controls shaping intensity.
 - These branches induce corresponding transition means $\mu^{\pm}_{\theta,t}$, and step-wise Mahalanobis errors $E^{\pm}_{\theta,t}$ against the observed next state $a_{next,t}$ are calculated.
 - The DBS loss, $L_{DBS}(\theta) = E_{(\cdot)\sim B} [Softplus(\frac{1}{2} y \cdot (E^{+}_{\theta,t} - E^{-}_{\theta,t}))]$, leverages the signed SPL label $y$ as a ranking constraint.
 - When $y>0$ (success), the loss drives $E^{+}_{\theta,t} < E^{-}_{\theta,t}$, steering the velocity field toward success-producing directions. When $y<0$ (failure), the inequality reverses, pushing it away from failure-producing directions. Softplus ensures smooth gradient magnitudes.
 - To prevent catastrophic forgetting of initial skills, DBS is regularized by the real-demonstration SFT anchor: $L_{total}(\theta; D_{real}) = L_{DBS}(\theta) + \lambda L_{SFT}(\theta; D_{real})$.
 - The rollout policy is updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA), and the exploration buffer is cleared after each iteration to maintain on-policy shaping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The DLS pipeline was evaluated on three real-robot manipulation tasks: Pick & Place, Sweep, and Plugin, using two flow-based VLA backbones ($\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$). Evaluation involved 30 real-robot rollouts per policy under in-distribution (ID) and unseen conditions (altered lighting, background, and randomized object positions).

**Key Quantitative Results:**

1. **Critic-free boundary shaping outperforms baselines, with increased margins under distribution shift:**
 - DLS achieved the highest average success rates across both backbones and conditions: 84.4% / 72.2% (ID / Unseen) on $\pi_{0.5}$ and 78.9% / 71.1% on $\pi_0$.
 - Compared to PPO (a standard online RL baseline), DLS showed improved performance, with the margin widening under unseen conditions. For $\pi_{0.5}$, DLS outperformed PPO by +3.3% ID and +4.4% Unseen. For $\pi_0$, it improved by +1.1% ID and +5.5% Unseen. This was achieved without requiring a critic head or intra-group rollout comparisons.
 - Direct RL methods applied without the Stage 1 real-grounded behavioral prior resulted in near-zero success rates, confirming that a competent prior is essential for effective failure-boundary discovery.

2. **DLS with 50 real demonstrations surpasses double-budget SFT baselines:**
 - DLS, utilizing only 50 real demonstrations, consistently exceeded the success rates of Real-only SFT policies trained with 100 real demonstrations under unseen conditions. For example, on $\pi_{0.5}$, DLS achieved 72.2% (Unseen), an absolute margin of +17.8% over Real-only SFT (N=100) which achieved 54.4%.
 - This indicates that on-policy simulation rollouts are more effective at surfacing diverse failure modes and expanding the competence boundary than simply scaling up fixed real demonstration datasets. Real demonstrations primarily serve as a grounding anchor, while the digital twin provides the necessary scale for failure-boundary coverage.

3. **Distributional robustness gains consistently exceed in-distribution gains:**
 - On the Plugin task with $\pi_{0.5}$, DLS achieved 90.0% (ID) and 70.0% (Unseen) success rates, representing significant gains over the Sim-Real SFT prior (+33.3% ID and +36.7% Unseen).
 - This pattern, where unseen gains often surpassed in-distribution gains, suggests that SPL-guided shaping contributes to structural robustness against out-of-distribution conditions rather than merely promoting task-specific memorization.

**Ablation Studies:**

1. **SPL vs. Binary Reward:** Comparison of training curves demonstrated that Semantic Progress Localization (SPL) consistently yielded faster convergence and higher final success rates across all tasks compared to binary reward signals. Binary rewards, by conflating different types of progress into a single bit, led to less reliable convergence. This confirms SPL's role in localizing the exact point of failure for targeted correction.

2. **SPL vs. Alternative Progress-aware Reward Designs:** SPL outperformed alternative progress-aware designs (step-count penalty and distance-based potential) on $\pi_{0.5}$. SPL achieved over 6.7% higher success rates while also reducing average episode length comparably to a step-count penalty. This validates that semantic phase localization is critical for both higher task success and more efficient task completion.

3. **Sensitivity to SFT Regularization Weight ($\lambda$):** An inverted-U relationship was observed between the regularization weight $\lambda$ and success rate. Removing the SFT anchor ($\lambda=0.0$) caused catastrophic forgetting of the real-grounded prior. Performance peaked in the $\lambda \in [0.5, 1.0]$ range, then mildly declined with over-regularization. This supports the dual-objective design, balancing foundational skill retention with DBS-driven exploration.

4. **Data Efficiency under Few-shot Regime:** DLS consistently surpassed Real-only SFT success rates (Unseen) at various real demonstration counts. This further validates that on-policy failure discovery in simulation effectively compensates for sparse real-world data, expanding competence boundaries that fixed datasets cannot anticipate.

**Additional Ablations:**

1. **Behavioral Prior Ablation:** The ablation showed that the Sim-Real SFT prior (Stage 1) was crucial. Policies without SFT or trained with only real-only SFT or sim-only SFT performed significantly worse. This indicated that both simulation coverage for exploration and real-world grounding for deployment domain alignment are indispensable.
2. **Sensitivity to Sim-Real Co-training Ratio ($\alpha$):** Performance peaked around $\alpha=0.5$. A real-dominated prior ($\alpha \rightarrow 0.1$) degraded performance more significantly than a sim-dominated one ($\alpha \rightarrow 0.9$), suggesting that achieving broad exploration coverage is a more stringent constraint, while insufficient real grounding can be partially mitigated by subsequent regularization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DLS pipeline presents a significant advancement in the field of robust robot manipulation with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by explicitly addressing the problem of learning from policy failures. Its core contribution is the reframing of post-SFT adaptation as "Failure-Boundary Learning," which moves beyond merely fitting expert demonstrations to actively identifying and mitigating the conditions that lead to task breakdown.

**Significance:**
- **Novel Learning Paradigm:** The work introduces a new paradigm for VLA adaptation that focuses on where policy behavior breaks down rather than solely on where it succeeds. This perspective is formalized into a coherent Discover–Localize–Shape pipeline.
- **Critic-Free Flow Shaping:** DLS provides an effective method for shaping flow-based VLA dynamics directly in the velocity field, bypassing the complexities of action log-likelihoods required by traditional policy gradient methods and avoiding the challenges (e.g., overfitting, reward hacking) associated with learned critic networks.
- **Granular Failure Signal Generation:** Semantic Progress Localization (SPL) is a key innovation, transforming coarse binary trajectory outcomes into rich, progress-aware, phase-localized failure signals. This mechanism leverages privileged simulator states to automatically generate diagnostic and corrective labels without requiring human annotation or learned components, providing a higher-resolution understanding of task progression and failure modes.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Data Efficiency:** The experimental results demonstrate that DLS significantly improves policy robustness against randomized initial states and unseen visual conditions, a critical challenge for real-world deployment. Notably, DLS achieves superior performance with fewer real-world demonstrations (N=50) compared to SFT baselines trained on twice the amount of real data (N=100), highlighting its data efficiency.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The accompanying theoretical analysis rigorously establishes that SPL can uniquely identify the oracle failure phase from a scalar trajectory credit and that DBS gradients are aligned to suppress failure-inducing denoising perturbations and reinforce successful ones.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Reliable Robotic Systems:** By explicitly learning and shaping policies away from failure boundaries, DLS can lead to the development of more robust and reliable robotic manipulation systems that are better able to recover from unexpected deviations and operate in diverse, less structured environments.
- **Reduced Data Requirements:** The demonstrated data efficiency, particularly the ability to leverage simulation for scalable failure discovery, can substantially reduce the need for extensive and costly real-world data collection, accelerating the development and deployment cycles for robotic applications.
- **Bridging Sim-to-Real Gaps:** The methodology effectively utilizes the strengths of digital twins by exploiting privileged simulator information to generate high-quality training signals, thus contributing to more effective Sim-to-Real transfer strategies.
- **Influence on Future VLA Research:** The proposed framework and the concept of Failure-Boundary Learning may inspire future research in VLA model adaptation, especially in developing new methods for extracting granular feedback from environments and integrating it into policy optimization.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges two primary limitations. Firstly, the effectiveness of DLS is contingent upon the **physical fidelity of the digital twin**. Significant discrepancies between simulation and the real world (sim-to-real gaps) could degrade the quality of SPL labels, particularly for tasks demanding high contact precision, potentially leading to policies that perform suboptimally in reality. Secondly, the current pipeline requires **pre-existing simulation assets for each task object**. Extending DLS to novel objects would necessitate additional engineering overhead, such as 3D scanning or reconstruction, which may limit its immediate out-of-the-box applicability in highly dynamic or unstructured environments with unknown objects.
