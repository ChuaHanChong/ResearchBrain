# ChatVLA-2: Vision-Language-Action Model with Open-World Embodied Reasoning from Pretrained Knowledge

> [!summary] Summary
> ChatVLA-2, developed by Midea Group and East China Normal University, introduces a Vision-Language-Action model designed to address the erosion of pre-trained knowledge in VLMs during fine-tuning for robotic tasks. The model achieves an 82.7% manipulation success rate in novel mathematical reasoning tasks and an 81.4% success rate in open-world spatial reasoning tasks on real robots, demonstrating enhanced generalization in out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems experience "erosion of pre-trained knowledge" from their Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones during fine-tuning for robotic control tasks.
- This degradation limits VLA models' ability to generalize to "open-world" scenarios involving novel objects, unseen instructions, or unexpected environmental variations.
- Current chain-of-thought (CoT) methodologies for embodied reasoning are often explicitly trained, confining their reasoning capabilities to the knowledge within their training datasets and limiting true out-of-distribution generalization.

## Method

- Introduces a ==Dynamic Mixture-of-Experts== (MoE) within the VLM backbone to explicitly disentangle conflicting feature spaces while adaptively preserving shared representations, preventing the erosion of pre-trained knowledge.
- Implements a Reasoning Following Enhancement Module, incorporated into the latter half layers of the action expert, to ensure robotic actions accurately reflect the model's internal reasoning, especially for diverse, novel reasoning types.
- Employs a two-stage training strategy: co-training with image-text and robot data to establish open-world reasoning capabilities, followed by freezing the VLM backbone and training only the action expert to enhance reasoning-following and action consistency.

## Results

- Achieved an **82.7%** manipulation success rate in real-robot mathematical reasoning tasks involving equations and typography unseen during training, a substantial improvement over comparison methods that showed near-zero effectiveness.
- Demonstrated an **81.4%** average success rate in open-world spatial reasoning tasks, representing a 3.52-times improvement over DexVLA, by effectively handling novel target objects, reference objects, and directional instructions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of the Dynamic Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) in preventing knowledge erosion and the two-stage training strategy in developing open-world reasoning and ensuring actions accurately follow that reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Successfully mitigating "erosion of pre-trained knowledge" requires architectural innovations that disentangle multimodal understanding and robotic manipulation feature spaces.
> - Explicitly aligning robotic actions with the VLM's internal reasoning, particularly for out-of-distribution reasoning, is crucial for robust generalization.
> - A multi-stage training approach, starting with co-training of VLM knowledge and robot data, then focusing on reasoning-to-action alignment, effectively builds and preserves open-world embodied reasoning.
