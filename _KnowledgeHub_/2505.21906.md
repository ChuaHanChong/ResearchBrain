# ChatVLA-2: Vision-Language-Action Model with Open-World Embodied Reasoning from Pretrained Knowledge

> [!summary] Summary
> ChatVLA-2, developed by Midea Group and East China Normal University, introduces a Vision-Language-Action model designed to address the erosion of pre-trained knowledge in VLMs during fine-tuning for robotic tasks. The model achieves an 82.7% manipulation success rate in novel mathematical reasoning tasks and an 81.4% success rate in open-world spatial reasoning tasks on real robots, demonstrating enhanced generalization in out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems experience "erosion of pre-trained knowledge" from their Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones during fine-tuning for robotic control tasks.
- This degradation limits VLA models' ability to generalize to "open-world" scenarios involving novel objects, unseen instructions, or unexpected environmental variations.
- Current chain-of-thought (CoT) methodologies for embodied reasoning are often explicitly trained, confining their reasoning capabilities to the knowledge within their training datasets and limiting true out-of-distribution generalization.

## Method

- Introduces a ==Dynamic Mixture-of-Experts== (MoE) within the VLM backbone to explicitly disentangle conflicting feature spaces while adaptively preserving shared representations, preventing the erosion of pre-trained knowledge.
- Implements a Reasoning Following Enhancement Module, incorporated into the latter half layers of the action expert, to ensure robotic actions accurately reflect the model's internal reasoning, especially for diverse, novel reasoning types.
- Employs a two-stage training strategy: co-training with image-text and robot data to establish open-world reasoning capabilities, followed by freezing the VLM backbone and training only the action expert to enhance reasoning-following and action consistency.

## Results

- Achieved an **82.7%** manipulation success rate in real-robot mathematical reasoning tasks involving equations and typography unseen during training, a substantial improvement over comparison methods that showed near-zero effectiveness.
- Demonstrated an **81.4%** average success rate in open-world spatial reasoning tasks, representing a 3.52-times improvement over DexVLA, by effectively handling novel target objects, reference objects, and directional instructions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of the Dynamic Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) in preventing knowledge erosion and the two-stage training strategy in developing open-world reasoning and ensuring actions accurately follow that reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Successfully mitigating "erosion of pre-trained knowledge" requires architectural innovations that disentangle multimodal understanding and robotic manipulation feature spaces.
> - Explicitly aligning robotic actions with the VLM's internal reasoning, particularly for out-of-distribution reasoning, is crucial for robust generalization.
> - A multi-stage training approach, starting with co-training of VLM knowledge and robot data, then focusing on reasoning-to-action alignment, effectively builds and preserves open-world embodied reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of robotics, specifically within the domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLAs represent a significant paradigm shift from traditional imitation learning methods in robotics. By integrating powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) – such as PaliGemma or Qwen-VL – VLAs aim to equip robots with enhanced capabilities for understanding complex instructions, perceiving the environment, and executing intricate physical tasks. The success of VLAs stems from their ability to leverage the vast knowledge embedded in large-scale multimodal pre-training, enabling robots to move beyond rigid, task-specific programming.

However, a critical challenge faced by existing end-to-end VLA systems is the "erosion of pre-trained knowledge." As these models are fine-tuned for specific robotic control tasks, they often lose core competencies inherited from their VLM backbones, such as general object recognition, spatial reasoning, mathematical problem-solving, or text comprehension. This degradation limits their ability to generalize to "open-world" scenarios – tasks involving novel objects, unseen instructions, or unexpected environmental variations that were not explicitly present in their robotics training data. The paper highlights that even models like ChatVLA (a predecessor by some of the same authors) suffer from this issue, failing at tasks that are trivial for humans if not precisely in the training dataset.

Current research on embodied reasoning in VLAs often employs "chain-of-thought" (CoT) methodologies, inspired by Large Language Models (LLMs). While CoT approaches (e.g., Embodied-CoT, CoA-VLA, CoT-VLA, DiffusionVLA, DexVLA, π0.5) have shown promise in enabling more sophisticated reasoning, their reasoning capabilities are typically *explicitly trained* and thus remain confined to the knowledge within their training datasets. This limits their true generalization to novel, out-of-distribution scenarios.

ChatVLA-2 directly addresses this fundamental limitation. Its contribution lies in proposing a novel architecture and training strategy specifically designed to *preserve and actively leverage* the pre-trained open-world knowledge from the VLM backbone, rather than letting it degrade during fine-tuning. By doing so, it aims to enable end-to-end robotic systems to generalize across diverse tasks that traditionally require explicit planning or extensive, specific demonstrations. This work represents a significant step towards developing truly generalizable robotic foundation models that can autonomously reason and act in complex, unstructured environments, moving beyond the brittle nature of data-dependent imitation learning. It positions itself as an advancement over state-of-the-art imitation learning methods like OpenVLA, DexVLA, and π0, particularly in open-world reasoning and understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of ChatVLA-2 is to build a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that can effectively retain and leverage the vast pre-trained knowledge of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to achieve superior generalization in robotic control, especially in open-world scenarios. This directly counters the common problem where fine-tuning VLAs for robotic tasks leads to an "erosion" of their inherent VLM capabilities.

The authors identify two fundamental principles to achieve this generalization:

1. **Identifying Overlapping Feature Spaces and Disentangling Conflicting Ones:** VLA models deal with two distinct types of data: image-text data (for multimodal understanding) and robotic control data (for physical action). These often compete for shared parameter spaces within a model, leading to performance degradation in one domain when optimizing for the other. The objective is to design an architecture that allows for the separation of task-specific features (e.g., dedicated experts for multimodal understanding and robotic control) while adaptively identifying and preserving mutually beneficial features (e.g., spatial reasoning) that are crucial for both.
2. **Ensuring VLA Models Act According to Their Internal Reasoning:** While VLAs may possess sophisticated internal reasoning capabilities, it is not guaranteed that their generated robotic actions accurately reflect or follow this reasoning. The motivation here is to develop mechanisms that strongly align the robot's physical actions with the model's internal thought processes, particularly for novel or out-of-distribution reasoning. This "reasoning following" is critical for robust generalization beyond seen training data.

In essence, ChatVLA-2 is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of pre-trained VLMs in robotics, enabling robots to handle tasks requiring common sense, mathematical reasoning, and novel spatial understanding without requiring bespoke training data for every single variation. The goal is not to create an "omnipotent" robot but to demonstrate the feasibility of leveraging foundational VLM knowledge for enhanced generalization in end-to-end robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ChatVLA-2 introduces a novel VLA model architecture coupled with a specialized two-stage training pipeline to achieve open-world embodied reasoning.

#### 3.1. Foundational Model and Backbone

The authors adopt **DexVLA** as their foundational model architecture, specifically employing the **Qwen2-VL** model as its core VLM. The image encoders project visual observations into the same embedding space as language tokens. For multi-camera views, visual embeddings are concatenated. The VLM produces reasoning and action tokens. The action tokens are processed by a projection module (two linear layers and a LayerNorm). The pre-trained **1B ScaleDP** module serves as the action expert. DexVLA's ability to output unstructured textual reasoning is key, as it allows ChatVLA-2 to harness the VLM's pre-trained knowledge effectively.

#### 3.2. Core Architectural Innovations

- **Dynamic Mixture-of-Experts (MoE):**
 - **Rationale:** Dense VLA models often experience "erosion" of multimodal comprehension due to parameter space conflicts between multimodal understanding and robotic manipulation tasks. Static or shared experts would disrupt the original VLM structure (Qwen2-VL lacks native MoE), degrading pre-trained knowledge.
 - **Approach:** ChatVLA-2 integrates a **Dynamic MoE** within the VLM backbone. This design explicitly disentangles conflicting feature spaces while adaptively identifying and preserving shared representations.
 - **Mechanism:** An adaptive routing strategy dynamically selects expert modules based on visual and textual input characteristics. Some experts specialize in task-specific features (multimodal understanding, robot control), learning dedicated representations. Others capture mutually beneficial features shared across tasks (e.g., spatial reasoning). The gating network intelligently evaluates input data to select the most appropriate subset of experts for activation.
 - **Implementation:** Utilizes a total of **eight experts**, dynamically selecting **two experts** during inference. MLP layers for experts are initialized with pre-trained MLP weights.

- **Reasoning Following Enhancement Module:**
 - **Rationale:** To ensure robotic actions accurately reflect the model's internal reasoning, especially for diverse, novel reasoning types not encountered in training. Previous methods (e.g., DiffusionVLA, DexVLA) often rely on FiLM layers for in-domain reasoning, which is insufficient for out-of-distribution scenarios.
 - **Approach:** Replaces the original observation embedding with reasoning tokens projected through an MLP. This reasoning representation is combined with current timestep embeddings to condition the generation of scale and shift parameters for the action expert.
 - **Key Design Choice:** This mechanism is incorporated **exclusively into the latter half layers** of the action expert. This aligns with prior findings (PointVLA, GR00T N1) suggesting that modifications to deeper layers have less impact on robot control stability, allowing robust handling of open-world reasoning without sacrificing in-domain accuracy.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Strategy

- **Rationale:** Mixing image-text data and robot data during training can make controlling the learning process challenging. A dual-stage strategy enhances smoothness of robotic control and task success rates.

- **Stage 1: Empowering VLA with Open-World Embodied Reasoning and Understanding (Co-training):**
 - **Goal:** Establish connections between pre-trained multimodal knowledge and robotic actions.
 - **Data:**
 - **Image-Text Data:** COCO, TextVQA, GQA (large-scale datasets for general VLM capabilities).
 - **Robotics-related Image-Text Pairs:** Custom dataset for fine-tuning, including 2k samples from simulated RoboPoint (to enhance spatial understanding despite lower visual quality) and 5k samples from real-world tabletop/broader scenes, annotated with question-answering structures similar to LLaVA. Text augmentation is applied to increase query diversity.
 - **Robot Data:** 600 trajectories from a math-matching game and 300 from a toy placement experiment. All robot data is annotated with sub-reasoning phrases (initialized with fixed templates and augmented by GPT-4o for flexibility).
 - **Process:** Co-trains the model on both image-text and robot data. The image-text to robot data ratio is maintained at 1:3. Training for 50k steps with a cosine learning rate scheduler.

- **Stage 2: Enhancing Reasoning-Following in VLA:**
 - **Goal:** Strengthen the connection between reasoning and action, ensuring actions accurately follow and execute reasoning outcomes for generalizable robot control, especially for unseen reasoning.
 - **Process:** The entire VLM backbone (including the dynamic MoE and visual encoder) is **frozen**. Only the action expert remains trainable. This forces the action expert to learn to generate actions consistent with the internal reasoning derived from the VLM, preserving the pre-trained knowledge from Stage 1 while improving responsiveness to novel reasoning scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validates ChatVLA-2 through extensive real-robot experiments, specifically designed to assess its open-world reasoning and understanding capabilities beyond what current simulation benchmarks can capture.

#### 4.1. Mathematical Reasoning: Math Matching Game

- **Task:** Robot interprets a handwritten mathematical equation on a whiteboard and picks the corresponding numerical answer card from a table.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Manipulation success rate, OCR score (recognizing handwritten numbers, card values, and signs), and Mathematical Reasoning score (correct answer and card selection).
- **Key Findings:**
 - **In-Domain Performance:** ChatVLA-2 shows comparable performance to state-of-the-art models like DexVLA and π0 in in-domain scenarios, achieving near-perfect scores for OCR, math reasoning, and manipulation success.
 - **Open-World Performance (Out-of-Distribution):** This is where ChatVLA-2 demonstrates significant superiority. When presented with mathematical equations entirely absent from the training data (unseen equations and typography), most comparison methods (Octo, Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA, GR00T N1, ChatVLA, π0) almost completely fail, achieving near-zero manipulation success rates. In stark contrast, ChatVLA-2 achieves:
 - **82.7% manipulation success rate** (43/52 trials), representing a substantial leap from zero effectiveness.
 - **3.58/4 OCR accuracy** and **1.73/2 mathematical reasoning accuracy**.
 - **Conclusion:** ChatVLA-2 successfully handles novel mathematical equations and unfamiliar typography, effectively leveraging pre-trained VLM knowledge for tasks it was not explicitly trained on.

#### 4.2. Spatial Reasoning: Toy Placement

- **Task:** Robot picks up a toy and places it at specific positions relative to various reference objects (e.g., right, left, front, behind, top, or bottom of objects). Many objects and directional instructions are entirely unseen during training.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Robot action success rate, open-world object recognition performance (bounding box output for target objects), and spatial affordance.
- **Key Findings:**
 - **In-Domain Performance:** ChatVLA-2 performs comparably to DexVLA and π0 in familiar settings, achieving high success rates.
 - **Open-World Performance:** In scenarios with entirely unseen target and reference objects, and novel spatial instructions, ChatVLA-2 again significantly outperforms competitors.
 - It achieves an **81.4% average success rate** (127/156 trials), a **3.52-times improvement over DexVLA**.
 - It demonstrates strong **0.94 object recognition** and **0.88 spatial affordance** in open-world settings.
 - **Conclusion:** The model exhibits strong spatial reasoning capabilities and effective reasoning-following, interpreting and executing novel directional instructions involving previously unseen objects.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Importance:**
 - Experiments confirm that the Dynamic MoE is crucial. Without it (using Static/Shared MoE or dense models), open-world reasoning capabilities (OCR, Math) and manipulation success rates significantly drop. This validates the hypothesis that MoE effectively disentangles conflicting feature spaces, preventing knowledge erosion. Even larger dense models (7B parameters) failed to alleviate these conflicts.
 - The choice of 8 experts with 2 active (Top-k) proves most effective for balancing generalization and resource consumption, as increasing/decreasing these numbers showed a decrease in performance.
- **Two-Stage Training Strategy Importance:**
 - **Stage 1 (Co-training)** is critical for developing open-world reasoning capabilities. Without it, the model shows near-zero open-world OCR and mathematical reasoning scores.
 - **Stage 2 (Reasoning-Following Training)** is vital for effectively translating generated reasoning into actionable steps. Without Stage 2, open-world robot control performance dropped from 82.7% to 23%, indicating that while reasoning might be generated, it's not effectively injected into action execution.
- **Reasoning-Following Enhancement Module Injection Layers:** Injecting the reasoning tokens into the *latter half layers* of the action expert is optimal. Full-layer or former-half-layer injection significantly increases action instability and reduces success rates, suggesting that early layers of the action expert rely on the original observation embedding for critical information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ChatVLA-2 represents a substantial advancement in the field of robotic foundation models, particularly in addressing the critical challenge of enabling robots to perform generalized, open-world tasks.

**Significance:**
- **Overcoming Knowledge Erosion:** The most significant contribution is successfully mitigating the "erosion of pre-trained knowledge" that plagues existing VLA models. By retaining and actively leveraging the rich, innate reasoning and understanding abilities of large VLMs, ChatVLA-2 allows robots to perform tasks requiring general intelligence (like mathematical problem-solving or interpreting novel spatial relationships) that they were never explicitly trained on in a robotics context. This marks a "significant advancement from zero to effective generalization capability" in challenging open-world scenarios.
- **Novel Architecture and Training Paradigm:** The introduction of a dynamic Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) integrated atop a pre-trained VLM backbone, along with a specialized two-stage training strategy and a targeted reasoning-following enhancement module, provides a robust framework. These innovations effectively manage conflicting task requirements, disentangle feature spaces, and ensure that the robot's actions are faithfully aligned with its internal reasoning.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Generalization:** The rigorous real-robot experiments in math matching and toy placement tasks, especially under out-of-distribution conditions, provide compelling evidence of ChatVLA-2's superior generalization capabilities compared to state-of-the-art methods.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** By leveraging pre-trained VLM knowledge, ChatVLA-2 could significantly reduce the need for extensive, task-specific robot demonstration data, accelerating the deployment of robotic systems in new and varied environments.
- **Enhanced Autonomy and Adaptability:** Robots equipped with ChatVLA-2's capabilities would be far more autonomous and adaptable, capable of handling unforeseen objects, interpreting novel instructions, and performing tasks in dynamic, unstructured real-world settings without human intervention or re-training. This is crucial for applications ranging from home assistance to agile manufacturing and logistics.
- **Foundation for Generalist Robots:** This work contributes directly to the vision of "generalist robots" or "robot foundation models" that can learn a wide range of skills and apply them across diverse scenarios, analogous to how large language models have revolutionized natural language processing.
- **Bridging AI Reasoning and Embodied Action:** It further bridges the gap between high-level AI reasoning capabilities (traditionally found in LLMs/VLMs) and low-level embodied robotic control, moving towards truly intelligent agents that can think and act coherently in the physical world.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges that it is currently "unable to fully retain the pre-trained knowledge from VLM," observing that some capacity loss is "inevitable." This highlights a persistent challenge in integrating large pre-trained models into specialized domains. Future work aims to expand the embodiment beyond tabletop tasks to mobile manipulators, enabling more long-horizon and complex real-world tasks, further pushing the boundaries of generalizable robotic control.
