# Diversity You Can Actually Measure: A Fast, Model-Free Diversity Metric for Robotics Datasets

> [!summary] Summary
> FAKTUAL measures robotics-dataset diversity by computing Shannon and von Neumann entropy on the eigenvalues of a signature-kernel Gram matrix over demonstrations, then greedily selecting an entropy-maximizing (plus determinant-maximizing) subset. The metric is model-free, needs no policy or rollouts, and curated subsets beat random selection while often matching or exceeding the full dataset across RoboMimic, MetaWorld, and four real robot-arm tasks.

## Problem

- No accepted diversity measure exists for robot demonstration data: trajectories vary in length and mix states, actions, and high-dimensional video, defeating pointwise distance metrics.
- Existing curation methods train auxiliary scoring models or require online policy rollouts, incurring upfront cost comparable to training the target imitation policy itself.
- That cost confines prior curation to small task-specific fine-tuning sets, and existing selection pipelines never explicitly preserve dataset diversity or coverage density.

## Method

- Signature-kernel entropy: demonstrations become paths (ViT image embeddings concatenated with states and actions), and Shannon or von Neumann entropy on the normalized Gram matrix eigenvalues defines diversity.
- FAKTUAL curation greedily approximates the NP-hard entropy-maximizing m-subset, then mixes in a determinant-maximizing volume subset by proportion p to retain informative extreme demonstrations.
- Fully model-free pipeline with no auxiliary scorer, policy access, or rollouts; kernel computed by truncated signatures via dynamic programming or random Fourier features, or the untruncated PDE kernel trick.

## Results

- RoboMimic Can (PH) at 120 demos reaches 95% success versus 86% for random and 90% for the full 180-demo dataset; low-dim variant gives 86±3 versus 68±7.
- Signature entropy correlates strongly with downstream success across RoboMimic tasks, Pearson r of 0.74 to 1.00; two of four real SO-ARM101 tasks beat the full dataset.
- Ablations: curating with a global alignment kernel only matches random selection; on hard Transport at 60 demos FAKTUAL scores 8% versus random 9%, and rollout-informed baselines stay stronger.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reparameterization-invariant trajectory kernels supply a similarity notion respecting path geometry, making entropy computable over variable-length multimodal demonstrations without any learned scoring model.
> - Diversity-aware selection overtakes random only above a task-dependent demo budget; harder tasks need local density first, so pruning redundancy helps late, not early.
> - Diversity curation is complementary to, not a replacement for, quality curation, and can backfire when the policy is too weak to absorb multimodal data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the challenge of quantifying and leveraging dataset diversity within the field of robotics, particularly for imitation learning (IL). Deep learning advancements in various domains have highlighted dataset diversity as a crucial factor for model performance, alongside architectural innovations and scale. In robotics, imitation learning increasingly relies on large datasets, yet a widely accepted and principled method for measuring diversity in such data remains elusive.

The difficulty in quantifying diversity in robotics datasets stems from two primary characteristics: (1) demonstrations consist of long-horizon trajectories of variable lengths, encompassing multiple modalities like states, actions, and high-dimensional observations (e.g., RGB video); and (2) a suitable diversity metric must define similarity between demonstrations in a way that respects their underlying sequential and geometric structure, rather than relying on simple pointwise distances.

Existing data curation strategies in robotics, such as DemInf, Cupid, and Demo-SCORE, often involve training auxiliary models or require access to policy rollouts to estimate demonstration utility or quality. These methods can be computationally intensive and incur significant upfront costs, making them less practical for large-scale datasets or early-stage dataset development.

This paper draws upon the mathematical framework of the signature transform and its associated kernel, which provides a robust method for representing and comparing paths or trajectories of variable length while preserving their geometric and sequential properties. Prior work has applied signature-based methods to problems like trajectory optimization and human action recognition. The research also builds upon concepts from information theory, specifically Shannon and von Neumann entropy, and connects to the Vendi score, a kernel-based diversity metric developed in general machine learning.

The work's contribution to the landscape is the introduction of a fast, model-free diversity metric specifically tailored for robotics datasets, which leverages the signature kernel, and a corresponding data curation algorithm. This approach aims to provide a computationally efficient alternative or complement to existing quality-based or model-dependent curation methods, addressing a gap in scalable dataset management for robot imitation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to characterize the relationship between the diversity of a demonstration dataset and the performance of a closed-loop robotic imitation learning policy, and to leverage this relationship to develop a diversity-based data curation method.

The motivation for this work is multifaceted:
- **Complexity of Robotics Data**: Robotics demonstrations, particularly for imitation learning, are complex. They involve long sequences of states, actions, and high-dimensional observations (e.g., video) with varying lengths. Traditional diversity metrics often struggle to capture meaningful differences in such structured and multimodal data, as they may not adequately account for the temporal and geometric relationships within trajectories.
- **Importance of Diversity**: While scaling dataset size and improving model architectures have driven significant advances in deep learning and robotics, the diversity of training data is also a critical determinant of model generalization and performance. Anecdotal evidence from large-scale robotics datasets suggests that sheer quantity alone is insufficient; datasets can contain redundant or sub-optimal behaviors that may hinder learning, even if all demonstrations technically succeed. A well-curated, diverse dataset can provide broader coverage of behaviors and states, leading to more robust policies.
- **Limitations of Existing Curation Methods**: Current data curation strategies in robotics often depend on training auxiliary models to score demonstrations (e.g., based on quality or informativeness) or require running costly policy rollouts. These approaches are computationally expensive and can add significant overhead, sometimes comparable to the cost of training the main imitation policy itself. This limits their applicability, especially for large, multi-task datasets or in scenarios where rapid, offline curation is desired.
- **Need for a Principled and Efficient Metric**: There is a recognized need for a principled, computationally cheap, and model-free diversity metric that operates directly on demonstration datasets. Such a metric would not require access to the imitation policy or its rollouts, making it highly efficient and scalable. If diversity can be reliably and cheaply computed, it could serve as a practical dataset-level signal for curation, complementing quality-based approaches.
- **Hypothesized Correlation**: The authors hypothesize that a measure of dataset diversity, specifically one that captures the "information content" or "spread" of trajectories, should positively correlate with the success rate of a policy trained on that dataset. If this correlation holds, such a measure could guide the selection of smaller, yet equally or more effective, subsets of demonstrations.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives are:
1. To develop a novel, principled, and computationally efficient method for measuring the entropy and diversity of robotics datasets. This method explicitly incorporates multimodal data (states, actions, observations) and respects trajectory structure by leveraging the signature kernel framework.
2. To propose **FAKTUAL (FAst trajectory Kernel enTropy cUration for imitation Learning)**, a data curation algorithm grounded in this new diversity metric. FAKTUAL aims to select a subset of demonstrations that maximizes entropy given a budget, thereby reducing redundancy and promoting uniform coverage of the demonstration space.
3. To empirically evaluate FAKTUAL across a range of simulated and real-world robot manipulation tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving downstream success rates compared to random selection and its computational efficiency relative to other data curation baselines.
4. To establish an empirical correlation between the proposed signature-based entropy and the success rate of trained imitation learning policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on defining and utilizing a novel signature transform-based entropy metric for robotics trajectories and integrating it into a model-free data curation algorithm.

**Core Technical Concepts:**
1. **Signature Transform and Signature Kernel**:
 - The signature transform maps a continuous path or trajectory into a countably infinite sequence of iterated integrals. This sequence captures the geometric and temporal ordering information of the path, offering an injective (for non-tree-like paths) and reparameterization-invariant representation.
 - The signature kernel is then defined as the inner product between the signatures of two paths. This provides a measure of similarity between trajectories that intrinsically respects their sequential and geometric structure, unlike simpler point-wise distance metrics.
 - **Practical Computation**: The full signature transform is infinite-dimensional. In practice, it is approximated by truncation at a finite level `L` (e.g., Equation 2). The paper discusses efficient computation methods for the signature kernel, including dynamic programming and approximation using Random Fourier Features (RFFs) (Appendix C), which allows for scaling to larger datasets. The untruncated signature kernel can also be computed by solving a linear second-order hyperbolic partial differential equation (Theorem 2).

2. **Converting Demonstrations to Paths**:
 - Robotics demonstrations often comprise multiple modalities. The paper proposes several methods to convert these into a single multivariate path suitable for signature transform:
 - **Flatten and Stacked**: At each timestep `t`, high-dimensional visual observations (e.g., RGB images) are first embedded into a fixed-dimensional vector (e.g., using a Vision Transformer (ViT) or extracting low-dimensional representations like point clouds). This embedding is then concatenated with other per-timestep modalities such as robot states, actions, and rewards to form a comprehensive feature vector `X_i(t)`. The sequence of these vectors forms a multivariate path.
 - **Convex Combination**: Alternatively, each modality (states, actions, observations) can be treated as an independent path, and a signature kernel is computed for each. These individual kernels are then combined via a convex combination to yield a composite kernel that integrates information from all modalities.
 - **Low-dim Convex Combination**: A simpler approach combines an RBF kernel (on initial observation embeddings) and a signature kernel (on end-effector paths).
 - **Practical Considerations**: Appending a monotonic "time" coordinate to paths can sometimes be useful to ensure non-tree-likeness for the signature transform. Sub-sampling timesteps can reduce computational load for very long trajectories. Recommendations are also provided for camera selection to avoid noise.

3. **Defining Entropy and Diversity Metrics**:
 - **Signature Shannon Entropy (H_sig_shannon)**: This is defined for a set of `n` demonstrations {d_1..., d_n} by computing the normalized signature kernel matrix K_sig/n. The Shannon entropy is then calculated as -Σ λ_i ln λ_i, where λ_i are the eigenvalues of this normalized kernel matrix. This formulation allows entropy to be computed directly from the dataset samples via their similarity kernel.
 - **Signature von Neumann Entropy (H_sig_von_neumann)**: This is the quantum analogue of Shannon entropy, defined as -Tr(ρ ln ρ), where ρ is the normalized signature kernel matrix. The paper proves that in this context, H_sig_shannon = H_sig_von_neumann, providing a unified information-theoretic measure of diversity.
 - **Determinant as Diversity (∆)**: The determinant of the signature kernel matrix, det(K_sig), is also used as a measure of diversity, representing the volume spanned by the feature embeddings of the samples in the Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS).

**FAKTUAL Data Curation Algorithm:**
FAKTUAL aims to select a subset of `m` demonstrations that maximizes the defined entropy (or determinant). This problem is known to be NP-Hard (a variant of maximum coverage). The approach utilizes approximate solutions:
- **Greedy Selection Strategy**: To handle the NP-hard nature, FAKTUAL employs a two-stage greedy selection strategy:
 1. A proportion `p` (e.g., 0.5-0.9) of the target subset size `m` is selected by iteratively adding the demonstration that maximally increases the signature entropy of the current subset. This promotes uniform randomness.
 2. The remaining `m(1-p)` demonstrations are selected from the *rest* of the full dataset by iteratively adding the demonstration that maximally increases the determinant (volume) of the current subset. This helps include potentially extreme or unique data points not covered by entropy maximization alone.
 3. The final curated dataset is the union of these two subsets.
- **Algorithms for m-Subset Maximization**: The paper refers to standard combinatorial optimization algorithms for approximating maximum coverage problems, such as Greedy Local Search (Algorithm 1) and Stochastic Greedy (Algorithm 2). For the determinant objective, log det(K_sig + μI) is used for numerical stability and its submodular property, which guarantees approximation bounds for greedy algorithms. Sampling from a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) is also mentioned as another principled way to select diverse subsets (Algorithm 3).

**Experimental Design:**
The method is evaluated on both simulated and real-world robot manipulation tasks:
- **Simulated Benchmarks**: RoboMimic (Can, Square, Transport tasks with Proficient-Human (PH) and Multi-Human (MH) datasets) and MetaWorld (Stick Push, Door Open, Shelf Place).
- **Real-World Benchmarks**: Four manipulation tasks (Drawer Open, Mug Drag, Pick-and-Place Marker, Organize Objects) using an SO-ARM101 robotic arm.
- **Imitation Learning Policies**: Various behavioral cloning (BC) architectures were used, including RNN-based, Diffusion Policy, and SmolVLA, to ensure generalizability of the curation method.
- **Baselines**: Performance is compared against random selection, and other data curation methods such as DemInf, Cupid, Demo-SCORE, a Success Similarity baseline, and an Oracle (approximating an upper bound).
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Policy success rates are computed over multiple rollouts and checkpoints and averaged across random seeds. The correlation between signature entropy and success rate is also quantified using Pearson correlation coefficients.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrated several key findings regarding the effectiveness and characteristics of the proposed signature-based diversity metric and the FAKTUAL data curation algorithm:

- **Improved Downstream Performance**: FAKTUAL generally improved or matched the success rates of imitation learning policies compared to random selection across a variety of simulated and real-world tasks.
 - On RoboMimic's Can task, FAKTUAL consistently outperformed random selection, especially for proficient-human (PH) datasets and at lower demonstration counts, indicating that diversity-aware curation can efficiently boost performance even with limited data.
 - For the Square task, the advantage of FAKTUAL over random selection was less pronounced but still consistent for PH datasets at higher demonstration counts, suggesting benefits when baseline data quality is already high.
 - In two out of three real-world manipulation tasks (Pick-and-place marker, Mug drag), the policy trained on a FAKTUAL-curated subset achieved higher success rates than the policy trained on the full dataset, implying that the method can effectively identify and remove redundant or less informative demonstrations.
- **Correlation between Diversity and Success**: A strong positive correlation was observed between the signature entropy of demonstration subsets and the downstream policy success rates across RoboMimic tasks (Pearson `r` ranging from 0.74 to 1.00). This empirical evidence supports the premise that higher dataset diversity, as measured by signature entropy, is associated with better policy performance.
- **Computational Efficiency**: FAKTUAL is model-free and does not require training auxiliary models or executing online policy rollouts. This design choice results in negligible computational overhead relative to policy training, making it substantially more efficient than existing data curation methods like DemInf, Cupid, and Demo-SCORE (Table I).
- **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - FAKTUAL generally performed better than simple random pruning and achieved performance comparable to DemoSCORE, another quality-informed method, in some scenarios.
 - However, FAKTUAL tended to perform below more computationally intensive, quality-informed baselines (e.g., Cupid, DemInf) when datasets contained a mix of high- and low-quality demonstrations (e.g., Multi-Human (MH) datasets). This highlights a trade-off: FAKTUAL offers a fast, model-free "good-enough" solution, while quality-aware methods may achieve higher peak performance at greater computational expense.
- **Task-Dependent Behavior**:
 - On harder tasks, especially with very small demonstration budgets, diversity-maximizing curation could sometimes perform comparably to or slightly below random selection. The benefit of discarding redundant demonstrations became more evident as the demonstration budget increased, suggesting that a certain minimum density of data might be crucial before diversity optimization becomes broadly beneficial.
 - For the "Pick-and-place marker" task, FAKTUAL curation approximately equalized the distribution of marker positions, mitigating biases in the original dataset.
 - In the "Drawer open" task, FAKTUAL did not effectively differentiate between varied lighting conditions, potentially due to the limitations of ViT embeddings or low-dimensional observations in capturing global illumination differences.
 - For goal-conditioned tasks like "Organize objects," FAKTUAL did not systematically favor trajectories ending in one goal configuration over another.
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - The optimal number of signature levels was found to be dataset-dependent. Increasing levels initially improved success, then led to a decrease in some cases, suggesting a balance between capturing coarse (low-level) and fine (high-level) geometric information, where excessive detail might amplify noise in rough trajectories.
 - The proportion parameter `p` in the two-stage greedy algorithm was also task-dependent, but values between 0.2 and 0.5 generally yielded improvements. Setting `p=0` (pure determinant maximization) also performed well in practice.
 - Comparison with the global alignment kernel showed that the signature kernel was more effective for diversity-based curation, as the global alignment kernel resulted in performance similar to random selection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel and practical approach to address the challenge of managing and improving the quality of robotics datasets for imitation learning. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novelty in Diversity Quantification**: The paper pioneers a principled, model-free, and computationally efficient method for measuring dataset diversity in robotics. By extending Shannon and von Neumann entropy concepts through the signature kernel, it provides a metric that intrinsically accounts for the complex, long-horizon, and multimodal nature of robotics trajectories, a notable advancement over prior approaches.
- **Practical Data Curation Tool**: FAKTUAL offers a fast and readily applicable data curation strategy for robot imitation learning. Its model-free nature eliminates the need for auxiliary models or costly policy rollouts, making it a valuable tool for efficiently curating large datasets. This can significantly reduce the computational burden and time associated with dataset preparation, particularly in scenarios involving extensive pre-training or frequent dataset updates.
- **Improved Policy Generalization and Efficiency**: By promoting diversity and reducing redundancy, FAKTUAL helps create more informative and balanced training datasets. The consistent improvements in downstream policy success rates, or maintaining performance with fewer demonstrations, suggest enhanced generalization capabilities and a more efficient utilization of collected data resources. This can lead to more robust and reliable robot policies in diverse environments.
- **Insight into Dataset Properties**: The demonstrated strong positive correlation between signature entropy and policy success rate provides a quantitative means to assess the "information content" or "spread" of a robotics dataset. This metric can serve as a diagnostic tool for researchers to understand the characteristics of their datasets and predict potential policy performance, guiding future data collection or augmentation strategies.
- **Complementary Approach**: The research positions FAKTUAL as a complementary tool to existing quality-based curation methods. It highlights the potential for combining diversity-aware selection with quality filtering to create datasets that are both broad in coverage and high in individual demonstration quality, leading to more comprehensive dataset optimization pipelines.
- **Catalyst for Future Research**: The work opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - Investigating strategies to combine diversity-based curation with existing quality-based methods to leverage the strengths of both approaches.
 - Exploring FAKTUAL's applicability in datasets that contain adversarial or pathological trajectories, where explicit quality filtering might be critical.
 - Deepening the understanding of the causal relationship between specific training examples and policy behavior, potentially integrating FAKTUAL with influence function-based methods.
 - Examining the interplay between dataset diversity and model capacity, especially in cases where promoting diversity might be detrimental if the model lacks the ability to capture multimodal data effectively.

In summary, this paper contributes a practical and theoretically grounded method for enhancing robotics imitation learning by addressing the previously challenging problem of measuring and leveraging dataset diversity. Its model-free and efficient nature positions it as a valuable asset for the scalable development of robot learning systems.
