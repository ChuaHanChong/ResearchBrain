# DISC: Decoupling Instruction from State-Conditioned Control via Policy Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> DISC introduces a robotic manipulation framework that generates an entire visuomotor policy's parameters from a natural language instruction, thereby structurally decoupling task specification from state-conditioned control. This approach prevents policies from relying on visual shortcuts and improves language grounding, achieving 94.3% success on LIBERO-90, an 7.7% improvement over strong entangled baselines, and demonstrating superior few-shot adaptation.

## Problem

- Current language-conditioned robotic manipulation policies use "task-state entangled" architectures, processing both linguistic instructions and visual observations through shared network parameters.
- This entanglement leads to "observation leakage," where policies learn scene-to-action shortcuts from visual cues correlated with task identity, bypassing true language grounding.
- Observation leakage results in policies that are brittle to instruction variations, fail to generalize across visual contexts, and degrade on complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- **DISC** employs a ==two-stage hypernetwork== that takes only a natural language instruction as input to generate the complete parameter set of a lightweight, task-specific visuomotor policy.
- The generated policy then operates exclusively on visual observations without direct access to the language instruction, ensuring structural decoupling of task specification from control.
- The hypernetwork includes a ==Weight Initialization Network (WIN)== and a learned ==iterative refinement module==, which incorporates ==optimization-inspired inductive biases== to generate high-quality, coherent policy parameters.

## Results

- DISC achieved **94.3%** overall success on LIBERO-90, outperforming the strongest trained-from-scratch entangled baseline (OTTER) by **7.7%** and remaining competitive with large-scale pretrained models like π0.5.
- It demonstrated superior sample efficiency and rapid convergence in few-shot adaptation to unseen tasks across various data regimes, particularly in extreme low-data settings.
- In real-world tests with identical visual contexts, DISC achieved the highest average success rate of **86.4%**, substantially outperforming Octo (**78.5%**) and confirming its reliance on language-generated parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structurally decoupling instruction processing from state-conditioned control inherently prevents observation leakage and enforces genuine language grounding in robotic policies.
> - Generating the entire parameter set for a task-specific policy from language creates a semantically structured parameter manifold, facilitating efficient few-shot adaptation to novel tasks.
> - Incorporating optimization-inspired inductive biases within a feed-forward hypernetwork can effectively generate high-dimensional, functionally coherent policy weights, leading to robust performance without extensive pretraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of language-conditioned robotic manipulation aims to enable robots to perform tasks specified by natural language instructions. Current approaches in this domain predominantly rely on "task-state entangled" architectures. These architectures process both the linguistic task instruction and the visual observations through shared network parameters. This includes a wide range of methods, such as Transformer-based models (e.g., RT-1, Gato, Octo, VQ-BeT, BAKU), which tokenize and concatenate multimodal inputs, and Diffusion-based methods (e.g., Diffusion Policy, DiT, RDT-1B, MoDE, DP3, 3D Diffuser Actor, PointVLA), which often integrate language by conditioning visual features or inputs. Even approaches like FiLM conditioning, which offer partial separation by modulating visual features with language embeddings, retain a large proportion of task-agnostic parameters.

The paper identifies a critical limitation in these entangled designs: they are susceptible to "observation leakage." This phenomenon occurs when networks learn to exploit visual cues that correlate with task identity in limited datasets, leading to scene-to-action shortcuts that bypass the semantic grounding of language instructions. This can result in policies that are brittle to instruction variations, fail to generalize across visual contexts, and degrade on complex, long-horizon tasks. Evidence from the broader vision-language community supports this pattern, manifesting as modality competition, visual neglect, and entity grounding failures.

DISC positions itself as an architectural alternative that structurally addresses task-state entanglement. It builds upon a distinct paradigm of using hypernetworks to generate task-specific policies, an area explored by works like HyPoGen, HyperZero, and Hyper-GoalNet. Unlike these prior hypernetwork approaches, which may use compact MLP generators or condition on goal images and observations, DISC focuses on generating *the entire parameter set* of a visuomotor policy solely from language and introduces a specific optimization-inspired hypernetwork architecture to achieve high-quality parameter generation. By completely decoupling the instruction processing from state-conditioned control, DISC aims to provide a structural guarantee against observation leakage and directly enforce language grounding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic manipulation policy that reliably treats the natural language task specification as the definitive source of task identity, rather than allowing visual cues to implicitly override or shortcut the instructed behavior. The authors propose an architectural solution called DISC (Decoupling Instruction from State-Conditioned Control via Policy Generation) to achieve this.

The motivation for DISC stems from two identified fundamental problems with current "task-state entangled" architectures in language-conditioned manipulation:

1. **Competing Functions within Shared Parameters:** Current policies process two qualitatively different inputs: a fixed instruction (specifying *what* task) and continuously evolving observations (capturing *what* the current state is). In entangled architectures, both inputs are handled by the same set of network parameters. For example, in transformer-based policies, language and visual tokens are concatenated and processed by identical layers; in diffusion policies, shared parameters learn both language understanding and visuomotor control. The authors argue that these two roles have conflicting demands: language understanding benefits from semantic abstraction and visual invariance, while visuomotor control requires sensitivity to precise spatial details. Forcing both functions through shared parameters creates an optimization tension that can degrade either or both core capabilities.

2. **Observation Leakage Enabling Shortcut Learning:** This is identified as a more severe issue. In robotics datasets, visual context often correlates strongly with task identity (e.g., a specific object or scene layout might frequently appear with certain tasks). Entangled architectures can exploit these correlations by learning direct mappings from visual patterns to actions, effectively bypassing the language instruction. This "shortcut learning" minimizes training loss without requiring the network to genuinely ground linguistic meaning. Consequently, policies developed this way may succeed in familiar visual scenes but fail when instructions change, when visual context is ambiguous, or on tasks requiring precise, multi-step execution. Examples provided include a policy approaching a microwave instead of a "white bowl" as instructed, or skipping a stove activation subgoal to directly place a pan, based on scene-level visual priors. These failures demonstrate that the policy is driven by visual shortcuts rather than explicit instruction following.

To overcome these limitations, the key objectives for DISC are:
- **Structural Decoupling:** To design an architecture where the task specification (language) and state observation are processed by entirely separate components, preventing any shared-parameter pathways for observation leakage.
- **Full Policy Parameter Generation:** To use a hypernetwork that processes *only* the language instruction to generate the *entire set* of parameters for a task-specific visuomotor policy. The generated policy then operates exclusively on visual observations without direct access to language.
- **Coherent Parameter Generation:** To address the challenge of generating high-dimensional, functionally coherent policy weights. This involves developing a two-stage hypernetwork with an iterative refinement module that incorporates optimization-inspired inductive biases in a feed-forward manner.

By achieving these objectives, DISC aims to ensure that task semantics unequivocally drive behavior and that policies exhibit improved language grounding, generalization, and adaptation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DISC’s methodology is centered on the architectural principle of decoupling instruction processing from state-conditioned control. This is achieved through a two-stage hypernetwork that generates the complete parameter set of a task-specific visuomotor policy from a language instruction.

#### 3.1. Decoupling Instruction from State-Conditioned Control

The core idea is to separate the network responsible for interpreting the task specification from the network performing visuomotor control.
- **Task Specification Processing:** A natural language instruction `l` is first encoded into a task embedding `e_l` using a frozen language encoder (e.g., T5-small).
- **Policy Parameter Generation:** A hypernetwork `H_ϕ` takes this task embedding `e_l` as its sole input and generates the complete set of parameters `θ_π` for a target policy.
- **Visuomotor Control:** The generated target policy `π(o_t; θ_π)` then maps visual observations `o_t` to actions `a_t` using only the generated parameters `θ_π`. Crucially, this target policy has no direct access to the language instruction.

This structural separation provides two key advantages:
1. **Elimination of Optimization Tension:** The hypernetwork specializes in interpreting task specifications, while the target policy specializes in visuomotor control. This prevents conflicting demands on shared parameters.
2. **Structural Guarantee Against Observation Leakage:** Since the target policy never directly accesses the instruction, task information can only influence behavior through the parameters `θ_π` generated by the hypernetwork. This structurally enforces that the hypernetwork must encode task semantics into the weights, eliminating pathways for visual shortcuts.

#### 3.2. Hypernetwork Architecture

Generating coherent, high-dimensional policy parameters from a task embedding is a significant challenge. DISC addresses this with a two-stage hypernetwork designed to incorporate the structural inductive bias of iterative optimization.

- **Stage 1: Weight Initialization Network (WIN):**
 - This component (`WIN_ϕ1`) maps the language embedding `e_l` to a coarse initial set of policy parameters `θ_π^(0)`.
 - The WIN aims to position the parameters in a semantically informed region of the weight space (e.g., distinguishing grasping tasks from pushing tasks).
 - In the implementation, a 4-layer transformer with cross-attention blocks is used for the WIN.

- **Stage 2: Learned Iterative Refinement Module:**
 - This module refines the initial parameters `θ_π^(0)` over `T` learned update steps. The update rule is `θ_π^(t+1) = θ_π^(t) + Δθ^(t)`, where `Δθ^(t) = f_ϕ2(θ_π^(t), e_l)`.
 - This is not a traditional gradient-based optimization. Instead, the refinement module `f_ϕ2` is a neural network with fixed, learned parameters `ϕ2`.
 - At inference time, the entire generation process is feed-forward, requiring only `T` passes through `f_ϕ2`.
 - The refinement module is architecturally decomposed to mirror the structure of optimization:
 - **Forward Pass Simulation:** `F_Forward` estimates task-specific activation patterns (`z_0, ..., z_n`) from the current parameters `θ_π^(t)` and `e_l`.
 - **Backward Pass Simulation:** `F_Backward` estimates pseudo-gradient signals (`∂L/∂z_n, ..., ∂L/∂z_1`) based on `θ_π^(t)`, `e_l`, and the simulated activations. These are learned signals, not analytical gradients.
 - **Meta-Update Network:** `F_Meta` computes the parameter updates `Δθ^(t)` using `θ_π^(t)`, `e_l`, and the pseudo-gradients.
 - **Implementation Details (from Appendix D):** Cross-attention mechanisms are used to model neural gradient computation. Parameter matrices are tokenized row-wise (`ω_i`), and activations are tokenized as individual neurons (`τ_i`). Cross-attention (`CrossAttn`) is used to simulate layer-wise forward computation (`τ_i = CrossAttn(ω_i, τ_{i-1}, τ_{i-1})`) and backward pass Jacobian estimation/chain rule computations.

#### 3.3. Target Policy

The generated parameters `θ_π` configure a compact visuomotor controller. Because this policy is specialized for a single task, it can be significantly smaller than monolithic architectures. In the implementation, the target policy is a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), facilitating efficient inference for high-frequency robotic control.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference

- **Training:** The entire DISC framework (hypernetwork parameters `ϕ = {ϕ1, ϕ2}` and the target policy) is trained end-to-end using a behavior cloning (BC) loss: `L_BC(ϕ) = E_((o_t,a_t,l)~D) ||π(o_t; H_ϕ(Φ_L(l))) - a_t||^2_2`. For each training sample, the hypernetwork generates `θ_π`, the target policy predicts actions, and the prediction error backpropagates through the entire generation process to update `ϕ`.
 - A ResNet-18 visual encoder (ImageNet pretrained, fine-tuned) and T5-small language encoder (frozen) are used.
 - AdamW optimizer, cosine annealing schedule, and mixed-precision training (BF16) are employed.
- **Inference:** For a new instruction, `e_l` is encoded once, `H_ϕ` runs forward once to generate `θ_π`, and then the compact target policy `π` executes in the environment using `θ_π` without further hypernetwork calls.

#### 3.5. Few-Shot Adaptation (Optional Capability)

DISC's decoupled architecture facilitates efficient adaptation to novel tasks with minimal demonstrations (`D_new`).
1. **Initialization:** The frozen hypernetwork `H_ϕ` generates an initial policy `θ_init_π` from the new instruction `l_new`. This initialization is semantically informed.
2. **Fine-tuning:** Only `θ_init_π` is fine-tuned on the few provided demonstrations `D_new` using the BC loss. The hypernetwork `H_ϕ` remains frozen. This transforms adaptation into a local refinement of a compact, well-initialized policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to validate DISC’s performance across multi-task learning, few-shot adaptation, language grounding, and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Multi-Task Performance (RQ1)

- **LIBERO-90:** DISC achieved an overall success rate of 94.3%, outperforming the strongest trained-from-scratch entangled baseline, OTTER (86.6%), by 7.7% absolute. This advantage was more pronounced on complex, long-horizon tasks, where DISC achieved 92.7% compared to OTTER's 84.3% (+8.4%). Notably, DISC, trained from scratch, surpassed the large-scale pretrained π0 (91.6%) and remained competitive with π0.5 (95.7%). Analysis showed DISC excelled on multi-step tasks requiring precise language-grounded sequencing, suggesting that architectures like π0.5, despite pretraining, might still exploit scene-level visual priors.
- **Broader LIBERO Suite:** DISC achieved the highest overall success rate of 98.7% across LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, and LIBERO-Goal suites, slightly exceeding both pretrained π0 and π0.5 without relying on large-scale pretraining.
- **Meta-World ML45:** DISC achieved 92.2% overall success, maintaining advantages across various manipulation primitives, including a 5.0% improvement over Octo on pick&place tasks. This indicates the benefits of decoupling generalize across diverse task categories.

#### 4.2. Few-Shot Adaptation to Unseen Tasks (RQ2)

- On held-out LIBERO-Spatial tasks, DISC demonstrated superior sample efficiency across various data regimes (K = 1, 3, 10, 20 demonstrations).
- In extreme few-shot settings (K=1, 3), DISC achieved non-trivial success rates where diffusion-based methods often showed near 0% performance.
- DISC exhibited rapid convergence, with performance curves rising sharply within the first 200 fine-tuning steps, outpacing baselines like Octo. This supports the claim that the hypernetwork provides a semantically informed initialization.

#### 4.3. Language Grounding and Structure Analysis (RQ3)

- **Semantic Grounding under Linguistic Variation:** When retrained with paraphrased language data and evaluated on held-out descriptions, DISC achieved 85.4% on LIBERO-90 (vs. Octo’s 80.6%) and 91.8% on Meta-World (vs. Octo’s 90.3%). This indicates that DISC grounds task meaning rather than memorizing surface-level phrasing, attributed to the hypernetwork's necessity to encode task semantics into policy parameters.
- **Parameter Space Visualization:** A t-SNE visualization of the generated policy parameters revealed a semantically structured manifold. Tasks clustered by functional meaning, exhibiting: (1) action-type separation (e.g., articulated object manipulation vs. pick-and-place), (2) functional grouping (e.g., Cabinet/Drawer interactions vs. Stove/Microwave operations), and (3) semantic continuity (e.g., inverse operations like "Turn On/Off Stove" appearing as nearest neighbors). This structure provides an explanation for DISC's few-shot adaptation advantages.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment (RQ4)

- On a real-world combinatorial benchmark of 9 tasks with identical visual context (forcing reliance on language), DISC achieved the highest average success rate of 86.4%, substantially outperforming the best entangled baseline, Octo (78.5%).
- This result directly confirms that language-generated policy parameters, not visual shortcuts, drive behavior in DISC. Entangled baselines often achieved correct localization but suffered from orientation misalignment, consistent with observation leakage where policies learn coarse scene-to-action associations. HyperZero (a simpler hypernetwork baseline) failed due to spatial precision errors, validating the necessity of DISC's iterative refinement module.
- DISC's few failures were attributed to execution-level imprecision of the compact MLP target policy for physically demanding placements, indicating a potential area for future improvement.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (from Appendix)

- **Language Encoder Choice:** T5-small was found to be the most effective language encoder for DISC, outperforming CLIP-base and BERT-base, suggesting that T5's text-to-text pre-training objective provides richer semantic structure for hypernetwork parameter generation.
- **Hypernetwork Architecture:** Both the Weight Initialization Network (WIN) and the iterative refinement module were shown to be critical. Removing either component significantly degraded performance, especially on long-horizon tasks, demonstrating their complementary roles in providing coarse initialization and fine-grained precision.
- **Number of Tasks:** DISC demonstrated strong positive scaling with an increased number of training tasks, showing consistent performance improvement from 10 to 90 tasks. This contrasts with Diffusion Policy (negative scaling) and Octo (flat scaling), suggesting DISC's hypernetwork effectively leverages greater task diversity to learn more generalizable policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DISC as a novel architectural approach that fundamentally re-evaluates how language instructions are integrated into robotic manipulation policies. The central tenet of the work is that the architectural placement of language input is more critical than its encoding method. By structurally decoupling instruction processing from state-conditioned control via full policy parameter generation, DISC directly addresses and mitigates the problem of "task-state entanglement" and its associated "observation leakage" failures inherent in shared-parameter designs.

The significance of DISC lies in several key areas:

- **Enhanced Language Grounding:** DISC's design guarantees that task identity is solely derived from the language instruction, preventing the policy from relying on visual shortcuts. This leads to more robust instruction following, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks and ambiguous visual environments. The real-world combinatorial benchmark directly validates this, demonstrating that DISC policies are driven by language-generated parameters even when visual context is identical across tasks.
- **Improved Generalization and Adaptation:** The learned parameter manifold, where semantically similar tasks cluster geometrically, enables efficient few-shot adaptation to novel tasks. This suggests that the decoupled architecture facilitates the learning of a more interpretable and structured representation of task space, which is valuable for lifelong learning and rapid deployment in new scenarios.
- **Architectural Inductive Bias:** DISC demonstrates that explicit architectural inductive biases, specifically mimicking optimization processes in a feed-forward hypernetwork, can recover substantial performance benefits traditionally attributed to large-scale pretraining. This provides an alternative or complementary path to building capable robot policies without necessarily requiring massive external datasets.
- **Practical Applicability:** For applications prioritizing reliable language grounding, efficient control, and adaptation from limited robotic data, DISC offers a compelling solution. The "generate once, act many" paradigm, with a compact generated target policy, allows for high-frequency control in real-world settings after the initial parameter generation.

The potential impact of this work extends to:
- **More Trustworthy Robotic Systems:** By reducing reliance on spurious visual correlations, DISC could lead to more predictable and robust robot behavior, which is critical for deployment in unstructured human environments.
- **Advancements in Meta-Learning for Robotics:** The structured parameter manifold learned by DISC's hypernetwork could inspire new approaches to meta-learning, where foundational knowledge about task structures is encoded into generative models.
- **Rethinking VLA Architectures:** The findings encourage a re-evaluation of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designs, prompting researchers to consider the implications of modality fusion points on policy learning and robustness.

**Limitations** acknowledged by the authors include higher training memory and longer optimization times compared to static policies. Additionally, underspecified instructions might explicitly surface uncertainty in DISC, whereas entangled models might silently resolve such ambiguity through observation leakage. These limitations suggest future research directions such as more efficient refinement mechanisms, uncertainty-aware clarification queries, and broader multi-modal extensions beyond pure language inputs.
