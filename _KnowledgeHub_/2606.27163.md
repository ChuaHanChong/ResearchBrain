# Learning to Fold: prizewinning solution at LeHome Challenge 2026 (1st place online, 2nd offline)

> [!summary] Summary
> An independent researcher developed a robust robotic system for bimanual garment folding, securing 1st place in the online round and 2nd place in the real-world final of the LeHome Challenge 2026. The solution combines Vision-Language-Action models with reinforcement learning techniques and extensive engineering for sim-to-real transfer, achieving a 79.63% success rate in simulation.

## Problem

- Effectively manipulating highly deformable objects like garments with bimanual robots due to their high degrees of freedom and complex state estimation.
- Overcoming sparse, binary success rewards in robotic tasks, which hinder effective reinforcement learning and policy robustness.
- Ensuring robotic policies generalize to unseen deformable objects and across different physical robot setups without direct access to target hardware.

## Method

- An asynchronous, distributed "flywheel" architecture combines Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR), RECAP-style advantage conditioning, and human-in-the-loop (DAgger) learning.
- A dense reward signal is constructed from sparse binary outcomes using intermediate checkpoints, success probability predictions, and completion shaping.
- Extensive simulation-centric data collection, heavy domain randomization, and aggressive data augmentation enhance generalization and robustness, complemented by inference-time optimization using a Thompson sampling bandit and best-of-N candidate selection.

## Results

- Secured 1st place in the online (simulation) round of LeHome Challenge 2026 with a **79.63%** overall success rate, surpassing the second-placed team by **6.1** percentage points.
- Achieved 2nd place in the real-world final at ICRA 2026, scoring 865 out of a possible 1080 points.
- The system demonstrated high performance across diverse garment types in simulation, including **93.5%** success on short pants, while maintaining identical retrieval quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language-Action policies can be significantly enhanced by integrating reinforcement learning techniques, especially when augmented with auxiliary heads that act as a value function for success prediction.
> - Robustness and generalization in deformable object manipulation are critically achieved through meticulous engineering practices, including distributed training pipelines, diverse data collection, and extensive domain randomization.
> - Effective sim-to-real transfer, even without direct access to the target robot, is feasible by focusing on environment alignment, targeted model fine-tuning, and robust motion intensity matching across data sources.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the challenges of deformable object manipulation in robotics, specifically focusing on bimanual garment folding. This area is recognized as complex due to the high degrees of freedom inherent in cloth, which makes precise control and state estimation difficult. The paper builds upon existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically extending architectures such as π$_{0.5}$ and the author's team's BEHAVIOR-1K Challenge 2025 solution. The work integrates techniques from Reinforcement Learning (RL), including Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR), RECAP-style advantage conditioning, and Human-in-the-Loop (HIL) learning (DAgger), to enhance VLA policy performance and robustness.

A significant aspect of the work involves sim-to-real transfer, a common challenge in robotics where policies trained in simulation must generalize to physical robots. The paper contributes an engineering case study in the context of a competitive robotics challenge (LeHome Challenge 2026, an ICRA 2026 competition), where practical solutions for robustness, generalization, and efficient data utilization are paramount. It explores methods for dense reward design from sparse binary outcomes, online inference-time optimization, and various data augmentation strategies to bridge the simulation-reality gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work was to develop a robotic system capable of bimanual garment folding to compete in the LeHome Challenge 2026. The author's system aimed to achieve high performance in both the online (simulation) round and the real-world final.

The motivation for the specific technical approaches adopted stemmed from several key challenges inherent in the garment folding task and the competition environment:

1. **Deformable Cloth Manipulation Complexity**: Garments are deformable objects, making their manipulation challenging. Small variations in robot trajectories can lead to significant changes in garment state. The provided behavior cloning (BC) demonstrations from the organizers were "clean and inflexible," limiting the robustness of a policy trained purely on imitation. This motivated the integration of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to enable the policy to learn more robust and adaptive behaviors, including recovery from non-ideal states.

2. **Sparse and Binary Reward Signal**: The competition defined success as a binary outcome based on geometric keypoint conditions at the end of an episode. This sparse reward structure makes it difficult for standard RL algorithms to learn effectively, as intermediate actions receive no direct feedback. This motivated the development of dense intermediate reward signals and the use of auxiliary prediction heads (e.g., for success probability and task completion) to provide continuous learning signals.

3. **Generalization to Unseen Garments**: A substantial portion of the evaluation garments in the competition were unseen during training, lacking any provided demonstration data. This necessitated strategies to ensure the policy's ability to generalize to novel garment instances. Heavy domain randomization in simulation and robust augmentation during training were employed to address this.

4. **Absence of Access to the Evaluation Robot**: For the real-world final, the author did not have direct access to the specific evaluation robot. This implied a more complex transfer pipeline (sim → author's robot → evaluation robot), requiring robust sim-to-real transfer methods and strategies to cope with potential domain shifts between different physical setups.

The work's overarching motivation was to recombine existing RL and VLA concepts with engineering and optimization contributions into a practical "recipe" that could be applied either as a complete system or individually. The report is presented as an engineering case study, emphasizing practical solutions developed under competition pressure.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The system is structured as an asynchronous, distributed "flywheel" architecture, where training, rollout collection, and human-in-the-loop (DAgger) data collection operate independently and communicate via HuggingFace Hub.

**RL Training Framework:**
The training incorporates **Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR)** and **RECAP-style advantage conditioning**. AWR is applied through prioritized sampling, where frames with higher advantage are sampled more frequently for training. Auxiliary losses (e.g., for value prediction) are debiased using importance weights. RECAP-style conditioning feeds the computed advantage as an input to the action expert, enabling **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)** during inference. This combination aims to redistribute probability mass towards actions the policy already produces well, rather than exploring new behaviors, which is noted as beneficial for this competition's requirement of reliable folding. Training utilizes dynamic, runtime multi-dataset sampling, combining initial BC data, DAgger sessions, and RL rollouts, with older RL data decaying in sampling priority. Checkpoint rollbacks are periodically used to mitigate convergence to local optima.

**Data Collection:**
Data collection is simulation-centric, using NVIDIA Isaac Lab (Isaac Sim) with a bimanual SO-ARM101 robot. Multiple simulation instances run in parallel, connected to a stateless policy server. Strategies include:
- **Unbiased Rollouts**: Random, full-coverage, and curriculum-based (difficulty-targeted) sampling of garments.
- **Replay Strategies**: Utilize saved physics states (particle positions, joint states) for:
 - **Success Replays**: Re-running early-episode states of successful episodes with heavier augmentations to multiply scarce successful data.
 - **Failure States (Hard Mining)**: Re-running states where success prediction dropped significantly.
 - **Semi-Success States**: For near-success failures, used for re-tries or DAgger.
**Environment Augmentations**: Both episode-level (garment texture, color, pose/scale/roughness, camera/robot jitter, lighting) and per-step (color tint, arm color, light variation) augmentations are applied to enhance generalization.
**Manual DAgger**: A human teleoperates the robot in simulation to correct failed states, with the corrected trajectories integrated into the training data. This process is designed to be asynchronous to maximize human efficiency.

**Policy Architecture:**
The policy is an extension of a VLA model previously used in the BEHAVIOR-1K Challenge, which is based on π$_{0.5}$. Key components include:
- A frozen **SigLIP-So400m/14 image encoder** processing three RGB camera views (resized to 224x224).
- A **Gemma-2B prefix transformer** that processes image tokens, robot state, and auxiliary tokens.
- A **Gemma-300M action expert** that generates 30-step, 12-dimensional joint delta action chunks via flow matching.
The architecture incorporates:
- No language input (text prompt is removed).
- Correlated flow-matching noise (drawn from empirical action covariance).
- Soft inpainting at chunk boundaries for smooth trajectories.
- Cross-layer KV-cache mixing to allow the action expert to select VLM depths.
- Multi-sample flow matching (5 samples per batch item).
**LeHome-specific additions**:
- **Garment-type input token**: A learned embedding identifying garment type, inferred at evaluation time.
- **Advantage conditioning token**: A learned token in the prefix, masked for negative advantage during training to enable CFG.
- **Multi-signal AdaRMS conditioning**: Modulates RMSNorm layers in the action expert with flow time, garment type, and advantage signals.
- **Exclusive Self-Attention (XSA)**: A mechanism applied to VLM and action expert to remove self-attention paths.
- **Smooth per-timestep action normalization**: Action deltas are normalized using a smooth fit of empirical mean and standard deviation per horizon timestep.

**Auxiliary Prediction Heads (Policy as Value Function):**
The policy incorporates auxiliary heads to predict task-relevant quantities, serving as a value function and a quasi-world model:
- **Current-frame heads** (from a shared "current query" token): Success probability (main value function), task completion (t/T), garment type, and keypoint distance ratios.
- **Future-prediction heads** (predicting 30 frames ahead):
 - **FAST query** (training-only): Predicts future success, completion, and keypoint distances.
 - **FM query** (at action expert tail): Predicts future keypoint distances and a success residual (∆$_{success}$), acting as a Q-function. These action-conditional predictions are used for best-of-N candidate selection.

**Reward Design and Advantage Computation:**
A dense reward is constructed from the binary success conditions:
- **Intermediate checkpoints**: Awards 0.5 reward for reaching key folding stages, with the first checkpoint gradually allocated based on primary distance reduction. Full success brings cumulative reward to 1.0.
- **Failure withdrawal**: All accumulated reward is withdrawn if the episode ultimately fails, ensuring the total return remains binary.
- **Value function**: The success probability prediction from the auxiliary head (dampened by αs=0.5 and smoothed via EMA) serves as the value function, V(st) = P(success | st) - Rcumt.
- **Completion shaping**: The completion prediction (t/T) provides a second, stable progress signal.
- **Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE)**: GAE (γ=0.999, λ=0.99) is applied to both the dampened success value and the completion shaping.
- **Stale Rollouts**: For older, off-policy data, the advantage calculation blends toward an outcome-only segment baseline (GRPO-style) that relies solely on known episode outcomes, not evolving policy predictions.
- **Precision Boost**: Top 20% successful episodes (ranked by how tightly conditions were met) receive a fixed advantage bonus to encourage high-quality folds.

**Inference-Time Optimization:**
Several hyperparameters are tuned per garment type to optimize performance without retraining:
- Execution length (ne), playback stretch (k), anchor length (na), inpainting onset (tip), guidance scale (α), noise temperature (τ), and number of candidates (N).
- **Thompson sampling bandit**: These parameters are optimized online during rollout collection using a per-parameter Thompson sampling bandit.
- **Classifier-free guidance**: Amplifies the advantage conditioning using a guidance scale α.
- **Best-of-N candidate selection**: N action chunks are sampled, and the one with the best predicted action-conditional success residual (∆$_{success}$) from the FM query head is executed.
- **Garment-type bootstrap**: The policy infers the garment type from initial observations, which is then fixed for the episode.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer (Final Round):**
The sim-to-real strategy involved fine-tuning a strong, but not maximally sim-overfit, checkpoint from the simulation round.
- **Model Stripping**: Sim-specific auxiliary heads (keypoint distances, world models) and pipeline components (advantage conditioning, CFG, best-of-N) were removed or frozen, leaving primarily the action, garment-type, and completion heads for real training.
- **Training Mix**: Fine-tuning used a three-bucket data mix: organizer BC data (60%), author's teleop/DAgger data (30%), and heavily augmented sim success-replays (10%).
- **Environment Alignment**: A camera-overlay utility was developed to align the author's physical robot setup and the simulator as closely as possible to the evaluation environment. Deliberate rig randomization was also employed to enhance robustness.
- **Heavy Augmentation**: Aggressive training-time augmentations (color jitter, gain/gamma, blur, noise, crop/rotate/zoom/translate, cutout, camera dropout, state noise/dropout) were applied, significantly stronger than in the sim round.
- **Motion Intensity Alignment**: Per-source speed factors were applied to rescale the time axis of different data sources, aligning their motion intensity to the organizer's baseline.
- **DAgger Collection and Weighting**: Real-robot DAgger data was collected, with frames weighted by proximity to human intervention (human corrections weighted highest, frames immediately preceding intervention weighted lowest).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Online Round (Simulation):**
The author's system secured 1st place among 62 participating teams in the online (simulation) round of the LeHome Challenge 2026.
- **Overall Success Rate**: 79.63%.
- **Performance Gap**: The system outperformed the second-placed team by 6.1 percentage points.
- **Per-Garment Type Scores**:
 - Long Tops: 74.5%
 - Short Tops: 70.0%
 - Long Pants: 80.5%
 - Short Pants: 93.5%
The performance indicated broad competence across garment types, with the highest absolute score on short pants (93.5%) and the most challenging being short tops (70.0%). The final simulation policy utilized approximately 12,500 rollout episodes (~4.3 million frames) for training.
- **Qualitative Failure Analysis**: Remaining failures were attributed to issues with dexterity and precision (garments being slightly misaligned), occasional simulation physics inaccuracies (e.g., failed grasps), and a limited capacity for recovery from out-of-distribution states due to the policy not being explicitly trained for such scenarios in simulation.

**Real-World Final (Sim-to-Real):**
The system achieved 2nd place in the real-world final, evaluated on-site at ICRA 2026.
- **Score**: 865 points (out of a maximum possible 1080 points), behind the first-place team which scored 895 points.
- **Sim-to-Real Challenges**: Zero-shot transfer from simulation to the real robot was not feasible. This was attributed to overfitting to simulation-specific rendering artifacts, observed differences in how garments were folded in real-world demonstrations versus simulation, and the multi-stage transfer process (sim → author's robot → evaluation robot). A significant observation was the model's sensitivity to minor image processing changes, indicating overfitting.
- **Successful Transfer Strategies**: Despite the challenges, the systematic application of environment alignment tools, heavy data augmentation, motion intensity matching across data sources, and real-robot DAgger for collecting recovery data contributed to the policy's performance in the physical environment. The policy demonstrated robustness to day-to-day variations in the author's physical setup, which was attributed to deliberate rig randomization and extensive augmentation.
- **Impact of a "Units Bug"**: A subtle data bug related to joint state representation units between different software versions caused a loss of training time and potentially some final performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by demonstrating a high-performance solution for a complex deformable object task within a competitive setting. The primary significance lies in the practical integration and optimization of various machine learning techniques to achieve robust bimanual garment folding.

**Key Contributions and Implications:**
- **Integrated VLA-RL Framework**: The work showcases a method for enhancing Vision-Language-Action policies with reinforcement learning, specifically using AWR and RECAP-style conditioning. The concept of the policy acting as its own value function, by incorporating auxiliary prediction heads for success, completion, and future states, offers a streamlined approach to training and deployment, potentially reducing architectural complexity and computational overhead compared to separate actor-critic systems.
- **Robustness through Engineering**: The report serves as an engineering case study, detailing a "recipe" that combines numerous practical optimizations. These include asynchronous distributed training pipelines, diverse data collection strategies (e.g., curriculum learning, success/failure replays), extensive domain randomization, and aggressive data augmentation. These elements collectively contribute to a policy's ability to generalize and remain robust across varying conditions, including different physical setups.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The strategies employed for sim-to-real transfer, such as environment alignment tools (e.g., camera-overlay utility), heavy augmentation, and motion intensity matching, offer practical insights for bridging the reality gap in robotics without direct access to target hardware. This is particularly relevant for scenarios where deploying to novel environments is necessary.
- **Inference-Time Optimization**: The use of a Thompson-sampling bandit for online optimization of inference hyperparameters (e.g., execution length, guidance scale, number of candidates) demonstrates a method for extracting additional performance from a trained policy without requiring further training. This approach can adapt to a policy's evolving capabilities and task-specific requirements.
- **Human-in-the-Loop for Recovery**: The implementation of DAgger for collecting recovery data, especially on the real robot, highlights its value in acquiring out-of-distribution behaviors that automated exploration might struggle to discover. This suggests a continued role for human intervention in improving robot resilience.

**Potential Future Directions:**
The author identifies several areas for further research:
- **Autonomous Exploration and Recovery**: The system demonstrated limitations in automatically exploring and recovering from novel, out-of-distribution states. Developing methods for flow-matching policies to generate useful recovery attempts without human intervention remains an open problem.
- **Unified Sim-to-Real RL Pipeline**: A significant proposed next step is to combine the strengths of both competition rounds into a single pipeline. This would involve developing a real-side reward/value function to drive advantage conditioning and best-of-N selection on the real robot, alongside prioritized sampling over real rollouts and DAgger interventions weighted by value signals. Such a unified approach could achieve higher performance by leveraging the cleanliness of RL training with the recovery capabilities of real-world human data.
- **Model Efficiency**: The author suggests that the task might be solvable with a smaller model than the full-scale VLA used, implying potential for work on more computationally efficient architectures for similar manipulation tasks.
- **Leveraging Cheap Image Augmentations**: The observation that simple, widely-used computer vision augmentations are underutilized in robotics despite their effectiveness warrants further investigation and broader adoption.

Overall, this work provides a comprehensive account of developing a competitive robotic manipulation system, showcasing the synergy between VLA models, advanced RL techniques, and meticulous engineering for deformable object tasks.
