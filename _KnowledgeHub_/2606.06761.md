# AxisGuide: Grounding Robot Action Coordinate System in RGB Observations for Robust Visuomotor Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Korea University and KT R&D Center developed AxisGuide, a lightweight method that visually grounds the robot's base-frame action coordinate system directly within RGB observations. This approach enhanced visuomotor manipulation policies, increasing success rates by up to 19.88 percentage points in real-world novel object position tasks and improving generalization across various manipulation tasks and viewpoints.

## Problem

- Visuomotor manipulation policies struggle to reliably execute low-level actions under distribution shifts, such as objects placed at novel locations, despite strong semantic scene understanding.
- RGB images often lack explicit visual cues for the robot's base-frame action coordinate system, leading policies to memorize image-action correlations rather than learning generalizable action semantics.
- The inherent difficulty in establishing clear correspondence between pixel space and the 6D Cartesian action space, especially when the robot's base is out of view or occluded.

## Method

- **AxisGuide** projects the robot's end-effector position and unit translation vectors along the base-frame axes (x, y, z) onto the 2D image plane.
- These projected directions are rendered as arrows originating from the end-effector's pixel location, forming a 3-channel ==action coordinate cue image== (red for +x, green for +y, blue for +z).
- The cue image is then ==concatenated channel-wise== with the original RGB observation, creating a 6-channel input for the vision backbone, and the policy is trained with this augmented observation using ==behavior cloning==.

## Results

- AxisGuide increased success rates in a real-world 'Pick Up' task with novel object positions from **30.12%** to **50.00%** (**+19.88 percentage points**) and in simulation from **52.38%** to **65.71%** (**+13.33 percentage points**).
- It demonstrated consistent improvements across diverse single-task and multi-task setups in both simulation (e.g., **+13.34 percentage points** on 'Pick & Place (Bowl)' in LIBERO) and real-world environments (e.g., **+20.00 percentage points** on 'Flip Pot' with multi-view).
- The method proved robust to simulated camera calibration errors at inference time and introduced negligible computational overhead, adding only **0.13%** parameters and **~5.41 ms** latency per inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly grounding the robot's action coordinate system in RGB observations significantly improves a policy's ability to adapt end-effector movements to novel spatial configurations.
> - Channel-wise concatenation of the action coordinate cue is more effective than overlaying it, and positioning the cues relative to the end-effector outperforms center-aligned positioning.
> - Normalizing projected action directions to unit vectors helps the policy focus on directional information, further enhancing performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in visuomotor manipulation policies, particularly those leveraging large-scale behavior cloning and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, have demonstrated enhanced semantic scene understanding. These models are designed to interpret complex visual information and language instructions to guide robot actions, leading to broader generalization across various tasks and objects. However, a persistent challenge in this field is the translation of high-level semantic understanding into reliable, low-level action execution, particularly under distribution shifts. For instance, policies may semantically identify an object and the task, but struggle to execute precise manipulations when the object's exact spatial placement differs from training examples.

Current visuomotor policies typically learn to map RGB image observations directly to actions, often defined as relative end-effector displacements in the robot's base frame. The inherent difficulty lies in establishing a clear correspondence between the pixel space of the image and the 6D Cartesian action space. RGB images frequently lack explicit visual cues that anchor the robot's action coordinate system, and the robot's base, which defines this system, is often outside the camera's field of view or occluded. This can lead policies to memorize image-action correspondences, hindering generalization to novel spatial configurations.

Prior research has attempted to augment visuomotor policies with additional inputs, such as 2D trajectory sketches, predicted keypoint affordances, motion traces, depth-derived reticles, or hardware context like camera geometry. While these methods enrich observations with task intent, spatiotemporal context, or embodiment-specific information, they generally do not explicitly address the interpretation of the policy's action coordinate system (e.g., how a "+x translation" manifests visually).

AxisGuide positions itself within this landscape by focusing specifically on "action understanding" in the context of the robot's base-frame action coordinate system. It introduces a lightweight method to visually ground these action semantics directly within RGB observations, thereby bridging the gap between semantic scene understanding and the low-level interpretation of the robot's action space. This approach is complementary to existing input augmentation strategies, providing a direct reference for action dimensions in the image space, which is an underexplored aspect of improving visuomotor policy generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and generalization capabilities of visuomotor manipulation policies by addressing a identified gap in "action understanding." The authors observe that despite strong semantic scene understanding enabled by large-scale behavior cloning and VLA models, policies often fail to reliably execute low-level actions when encountering distribution shifts, such as objects placed at novel, unseen locations.

The core motivation stems from the hypothesis that this performance degradation arises from an insufficient understanding of the robot's base-frame action coordinate system within the image space. Visuomotor policies infer actions from pixels, and if actions are defined as relative end-effector poses in a 6D (x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw) space, successful execution requires the policy to comprehend what each action dimension signifies visually. For instance, the policy needs to discern which direction in an RGB image corresponds to a "+x" motion or a "yaw" rotation relative to the robot's base frame.

However, conventional observation-to-action settings present an inherent challenge in acquiring this correspondence. RGB images and robot states typically lack direct, explicit visual references to anchor the action coordinate system. Even when the robot's base is visible, it may not provide a clear semantic cue for the action axes. Furthermore, in many robotics datasets, the robot's base is frequently outside the camera's field of view or partially occluded, further complicating implicit inference. This forces policies to often resort to memorizing correlations between images and numeric action vectors, which limits their ability to generalize to novel scenarios.

To illustrate this, the paper describes an experiment where a VLA model trained on a pick-up task with randomized object placements frequently fails at unseen object locations, despite consistent object appearance and scene layout. This failure highlights the model's inability to compose new relative end-effector displacements in response to shifted targets, pointing to a lack of action coordinate understanding.

Therefore, the key objective is to provide policies with an explicit and direct visual association between the action space and image observations. By visually representing the meaning of +x, +y, and +z motions in the image space, AxisGuide aims to enable policies to adapt their end-effector movements more reliably, leading to improved success rates and enhanced generalization across a broader range of spatial configurations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AxisGuide introduces a lightweight guidance method that augments RGB observations with explicit visual cues representing the robot's base-frame action coordinate system. The approach involves projecting the robot's base-frame axes onto the 2D image plane and incorporating these projections as additional input channels for the visuomotor policy.

The methodology can be broken down into two main parts:

**A. Computing Action Coordinate Cue Image:**
1. **Coordinate Transforms:** The method relies on known camera parameters, specifically camera extrinsics (world-to-camera transformation matrix T<sub>c←w</sub>) and intrinsics (K). These parameters are used to project 3D points from the robot's world frame to the 2D image plane.
2. **Gripper Position Projection:** At each timestep, the current 3D position of the robot's end-effector (gripper) in the world frame (p<sub>w</sub> ∈ ℝ³) is projected onto the image plane using the camera parameters to obtain its pixel location, o = Π(p<sub>w</sub>) ∈ ℝ².
3. **Unit Translation Projection:** To visualize the meaning of base-frame translations (+x, +y, +z), three canonical unit translation vectors are considered: Δp^(x)<sub>w</sub> = εe<sub>x</sub>, Δp^(y)<sub>w</sub> = εe<sub>y</sub>, and Δp^(z)<sub>w</sub> = εe<sub>z</sub>. Here, e<sub>x</sub>, e<sub>y</sub>, e<sub>z</sub> are the robot base-frame basis vectors, and ε > 0 is a small step size. These "translated" points (p<sub>w</sub> + Δp^(k)<sub>w</sub>) are then projected onto the image plane.
4. **Image-Space Displacement Vectors:** The image-space direction vectors corresponding to these unit translations are computed as the difference between the projected translated point and the projected gripper position: Δu^(k) = Π(p<sub>w</sub> + Δp^(k)<sub>w</sub>) − Π(p<sub>w</sub>), for k ∈ {x, y, z}.
5. **Normalization and Rendering:** These image-space displacement vectors are normalized to obtain unit image-space direction vectors, ˆd^(k). These normalized vectors are then rendered as arrows on an image. The arrows originate from the projected end-effector pixel location (o), emphasizing an end-effector-centric directional reference. The x, y, and z directions are encoded in the red, green, and blue channels, respectively, forming a 3 × H × W action coordinate cue image A<sub>t</sub>. This cue image explicitly visualizes where the robot would move from its current state in the image for each base-frame axis.

**B. Policy Learning With Action Coordinate Cue Image:**
1. **Channel-wise Concatenation:** Instead of overlaying the cue image directly onto the RGB observation, which could occlude details or alter the raw appearance, AxisGuide employs channel-wise concatenation. For each camera view (v), the original RGB image I^(v)<sub>t</sub> (3 channels) is concatenated with its corresponding action-coordinate cue image A^(v)<sub>t</sub> (3 channels) to form a 6-channel augmented image: ˜I^(v)<sub>t</sub> = [I^(v)<sub>t</sub> ; A^(v)<sub>t</sub>] ∈ ℝ⁶ˣᴴˣᵂ.
2. **Vision Backbone Adaptation:** To accommodate the 6-channel input, the input channel dimension of the vision backbone's first convolution layer (e.g., in ResNet or other CNN architectures) is increased from 3 to 6. The remaining architecture of the backbone and the policy head remain unchanged.
3. **Policy Training:** The policy is then trained using the standard behavior cloning objective, where the original observation o<sub>t</sub> is replaced with the augmented observation ˜o<sub>t</sub> (comprising all augmented camera views {˜I^(v)<sub>t</sub>} and proprioceptive state q<sub>t</sub>). This allows the policy to directly learn from the combined visual and coordinate cue information.

The approach is designed to be lightweight, not requiring depth information, additional supervision, or significant architectural modifications beyond the input layer. It utilizes existing camera calibration data, which is common in robotic setups. For experimental validation, the authors employ Diffusion Policy (DP) for single-task experiments and SmolVLA for multi-task experiments, modifying their input pipelines as described.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated that AxisGuide consistently improved performance and generalization across various visuomotor manipulation tasks in both simulation (LIBERO benchmark) and real-world environments.

**A. Action Coordinate System Understanding (Q1):**
- **Novel Object Positions:** In a "Pick Up" task where objects were placed at unseen locations (LIBERO-Spatial for black bowl, real-world for pear), AxisGuide significantly improved success rates. In simulation, the success rate increased from 52.38% to 65.71% (+13.33%p). In the real world, it improved from 30.12% to 50.00% (+19.88%p).
- **Qualitative Observations:** Baseline models often failed to adapt to shifted targets, drifting towards training-like configurations. Policies augmented with AxisGuide consistently redirected and reached the shifted targets more precisely, indicating a better understanding of action adjustments based on visual cues. A single-view (front camera only) evaluation in simulation also showed similar gains (+8.10%p).

**B. Control Performance and Task Success Rate (Q2):**
- **Single-View Setup (Simulation):** AxisGuide yielded improvements across diverse LIBERO tasks:
 - Pick & Place (Bowl): 69.33% to 82.67% (+13.34%p)
 - Drawer: 86.67% to 93.33% (+6.66%p)
 - Stove: 92.00% to 100.0% (+8.00%p)
 - AxisGuide also outperformed a camera-conditioning baseline (KYC) by 9.34%p on Pick & Place (Bowl).
- **Single-View Setup (Real-World):** Similar trends were observed in real-world tasks with a single front camera:
 - Pick & Place (Grape): 83.33% to 93.33% (+10.00%p)
 - Flip Pot: 36.65% to 50.00% (+13.35%p)
 - Close Pot: 33.33% to 40.00% (+6.67%p)

**C. Exploiting Multi-View Observations (Q3):**
- **Single-Task Multi-View (Simulation):** AxisGuide improved success rates on more complex LIBERO-Long tasks:
 - Pick & Place (Bowl): 74.7% to 88.0% (+13.3%p)
 - Put Both: 78.8% to 86.7% (+7.9%p)
 - Stove (Long): 74.7% to 78.7% (+4.0%p)
- **Single-Task Multi-View (Real-World):**
 - Pick & Place (Grape): 93.39% to 96.66% (+3.27%p)
 - Flip Pot: 73.33% to 93.33% (+20.00%p)
 - Close Pot: 36.66% to 53.33% (+16.67%p)
 - Larger gains were observed in tasks benefiting from wrist-view.
- **Multi-Task Multi-View (LIBERO Suites):** AxisGuide consistently improved performance across all four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long) when integrated into a SmolVLA model. For example, it outperformed a fully trained SmolVLA by 7.5% on LIBERO-Object, suggesting its effectiveness even with increased task diversity and language conditioning.

**D. Comparison with Existing Visual Cue Baselines (Q4):**
- On the Novel Object Position task, AxisGuide (65.71%) demonstrated superior performance compared to TraceVLA (51.90%) and AimBot (52.38%), which aim to enhance spatial awareness but do not explicitly ground action coordinate systems.

**E. Robustness to Calibration Errors (Q5):**
- AxisGuide maintained robustness under simulated extrinsic (camera translation and rotation) and intrinsic (focal length and principal point offsets) perturbations at inference time. Performance degraded marginally but remained above the unperturbed baseline, indicating resilience to realistic calibration noise.

**F. Ablation Study of Design Choices (Q6):**
- **Injection Method:** Channel-wise concatenation (current design) outperformed overlaying cues on the RGB image.
- **Positioning:** End-effector (EEF)-aligned positioning of cues consistently outperformed center-aligned positioning, indicating the importance of localizing the cue where actions are executed.
- **Normalization:** Normalizing projected action directions to unit vectors further improved performance compared to using raw projected vectors, suggesting that removing scale variations helps the policy focus on directional information.
- The optimal configuration (channel-wise concatenation, EEF-aligned, normalized) yielded the best success rate (+13.33%p over baseline), confirming the effectiveness of these design choices.

**G. Efficiency Analysis (Supplementary):**
- AxisGuide introduced a negligible overhead, adding only 0.590M parameters (+0.13%) to a 450.046M parameter SmolVLA model.
- The runtime latency increase was minimal, approximately +5.41 ms per inference (batch=1), making it suitable for practical deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "AxisGuide" contributes to the field of visuomotor robot control by identifying and addressing a specific gap in visuomotor policy capabilities: the insufficient understanding of the robot's base-frame action coordinate system in image space. By providing explicit visual cues for action semantics, AxisGuide offers a method to bridge the divide between high-level semantic scene understanding and robust, low-level action execution.

The demonstrated improvements in performance and generalization, particularly to unseen object locations and across diverse tasks and camera configurations, suggest several potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** AxisGuide enables policies to generalize more effectively beyond their training data, adapting to novel spatial configurations and viewpoints. This can lead to more reliable robot deployment in varied and unstructured environments where exact object placements or camera angles cannot be guaranteed.
- **Improved Action Interpretation:** By making the meaning of 6D Cartesian actions visually explicit, AxisGuide helps policies to learn fundamental action semantics rather than merely memorizing image-to-action mappings. This could potentially reduce the data requirements for training robust policies, as they gain a more foundational understanding of how to move.
- **Complementary to Existing Methods:** AxisGuide is a lightweight input augmentation method that can be integrated with existing state-of-the-art visuomotor policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, VLA models) without requiring significant architectural changes or complex additional supervision. This compatibility makes it a readily deployable enhancement.
- **Foundation for More Capable Generalist Robots:** By improving the robot's ability to consistently interpret action dimensions, AxisGuide contributes to the development of more generalist robot policies that can perform a wider range of tasks reliably, even under varying conditions. It addresses a core aspect of transferring learned behaviors to new contexts.

Despite its benefits, the authors acknowledge limitations, including the reliance on calibrated camera parameters and potential persistent failures in highly contact-rich stages, which may require additional perception or reward signal improvements. Furthermore, the evaluation scale, while extensive, is not as massive as some large-scale robot datasets, suggesting that future work could explore its efficacy under even broader distribution shifts and larger data regimes. Overall, AxisGuide represents a promising direction for improving the fundamental action understanding of visuomotor policies, paving the way for more robust and adaptable robotic systems.
