# Benchmarking Different QP Formulations and Solvers for Dynamic Quadrupedal Walking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI) performed a comprehensive benchmark of Quadratic Program (QP) formulations and solvers for dynamic quadrupedal walking. The study, which introduced "Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW)" as a novel efficiency metric, demonstrated that ARM architectures offer superior energy efficiency for robotic control, while sparse formulations with IPM-based solvers like HPIPM provide the fastest and most robust performance for Model Predictive Control (MPC).

## Problem

- Existing literature presented conflicting findings regarding the benefits of sparse versus dense QP formulations, particularly for Model Predictive Control (MPC) in legged robotics.
- A lack of comprehensive benchmarks simultaneously evaluating QP formulations, solver performance, and various computing hardware, especially concerning critical energy consumption constraints for autonomous missions.
- The necessity for real-time, computationally efficient QP solutions in both MPC and Whole-Body Control (WBC) for stable and dynamic quadrupedal locomotion, which operate under strict power budgets.

## Method

- A systematic benchmarking approach was employed, using a standard dynamic walking controller for a Unitree Go2 quadruped simulated in the Drake toolbox.
- Different **QP** formulations (sparse, partially condensed, fully condensed) were compared with a range of state-of-the-art solvers (HPIPM, OSQP, qpOASES, DAQP, Eiquadprog, PROXQP) for both ==MPC== and WBC tasks.
- Solver performance and energy efficiency were evaluated across diverse hardware architectures, including x86 desktop, x86 single-board (LattePanda Alpha), and ARM single-board (Jetson Orin NX), using the novel "Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW)" metric.

## Results

- HPIPM in `speed_abs` mode, utilizing sparse formulations, consistently achieved sub-millisecond solve times for MPC on the desktop PC and significantly outperformed other solvers across all tested hardware and condensing levels.
- The Jetson Orin (ARM) demonstrated more than double the energy efficiency (SFPW) of x86 platforms for MPC and approximately four times for WBC, despite the x86 desktop having the lowest absolute solve times.
- QP solve times for MPC generally increased with higher QP density (more condensed formulations), but IPM solvers maintained robust, approximately normally distributed solve times, indicating better stability than ASM/ALM solvers which showed scenario-dependent variability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - IPM-based sparse solvers, particularly HPIPM, are generally the fastest and most robust for MPC, especially for longer prediction horizons, exhibiting stable performance across various dynamic scenarios.
> - ARM-based single-board computers (e.g., Jetson Orin) offer significantly higher energy efficiency (SFPW) compared to x86 systems, making them highly suitable for power-constrained autonomous robot applications.
> - For Whole-Body Control, most open-source solvers perform adequately with generally low computation times, suggesting that engineering efforts should prioritize robust problem formulations over specific solver selection in this context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of quadrupedal robots has advanced significantly in recent years, with several platforms achieving industrial maturity. A core aspect of this progress lies in numerical methods for trajectory optimization and their real-time implementation through Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Whole-Body Control (WBC). These control paradigms are central to modern legged locomotion, with a substantial portion of recent research in legged robotics focusing on receding horizon control or MPC.

Quadratic Programs (QPs) are fundamental to both MPC and WBC, as optimal control problems in these contexts are typically formulated as linearly constrained QPs. Consequently, extensive research has been dedicated to developing efficient and stable QP solvers, primarily based on Active-set Method (ASM), Interior-point Method (IPM), or Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM) approaches. Each method and solver possesses particular strengths; some are optimized for small QPs on embedded systems, others for larger problems in machine learning, and some leverage sparsity in the QP structure, which is common in MPC due to the diagonal stacking of system matrices over a prediction horizon.

A recurring discussion in MPC literature involves the formulation of QPs, specifically regarding sparse versus dense representations. Condensing, a process of eliminating state variables to reduce QP size, results in denser but potentially smaller matrices. While some studies suggest speed-ups from dense, compact formulations, others argue that sparse formulations are advantageous in MPC, as their computational and memory requirements scale linearly with the prediction horizon (O(N)) if sparsity is exploited, compared to O(N^3) for IPM or O(N^2) for ASM with dense variants. This apparent contradiction highlights a need for more thorough investigation into the effects of partial or full condensing on solver performance.

Existing open-source benchmarks for QP solvers primarily focus on accuracy and solution time. However, for applications like space exploration or long-duration autonomous missions involving legged robots, which operate under strict energy and computational constraints, metrics such as power consumption and computational efficiency are critical. There has been a recognized lack of comprehensive studies that comparatively examine the performance impact of QP formulation (sparse, partially condensed, condensed), prediction horizon, solver choice, and the underlying computing hardware (HW) simultaneously, especially concerning energy constraints. This paper addresses this gap by providing a multi-faceted benchmark specifically tailored for dynamic quadrupedal walking.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to comprehensively compare different QP formulations and solving methods on various hardware architectures to identify optimal combinations for computational efficiency in dynamic walking of four-legged robots.

The key objectives can be broken down into the following specific aims:
1. **Evaluate QP Formulations:** Compare dense and sparse QP formulations, including partially condensed variants, to understand their impact on computational efficiency within MPC for dynamic quadrupedal walking.
2. **Benchmark QP Solvers:** Assess the performance of multiple state-of-the-art QP solvers, representing different principled methods (ASM, IPM, ALM), in the context of both MPC and WBC.
3. **Cross-Hardware Performance Analysis:** Compare solver performance and efficiency across different hardware architectures, specifically x86 (desktop and single-board) and ARM (single-board) target computers.
4. **Introduce Novel Efficiency Metric:** Propose and utilize the "Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW)" as a performance measure to enable a cross-hardware comparison of the energy efficiency of QP solvers.
5. **Assess WBC Solvers:** Benchmark different (dense) QP solvers used for Whole-Body Control (WBC), which is critical for trajectory stabilization in quadrupedal walking.
6. **Provide Recommendations:** Generate practical recommendations for selecting QP formulations, solvers, and hardware architectures to optimize computational efficiency in dynamic legged locomotion.
7. **Identify Future Research Directions:** Pinpoint areas where further technical effort should be concentrated in this domain.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical aspects of modern legged robot control:
- **Contradictory Findings:** Existing literature presents conflicting insights regarding the benefits of sparse versus dense QP formulations, particularly for MPC, necessitating a direct comparative study.
- **Computational Constraints:** MPC and WBC are computationally intensive, requiring real-time QP solutions. Efficient execution is crucial for stable and dynamic robot locomotion.
- **Energy Efficiency for Autonomous Missions:** For long-duration autonomous missions, especially in remote or extreme environments (e.g., space exploration, as illustrated by the volcano field test scenario), onboard computing power and battery capacity are limited. Therefore, power consumption and computational efficiency become paramount factors, often overlooked in benchmarks primarily focusing on speed or accuracy.
- **Lack of Integrated Benchmarks:** Existing benchmarks typically focus on solver performance in isolation or on specific aspects of QP formulation. A comprehensive study integrating hardware, QP formulation, and solver performance for a specific application like quadrupedal walking was lacking.
- **Practical Guidance:** Robotics engineers and researchers currently face significant time and expertise requirements to select suitable QP formulations and solvers for their applications. This work aims to provide concrete, data-driven recommendations to streamline this process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic benchmarking approach using a standard dynamic walking controller for quadrupedal robots, evaluated on a Unitree Go2 robot in simulation.

**A. Dynamic Walking Controller Architecture:**
The controller follows a general architecture inspired by existing works, outputting joint commands based on target linear/rotational walking velocity and body posture. It comprises four sub-components running in parallel at different control rates:
- **Gait Sequencer (GS):** (100 Hz) Heuristically determines a gait sequence, including foot contact plans and target robot poses/velocities over a prediction horizon (N).
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** (100 Hz) Calculates optimal contact forces over the prediction horizon to achieve target poses and velocities, assuming single rigid-body dynamics (SRBD).
- **Swing Leg Controller (SLC):** (500 Hz) Computes swing leg trajectories using Bézier curves for feet not in ground contact.
- **Whole-Body Control (WBC):** (500 Hz) Computes optimal joint accelerations and torques to achieve desired body/feet postures and contact forces, considering full whole-body dynamics.
The MPC and WBC components generate the largest computational load, as they both require solving QPs in real-time.

**B. Model Predictive Control (MPC) Formulation:**
1. **Standard Formulation:** The MPC problem is formulated as a convex optimization problem.
 - **Objective Function:** Minimizes a weighted sum of state error (deviation from desired state `x_d`) and control input `u`.
 - **Decision Variables:** State `x_k` (13 elements: base orientation, position, linear/angular velocity, gravity) and control input `u_k` (12 elements: four 3D leg forces `f_j`).
 - **Constraints:**
 - Equality: Linearized system dynamics (`x_{k+1} = Ax_k + B_k u_k`).
 - Inequality: Bounding-box constraints on contact forces (minimum/maximum, zero for swing phase) and linearized friction cone constraints.
 - Initial State: `x_0` is set to the current estimated state.
2. **Partial Condensing:** To investigate the effect of sparsity, the original prediction horizon `N` is divided into `N_p` blocks. Within each block, states (except the first) are eliminated using system dynamics, resulting in a partially condensed QP. This allows controlling the degree of sparsity, ranging from fully sparse (when `N_p = N`) to fully condensed (when `N_p = 0`, equivalent to eliminating all states, including the terminal one). The final state is always kept at size 1.

**C. Whole-Body Control (WBC) Formulation:**
The WBC is adapted from existing literature and solves for joint accelerations `¨q` (18 elements) and contact forces `u` (12 elements) in a single QP.
- **Objective Function:** Minimizes a weighted sum of task-space acceleration errors (for body/feet trajectories) and the difference between desired and actual contact forces.
- **Constraints:**
 - Equality: Rigid body dynamics, ensuring rigid and slip-free foot contacts.
 - Inequality: Linearized friction cone constraints, joint torque limits.
- **WBC Variants:**
 - **Reduced TSID:** Solves for `¨q` and `u`, with joint torques computed via inverse dynamics.
 - **Full TSID:** Formulates joint torques as additional optimization variables, leading to a larger QP but avoiding separate inverse dynamics. A weighting scheme is used for prioritization.

**D. Performance Metrics:**
1. **Solve Time:** The duration required for the solver to produce a solution, including any condensing time.
2. **Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW):** A novel metric introduced to assess computational efficiency independent of hardware.
 - `SFPW = (solve time)^-1 / CPU power consumption (Hz/W)`
 - CPU power consumption is measured using Intel RAPL Interface for x86 systems and the `tegrastats` utility for ARM, sampled at 10 Hz.

**E. Experimental Setup:**
1. **Target Computers:**
 - **Jetson Orin NX (ARM64):** 8-core ARM, 16 GB RAM (Single-board, low power).
 - **LattePanda Alpha (x86-64):** 2-core M3-8100Y, 8 GB RAM (Single-board, low power).
 - **Desktop PC (x86-64):** 10-core i9-10900K, 16 GB RAM (High-performance desktop).
2. **Robot and Simulation:** Unitree Go2 quadruped simulated using the Drake toolbox. The controller is implemented with ROS 2.
3. **QP Solvers:**
 - **MPC:** HPIPM (IPM, Sparse/Dense), OSQP (ADMM, Sparse), qpOASES (Parametric ASM, Dense), DAQP (Dual ASM, Dense). HPIPM is tested in `balance` and `speed_abs` modes.
 - **WBC:** Eiquadprog (Dual ASM), HPIPM, PROXQP (ALM), qpOASES.
 - All solvers are warm-started where possible and used with standard hyperparameters to ensure comparability.
4. **Problem Sizes:**
 - **MPC:** Short prediction horizon (N=10) and medium (N=20). Original QP dimensions are 263 decision variables / 143 equality / 280 inequality constraints for N=10, and 513/273/360 for N=20. Partial condensing varies `N_p` from `N` down to `1`. Full condensing is `N_p=0`.
 - **WBC:** Reduced TSID (30 decision variables / 18 equality / 28 inequality constraints), Full TSID (42/30/28).
5. **Test Scenarios:**
 - **Dynamic Trotting:** Quadruped performs various velocity profiles (up to 0.5 m/s, 40 deg/s rotational) for approximately 40 seconds.
 - **Static Standing:** Quadruped responds to commanded body roll, pitch, yaw movements (up to ±30 degrees) and height adjustments (±10 cm) for approximately 20 seconds.
 - Attempts are repeated twice if the robot falls. The dynamic simulation runs on a separate computer to avoid measurement interference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. MPC Solve Time Analysis:**
- **Sparsity Effect:** Mean MPC solve time generally increases with increasing QP density (decreasing `N_p`). For sparse solvers (HPIPM, OSQP), solve time exhibits an approximately constant behavior for `N_p > N/2`, but increases exponentially when `N_p <= N/2` (higher density).
- **Solver Performance:**
 - For `N=20`, solvers exploiting sparsity (HPIPM, OSQP) significantly outperform dense solvers (DAQP, qpOASES). Some dense solver trials for `N=20` failed due to excessive solve times.
 - **HPIPM speed abs:** Consistently the fastest solver across all computer architectures, condensing levels, and prediction horizons, achieving sub-millisecond solve times on the desktop PC.
 - For `N=10`, qpOASES and DAQP perform closer to OSQP (sparse) for fully condensed problems (`N_p=0`), indicating potential advantage for smaller problems.
- **Hardware Performance:** The x86 desktop architecture is the fastest. The Jetson Orin and LattePanda perform similarly, with the LattePanda being marginally faster.
- **Scenario Comparison (Trotting vs. Standing):**
 - Mean solve times are lower for the standing scenario compared to trotting across all solvers.
 - ASM/ALM-based solvers (DAQP, OSQP, qpOASES) show more significant differences in solve times between scenarios, and their solve time histograms for trotting exhibit two distinct peaks. This effect is most pronounced for qpOASES.
 - IPM solver (HPIPM) demonstrates robustness to changing problem structures, with approximately normally distributed solve times in both scenarios, indicating stable performance regardless of dynamic motion.
 - The slowest system (LattePanda) shows the highest variance in solve times.

**B. WBC Solve Time Analysis:**
- **Overall Performance:** All evaluated WBC solvers demonstrate relatively low computation times, averaging less than 0.5 ms per cycle.
- **Eiquadprog:** Consistently the best-performing solver across all comparisons.
- **WBC Formulation Impact:** The full TSID formulation, which includes joint torques as optimization variables, results in slightly larger computation times on average compared to the reduced TSID formulation.

**C. Efficiency Analysis (Solve Frequency per Watt - SFPW):**
- **Solver-Specific Efficiency:** The efficiency (SFPW) of solvers on a given target system is inversely proportional to their solve time. Consequently, HPIPM speed abs is the most efficient solver for MPC, and Eiquadprog for WBC, on their respective platforms.
- **Hardware Efficiency:**
 - **Jetson Orin (ARM):** Shows superior efficiency, performing more than twice as efficiently as the LattePanda and approximately three times as efficiently as the desktop PC for MPC. For WBC, it is roughly four times more efficient than the other two platforms.
 - **LattePanda (x86):** Offers comparable efficiency to the desktop PC, often being slightly more efficient in the range of 10 Hz/W depending on the specific setup.
 - **Desktop PC (x86):** While providing the fastest solve times, its higher power consumption results in lower overall SFPW compared to the Jetson Orin.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work provides a comprehensive benchmark for QP solvers in the context of dynamic quadrupedal walking, offering practical guidance and contributing to the understanding of optimal control implementation for legged robots.

The significance and potential impact of these findings are multifaceted:

1. **Actionable Recommendations for Controller Design:** The research provides concrete recommendations for selecting QP formulations, solvers, and hardware architectures, which can significantly reduce the time and expertise required for developing robust and efficient quadrupedal robot controllers. This is particularly valuable for engineers and researchers working on real-world robot deployments.

2. **Validation of Sparsity and Solver Choice for MPC:** The findings confirm that for larger MPC problems, characteristic of dynamic quadrupedal walking with longer prediction horizons, sparse QP formulations solved by sparse solvers (especially IPM-based like HPIPM) are generally more advantageous than dense formulations. This aligns with theoretical considerations regarding computational scaling. The identified robustness of IPM solvers to changing problem structures (e.g., varying contact schedules) is critical for dynamic locomotion tasks.

3. **Refocusing Effort in WBC Development:** The observation that most open-source WBC solvers perform well with generally low computation times suggests that the choice of solver is less critical for WBC. Instead, engineering efforts in WBC should be directed towards developing more robust and stable problem formulations themselves, potentially leading to more advanced and reliable whole-body control strategies.

4. **Emphasis on Energy-Efficient Hardware:** The introduction and application of the Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW) metric highlight the importance of energy efficiency. The finding that ARM architectures (specifically the Jetson Orin) offer significantly higher SFPW than x86 systems (desktop and single-board) indicates their suitability for resource-constrained applications. This has a substantial impact on the design and deployment of autonomous legged robots, where onboard computing power and battery life are critical factors for extended missions. It suggests a strategic preference for ARM-based platforms in such scenarios.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** By identifying the most efficient combinations of hardware, formulation, and solvers, the study lays a foundation for future research. It explicitly suggests extensions to other hardware architectures (e.g., CUDA), additional solvers, and more complex robotic systems (e.g., humanoids), allowing for continued advancements in optimization-based robot control.

6. **Open-Source Contribution:** Making the entire benchmark code, including the quadruped controller, open-source is a significant contribution. It allows other researchers to reproduce the results, build upon the work, and facilitate further development and validation of QP solvers and control strategies in legged robotics.
