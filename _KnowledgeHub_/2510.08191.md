# Training-Free Group Relative Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tencent Youtu Lab developed Training-Free Group Relative Policy Optimization, a method that enhances LLM agent performance in specialized tasks by learning and integrating experiential knowledge as a token prior without modifying model parameters. This approach achieved substantial performance gains on mathematical reasoning and web searching benchmarks with significantly reduced data and computational costs, leveraging the full capabilities of frozen large LLMs.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to perform optimally in specialized real-world domains, often lacking deep domain knowledge and effective tool integration.
- Existing agentic reinforcement learning (RL) methods, which rely on fine-tuning LLM parameters, incur prohibitive computational costs and environmental impact, especially for very large models.
- Parameter-tuned LLM agents often exhibit poor generalization across different domains and require large volumes of expensive, high-quality data, leading to a "cost-performance dilemma".

## Method

- Introduces ==Training-Free GRPO==, which adapts LLM agents to specialized tasks by learning "experiential knowledge" in natural language, avoiding direct parameter updates.
- The method leverages the base LLM itself to generate =="group relative semantic advantages"== for its own outputs, identifying reasons for success or failure.
- This distilled knowledge is then used as a dynamic =="token prior"== in subsequent prompts, guiding the frozen LLM's inference to improve its policy in a cost-effective manner.

## Results

- Achieved substantial performance gains on mathematical reasoning (AIME24, AIME25) and web searching (WebWalkerQA) benchmarks, with DeepSeek-V3.1-Terminus showing improvements like **+5.4%** on AIME25.
- These gains were realized with exceptional data efficiency (**100 training examples**) and a minimal training cost of approximately **$18**, an order of magnitude less than traditional RL approaches.
- Demonstrated superior cross-domain generalization and transferability, adapting a single frozen LLM to diverse tasks by simply integrating domain-specific learned experiences, and showed robustness to the absence of ground truth labels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy optimization effects analogous to traditional reinforcement learning can be achieved by learning and integrating experiential knowledge into the LLM's context, rather than modifying its parameters.
> - Encoding "group relative semantic advantages" in natural language allows for efficient distillation of high-quality experiential knowledge from LLM rollouts.
> - The approach enables powerful, frozen LLMs (including API-based models) to be adapted to specialized tasks, circumventing the computational and data demands of fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have positioned them as powerful general-purpose agents capable of complex interactions with real-world environments. They excel in tasks ranging from problem-solving and web research to code generation and computer use. However, a significant challenge arises when deploying these general-purpose LLM agents in specialized real-world domains. Such scenarios often demand deep domain-specific knowledge, effective integration of external tools (ee.g., calculators, APIs, databases), and highly tailored prompting strategies. Out-of-the-box LLM agents frequently underperform due to their limited familiarity with these specific requirements.

To address this performance gap, "agentic training" has emerged as a crucial strategy, with a notable focus on agentic reinforcement learning (Agentic RL). Traditional Agentic RL approaches, particularly those employing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and its variants, aim to align LLM behaviors by performing costly parameter updates. This typically involves processes like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by an RL phase, which alters the model's output distribution by tuning its parameters. While effective, these parameter-tuning methods face several inherent limitations:
- **Prohibitive Computational Cost**: Fine-tuning even smaller LLMs demands substantial computational resources, rendering it expensive and environmentally taxing. For the increasingly large state-of-the-art LLMs, fine-tuning becomes economically prohibitive.
- **Poor Generalization and Deployment Complexity**: Parameter-tuned models often exhibit limited cross-domain generalization, necessitating the deployment of multiple specialized models for a comprehensive set of tasks, thereby increasing system complexity and maintenance overhead.
- **Data Scarcity**: Fine-tuning requires large volumes of high-quality, annotated data, which is often scarce and expensive to acquire in specialized domains. Limited data also makes models susceptible to overfitting.
- **Diminishing Returns**: The high training costs often force researchers to fine-tune smaller LLMs (e.g., <32 billion parameters), despite larger, API-based LLMs offering superior cost-performance ratios through scalability and continuous updates. This creates a "cost-performance dilemma" where general-purpose large models underperform in specialized tasks where fine-tuning is necessary.

Against this backdrop, "Training-Free GRPO" introduces a paradigm shift. It challenges the assumption that RL-driven performance enhancement must occur in the parametric space. Instead, it aligns with a growing body of "training-free methods" that aim to improve LLM behavior at inference time without modifying model weights. This includes in-context learning (ICL), which leverages demonstrations in prompts, and iterative refinement techniques like Self-Refine and Reflexion, which use self-generated feedback. Training-Free GRPO distinguishes itself from these methods by mirroring traditional RL's multi-epoch learning from a *separate dataset* to refine a *shared, high-quality experience library* for out-of-domain queries, rather than focusing on within-sample improvement for a single query. By learning a "token prior" from experiential knowledge, it seeks to replicate the policy optimization effects of GRPO in a non-parametric, context-driven manner, thus offering a more efficient and generalizable alternative to existing Agentic RL approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a cost-effective and efficient method to enhance the performance of Large Language Model (LLM) agents in specialized, real-world domains without requiring any parameter updates or fine-tuning of the base LLM.** This directly addresses the significant challenges posed by traditional agentic reinforcement learning (RL) methods, which rely on costly parameter tuning.

The motivation stems from several critical limitations of existing parameter-tuning approaches for LLM agents:
- **Computational Cost and Environmental Impact**: Fine-tuning large LLMs demands immense computational resources, making it financially and environmentally unsustainable, especially for very large models.
- **Lack of Generalization**: Models specialized through fine-tuning often perform poorly on out-of-domain tasks, necessitating the deployment of multiple specialized models, which increases system complexity.
- **Data Scarcity and Overfitting**: Acquiring large volumes of high-quality, annotated data for specialized domains is challenging and expensive, and limited data leads to overfitting when fine-tuning.
- **The Cost-Performance Dilemma**: Researchers are often forced to fine-tune smaller, less capable LLMs due to resource constraints, even though larger, general-purpose LLMs (often API-based) offer superior foundational capabilities and cost-performance ratios. However, these larger models underperform in specialized tasks without adaptation.

The authors pose a fundamental research question: "Is applying RL in parametric space the only viable approach? Can we enhance LLM agent performance in a non-parametric way with lower data and computational costs?" By proposing Training-Free GRPO, they aim to answer this question affirmatively, demonstrating that LLMs can achieve similar policy optimization effects by learning and integrating experiential knowledge as a lightweight token prior, thereby overcoming the practical limitations of parameter-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of Training-Free GRPO is to achieve the policy optimization benefits of vanilla Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) in a non-parametric, inference-only manner, without updating the LLM's parameters. This is achieved by leveraging group-relative *semantic* advantages to distill experiential knowledge into a "token prior" that guides model behavior.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key steps, contrasting it with vanilla GRPO:

1. **Frozen Policy and Experiential Knowledge:**
 - **Vanilla GRPO:** Uses a trainable policy LLM $\pi_\theta$ and updates its parameters $\theta$ via gradient ascent.
 - **Training-Free GRPO:** The base LLM parameters $\theta$ are permanently *frozen*. Instead, an external "experiential knowledge" library $E$ (initialized as empty) is maintained and iteratively updated. The policy conditions on this knowledge: $\pi_\theta(o_i|q, E)$.

2. **Rollout and Reward:**
 - Both methods perform a parallel rollout process: for a given query $q$, the LLM generates a group of $G$ outputs $\{o_1, o_2, \ldots, o_G\}$.
 - Each output $o_i$ is then scored by a reward model $R$ to obtain a scalar reward $r_i = R(q, o_i)$. This part of the process is identical.

3. **Group Advantage Computation:**
 - **Vanilla GRPO:** Computes a numerical advantage $\hat{A}_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(r)}{\text{std}(r)}$ for each output $o_i$. This numerical value quantifies relative quality and provides a gradient direction.
 - **Training-Free GRPO:** Instead of numerical advantage, it produces a "group relative semantic advantage" in natural language.
 - **Condition for Advantage Generation:** Semantic advantages are generated only for groups with "clear winners and losers" (i.e., when $\text{std}(r) > 0$), mirroring the $\hat{A}_i=0$ case in vanilla GRPO when all rewards are identical.
 - **Summarization:** For each output $o_i$, the *same LLM* $M$ (the frozen policy model) is prompted ($p_{\text{summary}}$) to generate a summary $s_i = M(p_{\text{summary}}, q, o_i)$ describing its trajectory.
 - **Experience Extraction:** Given these summaries $\{s_1, \ldots, s_G\}$ and the current experiential knowledge $E$, the LLM $M$ is prompted again ($p_{\text{extract}}$) to introspect and articulate the reasons for relative success or failure, extracting a concise natural language experience $A_{\text{text}} = M(p_{\text{extract}}, q, s_i, E)$. This $A_{\text{text}}$ serves as the semantic advantage, encoding knowledge about high-reward actions.

4. **Optimization (Experience Update):**
 - **Vanilla GRPO:** Maximizes a PPO-clipped objective function $J_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta)$ via gradient ascent, updating the LLM's parameters $\theta$.
 - **Training-Free GRPO:** The experience library $E$ is updated using all semantic advantages $A_{\text{text}}$ from the current batch. The LLM is prompted to generate a list of operations on $E$:
 - **Add:** Append a new experience described by $A_{\text{text}}$.
 - **Delete:** Remove a low-quality or redundant experience based on $A_{\text{text}}$.
 - **Modify:** Refine or improve an existing experience in $E$ based on insights from $A_{\text{text}}$.
 - **Keep:** $E$ remains unchanged if no updates are necessary.
 - This updated experience library $E$ then serves as a context (token prior) for subsequent LLM API calls, implicitly shifting the output distribution towards higher-reward behaviors. This non-parametric shift in context space mimics the policy updates of GRPO, while the frozen base model ensures stability, analogous to GRPO's KL-divergence constraint.

The overall approach is "multi-epoch learning" on a minimal ground-truth dataset. By iteratively distilling high-quality experiential knowledge and integrating it into prompts, Training-Free GRPO guides LLM behavior effectively without any parameter modifications, making it highly data and computationally efficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Training-Free GRPO was rigorously evaluated across two distinct and challenging domains: mathematical reasoning and web searching. The main findings consistently demonstrate its superiority in terms of performance, data efficiency, computational cost, and generalization compared to traditional parameter-tuning methods.

#### 4.1. Mathematical Reasoning (AIME24 and AIME25 Benchmarks)

- **Performance Gains:** When applied to DeepSeek-V3.1-Terminus (a 671B LLM), Training-Free GRPO significantly improved performance on the AIME benchmarks. For tool-augmented ReAct setup, it boosted Mean@32 from 80.0% to 82.7% on AIME24 (+2.7%) and from 67.9% to 73.3% on AIME25 (+5.4%). Even without tool use (direct prompting), it achieved gains of +4.0% and +1.1%.
- **Data and Cost Efficiency:** These significant gains were achieved with only **100 out-of-domain training examples** (from DAPO-100 dataset) and an approximate cost of **$18**. This contrasts sharply with state-of-the-art RL methods like ReTool and AFM (trained on 32B LLMs), which typically require thousands of training examples and costs exceeding $10,000 to achieve competitive, but often lower, performance.
- **Learning Dynamics:** A 3-step learning process (3 epochs with one batch/epoch) showed steady improvement in training set performance (Mean@5) and out-of-domain AIME performance, validating the necessity of multi-step learning. The method also reduced the average number of tool calls, indicating that the learned experiences encourage more efficient and judicious tool use.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effectiveness of Learned Experiences:** Directly generated experiences (without the GRPO-like optimization) failed to improve performance, underscoring the necessity of the structured experience learning process in Training-Free GRPO.
 - **Robustness to Reward Signal:** A variant without ground truth answers (relying on implicit majority voting, self-discrimination, and self-reflection) still achieved impressive results (80.7% on AIME24, 68.9% on AIME25), demonstrating its applicability to domains where ground truths are scarce.
 - **Importance of Group Computation:** Removing group computation (setting group size to 1) significantly harmed performance, confirming that comparing multiple trajectories within a group is crucial for distilling effective semantic advantages.
- **Applicability to Different Model Sizes:** Consistent improvements were observed when Training-Free GRPO was applied to smaller LLMs like Qwen3-32B and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, further confirming its general utility across models of varying capabilities, albeit with higher absolute performance when paired with larger, more powerful foundation models.

#### 4.2. Web Searching (WebWalkerQA Benchmark)

- **Performance Gains:** On the WebWalkerQA benchmark, Training-Free GRPO improved DeepSeek-V3.1-Terminus's pass@1 score from 63.2% to 67.8% (+4.6%), using only 100 queries from the AFM dataset (AFM-100). This confirms its effectiveness in enhancing agent behavior in interactive web tasks.
- **Ablation Studies (Web Searching):** Similar to math reasoning, directly generated experiences degraded performance. Training-Free GRPO without ground truth maintained baseline pass@1 but improved pass@3, showing enhanced consistency. The full method achieved the best results.
- **Model Capability Prerequisite:** When applied to a smaller QwQ-32B model, performance significantly dropped below its baseline (25.5% vs. 27.5%), suggesting that the effectiveness of Training-Free GRPO is dependent on the underlying model's inherent reasoning and tool-use capabilities, especially in complex tool-use scenarios.

#### 4.3. Cross-Domain Transferability (Comparison to RL on Parameter Space)

- Training-Free GRPO demonstrated superior cross-domain transferability. Parameter-tuned models (e.g., ReTool trained on math tasks) showed dramatic performance drops when transferred to different domains (e.g., web searching). In contrast, Training-Free GRPO, by simply "plugging in" domain-specific learned experiences to a *frozen* LLM, achieved state-of-the-art performance in both mathematical reasoning and web searching simultaneously, showcasing its robust generalization power.

#### 4.4. Computational Costs

- **Training Cost:** Training-Free GRPO incurred an approximate cost of **$18** for its learning process (DeepSeek-V3.1-Terminus, 100 samples), a drastic reduction of over two orders of magnitude compared to typical vanilla RL training (e.g., ReTool on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct costing around $10,000).
- **Inference Cost:** While per-query inference with a large API-based model (leveraged by Training-Free GRPO) is more expensive than with a dedicated fine-tuned small model ($0.02 vs. $0.005 per problem), Training-Free GRPO offers significant advantages in irregular or low-traffic real-world applications by eliminating fixed infrastructure costs and aligning costs directly with actual usage (pay-as-you-go).

In summary, the main findings highlight that Training-Free GRPO provides a highly effective, data-efficient, and cost-efficient alternative to parameter-tuning for enhancing LLM agent performance. It achieves superior generalization and leverages the inherent capabilities of powerful frozen LLMs, making advanced agentic capabilities more practical and accessible.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Training-Free GRPO marks a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) agents, proposing a novel paradigm that shifts policy optimization from the computationally intensive parameter space to the highly efficient context space. This work carries profound significance and potential impact across several dimensions:

1. **A New Training-Free RL Paradigm:** This is arguably the most significant contribution. By demonstrating that RL-like policy optimization can be achieved through iterative distillation of "semantic advantage" into "experiential knowledge" (token priors) without gradient updates, Training-Free GRPO opens up an entirely new avenue for LLM agent training. This non-parametric approach challenges the conventional wisdom that effective policy alignment necessitates direct manipulation of model weights.

2. **Unlocking the Full Potential of Frozen LLMs:** Training-Free GRPO empowers the use of powerful, large, and often proprietary or API-based LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-V3.1-Terminus) in specialized tasks without the need for expensive fine-tuning. These models often possess superior foundational capabilities, and this method provides a way to adapt them efficiently. This effectively resolves the "cost-performance dilemma" faced by researchers and practitioners who previously had to choose between less capable, fine-tunable models and powerful but rigid general-purpose models.

3. **Dramatic Reduction in Computational Cost and Environmental Footprint:** By eliminating the need for parameter updates, the method drastically cuts down on GPU hours and associated energy consumption. The reported cost of approximately $18 for training, compared to tens of thousands of dollars for traditional RL methods, makes advanced agentic capabilities accessible to a much wider range of researchers and organizations, promoting more sustainable AI development.

4. **Exceptional Data Efficiency:** The ability to achieve significant performance gains with "just a few dozen training samples" (e.g., 100 examples) is transformative. Data scarcity is a major bottleneck in many specialized domains, and Training-Free GRPO's minimal data requirement makes agentic learning feasible in scenarios where extensive labeled datasets are unavailable or prohibitively expensive to collect.

5. **Superior Generalization and Reduced Deployment Complexity:** A key limitation of parameter-tuned models is their poor cross-domain generalization, often requiring multiple specialized models. Training-Free GRPO, by keeping the base LLM frozen and simply "plugging in" domain-specific token priors, maintains the base model's inherent generalization power. This simplifies deployment, as a single powerful LLM can serve multiple specialized tasks by dynamically switching its experiential knowledge, significantly reducing system complexity and maintenance overhead.

6. **Enhanced Robustness and Interpretability:** The mechanism of learning "semantic advantages" in natural language form could potentially lead to more interpretable and debuggable agent behaviors, as the "reasons" for policy optimization are explicitly articulated and stored as human-readable experiences. The robustness to reward signals (even without ground truths) further broadens its applicability to more ambiguous real-world settings.

7. **Catalyst for Future Research:** This work is likely to inspire further research into non-parametric and training-free approaches for LLM agents, exploring different ways to represent, learn, and integrate experiential knowledge. It also highlights the untapped potential of in-context learning and self-reflection mechanisms within LLMs for complex, long-horizon tasks.

In conclusion, Training-Free GRPO represents a pragmatic and powerful step forward in making advanced LLM agent capabilities more accessible, efficient, and broadly applicable. Its implications extend from academic research, by offering a novel theoretical framework, to industrial deployment, by providing a cost-effective and highly scalable solution for real-world agentic AI.
