# OneVision-Encoder: Codec-Aligned Sparsity as a Foundational Principle for Multimodal Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> OneVision-Encoder, developed by Glint Lab, AIM for Health Lab, and MVP Lab, introduces a multi-modal AI model based on the principle of "codec-aligned sparsity" inspired by video compression. This approach enables efficient visual information processing by focusing on salient patches, yielding superior performance across various image, video, and document understanding benchmarks while reducing patch processing by 75.0%–96.9%.

## Problem

- Existing vision models often rely on dense token sequences from sparsely sampled frames, assuming uniform importance across spatial regions and temporal frames.
- This dense processing leads to inefficient computation by expending resources on redundant or static background information.
- Current self-supervised learning paradigms often lack explicit architectural alignment with the fundamental information-theoretic structure of visual data, limiting efficiency and semantic coherence.

## Method

- Developed **OneVision-Encoder**, an ==HEVC-style Vision Transformer== that aligns its architecture with video codec principles, explicitly decomposing visual signals into stable spatial context (I-frames) and sparse temporal updates (P-frames).
- Implemented ==Codec Patchification==, a mechanism that selectively encodes only 3.1% to 25% of patches rich in signal entropy (motion vectors and residuals) for P-frames, while fully encoding I-frames.
- Employed a shared ==3D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)== to unify spatial and temporal reasoning, accommodating irregular token layouts generated by sparse patch selection across dense video, chunk-wise, and single-image inputs.

## Results

- OV-Encoder-Lang, when integrated into Large Multimodal Models, consistently outperformed SigLIP2 and Qwen3-ViT across **16 multimodal benchmarks** for video, image, and document understanding tasks.
- Under attentive probing, OV-Encoder achieved state-of-the-art representation quality, surpassing strong vision baselines like SigLIP2 and DINOv3 across **7 video understanding benchmarks**, including a **17.1% Top-1 accuracy gain** over SigLIP2 on Diving-48.
- The codec-aligned approach reduced visual token processing by **75.0%–96.9%** for a **64-frame video** input while maintaining long-range temporal coverage and achieving superior performance, demonstrating a positive correlation between efficiency and accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Artificial general intelligence can be viewed as a compression problem, where architectural alignment with the fundamental structure of data, such as the codec-inspired decomposition of video, leads to more effective visual representation learning.
> - Discriminative visual information is inherently sparse, and focusing computational resources exclusively on these salient, unpredictable regions (motion and residuals) improves both model efficiency and accuracy.
> - A unified positional encoding scheme, like 3D-RoPE, is essential for maintaining structural consistency and enabling coherent attention across diverse and irregularly sparse spatiotemporal token arrangements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal AI, particularly for video understanding, has seen significant advancements with Transformer-based models, which excel at capturing long-range spatiotemporal dependencies. Existing approaches generally fall into several categories:
- **Reconstruction-based self-supervised learning** (e.g., MAE, V-JEPA) focuses on pixel- or feature-level prediction, capturing low-level correlations but often lacking explicit semantic structure.
- **Contrastive learning paradigms** (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP) leverage external language supervision for instance-level discrimination, which can limit their ability to model intra-class consistency and fine-grained inter-class relationships.
- **Recent cluster discrimination methods** (e.g., UNICOM, MLCD, RICE, UniViT) have emerged to address this by promoting semantically related entities to form coherent clusters, leading to more structured, object-centric representations.

Despite these advances, a common limitation across many video transformers is their reliance on representations constructed from sparsely sampled frames, which are then processed as dense token sequences. This frame-centric design implicitly assumes uniform importance across spatial regions and temporal frames, leading to inefficient computation.

OneVision-Encoder directly challenges this prevailing assumption by introducing a novel architectural principle: **codec-aligned sparsity**. It posits that visual signals are inherently redundant, and true discriminative information (surprise, motion, meaning) is sparse. This insight is directly inspired by modern video compression codecs (e.g., H.264, H.265/HEVC), which explicitly decompose video into stable spatial context (I-frames) and sparse temporal updates (P-frames via motion compensation and residuals). Unlike prior work that aims to improve compression efficiency or replace codecs, OneVision-Encoder *draws inspiration from the structural principles* codecs expose to guide visual representation learning.

This work integrates and extends several lines of research:
- **Efficient Video Representations:** It departs from traditional token dropout or merging strategies by using codec-derived signals for *informed patch selection*, focusing on informative regions rather than random removal or aggregation of existing tokens. This differs from methods like AuroraCap, AuroraLong, or adaptive sampling which might still process dense patches within selected frames or rely on temporal downsampling.
- **Positional Encoding:** It introduces a 3D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) to handle the irregular spatiotemporal layouts created by sparse patch selection, unifying reasoning across diverse input modalities (dense video, chunk-wise, single-image).
- **Self-supervised Learning:** It builds upon cluster discrimination paradigms but scales it to a massive concept bank of over one million clusters, enabling joint modeling of object-level permanence and motion dynamics.

By redefining visual modeling as "predictive compression" and aligning architectures with the information-theoretic principles of video codecs, OneVision-Encoder seeks to resolve the dichotomy between dense grids and sparse semantics. It positions efficiency and accuracy not as a trade-off but as positively correlated, offering a foundational shift in how visual intelligence can be scaled for universal multimodal understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The foundational premise of this research is that **artificial general intelligence, at its core, is a compression problem**, and that **effective compression necessitates architectural alignment with the fundamental structure of the data**. The authors argue that modern vision architectures have deviated from this principle, particularly in video understanding.

The key motivations and objectives driving the OneVision-Encoder project are:

1. **Addressing Computational Waste:** Current vision models process dense pixel grids uniformly, expending significant computational resources on redundant or static background information. This overlooks the inherent sparsity of discriminative information in visual signals (e.g., motion and significant changes).
2. **Aligning Architecture with Information Theory:** The central objective is to align deep learning architectures with the information-theoretic principles of video, explicitly taking inspiration from how video codecs (like H.264 and H.265/HEVC) decompose visual signals into stable spatial context (I-frames) and sparse temporal updates (P-frames encoding motion and residuals). The goal is to focus computation exclusively on regions rich in signal entropy.
3. **Developing a Unified, Efficient Vision Encoder:** To create OneVision-Encoder (OV-Encoder), an HEVC-style Vision Transformer capable of encoding video by compressing predictive visual structure into semantic meaning.
4. **Introducing Codec Patchification:** To formulate a codec-inspired input strategy that leverages temporal signals (motion vectors and residuals) to selectively encode only 3.1% to 25% of regions rich in signal entropy, while still preserving dense temporal coverage. This patchification scheme aims to unify dense video, chunk-wise sampling, and single-image inputs.
5. **Enabling Coherent Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** To employ a shared 3D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) to unify spatial and temporal reasoning under irregular token layouts resulting from sparse patch selection.
6. **Learning Structured Semantic Representations:** To train the model using a self-supervised cluster discrimination objective over more than one million semantic concepts, jointly capturing object permanence (from images) and motion dynamics (from videos).
7. **Demonstrating Superior Performance and Efficiency:** To validate that this codec-aligned approach not only improves efficiency but also enhances accuracy across various image, video, and document understanding benchmarks, proving that efficiency and accuracy are positively correlated.
8. **Establishing a Foundational Principle:** To demonstrate that codec-aligned, patch-level sparsity is not merely an optimization trick but a foundational principle for developing next-generation visual generalists and scalable multimodal intelligence.

Ultimately, the motivation is to build a more biologically plausible and computationally efficient system for visual understanding that "sees, updates, and reasons over time" by focusing on the "surprise" or predictive residuals that truly define motion and meaning in visual data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OneVision-Encoder's methodology is grounded in the principle of "visual intelligence as codec-aligned predictive compression," aiming to encode video by compressing predictive visual structure into semantic meaning.

#### 3.1. HEVC Guided Patch Selection

The core of the approach involves treating video as decomposed by modern codecs.
- **Codec-Based Video Factorization:** Each input video is divided into Groups of Pictures (GOPs), as in High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC). Applying the HEVC codec yields intra-coded frames (I-frames) that establish global context and predicted frames (P-frames) that encode inter-frame variations.
- **Motion and Residual Signals:** For P-frames, the model extracts motion vectors (representing block-level displacements) and residual signals (capturing appearance changes not explained by motion compensation). These signals serve as a principled proxy for "unpredictability" or "new visual evidence."
- **Sparse Patch Selection:** For each P-frame, motion magnitude and residual energy are aggregated at the ViT patch level to compute a saliency score. A fixed proportion (e.g., 3.1%-25%) of the most salient patches are then selected, forming a binary mask. I-frames are always fully encoded to establish complete spatial context.

#### 3.2. Codec Patchification

This mechanism translates the codec-inspired signals into three distinct, yet unified, input formulations for the Transformer encoder:
- **Dense Video-Codec Patchification:** For video inputs, I-frames are patchified densely, while P-frames are patchified sparsely using the codec-derived saliency mask. These selected RGB patches are concatenated along the token dimension. This allows for full temporal coverage (e.g., 64 frames) while drastically reducing the total number of visual tokens (e.g., 87.5% reduction for a 64-frame clip compared to dense processing).
- **Chunk-wise Patchification:** For scenarios requiring sparse temporal sampling, videos are partitioned into temporal chunks, and one frame is randomly sampled from each chunk. These frames are then patchified.
- **Single-Image Spatial Patchification:** Single images are patchified in a row-wise manner, ensuring a deterministic spatial ordering for static content.
All three formulations produce token sequences compatible with a shared Transformer backbone.

#### 3.3. Tokenization and Transformer Encoding

The patch sequences from Codec Patchification are uniformly tokenized and encoded by a shared Vision Transformer (ViT-L/14 architecture, 24 layers). A linear projection head then maps the encoder's output features to latent embeddings. Finally, an attentive pooling head aggregates these token embeddings into a compact video-level representation.

#### 3.4. 3D Rotary Position Embedding (3D-RoPE)

To handle the irregular spatiotemporal layouts generated by Codec Patchification and ensure coherent attention, a relative positional encoding scheme, 3D-RoPE, is employed. It encodes relative offsets (∆t, ∆x, ∆y) for different input types:
- **Dense Video-Codec:** Full spatiotemporal offsets (t_i - t_j, x_i - x_j, y_i - y_j), emphasizing inter-frame residual alignment.
- **Chunk-wise:** Inter-chunk temporal disparity (c_i - c_j, x_i - x_j, y_i - y_j).
- **Single-Image:** Purely spatial offsets (0, x_i - x_j, y_i - y_j) with no temporal shift. This unified scheme maintains structural consistency.

#### 3.5. Image and Video Clustering

A single-step, offline clustering approach is used to generate pseudo-labels for training:
- **Object-level Semantics (Images):** Image embeddings (extracted using a frozen metaclip-h14 model) are clustered into millions of object-level semantic centroids.
- **Motion-level Semantics (Videos):** Video embeddings (from fixed-length 16-frame inputs, processed with an L2 normalization and concatenation strategy) are clustered into hundreds of thousands of motion-level semantic centroids.
A unified semantic centroid set is then created by combining object and motion centroids.

#### 3.6. Training Objective (Cluster Discrimination)

The model is trained using a self-supervised cluster discrimination objective. For each visual embedding, multiple positive semantic labels are identified from the unified centroid set. The loss is computed separately for object-level and motion-level granularities, contrasting samples against positive and sampled negative centroids. This multi-label semantic discrimination objective enforces spatiotemporal consistency and promotes discriminative representation learning.

#### 3.7. Pretraining Data and Pipeline

OneVision-Encoder is pretrained on a large-scale corpus combining image (e.g., LAION-400M, COYO-700M) and video datasets (e.g., HowTo100M, Panda-70M, Kinetics-710, SSV2), along with OCR data (OBELICS, Zero250M).
- **Stage 1:** Initial training on image-only data (224x224 resolution) for general visual representation learning, using 2 million classes and 13 billion samples.
- **Stage 2:** Continuation of training by introducing OCR and video data (448x448 image resolution, 224x224 video resolution). This stage incorporates Codec Patchification with a fixed token budget (e.g., 2048 tokens for a 64-frame clip), and a video-to-image ratio of 1:1. Loss is calculated separately per modality.

This comprehensive approach, from codec-inspired input processing and unified positional encoding to large-scale cluster discrimination, allows OneVision-Encoder to learn powerful and efficient multimodal representations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluations of OneVision-Encoder demonstrate its efficacy across both multimodal and representation-level tasks, consistently validating its core hypothesis that efficiency and accuracy are positively correlated through codec-aligned sparsity.

#### 4.1. LMM Probing Evaluation (Language Alignment)

When integrated into Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), OV-Encoder shows robust transferability and superior reasoning capabilities.
- **Comparison with SigLIP2:** Under identical multimodal fine-tuning conditions (using a 1.5M-scale LLaVA-Next and LLaVA-Next-Videos instruction-tuning corpus and Qwen3-4B-Instruct2507 as the language backbone), OV-Encoder-Lang (the language-aligned variant) consistently outperforms SigLIP2 across **16 benchmarks** spanning video, image, and document understanding tasks.
- **Comparison with Qwen3-ViT:** OV-Encoder-Lang also outperforms Qwen3-ViT across the same 16 benchmarks, even though OV-Encoder was pretrained on substantially fewer visual-text tokens (approximately 100B caption tokens) compared to Qwen3-ViT's >2.1T caption and instruction-aligned tokens. This highlights that the gains stem from *more effective visual representation learning* rather than just increased pretraining scale.
- **Stage-wise Multimodal Training Analysis:** An ablation study confirms that the second pretraining stage, which introduces OCR and video data with Codec patch selection, significantly improves performance on multimodal and OCR-related benchmarks compared to the image-only Stage 1, demonstrating the critical role of multimodal supervision in enhancing the encoder's suitability for LMMs.

#### 4.2. Attentive Probing Evaluation (Representation Quality)

Under attentive probing, which assesses intrinsic representational strength with frozen backbones and a lightweight classifier, OV-Encoder achieves state-of-the-art representation quality.
- **Overall Superiority:** OV-Encoder consistently outperforms strong vision baselines such as DINOv3, SigLIP2, AIMv2, MetaCLIP2, and CLIP across 7 video understanding benchmarks (SSV2, Diving48, Perception Test, CharadesEgo, Epic-Verb, Epic-Noun, Kinetics-400, HMDB51) under both 8-frame/2048-patch and 16-frame/4096-patch settings.
- **Significant Gains on Motion-Sensitive Tasks:** Notably, OV-Encoder achieves a 17.1% and 8.1% Top-1 accuracy improvement over SigLIP2 and DINOv3, respectively, on **Diving-48** under an identical patch budget (2048 tokens). This demonstrates superior motion modeling capability without sacrificing performance on appearance-dominated datasets like Kinetics-400.
- **Patch-Efficient Video Understanding:** OV-Encoder (Codec) yields superior performance to SigLIP2 while processing only 3.1% to 25% of the patches compared to dense processing of a 64-frame video. This translates to a **75.0%–96.9% reduction in patch processing** while maintaining long-range temporal coverage.

#### 4.3. Ablation of Codec-guided Patch Selection

Controlled interventions rigorously demonstrate that the observed performance gains are directly attributable to the content and semantic specificity of codec-selected motion patches:
- **Patch Content Necessity:** Replacing motion-heavy patches with non-motion patches from the same video significantly degrades performance, especially on motion-sensitive tasks.
- **Semantic Specificity:** Substituting codec-selected motion patches with motion patches from *unrelated videos* leads to even larger performance drops, indicating the model relies on semantically aligned motion cues, not just generic motion.
- **Patch-Position Shuffle (Negative Control):** Randomly permuting spatiotemporal positions of patches causes a substantially larger performance drop, confirming the critical importance of coherent structural alignment.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Visualizations confirm that the codec-guided selection mechanism effectively identifies and focuses computation on dynamic foreground objects (e.g., moving pedestrians) characterized by high residual magnitudes and strong motion vectors, while de-emphasizing static background regions. Case studies on continuous motion (Diving) and sparse key-frame events (Cooking) further illustrate how codec-style patch extraction efficiently distributes tokens across a long temporal duration, capturing critical, brief moments that uniform frame sampling might miss.

In summary, OneVision-Encoder redefines the performance frontier by demonstrating that codec-aligned, patch-level sparsity is a powerful principle, delivering both efficiency and state-of-the-art accuracy across a broad spectrum of multimodal understanding tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OneVision-Encoder represents a significant conceptual and empirical contribution to the fields of computer vision and multimodal artificial intelligence, with profound implications for the future development of generalist AI models.

#### 5.1. Foundational Principle for General AI

The paper's most impactful claim is that **codec-aligned, patch-level sparsity is not an optimization trick but a foundational principle for next-generation visual generalists.** By aligning deep learning architectures with the information-theoretic principles of video codecs – specifically, the explicit decomposition of visual signals into stable context and sparse, motion-driven updates – OneVision-Encoder offers a paradigm shift. It challenges the conventional uniform processing of dense pixel grids, advocating for a more intelligent allocation of computational resources to genuinely informative regions. This could inform architectural design across various data modalities that exhibit similar redundancy and sparsity.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability

One of the most critical challenges in scaling large multimodal models is computational cost. OneVision-Encoder significantly addresses this by demonstrating that substantial reductions in visual tokens (75.0%–96.9%) do not compromise, but rather *improve*, performance. This principle of "predictive compression" enables models to operate on full temporal contexts (e.g., 64 frames) with a fixed, small token budget, offering a scalable engine for universal multimodal intelligence. This efficiency is crucial for deploying sophisticated AI models in real-world applications, especially those with stringent computational or latency requirements.

#### 5.3. Superior Visual Representation Learning

The consistent outperformance of strong baselines like SigLIP2, DINOv3, and Qwen3-ViT across a wide array of image, video, and document understanding benchmarks, often with fewer pretraining tokens, underscores the quality of representations learned by OV-Encoder. The particular strengths in motion-sensitive tasks (e.g., Diving-48) highlight its ability to capture fine-grained temporal dynamics more effectively. This suggests that the codec-aligned approach fosters a deeper, more semantically coherent understanding of visual content.

#### 5.4. Unifying Modalities and Data Structures

The methodology's ability to unify various input formulations—dense video with sparse patches, chunk-wise sampling, and single images—through a shared 3D-RoPE and Transformer backbone is highly significant. This unified framework simplifies the development of multimodal models, allowing a single encoder to flexibly handle diverse visual data types and structures without architectural modifications or modality-specific parameters.

#### 5.5. Democratization of Research

The commitment to releasing code, training protocols, and model parameters is a major contribution to the research community. This promotes transparency, reproducibility, and significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers to build upon and experiment with these advanced multimodal capabilities. This aligns with the "EvolvingLMMs-Lab" ethos of fostering open and accessible AI development.

#### 5.6. Future Impact on Multimodal AI

OneVision-Encoder lays groundwork for:
- **Next-Generation LMMs:** It provides a highly efficient and effective vision backbone that can be seamlessly integrated into LLMs, potentially leading to LMMs with superior long-context video understanding capabilities and reduced computational footprints.
- **Domain-Specific AI:** The principle of leveraging inherent data structure (like codec information) could be extended to other complex domains, such as medical imaging (e.g., dynamic MRI sequences) or scientific simulations, where identifying salient changes and reducing redundancy is critical.
- **Edge AI and Real-time Processing:** The efficiency gains could enable more complex visual AI models to run on edge devices or in real-time streaming applications, expanding their practical applicability.

In conclusion, OneVision-Encoder presents a compelling argument for a principled approach to visual intelligence, demonstrating that by deeply understanding and aligning with the underlying information-theoretic structure of visual data, we can build more powerful, efficient, and scalable multimodal AI systems. It represents a crucial step towards developing truly generalist AI models capable of understanding and reasoning about the complex visual world.
