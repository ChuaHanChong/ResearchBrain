# OneVision-Encoder: Codec-Aligned Sparsity as a Foundational Principle for Multimodal Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> OneVision-Encoder, developed by Glint Lab, AIM for Health Lab, and MVP Lab, introduces a multi-modal AI model based on the principle of "codec-aligned sparsity" inspired by video compression. This approach enables efficient visual information processing by focusing on salient patches, yielding superior performance across various image, video, and document understanding benchmarks while reducing patch processing by 75.0%–96.9%.

## Problem

- Existing vision models often rely on dense token sequences from sparsely sampled frames, assuming uniform importance across spatial regions and temporal frames.
- This dense processing leads to inefficient computation by expending resources on redundant or static background information.
- Current self-supervised learning paradigms often lack explicit architectural alignment with the fundamental information-theoretic structure of visual data, limiting efficiency and semantic coherence.

## Method

- Developed **OneVision-Encoder**, an ==HEVC-style Vision Transformer== that aligns its architecture with video codec principles, explicitly decomposing visual signals into stable spatial context (I-frames) and sparse temporal updates (P-frames).
- Implemented ==Codec Patchification==, a mechanism that selectively encodes only 3.1% to 25% of patches rich in signal entropy (motion vectors and residuals) for P-frames, while fully encoding I-frames.
- Employed a shared ==3D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)== to unify spatial and temporal reasoning, accommodating irregular token layouts generated by sparse patch selection across dense video, chunk-wise, and single-image inputs.

## Results

- OV-Encoder-Lang, when integrated into Large Multimodal Models, consistently outperformed SigLIP2 and Qwen3-ViT across **16 multimodal benchmarks** for video, image, and document understanding tasks.
- Under attentive probing, OV-Encoder achieved state-of-the-art representation quality, surpassing strong vision baselines like SigLIP2 and DINOv3 across **7 video understanding benchmarks**, including a **17.1% Top-1 accuracy gain** over SigLIP2 on Diving-48.
- The codec-aligned approach reduced visual token processing by **75.0%–96.9%** for a **64-frame video** input while maintaining long-range temporal coverage and achieving superior performance, demonstrating a positive correlation between efficiency and accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Artificial general intelligence can be viewed as a compression problem, where architectural alignment with the fundamental structure of data, such as the codec-inspired decomposition of video, leads to more effective visual representation learning.
> - Discriminative visual information is inherently sparse, and focusing computational resources exclusively on these salient, unpredictable regions (motion and residuals) improves both model efficiency and accuracy.
> - A unified positional encoding scheme, like 3D-RoPE, is essential for maintaining structural consistency and enabling coherent attention across diverse and irregularly sparse spatiotemporal token arrangements.
