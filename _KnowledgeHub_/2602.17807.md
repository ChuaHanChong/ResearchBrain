# VidEoMT: Your ViT is Secretly Also a Video Segmentation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> VidEoMT introduces an encoder-only Vision Transformer architecture for online video segmentation that integrates object segmentation and temporal tracking. The model demonstrates competitive accuracy across various video segmentation benchmarks while achieving speedups of 5x to over 19x compared to existing complex, multi-component models.

## Problem

- Existing state-of-the-art video segmentation models rely on complex architectures, typically decoupling segmentation and tracking into multiple specialized modules.
- This architectural complexity introduces considerable computational overhead, making real-time online video processing challenging.
- Specialized components like ViT Adapters, pixel decoders, Transformer decoders, and re-identification layers are often employed for temporal association.

## Method

- A progressive architectural simplification is applied, removing components like complex segmenters, context-aware features, and re-identification layers from state-of-the-art models.
- **VidEoMT** is introduced as an encoder-only ==Vision Transformer== architecture, leveraging a large-scale pre-trained ViT (e.g., DINOv2) for both segmentation and temporal association.
- Temporal modeling is achieved through query propagation, which passes object query embeddings from previous frames, and query fusion, which combines these with learned queries to detect new objects.

## Results

- VidEoMT achieves competitive accuracy, e.g., 68.6 AP on YouTube-VIS 2019, comparable to the original CAVIS's 68.9 AP.
- The model exhibits substantial efficiency gains, operating at **160** FPS on YouTube-VIS compared to CAVIS's **15** FPS, representing over a **10x** speedup.
- VidEoMT consistently delivers **5x** to over **19x** faster inference across various video segmentation benchmarks (VIS, VPS, VSS) while maintaining or exceeding the performance of state-of-the-art, more complex models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale pre-trained Vision Transformers (e.g., DINOv2) possess sufficient intrinsic capabilities to handle both object segmentation and temporal association.
> - Specialized architectural components, such as pixel decoders, context-aware features, re-identification layers, and dedicated tracking modules, can be largely replaced by the inherent feature learning of a strong ViT encoder.
> - Simple mechanisms like query propagation and fusion effectively reintroduce temporal modeling into an encoder-only architecture, maintaining high accuracy without increased complexity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video segmentation, encompassing tasks such as video instance segmentation (VIS), video panoptic segmentation (VPS), and video semantic segmentation (VSS), has seen significant advancements. Current state-of-the-art models typically rely on complex architectures that combine per-frame segmenters with specialized tracking modules. These modules often include components like Vision Transformer (ViT) Adapters, pixel decoders, Transformer decoders, context-aware features, and re-identification layers. While effective in achieving high accuracy, this paradigm introduces considerable architectural complexity and computational overhead, limiting their application in real-time online video processing.

Concurrently, a trend has emerged in computer vision leveraging large-scale pre-training of Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), particularly Vision Transformers (ViTs) like DINOv2. These powerful encoders have demonstrated robust feature learning capabilities across various downstream tasks. Notably, recent work in image segmentation, such as Encoder-only Mask Transformer (EoMT), has shown that accurate image segmentation can be achieved by simply integrating learnable queries into a large, pre-trained ViT encoder, bypassing the need for specialized decoders or other task-specific components.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these two trends. It challenges the conventional wisdom in video segmentation that necessitates complex, specialized modules for temporal tracking. By drawing inspiration from the architectural simplification observed in image segmentation with EoMT, the authors investigate whether the inherent capabilities of powerful, pre-trained ViT encoders can extend to handle the additional requirement of temporal association in video segmentation, thereby making the specialized tracking components redundant. The work aims to demonstrate a simplified, encoder-only approach that unifies segmentation and tracking, potentially setting a new direction for more efficient video analysis models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a video segmentation model that offers a superior balance between accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing methods, by significantly simplifying its architecture. The authors specifically aim to eliminate the need for dedicated tracking modules and other specialized components commonly found in state-of-the-art video segmentation models.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Architectural Complexity and Computational Overhead:** Current video segmentation models, while achieving high accuracy, suffer from increasing architectural complexity. This complexity, arising from combining multiple specialized components for object localization, classification, and tracking, leads to significant computational overhead. This overhead often results in slow inference speeds, making real-time online video processing challenging or impractical for many applications. The authors are motivated to explore if this trend of increasing complexity is necessary or if a simpler approach can achieve comparable results.

2. **Leveraging the Capabilities of Pre-trained Vision Foundation Models (VFMs):** The authors are motivated by the observed power of modern Vision Foundation Models, particularly large Vision Transformers (ViTs) pre-trained on extensive datasets (e.g., DINOv2). These models have demonstrated an ability to learn rich, general-purpose visual representations. Recent studies, such as EoMT for image segmentation, suggest that a sufficiently large and pre-trained ViT encoder can effectively handle complex tasks like object localization and classification without requiring additional specialized decoders. The authors hypothesize that these strong pre-trained ViTs might also possess intrinsic capabilities for temporal tracking.

3. **Exploiting Cross-View Consistent Features for Tracking:** A specific motivation for the hypothesis regarding ViT's tracking capabilities stems from the training objectives of DINO-style models. These models are designed to learn consistent feature representations for the same object across different views. The authors suggest that this inherent cross-view consistency is a fundamental property crucial for tracking objects across consecutive frames in a video, thus making these pre-trained ViTs highly suitable for video segmentation tasks, including temporal association.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to validate the hypothesis that a powerful, pre-trained ViT can assume many functionalities traditionally handled by specialized components, thereby unifying segmentation and temporal tracking within a single, efficient encoder-only architecture. The ultimate goal is to enable faster, online video segmentation without a significant degradation in accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this work involves a systematic simplification of existing state-of-the-art video segmentation architectures, followed by the introduction of novel mechanisms to enable temporal modeling within an encoder-only Vision Transformer (ViT).

**Online Video Segmentation Task Definition:**
The task is defined as online video segmentation, where for each frame in a video sequence, the model must output a set of binary segmentation masks and corresponding class labels for all objects, while maintaining consistent object identities across frames. Importantly, predictions for the current frame ($I_t$) may only depend on the current frame and previously processed frames ($I_1, \ldots, I_{t-1}$).

**Preliminaries – Deconstruction of State-of-the-Art:**
The authors analyze the conventional paradigm in state-of-the-art online video segmentation models (e.g., CAVIS), which typically decouple the process into a segmenter and a tracker.
- **Segmenter:** Comprises a pre-trained ViT, a ViT-Adapter (for multi-scale features), a CNN-based pixel decoder, and a Mask Transformer decoder with learnable queries to produce per-frame masks and class labels.
- **Tracker:** Consists of multiple Transformer blocks that take object-level query embeddings from the current frame and temporally updated queries from the previous frame to establish cross-frame correspondences.
- **Specialized Components:** Beyond the core segmenter and tracker, these models often incorporate:
 - **Context-Aware Features:** These features are extracted by pooling smoothed features at object boundary pixels from high-resolution feature maps, aiming to enrich query embeddings with local neighborhood information for robustness.
 - **Re-identification Layers:** Typically multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) paired with contrastive training objectives to enhance instance discrimination and enforce similarity for the same object across frames.

**Removing Task-specific Components (Stepwise Simplification):**
Inspired by the efficiency of EoMT for image segmentation, the authors progressively remove specialized components from the CAVIS architecture to evaluate their necessity:
1. **Replacing the Segmenter with EoMT:** The complex ViT-Adapter, pixel decoder, and Transformer decoder are replaced by EoMT's simpler mechanism. In EoMT, learnable queries are directly injected into the last $L_2$ layers of the ViT encoder and processed jointly with patch tokens to produce object representations.
2. **Removing Context-Aware Features:** The computationally intensive context-aware feature extraction module is eliminated, hypothesizing that a strong pre-trained ViT's features are already sufficiently rich.
3. **Removing Re-identification Layers:** These layers and their associated memory-intensive contrastive losses are removed, based on the hypothesis that large-scale pre-training already endows the ViT encoder with adequate instance-level discriminative knowledge.
4. **Removing the Tracker Entirely:** This step reduces the model to a purely per-frame EoMT, serving as a baseline to quantify the performance degradation when no explicit temporal modeling is present. Object identity is maintained naively by assigning predictions from the same query index across frames to the same track.

**VidEoMT Architecture (Unified Encoder-Only Design):**
After simplifying the architecture by removing the tracker, the core challenge becomes reintroducing temporal modeling within the encoder-only framework without reintroducing complexity. VidEoMT addresses this with two key mechanisms:
1. **Query Propagation:** For the initial frame ($t=0$), standard learnable queries ($Q_{lrn}$) are fed into the last $L_2$ ViT blocks. For subsequent frames ($t>0$), instead of re-using $Q_{lrn}$, the object query embeddings ($Q_S_{t-1}$) produced from the previous frame are directly propagated and used as input to the last $L_2$ layers of the ViT. This allows information about objects to be carried forward in time without additional computational layers.
2. **Query Fusion:** To counteract the diminishing influence of $Q_{lrn}$ over time and ensure the model can detect newly appearing objects, a lightweight query fusion strategy is introduced. The propagated queries from the previous frame ($Q_S_{t-1}$) are transformed by a linear layer and then element-wise added to the original learned queries ($Q_{lrn}$). This combined set of queries ($Q_F_t = \text{Linear}(Q_S_{t-1}) + Q_{lrn}$) is then fed into the ViT. This mechanism balances the temporal context from past frames with the adaptability of learnable queries for new content.

**Training:**
VidEoMT is trained using the Mask2Former objective function, which includes cross-entropy loss for classification and binary cross-entropy and Dice losses for segmentation. Temporal consistency during training is enforced by adapting the ground-truth matching strategy from DVIS++, where a ground-truth object is matched to a query in its first appearance frame and maintains that match across subsequent frames. The training involves two stages: an initial image segmentation pre-training stage (on COCO and the target video dataset without temporal supervision), followed by a fine-tuning stage for video segmentation where temporal modeling is introduced. Crucially, the ViT encoder is fine-tuned throughout, unlike some baseline approaches that freeze it after initial pre-training. A DINOv2-pretrained ViT serves as the default backbone. Optimization uses AdamW with layer-wise learning rate decay.

**Evaluation:**
Models are evaluated on various video segmentation benchmarks (YouTube-VIS, OVIS for VIS; VIPSeg for VPS; VSPW for VSS) using standard metrics such as Average Precision (AP), Video Panoptic Quality (VPQ), and mean IoU (mIoU). Computational efficiency is measured in Frames Per Second (FPS) and GFLOPs on an NVIDIA H100 GPU, leveraging FlashAttention v2 and torch.compile.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings demonstrating the efficacy and efficiency of VidEoMT:

**Stepwise Transformation from CAVIS to VidEoMT (Table 1):**
The progressive removal of specialized components and the introduction of VidEoMT's unique mechanisms yielded the following insights on YouTube-VIS 2019:
- **Segmenter Replacement:** Replacing CAVIS's complex segmenter with EoMT significantly increased speed by nearly 3x (from 15 FPS to 42 FPS) with only a minor 0.8 AP drop (from 68.9 to 68.1).
- **Removal of Context-Aware Features and Re-identification Layers:** These removals further boosted speed to 74 FPS without a substantial impact on accuracy (AP remained around 68.0-68.4). This indicates that the DINOv2 ViT encoder can indeed replicate the functionalities of these components.
- **Removal of the Entire Tracker:** Eliminating the tracker led to a speedup of over 10x (162 FPS) compared to original CAVIS, but resulted in a substantial 7.6 AP drop (to 61.3), confirming the importance of temporal modeling. However, the residual accuracy suggested some inherent consistency from the queries.
- **Query Propagation:** Introducing query propagation improved AP by 2.6 points (to 63.9) without additional computational cost, demonstrating its effectiveness in reintroducing temporal modeling.
- **Query Fusion (VidEoMT):** The final addition of query fusion recovered most of the accuracy, achieving 68.6 AP, which is comparable to the original CAVIS (68.9 AP), while maintaining a high speed of 160 FPS.
These results establish that VidEoMT achieves an advantageous balance of accuracy and efficiency, largely due to its streamlined ViT-only architecture that leverages hardware/software optimizations effectively.

**Comparison with State-of-the-Art Models:**
VidEoMT consistently demonstrated competitive accuracy with significantly higher efficiency across various video segmentation benchmarks:
- **Video Instance Segmentation (VIS - YouTube-VIS, OVIS):** VidEoMT (ViT-L DINOv2) achieved AP scores comparable to or, in some cases, superior to DVIS, DVIS++, DVIS-DAQ, and CAVIS. For instance, on YouTube-VIS 2019, VidEoMT obtained 68.6 AP at 160 FPS, compared to CAVIS's 68.9 AP at 15 FPS, representing a speedup of over 10x. On YouTube-VIS 2022, VidEoMT (42.6 AP) surpassed CAVIS (39.5 AP) and DVIS-DAQ (42.0 AP) while being considerably faster. Across all VIS benchmarks, VidEoMT was 5x to over 14x faster than its counterparts.
- **Video Panoptic Segmentation (VPS - VIPSeg):** VidEoMT exhibited a minor decrease in VPQ (55.2 VPQ) compared to DVIS-DAQ (57.4 VPQ), but achieved a nearly 19x speedup (75 FPS vs. 4 FPS).
- **Video Semantic Segmentation (VSS - VSPW):** VidEoMT outperformed existing methods, improving mIoU by +2.1 (to 64.9) and achieving higher temporal consistency (95.0 mVC) compared to DVIS++, while being over 5x faster (73 FPS vs. 13 FPS).

**Further Analyses:**
- **EoMT as a Segmenter (Table 6):** Comparing VidEoMT to approaches where EoMT serves as a segmenter augmented with existing trackers (e.g., EoMT + CAVIS), VidEoMT yielded slightly better AP (68.6 vs. 68.1) and was nearly 4x faster, supporting the superiority of the unified encoder-only design.
- **Query Propagation Methods (Table A and 7):** Different query propagation strategies were evaluated. The "propagation only" variant showed improvement over no propagation (63.9 AP vs. 61.3 AP). The query fusion approach in VidEoMT proved most accurate (68.6 AP) and efficient (160 FPS) compared to alternatives like "Non-object reset" (67.8 AP, 157 FPS) or a TrackFormer-based approach in an encoder-decoder setting (68.0 AP, 32 FPS). This confirmed the effectiveness of the encoder-only architecture and the proposed fusion strategy.
- **Impact of Pre-training (Table 8):** The effectiveness of VidEoMT was strongly correlated with the scale of pre-training. With large-scale pre-training (DINOv2, DINOv3, EVA-02), VidEoMT performed comparably to CAVIS. However, the performance gap between VidEoMT and CAVIS widened significantly as the pre-training scale decreased (e.g., ImageNet-1K resulted in a 2.7 AP difference). This finding strongly supports the hypothesis that large-scale VFM pre-training is essential for VidEoMT to take over specialized component functionalities.
- **Impact of Model Size (Table 9):** Increasing the ViT model size (from S to L) positively impacted VidEoMT's performance, reducing the accuracy gap with CAVIS. VidEoMT with a ViT-L backbone demonstrated significantly higher accuracy than CAVIS with a ViT-S backbone, while still maintaining an order-of-magnitude speed advantage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VidEoMT represents a notable development in video segmentation, offering significant implications for both research and practical applications.

**Architectural Simplification:** The work demonstrates that the prevailing paradigm of using complex, decoupled architectures with numerous specialized modules for video segmentation is not a strict necessity. By achieving competitive accuracy with an encoder-only Vision Transformer, VidEoMT suggests a potential re-evaluation of design principles in the field, advocating for simpler, more unified models. This simplification reduces the engineering overhead associated with developing and maintaining complex pipelines.

**Substantial Efficiency Gains:** A primary impact of VidEoMT is its remarkable efficiency. The model achieves speedups ranging from 5x to over 19x compared to current state-of-the-art methods, processing video frames at up to 160 FPS with a ViT-L backbone. This level of performance is critical for enabling online, real-time video processing in scenarios where computational resources or latency are significant constraints. Applications in autonomous driving, robotics, surveillance, and live video analysis could directly benefit from these speed improvements, making real-time deployment more feasible.

**Validation of Vision Foundation Model Capabilities:** The research provides further evidence for the robustness and versatility of large, pre-trained Vision Foundation Models. It validates the hypothesis that a sufficiently large and well-pretrained ViT encoder possesses inherent capabilities to handle complex spatio-temporal tasks like object segmentation and temporal association simultaneously, thereby rendering many traditionally "specialized" components redundant. This finding encourages further exploration of the intrinsic functionalities of VFMs and their potential to simplify architectures across various computer vision domains.

**Foundation for Future Research:** VidEoMT establishes a new baseline for efficient video segmentation and opens several avenues for future research. This includes investigating encoder-only designs for other video analysis tasks (e.g., action recognition, object detection in video), exploring further optimizations for ViT architectures to enhance efficiency, and studying how different pre-training strategies or foundation models might further improve the capabilities of such simplified architectures. The work's emphasis on leveraging pre-trained features may also stimulate research into more efficient fine-tuning strategies for downstream video tasks.

**Potential Paradigm Shift:** The success of VidEoMT, particularly its ability to maintain accuracy while drastically improving speed through architectural simplification, indicates a potential shift in the methodological paradigm for video segmentation. Moving away from component-heavy, complex systems towards streamlined, VFM-driven encoder-only models could lead to more scalable, deployable, and resource-efficient solutions for real-world computer vision challenges.
