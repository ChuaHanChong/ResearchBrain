# Manual2Skill: Learning to Read Manuals and Acquire Robotic Skills for Furniture Assembly Using Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Manual2Skill framework enables robots to interpret visual instruction manuals and acquire complex manipulation skills for furniture assembly. It leverages Vision-Language Models to generate hierarchical assembly plans and then estimates component poses for successful real-world assembly of various IKEA furniture items.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to interpret abstract human instruction manuals (like IKEA's) for complex manipulation tasks, unlike humans.
- Traditional robot learning methods often require extensive, fine-grained, and labor-intensive demonstration data.
- Existing robotic assembly systems typically focus on isolated subproblems or simplified scenarios, lacking a comprehensive framework to interpret manuals for end-to-end assembly.

## Method

- **Manual2Skill**'s VLM-guided pipeline uses ==GPT-4o== to interpret manual images and real-world scenes, generating a ==hierarchical assembly graph== that outlines the task structure and part relationships.
- A per-step assembly pose estimation module, based on a multi-modal ==Graph Neural Network==, predicts 6D poses of all components at each assembly stage by fusing image and point cloud features.
- Motion planning, using ==RRT-Connect==, translates predicted poses into collision-free robot actions for grasping and placing components, guided by a stable base part's real-world pose.

## Results

- The VLM-guided graph generation achieved an average success rate of **32.4%** across varying furniture complexities, significantly outperforming heuristic baselines.
- The per-step assembly pose estimation module surpassed baselines, achieving an **86.8%** Part Accuracy for chair assembly, attributed to multi-modal fusion and GNNs.
- Manual2Skill successfully assembled **29 out of 50** furniture pieces (**58%** success rate) in simulation and achieved Average Completion Rates between **60.0%** and **85.0%** for four real IKEA furniture items using xArm6 robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively bridge the gap between abstract human instructions and structured robotic task planning by extracting hierarchical assembly graphs.
> - Integrating multi-modal features (image and point cloud) with a Graph Neural Network significantly improves 6D pose estimation for complex, multi-part assembly tasks.
> - Learning from abstract instruction manuals offers a scalable alternative to data-intensive fine-grained demonstrations, making robot skill acquisition more practical and generalizable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "Manual2Skill" addresses a critical challenge in robotics: enabling machines to interpret abstract human instructions, a capability that humans acquire with remarkable ease. This work sits at the confluence of several active research areas, including robotic assembly, vision-language model (VLM) guided robot learning, and learning from demonstrations (LfD).

Historically, robotic assembly has been approached through two main paradigms: **geometric assembly** and **semantic assembly**. Geometric methods rely on precise shape matching and contact analysis, while semantic methods use high-level contextual information about parts. Furniture assembly, particularly IKEA furniture, serves as a prime example of a semantic assembly task, requiring an understanding of part roles and intuitive relationships. Previous work in furniture assembly has tackled subproblems like motion planning, multi-robot collaboration, and assembly pose estimation. However, a significant gap remains in developing a *comprehensive framework* that can interpret human-designed manuals to guide an entire assembly pipeline. Manual2Skill directly aims to bridge this gap by integrating abstract manual interpretation into an end-to-end robotic assembly system, distinguishing itself from prior efforts that often focus on isolated components or simplified scenarios.

The rise of **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** has revolutionized how robots perceive and interact with their environments. VLMs have shown promise in task description, environment comprehension, and high-level task planning. However, developing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that can directly generate actions from multi-modal inputs is still data-intensive and struggles with long-horizon, complex manipulation tasks. Manual2Skill strategically leverages VLMs not for direct action generation, but as powerful *interpreters* of abstract instructions, extracting structured information (hierarchical graphs) to guide downstream robotic learning and execution modules. This approach circumvents some of the limitations of direct VLA models by decomposing the complex problem into manageable sub-tasks where VLMs excel, such as semantic understanding and reasoning over visual and textual cues.

Furthermore, this work extends the paradigm of **Learning from Demonstration (LfD)**. Traditional LfD often requires fine-grained demonstrations (e.g., robot trajectories or videos), which are labor-intensive to collect and challenging to generalize. While some research has explored coarse-grained demonstrations like hand-drawn sketches, these are often limited to simpler tabletop tasks. Manual2Skill pushes the boundaries of LfD by demonstrating learning from *abstract instruction manuals*, a highly coarse-grained and human-centric form of demonstration. By enabling robots to derive actionable strategies from such abstract inputs, this research significantly reduces the data demands traditionally associated with complex robotic skill acquisition, making it a more practical and scalable approach for real-world applications.

In essence, Manual2Skill represents a significant advancement by synthesizing robust VLM capabilities with structured robotic task planning and execution. It moves beyond solving isolated subproblems in robotic assembly by proposing an integrated framework that learns long-horizon manipulation skills from a ubiquitous form of human instruction, thereby pushing towards more autonomous and human-compatible robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The fundamental motivation behind Manual2Skill stems from the stark contrast between human and robot capabilities in executing complex manipulation tasks based on instructions. Humans possess an "extraordinary ability" to interpret abstract instruction manuals, such as those for IKEA furniture or LEGO models, and translate them into a sequence of executable physical actions. Robots, however, typically lack this cognitive flexibility, relying instead on labor-intensive and data-heavy methods like imitation learning or reinforcement learning. The manuals, though designed for human understanding with schematic diagrams and symbols, present a substantial challenge for robots to comprehend and translate into actionable strategies.

The paper identifies several core motivations for this research:
1. **Bridging the Human-Robot Instruction Gap:** The primary goal is to enable robots to learn and execute long-horizon manipulation skills from abstract instructions (visual manuals), akin to how humans do. This capability would significantly expand robots' capacity to tackle complex tasks.
2. **Leveraging Inherent Manual Structure:** Manuals inherently encode valuable structural information about complex tasks. They decompose high-level goals into mid-level subgoals, capturing task flow, dependencies, and potential for parallelizable subtasks. Extracting this structure is crucial for robots to manage complexity effectively.
3. **Inferring Specific Task Information:** Beyond structure, robots need to infer specific details for each step from manual images and text, such as the involved components, their types, and their precise spatial relationships.
4. **Generating Actionable Sequences:** Ultimately, the robot must translate this interpreted information into a sequence of executable physical actions, including grasping, placing, and connecting components.
5. **Addressing Data Scarcity in LfD:** Current LfD methods often require fine-grained demonstrations, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire. Learning from abstract manuals offers a way to reduce this dependence and improve practicality.
6. **Tackling Real-World Complexity:** The assembly of IKEA furniture is presented as a "challenging and practical task" that demands complex manipulation skills, making it an ideal benchmark for this research.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the Manual2Skill framework are:
- To propose a novel framework, Manual2Skill, that leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to learn robotic skills from visual instruction manuals, specifically for generalizable IKEA furniture assembly.
- To develop a hierarchical graph generation pipeline using VLMs to extract structured information for assembly tasks, making it applicable to real-world assembly and extensible to other domains.
- To define and implement a novel "per-step assembly pose estimation" task that predicts the 6D poses of all involved components at each assembly step, addressing real-world assembly precision requirements.
- To evaluate the effectiveness and applicability of the proposed framework by successfully assembling multiple real-world IKEA furniture items, demonstrating its ability to manage long-horizon tasks with efficiency and precision.
- To advance robotic systems towards human-like understanding and execution of complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Manual2Skill is an innovative, comprehensive framework designed to automate furniture assembly by interpreting visual instruction manuals and guiding robotic execution. The pipeline can be broadly divided into three main stages: VLM-guided hierarchical assembly graph generation, per-step assembly pose estimation, and robot assembly action generation.

**Overall Framework Overview:**
Given a set of manual images and real furniture parts, the system first generates a hierarchical assembly graph. Then, a pose estimation model predicts 6D poses for components at each step. Finally, a motion planning module generates and executes robot actions for assembly.

**A. VLM-Guided Hierarchical Assembly Graph Generation:**
This module leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4o, to interpret IKEA-style manuals and generate a high-level assembly plan represented as a hierarchical graph.
1. **VLM Capabilities and Prompt Structure:** The task's complexity, involving abstract manual sketches and high-resolution real-world images, necessitates advanced visual recognition and spatial reasoning—strengths of VLMs trained on vast datasets. Prompts include an "Image Set" (all manual pages and a real-world pre-assembly scene image) and "Text Instructions" (task-specific context, Chain-of-Thought reasoning, and in-context learning examples specifying JSON or natural language output format).
2. **Stage I: Associating Real Parts with Manuals:**
 - **Goal:** Link physical parts in a pre-assembly scene image to their roles as depicted in the manual's cover sketch.
 - **Process:** The VLM semantically interprets illustrations, analyzing spatial, contextual, and functional cues. Set of Marks and GroundingDINO are used to label physical parts with numerical indices in the scene image.
 - **Output:** JSON triplets `{name, label, role}` for each physical part. This stage also identifies "equivalent parts" (parts with identical geometric shapes but different labels, e.g., four chair legs), which is crucial for downstream modules.
3. **Stage II: Identifying Involved Parts in Each Step:**
 - **Goal:** Determine which specific parts and subassemblies are involved in each individual manual page.
 - **Process:** The VLM reasons through illustrated assembly steps, using the triplets and labeled scene from Stage I as hints. Critically, *cropped manual images* (focusing only on furniture parts, not extraneous elements like nails or human figures) are used as input to enhance VLM focus and performance.
 - **Output:** Nested lists `(Step N, Labeled Parts Involved)` for all steps, which are then transformed into the hierarchical assembly graph. This graph represents the assembly as a tree where leaf nodes are atomic parts and non-leaf nodes are subassemblies, allowing for flexible, non-unique assembly orders (e.g., parallelizable subtasks).

**B. Per-step Assembly Pose Estimation:**
This module estimates the 6D poses (translation and rotation) of components for each assembly step.
1. **Data Preparation:** A custom dataset is constructed from PartNet categories (chair, table, lamp). It includes manual images, point clouds, and target poses for each component in the camera frame. Variations in furniture shapes, subassemblies, and camera perspectives are incorporated.
2. **Canonicalization:** For each component's point cloud, its centroid is translated to the origin, and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to align it to a shape-driven canonical coordinate frame, ensuring consistent orientation.
3. **Model Architecture:**
 - **Image Encoder (E_I):** DeepLabV3+ encoder (MobileNet V2 backbone, Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling - ASPP) extracts a multi-channel image feature map, which is then mean-max pooled into a global vector.
 - **Point Cloud Encoder (E_P):** PointNet++ processes component point clouds to generate part-wise features.
 - **Cross-modality Fusion (E_G):** A Graph Neural Network (GNN), specifically a three-layer graph transformer, fuses the global image feature and part-wise point cloud features, treating them as nodes in a complete graph to update information.
 - **Pose Regressor (R):** A three-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) takes the updated point cloud features and predicts the 6D target pose (rotation and translation) for each component.
4. **Loss Function:** A weighted sum of multiple terms:
 - **Rotation Geodesic Loss:** Measures distance between predicted and ground-truth rotations.
 - **Translation MSE Loss:** Measures Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth translations.
 - **Chamfer Distance Loss:** Minimizes holistic distance between predicted and ground-truth point clouds.
 - **Pointcloud MSE Loss:** Measures distance between rotated point clouds.
 - **Equivalent Parts Loss:** For interchangeable components, it encourages distinct placements by maximizing Chamfer distance between transformed equivalent parts. The minimum loss across all permutations of equivalent parts is selected.

**C. Robot Assembly Action Generation:**
This module translates the predicted poses into executable robot actions.
1. **Align Predicted Poses with the World Frame:**
 - The pose estimation module outputs poses in the manual image's camera frame. For real-world execution, these must be transformed into the robot's world frame.
 - A stable base part in the scene is identified using VLM, and its real-world 6D pose is obtained via FoundationPose. This allows calculation of a 6D transformation matrix between the manual camera frame and the world frame.
 - This transformation is then applied to all predicted component poses to convert them to the robot's world frame.
2. **Assembly Execution:**
 - **Part Grasping:** FoundationPose and Segment Anything Model (SAM) obtain initial 6D poses of all real-world parts. Heuristic grasping methods are employed based on component geometry (e.g., centroid grasp for stick-shaped, thin boundary grasp with fixtures for flat/thin shapes). General grasping algorithms like GraspNet are noted as viable alternatives, but simplified heuristics are used here.
 - **Part Assembly Trajectory:** Once a component is grasped, its 6D pose is re-evaluated. RRT-Connect, a motion planning algorithm, generates a collision-free path for the grasped component to its target pose. All collision objects (represented as point clouds) are fed into the planner.
 - **Assembly Insertion Policy:** This is a acknowledged limitation. Due to the complexity of contact-rich tasks requiring multi-modal sensing (e.g., force feedback), human experts *manually perform the final insertion action* in the current framework. This is explicitly stated as an area for future work.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Manual2Skill framework was rigorously evaluated through a series of experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness across different stages and in real-world scenarios.

**1. Hierarchical Assembly Graph Generation (VLM-guided):**
- **Effectiveness:** The VLM-guided approach (using GPT-4o) significantly outperforms heuristic baselines (SingleStep, GeoCluster) in extracting structured information from manuals. For furniture with up to 6 parts, Manual2Skill achieved an average success rate of 55.6% (compared to 16.7% for SingleStep and 2.8% for GeoCluster), with an overall average success rate of 32.4% across furniture of varying complexity (up to 9+ parts). This highlights the VLM's superior capability in visual-spatial reasoning and understanding abstract instructions.
- **Generalization:** The VLM demonstrated good generalization ability, with minimal impact on performance (less than 1% drop) when tested with pre-assembly scene variations (randomly shuffled or rotated parts).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Necessity of Manuals:** Removing manual images entirely resulted in a drastic performance drop (average success rate of 3.9%), indicating that VLMs cannot rely solely on learned priors for complex assembly planning without explicit manual guidance.
 - **Impact of Segmentation:** Using non-segmented manual pages (i.e., full manual pages with extraneous details) significantly impaired VLM performance (average success rate of 15.6%), suggesting that additional irrelevant information distracts the VLM from core high-level planning. Cropped and segmented manual images are crucial.

**2. Per-step Assembly Pose Estimation:**
- **Superior Performance:** Manual2Skill's pose estimation module consistently outperformed two baselines (Li et al. and a Mean-Max Pool variant) across all evaluation metrics (Geodesic Distance, RMSE, Chamfer Distance, Part Accuracy) and furniture categories (Chair, Lamp, Table). For example, on chair assembly, Manual2Skill achieved a Part Accuracy (PA) of 86.8%, significantly higher than Li et al. (26.8%) and Mean-Max Pool (45.7%).
- **Contribution of Components:** The strong performance is attributed to the effectiveness of its multi-modal feature fusion (image and point cloud data) and the use of a Graph Neural Network (GNN) for capturing complex spatial relationships between parts.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Image Input:** Removing the image input dramatically reduced performance, confirming its crucial role in accurate pose estimation.
 - **Equivalent Parts Permutation:** Disabling the permutation mechanism for equivalent parts led to a drop in performance, as the model struggled to differentiate between interchangeable components, often placing them in similar, incorrect positions.
 - **Per-step Data:** Training and testing with per-step subassembly data (rather than only fully assembled shapes) significantly improved assembly prediction accuracy (PA improved from 75.3% to 98.3%), underscoring the benefit of granular, step-wise inference.

**3. Overall Performance Evaluation (Simulation):**
- **Success Rate:** In a PyBullet simulation environment, Manual2Skill successfully assembled 29 out of 50 furniture pieces (58% success rate) from the IKEA-Manuals dataset, outperforming a baseline (Li et al. + heuristic order) which assembled only 15 pieces (30%).
- **Failure Analysis:** The most common failure mode was identified as the VLM's inability to generate a perfectly accurate assembly graph, which subsequently led to misalignment errors in pose estimation. Other failures included parts being too far from ground truth, collisions during motion planning, or parts suspending in mid-air.

**4. Real-world Assembly Experiments:**
- **Practicality:** Manual2Skill was successfully deployed to assemble four real IKEA furniture items (Flisat, Variera, Sundvik, Knagglig) using two UFactory xArm6 robots and a RealSense D435 camera.
- **High Completion Rates:** The system achieved high Average Completion Rates (ACR) ranging from 60.0% to 85.0% across the items, significantly outperforming the Mean-Max Pool baseline (40.0% to 70.0%). While an Oracle Pose (ground truth) method showed higher ACRs (72.5% to 90.0%), Manual2Skill demonstrates strong real-world applicability.
- **Real-world Failure Modes:** Real-world failures primarily stemmed from motion planning limitations, such as collisions with the robotic arm or surrounding objects, or suboptimal grasping poses. The final insertion steps were manually performed by human experts due to the current limitation in handling contact-rich tasks requiring multi-modal sensing.

**5. Generalization to Other Assembly Tasks:**
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** The VLM-guided hierarchical graph generation module demonstrated remarkable zero-shot generalization by successfully generating assembly graphs for three distinct, non-furniture tasks: a Toy Car Axle, an Aircraft Model, and a Robotic Arm. This achieved a 100% success rate over five trials for each task, with generated graphs aligning perfectly with ground-truth sequences. This finding strongly supports the framework's versatility beyond its primary furniture assembly domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Manual2Skill represents a pivotal step forward in the quest to enable robots to understand and execute complex manipulation tasks based on human-designed instructions. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

**1. Advancing Human-Robot Collaboration:** The ability of robots to interpret abstract instruction manuals, a task intuitive to humans but challenging for machines, bridges a critical gap in human-robot interaction. This allows for a more natural and efficient transfer of knowledge from humans to robots, moving beyond laborious programming or data-intensive demonstration methods.

**2. Enhancing Robotic Autonomy in Complex Tasks:** By successfully demonstrating the autonomous assembly of real-world IKEA furniture, Manual2Skill pushes the boundaries of long-horizon manipulation capabilities. This is a complex task requiring sequential reasoning, precise pose estimation, and collision-free motion planning over multiple steps and diverse parts. Such autonomy has profound implications for industries like manufacturing, logistics, and even household assistance.

**3. Reducing Data Dependency in Robot Learning:** Traditional robot learning often requires extensive, fine-grained demonstration data. Manual2Skill's approach of leveraging abstract manuals as a form of "coarse-grained demonstration" significantly reduces this data burden. This makes the acquisition of new robotic skills more scalable, cost-effective, and practical for a wider range of applications where detailed demonstrations are impractical to obtain.

**4. Generalizability Across Domains:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization of the VLM-guided graph generation to non-furniture assembly tasks (toy cars, aircraft models, robotic arms) highlights the framework's broad applicability. This suggests that Manual2Skill is not merely a solution for furniture assembly but a generalizable paradigm for learning from visual instructions, potentially impacting diverse fields from industrial assembly lines to custom prototyping and educational robotics.

**5. Leveraging State-of-the-Art AI:** The strategic integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like GPT-4o as intelligent interpreters of visual and semantic information is a key strength. As VLMs continue to improve, the performance and robustness of frameworks like Manual2Skill are poised to increase, ensuring future scalability and adaptability to even more complex instruction sets.

**6. Future Directions and Unlocking New Avenues:** While the paper acknowledges limitations (e.g., manual insertion for contact-rich tasks, overlooking fine-grained manual details like grasping points or fastener locations), it lays a strong foundation for future research. Addressing these limitations, for instance, by integrating force/tactile sensing for automated fastening or developing low-level adaptive motion policies, will further enhance the robustness and practical utility of such robotic systems. This work could inspire new research into multimodal sensing, advanced manipulation strategies, and even more sophisticated VLM integration for richer instruction interpretation.

In conclusion, Manual2Skill represents a significant leap towards developing more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems. By enabling robots to "read" and comprehend human-designed instruction manuals, this research brings us closer to a future where robots can independently acquire and execute a vast array of complex manipulation skills, mirroring human capabilities and unlocking new possibilities for automation and assistance.
