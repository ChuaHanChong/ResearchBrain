# Direction-aware 3D Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University and The University of Tokyo developed a framework, including PoseRecover and PoseAlign, that integrates explicit ego-pose information into 3D Large Multimodal Models. This approach addresses the ambiguity of directional queries in existing datasets, enhancing spatial reasoning capabilities and achieving up to a 30.0% mIoU increase in referring segmentation on ScanRefer.

## Problem

- Existing 3D indoor datasets lack explicit ego-pose information, rendering a significant portion of directional queries (e.g., "left," "right") ill-posed for spatial reasoning by 3D LMMs.
- Without persistent awareness of ego poses and spatial directions, 3D LMMs struggle with robust and alias-free spatial reasoning, accurate understanding, and safe navigation for embodied agents.
- Prior methods either created new datasets with implicit ego-situational information or used 2D image-based approaches, which often introduced textual ambiguity or did not directly rectify the ill-posed nature of 3D point cloud benchmarks.

## Method

- Developed ==PoseRecover==, an automatic pipeline that retrieves mission-critical ego poses from raw RGB-D sequences by calculating object-frustum intersection rates and employs a robust "Clip" strategy for online pose selection.
- Introduced ==PoseAlign==, a method to integrate these identified ego poses into 3D LMMs, with three variants: PoseAlign-Transform (directly transforming point clouds into the camera coordinate frame), PoseAlign-Embed (modulating the projection layer with pose embeddings), and PoseAlign-Prompt (serializing pose info into textual prompts).
- Applied the framework to various 3D LMM architectures (e.g., 3D-LLAVA, LL3DA) and fine-tuned the multimodal projector and LLM using LoRA, while typically freezing the 3D encoder to focus improvements on directional awareness.

## Results

- Achieved substantial performance improvements across multiple 3D LMM backbones and datasets; for 3D-LLAVA with PoseAlign-Transform, mIoU increased by **30.0%** (from **42.6%** to **55.4%**) on ScanRefer and by **12.9%** (from **48.1%** to **54.3%**) on Multi3DRefer.
- Improved LLM-as-judge accuracy on question answering and captioning tasks, with a **3.5%** increase on ScanQA (to **47.3%**) and an **11.7%** increase on Scan2Cap (to **31.4%**) for 3D-LLAVA.
- Qualitative results demonstrated that PoseAlign-enhanced models successfully avoided directional ambiguity, providing robust spatial reasoning and more accurate responses to directional queries compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly transforming the input point cloud into the recovered camera coordinate frame (PoseAlign-Transform) is the most effective method for instilling directional awareness and achieves superior performance in refer segmentation tasks.
> - The systematic integration of explicit ego-pose information through PoseRecover and PoseAlign consistently improves the performance of 3D LMMs across diverse tasks and benchmarks by resolving directional ambiguity.
> - A significant percentage (40-95%) of queries in current 3D LMM benchmarks are direction-critical, highlighting a fundamental flaw that the proposed ego-pose integration addresses effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D Large Multimodal Models (3D LMMs) aims to integrate 3D scene understanding with natural language processing, enabling models to comprehend and reason about complex 3D environments. These models are designed for various tasks, including object grounding, referring, question answering, ego position reasoning, object captioning, and segmentation. A key objective for advanced 3D LMMs is to possess persistent awareness of ego poses and spatial directions to facilitate accurate understanding, robust spatial reasoning, and safe navigation for embodied agents.

However, the existing landscape of 3D indoor datasets, such as ScanRefer, ScanQA, and Scan2Cap, predominantly focuses on third-person perspectives and often lacks explicit ego-pose information. While these datasets include a substantial number of directional queries (e.g., "left," "right," "front," "back"), the absence of a defined ego-centric reference frame renders these queries inherently ill-posed for spatial reasoning. Prior research has attempted to address this by either creating new datasets that implicitly or explicitly encode ego-situational information (e.g., SQA3D, View2Cap) or by using 2D image-based methods that naturally contain implicit ego-pose information. However, these approaches often introduce textual ambiguity, rely on complex pose inference, or do not directly rectify the ill-posed nature of existing 3D point cloud benchmarks.

This work addresses a fundamental gap by proposing a systematic paradigm to integrate explicit ego-pose information into existing 3D point cloud benchmarks and models. It directly leverages readily available pose data, which is often generated during the collection of indoor point clouds via technologies like Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), to resolve directional ambiguity and enhance spatial reasoning capabilities across diverse 3D LMM architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a rigorous paradigm for enabling direction-aware 3D LMMs. This involves addressing the challenge posed by the absence of explicit ego-pose information in many existing 3D point cloud benchmarks, which leads to ambiguity in spatial reasoning tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:

1. **Ill-Posed Directional Queries:** A significant proportion (ranging from 40% to 95% according to the authors' analysis) of questions in prevalent 3D indoor datasets require explicit lateral direction reasoning (e.g., "Where is the bathroom placed relative to the bed?"). Without an explicit ego pose, such questions become ill-defined. For instance, in a room-level point cloud, the concepts of "left" or "right" relative to an agent become ambiguous if the agent's viewpoint or orientation is not specified.
2. **Criticality of Ego-Pose Awareness:** For an ideal generalist 3D LMM, persistent awareness of ego poses and spatial directions in the world coordinate is essential. This awareness is crucial for tasks like robust and alias-free spatial reasoning, accurate understanding of 3D input data, and safe navigation in 3D environments. Egocentric directions define spatial relations relative to an agent's body axes, while allocentric directions define them relative to an external anchor. In indoor scenarios, allocentric directions often fall back to an ego agent's perspective, making ego-pose fundamental for consistent spatial understanding.
3. **Availability of Pose Data:** In practical applications, especially with embodied agents collecting indoor point clouds using SLAM systems, ego poses are readily available. The authors argue that inferring ego poses as a latent variable within LMMs is conceptually redundant and adds unnecessary complexity when direct pose data can be incorporated.
4. **Limitations of Prior Approaches:** Previous attempts to enhance directional awareness often involved creating entirely new datasets or implicitly conveying ego poses through textual descriptions, which can introduce ambiguity. Methods that involve regressing camera poses are also limited as they are distinct from textual reasoning with direction awareness. The current work aims to rectify existing benchmarks rather than solely creating new ones, thereby extending the utility of established datasets.

To achieve its objective, the research proposes to identify and supplement ego poses into existing point cloud benchmarks and subsequently transform the corresponding point cloud data to align with these identified ego poses. This approach seeks to provide a principled solution to the problem of ill-posed directional reasoning, leveraging existing data more effectively.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work consists of two main novel designs: **PoseRecover**, an automatic pipeline for generating mission-critical ego poses for existing benchmarks, and **PoseAlign**, a method for incorporating these identified ego poses into 3D LMMs.

#### 3.1. PoseRecover: Automatic Pose Generation Pipeline

PoseRecover addresses the lack of explicit ego poses in existing 3D point cloud datasets. It retrieves question-related camera poses from raw RGB-D sequences of datasets like ScanNet-v2 by matching camera frustums with relevant object annotations. The process is divided into offline data generation and online pose selection strategies:

- **Offline Data Generation:**
 - **Frustum Computation:** For each camera pose in the RGB-D sequence, the corresponding 3D camera frustum (a visible pyramid with near and far cutoffs) is computed using intrinsic and extrinsic parameters.
 - **Object-Frustum Intersection:** PoseRecover calculates the spatial intersection rates between these camera frustums and question-related object annotations (e.g., segmentation masks, bounding boxes, object locations). Three types of object annotations are supported:
 - **Segmentation-based:** For objects defined by segmentation masks, each point in the object's point cloud is projected onto the image plane using camera parameters. A Z-buffer is constructed, and a visibility mask is established by comparing the projected depth of object points with the Z-buffer, ensuring only visible points are counted. The intersection ratio ($\phi_{seg}$) is the proportion of visible object points within the frustum.
 - **Bounding-box-based:** For objects defined by bounding boxes, Monte-Carlo sampling is applied within the box to estimate the intersection ratio ($\phi_{box}$) with the frustum.
 - **Point-based:** For datasets providing only a single object location, the normalized pixel distance ($\phi_{point}$) between the projected object pixel and the image center is computed, where smaller distances imply larger intersections.
 - **Pose List Generation:** These intersection ratios are exhaustively calculated for all camera poses and objects in a scene, creating a camera-object intersection matrix (pose list) for each scene. This process is vectorized for efficiency.

- **Online Pose Selection Strategy:** During training or inference, a question-specific column from the camera-object intersection matrix is retrieved using the ground-truth object annotation. Among the candidate camera poses with non-zero intersection, two strategies are evaluated:
 - **Top:** Selecting the pose with the highest intersection ratio.
 - **Clip:** Randomly sampling poses after discarding a top and bottom *X*% of non-zero scores. The "Clip" strategy (with *X* = 0.3) is empirically found to perform better, reducing outlier cases (e.g., opposite views) while retaining variety. This chosen pose serves as the ego reference frame.

#### 3.2. PoseAlign: Ego-Pose Integration into 3D LMMs

PoseAlign integrates the ego-pose information identified by PoseRecover into existing 3D LMMs. The authors propose and compare three variants to augment a common backbone (e.g., 3D-LLAVA style architecture):

- **PoseAlign-Transform (Default and Most Effective):** This variant directly transforms the input point cloud into the recovered camera coordinate frame. The point cloud *P* is rotated and translated such that the camera is at the origin, and its viewing direction aligns with a canonical forward direction (e.g., +X axis for front, +Y for left, +Z for up). This operation aligns the spatial distribution of scene points with the ego-viewing direction, ensuring consistency for "left" and "right" in the egocentric frame. No architectural modifications are required downstream as geometric relationships are preserved.
- **PoseAlign-Embed:** This variant keeps the point cloud unchanged but modulates the projection layer of the multimodal projector with an embedding of the 6-DoF pose. It encodes manual geometric features of the coordinates of the input tokens into their original feature vectors using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), introducing pose awareness at the feature-projection stage.
- **PoseAlign-Prompt:** In this approach, the pose information (position, up, front, left vectors from the rotation matrix) is serialized as numeric tokens and prepended to the textual prompt given to the Large Language Model (LLM). This allows the LLM to explicitly reason about spatial direction through text.

For all variants, the multimodal projector and, if applicable, the LLM are fine-tuned jointly using a LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) based adaptation scheme. The 3D encoder (point cloud encoder) is typically frozen to prevent it from forming preferences over objects based on their absolute positions and to ensure that improvements are attributed to the enhanced directional awareness rather than changes in base 3D feature extraction. Data augmentations like random rotation and flipping are disabled for PoseAlign variants to precisely maintain egocentric alignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive experimental validation across multiple 3D LMM backbones (LL3DA, LL3DA-SONATA, Chat-Scene, 3D-LLAVA) and datasets (ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer, ScanQA, Scan2Cap).

#### 4.1. Impact of Ego-Pose Integration

- **Prevalence of Directional Queries:** An initial analysis using GPT-OSS-20B revealed that 40% to 95% of questions in existing datasets (ScanQA, Scan2Cap, SQA3D) require directional reasoning, affirming the ill-posed nature of these benchmarks without ego-pose information.
- **Consistent Performance Improvements:** The PoseAlign modifications yielded consistent performance improvements across all tested 3D LMM backbones and datasets.
 - For **3D-LLAVA** with **PoseAlign-Transform**, significant gains were observed:
 - **ScanRefer mIoU:** 55.4% (an increase of 30.0% from baseline 42.6% when comparing PoseAlign-T(Clip X=0.3) to Baseline)
 - **Multi3DRefer mIoU:** 54.3% (an increase of 12.9% from baseline 48.1% when comparing PoseAlign-T(Clip X=0.3) to Baseline)
 - **ScanQA LLM-as-judge (L-A) Accuracy:** 47.3% (an increase of 3.5% from baseline 45.7%)
 - **Scan2Cap L-A Accuracy:** 31.4% (an increase of 11.7% from baseline 28.1%)
 - Similar positive trends were observed for LL3DA, LL3DA-SONATA, and Chat-Scene, with average bumps of 1% and 0.6% on ScanQA and Scan2Cap for traditional metrics (CiDEr, BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L), and greater increases for LLM-as-judge accuracy.
- **Effectiveness of PoseAlign-Transform:** Ablation studies on 3D-LLAVA demonstrated that PoseAlign-Transform consistently outperformed PoseAlign-Embed and PoseAlign-Prompt, particularly for refer segmentation tasks. This suggests that directly transforming the point cloud into the ego reference frame is the most effective method for introducing directional awareness.
- **Impact on Direction-Critical Subsets:** Performance analysis on direction-critical subsets showed that baseline models struggle significantly with these questions. PoseAlign-Transform consistently improved all metrics on these subsets, particularly LLM-as-judge accuracy, narrowing the performance gap with direction-agnostic questions. For ScanQA, PoseAlign-T improved CiDEr by 4.9% and L-A accuracy by 6.6% on the direction-critical subset.

#### 4.2. PoseRecover and Pose Selection Strategy

- **Importance of PoseRecover:** Testing a well-trained 3D-LLAVA baseline on PoseAlign-Transform data (without specific training) resulted in a significant degradation of metrics, indicating that the pose information provided by PoseRecover is not merely a shortcut but a critical input for trained models.
- **Clip Strategy Robustness:** The "Clip" strategy for pose selection, which samples after discarding extreme views, showed robustness across various clip ratios (0.0 ≤ X ≤ 0.49). A moderate clip ratio of X=0.3 yielded the best balance, improving results by stabilizing performance by reducing outlier views while maintaining data diversity.
- **Random Poses:** Using random camera poses instead of those identified by PoseRecover led to a decrease in model performance compared to the baseline, highlighting the importance of the PoseRecover design for selecting authentic, mission-critical poses.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- Qualitative examples illustrated that baseline models struggled with spatial relationships and directional queries due to non-informative world coordinates. In contrast, PoseAlign-enhanced models successfully avoided directional ambiguity, providing robust and consistent spatial reasoning, as shown by improved answers to "Where is the shelf located?" or "What is on the left of the light wood colored table?".

#### 4.4. Metrics and Limitations

- **LLM-as-judge Metric:** The LLM-as-judge accuracy (using GPT-OSS-20B and GPT-5-mini) was found to be a consistent and reliable metric, demonstrating consistent improvements across PoseAlign variants and robustness to different judgment models.
- **Limitations:** The method showed negative results on SQA3D because it lacks object annotations, preventing PoseRecover from determining authentic poses. Other limitations include the assumption of SLAM accuracy, the discrete nature of recovered poses limiting viewpoint variety, and the focus on static indoor environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents **PoseRecover** and **PoseAlign** as a framework that introduces a rigorously defined direction-aware paradigm for 3D Large Multimodal Models. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Rectification of Ill-Posed Benchmarks:** By automatically reconstructing and supplementing mission-critical ego poses, PoseRecover addresses a fundamental flaw in many existing 3D LMM benchmarks. This rectifies ill-posed directional queries, enabling more accurate and meaningful evaluation of spatial reasoning capabilities. This contribution is particularly relevant for datasets like ScanRefer, ScanQA, and Scan2Cap, which contain a high percentage of direction-critical questions.
2. **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning:** PoseAlign enables existing 3D LMM architectures to achieve persistent directional awareness. By aligning the input point cloud (or its features) to an ego reference frame, the ambiguity in ego direction is resolved, leading to substantial and consistent improvements in spatial reasoning performance across various vision-language tasks. The demonstrated improvements, especially in mIoU for referring segmentation and LLM-as-judge accuracy for question answering, suggest that this approach unlocks latent potential in current 3D LMMs.
3. **Generalizability and Efficiency:** The proposed framework is described as lightweight, generic, and training-efficient. It is applicable across diverse 3D LMM architectures (e.g., LL3DA, Chat-Scene, 3D-LLAVA) without requiring retraining of the core point cloud encoder. This makes it a practical solution for enhancing existing models and establishing a strong baseline for future direction-aware 3D-LMM development.
4. **Implications for Embodied AI:** The work directly supports the advancement of embodied AI. By providing 3D LMMs with a robust mechanism for understanding egocentric spatial relationships, it contributes to building more intelligent agents capable of accurate 3D scene understanding, robust navigation, and context-aware interaction in real-world environments. The argument that ego poses are "free-lunch" data from SLAM in embodied workflows further strengthens its practical relevance.
5. **New Research Directions:** The identification of "direction-critical" subsets within existing datasets provides a valuable diagnostic tool for researchers. The discussion of limitations, such as the reliance on SLAM accuracy, the discrete nature of recovered views, and the focus on static environments, also points towards clear avenues for future research, including robustness to noise, handling dynamic scenes, and exploring more diverse viewpoints.

In essence, this research provides a principled and effective method to bridge a critical gap in 3D LMMs, paving the way for more capable and context-aware embodied intelligent systems.
