# FinPercep-RM: A Fine-grained Reward Model and Co-evolutionary Curriculum for RL-based Real-world Super-Resolution

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces FinPercep-RM, a reward model with fine-grained perceptual and diagnostic capabilities, paired with a Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning strategy for Real-world Super-Resolution (Real-ISR). The system effectively prevents "reward hacking" and stabilizes training, enabling Real-ISR models to generate images with significantly enhanced perceptual quality, reduced artifacts, and higher human preference scores for realism and fidelity.

## Problem

- Existing Image Quality Assessment (IQA) models, commonly used as reward signals in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for Real-ISR, are insensitive to localized distortions and output only a single global score.
- This insensitivity leads to a "reward hacking" problem where Real-ISR generators produce perceptually undesirable local artifacts despite achieving high global IQA scores, misaligning with human perception.
- Integrating more sensitive, fine-grained reward signals, while necessary, can introduce complexity and instability during the generator's policy learning, hindering effective convergence.

## Method

- A Fine-grained ==Perceptual Reward== Model (**FinPercep-RM**) was developed, designed as an encoder-decoder network to provide both a global quality score and a spatially localized Perceptual Degradation Map (Fg-PDM), with the global score explicitly coupled to local defect detection.
- A novel dataset, FGR-30k, was constructed with 30,000 samples containing diverse, subtly distorted images and ground-truth spatial annotations of these defects, crucial for training FinPercep-RM's diagnostic capabilities.
- A Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning (CCL) strategy was proposed, which involves the synchronized, progressive evolution of both the Reward Model (increasing complexity) and the Generator (adapting to increasingly fine-grained rewards) to ensure stable training and convergence.

## Results

- FinPercep-RM consistently improved perceptual quality metrics (MUSIQ, MANIQA, LIQE) and reduced LPIPS distortion across three Real-ISR baselines (DiffBIR, SeeSR, DIT4SR) on four real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance over methods prone to 'reward hacking'.
- A user study confirmed that Real-ISR models integrated with FinPercep-RM were significantly preferred for both realism (**78.9%** preference for DIT4SR) and fidelity (**76.8%** preference for DIT4SR) compared to baseline approaches.
- FinPercep-RM provided stable and effective optimization signals across various RLHF algorithms (REFL, DPO, GRPO), whereas using a standard global IQA model as a reward consistently resulted in performance degradation or failure due to reward hacking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global image quality assessment metrics are insufficient for robustly guiding Real-ISR models, as they fail to penalize local, perceptually significant artifacts, leading to problematic 'reward hacking'.
> - Explicitly coupling a global quality score with spatially localized degradation information within a reward model effectively aligns machine optimization with human aesthetic preferences for fine-grained details.
> - A progressive, co-evolutionary curriculum for both the reward model and the generator is essential to balance training stability with the robustness provided by complex, fine-grained reward signals during reinforcement learning.
