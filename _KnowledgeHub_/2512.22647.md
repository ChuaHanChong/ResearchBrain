# FinPercep-RM: A Fine-grained Reward Model and Co-evolutionary Curriculum for RL-based Real-world Super-Resolution

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces FinPercep-RM, a reward model with fine-grained perceptual and diagnostic capabilities, paired with a Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning strategy for Real-world Super-Resolution (Real-ISR). The system effectively prevents "reward hacking" and stabilizes training, enabling Real-ISR models to generate images with significantly enhanced perceptual quality, reduced artifacts, and higher human preference scores for realism and fidelity.

## Problem

- Existing Image Quality Assessment (IQA) models, commonly used as reward signals in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for Real-ISR, are insensitive to localized distortions and output only a single global score.
- This insensitivity leads to a "reward hacking" problem where Real-ISR generators produce perceptually undesirable local artifacts despite achieving high global IQA scores, misaligning with human perception.
- Integrating more sensitive, fine-grained reward signals, while necessary, can introduce complexity and instability during the generator's policy learning, hindering effective convergence.

## Method

- A Fine-grained ==Perceptual Reward== Model (**FinPercep-RM**) was developed, designed as an encoder-decoder network to provide both a global quality score and a spatially localized Perceptual Degradation Map (Fg-PDM), with the global score explicitly coupled to local defect detection.
- A novel dataset, FGR-30k, was constructed with 30,000 samples containing diverse, subtly distorted images and ground-truth spatial annotations of these defects, crucial for training FinPercep-RM's diagnostic capabilities.
- A Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning (CCL) strategy was proposed, which involves the synchronized, progressive evolution of both the Reward Model (increasing complexity) and the Generator (adapting to increasingly fine-grained rewards) to ensure stable training and convergence.

## Results

- FinPercep-RM consistently improved perceptual quality metrics (MUSIQ, MANIQA, LIQE) and reduced LPIPS distortion across three Real-ISR baselines (DiffBIR, SeeSR, DIT4SR) on four real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance over methods prone to 'reward hacking'.
- A user study confirmed that Real-ISR models integrated with FinPercep-RM were significantly preferred for both realism (**78.9%** preference for DIT4SR) and fidelity (**76.8%** preference for DIT4SR) compared to baseline approaches.
- FinPercep-RM provided stable and effective optimization signals across various RLHF algorithms (REFL, DPO, GRPO), whereas using a standard global IQA model as a reward consistently resulted in performance degradation or failure due to reward hacking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global image quality assessment metrics are insufficient for robustly guiding Real-ISR models, as they fail to penalize local, perceptually significant artifacts, leading to problematic 'reward hacking'.
> - Explicitly coupling a global quality score with spatially localized degradation information within a reward model effectively aligns machine optimization with human aesthetic preferences for fine-grained details.
> - A progressive, co-evolutionary curriculum for both the reward model and the generator is essential to balance training stability with the robustness provided by complex, fine-grained reward signals during reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges within the field of Image Super-Resolution (ISR), particularly in the context of Real-world ISR (Real-ISR). Traditional ISR methods often focus on pixel-level fidelity, which can result in overly smooth images lacking realistic textures when applied to complex, real-world degradations. Recent advancements in generative models, especially those leveraging large-scale pre-trained text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models, have demonstrated potential for synthesizing rich textures and achieving improved perceptual quality in Real-ISR.

The paper integrates these generative approaches with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), a paradigm that has shown success in aligning image generation models with human preferences. RLHF relies on Reward Models (RMs) to quantify visual aesthetics and guide model optimization. However, the authors identify a critical limitation when applying existing Image Quality Assessment (IQA) models, typically used as RMs, to Real-ISR. These IQAs primarily provide a single global score and lack sensitivity to local, fine-grained distortions. This deficiency leads to a "reward hacking" problem, where ISR models learn to generate visually unappealing local artifacts that nevertheless achieve high global IQA scores, thereby misaligning with human perception.

This research aims to bridge the gap between the generative capabilities of T2I models and the need for perceptually aligned, artifact-free Real-ISR by developing a reward mechanism that accounts for fine-grained local quality and a training strategy to stably integrate this complex reward signal. It contributes to the ongoing effort to enhance the realism and perceptual quality of images generated by deep learning models, moving beyond traditional pixel-wise metrics towards human-centric evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified limitation in applying Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to Real-world Image Super-Resolution (Real-ISR). While RLHF, particularly with reward models (RMs) based on Image Quality Assessment (IQA), has shown promise in guiding generative models, the authors observe that conventional IQA models are inadequate for the specific demands of Real-ISR.

The core motivation can be elaborated through the following points:

- **Insensitivity of Existing Reward Models to Local Distortions:** Current IQA models typically output a single global quality score for an image. This coarse-grained perception fails to accurately identify and penalize localized, subtle distortions or artifacts that are common in Real-ISR outputs. For instance, an image might have a high overall score despite containing specific regions with unrealistic textures or structural anomalies.
- **The "Reward Hacking" Problem:** Due to the global and insensitive nature of existing IQA models, Real-ISR generators, when trained with these as reward signals, can exploit the reward function. They learn to produce images that achieve spuriously high global IQA scores but contain perceptually undesirable artifacts (e.g., an "unrealistic painterly" appearance or local distortions) that humans would easily detect and dislike. This leads to a misalignment between the model's optimized objective and human aesthetic preferences. The authors illustrate this with examples where standard IQAs assign similar high scores to both original and subtly distorted images, in contrast to human judgment.
- **Computational Cost of Large-scale IQAs:** While some large-scale IQA models might offer improved fine-grained perception, their substantial computational requirements render them impractical for iterative training within an RLHF framework, where the reward model is queried frequently.
- **Training Instability with Complex Rewards:** The development of a more sensitive, fine-grained reward model, while crucial for addressing reward hacking, introduces a new challenge: increased complexity in the reward signal can lead to instability during the generator's policy learning, hindering convergence or causing oscillations. This presents a dilemma between achieving robust, fine-grained rewards and maintaining training stability.

Based on these motivations, the research establishes several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Fine-grained Perceptual Reward Model (FinPercep-RM):** The primary objective is to design a diagnostic reward model that can not only assess the overall image quality ("What is the quality?") but also spatially localize and quantify specific defects ("Where is the defect?"). This model should explicitly couple global quality assessment with sensitivity to local imperfections to prevent reward hacking.
2. **Construct a Dedicated Dataset (FGR-30k):** To train the proposed FinPercep-RM, especially its ability to diagnose local defects, a new dataset is required that contains diverse, subtly distorted images with ground-truth spatial annotations of these defects. Existing IQA datasets lack this fine-grained spatial information.
3. **Propose a Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning (CCL) Strategy:** To address the dilemma between reward robustness (provided by FinPercep-RM) and training stability, the objective is to devise a curriculum learning strategy. This strategy should enable a smooth transition from stable, simpler rewards in early training stages to more complex, fine-grained rewards in later stages, allowing the generator to converge reliably while leveraging the full diagnostic power of FinPercep-RM.
4. **Enhance Real-ISR Model Performance:** Ultimately, the objective is to leverage FinPercep-RM and CCL to refine existing T2I-prior-based Real-ISR models, guiding them to generate restoration results with higher perceptual quality, fewer artifacts, and better alignment with human perception, thereby achieving state-of-the-art results.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology addresses the identified challenges of reward hacking and training instability in RL-based Real-ISR through a three-component framework: a Generator, a Diagnostic Reward Model (FinPercep-RM), and a Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning (CCL) mechanism.

#### 3.1. The Architecture of FinPercep-RM

FinPercep-RM is designed as an encoder-decoder network to provide both a global quality score and a spatially localized Perceptual Degradation Map (Fg-PDM).

- **Encoder:** Utilizes a pre-trained Image Quality Assessment (IQA) backbone (e.g., CLIP-IQA) to extract multi-scale feature representations from the super-resolved image ($I_{SR}$). These features contain both high-level semantic information for global assessment and low-level spatial details for defect localization. The deepest representation ($f_N$) serves as the primary global feature.
- **Decoder and Fg-PDM:** The decoder receives the multi-scale features from the encoder. Through upsampling and cross-layer fusion, it reconstructs a Perceptual Degradation Map ($M_{fg-pdm}$) at the same resolution as the input image. This map is normalized via a Sigmoid activation, with higher values indicating a greater likelihood and intensity of local defects.
- **Fine-grained Calibrated Global Score ($S_{fgc-global}$):** A key design is that the global score is explicitly dependent on the local defect map. The deepest global feature ($f_N$) is element-wise multiplied by an interpolated version of the $M_{fg-pdm}$. This modulated feature is then fed into an MLP head to regress the scalar score $S_{fgc-global}$. This architectural coupling ensures the global score is inherently sensitive to local imperfections.

#### 3.2. FGR-30k: A Fine-grained Reward Dataset

To train FinPercep-RM's diagnostic capabilities, particularly its decoder, a novel dataset called FGR-30k was constructed, comprising 30,000 samples with spatial defect annotations.

- **Synthesis of Fine-grained Distortion Samples:**
 - High-quality ground-truth images ($I_{GT}$) are collected.
 - Real-world degradations are applied to generate low-resolution images ($I_{LR}$).
 - Multiple diffusion-based Real-ISR models process $I_{LR}$ to produce super-resolved images ($I_{SR}$), which serve as sources of artifacts.
 - A "Region Swapping" strategy is employed to create fine-grained distorted samples ($I_{syn}$). This involves swapping regions between $I_{GT}$ (clean) and $I_{SR}$ (artifact-rich) using two types of masks: random masks (rectangular/free-form) for non-semantic artifacts and semantic masks (from SAM) for object-based degradations.
- **Ground-Truth PDM Generation ($M_{gt}$):** For each $I_{syn}$ sample, a ground-truth perceptual degradation map ($M_{gt}$) is generated by fusing two levels of dissimilarity:
 - **Pixel-level Dissimilarity ($Diff_{pixel}$):** Computed as the L1 distance between $I_{syn}$ and $I_{GT}$.
 - **Feature-level Dissimilarity ($Diff_{feat}$):** Computed as the spatial Cosine Distance between deep feature embeddings of $I_{syn}$ and $I_{GT}$, extracted using a pre-trained DINOv3 model. This captures structural and semantic inconsistencies.
 - $M_{gt}$ is a weighted sum of $Diff_{pixel}$ and $Diff_{feat}$, followed by normalization. This composite ensures sensitivity to both low-level and high-level perceptual discrepancies.

#### 3.3. Training FinPercep-RM with FGR-30k

FinPercep-RM is trained using a total loss function ($L_{total}$) that combines three components:

- **Dense Heatmap Loss ($L_{map}$):** An L1 loss supervises the predicted $M_{fg-pdm}$ against the ground-truth $M_{gt}$, encouraging accurate spatial localization and quantification of defects.
- **Triplet Ranking Loss ($L_{rank}$):** This loss ensures that the $S_{fgc-global}$ scores maintain a correct relative order: the score for the super-resolved image ($S_{SR}$) should be lower than that of the partially distorted image ($S_{syn}$), which in turn should be lower than the ground-truth image ($S_{GT}$). This is implemented using a hinge loss with margins.
- **Anchor Alignment Loss ($L_{align}$):** To prevent 'score drifting' and maintain the absolute scale of scores across training stages, an L1 loss aligns the $S_{GT}$ score from FinPercep-RM with the score from the original pre-trained IQA model for the ground-truth image.
The total loss is a weighted sum of these three components.

#### 3.4. Integrating FinPercep-RM for ISR with Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning (CCL)

CCL is introduced to manage the stability-robustness trade-off arising from FinPercep-RM's complex reward signal. It involves the synchronized evolution of both the Reward Model and the Generator.

- **Reward Model Progressive Parameter Expansion:** The RM evolves through a sequence of models ($RM_0..., RM_N$) with increasing complexity. It starts with a simple global IQA model ($RM_0$, e.g., pre-trained CLIP-IQA) for initial stability. In subsequent stages, the decoder (D) and adapter modules within the encoder (E) are gradually introduced and their parameter counts scaled. This progressive growth ensures a smooth increase in diagnostic capability.
- **Generator Curriculum Co-evolution:** The generator's RL training is synchronized with the RM's evolution.
 - **Early Stages:** The generator initially trains using the stable, global reward from $RM_0$. This allows for fast and stable convergence towards a general direction.
 - **Later Stages:** As the RM progresses to more complex $RM_k$ versions (with fine-grained capabilities), the generator refines its policy under these increasingly sensitive reward signals. This easy-to-hard matching helps maintain stability while progressively suppressing artifact generation and optimizing local perceptual quality.
The policy optimization for the generator can utilize various RLHF algorithms, such as REFL, DPO, or GRPO, guided by the $S_{fgc-global}$ output from the current stage's $RM_k$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of FinPercep-RM and CCL.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Other Methods (Quantitative)

Experiments were conducted on three Real-ISR baselines (DiffBIR, SeeSR, DIT4SR) across four real-world datasets (DrealSR, RealSR, RealLR200, RealLQ250) under a 4x upscaling setting. Evaluation metrics included LPIPS (for fidelity) and no-reference perceptual quality metrics (MUSIQ, MANIQA, ClipIQA, LIQE).

- **Superior Performance:** Incorporating FinPercep-RM with all three baseline models consistently led to performance improvements. The models achieved better scores on MUSIQ, MANIQA, and LIQE, indicating enhanced perceptual quality, and also reduced LPIPS distortion, signifying improved fidelity.
- **Prevention of Reward Hacking:** Directly using the original CLIP-IQA as a reward signal ("w/ IQA") often resulted in "reward hacking." While the CLIP-IQA metric itself might show improvement, most other perceptual quality metrics deteriorated. This indicates that models learned to optimize for the inadequate CLIP-IQA signal rather than true perceptual quality. FinPercep-RM, with its fine-grained perception, provided a more robust and accurate reward signal that prevented this hacking, guiding generators to enhance perceptual quality while suppressing distortions.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Comparison

Visual comparisons showed that Real-ISR models, when fine-tuned with FinPercep-RM (using REFL), produced significantly improved visual results. These outputs exhibited enhanced details and effectively suppressed artifacts, appearing more faithful to ground truth images compared to original baselines and other state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.3. User Study

A user study was conducted to comprehensively assess both fidelity and realism. The results indicated that the DIT4SR model, when integrated with FinPercep-RM, was significantly preferred over other compared approaches for both realism and fidelity. For instance, it was preferred over DIT4SR (baseline) 78.9% for realism and 76.8% for fidelity.

#### 4.4. Effectiveness of Different RLHF Strategies

To demonstrate FinPercep-RM's robustness as a reward signal, experiments were conducted using three popular RLHF algorithms (REFL, DPO, GRPO) on the DIT4SR model.

- When standard CLIP-IQA was used as the reward, all three RLHF strategies either failed to improve performance or caused degradation due to reward hacking.
- In contrast, replacing CLIP-IQA with FinPercep-RM as the reward consistently led to significant improvements across all three RLHF strategies, confirming that FinPercep-RM provides stable and meaningful optimization signals that are generalizable across diverse RLHF algorithms.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the RealLQ250 dataset examined the contributions of different FinPercep-RM and CCL components.

- **M_fg-pdm Construction:** Relying solely on pixel-wise differences for ground-truth PDM generation resulted in a significant performance drop, emphasizing the importance of feature-level differences for generalization.
- **Coupled Reward Model:** Decoupling the fusion between the encoder output and decoder output in FinPercep-RM (i.e., using a simple weighted combination of global score and maximum PDM value) led to performance degradation. This validated that architecturally coupling the global score with fine-grained perception is beneficial for overall optimization.
- **CCL Design:**
 - Removing CCL (exposing the generator to high-variance rewards from the outset) made convergence difficult.
 - A variant where FinPercep-RM's parameter size was fixed but the weights of fine-grained perception losses were progressively increased ("Weight") improved stability to some extent but did not match the overall efficacy of the default parameter-incremental CCL ("Param"). This confirms the critical role of the co-evolutionary curriculum, especially the progressive parameter expansion, in balancing stability and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of image generation and restoration:

- **Addressing Reward Hacking in Perceptual Tasks:** The primary significance lies in identifying and effectively addressing the "reward hacking" problem, which is a common challenge when applying RLHF to image generation tasks that require high perceptual quality. By proposing FinPercep-RM, a reward model with fine-grained diagnostic capabilities, the paper provides a mechanism to align machine-generated image quality assessments more closely with human perception, especially concerning local details and artifacts.
- **Novel Diagnostic Reward Model:** The FinPercep-RM architecture, which couples global quality scores with spatially localized degradation maps, represents a novel approach to image quality assessment in an RL context. This diagnostic capability could be extended to other image generation or editing tasks where specific local defects are critical for human perception.
- **Creation of a Specialized Dataset:** The FGR-30k dataset fills a gap in existing resources by providing spatially annotated, fine-grained distortion samples. This dataset is crucial for training diagnostic reward models and could serve as a valuable resource for future research in fine-grained image quality assessment and artifact detection.
- **Stabilizing Complex RL Training:** The Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning (CCL) mechanism offers a generalizable strategy for managing the stability-robustness trade-off when integrating complex, high-variance reward signals into RL training. This approach could be beneficial in other domains where detailed feedback is necessary but could destabilize initial learning phases.
- **Advancing Real-world Image Super-Resolution:** By enabling the stable and effective use of fine-grained perceptual rewards, the method demonstrably improves the perceptual quality and realism of Real-ISR models. This leads to outputs that are not only quantitatively superior on perceptual metrics but also qualitatively preferred by human evaluators, pushing the state-of-the-art in human-aligned image restoration.
- **Generalizability Across RLHF Algorithms:** The finding that FinPercep-RM consistently improves performance across different RLHF algorithms (REFL, DPO, GRPO) underscores its robustness and broad applicability as a superior reward function, independent of the specific policy optimization method employed.

The potential impact of this research extends to various applications requiring high-fidelity and perceptually pleasing image outputs, including photo editing, medical imaging, and creative content generation, where subtle local artifacts can significantly detract from the overall quality. By providing tools and strategies for more human-aligned evaluation and optimization, this work contributes to the development of more sophisticated and perceptually intelligent AI systems.
