# DINOv3

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta AI Research, WRI, and Inria developed DINOv3, a self-supervised learning framework that produces highly versatile visual foundation models. The system successfully addresses the degradation of dense features in large-scale models using a novel Gram anchoring technique, leading to state-of-the-art performance across numerous dense and global computer vision tasks without requiring fine-tuning.

## Problem

- The inherent reliance on manual data annotation for scaling vision models, which limits the potential for leveraging vast, untagged datasets.
- Observed degradation of dense, patch-level feature quality when training very large self-supervised learning models for extended periods, negatively impacting fine-grained and geometric tasks.
- The need for a truly versatile vision foundation model capable of broad generalization across diverse tasks and domains without requiring extensive task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

- Employs a sophisticated ==data curation pipeline== from 17 billion Instagram images, using clustering-based and retrieval-based methods, strategically mixed with public datasets during training.
- Scales the ==DINOv2 self-supervised learning== recipe to a **DINOv3** ==ViT-7B== architecture, combining image-level and patch-level objectives with ==Rotary Positional Embeddings== and ==register tokens== for robust training.
- Introduces ==Gram Anchoring==, a novel regularization loss that enforces consistency between the student model's ==Gram matrix== and an early-iteration "Gram teacher," specifically designed to preserve high-quality dense features at scale.
- Utilizes post-training strategies including ==resolution scaling== for high-resolution inference, efficient ==multi-student distillation== to create a family of models, and ==text alignment== for enhanced zero-shot capabilities.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on dense prediction tasks, including semantic segmentation (**>6 mIoU points** over SSL baselines on ADE20k), monocular depth estimation, 3D correspondence, and unsupervised object discovery.
- Established strong results on global image understanding, outperforming previous SSL backbones and matching top weakly-supervised models on challenging out-of-distribution classification benchmarks (ImageNet-R, -Sketch, -A) and fine-grained classification (**89.8%** on iNaturalist21).
- Serves as a powerful frozen backbone for complex computer vision systems, enabling state-of-the-art results in object detection (**66.1 mAP** on COCO), semantic segmentation (**63.0 mIoU** on ADE20k), and 3D understanding tasks by simply replacing prior backbones.
- Demonstrated impressive domain generalization, achieving state-of-the-art results in geospatial applications like canopy height estimation and various Geo-Bench tasks, often using only RGB input.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gram anchoring effectively prevents the degradation of dense feature maps in large self-supervised models, which is crucial for achieving superior performance on fine-grained and geometric tasks.
> - Self-supervised learning, when properly scaled with refined techniques, can produce visual representations that match or exceed the versatility and performance of supervised and weakly-supervised models across a comprehensive range of tasks.
> - Knowledge distillation from a large foundation model to smaller variants can efficiently democratize powerful visual representations, making them accessible across diverse computational budgets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

DINOv3 emerges at the forefront of the rapidly evolving landscape of foundation models in computer vision. These models, trained on vast datasets, aim to achieve broad generalization across diverse tasks and domains without extensive fine-tuning. The paper specifically positions itself within the self-supervised learning (SSL) paradigm, contrasting it with fully-supervised learning (SL) and weakly-supervised learning (WSL).

Historically, SL, exemplified by ImageNet-pretrained models, set early benchmarks but requires costly, manual annotation. WSL, popularized by models like CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), leverages noisy text-image pairs from the web, offering greater scalability and zero-shot capabilities. However, WSL still relies on metadata and often struggles with precise, dense visual understanding. SSL, by learning from raw pixel data alone, promises to unlock truly unlimited training data, offering robustness to distribution shifts and generating rich, versatile features without task-specific biases.

DINOv2 marked a significant milestone, demonstrating that an SSL model could match or surpass open-source CLIP variants on various tasks. However, scaling SSL further introduced new challenges, particularly the degradation of dense feature maps during prolonged training of very large models. DINOv3 directly addresses these scaling issues, especially the instability and loss of local feature quality observed in larger SSL models. By tackling these problems, DINOv3 aims to establish SSL as the superior paradigm for creating truly universal and powerful visual encoders, even for complex tasks traditionally dominated by supervised or weakly-supervised methods that rely on explicit annotations or metadata. It seeks to bridge the gap where SSL traditionally excelled in global representations but sometimes lagged in fine-grained, dense understanding at extreme scale.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of DINOv3 is driven by a clear set of objectives, stemming from the promise and challenges of scaling self-supervised learning:

1. **Eliminate the need for manual data annotation:** A core motivation of SSL is to enable models to learn from raw, unconstrained data, thus scaling effortlessly to massive, untagged datasets and larger architectures. DINOv3 aims to further realize this vision, making training feasible on virtually unlimited image collections without human intervention.
2. **Develop a versatile vision foundation model:** The goal is to create a single, reusable model capable of broad generalization across a wide range of tasks and domains. This includes generating robust generalist features applicable "off-the-shelf" to various vision tasks (e.g., image understanding, histopathology, remote sensing) without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.
3. **Address the degradation of dense feature maps at scale:** A critical observation from previous scaling efforts (like DINOv2) was that while global task performance improved with larger models and longer training, the quality of dense, patch-level features would often degrade. DINOv3 is explicitly motivated to solve this "known yet unsolved issue," ensuring high-quality dense features for geometric and fine-grained tasks.
4. **Disseminate a family of off-the-shelf models:** Beyond the flagship large model, a practical objective is to create a suite of DINOv3 models of varying sizes, enabling scalable solutions for diverse resource constraints and deployment scenarios.
5. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance without fine-tuning:** A key ambition is for DINOv3, as a frozen backbone, to outperform specialized state-of-the-art methods across a broad range of settings, including dense prediction tasks where it aims to significantly surpass previous self- and weakly-supervised foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DINOv3 leverages a multi-pronged approach encompassing careful data handling, architectural enhancements, a novel regularization strategy, and post-training refinements to achieve its ambitious goals.

#### 3.1. Data Preparation and Curation

Recognizing that naive data scaling isn't sufficient, DINOv3 employs a sophisticated data curation pipeline from a vast pool of 17 billion Instagram images. This process creates three distinct dataset parts:
- **Clustering-based curation:** Uses a hierarchical k-means method (from Vo et al., 2024) on DINOv2 embeddings to create LVD-1689M (1.689 billion images), ensuring balanced coverage of visual concepts.
- **Retrieval-based curation:** Similar to DINOv2, this retrieves images from the data pool that are similar to selected seed datasets, focusing on relevance for downstream tasks.
- **Publicly available datasets:** Includes raw ImageNet1k, ImageNet22k, and Mapillary Street-level Sequences to optimize for specific benchmarks.
During pre-training, these parts are strategically mixed, with 10% of batches consisting solely of high-quality ImageNet1k data and the rest being heterogeneous mixtures. This balanced sampling strategy is crucial for both generalizability and performance.

#### 3.2. Large-Scale Self-Supervised Training

The initial training phase extends DINOv2's SSL recipe to a significantly larger scale:
- **Learning Objective:** DINOv3 combines an image-level objective (L_DINO, inspired by Caron et al., 2021) with a patch-level latent reconstruction objective (L_iBOT, from Zhou et al., 2021). Both objectives incorporate Sinkhorn-Knopp (from SwAV) for centering. A Koleo regularizer (L_DKoleo) encourages features to spread uniformly.
- **Updated Model Architecture:** The model scales to a **ViT-7B** (6.7 billion parameters), maintaining 40 blocks but increasing embedding dimension to 4096 and using a patch size of 16. It incorporates **Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE)** (Su et al., 2024) with "RoPE-box jittering" for enhanced robustness to varying resolutions, scales, and aspect ratios. Crucially, it uses **register tokens** (Darcet et al., 2024) to mitigate high-norm patch outliers.
- **Optimization:** To handle the difficulty of pre-determining optimal training horizons for vast datasets, DINOv3 adopts constant learning rates, weight decay, and teacher EMA momentum after an initial linear warmup. This allows indefinite training as long as performance improves.

#### 3.3. Gram Anchoring: Enhancing Dense Features

This is a core methodological innovation addressing the degradation of dense features at scale:
- **Problem Identification:** Prolonged training of large models improves global metrics but degrades dense task performance, linked to increasing cosine similarity between CLS and patch tokens, making patch features less localized and similarity maps noisier.
- **Gram Anchoring Objective (L_Gram):** A novel loss function is introduced, operating on the Gram matrix (matrix of pairwise dot products of patch features). This loss enforces that the Gram matrix of the student model remains consistent with that of a "Gram teacher."
- **Gram Teacher Selection:** The Gram teacher is an *early iteration* of the teacher network, specifically chosen because early models exhibit superior dense properties before degradation sets in. The Gram teacher is updated every 10k iterations, effectively guiding the student toward stable, high-quality dense feature structures.
- **Leveraging Higher-Resolution Features:** To further enhance dense feature quality, the Gram teacher's features are extracted from higher-resolution inputs (e.g., twice the normal resolution) and then down-sampled. This distills the finer, smoother patch consistency of high-resolution features into the student model. This refinement step (L_HRef) combines L_DINO, L_iBOT, L_DKoleo, and L_Gram.

#### 3.4. Resolution Scaling

A high-resolution adaptation phase is applied for 10k additional iterations. This involves training with mixed resolutions (global crop sizes from {512, 768}, local from {112, 168, 224, 336}) and critically, reapplying Gram anchoring (using the 7B teacher as the Gram teacher) to maintain feature quality across scales. This enables stable and consistent dense features even at very high inference resolutions (up to 4K).

#### 3.5. Model Distillation

The knowledge from the large ViT-7B model is distilled into a "DINOv3 family" of smaller, more efficient Vision Transformer (ViT-S, B, L, H+) and ConvNeXt (CNX-T, S, B, L) variants.
- **Efficient Multi-Student Distillation:** A novel parallel distillation pipeline is designed. It allows training multiple student models concurrently by sharing the large teacher's inference cost across all GPUs, thus significantly improving efficiency and reducing overall compute. No Gram anchoring is needed here as the teacher is fixed.

#### 3.6. Text Alignment (dino.txt)

To imbue DINOv3 with zero-shot capabilities, a text encoder is aligned with the frozen DINOv3 visual backbone (specifically ViT-L). Following the LiT (Zhai et al., 2022b) paradigm and Jose et al. (2025), a text encoder is trained from scratch with a contrastive objective. A key enhancement involves concatenating mean-pooled patch embeddings with the CLS token output, enabling alignment of both global and local visual features to text, thereby supporting dense multi-modal tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DINOv3 demonstrates remarkable performance across a wide spectrum of visual tasks, often setting new state-of-the-art benchmarks, particularly in dense prediction, and showcasing its versatility as a foundation model.

#### 4.1. Exceptional Dense Features

DINOv3 significantly excels in dense prediction tasks, largely attributed to Gram anchoring:
- **Qualitative Analysis:** PCA visualizations (Fig. 13) reveal DINOv3's features are sharper, less noisy, and semantically more coherent than those from other leading models (SigLIP 2, PEspatial, DINOv2).
- **Dense Linear Probing:** On semantic segmentation, DINOv3 outperforms self-supervised baselines by >6 mIoU points on ADE20k and >13 points on weakly-supervised ones. It surpasses even agglomerative models like PEspatial and AM-RADIOv2.5 (derived from SAM) on ADE20k and Cityscapes. For monocular depth estimation, DINOv3 consistently beats all competitors on NYUv2 and KITTI, often by significant margins, demonstrating superior geometric understanding.
- **3D Correspondence Estimation:** DINOv3 achieves the best recall on both geometric (NAVI) and semantic (SPair) correspondence tasks, outperforming DINOv2 by +4.3% and +2.6% respectively, highlighting its strong 3D awareness.
- **Unsupervised Object Discovery:** DINOv3 achieves the highest CorLoc scores on VOC07 (+5.9 over DINO), VOC12, and COCO, demonstrating its ability to accurately segment class-agnostic objects without annotations.
- **Video Segmentation Tracking:** Without explicit video tuning, DINOv3 consistently outperforms all competitors across resolutions on DAVIS, YouTube-VOS, and MOSE. Its features show strong temporal consistency, improving with higher resolutions.
- **Video Classification:** DINOv3's dense features prove suitable for high-level video classification, performing comparably to weakly-supervised models and outperforming DINOv2 and AM-RADIO on UCF101, SSv2, and Kinetics-400.

#### 4.2. Robust and Versatile Global Image Descriptors

DINOv3 also establishes strong performance on global image understanding tasks:
- **Image Classification with Linear Probing:** DINOv3 significantly surpasses previous SSL backbones and achieves results comparable to the best weakly-supervised models (SigLIP 2, PE) on challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets like ImageNet-R, -Sketch, -A, and ObjectNet. It also shows the best robustness to corruptions on ImageNet-C. This marks a first for SSL models to reach such parity with SL/WSL models in this domain.
- **Fine-grained Classification:** DINOv3 excels on fine-grained tasks, achieving the highest accuracy on iNaturalist21 (89.8%) and competitive results on Places205 and a suite of smaller datasets.
- **Instance Recognition:** DINOv3 sets new state-of-the-art on instance-level retrieval benchmarks (Oxford, Paris, Met, AmsterTime), demonstrating superior robustness and versatility even across diverse domains like art and historical images.

#### 4.3. DINOv3 as a Foundation for Complex Computer Vision Systems

Beyond simple probes, DINOv3 serves as a powerful frozen backbone for advanced CV systems:
- **Object Detection:** With a lightweight Plain-DETR decoder trained on top of a frozen DINOv3, the model achieves state-of-the-art mAP on COCO (66.1 with TTA) and COCO-O (36.8 ER), outperforming systems with significantly more trainable parameters.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** Using a frozen DINOv3 backbone with a Mask2Former-based decoder, the model achieves state-of-the-art mIoU on ADE20k (63.0) and other datasets, demonstrating its strong per-pixel prediction capabilities despite transformer patchification.
- **Monocular Depth Estimation:** Integrating DINOv3 into the Depth Anything V2 pipeline yields new state-of-the-art results on five real-world datasets (NYUv2, KITTI, ETH3D, ScanNet, DIODE) for zero-shot scale-invariant depth, while keeping the backbone frozen. This validates DINOv3's strong sim-to-real generalization.
- **3D Understanding:** By simply replacing DINOv2 with DINOv3 (ViT-L) in the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) pipeline, the system achieves new state-of-the-art results across various 3D tasks, including camera pose estimation (Re10K, CO3Dv2), multi-view estimation (DTU), and two-view matching (ScanNet-1500).

#### 4.4. The DINOv3 Family of Models

The distilled ViT and ConvNeXt models offer efficient solutions:
- The **ViT-H+ (0.8B params)** performs comparably to the **ViT-7B teacher**, demonstrating effective knowledge distillation.
- The entire DINOv3 ViT family consistently outperforms open-source alternatives (DINOv2, SigLIP 2, PEcore) on dense tasks and remains competitive on global tasks across various computational budgets.
- **ConvNeXt (CNX) variants** show robustness to higher resolutions and significant improvements on OOD classification and dense tasks compared to ImageNet-22k supervised baselines, effectively bridging two architectural paradigms.

#### 4.5. Application to Geospatial Data

DINOv3 demonstrates impressive domain generalization to satellite imagery:
- A **DINOv3 7B model pre-trained on SAT-493M** (satellite images) achieves state-of-the-art performance on canopy height estimation (SatLidar, Open-Canopy), outperforming domain-agnostic DINOv3 Web and previous methods.
- Both **DINOv3 Sat and DINOv3 Web models** set new state-of-the-art results on 12 out of 15 Geo-Bench tasks (classification, segmentation) and high-resolution remote sensing benchmarks (LoveDA, iSAID, DIOR), often surpassing specialized models using multi-spectral bands with only RGB input.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DINOv3 represents a landmark achievement in self-supervised learning, with profound significance and potential impact across several dimensions:

1. **Accelerated Foundation Model Development:** By effectively eliminating the need for manual data annotation and metadata, DINOv3 unlocks the ability to scale visual foundation models with virtually unlimited, unconstrained data. This paradigm shift can significantly accelerate the development of future vision models, mirroring the rapid progress seen in large language models.
2. **Unprecedented Versatility and Robustness:** DINOv3 delivers exceptionally versatile and robust visual representations. Its ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance across a diverse range of tasks—from fine-grained segmentation and 3D understanding to OOD classification and video tracking—without any fine-tuning of the backbone is a testament to its generalist capabilities. This eliminates the need for task-specific pre-training, leading to substantial computational savings and simplified deployment.
3. **Breakthrough in Dense Feature Quality at Scale:** The introduction of "Gram anchoring" is a critical technical innovation. By solving the long-standing problem of dense feature degradation in large SSL models, DINOv3 provides "clean" and "sharp" local features that are crucial for applications requiring precise spatial and geometric understanding, a domain where SSL previously faced limitations at extreme scale.
4. **Enabling New Scientific and Commercial Applications:** The demonstrated success in specialized domains like geospatial imaging (satellite data) highlights DINOv3's domain-agnostic learning capabilities. This opens doors for applying foundation models to fields rich in observational data but poor in annotations (e.g., medical imaging, biological sciences, astronomy, robotics), fostering scientific discovery and technological innovation.
5. **Practical Accessibility through Model Family and Distillation:** The release of a full family of DINOv3 models, including smaller, efficient variants (ViT-S to H+, ConvNeXt), ensures that the powerful representations are accessible across diverse computational budgets and deployment scenarios. The novel multi-student distillation technique is a significant practical contribution to efficiently democratize large model capabilities.
6. **Advancement in Multi-Modal Learning:** The successful alignment of DINOv3 with a text encoder for zero-shot tasks (dino.txt) demonstrates its potential as a strong visual backbone for multimodal foundation models, further enhancing its utility in complex vision-language applications.
7. **Setting New Benchmarks for Generalization:** DINOv3’s performance often surpasses even highly specialized, supervised, or weakly-supervised models on their respective tasks, establishing a new standard for generalization in computer vision.

While acknowledging the significant computational resources and carbon footprint associated with training such a large model, DINOv3’s contributions push the frontier of what is possible with self-supervised learning, paving the way for more autonomous, adaptable, and powerful AI systems in computer vision.

#### 5.1. Conclusion

DINOv3 stands as a significant advancement in self-supervised learning, addressing key challenges in scaling vision foundation models. By meticulously curating data, refining training objectives, and introducing the novel Gram anchoring method, the researchers at Meta AI, WRI, and Inria have developed a versatile vision model that delivers exceptional performance across an extensive array of dense and global computer vision tasks. The ability of DINOv3 to maintain high-quality dense features even at extreme scales, coupled with its impressive domain generalization, particularly in geospatial applications, marks a new era for self-supervised learning. The release of the DINOv3 model family, made efficient through innovative distillation techniques, further democratizes these powerful capabilities, solidifying DINOv3's position as a pivotal contribution to the future of AI in computer vision.
