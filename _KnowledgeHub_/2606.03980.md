# Skill-RM: Unifying Heterogeneous Evaluation Criteria via Agent Skill

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba's Qwen Large Model Application Team developed Skill-RM, a unified framework that redefines reward modeling as the execution of a reusable 'Reward-Evaluation Skill' to integrate diverse evaluation criteria. This system, which externalizes evaluation logic into an explicit, evidence-bearing judgment process, consistently outperforms traditional judge baselines, achieving an average score of 86.2 on reward benchmarks and 97.8% accuracy on GSM8K best-of-N selection.

## Problem

- Modern Large Language Models (LLMs) with advanced capabilities (reasoning, coding, tool-use) demand heterogeneous evaluation criteria that existing reward models struggle to integrate cohesively.
- Current reward models, such as opaque scalar RMs and unstructured LLM-as-a-Judge systems, lack interpretability, flexibility, and a unified mechanism for synthesizing diverse evidence types.
- There is a need for a reusable abstraction to seamlessly integrate varied evaluation resources, adaptively select relevant evidence, and enable explicit, evidence-grounded reward computation across diverse scenarios.

## Method

- **Skill-RM** proposes a unified framework that reframes reward modeling as the execution of a reusable 'Reward-Evaluation Skill,' moving beyond monolithic scoring or unstructured prompting.
- The framework defines a Reward-Evaluation Skill (S_RM) comprising a procedural skill specification (M_RM) and a bank of heterogeneous resources (U_RM), including rubrics, references, verifiers, and tools.
- An agentic judge model performs skill-mediated judgment by dynamically orchestrating resource selection and execution according to M_RM, producing structured, evidence-bearing judgments that are traceable to underlying evidence.

## Results

- Skill-RM (Qwen3.5-**27B**) achieved an average score of 86.2 across RewardBench2, RM-Bench, and JudgeBench, outperforming the matched Qwen3.5-**27B** LLM-as-a-Judge baseline (83.9).
- Ablation studies confirmed that performance gains are due to the skill-mediated orchestration of resources, not just the availability of additional information, showing a significant lead over unstructured prompting or simple tool provision.
- In best-of-N response selection, Skill-RM consistently improved performance, achieving **97.8%** accuracy on GSM8K reranking, and obtained the highest average Kendall correlation (**0.524**) on IF-RewardBench for instruction-following tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing reward modeling as an active, skill-mediated orchestration of resource selection and execution enhances evaluation transparency and interpretability compared to passive knowledge absorption.
> - The explicit, externalized procedural specification (M_RM) and structured, evidence-bearing judgment outputs ensure traceability and consistency in reward computation.
> - Dynamic disclosure and integration of diverse evaluation resources, tailored to specific inputs, significantly improve the fidelity and adaptability of reward signals.
