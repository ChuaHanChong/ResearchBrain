# Skill-RM: Unifying Heterogeneous Evaluation Criteria via Agent Skill

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba's Qwen Large Model Application Team developed Skill-RM, a unified framework that redefines reward modeling as the execution of a reusable 'Reward-Evaluation Skill' to integrate diverse evaluation criteria. This system, which externalizes evaluation logic into an explicit, evidence-bearing judgment process, consistently outperforms traditional judge baselines, achieving an average score of 86.2 on reward benchmarks and 97.8% accuracy on GSM8K best-of-N selection.

## Problem

- Modern Large Language Models (LLMs) with advanced capabilities (reasoning, coding, tool-use) demand heterogeneous evaluation criteria that existing reward models struggle to integrate cohesively.
- Current reward models, such as opaque scalar RMs and unstructured LLM-as-a-Judge systems, lack interpretability, flexibility, and a unified mechanism for synthesizing diverse evidence types.
- There is a need for a reusable abstraction to seamlessly integrate varied evaluation resources, adaptively select relevant evidence, and enable explicit, evidence-grounded reward computation across diverse scenarios.

## Method

- **Skill-RM** proposes a unified framework that reframes reward modeling as the execution of a reusable 'Reward-Evaluation Skill,' moving beyond monolithic scoring or unstructured prompting.
- The framework defines a Reward-Evaluation Skill (S_RM) comprising a procedural skill specification (M_RM) and a bank of heterogeneous resources (U_RM), including rubrics, references, verifiers, and tools.
- An agentic judge model performs skill-mediated judgment by dynamically orchestrating resource selection and execution according to M_RM, producing structured, evidence-bearing judgments that are traceable to underlying evidence.

## Results

- Skill-RM (Qwen3.5-**27B**) achieved an average score of 86.2 across RewardBench2, RM-Bench, and JudgeBench, outperforming the matched Qwen3.5-**27B** LLM-as-a-Judge baseline (83.9).
- Ablation studies confirmed that performance gains are due to the skill-mediated orchestration of resources, not just the availability of additional information, showing a significant lead over unstructured prompting or simple tool provision.
- In best-of-N response selection, Skill-RM consistently improved performance, achieving **97.8%** accuracy on GSM8K reranking, and obtained the highest average Kendall correlation (**0.524**) on IF-RewardBench for instruction-following tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing reward modeling as an active, skill-mediated orchestration of resource selection and execution enhances evaluation transparency and interpretability compared to passive knowledge absorption.
> - The explicit, externalized procedural specification (M_RM) and structured, evidence-bearing judgment outputs ensure traceability and consistency in reward computation.
> - Dynamic disclosure and integration of diverse evaluation resources, tailored to specific inputs, significantly improve the fidelity and adaptability of reward signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reward models (RMs) are an integral component in the post-training and alignment of large language models (LLMs), particularly within pipelines such as reinforced fine-tuning (RFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). These models provide critical feedback signals that guide LLMs toward desired behaviors, influencing processes like re-ranking candidate responses, curating high-quality training data, and generating signals for alignment.

The increasing capabilities of LLMs in areas such as reasoning, coding, and tool-use have introduced more complex demands for reward modeling. Consequently, the criteria for RM evaluation have evolved from simple scalar preferences to intricate, resource-intensive verification processes. For instance, tasks requiring factual accuracy necessitate external references, mathematical or coding tasks demand verifiable execution, agentic training involves validating multi-step tool trajectories, and safety enforcement relies on strict constraint decomposition or policy-based vetoes. This evolution highlights that high-fidelity reward assignment is no longer a monolithic process but a multi-faceted endeavor requiring diverse resources.

Existing RM designs, however, exhibit limitations in integrating these heterogeneous criteria into a coherent framework. Traditional scalar RMs compress complex, resource-grounded evidence into opaque scores, hindering interpretability and flexibility. LLM-as-a-Judge systems, while offering natural language reasoning, typically employ unstructured, flat-prompting approaches where rubrics, examples, and tools are concatenated, leaving crucial aspects like resource selection, evidence tracking, and signal aggregation implicit. Recent research has introduced criteria-conditioned, rubric-centered, or tool-augmented evaluation systems, but these often focus on isolated mechanisms, exposing only one resource modality at a time. This landscape reveals a critical need for a unified, reusable abstraction capable of seamlessly synthesizing heterogeneous resources, adaptively selecting evidence, and facilitating explicit, evidence-grounded reward computation across varied evaluation scenarios.

This paper positions its proposed Skill Reward Model (Skill-RM) as a response to this need by specializing the concept of agent skills for reward modeling. Agent skills, in recent agentic research, are defined as self-contained, filesystem-based packages that encapsulate procedural logic and supporting resources. This design provides a structured approach that surpasses the limitations of flat prompting (which merely adds context) and atomic tool-use (which exposes isolated executables). By leveraging agent skills, Skill-RM aims to unify heterogeneous evaluation criteria, transforming reward modeling from a process of passive knowledge absorption into an active orchestration of resource selection and execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the evolving landscape of Large Language Model (LLM) capabilities and the concurrent challenges in accurately evaluating and aligning them. As LLMs become more sophisticated in complex tasks such as multi-step reasoning, code generation, and tool utilization, the demands placed on reward models (RMs) for effective post-training feedback have intensified.

Specifically, the authors identify several critical issues motivating their work:
- **Heterogeneous Evaluation Criteria:** Modern LLM tasks often require diverse evaluation methods, including rule-based verifiers, comparisons against ground-truth references, adherence to procedural checklists, and nuanced assessments via complex rubrics. Existing reward models struggle to integrate these varied types of evidence within a unified framework.
- **Limitations of Current RM Designs:**
 - **Scalar RMs:** These models typically output a single numerical score, compressing complex evaluation factors into an opaque value. This approach lacks transparency and interpretability, making it difficult to understand the rationale behind a given reward.
 - **LLM-as-a-Judge Systems:** While offering natural language reasoning, these systems often rely on unstructured prompting. This involves concatenating various rubrics, examples, and tools into a single prompt, which can lead to implicit and unmanaged processes for resource selection, evidence tracking, and signal aggregation.
 - **Isolated Resource Mechanisms:** Recent advancements have introduced criteria-conditioned or tool-augmented evaluation systems. However, these often focus on single modalities, exposing only one type of resource at a time and failing to provide a cohesive approach for synthesizing multiple evidence types.
- **Need for a Unified Abstraction:** There is an unmet demand for a reusable abstraction that can seamlessly integrate heterogeneous evaluation resources, adaptively select the most relevant evidence for a given input, and facilitate explicit, evidence-grounded reward computation across diverse evaluation scenarios.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the Skill Reward Model (Skill-RM) research are:
- **To propose Skill-RM:** Introduce a novel, unified framework that reframes reward modeling as the execution of a reusable Reward-Evaluation Skill.
- **To establish a consistent interface:** Develop a mechanism that provides a consistent interface for orchestrating various heterogeneous evaluation resources.
- **To enable dynamic evidence orchestration:** Allow for the dynamic selection and aggregation of evidence tailored to the specific requirements of each input, thereby enabling reward models to move beyond static evaluation.
- **To ensure consistency and transparency:** Design a system that promotes consistency and transparency throughout the reward computation process by making the evaluation logic explicit and evidence-grounded.
- **To demonstrate superior performance:** Conduct extensive experiments on reward benchmarks and downstream applications (e.g., best-of-N selection and reinforcement learning) to show that Skill-RM consistently outperforms traditional judge baselines.
- **To validate the agentic approach:** Provide empirical evidence that structuring evaluation as an executable procedure significantly enhances reward quality, offering a scalable path toward higher-quality LLM post-training and judgment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Skill-RM proposes a unified framework that redefines reward modeling as a skill-mediated execution procedure, moving away from monolithic scoring or unstructured prompting. The approach centers on a reusable "Reward-Evaluation Skill" that actively orchestrates the selection, execution, and synthesis of evaluation resources.

The Skill-RM framework consists of three core components:

1. **Reward-Evaluation Skill (S_RM):**
 - **Structure:** Defined as a tuple `(M_RM, U_RM)`, where `M_RM` is the procedural skill specification and `U_RM` is the resource bank.
 - **Specification (M_RM):** This component, typically formatted as a `SKILL.md` document, explicitly governs: (i) the definition and scope of evaluation criteria, (ii) the invocation protocol for resource selection and execution, (iii) the structured schema for evidence collection, and (iv) the output contract for the resulting structured judgment `z`. This externalizes reward knowledge as a reusable skill, encoding comprehensive evaluation logic.
 - **Structured Judgment:** The final judgment `z` is designed to be evidence-bearing, not merely decision-bearing. It comprises `(E, d)`, where `E` is a set of local evidence items `{e_m}` for each criterion `c_m`, and `d` is the conclusive decision. Each `e_m` is a tuple `(c_m, q_m, s_m)`, representing the criterion, supporting observation from invoked resources, and local assessment (e.g., satisfied/violated, scores). This design ensures the ultimate reward is traceable to underlying evidence.

2. **Reward-Evaluation Resources (U_RM):**
 - **Definition:** These are structured units of task-relevant evidence or executable evaluation procedures consultable during skill execution. The resource bank `U_RM` is a collection `{u_1, u_2..., u_K}`.
 - **Resource Specification:** Each resource `u_i` is characterized by its type, semantic content, applicability scope, and access action (`act_i`). Access actions can involve inspecting textual content (like rubrics), executing deterministic procedures (like verifiers or tools), or retrieving aggregation rules.
 - **Resource Taxonomy:** The paper categorizes resources into:
 - **Rubric & Criterion:** Define judgment dimensions (e.g., helpfulness, correctness).
 - **Reference:** Ground factual or task-specific correctness checks (e.g., answer keys).
 - **Checklist & Constraint:** Decompose instruction following into verifiable conditions (e.g., required response formats).
 - **Verifier & Tool:** Produce executable observations (e.g., Python sandbox, code checker).
 - **Calibration & Aggregation Rule:** Resolve evidence conflicts and map observations to judgments (e.g., score calibration).
 - **Dynamic Disclosure:** Unlike traditional methods that provide monolithic contexts, Skill-RM maintains a latent resource repository, activating and disclosing only the task-relevant subset of resources during judgment, thereby minimizing contextual noise.
 - **Curation Process:** Resources are curated through an LLM-assisted pipeline that aggregates materials from existing literature, judge protocols, and benchmark documentation. This process involves defining explicit applicability conditions, merging redundancies, and generalizing modules to ensure reusability. The finalized resource bank is version-controlled and frozen for reproducibility.

3. **Skill-Mediated Judgment:**
 - **Execution Flow:** When presented with a prompt `x` and candidate responses `Y`, an agentic judge model (`π_φ`) invokes the `S_RM` artifact. The judge loads `M_RM` as its execution blueprint and dynamically retrieves or executes resources from `U_RM` on demand, adhering strictly to `M_RM`'s invocation protocol.
 - **Action-Observation Trace:** The evaluation is transformed into an action-observation trace: `τ = (a_1, o_1..., a_T, o_T, z)`, where `a_t` is a judge action, `o_t` is the observation, and `z` is the final structured judgment. Actions include listing resources, inspecting evidence, executing tools, and finalizing judgment.
 - **Staged Logic:** The process unfolds in stages: (1) identifying evaluation targets and output format, (2) activating relevant criteria from `M_RM`, and (3) dynamically retrieving necessary resources. Observations are mapped into criterion-level evidence, ensuring `z` is populated only after all mandatory evidence fields are met.

4. **Reward Readout:**
 - **Deterministic Function:** A deterministic readout function `A(·)` parses the structured judgment `z` from the execution trace `τ` to produce the task-required reward output.
 - **Output Space:** This function projects `z` into the appropriate reward-output space: a scalar `ℝ` for pointwise evaluation (K=1) or a selection `{1..., K}` for multi-candidate tasks (K≥2), treating pairwise comparison as a special case where K=2. This unifies different reward types under a single evidence-bearing process.

**Experimental Approach:**
The experimental setup evaluates Skill-RM across three tracks:
- **General Reward Benchmarking:** Utilizes RewardBench2 (multi-dimensional capability), RM-Bench (content subtlety, style-bias robustness), and JudgeBench (correctness-focused).
- **Best-of-N Selection:** Employs JETTS candidate pools for response reranking.
- **Instruction-Following Reward Use:** Assessed on IF-RewardBench (Kendall correlation) and downstream IFEval, IFBench, and AdvancedIF scores.

**Baselines and Ablations:**
The study includes comparisons against various baselines: scalar/learned RMs, generative/reasoning RMs, rubric/resource-based systems, and agentic/verifier-based judges, as well as LLM-as-a-Judge systems (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3.5, Qwen3.5). Ablation studies are conducted to isolate the gains from skill-mediated resource organization by comparing Skill-RM to scenarios where resources are merely appended to the prompt or Python tools are provided without the skill framework. Controlled Qwen3.5 LLM-as-a-Judge runs are used for matched-backbone comparisons.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across various evaluation benchmarks and downstream applications, demonstrating the efficacy of the Skill Reward Model (Skill-RM).

#### 4.1. Main Reward-Modeling Results (Table 2)

- Skill-RM (Qwen3.5-27B) achieved an average score of 86.2 across RewardBench2, RM-Bench, and JudgeBench, representing an improvement over the matched Qwen3.5-27B LLM-as-a-Judge baseline, which scored 83.9. This improvement was consistent across all three individual benchmarks.
- Among all methods evaluated, Skill-RM (Qwen3.5-27B) recorded the strongest average score for complete rows.
- An alternative configuration, Skill-RM (Qwen3.5-122B-A10B), achieved the highest score on the JudgeBench metric, indicating adaptability across different backbone models.

#### 4.2. Reward-Modeling with Sample-Specific Resources (Table 3)

- When sample-specific resources (e.g., references, task constraints, or verifier outputs, often drawn from OpenRS) were mounted through the Reward-Evaluation Skill, Skill-RM's average performance with the Qwen3.5-27B backbone further increased from 86.2 to 89.1.
- This suggests that Skill-RM can effectively leverage and integrate additional per-instance evidence when such resources are provided, demonstrating its capability to adapt to richer evaluation contexts. OpenRS, a baseline, remained strong on JudgeBench, but Skill-RM achieved the best overall average with sample-specific resources under the same backbone.

#### 4.3. Resource-Use Ablation (Table 4)

- Ablation studies were conducted using the Qwen3.5-27B backbone to differentiate the impact of skill-mediated organization from mere resource availability.
- Directly appending all skill and resource documents as unstructured context to the baseline prompt resulted in a decrease in average score from 83.9 (Baseline) to 81.0. Appending sample-specific resources similarly performed below baseline at 82.0.
- Providing access to a Python tool without the skill framework yielded an average score of 83.6, close to the baseline but not superior.
- In contrast, Skill-RM, with its structured organization of resources, achieved an average of 86.2, and Skill-RM with sample-specific resources reached 89.1.
- These results indicate that performance gains are primarily due to the *skill-mediated orchestration* of resources rather than simply providing additional information or tools in an unstructured manner.

#### 4.4. Best-of-N Response Selection (Figure 2)

- In best-of-N response selection tasks using JETTS reranking pools, Skill-RM consistently showed positive performance improvements over both the Qwen3.5-27B direct judging baseline and Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-8B.
- Notable gains were observed on IFEval and HumanEval+.
- On the GSM8K dataset, Skill-RM reached 97.8% accuracy, which is very close to the Oracle@10 upper bound of 97.9%, suggesting near-optimal selection in this domain.
- For BigCodeBench, while Skill-RM provided a positive gain over the baseline, there remained substantial headroom compared to the Oracle@10, indicating challenges in code-oriented selection.

#### 4.5. Instruction-Following Reward Use (Table 5 & 6)

- **IF-RewardBench (Judge-side Validation):** Skill-RM obtained the highest average Kendall correlation of 0.524 on overall assessment for instruction-following tasks, surpassing Gemini-3-Flash (0.513) and the Qwen3.5-27B baseline (0.411). It performed particularly well on the Single-Turn (0.619) and Multi-Turn (0.540) subsets. Gemini-3-Flash showed stronger performance on the System-Prompt subset, identifying a specific area for future work.
- **Downstream RL (Policy Optimization):** In downstream instruction-following reinforcement learning, Skill-RM achieved the highest average score of 45.9 across IFEval, IFBench, and AdvancedIF metrics. This compared favorably to VerIF (44.7) and Tulu 3 (45.1), with clearer improvements observed on IFEval and AdvancedIF. These results support the use of Skill-RM as an effective reward source for instruction-following policy optimization.
- **Anchored-Pairwise Ablation (Appendix D, Table 10):** Supplementary experiments with an anchored-pairwise GRPO protocol further corroborated that Skill-RM-style rewards generally outperform direct or tool-only judging. It also highlighted the sensitivity of downstream RL to how verification evidence is presented to the judge model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Skill Reward Model (Skill-RM) introduces a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) evaluation and alignment, addressing several limitations of existing reward modeling approaches. The proposed framework has implications for improving the reliability, transparency, and scalability of LLM post-training processes.

**Key Significance:**
- **Unified Evaluation Framework:** Skill-RM provides a unified and consistent mechanism for integrating diverse and often heterogeneous evaluation criteria, such as rubrics, references, checklists, and verifiers. This is a notable departure from previous fragmented or opaque methods, offering a cohesive solution where a unified mechanism was previously unexplored.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Transparency:** By externalizing evaluation logic into an explicit, reusable "Reward-Evaluation Skill" and requiring evidence-bearing judgments, Skill-RM inherently increases the interpretability and transparency of the reward computation process. This allows for clear traceability of the final reward decision back to the underlying evidence and criteria, moving beyond the black-box nature of scalar reward models.
- **Adaptive and Modular Evaluation:** The skill-mediated approach enables reward computation to be instance-adaptive and modular. Resources are dynamically selected and executed based on the specific requirements of each input, rather than relying on static or monolithic evaluation procedures. This flexibility ensures that the evaluation is precisely tailored to the task at hand, potentially leading to more accurate and relevant feedback.
- **Superior Reward Quality:** The extensive experimental results across various benchmarks (RewardBench2, RM-Bench, JudgeBench, JETTS, IF-RewardBench) and downstream applications (best-of-N selection, instruction-following RL) consistently demonstrate that Skill-RM outperforms traditional judge baselines. This indicates that the structured, skill-mediated approach yields higher-fidelity reward signals, which are crucial for effective LLM alignment.
- **Scalable Pathway for LLM Alignment:** By transforming reward evaluation into a rigorous, protocol-governed computation, Skill-RM offers a scalable pathway toward more reliable and high-quality LLM alignment. This systematic approach can facilitate the development of more robust and trustworthy LLMs.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Improved LLM Performance:** Higher-quality and more consistent reward signals from Skill-RM can directly lead to better aligned and more capable LLMs in downstream tasks, particularly those requiring complex reasoning, adherence to constraints, or factual accuracy.
- **Easier Development of Complex LLM Agents:** For advanced LLM agents that need to perform multi-step tasks or interact with various tools, Skill-RM's ability to evaluate complex trajectories and tool usage with structured verification could accelerate their development and refinement.
- **Reduced Annotation Burden and Bias:** While the initial skill curation requires effort, the reusability and explicit nature of evaluation skills could reduce the long-term burden of creating and maintaining evaluation criteria, potentially also mitigating biases inherent in less structured human or LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** Skill-RM establishes a new paradigm for reward modeling, which could serve as a foundation for future research in several areas, including multimodal evaluation, self-improving skill automation, and efficient inference for skill-mediated processes.

In conclusion, Skill-RM represents a methodological shift in how reward models are conceived and implemented, moving towards an agentic, explicit, and unified framework that enhances the quality and transparency of LLM evaluation, thereby impacting the broader field of LLM alignment and development.
