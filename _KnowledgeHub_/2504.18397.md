# Unsupervised Visual Chain-of-Thought Reasoning via Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for Unsupervised Visual Chain-of-Thought (UV-CoT) reasoning enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform image-level reasoning by automatically generating preference data and using a novel Score-DPO, surpassing supervised methods and demonstrating robust generalization across diverse visual tasks.

## Problem

- Existing text-level Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in MLLMs suffers from information loss and struggles with fine-grained visual comprehension due to fixed-granularity image processing.
- Prior image-level Visual CoT methods rely heavily on supervised fine-tuning with expensive, human-labeled bounding box data, limiting scalability and generalization.
- Traditional preference optimization methods often rank responses without quantifying the intensity of preference, which is insufficient for nuanced visual reasoning.

## Method

- An automatic, two-step pipeline uses MLLMs to generate diverse ==bounding box proposals== and evaluate their quality by considering both immediate and future reasoning chain utility.
- A novel ==Score-DPO (sDPO)== algorithm optimizes the target MLLM by incorporating the degree of preference between visual reasoning steps, explicitly differentiating key visual regions.
- An ==iterative learning== approach dynamically refines the target MLLM by generating fresh preference data with its evolving policy, enhancing training robustness and scalability.

## Results

- UV-CoT, using **10%** of supervised labels for initialization, outperforms fully supervised Visual-CoT-7B by an average of **2.1%** across various VQA datasets, demonstrating superior data efficiency.
- The framework exhibits robust zero-shot generalization, surpassing all baselines by an average of **2.5%** on unseen datasets and achieving over **50%** gains on high-resolution OCR tasks compared to non-CoT baselines.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of image-level CoT, Score-DPO (naïve DPO yields **1.9%** average drop), and iterative learning (omission causes **3.5%** average drop) for overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit image-level visual CoT is crucial for complex multimodal reasoning, consistently outperforming text-only CoT approaches by dynamically integrating visual cues.
> - Unsupervised generation of visual preference data, combined with a refined preference optimization technique (sDPO), can match or exceed the performance of supervised visual CoT methods.
> - Evaluating visual regions based on their cumulative impact on the entire reasoning chain, not just immediate relevance, is vital for training MLLMs to construct effective visual CoT.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, and Preference Learning.

**Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning in LLMs:** The concept of CoT reasoning originated in Large Language Models (LLMs), demonstrating that by prompting models to generate intermediate reasoning steps, their problem-solving abilities and interpretability could be significantly enhanced (e.g., Wei et al., 2022; Kojima et al., 2022). This paradigm shift improved performance on complex tasks requiring multi-step logical deduction.

**MLLMs and Text CoT Limitations:** With the advent of powerful MLLMs like LLaVA, Qwen-VL, and OmniLLM, efforts have been made to extend CoT reasoning to multimodal tasks. However, most existing approaches, termed "text CoT" in the context of MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-CoT, MM-REACT), primarily convert visual information into a textual format (e.g., captions, summaries) and then apply LLM-style text-based reasoning. The paper highlights a critical limitation here: this conversion process often leads to significant information loss, and the models struggle with "fixed-granularity image processing," meaning they cannot dynamically focus on specific visual regions crucial for fine-grained understanding or spatial reasoning. Textual descriptions alone often fall short in complex visual tasks.

**Emergence of Visual CoT:** Recognizing the shortcomings of purely text-based CoT in multimodal contexts, a very recent line of work, exemplified by Visual-CoT (Shao et al., 2025), initiated the idea of *image-level* CoT. This involves the MLLM actively identifying and reasoning over key visual regions (e.g., via bounding boxes) as intermediate steps. While a significant advancement, Visual-CoT is heavily reliant on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using extensive, human-labeled bounding box data. This dependency poses two major drawbacks: high annotation cost and limited generalization to unseen or ambiguous scenarios due to learning only from positive, labeled examples.

**Preference Learning and DPO:** Concurrently, preference learning techniques have gained prominence for aligning AI models with human values and improving performance without direct supervision. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and AI Feedback (RLAIF) have been successfully applied to LLMs and MLLMs. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2023) emerged as a more stable and efficient alternative to RL-based methods by directly optimizing a policy against a reference model using preference pairs, circumventing the need for a separate reward model. While DPO has been explored for text-level CoT, its application to *visual* CoT, especially in an unsupervised manner, remained largely unexplored.

**The Contribution of UV-CoT:** This paper introduces Unsupervised Visual CoT (UV-CoT) as a critical advancement that bridges these gaps. It provides an unsupervised framework for image-level CoT reasoning that avoids the data annotation bottleneck of Visual-CoT by generating preference data automatically using MLLMs themselves. Furthermore, it introduces Score-DPO (sDPO), an enhanced preference optimization method tailored to capture the *degree* of preference for visual regions, allowing for more precise differentiation of visual cues. By combining unsupervised data generation with a refined preference optimization technique, UV-CoT aims to deliver scalable, generalizable, and robust visual CoT capabilities, pushing the boundaries of multimodal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel and effective framework for **Unsupervised Visual Chain-of-Thought (UV-CoT) reasoning** that significantly improves the interpretability and problem-solving abilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Address Limitations of Text-Level CoT in MLLMs:** Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods in MLLMs primarily operate at the text level, converting visual information into textual summaries. This approach suffers from inherent **information loss** and prevents MLLMs from dynamically focusing on crucial visual details or spatial regions within an image. Consequently, MLLMs struggle with fine-grained visual comprehension, particularly in tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning where textual descriptions are insufficient. The motivation is to enable MLLMs to *explicitly integrate visual cues* into the reasoning process.

2. **Overcome Data Dependency of Supervised Visual CoT:** The only prior work on image-level visual CoT, Visual-CoT, relies heavily on **supervised fine-tuning (SFT)** with extensive, human-labeled bounding box data. This dependence makes it **costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale** to diverse tasks and large datasets. A significant motivation is to eliminate this reliance on manual annotation, thereby making visual CoT training more **scalable and accessible**.

3. **Enhance Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** SFT models learn primarily from positive examples in labeled datasets, which can limit their **generalization ability** to novel or ambiguous scenarios that require dynamic interpretation or intermediate reasoning steps not explicitly covered in the training data. The researchers are motivated to develop a method that learns more robustly and generalizes well to unseen datasets and tasks, fostering greater adaptability in MLLMs.

4. **Improve Precision in Visual Region Selection and Reasoning:** Traditional preference optimization methods often only rank preferences without quantifying their intensity. For visual CoT, where the influence of different visual regions can vary significantly, a more nuanced approach is needed. The motivation is to develop a preference optimization technique that can not only identify preferred visual regions but also **distinguish the *degree* of preference**, leading to more precise optimization of the MLLM's ability to select and reason with key visual cues.

In essence, the paper aims to empower MLLMs with a sophisticated, self-sufficient visual reasoning capability that mirrors human perception—first identifying salient visual elements and then reasoning based on them—without the prohibitive cost of human supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The UV-CoT framework proposes a novel **unsupervised approach to visual Chain-of-Thought reasoning via preference optimization**. It consists of two key parts: an automatic data generation pipeline and a refined preference-based model training strategy.

#### 3.1. Preference Data Generation

The core innovation lies in an **automatic, two-step pipeline for generating high-quality preference data** without human annotation, leveraging the capabilities of pre-trained MLLMs. Given an image and a question (query `x`), the process unfolds over `T` reasoning steps (Algorithm 1):

1. **Response Generation (Seed Bounding Boxes and Answers):**
 - A **target MLLM (f_tar)**, initialized with a model like LLaVA-1.5-7B, receives the current image and question.
 - Using a template prompt, `f_tar` is guided to identify informative image regions and specify their locations via bounding box coordinates.
 - To encourage diversity, stochastic decoding is applied to `f_tar` with `n` different random seeds, generating multiple "seed" bounding boxes and corresponding intermediate responses (`{y_i^t}_{i=1}^n`). A response here refers to any model output, including a bounding box and an answer based on that region.
 - Each bounded region is then extracted (cropped) by a visual sampler.
 - The `f_tar` then integrates visual tokens from both the original and the cropped images to generate a more precise answer for each seed bounding box.

2. **Response Evaluation (Quality Assessment):**
 - An **evaluator MLLM (f_eval)**, such as OmniLMM-12B, assesses the quality of each generated response (`y_i^t`).
 - The evaluation is cumulative: it not only scores the current response (`s_i^{cur} = f_{eval}(y_i^t | y_{0:t-1})`) but also considers its impact on the *next* potential response in the chain (`s_i^{nxt} = E[f_{eval}(y_1^{t+1} | y_{0:t-1}, y_i^t)]`).
 - A hyperparameter `γ` (gamma) combines these scores (`s_i = s_i^{cur} + γs_i^{nxt}`), quantifying the utility of each bounded region for the overall reasoning process. At the last step, `γ` is 0.

3. **Pair Construction:**
 - At each timestep `t`, `k` preferred and dis-preferred pairs are randomly selected from the `n` generated responses based on their assigned scores `s_i`.
 - These pairs form "preferred chains" (`y_w^t`) and "dis-preferred chains" (`y_l^t`), each concatenated with the past response chain `y_{0:t-1}` and including their respective scores (`s_w, s_l`). This collection constitutes the preference dataset `D_t`.

4. **Response Selection:**
 - For the subsequent reasoning step (`t+1`), only the single highest-scoring response chain from timestep `t` is preserved and used as the "past response chain" (`y_{0:t}`) input. This ensures that the model always proceeds with the most promising reasoning path.

#### 3.2. Unsupervised Learning via Score-DPO (sDPO)

With the preference dataset `D` generated, the target model `f_tar` is optimized using a modified version of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), named **Score-DPO (sDPO)** (Equation 2).

- **Refining DPO for Visual CoT:** Standard DPO ranks responses without quantifying the intensity of preference. However, in visual reasoning, the influence of key regions varies significantly. sDPO addresses this by introducing a margin that captures the degree of preference.
- **Loss Formulation:** The sDPO loss includes a term `(g(s_w) - g(s_l))` which is the difference in preference scores between the preferred (`y_w`) and dis-preferred (`y_l`) responses, mapped into the logit space by a monotonic function `g(·)`. This explicitly optimizes the model to differentiate not just the order but also the *magnitude* of preference, allowing for more precise distinction of key visual regions.
- **Theoretical Connection:** The paper establishes a connection between sDPO and the Gumbel distribution (Appendix B), demonstrating that the loss function optimizes the probability of a preferred response being better than a dis-preferred one by a certain margin (`∆r`).

#### 3.3. Iterative Learning

To mitigate distributional mismatch between the static preference data and the evolving target model, an **iterative learning approach** (Algorithm 2) is adopted:

- The total image-question query set `X` is divided into `m` subsets.
- The target model `f_tar` is incrementally refined over `m` iterations.
- In each iteration `i`, preference data `D_i` is generated using the *current* target model `f_i^{tar}` on subset `X_i`.
- This newly generated `D_i` is then used to train the next iteration of the target model `f_{i+1}^{tar}` using the sDPO loss.
- This dynamic updating of preference data ensures the model learns from data aligned with its evolving policy, enhancing training robustness.

In summary, the UV-CoT methodology innovatively combines automated data generation by MLLMs with a specialized preference optimization loss and iterative learning. This creates a self-improving loop that allows MLLMs to learn image-level CoT reasoning in an unsupervised, scalable, and generalizable manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on ten diverse datasets across five domains (Text/Document, Chart, General VQA, Relation Reasoning, High-Resolution Image Reasoning) demonstrate the significant superiority and generalization capabilities of UV-CoT.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods (Table 1)

- **Effectiveness of Image-level CoT:** Models incorporating image-level CoT (Visual-CoT, UV-CoT) consistently outperform models relying on text-level CoT (LLaVA-1.5, MiniCPM-o-8B) and even larger MLLMs like LLaVA-1.5-13B. This validates the critical need and benefit of explicitly integrating visual cues into the reasoning process.
- **UV-CoT Outperforms Supervised Visual-CoT:** Despite using significantly less data (249K unlabeled preference pairs vs. 376K human-labeled pairs), the completely unsupervised UV-CoT matches or surpasses Visual-CoT-7B on several datasets (e.g., +1.3% on TextVQA, +1.6% on VSR). When UV-CoT is initialized with a small fraction (10%) of supervised data (UV-CoT (10% labels)), it substantially outperforms Visual-CoT-7B (100% labels) by an average of 2.1%, with notable gains on TextVQA (+2.5%), GQA (+2.2%), and VSR (+2.1%). This is a key finding, demonstrating that UV-CoT's unsupervised approach is not only scalable but also more data-efficient and ultimately more effective.
- **Beyond Distillation:** UV-CoT outperforms its evaluator model, OmniLMM-12B, by an average of 5.1%. This indicates that UV-CoT is not merely distilling knowledge from a larger model but genuinely learning to reformulate the complex task of bounding box generation into a more tractable ranking problem, leading to emergent capabilities.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Generalization (Tables 2 & 3)

- **Strong Generalization:** UV-CoT exhibits robust zero-shot performance on four unseen datasets (SROIE, DUDE, Visual7W, V*Bench) not used during its preference data generation. It outperforms all baselines by an average of 2.5%, with significant gains on DUDE (+3.5%) and Visual7W (+3.5%).
- **Enhanced Generalization with More Unlabeled Data:** A variant, UV-CoT* (trained with additional unlabeled preference data from the zero-shot datasets), further improves performance by an average of 5.1%, underscoring the power of unsupervised learning for generalization.
- **Excellence in High-Resolution Reasoning:** On the challenging V*Bench dataset (high-resolution images), UV-CoT drastically outperforms non-CoT baselines (e.g., over 50% gains on OCR tasks) and surpasses Visual-CoT-7B by an average of 5.5%. This highlights UV-CoT's advantage in complex, fine-grained visual reasoning scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 4)

- **Necessity of Image-level CoT:** Removing UV-CoT inference ("w/o UV-CoT") leads to a substantial performance drop (average -7.7%), affirming that visual CoT is critical for complex multimodal reasoning. The significant gap between UV-CoT and using ground truth bounding boxes ("UV-CoT w/ G.T. BBox") on some datasets (e.g., DocVQA, InfographicsVQA) indicates that accurate region localization remains an area for improvement.
- **Superiority of Score-DPO (sDPO):** Using standard DPO loss ("w/ naive DPO") results in degraded performance (average -1.9%), particularly on DocVQA and Flickr30k. This validates the effectiveness of sDPO in incorporating preference scores to better differentiate visual regions and optimize model learning.
- **Importance of Iterative Learning:** Training without iterative updates ("w/o iterative learning") causes a significant performance decline (average -3.5%). This confirms that dynamically updating preference data, aligning it with the model's evolving policy, is crucial for robust training.
- **Crucial Role of `γ` in Response Evaluation:** Setting `γ=0` ("w/o γ model") to ignore the impact on the next response leads to a major performance drop (average -8.8%, especially -17.2% on TextVQA). This highlights that evaluating regions based on their *cumulative impact* on the reasoning chain, not just immediate relevance, is vital for training effective visual CoT.

#### 4.4. Bounding Box Evaluation and Evaluator Model Insights (Fig. 3 a&b, Table 5)

- **Higher Quality Bounding Boxes:** UV-CoT consistently generates higher quality bounding boxes compared to Visual-CoT-7B, both on training and zero-shot datasets. This directly correlates with its superior end-task performance and generalization.
- **Robustness of Self-Evaluation:** Even when the target model itself serves as the evaluator ("Evaluator: self-evaluated"), UV-CoT still significantly outperforms the baseline target model, demonstrating its robustness and efficiency without requiring a larger, separate evaluator.
- **Value of Bounding Box Information:** Incorporating bounding boxes generated by UV-CoT directly into other MLLMs (e.g., OmniLMM-12B + CoT) significantly boosts their performance (e.g., +7.4% for OmniLMM-12B), even without fine-tuning these models. This confirms that the generated visual cues are highly informative and that UV-CoT effectively simplifies the task of complex spatial annotation for MLLMs.

#### 4.5. Visual Token Efficiency (Fig. 3c)

- Image-level CoT models (UV-CoT, Visual-CoT-7B) demonstrate better visual token efficiency than standard answer generation pipelines, achieving higher performance with fewer visual tokens. UV-CoT consistently outperforms Visual-CoT-7B across all scales, indicating superior efficiency.

In essence, UV-CoT demonstrates state-of-the-art performance by effectively learning unsupervised visual CoT, generalizing robustly, and leveraging a novel preference optimization strategy, all while being more data-efficient and scalable than its supervised counterparts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Unsupervised Visual Chain-of-Thought (UV-CoT) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, with profound implications and potential impact:

1. **Enabling Scalable Visual Reasoning:** The most critical impact of UV-CoT is its ability to learn image-level CoT reasoning *without relying on human-labeled bounding box data*. This fundamentally addresses the **data annotation bottleneck** that has limited the scalability and applicability of previous supervised approaches like Visual-CoT. By automating the data generation process, UV-CoT makes sophisticated visual reasoning accessible for a much wider range of datasets and real-world scenarios, where manual annotation is impractical or prohibitively expensive.

2. **Enhanced Multimodal Comprehension and Interpretability:** By dynamically identifying and reasoning over key visual regions, UV-CoT significantly improves MLLMs' **visual comprehension**, particularly for tasks requiring fine-grained spatial understanding or complex object relationships. This approach closely mimics human perception, where we focus on relevant areas before forming conclusions. This structured, step-by-step visual reasoning process also inherently boosts the **interpretability** of MLLMs, allowing users to understand *why* a model arrived at a particular answer by observing the highlighted visual cues.

3. **Superior Generalization Capabilities:** UV-CoT's unsupervised nature and preference-based learning foster robust generalization. Its strong zero-shot performance on unseen datasets demonstrates that the model learns more abstract and adaptable visual reasoning patterns, rather than overfitting to specific annotation distributions. This is crucial for deploying MLLMs in diverse, real-world applications where training data might not perfectly match deployment conditions.

4. **Advancement in Preference Learning for Multimodality:** The introduction of Score-DPO (sDPO) specifically tailored for visual regions is a notable methodological contribution. By incorporating the *degree* of preference into the optimization, sDPO enables finer differentiation of visual cues, pushing the boundaries of how preference learning can be applied to complex multimodal tasks beyond just textual alignment. This could inspire new research into preference-based learning for other structured or spatial prediction tasks in vision.

5. **Efficiency and Resource Optimization:** UV-CoT achieves state-of-the-art results with greater data efficiency than supervised methods and demonstrates superior visual token efficiency. The robustness of UV-CoT even with self-evaluation (using the target model as the evaluator) suggests potential for reducing reliance on larger, more resource-intensive evaluator models, making it more practical for researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources.

6. **Broader Applications:** The enhanced visual reasoning capabilities of UV-CoT have the potential to impact various downstream applications:
 - **Document AI:** More accurate information extraction from complex documents, invoices, or charts.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Better interpretation of medical scans by focusing on critical pathological regions.
 - **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Improved scene understanding and decision-making by prioritizing relevant visual inputs.
 - **Accessibility:** Developing more advanced visual aids for individuals with visual impairments by precisely describing key objects and their relationships.

**Potential Negative Societal Impacts (as acknowledged by the authors):**
Like other powerful MLLMs and LLMs, UV-CoT carries potential risks. These include:
- **Privacy concerns:** The ability to process and interpret visual information more deeply could inadvertently expose sensitive personal or proprietary data.
- **Bias reinforcement:** If the underlying MLLMs or the automatically generated preference data contain biases, UV-CoT could amplify these, leading to unfair or discriminatory outcomes.
- **Misinformation:** Advanced image understanding could be misused to generate or propagate misleading visual content, especially in combination with generative AI.
- **Job displacement:** Automation of tasks requiring complex visual reasoning could impact jobs in fields like data analysis, medical imaging interpretation, or quality control.

Addressing these potential harms necessitates responsible development, transparent model design, continuous monitoring for biases, and the implementation of ethical guidelines to ensure that this powerful technology benefits society while mitigating unintended negative consequences.

In conclusion, UV-CoT marks a pivotal step towards truly intelligent multimodal AI systems that can reason visually, learn autonomously, and generalize effectively, thereby accelerating progress in a wide array of AI-driven applications.
