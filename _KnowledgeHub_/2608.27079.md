# GRAFT: Grounded and Efficient Online Reinforcement Adaptation for Fine-Grained Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GRAFT adapts pretrained vision-language-action policies online for fine-grained biomedical manipulation. It learns view-specific visual anchors supervised by training-only region proposals via an identity-free multi-region FreeDice loss, needing no proposals at deployment, and cuts learner cost through frozen-prefix KV caching plus single-step consistency action-chunk generation. Across four real-robot lab tasks under matched 45-minute budgets, it reaches 82.5% final-stage success, 25 points above the union-supervision variant.

## Problem

- Scalar task-level rewards and demonstrations give no spatial guidance about which small local visual cues drive success in fine-grained laboratory manipulation.
- Prior grounding methods feed masks, crops, or proposals to the policy at execution time, creating a deployment dependency on external proposal generators.
- Repeated VLA inference and replay-based optimization are computationally expensive, limiting how much experience a policy can exploit within a fixed wall-clock adaptation budget.

## Method

- ==View-specialized visual anchors==: eight anchor templates per camera view cross-attend to frozen view tokens, reweighted by global context, giving proposal-free local grounding at inference.
- ==FreeDice== identity-free multi-region supervision plus a redundancy penalty matches anchor attention to multiple candidate proposal masks per frame without fixed anchor-proposal correspondence, avoiding union collapse.
- ==Asynchronous actor-learner loop== with ==frozen-prefix KV caching== for replayed observations and a ==single-step consistency action head==, trained by ==behavior cloning== plus ==twin-critic Q-maximization==.

## Results

- Under matched 45-minute budgets, GRAFT hits **33/40 (82.5%)** rolling success across four tasks versus **23/40 (57.5%)** for the union-supervision ablation and **20/40** for the consistency-policy baseline.
- Largest gain on Centrifuge Tube Loading: **9/10** versus **2/10** for union supervision; every task reaches at least **7/10**, though liquid transfer trails a baseline **8/10** versus **9/10**.
- Prefix-KV caching raises learner throughput from **2.21** to **21.96 steps/s** for the consistency head (**9.94x**) and **1.99** to **6.30 steps/s** for flow matching (**3.17x**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial supervision can be a training-only scaffold: grounding signal is absorbed into learned anchors, so deployment stays free of proposal generators and mask pipelines.
> - Preserving several distinct candidate regions beats merging them into one union target, especially when success needs coordinating multiple spatial cues simultaneously.
> - Freezing the perception backbone yields a second dividend beyond stability: its cached key-value states make replay updates far cheaper, compounding with single-step action decoding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of robot manipulation, specifically focusing on the online adaptation of pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for fine-grained tasks. VLA policies offer substantial semantic and visuomotor priors for robotic control, yet their direct application to tasks requiring precise local interactions, such as those found in biomedical laboratories, presents several challenges.

The broader landscape includes several concurrent research directions. One involves enhancing spatial reasoning in VLA policies through methods like 3D-aware representations, point-cloud features, and explicit visual grounding. GRAFT aligns with this by developing a mechanism for learning task-relevant local representations but differentiates itself by focusing on online adaptation from multiple robot cameras. While some existing methods rely on providing explicit masks or object crops at execution time, GRAFT employs auxiliary spatial supervision *during training only*, aiming to eliminate the need for such external inputs during deployment.

Another significant area of research concerns the efficiency of VLA adaptation and learning. Real-world robot learning is constrained by factors such as wall-clock time, requiring computationally efficient methods for repeated action generation and policy updates. Existing approaches have explored compact architectures, parallel action generation, and caching visual representations. GRAFT contributes to this by proposing a novel form of computational reuse on the learner side, specifically by caching the key-value states of the frozen visual-language prefix, alongside single-step action generation.

Within the context of laboratory automation, previous work has addressed infrastructure, workflow autonomy, and reward design. GRAFT’s focus is complementary, investigating how a pretrained visuomotor policy can acquire knowledge about critical local visual cues through limited real-robot interaction. Therefore, this research bridges the gap between high-level VLA priors and the precise, spatially-dependent requirements of fine-grained manipulation, simultaneously addressing the computational overhead inherent in online VLA adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties in adapting pretrained VLA policies to fine-grained manipulation tasks, particularly in environments such as biomedical laboratories. These tasks often depend on subtle, view-dependent visual cues and precise local contact geometry, rather than general scene-level understanding.

The key challenges that GRAFT aims to address are:
1. **Lack of explicit spatial guidance:** Standard demonstrations and scalar task-level rewards often provide insufficient spatial information regarding which local visual regions are critical for success or failure. This necessitates that the policy learns task-specific, fine-grained visual grounding from limited real-robot interaction, which is a demanding requirement.
2. **Computational overhead of online adaptation:** The iterative process of VLA inference and replay-based policy optimization during online adaptation can be computationally expensive. This limits the number of policy updates and, consequently, the rate of learning within a constrained wall-clock budget for real-robot interaction.

Against this background, the central objective of GRAFT is to develop a framework that can:
- **Enable task-relevant visual grounding:** Introduce a mechanism for a VLA policy to rapidly learn which local visual cues are important during online adaptation. This includes learning view-specific visual anchors that focus perception on task-relevant regions.
- **Ensure proposal-free deployment:** Achieve this detailed visual grounding without requiring region proposals, masks, or external proposal generators during the actual deployment or inference phase of the policy.
- **Enhance online learning efficiency:** Reduce the computational cost associated with repeated acting and learning. This involves accelerating both the action generation process and the policy update steps to maximize task improvement within a fixed real-robot interaction budget.

In essence, the research is motivated by the question of whether region-level supervision can effectively guide a VLA in identifying crucial local visual cues during adaptation, while simultaneously maintaining a computationally efficient, proposal-free deployment and optimizing learning within practical time constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GRAFT (Grounded Reinforcement Adaptation for Fast Task Learning) is a framework designed for efficient online VLA adaptation through grounded perception. It integrates several components: single-step action-chunk generation, frozen-prefix replay reuse, and training-time supervision of view-specialized visual anchors.

The problem is formulated such that at any time `t`, the policy receives an observation `s_t` comprising side- and wrist-view RGB images, a language instruction `l`, and an adapter state `q_t`. It then predicts an action chunk `a_t:t+H-1` (where `H=10`). A frozen VLM prefix processes these inputs into view tokens, pooled context, and prefix key-value states. Critically, while training observations may include patch-aligned masks, these masks are used solely for supervision during adaptation and are not inputs to the policy during deployment.

**A. View-Specialized Visual Anchors**
GRAFT introduces distinct sets of `N_v = 8` anchor templates for each camera view (side and wrist). These anchors share context and cross-attention projections but are trained to read only their assigned view. Each anchor, represented by `z_v_i`, is an observation-dependent visual feature derived from its view's visual tokens and a learned template. This design allows anchors to capture complementary task-relevant local cues across different viewpoints. A global context reweights these anchors within each view before their fusion into the policy's representation, and a centered-tanh adapter is applied.

**B. Training-Only Multi-Region Guidance**
To supervise these visual anchors, GRAFT employs offline-generated region proposal masks during training. Unlike methods that merge all candidate regions into a single target, GRAFT utilizes an identity-free multi-region FreeDice loss (`L_v_FD`). This loss matches proposals to anchors and anchors to valid proposals dynamically, without requiring fixed assignments between anchor identities and region identities. This mechanism encourages the visual representation to capture multiple plausible regions, preserving distinct local cues. Additionally, a redundancy penalty (`L_dup`) is applied to regularize the overlap between the attention maps of different anchors, preventing them from collapsing into redundant representations. The combined grounding loss (`L_ground`) is applied periodically during actor updates. It is important to note that this region-level supervision is strictly used during adaptation; at deployment, the policy does not require region masks or external proposal generators.

**C. Efficient Replay Adaptation**
To address computational costs, GRAFT implements two efficiency mechanisms:
1. **Cached Visual-Language Prefix Reuse:** During learner updates, the key-value (KV) states, pooled summaries, and visual tokens from the frozen visual-language model (VLM) prefix are cached. When replay samples are revisited, these cached states are reused, avoiding redundant computation of the frozen prefix. This significantly reduces the cost of online optimization, as only the trainable anchor and action pathways need to be recomputed. Live observations, however, always undergo a fresh prefix forward pass.
2. **Single-Step Action Generation:** GRAFT replaces iterative flow-matching action generation with a single-step consistency policy. This means a complete action chunk (`a_t:t+H-1`) is predicted in one forward pass, reducing latency for real-time control while retaining stochastic exploration.

**D. Online Adaptation Pipeline**
The system operates with asynchronous actor and learner processes. The actor collects interaction data and human corrections, uploading them at episode boundaries. Once sufficient online replay data is accumulated (e.g., 100 valid windows), the learner begins sampling equally from both online replay and demonstration data. The policy is optimized using a combination of behavior cloning (`L_BC`) and Q-maximization (`L_Q`) for the actor, and Bellman error minimization for twin critics (`L_critic`). The actor policy is updated after two critic updates, and the grounding loss (`L_ground`) is applied to the actor periodically using proposal-valid demonstration windows. At deployment, the policy relies solely on observations, language, adapter state, learned anchors, and the action head, with proposal generators, masks, grounding losses, and critics entirely absent.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GRAFT was conducted across four fine-grained biomedical manipulation tasks on a JAKA robotic arm: Petri Dish De-lidding, Centrifuge Tube Loading, Precision Liquid Transfer, and Pipette Tip Attachment. The experimental protocol included 10 teleoperated demonstrations per task and a matched 45-minute online adaptation budget for each method. Objects were randomly perturbed, and success was determined by predefined task-specific criteria.

**A. Comparison of Grounding Supervision Objectives (GRAFT vs. GRAFT-Union)**
The study directly compared GRAFT, which utilizes identity-free multi-region FreeDice supervision, against GRAFT-Union, which employs proposal-union supervision for its view-specialized anchors. Under a 45-minute interaction budget, GRAFT achieved 33 successes out of 40 final episodes (82.5%), while GRAFT-Union achieved 23 successes (57.5%). This represents an absolute improvement of 25 percentage points for GRAFT. The most significant gain was observed on Centrifuge Tube Loading, where GRAFT improved success from 2/10 to 9/10. This controlled ablation supports that identity-free multi-region FreeDice supervision contributes to more effective adaptation for fine-grained manipulation.

**B. Overall Online Adaptation Performance**
GRAFT demonstrated stronger overall final-stage adaptation under the fixed interaction budget compared to other baselines. It achieved near-zero intervention rates on three of the four tasks and maintained a low intervention rate on Pipette Tip Attachment, indicating that performance gains were primarily due to improved autonomous behavior rather than increased human assistance. GRAFT consistently achieved at least 7/10 successes across all tasks in the final stages of adaptation. The benefit was particularly noticeable in tasks requiring the coordination of multiple spatial cues, such as Centrifuge Tube Loading, suggesting the utility of separate supervision for multiple local regions.

**C. Learner-Update Efficiency**
Measurements of learner-update throughput on the Centrifuge Tube Loading task, using 1000 learner steps, quantified the impact of GRAFT's efficiency mechanisms.
- **Prefix-KV Caching:** For the RL-FM policy, prefix reuse increased throughput from 1.99 learner steps/s to 6.30 steps/s (a 3.17x speedup). For the RL-CP policy, it boosted throughput from 2.21 steps/s to 21.96 steps/s (a 9.94x speedup).
- **Single-Step Action Generation:** With caching enabled, RL-CP (single-step generation) was 3.49 times faster than RL-FM (multi-step flow-matching).
These results indicate that the combination of single-step action generation and prefix-KV caching substantially increases learner-update throughput, allowing for more policy updates within a given wall-clock time.

**D. Grounding Diagnostics and Deployment**
Qualitative visualizations of spatial attention revealed that GRAFT's anchors produced more spatially concentrated responses around manipulation-relevant regions (e.g., pipette tips, centrifuge tubes, liquid-transfer targets) compared to GRAFT-Union. This specialized attention pattern is consistent with the observed performance improvements. The visualizations also showed complementary roles for the two camera views: side-view anchors focused on global approach and object alignment, while wrist-view anchors concentrated on local contact and insertion regions, aligning with the intended view-specialized design. Crucially, the policy inference phase of GRAFT operated without any reliance on proposal generators or region masks, confirming its proposal-free deployment capability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GRAFT framework represents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring fine-grained interaction. Its significance can be understood through its dual focus on enhancing task-relevant visual grounding and improving the computational efficiency of online learning.

The key contributions of this work are:
1. **Effective Visual Grounding:** The introduction of a multi-view visual anchor mechanism, supervised by an identity-free multi-region objective, demonstrates an effective method for learning task-relevant local representations. This is achieved during online adaptation and, importantly, without requiring any region proposals or masks during deployment, which simplifies the inference pipeline.
2. **Accelerated Online Adaptation:** The efficient online actor-learner design, which leverages frozen-prefix Key-Value (KV) state reuse during replay updates and combines this with single-pass action-chunk prediction, significantly boosts learner-update throughput. This computational efficiency is crucial for real-robot learning scenarios where interaction time is limited.
3. **Demonstrated Performance on Real-Robot Tasks:** The comprehensive evaluation across four fine-grained biomedical manipulation tasks using a matched 45-minute adaptation budget provides empirical evidence for GRAFT's effectiveness. The framework achieved an absolute improvement of 25 percentage points in final-stage task success rates compared to a baseline with proposal-union supervision, alongside a substantial increase in learner-update throughput (up to 9.94x speedup).

The potential impact of GRAFT extends to applications in scientific and laboratory automation, where robotic systems need to perform delicate and precise manipulations. By enabling robots to acquire fine-grained visual skills from limited interaction and update policies efficiently, GRAFT could facilitate the deployment of more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems in biomedical research, chemical synthesis, and other precision industries. This could lead to increased throughput, reduced human error, and improved reproducibility in experimental workflows.

A recognized limitation of the current GRAFT design is its reactive nature, as the policy does not explicitly track previous actions or task progress. This reactive approach may constrain its performance on longer-horizon tasks where the relevant visual target or necessary actions evolve over time. Future research could address this by incorporating temporal context or memory mechanisms into the policy to enable more complex sequential decision-making and better handling of state-dependent tasks.
