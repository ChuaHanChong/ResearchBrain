# GenLit: Reformulating Single-Image Relighting as Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> GenLit reformulates single-image relighting as a controllable video generation task, leveraging a fine-tuned video diffusion model to dynamically adjust lighting from a moving point source. This approach achieves superior quantitative performance on synthetic data (e.g., RMSE 0.0309, LPIPS 0.0209) and generalizes effectively to real-world images, generating plausible shadows, reflections, and indirect lighting effects.

## Problem

- Single-image relighting is an ill-posed problem due to ambiguities concerning light sources, object albedo, and lack of depth information.
- Traditional inverse rendering methods rely on explicit 3D asset reconstruction and computationally expensive physically based rendering (PBR) simulations.
- Existing neural relighting approaches often struggle with handling complex, spatially varying materials or require specialized light-stage setups and extensive data.
- Achieving fine-grained, controllable local illumination with realistic high-frequency details like sharp shadows and specular reflections from a single image is challenging.

## Method

- The method reformulates single-image relighting as a controllable video generation task, where a point light source moves dynamically over static objects in a video sequence.
- It fine-tunes a pre-trained ==Stable Video Diffusion== (SVD) model by introducing a trainable control branch (similar to ==ControlNet==) that takes a 5D vector as input for light control.
- A custom synthetic dataset, Objaverse-**GenLit**, featuring diverse 3D assets, varied environments, and dynamic point light trajectories, was created for training.
- The 5D control signal precisely defines the point light's 3D position, intensity, and ambient light intensity for each frame, enabling granular manipulation of local illumination.

## Results

- GenLit demonstrated superior quantitative performance on synthetic datasets, achieving lower RMSE (**0.0309**), LPIPS (**0.0209**), and higher SSIM (**0.9780**), and PSNR (**40.7174**) compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- The model exhibited strong zero-shot generalization to real-world images, synthesizing plausible shape-appropriate shadows, reflections, and indirect lighting effects like color bleeding from unseen objects.
- A perceptual study confirmed the realism of GenLit's outputs, with participants consistently rating results on real images as "realistic" (mean/median Likert scores close to 4 out of 5).
- The system effectively diminishes existing shadows in input images and replaces them with new, consistent shadows from the inserted light source, enhancing the overall realism of the relighting process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video foundation models, trained on extensive image and video datasets, implicitly learn a robust understanding of 3D scenes and how light interacts with materials.
> - By modeling relighting as a temporal change in illumination, the inherent capabilities of video diffusion models to synthesize consistent changes over time can be exploited.
> - Training on a carefully constructed synthetic dataset featuring dynamic lighting allows the model to generalize effectively to real-world images without requiring explicit 3D reconstruction.
> - The dynamic change from ambient to point light source, alongside its movement, is crucial for convincing relighting and the synthesis of realistic shadow details.
