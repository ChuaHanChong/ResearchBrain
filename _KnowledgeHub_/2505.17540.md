# RePrompt: Reasoning-Augmented Reprompting for Text-to-Image Generation via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> RePrompt introduces a framework that trains a language model to produce reasoning-infused enhanced prompts for text-to-image (T2I) generation. This approach significantly improves T2I models' ability to interpret complex compositional elements, leading to more accurate spatial relations and object counts without requiring retraining of the T2I backbone.

## Problem

- Current text-to-image (T2I) models often misinterpret or fail to fully account for user intentions in short, vague, or underspecified prompts, leading to visual inconsistencies.
- Existing prompt enhancement methods either suffer from high latency due to iterative refinement or produce linguistically rich but visually unrealistic content from single-pass LLM rewrites.
- T2I models frequently struggle with accurately generating images that reflect complex compositional attributes, such as precise object counts, correct spatial relationships, and attribute bindings.

## Method

- A ==prompting policy==, implemented as a fine-tuned language model (Qwen2.5-3B), generates both a step-by-step reasoning trace and a detailed, visually coherent enhanced prompt from an initial user prompt.
- An ensemble, image-grounded reward model provides multi-faceted feedback, incorporating visual-reasoning rewards (ImageReward and VLM-Reward), a structure reward for format, and a length reward for prompt token count.
- The prompting policy is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), leveraging the non-differentiable visual feedback from an off-the-shelf T2I synthesizer to improve compositional correctness.

## Results

- RePrompt achieved a state-of-the-art overall accuracy of **0.76** on the GenEval benchmark, demonstrating significant improvements in spatial layout understanding (e.g., +**122.2%** on Pixart-Σ) and object counting across diverse T2I models.
- The framework consistently boosted performance across all compositional generalization dimensions in the T2I-Compbench, with notable improvements in spatial compositional scores and numeracy understanding.
- RePrompt maintained efficient inference at 30 seconds per image, delivering superior accuracy while being considerably faster than other optimization-heavy baselines that required 110-140 seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit, structured reasoning into the prompt generation process allows the LLM to 'internally simulate visual implications,' leading to better anticipation and mitigation of compositional errors.
> - Optimizing prompt generation directly with reinforcement learning, grounded in diverse visual feedback, is crucial for aligning LLM-generated prompts with physical reality and enhancing T2I fidelity.
> - Decoupling prompt enhancement from the T2I backbone creates a modular, efficient, and model-agnostic solution that can be broadly applied to various T2I architectures without expensive retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image (T2I) generation has witnessed rapid advancements, driven by large-scale diffusion models like DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, FLUX, SDXL, and Pixart-Σ. These models are capable of generating highly photorealistic and diverse images from textual descriptions. However, a persistent and critical challenge remains: the models often struggle to accurately interpret and fulfill user intentions from short, vague, or underspecified prompts. This "semantic-visual alignment gap" leads to outputs that may misrepresent object counts, overlook spatial relations, or violate real-world plausibility.

This paper addresses this crucial gap by situating itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving areas:
- **Text-to-Image Generation:** It aims to enhance the fundamental capability of T2I models to follow complex instructions, particularly regarding compositional attributes and spatial relationships. Instead of developing a new T2I backbone, it focuses on improving the input prompt, making it a plug-and-play solution.
- **Prompt Engineering and Enhancement:** Traditional prompt engineering has relied on manual crafting or simple LLM-based rewrites. The paper critiques existing LLM-based prompt enhancement methods (e.g., DALL-E 3's internal rewriters) for often generating stylistically rich but semantically inconsistent or visually implausible content due to insufficient grounding in visual semantics. This work offers a novel, learning-based approach to overcome these limitations.
- **Reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs):** Inspired by advances in chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning and self-reflection in LLMs, RePrompt integrates explicit, structured reasoning into the prompt enhancement process. This acknowledges that complex visual generation benefits from a deliberative, step-by-step interpretation of user intent, rather than a direct stylistic rewrite.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Generative Models:** The paper leverages reinforcement learning, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to train the prompt-generating LLM. This is a significant trend in aligning powerful generative models with human preferences and task-specific objectives, often referred to as RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) or RLAIF (Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback). By using downstream visual feedback as a reward signal, the model learns to generate prompts that are "image-grounded," a critical distinction from language-only LLM prompting.
- **Modular AI Systems:** RePrompt proposes a modular framework where a separate language model is trained to enhance prompts, keeping the T2I backbone fixed. This "auxiliary LLM" approach allows for greater flexibility, model-agnostic generalization, and efficiency, as expensive T2I models do not need to be retrained. This contrasts with end-to-end retraining or iterative refinement approaches that incur high computational costs.

By combining reasoning capabilities from LLMs with reinforcement learning grounded in visual feedback, RePrompt positions itself as a state-of-the-art solution for improving the compositional fidelity and semantic alignment of T2I models, without sacrificing inference efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **bridge the gap between concise, underspecified user prompts and the precise visual logic required for faithful text-to-image (T2I) generation.** Existing T2I models, despite their impressive photorealism, frequently struggle with accurately interpreting complex compositional elements, such as object counts, spatial relationships, and attribute bindings.

The key motivations driving RePrompt are:
- **Addressing Prompt Ambiguity and Under-specification:** Users naturally provide short, high-level descriptions. Current T2I models often hallucinate content or misinterpret crucial spatial and attribute-level relationships from such prompts, leading to semantically inconsistent or visually implausible images.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Prompt Enhancement Methods:**
 - **Iterative Refinement Methods:** While capable of improving image quality, these methods suffer from high latency and computational overhead due to repeated image generation rounds. They also often lack explicit scene semantics or compositional reasoning.
 - **Single-Pass LLM Enhancement:** Methods that use LLMs to enrich prompts in one go often generate linguistically fluent descriptions that still result in unrealistic images. This is because the LLMs lack grounding in physical reality and visual semantics, leading to "rich language but unrealistic" content (as illustrated in Figure 1). They might fail to incorporate feedback from downstream visual tasks.
- **Leveraging Advanced Reasoning Capabilities of LLMs:** Inspired by chain-of-thought (CoT) and self-reflection in LLMs, the authors aim to introduce explicit reasoning into the prompt enhancement process. This allows the model to "internally simulate the visual implications" of a prompt, anticipating potential errors (e.g., conflicting object positions) before image generation.
- **Achieving End-to-End Training without Human Annotation:** The goal is to develop a framework that can learn to generate better prompts by optimizing directly for image-level outcomes, rather than relying on expensive human-annotated reasoning traces or handcrafted rules.
- **Developing a Generalizable and Efficient Solution:** The objective is to create a prompt enhancement method that is not only effective but also transferable across diverse T2I backbones and efficient in terms of inference time, making it practical for real-world deployment.

In essence, RePrompt seeks to enable an auxiliary language model to act as a sophisticated "visual planner" that, given a user's prompt, generates a detailed, semantically grounded, and visually coherent enhanced prompt through a reasoning process, guided by reinforcement learning from visual feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RePrompt introduces a novel reasoning-augmented reprompting framework for T2I generation, which trains a language model to produce structured, semantically rich prompts without modifying the underlying T2I backbone. It optimizes this process using reinforcement learning (RL) to directly improve downstream image quality, compositional correctness, and usability.

The framework comprises three main modules (illustrated in Figure 2):

1. **Prompting Policy ($\pi_\theta$):** This is a language model (specifically, Qwen2.5-3B is used as the base LLM) that, given an original user prompt (P), generates two correlated outputs:
 - A **reasoning trace (H)**: A step-by-step breakdown of how to interpret the user's prompt for visual composition, anticipating potential issues like conflicting object positions or spatial incoherence.
 - An **enhanced prompt (P')**: A detailed, semantically grounded, and visually coherent prompt that incorporates the insights from the reasoning trace.
 The policy samples a pair `y = (H, P')`.

2. **T2I Synthesizer ($f_\phi$):** This is a fixed, off-the-shelf text-to-image generation model (e.g., FLUX.1-dev, SD3, Pixart-Σ). It takes the enhanced prompt (P') as input and generates a candidate image (I). Crucially, this component is non-differentiable and its parameters ($\phi$) are kept fixed during RePrompt's training.

3. **T2I RePrompt Reward Model ($R_{total}$):** This is an ensemble, image-grounded reward function designed to provide feedback for the prompt refinement task. It evaluates the generated image (I) in the context of both the original (P) and enhanced (P') prompts. The reward model ensures stable optimization, multi-faceted supervision, and cross-model generalization. It consists of three components:
 - **Visual-Reasoning Reward ($R_{vis}$):** Captures user-aligned preferences and semantic correctness at the image level. It is a weighted sum of:
 - **ImageReward ($R_{IMG}^{pref}$):** Evaluates whether generated images align with human preferences, indicating overall aesthetic quality and realism.
 - **VLM-Reward ($R_{VLM}^{sem}$):** Obtained from a Vision-Language Model (VLM) like GPT-4V, it assesses semantic consistency between the image and the prompt, as well as general visual quality.
 - **Structure Reward ($R_{struc}$):** A binary reward (+1 or -1) that enforces a structured output format for the Prompting Policy: `<reason>...</reason><prompt>...</prompt>`. This ensures interpretability and simplifies parsing.
 - **Length Reward ($R_{len}$):** A binary reward (+1 or -1) that constrains the token length of the enhanced prompt (P') to be within a predefined range (e.g., 15 to 77 tokens), ensuring compatibility with real-world T2I models.

**Optimization Approach:**
The prompt generation process is formulated as a single-step Markov Decision Process (MDP) where the initial prompt P is the state, and generating `y = (H, P')` is the action. The reward `r` is the total reward $R_{total}(I, P, P')$.
The Prompting Policy ($\pi_\theta$) is trained to maximize the expected total reward using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**. GRPO is chosen for its stability in updating policies based on group-wise reward comparisons, which is essential given the non-differentiable nature of the T2I synthesizer. For each update step, multiple candidate `(H, P')` pairs are sampled, images are generated, and rewards are computed to estimate normalized advantages, guiding the policy update.

**Key Design Principles:**
- **Decoupling:** The prompt generation (by $\pi_\theta$) is decoupled from image generation (by $f_\phi$), allowing $\pi_\theta$ to be optimized independently.
- **Reinforcement Learning from Visual Feedback:** Learning from downstream visual feedback directly (via $R_{total}$) allows the policy to develop robust reasoning strategies that are visually grounded, rather than just linguistically fluent.
- **Modular and Generalizable:** The framework is designed to be model-agnostic, applicable to any off-the-shelf T2I model. The reward model also generalizes across different T2I architectures.
- **Variance Reduction:** The explicit reasoning trace (H) conditions the policy on structured compositional information, reducing the variance in the reward signal, which leads to more efficient and stable RL training (as detailed in Appendix A).

**Training Details:**
- The base LLM is Qwen2.5-3B.
- Trained on a curated corpus of 9,000 prompts, adapted from GenEval object-centric templates. 8,000 prompts for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and 1,000 for RL.
- Training done using TRL$^2$ framework on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs for about 6 hours.
- Reward weights for ImageReward and VLM-Reward are both 0.5.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RePrompt demonstrates significant improvements in compositional accuracy, semantic fidelity, and spatial coherence across diverse T2I models and benchmarks, while maintaining computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Performance on GenEval Benchmark (Table 1)

- **Superior Spatial Layout Understanding (Position):** RePrompt achieved remarkable gains in the "Position" category, which assesses the model's ability to understand and generate images with correct spatial relations between objects.
 - **FLUX:** +77.1% improvement over Qwen2.5 3B baseline (0.62 vs 0.35).
 - **SD3:** +78.8% improvement (0.59 vs 0.33).
 - **Pixart-Σ:** +122.2% improvement (0.40 vs 0.18).
 This highlights RePrompt's strength in grounding spatial relationships.
- **Enhanced Counting and Attribute Binding:**
 - Substantial improvements in "Counting" (+13.2% to +22.2% across backbones).
 - Moderate gains in "Attribute Binding" (+2.1% to +9.4%).
- **Overall State-of-the-Art Accuracy:** RePrompt achieves the best overall accuracy (0.76) among all evaluated methods and T2I backbones, consistently boosting scores for FLUX (+11.8%), SD3 (+10.3%), and Pixart-Σ (+6.9%) compared to Qwen2.5 3B-enhanced baselines.

#### 4.2. Generalization Performance on T2I-Compbench (Table 2)

- **Consistent Improvements:** RePrompt consistently improves performance across all six compositional generalization dimensions (color, shape, texture, spatial reasoning, numeracy, complex) and across all evaluated T2I backbones (FLUX, SD3, Pixart-Σ).
- **Significant Spatial and Numeracy Boosts:** It notably boosts "Spatial compositional scores" (e.g., from 0.2494 to 0.3301 on FLUX) and enhances "Numeracy understanding," which are historically challenging areas for T2I models.
- **Versatile Plug-and-Play Enhancement:** These results confirm RePrompt's robustness and generalizability as a versatile, model-agnostic enhancement for diverse T2I systems.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **SFT vs. RL (Table 3):**
 - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) alone offers modest gains (+0.01 overall), primarily improving positional understanding.
 - Reinforcement Learning (RL) alone yields larger boosts (+0.04 overall), directly optimizing for visual correctness.
 - **Combining SFT and RL achieves the best overall results (0.76)**, demonstrating that SFT provides useful priors, while RL is crucial for compositional robustness.
- **Reasoning Contribution (Table 6, Appendix C):**
 - RL without explicit reasoning achieves similar overall improvement to a baseline LLM enhancement (0.68).
 - **RL *with* reasoning yields a more substantial improvement (0.72 overall)**. Categories requiring complex semantic understanding like "Colors" and "Attribute binding" benefit most, confirming that step-by-step reasoning helps decompose and interpret textual prompts.
- **Visual Reasoning Rewards (Table 7, Appendix C):**
 - Individual reward components (ImageReward or VLM-Reward alone) show gains but have limitations, especially in complex tasks like Position and Attribute Binding.
 - **The ensemble reward formulation (combining ImageReward and VLM-Reward) achieves the best overall performance (0.72)**, with consistent improvements in Counting, Position, and Attribute Binding, underscoring the strength of composite reward design for holistic supervision.
- **Training Dynamics with Rewards (Figure 7, Appendix C):** Training with a single reward model leads to unstable reward fluctuations and suboptimal performance. The proposed multi-reward formulation enables smoother optimization and more consistent improvements, highlighting the importance of complementary reward signals.
- **Generalization Across T2I Models (Figure 4):** RePrompt consistently boosts compositional scores across FLUX, SD3, and PixArt-Σ, validating its model-agnostic design. Stronger T2I models used during training led to better generalization.

#### 4.4. Accuracy and Latency Comparison (Table 4)

- RePrompt achieves the **best accuracy (0.76)** on the GenEval overall metric.
- It maintains a significantly **lower latency (30s per image)** compared to optimization-heavy baselines like Idea2Img (140s) and PARM++ (110s), which have lower accuracies (0.69 and 0.72, respectively). This demonstrates a superior balance of quality and efficiency.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Comparisons (Figures 1 and 8-11)

- Baseline models (e.g., DALL-E 3) often fail on spatial relations, object interactions, or attribute binding, generating unrealistic or merged objects.
- RePrompt, by leveraging explicit reasoning and enhanced prompts, accurately grounds complex compositional cues, resulting in images that faithfully reflect the intended semantics (e.g., correct relative positions, distinct objects, and precise attribute assignments).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RePrompt makes several significant contributions to the field of generative AI, particularly in text-to-image synthesis, with substantial potential impact:

- **Elevating T2I Controllability and Fidelity:** The most immediate impact is the significant improvement in the ability of T2I models to accurately interpret and generate images from complex, compositional prompts. By addressing long-standing challenges like spatial reasoning, object counting, and attribute binding, RePrompt pushes the boundaries of T2I controllability, leading to more reliable and predictable outputs.
- **Practical and Efficient Prompt Enhancement:** Unlike computationally expensive iterative refinement methods, RePrompt offers a much faster inference time (30s vs. 110-140s for comparable accuracy methods) while achieving state-of-the-art results. This efficiency is critical for real-world deployment in consumer applications, creative tools, and large-scale content generation pipelines.
- **Generalizable and Model-Agnostic Solution:** RePrompt's design as a plug-and-play auxiliary LLM means it can enhance any existing off-the-shelf T2I model (demonstrated across FLUX, SD3, Pixart-Σ) without requiring expensive retraining of the image generator. This broad applicability reduces barriers to adoption and extends the lifespan and capabilities of diverse T2I architectures.
- **Novel Integration of LLM Reasoning and Visual Grounding:** The core innovation lies in explicitly integrating chain-of-thought reasoning into the prompt generation process, which is then grounded in visual feedback through reinforcement learning. This method effectively "teaches" the LLM to think visually about a scene's composition, anticipating potential errors, a mechanism largely absent in prior LLM-based prompt enhancement.
- **Robust Reward System for RL in T2I:** The development of an ensemble, image-grounded reward model (combining human preference, VLM-based semantic alignment, and structural/length constraints) is a crucial advancement. This multi-faceted reward provides stable, dense, and structured feedback, mitigating common challenges of sparse rewards in RL and enabling effective policy optimization.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** RePrompt opens new avenues for research at the intersection of LLMs, RL, and generative models. It suggests that specialized reasoning modules, trained with visual feedback, can significantly augment the capabilities of multimodal systems. Future work could explore more advanced reasoning patterns, more sophisticated reward models (perhaps trained with active learning), or the integration of RePrompt into more complex multimodal workflows.

**Limitations:**
While highly impactful, the paper acknowledges certain limitations:
- **Modest Gains in Numeracy:** Performance improvements on precise object counting tasks remain modest, suggesting that highly accurate quantitative reasoning in visual generation is still a challenging area.
- **Reliance on Compositional Supervision:** The method requires fine-tuning with compositional supervision, which entails computational cost for data curation and training, although it avoids the need for human-annotated reasoning traces.
- **Reward Model Quality Dependence:** The effectiveness of RePrompt is inherently linked to the quality and fidelity of the underlying reward model. Improvements in reward model robustness and human alignment could further enhance performance.

Despite these limitations, RePrompt presents a powerful and practical framework that significantly enhances the compositional understanding and fidelity of text-to-image generation, marking a notable step forward in making generative AI more controllable and aligned with complex user intent.
