# Exploring Spatial Representation to Enhance LLM Reasoning in Aerial Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A training-free, zero-shot framework for aerial Vision-Language Navigation is proposed, employing a Semantic-Topo-Metric Representation (STMR) to boost Large Language Model (LLM) spatial reasoning. The approach achieved superior navigation success rates in both simulated and real-world UAV experiments, establishing a new baseline.

## Problem

- Existing LLM/VLM-based Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) methods struggle with the unique complexities of large-scale aerial environments, including intricate spatial relationships from a top-down perspective.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) lack inherent precise spatial reasoning capabilities when processing raw visual inputs, often leading to misinterpretations or hallucinations in complex outdoor scenes.
- There is a need for generalizable, training-free, and zero-shot navigation frameworks for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) to adapt to new environments without extensive data collection.

## Method

- A ==training-free, zero-shot framework== is introduced, leveraging a Large Language Model (GPT-4o) as the core agent for action prediction in aerial VLN tasks.
- The **Semantic-Topo-Metric Representation (STMR)** module incrementally builds a ==text-based matrix==, encoding semantic (object identities), topological (relative positions), and metric (distances) information from visual observations.
- An LLM-based navigator uses ==structured prompts==, incorporating the STMR, ==sub-goal extraction==, and ==Chain-of-Thought reasoning==, to decompose instructions and predict fine-grained navigation actions.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a **76.7%** success rate (SR) in simple simulated tasks, an absolute improvement of **26.8%** over the second-best baseline, and maintained leading performance in complex simulated tasks (**12.6% SR**, **+5.8%** over AerialVLN baseline).
- In real-world UAV experiments, the framework demonstrated superior performance with a **40%** success rate and **70%** Oracle Success Rate, significantly outperforming other LLM-based and pretrained VLN methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the comprehensive Semantic-Topo-Metric Representation (STMR) is essential for robust performance, outperforming methods relying solely on partial spatial information or direct visual inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured textual representations, such as STMR, are crucial for enabling LLMs to effectively acquire and apply spatial reasoning in complex, large-scale aerial environments.
> - Direct visual input to VLMs (e.g., GPT-4V, GPT-4o) is insufficient for precise spatial reasoning in aerial VLN tasks, highlighting the necessity for specialized visual encoding like STMR.
> - Dynamically updating sub-goals and leveraging Chain-of-Thought reasoning significantly improves an LLM's robustness and accuracy in sequential decision-making for navigation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses the critical and rapidly evolving field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), specifically extending it to the challenging domain of *aerial* VLN. VLN tasks aim to enable autonomous agents to navigate environments based on natural language instructions and visual observations.

Historically, VLN began with ground-based agents navigating indoor environments using sequence-to-sequence models or reinforcement learning. Recent advancements have seen the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as powerful agents for parsing instructions, understanding scenes, and predicting actions. These LLM/VLM-based methods leverage their powerful reasoning and generative capabilities, often by describing visual observations as text or integrating visual and textual data.

However, the existing LLM/VLM-based VLN methods, while successful in indoor or ground-based outdoor scenarios, face significant limitations in *aerial* environments. Aerial scenes are characterized by large scale, complex spatial relationships, and unique perspectives that make traditional methods struggle to effectively encode spatial information. Challenges include:
- **Scale and Complexity:** Vast outdoor environments with diverse objects and intricate spatial arrangements.
- **Spatial Reasoning:** Difficulty for LLMs/VLMs to infer precise spatial relationships from raw visual inputs or simple textual descriptions like "next to" or "aside."
- **Instruction Irrelevance/Hallucination:** LLMs may overemphasize irrelevant objects or hallucinate outputs when spatial context is ambiguous or insufficient.

This paper specifically tackles these challenges by proposing a novel, *training-free, zero-shot framework* for aerial VLN that leverages LLMs. Its primary contribution to the broader landscape is the introduction of the **Semantic-Topo-Metric Representation (STMR)**. By encoding semantic, topological, and metric information into an LLM-friendly matrix format, the work directly addresses the spatial reasoning deficiencies of LLMs in complex aerial contexts. This represents a significant step towards practical and robust UAV navigation systems capable of understanding human language commands in real-world outdoor settings, moving beyond the constrained environments where most prior VLN research has flourished. It sets a new baseline and provides valuable insights for integrating LLMs into embodied AI for aerial robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) for robust and effective Aerial Vision-Language Navigation (VLN)**. This aims to enable Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) to navigate complex outdoor environments solely based on natural language instructions and visual cues, eliminating the need for manual human piloting.

The key motivations driving this research are:

1. **Addressing Challenges in Aerial VLN:** Existing VLN methods, particularly those leveraging LLMs and VLMs, primarily focus on indoor or ground-based outdoor environments. These methods struggle with the unique complexities of aerial scenes, such as large-scale environments, intricate spatial relationships viewed from a top-down perspective, and the potential for LLMs to misinterpret or hallucinate in the absence of precise spatial context.
2. **Improving LLM Spatial Reasoning:** Current LLMs and VLMs, despite their powerful reasoning abilities, often lack an inherent capacity for detailed spatial reasoning when processing raw visual inputs. Simple textual descriptions of spatial relationships are often ambiguous in open, large-scale outdoor settings. The motivation is to provide LLMs with a richer, more structured representation of space to overcome this limitation.
3. **Facilitating Training-Free and Zero-Shot Navigation:** Developing a navigation framework that does not require extensive task-specific training data or fine-tuning. A training-free, zero-shot approach is highly desirable for practical applications, as it promotes generalizability and adaptability to new, unseen environments and instructions without burdensome data collection and model retraining.
4. **Enabling Practical UAV Applications:** The ability for UAVs to interpret and execute natural language instructions autonomously has immense potential for real-world applications. These include critical tasks like rescue operations, search missions, and delivery services, where human intervention can be minimized, and efficiency and safety can be improved.
5. **Establishing a Strong Baseline:** By proposing a novel framework that significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, the authors aim to establish a new, strong baseline for future research in zero-shot aerial VLN tasks, thereby stimulating further advancements in the field.

In essence, the research seeks to bridge the gap between powerful language understanding and precise spatial navigation for autonomous aerial robots, moving towards more intelligent and adaptable UAV systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for aerial VLN is **training-free and zero-shot**, leveraging a Large Language Model (LLM) as the core agent for action prediction. It comprises three main modules: Sub-Goal Extraction, Semantic-Topo-Metric Representation (STMR), and an LLM Planner.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The aerial VLN task is defined as guiding a UAV through an environment using a natural language instruction $L$. At each time step, the UAV observes its environment (RGB and depth images) and its current pose $P = [x, y, z, \phi, \theta, \psi]$ (position and orientation). It then predicts an action (e.g., go forward 10 meters, turn left 90 degrees) from a predefined action space. Navigation is successful if the UAV stops within 20 meters of the target location.

#### 3.2. Semantic-Topo-Metric Representation (STMR)

The STMR module is central to enhancing the LLM's spatial reasoning. It incrementally builds a textual representation of the environment, integrating semantic, topological, and metric information.

##### 3.2.1. 2D Visual Perception

At each step, the UAV captures an RGB image $I^R_t$ and a depth map $I^D_t$. A 2D visual preceptor, composed of **Grounding DINO** (for open-vocabulary object detection) and **Tokenize Anything** (for captioning and segmentation), processes these images.
1. **Landmark Extraction:** An LLM-driven Landmark Extractor $LE(\cdot)$ identifies detailed landmark categories $C$ from the overall natural language instruction $L$.
2. **Bounding Box Detection:** Grounding DINO $GD(\cdot)$ identifies bounding boxes for these categories within the current RGB image $I^R_t$.
3. **Semantic Mask Generation:** Tokenize Anything $TA(\cdot)$ generates 2D semantic masks $m^{(t)}$ and captions $h^{(t)}$ for the objects within these bounding boxes.
4. **Robustness Filtering:** To prevent over-classification and focus the LLM on relevant objects, a TF-IDF-based text-matching method filters the generated semantic masks. It calculates the cosine similarity between the vectorized instruction landmarks and the generated captions $h^{(t)}$. Only masks exceeding a similarity threshold $\tau$ (0.8) are retained, ensuring that only instruction-relevant objects are considered.

##### 3.2.2. Sub-goal-driven Top-down Map

The 2D semantic masks are projected into a dynamically growing 3D point cloud and then into a top-down map, which captures spatial and topological relationships.
1. **3D Projection:** Depth images $I^D_t$ are used to map each pixel from the segmented image to a 3D point $(X, Y, Z)$ using camera parameters. Semantic labels from the filtered masks are associated with these 3D points.
2. **Voxelization and Semantic Aggregation:** The 3D point cloud is partitioned into discrete voxels. Each voxel aggregates its points, and the semantic category is determined. To handle objects at different heights (e.g., 'vegetation' on a 'building'), the semantic label of the highest object in a column of voxels is typically projected onto the top-down map.
3. **Sub-goal Prioritization:** Crucially, if a category identified in the current *sub-goal* appears in any voxel within a column, that sub-goal-relevant category takes precedence and is projected into the top-down map, ensuring the LLM focuses on immediate navigation objectives. This makes the map "sub-goal-driven."

##### 3.2.3. Matrix Representation

To make the visual map "LLM-friendly," it is transformed into a text-based matrix:
1. **Local Map Extraction:** A fixed-size (100m x 100m) local map, centered on the UAV's current position, is extracted from the global top-down map. This local map slides dynamically with the UAV.
2. **Grid Division:** The local map is divided into a 20x20 grid, with each grid cell representing a 5m x 5m area. This grid size balances precision with the need to represent common small landmarks within a single cell and larger ones across multiple cells.
3. **Semantic Numbering:** Semantic max pooling is applied within each grid cell, assigning the most frequent semantic category (represented by a unique number, e.g., '1' for road, '2' for building) to that cell.
4. **Metric Information:** The grid size (5m) is explicitly provided to the LLM as metric information, allowing it to understand distances.
This matrix effectively combines topological (relative positions of objects), semantic (what objects are), and metric (distances) information.

#### 3.3. LLM-based Navigator

The LLM (specifically GPT-4o) acts as the navigator, taking a structured prompt as input and outputting actions.
1. **Sub-Goal Extraction Module:** This module decomposes the high-level natural language instruction into a sequence of executable sub-goals, facilitating step-by-step reasoning. The status of each sub-goal (todo, in process, completed) is updated dynamically to mitigate LLM hallucinations.
2. **Prompt Construction:** The prompt consists of two main components:
 - **Task Definition:** Describes the aerial VLN task, input format (matrix size, semantic label-to-number mapping), and output format (action space: right, left, up, down, forward, backward, with distance/angle ranges).
 - **STMR-based Planner:** Includes the text-based matrix representation of the current local map, the current sub-goal instruction, and historical actions.
3. **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** The LLM is instructed to use Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, thinking step-by-step (observation-thought-planning-history) before predicting the next action. This structured thinking process helps the LLM make more coherent and accurate decisions.

By integrating these modules, the framework provides the LLM with a rich, dynamic, and LLM-interpretable representation of the aerial environment, enabling it to perform robust zero-shot navigation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the significant effectiveness and robustness of the proposed STMR-enhanced LLM framework for aerial VLN.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:**
 - **Simple Navigation Test Set (Custom):** 1,000 trajectories, up to 100m long, with 1-2 landmarks in common scenarios (public transport, urban).
 - **Complex Navigation Test Set (AerialVLN-S benchmark):** Replicates real-world urban environments, with over 870 object categories. Trajectories average 326.9m, with complex instructions (avg. 83 words).
 - **Real-World Experiments:** 10 outdoor scenes (street, forest), 50m to 200m ground-truth lengths, using a Q250 UAV with Intel RealSense D435i and NVIDIA Jetson Xavier NX, managed by a mobile ground station.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Navigation Error (NE):** Distance from stopping point to destination.
 - **Success Rate (SR):** Percentage of navigations stopping within 20m of the destination.
 - **Oracle Success Rate (OSR):** Idealized SR if any point on the trajectory comes within 20m of the destination.
 - **Normalised Dynamic Time Warping (SDTW):** Combines success with trajectory similarity to ground truth.
- **LLM:** GPT-4o (online API) was used for reasoning.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Rule-based:** Random, Action Sampling.
 - **Learning-based:** LingUNet, Seq2Seq, CMA, AerialVLN (original baseline), Navid (retrained indoor VLN), OpenUAV.
 - **LLM-based:** MapGPT, NavGPT (indoor VLN, adapted with manually defined waypoints for fairness).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results in Simulator

- **Simple Tasks (Table 1):** The proposed method significantly outperformed all baselines.
 - **SR:** Achieved 76.7%, an absolute improvement of 26.8% over the second-best (OpenUAV at 49.9%).
 - **OSR:** Achieved 90.3%, an absolute improvement of 22.3% over the second-best (Navid at 79.9%).
 - **NE:** Reduced to 53.9m, a decrease of 45.6m compared to Navid (99.5m).
 - These results highlight the UAV's superior understanding of spatial position and semantic constraints under STMR guidance in simpler scenarios.
- **Complex Tasks (AerialVLN-S, Table 2):** Even in highly challenging environments, the proposed method showed consistent improvements.
 - **SR:** Achieved 12.6%, an absolute improvement of 5.8% over AerialVLN (7.2%).
 - **OSR:** Achieved 31.6%, a 14% improvement over AerialVLN (15.7%).
 - While overall success rates for all methods decline significantly due to complexity, the STMR-based approach maintained its leading performance, indicating its robustness in complex, large-scale outdoor settings.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Results in Real Environment

- **Performance (Table 3):** The proposed method again demonstrated superior performance compared to both pretrained VLN models and other LLM-based VLN methods in real-world settings.
 - **SR:** Achieved 40%, significantly higher than MapGPT (20%), Navid (10%), and others (0-10%).
 - **OSR:** Achieved 70%, considerably higher than MapGPT (20%) and NavGPT (20%).
 - **Runtime:** The average runtime for LLM-based methods (including ours) was higher (117s for ours, 150-176s for others) due to LLM API latency, compared to learning-based methods (75-82s). The authors anticipate this will decrease with LLM and hardware advancements.
- **Visualization (Fig. 6):** Examples show successful alignment of visual and textual landmarks, guiding the UAV to its destination.

#### 4.4. Case Analysis

- **STMR Effectiveness (Fig. 4):** A demonstration shows the LLM successfully reasoning about its surroundings using the STMR. It accurately identifies objects ("building" on its left) and estimates distances ("river" 50 meters away), proving STMR's value for spatial awareness in vast outdoor environments.
- **Failure Cases (Fig. 5 & 6):**
 - **Ambiguous Instructions:** Lack of clear landmarks in continuous commands ("turn left, then move right") leads to repeated actions or misinterpretations.
 - **Visual Perception Inaccuracy:** Errors in modern perception models due to varying observation angles/distances can cause key landmarks to be missed in STMR, leading to incorrect navigation decisions (e.g., failing to recognize a road).

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Different Spatial Information Types (Table 4):**
 - **Topo (linguistically formed map):** Low SR (4.9%), OSR (12.8%).
 - **Metric (directional distance descriptions):** Improved SR (6.1%), OSR (13.7%) but still poor, as region-level information (like roads, rivers) is hard to capture with point-distance descriptions.
 - **Ours (STMR combining semantic, topological, metric):** Significantly superior with SR (15.0%) and OSR (28.0%), demonstrating STMR's comprehensive spatial representation capacity.
- **Direct Visual Input vs. STMR (Table 5):**
 - Direct input of RGB images into VLMs (GPT-4V, GPT-4o) yielded significantly worse performance (SR 1.1-1.5%, OSR 9.7-10.0%).
 - STMR-based input to LLMs performed much better (SR 10.2-15.0%, OSR 22.4-28.0%). This strongly supports that proper visual encoding (like STMR) is crucial for LLMs/VLMs to acquire effective spatial reasoning abilities.
- **Grid Size of STMR (Fig. 7):** A 5m grid size proved to be an optimal trade-off between resolution and the number of grid cells, providing sufficient detail for outdoor spatial reasoning without overwhelming the LLM.
- **State Update of Sub-goal (Table 6):** Updating the state of the *current* sub-goal (todo, in process, completed) instead of re-planning the entire multi-step path (which didn't significantly improve OSR) positively impacted the Success Rate (SR from 9.0% to 15.0%), indicating it helps with immediate decision-making robustness.

In summary, the main findings strongly support that the Semantic-Topo-Metric Representation (STMR) is a highly effective method for imbuing LLMs with superior spatial reasoning capabilities, leading to state-of-the-art performance in complex aerial VLN tasks across both simulated and real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Exploring Spatial Representation to Enhance LLM Reasoning in Aerial Vision-Language Navigation" carries substantial significance and potential impact across several domains:

1. **Advancement in Aerial Vision-Language Navigation (VLN):** This paper makes a pioneering contribution by designing the *first LLM-based training-free framework for aerial VLN*. This is a critical step towards enabling UAVs to understand and execute natural language instructions in complex, large-scale outdoor environments. By establishing a new state-of-the-art and a strong baseline, it opens new avenues for research in this challenging domain.
2. **Enhanced LLM Spatial Reasoning:** The core innovation, Semantic-Topo-Metric Representation (STMR), directly addresses a known weakness of LLMs: their limited ability to perform precise spatial reasoning from raw or loosely structured visual information. By converting complex visual data into a structured, text-based matrix that explicitly incorporates semantic, topological, and metric information, STMR provides LLMs with a richer context, significantly improving their spatial awareness and decision-making for navigation tasks. This approach could be generalized to other embodied AI tasks requiring fine-grained spatial understanding.
3. **Generalizability and Adaptability (Zero-Shot & Training-Free):** The training-free and zero-shot nature of the proposed framework is highly significant. It means the system does not require extensive task-specific data collection and retraining for new environments or instructions, making it highly adaptable and scalable. This drastically reduces the barrier to deployment in diverse, unseen real-world scenarios, a crucial factor for practical robotic systems.
4. **Practical Applications of UAVs:** The ability for UAVs to navigate autonomously guided by natural language has profound practical implications.
 - **Search and Rescue:** UAVs could be deployed rapidly to search specified areas based on verbal commands, improving response times and covering dangerous terrain without human risk.
 - **Delivery Services:** Autonomous drone delivery could become more robust and flexible, with operators providing high-level instructions rather than precise flight paths.
 - **Environmental Monitoring and Inspection:** Drones could follow complex instructions to inspect infrastructure, monitor wildlife, or survey land, enhancing efficiency and accuracy.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** This work improves human-UAV interaction by allowing natural language commands, making UAVs more accessible and intuitive for a broader range of users.
5. **Robustness in Real-World Scenarios:** The demonstrated success in both simulated and real-world environments, including complex AerialVLN-S benchmarks and custom outdoor scenes, validates the robustness of the approach. While LLM API latency remains a current limitation, its effectiveness even with current hardware and model speeds is promising.
6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also clearly outlines its limitations, such as handling very precise 3D distances and improving the robustness of pretrained perception models under varying observation angles. These identified challenges offer clear directions for future research, including integrating primary viewpoint assistance and developing more resilient perception strategies, further driving innovation in the field.

In conclusion, this research marks a substantial stride in enabling intelligent UAV navigation. By providing LLMs with a powerful spatial representation, it not only pushes the boundaries of autonomous aerial systems but also offers valuable insights into enhancing LLMs' understanding of the physical world, paving the way for more capable and context-aware embodied AI agents.
